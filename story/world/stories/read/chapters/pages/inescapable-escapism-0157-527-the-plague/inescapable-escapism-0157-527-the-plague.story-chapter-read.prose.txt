My knees were weak as I stood there, my heart pounding. My eyes were fixed on the door in front of me, my palms were slick with sweat, and I knew I had to knock, but I couldn’t bring myself to do it. It felt impossible, and no matter how much I wanted to, my arms refused to obey me.

It felt like I’d been out there for too long, though. I had no idea how much time had passed since I’d reached Duncan’s door, but I knew it was at least a couple of minutes, so I needed to just hurry up and knock.

I wanted to. I really did, but I couldn’t. My hands felt like they were filled with concrete. I wasn’t strong enough to lift them, and I was far too aware of how fast my breathing was. I wanted to get it more under control, to calm down a bit, before I faced Duncan, but that hadn’t happened. If anything, it just seemed to be getting more and more shallow, and I was starting to hyperventilate. If I waited much longer, I would be panting, and that would be so weird.

The sudden urge to spin and sprint away from Duncan’s house gripped me again, and I felt my body start to move. My feet shuffled instinctively, turning me away from the front door before I could catch myself, but that was as far as I got.

But I didn’t want to leave. Not really. I knew that I’d need to face Duncan at some point. Even if I did run away, he’d probably text me. And then, when I didn’t answer his messages, he’d phone me. If I ignored those too, he’d probably swing by my house to make sure I was alright, and then I’d need to find a way to explain what had happened to Mom without her getting mad at me for it. Even if I somehow managed to do that, I’d still need to talk to him at some point. We went to the same school, and there was no way I could avoid him there.

I didn’t want to, though. I liked Duncan too much to never see him again. The thought of that made my heart ache. He was my friend. I couldn’t just ignore him, and Phoebe was right. Everything was going to be fine, and I’d probably have a great time. I just needed to knock.

Letting out a shaky exhale, I slowly lifted my hand. My muscles strained, fighting against me as if I were trying to lift a building, but eventually, I managed to do it. My fingers trembled as I squeezed them into a fist, and my pulse was racing in my ears. I didn’t feel ready, but I knew I had to act quickly. If I allowed myself to hesitate, I knew I’d chicken out again, and then I’d be stuck there forever.

My knuckles slammed against the door, the noise far louder than I expected, and birds exploded out of the tree next to the house as if a gun had been fired. My head snapped around, and my heart raced even faster. It felt like it was vibrating in my chest so hard it might burst through my skin, and I scanned the driveway behind me.

It was completely irrational, and I knew it, but part of me was scared that my knock had somehow been loud enough to wake up my mom. I almost expected to see her standing at the bottom of the drive, her face contorted with rage, but there was no one there.

I hadn’t made that much noise, I tried to tell myself. Obviously, the knock had been loud, but my house was too far away. There was no chance mom would have been able to hear it. I could have screamed at the top of my lungs, and she still wouldn’t have heard anything. Even if she did, she probably would have ignored it.

But it did feel like someone was watching me. I could feel the familiar creeping sensation of eyes lingering on my skin, and I couldn’t help but look around again. The driveway was empty, though. I was the only person there, and even the road at the bottom appeared clear. There were no cars approaching or anything.

It was probably just the birds, I decided. They’d started to return to the trees around me, and it felt like they were looking at me. They squawked loudly, their beady eyes fixed in my direction as if waiting to see whether I’d scare them off again.

Behind me, the door was thrown open, and my head snapped around again as my heart leapt into my throat. Somehow, I was simultaneously scared that some random person had opened the door other than Duncan, that he was beaten and tied up somewhere inside, whilst also being weirdly embarrassed at the thought of Duncan seeing me act so weird. Normal people just knocked and waited patiently for the person to answer, and I knew that. They didn’t look around like they were being stalked by a murderer.

Duncan’s familiar face broke into a wide grin as he came into view, and I felt my heart drop back into my chest. The sight of him both soothed my anxiety and made it much worse. I was glad to see him, relieved that he was okay, but also worried and uncertain about everything that was about to happen.

“Hey!” he cried, stepping towards me, his arms outstretched.

I wrapped my arms around him, letting my eyes flutter shut as we hugged. There was something so nice about hugging Duncan. I’d done it before, obviously, but it felt different. I couldn’t quite work out why, but something had changed.

Maybe it was how long it was lasting. Before, I’d just hugged him for a couple of seconds before we both pulled away, but that wasn’t happening. He didn’t let go of me or pull back, and I didn’t either. I didn’t want to. Being held by Duncan just felt too nice, and I didn’t want to let go of him so soon. I was enjoying being wrapped in the warm, comforting bubble of our hug too much.

Plus, he smelled nice. I didn’t normally pay much attention to how he smelled; that felt like a weird thing to notice, but I couldn’t help it as we stood in his doorway, my face pressed against his chest. The scent was somewhat woody and fresh. It was similar to the way the forest near my grandparents’ house smelled after it had rained, but there was an undercurrent of something almost… sweet?

I wasn’t sure what it was, but I definitely recognised the aroma. I’d smelt it before, but I had no recollection of when. Maybe it was just his normal deodorant or aftershave or whatever it was. Perhaps it was the same one he always used, and I’d just not noticed it properly before.

That didn’t feel right, though. A niggling whisper at the back of my mind told me I was wrong. I was confusing Duncan with someone else, someone I’d hugged before. Done more than just hugged, actually.

Seth.

The realisation slammed into me so violently that I almost jerked away from Duncan. I was almost certain that the aftershave Duncan had used was the same one that Seth also wore, and that was where I’d smelt it before. But that didn’t make sense, I told myself. I’d never hugged Seth, much less done anything more with him, and I was certain something had happened with the other person who smelled like Duncan. I could remember the sweetness of his skin against mine.

Blood crept into my cheeks, and I tried to push the thought of that guy, whoever he was, and Seth out of my head. I shouldn’t have been thinking about either of them. I was in reality. We were hugging, and that was all I should have been thinking about.

However, despite how hard I was trying to ignore all thoughts of Seth, guilt had settled in the pit of my stomach, and it was almost impossible not to notice. It felt strangely wrong to be around Duncan when I knew I had Seth in the other world.

We weren’t dating or anything; we barely even flirted, but I was pretty sure I liked him. I didn’t think he liked me, but it still felt almost like I was cheating on him, and I knew it didn’t help that I hadn’t spent any time in the Academy properly for a little while. Part of me was still there, even when my consciousness was not, so it was fine. I still existed. It just made me feel bad.

Maybe Duncan was waiting for me to pull back. The thought came out of nowhere, interrupting my other worries. Perhaps that was why we were still hugging. I could have been gripping him too tightly or something, and maybe he was just hoping I’d let go soon. I might have been making him uncomfortable.

Anxiety swelled within me, and I couldn’t help but pull back, my arms dropping to my sides as Duncan did the same. He was still smiling, though. That had to be a good sign. I couldn’t have made him that uncomfortable, right?

“Hey,” I blurted out, realising I’d never actually greeted him.

That was weird too, I told myself. Normal people greeted each other, and I’d gotten too distracted by the hug and my racing thoughts. I hadn’t even realised that I hadn’t said anything until then.

“Come in,” Duncan said, stepping back to let me enter.

My heart was still beating far too quickly as I followed him into the house. Duncan shut the door behind me as I came to a stop in the hall and carefully slipped my trainers off, tucking them away next to the storage bench.

“You brought a bag?”

Something about the way Duncan said that sounded like a question, and I glanced up at him in confusion.

“Uh… yeah?” I replied, my tone equally uncertain.

“Are you planning on… staying over?” he asked haltingly, his voice rising ever so slightly at the end of the question.

I stared at him as shock crashed into me.

“Oh,” was all I could get out.

“I mean, you totally can if you want to,” Duncan quickly added. “Obviously. You always can, you know?”

He sounded almost nervous as he rambled. He wasn’t meeting my gaze, and his cheeks had turned pink. I could feel mine doing the same. It felt like my face had been set on fire, and I was almost certain he could feel the heat radiating from it from where he was standing.

Duncan thought I was planning on staying over at his house, I thought slowly as horror and embarrassment dawned on me. He must have thought my bag had pyjamas in or clothes for tomorrow or something, that I just assumed I’d be staying at his after our date or maybe that it would be continuing all night, and I hadn’t even considered that.

An image flashed into my head again, a smile in the darkness. I could almost feel the delicious sensation of fingers trailing along my skin, of lips pressing against my neck, and I had no clue who had been kissing me or which world that was in, but I knew it was the guy who smelled like Duncan.

Maybe it was him.

“Oh! No, no,” I cried out. “It’s just gym stuff!”

Duncan’s eyes found mine again, and his eyebrows pulled together.

“Oh… are you going to the gym later?”

He sounded just as unsure as he had when he’d asked whether I was staying at his, and the urge to slip the bag from my shoulders and pull it open thrashed within me. Part of me wanted to prove that was what I had in the bag. I was so worried that he didn’t believe me, that he’d think I just assumed I’d be staying over and was scrambling to come up with an excuse, but I knew it would be weird if I did that. It would seem like I was trying too hard to act normal, and that was worse.

“No,” I told him before a high-pitched giggle escaped my lips. “It was just so I could tell Mom I was going there rather than… you know, coming here. She can be kind of weird about stuff, you know?”

The confusion cleared from Duncan’s face, and he nodded.

“Oh, yeah. That makes sense,” Duncan replied, the smile reappearing on his lips as he moved away from the door. “Have you had lunch yet, by the way?”

“Uhh, no. Not yet.”

I hadn’t really even thought about food that morning. I’d been too anxious and then in too much of a hurry to get ready. It hadn’t even crossed my mind.

“Cool,” Duncan said as he started to head towards the kitchen. “So, did she buy it?”

“Well, she was taking a nap when I went out…” I said, following after him.

Duncan laughed as he looked back at me.

“So, you’re probably fine?”

“I hope so. I’ll find out later, I guess.” I shrugged, but the smile on my face made it clear I wasn’t particularly worried.

It would most likely be okay. Hopefully, Mom would be asleep for most of the day, and then she wouldn’t even question where I’d been when I got back, but if she did, I had my gym stuff with me. I could easily pretend I’d spent the day there.

“What are you feeling?” Duncan asked as we turned into the huge kitchen. “I’ve got a bunch of snacks, crisps, maltesers, magic stars, chicken nuggets… probably some other stuff too…”

He’d pulled open the fridge and was peering into it, and I hovered behind him awkwardly. I always felt so uncomfortable eating food in someone else’s house, and I wasn’t sure why. I even felt weird doing it at Phoebe’s house, and I spent so much time there. Her mom always told me to help myself to anything I wanted, but I still couldn’t bring myself to do it. It just felt like I was doing something wrong.

But Duncan was expecting me to answer him. He’d leaned away from the fridge and glanced back at me, clearly waiting for me to say something, but my brain had turned blank. I couldn’t think of anything, couldn’t form any coherent thoughts, and I could barely remember the items he’d mentioned just a few seconds ago. They’d been chased out of my head by unnecessary panic.

“Umm, I’m good with whatever,” I said.

That felt like the safest option. It was what I normally defaulted to when I was at someone’s house and they asked me about food. Then, Duncan could just pick whatever he was feeling, and I’d have some of it too. I’d still feel a little weird about eating there, but I tended to feel less guilty that way.

Duncan pressed his lips together in a thoughtful expression as he looked back at the well-stocked fridge, his eyes scanning the options. His head cocked to the side for a moment before he pulled both doors shut.

“How about chicken nuggets then?” he asked, before adding, “Quorn ones. Obviously.”

A smile pulled at my lips.

“That sounds good.”

Duncan grinned before pulling the freezer open and rooting around in it for the bag of vegetarian chicken nuggets. I trailed after him as he moved towards the oven and poured the entire bag onto a tray.

“Hey Siri, set a fifteen-minute timer.”

Our eyes strayed towards the device sitting on the counter as we waited to see whether she’d answer, but no response came. I couldn’t help but laugh as the silence stretched on, and Duncan let out a dramatic sigh.

“You might need to try again,” I told him.

He sighed again, dropping his head back for a few seconds.

“Hey, Siri,” he said, his voice louder and his words more clipped. “Set a fifteen-minute timer.”

“Fifteen minutes counting down,” came the response.

Duncan let out a cheer, lifting his arms.

“It worked! Second time’s the charm,” he cried.

“I mean, that’s not too bad,” I said, still smirking slightly.

“Definitely better than last time. I swear it took me, like… five tries, and then it set a three minute timer rather than a thirty minute timer.”

Another laugh slipped from my lips.

“Oh no.”

“I know. I didn’t realise at first, and I was so annoyed,” Duncan said, leaning his hip against the counter. “Do you want a drink or anything? I can do you a great deal on Diet Coke or green mystery drink. Although, it turned out kinda… blue-ish this time.”

A grimace appeared on my lips.

“Wait, why?” I couldn’t help but ask. “What did you put in it?”

“I honestly have no idea! I swear I didn’t put anything that weird in!” he promised. “There’s definitely some rum and vodka in there, and then I added some of the green stuff Mom and Dad brought back from Paris, which tastes awful, by the way, and then a bit of some other stuff, but I don’t think there was anything that should have made it so… neon.”

Equal parts concern and intrigue washed over me.

“Neon?” I repeated.

Duncan nodded, his expression turning playfully alarmed.

“Yeah… I’ll show you when we go upstairs, but it kinda looks like it’s glowing,” he said. “But I’m sure it’ll be fine. Blue things always taste good, right?”

“Right,” I agreed. “Did you try some already?”

He laughed again before smiling at me sheepishly.

“Maybe…”

“Oh no,” I said. “And?”

His tone did not fill me with confidence. He seemed reluctant to answer my question, and my concern grew as I saw him wince. He lifted a hand to his hair, running his fingers through it as he avoided meeting my gaze.

“It’s not too bad,” he replied in a tone that made it clear he was lying.

I couldn’t help but burst out laughing again, and he joined in.

“That bad?” I asked once I’d stopped.

“I mean… I’ve definitely had worse?” he said. “But I learnt my lesson, so it didn’t curdle this time.”

“Oh, good.”

“You’ll see.”

Duncan grinned at me as he ruffled his hair again, and I found myself watching the movement. My eyes felt drawn to it, and it took me a few seconds to realise that his hair looked different to the last time I’d seen him. It was shorter.

The urge to run my fingers through it came out of nowhere, and I had to press my back against the edge of the counter I was leaning against to stop myself from moving towards him. It just looked so soft and fluffy.

“Did you get a haircut?” I asked, realising silence had settled over the room again.

“Oh, yeah,” Duncan replied, looking away quickly. “I got it done this morning. It was getting kind of long, so…”

I nodded, unsure of what else to say. At first, I didn’t think anything of it, but a quiet voice in the back of my head whispered that it was interesting timing. Our date was planned for that afternoon, so perhaps…

It was probably just a coincidence, though. It seemed unlikely that he got it done specifically because we were going to see each other. He was probably just walking past the barber shop and realised he needed a cut. It was just good timing, nothing more.

“It looks nice,” I said, the words leaving my mouth before I could stop them. Immediately, embarrassment swelled within me. “I mean… that length suits you.”

My cheeks had only just had the chance to return to a normal temperature before they began to burn again, and I tore my eyes away from Duncan, staring at the tiled floor. That was a terrible save. I’d hoped that by telling him the haircut suited him, it would sound less like I was complimenting his looks and more just… stating a fact, but I knew it didn’t.

It had just slipped out, though. I hadn’t meant to say it out loud. It was just a thought, and I needed to be more careful. Otherwise, something else could get out, and I wasn’t sure what it would be, but I didn’t want to find out.

I kept my eyes fixed on the floor for as long as I could, not wanting to meet his gaze again, but I couldn’t bear it. I needed to know how he’d reacted to my mistake, and my eyes darted towards him before I could stop myself.

He was looking down, but the smile tugging at his lips made something inside me melt. My heart clenched, and my stomach dropped, leaving me strangely breathless. I couldn’t look away even as he lifted his gaze and met mine once more.

“Thanks,” he said, his voice soft. “You look beautiful.”

I thought my cheeks had been hot before, but somehow, they grew even more scorching. My entire face burned, and I ripped my gaze away from Duncan, looking anywhere else apart from at him.

He didn’t mean it. That was my immediate thought. He’d just said it before I’d said his hair looked good. That was it. He didn’t truly believe I looked beautiful. He couldn’t. At best, I looked fine. I didn’t look awful, but I wasn’t actually beautiful.

“Thanks,” I muttered.

Silence settled over us again, but that time, I couldn’t force myself to break it. I was fully aware of how long it had been since either of us had spoken, and the longer the silence stretched on, the more uncomfortable I got, but my mind refused to come up with anything to say. It was too distracted, too preoccupied by what Duncan had just said.

“So…” Duncan started. “How was Scotland? Oh, wait, fuck! I offered you a drink before! What do you want?”

I laughed, relieved that he’d been the one to speak and that he’d changed the subject. I’d completely forgotten that he’d even asked what I wanted, and suddenly, I was parched.

“Oh, yeah. A Diet Coke would be great if you have some?”

“Of course,” he replied, pushing away from the counter again. “So, Scotland?”

My eyes followed him across the room, watching as he stopped in front of the fridge and pulled it open.

“It was okay,” I told him with a shrug. “Same as it is every year. Thanks.”

I took the can he offered me and cracked it open, taking a sip of the sparkling drink inside.

“No worries. You get up to anything fun when you were there?”

Part of me was almost tempted to lie because I wished I had something to tell him, but there was nothing. I just spent my days reading, in another world, and working out, and I knew that was boring. There wasn’t much to say about that.

“Not really. I mean, I mostly just chilled and texted you and Phoebe.”

“Fair. How’s she finding Paris now? The last I heard, she was debating setting fire to a bakery?” he said with a chuckle.

“She was?” I asked. “I do not remember her saying that. But then again… she does threaten to do that quite often.”

Duncan snorted.

“She does. Yeah, she posted a story on Instagram about it a couple of weeks back. Apparently, the bakery just under their apartment stopped selling one of the pastries she loved, and when she asked if they were going to start doing it again, they basically told her ‘maybe’?” he said, his eyebrows drawing together as he tried to recall what had happened. “Or they said something like ‘perhaps we will, if we want to’, I think?”

I laughed. She’d sent me several messages about it towards the beginning of the summer, and every single one was hilarious to me. There was another bakery less than three minutes away from the place she was staying that did the pastry she wanted, but it just wasn’t the same, she’d claimed.

“Oh yeah,” I said. “That was it.”

“Has she recovered from it yet, or is she still distraught?” Duncan asked with a barely concealed grin.

Cocking my head to the side, I considered Duncan’s question. Phoebe hadn’t mentioned it in a few weeks, so I assumed she’d gotten over it, but there was no way of knowing.

“I think so,” I told him. “Although, something else did happen the other day.”

“Oh?” Duncan asked as he lifted his can to his lips.

I had to pause to gather myself as I struggled to hold back the laugh that threatened to escape.

“Yeah… she thinks she might have the plague.”
