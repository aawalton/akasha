After the conversation wound down, I noticed that Vera was quite pensive.

“What are you thinking about?” I asked her.

“Uh? Nothing,” she said quickly.

But she also kept staring into the distance, as if through me.

“Do you always wear that key around your neck?” she asked.

“What, this one?” I asked, pulling out my house key for a brief moment.

She smiled. “That one, yeah. I was just wondering why you wear your house key like it’s jewelry, but then again, it sort of is, isn’t it? It has such a strange design.”

“It does,” I agreed.

“Was it one of your grandpa’s quirks? Maybe he was big into gothic jewelry, or a metalhead, or he’d been one of those punks when he was younger.”

I chuckled. “Maybe,” I said. Noticing that she was still looking, I pulled the key out again and let her examine it for a while.

Maybe she liked it. It was certainly interesting, and after wearing it for so long I think it had grown on me as well. I also appreciated her attempt at making me think about Gus through another lighter lens, after all the heavy talks.

“It certainly stands out, doesn’t it?” I asked when she gave it back and I put it back on. Under the shirt it was hidden enough, but anyone who even caught a glimpse of it surely saw how strange it was.

“Perhaps you should wear more accessories, you know, to camouflage it,” Nadia offered. “I think they would look good on you.”

Vera was nodding along. “I concur. But it has to be something as nice and particular as the key, you know?”

“Ohhh, I know!” Nadia said, throwing herself at me to fish out the key again. “Let’s go shopping together tonight after your winter market shift, shall we?”

And as we flirted and talked, I saw a shadow pass over our manager’s face while Nadia wasn’t looking. Perhaps she thought I didn’t see it, and I almost wouldn’t have, if not for the fact that I stopped time on purpose to study her and I stared at her for the whole duration of the stop.

It felt surreal, and I stood perfectly still so that nobody would notice any jump when the duration finally ended, but even then, as I looked at her, I felt something in me. In her face, I thought I could read longing, and a bit of sadness, and those same emotions welled up within me as if in a mirror of what I was seeing.

She was so alone, wasn’t she? And she clearly liked the jewelry, or at least the idea of it, but it would surely clash with her clean, polished looks. She would never buy it on her own, would she? Not the gothic, weird stuff. And why, because society?

Then time resumed, sound and smells rushing back to me. I tried to let it go, of course, but in hindsight I don’t think I did a good job of it. An idea was forming already, for later, for the shopping trip.

After we left the bistro, a little mystery was solved. I looked back and saw the sign hanging above the entrance, “Claire’s Table” in big bold red letters. That’s how Vera knew Claire’s name. Always observant, our manager. And I was sure Nadia was too, it was I who was a bit scatterbrained at times. But then again, with two capable women like them keeping me in line, I could perhaps afford to be. In moderate amounts.

As it was, it was time for me to go back home and clean up for the night shift at the market, and for Vera to return to the CRC. We said our goodbyes and left, with Nadia dragging me home, eager to get some attentions after a long and busy (boring, for her) morning.

***

Alright, well, now I was bored. The shift was uneventful like always, which was a good thing with all these people around, and I was happy for them. But there’s a limit to how much people watching you can do, and how happy you can be that others get to enjoy the Christmas atmosphere and the colorful lights and all the foods and smells and sounds of the market before you start wishing you could partake in that yourself.

At least I would, once the shift was over.

The old fantasy came back. Me, slipping into the crowd, stealing the powers from people with a single touch they could never detect until it was too late. The anger from earlier seemed to fuel it, and I almost felt a righteous sense of urgency at the thought.

Nadia’s mutation was still there, still holding my only slot, and I could see her face and vulnerability as if she was there with me. If I had touched someone and left them alive, they would still be there afterward, powerless, a living proof that powers could be taken, and that would be as bad as a body. The take was never free. That was what Nadia had meant, the slot was not the only thing stopping me. Gosh, I loved her so much, and I didn’t even realize how much the thought of her let me calm down until the shift was finally over and I didn’t even feel the time pass.

“And now,” Nadia said after I changed back into my civilian clothes. “Time for our nice date.”

It was a nice date. We strolled through the market, and we did all the things I watched the others do. We got hotdogs and fries, bought tiny things to decorate the house, and wandered through the streets under the lights as the night deepened and the people slowly began to wander away.

We talked about the day, a long day for both of us, boring and hectic at opposite times. Nadia told me she worked on the garden some more while I was on shift, getting busy while all I did was stand there and stare at the people menacingly. Just like she had just sat there in the morning while I was getting busy hunting pests.

So, according to her, this made it even.

“And,” she added. “I’d say the long day was more than worth the money, wouldn’t you?”

“Oh yes,” I agreed, stopping at a particular stall.

“Speaking of long days,” she said. “How did it go with the intrusive thoughts, my prince?”

“They were there,” I admitted. I would never lie to her, I decided. Not anymore. “But I managed. It feels a bit easier now.”

“I’m glad,” she said with a smile, then she noticed where I had stopped and a grin appeared on her face. “Oh, doing some shopping?”

Indeed, the stall was full of Nordic, Celtic and gothic jewelry of all kinds. “Why not? We said I should wear more of this stuff didn’t we?”

She helped me choose a couple trinkets. Another necklace, because why not indeed, and a ring. Then I told her she could buy whatever she wanted, so that we could match styles, and she froze for a moment.

Gingerly, her hands went towards a little item I had already noticed she had been eyeing. Something that was clearly meant to be worn to simulate pointed ears, something straight out of a fantasy story.

Or, in her case… something that would be just perfect on her mutated, fae looks. I said nothing about it, even though she noticed, and held her hands and kissed her little nose while she looked at me misty eyed. I paid, and then saw something that drew my eye and reminded me of the half-formed plan from earlier at the bistro.

“Say,” I began. “Do you think Vera would appreciate a little gift?”

“She’d love it,” Nadia replied immediately, as if waiting at the ready for me to say the line.

“Just as a little thank you,” I said.

She chuckled. “Of course, did you see something that calls to you?”

“That over there,” I said, pointing.

The man at the stall was just about to turn around and serve some other customers when he stopped and smiled knowingly. He was short, looking the part of a dwarf selling his wares, clad in his own stuff. He clearly believed in what he sold, unlike some other fake jewelry stalls we’d seen, and he’d been the reason we stopped here in the first place.

On the Nordic tray sat two ravens, metal and black shiny feathers, precious stones on a silver chain meant to be worn as a necklace. They were all alone, perched on a tall piece of roughly cut wood, still with its peeling bark clinging to it. Like royalty, with all the other little things on display below them, except they didn’t look arrogant.

“Ah,” the man said. “You have a good eye, young lad. Gothi and Gethli, from the old northern stories. The one-eyed god’s loyal animals, and even though his name is now lost, theirs is not. They have another name each. Memory and Thought.”

I reached for the one on the right. “Gethli, Thought,” the man said. “You aren’t thinking about leaving poor Gothi all alone, are you?”

We paid for the one, and we left.

“What a strange man,” Nadia said. “Trying to get you to buy two.”

“And what a strange legend,” I added.

I began to think about how and when I’d give Vera her raven. Even though the details of the story the man told us faded, Gethli–Thought–was the one I’d always meant for her. Back then I didn’t even know why, but I felt it was right.

Gothi, the female raven, Memory, stayed on the tray, all alone, without her mate.
