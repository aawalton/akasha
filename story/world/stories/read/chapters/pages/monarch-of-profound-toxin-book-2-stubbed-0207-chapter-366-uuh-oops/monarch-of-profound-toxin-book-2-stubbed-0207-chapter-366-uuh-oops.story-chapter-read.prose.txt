Chapter 366

“She’s doing what?” Eik asked.

He and Andihar had been sitting together at a cafe and discussing the feasibility of a total evacuation of Gimleh in case of a major invasion of the region by the Lord of the Moon and his forces, when the topic had drifted and eventually landed on the former Oracle of the cult of Moon Shall Swallow.

Moon Shall Swallow had been a cosmic cult operating according to the creed of the Lord of the Moon. Since their inception ages ago, a long line of successive oracles installed in roles as spiritual figureheads, had prophesized the return of the Lord of the Moon brought about by the cult.

And that, upon his return, he would bring about a glorious revival of the Unified Mass that would eradicate what they considered to be aspects that prevented a higher, more meaningful existence, and establish all people would need to reach enlightenment.

To the cult and its zealous members, no deed had been too vile to be justifiable if it brought them closer to the goal of bringing back their Lord. Longer than anybody could remember, the Nidafjeld Alliance had been one of the greatest opposing forces to this mission.

This had lasted all the way until the final, and now former, oracle had one day, somehow, tapped into a different prophecy. A prophecy that showed no such utopia, but instead revealed to her what would really follow an unchallenged resurrection of the mighty Worldbreaker. A universe swallowed up by an eternal, all-consuming darkness.

When she had discovered Eik locked up in the cult’s capital city of Luna and pulled him into a plan to quash the very mission she had previously been working to complete, he had been both confused and well and truly inadequate for what he was supposed to accomplish.

He couldn’t even wake up on time, so how had he been meant to safe anything, let alone the universe?

Well, as it turned out, it had actually gone kind of okay. The Lord of the Moon was very much at large, but the main force of the cult had been exterminated successfully, and even whatever cells might be left scattered throughout the Unified Mass would not be able to do anything significant.

And now, Andihar, who had been giving the Oracle of Moon Shall Swallow room and board, was saying that… “She has been locked in her room for I don’t know how many days now.”

“Why?”

“Truthfully, I am not sure. I regret to say that I myself have been rather busy these past few weeks. One of my employees, who has been delivering meals to her since she first came to live at the estate, was the one to inform me.”

“And what did she say when you asked her what was going on?”

“I haven’t talked to her yet.”

“Really?”

“My wife tried, but the Oracle has actually put up a relatively powerful barrier around her room and—”

“A barrier?” Eik exclaimed, flying out of his chair, the legs screeching against the floor. Around the cafe, eyes stared and Eik sheepishly sat down again. A mask concealing his face was the only reason that their table wasn’t already swarmed by people looking to express their gratitude for what he was doing for them or get a foot in the door of his business.

And while that was certainly a nice gesture and definitely something Eik appreciated. It was becoming a little tough to deal with day in and day out.

“Why are you letting a barrier stay up in your own house?”

Andihar sighed. “I said a relatively powerful barrier. One of my friends peered right through and she’s just sitting cross-legged on her bed. I also had a few people check, and there is no other magical activity besides the barrier. I’m honestly not nervous that she’s communicating with anyone outside, if that’s what you’re concerned about.”

Eik was silent for a few moments, his frown growing deeper by the second. “Well, I wasn’t before, but now I am… I just figured if it was me, I would hate it if someone erected a whole ass barrier in my house.”

“My house is quite big. I didn’t even know until someone told me.”

“Okay, okay, rich guy.”

“You’re many times richer than me, Eik.”

“Yeah, well… Screw you, homie.”

Andihar chuckled and dug into a flat tart topped with some kind of purple puree. None of them had been to this cafe before so exploring their assortment of pastries was a bit of an adventure. “It’s good.”

“You seriously think she’s trying to communicate with the Lord of the Moon?” Eik asked.

The muscled elf blinked. “No, I specifically said that’s not what I think.”

“Yeah, sure, but that’s what people say when they aren’t ready to admit that what they actually think is the opposite of whatever they’re saying,” Eik said, tilting his head from side to side. “Therefore, what you must really be thinking is that she really is communicating with someone on the outside.”

Andihar leaned back and shot him an arched brow, chewing on his second mouthful. “No, I really don’t. But if you’re that worried, you can come home with me and check on her. The only reason that I’ve let the barrier stand is that I’m afraid it’s damaged the surrounding rooms. I’ve been waiting for a guy to come to remove it safely. But I guess I can just have you pay for the repairs.”

***

Andihar’s house was as lavish as always.

There were enormous sitting rooms on every floor with way higher ceiling than any person could possibly need. There were so many spare rooms that Eik wouldn’t be surprised if the family had at least a dozen squatters holed up somewhere in the far corners of the building.

In fact, Eik was willing to bet that Andihar didn’t even know how many spare rooms there were in his house.

Every time he came here, it made Eik appreciate how nice and cozy his own home was.

“So this is it, huh?” Eik wondered out loud, knocking on the surface of the barrier with a finger. “I’m definitely not feeling any energy signatures leaving or entering, so you’re probably right that there’s nothing suspicious going on.”

“That’s what I said.”

“Then what’s the point of it?”

Andihar shrugged. “I don’t care much for what a cultist of the Moon Shall Swallow is up to.”

“Fair enough. I’ll break it then.”

“Do as you’d like.”

Hands buried in his pockets, Eik walked into and through the barrier, the wall of energy an utterly meaningless obstacle that he could annihilate as easily as if it had been made of soap bubbles. “Oracle?” he called into the room as he pushed the door open.

Something heavy obstructed the wooden door but Eik didn’t have a problem ignoring that either. As he stepped inside, something tumbled across his path in front of his feet, the soft sound of fabric slapping on the floor.

“Vendekaabe?” Eik asked and knelt down. “Hey, can you hear me?” He flipped the man over onto his back, the deep hood of his cult robes falling away from his face. The cultist who had betrayed his own beliefs on the order of his oracle looked like he had been dead for months.

Ashen and gray skin had dried up like parchment, now pulled against his facial bones so tightly that it looked more like a fleshless skull. His eyes were sunken and dried up to the point of nearly disappearing.

A pulse of aura through the body convinced Eik against any doubt that the man was one hundred percent dead.

“What in the world?” Andihar breathed.

Eik was already moving to the Oracle. Just as Andihar had described, she was sitting cross-legged on her bed, her hood falling over her face as well. And she wasn’t moving at all.

But she was alive. There was no reason to send aura through her to know that. He could see her breathing, even if it was slow and terribly shallow.

“What’s going on with them?” Eik asked. “Vendekaabe looks like he has just been dug out of a grave.”

Tentatively, he put a hand on the Oracle’s shoulder and shook her lightly. She had been sitting upright as if in a trance, but the moment he moved her, her head lolled forward lifelessly, her chin coming to rest against her chest. “Oracle?” Eik called into her ear. She didn’t react. “I never actually learned her real name. I wonder if she even had one or if any identity she might have had before was forcibly wiped in favor of the whole enlightened prophet thing. Did you ever find out?”

“I never asked,” Andihar said gruffly.

“Ah, right. Of course.” Andihar, Gul, and really all of the other Alliance members who had interacted with the Oracle one way or the other, had shown varying degrees of distaste for her. And none of them had ever fully trusted her. Eik hadn’t really either, but he had probably been the one most willing to give her a chance.

Even now, whether her information had been good and their successes were a result of this, or her information had been bad and lead to their failures was honestly not entirely clear, and at this point, Eik didn’t really care.

The fact that Andihar had been willing to let her remain at his estate despite his suspicions spoke volumes about his character. Eik respected that.

“Hey, Oracle,” he called again, louder this time. “Wake up!” As he shook her once more, he also sent a pulse of aura through her body.

The moment his energy entered her, she abruptly snapped back as if electrocuted. It was audible when the vertebrae in her spine broke and the back of her skull struck her shoulder blades with force. He may have been X-rank, but Eik was so surprised that he nearly pissed his pants right then and there, and he had to stop himself from annihilating her on pure reflex.

“What the fuck!” he yelped and leaped back, crushing a small nightstand as if it had been made of dry crackers.

Like the possessed child in a horror movie, she sat silent and still for a moment as the two men stared. Her neck hung down her back so they couldn’t see her face.

Then, at once, she flew forward toward Eik with a shriek like a banshee. Black miasma flooded from her eyes, ears, nostrils, and mouth.

What would have happened after that, they never did find out, because, frightened, Eik’s reflexes took over properly this time, and he slapped her in the face with considerable force. Her screech was cut off abruptly as her head and entire torso exploded in a bright, red vapor.
