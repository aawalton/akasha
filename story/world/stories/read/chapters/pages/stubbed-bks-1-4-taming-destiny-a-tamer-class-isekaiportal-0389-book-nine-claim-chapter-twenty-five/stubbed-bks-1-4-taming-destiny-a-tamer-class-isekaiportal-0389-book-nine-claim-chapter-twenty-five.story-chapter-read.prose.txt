I wish Kalanthia were here! I’m actually sweating despite my effort being entirely magical and mental. It must be a psychosomatic response.

Mana pours out of me. Every second that passes sees my reserves shrink. I’ve both consumed the best mana regeneration potion I had and have applied Willpower Leverage to Wisdom. At only five percent of my Willpower stat, it doesn’t offer a huge amount, but every little bit of extra mana helps. The sooner I can rank up the Skill, the better.

Before me, I see the fruit of my efforts: a massive ball of red- and black-tinged orange molten rock hovering in mid-air. Creating the lava is the biggest drain of my mana, but keeping it up in the air is taking the majority of my focus. I’m treading a knife’s edge – creating enough air-pressure to float the heavy rock, but without stirring the air currents so much that the roc realises I’m here.

The best method I’ve found is to keep the molten rock spinning, wrapped in several counter-rotating shells of air that prevent plumes of hot air or drips of molten rock escaping and alerting the murderous Tier four beast below me to my presence. The rotating shells have the added advantage of repurposing the heat given off to power the air currents that keep it in the air, reducing my mana drain by a little.

It’s a massive challenge, and I wish that my expert Earth-Shaper companion could be with me. If Kalanthia were here, she’d at least be able to take control of the lava after it was created, allowing me to focus on creating it.

As it is, she’s still down on the ships. I’d summon her but I just don’t have the mana – I’m not sure I would have even with a full resource pool. Besides, powerful as she might be, she’s little more than a kitten against a Tier four. Especially when, literally, out of her element.

Aingeal is someone else I wish were here, but I don’t dare call him to me – if the roc traces his path to my position, I’ll be toast.

I have to just deal with what I have. I’m determined to push this as far as I can – when considering the size of the bird below me, I need to go all-in if I want to have any noticeable effect.

When the effects of the mana potion run out, I pull out another. This will be the last I can take for a while, though – I already took my first of three while getting Noir out of the tornado and I don’t have any cleansing potions on me.

When those effects run out too, I pull on another source – my companions. Not all of them are able to help right now. Hunter and River are both using their mana – Hunter is probably activating runes, and River is probably using her Fire Herbalism to whip up healing potions for the injured. Kalanthia, however, willingly sends me a good deal of magic – based on the frustration that accompanies it, she’s annoyed that she can’t help more. No one else is able to give as much mana as she is, but, again, every little bit helps.

By the time I really run out of mana to use to generate more lava – I still need some to keep both it and myself up in the air – the ball has grown to more than twice my height in diameter. It seems immense when I look and feel it with my magic. But when compared to the roc, it seems like a pitiful amount.

But I can’t hold this any longer. I will have to hope it’s enough.

Having no idea how the roc will react, I reach out through the Bond network, letting them know where I am in my scheme.

I’m about to drop it. Be prepared for anything.

Be careful, Markus! River worries and several others echo her words. I accept their concern grimly – my Stealth and Fade are still in place, but that’s no guarantee against a Tier four. I rise a bit further into the air to give myself some more breathing room. But not too far, or the lava will begin to cool before it hits.

As I relax my hold on the air-magic surrounding the sphere, I instead redirect my focus into entering the lava and encouraging more heat into it. Master Fire-Shaping makes that an easy task, even with limited magic and incredibly hot molten rock. The orange brightens and the black-tinges disappear as rock that’s starting to solidify melts again.

The ball of lava, no longer held up, starts to fall through the air. At first, it’s relatively slow, but it soon gathers pace. The ball flattens into a pancake, and then fragments a little as small bits at the edges break off. I don’t make an effort to bring it back together – I’d rather concentrate on heating what I can, while I can. Anyway, the more of the roc is affected, the better.

I know the instant the lava strikes. The molten orange liquid splatters across the centre of the roc’s back – bull’s eye. The roc stiffens, and then screeches in agony, its wings extending impossibly wide. The boom of its cry shudders through the air, unbearable even above it and out of the sound’s direct path. My consciousness is knocked entirely out of the lava.

That’s not a bad thing – I might not be able to heat the lava any more, but it does mean that I’m able to get out of the way of the roc’s inevitable attempt to take revenge.

Only, when I return my consciousness to my body, I find that I’m already moving.

My upper arms are clutched in black claws and black wings beat hard, pulling us in the direction of the roc’s tail.

Noir! I exclaim, surprised at his emergence.

You shouldn’t make yourself so vulnerable without me there, he scolds – deservedly, I suppose.

I thought you weren’t feeling great, I argue. I wanted to give you time to process.

Noir grunts, the sound unmistakably disgusted.

As if I would remain safe while you put yourself in danger.

He makes a good point, and I send him a sense of gratitude. A brief flurry of activity has me on his back instead of held by his claws. It’s a relief to be able to sink into his scales instead of subconsciously battling to keep myself up in the air.

I look at the roc and am pleasantly surprised. It’s further away than it was before – either the weight of the rock on its back or the distraction of the agony has made it sink in the air. It seems fully occupied with the blow I’ve dealt it, not even searching for the one who caused the agony.

Get closer, I urge Noir. He responds dubiously, but wheels around in the sky and dives closer to the roc.

Triggering my magic sight, I’m further heartened. The earth-magic within the lava is warring with the roc’s air-magic. It’s struggling to regenerate itself because of the way earth counters air – as I experienced when trying to hold the lava up in the air, it’s far easier for a stone to pierce air than it is for air to resist earth.

Curiously, the heat of the lava is actually less useful than I thought. It burns the roc’s flesh, but gives more lift to its air-magic. Which makes sense, I suppose – hot air rises. It’s the rock that is the most detrimental.

That gives me a clear path forward. My Earth-Shaping isn’t that great at creating rock out of nothing, but if I use Lava-Shaping to generate low-temperature – relatively-speaking – lava, I have to use far less magic than when I was trying to generate the completely molten stuff earlier. I start sending out chunks of the thick, black-tinged substance as frequently as my mana regeneration allows.

With the roc so distracted by the lava, I'm even able to grab some of the heat in my environment and transform it back into mana for me to use – with the positive benefit of making it harder for the roc to hold itself up in the air.

With the roc so distracted, there’s another possibility now too.

Ivor, are you able to bring Kalanthia up here? I ask the other alcaoris. By this point, I’m not sure who is stronger between him and Noir – Ivor definitely beat him before the black dragon got his Class, but now….

Ivor seems uncertain for a moment, and then sends me a strong positive feeling. It seems like it takes forever, but it’s probably only a couple of minutes before I see the pair appear.

They look more than a little odd – an elephant-sized nunda perched on the back of a horse-sized alcaoris, and neither appearing all that happy with the idea. But they’re here, and the shimmering at their edges indicates that it’s not only the roc’s distraction that prevented it from spotting them. Kalanthia’s also pulled her domain in tightly so the Tier four won’t feel it.

Thanks for joining me, I tell them gratefully. Kalanthia sends me a grumpy look.

You owe me Cores, she informs me firmly. I don’t take it to heart – we both know I’ll give her what she needs. She just wants to express how much she dislikes this.

Then, going against what we planned, she leaps off Ivor’s back.

Kalanthia, what are you doing? I exclaim, watching as she lands lightly on a crust of hardened lava. Her claws clamp down to hold her in place, incredibly actually crunching straight through the crust. Immediately, the rock starts spreading noticeably. Miraculously, the roc doesn’t react.

It’s better this way, she dismisses. Given the evidence, I decide not to argue.

Ivor, please remain close and catch her if the roc throws her off. Noir, let’s focus on another area.

It becomes a battle of attrition, though one that we’re destined to lose without any other help – the Tier four is just too big and too powerful. The one good thing about the heat of the lava was that it kept the roc distracted with the awful searing into its flesh. As the lava cools, that distraction fades. And, even as we fight the air-magic embedded in its flesh with our earth-magic, the roc itself begins to search for the attackers that have so injured it.

It twists its head back and forth. Noir and I both use Stealth to hide from it and I also have Fade active while I continue working grimly.

But, when a nearby screech shocks me almost out of my skin and one silver eye fixes itself directly on me, I realise that my low-level Skills simply can’t hold up against an observant Tier four who’s making an effort.

Noir dodges the wind tendrils that dart towards us, but there are too many. We’re wrapped in them from head to toe.

I try to breathe, but I can’t – the air around me is held under the control of another and it is not permitting me access. My lungs and throat burn as I instinctively put more effort into my attempt to draw in air – to no avail.

Noir is no better and throws his head around frantically even as terror once more thrums the Bond between us.

Calm, I soothe him. We’ll get out of this, I promise. Moving fast just uses up more oxygen.

It’s hard to follow my own advice – my body shouts at me to move, to struggle, to get out and to breathe.

Suddenly, inspiration strikes. I activate my shield tattoo, crossing my fingers that this will work.

Abruptly, I’m able to breathe once more. Feeling my tattoo with my magical senses, I can tell that it won’t last long. As I hoped, upon detecting the hostile magic around me, it created a bubble shield rather than a flat plane before me, but that in turn means that it’s expending its charge faster.

Noir, use your shield! I urge, sending him a sense of exactly what I mean. He does so and the panic spiking from his side of the Bond reduces a little.

But it’s only a very temporary measure. Worse, we’re still trapped in the roc’s grasp and are rapidly being brought to its open maw once more.

I think frantically of something I can do to get out of this. Noir can dive back into my tattoo – but that won’t help him if I’m eaten. But maybe his disappearance will cause enough disruption that I’ll be able to slip out? Maybe if I coat my hands with glass, it will help me cut my way through the disrupted currents?

Already bringing my mana to my hands even as I reach out to Noir, I’m shocked when the grasp around us suddenly releases. The roc’s maw snaps shut only metres away, the draft caused by the sudden close sending both Noir and me tumbling backwards through the air.

We fall, only to land heavily on the roc’s back. My hands sink into wet feathers, currently corporeal.

Wait. Wet?
