The white floor held him up while his lungs relearned their job.

Kage stayed on his knees longer than he wanted to admit, one palm flat against the page, feeling the faint grain of it like cold vellum, and somewhere behind his ribs the image of a breaking crown kept replaying itself on a loop he had no menu option to close. Six pieces. Six falling lights. A man writing a world alone because the old one had stopped agreeing to exist.

Okay. Process later.

A chime cut through the static in his skull. Gold light bloomed at the corner of his vision, warm as a struck match.

[Poetic Insight has triggered.]

[You have witnessed an event of immense narrative weight.]

[Poet's Lexicon: New Keyword Acquired.]

[Origin] (1%)

The word sat in his Lexicon beside [Memory], both of them gleaming that same dazzling gold. One percent resonance. He understood almost nothing about it. The system was being honest for once.

"Kage!" Lily's voice, close and getting closer. Boots on the page-floor, nine sets of them, the whole raid converging on his position like he was a quest marker. "Are you—"

"He's up," Zara said. "Look at his face. He's reading something."

"Klaid." That was Jax. The name landed like a dropped plate. "Before anything else happens, we need to talk about—"

The world disagreed.

The chime that interrupted Jax was a fanfare in three ascending notes, brass and bell together, and the air above the party tore open into a banner of light wide enough to shade all ten of them.

[RAID COMPLETE: The Shifting Galleries]

[Grade: LEGACY]

[The Sealed Domain has acknowledged its conquerors.]

[Distributing rewards...]

And then, beneath the banner, materializing out of nothing with the smug ceremony of a stage magician's finale, a chest. Calling it a chest undersold it. The thing was the size of a coffin, lacquered in deep gallery-green, its corners wrapped in gold leaf worked into the shapes of quills and brushes, its lid inlaid with a relief of the very platform they stood on. It thudded onto the page-floor and the floor flexed under the weight.

Zherdev made a sound that no adult man should make in public.

Before anyone could move, another panel unfolded in front of Kage's face. Personal. Bordered in laurel.

[WORLD FIRST!]

[You are among the first players in Crown of Destiny to complete a Legacy-grade raid.]

[Would you like your name announced in the World Event broadcast?]

His finger was on No before he finished reading the question. Muscle memory at this point. Reflex bordering on religion.

[Your choice has been recorded.]

Around him, nine other faces lit up, and nine fingers, with varying degrees of hesitation, went the other direction. Zherdev stabbed his Yes so hard he nearly punched through the hologram. Lily tapped hers with a small, guilty smile. Even Ren, after a pause that lasted a full breath, accepted.

The sky of the white room dimmed. Every player in the world was about to get the same screen, and for a moment Kage could almost feel it, millions of hands pausing mid-craft, mid-fight, mid-haggle.

[WORLD ANNOUNCEMENT]

[THE FIRST LEGACY-GRADE RAID HAS FALLEN.]

[THE SHIFTING GALLERIES, SEALED SINCE THE WORLD'S FIRST WORD, HAS YIELDED TO TEN ADVENTURERS:]

[Zherdev_The_Great. Vami. Jules. Ren. Zara. Lily. Jax. Finn. Valdrias.]

[AND ONE WHO CHOSE TO REMAIN UNWRITTEN.]

"UNWRITTEN," Zherdev bellowed, reading it aloud for the benefit of people standing two meters away. "Bro. BRO. Do you understand what you've done? You're going to be a legend. An urban myth! Forums are going to lose their MINDS, there's gonna be conspiracy threads, fan art, people claiming THEY'RE the tenth—" He grabbed his own horned helm with both hands. "This is the coolest thing I have ever been adjacent to."

"You realize," Zara said dryly, eyes still scanning her own notifications, "that 'one who chose to remain unwritten' is infinitely more attention-grabbing than just listing a tenth name."

Yes, Kage thought sourly. The system has a flair for the dramatic. The system is, as far as I can tell, the single biggest drama enthusiast in existence, and I'm contractually tied to it.

The notifications kept coming. His personal feed had turned into a waterfall.

[Level Up!]

[You have reached Level 28.]

[You have 2 Stat Points available.]

[Global Ranking Update.]

[Current Standing: 19.]

[FAME +200]

He stopped breathing for a second.

Nineteen. Two digits. He felt the old paranoia twitch, the part of him that knew leaderboards attracted eyes and eyes attracted problems, and then he reminded that part of himself that his level was hidden, his name was hidden, and as far as the world knew, rank 19 was a ghost.

Good. Ghosts don't get headhunted. Ghosts don't get stream-sniped. Ghosts get to go home and sleep.

[You have gained: 1x Specialization Stone.]

[Specialization Stones may be invested into your Subclass skill branches: The Samurai.]

A second stone. He filed that next to the first decision he'd been chewing on since the bench in Korros, between the apothecary and the paint shop. Two stones now. The math of the skill trees was about to get interesting.

[Loot Acquired]

[Ink of Origin (Mythic) ] x1

The item slotted into his inventory. The icon was a small glass vial of black that refused to render as flat color. Mythic-grade. He didn't open the description. His hands were shaking just enough that he noticed, and he had the distinct conviction that reading the tooltip of a substance he had just watched unmake an army and, possibly, make a world was a task for a man who had eaten and slept first.

He closed the feed.

The feed refused to be closed.

The next message arrived differently. The standard panels were clean light and crisp brackets. This one assembled itself out of parchment. Actual textured parchment, cream-colored, edges deckled and soft, the text on it laid down in slow strokes as though an unseen hand were writing it in real time, and at the bottom, a wax seal pressed itself into existence with a sound like a held breath being released.

[DEED OF STEWARDSHIP]

[By right of Axiom Challenge, witnessed and sealed, ownership of the Sealed Domain "The Shifting Galleries" is hereby transferred.]

[Owner: Kage]

[You now hold Ownership of a raid-grade Domain.]

Kage read it twice.

"You can own a dungeon," he said quietly, to no one. The words came out flat with disbelief.

He'd assumed the Axiom Challenge stakes ran one direction. His class on the table, the Curator's authority as the prize, winner keeps the room. He had won the room. Apparently the room came with the rest of the building.

The parchment kept writing.

[WORLD FIRST: You are the first player to acquire Ownership of a Domain.]

[Would you like this information made available to the world?]

He raised a finger toward the No, then stopped.

Wait.

That phrasing. Every World First prompt until now had asked the same question: do you want your name announced. This one asked whether the information should exist publicly at all. Whether the world should even learn that dungeon ownership was possible.

He turned that over, and the answer assembled itself the way market math always did, fast and a little ugly. If players knew a cleared raid could be owned, that somebody, somewhere, profited every time the content was run, a meaningful slice of them would boycott on principle. Nobody likes paying rent to a stranger, especially in a game they escaped to specifically to stop paying rent to strangers. Forced disclosure would poison every owned dungeon's traffic. So the system made disclosure optional. Made the concept itself optional.

Which means Morpheus thought about player psychology down to the level of spite. He felt a grudging professional respect.

"No," he murmured, and pressed it.

[Your choice has been recorded.]

[All Domain information, configurations, and settings may be accessed via The Desk.]

The Desk.

He turned. It stood exactly where it had been since they entered this final chamber, far across the white expanse, small with distance: the dark wooden desk where a man made of pages had sat and waited for a master who never came. Kage looked at it and the parchment deed folded itself away, and that was when he noticed his eyes were struggling.

The edges of the desk were blurry. Faintly doubled, like a misprint.

He blinked, ran a quick diagnostic on his own vision, found nothing wrong, and then realized the blur extended past the desk. The floor near the horizon shimmered. The distant boundary of the room had a softness to it, a wet-paint quality. He tilted his head back.

Above the chamber, trailing up from the entire white void the way steam rises off a lake at dawn, was a wisp.

Calling it a wisp was like calling the loot chest a box. The thing was a column of translucent silver-grey, broad as a city block at its base, tapering as it climbed into a height that had no ceiling to stop it. It moved the way the Curator's pages had moved, in slow continuous turnover, and looking at it produced a sensation in Kage's back teeth like a tuning fork pressed to bone.

[Conceptual Purity has triggered.]

The source is the dungeon.

He stared up at the column. Conceptual Materials came from defeated foes. Powerful ones, unique ones, monsters with enough story in them to leave a residue. Grom's century of waiting had left one. They came from things, from creatures with a beginning and an end.

This one was coming off the entire domain. Every frame, every drowned reef, every burning draft and crumpled moon, the whole abandoned graveyard of The First Maker's rough work, all of it exhaling a single harvestable concept upward into nothing.

Can I harvest a… place? His mouth had gone dry. The Operator in him started listing reasons to be careful: unknown yield, unknown cost, possible structural consequences to a domain he now legally owned, and the Artist in him simply stood there with his head back, looking at a pillar of distilled meaning wide enough to land a ship on, and wanted it with an intensity that embarrassed him.

Later. Test it later. Talk to the Curator too, he added, glancing at the vast black book lying closed on the white floor, the small pale flame hovering above its cover like a patient candle. A bound construct of the First Maker, defeated, demoted, and almost certainly stuffed with more first-hand information about his class than every Lore Echo combined. He has to answer to the owner now. Probably. Hopefully. That's an asset I'm going to milk until it files a complaint.

So much new ground. The keyword. The stone. The Ink. The deed, the Desk, the wisp, the book. He pulled in a breath and started building the order of operations, and that left only the che—

"GUYS, IT'S NOT EVEN LOCKED!"

Kage spun around.

Zherdev had both hands under the coffin-sized lid and his entire magma-pauldroned back into the lift, face shining with pure uncut triumph. The lid swung up. Golden light poured out of the chest and up his arms and across his awestruck face, and somewhere in that light Kage registered Jax lunging a half-step too late, Zara pinching the bridge of her nose, and Lily laughing before she could stop herself.

"Zherdev," Jax said, strangled.

"What? It's loot! Loot is FOR opening! That's the whole—oh. Oh wow. Oh wow."

The chest answered. Light gathered above its open mouth and unfolded into a shared panel, broad as a banquet table, visible to all ten of them at once.

[RAID REWARDS: The Shifting Galleries]

[Currency distributed: 10 Gold to each participant.]

Ten gold dropped into Kage's pouch with a weight his Synesthesia rendered as a warm handful of river stones. Around him, several people made small involuntary noises. For most of them, that one line had just quintupled their net worth four days into the game's life.

The panel kept unfolding.

[Item Roster: 10]

The names wrote themselves down the panel one by one, each in its rarity's color, and the chamber went very quiet.

[Gloves of the Final Draft] - Legendary

[Magnum Opus, First Edition] - Legendary

[Chiaroscuro Half-Cloak] - Epic

[Fresco-Saint's Aegis] - Epic

[Diadem of the Patient Restorer] - Epic

[Conductor's Baton, Winter Movement] - Rare

[Vellum-Strung Recurve] - Rare

[Scabbard of the Unsigned Duelist] - Rare

[Greaves of the Gilded Frame] - Rare

[A Perfectly Adequate Paintbrush] - Common

Two lines of deep, smoldering orange sat at the top of the roster like twin suns, and below them the purples and blues marched down to a single, defiant line of grey.

Nobody spoke. Ten pairs of eyes traveled down the list, then back up to the orange, then sideways at each other, the silent arithmetic of who gets what already running behind every face in the room.

Zherdev broke first, of course. His whisper carried across the entire white void.

"Magnum Opus."

Here we go, Kage thought.
