Entry Seventeen, from the memoir of Imperatrix Ira Kin’jal, First of her line. The Age of Unity, Year 1.

For every Raja and Rajni, for every King and Queen, for every ruler great and small, the days and weeks following the death of the Akh Nara blurred together, and it was no different for me.

Before I knew it, entire months had passed despite the sleepless nights. A sort of waking dream, if you will. A tumultuous time I weathered only thanks to the incredible help I had. From Hema, Saanvi, Neeti, and countless others.

When Saint Tiyana plunged her talwar into the Akh Nara’s belly, none of us knew what would happen.

Well, that’s not strictly true. Everyone—myself included—thought the Samsara would fall, withering away to nothing as the rumors said.

Needless to say, this did not, in fact, happen. It looked very much like nothing at all happened at first.

It was a good thing that they did not perish. For one, it gave us pause, and it seemed to confuse the demons more.

Too much was wrong. The Akh Nara… the Samsara… Tia breaking down sobbing in the middle of the battlefield over her mortal enemy.

It was as the Akh Nara’s death had sucked the very will to fight out of every soul present.

More than once, I have wondered whether this was Vir’s aim. Knowing him, I have to guess it was.

Sacrificing himself for the realm seemed very much like something he would do…

The confusion did not last long, however.

Tia immediately declared the war over, and with my backing, the rest of humanity’s lords and leaders fell in line.

A feat only possible because demonkind did the same.

Or, I should say, because Cirayus did the same.

Yet another sneaky trick Vir played upon all of us. It seems he’d named his Godfather the next in line for the Gargan crown should he pass on.

Given Cirayus’ quite frankly absurd popularity, not one demon questioned the transfer of power, and his first act as Raja was to declare that the Garga publicly disavow the war, and that they would at once begin making reparations with humanity for damages incurred.

The Asuras immediately pledged their loyalty to Cirayus, and Clans Baira, Panav, and Chitra threw in their support, officially withdrawing from the war. The Iksana followed only hours later.

Left out on their own and deprived of the Asura, the Samsara, and their patron deity, Jagath Aindri had no choice but to follow suit.

What followed was a period of intense and earnest negotiation with none of the politics that normally plague such matters. Made possible, of course, only because Tia stepped up to assume the mantle of Saint in earnest, with Cirayus doing the same on demonkind’s side.

It truly is incredible how good leaders can make even the most intractable of issues appear simple, though I am sure Tia would disagree.

Walls built over years were knocked down in days. Land in the new realm was parceled amicably, and it was agreed that half the Asura corps would report to Saint Tia, with the other half serving demonkind under Cirayus.

None of it possible, of course, without the countless hours of effort on both their sides. As I know better than most, such things always appear simpler from the outside.

I think it was precisely because humanity was so lost that Tia had little trouble filling Maiya’s shoes, as insecure as she herself was about the task.

For a time, it seemed like she came running to me for advice on every other decision. But when a leader is forced to make as many critical decisions as she was, one cannot help but grow into the role.

I must admit, it was a pleasure to have been a part of that process, however small.

As for Cirayus, well… he made it look easy, sliding into Vir’s role seemingly overnight.

Yes, it helped that he was so close to the Akh Nara, but it was his centuries of experience and his immense popularity that made it possible. I can think of no demon more loved than he, and the feeling extends to me. We’ve grown rather close as of late, in Maiya’s absence.

As for myself, well, I daresay anyone reading this already knows. I will, however, mention how incredible and fickle the thing we call public sentiment truly is.

Five thousand troops held in reserve at Khanthagati made me a villain overnight, while that very same action became the beacon around which Kin’jal rallied once the war was over.

“The only ruler who held true to her values,” they said. “The only leader who truly wished for peace.”

Needless to say, my reputation was not only restored, it soared to heights I could never have before hoped. Second, perhaps, only to the Saint herself.

The time that followed was one of grief, of course. And introspection. We’d never really recovered from the Ash Tears when all this chaos began. I ensured my people had all the time they needed.

But we’re past that now. The country now flourishes like never before, with new shops and trades springing up on every corner seemingly every day. People walk with their chins held high, and optimism is thick in the air.

What’s more, demons now walk in the open, trading with the human nations on a scale never before seen. To say nothing of the countless routes crisscrossing across the uncharted lands.

Much remains to be done, of course. Cities to be founded, new land to be settled. I daresay my work will keep me busy for years to come with scarcely a moment to myself.

I welcome it openly. The Kin’jal I’d dreamed of, ever since I was but a girl seems like it’s on the cusp of coming to be.

All on account of my beautiful friends, Maiya and Vir, whom I miss with every passing moment.

Mourning their loss. Wondering. If there could not have been another way.

Another path in which they lived.

For an Age of Unity dawns upon the realm. A dazzling future bursting with hope, just waiting to be born.

Humanity and demonkind have taken their first steps in a relationship that I hope, will outlive even Ages. A bond strong enough to defy time itself.

And the creators of this historic miracle, of which stories will be sung for Ages to come…

Are no longer here to witness it.
