Chapter 369

“Wh… What are you doing?” the youth asked before his mother could get a hold of him and pull him away. “Just what do you think you're doing?”

Eik offered a glance but didn’t stop the slow construction of the final pieces of the structure. When it was finished, Eik turned his full attention to the boy who was being reluctantly dragged away by his mother. “I am demonstrating the truth of my word, and the consequences of an unforgivable transgression.”

If not for his mother’s hand covering his mouth, the youth would surely have spat a venomous jab back. The mother’s eyes never left Eik’s, watching like a hawk for any sign of a change of mind. Well, if Eik decided to come after them, there wasn’t much any of them could do about it, but her willingness to sacrifice herself for her son was commendable nonetheless.

Before they could disappear, Eik spoke quietly but sent his voice directly to her ear with a gentle but precise flex of his aura. “Listen to me now. Before this little show begins, you should take your son indoors and shield his eyes from what is to come.”

She offered no real response to his words but before she ducked behind one of the smaller buildings bordering the plaza, still carrying her son bodily under one arm with the other hand clasped over his mouth, Eik thought he saw something resembling gratitude in her hard eyes.

Then, when they were gone, Eik jumped. Clearing the entire height of the pillar in a single, casual move, he landed on the center of the large T-shaped structure. Here, he could see the entirety of Rorgherd sprawled out before him.

Or rather, he would have been able to see the entirety of Rorgherd, if not for the fact that so much smog and smoke filled the air. When he had first come here to deliver his first message to the Gohkamorian people, he had assumed that he had simply landed somewhere close to one of the larger artisans’ districts of the city, but as it had turned out, that was not the case at all.

The simple truth of it was that the Gohkamorians loved it. They actually enjoyed the stench of the smoke. Fumes from the melting of metal, common smoke from the burning of wood, as well as a variety of other types of smoke like gases emitted from chemical fires and the mixing of materials such as cement and asphalt.

It severely obscured his view of the city. Fortunately, he was very powerful and had ways to deal with that. A single, vast burst of aura swept across the roofs and carried the dark fumes away with it. Proficient at aura manipulation as he was, Eik managed to omit any aspect of the energy that would have killed or otherwise incapacitated those struck by the wave, allowing it to pass over them with little more than an unsettling feeling in their stomachs.

The city was gigantic. On the horizons in all directions stood skyscrapers, domes, and piercing spires as dominating figures of the Rorgherd skyline, proving that the Gohkamorians were not only large in physical size but also in architecture. He hated to say it, but it looked impressive. It made for quite an intimidating sight.

Down on the ground, on the streets, at the stalls, between the houses, and in the windows, hundreds of thousands watched as the most prominent antagonist in Gohkamorian history prepared to show them exactly how serious he was. No, hundreds of thousands didn’t do it justice. Now that the smog had been dispersed, the number reached the millions.

Like a wildfire spreading across dried weeds and crunchy twigs, word spread among the Gohkamorians, neighbors pulling each other from the houses in alarm.

In fact, with the size of most buildings, Eik actually decided to grow the main supporting pillar by another few hundred meters, just to clear most of the larger buildings. While it’s true that it might make it difficult for some of the lower ranked Awakened Gohkamorians watching from the streets to properly see detail that high up, it was better than obscuring the show from the many people whose lines of sight were blocked by skyscrapers.

Arms spread wide, the avatar of the Monarch grew further as well, reaching a height of more than a kilometer of translucent, cloaked terror. Deep within the seemingly endless void of the hood, its eyes shone like a pair of distant but bright stars. It was almost hypnotizing.

Its presence was crushing as Eik sent his voice from where the titan’s mouth would have been, creating a near perfect illusion of a god descended into the physical world.

“Dear Gohkamorians,” his voice flowed. Like ethereal silk, it enveloped them and permeated their bodies, the volume neither a shout nor a whisper. Even to those listening at the very edge of the city, it sounded like he was standing right next to them and speaking directly into their ears. “I have come here today to demonstrate something to you.”

Scattered shouts responded to him but he paid it no mind. All they had to do here was watch.

“I will show you the embodiment of rage. You might want to lock your children inside the house for what’s to come next. Or don’t. I don’t really care.”

As he spoke, hundreds of Profound Toxic beasts could be seen scaling the pillar of crystal at speed. Each of them carried anywhere from one to three unconscious Gohkamorians and quickly spread out across the massive horizontal beam to decorate the many crosses with the limp bodies of the guilty. More than a dozen crystal spikes impaled each of the transgressors through various parts of their bodies.

The sheer pain of it woke up most of them in screams. Those screams only grew more frantic and frightened when the spikes embedded in their bodies shifted from bright blue to deep, sickening purple.

Each of the spikes appeared to subtly shatter before spider webs of smaller veins of crystal sought skin and penetrated again, increasing the number of puncture wounds per transgressor from about a dozen to over a hundred.

“Behold those who had committed unforgivable sins against my people,” the Monarch uttered with raised hands. “Their minds are being tormented by the effects of the Visage of Death as they helplessly await judgment. Today, finally, they will pay the price for what they have done.”

Back home, in Forest, as well as some of the other larger settlements the delegates of Forest had made contact with in search of other human survivor on Earth, this entire thing was being broadcast like some kind of great performance, courtesy, again, of the highly skilled members of Eik’s Great Group of Guys. And Eik didn’t doubt for a second that every person who remembered the terrors humanity had to go through during those first ten years would watch with glee as some of their pain was repaid.

The Gohkamorians had managed to expertly strip away any sympathy humanity could possibly have had for them. As a matter of fact, a majority of humans, if given the option to choose, would probably have chosen to dish out punishment in broader strokes, without caring too much about which Gohkamorians were actually responsible for human suffering or not.

“Raise them up,” Eik commanded, and the crosses followed his command and stretched upward as the vertical protrusion behind the necks of the guilty fell away, leaving their necks free.

“Blade.” Reaching up into the churning blue above its head, the leviathan’s hand was engulfed completely before it pulled out a crystalline katana of impossible size. Measuring more than a kilometer in length, it cast a looming shadow over the buildings that promised certain death.

It glittered as it moved, the tip of the blade cleaving the air so rapidly that it shattered the sound barrier and sent harsh, buffeting winds across the city in great gusts. Held in a two-handed grip, Eik brought the weapon into a ready stance.

Holding it perfectly level with the top of the now T-shaped crosses, the air crackled as the Monarch’s ethereal muscles tensed in anticipation of the death blow.

The screams of the damned rang clearly as Eik held the stance for a few seconds, tension building to a peak.

“For Earth,” the echoing voice of the sovereign hissed.

As an observer, the was difficult to determine exactly how fast the blade moved. On the one hand, it felt like it took minutes for it to reach the other side of the enormous execution stand from the starting position, but on the other, not a single pair of eyes on the ground was capable of following its path.

From one instant to the next, all scream were cut off into silence. Only the hiss of the wind and the barely audible thuds of more than a thousand severed heads tumbling across the massive crystal structure and into free fall above the city making any noise.

Almost immediately, the katana dispersed into trillions of motes of brilliant, inert toxin that scattered on the wind. The leviathan Monarch followed shortly after.

Hands in his pockets, Eik himself floated leisurely in to land on top of the massive structure that still held fast the corpses of the judged.

For many long moments, he simply watched the city, eyes scanning the crowds that had gathered in the streets. At his sides a thousand headless corpses bled, their lifeblood falling down upon the roofs of Rorgherd like crimson midday rain.

Despite the incredible length of the blade that had taken their heads, Eik had made it as thin as a scalpel, so as to slice cleanly rather than bash and rupture.

Finally, the Worldbreaker spoke. “Thank you,” he said, directing his words to the countless Gohkamorians below. “Thank you for witnessing this atrocity. I am sure you feel insulted and horrified that I would carry out this deed of vengeance right here in your capital of all places. You must believe me a cruel demon whose only intention is to cause you as much suffering as I possibly can.”

Briefly, he paused, just in case someone voiced their opinions. None did.

“And, to tell you the honest truth, I can’t deny a desire for your suffering, just as my people have suffered. You are welcome to hate me, but that will change nothing. And I swear that is not my only reason. I wish for you to witness this judgment for a very simple reason,” he said, voice flowing to their ears like the wind. “I want to prove to you the truth of my promise. Hand over those responsible for the terror of my family, and I will let you live and thrive. What you witnessed here today was the proof of my promise to you. This is your way out. This is how you survive.”

And with that, the crystalline structure he was standing on shattered at once. The corpses were eaten up by Profound Toxin before they could fall to the cobblestone below. The families would probably want the corpses to bury or whatever funeral rituals the Gohkamorians had, but they were Eik’s to claim. Those corpses were his trophies.
