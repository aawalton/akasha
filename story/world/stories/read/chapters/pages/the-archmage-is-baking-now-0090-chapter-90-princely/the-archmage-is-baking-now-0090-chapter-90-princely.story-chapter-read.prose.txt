The captain moved fast when he needed to. Within minutes, he had secured two horses from the inn's stables, and stableboys to saddle them and get them ready for them. For both him and the inn, this seemed like a common enough occurrence, something that happened enough for them to be good at making it happen fast.

Now Jane would have to ride a horse, however. The fact that it was her idea did nothing to remedy the fact that it had been a while.

Horse-riding was taught at the academy, though the senior mages brought in outsiders to do the actual instruction. The theory was that mages often had to move quickly, especially as they became more powerful and could solve a wider variety of problems. Some mages, like Cecelia, didn’t need the horses after all. Others relied on them all the time as they skipped from one potential disaster to the next.

The classes were popular by means of being variety, but Jane had never loved riding the way some of her fellow students had. Even now, she wasn't an expert rider in any sense, despite having spent dozens of hours in the saddle over the years. She was competent, however, and that was all she needed to be.

Once her horse was saddled, she ran to it. The hardest part of riding had always been getting securely into the saddle, something being short didn’t help with at all and she had failed to accomplish cleanly any number of times. Today, though, excitement bridged the gap. She was up and seated before she knew it. Adrenaline had more or less launched her there.

The captain mounted his own horse and looked to her for direction.

"This way," Jane said, pointing toward a smaller road leading away from the main road, then out into the wilderness from the edge of town. "The laundress said her sister lives a few hours out. We should be able to make it there faster than that. I don’t think she was counting on horses."

They started off at a gentle trot. Jane knew better than to push for more speed while they were still in town. Galloping horses through populated streets was a good way to hurt the animals, and it was a stupid move when they were this close to their goal anyway.

Once they were about a mile down the road and well out of the town, Jane called for a stop. She dismounted and drew her circle quickly. It was pretty much automatic now, having been practiced so many times in the last few days. She pushed magic into it and watched the road flicker to life.

There they were. Dave and Brand again, almost alone on this much less used path, as clear as an expert painting.

"We've picked up the trail again.”

"Yes." Jane let the spell fade and climbed back onto her horse. "It's good. But we need to keep moving."

They rode on. The forest was bright where the moonlight cut through, but their riding angle assured that was rare. After an hour passed, the moon finely rose to an angle that defeated the tree-cover, letting them move even faster.

The excitement that had carried her through the last hour was fading. For a bit right after they got the horses, she had been full of energy. Now only the steady motion of the horse beneath her was keeping her awake. Yet the rhythmic clopping of hooves was simultaneously lulling her to sleep, creating a sort of off-balance state she maintained only through great difficulty.

The captain seemed to notice. "We're close," he said. "Just a bit further."

They stopped twice more for Jane to cast her spell. Each time, the images showed Dave and Brand still traveling, still safe. The trail was holding. Whatever had happened to them, it had happened somewhere ahead.

Eventually, the captain pointed to a small path branching off to the right. It was barely visible in the darkness.

"There," he said. “Is that your path?”

Jane looked and recognized the markers the laundress had described. A dead tree and a large rock sat almost leaned together on the left side of the turn-off, unmistakably confirming their direction.

The path was narrow, barely wide enough for the horses to pass single file. Jane took the lead. It only took a few minutes before the trees began to thin. Jane could see open sky ahead, and then they emerged into a small clearing.

The space had been carved out of the forest with real work, Jane saw. The ground was level, plowed, and de-stumped until what had once been wild woodland was now arable farmland. Jane could see what was grown had some variety, even by the moonlight. There was too much here for just one person or family, but a lot of it seemed to be grown for personal use anyway.

The only buildings were three structures lined up at the far side of the clearly. A fairly large tool shed stood to the left, probably holding whatever plows, hoes, and shovels the farming woman used to do her work. A small horse barn occupied the right side, big enough for maybe two animals and a cart. Sandwiched between the two was a very small house that looked like it had been built to accommodate one or two people, at the most.

The crops looked healthy. The buildings were well-maintained. There weren’t any signs of violence. For a long moment, it seemed as if there was nothing to worry about.

Then she saw the grave.

It was outside the house, fully visible in front of the small porch. The earth was freshly turned, still dark from moisture and standing out against the grasses of the clearing like a sore thumb. A simple wooden marker had been driven into the ground at one end, though Jane couldn't read what was written on it from this distance.

The grave was new.

Jane dismounted. The captain followed suit, his hand moving to the hilt of his sword. They approached the house together with no fault to stealth. Jane realized, wryly, that they were probably the most powerful duo moving in the world at that moment.

The door to the house was closed but not locked. Jane pushed it open and stepped inside.

The interior was dark, but enough moonlight filtered through the windows for her to see the basic layout. It was a single room, furnished in rough-cut home-made furniture. It was neat and clean, but it was also empty. There was no one inside.

"The barn," the captain said quietly.

They crossed the clearing to the horse barn. This door was closed as well, but Jane could hear movement inside. She looked at the captain, who nodded and drew his sword. He needed to keep his hands free. She pushed the door open.

The interior of the barn was lit by a single lantern, hanging from a hook near the entrance. It gave just enough light to reveal absolutely everything, and in its glow Jane could see two figures sitting against the far wall.

Dave looked up first. When he saw Jane standing in the doorway, his expression changed from mere surprise at being found to shock..

"Jane?" His voice cracked. "How did you find us?"

Brand was beside him. He rose, his court manners forcing him to his feet in the presence of a lady where she was sure he otherwise would have stayed seated.

"The whole kingdom is looking for you, practically," Jane said. She stepped into the barn and took a look at them, checking for injuries. "Are you hurt? What happened?"

"We're fine." Dave shook his head. "We're fine. But the woman who lives here wasn't."

Jane looked at the grave marker visible through the barn's open door.

"We thought that held one of you, at first. What happened to her?"

"We found her collapsed in her garden when we arrived," Brand said. "We were just supposed to be a running an errand for an old woman who couldn’t afford to get it done any other way, and there she was, all of the sudden. She woke up once we got her inside."

"We couldn't just leave her," Dave added. “She told us there wasn’t any use, anyway. Some sickness had been growing in her for a long time. She said the last time she talked to a doctor, he said there was no use fighting it anymore. Just to take her time. And she said her time was out.”

"We stayed with her for the days it took to prove her right,” Brand said. “It was the decent thing to do.”

"Your parents are worried sick," Captain Harris said. "The whole royal guard has been searching for you."

Brand's expression flickered with guilt, but also annoyance. He hid the latter quickly, but not before Jane got a good look. She determined to ask him about it later.

"I know. I'm sorry. We should have found a way to send word. But there was no way. It wouldn’t have been right to leave her, and nobody came by. I tried to send Dave, but he wouldn’t go.”

"I couldn't go," Dave said. "I promised the king and queen I’d stay with him. What if something had happened while I was gone?”

Jane understood, better than she let on initially. They had made choices for the good of an old woman they had just met. A week ago, maybe she wouldn’t have understood. But having run her own vigil over a new and ailing friend, she understood all too well what was necessary here.

In her opinion, they hadn’t done anything she wouldn’t do. They were young, and they had been faced with a situation they weren't prepared for. They had done their best, and she felt they had done right.

"We have horses," she said. "How long until you can get the wagon moving?"

“Not long,” Dave replied. “But it’s dark. You’ll have to guide us, and we’ll have to go slow.”

"We'll go as slowly as you need."

Brand went out to talk to the captain while Dave hitched his wagon and prepared what little there was to prepare before they left. Jane spotted an unfamiliar bundle of goods all wrapped up in a quilt, and she immediately understood it to be the deceased’s inheritance goods, packaged to be passed down to an old laundress who might not get to her home any time soon.

"Dave.”

He looked up.

"You did well."

—

The return journey was slower than the outward one had been, since the wagon simply had trouble with roads this rough and could only move so fast. But they made steady progress, and by the time they reached the royal carriage, the first hints of dawn were beginning to lighten the eastern sky.

The guards who had stayed with the carriage snapped to attention when they saw the prince. One of them immediately began preparing the horses as the others waited for their commands from their leader.

"Send a rider ahead," the captain ordered. "Let the king know his son has been found safe."

"Yes, sir."

Jane watched the rider gallop off, carrying the news that would put the king and queen at ease at last. Then she climbed into the carriage herself, settling onto the seat across from Brand and Dave.

Whatever serious discussions there were to have could wait. Right now, all of them needed sleep.

Yet the carriage had barely begun moving when Brand’s voice boomed out.

"Stop the wagon."

The driver pulled up short. Captain Harris looked at Brand.

"Your Highness?"

Brand was already reaching for the door handle. The captain's hand shot out and caught his wrist.

"What do you think you're doing?"

Brand pointed out the window.

"I have a message to deliver," Brand said. "You know I do."

"My responsibility is getting you home safely."

"And I'm telling you I need to do this first." Brand's voice was steady, but there was steel in it that Jane hadn't heard before. "The laundress deserves to know. It would be monstrous not to tell her.”

The captain's grip didn't loosen. "Your Highness, your parents are waiting. Every minute we delay…"

"I command you to let me go."

Jane watched the captain's face shift through several expressions over the course of just a moment. Offense, she thought, and then surprise, then eventually resignation. He let go.

Brand opened the door and stepped down onto the street. He walked towards a small house Jane recognized from her questioning the night before. The laundress's operation was visible around the side of the building, inactive and empty of clothes for the time being.

Brand knocked on the door and waited.

Jane watched from the carriage window. She couldn't see the laundress very well when the door finally opened, but she could see Brand's face clearly. He spoke for a while. The laundress's posture changed as he talked, tensing and then resigning itself to the news. Brand reached into his coat and produced something Jane couldn't quite make out. It was the bundle of goods, she realized. He must have grabbed it from the wagon before they abandoned it at the edge of town.

The conversation continued. At one point, the laundress reached out and touched Brand's arm. He nodded at something she said, then bowed and turned back towards the carriage.

His face was composed when he climbed back in, but his eyes were red. Jane could only imagine how hard that had been for him to do. Having done it, she felt, especially when he could have had no experience at all with hard news, was regal. It might have been the first truly princely thing she had seen from the boy.

He nodded to the captain. "We can go now."
