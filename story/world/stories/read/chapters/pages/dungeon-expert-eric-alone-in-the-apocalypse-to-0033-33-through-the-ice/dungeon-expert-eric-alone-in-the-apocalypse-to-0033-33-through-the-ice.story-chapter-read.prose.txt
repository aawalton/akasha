Eric edged closer to the treasure chest. It sat in a small clearing, ringed by slender trees that were all themselves rimed with ice, as if a localized ice storm had formed in the center of the clearing and spat ice outward. The Ice Elementals bobbed in the air, circling peacefully around the chest. The chest itself was encased in a large crystal of ice, and Eric wasn’t completely convinced that the Ice Elementals were protecting the chest versus the crystal. From the Game’s perspective, they were absolutely protecting the chest; from their perspective, though, they seemed more interested in the ice coating it. Every now and again, one would drift over and exude freezing air toward the crystal, thickening its surface. The crystal trembled, and for a split second, its mana shifted; rather than one big crystal, it glowed in six different places, as if it contained six smaller Ice Elementals just waiting to break free.

Eric blinked. No way. Is that the equivalent of an egg sac for Ice Elementals? Crazy. Forget the treasure chest; he had to shatter that thing, and fast, before he had twice as many Ice Elementals to deal with.

Cy, are you in place?

Yes, yes. And let me commend you on your rapid comprehension of communication with your superior. You’re quite an adequate familiar.

Rather than let the rabbit continue blathering into his mind, Eric reached out to him, pushing the Firebolt through Cy just like he’d pushed it into the sword. The first Firebolt burst from a bush on the far side of the clearing from him. One of the elementals dropped, falling still to the floor. Instantly, the Ice Elementals whirled on it, only for Eric to blast another one out of the sky with a Firebolt. They whirled on him instead, and he picked off another before pushing a second Firebolt through Cy. A fourth elemental dropped, leaving one. It darted toward the bushes, then toward Eric, bushes, Eric, caught between the two—then pulsed with brilliant white light. The entire band of ice crystals around its exterior whirled faster than ever before. The central crystal pulsed again, and every smaller crystal abruptly sharpened, then burst out, dozens of razor-sharp ice shards flying through the air. Eric dropped to the ground, throwing his arms up to protect his head. Ice bounced off the invisible puffer coat with enough strength to bruise him beneath it, but none of it pierced through.

The ice stopped. Eric peeked up. The elemental’s central crystal drifted alone, almost sagging in the air. It drooped, then turned, flying slowly, almost drunkenly toward the huge crystal with the chest in it.

I don’t know what that’s going to do, but I know I don’t want to find out. Eric lifted his hand and struck the central crystal with a Firebolt. The last Ice Elemental dropped out of the air with a blast of steam and a shrill screech almost like a kettle.

Cy hopped out of the bushes on the other side, preening his fur. He looked the crystal up and down, then fluttered his ears and stood on his hind legs to get a better look at it. “Oh my. What a large breeding crystal.”

“Is that what it’s called?” Eric lifted his hand, charging a Firebolt.

“It’s what we called it. I don’t suppose your world has a word for such a thing, since I doubt a mana-dry world like this had elementals, so you might as well use it.”

“Sure.” He loosed the spell. Fire splashed over the large crystal, melting a hole in it. Two of the bright white lights inside the crystal winked out. Grimacing, Eric called up another Firebolt. It’d take a couple to melt through this thing.

“The classic way to do away with them is to build a large fire nearby and let the crystal melt, but I suppose we don’t have the luxury of time, so you might as well Firebolt it away.” Cy hopped over to Eric’s side, facing out of the clearing and toward the rest of the forest, his ears twitching.

“Hear anything?” Eric asked, hitting the crystal again. It melted faster than an actual ice crystal of its size would have under these conditions, and each Firebolt cleared out more than he expected it to.

“Not right now. The forest is quiet.”

“Good.” One more Firebolt, and the crystal melted, releasing the chest. It’d be a bastard of a game that froze a mimic inside an ice crystal… but there was no such thing as too cautious. Eric moved to the side, swapped to his bat, and gently pried the chest open from beside it.

Cy watched him do that, then tilted his head. “What are you doing?”

“Mimics. It’s a classic video game thing from our world. Looks like a chest, actually an enemy that tries to eat you. There’s all kinds of variants, from chests with teeth to chests that grow spider legs and try to stab you. You can’t be too careful around chests.”

Cy blinked. “And you’ve encountered one of these?”

The fact that Cy hadn’t ever heard of mimics did suggest they weren’t real, but then, who knew how many games ago Cy had last interacted with a treasure chest? They could’ve added mimics since then. Hell, he was out here on the frontlines, experiencing live how much the Game shifted and changed with each iteration. They might not have existed last Game, but they might very well exist now. All that to say that just because Cy hadn’t encountered a mimic didn’t mean they didn’t exist. It just meant Cy hadn’t encountered one.

The chest was open, and it hadn’t attacked him. Eric stepped closer to peer into it. “No. But I don’t want to be the idiot who gets ambushed by a mimic and dies. Imagine how stupid I’d look.”

Eric snorted. The Game did love its snuff. “It might be very popular, but it wouldn’t help me much.”

Will that be me, one day? My body flopping over and over to music while lasers flash over it, or whatever makes the kids’ eyes light up?

It was mostly empty, except for a small, thin book at the bottom with a blue cover. He picked it up, turning it over in his hands. It was cold, the way he might expect from a book frozen in an ice crystal, but it was more than just cold; it radiated cold. The inside was blank, the pages as smooth and unmarred as fresh snow, but a yet fiercer cold radiated from the pages.
