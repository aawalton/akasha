Entering the water with admittedly slightly less grace than Sarah had, Samantha put that thought behind her as she angled herself down and swam. Sefta’s mother, Mags, had spent hours teaching her the basics of not only how to swim, but how to best utilize her Water element with the skills she had available to her. Since then, Samantha had spent plenty of her own time practicing to ensure she was as capable and confident in the water as she was on land and in the sky. She missed having her bow, but like she’d told Asher, she was far from defenseless.

Undulating her body, she shot after Sarah, watching the curious woman as she got straight to work on that whirlpool defense she’d told them about. Unsurprisingly, seeing as she had literal gills, Sarah swam through the water as if she’d been born in it. From what Samantha could tell, it looked like Sarah had a skill to manifest her own powerful current, which she was currently swimming through as she shot around under the water like an arrow through air. She’d already made one large lap a hundred or so feet below the city and was quickly finishing up her second one.

Once she’d finished her third lap, the water within her loop began to move on its own. It moved slowly at first, but soon began to pick up speed and start churning as Sarah completed lap after lap and kept creating those strange currents of hers. It wasn’t long before Samantha needed to swim farther away, lest she get caught up in the powerful whirlpool that was being created out of nowhere.

That’s some skill… she thought. Samantha drifted there aimlessly in the water, admiring the grace and power that Sarah swam with as she kept going around and around, ever increasing the whirlpool’s size and strength. Somehow, just as the woman had promised, her skill was keeping the whirlpool entirely contained underwater. Poltar wasn’t at risk of being swept up or destroyed from her actions, though the fact that it looked like she could have personally done a lot of damage to the city if she’d wanted to was enough to send a shiver down Samantha’s spine that had nothing to do with the cold water she was surrounded by.

And she’s not even one of these so-called ‘greater powers’ like Groln or Amanda. Just someone in the Stage of Power…

Witnessing what those at the peak of elemental advancement were capable of was eye-opening on multiple levels. Constructing a ten-story-tall energy projection of oneself capable of crushing villages with a single foot… Leaping into the sky and turning into a falling sun… Teleporting between the realms themselves with nothing more than an errant thought… The more Samantha saw what people were capable of, the more worried she became. But not because she was afraid of them using that power against her.

She was afraid of being responsible for wielding that level of power herself one day.

Samantha shook her head while she watched Sarah’s whirlpool continue to grow larger and larger. Taking a moment to secure the loose strands of her hair drifting around her face into a neat braid, she contemplated the future.

At the moment, she was in the Stage of Bleed. She’d yet to even receive her first blended skill, but she knew it was only a matter of time. Gone were those occasional thoughts that had popped up months ago, wondering when she’d eventually hit her limit and be forced to step back and watch Asher continue growing without her. She’d come far enough at this point to realize that she had what it took to go all the way with him at her side. She didn’t think she’d ever be quite as powerful or fearsome as The Reaper, but that was fine by her. She didn’t need or want that level of responsibility. All she wanted was to make those bastards who destroyed her village pay for their crimes, and to ensure that traumatic events like that didn’t happen to anybody else.

If that meant she had to descend into the dark depths of the lake and fight a small army of sea serpents, then so be it.

A powerful wave of noise from down below knocked her out of her thoughts, and Samantha’s face paled when the entire lake seemed to vibrate around her. Her own teeth began to rattle within her skull as dozens of rumbling screeches merged together and swept through her. That seemed to be what Sarah had been waiting for, because the slayer finally broke off from her whirlpool, shooting out of the powerful current before darting over to her.

“Ready?” she asked, giving Samantha an encouraging smile. Her words traveled through water as easily as if they were speaking on dry land, which Samantha could only assume was another skill of hers. “Ever fought a sea serpent before?”

“I haven’t,” Samantha admitted, trying to keep her heart rate calm as she shook her head. Her fingers kept twitching as they tried to go for the bow that wasn’t secure around her back, and she took a deep breath of water to try and calm herself down. “Any tips?”

“Avoid the giant teeth,” Sarah winked. The advice was so ridiculous and unexpected that Samantha couldn’t help but laugh. Some of her nerves bled from her as she returned the woman’s smile. She was going to ask if she had any actual advice before motion from above caught her attention, and she spotted dozens of people enter the water and start swimming down toward them.

“What is going on?!” a man shouted as he swam up to them, a harpoon at the ready in his powerful hands. His chest was emblazoned with a large crest that Samantha recognized from Mag’s own attire—a giant sea serpent curled up around itself to form a circle. Pointing his harpoon at them, he frowned. “Explain yourself this instant or…”

His words puttered out as he and everyone else detected motion from down below. Samantha followed everyone’s gaze, and her heart skipped a beat when she spotted the source of the movement down deep within the lake.

Sea serpents. Dozens upon dozens of giant sea serpents.

They were far enough away that it looked like a tangled mess of water snakes were creeping up toward them, but Samantha knew from Asher’s tales just how large the sea serpents were. Each one was a hundred feet long and capable of biting a person in half, and they quickly grew in size as they shot toward the surface of the water and the town floating up above.

“No time for explaining,” Sarah said, her gaze calculating as she checked to make sure her bracers were still tight against her forearms. “If you want Poltar to live to see tomorrow, now is the time for action.”

Not waiting for a response, the slayer took off down toward the serpents, a wake of bubbles trailing behind her as she shot through the water. Reaching the first of the serpents in only a few seconds, Sarah nimbly dodged out of the way of its gnashing teeth, swirling around its body while she dragged her bracers along its flesh from head to tail. The sea serpent screeched as its body was carved open in moments, but the monster was large enough that not even Sarah’s devastating wound was enough to finish it off.

That was all that Samantha was able to witness, however, as she was soon busy with her own battle.

The small crowd of warriors all scattered as they swam down to meet the serpents as well, and Samantha somehow found herself right in the center of them, her own body moving without thinking. Her heart pounded away within her chest as dozens of gaping maws opened wide and angry eyes the size of her entire head approached, but she didn’t let herself slow down. She didn’t have any harpoons or swords or any of the countless other weapons she witnessed people prepare all around her, but that was fine.

She had something far more dangerous.

Large, emerald wings erupted out of her back at the last moment, and she used them like an extension of her body to shove herself to the side just as the first of the sea serpents reached her. Rather than get a mouthful of her flesh and blood, the serpent screeched when Samantha used Razor Wing, slicing the monster’s jaw clean off and turning the surrounding water red with its blood. She didn’t have any time to celebrate, however, as a second serpent took its place almost instantly, swimming directly at her in the hopes of doing what its fellow monster had failed to.

Taking a trick out of Mag’s book, Samantha used Fountain, shooting a powerful blast of water out from her hands in order to propel herself out of the serpent’s path. Adjusting the angle of her hands, she shot through the water, shifting her wing and using the razor-sharp edge to cut deep into another serpent and spill another few gallons of blood into the water. Using both her wings and Fountain in this way was beyond difficult, but she hadn’t spent all those hours of practice for nothing.

Shooting through the water, narrowly dodging another sea serpent before scoring a deep gash down its side, Samantha nearly crashed into the side of another sea serpent when she rocketed forward out of nowhere. Her head whipped back as water exploded out of the back of her wings, the force of the twin geysers strong enough that she went screaming through the water. As her new water-propelled wings cleaved straight through another serpent, she laughed when she quickly gained control of her new speed and noticed the surprise notification.

Water Wings (active) - lvl 1 - Propel yourself through the water with your wings as if you were flying.

Water Wings - lvl 2

Samantha wanted to whoop for joy at the realization she’d just unlocked her first blended skill, but the gnashing jaws of another serpent quickly snapped her out of her celebration as she nearly lost a leg. She was now faster than ever before in the water, but the rest of the sea serpents had finally caught up with the forerunners. Even as the gathered warriors of Poltar met the attacking force head on, they were quickly being overwhelmed by sheer numbers. From what she’d been told, it normally took a solid handful of people working together to take down even a single sea serpent. Not only were there dozens swimming up from the lake bed, their eyes were enraged and their hunger seemingly endless.

It wasn’t long before the water was filled with just as many warbling, human screams as it was with dying screeches from the serpents. Samantha gritted her teeth and continued soaring around in the water, burning energy at a concerning rate as she did everything she could to help out. To her astonishment, it soon became clear that after Sarah, somehow Samantha herself was the one doing the most damage to the small army of serpents. The warriors of Poltar were skilled, there was no question about that. But it seemed almost as if they were continuously being caught off guard as they battled the serpents. It took her a few minutes to realize the problem, before she spotted one harpoon wielder move to strike and dodge in a very specific way, only for the serpent to jerk unnaturally after him and smash its head into the warrior’s body, cracking bones and sending him spinning through the water.

All these people have trained against sea serpents and are used to the monsters fighting a certain way… but the lich enraged them. Their own muscle memory and training is coming back to bite them now.

Asher always complained that fighting against monsters was boring because monsters couldn’t think for themselves and were incredibly predictable. These warriors had spent their whole lives growing used to the sea serpents’ attack patterns, only to suddenly find that knowledge useless and doing more harm than good. While a handful of them seemed to have come to the same realization she had and were doing their best to adapt, this wasn’t exactly the most ideal training ground. Other than the powerful whirlpool swirling up above which had already stopped a number of sea serpents in its tracks—the currents catching the monsters and hurling them right back down—the entire lake felt like it was filled with sea serpents.

Steeling her resolve as she watched another warrior from Poltar fail to dodge out of the way and get his arm snapped off by an enraged serpent, Samantha angled her wings and rocketed toward her next target. If everything went according to plan, Asher would find the lich responsible for all this and finish them off before long. That would end the enraged effect, but that wouldn’t make the army of giant monsters vanish.

If it meant saving Poltar and the thousands of innocent lives up above, then so long as she had even a speck of energy left within her core, she would keep fighting. It might be too late for the people of her own village.

But so long as she still drew breath, the Council of Death wouldn’t take anyone’s home out from under them ever again.
