I stood on the edge of a cliff while the wind whipped at my clothes and the ocean crashed far below.

Two men fought on the narrow stretch of rock in front of me.

One wore a mask, and the other had long hair.

I recognized them immediately.

Westley and Inigo Montoya.

Their blades flashed in the sunlight as they circled each other carefully. Every strike and parry unfolded exactly as I remembered. The duel played out with theatrical precision, like the famous cliff fight scene from The Princess Bride.

Steel rang against steel.

I watched quietly.

Then the world shifted.

The wind vanished. The roar of the ocean faded away. The rocky cliffs dissolved like mist, and suddenly I was sitting in the comfortable darkness of my father's high-rise condo. Somehow, I did not even notice the lapse in continuity.

The massive home cinema screen filled the wall in front of me.

The same duel continued on the screen as if nothing had changed.

I sank deeper into the couch.

I was not alone.

Spread across the couches beside me were Manal, Nina, Louis, Ja’a, Vena, and Nakera. They lounged comfortably as if movie nights together had always been normal.

The duel continued.

On the screen, Inigo Montoya suddenly switched his grip.

"I am not left-handed."

At that moment, everyone reacted differently.

Vena leaned forward slightly and narrowed her eyes, treating it with far more seriousness than the scene deserved.

Nina and Ja’a burst into laughter and clapped enthusiastically.

Louis grinned and shook her head.

Nakera booed loudly at the screen.

I blinked slowly.

Something felt slightly wrong, but the thought left me as soon as it arrived, and I continued watching.

The movie accelerated.

Scenes skipped forward without warning until we suddenly reached the castle.

Inigo Montoya faced Count Rugen, stumbling because of his wound, but his voice remained steady.

"Hello. My name is Inigo Montoya. You killed my father. Prepare to die."

The line repeated again and again as the fight intensified.

I rubbed my temple.

There was a faint pressure in my head. It was not exactly pain, but something pushed uncomfortably against my thoughts.

I blinked hard and forced the sensation away.

The duel ended the way I remembered it.

Inigo drove his blade forward.

The count collapsed, and the music swelled.

I turned toward Manal with exaggerated disbelief.

"I cannot believe you have never watched The Princess Bride," I said in a reproachful tone.

Manal rolled her eyes.

"Do not start with me," she said calmly. "I bet you have never consumed any Tunisian media either."

I opened my mouth to protest.

"You westerners produce a thousand movies every year," she continued. "I cannot possibly watch all of them."

"But The Princess Bride is a classic," I insisted.

Manal gestured toward the rest of the couch.

"If it is such a classic, how come none of your friends have seen it either?"

I waved vaguely toward the others.

"Because they come from a medieval fantasy world."

I paused.

My brow slowly furrowed.

Realization crept across my mind.

"Ah," I said slowly. "I am dreaming, aren't I?"

"Yes," Nina answered casually.

I turned toward her.

Out of everyone there, Nina looked the most comfortable. Her wings were folded neatly behind her while she leaned back with relaxed amusement.

I studied her carefully.

"Nina, is that actually you, or are you just a figment of my mind?"

She shrugged.

"It is not nighttime yet, and I am not sleeping," she said. "So I am probably not real."

I sighed.

"This is getting far too meta."

I leaned forward and rubbed my face again.

"Good news," I said after a moment. "I am now lucid."

I glanced around the room again.

"Bad news. I was pretty sure we were under attack."

I closed my eyes and tried to wake up.

Nothing happened.

I tried again, forcing the dream to collapse.

The room remained stubbornly solid.

I opened my eyes and looked back at Nina.

"Hey Nina," I asked, "how do I wake myself up?"

She raised an eyebrow.

"You know you are technically talking to yourself right now."

I nodded thoughtfully.

"I know. I figured you probably represent my subconscious dreamer knowledge or something symbolic like that."

Nina snapped her fingers.

"Bingo."

She leaned forward slightly.

"If you want to wake up, you need to do something symbolic that represents awakening for you."

I concentrated and tried to change the dream.

The cinema flickered briefly but refused to shift completely.

I frowned.

"Someone else is forcing you to stay asleep," Nina said thoughtfully. "Try finding your dream sanctuary first. You will have better control there."

I considered that.

My dream sanctuary always appeared as my college flat.

Except my college was in a completely different city from my father's condo.

I blinked.

Of course.

This was a dream.

Physical distance meant nothing.

I stood up from the couch, leaving the home cinema.

Instead of heading toward the apartment exit, I walked down the hallway toward my old bedroom.

My father had always insisted I keep a room there. I only slept in it for a week or two every year when I visited. Still, the room remained mine.

I grabbed the door handle and opened it.

The room looked exactly as I remembered; a tidy guest bedroom that never quite felt lived in. Still, it was a nice gesture that I rarely appreciated when I was on Earth. It told me that he cared in his own way, even if he rarely had enough time for me when I visited.

I stepped inside and looked around.

"Not quite right," I muttered.

I stepped back into the hallway, closed the door, and opened it again.

The room changed.

Now it was my childhood bedroom from before my parents divorced; bright colors, posters on the wall, and shelves cluttered with old toys.

A bittersweet memory stirred inside my chest.

I hesitated for a moment.

Then I closed the door again.

I opened it once more.

Now I stood inside my room at my mother's house.

"Nope," I said firmly.

I slammed the door shut harder than I needed to and opened it again.

This time, the room changed again. My dorm room appeared.

I stepped inside slowly. The air felt different, sharper and clearer. As I crossed the threshold, a wave of control settled over my thoughts.

The dream stopped resisting me. This was my sanctuary. Here, inside my own mind, I finally felt completely in control.

First things first, I checked my phone.

The screen lit up immediately.

I had learned that the battery in my dream represented my Inspiration, the nebulous fuel that dream magic worked on.

Full charge.

I had expected as much. I had been experiencing a ton of new things lately and having a lot of fun. Inspiration tended to recharge when the mind was active and curious. Besides, I had not accessed this place in quite a while.

I put the phone back into my pocket and then tossed myself onto my bed, stretching my arms and cracking my back slightly.

"Alright," I muttered to myself. "Now, how do I wake up?"

One obvious method came to mind: Falling.

Dreams hated sudden impacts. You would always find yourself awake before hitting the ground. Falling on my back from a high place should work.

I got out of my bed and walked toward the tarot table in the middle of the room.

It was an odd piece of furniture for a dorm room, probably because it was the only thing that was not a one-to-one replica of my real-life dorm room. I had added this piece to the space when expanding my sanctuary.

At the center of the table lay the Fool card.

I did not need to flip through the deck to find the card I was looking for. I simply picked the topmost card.

The Star.

I placed the Star at the center in place of the Fool and then placed the Fool on top of the deck.

The moment I finished the switch, the room shifted. The walls of my dorm room dissolved like smoke. A cold wind replaced the still air of the apartment. The floor beneath my feet turned into packed dirt.

When the transition finished, I stood inside my makeshift camp in the Sunless Reach. The dark sky stretched endlessly overhead.

Perfect.

I pulled my phone out again and slipped my earbuds into my ears.

I selected a song, then pressed play.

The music started, soft at first, a distant horn, then a slow, rising tension.

With a small push of will and a flicker of Inspiration, I altered the dream.

The sky responded instantly.

Stars began to fall.

A meteor shower spread across the heavens, streaks of light tearing through the darkness as burning fragments carved brilliant lines across the sky.

It looked magnificent.

I closed my eyes as I felt star mana fill my body.

"I wish I were falling from up high," I murmured.

Gravity shifted as the ground vanished beneath my feet.

For a moment, I simply floated.

Then the pull began.

The invisible force grabbed hold of me and yanked me downward.

My stomach lurched violently as the drop accelerated.

Wind roared in my ears as the music swelled.

Then the singer's voice began. The familiar French lyrics flowed through my headphones.

Non, rien de rien. ♪Non, je ne regrette rien. ♫

My body continued falling through the endless darkness.

The sensation twisted my gut and tightened my chest.

My mind braced itself for the impact.

My eyes opened, awake.

For a moment, the music was still playing in my ears. Then the sound vanished, and all that remained was the smell of smoke.

My brain refused to process what I was seeing at first. The metal thing looked almost like an abstract shape from this perspective, something from a diagram in a textbook. I had to blink away the confusion to recognize it as a spearhead. The tip hovered a few centimeters above my face.

Then the rest of the world rushed back in.

Sand under my back, the distant smell of burning, and the woman holding the spear.

She stood over me with brown wings half spread for balance, the weapon steady in her hands. Her expression was focused, and she was looking somewhere in the distance rather than at me.

I had no idea who she was.

The last clear memory I had was of me drying in the sun and of brown feathers… her feathers.

She must be the dreamer who put me to sleep.

My thoughts raced.

What happened to the team?

Why was someone trying to kill me?

I forced myself to keep breathing steadily and flicked my eyes sideways, trying to take in as much of the battlefield as I could without moving my head.

Four people, including the dreamer valkeren girl, were standing over me. At least four. There could be more. I could not tell.

Someone lay on the ground beside me.

Vena.

Her eyes were closed, but she was still breathing.

Further away, I saw three figures standing in opposition to my captors.

I could not make out their faces from this angle, only silhouettes shifting against the light. The closest one was feminine, probably Kan.

My brain tried to assemble the fragments of information into something coherent.

It felt like waking in the middle of a movie without knowing the plot.

The woman above me adjusted her grip on the spear as if she were getting ready for something.

The point lowered slightly.

Right… Survive first. Questions later.

I moved.

My hand shot up and grabbed the shaft of the spear. Before she could react, I shoved the weapon upward toward her chin with all the strength I had.

The motion was clumsy and desperate, but it worked.

She jerked her head back instinctively to avoid the attack, taking half a step back, and her grip loosened for the smallest fraction of a second.

That moment was enough.

I twisted sideways and rolled across the ground, sand spraying everywhere as I grabbed Vena’s arm and rolled her with me.

We kept rolling down the slope of the terrain, letting gravity pull us both down the ridge.

Dust exploded into the air around us.

I took advantage of the cover and summoned my spear back into my hand. I gathered half the Star mana stored inside it and made a wish.

“I wish we were relatively safe next to our allies.”

Then the world folded.

The next moment, the ground snapped back into place beneath my feet. I was now standing while holding Vena in a hug. She seemed to be waking from the sudden movement.

I had expected to be teleported near Kan. I guess that was not safe enough for the “relatively safe” condition of the wish.

We were somewhere else, a few dozen meters away from where we had been.

We stood on top of a low ridge overlooking the battlefield, behind me, two figures still lay motionless on the ground.

Katar and Shingo.

I guessed they had been targeted by the dreamer's magic while standing farther away than the rest of the group.

At least I assumed it had been the dreamer’s attack.

“Are all members of your team resistant to Dreamer magic?” I heard someone shout in frustration, disbelief sharp in her voice.

I ground my teeth and forced myself to focus, assessing the surroundings once again. It seemed Raik and Calr were already awake, while Ja’a was still sleeping behind a boulder.

I needed everyone to wake up.

I grabbed my bident spear and jabbed the butt of it into Katar’s side.

Once. Twice. Three times.

Katar woke like a coiled spring snapping loose.

His eyes opened, and his body moved before he was fully conscious. In one smooth motion, he rolled to his feet with his blade already drawn, scanning the battlefield with the cold focus of a predator.

His gaze locked onto the winged woman across the field. I could see the fury ignite in his expression. It was not hard to deduce who he believed was responsible for him finding himself face-first in the dirt.

I jabbed Shingo next.

He groaned and swatted at the spear before finally pushing himself upright.

Behind me, Vena gasped.

By the time I turned around, she was already looking across the battlefield.

Her eyes widened.

“Raik,” she whispered.

She took off running down the ridge before I could even ask what she had seen.

I followed her gaze and finally noticed it.

Something I had missed upon first inspection. Raik might have been awake and standing, but his armor was blackened, and his chest was covered in ugly burn wounds.

So that explained the smell of smoke.

The battlefield erupted into motion around us.

Across the field, the enemy group had clearly realized the tide was shifting.

The four of them stood together. At first, I did not recognize any of their faces. Except one of them had blood red hair. Could she be the same girl from Ectamel earlier this week?

They exchanged quick glances.

Then they ran.

The red-haired girl glanced back once before sprinting after the others.

All four bolted southward at full speed.

Katar did not hesitate.

With a snarl, he leapt down from the ridge and sprinted after them, blade flashing in the fading sunlight.

He was fast. Terrifyingly fast.

But the gap was already wide.

One of the fleeing enemies, a girl with magenta hair, turned while running and threw a fireball. It streaked backward through the air.

Katar swerved to avoid the blast as it struck the ground and exploded in a plume of flame and sand.

More fireballs followed.

Each one forced him to slow down.

Behind me, the rest of the team scrambled to their feet.

Shingo was already moving.

Calr raised his crossbow and steadied his aim.

For a moment, I expected him to fire. His finger rested on the trigger.

Instead, he slowly lowered the weapon.

Down below, Vena had reached Raik.

She dropped to her knees beside him and pressed a glowing hand against the worst of the burns across his chest. Golden light poured into the torn skin.

Raik winced as the healing started working. He pushed himself to remain standing and looked toward Katar, who was still chasing the retreating enemies across the dead zone.

Raik inhaled sharply and raised his voice.

“Katar, back!”

The command carried across the battlefield.

Katar slowed.

The sun was already sinking toward the horizon, and long shadows stretched across the broken ground.

Chasing them into unknown territory at dusk would have been suicide, especially in the undead dead zone where monsters became more active at night.

Katar stood there for a moment, breathing hard.

Then he finally turned back, fury written across his face.

By the time he reached us again, Vena was still kneeling beside Raik, praying softly as she finished mending the worst of his burns.

Golden light flowed steadily from her hands.

Raik exhaled slowly in relief. He had probably been in agony.

Kan stood nearby with a worried expression. She had a few cuts of her own, but nothing nearly as bad as Raik’s injuries.

For a moment, no one spoke.

The battlefield grew quiet again.

Only the wind moved through the dead zone, whispering across the cracked earth and stirring loose sand into thin, drifting lines.

Far to the south, the silhouettes of our enemies grew smaller as they ran, their figures slowly fading into the haze of distance.

I watched them for another moment, half expecting them to turn around and come back.

They did not.

Once Vena finished checking Raik’s wounds, I finally relaxed a little and ran toward Ja’a.

She was still lying behind the boulder where I had last seen her, completely motionless. For a second, my chest tightened with worry, but when I knelt beside her, I could see the steady rise and fall of her breathing.

I crouched down and placed a hand on her shoulder, giving her a firm shake.

“Wake up.”

Ja’a’s eyes snapped open instantly.

She bolted upright like someone who had been pulled from deep water.

“We are under attack, southward!” she shouted, already turning her head as if looking for targets.

Then she blinked.

Her gaze swept across the battlefield again, slower this time.

“Oh,” she said after a moment. “They are running away.”

She pushed herself fully to her feet, brushing sand off her clothes as her eyes moved toward the rest of the group.

Her attention quickly locked onto Raik.

Vena was still kneeling beside him with both hands glowing softly as she finished the last of the healing prayer.

Ja’a tilted her head slightly, her expression shifting to confusion.

“Raik, are those burn wounds?” she asked. “Since when do you burn?”

Raik flexed his fingers experimentally.

“Since after getting my power stolen, apparently,” he replied.

Katar, who had been pacing like a caged animal near the edge of the ridge, stopped abruptly and turned toward him.

“They took your power?” he asked with a scowl.

His hand tightened around the hilt of his blade.

“Why did you call me back?” he continued, frustration clear in his voice. “We need her to give it back to you.”

Raik shook his head slowly.

“It is temporary,” he said. “I think I can feel my soul pushing back against her curse.”

Calr nodded in agreement from where he stood a few meters away, his crossbow still hanging loosely in his hands.

“It is temporary,” he said calmly. “Your power will return once she gets too far away from you.”

Almost as if the world had been waiting for him to say it, heat suddenly bloomed from Raik’s hand. Then a faint shimmer of flame curled around his fingers.

Raik stared at it for a moment.

Then a visible wave of relief spread across his face.

His shoulders relaxed, and he let out a long breath he had probably been holding since the fight started.

A lot had clearly happened while I was asleep.

Watching them talk about stolen powers and curses, I felt a small knot of embarrassment twist in my stomach.

Everyone else had been fighting while I lay unconscious. The thought quickly turned to anger. How dare they ambush us like that?

I still had one teleport left in my spear. I could follow them for a while… then teleport back.

I shook my head. That was a stupid idea. Think like a scholar, Alice. I might have killed a troll, but that doesn’t make me qualified to hunt fugitives through the Deadzone.

I should go back to a quiet life once Raik’s challenge is over. Otherwise, I might find myself fighting in an arena or something equally insane.

“I am sorry,” Ja’a said suddenly, rubbing the back of her neck with an awkward expression. “It appears they have a soul seer as well, and she outranged me. That is why I did not see them coming.”

“Suu the archer is the soul seer,” Calr said.

Ja’a frowned slightly as she processed that information.

Raik’s eyes narrowed as he looked toward Calr.

“You seem to know a lot about them,” he said slowly. “You also hesitated to shoot them while they were running.”

Calr shifted his weight slightly and looked away toward the horizon.

For a moment, he said nothing.

Then he sighed.

“It is a long story.”

Raik straightened up slowly, rolling his shoulders as if testing how much strength had returned to his body.

“You are right,” he said after a moment. “Let us head back to camp before we get stranded here by nightfall.”

The sun was already dipping toward the horizon, painting the sky in dull orange light.

In the dead zone, darkness came fast.

And when night came, the undead became far more active.

I turned my head south again, staring toward the direction our attackers had fled.

“I wonder if they have a way to deal with the undead,” I said.

Ja’a followed my gaze.

“They are heading south,” she said thoughtfully. “I remember Todor and Izar also ran straight west from Pikar, which was much farther south than where we are now.”

Kan shrugged lightly.

“Maybe they know of a safe place,” she said. “Or a hideout.”

Raik clapped his hands once, drawing everyone’s attention back to him.

“Anyhow, grab your gear,” he said firmly. “The picnic is over. Back to camp.”

No one argued.

One by one, we started gathering our equipment from the battlefield, mostly our discarded armor that we had taken off before the swim. That had been a stupid idea in hindsight, but we were not expecting a human ambush deep in undead territory.

I gave the lake one last look before we left, watching the sauropod still bathing at the center without a care that the lake shore had been an active battlefield.

To such large creatures, our human squabbles must have seemed insignificant.
