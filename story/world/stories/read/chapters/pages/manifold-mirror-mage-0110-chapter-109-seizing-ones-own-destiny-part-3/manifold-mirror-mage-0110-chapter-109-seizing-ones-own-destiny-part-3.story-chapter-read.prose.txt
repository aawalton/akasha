The Scriptorium, then. I wonder if he’s finally going to give me the official sales pitch.

At the very least… She’s good with words.

Alecto smiled at her protege. She summoned a curious box that reminded Gio of the ones present in Saint Baphelus’s ‘Vault of Infinite Repose’. It was lined with gold, and a scrawl of protective enchantment nearly obscured the object within… but it was clearly some kind of parchment scrap.

“Well, it certainly must be nice to be royalty.” Saint Vaught dryly joked.

Legendary?!

This spell is also from my private collection. One of five known surviving samples of the work of legendary master spellcrafter, Cyr of Thornbough.”

If I may be so unprofessional… those of you who have high-minded aspirations may find yourself reaching back to this moment for years to come in your mind’s eye. Gio hungrily eyed the box.

Is… is it a bad idea…

Before he could convince himself otherwise, Gio began clandestinely fiddling with something in his pocket… a compact mirror. He clumsily traced a message into the glass, using the smear of the oil on his fingers as a makeshift ink. He knew that there was no way that he could get away with casting any magic under the noses of the spirits and the professors… but perhaps Rio could get a better look at the supposedly legendary spell in front of him.

Sybil stepped up to the platform. She cast some sort of variation of the [Cleanse] spell on her hands that stripped the fine red polish on her fingers away, before she delicately unclasped the box, retrieving the fragile parchment inside.

From the moment it left the box, Gio felt it.

It was a spotlight of attention. It wasn’t bright, or dripping with mana, or the source of some great gust of wind, or any of the usual ‘this thing is magical’ symptoms… but this singular piece of paper with crease lines and faded ink was the focal point of so much.

In a subtle motion, all of the statues of spirits in the back of the arena shifted their gazes to focus on the girl holding the paper.

The Ataraxian Princess closed her eyes and began to hum. It was a lilting tune, not incredible or particularly memorable… but a perfect match for the spell that began unfolding around her. From nothing, a sturdy oak chair appeared underneath her, and she sat down as if it had been pulled out by a servant specifically for her. In front of her, a desk appeared, upon which she delicately set the legendary spellform.

The room began to shift. The ambient light of the ceiling above was no more, replaced by a grey expanse of clouds. Gio was tempted to compare the endless gray above him with the perpetual fog of [Telchine’s Garden], but this sky made the imitation of the dungeon look so… fake.

That’s what this sensation is. This feels real.

So real, in fact, that Gio thought he felt the first droplet of rain.

“[A Perfectly Cloudy Day].” Sybil invoked.

The walls melted away, and Gio felt that he was somewhere else. A cottage somewhere amongst a tall hillside, where he was knee-deep in a sea of wind-blown grass. A pleasant mist dappled his cheeks, the promise of springtime soon to come. He walked towards the cottage.

Lighting a candle, he moved through the cozy hall towards his desk. Gio watched the faint trails of raindrops race to reach the sill of the window in front of him as he returned to his work.

And work he did. Ideas flowed to and through him as he sat with his spell, clumsily taking his favorite quill to the parchment and drafting - by hand- the symbols of the magical working. Though mistakes were frequent, the year’s harvest profits had been good, and the parchment would last until the caravan came to restock him. He thought of his time at Vitrium, where he-

He thought-

Where am I? What am I doing?

Gio looked across the old wooden table at a young woman with black hair and piercing silver eyes that reflected his own. Sybil looked at him curiously, still humming her tune.

Gio looked around, spotting a sparse handful of other faces at the table looking his way. Saint Vaught had noticed his presence there, as had Professor Anaximenes, the mysterious cloaked professor, Alecto Stradivari… and the Owl.

Gio desperately wanted to ask what was happening, but was exceedingly wary of saying anything to disrupt the spell, so instead, he allowed his eyes to look around the illusory room. The desk he had been sitting at mere moments ago was pressed up against a window that displayed the serene hillside beyond, and the gentle waves of the clouds beyond that passed by. True to the name of the spell, those clouds were hypnotizing to watch.

Though Gio was no longer subject to whatever confusing illusion or memory had taken over him, he still felt a sense of serene calm in this place. Looking around the quiet table, it seemed that others had closed their eyes, likely also enjoying the sensation.

Gio joined them. He instinctively tried meditating, finding that the attempt was instantly successful. Distractions vanished, and he plunged into a crystalline pool of focus and clarity.

But, all too soon, he was gently taken from his focus by the cessation of Sybil’s humming.

Only… the calm didn’t seem to end. He looked around the stands, finding some students sleeping… and others furiously scratching notes into their papers.

Is… this the point of the spell?

Gio recalled the writing desk. The sensation of rewarding work and the perfect environment.

“It’s… a perfectly cloudy day. The author imbued the spell with the focus of a day when everything was just right for him to perform at his highest level.” Gio muttered.

“A-Astonishing,” Papyrus whispered.

“Well, obviously, you pass.” Saint Vaught said. “Student body… if you wish to induct Miss Sybil amongst your ranks, please raise-”

A sea of hands raised instantaneously. Gio thought for a moment…

A bribe. That’s what this is. This lingering sense of calm will undoubtedly be beneficial for those who have yet to cast their domains. Well played.

But did he want the Ataraxian Princess at the school? Did she deserve to attend classes alongside him and the other students after having missed out on most of the first year of classes? He thought of the Telchines and their treatment at the hands of Cybele, goddess of Ataraxia. He thought of the gross display of affluence that Sybil’s mentor had dropped to sweeten the pot for the professors.

Gio sighed before raising his hand as well.

I cannot personally hold her responsible for the actions of her nation’s goddess. That’s just dumb. I don’t love that she’s bribing her way into the Academy… but this school is already full of rich kids, and she’s undoubtedly a great mage. To have cast a legendary spell… and to this insane degree of proficiency… she’s more than just a rich princess. If I didn’t raise my hand, it would be out of jealousy or petty reasons… not because I genuinely believe that she didn’t deserve to be here.

“Well, it seems that the class has spoken. Miss Quinta, please join your classmates at the end of the hall.”

A whooping round of applause echoed outward from the stands, and Sybil bowed before making her way to the other side of the room.

She locked eyes with Gio, beelining straight towards him.

Uhh…
