Chapter V.XXX (5.30) -  Run

As Kizu dashed down the hallways, he instructed Mort to jump to the academy’s beacon to pull him out of the earthy containment Professor Krimpit had trapped within. While Wakino was actively smothering spatial transportation inside the academy, the beacon’s pull remained strong enough to draw the monkey in.

He felt his familiar comply with Kizu’s request and disappear from the tunnel at the same moment he reappeared in Kizu’s senses on the other side of campus.

As expected, there was a welcome party in the beacon room. Two students were paralyzed on the ground, both with glittering blue handprints. They’d likely also attempted some form of spatial transportation. While Kizu was the only student who could jump, he’d heard rumors that a few of the students from extremely rich families had artifacts to help them move around short distances. For over a month now, Kizu had been intending to look into one girl who had a pair of boots that stepped between shadows, but had never found the time for it. He saw her now though. Her boots hadn’t gotten her far when pitted against their current opponents.

Mort tugged and tried to move, but found his feet stuck in a thick adhesive lathered onto the stone tiles.

“Little monkey!” Professor Knoff crooned.

The crazed professor stepped forward with hands clasped behind his back. Unsurprisingly, for this event he existed in his less civilized form. Chaotic white hair, thin frame, and a devilish smile.

Kizu, ducking around a corner into a hallway closet, focused on Mort’s predicament.

Mort tugged on the ground. He was firmly stuck in place. Whatever was coating the floor tiles in this room, it was not releasing him easily. Kizu could feel his familiar’s frustration with having just left a prison of earth only to be immobilized in a different way.

Thankfully, Kizu could make some educated guesses at what the substance was. While not exactly the same as the adhesive he’d brewed last semester, he recognized the signs of overlapping ingredients in this trap. The charcoal black coloring and tar-like texture both were key clues that granted him a solid base of understanding.

“Burn it,” Kizu suggested. Without thinking, he spoke the words out loud. Thankfully, the janitorial equipment in the closet didn’t seem disturbed by his verbal outburst.

Following Kizu’s advice, Mort lit himself aflame with elemental magic. The adhesive that bound him to the stone floor bubbled as it liquidized under the magical heat. Mort seized on his freedom and leaped away from the adhesive-cover floor.

“Tutt, tutt,” Knoff tutted. He waggled a finger in Mort’s direction. “Naughty monkey. What am I to do with you? Let you run off to Mr. Kaga? You aren’t a student but it would hardly be prudent to let you slip away. Hmm. Bait? Yes, I like that idea. How about I use you as bait? I know Mr. Kaga can be here in just a moment, should he wish it. An excellent opportunity!”

Kizu clenched his fist. It could be a bluff. Not even Krimpit would go so far as to threaten Mort. But this version of Knoff… well, Kizu just wasn’t certain. And in past conversations the sane Professor Knoff had casually mentioned that his other self bore Kizu a bit of ill will since he’d been working on a solution to merge the fragmented soul into something whole again. This version of Knoff hadn’t been seen by Kizu in months so he’d put it out of his mind, thinking it inconsequential. A mistake.

Knoff laughed manically and pulled out four vials. Each glowed a different color. Green, gray, blue, and purple. He threw them all up into the air. Kizu tensed as they descended. But Knoff caught them, one by one. Not a single one coming close to Mort.

Knoff began to juggle the potions. They flew in an arc overhead. Then the crazed professor slammed a palm into the purple one as it fell and the vial shot forward.

Mort, still aflame, darted to the left. The potion vial shattered and the brilliant purple color expanded rapidly. A gas attack.

Thankfully, Kizu and Mort knew a simple solution. Mort created an elemental orb of air around himself. The miasma split as its cloud rolled over his defense.

Through the thick mist, Mort’s eyes spotted one of the downed students. Kizu paid close attention as the girl’s skin bulged as boils grew. Hitting subdued students as collateral hardly seemed allowed, let alone sporting. But who was Kizu going to complain to? He couldn’t just run across the campus to the headmaster.

Then it hit him. Kizu knew exactly where he needed to go. For the past minute he’d been huddled in a closet but this was hardly the secure location he would have liked. If someone opened the closet door he’d be completely out of options and out of the game. Well… he supposed one option would be to jump to the academy beacon and get caught there instead. But that was hardly a real solution. No. He now knew where he needed to go.

However, for the moment, Mort’s predicament remained.

Kizu closed his eyes and focused. He knew the academy beacon’s room’s layout extremely well, having spent hours in there training himself on resistance techniques. Based on Mort’s current position as well as Knoff’s, he formed a plan.

While he’d never attempted to cast this spell using Mort as a conduit, Kizu still decided to take the risk. Normally, this spell would require somatic movements to properly cast, but with enough focus Kizu believed he could bypass those requirements. He just needed to utilize its most basic usage.

Mid-juggle, Knoff smacked a green vial in Mort’s direction. It soared through the air and left a poof of clean air through the miasma in its wake.

Kizu stretched the space in front of his familiar and snapped it.

Mort flew into the air. The green vial shattered in his wake and a green cloud of smog formed into an ethereal grasping hand. Kizu felt a shudder ripple up Mort’s spine as the glowing fingertip brushed the end of his tail. Just out of reach.

“Nooooo,” Knoff wailed. “Come back little primate! Return to me!”

Landing gracefully, Mort sprinted out of the room. His flames waned as he released the fiery spell.

It suddenly occurred to Kizu that all Knoff would have needed to do to trap Mort was close the door. All of Kizu’s efforts and thrown-together schemes could have been countered by a simple doorknob. He almost laughed at the oversight. But now Mort was free. And he knew as well as Kizu where they needed to go. He trusted his familiar to reunite without Kizu micromanaging his journey.

Kizu cracked the closet door open and peered out. His breath caught in surprise. The hallway wasn’t quite as empty as he remembered it from a minute earlier. A boy was paralyzed on the ground with a three toed print sparkling on his shoulder. Unless another professor was under the effects of a fowl polymorph potion, that was a clear indicator of Professor Oasaji.

Cornered and with no better option on hand, Kizu ran.

He expected to hear the flap of wings or the professor’s voice. Instead, a howl sounded in the hallway left of him. A glance over his shoulder revealed a wolf with long strands of hair that jutted out in unnatural angles. While it wasn’t fully multi-headed, it did have an additional snout piercing through each of its shoulders.

Kizu immediately placed a barrier between himself and the summoned monster.

And he rounded a corner only to encounter two more of the beasts, identical to the first. They howled at the sight of him and then lunged in his direction.

Unwilling to allow the beasts to remove chunks of his leg’s flesh, Kizu used spatial magic to snap himself deftly to the side. He then launched himself off of the wall with his monster leg, tearing through the wallpaper and removing a chunk of stone from the wall.

He rolled with the impact as he landed behind the summoned dogs.

As he sprinted through the hallways, Kizu passed one of the spots he’d set up a divination watch point for the token challenge. He dearly wished he’d thought to bring his scrying orb today. He’d be able to far better optimize his pathing if he knew where the professors’ obstacles had been placed. But, then again, he also knew the academy had a means in which to track the orbs. So maybe it was best he’d left it on Owl’s Respite.

Downed students were scattered throughout the hallways. Nine out of every ten of them had Oasaji’s talon print on them. However, the human handprints disturbed Kizu far more than the talons, as it signified an unknown professor nearby.

Only once did Kizu encounter someone still up and actively participating. And that girl glared at him, then ducked into the Art Club’s room as he passed by. The colorful door slammed shut. Too bad. He wouldn’t have minded cooperating.

Another snarling wolf attempted to intercept him, but this time Kizu was the one to lunge forward. His monster leg struck out, slamming into the summoned beast and launching it into a nearby suit of armor. He left behind the clanging mess of canine and metal.

As he looked back to fix his spatial barrier in place to block the wolf, a glint of red on the stones caught his attention.

Kizu skitted to halt. Blood. He glanced down at his pant leg and cursed. The rip from where Krimpit had slashed his calf had become soaked in blood from a wound he hadn’t even properly processed.

Kizu prodded at it. It wasn’t deep. His mind raced and he bit his lip. Then he slowly ran a finger over the gash. His skin closed at his touch. After a dozen seconds, all that remained was a smear of blood and a freshly scabbed over wound. It would open up if he put too much strain on it, but it should be good enough. He just needed to get a bit further.

Next he washed the blood off his torn pants with an orb of water, then tossed the orb as far as possible down the hallway to his right. It plopped against the stone floor with a distant sploosh.

There were constant howls behind Kizu. The wolves clearly had the ability to track him and the blood had been an easy trail for them to follow. He needed to end that. Now at the final stretch, he didn’t want to accidentally lead the wolves right to his safe house’s door.

A massive pendulum swung back and forth in front of him. Kizu snapped space in front of himself to clear the hurdle. He flew past the pendulum and landed safely on the other side, a spatial barrier catching his fall to keep his scent from touching the floor.

“Kaga Kizu?” a confused voice called out to him.

Kizu glanced over his shoulder. He blinked. A familiar Tainted boy clung to the back of the pendulum as it swung.

“Gregor?” Kizu replied.

“Shush!” Gregor called down, not quietly. “You’ll give away my position.”

Kizu hesitated and watched his Music F classmate swinging on the back of a massive slab of metal. “You know, I think I have a….”

“No need. I like this spot. And it’s mine. Served me well so far. Shouldn’t have called out to you in the first place but you surprised me is all.”

Kizu lifted his hand. “Best of luck to you then.”

“Likewise to you.” Gregor saluted him and nearly fell off the pendulum.

In a moment, Kizu created a barrier to catch the Tainted student’s feet to help him adjust his grip. Then Kizu resumed his sprint away without another word exchanged. Every step he created a small barrier underfoot to never touch the ground itself. He figured that would minimize his chances of being tracked by the wolves.

Gregor had the right idea. It wasn’t just about hiding, it was about locating a place so outlandish that no professor would ever think about checking there.

Finally, Kizu arrived at his destination. Panting, he took a moment to compose himself.

Mort scampered up and leaped onto his shoulder.

Kizu raised his fist for one final blow. And knocked.
