Lord Vaun walked over, kicking aside the mana stones and breaking the barrier. He approached Veronica and carefully took the badge from her hand.

He examined the surface, checked the edges, then flipped it over and did the same. His expression tightened with focus, as if he were staring at something fragile and refusing to let it disappear.

Slowly, he closed his eyes and pressed the badge to his forehead.

The appraisal section went quiet.

A minute passed.

Nasha and Gwen walked forward, returning to Veronica’s side now that the barrier was gone.

“Did it work?” Gwen whispered. “Why’s he just standing there?”

“It worked,” Veronica said, not caring about the volume of her voice. “That looks like a Last Rites Badge.”

A few murmurs came from the crowd behind them. Even Harris and the other two junior mages looked at her, before glancing nervously over at Lord Vaun.

“What’s a Last Rites badge?” Gwen asked, no longer whispering.

“It’s an artifact from a revelation mage,” Nasha said. “It holds the last memories of the person wearing it before they die.”

Veronica’s gaze flicked over to Nasha briefly, but she disregarded it. Nasha knowing about it wasn’t too strange. Rogues, especially experienced ones, tended to brush against some of the darker professions. It was as common as lumberjacks being retired adventurers from what Viya had told her in the past.

“Whoever wore it must have been important to him. He’s scrying through the memories now.”

A few more seconds passed before Lord Vaun finally lowered the badge. His eyes remained on Veronica. “Veronica… Everwells, was it?” he asked.

She nodded.

Lord Vaun placed one hand over his heart and bowed. “Thank you for what you have done today. I will not forget this favor.”

A few people widened their eyes. Even the three scholarly mages were taken aback, their eyes flicking wildly between him and everyone present, as if what he saw in front of him was simply an illusion, and wondered if anyone else had caught on.

But it wasn’t an illusion.

“Raise your head, Lord Vaun,” Veronica said. Her expression hadn’t changed, nor had her tone of speaking. “It’s not befitting of someone of status to bow down to a commoner.”

Although part of what she said was simple etiquette and formal respect, she herself also believed those words.

While Veronica was never a noble in the sense of formality, titles, or distinguished lineage or descent, she was still respected as someone similar, but of the highest degree. She’d never bow her head or apologize to someone of a lesser rank, unless they were an acquaintance or aide of hers.

But three years during the demon invasion, where all nobility and prestige were thrown out the fractured window, made her forget that notion.

Lord Vaun straightened. His expression had softened, though Veronica couldn’t tell whether he had found hope or lost the last of it. “As promised, I will accompany you to the upper floors of the archives.”

“That would be appreciated,” Veronica said with a light smile.

Lord Vaun slipped the badge into an inner pocket of his coat. His hand lingered there for a moment, fingers pressing lightly against the fabric.

“May I ask what that badge was?” Veronica asked. “If it’s private, you need not answer.”

“It is private,” Lord Vaun said. “But not something I need to hide from the person who helped me recover it. Once we are away from the first floor, I can explain.”

Veronica nodded. “That’s fine.”

Murmurs rippled through the nearby crowd. Some mentioned balefire. Others whispered about calamity magic. A few still stared at the refinement table, where Harris and his students were inspecting the engraved lines with sour, confused expressions.

Lord Vaun turned toward the staircase. “I need to report this to a few important individuals. So let us—”

“Wait.”

Nasha’s voice came lightly from the side.

Everyone looked toward her.

She smiled directly at Harris. “Aren’t we forgetting something?”

Harris’ expression darkened. “We are not.”

Gwen, seeing what was going on, caught on. She stepped forward. “Oh?” she said, tilting her head. Then she straightened and crossed her arms. “Don’t tell us you forgot about the bet. Something about Lord Vaun being witness.”

She turned to look at the noble. “Isn’t that right?”

Normally, he would have taken offense to being referred to in such a manner by a commoner. However, seeing that the two women were aides of the one who had retrieved the Last Rites Badge, he let it go.

“She is right,” Lord Vaun said plainly, looking over at the scholars. “A kneel and apology.”

The murmuring in the crowd changed as many began to stare at the three mages.

Veronica looked over at Harris. “I believe the condition was that if I successfully melted the resin and retrieved the badge, you would kneel and apologize. So don’t take too long. I’m waiting.”

Harris’ jaw tightened. He looked beside him at his juniors, but they immediately stepped backward.

“This was a bet between Senior and her,” Marcen said.

“Yeah,” the female mage agreed, stepping back. “We aren’t involved.”

“That’s fine. It was indeed only a bet between me and your senior here. Can’t have him leading with dishonesty, after all,” Veronica said.

Harris’ fists tightened at his sides. He wanted to retort, but everyone was already staring at him. Lord Vaun. The crowd. Their gazes felt like daggers. He had been made a fool of, but he had no escape. Even if he accused her of cheating or using an artifact, it wouldn’t work. Lord Vaun was firmly on her side now.

To the surprise of the watching crowd, Harris lowered himself onto one knee. The shocking part wasn’t Harris himself kneeling; it was that a scholar representing the city archives had been forced to kneel.

“I apologize for doubting your capabilities, Miss Veronica,” Harris said begrudgingly. “You have won our bet fair and square.”

Veronica raised her palm. “Very well. I accept your apology. You may stand.”

A vein nearly bulged from his head as he heard the command. As if she were the one in power, and standing would only be allowed if she willed it.

“Thank you for your kindness,” he said lowly.

“Shall we head up to the upper floors, then, Miss Everwells?” Lord Vaun asked.

Veronica nodded. “Let’s go.”

The crowd parted as the four of them left and headed toward the staircase.

The three scholars, Harris and his two juniors, walked away, heading into a room in the back of the appraisal section. They hid their faces well, but most could tell that Harris had been humiliated.

Second Floor, Starfault Archives.

The second floor of the archives was less dense than the first. But that made sense. As Veronica walked toward the first row and looked at the nearest shelf, she noticed that all of the texts were quite old. None seemed to have been written within the past ten, maybe twenty years. While the first floor felt more like a library, the second floor felt much more like an archive of old knowledge.

The air itself was different, too.

A hint of dust in the air. Quiet, and calm.

Not because there were no people, but because those allowed on this floor clearly understood the value of silence. A few robed scholars sat at private tables, copying notes from brittle manuscripts. Two older men in noble attire stood beside a display case, speaking in low voices while an attendant explained something about an old treaty document. Farther in, a young mage walked past with three books floating beside him.

“What is it that you are looking for, Miss Everwells?” Lord Vaun asked, waiting patiently as she looked through a few books.

“I’m looking for anything about old blooming magic. Spellbooks, specifically. Not theory,” she said, setting a book back in place.

“Any specific pathways?”

“Path of Longevity or Path of Splitting. I’m interested in any works by Fia Twindale, the ninth-tier archmage.”

“Fia Twindale… I see. I’m not too familiar with magic, but I can ask the clerk to send someone around to search for you.”

She turned to face him. “Can you do that?”

He nodded. “I assume you may not know who I am, but I am Lord Vassius Vaun of the Vaun family, one of the five great noble houses here in Maftem. My status is more than enough to grant you access to the top floor of the archives.”

Upon hearing his title, Veronica’s expression didn’t change. “I see. Then that would be very helpful.”

Seeing her unfazed, he nodded. “I’ll speak to the clerk and have them grant you the right to visit the top floor, if you wish. However, I’ll need to be on my way soon to relay the information recorded in this badge.”

“Oh, hey!” Gwen called from a few shelves down. “It’s a book on spirits!”

The two of them looked over at her, then quickly resumed their conversation.

“Is it something dire?” Veronica asked. “You seemed quite desperate to retrieve the badge.”

With a sigh, Lord Vaun nodded. The light in his eyes dimmed as he managed an answer. “This badge belonged to one of my sons. Tancred. He was… on an expedition to the west. Some trade-related matters.”

“What happened?”

Considering he had left behind a Last Rites Badge, something must have gone wrong.

“On his way there, he and his group encountered a demon. They were… all killed.”

Veronica’s expression tightened. “A demon? What rank was it?”

“My son had two sixth-tier mages accompanying him, along with several fifth-tier knights. They fought valiantly, but barely managed to leave more than a flesh wound on it.”

The worry on Veronica’s face only grew. “A commander-type demon?”

Lord Vaun shook his head. “I’m… not sure. But what I do know is that none of them were able to defeat it. It toyed with them.”

“How did you retrieve the badge? And why was it in resin?”

Lord Vaun pulled out the badge and stared down at it. His eyes were nearly hollow. “I had a mage cast a retrieval spell on it beforehand. If it were ever tampered with, it would teleport back to my desk. As for why it was encased in resin… the demon is to blame. It used some type of demonic power to create the resin. Walls, weapons, even chains to bind several of the knights. It manipulated the substance almost like a mage controlling water. I was desperate to break the resin apart, but couldn’t find a proper method until I met you.”

“When did this happen?”

“Roughly two weeks ago. Since then, I’ve tried every method I could to melt the resin, until I received a tip to check the Starfault Archives.”

Gwen had gone quiet at the end of the shelf. Even with the book about spirits still in her hands, her excitement had faded. Nasha also turned fully toward them, her casual expression thinning into something more serious.

“A demon appeared on a trade route two weeks ago?” Nasha asked. “Did anyone report it?”

Lord Vaun’s lips pressed into a thin line. “The relevant people know.”

“That wasn’t what I asked.” Her eyes were sharp, as if she had spotted a lie.

Veronica glanced toward Nasha. Lord Vaun did as well.

For a moment, his noble demeanor returned, as if instinctively preparing an answer to an accusation. But then his gaze fell back to the badge in his palm.

That demeanor disappeared almost entirely. “No,” he said after a moment. “I did not report this to anyone. I can’t.”

Gwen frowned. “Why not? A demon that killed… sorry, I’m bad with my words. But a demon attacking people seems like something that should be reported, right?”

Veronica turned to face Lord Vaun. She had caught a hint of it when he was purposely vague about who exactly he had been trading with.

“You can’t, can you?” she said.

He glanced at her, but refused to make eye contact. His gaze dropped back to the badge in his hand before he tucked it away inside his coat. “No,” he said. “I cannot.”

“Why not?” Gwen asked.

“It was for Oprus, wasn’t it?” Veronica said, answering for him.

“…Yes.”

“Don’t we have sanctions against Oprus?” Nasha asked.

“Sanctions?” Gwen’s eyes widened slightly. “Then… illegal trade?”

Lord Vaun sighed. “It was not illegal trade. Just… unregulated. There is nothing explicitly illegal about it.”

“The kingdom might have different thoughts,” Veronica said. “It depends on what you were trading. Vitian has never had good relations with Oprus. Not to the point of war yet, but there is certainly strife.”

Lord Vaun’s expression tightened, but he did not argue. “I would rather not be implicated in anything unnecessary while I am trying to settle matters surrounding my son’s death,” Lord Vaun replied solemnly. “The issue with this demon will have to be handled at another time.”

Veronica studied him for a moment.

That answer was not satisfying. But it was understandable. People rarely became more honest when cornered, especially nobles carrying evidence tied to both a dead son and unregulated foreign trade. He was fearful, and he had every right to be.

Lord Vaun turned and looked back toward the staircase. “Apologies, but I will have to take my leave now. There are many things I must do now that my son’s final memories have been recovered.”

Veronica nodded. “I understand.”

“I will speak to the clerk before I leave and have him grant you access to the floors you require.”

“Thank you,” Veronica said.

Lord Vaun paused. Then he turned back toward her fully. “If you need anything further while you remain in Maftem, you may come to my estate and ask for me directly.”

Veronica raised a brow.

His expression remained serious.

“The reward of granting you access to the upper floors is nothing compared to saving me one hundred thousand vix,” he said. “Your display on the first floor was… impressive. Reckless, perhaps. But impressive. I do not know many third-tier mages who would walk into the Starfault Archives, interrupt a senior mage, insult him, make a wager, and then prove themselves correct in front of half the archive.”

“I didn’t insult him first,” Veronica said.

Nasha smiled faintly with a tilt of her head. “Debatable.”

Veronica ignored her.

For the first time, Lord Vaun’s lips shifted into something close to a real smile. “Regardless, House Vaun remembers its debts,” he said.

“I’ll remember that,” Veronica replied.

He placed one hand over his chest and gave a smaller bow this time. Not as deep as before, but still formal enough for nearby attendants to notice. Then he turned and walked toward the staircase, one hand resting near the badge hidden within his coat.

Veronica watched him leave.

Gwen waited until he was several steps away before whispering, “So… did we just get a noble backer in Maftem?”
