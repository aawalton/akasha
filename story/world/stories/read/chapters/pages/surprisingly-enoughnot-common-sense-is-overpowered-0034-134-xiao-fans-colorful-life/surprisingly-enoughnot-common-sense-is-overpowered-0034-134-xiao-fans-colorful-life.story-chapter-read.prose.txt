Black Boar Forest was nothing new to Ning. At this point, he knew its terrain quite well. It was his primary hunting ground for one main reason:

No Foundation Establishment beasts.

Any monster that reached that level was instantly eliminated by the sect's patrol forces. Overkill? Maybe.

But this was standard practice in the Eastern Wilderness. Compared to the Western Desert, Northern Sea, Central Plains, or even the Southern Miasma Marshlands, the Eastern Wilderness was the region most abundant in monsters.

In truth, humans and monster beasts had contended for hegemony over this land for countless generations. Only because the established sects regularly cleared their surroundings did the area seem peaceful.

A Golden Core sect simply couldn't allow a Foundation Establishment beast to run wild. If one escaped deeper into monster territory and evolved further, it could eventually return to take revenge, or worse, lead a horde.

But all of that was far above Ning's pay grade.

Right now, he cared only about the black boar snorting ten meters in front of him.

This one was huge, easily several hundred kilos, its muscles packed like iron plates beneath its bristled hide. Its tusks curved outward, thick and stained from digging up spirit herbs... or other animals.

Ning crouched behind a tree root and reached for his bow. His Turtle Breathing was running at full strength as he concealed his qi.

Hunting a black boar was never easy.

They had three major strengths:

Thick hide. Mortal weapons barely scratched them.

Ridiculous vitality. Boars were basically tanks with legs.

Explosive aggression. The moment they were injured, they stopped thinking and only charged.

The first rule of hunting black boars was simple:

Never let them charge.

Ning pulled the bowstring back to his cheek, feeling the tension ripple through his arms. His eyes narrowed.

The ear was the boar's weak point. Thin skin, close to the brain.

He exhaled softly and released.

The arrow hissed through the air.

It was slightly off course, striking the ear sideways, but it still drew blood.

Ning didn't waste time regretting the missed kill shot. He swiftly nocked another arrow just as the beast went delirious with pain.

This time, he aimed for the leg joint. Crippling its mobility first was the most reliable strategy.

Thwack!

The arrow struck true. The boar stumbled, its hind leg buckling. But it didn't fall. Instead, it roared and tried to charge anyway, tearing up soil and sending leaves flying.

Then the poison struck.

Poison Lily venom was more paralytic than lethal, quick-acting and especially effective against black boars that relied on momentum to build strength.

Ning didn't emerge immediately. Only after triple-tapping the beast did he finally step out of hiding.

"A black boar equivalent to a Third Stage Qi Refining cultivator. It'll fetch a pretty good price." Ning smiled.

Monster beasts were filled with treasure. Their skin could be made into talisman leather, their bones into spiritual weapons, and their tusks into ornaments. Even their flesh and blood were valuable for spiritual cooking, formations, and alchemy.

Of course, the inverse was also true. Monster beasts benefited greatly from devouring humans. Their evolutionary path relied on awakening bloodlines and consuming spiritual energy. To a beast, a cultivator was just a walking qi-pack.

Humans and monsters had been exploiting each other since ancient times.

Higher-tier black boars existed, but Ning made it a point to fight only weaker ones around the Third Stage.

Was it dirty?

Tell that to beasts with thick hide, insane vitality, and instincts honed to kill. A one-on-one fight between a cultivator and a beast was always risky. Early-stage cultivators, especially, were outclassed by monster physiques.

There were solutions, of course. Cultivators could make use of the hundred professions of the immortal path, or simply form hunting teams.

He'd considered joining one himself, but ultimately chose not to.

In groups, treasures caused conflicts. Spoils caused conflicts. Stupidity caused conflicts. Spoiled teammates, arrogant teammates, too many variables.

So Ning had no choice but to pick weaker beasts instead.

It wasn't his fault, really.

Even then, he took precautions: masking his scent with leaves, obscuring his face, and positioning himself safely within the trees. He shot from different angles, ensuring distance and unpredictability. Beasts weren't dumb; they could follow the direction of arrows.

Even if one located him, Ning could simply flee. Even if the beast was faster, the poison would slow it down.

With every hunt, Ning prepared layers of strategy, methodically stalking and then executing his prey.

If it were Xiao Fan, Ning felt the guy would probably charge head-on, almost die, then suddenly awaken a hidden power and kill it with one blow.

After harvesting the spoils, Ning ventured deeper into the forest in search of another target.

That was when he heard it.

"Trash, do you know who you've offended?"

Just hearing that line was enough for Ning to guess the situation.

Speak of the devil, and he shall appear.

…

Ning concealed himself instantly, relying on his camouflage. Lowering his presence, he observed the scene unfolding before him.

Three people surrounded a single figure, and who else could it be but their resident protagonist, Xiao Fan?

With his enhanced hearing, Ning caught snippets of their conversation.

"You ignorant trash, do you know who you've offended?" the tall leader barked.

Xiao Fan, looking dazed and confused, croaked, "Who... are you guys?"

"It doesn't matter who we are," the leader sneered. "You brought this upon yourself by disrespecting your senior brother. You should've stayed away from Senior Sister Xiao."

Another disciple spat, "Exactly! You're just a toad trying to eat swan meat! Know your place, trash!"

Ning was stunned. They had been sent to assassinate someone, yet they exposed the entire motive out loud.

One need not fear competent enemies, only incompetent allies. Ning suddenly felt much better about going solo.

Xiao Fan seemed to realize the same thing. His eyes widened before blazing with overwhelming fury, and his hands began to tremble, not with fear, but with rage.

"XIAO HONG!!!"

His roar thundered across the forest, sending birds scattering from the nearby trees. Even the assassins staggered under his killing intent.

The leader swallowed hard before forcing out a shout. "W-What are you afraid of?! He's alone! Kill him!"

The other two steadied themselves and charged from opposite directions.

Xiao Fan's lips curled into a cold smile as he lowered his stance. He didn't retreat or rush forward to meet them. Instead, he remained where he was until the first swordsman entered striking distance.

The sword came down toward his shoulder.

Xiao Fan shifted half a step to the side, allowing the blade to pass beside him before his fist shot upward and struck the man's wrist. There was a sharp crack, and the swordsman's fingers immediately went numb, sending his weapon spinning into the grass.

The man barely had time to register what had happened before Xiao Fan stepped into his guard. His palm slammed directly into the man's sternum, and the force lifted him from the ground before sending him crashing several meters away.

He didn't get back up.

Ning's expression became more serious.

As expected of a xianxia protagonist, his battle instincts were terrifyingly sharp.

Xiao Fan hadn't overwhelmed the man with some enormous difference in cultivation. He had simply waited for the attack, avoided it by the smallest necessary distance, and struck twice before his opponent could recover.

The remaining two didn't give him time to breathe. One came from the left while the other attacked from behind, their swords flashing with qi as they tried to catch him between them.

Xiao Fan stepped toward the first attacker instead of retreating. The movement brought him inside the man's reach while simultaneously carrying him away from the sword behind him. He twisted his body, letting the first blade scrape past his clothes, then raised one hand directly into its path.

The sword struck his fist with a metallic clang.

Ning's eyes narrowed.

Those gloves. Definitely a spiritual artifact.

The swordsman clearly hadn't expected his weapon to be stopped barehanded. He hesitated for an instant, and Xiao Fan immediately took advantage of it. His fingers closed around the blade as qi surged from his arm, wrapping around the sword and forcing the man's weapon downward.

The swordsman panicked and poured more qi into the blade.

A faint ringing spread through the clearing.

Snap.

The sword shattered.

Before the man could retreat, Xiao Fan stepped forward and drove his elbow into his throat. The cultivator stumbled backward, both hands flying to his neck, before collapsing into the dirt with a strangled gasp.

The leader had stopped attacking entirely.

He stared at the two bodies, then at Xiao Fan, and whatever courage he'd gathered disappeared from his face. He immediately stumbled backward.

"H-How?! You're just a low—"

Xiao Fan rushed forward before he could finish. The leader barely managed to raise his arms before a fist struck his chest, sending him flying backward into a tree hard enough to shake its branches.

He fell to the ground, coughing blood.

"D-Don't kill me! It was Xiao Hong's fault!"

Xiao Fan walked toward him. "His retribution will come. For now, you will suffice."

He raised his fist.

"I know a treasure!" the man suddenly shouted. "Don't kill me!"

Xiao Fan's fist stopped.

"Treasure?"

The man nodded hurriedly, as if a single second of delay would doom him, which, honestly, wasn't wrong. "If you go north of this forest, there's a mid-stage boar. We found that in its lair, there's a ripening Dragon Qi Grass."

"Dragon Qi Grass?"

Both Xiao Fan and Ning were taken aback.

Dragon Qi Grass was a rare First-Rank magical herb. Not only was it extremely useful for body-refining cultivators, especially those practicing Heaven-Strengthening Body or Hundred-Treasure Body, but it was also an enormous source of spiritual energy.

Overall, it was one of the rare herbs effective for both the body-refining and qi-refining paths.

"Are you sure?" Xiao Fan asked, clearly tempted.

"Yes, yes!" The man nodded repeatedly. "I found it a few years ago, but it wasn't ripe yet, so I waited. Now that it's finally about to ripen... well, since a strong expert like you is here, the opportunity should obviously belong to you."

His face twisted in pain even as he tried to flatter Xiao Fan.

Ning could understand that pain. Imagine finding a treasure, waiting years for it to ripen, only to be forced to hand it over at the very last moment.

"If what you say is true, then I can spare you," Xiao Fan said.

The man exhaled in visible relief.

"Wait here. I'll take care of this mess first, then we'll go."

Xiao Fan turned toward the other bodies, seemingly no longer paying attention to him.

The injured man's expression immediately changed.

His eyes sharpened, and he pushed himself off the ground with a sudden burst of qi. "GO DIE!"

He lunged at Xiao Fan's back, pouring everything he had left into a fist technique. Xiao Fan didn't even look surprised. He shifted aside at the last moment, allowing the fist to pass beside him, then caught the man's arm and drove his own fist into his ribs.

Several cracks sounded at once.

The man folded around the blow, but Xiao Fan didn't stop there. A second punch struck his head before he could recover, and he dropped lifelessly to the ground.

"They always fall for this trick," Xiao Fan muttered, glancing at the corpse.

From his hiding spot, Ning stared at him.

He'd assumed the protagonist would naively spare the man, inevitably allowing him to return and cause trouble later. Instead, Xiao Fan had deliberately pretended to lower his guard and given the man an opportunity to attack. The moment he took it, Xiao Fan had all the justification he needed to finish him off.

He had eliminated future trouble and learned about a treasure.

Xiao Fan was a bit different from Ning's imagination.

Killing three men wasn't surprising. Every xianxia protagonist would probably have thousands of lives on his hands eventually. What surprised Ning was how practiced Xiao Fan seemed at all of this.

What came next only reinforced that impression.

Xiao Fan crouched beside the corpses and calmly stripped them of anything useful. Storage pouches, weapons, talismans, pills—anything remotely valuable disappeared into his possession. Then he used a fire spell to burn the bodies before scattering some kind of powder over the remaining bones, which quickly began to dissolve.

Watching those extremely familiar movements, Ning couldn't help but think:

So Xiao Fan was the Bay Forest Butcher all along.

After finishing, Xiao Fan dusted off his hands and prepared to leave. He had only taken a few steps when he suddenly stopped and slowly scanned the surrounding forest.

"I know you're there. Come out."

Ning's heart nearly stopped.

His hand tightened around the talisman he had prepared, but he forced himself to remain completely still and trust his concealment.

[Turtle Breathing: Great Accomplishment (12/400)]

As his most-used martial art, Ning had reached Great Accomplishment fairly quickly. At this level, he'd learned its ultimate application, Feigning Death. His qi dimmed until it was barely perceptible, his heartbeat slowed, and his breathing became so faint that even the movement of his chest nearly disappeared.

Unless someone searched his exact location carefully with spiritual sense, Ning was confident he could remain hidden as long as he didn't move.

It was one of the reasons he felt comfortable hunting alone in Black Boar Forest. The First-Tier Swift Wind Talisman in his hand, which had cost him forty spirit stones, served as his final insurance in case concealment failed.

Knowing Xiao Fan's personality, Ning was fairly certain the shout was only a bluff. If Xiao Fan had actually discovered his position and considered him an enemy, standing in the middle of the clearing and politely asking him to come out made very little sense.

After what Ning had just witnessed, this was probably another bait.

Xiao Fan had already proved himself to be a master baiter.

Sure enough, after receiving no response for a while, Xiao Fan frowned, muttered something about imagining things, and eventually walked away.

Ning remained exactly where he was.

One minute passed.

Then two.

Only after five full minutes had passed, with Xiao Fan completely gone and Ning's senses detecting no movement nearby, did he finally crawl out of hiding.

"Dude's terrifying."

Ning released the breath he'd been suppressing. Watching Xiao Fan kill three people had been eye-opening, but somehow, watching him repeatedly bait people into revealing themselves had made Ning even more nervous.

Fortunately for Ning, Xiao Fan still fell victim to the classic cliché of sensing that something was wrong, only to dismiss it a moment later.

Still somewhat shaken, Ning resumed his task. He needed another black boar, and unfortunately, watching protagonists murder people didn't count toward his quota.

For the next few minutes, he searched for traces of another one. It wasn't particularly difficult. Subtlety was not exactly part of a black boar's nature, and broken undergrowth, disturbed soil, and hoofprints made their movements relatively easy to follow.

Ning had just found a fresh trail when he felt a faint tremor beneath his feet.

Something was moving through the forest extremely quickly.

His intuition tingled.

Xiao Fan.

"So fast?" Ning muttered.

It hadn't even been fifteen minutes since Xiao Fan left, yet he was already returning from the direction of the treasure.

Ning climbed the nearest tree and soon spotted him between the branches.

Xiao Fan was running at full speed, clutching something glowing in one hand. A talisman was stuck to his leg, red qi streamed from his body, and judging by the expression on his face, he had absolutely no intention of slowing down.

The reason became apparent a moment later.

A massive black boar crashed through the forest behind him.

The beast easily weighed a thousand pounds, and it wasn't bothering to go around anything in its path. Bushes disappeared beneath its hooves while smaller trees cracked apart as it barreled after Xiao Fan.

Ning's amusement disappeared when he sensed the qi radiating from it.

That lackey had lied.

The beast wasn't mid-stage.

It was late-stage... and furious.

Ning watched Xiao Fan disappear deeper into the forest with the enormous boar still chasing him. Only then did his gaze slowly shift toward the direction they had come from.

The boar's lair.

Black boars were generally solitary. A beast this strong would have claimed a territory of its own, and Ning had seen no indication that another one lived nearby.

More importantly, Xiao Fan had been clutching something glowing while running for his life.

That was almost certainly the Dragon Qi Grass.

So the actual treasure was already gone.

Ning rubbed his chin thoughtfully. Still, a late-stage monster beast had occupied that lair for who knew how many years. Even if Xiao Fan had taken the Dragon Qi Grass, there could be shed tusks, spiritual materials, leftover herbs, or something else the protagonist hadn't bothered to notice while being chased by an angry thousand-pound boar.

Of course, Ning wasn't going to stroll straight into the cave just because its owner had temporarily left.

Checking the surroundings from a safe distance first, however, was another matter.

Ning looked toward the direction Xiao Fan had disappeared, then back toward the abandoned lair.

"...Does this count as a lucky opportunity for me?"
