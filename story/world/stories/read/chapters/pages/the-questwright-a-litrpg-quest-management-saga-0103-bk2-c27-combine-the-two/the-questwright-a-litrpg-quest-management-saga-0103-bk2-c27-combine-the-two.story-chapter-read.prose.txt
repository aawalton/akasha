The ride to their apartment was fast. For the first time since being in Liberty, Cass wasn’t relegated to the back of a cart. That meant most of what he was seeing was new, and he couldn’t help but notice how tall, thick, and foreboding each gate was.

It was a wonder of wonders that several of them had been designed to fold and move. The gate stationed between the first and the second ring was five times the size of those that followed. But that didn’t mean Liberty had skimped on the others. Each was incredibly thick, the dull metal seeming to challenge anyone to try entering uninvited.

He also noticed how quickly they moved through them. Holding onto Adya’s firm frame, they passed through the rings at a slow trot as the draft-horse preened to any eye looking their way.

As they entered the second ring, Cass commented on their speed.

“I did a little scouting last night,” Adya said quietly over the loud clopping of Bella’s hooves. “Leaving the inner rings is much faster than getting in. They only check passes as you move from the 5th to the 4th and so on up to the first.”

“You still haven’t told me how you got in. I know for a fact you don’t have a pass.”

“My little secret, dear Cassio.” She patted one of the hands holding his stomach, then leaned a little forward and urged Bella on.

Again, Alan had proven true. The apartment they’d been set up in was less than a five-minute walk from Caligula’s End. With two bedrooms and working water, it was much larger than many other living arrangements around the area. Or so he’d told Orla.

Seated on the semblance of a couch, Cass and Adya listened as she strutted back and forth in front of them, “This isn’t good enough. It’s fine to start, but I’m not meant to be living like this. There’s no grandeur.”

“No, what?” Gary asked from the kitchen as he cooked up dinner.

“Grandeur. You know, magnificence, splendor, other terms that imply this is not the proper living situation for a Merchant. Not that I mind sleeping next to you, Adya.”

The Scout raised an eyebrow, but it was Pellin who protested her words, “You should try sleeping next to Gary! He snores SO…LOUD.”

“It’s true,” Adya said quietly to Cass beside her, “Many times in our travels, the sound of his snores drew monsters to our position.”

“I’m built big,” The hefty Baker said unapologetically while sprinkling something green on their food. He quickly served them all as they sat in a rough circle. Everyone seemed happy with the food, though Orla wasn’t a fan.

“There’s not enough meat.”

Gary shrugged, “It’s your budget. If you wanted meat, you should’ve given me more money.”

She glared at Cass, “You see what I do for you? Do you see the squalor I’ve placed myself in for your benefit?”

“I think it’s nice,” Cass replied, wiping his mouth with a small napkin he’d squirreled away in his bag. Then he grimaced, “I forgot to tell Ana that I wasn’t going to be in the Tower tonight. Hope she isn’t mad.”

“Who is Ana again?” Gary said with a finger raised. “Oh, that’s right. Your maid.”

“Servant,” Cass corrected. “And my friend, really. She was the first person to really be nice to me here.”

“Is she pretty?” Adya said innocently beside him.

“Mmm,” Cass missed the warning signs as he thought about it. “For an older woman, I guess she is, yeah.”

“Of course,” The Scout nodded, “Who else would be serving QuestWrights?”

“I don’t know if that’s exactly a prerequisite to being a Servant at the Tower,” Cass replied, still not seeing the signs, “but I wouldn’t be shocked if it were true.” Seeing the beginning of a scowl on her face, he quickly changed the subject, “Liberty sucks.”

Gary nodded in support after noticing, “Super sucks.”

“The suckiest,” Pellin added, a nervous look sent Adya’s way.

Oblivious to the social commentary, Orla spoke with a smile, fully prepared for what was to come next, “I kind of like it.”

“Boo!”

“Get outta here!”

“You’re the devil!”

Orla lapped it up, “You’re all just whiners and babies. This city is filled to the rafters with Crests. I’m going to make my bones here, just you wait and see. From what I’ve learned already, anything is for sale here. Anything.”

“Including souls,” Cass added. “I’ve been here the longest.”

“By a week,” Adya said with a snort.

He waved her off, “Still, I’m in the Tower. I’m looking at Quests, Re-Quests, and deliveries all over the city. Everything I’ve seen about this place is imbalanced. How they treat their citizens, how Callings are handled. It’s not just money. I mean, I only got a few dockings this week compared to what I expected.” When they looked confused, he explained the program, then continued, “But it’s weird being here. Like, I can feel the other shoe waiting to drop.”

The rest of the room was quiet as he shook his head with finality.

“Your family line, your job, and where you live…matter. We all watched the Lottery yesterday. Didn’t you notice that the crowd didn’t cheer once until the last ten or so were called? I’m betting those were all people from the 5th ring. Everyone else just had money or status.”

“Is it really that bad?”

Cass smiled, “It’s way different than Liora, that’s for sure. You’re an optimist, Gary. You prefer to see the good in the world, no matter the circumstances. I’m afraid I can’t look at that illusion anymore. Things are bad here. The ship is off-keel.”

Adya, who knew him best aside from Gary, touched his knee as she spoke, “So what are we going to do about it?”

“I’ve been thinking about that a lot, and there are a few things I think we can try. But first, there’s something you four deserve.” Setting aside his empty plate, Cass made a flourish and flicked his wrist. The luminescent Quill entered the world, dropping into his hand just so.

“Holy shit!” Orla yelled out, “How much is that thing worth!”

Pellin looked away, “That’s way, way too bright.”

He’d planned to give them each an Exponential Daily, but from their reactions, he knew it would have to wait. Bit by bit, Cass explained about the corrupted vellum, how the lure worked, and the tutorial that had sprung up the moment all of his notifications had come in. They commiserated with him, but Pellin pointed something out.

“You said the Quill works through emotion?”

“Yeah, that’s right. I’ve been stuck on a single step for a while now.”

“Well, I may be wrong here, but you could be going about this the wrong way. Maybe you need to think less like a QuestWright, and more like a System Engineer.” Pellin tapped his chin, “The trick could be that drafting through emotions is wrong.”

“But even the System says it’s about emotions,” Cass pointed out.

“System Engineers do things through processes and procedures. Every time I build something, I have to create a set of items like a checklist. Maybe that’s what you should try.”

Cass leaned forward, “So how do I go about doing that?”

“I mean, don’t think of drafting as emotions but as processes to follow. I’m guessing a lot of your Quests have come out funny when you’re drafting them?”

Adya blushed beside him as Cass confirmed, “Yeah, since the very beginning.”

“Well, pull up the tutorial in front of us and let’s see what it looks like.”

Cass shrugged and did as he was asked, not like it’ll hurt me for my friends to see me fail.

The words appeared in the air, golden script floating just over everyone’s heads.

Step 3: Combine the two

Pellin moved around the writing, “That’s not a lot of information.”

“Combine the two what?” Gary said while swiping his hand through the lettering. He pulled it back to look at his skin closely.

Cass leaned back against the weak frame of the couch, “The first required me to change the coloring of-” he gestured at the words,” that. The second made me delay its activation. I’ve been stuck on this for a while. What I thought was two thoughts is really three.” He displayed his Outliner on the ceiling.

“The first thought is the structure of the Quest itself. The second is the color, and the third is delayed activation. So while I’m building the Quest, I also have to be pushing for the color and time delay. It’s hard to hold that many thoughts in my head at the same time.”

“No, it’s simple,” Pellin said with a smile. “I was right, you’re thinking about this wrong. Do you have any idea how many processes I’m running at once when working with the System?”

“Ten!” Orla yelled out as if it were a game.

“Fourteen!” Gary tried, slapping through the air a second time.

“Sometimes twenty, sometimes fifty,” Pellin replied, shutting them both down with a grin. “Cass, you’re probably thinking one thing, feeling another, and writing a third. Of course it’s not going to work and your Quests are all going to come out funny. It’s about efficiency.”

There’s that word again.

“Maybe… so walk me through what you think I need to do.”

“Thinking like a System Engineer, you need to make a checklist. When you draft a Quest, what’s going through your mind?”

“The specifics of the Quest, of course. Who the Questor is, what they need to do, yada yada.”

“No, no yada yada,” Pellin grabbed Gary’s arm with a wince to stop him from slicing through a third time. The Baker smiled sheepishly, then began clearing the dishes. “The yada yadas are your problem. You need to think of Quests unemotionally. They’re getting in the way. Instead, focus on building a checklist of things you need to consider for the Quest, then push that with your mind’s intent. Changing the color or delaying the Quest’s activation are just a few items on the checklist.”

Cass shook his head, “Here’s where I think you’re wrong.” He smiled to take the sting out of it. “Quests are always emotional. I’ve tried and tried to keep my feelings out of things, and that only made my Quests worse. If the problem is efficiency, then the solution lies in how my feelings are shaped. Not the other way around.”

Pellin shrugged, ”I’m just telling you how System Engineers do things. I could be wrong, and it’s your Calling so you certainly know better than me, but checklists are always helpful when trying to understand complicated problems and procedures.”

Cass looked up at the Outline on the ceiling in thought.

My emotions make up who I am… It’s the part of my soul that can’t be replicated. They’re unique to each and every one of us. I don’t think I can truly separate who I am from what I’m asking Questors to do. That would constitute some kind of ethical violation. But that doesn’t mean I have to ignore all of his advice.

“Maybe that’s how things work for System Engineering, but Quest Drafting is closer to… having a conversation. Every time I make a Quest, I’m expressing my will to the world. Don’t think I’m ignoring what you’re saying, or don’t appreciate your advice. I just need to find a way to integrate pieces of your checklist drafting into what I’m doing.”

Pellin smiled, “You wouldn’t be Cass if you didn’t find a weird way to go about things. Take your time.”

“But not too long,” Adya said as she patted his shoulder before standing. “I have plans for tonight.”

“Can’t wait.” He replied immediately, then, recognizing he had some work to do, they each left him alone.

Orla and Pellin departed first, speaking about finding a small shop with a large warehouse for their plans. Meanwhile, Adya left right after, saying she had a training meeting with Bella and Alan. All that was left in the apartment was low-grade furniture, Gary writing down something in a small book, and Cass, eyes closed as he tried to figure out how to change his drafting style.

He smiled at the memory of the first time he’d ever created a Quest. He hadn’t been sure it would truly work. It was one thing to be told you had a Calling, and another to see the results of your abilities. Kara had laughed at how twisted the draft had become, influenced by his feelings at its creation.

“The woman is good.” He whispered to himself.

But I’m not that QuestWright anymore. I’ve drafted hundreds of Quests across over a dozen different types. I need to evolve how this process works, and that’s what the tutorial is forcing me to do. If I don’t figure this out, I’ll be stuck on the third step and never find out what the next ones are. That’ll screw up all of my big plans for being in Liberty.

There are no mentors here. It’s just the System and me.

So he sat there and processed, just not in the way Pellin suggested.

Every Quest he could remember that came out funny, or weird, or sometimes even mean went through his mind. He considered Pellin’s advice, the bits of conversation he’d had with Moore and Hollis, then the Guildmaster’s old vellums on the board.

There was something he was missing within his Quests, with even Liberty pointing out that he had an efficiency problem.

My method has always been the same. Check the objectives, set the Outline, then try to hold back what I’m feeling so everything comes out clean.

Maybe I’m thinking about things wrong…If Quests are directions, or Orders as Hollis’s letter once said, then there’s a step I’m not climbing. I bet Pellin’s idea of a checklist isn’t too far off from what I need to do. It just doesn’t feel perfectly right. Like a half-finished explanation.

A thought swished through his brain that was simpler than expected. Wanting to test out a theory, Cass removed the extra Vellum he’d received from completing his daily Guild allotment. The thick, tough paper sat in his hand as he stared at it, considering the new process required to complete the trial.

Not wanting to be distracted, he closed the tutorial and let the Quill fade back to wherever it went. Then, looking at the outline on the ceiling, Cass moved it over to the Exponential Daily. His friends deserved that much, at least, and it was a good test of what he was trying to do.

Pellin said I need to push at things with my mind's intent, and that’s got me thinking. Intent is like being resolved to something, which is just another emotion. If I consider my feelings as fuel, and intent as direction, then... I need to stop looking at this as two, three, or four thoughts at once. Rather than separate everything, I should consider it all as one whole idea. One complete thought. Or-”

A light bulb went off, “A conversation.”

Up until now, he’d been throwing logs into a fire and hoping the smoke drifted the right way. Every time he’d drafted a Quest, it had been done in small bursts of feeling. Do this, don’t do this…

Try not to die.

But if he considered the design of the Quest as a conversation between him and the System, he could give it much more thorough or specific directions. The only limit was in what he said. After all, the smoke of emotions would still be there, but with this idea, he should be able to use his words as an intent chimney to point the direction.

Already knowing what the Exponential Daily looked like, it was a good choice for how he wanted to frame the Quest. Short, simple, and to the point. Closing his eyes, Cass dove into the feelings it gathered.

Still holding the Vellum in one hand, he hit the draft button without looking at the world. What he was attempting to do was different than anything he’d tried in the past. As he felt the familiar sense of the System listening in on his thoughts and feelings, Cass took his shot.

The Rare Exponential Quest is a daily event that draws from a series of randomly selected escalating options. Each morning, as you open your eyes, a new event will appear, offering you the chance for greater experience the more you complete them. But they’ll also get harder. If you accept this, then be prepared for some oddities. This Quest is for Baker Gary, my best friend in the world…after Bella.

Opening his eyes, Cass was holding a golden, glowing Quest. He read it over and immediately found several problems, though it was a little clearer than others he’d produced in the past. To a small degree, that proved his theory a success.

[TIER 3 (RARE) EXPONENTIAL QUEST]

You will receive a new Daily Quest each morning upon waking.

Each completed Quest will increase the difficulty of the next.

The experience reward scales with the risk and effort required on the part of the Questor.

These Quests may at times seem unusual. Accept them anyway.

This is for second best-friend, Baker Gary.

Cassio Vale

Liberty Guildtower

QuestWright

“That’s close to exactly what I said. Okay, so we just need to have a chat from now on. It looks like the order of the conversation I have with the System matters. Noted.” Sitting up, he looked at the ceiling and began to organize the conversation in his mind, “Getting better at this should be easier than having to edit my Quests over and over again.”

He smiled to himself, “It’s about efficiency.”
