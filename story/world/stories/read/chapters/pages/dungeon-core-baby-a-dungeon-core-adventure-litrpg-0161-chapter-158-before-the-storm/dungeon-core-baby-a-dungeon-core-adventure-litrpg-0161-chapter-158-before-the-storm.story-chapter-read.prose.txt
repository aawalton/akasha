Vetica

She leaned against a column of the general goods store with a good view of the tower. Now that they’d found Mary, they couldn’t risk her being moved while they weren’t paying attention. After the performance ended and everyone had drifted off, she’d disappeared into the shadows to watch the tower. Now, it was the afternoon of the following day. Tonight, the hex storm would arrive, and all their work and planning would be put to the test. She just had to make sure the woman they’d come to save was where they expected her to be.

She divided her attention between the tower and the people of the bustling fort. The townspeople were a flurry of activity today. Aveo’s workers were putting the finishing touches on the last minute shelter, which she doubted would actually seem last minute knowing the high quality of Aveo’s work. The knights were represented heavily atop the walls, and many of them scanned not just the forest, but the skies above as well. The latter even more than the former. Townsfolk were busy securing their belongings and taking advantage of the protective features Aveo had implemented on all the homes. Thick storm shutters that encased windowpanes in hardy specialized wood. Powerful braces to reinforce doors, and broad wooden chests for important personal belongings and family heirlooms. A few men whacked away at wooden training dummies, and to her surprise they appeared to be villagers, not knights.

She’d been in the middle of wondering about that when the thick stone gate of the fort grated open. A single knight arrived, and his armor looked as if he had fallen into mud. He limped heavily and looked unwell. That worried her. The scalers wouldn’t actually fight the knights. That wasn’t the plan. If Richard had done this, then he’d been discovered, or forced to act for some reason.

The gate guards moved in a flurry to check on the limping knight, but he brushed past them. Two other knights met him, and they hurriedly exchanged words. Vetica squinted. Something was off. She had a weird feeling about this.

She gently rubbed the lump hidden within her pants pocket. “Mousifer, are you awake?” she asked.

A moment later, he popped his face out of her pocket and looked at her with a groggy expression. He rubbed his face with his paws. “Squea,” he said softly.

The two knights the man had hurried toward nodded, and hurried off. They weren’t heading to the tower, where she might have expected them to go. They were heading to the section of the fort where most of the civilians live. She bit her lip. She couldn’t leave. But Mousifer could. It would be close, but he could make it. She trusted him with that. “Go and warn Charlie and the others. Something’s wrong,” she whispered so as not to draw attention.

Mousifer perked up in an instant. He saluted her, and then scampered down her pocket and ran around the rear side of the shop. He’d be able to travel fairly inconspicuously as long as he stuck to the back of the buildings. She hoped it was nothing, but her instinct was honed enough to know things were about to go down.

Charlie

“How do you spot a weakness?” Charlie asked in the room Arkady used to train. He wanted a last minute training session before the rescue tonight.

Arkady thought about the question for a moment. “Well, sometimes it’s an old wound, sometimes it’s a part of the body the rest of the body relies on—the eyes, the heart, the throat, etc. Sometimes, it’s about using an opponent’s strength against them. It depends on the opponent and the situation. I have a hex that shows me, but other people, like Richard for instance, develop an instinct over a lifetime of fighting; their experiences help them spot little openings, advantages, weak spots that they can exploit. You don’t have a hex like mine, but you can get experience, and the more you fight, and train, and hunt, the better you’ll get at spotting the right time and place to strike.”

Charlie grinned really big. “Okay, I’ll keep practicing and try to look for that kind of stuff!”

Orb deflated. “Charlie is super strong. Does he really need to worry about a weakness?”

Arkady chuckled. “That’s true, he is. But he might run into someone even stronger one day, or meet an opponent that his powers aren’t a good match for. Almost all great fighters learn to strike their opponents where it will be the most effective. Charlie’s going to take on the world someday, but in the meantime, keep training, you two.”

“Wait what? Me too?” Orb sighed.

Charlie did his best telepathic drill Sergeant voice. “Get to work!”

Arkady

He couldn’t believe how well it was going. He’d felt good about Charlie’s training earlier. And they’d found Mary. Mary! They were so close to saving Richard’s wife. They’d all traveled together for a while after escaping the town he’d spent most of his adult life in. When he left his guild behind. Richard was stern, quiet at times, but he had a good heart. Mary was bubbly, happiness personified, and the warmest, kindest soul he’d ever met. He wouldn’t let Richard down; he’d help her escape even if it killed him.

They’d found Mary, and he’d found something with Orielle. He couldn’t quite put a finger on it; he didn’t know what to call it. But it made him happy, and he sensed it without using his powers. He had a new weakness. One he couldn’t train away or work to mitigate. His weakness was in the form of a person. One he was starting to care about very much.

“You look serious. What’s on your mind?” Orielle asked, looking back at him.

Arkady looked up in surprise. They’d said their goodbyes after the festival the night before, but she’d come by to see him first thing this morning at the house where he, Charlie, and everyone else were staying. He’d been training with Charlie early when she arrived. Obie was busy today with his hex knight duties, so Tomlin had forced Orielle to bring him so he could hang out with Charlie instead. Even now, Arkady could hear them playing in the other room under Merlin’s supervision.

Now, the two of them were in the kitchen. Orielle had bought ingredients to cook, and was currently making lunch for everybody. She worked effortlessly at the stove, on a pot full of something that smelled delicious, and possibly a little spicy. Arkady liked spice.

“Sorry, I was just thinking about how nice last night was,” he said.

Orielle nodded. “It was. You know…after the storm passes, we’ll be throwing a party so big that last night will seem tame in comparison. We could go…together, I mean,” she said. This time she didn’t look back, but Arkady caught the hint of a blush on her cheek.

His gaze cast down, and a quiet sadness filled him. When the storm passed, he would be gone with the rest of his friends. He wouldn’t be able to stay here. Even if he wanted to, his connection to Charlie and the others was well known at this point. When the storm passed and Mary was gone, and the only people in the town missing would be Charlie and his ‘parents’, people would quickly realize who was responsible for freeing her. Staying would only risk putting Orielle in harm’s way. But he couldn’t tell her that, and he couldn’t tell her no because…that might ruin what remaining time they had. So instead, he composed himself, stood up, and went over to her. “Orielle, I’d like nothing more than that.” It was true. That was exactly what he wanted. But he knew it was a lie of omission, and it was like his very soul screamed from within, urging him to tell her the truth.

She smiled and swirled a ladle in the pot once more before turning to face him. She reached into her pocket and pulled something out, but covered it with her other hand to keep it hidden.

“What’s that?” Arkady asked.

She unveiled the object in her hand. The wooden toy was every bit his spitting image. She’d made a toy figurine of…him.

“Is that…me?” he asked incredulously. The small toy version of Arkady stood as if it were facing a monster. She pulled on the arm, and he saw that the figure’s limbs could be adjusted. The hand had a small, barely noticeable hole in it, and she acted before he had time to react.

She giggled and pressed a switch hidden under toy Arkady’s cloak, and a spurt of water splattered Arkady’s face. “The perfect toy for someone with a grumpy old man in their life! Don’t worry, I already got Papa with this one too. That reminds me, he said Big Arkady is going to have to pay for the sins of Little Arkady. Whatever that means.”

Arkady swallowed. “Could you not sick your axe-wielding father on me? I’m afraid he’ll make a house out of my bones.” He smiled. “But seriously, Orielle, I love it. When did you have the time to make this?” he asked.

“Last night before bed.”

Arkady shook his head. “There’s no way you had any time to sleep then, I…” He stopped; this wasn’t the time to lecture her. “Thank you; it means a lot to me,” he said, taking the figurine in his hands.

The pot next to her threatened to boil over, and she quickly tended to it. “Of course, I’m glad you like it.” She grabbed a spoon nearby and used it to get some of the broth from her pot. She blew on it and then took a sip. “Mmm! This is good! It needs a little…” She looked around, a confused expression on her face. “I know I just had it. Did I set it in the drawer by accident?” She reached down and opened a drawer in search of whatever spice she’d misplaced.

Arkady realized what she was reaching for and tried to stop her. His hand clasped over hers, shutting the drawer back, but not before the rolled-up blueprint he’d taken from her house was visible.

She didn’t react to his trying to shut the drawer. She stared at it as if trying to piece together what was going on and then looked back up at him. “Why do you…why do you have one of my father’s blueprints?” she asked.

“Orielle, it’s...” He paused. He didn’t want to lie to her, but they were so close to saving Mary, and if she…no, she wouldn’t turn him over to the knights. Would she? As far as she knew, he’d only stolen a blueprint. He took a breath. “I just wanted to get a closer look. I thought they were interesting.”

She reached for the drawer again, and this time, he didn’t stop her. He couldn’t bring himself to. She unraveled it on the counter, ignoring the pot getting ready to spill over once again. “This is…a blueprint of the fort. You were asking a lot of questions at dinner the other night. I thought maybe you were just trying to impress my father, but now that I think about it…you showed up at my house looking for him as well. What’s going on? What are you up to?”

Arkady took a step towards her, but she stepped back. “Orielle, I…it’s not like that. I can explain. At least I can explain some things. But there’s a lot at stake here, and—”

“Were you just using me to get to him? To his designs? What else did you steal?” she shook her head, and then took another step back. “Of course. Of course, I think I’ve gone and met someone kinda cool, and he turns out to be…whatever this is.” She gestured to him. “Stay away from me.”

Arkady had scarcely had time to process before the front door slammed shut.

The couch groaned in the other room as Merlin shot up from an apparent nap. “I’m awake! What’s going on?” he called out. Charlie and Tomlin giggled.

Arkady looked at the pot, and carefully moved it off the stove to avoid it making any further mess, and then he pulled out a chair and slowly sank into it. Just like that? Can you really have something so sweet one moment and lose it that quickly? Just like that?

His throat felt dry, his heart ached, and then he looked up at the blueprint one last time. No, not like this. He had to try. He stood and rushed after her. Orielle was there, in the distance, hurrying back towards her home. She wiped her face with her sleeve, but several people passed her and asked what was wrong. She was crying. Orielle was crying, and it was his fault. He ran after her, and his mind was so focused on salvaging this however he could, that he never stopped to wonder where the two hex knights walking past him were going.
