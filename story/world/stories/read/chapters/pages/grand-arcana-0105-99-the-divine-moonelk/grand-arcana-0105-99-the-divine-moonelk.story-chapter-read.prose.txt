I didn’t set foot in the barracks in which Lord Steen prepared for me, spending the time in one of the taverns instead. I was fully aware that he was still keeping an eye on me, but I preferred the atmosphere here.

You know I can kill him, right? Serp asked, playing the usual tune.

“I’m sure you can kill the entire city as well,” I whispered, taking a drink from my glass.

The wine had a nice fruity taste, but was extremely weak, making it seem as if I were drinking juice.

Not at once… the blade admitted.

Even his ability to grow had limitations. In order to change that, I needed to improve him, and for that I needed to find the elk.

I remained in the tavern till late at night, listening in on conversations that took place in the room as well as the surrounding streets. For the most part, nothing interesting was said. People were still talking about me—the last person to enter the village unharmed. By now I was certain that the other participants had to have dropped in, but as usual, there was no sign of them.

Some of them had probably chosen to keep away from any settlements. It wasn’t the worst move, though still a rookie one. The first thing one needed when hunting, after weapons, was information, and there wouldn’t be a lot of it in the wilderness, deities or not. Also, there were the additional tasks to think of. Booster spheres were pretty much as valuable as cards at this point. The only exception was if someone was trying to complete a set.

I waved to a barmaid and pointed to my cup. She nodded, then hurried to fetch a new jug of wine. Clearly, the lord’s people had spoken to her, for so far neither she, nor the tavern keeper had asked for payment. Naturally, that didn’t keep me from sliding a few silver coins each time.

When even the regulars started leaving, it was clear that none of my group would be showing up. That was a pity. I had hoped that Sparta would have come here.

Finishing the last of my drink, I left the cup on the counter, then went out into the streets. Eyes were constantly following my every move. Some even had the illusion that they were moderately good at it. Ignoring them, I went all the way to the front gate.

“Sir.” One of the guards addressed me as I approached. Apparently, he had also been told about my situation. “There’s a moon outside…”

“I know.” I didn’t need to provide any explanations. The deal that had been forced upon me had its benefits. “Open it.”

The guard nodded and turned around. One wave and two men were already up to the ledge. If my Acrobat was developed to the max, I could have leaped over the wall by myself.

“Will you be… hunting it?” the guard whispered as he moved closer.

“No, I’m going after something else today,” I said. “I need to be sure I’m ready for it.”

The explanation made enough sense for him to accept it as true. For the most part, it wasn’t. Unless forced otherwise, I planned on getting as many rewards during this trial as possible. Ideally, I could also try and find some caches, although that was going to wait for the very end. Unlike normal trials, people tended to get upset when I claimed loop catches in a trial-world. From their perspective, that was theft.

With my opening ready, I climbed up and leaped down outside. A guard shouted that I would be checked again before entering. I just waved without turning around.

“You can have the next core, Serp,” I said.

Hmm… why? he asked, suspicious of my change of heart.

“I only need the antlers. The real rewards will pour in after I get more information. I expect no complaints then.”

The message was clear. The small indulgence he was getting now required that he behave later on.

Thinking back, I tried to remember everything associated with the moonelk. Going by the name, most expected it to be a magnificent, almost ethereal being. In truth, it was as aethereal as a tractor made of moonlight. Massive in size, it had antlers large enough to uproot an entire house. Normal weapons were of little use, as were most forms of magic. I definitely wouldn’t be able to do much with the four spells I knew. Thankfully, all the weapons I had were special.

Sounds filled the forest, marking the respective territories. I could hear wolves howling in the distance, birds cawing, as well as a whole host of other sounds. None of the creatures dared approach me or the road.

I hastened my pace, quickly leaving the forest. From there, I went in the direction of the Crystal Fields.

Err, are you sure you know what you’re doing, old man? Serp asked.

“Don’t worry,” I tried to sound casual about it. “If anything happens, I’ll summon you out of there.”

Huh?

I had forgotten that the blade didn’t share my past memories.

“The crystals don’t drain a lot,” I said. “We’ll be fine if we stay at the edge of the field.”

Why do we need to do that?

“That’s where the elk will be.”

It was difficult to believe. I, myself, would have had plenty of doubts if I hadn’t seen all this before. The Crystal Fields were the monster’s grazing grounds. Three days per month, it would appear there and start consuming the smaller crystals in order to boost its magical energy. Lethal to ordinary creatures, the crystals provided the moonelk with additional aether, which he could digest, growing in size. From what I had been told, the older the elk, the larger it got. Given that this was the very start of the quest, I expected the creature to be twice the size of a small mansion.

Reaching the hill overlooking the valley of crystals, I stopped and looked around. There was no indication that any other participants were in the area, though I knew that they had to be out there. There were no fresh bodies visible among the crystals, so they hadn’t been foolish enough to try and walk through. That only left the forest.

I don’t see anything, Serp noted.

“It’ll show up,” I said as I continued towards the edge of the field. “Give it time.”

Why do we have to wait there, then? Can’t we stay here until it appears?

“It doesn’t work that way.”

There were a few rules when it came to moonelk. Despite their impressive size and strength, they didn’t like the presence of humans. The only way to catch it in the open was to sneak up on it or lure it into coming. The first was difficult with the skills I had, which left the second option.

Upon reaching the edge of the field, I reached out, placing my left hand on the first large crystal shard. Pins and needles instantly ran over my palm as the crystal drained the mana from my hand.

So, this is what it feels like, I thought.

I tried to cast a spell. A circle formed only to be shattered moments later. Clearly, I wasn’t the right person to be casting spells in a place like this. Then again, I didn’t have to be. All that was needed was for one of the shards to soak up enough aether.

A faint glow emanated from the crystal, making it stand out from the rest in the field.

“Get ready,” I said.

For several seconds, the crystal kept draining my magic energy. When suddenly a ray of moonlight struck the ground a few dozen feet away.

Light grew, changing shape. Crystals shattered as a massive beast gained form.

I pulled my hand back. This wasn’t what I had planned.

Once formed, the elk stood thirty feet tall. The width of its antlers was just as large. There was no way I’d be able to transport those back to the city. If nothing else, they weren’t going to fit on the path through the forest.

The beast sniffed the air, trying to find the new source of aether. Spotting me, it snorted. I couldn’t tell whether it considered me insignificant, or was laughing at the amount of magic I had baited it with.

Without hesitation, I summoned Serp and sent him flying right at the elk’s nose.

The blade transformed into a snake, quickly growing to bite off part of its prey. Before it could reach, however, the elk lowered its head, meeting the weapon with its antlers. One flick of the head and Serp was sent flying upwards, without causing a single scratch on the beast.

“Freeze!” I shouted, using my Emperor card.

The elk froze in its tracks. This was my chance. Drawing the divine blades from my inventory, I let out an aether strike in its direction. The thread of magic vanished into the glowing form of the beast, causing no harm whatsoever.

Apparently, the creature had the ability to drain magic as well. That was a detail I didn’t remember from my previous run. Then again, we had fought it in a very different fashion. We had been close to twenty at the time, each of us stabbing it with blades from a close distance. Most of the weapons didn’t cause any damage, though they were instrumental in keeping it pinned down, so the participants with adequate weapons could kill it. There was only one substantial difference: that creature was half as large.

How will you like this? I returned the blades to my inventory, then unsummoned and summoned Serp.

Aiming at the beast’s left eye, I thrust the weapon through the air.

Faster than a bullet, Serp went straight towards his target… then flew straight through.

“What?!” I stared.

That was unexpected.

There’s nothing for me to cling to! Serp shouted, just as confused.

Fucking hell! I knew that ordinary weapons wouldn’t have an effect, but I had assumed that living ones would.

Harlequin’s Sight! I focused on the creature.

FULL MOON MOONELK

ALIGNMENT: RESTRICTED

LEVEL: 30

Strength: MEDIUM

Reflexes: LOW

Memory: LOW

Resistance: MEDIUM

Perception: LOW

Magic: MEDIUM

Divinity: Selene

CARDS: RESTRICTED

SPECIALS: Lightwalker, Aethereal, Arcana Protection

Arcana protection? I had seen that special once before. It was beyond rare. Entities with it were protected from the direct effects of any card within the Grand Arcana. Despite all his newly found strength, Serp remained a card, which rendered him unable to kill or even wound the creature. The same could be said for any other card at my disposal.

“Shit!” I dashed to the side.

If the moonelk was protected, it meant that my Emperor’s card had no effect whatsoever.

Aware that the share had ended, the elk sprang into action again, blowing through the field. Crystals shattered behind me as the monster quickly reduced the distance. There was no telling whether it wanted to eat me or just trample me, but I preferred neither.

Earth circle!

A pillar of rock lifted me up from the ground. Less than a second in, it stopped growing.

Not losing a moment’s concentration, I cast the spell twice more in immediate succession, then activated my Acrobat card.

Combined, the height of the flimsy column was barely over twenty feet. Thanks to the strength acquired at the arena and my Acrobat card, I managed to leap just as high into the air before the elk ran beneath me.

Watching it was like looking at a small mill pass by. It was large and deadly, even now emanating a gentle glow as if it were made of living moonlight. In many aspects, maybe it was.

Quite an interesting creature you wanted me to face, deity, I thought.

Technically, I could focus on chopping off its antlers and call it a day. That wasn’t going to be enough. Even with Harlequin and all the deities watching me, I had to prove that I had the strength to deal with such a threat. If I couldn’t defeat this, what chance did I stand against a real deity aiming to kill me?

Any way one looked at it, I hadn’t actually defeated any strong enemies purely on my own. I’d always had help, distractions, not to mention the severe trial limitations that the creatures had been subjected to. The elk was the closest thing to a solo fight I started and had no intention of halfassing it.

Spell circle! I cast another spell.
