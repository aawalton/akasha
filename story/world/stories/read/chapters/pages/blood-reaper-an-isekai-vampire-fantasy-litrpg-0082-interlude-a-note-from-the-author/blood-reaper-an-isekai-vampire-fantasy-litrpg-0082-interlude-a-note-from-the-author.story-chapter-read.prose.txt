Author's Note

Hey darlin’,

Forgive me for a moment while I wax poetic.

It's wild to think about the journey that led to the creation of Blood Reaper. Looking back at my headspace when I began writing, I see now it emerged from a place of pain. Just… so much pain. Physical. Mental. Emotional. Existential. I was questioning my place in the world, what I wanted out of life, and why I was even here.

It was a rough time. Not gonna lie. I was close to just giving up on everything.

The only reason I’m still going is because I’m too damn stubborn to quit.

After 2023 tried to kill me, I channeled all that rage into creating something. I had to. It’s just who I am. There was something cathartic about building a world where violence had purpose, where survival required both strength and strategy. I wasn't interested in crafting another predictable hero's journey with convenient plot armor and moral certainty. Instead, I wanted to explore what happens in the shadows, where good intentions crumble under the weight of necessity and characters must embrace their darker nature to survive.

Originally, I started writing in such an irreverent tone because it was different and, in a way, liberating. But after a while, I realized it was masking something more meaningful.

With this book, I approached storytelling with the same dark humor that helped me through my own battles back then, thereby creating a universe where characters faced genuine consequences, and where death came without warning or meaning. There was honesty in that brutality, a reflection of how random and unforgiving life can be.

I needed that.

And I suspect, in a way, you needed it too.

Beneath the bloodshed and profanity is a fundamental question about identity and transformation that I hadn't fully recognized when I started typing. What began as my middle finger to a brutal year evolved into an exploration of becoming—of how we rebuild ourselves when everything we thought we were is stripped away.

That’s the best thing about stories. They have a way of revealing something about you that you couldn’t see before.

Between the blood splatter and the body count, I slowly started thinking about the concept of identity. It took awhile, but I realized that Ace's journey isn't just about survival. It's about the excruciating process of becoming. This Marine—who defined himself by duty, loyalty, and service—suddenly loses everything that made him who he was. His squad is gone. His body is different than before. His hunger is no longer just for food.

Every kill reshapes him through the EXP Solute system—which is just a thinly veiled metaphor for the way our choices fundamentally change us.

And then there’s the System herself.

I wanted her to be a character from the start. I just thought it was fun, at first, but over time the System emerged as more than just a sadistic gamemaster. She became the embodiment of those cruel, arbitrary forces in life that seem to delight in our suffering. We've all encountered versions of her—circumstances that mock our pain while forcing us to adapt, or people in our lives who simply like to make everything harder.

I guarantee that someone’s face just popped up for you when I said that.

As for the Fang Gang—well, they surprised me too. What started as vampire cannon fodder evolved into something resembling a family: Tara with her healer's conscience; Marcus using humor to mask terror; Olivia's disturbing fascination with the aesthetics of violence; even poor Rachel, whose death wasn't planned but felt necessary to demonstrate the world’s brutality. They became an exploration of how shared trauma creates bonds that transcend background, personality, or any of the boundaries we like to think separate us all from each other.

I still don't want to preach to you. That’s not what this is.

With this little love letter to you, I simply hope that somewhere between the gore and the sarcasm, you found something that resonated with your own experience of becoming. Maybe it's the struggle to maintain your core self when circumstances push you toward terrible choices. Maybe it's finding strength in your vulnerability, or discovering your own found family in the most unlikely places.

Whatever brought you to this story—to me—I'm grateful our paths crossed. There's something intimate about this exchange, really. I poured my soul onto these pages, and now you can choose what you want to do with it.

The journey is just beginning. Becoming a vampire was only Ace's first transformation.

What comes next might surprise us both.

Until next time,

Boyce
