Just as the sun was touching the horizon, Alric and Calvern were concluding the roast pork competition. Like the sausages, innocent bystanders had been called to weigh in.

The cold was coming back with a vengeance; Alric had gotten his cloak from his item box, even pulling a spare for Calvern, who apparently hadn’t planned to stay so long but was now determined to see this night through. As the games had wound down, dancing had taken over. Musicians gathered near the larger fires, as circles formed, people often dancing from one circle to another.

Alric and Calvern laughed, watching two soldiers try to run in opposite directions while linking arms, apparently to see who would fall first.

“Mister Alric!”

Alric turned; one of the beer women was waving with a big smile. She pointed toward the barrels, “Two are done!”

“Already?” Alric shouted. He glanced at his own stein. He had five lines, which he considered a pathetic contribution. He nodded to Calvern before getting up and heading toward the beer dragon, still carrying his stein.

Calvern jogged after him, managing not to spill his beer, which was frankly the most impressive athletic feat of the evening. "Is something wrong?" Calvern asked, frowning at the beer wagon.

“No, no, nothing like that. This is good. Two barrels are empty!” Alric said with a big grin, pointing to the beer dragon.

Calvern glanced at the beer wagon and back at Alric. “That is good?”

“It’s the whole point of this!” Alric said, laughing.

Calvern looked over at the field, the fires, and the general air of soon-to-be regret. “You mean the point isn’t to get several hundred people drunk?” Calvern asked, in concern.

“No, that’s the method,” Alric said, still laughing.

They approached the wagon; two beer maids gestured to the two barrels by rocking them. Alric reached forward as one disappeared. A huge cheer rang out, followed by a shout of sixteen. Alric did the same for the second, getting another huge shout and more chants of sixteen.

Alric came back to Calvern just as the chants quieted down, still laughing. Calvern was staring at the empty space.

“Everyone says you love barrels, but surely you didn’t think someone would steal your barrels here?” Calvern asked, both brows raised.

Alric shook his head, waving away with his hand. “Nothing like that. The barrels are why we're having the party. You know how you age whisky in wine casks, yes?” Alric asked, to which Calvern nodded, “This is similar. I made this specific beer to age whisky in the barrels after.” Alric explained. He lifted his stein and took a sip.

Calvern's eyebrows rose very quickly, which felt like the wrong reaction to Alric.

“Huh.” Was all Calvern said, glancing across the field.

Alric wondered if he had said something wrong. “What’s wrong with that?”

Calvern smirked; it somehow made Alric uncomfortable. “Nothing is wrong with it. It’s just that such an idea is revolutionary. I’ve just never heard of such.” Calvern said, still smirking. Alric said nothing, his face still confused. “Not even in dwarven holds.”

Alric opened his mouth to comment before an icy feeling washed through him that had nothing to do with the cold. He closed it quickly. Calvern’s smirk stayed for maybe a second longer before it faded to a frown.

Noise pushed in from every direction; someone near the beer dragon argued that accidentally spilled beer should count toward the sixteen. The beer woman told him it counted to him being an idiot, which got a loud laugh from people nearby.

Alric shrugged at Calvern, saying nothing.

Calvern glanced out over the party, his frown still in place. He smoothed his hair, not looking at Alric when he spoke, “My apologies, that was unfair.” Calvern said. Alric just waited. Calvern looked across at the city walls. “After your first visit to the palace, the king wanted to look into your story of being from a dwarven hold. The queen too.”

“Oh?” Alric asked, pretending not to be interested.

Calvern nodded, still looking at the walls. “Your story is convenient, too convenient,” Calvern said with a shrug. There was no smug grin now; he was still frowning. “A village outside some hold, new brewing techniques, old dwarf magic, no family, no one in Avengard who could know you before you arrived.” Calvern waved a hand toward the city as he spoke, “They wanted to send someone to look into it. I spoke against it.”

There was a slight pause before Alric spoke, “I see. Well, they are welcome to. If they change their minds, can you let me know so I can send a letter to my parents through them?” Alric asked, which made Calven turn back to him, his brows up. Alric just shrugged.

Alric almost asked why he had spoken against it before realizing just in time that it might have been a trap.

“I see,” Calvern said, looking at Alric closely.

“If you decide to send someone, make it someone trustworthy, please. I would love to send some money home.” Alric said, tilting his head to Calvern. Calvern opened his mouth and closed it again.

Before Alric could decide how best to leave this conversation, music picked up from one of the larger groups with a drum. Both glanced at it.

“That’s my cue, Alric. I know this song.” Calvern said, beginning to dip his head in time to the beats.

Alric just nodded as Calvern headed off toward the dancing group. He let out a deep breath.

For a moment there, he had nearly slipped badly. Watching him join the circle, he briefly considered a new thought.

What if I just told him the truth?

That was when he caught himself. He remembered how Calvern had arrived knowing next to no one. In half an hour, adventurers were exchanging stories with him by a fire; people had slapped him on the back. He’d lost a drinking contest, and that was exactly how he won everyone over.

Winning trust is exactly what he is good at.

The thought was mean, rude, but deeply honest. He liked Calvern, but he realized this was exactly how Calvern was dangerous. He wasn’t sure if Calvern believed him, but he wasn’t going to test that.

Wait, I do like him. That might be the most suspicious thing of all.

Alric briefly considered how Calvern had arranged just what he needed, how he’d brought the seasoned barrels, being charming and disarming. He glanced to the circle he’d last seen him go.

I can never trust anything from him now, especially because he’s incredibly good at making me feel that way.

He briefly remembered Calvern’s frown; he had looked genuinely sad. He wondered if Calvern had seen the same thing a bit before he did. “Tomorrow’s problem,” Alric muttered to himself. He was about to take another sip before seeing his stein was empty.

Alric turned around, walking the short distance to the beer dragon. The servers had gotten on top of demand; whenever lines started forming, more servers joined. Currently, it was quiet. Alric held up his stein to one who took it with a beaming smile.

Glancing around, Alric noticed how serious the marking competition had become now. On the opposite side, a serving woman licked her thumb and removed a line, “That’s not the same chalk!” She shouted; a chorus of boos came from nearby onlookers as she handed the tankard back to the adventurer whose shoulders slumped.

The serving woman added a mark to Alric’s stein and handed it back to him, still beaming. He thanked her.

Moving away from the wagon, Alric looked around; this was one of the first times he’d been alone in a while. Hearing another cheer, Alric spotted Tarrow sitting at a table, knocking tankards with someone. Alric headed over. As he approached, he was greeted with a cheer. He raised his stein, and the whole table joined; they shifted to make room.

“No, no,” Tarrow said, standing and laughing, “I must do the rounds. You boys keep going.” He approached Alric with a wide grin and raised his tankard. Alric knocked it and took a sip, but his eyes were drawn to Tarrow’s tankard. That was a lot of lines.

“You remember how I said the adventurer’s fest was the best time of my life?” Tarrow said with a grin. He gestured to the muster yard. “This takes a close second.”

Alric laughed out loud. He took a sip. “You just like getting drunk, Tarrow. We're getting sloshed in a field here.” Alric said, gesturing to it.

Tarrow didn’t argue; he just gave a big nod. Nearby, an adventurer was shouting for his tankard; apparently, it had thirteen marks, and he had lost it. He was looking under his table. Alric and Tarrow stood by watching and chuckling.

“Surely I can move em to a new tankard?” He yelled as if his life had lost all meaning.

“Not on ya life, lad. Ya ‘ave ta start over.” A voice Alric knew well came from nearby. Tarrow and Alric were too busy laughing to get involved. The adventurer looked devastated.

“Tarrow!” Someone shouted nearby, Alric and Tarrow turned to the voice, a young woman came jogging across the field, she paused in front of him, “Man fell in the climbing game, can you come take a look.” She asked Tarrow. Tarrow nodded and gestured for her to lead the way. Alric followed.

An adventurer stood slightly apart from the group around the king's perch game; he saw Tarrow approach and moved over to him, holding out his arm. The skin on his forearm was badly torn; blood ran from the wound. Alric considered that a vein must have been sliced; he was surprised the man was still standing.

“Anything broken?” Tarrow asked, taking the man’s arm. He moved one of the man's fingers, and he winced.

“Don’t think so, father,” He said, still wincing.

“Hold still,” Tarrow said, nodding.

Tarrow raised a hand over the area; a faint green light emanated from it. Alric watched closely, having not seen this before. Tarrow started moving his hand up the arm.

Alric tried to track with his mana senses and could sense very faint warm mana, but the warmth felt like something else.

Slowly, the bleeding stopped. Alric watched more closely; the skin began drawing itself toward the two edges, the parts underneath moving as well. The man winced badly, gritting his teeth.

Wait? That hurts? That must be agonizing.

Slowly the wound knitted itself closed. The man breathed heavily, taking deep breaths. He had turned pale; Alric couldn’t tell whether it was pain, blood loss, or the cold.

“Thank you, Father.” The adventurer said, flexing his fingers and his arm.

“No more stupid games,” Tarrow said with a frown.

“Can I still drink?” The man asked quickly, his brows raised in alarm.

More people around him, including Alric, burst out laughing.

Tarrow put his hands on his hips. “I’m a priest, not your mother,” Tarrow said, which got another cackle from the crowd. The adventurer smiled, “Go eat first, though, boy.” Tarrow added, pointing to the inns and putting on a feminine voice. The crowd lost it, laughing loudly; the adventurer laughed and nodded.

Alric grinned; it struck him again how Tarrow had changed. He really did seem more dignified now, but then he would make comments like that and still seem like the same old Tarrow. Tarrow turned back to Alric, shaking his head slowly. Someone nearby handed Tarrow his tankard back; he thanked them.

“You really have come to your own, you know?” Alric used his Stein to gesture to Tarrow.

Tarrow grinned at Alric and nodded, “In part thanks to you, but after you said it. I have seen it.” Tarrow glanced over at the party, “I lacked a place, as you said.” He raised his tankard to the yard and took a sip. Alric joined him.

“Alric!” Came a call from nearby. Alric and Tarrow glanced across at Pavise; he was approaching, carrying a board of food. “Simon said you like pepper,” Pavise said, holding the board out in front of Tarrow and Alric.

On the board were strips of steak, slightly rare, but a thick, creamy-looking pepper sauce was smeared over the lot. Alric and Tarrow didn’t even hesitate, taking pieces with their fingers. “Simon is becoming dangerous, wow. That’s good.” Alric said. Tarrow nodded, taking another.

Pavise just chuckled, “I wanted to thank you both again.” Pavise said, nodding to the pair.

“We had this conversation,” Alric said, taking another piece.

“I’m just grateful to be here, and you did put it together,” Tarrow said.

The three let out a large, collective shrug and then laughed.

“I did mean to tell you, Alric, your hull cracker stones. They are late.” Pavise said and glanced off at the river, “I’m waiting on Avenport to send word.”

Alric shrugged. The magic stones that could move air or water were useful but less so for him in winter. “I’ll get them when they get here,” Alric said, taking more steak. Pavise nodded, glancing around at the party again. Alric noticed the guild master's grin had turned decidedly goofy; he also had more marks on his stein than Alric.
