“WOOD PRIEST!”

The shout echoed across the entrance hall the moment the transit portal opened.

Bart Sanders closed his eyes already dreading what came next.

Gregor looked up instantly from a stack of books with a grin.

“Nobb?” Gregor called back.

“THREE PIG MAN!” a second, equally loud voice confirmed.

It was unmistakably them but as Nobb and Hedd marched into the Infinite Archive, Bart noticed a significant difference in their demeanour. They were not running or pointing excitedly at the ceiling.

They were carrying a thick, square bundle wrapped carefully in three layers of cured animal hide.They approached the Patron Services desk and placed the bundle on the counter with reverence. Nobb carefully untied the leather cord and peeled back the protective layers.

Inside sat a perfectly clean, dry, undamaged copy of Illustrated Foundations, Drainage, and Timber Supports.

Bart scanned the spine.

ITEM CONDITION: ACCEPTABLE

LOAN STATUS: RETURNED

Bart was almost suspicious. “Nothing happened to it?”

“Book safe,” Nobb reported proudly.

“Roof safe too,” Hedd added.

Ada, who had been standing at the end of the counter reviewing a batch of facilities reports, stopped writing. She looked over.

“Roof safe?” Ada asked.

Nobb brightened immediately and pointed at her. “Adequate!”

“Ada Kett,” she corrected automatically.

“Very Adequate,” Hedd agreed, nodding along.

Ada had long since stopped trying to correct their understanding of her name. She stepped over to Bart’s station. Nobb reached into his tunic and produced a folded, heavily smudged piece of parchment. He smoothed it out on the counter.It was not a beautiful architectural diagram but as Ada studied the crude charcoal lines, the structural logic became clear.

“You moved the water away from the supports?” Ada asked, tracing a jagged line on the parchment.

Nobb pointed. “Water go there now.”

“And you replaced the softened post?”

Hedd nodded . “Soft wood bad.”

Ada paused,  It was exactly as the solution she had translated for them on their previous visit. Hedd pointed proudly to a cluster of heavily pressed charcoal marks beside one of the drawn timber supports.

“Three hand,” Hedd translated, reading his own notation. “Plus two finger.”

“That is not a recognised unit of measurement,” Ada noted.

“But did the roof stay up?” Gregor asked.

Ada looked at the crude drawing again. “Apparently.”

She checked the validated book return, then looked at the two cave goblins.

“You followed the instructions,” Ada said.

“Pictures good,” Nobb said.

“Words less bad now,” Hedd added while Gregor looked extremely pleased with himself.

Ada looked from the repaired roof sketch to Gregor. The last time Ada had seen them, she had discovered that Gregor had taught them enough reading to use the library

“They manage to learn,” Ada said to Gregor

Gregor shrugged, trying to mask his pride. “That's what books are for, isn't it?”

Ada looked at him for a moment.

“Yes,I think they are”

She turned back to Nobb and Hedd.

“Good work,” she told them.

Both cave goblins froze. The Fancy Goblin, the very Adequate, the licensed engineer from Tessera had just praised them.

“Good?” Nobb repeated, stunned.

“Do not make me repeat my professional assessments,” Ada warned.

Before the celebration could escalate, the main interface chimed softly.

STAFF RETRIEVAL TASKS PENDING: 14

QUALIFIED ROUTE STAFF AVAILABLE: 1

Gregor looked at the glowing blue number. “Ah man”

“I haven't said anything,” Bart pointed out.

“You looked.”

Nobb and Hedd both looked at Gregor.

“Three Pig Man work?” Nobb asked.

“Yes, unfortunately” Gregor sighed.

Nobb stood up straighter. “We work.”

“No,” Bart said immediately.

Hedd looked offended. “Why no work us Wood Priest?”

Bart mentally prepared a lecture. “Because retrieving materials from the stacks requires staff-only access. It requires strict adherence to route protocols, collection handling requirements, baseline literacy, and certified safety training.”

Ada listening to his lecture interrupted him.

“They repaired a load-bearing shelter using written instructions,” she pointed out.

Ada continued on smoothly. “They can follow diagrams. They can read basic warnings. They understand stop instructions.”

“Mostly,” Gregor muttered from the side.

“Supervised then” Ada amended.

Bart looked at her, the Archive’s own engineer was proposing a possibility to lighten the load and smoothen operations.

Bart tapped the interface,He did not immediately hire them but created a highly restricted, temporary classification.

PROVISIONAL RETRIEVAL OBSERVERS

PURPOSE: TRAINING ASSESSMENT

ACCESS: CERTIFIED LOW-RISK STAFF ROUTE ONLY

SUPERVISION: DIRECT

INDEPENDENT HANDLING: PROHIBITED

EMPLOYMENT STATUS: NONE

CERTIFICATION STATUS: NONE

Nobb leaned over the counter, staring at the bolded text. He sounded it out with painful concentration.

“Pro… vis…”

“Means you're learning,” Gregor told him.

Nobb nodded happily. “Work learn.”

“Close enough.”

Bart automatically looked across the foyer toward the Chief Custodian.

Albert, who was currently dusting a display case 50 ft away, spoke without turning around.

“No, sir.”

“I didn't even ask,” Bart said.

“You were about to,” Albert replied calmly.

“Gregor is still learning himself,” Bart argued.

Ada picked up her facilities reports. “Then he remembers what beginners get wrong.”

It was the same logic Bart had used earlier when he suggested that Gregor teach the cave goblins to read.

Gregor realised what was happening. “Oh no.”

The interface chimed once more.

SUPERVISING ROUTE TECHNICIAN: GREGOR

“Boss,” Gregor complained.

“Congratulations,” Bart replied.

Gregor sighed, turning to face his new trainees. He selected a low risk, fully certified route he knew well.

“Stay where I can see you,” Gregor instructed. “Read every marker. If the marker changes, stop. Don't touch anything unless it's on the slip.”

Hedd raised his hand. “What if good book?”

“Especially good book,” Gregor said firmly.

Bart leaned forward to add his own layer of instruction. “Maintain continuous situational-”

“Look around,” Gregor translated seamlessly.

Nobb and Hedd nodded in unison. “Look around.”

Ada glanced at Bart and he stopped talking.

Nell issued them two crude printed badges on lanyards.

PROVISIONAL RETRIEVAL OBSERVER

Nobb immediately put his on upside down while Gregor led them toward the corridor.

Ada watched the three of them disappear into the corridor.

Bart noticed her expression. “You seem pleased.”

“Their roof did not collapse,” Ada said simply.

“That is a good start, for a bunch of cave goblins”

.

Inside the stacks, the air was cool and smelled of old paper. Gregor stopped at the first major junction. He pointed to the marker bolted to the wall.

“Read it,” Gregor said.

Nobb squinted at the stamped metal. He traced the letters in the air with a finger.

“WEST…” Nobb started slowly. “WESTERN.”

Hedd stared at the second word. “CIV… CIVIC.”

“Good,” Gregor said. He pointed to the directional arrow beneath the words. “Route?”

“B,” they said together.

Gregor nodded and led them down the corridor.

They reached the designated aisle.

“14-K,” Gregor announced.

Nobb scanned the shelf, moving his finger along the spines until he found the exact alphanumeric match for the requested item. He pointed to it triumphantly.

Hedd, however, was looking at the book immediately beside it.

It had a fascinating, deeply embossed cover depicting what looked like a warhammer.

Hedd reached for it.Gregor’s hand shot out, catching Hedd’s wrist before his fingers could touch the book.

“No,” Gregor said.

Hedd looked at him. “Good book.”

“Maybe but not our book.”

“Could read.”

“Later,” Gregor said firmly. “As patron. When you're working, you take what the slip says.”

Hedd looked at the fascinating cover for another long moment. Then, with visible reluctance, he put his hands safely behind his back.Nobb carefully retrieved the correct book and handed it to Gregor.They returned to the entrance hall 20 minutes later. There had been no structural collapse and returned with exactly one book.

Ada was still standing near the desk when the System registered the completed assessment.

SUPERVISED RETRIEVAL ASSESSMENT

ROUTE COMPLETED: YES

ROUTE DEVIATIONS: 0

WARNING COMPLIANCE: SATISFACTORY

READING ASSISTANCE REQUIRED: LIMITED

UNAUTHORISED ITEM CONTACT: PREVENTED

SAFETY INCIDENTS: 0

Bart examined the readout.

“Did not touch good book,” Hedd announced proudly.

“He nearly touched the said good book,” Gregor clarified.

“Did not.”

“That is acceptable,” Bart ruled.

The System chimed again, requesting the final administrative step.

SUPERVISOR ASSESSMENT REQUIRED

Bart instinctively reached for the interface to fill it out.

AUTHORISED ASSESSOR: GREGOR

Bart stopped. He moved his hand away and asked Gregor to fill it in instead.

Gregor looked at the screen. He read the form slowly, carefully  filled it in.

For Nobb, he entered:

RECOMMENDATION: CONTINUE SUPERVISED TRAINING

For Hedd, he added a line.

RECOMMENDATION: CONTINUE SUPERVISED TRAINING

SPECIAL INSTRUCTION: ONLY BRING BOOK ON PAPER

Hedd leaned over to read the glowing text. “Paper says what book.”

“That's the point,” Gregor told him.

Ada opened her tool case, she produced two small pieces of ordinary orange marking chalk and handed one to each cave goblin.

“For route notes,” Ada instructed. Nobb held the chalk reverently in both hands. “Fancy goblin chalk.”

“Training chalk,” Ada corrected.

“Very fancy training chalk,” Hedd agreed in a hushed whisper.

Ada let it go. She turned to Bart.

“They can learn,” Ada said.

“Yes, they seem to catch on fast” Bart agreed.

Ada looked toward Gregor. “So can he.”

Gregor, who was currently trying to convince Hedd not to eat the chalk, looked over. “I heard that.”

Bart opened the main patron registry to update their administrative files.

PATRON 1: NOBB

PATRON 2: HEDD

He added the new classification.

TRAINING STATUS: SUPERVISED RETRIEVAL ASSESSMENT - PASSED

Gregor walked around the counter and looked over Bart’s shoulder at the glowing text.

Then, Gregor took the report from Bart and carefully wrote their names beneath the assessment.

He wrote Nobb.

He moved his chalk down and started a completely separate line.

He wrote Hedd.

Bart noticed the spacing when Gregor caught him looking.

“Two people,” Gregor said quietly.

Bart smiled slightly . “Yes.”

Nobb leaned over the counter and pointed at the line.

“Small Nobb,” he declared.

Gregor closed his eyes.

“We are not doing that again.”
