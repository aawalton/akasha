I wasn’t curled against the witchwood.

I wasn’t sure where I was at all. The world was a sea of gold. There were no cold stones digging into my knees, no murmuring leaves against my skin.

Just… warmth. Sunlight. It washed over me. Sank into me. Calmed every trembling nerve, soothed every aching muscle.

I blinked against the light, inhaling scents so lovely I sighed; rich cinnamon and melting chocolate, freshly kindled fires, warmed earth and amber honey. The sounds followed after. Chirping songbirds and buzzing bees. Faint, but merry. A distant, babbling stream. Unhurried wind winding through an ocean of grass.

I couldn’t see any of it. All I saw was light. Bright, all-consuming, achingly warm.

Then… there. Taking shape in the brilliance. A figure brighter than every fragment of this shining world.

Skin of carved bronze, hair a burnished gold. A face neither young nor old, but regal and proud. Eyes that held me. Not with the cruelty or claim I’d shivered under moments ago, but with something much fiercer. Something good.

I was safe. His very presence vowed it. I knew it in my bones, down to my very core. Beyond any shadow of a doubt. In the presence of this being, I was safe.

Velidan’s cruel hands could have closed around my throat. Baltha could have loomed before me, yellow teeth gnashing. Forthys and Vessa and every shadow in all the realms could have hissed at my heels.

And still, I would not have even trembled. Nothing could harm me before this man. The certainty thrummed in every beat of my heart, swelled with every breath. Fear dripped like shadows through sunlit fingers as my heart swelled with wordless joy.

I could have stood forever. Stared forever. Knelt at his feet, gazing eternally into a face raw with devotion.

“This vow.”

His mouth moved, but the words came as distant, rolling thunder.

“Did not end.”

Ancient, solemn eyes watched me. Unblinking. No whites, just vivid blue. Irises a sea of burnt orange, fiery crimson and rich gold. Pupils alive with flame. Twin, rising dawns.

“In death.”

The world trembled.

I felt the heat, first.

Steady. Reassuring. My fingers curled, of their own accord, around familiar steel. Velvet wound down my wrist, twining into the warmth. Traced my fingers.

My eyelids cracked open. Slowly, breath held, disbelieving even as I looked down; the shard lay nestled in my palm. Gold. Bright. Warm.

My lips parted in baffled, wordless wonder. I could still feel the sunlight beneath my skin, could still feel the weight of that ancient gaze on me.

I’m safe. I feel… safe.

Perhaps it was foolish. Perhaps the feeling would fade the next time I lay down to sleep. A face darkened by cruelty, and surely now by fury, would haunt me again tonight.

But right now, at least… right now I’m okay. And I haven’t failed. Somehow.

I looked up slowly. The sky was a misty gray haze, just visible between a canopy of lush, swaying firs. Cool air breathed against my skin.

Renner sat against a pine nearby, head tilted back. Eyes half-closed. The sword lay bare across his lap, one gloved hand wrapped around the hilt. His neck and collar were a mess of dried blood. Weary shadows, streaked dirt, and faded trails of crimson covered his face.

The sight made something deep inside of me ache. He’d been sitting there for hours, if the sky was any indication. After miles upon miles of travel, after fighting for his life against men and monsters, after a night of no rest. After nearly dying.

He didn’t deserve any of this. He’d been cruel and harsh, self-serving and threatening, but… he’d been a lot of other things, too.

I bit my lower lip. “I’m sorry.”

Copper snapped into focus. Swept over me alongside a sharp intake of breath before settling on my hand and the light within it. “Changed your mind, huh?”

“What?”

He winced, free hand drifting up. Towards his head. “Any idea how long I’ve got?”

“How long… oh. Oh. No. I didn’t change my…” I sat up, inhaling crisp pine and warm earth. “I gave it to him.”

He closed his eyes briefly. “And?”

I stood on legs that, for the first time in recent memory, weren’t shaking. Silky leaves tangled into my fingers as I lifted the steel, lips curving into a wry smile. “And… I think it decided to come back with me.”

Renner blinked up, shaking his head a little. “You think it…” He heaved a sigh, dragging his free hand across his face. “Sure. Right. I’m too exhausted to think about what that means.”

I offered a weak smile. “You look pretty rough. No offense.”

“Uh-huh,” he grumbled. Then his expression hardened.

My face grew warm as he looked me up and down again. Intently, gaze lingering on every inch and curve. Eyes simmering, jaw tight.

I swallowed, brushing tangled hair behind one ear. He looked at me like that in the cave. At my mouth. After… oh. “I’m alright,” I said softly, looking down.

Renner scowled, rising to his feet. Metal hissed as he sheathed the sword. “You’re a bad liar.”

“I… I know. I am, though. Alright. I….” I closed my eyes against the feel of hot breath along my neck, of iron strength pressing me into stone. Didn’t fight the warmth sliding down my cheeks, but didn’t collapse beneath it all, either.

I breathed. Pine. Earth. Soil. Familiar things.

“I’m not hurt,” I whispered, keeping my eyes closed. “I’m… here. I’m not… I’m not there. I’m not with h-him. I’m here. I’m safe. I’m safe. I’m…” Another thought crept in. An ugly, dark one. I looked up slowly, Renner’s face a watery blur. “A-am I? Right now?” I held my breath. “Safe?”

He grew very still. Then gave a single, jerky nod.

I sniffled, lifting the cloak to dry my tears. “Okay. I’m… I’m alright, then. I am.” I spent a few moments calming myself. The fingers of one hand clenched around molten gold, while the other grazed whispering leaves. “Renner?”

“Yeah?” His voice was hoarse.

“I’m sorry. Really. For dragging you into all this. For everything you went through last night. For lying to you.”

He looked away, face dark. “Yeah. Well… I’m sorry you had to.”

I blinked away the last of my sorrow, almost wondering if I’d misheard him.

His mouth twisted into a grimace and he ran both hands through his hair. “Look, I wasn’t there for your first deal, but I sure as salt saw the second one. And…” he heaved a sigh, eyes drifting skyward. “I don’t know, ladyship. I just keep thinking… maybe… maybe you didn’t do anything wrong.”

I burst into fresh tears as soon as the words were out. They were swallowed almost immediately by bright swells of laughter. I practically threw myself at him, shaking with both.

Renner froze at my embrace. Inhaled deeply. Then, with a slowness that ached, curled one arm around my waist.

“That’s... that’s exactly… what I’ve… saints, thank you.” I sobbed and laughed helplessly, burying my head against his shoulder. Rough stubble scraped the crown of my head as, rather than release me right away, his grip tightened. “Sorry. I’m sorry. Salt, but, thank you! You have no idea how much I’ve… how many times I’ve said that exact… thank you.” I pulled back, wiping my streaming eyes and shaking with helpless, exhausted laughter.

His lips twitched. “Don’t mention it.”

We walked side by side back to Snowmelt. The forest was still, save the occasional wintry breeze. The skyline beyond town held shades of steel and dusky violet as we broke through the treeline.

Boots tapped against frosty stones when we reached the street. A few townsfolk milled about, stopping in their tracks when they saw us and offering wary, wide-eyed gazes.

News travels fast in a place like this, I thought wryly. I wonder if the whole town knows what happened.

Whatever shock they may have felt, none of the townsfolk dared to approach or ask questions.

Renner’s blood-spattered visage might have had something to do with that.

When the inn came into sight, a familiar- and jarringly out-of-place- sound echoed down the cobblestones. A whinny. A large bay horse stood tethered outside the inn. Its coat glistened with sweat as thick black hooves stamped impatiently against the stones.

Good timing, I suppose. Things might’ve gone very differently if there’d been another traveler staying here for all this mess.

Renner reached the door first. He opened it and cocked an eyebrow at me, waiting.

I smiled at him. “Thank-”

“-this tall, really pretty, with big gray eyes and brown hair.”

I froze at the familiar baritone. My jaw dropped, eyes growing wide as the figure beside master Lewen turned to face me.

“Royce?!”
