Charlie

He shrieked in excitement when he heard the news that Vetica had won. He knew she would, but it was still exciting. Just up ahead, in the next room, Mary was waiting. One trial. One fight. Win that, and he could save Mary and leave this fort behind. He just needed to win.

“Come on, Orb!” Charlie said, holding his hands out to reach for his floating dungeon core partner in crime.

“Ya know, it’s a little weird that a dungeon baby is asking for uppies. Just saying!” Orb floated closer as he spoke, and Charlie clutched him. The two of them floated, skipping what would otherwise be a long and grueling crawl up the stairs. Even after all this time, Charlie still had some obstacles that were inconvenient for a baby. As they floated past, a window grated open to their surprise. The previous hallway hadn’t included a window, but apparently this one did. Now that Vetica had won her trial, it seemed the tower was opening not only the door that would lead them upward but also the window looking down upon the fort.

It made sense. They were the ones who wanted to beat the tower. Now that the second trial was complete, there was no reason not to let them peer outside.

The window finished opening long before the doorway. That was taking more time. While they waited, Orb floated over to the window so they could look. The storm outside still raged, and the hex knights were still fighting. But the fighting wasn’t as far away as it had been before. The monsters had pressed forward, and now many of them were scaling the walls as knights attempted to repel them. The powerful, long-haired knight from earlier was still waging war with that demonic, giant turtle, and Charlie had a bad feeling about it all.

“Orb, the monsters are getting close to the village! What if they get inside?” Charlie asked.

“All the more reason to get Mary and get out of here. There’s nothing we can do, Charlie. If we leave now, we may never get a chance to save Mary.” Orb sighed. “We can give up and help the villagers, or we can save Mary. We can’t do both, Charlie. If we leave this tower, that’s it. The villagers will be fine. These are the freaking hex knights we’re talking about! If they weren’t strong as heck, we wouldn’t have worried about them chasing us all across Aysela, now would we?”

Charlie frowned. He had to save Mary, but maybe there was something he could do. Even if it would take some time. As he enacted his plan, Orb turned, and they focused on the door slowly opening up ahead.

Sir Gregory was just on the other side.

Arkady

“Scarlet Hex Search!”

Three of them this time, all different species. His hexes illuminated their weak points. When his dagger flashed, he cut them down as if he’d killed hundreds of their kind before. He’d never so much as seen any of them. That was the beauty of having a hex that identified weaknesses.

Fur, hair, and gelatinous flesh littered the forest floor as he pressed onward through the corpses of his prey.

He flattened the grass of the forest floor with each step. Thick raindrops broke through the canopy above and stung his skin. Arkady’s clothes felt like they’d been doused in ice water. The wind didn’t help either. Heavy gusts offered severe resistance, but he pushed through. Despite the fact his muscles screamed and his mana was rapidly depleting, he pushed through.

Orielle’s little brother was in danger.

Tomlin was out here, in the center of a hex storm and a forest full of mana beasts. That little boy was out here and Arkady was going to save him.

“Tomlin!” he screamed without any regard for his own safety. He was only attracting more of the beast, but that was okay. Each one that pursued him was another dead. Another one not hunting the boy. So far, the most powerful beasts were concentrated around the fort. But the hex storm showed no signs of stopping. A quick glance through the tree line was all the proof he needed. The clouds above were so black not even the moon’s light could penetrate them. In a dense forest such as this, he only had the glow of his hex to light his way.

“Tomlin, where are you?” He could hear the desperation growing in his own voice. Arkady had promised he’d come back with Tomlin. But the longer the boy was out here alone—

No, he couldn’t think like that. He couldn’t give up. He’d spent the last few months chasing after Charlie and his friends. If anyone could track down a lost little boy in the woods, it was him.

If only his scarlet hex of searching worked the conventional way.

He saw a tail swinging from side to side up ahead. It poked out from behind a tree. Arkady gripped his dagger, ready for a fight. But as he got closer the noises he heard gave him pause.

He slowed to a stop, tilting his ear toward the tree.

Crunching. Gnawing. Eating.

This creature was eating something.

Arkady’s heart thumped in his chest. He looked down and saw crimson liquid mixed in puddles of rainwater.

Blood.

He shook his head.

“No…please don’t be…”

He walked around the corner to see what the beast was eating. A wolf.

It had caught a wolf.

Arkady sighed in relief. The beast finally noticed him and turned to attack.

It fell to the ground a moment later as blood poured from a fresh wound in its neck.

“That’s what you get for scaring me.”

“Arkady…” The young voice cracked as he spoke. It was barely audible above the thunder and rain, but Arkady heard the familiar voice nonetheless.

He looked up. Tomlin was sitting on a tree branch, just above him.

“Tomlin?! How did you…why are you up there?” he asked.

“That dog was chasing me. I climbed up the tree but it wouldn’t go away. Then that…monster came and killed it. I didn’t know what to do.”

Arkady looked back down at the wolf. He shuddered to think what an animal like that could do to a boy Tomlin’s age. Still, it was odd. What was a normal animal doing outside in a hex storm? It had to be on the verge of starvation if it was hunting in this weather. Had it struggled to find food lately to the point it was desperate enough to chase Tomlin?

He shook his head. That didn’t matter. Not now. His skin crawled, and he turned to look over his shoulder. He could sense something coming. Something powerful. He squinted. No, not just something. A group of them. “Tomlin we have to go now. The storm is still attracting more monsters. If we don’t get back to the fort, we won’t make it.”

“O…okay!” Tomlin scrambled to climb down.

Thunder cracked, and monsters screamed in the distance.

Arkady cursed under his breath. They’d caught the scent of humans.

They were being hunted.

“Jump! I’ll catch you! There’s no time!”

Tomlin looked terrified, but the boy put on a brave face and let go. Arkady scooped him out of the air and ran. It was moments like this that made him grateful he trained so often. He cradled Tomlin against his chest and sprinted as hard as he could.

“Did you kill all those?” Tomlin asked as they passed the trail of carcasses Arkady had left behind in his search.

“Close your eyes bud. Don’t look. I’ve got it all covered, alright? You just close your eyes and relax. I won’t let anything happen to you.”

Tomlin nodded slowly. He clenched his eyes shut tight and leaned his head against Arkady’s chest. His whole body was shaking.

It didn’t take them long to reach the edge of the forest’s clearing, but the snarling and barking from behind was growing louder and louder. It didn’t take an expert on monsters to realize the creatures would overtake them long before they made it back to the fort. Just because they’d escaped the forest didn’t mean they were safe. Even now, the fort’s walls were being assaulted. It was mostly from the eastern side, but more creatures were emerging from the forest all over, and the knights were struggling to man all the walls.

A strange smell wafted across his nose, and Arkady’s eyes threatened to pop out of his head. It was the smell of smoke mixed with rotten eggs. That, combined with the noises they’d heard from the pack behind him, told him exactly what was chasing them. If he was right, this was bad. Not just for them, but for everyone in the fort.

He turned to look over his shoulder. He could see them in the distance now. They looked like an odd mix between wolves and armadillos. They had the body of a brown wolf, with a shell on their back and glowing red bellies. Their bellies grew brighter and brighter as they approached, a mix of orange and red, as if their stomachs contained fire itself.

They were rarely spotted in Aysela, mostly because Hex Knights had endeavored to hunt them to extinction. A pack of these could wipe out an entire town.

Or in this case, a fort.

Bombaluns.

“Tomlin, you’re going to have to run.”

Tomlin shook his head. The look he gave Arkady broke his heart. He’d just promised to protect the boy, and here he was sending him toward the fort alone. “I need you to trust me, okay? You’re going to have to be a big boy, alright?”

Tomlin’s eyes watered, and not just from the torrent of rain. He noticed the movement and spotted the monsters chasing them. He shook his head fervently. “No. Please, let’s keep running. Please, please, please!”

“We can’t. You have to.”

“I can’t,” Tomlin shook his head.

Arkady placed a hand on his shoulder. “Those creatures are dangerous. If they get close to the walls, they’ll explode. Then the knights won’t be able to protect everyone inside. Your father, your sister, all your friends? They’ll be in danger. There’s no time for this. I need you to run and warn the knights. Can you do that for me?” He held up a hand and used his hex.

A small scarlet hex appeared in front of Tomlin. Then it shattered under Arkady’s will.

“Look at that, my hex says you don’t have any weakness at all!” He smiled encouragingly. Hopefully the little white lie would encourage the boy a bit.

Tomlin swallowed. He knew he didn’t have a choice, but he was brave. He nodded slowly. Arkady surveyed the field before them. More and more monsters were emerging from the forests. As soon as they spotted a human running outside the walls, they’d go after him.

So, Arkady would have to make sure he presented a better target.

“Run for the front gate, run and don’t stop. No matter what you hear, no matter what you see. Run and scream for help as soon as you’re close. The knights will save you.” As much as he despised Brandt and his kind, he knew they’d save the kid. “And do me a favor, okay?”

“Okay. What is it?”

“Tell your sister I—” This wasn’t the time for a deathbed confession. “Tell her I’m sorry. Now, go!” He gave Tomlin a little push and sent him on his way. The boy ran as fast as he could. Several creature’s heads swiveled when they saw him fleeing through the grass.

Arkady gritted his teeth and rolled up his sleeve. He knew exactly where to cut to avoid doing any permanent damage, but that didn’t mean it wasn’t going to suck. The tip of his dagger slid against his skin and tore through. He held his bleeding arm into the air and screamed. “Come and get me! Come on!”

Even through the rain, generations of evolution had worked to create predators that could detect even the faintest whiff of blood. The only thing more appealing than a weak, defenseless child, was an injured adult that would offer a way more substantial meal.

Most of them turned their attention to Arkady. Not all of them, but most. Some of the smaller creatures continued to chase Tomlin, but if the boy kept his current pace, he’d be alright.

Arkady took a breath. He turned to face the Bombaluns. They were the most pressing threat. The others he could deal with, but if that pack reached the walls everyone in Fort Stonehaven was as good as dead.

Arkady wasn’t letting that happen.

He remembered telling Charlie there was going to come a day where he faced a fight he couldn’t possibly win. He never expected it to come so soon. But one thing was for certain.

He may not win this fight, but he certainly wouldn’t let these creatures win either.

As the first of the Bombaluns reached him. He shouted defiantly.

“Come on, we’re all losing today!”
