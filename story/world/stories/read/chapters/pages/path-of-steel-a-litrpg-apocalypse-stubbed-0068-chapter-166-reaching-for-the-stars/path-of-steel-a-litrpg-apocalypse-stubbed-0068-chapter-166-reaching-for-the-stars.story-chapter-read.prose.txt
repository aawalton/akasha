The gym was, indeed, still open. Most beef boys and climbers were fast asleep on the grass, but a few still practiced, focusing on quiet exercises. No one sparred or worked the boxing bags.

Gary walked up to the board, all by himself. He eyed the rewards.

Last time he’d checked his merit points before the bosses attacked, he’d had just over seven hundred points. His team must have participated in some big battle on their way out, because his points had risen to almost eleven hundred. Then, he’d received this screen.

Mission failed! For your contribution to the war objective, you have been awarded an extra 500 merit points.

That brought him to sixteen hundred merit points. It was a prodigious amount, but as he looked at the item he most wanted in the list, he was just a little short. He’d need to either wait until after the next mission, or be smart about it.

He left the gym and spotted a group of still awake climbers around a bonfire. “Hey. Can anyone lend me some merit points?” he asked.

The people—a group of seven Birth of Aura legionaries—stopped their conversation and stared him up and down. “You’re Gary Steel,” one of them said.

“That’s right,” Gary replied.

“I don’t know if it’s even possible to give merit points.”

“I heard some guys pooled their points to get a life-saving treasure. It’s possible,” Gary said.

The legionaries glanced at each other. “How much do you need?” another asked.

“Four hundred and nineteen.”

Their eyes widened. “I’ve only made a hundred throughout the campaign,” one of them said.

“Yeah. That’s way too much,” a second added.

“We can’t help you,” a third said.

“I can,” a voice came from behind Gary. He turned to find Feng Shen walking over. “Four hundred and nineteen, right?”

“I couldn’t possibly take it from you. You already helped me a lot,” Gary said, but the number one disciple smiled.

“Come on, Gary. I’m saving up for an item, but I need a few thousand more. You can have it now, but you must pay me back double after the next mission.”

“And if I die?”

“Then that’s my loss.”

Gary sighed. “Alright. Thank you, Feng Shen. I will remember this.”

“Don’t mention it,” Feng Shen said. Gary’s merit point balance ticked up to a pretty two-thousand.

After thanking the cultivator again, Gary returned to the merit board. There was one item he needed more than anything else. It was a no-brainer.

“System, give me a chunk of Star-Forged Steel,” he said. The two thousand merit points disappeared as a heavy block of steel appeared in his palm. He raised a brow at its weight. The metal was jagged and hexagonal, almost crystal-like in appearance, but its hardness left no doubt of its true nature. Most importantly, it was choke-full of astral aura, a quantity vastly eclipsing the Active Dirt Gary had previously consumed. Though he’d sucked out all his astral energy during the breakthrough, just this chunk of metal could saturate his entire bloodstream, or close.

“Wow,” he muttered.

“You should also get a babysitter when you find the money,” Goldie said.

“What?”

“Someone to take care of the beef boys while you’re out in the front. You’ll be much more aggressive now, right? Someone needs to protect your investment.”

“Who are you and what did you do to Goldie?” he asked.

“Hey! I’m not stupid, Gary, I know this stuff! My daddy used to say that investments are like a flock of sheep. You can take care of them and grow them, but you also need a dog to make sure nothing eats them.”

“He really said that?”

“Yes! But I don’t think it was a metaphor because he really owned sheep.”

“Huh. Well, I like that idea. I’ll get a pro boxer mercenary to watch out for the beefs and the bosses if I can, but I’m broke. We’ll revisit when I find more merits or aura stones.”

“Okay!” Goldie replied.

While beef boy mercenaries couldn’t be purchased with merit points, pro boxers could. They were priced at five hundred each.

Gary took the steel and returned to the grass field around the gym. He sat down cross-legged and placed it on his lap, then cycled his aura as instructed in the Starvein Immortal Body Manual. It wasn’t a simple process, but after doing it with the Star Grass and the Active Dirt, he was now familiar. The steel resisted, however. Its aura was harder to pluck than either the grass’s or the dirt’s. Gary persisted, rotating his aura as much as he needed to, picking it off piece by piece.

Little by little, the metal emptied, and Gary’s veins filled up. He could sense the stars in his blood. They were tiny blue lights coursing through his veins, lighting him up. Where they passed, the blood was stronger, and that strength spread to his entire body through the oxygen it carried. As Gary absorbed more and more of the starlight, he sensed himself growing stronger, denser, powerful.

With the last drip of power, Gary was full.

He drew a sharp breath. The metal crumbled in his lap, a heap of gray ash, and Gary clenched his fists to feel his power. Something had clicked inside him. Starlight now filled his veins, travelling alongside the blood, but as it traveled, it demanded different trajectories. Gary had read about this part. He lied down on the grass, spreading his limbs wide, and stayed as still as he could.

It was like ants crawling inside his flesh. Not painful, but deeply unpleasant. He resisted the urge to fight back as the starlight tugged at his veins, dislodging them from their permanent positions based on its own, mystical set of rules. Veins and arteries slid through Gary’s body, passing between his bones and organs, crawling under and over each other. The once human blood system turned complex, housing seemingly unnecessary twists and turns.

Gary gritted his teeth through the process until whatever mystical laws guided his vessels were satisfied. Only then did he spread his aura through his body to find his veins and arteries twisting, jumbled, circling and coiling around each other in sharp twists. Yet, the starlight knew what it was doing. Despite all the changes, his blood still flowed, if at a slightly longer route, and the starlight followed obediently like a cart on rails.

By flowing down the trail it had created, the starlight drew subtle patterns of power, circulating its aura through Gary’s entire body, not just his blood vessels. He could sense how his flesh, washed in the starry aura, grew denser and stronger. How his bones toughened and his muscles coiled deeper.

Suddenly, Gary saw faint starlight rising from his body, similar to that falling from the night sky, and as he raised his head to look at himself, he saw a constellation carved on his body, made up of five great stars and eleven smaller ones. Each of those stars was an energy node glowing through his veins, and the lines connecting them were the patterns of astral aura as it traveled across his body.

A few more people had noticed, too, and they stared in amazement.

“So pretty…” Goldie said.

Over the next few minutes, the glow faded, until Gary’s body was normal again. But the power had not faded. It was everywhere inside him, spreading like starlight.

He opened his stat screen. His eyes widened. “Holy shit!”

Strength: 76

Speed: 96

Vitality: 136

His Speed was almost one hundred. He tried to remember what he had before and realized that just completing the first stage had given him twenty points in EACH attribute.

“That’s so fucking much,” he muttered. “How is this a waste of time? I’ll kick the ass of every cultivator at my level, and all it took was one chunk of Star-Forged Steel. Is body cultivation secretly broken?”

He stayed on the grass, reeling for some time.

“I guess it was expensive. Gathering two thousand merit points would be almost impossible at Birth of Aura, and twenty points in each stat aren’t THAT much to a Core Formation cultivator…but it’s still a lot! Wow. Going by the twenty-to-one ratio of aura stones to merit points the coach gave me, doesn’t this mean that someone could buy this with just forty thousand aura stones? It is a fortune, but if your clan is rich…”

“What are you muttering there?” Goldie asked.

“This body cultivation thing is so powerful it’s unfair. It gave me the stat equivalent of seven levels in Core Formation.”

“Wooow. Why doesn’t everyone do it, then?”

“That’s exactly what I was wondering.”

“Maybe it secretly sucks?”

“There is no way, Goldie. There has to be a reason, but it’s not that.”

“I guess we’ll figure it out,” she said, zooming into the spear.

What should I do next? Gary wondered. Suddenly, however, he was assaulted by a heavy, irresistible sleepiness. He fell right there on the grass.

What are the rest of the floors like? he wondered, looking up. Ninety-six to go…

He’d only completed the first and easiest stage of the Starvein Immortal Body Manual, and it already gave him such power…
