The other lords are uneasy about my suggestion. I’m not surprised – the ability of Titanbends to Bind even humans is, after all, the most famous aspect of our Class. Whether they even knew about Tame is in question; they certainly haven’t heard of Alliance.

Valence is the first to agree, something that pleases me. I figured he’d probably be the last person to volunteer – he’s already experienced what a Bond with me is like. Or perhaps that’s why – he knows the worst of it, and remembers that I released him even when I had every right to hold him.

When I extend the Alliance Bond to Valence, officially declaring our intentions to cooperate and our need for easy communication to do so, he accepts it with a wariness that’s obvious on his face. It’s even more obvious through the Bond as it settles into place and before he realises how to control what he’s transmitting down it. But, as he feels the differences between this and Dominate, I see his body language shift into relief.

“It’s light,” he assures the others. “No chain, just…a connection.”

“You don’t feel any need to obey?” Elarion asks, sounding quietly anxious. I flick a glance his way. His face is blank but I spot the way his fingers are nervously rubbing the material of his trousers. I suspect that a Bond with a Titanbend features in his nightmares. I’m rather impressed he hasn’t run screaming from the room yet.

“I do not,” Valence assures him. “And believe me, I know well what obligation feels like.” He meets Elarion and I notice a movement of sympathy pass between them. I wonder idly if there will be a conversation in their future, Elarion finding out what exactly his future holds – according to his expectations, anyway. I’m still very much uncertain about whether I will follow through on the Titanbend tradition simply because there is one generation to go on the sentence.

“What are the effects of it?” Josiah asks curiously, breaking the moment.

“As I said, connection,” Valence replies, glancing over at the lord. “I suppose…a faint sense of loyalty. But it does not feel forced – merely that I have chosen to ally with him, and so I am inclined to uphold my vow.” he closes his eyes and a thoughtful expression creases his brows as I sense him touch the Bond. His grasp is clumsy, like he is brushing against it open-handed rather than exploring with his fingertips. “I can tell where Lord Markus is, and I sense I can reach out to him. I do not know if there are any limits on this.”

“Intention and distance,” I answer his implied question. Everyone turns to look at me, several with confused expressions. “Intention – your desire to be reachable or not. Communication through this Bond requires both participants to be willing to receive from the other. Distance – the further you are from me, the more difficult and vague the communication.” Then I hesitate. “I am uncertain about how it will work if there are several of us in the Alliance and you wish to converse with another without connecting to me.”

“We would be able to converse without your awareness?” Elarion asks, faint surprise colouring his voice.

I shrug.

“I assume so. I haven’t tested it.” Although I enabled the Alliance Bond between Raven and Kalanthia in the other world, they never had much interest in speaking to each other when they were not nearby. So I don’t know if they would have been able to speak to each other through the Bond without me being aware. But I would guess it’s possible – it is a Tame-derivative, after all.

“I am willing to join,” Layton announces suddenly. Josiah sends him a quick look.

“Layton, are you sure?” he questions, not quite quietly enough to go unheard by others.

The black-haired and pale-skinned Mage nods decisively, then casts a glance around the others.

“Lord Markus makes good points. The last two battles have been all-too clear: if we wish to make it to the end of the competition – or even to the Lost Continent in the first place – we must work together. If this will help us do so better, then I am in favour.” Then he looks towards me and meets my gaze with a steady, cool one of his own. “Given how Lord Markus dealt with Heir Torrent, how he did not even attempt to take what the King might have given him, I trust his respect for Moriaxar laws in this matter.”

It’s as much a pointed reminder as it is a statement of trust. I see a thoughtful expression bloom on Josiah’s face and uncertainty creeps into Elarion’s. Sor’az still has his lips set in a thin line, his arms crossed. Garous, of course, is looking down – since I have a Dominate Bond with him, it’s semi-irrelevant for him. Anyway, I suspect that it would be pushing too much to ask for a slave to be accepted in an Alliance with lords, Bond or not.

Layton steps forwards and I extend the same Alliance to him as I did to Valence – that way everyone will be in the same Bond.

He accepts and then rolls his shoulders a couple of times, a considering expression pulling at his lips and eyebrows.

“I can certainly feel something settle deep within, though it is faint and unrestrictive as you indicated,” he comments to Valence. “I feel the pull towards loyalty that you mentioned – to Markus, but also to you in a small measure.”

Valence gains a faintly-startled expression, then nods his head fractionally.

“I feel what you mean, my lord.”

Layton gives him a smile.

“Layton, please. I think we are past titles, do you not?”

Valence’s lips quirk in a small but pleased smile.

“Thank you – Layton.”

We watch as they proceed to satisfy our earlier curiosity about communication – Layton and Valance are able to reach out to each other without me being aware. I’m pleased to see them becoming increasingly enthusiastic and able to experiment without me needing to be involved. I’m honestly rather relieved that they’ve taken it this well so far.

When I turn my gaze on the remaining three, Josiah only sends a brief, searching glance at Layton. I watch him take in his cousin’s excited smile and see his decision crystalise. I’m not surprised when he volunteers to be next.

After accepting the Bond and making a couple of odd expressions on his face, he nods at me and then joins the other two in testing the capabilities of the new Alliance.

Then, it is only Elarion and Sor’az left of the ship leaders.

Sor’az shakes his head.

“Ah appreciate what ye’ve done fer me an’ my crew, but we’ll not be allied te a human. No’ like tha’.” His tone holds an implacable warning and his body language is tense.

I raise an eyebrow at him.

“You think I’ll insist?” Sor’az doesn’t nod, but the increased tension and his guarded gaze is answer enough. I raise my hands, only just remembering to close them into fists and cross them at the wrist before I accidentally fall back into old habits. “I won’t. This is an offer; not a demand. The mirrors are working, though they’re not ideal. If you wish to remain with that as a communication method, so be it.”

As my gaze shifts to Elarion, I see Sor’az relax out of the corner of my eye. The Great House heir is more than a little thoughtful and his fingers have stopped rubbing at the material of his overrobe; they are now just tapping it faintly.

“The same goes for you,” I tell Elarion frankly. “I believe that having an easy way of communicating with each other will help us, but you are under no obligation to accept.”

For once, Elarion’s face is easy to read. Deep-rooted fear wars with curiosity.

“Does…does this make it easier to create any…other Bond?”

I hesitate, then look at him speculatively.

“I don’t know,” I reply honestly. “I assume you’re speaking of the…arrangement between our two families?” He doesn’t move to confirm or deny my guess, but his gaze flickers almost imperceptibly. “Tame and Dominate Bonds work quite differently. I do not believe that one makes it easier for the other to take place.” Then I feel the need to qualify my statement to be completely honest. “Though any previous Bond makes it possible for a Companion Bond to take root, but if you were ready to give that kind of commitment, you wouldn’t be concerned about any other Bond. It’s not something you need to worry about.”

Elarion is silent and unmoving for a long moment. I wait as patiently as I can, trying to push the pain and fatigue of the battle away – not easy when I don’t have other distractions helping. I figure it’s even odds whether he’ll accept. I know that Alliance won’t oblige him to do anything else, but I can understand how difficult it is for him to trust a Titanbend.

The pause extends long enough that I become convinced he wants to reject it but, for whatever reason, just doesn’t dare. I nod my head and start turning away. He doesn’t need to make the decision now.

A soft sound catches my attention and I turn my head back in Elarion’s direction, while my shoulders remained half-turned away.

Fear still obvious in his eyes, I see him swallow. He nods his head fractionally, bracing himself as if to take a blow.

“Very well. I will join your Alliance.”

Elarion rolls his shoulders as Markus turns away to pull the others back into the conversation. The Bond is not heavy; Heatwave and Fell are right about that. However, it irritates him like a badly-fitting tunic. Elarion is self-aware enough to know that it is more to do with his own perceptions than anything inherent in the Bond.

What would my father think? he wonders to himself, a note of hysteria to his mental voice. Willingly volunteering to take a Bond from a Titanbend!

He breathes in and out slowly, passing it through the ice sapling within his being to centre itself. The breath is pulled downwards – through its branches to its trunk, from its trunk to its roots, and then out into him again. Its icy cold spreads through his veins, leeching away the heat of passion, the fire of burning resentment. I need to increase my meditation time, he decides. This is getting out of hand.

Not that that is surprising – when his most negative emotions have so little outlet. Markus is just so…reasonable. Elarion never realised that such a thing would irritate him so much. He had accepted his father’s command to befriend and ally with Heir Titanbend in order to improve his own future prospects, but he’d expected it to be a trial.

In many ways it is, but mostly because the young heir is so utterly incomprehensible at times. He gives up advantages left, right, and centre; he acts in ways that are so diametrically opposed to expectations of Moriaxar nobles. Elarion is starting to understand that what he saw at the palace was Markus trying to fit in.

Elarion is surprised at how much resentment Markus’ behaviour elicits in him. He acts like he’s free of chains, like he can behave the way he wants with no consequences. Elarion, who has known all his life that, for all he was born to one of the highest seats in their land, he is constrained by his grandfather’s poor decisions, finds that he is desperately envious of that freedom – and resentful that he will lose what little liberty he has to one who doesn't even seem to comprehend what he possesses.

And then Markus does things like being understanding, and fighting for justice, and not even executing people out of hand who had attempted to kill him despite having every right to do so, and Elarion feels completely wrong-footed.

He swallows again, pressing more magic into the tree of ice in his chest and feeling its coolness radiate out more strongly. His tumultuous emotions smooth and bring him a sense of peace and indifference.

He has made the choice to trust Markus with this much. Time will tell if it is betrayed.
