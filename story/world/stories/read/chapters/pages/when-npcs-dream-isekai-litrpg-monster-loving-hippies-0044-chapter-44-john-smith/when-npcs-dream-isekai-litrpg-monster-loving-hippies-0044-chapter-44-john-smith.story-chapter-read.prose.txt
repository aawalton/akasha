Lori stepped out of the back room wearing a short black dress—tight, cut high and low in all the right (and wrong) places, hugging every curve, showing a lot of skin.

I went red instantly.

“That’s… a very pretty dress, Lori,” I stammered. “Jesus! How did you even—never mind. You look beautiful.”

Lori usually wore plain work clothes — a rough linen blouse, rolled sleeves, sturdy skirt or trousers. I never imagined her in something like this.

Okay, that’s not true. I obviously imagined it. but seeing it for real was… something else.

She smiled, spinning halfway to show it off.

The dress was beautiful. Lori was beautiful.

I knew something was suspicious here — wearing that dress was the opposite of something Lori would normally do. But seeing how happy she was, I figured it wasn’t that important.

Lori then stepped closer—so close I blushed again.

“I’ll trade you a cup of tea from the glade’s herbs,” she said, “if you trade me your tales.”

She wanted to hear about my many adventures.

“I’d love to,” I said. “Actually, I couldn’t wait to tell you.”

She went and brewed the tea, then curled up on the couch.

I sat beside her.

And started talking excitedly about everything.
