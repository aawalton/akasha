Xavier lay on his back staring up at the slab-stone ceiling, blood leaking from his chest and too many of his bones broken to bother counting.

A few feet away, Palini lay on the same floor.

Arrack stood at Xavier’s feet, that same look of shock frozen on his face.

Xavier’s breathing was slow and laboured. That spear had done a lot of damage, damage that hadn’t started healing yet. He may have dislodged himself from Arrack’s spear, trapping the A Grade outside the time dilation field, but the Locked Down spell was still functioning.

And worse, Xavier had expended almost every drop of Soul Energy he had in his reserve, causing him to suffer from a bout of soul sickness that was just as, if not more crippling than the Locked Down spell.

So he didn’t know when his enhanced healing would kick in, or even if it would.

It would take months—maybe a year, with how strong his soul had become—to recover from this level of soul sickness.

And yet the prevailing feeling Xavier had was one of relief.

I got off the spear.

A slow smile appeared on his face. Then he coughed and choked on some blood, and the reality of the situation fell down on him as though the ceiling itself had collapsed.

I got off the spear, but Palini’s still dead, my cores and energy channels are not only Locked Down, but damaged by the soul sickness. I have a literally wounded heart, and I doubt I can even stand.

For the first time in what felt like an age, Xavier checked his health.

Except he couldn’t.

Locked Down. It suppresses System notifications.

Arrack had been able to send him the notification that proved Palini’s death, but as the spell’s caster he must have suspended that particular status effect.

Xavier didn’t need the System to tell him how bad the situation was. As he lay there, his life slowing ebbing away, he knew he was at below 50 percent health and dropping.

He didn’t try to move. Not yet. He did what Palini—and later Palini’s ghost—had implored him to do and thought, hard, on the problem.

He tried to tap into his new ability, the Immortal Soul lens. The energy in the room flickered into existence, then fell away just as fast. Whatever mental state he’d managed to achieve when he’d allowed himself to feel Palini’s death and the agony of Arrack’s torture was something he wasn’t able to access in that moment.

And he feared that if he let in that grief and the guilt heaped on top of it, along with the emotions surrounding his own probable death, then he’d never climb back up off that cold stone floor.

The impact of what he’d been able to do—controlling raw Celestial Energy outside his body and even disabling an A Grade’s spell with pure energy manipulation alone—wasn’t lost on him. But that it had taken just about his entire energy reserve the disable one A Grade spell…

That’s a massive cost.

He catalogued everything available to him: the time dilation field was active, theoretically giving him as much time as he needed to figure out a solution to this mess; he was confident he could achieve the same mental state that enabled him to use the Immortal Soul lens again if needed—

Xavier tried to think of a third thing but kept coming up short.

Even if I used the Immortal Soul lens, actually controlling the Celestial Energy relies on Soul Energy. So how the hell do I take advantage of that with a depleted reserve?

In his current state, he barely had the ability to discern how much Soul Energy he had left, and he wondered if the Locked Down effect and the soul sickness would severely limit his Soul Energy recovery in the first place.

What felt like another percentage point of health bled into the stones.

Xavier released a laboured sigh, and god it hurt his chest to let that out.

No more feeling sorry for myself.

He tried to move his body. The pain was immense. His initial wounds had mostly healed when Xavier had interrupted Arrack’s first Locked Down spell, but the torture that had followed after his Void Energy gambit had failed had undone that healing and then some.

Flexing the fingers of his right hand, he noticed something… unusual.

As Arrack had raged at him following Palini’s death, he’d stomped down on both Xavier’s hands, and yet his right hand was severely less damaged then his left.

Of course. It’s stronger…

Finally, the wheels in his damaged mind started turning again.

The hand wasn’t just less harmed but actively healing despite the Locked Down spell and the damaged channels. All the refined Celestial Energy he’d packed into that area had allowed him to wield the power of two galaxies—even if he’d lost his hand in the process, that refinement hadn’t simply disappeared.

It had grown back, at the exact same strength as before.

A permanent upgrade.

He reached for his leg and gripped his pants with his good hand, pulling with all his remaining strength to put him into an awkward, sitting position. A hiss escaped his teeth as all his wounds flared anew. His broken wings limply spread out behind him.

Xavier stared at Palini’s corpse, then forced his eyes shut and turned his head away.

When he opened his eyes again he studied his hand, flexing the fingers, curling them into a tight fist—a fist he imagined wrapped around Arrack’s throat.

How can I use this?

He drew in a ragged breath. As he let it out, he looked back at that corpse, at Arrack’s frozen face, and allowed himself to feel again.

The Immortal Soul lens flickered on and off.

Xavier breathed as all that emotion and pain hit him, making him want to act. He pushed back against his instincts and forced himself to sit with what he felt, then expanded his awareness around it, rather than compartmentalising the emotions and locking them deeply away.

The Immortal Soul lens steadied. The energy in the room, and the energy within his body, his Denizen cores, his Reality Core, and his Soul Core, became exceptionally clear. Clearer than it had been even with his spiritual sense backed by the power of two galaxies.

This is power. Being able to see... everything. And the ability was with me all along. Not just with me—with everyone.

Xavier narrowed his focus in on his body. The first thing he saw was what amounted to his life energy leaking out from his chest. He pushed past that and examined his reinforced hand, and all the bonded Celestial Energy that had gone into it.

Then he turned his attention toward his soul reserve.

It wasn’t completely depleted. A scattering of points remained. He hovered the full perception of his Immortal Soul lens on his soul reserve and the bleeding wound. He lingered on these two places, sitting with all that emotion and all that pain.

And he waited.

His mind conjured up sounds that weren’t really there. His life energy was like a trickling stream on the stone floor. His soul reserve was silent.

Trickle, trickle, trickle...

And then, after a frightfully long moment, in the silence of his soul there was a single drip.

It wasn’t much. For that single drip in his soul reserve, he must have bled through 5 percent of his dwindling health.

Still, it might just be enough.

Problem was, he wouldn’t know until he tried. And if it wasn’t enough…

It will deplete the rest of my soul reserve. My spirit will no longer be able to cling to my body. It’ll drift away.

His wound would continue bleeding and his body would die, his corpse merely a few feet from Palini’s.

I should’ve summoned the Spirit of Time, but it’s too late for that now.

Xavier liked to think what he did next was a choice but given his options... it was either admit defeat or do down swinging.

He’d been through that before enough times, and he would always choose to fight—only this time it felt different.

He reached out and touched Palini, then he closed his eyes and prepared himself, focusing once more on his reinforced hand. Though he was still new to using this ability, his instincts told him it would be easier to control the energy already within his body than it would be to control the energy without. Even if he could manipulate the raw Celestial Energy around him, he couldn’t manipulate it enough for what he needed.

So he pulled at the refined Celestial Energy that he’d bound to his molecules and strengthened his hand. To do this he first separated the energy, drilling deep as he’d done against the A Grade’s spell. As he worked, he paid close attention to his soul reserve.

It dipped.

What little of it remained halved.

He’d only separated a small amount of refined Celestial Energy.

What will it cost to do what comes next?

He could wait until another drip entered his soul, but that would only allow his chest wound to bring him closer to death.

Xavier pushed the refined Celestial Energy he’d separated through his body. He couldn’t use his channels. Not in the state they were in. But his Living Soul allowed him to move the energy through his flesh.

It moved to where he needed it—to the wound slowly killing him.

Xavier bound a single point of refined Celestial Energy to a molecule at the worst part of the wound. The movement in his soul reserve was so minute he almost didn’t feel it—not even a whole point was lost.

So he kept going. Binding more and more of the refined Celestial Energy to the affected areas, strengthening them as much as he could, separating more points from his hand as needed.

Bit by bit, the trickle of life energy leaving him slowed.

Every now and then another drip fell into his soul.

He didn’t know how much time passed. All he could do was compare the trickle to the drip.

Until finally the two reversed—the trickle became the drip and vice versa.

Then, after what could have been hours or days, the wound stopped bleeding life energy entirely.

Xavier let out the longest sigh of relief he ever had.

Still alive.

He smiled, laughed—then he looked over at Palini’s corpse and the smile slipped away. He thought of the man’s wife, his children, their children, and so on down the line...

And if not for me coming into his life, he’d still be alive.

A thousand different scenarios of how this failed rescue could have played out differently ran through his mind.

Why hadn’t he summoned the Spirit of Time? Why had he come alone? Why had he insisted on trying to free everyone?

Xavier put his head in his hands.

This was his fault. He’d sent Palini back to the Orin sector to find the late Sovereign Rewke Fouran’s soul. Not only that, Xavier had sent what amounted to a challenge to all the old monsters and powerhouses of the inner sectors...

And this Arrack hadn’t even been from an inner sector.

If Xavier had been buoyed by the power of his territory, this would have been a different story. Arrack would be the one lying dead on the floor.

But he’d been alone.

He’d fallen right into the A Grade’s trap.

Trap...

Why had the man needed to set a trap for him in the first place, if he was so much more powerful than Xavier?

Because he knew about my time dilation field. I first met him in the Social Rooms, but he didn’t attack until the Memory Room. Until he had an A Grade mage on his side. And when I broke free of his Locked Down spell, he instantly lurched forward to grab me, worried I’d cast Time Alteration.

The man feared me. I felt it, even if only briefly.

A dark smile slipped onto Xavier’s lips. It was too late to save his friend—his spirit had already fled the mortal realm—but Xavier would make the man responsible pay dearly for what he’d done.

I’m going to find out who you really are. I’m going to find out if you’re working alone or carrying out orders. I’m going to find out everything you know.

Then I’m going to kill you.

All he needed to do first was dismantle the Locked Down spell, and with his Immortal Soul lens he could see the spell’s energy quite clearly.
