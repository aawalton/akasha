“What do you say we get some rest?” Adam said gently.

The swordsman and the mage both agreed.

Kennedy called up three rides—two to send their teammates to their respective hotels, and one for her and Adam. The elevator ride down, through a glass box, visually traveling from the top of the city to street level, was also in silence.

The healer’s limousine left first. By the glance he gave her, he seemed slightly amused. “We won 5-0,” he said. “Lighten up, guys. Training hasn’t even properly started yet. See you tomorrow.”

Their own limousine arrived. “Ms. Devereaux. Mr. Devereaux,” said the driver.

“Luke seems like he’ll be okay?” she said. She glanced at Adam—her twin brother was always better at reading people than she was—what they were feeling, at least.

What the swordsman had just seen could easily have been soul-crushing, especially in his mental state. She wasn’t certain the swordsman wasn’t crushed; she couldn’t tell what he was thinking.

“We’ll see how he reacts,” Adam said eventually. He seemed contemplative. “We’ll have a better idea in the next few days.”

Another few seconds, as the skyscrapers whipped by.

“Safe assumption we just met a member of the new batch,” Adam said casually.

The new batch of Outworlders who’d arrived on Earth, due to the spatial distortions that the new Towers had created.

There still wasn’t an incredible number of Outworlders, between than ten and twenty thousand worldwide.

They were already being absorbed into institutions, like Guilds or Professions’ workshops, at a startling pace. She knew of several new Templar recruits, in her class, who were Outworlders—she’d seen them in their class’s social media groups.

She glanced at Adam. “Do you think…?”

“I’ve never seen that level of Pure Mana Manipulation before, at Level 2,” she said, recalling it. “And his processing speed—”

A few seconds.

“We could also be washed,” said Adam, grinning. “Washed at Level 3?”

He was joking about it, which made her think the encounter didn’t give him the slightest bit of imposter syndrome.

She hadn’t truly been worried about Adam. Genius was a word that’d also been applied to him. She didn’t feel flustered by it either, for similar reasons. She was intrigued about the implications.

“He can’t be the only genius in the new batch,” she said, glancing at him.

“He isn’t,” Adam agreed. “The next few months will be pretty interesting.”

His eyes glinted as he said it.

The limousine rolled to a stop in front of one of the pencil-thin skyscrapers for the ultra-rich. The doorman greeted them, and they took the elevator up to the penthouse suite. Adam went up first; it dropped her off a floor below, at their private bar.

She ordered a drink—not a treasure yet, but one suitably strong for her physiology—and gazed into the distance, across Central Park, toward the Hudson river and the ocean beyond.

It felt like the past few hours had complicated things tremendously.

These days, it felt like the world—her world, and her teammates’ worlds—was moving very fast.

The world’s Class Level cap was rising above 130. Part of that was due to average rift levels rising globally at the top end, at an accelerating pace—but that also meant more and more Awakening stones were discovered; it was the quantity of Awakening Stones, not the quantity of talent, that was the bottleneck. Every day, more and more talent joined this invisible, global race to the top. You could see it on social media, with all new talents’ accounts she saw popping up every week, and in the sheer talent in the international dueling scene these days.

It was having strange effects. She recalled that certain instance items were becoming stunningly expensive, because of the sheer global demand for excellent items to equip the burgeoning wave of rising talent.

The level was rising worldwide.

The Templar development program was a microcosm of the reality of the world. It only prepared them for reality.

She accepted the necessity of it, but the fact that it was so all-encompassing was precisely why she’d tried to shield them for at least this week. A few hours, at least.

She felt foolish.

She sat there silently. She texted a few friends and glanced out over the city, following individual people walking about, living normal, unAwakened lives, blissfully unaware of it all; even as they walked under billboards plastered with Tomoyuki Ishida’s grinning face, advertising his upcoming duel at Madison Square Garden.

She wasn’t truly focusing on all that. Her mind started replaying the duel, which wasn’t by itself special. Her mind often started doing it, especially in duels she lost.

She hadn’t lost this one, but some part of her felt like it. She’d genuinely been a few seconds from drowning.

She spent a few minutes thinking. Then she opened Instagram, went to DM’s, opened a particular group chat with a few Awakened friends she considered her peers.
