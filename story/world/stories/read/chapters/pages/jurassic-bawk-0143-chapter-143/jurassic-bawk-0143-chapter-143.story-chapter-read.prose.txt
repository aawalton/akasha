Charlemagne and Phasima set off for the spot where the Lithari Knight had gone overboard.  The rooster had to limit his speed to allow for the Vessarian to keep up but was all right, since it gave him time to reach beneath the water’s surface with his mana senses and probe the ocean depths.

There were an awful lot of big creatures down there.

Ordinarily, the rooster wouldn’t have minded fighting giant monsters.  The bigger they were, the bigger the gains in his opinion.  And, of course, the larger the monster, the more meat its carcass provided for him to gorge on.  But the water changed that equation.  Perhaps, working together, he and Phasima could have boiled a small part of the ocean, at least enough of it to disable or kill one of the submarine behemoths, but he was too fixated on finding the rocky creature’s Core and consuming it.

As the rooster scanned the ocean floor, homing in on the delicious morsel that had gone overboard, he sensed a mana signature that he was sure he recognized.  But before he could get a good read on it, it had disappeared again.  What was worse: the slowly fading signal from the Core disappeared at that exact moment.

He dove in after it.  Water assaulted his flames, but the sheer heat from his body flash-boiled the area around him, allowing him to fall through the ocean without getting wet due to the confusingly named Leidenfrost effect.  But the ocean waters were relentless, and they eventually absorbed enough heat from the rooster’s body that the water beneath him did not instantly turn into steam.

The rooster’s momentum slowed considerably as he encountered water resistance for the first time during his dive.  He immediately began to flap his wings in terror, causing him to catapult toward the surface.  He couldn’t breathe.  Adrenaline rushed through his body, adding to his incredible strength and causing each stroke of his wings to send him further.  But his panic was a double-edged sword.  He was moving faster, yes, but the increased energy demands of his body both decreased his oxygen reserves and increased carbon dioxide buildup.  A tension that Charlemagne had seldom felt before mounted inside his chest: oxygen deprivation.

His Fop’s Raiment, sensing the issue, began to transform.  The rooster, in his panicked state, didn’t notice.

Lungs burning, Charlemagne finally burst through the ocean’s surface.  The plume of water that followed him sprayed Phasima, who did not appreciate the sudden deluge.

“Hey, watch out there, you crazy bird!” she yelled.

Charlemagne ignored her and returned to his ship to give the captain his orders.  He might not have secured the Core, but the other Knight’s vessel surely held a number of comestibles.

To the group’s profound disappointment, the ship did not upgrade again upon absorbing its third victim.

“It probably works on some sort of doubling system,” Josephine surmised.  Phatagin and Phasima nodded.  Charlemagne wanted to know if that meant they just needed one more ship, but he did not want to reveal his ignorance.

Even worse, when the other Knight’s ship was absorbed into his own, Charlemagne found that the provisions were absorbed as well.  The poor captain was forced to bear the full brunt of his anger, but the rooster managed to restrain himself from physically attacking the erstwhile commander.

The next few days passed quickly.  Charlemagne continued to scout by air, finding islands to plunder and ships to de-Knight and absorb.  Three more ships were added to his own.  Josephine’s guess was proven correct as the first of the three integrations triggered a further upgrade to Charlemagne’s ship, causing the vessel to transform into a triple-masted galleon with a top speed that greatly exceeded its Earthly inspiration.

The absorption process took up much of the crew’s effort.  Not because of the actual integration, that was handled by the System itself and only took a few moments.  The issue was that Charlemagne forced the perpetually on-duty sailors to transfer all the cargo from the enemy’s ship to his before he allowed the captured vessels to get close enough to trigger the absorption process.

That wasn’t to say that the gang was idle while the provisions were being transferred.  Charlemagne used this time to locate and ransack the local islands, which usually had a sort of Boss monster guarding a treasure chest.  The chests tended to contain consumable items that were useful to various members of the group.

For example, Charlemagne found and consumed a yellow tuber that the Squiggles claimed would increase his ability to manipulate mana.  While it did work, he didn’t think that it had helped all that much.  He also found a dark piece of flinty rock that claimed it would make his body more resistant to fire.  His eating Special Ability practically took control of his mouth as it begged him to ingest the rock.  He obliged it.

The rooster didn’t hog all the rewards, however.  Phatagin received a large, round fruit that was surprisingly springy when touched.  The Squiggles told Charlemagne that it would make his Squire better suited to handle sudden changes in momentum.  The rooster was pleased with his choice to pass on that particular treasure: it smelled disgusting when the pangolin cut the fruit into long, thin strips in order to consume it.

The ladies of the group profited as well.  Phasima received two treasures that were well suited for her.  The first was another of the yellow tubers that increased mana manipulation abilities.  The second one, however, was more unique.  It was an Item that allowed her to temporarily reduce her affinity with one or more elements in order to gain affinity with another.  In other words, she could do something like lower her affinities to earth, fire, and water to gain a massive boost to her ability to control air.  Even Josephine, who finally recovered from motion sickness after days of misery, received a tiny, brain-shaped lozenge that would aid her in her duties as an Attendant by increasing her ability to multi-task and remember small details.

Almost a week passed as the crew raided everything in an ever-widening radius, with nothing really new happening.  Aside from the occasional jaunt to explore an island that was large enough to actually stretch their legs, the group performed their assigned roles: Charlemagne explored, Phatagin pulled the ship, and the ladies managed the pirate crew as best they could.

That all changed when Charlemagne found something different.

The rooster had spotted an enormous rectangular vessel parked next to a small island.  There was a gangway set at almost a forty-five-degree angle that led from the island up to a small hatch that appeared to be the only way on or off the gigantic ship.  The area surrounding the island was teeming with almost a dozen identical ships sporting metal tubes sticking out their sides and matching black sails emblazoned with a human skull and two femurs.  Excited by his find, Charlemagne dove to attack.  He promptly slammed into a mana shield that he had sensed too late to avoid.

After searching the entire area for a spot that wasn’t covered by the shield and finding none, the rooster turned to more forceful methods.  His claws and beak did nothing to the powerful defensive barrier, but he hadn’t expected to be able to tear his way through a barrier that had been created by the Squiggles.  Yet.

Instead of brute force, the rooster tried to use Fire Flash to slip through the barrier, but he only managed to injure himself in the process.  After three consecutive tries, his fire was burning so hot that anyone observing him would have had difficulty seeing through the shimmering haze of pure heat that was over a meter thick.  He tried a fourth time and ended up losing his left wing entirely.  A close call with the surface of the ocean dissuaded him from further attempts at finesse.

Since he was unable to bypass the mana shield, Charlemagne resorted to force.  He dumped about half of his Ignition Core’s contents into a massive ball of blazing mana and sent it crashing into the shield.  When the flames died down, the barrier wasn’t even scuffed.

The rooster decided that it was time to let the brain trust go to work, since he was both out of options and ideas.  He returned to the ship and ordered the captain to set a new course.  By this time, the sun was about to set, and the rooster had a nice meal as he explained what he had found to the group.  He even let Phatagin have the rest of the day off so that the pangolin could listen in.  Charlemagne quickly explained the dilemma as the sun sank below the horizon.  Then he hustled off to his favorite nook to spend the night.

Early the next morning, the rooster emerged from below decks to find that the Ark was dead ahead, with the ship sailing straight for it.

“Bawk!” he yelled to the others in alarm, worried about his precious foodstuffs.

“Don’t worry,” Phasima answered.  “We are heading in nice and slow so that we don’t ram into the barrier protecting whatever that thing is.

“I do believe that this vessel is one of the ‘Arks’ that the System message mentioned,” Phatagin added.

“Whatever it is, it’s both huge and well-defended,” Josephine said, pointing to the dozen patrolling vessels that occupied the space within the mana shield.

“Bawk?” Charlemagne inquired.

“Hmmm, that is a good question, a good question indeed,” the pangolin agreed.  “I believe that our ship’s presence will affect the shield, since most of the Knights would be totally unable to approach the island by air like Charlemagne has.”

“I suppose that is the most plausible answer,” Josephine agreed.  “The System didn’t say anything about needing something special to get into the Ark.  It said that the Arks held keys that would allow entrance to ‘Bluebeard’s Chamber’.  So I am guessing that once we get close enough, we will either have to fight, negotiate, or pay tribute in order to access the vessel.  I am curious, however.  Phatagin, when you read me the System message about this Floor, you said that these Arks belonged to a ‘King Arthur’.  Who was he?”

The pangolin thought for a long moment before shrugging.

“I have never heard the name before.  It does sound like he is long dead, however, if his weapons have become ‘legendary’.”

“Bawk,” Charlemagne suggested.

“No, I believe that it is quite unlikely that this ‘King Arthur’ operated a bakery.  It is more likely that he was a famous warrior.”

“Bawk?”

“Because inside each Ark is a legendary weapon,” Josephine answered.  “Although, to be fair, the description only said that the Arks belonged to Arthur, not the weapons.”

The other three group members gave the Attendant an odd look.

“What?  I had a good memory, and it’s even better now,” she shot back in defense.

“If this Arthur was a King, then who was Bluebeard,” Phasima wondered.  “Perhaps he had a strong ice affinity.  That would explain the hair.”

“Could you please help me understand what the difference is between a water affinity and an ice affinity,” Phatagin asked.

Phasima was about to respond when the ship suddenly shuddered and began to accelerate.

“Something’s happening!” Josephine yelled.  “We’re…urp..oh no!”

While the Attendant ran off to a more secluded portion of the ship to find a suitable railing, the other three watched as the ship suddenly ceased its up-and-down motion through the waves and began to move even faster.  Charlemagne looked over the ship’s railing and found that the water had grown completely still.  As he processed that development, the mana shield that surrounded the island winked out of existence without a trace, causing the rooster to feel relief.  At the speed they were going, ramming into the barrier could have caused the entire ship to break apart.

Seeing that they were nearing the closest of the patrolling vessels surrounding the Ark, Charlemagne crouched and pushed off the deck in order to take to the skies.  Nothing happened.  He tried again, but his claws were firmly planted to the deck.  He took a step, and then another.  Then he tried once more to launch himself.  He went absolutely nowhere.

Before the rooster had a chance to explain this newfound limitation to the others, the Squiggles arrived with a new message.

Anticipation filled the rooster as he chose the only real option.  Charlemagne was growing sick of the ocean.  It was time to blow off some steam.
