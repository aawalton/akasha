Chapter 60

First Contact

Many groups in various countries had begun training, but the deployment plan had been considered from many different angles.

It had taken weeks of training and planning for different battle stages, but the hour of truth had arrived. And the training help came in a way no one expected.

Wishper’s leaders, headed by Jinat, met with officials from several countries to rapidly develop a training program for mixed teams—integrating humans with individuals from the hidden world. They would be the vanguard, opening the way for broader global integration within a month, while humanity’s armies did what they could in the meantime.

Communication had dusted off analog media now that the satellite network was a disaster, but at least the radios and laser would do their job while attempts were made to launch new satellites to at least facilitate communications. Some said that the rumors were that all the people in charge of calculating the orbital trajectory to avoid the cloud of debris in orbit had gone bald in a matter of weeks from doing calculations, creating models, and running simulations, and had given up, leaving the AI to handle it.

Meanwhile the talks had taken place just days after the invasion began, bringing together the big heads of the Tripartite Council, leaders of various nations and corporations, and the chiefs of different organizations from the hidden world.

Nevermore would become the public face of this initiative if it succeeded. After all, they had been presenting themselves for decades as an organization that researched the paranormal and studied Dark Events.

While all this was happening, Mai and many of her friends were in training, focusing on what they could do and leaving the big decisions to those in charge. Mai just hoped it wouldn't end with everyone dying on the first few tries.

The trial period had begun, and Mai found herself integrated into a mixed team of soldiers and espers—the unit that would receive its first major mission.

That morning, as Mai inspected her gear, her mind replayed the chain of events that had led her there. She had only met her teammates few days before, because the squad she had been assigned to  had trained with other feys for a week as a test, and it had worked. It was a forty-member unit with several fey members spread across various teams, Mai didn't know any of them. Now it remained to be seen whether sudden changes in membership could be good or bad. She, for her part, had trained on Siren Island in simulations of the new enemy, but this would be her electric baptism into the new war.

Since she was part moth, she hoped it wouldn't end with her getting fried like a bug in front of a lamp for insects.

She knew this operation was a kind of rehearsal—another attempt to see how humans and beings from the hidden world could adapt to working together effectively. The tension in the air was almost tangible among everyone. No one knew what would happen, but everyone understood this wasn’t a normal mission. Other groups around the world were already conducting test missions for squads to eliminate small groups of Fractus or to gather intelligence on larger groups, or the movements of certain groups in packs.

After the introductions, a bit of conversation, and a few but harsh days of training, it was time to see if she could finally join the rotating group of feys in combat.

They also would conduct a test mission in which they would test new weapons against three moving targets.

Their assignment took them to a remote region in Croatia, to an abandoned mountain small city. Once a small industrial settlement, it had become a desolate battleground after the arrival of the fractus through a spherical portal that had opened for few hours. Rumors said that a group of them had taken the area—and not just any group. Some of the same kind had been spotted in Sudan, and they had just given it a name. They were known as “ice basilisks,” those were capable of freezing their victims in seconds, plunging them into an absolute cold far beyond what any living being could survive. The challenge wasn’t only facing them… but finding a way to stop them without being frozen themselves.

They arrived in two ships, which quickly set up at the base camp that had already been established. It wasn’t as if they were going to be there for very long if the mission was successful, but at least it was good to know there was a base. After settling in, she checked the equipment she would be using, just like everyone else. Mai adjusted her weapon, feeling the weight of the electric arc in her hands. It wasn’t perfect—not yet.

The first prototypes of electric-arc weapons weren’t meeting the expectations for dynamic field deployment. While military engineers radiated optimism, the truth was that the coil output wasn’t nearly enough to deal with something as monstrous as ice basilisks or other big fractus. All the weapons currently being developed were being made with a modular structure so that they could be quickly dismantled for repairs, but it was still a bit complicated to learn the full functionality of the parts, in order to know what could fail and needed to be replaced. Although she was used to weapons—and even to using some magical ones—this was the first time she had ever used something like that. They were bulky and much heavier.

The next day, the captain assigned to the team—a man whose face was carved with a very deep scar on the left cheek, named Rainer—gathered all to explain and review the mission details. That didn't take long, the squads had known their positions and tasks almost from the moment they arrived.

Mai remained silent, but alert. More than once during past operations, things had changed at the last minute, and when it came to the Fractus, it was better not to let your guard down.

Observation drones had been tracking the targets for forty-eight hours whenever they emerged from the snow, searching for any unusual patterns in their movements. The results had been strange. The basilisks seemed capable of detecting nearby aerial activity and would hide shortly afterward. Ground targets, on the other hand, were approached with little hesitation.

For that reason, air strikes had been ruled out. A railgun round could damage them, but its effectiveness dropped dramatically unless the impact landed directly on the core or detonated close to it. The problem was that nobody yet knew the exact location of the core in this particular Fractus variant, nor how many consecutive hits would be required to reach it.

The technician responsible for observation and analysis found the behavior especially curious. The drones were relatively small and did not represent an obvious threat. Perhaps the basilisks could detect electromagnetic emissions or acoustic vibrations. Another theory suggested that, in their place of origin, they might have evolved alongside some kind of aerial predator. Nobody liked that possibility. Considering that the creatures were over nine meters long, it raised an uncomfortable question: what kind of predator could frighten something the size of a bus?

Whatever the explanation, ground combat remained the best option.

Once the evacuation of the area had been completed days early, the mission was assigned with several objectives. The first was to study the group behavior of the Ice Basilisk Fractus from distance first. The second was to test the mixed team and field-test recent improvements to the new electrical weapons. The fourth, if possible, was to recover one or more cores for research.

That last objective attracted particular interest among scientists. Mai had read several reports over the previous weeks. The Fractus appeared to possess internal structures comparable to a core, but the available evidence remained limited. The basilisks observed in Sudan had simply vanished before they could be properly studied, making it impossible to determine the exact location of the structure inside that particular type.

This time, the responsibility fell on them. They would have to fight, destroy the creatures, and determine exactly where the core was located so it could be recovered and examined.

“So,” he finished. “Our objective is clear: locate the target and neutralize them. This won’t be easy, so stay alert and don't let it touch you.”

“Why do you have to talk so dirty?” one of the team leaders joked.

Rainer just smiled. “If you’re in such a good mood, I’m going to send you and your squad out on recon.”

“I’ll behave.”

“Yeah, this is probably going to kill us all,” another muttered under his breath.

The optimism here really takes the cake, Mai thought, sitting with the group in the squad she was in.

Mai and the forty-member mixed team, divided into four groups, nodded without a word, knowing deep down that they were the only ones who truly understood the potential and limitations of the weapons they carried. The unit consisted of five soldiers, two additional espers, and herself. All eyes were on the weapons, with doubts lingering about the mission’s effectiveness, though no one spoke aloud.

Back at the barracks, Mai asked one of her teammates for a favor. She’d thought it over several times, but it was for the best. She cut her hair. Although she was used to having long hair, it was the best choice since it reached her calves, and braiding it every day was a hassle every morning under those conditions. Considering that some fractus could attack on mere contact, it was better not to give them an advantage. Her teammate cut it to shoulder-blade length. She didn’t want to cut it any shorter than that. After all, the weight of her hair still helped keep her antennae hidden and prevented them from vibrating. It felt strange not to have it long. Over the years, she’d only cut it as much as necessary. She sighed as her partner showed her the new cut in the mirror.

It would grow back… if I survive this.

The next morning was clear under a sickly yellow sun when they arrived at the abandoned town, a more biting chill enveloped them. Broken concrete structures and deserted streets gave the impression of walking through a frozen tomb in the Acrtic.

Mai adjusted her helmet and visor. She had never liked projection-based visors, but by now she had been forced to get used to them. The same was true of the anti-slip boots, the exoskeleton, and all the gear she was carrying, which weighed a good 30 kilos. The anti-slip boots were a paradox because, while they had a sliding function that could be used to move faster, it was so unusual that no one preferred to use it. Those who had trained the most with them were spearsmen, who were expected to use their skills while moving across the terrain.

For several hours they remained on watch, but for some reason none of the three fractus showed themselves. Drones were deployed over a wide radius, yet there was no sign of them. Snow had fallen during the night, and the storm had forced the drone operators to pull back because of the heavy blizzard. That had created a two-hour window, during which the area had been left without surveillance, though it was assumed the creatures had remained somewhere nearby.

The team inspected the area, but there were concerns that the drones themselves might be driving the creatures away. Either that, or something similar to what had happened in Sudan. Several ground drones were sent out as well, but they found nothing. In the end, Rainer ordered the teams to begin a systematic sweep of the terrain while maintaining drone surveillance several kilometers away in case of trouble. Remaining stationary only gave the creatures more time to relocate, assuming they had not already done so.

And so the entire force advanced.

Mai was assigned to the Second Squad, which was operating as part of the vanguard. She would have preferred to be farther back, but she was hardly in a position to complain. They advanced from the eastern side of the small town, covering and inspecting nearly two hundred meters at a time as they moved deeper into the settlement.

The industrial town had once been a small mining community during the previous century, until the deposits had been exhausted. Until only a few weeks ago, however, it had served as a rare-earth refining center processing materials brought in from the south. It was hardly a tourist destination, but because of its role it could be considered a surprisingly modern town despite its isolation. There were few tall buildings, and most of the structures were private homes belonging to people who had chosen to live close to their workplace.

There was nothing of particular interest beneath the surrounding mountains. At least not from a human perspective.

What was true, however, was that deep beneath the town ran a tectonic fault that intersected with three magical ley lines connecting Bosnia, Serbia, and the Adriatic Sea.

As she advanced, Mai found herself studying the pavement. Large craters nearly four meters across scarred the streets here and there. Several houses had been destroyed, yet portions of them remained completely frozen, as though they had once been part of an iceberg that was only now beginning to thaw. The damage clearly revealed the passage of the fractus. Whenever they moved at high speed, they generated something akin to a blizzard that froze everything around them.

The damaged concrete was even stranger.

It showed that the creatures broke through the pavement in order to burrow underground. Yet the soil beneath those openings remained untouched. There were shattered chunks of concrete, damaged walls, and broken structures, but many other surfaces remained perfectly intact. In fact, footage had shown that the creatures occasionally entered the ground without breaking it at all. That seemed to happen mostly during the day, particularly when sunlight was strong.

The explanation given to them was that fractus creatures moved through more than three dimensions. When diving into the earth, they encountered a medium that offered some resistance to movement, but they might be able to shift into a state where the particles composing their bodies became slightly separated, allowing them to pass through matter. Concrete, being denser and more rigid, perhaps needed to be broken first, like cracking a shell. The same might apply to metallic or similarly solid surfaces.

Their movement above ground required a different explanation. While interacting with the atmosphere, they apparently had to maintain a more solid physical state. Yet because the air offered little resistance, they were capable of moving at terrifying speeds.

The idea that a creature like that might itself have a predator did not sit well with Mai.

If the reports were true, the comparison would be like ants, worms, and a sparrow.

And naturally, humanity would be the ants.

Eventually they reached the center of town, where a small roundabout and a children's park lay beneath the snow, reflecting the pale sunlight of late morning.

Mai continued scanning her surroundings but detected nothing. If the creatures were underground, her echolocation would be of little use.

She was still wondering about that when a white warning icon suddenly flashed across her visor, sending a chill down her spine.

A second later, the display informed her that the temperature had dropped by ten degrees almost instantly.

And suddenly, a shape emerged from the children park: a gigantic figure, its scales reflecting the dim sun light like shards of ice and breaking it down like a prism. Then, further away, other shapes rose up from the surrounding area.

“Wait a second!” someone said. “Weren’t there supposed to be three?”

“Shit!” someone else muttered. “There are four!”

A fourth, smaller Ice Basilisk had appeared behind the other three.

They looked like crystal-clear worms crawling out of the ground—or at least that was Mai’s first impression—but she couldn’t deny that they were also beautiful, that kaleidoscopic shimmer as they refracted the light sent out flashes that bathed the scene in colors reflected in the snow, and at the same time could serve as a weapon to distract and blind the soldiers’ eyes. The ice basilisks advanced with terrifying agility, leaving a trail of deadly cold in its wake. Every step seemed to sink the temperature even further.

But what truly terrified Mai was realizing that this time they had not broken through the concrete when they emerged. They had simply passed through it as though they were ghosts.

A chill ran down her spine.

She remembered her adventures with Miho and the few spectral manifestations she had witnessed over the years. But this was different.

It reminded her more of those old video games from the beginning of the century, the ones with strange glitches that allowed characters to pass straight through walls and scenery.

She did not know why, but the comparison unsettled her.

It almost made her feel like one of those NPCs that existed only to populate the background and nothing more.

And if that were true...

Was there a player? Where were they? Who were they? More importantly, how had the Fractus arrived in the first place? Had someone summoned them from somewhere beyond?

Or were they simply part of a hidden dimension that had always existed alongside their own?

“In position!” shouted Rainer, snapping Mai out of her reverie, as the soldiers took their places and the espers activated their powers.

The squad captain gave the order and Mai raised her electric arc weapon, trying to aim with precision to the one that was closer to her squad. But a sudden flash of light blinded her and the rest of her team as they scrambled to take cover and regain their vision, which was now clouded by floating yellow spots. She knew they would need a combined effort to defeat the creature. The first discharges from the other squads hit the basilisk, but the expected effect did not occur.

Although the electrical discharges seemed to cause damage, it was only superficial, the impacted area appeared to shatter like ice or glass, but in the next instant, the surface seemed to reform and the damage vanished. Who knows how many hours they would have to fight to inflict enough damage to expose the core.

The creature barely flinched, and cold in the area intensified as it moved and attacked, writhing as if it were a drill made of jelly and glass, freezing part of the team who hadn’t had time to evade.

The other groups were also locked in their own battles, and the scene was a chaotic mix of cold wind, electrical discharges, and multicolored flashes under the cold sun. Each basilisk had two teams. Only three were supposed to have been sighted, but there were four. The fourth was considerably smaller, but that also gave it the advantage of being very fast. The espers from Squadrons Seven and Eight were locked in battle with him. He was clearly drawing fire toward himself, but since there was only one in each squadron, they acted as decoys, moving across the terrain while the others tried to hit him with their shots.

“Damn it!” yelled one soldier near Mai, struggling to free his frozen arm. The squad medic did his best, cracking the ice around the soldier's arm and dragging him to cover, while the others jumped around, trying their hardest to evade the basilisk.

Mai did not lose her composure. She approached Rainer, who was watching the scene with frustration with one eye still closed.

“The weapons aren’t enough. The energy isn’t reaching the levels needed to damage it effectively,” she explained, her tone calm but urgent.

“I know,” Rainer replied, through clenched teeth. “We don’t have time to figure it out in the field. We have to modify the weapons!”

“Captain, how are we supposed to modify the weapons in the middle of a fight?”

Rainer nodded. “By removing the power limiter.”

“Even if it means risking injury to everyone?”

“This is a test mission; the margin for failure is built into the simulation. But that doesn’t mean we can’t try.”

Mai swallowed hard.

Rainer gave the order, and the team members who were with the youngest deployed a small ground drone threw the decoy in the opposite direction nearly two hundred meters away. They hadn’t planned to use it, but it would buy them a few moments to cover their retreat. They fired one of the new thermal grenades—developed years ago by the United States but never used on the battlefield—from a launcher, and it worked. When it exploded at the far end of the battlefield, it acted as a decoy for the basilisks, and they charged toward the spot.

And so, in the heart of the city, the soldiers and espers took refuge in a ruined basement, while the sky clouded. Thanks to the maps, they knew the best places to hide, and since it was an industrial city, they had chosen a hiding spot where they could get a good view of the city.

One of the military technicians, a young man named Niklas, began working quickly. Meanwhile, Mai kept her eyes on the corridors, observing now the snow falling around them.

“We’ll need more power to make the weapons work. I’ll need these Tesla-U7 coils limiter removed. If we connect them this way to the electric arcs, we can amplify the amperage when the cores are discovered to the final blow,” explained Niklas to the other couple soldiers that were helping him, assembling the devices with speed.

“That’ll be if we don’t blow ourselves up first,” someone replied.

Tesla… Mai had been hearing that name a lot in recent weeks from new religious leaders. One clever soul had even created what was called the Church of Electricity.

Mai moved closer, watching him connect wires, coils, and improvised systems. She knew it wasn't simple, but if this gave them a chance to defeat the basilisks, she had to trust the process.

"This isn't safe. It could overload the circuits," Niklas said, a worried grimace crossing his face.

"We don't have any other choice," Rainer replied. "Let's do it. Those things will be on top of us again soon, and if we don't kill them, we'll be the next ones frozen solid."

"The plan stays the same as before," said Niklas's assistant. "Two people from each squad carry Nik's modified rifles while everyone else acts as bait. We don't need to modify every weapon."

Niklas looked at Rainer and nodded. "Alternating fire should give the cooling systems enough time to recover. Though with this much power, the batteries will drain faster, and there's a chance some internal components may fail."

Rainer turned toward the rest of the room.

"We can order more drones into the area and scare those things away, or we can give this a shot."

The soldier whose arm still showed signs of frostbite spoke up. "Let's do it. The only thing that would really bother me is knowing those things might move on to another town. I'm serious, Cap. This hurts like hell."

Everyone in the room nodded.

Rainer turned back toward Niklas.

"Nik, finish the damn thing already." Then he pointed at the injured soldier."You stay here with medical support until we can evacuate you."

Niklas finished his adjustments and powered up the device.

The upgraded—and highly dangerous—weapon began to hum, vibrating with far greater power than before.

Rainer walked over and took the rifle, giving Niklas a sidelong glance. "You sure this will work?"

Niklas tilted his head slightly. "As far as my kung fu can tell me, yes."

"If I get turned into charcoal and die, I'm coming back to haunt you and drag your feet at night."

A few people laughed while others stared at the weapon with obvious apprehension.

"What are you waiting for?" Rainer barked. "We still need to modify fifteen more! Hand over your rifles."

Mai swallowed hard but handed hers over as well. She was one of the squad's best marksmen.

The modifications took less than twenty minutes, though to Mai it felt much longer. With every weapon Niklas adjusted, he seemed to get faster and more confident.

Outside, the storm had worsened. Snow hammered against the broken windows while distant echoes rolled between the abandoned buildings. Every now and then the ground trembled beneath their feet as one of the basilisks moved through the depths of the city.

The delay at least gave them time to analyze the creatures.

Was it possible that their ability to pass through concrete was related to the enormous amount of energy stored within them?

One thing had become apparent. Once the sun disappeared behind the clouds, they could no longer move through solid ground like ghosts. Now dull impacts echoed in the distance as they smashed their way in and out of different parts of the city while the drone continued deploying thermal grenades.

They were running low.

When the last weapon was ready, Rainer gathered the squad leaders.

The plan was simple. That alone made Mai trust it even less.

She eyed her newly modified Crispy-Fryer 3000 rifle with growing concern.

The espers would draw the creatures' attention. Everyone else would provide covering fire and distractions. The modified rifles would focus their attacks on a single target at a time. If the theory was correct, the high-intensity electrical discharges would destabilize the Fractus' outer structure and expose the core.

If the theory was wrong, they would find out very quickly.

"Reassuring," someone muttered.

The teams left the shelter shortly after the final thermal grenade was launched. A report from the ground support unit revealed that the first casualty of the mission had been the poor ground drone. Apparently one of the creatures had discovered its position and decided to investigate personally.

The first basilisk found them before they found it.

Barely one hundred and fifty meters from their hideout, with only enough time to dive behind buildings, a café, and abandoned vehicles that now served as improvised trenches.

The creature erupted through the city's only butcher shop, sending concrete, ice, steel beams, and, naturally, meat flying into the air.

Its body twisted like a living drill made of crystal and frost, though now that the sky was overcast the multicolored reflections had vanished.

Mai took cover behind an old white van alongside three members of her squad. The rest were only a few meters away behind a sports car that had somehow been transformed into an abstract art installation involving ice chunks and what looked suspiciously like an industrial meat grinder resting on the hood.

"Fire!" Rainer shouted while removing a frozen steak that had landed on his helmet.

The new rifles roared and electric arcs streaked across the street.

Mai didn't have a clear angle, so she aimed toward the opposite side. Several others were doing the same. Just in case. Nobody wanted to end up trapped between two lines of fire.

The soldiers inside the café at the corner targeted the exposed flanks. The other basilisks didn't seem to be nearby, but nobody wanted to bet their lives on that assumption.

This time the results were immediate.

The basilisk convulsed violently and luminous cracks spread across its translucent body as the modified weapons tore away large portions of its mass. Where the damage had previously vanished almost instantly, entire sections of its structure now appeared unstable, flickering like a corrupted image.

"It's working!"

"Keep firing!"

The weapons vibrated violently in the soldiers' hands while support teams maintained suppressive fire and the modified rifles cooled down. Only a few seconds.

A few seconds that would determine whether they destroyed the creature or ended up preserved as museum specimens for a new Ice Age.

Warning lights suddenly began flashing on one of the rifles carried by a soldier from Squad Five.

The weapon exploded in a shower of sparks and smoke, hurling its operator against a nearby vehicle.

Fortunately, he was still alive.Probably. He smelled like burned meat but continued moving while cursing Niklas and every one of his ancestors, beginning with his mother.

The basilisk lunged forward.

Before it could reach the firing line, a wave of flames slammed into one of its flanks. Two espers from Squads Six and Seven combined their powers, engulfing part of the creature in a storm of heat. Two more from Squads Two and Three attacked from the opposite side while the remaining rifles continued firing.

The air filled with steam and the creature writhed violently, like a worm in a fish-hook.

And for the first time, they saw something.

Deep inside the crystalline mass, a dark shape appeared.

A core.

"The center!" Rainer shouted. "It's in the middle!"

Mai lowered her rifle and leaned out from behind cover. She was too far away to see clearly, but she could make out a darker form shifting wildly inside the creature.

More than a dozen modified rifles concentrated their fire on the same point. The surface fractured. The basilisk convulsed one final time before collapsing in on itself.

Its body disintegrated into thousands of shining fragments that slowly evaporated, leaving behind a dark structure half-buried in the snow.

Nobody celebrated. Three were still left.

Members of Squad Eight secured the core inside a specialized container while three soldiers escorted the injured man away, along with the one suffering from severe frost injuries on his arm.

The second battle was worse.

It began barely half an hour later on the city's main avenue.

Once again, vehicles, buildings, and stairways became cover.

This basilisk seemed to have learned something by watching the first.

It used blasts of freezing air to create walls of snow and reduce visibility. Several times it disappeared into the blizzard only to emerge somewhere else moments later. Entire squads were forced to abandon their positions while others provided precious seconds of covering fire to keep them alive.

The shots landed less often. The soldiers were beginning to tire.

One man was caught by a burst of cold so intense that they had to drag him out of combat while ice spread across part of his armor.

Mai tried not to look. Instead, she focused on firing alongside the other soldier in her squad carrying the same modified rifle. Their position was better now.

Fire. Take cover. Wait thirty seconds for the cooling system. Covering fire from the others.

Repeat.

Again and again.

The rifle vibrated so violently that her arms felt a little numb. It no longer felt like a weapon.

It felt more like a thunderstorm with a trigger.

After several minutes of fighting, the concentrated discharges opened new fractures throughout the basilisk's body. The espers seized the opportunity and forced it to change direction once more.

For barely a second, thanks to the combined attacks, the core became exposed. That was enough time.

A new storm of electricity tore through the creature.

The second basilisk disintegrated in flashes of bluish light.

"That's two down!" someone shouted.

"Then why do I still feel like we're all going to die?" another replied.

Nobody had an answer but the third basilisk appeared to provide one.

The creature erupted directly beneath Squad Three. The ground exploded.

Soldiers and debris were thrown in every direction. One vehicle rolled over and another vanished halfway into the newly formed crater.

For several minutes, the battle descended into chaos.

It was the smallest of the four. Small only in relative terms though. The little bastard still measured a good six meters in length and over a meter and a half in thickness, not to mention the fact that it moved around the battlefield with almost demonic speed.

The communication channels filled with overlapping orders, warnings, and calls for assistance.

Mai lost sight of half the teams.

The squads redistributed themselves while the espers acted as decoys. The modified rifles fired in alternating rotations to avoid overheating. By that point, many were silently praying to the Church of Electricity to protect them and keep those weapons from sending them flying with beautiful Lichtenberg figures etched across their skin.

The whole process felt painfully slow and difficult. They were like a tortoise racing a hare.

The only advantage they possessed was numbers, and the constant distractions working in their favor.

But it was working.

The minutes felt like another exhausting half hour. Even with powered exoskeletons assisting their movements, fatigue was becoming increasingly noticeable.

Little by little, cracks began spreading across the Fractus's body.

One. Then another. And another.

Until the entire creature seemed filled with lightning trapped beneath a shell of ice.

At last, the third basilisk retreated toward the center of the city with what little strength it had left.

It did not seem to be fleeing exactly, though nobody could be certain.

It looked as if it were heading somewhere.

The soldiers advanced after it through the snow-covered streets. They did not believe it was a trap. Reports attributed almost no intelligence to the Fractus—something closer to behavioral patterns found in certain terrestrial insects. Though even that remained a matter of debate.

They were exhausted. Some were wounded. And several weapons were on the verge of failure.

And they were so focused on pursuing their target that none of them noticed the fourth basilisk already moving beneath their feet.

The final shot belonged to Rainer. The round struck perfectly.

The little bastard exploded, and its core was launched dozens of meters away across the street.

But there was no time to secure it.

Because at the exact spot where it had landed, the last basilisk appeared.

"What the hell...?" someone muttered.

"Seriously?" another replied.

The fourth basilisk emerged in a cloud of frost and debris.

It was the largest of the four.

Mai was almost certain it was the same one they had first seen near the children's park.

The creature crossed the street like a living projectile.

One soldier barely had time to turn his head before a wave of cold froze part of his armor. Another was knocked off his feet by the shockwave when the basilisk burst through a bus stop and vanished underground once again.

"Contact! Contact!"

"It's beneath us!"

"Spread out! Spread out!" Rainer ordered.

The clustered squads broke formation and moved back into cover, taking positions behind vehicles, inside the shattered display window of a clothing store, and along the steps of the city hall.

Mai felt her heart pounding against her chest.

This was different from the previous battles.

The basilisk was no longer merely fighting them. It was hunting them.

Could it somehow be detecting the modified weapons?

The creature appeared for only a few seconds at a time, before diving back beneath the streets. Every time it emerged, it changed direction. Every time it resurfaced, someone ended up injured or separated from their squad.

The modified weapons could barely keep up.

And because of the sustained fighting, three of them had already exhausted their batteries.

"I can't get a lock on it!" someone shouted over the radio.

One discharge passed just alongside the Fractus. Another struck it directly. The creature convulsed but kept moving.

Far too fast.

Mai watched its movements while running alongside Squad Two, through the frozen remains of an avenue. Something felt wrong.

Everyone else was watching where they thought it would appear.

She began watching where it disappeared.

Every time it emerged, the basilisk turned slightly toward the same direction before diving underground again.

It was not moving randomly.

It was following something.

A route?

They had never observed that behavior in the others. Although the truth was that they were more interested in shooting, ducking and taking cover.

Mai stopped running.

The rest of Squad Two left her behind.

Their leader turned around. "What are you doing?"

A fault line.

The same tectonic fault that crossed beneath the city.

"The fault..."

"What are you talking about?"

Several of her teammates stared at her.

"Captain!" Mai shouted over the radio. "It's not surfacing wherever it wants! It's following the fault and Ley Lines!"

Several seconds of silence followed.

Then Rainer's voice came through.

"Can you predict where it'll emerge?"

Mai swallowed and looked at the tactical map projected inside her visor.

She calculated. Then calculated again.

And pointed toward a location ahead of them.

"I think so."

"You think?"

"We don't have time for anything better."

“Where is it?”

“Where we first saw it.”

Apparently that was good enough for Rainer.

"All teams. Suppression fire. Drive it toward Sector Delta. Back to the park!"

The soldiers obeyed.

While doing their best not to become frozen statues, the squads carried out the order.

At one point the creature burrowed underground and failed to reappear.

If Mai's theory was correct, they might finally end this at the next location.

No one knew why, but according to the maps, the roundabout and children's park sat directly above the tectonic fault where three ley lines converged.

Mai could not be certain, but she suspected that in ancient times some magically capable group might have used the location as a meeting place. Perhaps the ley lines converged because of that, although natural convergence was also possible.

Regardless, the basilisk reacted exactly as they expected.

As they approached the roundabout and the park, sensors began registering a sudden drop in temperature.

Directly beneath the marked position.

"Now," Rainer whispered.

The ground exploded and the creature emerged.

Once again electrical discharges illuminated and warmed the freezing midday air.

The ice basilisk twisted violently as sections of its body burst apart and failed to regenerate.

For a fraction of a second, the core became visible.

Mai pulled the trigger.

The discharge erupted with a deafening roar.

A tree of lightning spread across the roundabout, and Mai felt, in slow motion, her feet leave the ground.

Electricity pierced the dying basilisk from end to end. The core took the full impact.

There was a flash of white light. Then another.

And then the creature disintegrated. Thousands of crystalline particles rose into the air like snow illuminated by sunlight.

For several seconds, nobody spoke. Not even over the comms.

"Target neutralized," Rainer finally announced.

The silence was replaced by applause, curses, nervous laughter, and sighs of relief.

Meanwhile, the small moth girl had disappeared.

In the place where she had been standing, only moments earlier, remained a scorched patch of partially melted ground.

Realizing this, her teammates looked around.

They found her twenty meters behind everyone else, lying in the snow and loudly invoking Niklas's entire family lineage for the second time that day, in that battlefield.

With help from the others, Mai slowly got back to her feet and freed herself from the exoskeleton, which was no longer functioning.

Her arms hurt. Her back hurt.

And she smelled like an overcooked hamburger mixed with burnt hair.

She was probably going to hate that rifle for the rest of her life.

But she was alive.

Recovery teams secured the cores while the wounded were evacuated.

The city had survived. Not intact. Very far from it.

But it was still standing.

The mission had been a success. An expensive success.

And was only the beginning.

Over the following days, seven more basilisks appeared throughout nearby regions, forcing additional cleanup operations.

Reports began piling up. And so did the questions too.

Because if those creatures truly possessed the limited intelligence many experts claimed they did, nobody could explain why the last basilisk had attempted to lead them to that exact location.

And as winter continued to spread due to the climatic disruptions caused by the floating rock in orbit, Mai began to suspect that question would ultimately prove far more important than the battle itself.
