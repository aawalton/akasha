Endless stars glittered across eternal, regal black. A moon larger and brighter than belief shone down, blanketing my world in silver. Pearly walls and a pristine ivory dais glimmered not far away as I curled my toes into cool, soft soil and turned all around. Looking for Velidan.

Is he angry? Will he threaten me again? Say he’ll snap my neck if I don’t bring the shard back next time? Saints, and then probably just kill me anyways, so I can’t accidentally steal it a third time!

I stood for a long while, muscles coiled and teeth clenched. Swept my gaze across otherworldly plants and the treeline of graceful, silver-tipped willows. But, other than leaves and petals rustling in the warm breeze, there was no movement. Just fragrant, placid beauty.

And… what is… there’s something on the ground over there. Near the dais, beneath that rosebush. It’s black and bundled up. It almost looks like…

I approached the pile on cautious feet, quickly realizing that it was not a haphazard bundle but rather a neatly folded length of thick black wool. My cloak. It lay draped across a lidded brown basket and a familiar watering pail.

I picked the garment up, looking now with confusion as well as fear. My fingers curled into the thick fabric. It felt soft and clean. After an uncertain glance around, I examined the basket while half-expecting it to bite me.

It did not. My fingers glided across smooth wood, then lifted the covering to reveal… a small mound of unfamiliar, sweet-smelling purple fruits nestled in midnight-blue satin. Atop the pile was a cream-colored piece of vellum about the size of my palm. Curling black script along the surface read, Enjoy.

I blinked. Straightened. Turned in several slow, wary circles, gaze darting all around, waiting for the mocking laughter or the angry snarl.

Nothing.

He… left me a basket of fruit. Perhaps it’s… poison?

But, no, that didn’t make a shred of sense. If he wanted to hurt me, there were a thousand less roundabout ways to go about it. Leaving a pile of poisoned food in the hopes that I’d be foolish enough to eat it was just… mad.

My attention trailed to the watering pail. Its surface was polished to a lustrous gleam. It practically glowed with the reflection of moonlight.

Perhaps he’s not here? Busy? Off tormenting some other helpless human? Salt, I don’t know. Or perhaps he’s trying to lull me into a false sense of security. Make me let down my guard, make me think I’m safe, then he’ll appear and… and…

My mouth twisted downwards. I paced, squinting suspiciously at every plant.

Right. That’s just as foolish as the thought of poisoning me. ‘Let down my guard?’ What would be the point?

Another possibility sprang to mind. An odd one, but perhaps the most reasonable I’d had so far.

What if he expects me to… salt, work? Earn my keep, or such? Sweep the floors and polish all that awful marble? Show up and tend to his precious garden every night for the rest of my life?

I glowered at the rosebush. It was a large plant, dense with cascading emerald leaves and full, ruby blooms.

“I’m not going to do work for you,” I declared. My voice rang through the warm night air, briefly shattering the near-stillness. I glanced around nervously, half-expecting my captor to leap from a bush.

He did not.

I blew a sigh, examining the plant with a more critical eye. “And it’s not as though this one needs tending, anyways. There’s no weeds. At least, none that I can see. Though I don’t know what most of these plants are, so suppose they could all be weeds. But there’s no stems to be snipped, no wilted buds… and no blood this time, thank the stars. What would you even expect me to do?”

Wind crooned through the leaves and ruffled my hair.

I should tear it up. Rip the roots out and stomp on his precious roses. It would be the least he deserves.

The thought earned only a moment’s consideration. I didn’t have it in me to hurt a plant. Not even one that was evil by association.

Maybe this is some horrible game; maybe he’s watching me from a window, waiting to see how long it takes until I panic or collapse or burst into tears or… or… I don’t even know.

I sat with a huff, too nervous to put my back to the manor in case the Fae did decide to show himself, and wiggled my toes into the dirt. It was loamy and cool, nestling along the soles of my feet. The sensation was achingly familiar. My fingers sank in next, curling slowly into feather-soft moss and rich earth.

Well… if he’s planning to come tear my head off after some manual labor, there’s worse ways I could spend my last moments. This is almost… I wish I had a garden like this. I could spend all day in this place. Could even sleep here. Well, not here, of course, but if it was back in the mortal realm. I’d curl up on that patch of moss right there, by the blue stuff that smells like lavender, and just… mm. It’s a lovely thought.

My wary dread did not relent, and my spine stiffened at every rustling leaf, but the lovely thoughts kept me company as I sat waiting.

The night grew old around me, but my jailor made no appearance.

Boredom crept behind wary fear. I turned my attention to the basket of unusual fruit. I picked one up gingerly, curious despite myself. The skin was hued like a ripe plum and felt plump and tight, like a grape that was ready to burst. A pleasantly sweet fragrance filled my nose.

Fruit from a Fae Realm. I bet it’s either delicious beyond words or… or steals your memories, or poisons your blood, or something just as wicked. Perhaps all three. There’s no way I’m eating it. But… hm. I wonder…

I rolled the fruit thoughtfully between my fingertips, then dug one nail into its skin. It pierced easily. The flesh beneath was soft, like that of a peach, and a lovely shade of crimson. Syrupy red juice bled out along my thumb, carrying with it the scents of spicy cinnamon and warm ginger.

The juice smelled even more tantalizing than the fruit itself, but I ignored temptation and dug deeper, until my fingertips hit firm resistance. Once the fruit lay in pieces and an almond-sized black pit sat dripping in my palm, I scooted over to a relatively isolated patch of soil.

For all I know, whatever this is doesn’t even need dirt and water to grow. It certainly can’t need sunlight. Surely some other… rules, or whatever they are, about how plants work are also different here.

But… there’s no harm in trying.

I sifted my fingers into the loam, digging until I was satisfied, and nestled my prize into the hole. Burying the pit could have taken a mere handful of breaths, but I found myself lingering with each motion. Falling into familiar, soothing slowness. Dirt scooped and poured with peaceful calm, fingers tamping and smoothing and savoring the feel of soil. Pausing to rub bits of each handful between my thumb and forefinger. Every breath a steady inhale, filling my nose with the scents of clean herbs, sharp spices, delicate flowers, and nutty earth.

Once my little mound was set, I drew up the pail and sprinkled water out on top. The clear liquid smelled like honey. Then I stood, observing my work with a contented sigh.

Well, I suppose the jest’s on him. If this is some kind of twisted attempt to torment me, he’s gone about it all wrong. I could do this all night.

As if to prove the point, I strolled a few steps over and watered a shrub.

It probably doesn’t need the water, if that’s even what this is. He said he ‘helps’ the garden grow. With magic, I assume, which is really just cheating, when you think about it.

That thought gave me pause. I furrowed my brows down at the foliage, tongue trapped between my teeth.

Magic. He uses magic to make his plants grow, and to heal terrible wounds, and to… I don’t even know what else. Rip holes in the realms so he can come terrorize humans, and keep endless decanters of wine and watering pails that never run out and… and…

What else can he do?

Royce and Renner were dying, and he just… undid it. Like it was nothing. Like it was easy. What if it had been worse? What if they’d been…

My eyes drifted down, falling somberly onto a patch of dark soil. The image of a crowd of townsfolk all huddled around a cottage, hoods drawn tight and candles flickering in each hand, came unbidden.

What if I’d asked for Terrence, too? When we were in the cave, when he saved Renner and Teela… what if I’d begged for Terrence to be a part of it? Asked the Fae to go find his body and… Surely it wouldn’t have changed anything. Salt, he was ripped apart, there’s no… no undoing that. Such a thing couldn’t be possible.

Both hands twisted around the pail. My eyes fell again and I caught sight of my reflection in the gleaming silver. I looked… despondent. Clean, for the first time in days, but harrowed and weary.

I frowned, lips pressing into a tight line, and gave my image a stern look. “Stop. There’s no point in dwelling on things that can’t be changed.” There. That sounds wise. And true, I think.

True though it was, the face staring up at me remained unchanged. I gazed at my reflection, helplessly willing my eyes to no longer look so haunted, before turning to my surroundings.

I might as well pass the time somehow.

I spent a long time drifting from plant to plant, pausing to kneel and savor the sights and feels and smells of each. Moonlight tangled my hair, caressed my skin and beckoned me step by step.

Until a familiar sound ripped through the placid peace. Wordless and sharp, cracking the air like the distant snap of a bone.

Someone was screaming.
