The first faces James saw on the nearest screen did not impress him at first.

He looked just underneath the two fighters and found the System had helpfully identified the two men fighting.

“Wesley Miller vs. Jacques Leroi” were the names.

They were just using what looked like ordinary martial arts to James’s eyes, and they didn’t appear to be moving particularly quickly.

Something he could not easily name made him lean in and continue watching for a few minutes, though. He stood there with arms crossed, posture stiff, frowning at the screen.

Just a normal pre-System fight, really. There they go again. Same kinds of moves as before… There’s a chop, a block, a kick, a punch—wait, no, the guy opened his hand, and was that a flash of mana? He leaned in, curious. Some kind of System-related magical martial arts after all, then. How does it work?

The Fisher Emperor stood there transfixed for the next several minutes. The harder he paid attention, the more he noticed that the two men, who had similar styles of fighting to each other, were trading energy as much as they were trading physical blows. Every time one of them landed a hit, there was a transfer of mana from one body into the other. Usually, the largest energy transfers took place when James thought the physical blow didn’t look like it would hit very hard.

It’s like some old martial arts movie or maybe an anime or something, he thought. Where the real nature of the attack isn’t physical at all. It’s energy-based.

As he thought that, Miller slapped Leroi across his side. The hit seemed like it shouldn’t do any meaningful damage at all, but Leroi stiffened and staggered backward in response.

They’re kind of subtle about it, too. You can only see the mana for a fraction of a second, the same moment as the attack. If you’re actually in the fight, you won’t have the chance to see it at all before the hit lands.

Leroi bounced back from the slap, but James could see the man had not fully recovered. As the battle wore on, his movements grew clumsier, and at one point, a trickle of blood leaked from the corner of his mouth.

I guess it’s just about over, he thought. He turned his head and started looking for where the next screen was.

It was in the moment when his eyes were turned away.

He heard the sound of a boot scraping against tile, just a little more friction than normal.

James looked back and saw that on the screen he had been watching, Miller was the one staggering back now. He was clutching his chest, his hand just over his heart. His eyes were wide with pain and disbelief.

Leroi must have struck him in the chest with that power. Was he feigning weakness before?

Because Leroi looked healthy enough now. He stepped cautiously after Miller, palms up in a defensive posture, Leroi’s body clearly at least more under his own control than Miller’s was.

Miller fell forward, and James sensed it. The man was dead.

What a reversal. I didn’t even properly see what happened. Good for Leroi. Use every trick you can, including playing injured. He shook his head and moved on to the next screen. Now there was really nothing left to see from the Leroi-Miller fight.

James found a fight between Count Aleph and a human—since Aleph was a monster of human origins, he was in the human division—but he quickly moved on from that, since Aleph wasn’t among the opponents James had to worry about.

The Vampire Count had agreed to surrender if he and James ran into each other in the Tournament, and even if he didn’t, a Solar Ray ought to hit him in his core weakness as a Vampire.

On the screen after that, James saw something more interesting.

What had seemed to be the case at first in the Leroi-Miller fight, a plain pre-System brawl with no Skills active, was actually in evidence.

That was not because both parties were lacking in Skills, but because one of them was Han Jianguo, and he had used his power to render the fight completely Skill-free.

Is that actually a specialty of his? He prefers to fight without Skills every time? I guess I can see why.

The fight looked like it had been going on for a while, and it was brutal and bare-knuckled and horrendously violent—there was blood spattered across a wide area where the two combatants had been—but it was clearly going one way by this point.

Jianguo was destroying his opponent. He straddled him, raining punch after punch directly down onto his face—or what was left of it. Jianguo’s opponent didn’t seem weak. The man was as heavily muscled as any of the other participants in the tournament, and when he got little openings in the flurry of blows, he made movements to hit Jianguo, but it was like a child trying to fight an adult.

Yeah, Jianguo is actually specialized in kicking people’s asses without the use of Skills, James decided. That’s just fine. Try that shit with me, Jianguo. You’ll see there’s more than one way to fight without your Skills active.

That fight looked as if it was approaching its end. The man underneath Jianguo was slowing down and doing less to repel Jianguo’s blows with every passing minute. Unlike the other match James had spectated, there wasn’t likely to be much that could be done to reverse this fight’s momentum, since neither combatant was using any sort of subtle magical martial arts.

I guess I could still use mana to fight, now that I think about it, even if Skills are sealed. That was basically what Miller and Leroi were both doing. I’m sure they had a Skill guiding it, but punching someone with raw mana imbued into your fist would still do more damage than a regular punch.

He stepped away from Jianguo’s screen and sought out another match to watch.

He walked from one to another briskly, quickly bypassing the ones with people he had seen in the melee or combatants who only seemed to be using very basic Skills. It was what James had done in his own fight—he didn’t want other people to see what he could do when he needed to get creative, not until that was absolutely necessary—but it didn’t make for very interesting viewing.

Then he saw someone else he knew, and he stopped.

Zhang Chāo was fighting.

The battle had been going for a while, James sensed. Neither fighter appeared tired, but Chāo’s expression had taken on a serious aspect that James remembered from the Summit. He looked like he was done playing around.

Beside James, three others had stopped to watch this particular screen, whereas the others usually had one or no spectators standing by when James stepped over. Their eyes darted over to James as he settled in to watch, and the other viewers parted to give him a central position.

Every person in this waiting area was a fellow combatant, and they all recognized the man who was undeniably one of the favorites to win the whole Tournament.

So James had the chance to watch the person he considered his biggest known threat in the competition from the best vantage point possible.

He was observing Zhang Chāo’s moves, almost like the push and pull of a dance, when he heard a familiar sound of footsteps approaching. James didn’t want to turn around right when Zhang Chāo’s fight seemed to be heating up a bit—the back and forth getting faster and more intense—but he also didn’t want to leave the new arrival unacknowledged.

“Luna, how’s it going?” he asked without adjusting his position.

“My king, I remain active in the competition,” Luna said. “By the grace of fortune and thanks to your blessing and investment in me—”

“Almost entirely thanks to your own hard work,” James corrected gently. “I’ve done very little to make you stronger.”

“Whatever the cause, I won my first bout,” Luna replied.

“Excellent. I expected nothing less from you. I know your competition will only grow stiffer. Do you need any healing before the next round starts?”

He sensed rather than saw her shake her central head. “No, thank you, I am uninjured.”

Hopefully it stays that way. That’s a good sign she might last longer in her much larger and more difficult competition group.

As he stared at the screen, events heated up again. Zhang Chāo drew his sword and spoke a few words.

“Last chance to escape uninjured.”

The reaction from those around James was casual as he observed it. Chuckles. A few rolled eyes. They thought Zhang Chāo was being dramatic or something. Though they had watched the fight up to this point, they had not seen anything that suggested a large power gap that would justify his words.

James felt differently, of course.

Well, I hope Chāo doesn’t kill him. That would get his participation in the Tournament off to an ominous start.

The Fisher Emperor still remembered the vision in which Zhang Chāo had decapitated him with that same sword in one of these rings. He wished he could find some trick for keeping that weapon from being used in the fight he anticipated he would have with Zhang Chāo later.

The man standing opposite Zhang Chāo on the screen laughed quietly, casually dismissing Zhang Chāo’s quiet threat. And the fight resumed.

The pace of it had increased quickly, driven by Zhang Chāo growing more aggressive now that he had drawn his secret weapon. The longer the Mandate of Heaven was out, the more people would see him using it. That was probably his reason for wanting to make the fight quick.

“So, what are we watching?” Another somewhat familiar voice spoke up, from just outside of James’s range of automatic perception. That felt a bit uncanny.

James didn’t turn his head, though. Han Jianguo was probably trying to distract the viewers to keep them from paying close attention to the Heavenly King’s fighting style and techniques. After all, Zhang Chāo was Jianguo’s Ruler and his friend.

The Fisher Emperor devoted his attention to studying Zhang Chāo’s footwork, memorizing his preferred angles of attack, estimating his speed, and generally putting himself into the shoes of Chāo’s opponent.

When Han Jianguo repositioned to stand next to James, the Emperor gave Jianguo a little nod of acknowledgement but still didn’t say anything aloud.

The renewed dance was unfortunately not fated to last very long.

As James observed, Zhang Chāo slashed suddenly and unexpectedly to the right. His opponent, who had been dodging and parrying the Mandate of Heaven while avoiding himself or his equipment touching the edge—a testament to the man’s great skill as a close-quarters righter—was forced to directly block the attack.

Just as expected, the Mandate of Heaven chopped through both the weapon and wielder in a single, smooth stroke. The enemy’s right arm dropped to the ground, and the crowd watching on the other side of the screen went wild with shock and excitement. It took a moment before the wound began to bleed.

The opponent hissed with pain, staggered back, raised the sword he held in his left, dominant hand, and braced for a follow-up attack, but it didn’t come just then.

“Isn’t this enough?” Zhang Chāo asked coolly.

“Never!” the other man exclaimed. He lunged across the short distance that separated them and attacked with a fury that surprised James. The Fisher Emperor had assumed a debilitating wound would end the fight, something Zhang Chāo seemed to have expected as well.

But they were both wrong.

Chāo’s opponent was more tenacious than that. As if he was bound by some vow to never give up, he attacked with such energy that he actually forced Zhang Chāo onto the back foot for the next minute or so.

Then a crescent moon of blade motion sliced off the man’s right leg.

The opponent hurled himself bodily at Zhang Chāo, but the Heavenly King didn’t pause in his attack for even a second this time. The sword slashed to the right and severed the left leg, then came up and lopped off the remaining arm.

The other man struck Zhang Chāo with the momentum his body still carried, but his limbless form bounced helplessly off.

“Damn it,” the opponent said, teeth gritted in pain.

“We’re done here,” Zhang Chāo said, his expression turning cold and indifferent. “You will heal from this, yes?”

“Eventually,” the other man replied.

He managed to press himself into a semi-standing position on his stump legs, like a grotesque real world version of a Monty Python skit.

“Thank you for the fight,” the opponent said, bowing his head.

“It was good,” Zhang Chāo replied, finally cracking a tiny smile.

The screen went blank.
