The next ten miles were covered in about half a day. Though they could have arrived sooner, Luke had chosen caution over speed, wanting to ensure there were no signs of another army.

Thankfully, his concern proved unnecessary.

As they crested the final hill, Luke finally caught sight of the splendor of Xiandong Valley and the towering cliff face guarding its southern edge. His eyes widened briefly before narrowing in contemplation.

Now he understood why Tristan had been so adamant about not climbing up from the valley.

The cliff rose almost vertically for the first four hundred feet, sheer and unforgiving. A jagged rock jutted out midway like the nose of a giant, breaking the ascent. From there, the slope appeared more manageable—though only by comparison. For someone like Luke, an amateur climber at best, it was still a daunting challenge.

“So, you still want to climb up from the valley?” a deep voice asked, snapping him from his thoughts. The tone was dry, laced with disbelief.

Luke chuckled, shaking his head. “I’m not the one who’ll be scaling the wall,” he said, turning to Tristan. A mischievous grin tugged at the corners of his lips, deepening as confusion—and then alarm—spread across the man’s face.

Before Tristan could respond, Luke clapped a hand on his shoulder. “Don’t answer just yet. We’ll talk more about it tomorrow morning.”

“Huh? My answer won’t change by then…” Tristan muttered, incredulous.

Luke didn’t reply. Instead, he turned back to the others. “Let’s head further into the valley and set up camp,” he called, mounting his horse again.

Some time later, they settled on a sheltered spot just off the main path, tucked beneath a natural wall of sandstone. It offered protection from the bone-chilling winds that funneled through the valley.

As the fire crackled and the cooking pot began to bubble, the distant sound of hoofbeats echoed off the stone walls, sending a ripple of tension through the camp. Soldiers stiffened, hands inching toward weapons.

Only Luke remained calm.

He had already spotted the riders earlier using his Eagle Eye skill. To his surprise, the ability had improved since acquiring Will of Conquest, allowing him to see even farther than before.

The implications were exciting. If this one skill had advanced, what about the others? He would need to test them all—and soon.

“General! The scouts have returned!” Stanley called, panting slightly.

His voice prompted a collective exhale from the soldiers. Relief washed over them like a tide. Many had feared the enemy had caught their trail.

With only fifty men, any confrontation would be suicide.

“Mmm. Direct them to the tent,” Luke said casually, standing and brushing off his cloak. He gestured silently for the others to follow as he made his way over.

Tristan, Richard, and the second prince fell in step behind him, ducking into the tent moments later. A single candle lit the interior, casting long, wavering shadows across the hide walls.

A few moments later, the scouts entered. Their faces were grave, their eyes shadowed with tension. Both cupped their fists and bowed low before beginning to recount what they had found.

Throughout the report, Luke’s expression remained unreadable, his gaze steady and impassive. Beside him, Richard and Tristan both wore grim expressions, while the second prince looked pale—on the verge of fainting.

Luke asked a few pointed questions, trying to determine the identity of the opposing force. Unfortunately, the rugged terrain had made it impossible for the scouts to get close enough to identify any banners or gain a clear picture of the situation.

Still, the report confirmed what he had already begun to suspect.

The Marxx Kingdom had likely taken control of Xiandong Fortress—but now found itself under siege by an unknown army.

In all likelihood, it was the same force that had left behind the tracks they encountered days prior.

“That’s all we have to report, General,” the older scout said, cupping his fist respectfully.

“Well done. You may go,” Luke replied with a nod, dismissing them.

Once the scouts exited, a heavy silence settled over the tent. Only the soft crackle of the candle and the wind’s mournful howl rustling the canvas filled the air.

“So… what do we do now?” the second prince asked, voice strained. “Surely we’re not thinking of trying to save them…”

Out of the corner of his eye, Luke caught the flicker of disdain on Richard’s face. It vanished quickly, masked beneath the larger man’s usual calm, but Luke had seen it.

He understood the sentiment. Prince Lyon’s fear bordered on cowardice. But to be fair, the situation was dire.

Even if Luke managed to scale the sheer cliff and infiltrate Xiandong, the fortress was surrounded. Twenty thousand enemy soldiers. And no way back down once he was in.

His eyes drifted unconsciously to the other side of the tent—toward his pack. Inside were two thunder bombs. He had brought them as a contingency. But against an army of that size? It would barely make a dent.

“Prince Lyon,” Luke said, voice calm but firm, “please show some decorum. The men trapped in that fortress are soldiers of the Marxx Kingdom. They fly your father’s banner. We have a duty to try and bring them home.”

“Y-yes, General.” The prince bowed his head quickly, his meek reply surprising even Luke.

“I’d like to remain hopeful, General,” Tristan said, his tone low, “but we don’t know how many of our men are left inside Xiandong—or what their supply situation looks like. If we intervene without that knowledge, we could be marching straight to our deaths.”

Luke nodded, though he didn’t appear concerned.

He had already used Eagle Eye to scout the mountaintop. From his vantage, he had seen enough to confirm some details—though the enemy camp was still too far to make out clearly. If it weren’t, he wouldn’t have needed the scouts.

“There should be enough men holding the fortress,” Luke said. “But you’re right. Supplies will be the deciding factor. Without food, even a full garrison can’t fight.”

Breaking a siege wasn’t impossible—but the cost in blood would be high. He would only risk such a maneuver if there were no other option.

If the enemy is from Lhair... word of gunpowder might have already reached them. Maybe if I detonate both thunder bombs at once, it’ll scare them off, Luke mused.

But even as the idea took form, he dismissed it.

No. That was too risky.

“I feel like you’re skipping over something, General…” Richard said, his deep voice laced with disbelief. “Just how exactly are you planning to get into the fortress to lead the army?”

The others turned to Luke, their expressions a mix of confusion, concern, and in Tristan’s case—sheer anxiety.

“Tristan and I will scale the mountain tomorrow at dawn,” Luke replied, his voice calm and unwavering.

“Huh?” Richard’s eyes widened like saucers. He looked from the teenager to the man nearly in his fifties, disbelief plain on his face. He didn’t need to speak—his expression said everything.

“Y-you’re going to climb that mountain?” the second prince stammered, his pale face almost comical in its panic. He looked like he might faint at any moment.

But Richard, to his credit, recovered quickly. “Even if—by some miracle—you make it into Xiandong Fortress, what then? No offense, but even the Blood General himself would struggle to break out of a siege like this alive.”

Luke raised his hand, signaling for silence.

“That’s enough, Sir Richard,” he said flatly. “I appreciate your concern, but you’re here to follow my orders and protect the prince.”

“Yes, General.” The large man bowed curtly.

“I wish to speak with Tristan alone for a moment,” Luke added. “Go get something to eat. Rest while you can.”

The tent flap opened as Richard and Prince Lyon stepped out, letting in a cool breeze that made the candlelight waver.

For a while, neither man spoke. The silence between them was thick, filled with unspoken thoughts.

Then, without a word, Luke summoned Tristan’s status window. It was time to fulfill his earlier promise.

Subordinate: Tristan Gale

Title: Head Librarian

Stats:

Strength: B

Agility: C

Endurance: B

Combat: D

Loyalty: A

Stat points: 18

Eighteen points? Luke’s eyes narrowed. Is it because he’s a legendary-tier subordinate?

“Luke, this is far too dangerous,” Tristan said finally, breaking the silence. “Maybe we should just retreat—”

“Do you remember when I said I’d give you power?” Luke cut him off, a crooked grin tugging at his lips. “You didn’t actually think I meant a better position, did you?”

“Sorry…?”

Luke didn’t answer. Instead, he began assigning the stat points, watching the changes unfold with quiet satisfaction.

Subordinate: Tristan Gale

Title: Head Librarian

Stats:

Strength: S

Agility: C+

Endurance: S-

Combat: D

Loyalty: A

Stat points: 0

With eighteen points, he could have chosen to spread the growth evenly. But time—and terrain—didn’t allow for balance. Strength and endurance were all that mattered for the climb ahead.

Ten points into Strength. Seven into Endurance.

That single decision made Tristan the physically strongest subordinate in Luke’s service.

I can already picture Kayson’s face if he found out, Luke thought, amused.

Back in the tent, Tristan suddenly stumbled forward, nearly knocking over the candlestick.

“I… I feel exhausted all of a sudden,” he muttered, pressing a hand to his temple.

Luke shot to his feet and caught him before he collapsed.

“Let’s get you some rest. We’ve got a big day tomorrow,” Luke said as he walked Tristan to the other side of the tent and gently lowered him onto the bedding. Even as he unrolled the hide used as a makeshift bed, quiet chuckles escaped him, echoing softly through the tent.

“W-what’s… happening to me?” Tristan slurred, his voice groggy.

Luke hushed him gently, easing him down as though caring for an elderly man. Tristan offered no resistance—the exhaustion overwhelming him.

Within moments, he was fast asleep.

With his goal accomplished, Luke stood and stretched, working a few kinks from his back.

“Will S-grade strength be enough to haul me up the mountain too?” he muttered, glancing at the slumbering figure beside him.

A low growl from his stomach interrupted the thought. Right. Time to eat.

Casting one last look at Tristan, Luke slipped out of the tent, drawn by the rich aroma of stew wafting through the camp.

As he emerged, the murmurs of the soldiers fell silent. Every head turned toward him, eyes reflecting firelight—some wary, others anxious.

He didn’t need to ask. The scouts had clearly shared their report. The knowledge that their comrades were trapped in Xiandong was disheartening enough—but the fact that Luke had yet to order a retreat likely unsettled them more.

“I hope you saved some for me,” Luke said lightly, hoping to defuse the tension.

A few men gave half-hearted chuckles, but most remained quiet. The air was heavy with unspoken fear.

Luke ladled himself a bowl of the fragrant boar stew they'd hunted earlier in the day. He spotted Richard and Prince Lyon sitting nearby, both contributing in their own way to the gloomy atmosphere.

Taking a seat beside the hulking knight, Luke dug into his food. The warmth of the broth spread through his chest and belly, cutting through the cold air and lifting his spirits—if only slightly.

“Where’s the Deputy General?” Richard asked.

“He turned in early,” Luke replied between bites. “He’s got a big day tomorrow.”

The answer was cryptic, and it earned him a raised eyebrow—but no further questions.

“G-General…” a voice called.

Stanley stepped forward from the shadows, the flames of the campfire flickering in his eyes. One glance told Luke that he wasn’t here on his own behalf—he spoke for the others.

Luke took another spoonful of stew, savoring the flavor, before turning toward the man.

“The soldiers are worried, sir,” Stanley said. “Are we really… going to charge the enemy with just fifty men?”

The unease in his voice was undeniable.

Luke glanced at Richard, who returned a puzzled look. Clearly, he had no idea how the men had come to such a conclusion.

Seriously? Luke stifled a laugh. Sure, I’m crazy—but I’m not suicidal.

Still, his expression remained unreadable as he turned back to Stanley.

“Do you have a problem with that?” Luke asked, raising an eyebrow. “You are a soldier of His Majesty King Julius’s army—and I’m the one in command.”

He rose to his feet, his head barely reaching the older soldier’s chin. The camp fell utterly silent. For a moment, the flickering firelight seemed to illuminate not a young man—but a shadow of the ruthless general they had first met at the beginning of the campaign.

“If I tell you to jump, you ask how high. If I tell you to throw yourself off a cliff, you throw yourself off the damn cliff,” Luke said, doing his best to keep a straight face. A grin tugged at his lips, but he suppressed it.

Now standing directly in front of Stanley, Luke watched the man stiffen, as if he’d swallowed a stone. Even in the firelight, the pallor of his face was clear.

But before Luke could continue the act, he cracked.

A breath escaped—then a laugh. Then another. The tension shattered as Luke doubled over, unable to keep it in. Laughter poured out of him, clutching his ribs like the absurdity of it all was too much to bear.

Confused murmurs spread through the soldiers as they looked on, unsure if their general had lost his mind.

“General?” Stanley asked hesitantly, concern written across his face.

Luke tried to collect himself, wiping his eyes as he calmed down. But as he looked around, he realized the atmosphere hadn’t lightened as he’d intended. Anxiety and confusion still lingered on the soldiers’ faces.

Even young Chase Martin looked like he was about to cry.

Huh? They think I’m serious? Luke blinked in disbelief.

“I’m messing with you guys…” he muttered, rubbing his forehead. The laughter had drained out of him, leaving behind a hint of awkwardness. It seemed these men didn’t know him well enough yet to tell when he was joking.

“Wait… so you’re not going to make us charge Xiandong Fortress through the enemy?”

“No,” Luke said flatly. “Because that would be stupid.”

“Oh… haha.”

“Thank the gods!”

A collective sigh of relief swept through the camp like a fresh breeze. Chuckles followed. Then came full-throated laughter as the tension finally gave way to genuine amusement.

Luke let out a breath and looked around. This had been his goal all along—to ease their nerves, get them laughing, restore morale. It had worked, just… not quite the way he’d envisioned.

If it were Stoddard and his men… it would’ve landed better, he thought with a silent sigh, walking back to retrieve his now-cold soup.

At no point did he ever think he would miss the long-faced lieutenant commander.
