Ashscar heard the storm rumble—low, distant—a whisper barely audible amidst the thunderous clash of calamities and the incessant booms of exploding skills.

The rumble shouldn’t have been audible. Yet Ashscar had heard it as surely as he knew his own name within a storm.

The war had begun an hour ago, and this time, it was more brutal than anything they could have imagined. Lightning lit the sky in countless hues, thunder boomed incessantly, and rain poured down in sheets, giving the water cultivators more than enough resources to drown their enemies and freeze them to death.

But the heat refused to let that happen.

Above the battlefield, the sun blazed white—far brighter and hotter than anything Ashscar had ever felt before. Water evaporated before it could pool, ice melted almost instantly, and despite the rain, people were sweating buckets.

Something had to give. Either the sun or the rain.

But neither was willing to budge.

And so everyone pushed through, fighting and killing in a desperate attempt to upend the balance.

The rough terrain didn’t help matters either, and in only an hour, the new battlefield looked almost no different from the last. Blood covered the earth, broken limbs and severed bodies scattered across the field like debris after a storm.

It was hard to watch, but Ashscar kept his gaze fixed on the screen—waiting, listening for when the storm would speak again.

He only had to wait a second.

The rumble came again, its intent crystal clear, and Ashscar immediately turned toward a screen several meters to the left, watching as the view panned over a formation of solar and glass cultivators pressing against a formation of storm Mythics.

The storm wanted to smite them and rip them apart. But it needed a conduit. A herald to announce it. Energy to fuel it.

“Strike,” Ashscar muttered under his breath, staring intently at the screen, but he was too far from the battlefield. Too far to truly herald the storm. And an instant later, the storm formation broke, a beam of sizzling sunlight slicing them to shreds.

His breath hitched, but when he turned around, he realized no one else had noticed. In a war this massive, no one was watching the smaller engagements.

They were all watching the Calamities and Mythics with the biggest skills and even bigger explosions.

No one was watching the grunts except the storm itself.

And had he been on that battlefield, he could have prevented their deaths.

Yet here he stood, arms folded, waiting while men and women who had spent decades cultivating the storm died without recognition.

Inhaling deeply, he glanced at Elder Ghaltris. “How much longer do we have to wait?”

Elder Ghaltris barely tore his gaze from the screen as he replied. “Not too long now. We just have to be patient.”

Ashscar felt a pulse of anger spike within him, but he pushed it down. He was still too new to the workings of his path to say with certainty that he truly could have prevented it. Besides, Elder Ghaltris was a war veteran. He knew what he was doing.

Ashscar glanced back at the screen, reminding himself of those facts. But ten seconds later, another rumble came.

Another ten seconds later, another formation died.

Ashscar swallowed his frustration, his jaw ticking as he reminded himself of the reason Elder Ghaltris was keeping them back. The moment they stepped onto that battlefield, the enemy transmigrants would attack, drawing them into isolated battles and preventing them from affecting the wider war.

It was better if the enemy transmigrants revealed themselves first. That way, they could choose their engagements rather than have it chosen for them.

But with every passing second, Ashscar found it harder and harder to justify their inaction.

“Ashscar?” Aodhán called, a concerned frown on his face. “What’s going on?”

But before Ashscar could respond, another rumble echoed through his mind—stronger than the last two.

More urgent.

Ashscar turned toward the screen pulling at his spirit, and his breath hitched when he saw a massive formation of storm and wind cultivators locked in brutal combat against a swelling tide of blood cultivators.

But this wasn’t just any formation of storm cultivators. It housed over a dozen familiar faces, with Elder Cloudrend at the head.

His underling’s father.

Ashscar’s eyes widened. He glanced at Aodhán, then at Elder Ghaltris, fully aware of how insane what he was about to say would sound, before pointing at the screen.

“If I don’t arrive on that battlefield in the next ten seconds, that formation will cease to exist. And my underling’s father is among them.”

Shock rippled through the room.

Only for a moment.

And then everyone burst into action, startling even Ashscar himself with their blind trust.

“Ready the teleporter now!” Elder Ghaltris shouted, but Ashscar was already moving, Aodhán and the others hot on his heels. No one asked him questions. No one asked how he knew—or whether he was sure.

They simply trusted him.

But Ashscar was too aware of the ticking clock to even process it.

Six seconds.

They arrived at the teleporter just as it flared to life—all six of them. An instant later, the world blurred. Space hummed. Light flashed.

And then suddenly they were on the battlefield—wind screaming past them, rain lashing at their bodies, the heat of the blazing sun pressing down like a physical weight. The battlefield stretched in every direction, vast and brutal and alive with the sounds of dying.

Ashscar didn’t look at any of it.

He found the formation instantly, Mind of Storm guiding his eyes toward a flash of cloudy-gray hair amidst the chaos. The formation was still holding. For all intents and purposes, it didn’t even seem to be in danger.

But the storm had never been wrong in the last hour.

It couldn’t be wrong now.

Inhaling deeply, Ashscar raised his gaze toward the storm and whispered, “I am here now. Use me.”

Energy gathered around him immediately, almost as though it had been waiting impatiently. It surged toward him in torrents, far more than anything he could have handled or generated himself.

But it wasn’t his job to provide.

His job was to herald the storm. To act in its stead. To conduct its will.

And the instant he opened himself, all that energy poured into him—torrential, boundless, far beyond anything his body could have cultivated alone. It flooded his pathways, his core, the marrow of his bones, and still it came, the storm giving freely what would have taken him weeks to gather.

Then, from within him, willpower stirred—slow and vast as a turning tide. It rose to meet the ocean of energy roaring through him, and with a voice like a bell tolling across heaven itself, Ashscar spoke.

“The storm dooms you.”

All that energy drained from him in an instant and a second later, the sky cracked open.

No rumble. No warning. No gathering light or dramatic pause.

Only a single beam of pure white—so blinding it drained the color from the world, so vast it swallowed the horizon whole, so fast it existed and then it didn’t, the thunder arriving only after the silence had already settled.

And where the blood cultivators had been, there was nothing.

Hundreds of Mythics simply gone.

No bodies. No ash.

Nothing.

The earth beneath them had been scorched black in a perfect circle, smoke still curling from the edges, and for two full seconds, every person on that battlefield—ally and enemy alike—went utterly still.

Then they turned toward them. Toward him.

The herald.

Another rumble echoed, but this time, it wasn’t distant.

It was a crack that split the sky itself in two—the thunder arriving a full three seconds late.

Shocked silence reigned within the command center—if only for a moment. Then chaos erupted.

“How is this possible?” Malverin cried in disbelief. “He was the weakest. Ashscar was the weakest. How did this happen?”

“A better question is how he generated such power,” Varkas said, a deep frown on his face. “How did he even manipulate it?”

“We don’t know,” Mournwind cut across them both, his voice firm. “And it doesn’t matter. The storm has released its transmigrants. We must do the same.” He glanced at Aspwick and swallowed. “They’ve had enough time to bond. Release them.”

Aspwick nodded, and an instant later, he was gone, only a faint scent of copper still lingering in the air.

Malverin turned back toward the screen, her breath hitching as another beam of pure white slammed into their ranks, obliterating hundreds of cultivators in an instant.

Shuddering, she thanked the stars that Lysandra hadn’t chosen Ashscar as she’d suggested. At least that much she had gotten right.

Aodhán, by comparison, suddenly seemed almost safe.

But oh, how wrong she was.

Aodhán wasn’t the safer option. He was ruin incarnate.

And he was no respecter of illusions.

***

Aodhán floated high above the battlefield, the scent of ozone still sharp in his nostrils. His gaze roamed over the devastation Ashscar’s attack had caused—the complete obliteration—and rather than fear or revulsion, it was relief that coursed through him.

Relief that Ashscar wasn’t quite as weak as he had feared.

Relief that the storm truly was watching.

That it was speaking.

He smiled then, his figure cutting a terrifying silhouette against the sky as inky-black scales gleamed over his skin like liquid armor. Claws jutted from his fingers, horns curved from his head, and behind him spread a pair of draconic black wings.

Energy flooded his body, a subtle current of animalistic strength coiling through his veins like a barely leashed storm.

Only a few seconds had passed since their arrival on the battlefield. No doubt, their enemies were already racing to meet them.

But Aodhán wasn’t scared.

Not anymore.

He gazed down at the battle below with slitted golden eyes, waiting patiently for his opponent to arrive. In the meantime, he glanced at his status screen.

Skills {Innate}: [Storm Creation and Manipulation] [Lightning Creation and Manipulation]

{Other}: …[Ruinous Baptism] [Ruinous Layering] [Synaptic Ruin]

A lot had changed since he last checked his screen, most notable of which was his advancement to the 50th tier. But beyond that, he had also gained an extra seal, a new title, and a new skill.

Pride of Tempestuous Ruin: This is a title given to a cultivator who has earned the pride of their origin plane, grants a 10% increase in willpower pool and regeneration.

Synaptic Ruin: You have expanded your understanding of authority beyond storms to humans. Focus your will to disrupt, control, and manipulate the neural energy within a target, causing paralysis, blackout, or even a brief coma depending on their willpower.

Aodhán smiled as he reread the skill’s description. He hadn’t tested it yet.

Then again, he had been saving it for the right moment.

And that moment was—

Now.

He whipped around, wings unfurling as the approaching core came within arm’s reach. His claws tore through flesh in an instant, carving jagged furrows through veins, tissue, and arteries.

Blood sprayed. Eyes rolled back. A head flew.

It happened in a blink. Too fast for him to even use Synaptic Ruin. Too fast for him to activate any skill at all.

He had thought the person would defend. Or at least, put up a sort of fight… but it seemed he’d been wrong.

Adrenaline rushed through him like a raging river, disbelief and excitement flashing through him at how easy it had been. He glanced down at the severed head he had just torn free, blinking when he realized how beautiful his opponent was.

He shook his head. “Too weak.”

And though she was, a part of him couldn’t help but feel that the battle had been too easy. Too clean.

Scowling, he looked away from the corpse, his gaze sweeping across the skies as unease crept into him. This person was a transmigrant. Surely, they couldn’t die so easily. Even Crixis wouldn’t have died so easily.

But after several seconds of searching the skies and finding nothing, he turned toward his friends, intending to help them.

Just as he did, a voice echoed behind him. “Hasty, aren’t we?”

Aodhán spun around immediately, his eyes widening as he came face to face with the woman he had just killed—her body whole and perfect while her corpse still lay on the blood-covered ground.

Then, impossibly, the corpse… flickered. And flickered again, faltering as though it had never been real to begin with. The blood on the ground thinned and reversed—

And the woman before him smiled, her core bursting to life as though it hadn’t just died. As though he hadn’t just ripped her head from her shoulders with his claws.

She was whole. Untouched.

Perfect.

Aodhán grimaced.

Of course. He should have known no transmigrant could die so easily.

Inhaling softly, he glared at the woman. “Who are you?”

She smiled. “I am Lysandra Quinn, a transmigrant from Earth, and I shall be your downfall.”

The mention of Earth should have sparked familiarity within Aodhán, perhaps even curiosity, but instead his scowl only deepened, Core Sense across the battlefield in search of how all this was happening.

“How is this possible?” he asked when his core sense came up empty, and Lysandra chuckled.

“What is reality but an illusion that refuses to fade?” she replied, her smile almost gentle. “I would explain, but your simple mind could never comprehend something so… perfect.”

Her gaze hardened. “Not unless you die first.”

She blinked forward then, and before Aodhán could react, she was already before him, her beauty suddenly terrifying as her hand clamped tightly around his throat.

His eyes widened, his body going cold.

An instant later, bone snapped.
