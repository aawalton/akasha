Ning watched the apple-sized flame floating above his palm, bright orange around the edges and gradually deepening toward the center as he fed more qi into it. With a slight movement of his fingers, he compressed the Fireball until it was roughly seventy percent of its original volume.

Reaching Great Accomplishment meant he could freely adjust its size, compression, and release speed, and the hand seals he had once needed were long gone. When Ning tried compressing it further, however, the flame began to shudder.

He held it until the shudder worsened, then dispersed the spell and added another line to the sheet beside him.

Compression stays stable down to roughly seventy percent of normal volume. Control gets worse past that.

This had been his routine for the past several days, and his courtyard was beginning to show the consequences.

Image stones had been positioned around the training ground to record his movements from different angles, several books lay open beneath the shade of a nearby tree, and enough discarded paper had accumulated around the table to make Ning wonder whether Soil Refining could somehow be abused for recycling.

Hey, no one said a technique couldn't be used in a multitude of ways.

More unusual was the stack of yellow talismans held beneath a stone at the edge of the table.

Ning picked one up.

[Mirage Landscape Talisman]

It was a mid-grade First-Tier illusion talisman normally used in combat. The user formed a clear impression of an environment before activation, and the talisman used it to confuse the target's senses. It wasn't sophisticated, but suddenly finding yourself surrounded by flames or standing on the edge of a cliff could still buy a cultivator several valuable seconds.

Ning had bought twenty for an entirely different reason.

He closed his eyes and recalled a forest fire he had once seen in a documentary during his previous life. His memory wasn't perfect after all these years, but he still remembered enough: flames spreading through dry grass, trees burning from the bottom upward, wind carrying sparks ahead of the main fire, and smoke covering the sky.

Once the impression became clear, Ning sent qi into the talisman.

The paper burned away between his fingers, and when he opened his eyes again, the courtyard had disappeared.

Flames crawled through the grass and climbed the surrounding trees as thick smoke rolled overhead. Heat pressed against his skin and every breath carried the dry taste of smoke.

Ning slowly walked through it.

The talisman could only work with the impression he had given it, so Ning didn't take everything around him as fact. He was more interested in immersing himself in the element he was trying to understand. Visualization had always been important to spellcasting, and standing in the middle of a forest fire was probably one of the closest ways to feel the burn.

He watched how the fire moved through the grass and bent with the wind, how separate flames joined whenever they met, and how burning branches scattered sparks when they collapsed. Ning stayed until the illusion began to weaken, and the smoke and heat faded as his courtyard returned around him.

Fenghuang was still perched on the tree branch, far more interested in his spiritual fruit than whatever Ning was doing. His human was always doing something weird, and by now, Fenghuang had simply gotten used to it.

Ning ignored the bird and raised his hand.

[Fireball]

The spell formed almost immediately, and he spent the next several minutes changing its shape and compression, occasionally using [Crimson Flame Control] to divide it before joining the flames together again. Several ideas from the illusion proved useless the moment he tried them with actual spiritual fire, while a few were interesting enough to add to his notes for further testing.

The Fireball had always felt natural to him. Part of that was probably because the idea behind it was so straightforward: gather qi, convert it toward fire, shape it, stabilize it, and send the resulting ball of violence toward whatever had offended you.

It also helped that Fireball was one of the techniques Ning had the most otherworldly knowledge to draw from.

He looked at the flame above his palm.

For one thing, the spell made very little sense according to the fire he had known on Earth. There was no wood, oil, gas, or any other obvious fuel feeding it, yet the flame remained suspended above his palm in a stable sphere for as long as he continued supplying qi.

Obviously, spiritual qi was filling in several rather important blanks, which only made Ning more curious about how much of what he remembered still applied.

He compressed the Fireball slightly and watched the center brighten, then increased the amount of qi without allowing the spell to expand. The flame became hotter and increasingly unstable, forcing Ning to disperse it before finding out whether his face could withstand the result.

His next Fireball was wider while containing roughly the same amount of qi. The flame became less concentrated, and maintaining its shape felt noticeably different.

Ning recorded the result.

Then he tried making it spin. After all, when in doubt, add a spiral. Maybe he'd eventually make a drill that could pierce the heavens.

The first attempt barely rotated. By the fifth, however, Ning had a Fireball spinning rapidly above his palm.

He launched it at the target.

The Fireball flew noticeably straighter and held together better when it struck, concentrating the explosion into a slightly smaller area.

The target was scorched black at the center.

So spinning actually helped.

Not quite a drill that could pierce the heavens, but every great technique had to start somewhere.

[Scorching Purge]

A concentrated wave of heat spread from his fingers toward one of the practice targets. Ning aimed at a patch of moss growing across the wood and watched it quickly blacken, while the surface beneath remained mostly unharmed.

Scorching Purge had never felt as intuitive as Fireball. The difficult part was controlling exactly where the heat went, using enough to destroy the target without wasting energy or damaging everything around it.

Ning tried increasing the temperature while shortening the duration.

The moss burned away almost instantly. This time, the wood beneath it was barely scorched.

"Huh."

Higher heat, shorter exposure.

Ning noted that down as well.

Fireball was still more enjoyable. There was something satisfying about taking whatever fragments of knowledge he remembered from Earth, throwing spiritual qi into the equation, and seeing what survived contact with cultivation.

His gaze wandered toward Fenghuang.

The White Flame Raven was still eating.

"Fenghuang, I need your help."

Fenghuang cawed.

"What do you mean, again? I gave you that spiritual fruit specifically for helping with my experiments, didn't I?"

Fenghuang gave what Ning could only interpret as a bird's version of a sigh before quickly swallowing the rest of the fruit.

"Show me your flames again."

With another reluctant caw, Fenghuang raised one wing and released a small white flame.

Ning concentrated on their connection.

The sensation was difficult to put into words. Ning felt the warmth through his own skin, and under it, Fenghuang's awareness of the flame. The raven seemed able to sense differences throughout the flame with the same ease Ning could tell where his fingers were without looking at them. When one edge weakened, Fenghuang noticed it, and when the flame stretched, his awareness followed the entire thing without any obvious effort.

Then the white flame separated into two.

Ning instinctively tried to follow both.

Fenghuang's perception remained different. Even after separating physically, the two flames still seemed to exist as parts of the same whole in the raven's awareness.

Ning activated Crimson Flame Control and separated his own flame into two. His attention divided between them, and when he tried holding both while recalling the sensation he had received from Fenghuang, one of the flames began to wobble.

A distinct feeling passed through Beast Heart Communion.

Ning glanced at Fenghuang. "Was that amusement?"

The feeling came again.

"You know I control your food supply, right?"

The amusement vanished.

Such was the power of being the one who put food on the table.

Ning returned his attention to the flames. He couldn't reproduce Fenghuang's instinctive control, especially since the white flame came from the raven's bloodline, but the sensation gave him something new to work with.

He added a note beside his earlier observations on Twin Flame before remembering something else.

Fenghuang's white flame had changed slightly when the raven became amused, and Ning had noticed similar fluctuations in the flame before, whenever Fenghuang got angry or excited.

"Do that again."

Fenghuang tilted his head.

"Get annoyed."

The raven stared at him.

Ning considered the problem for a moment, then reached over, took a fresh spiritual fruit from the pile Fenghuang had been eyeing, and bit into it.

White flame flared around the raven.

Ning nearly choked from laughing. "Okay. Definitely something."

Fenghuang pecked him on the head.

"Ow! That's for science!"

Another peck followed, forcing Ning to retreat.

By the time Fenghuang had been pacified with two replacement fruits, Ning felt the amount of compensation demanded had been completely disproportionate to the suffering involved. Still, the experiment had worked.

Emotion clearly affected Fenghuang's flame. Whether human spells behaved similarly was another question, so Ning decided to start with irritation.

He thought about the suffering he endured whenever his master teased him about his novel.

Irritation came surprisingly easily.

[Fireball]

The spell formed normally.

Ning watched it for several seconds before frowning.

Nothing particularly interesting happened.

He tried again while calming himself, then repeated the process while deliberately recalling excitement and anger. There were small differences between the spells, but separating the effects of emotion from ordinary changes in his concentration and qi control proved difficult. Anger actually made the Fireball slightly worse.

Ning watched the flame wobble above his palm.

Perhaps emotion mattered more to the intent behind certain techniques. Fireball was straightforward enough that anger might simply interfere with his control, while a technique built around a particular state of mind could behave differently.

He wrote the thought down.

Something else to test later.

Fire had taken up most of Ning's attention over the past several days, but it was hardly the only thing he had been practicing.

The image stones helped him review his martial arts, Pure Eyes allowed him to examine details of qi that would otherwise escape him, and the Mirage Landscape Talismans gave him another way to strengthen the visualization behind techniques associated with environments he could clearly imagine.

He had already gone through quite a few of them.

Frozen lakes and snowstorms accompanied his practice of Hidden Ice Mist. A spring field filled with young plants had also seemed appropriate for [Spring Breeze Seedling], although it had been so pleasant that Ning accidentally fell asleep for nearly an hour.

Well, mental recovery was also important, right?

Most of these experiments ultimately had the same purpose.

Alchemy.

Fire control was the most obvious connection. Alchemy demanded precise control over heat, since some medicinal materials had to be refined slowly while others required sudden increases in temperature. The contents of a cauldron could change quickly enough that blindly maintaining the same flame throughout the entire process was just waiting for a failure.

Another technique Ning had been deliberately practicing was the Withering & Flourishing Technique.

He had cultivated it for years, so the basic principles were already familiar. Recently, however, Ning had begun practicing on plants of different ages and conditions while deliberately changing the amount and speed of the vitality he transferred between them.

A young spiritual herb didn't respond exactly like a mature one. A plant weakened by poor soil accepted transferred vitality differently from one damaged by insects, while injured roots created their own problems. Even two healthy plants of the same species could respond differently depending on how they had been cultivated.

All of that experience could eventually prove useful when handling medicinal plants in alchemy.

Yes, Ning was min-maxing.

If several of his existing techniques could improve skills he would need for the alchemy competition anyway, then training them together was simply efficient.

By evening, he finally stopped.

Ning poured himself a cup of water and dropped into the chair beside his notes. Fenghuang was now dozing off on the seat beside him.

Ning took a drink. "Panel."

The familiar interface appeared before him.

His eyes moved down the list before suddenly stopping.

[Withering & Flourishing (Mastery)]

Ning stared at it for a moment.

"Finally. Another technique at Mastery."

He set down his cup and reached toward a medicinal herb growing beside the table.

[Withering & Flourishing]

Vitality flowed beneath his fingers.

Ning had performed the same process countless times over the years, yet the difference became obvious once he began deliberately testing it. He could draw out a thin thread of vitality without disturbing the rest, increase the flow smoothly, stop it almost in an instant, or reverse the transfer with barely any hesitation.

More importantly, he no longer had to consciously manage every part of the process.

Ning shifted his attention between several leaves, the stem, and the roots beneath the soil. Years of farming had taught him how plants responded to injury, disease, poor soil, and cultivation, while years of using Withering & Flourishing had taught his hands how to act on that understanding.

Somewhere along the way, his understanding of the Wood element had deepened as well.

Wood qi had once been just another kind of spiritual energy. Now, working with a plant, he could sense how vitality gathered and circulated, how damaged parts recovered, and how a small change in one area affected the whole.

It was still far from anything Ning would call profound insight into the Wood element, but compared to when he had first learned the technique, the difference was obvious.

"Now only Fire and Metal are left."

Once he mastered a technique for each of those elements, he would finally have at least one Mastery-level technique for all five elements.

Ning was looking forward to it.

...

"It's so big."

Ning had known the Great Spirit Willow was enormous. He had seen it in videos, but standing directly beneath it was another matter.

The ancient willow occupied an entire valley within Beast Taming Peak, its massive trunk surrounded by roots half-buried beneath a faint mist of spiritual qi. Countless branches spread overhead, some as thick as ordinary trees, carrying curtains of pale green leaves that swayed in the wind. Even without extending his spiritual sense, Ning could feel the immense vitality within it.

The damage was just as obvious.

Several enormous branches had been severed, while deep cracks and dark scars covered parts of the pale bark. New leaves had begun growing around some of the damaged areas, but many branches remained bare.

More concerning were the faint dark veins beneath the bark.

Ning extended his spiritual sense. Most of the qi surrounding the willow carried the rich vitality he expected, yet something unpleasant still lingered within it.

"Yes. This is the Great Spirit Willow." Fu Xuan stood beside him, her gaze fixed on the tree. "Lei Fu had control of it for quite some time during the incident, and he left contamination behind."

Ning glanced toward her.

"Long Xiao somehow managed to be useful for once and found a method to cleanse most of the physical corruption, so its roots, trunk, and spiritual pathways are recovering normally now. The problem is its soul."

Ning's mouth twitched at the casual jab toward Xiao Fan's master, but he wisely left that alone.

Fu Xuan continued, "We've been removing the remaining contamination from its soul little by little, but that is much more troublesome. The soul isn't something we can treat as directly as its physical injuries."

That much Ning understood.

He looked upward again, following the Great Spirit Willow's enormous trunk until its branches disappeared among the hanging leaves.

"Thank you for giving me this opportunity, Grandmaster."

Fu Xuan glanced at him. "There's no need to thank me."

Ning smiled but didn't argue.

After hearing about the Great Spirit Willow from Xiao Fan, this had been one of the first possibilities to enter Ning's mind. His Soul Ferrying Scripture had remained largely untouched since the Heavenly Origin Tower, and although he had successfully condensed his first Merit Petal after ferrying Lei Shen, he still wasn't entirely certain what heavenly merit was actually doing for him.

Still, accumulating merit certainly wasn't going to hurt.

The problem was that Ning had absolutely no intention of wandering through mountains and graveyards looking for ghosts just because he wanted more of it.

Ghosts were strange creatures in this world. Some were barely stronger than ordinary mortals, while others were remnants of cultivators who had died at Foundation Building, Purple Mansion, or potentially even higher realms.

Going around looking for random ghosts without knowing what he might encounter sounded like an excellent way to become the victim of a horror story.

Ning had already decided on a much safer approach. Eventually, he would issue missions for information regarding suspected ghosts or resentful spirits, have other disciples investigate them first, and only go himself once he knew what he was dealing with.

There was no reason the ancient art of exorcism couldn't benefit from proper scouting.

"Besides," Fu Xuan said, bringing him back to the present, "many people have already attempted to help the Great Spirit Willow. One more attempt will hardly inconvenience anyone."

Ning turned toward her. "How bad is the contamination?"

"Complicated." Fu Xuan lifted her gaze toward the ancient crown. "Lei Fu didn't rely on a single method to control it. There were restrictions suppressing its consciousness, control marks interfering with its ability to resist commands, foreign spiritual energy forced into its soul, and traces of demonic influence mixed throughout the entire process. The Willow also damaged its own soul considerably while resisting him."

Ning frowned. Lei Fu had certainly been thorough, but considering he had been trying to control a tree equivalent to a Purple Mansion cultivator, it made sense.

"The sect has been treating each problem separately," Fu Xuan continued. "The Beast Hall has been maintaining a soul-calming formation to keep its consciousness stable, while the Formation Hall divided the damaged spiritual pathways into sections so the remaining control marks could be removed without disturbing one another."

She gestured toward the faint formation pillars surrounding the valley.

"The Alchemy Hall has pills purging the foreign influence, and the Talisman Hall stabilizes the damaged nodes afterward. The rest will take time, but the Great Spirit Willow has extraordinary vitality. Once the foreign influences are gone and its soul is stable, it should be able to recover much of the remaining damage on its own."

Ning nodded. For something comparable to a Purple Mansion cultivator, a slow recovery wasn't that surprising.

"Lei Fu also incorporated fragments of damaged and resentful souls into several of his restrictions," Fu Xuan continued. "Most of those restrictions have already been removed, but some of the fragments remain tangled with the Willow's consciousness. That's where you come in."

She paused before sighing. "If only Sect Master Ge Xuan hadn't disappeared somewhere again, this would be much faster."

"Nobody knows where he went?" Ning asked.

"He left a message."

"He did?" Ning was mildly impressed. That was considerably more responsible than he had expected.

"It only said, 'Out,'" Fu Xuan replied blandly.

Ning couldn't help but laugh.

Never mind. That sounded exactly like their Sect Master.

Before Ning could ask anything else, a figure descended from one of the nearby watchtowers and landed several paces away. He appeared to be a man in his forties, dressed in the dark green robes of the Beast Hall, with a short beard and a saber hanging from his waist. The spiritual pressure around him was restrained, but Ning could still tell that he had reached Foundation Building.

"Grand Elder Fu." The man cupped his hands respectfully. "I wasn't informed you would be visiting today."

"It was decided rather suddenly." Fu Xuan gestured toward Ning. "This is my grand-disciple, Ji Ning. He possesses a soul technique that may be useful for removing some of the resentful fragments still bound to the Great Spirit Willow."

The man's attention shifted toward Ning, and recognition quickly appeared on his face.

"Oh, I remember you." He laughed. "Ji Ning, wasn't it? The disciple involved in the Heavenly Origin Tower incident."

Ning cupped his hands. "Junior Ji Ning greets Senior."

"Han Zemin." The man returned the gesture before looking toward the Great Spirit Willow. "We've removed quite a few of those fragments already, though some of the deeper ones have proven troublesome. With your current cultivation, you'll have to be careful."

He paused before smiling toward Fu Xuan.

"Of course, with Grand Elder Fu personally accompanying you, I suppose there is little for us to worry about."

Fu Xuan gave a small nod. "I will ensure nothing goes wrong."

Ning maintained a perfectly respectful expression.

This Senior Han is good.

In two sentences, he had praised Ning, flagged the danger without insulting him, and made Fu Xuan the reason everyone could relax.

Han Zemin turned and signaled toward the guards stationed around the valley. Now that he looked properly, the Great Spirit Willow was far more heavily guarded than he'd realized. Several Foundation Building cultivators occupied the watchtowers surrounding the valley, while Qi Condensation disciples patrolled farther out in groups.

Formation pillars had also been driven into the earth around the Willow's roots, each connected by faint lines of spiritual light.

"The purification formation is currently inactive," Han Zemin said. "You may approach."

Ning thanked him before following Fu Xuan toward the Great Spirit Willow.

The closer he came, the more absurd its size became. One of the exposed roots alone rose higher than his head, and when Ning finally placed his palm against the trunk, the bark beneath his fingers felt like the wall of some ancient fortress.

Fu Xuan stood several steps behind him. "You accumulated the Infinite Circle beforehand?"

"To its current maximum," Ning confirmed.

"Use it."

Ning nodded.

The Soul Ferrying Scripture had one rather inconvenient weakness: souls didn't care very much about Ning's cultivation realm. When he had ferried Lei Shen, the remnant soul of a former Purple Mansion cultivator had required far more spiritual energy than Ning possessed, forcing the Tower Spirit to redirect and filter energy from the damaged ritual formation simply to keep the scripture running.

The Infinite Circle gave him another option.

It could provide a large reserve of prepared qi, allowing Ning to maintain an expensive technique much longer than his ordinary reserves would permit.

Ning closed his eyes.

The Infinite Circle spun within his dantian, dense qi flowing around itself in the familiar cycle he had spent so long constructing.

Ning guided the stored qi into the Soul Ferrying Scripture while Know Thyself kept watch over his meridians. Warmth gathered in his palms as the golden mark on his forehead began to glow faintly.

He began to chant.

[Heaven bears the breath; earth receives the bone.]

His awareness sank past the bark and into the immense vitality flowing throughout the Great Spirit Willow. The moment he touched its soul, Ning's breath caught.

Lei Shen's soul had been filled with centuries of hatred. The Great Spirit Willow was completely different. Its presence was enormous, carrying faint impressions of sunlight and rain, spirit beasts resting beneath its branches, and generations of Beast Hall disciples coming and going while the ancient tree remained.

As Ning went deeper, he began to feel the damage Fu Xuan had described. Foreign spiritual energy lingered throughout its soul; some areas felt strangely muted, and traces of old restrictions remained in others. Deeper still was an unpleasant influence that made his spiritual sense instinctively recoil.

Probably the demonic contamination.

Ning left all of it alone. He knew what he was here to deal with.

[Lost souls, see the river; bound souls, loosen the chain.]

The scripture reacted at once.

Fragments of anger, terror, grief, and unwillingness emerged within his awareness, tangled among the Willow's other injuries. Some were little more than lingering emotions, while others still retained traces of broken consciousness.

Golden threads spread from his palm. Ning chose one of the clearer fragments and followed it carefully, tracing where the resentment ended and the Willow's own soul began.

Once he was certain of the boundary, Ning continued the chant.

[The living return to self; the dead return to dust.]

The golden mark on his forehead brightened, and a cold sensation ran through his arm as the first fragment began to loosen.

For an instant, Ning heard someone screaming.

There was no name or memory left, only terror, hatred, and the desperate unwillingness to disappear after being used as material for someone else's technique. Ning allowed those emotions to enter his awareness without mistaking them for his own.

[Clear heart becomes the bank; ferrying mind becomes the lamp.]

A faint golden current appeared within his consciousness. The remnant finally slipped free from the Willow and disappeared into it.

Ning waited for a moment, carefully examining the place where it had been attached. The surrounding soul remained stable.

Good. That's one down.

He moved on to another.

The second fragment came away more easily now that Ning knew what to expect, while the third had become tangled around one of the damaged areas of the Willow's consciousness. Ning examined it for several breaths before leaving it alone and choosing another.

There was no reason to risk damaging the Willow just to remove one more.

He continued like this, only ferrying the remnants whose boundaries he could clearly distinguish. In his awareness, golden points lit up beneath the enormous trunk as one fragment after another loosened and slipped into the scripture's current.

Then the Great Spirit Willow trembled.

The guards around the valley quickly reacted, but Fu Xuan raised one hand.

"Don't interfere."

The enormous crown rustled overhead, countless leaves shifting at once. Ning could feel the Willow's consciousness stirring beneath his palm, though the areas he had treated seemed slightly calmer than before.

He continued for a little longer, but eventually Know Thyself warned him that his meridians were beginning to strain. Maintaining contact with a soul this powerful was already taking its toll.

That was enough for today.

Ning stopped searching for more fragments and completed the final circulation.

[By scripture and crossing, beneath heaven's witness and above earth's embrace, the wandering soul is ferried beyond the river of life and death.]

Golden light spread through the portions of the Willow he had touched before gradually fading away. Ning withdrew his hand, and the mark on his forehead disappeared with it.

For several breaths, the Great Spirit Willow remained still.

Then one of its hanging branches slowly descended and brushed several pale green leaves across Ning's hair.

"...Oh."

The branch withdrew.

Ning wasn't entirely sure what that meant, but he decided to take it positively.

Fu Xuan stepped forward and placed a hand against the trunk. She examined the Willow, then nodded.

"Good. You removed several fragments without disturbing the surrounding soul."

"It's thanks to Grandmaster's guidance." Ning cupped his hands respectfully.

He then checked his Merit Lotus. The first golden petal remained unchanged, but the merit gathered around it had grown noticeably denser. It wasn't anywhere close to forming another petal.

His first had come from ferrying the remnant soul of a former Purple Mansion cultivator, allowing him to skip directly past the earlier scattered-merit phase.

"You also knew which ones to leave alone," Fu Xuan said. "Some of the deeper fragments are too tangled with the Willow's soul for you to handle safely."

"They felt dangerous to touch, so I left them alone."

"That was the right choice." Fu Xuan nodded approvingly. At least her grand-disciple, unlike Ling Xin, possessed a healthy sense of caution when dealing with things he didn't understand.

Han Zemin approached from behind them and cupped his hands. "Grand Elder Fu, are you finished for today?"

"For today." Fu Xuan nodded. "Let the Willow rest. Do not activate another soul-cleansing method until tomorrow. Ji Ning's technique removed several remnants, and its soul needs time to settle."

"Understood."

Ning cupped his hands toward Han Zemin as well. "Thank you for allowing me to attempt it, Senior Han."

Han Zemin laughed. "You helped the Guardian Tree. If anything, the Beast Hall owes you thanks. Come again when Grand Elder Fu believes you're ready."

"I will." As Ning and Fu Xuan left the valley, another faint rustling came from behind them.

Ning glanced back.

High above, countless willow branches swayed beneath the afternoon light, and although many still bore the scars of the battle, the Great Spirit Willow somehow seemed a little quieter than when they had arrived.

Ning smiled faintly before turning away.

He and Fu Xuan left the valley together and began making their way down Beast Taming Peak. For a while, neither of them spoke, and Ning was content to enjoy the scenery while recovering from the strain of using the Soul Ferrying Scripture.

Eventually, Fu Xuan glanced at him. "The alchemy competition isn't far away. How is your preparation going?"

"Pretty well," Ning replied. "I've been practicing most of the things we discussed. I've also gotten much better at letting different stages overlap instead of treating every step separately."

"Good. I look forward to seeing how you perform in the competition."

Ning suddenly felt a little more pressure on his shoulders. "I'll do my best."

"Hm."

They continued down the mountain until Fu Xuan turned toward one of the small rest pavilions scattered across Beast Taming Peak. A stone table sat beneath its roof overlooking the forest below, and she took a seat before casually bringing out a familiar tea set.

Ning sat across from her.

Had he followed his grandmaster all the way here partly because there was a decent chance she would eventually bring out some Third-Grade spiritual tea?

Maybe.

There were some accusations a man simply didn't need to defend himself against.

Fu Xuan poured two cups and slid one toward him. Ning took a very appreciative sip.

"I've also been looking into that time-related material for you," she said. "It should be useful for the Eternal Severance Art. I haven't found a source yet, but I have a few leads."

"Hm?" Ning's cup stopped halfway back to the table.

He hadn't expected that. When he'd shown Fu Xuan his techniques, most of her attention had gone toward the Infinite Circle. She'd asked about the Eternal Severance Art as well, but they hadn't discussed it much afterward.

Fu Xuan replied. "I was surprised the Heavenly Origin Tower entrusted such a technique to you in the first place. Of everything you're currently practicing, it may eventually prove the most valuable."

Ning nodded slowly. "I think so too."

Even the incomplete Season-Reading Eye had already proven extremely useful, particularly in alchemy, where being able to perceive the subtle changes in medicinal ingredients as they aged, declined, or approached the proper moment for refinement gave him information that would otherwise have required far more experience to recognize.

Yet the Season-Reading Eye was only one portion of the inheritance, and compared to the deeper aspects of the Eternal Severance Art, its current usefulness was almost mundane.

The real technique could sever lifespan.

That alone placed it in an entirely different category from almost everything else Ning possessed.

Fu Xuan took another sip of tea before continuing. "I have a feeling the coming years will not be peaceful. Lei Fu's betrayal, the demonic cultivator behind him, the methods used against the Great Spirit Willow, and everything that happened beneath the Heavenly Origin Tower are too many problems to dismiss as unrelated accidents."

Ning's expression turned serious.

"If the Pure Qi Sect ever faces a genuine threat, and you possess sufficient mastery of the Eternal Severance Art by then, I want you to be willing to use it."

Ning remained silent for a moment.

Fu Xuan continued, "A technique capable of directly attacking lifespan is frightening. Some old cultivators would willingly suffer severe injuries for sufficient benefits because injuries can be healed, artifacts can be replaced, and even damage to one's foundation occasionally has remedies if someone is wealthy or powerful enough."

Her gaze settled on him. "Lifespan is different. Even if someone possesses treasures that can extend their life, such things are precious. No one would want to waste them recovering years they could have kept."

Ning understood immediately. "So if some old monster attacked the sect..."

"Even if you couldn't defeat them, the possibility of losing a portion of their remaining lifespan would make them think twice about whether attacking was worth the price." Fu Xuan nodded. "And it isn't limited to old cultivators. Young geniuses treasure it too, because every year lost is a year taken from the time they have to reach their next realm. Depending on how the Eternal Severance Art develops, losing enough vital essence may also affect far more than simply how long someone lives."

"So I'm like shit," Ning muttered.

Fu Xuan's hand stopped.

"...What?"

Ning realized that perhaps he should have kept that particular revelation inside his head. "Nothing, Grandmaster."

"Ning."

He laughed awkwardly and scratched his cheek. "I was just thinking that even if someone could easily step on shit, they would still rather walk around it because they don't want it on their shoe."

Fu Xuan stared at her grand-disciple.

Sometimes, she wondered how both her last disciple and now her grand-disciple possessed such an extraordinary ability to leave her speechless. She had never encountered this problem with any of her other disciples.

Crude as the comparison was, it sorta fit.

Fu Xuan slowly pinched the bridge of her nose and decided to ignore it altogether.

"Anyway, I want you to eventually be capable of defending the sect if the need arises. I'm not asking you to throw away your own lifespan carelessly, either. If you are ever required to use the Eternal Severance Art on behalf of the sect and it consumes your lifespan in return, the sect will naturally compensate you with lifespan-extending treasures."

Hearing this, Ning immediately straightened and placed a hand against his chest. "Grandmaster, you can rest assured. The Pure Qi Sect has sheltered me, taught me, fed me, provided me with cultivation techniques, and given me a place to call home. If the day ever comes when the sect faces destruction, this disciple will naturally step forward without hesitation and shed his blood for the righteous path."

Fu Xuan watched him over the rim of her cup.

Ning continued solemnly, "Even if powerful enemies surround our gates and the heavens themselves turn against us, I, Ji Ning, shall remain an unyielding pillar of the Pure Qi Sect."

Fu Xuan couldn't help but chuckle at the sudden display of righteousness. "That was quick."

Having successfully made his position clear, Ning quietly returned to his tea.

For a while, the two simply sat beneath the pavilion while the distant branches of the Great Spirit Willow swayed above the forest, and Ning was beginning to think the conversation had ended when Fu Xuan suddenly seemed to remember something.

"Oh, right."

Ning looked up.

"There has been some discussion about an event roughly six months from now. Several sects are planning a competition for younger Qi Condensation disciples, and the Pure Qi Sect has been invited."

Ning's hand paused around his cup.

Fu Xuan continued, apparently unaware of the terrible feeling that had suddenly entered his heart. "Considering how much attention you've attracted recently, your name will almost certainly be considered. It would also be useful experience for you, so I think you should participate."

Ning was already suspicious, but one detail mattered more than anything else.

"Who else is going?"

"Your friend Xiao Fan has already signed up."

Ning fell silent.

Was it too late to suffer a serious injury that would conveniently take a year or two to heal?
