Chapter 28

“The window is closing. Sixty generations have watched this room. I am the last to watch it in darkness. The next will watch it burn.”  - Codex of the Long Watch, Watcher’s Commentary

The falcons hit them fifty metres from the riverbank entrance.

Arthur heard the first one before knew where to look. All he got initially was a tearing sound, like fabric ripping at speed, followed by a concussive snap of air displacement. Something sliced the space where Steve's head had been a half-second before he had flinched sideways on instinct.

"Down!" Donna barked.

Arthur raised the Bullguard Shield and looked up. Above them, against the violet sky, shapes were circling. Six birds were riding the thermals above the Embankment in a staggered pattern, each positioned to cover the blind spot of the bird ahead.

He recognised the birds a second later. Peregrines.

London's falcons had nested on the Tate Modern chimney and the Barbican towers for years. These were the same birds the way the Mithraeum bull had been the same species as a cow. They had doubled in size with a wingspan of about six feet and chitin edging along the leading feathers turned each wing into a blade. Their talons gleamed like polished steel hooks.

And they were terrifyingly fast. This was fast in a way that made the foxes look old granny with a walking stick. The fastest animal on Earth had been given Ether and had become something that owned the sky.

"Post-mutation raptor variant," Archie said. "Stoop velocity exceeds baseline peregrine by approximately forty per cent. The chitin modification on the leading edge is consistent with offensive adaptation. Shield orientation at two hundred and seventy-three degrees will provide optimal deflection against the dominant attack vector."

The second stoop came from behind. Donna's Reflex Acceleration fired and she read the dive angle and hauled Steve sideways by his pack strap. Talons raked the air where his shoulders had been. The falcon pulled up with a wingbeat that cracked like a whip.

"Coordinated stoops, they are taking turns. Door forty metres ahead. Move together and keep tight." Donna said in a tight, flat voice.

Arthur raised the Bullguard over his head. The shield's surface area covered his skull and shoulders. "Donna, under my left. Steve, tight on my right, keep the Reacher up, catch them on the pull-up."

They shuffled as one, hurrying towards the door under the shield’s mobile roof.

The third one stooped at Arthur's flank and he managed to turn the shield just in time. The falcon struck the Bullguard at full dive speed which was now about four hundred km/h if what Archie said was correct. The bird bounced off the surface, dazed and Steve drove the Reacher down through its wing joint before it recovered.

Two more dived in rapid succession. The fourth dived a few metres away from the party and then levelled out, coming in low at ankle height, its talons targeting tendons. Donna's riot shin guard caught it, and the bird's angle deflected it upward into the arc of the Donna’s axe that was already coming down to meet it.

The fifth hit Arthur's stab vest at the back, between the shoulder blades, and exactly where the rigid panel sat. The impact drove him forward and talons screeched across Kevlar without penetrating. Earlier that day, those talons would have opened his back to the spine. Arthur twisted, swung the shield flat, and caught the bird against the river wall. He followed up with his hammer for good measure.

ETHER ABSORBED: 37

The remaining three circled and then started climbing, spiralling until they were specks in the sky. Three of the six were gone, the cost exceeded the reward. The predatory logic for juice-isn’t-worth-the-squeeze.

Donna hurried to the concealed door, fingers finding the pressure point. The panel swung inward.

"In. Now."

They ducked in and took a few moments to steady themselves. Predators falling from the sky was something they had not calculated before. Donna seemed especially pissed off about it.

But they were alive and the gear had worked.

"Thirty-four, forty-one, thirty-seven. Variable yield means different saturation levels, the larger ones absorbed more ambient Ether." Steve said, pulling out his guidebook. "Experience isn't uniform within a single species. Individual variation matters. This was the third such instance in the overworld, after the foxes and the dogs. I feel fairly confident the variation is Ether."

“Overworld?” Arthur raised an eyebrow.

“Working nomenclature for non-dungeon areas.”

Donna simply shook her head.

As they reached the transit chamber, familiar blue light washed over them. The platform sat in the centre, its dark stone glowing and waiting for them to step-on. Arthur set down his pack and sat against the wall instead.

"We rest. A few hours at least. Then we go."

Nobody argued.

***

Arthur slept harder than he had since the pub and dreamed of buildings.

Whole ones. Structures that held their loads perfectly, every compression or tension exactly as designed, nothing wasted, and nothing failed. The dream of an engineer who'd spent his career studying what went wrong, finally seeing what right looked like.

He woke to Steve shaking his shoulder.

"Arthur. Look."

Arthur sat up looking around for danger but found nothing. The chamber was as silent and as empty as it was when he had gone off to sleep. Then his mind made him aware of the absence. The pain was gone. He pressed his fingers and could still feel where the cracked rib had been, a ghost of pressure on his left side. But the rib itself was solid, knitted in his sleep.

Donna was rotating her shoulder freely without wincing at the movement.

"The chamber healed us," she said as an assessment.

"Accelerated," Steve said. He was examining his ankle, flexing it experimentally. The limp was gone. The stiffness remained but the swelling had disappeared entirely. "Our Constitution is doing the healing, but I think the chamber's Ether density just cranked up the speed.

And then Arthur looked at the message hanging at the corner of his eye, waiting for acknowledgement.

Arthur pushed himself up. The pull towards the platform was a low, active pressure, as if the stone had registered that he was finally awake and standing. Arthur chuckled to himself at the absurdity of the thought.

He walked to the platform and the concentric rings brightened before his boot reached the outer edge

The discharge hit before any of them knew what was happening. A nova of ether, rolling outward from the platform. Donna braced against the wall, Steve went down to all fours and stayed there.

"Archie," Arthur said, when he could finally speak. "What the bloody hell was that?"

"The platform authenticated your Nadi Matrices against the planetary transit registry.”

“And it gave us…..gave us six-hundred ether!” Steve was incredulous

"Six-hundred and seventeen. First activation of a portal node by a verified candidate is a singular event. The discharge accompanies the handshake. Subsequent transits through this chamber will not produce comparable absorption."

"A one-off bonus," Steve said. He'd already started writing. "For being the first through."

"The calibration is not a bonus or a reward. The node held latent Ether against the possibility of authentication. Authentication has occurred. The reserve is released."

"It's still a free level, though," Donna said.

"The Grid would not characterise it as free. It is not based on an accounting system."

"What about other nodes? When we activate them?" Steve asked, hunched over and scribbling furiously

“The activation sets the flow of Ether in the transit network. The other nodes will not have to store any. Ether is now available in the environment."

"We allocate before we step on that thing. I want us at full capacity for whatever's on the other side." Donna said.

Arthur went first. The restructurings were easier now; at level seven his body had become accustomed to the process. It was still painful. It still took about forty-five seconds for each point, but now it was more a discomfort than an emergency.

He started with four in Strength. His grip tightened and he could feel his muscles in his shoulder grow, his frame felt denser. He tested the lump hammer's swing and the weapon swung through the air with effortless ease. His attack damage was lagging behind, and he needed more strength to bring the full potential of the hammer to bear.

Then he put one in Dexterity. This was a new sensation he had not felt before. The restructuring felt more like his body was tensing up than the feeling of reinforcement he had with Strength and Constitution. A subtle acceleration in the neural connection between intent and movement and the difference between getting a shield up in time and not.

"Dexterity? That's off pattern." Steve said with a raised an eyebrow.

"The guardian threw me into a column because I couldn't reposition. A wall that can't turn is a wall that gets flanked." Arthur flexed his hands. "I need some give in the structure."

The last one went in Constitution, and he felt the familiar deepening of reserve and endurance. The sense of a body that could take slightly more punishment before something gave.

Donna looked at him for a long moment then she gave him a slow, genuine nod. A nod recognising growth in a person she'd been trying to protect from himself. And maybe a little relief.

Arthur paused in front of the platform; he could feel the three nodes above pulsing, and they would keep doing so whether he was around or not. That was the nature of infrastructure. You build it, and it keeps carrying the load after you’ve left.

"Archie," Arthur called. "How does this work?"

"The candidate steps onto the platform surface. Physical contact with the transit medium initiates conduit interface. The Grid identifies the next sequential node and engages transfer."

"And we just... go where it sends us."

"The conduit network does not offer route selection at your current authorisation level."

"We don't know what's on the other side. We don't know if there's air, or ground, or a hundred creatures waiting in the dark." Donna looked at the platform.

"We don't know if this thing scatters our atoms across the Channel or reassembles us inside a wall. This seems like a daft decision."

"We go together. If we're doing this, we do it as a party." Arthur said.

"I've been walking towards this my entire life. Every dig site, every symbol, every pattern I couldn't explain. It was all pointing here." Steve looked at Arthur. "I'm ready."

Donna checked her axe again and then her pry bar. She checked the straps on her vest and the security of her pack. Controlling what she could.

"If we die," she said with an exhale. "I'm blaming both of you."

The team stepped onto the platform and the concentric rings brightened. Arthur felt the Ether density spike, a pressure that closed around the platform and his body.

The white centre flared and the Grid took them.
