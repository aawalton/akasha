Thankfully, the inside of the dungeon wasn't as unpleasant as the outside. Sure, the others complained about how unhygienic it looked. Lia and Aura, for the most part. Me and Crudia had to trudge through a swamp on the first floor, so we didn't mind it much inside of the dungeon. The aesthetic of our surroundings did a complete 180 though.

“Really? A fire dungeon on an ice island? How original.” Mark commented.

Talisha asked about how we were doing, and he quickly responded to her, telling her about where we found ourselves. The walls weren't anything special, just black rock, but it immediately began falling off. I panicked for a second before I realized it wasn't the rocks that fell off, but ash that stuck to them.

"Well, this is unpleasant," I said, looking around. Honestly, I wasn't sure about what I liked less. Fire or ice… Decisions.

But after a few minutes of being outside of here, I was pretty sure that this actually won out. The ocean where I came from wasn't anywhere near this hot, but still it was more warm than not, and I already got used to the heat somewhat. The fire breath on the end of the last floor definitely helped with that too.

"I don't see anything," Lia said, looking at her map. "Not a monster, not a player, nothing."

"Crudia, could you please go invisible and scout the area ahead?" Mark pointed forward.

"Sure thing, boss! Be back in five!" She gave him a thumbs up and ran forward, disappearing just before she took the first corner. Before us was a single path, not a four-way one like in the Legendary Dungeon before. I liked that.

Just go forward and beat whatever is in front of you. No second guessing, no thinking about whether you'll have to go back and choose a different path later. If only everything in these dungeons could be so simple.

"That's really convenient," Aura said, scratching her chin. "Do you think we can learn an ability like that?"

"It's from her racial abilities, but I think you should be able to learn something similar from rogue class if you were to take one." Mark had a reply ready.

"Hmm, I don't think it would fit well with my racial abilities, and I just hope to make them stronger."

"Honestly, this is probably the best way to go around doing this. As much as the gods say that there is more freedom now that you can choose your classes. I kind of disagree," Mark mused, hovering towards where Crudia went. He peeked beyond the corner and then returned.

"Why do you say that? Do you think I couldn't be a mage if I wanted to?" I said.

"You want to be a mage, Shrimpie?"

"No fucking way. I just asked if I could be one if I was a wuss that wanted to fight from a distance." I bit my tongue, but if the bird ladies got offended, they didn’t say anything.

“Yeah, that figures. Well, you could theoretically be a mage, but it would take some time until your class abilities catch up with your bullet strike. But you'd have more chances to upgrade them. That means that you'd be a bit weaker for the second floor, but by the third or fourth you would catch up, and if it suits you more, then you could fight better. It's not that complicated.”

“Why can they just make all the race abilities and class abillities equal?”

“Are you seriously complaining? You, with your bullet strike?”

“Dusk's ability seemed pretty similar in strength.”

“Not really. Kind of. Bryga had her own share of strong abilities too, and Crudia. Well, I've never seen someone else go invisible, though we haven't met many other players.”

“Fink kind of went invisible?”

“Kind of. Yeah, there's a lot of rules. They had some of these things explained in the books, but they used alien animals, and I had no idea what they were.”

“Really? You couldn't have just compared them to us?”

“The types of animals that exist on some other planets don't even exist on Earth. There were some that weren't even carbon-based life forms.”

“I have no idea what you are talking about.”

“Actually, you lost me too,” said Lia, who sat on the ground a few feet away. I didn't even notice her listening.

“Well, it's not important to all of you. I'm just talking because I'm nervous.” Mark chuckled, looking at the path before us again.

I opened the chat, but Crudia didn't say a thing. Still, I was beginning to be worried too. Thankfully, after a minute, she came rushing back, appearing out of thin air on the corner she disappeared from.

“Hey guys!”

“Thank the gods I thought you just disappeared on us,” Lia said sarcastically and stood up, giving Crudia a soft smile.

“Well, found anything useful?” Mark asked, and we all listened intently.

“Actually, yes! There's a lot of monster corpses. When I analyzed them, it showed me that they were level 30 or so; some of them were bigger. There were a lot of them.”

“Good, we can all get you all to level 25 then. Anything else? Wait, corpses?”

“Yes, corpses! That's what I wanted to tell you: there's another player team. It's located a bit further out, but they are standing still. I counted three players.”

“Another player team! Good. We could fill out our team requirements quicker than expected.” Mark thumped his fist in the air. Why the hell did he do that?

I began walking forward while the others discussed how exactly we should approach this. What was there to discuss even? We would just meet them, add them to our group if they were strong enough, which, if they were in this dungeon, probably meant they were. Then we would go and complete the dungeon, kill the boss, get ourselves to level 25 at least, and move on from this damned freezing island. It was that easy.

"Shrimpie, wait, god damn it!” Mark shouted, but I pretended not to hear him, turning the corner. I expected the whole dungeon to look exactly like the room we entered from: rocky walls, a bit hot, maybe some fire monster on the very bottom. Instead, I found myself in front of a bridge. It was a wide one, the red tinted rocks even having protrusions at both sides, as if they served as railings.

Underneath, glowing, flowing magma was bubbling and sizzling. There were a few monsters here, lying on the bridge, all of them dead. They looked like snakes with wings. Oh, and they were black and orange, like this dungeon. In place of a few were just blood spots indicating that somebody looted the bodies.

The others followed behind me, shouting for me to slow down. The bridge, thankfully, didn't fork either, but disappeared into another tunnel on the other side. Because of Crudia exploring, I could see inside the tunnel as well. There was a fork there, but before it split in the opposite directions, there was a small place where apparently the players settled. I didn't see their dots yet.

“Wow, this is really cool,” Lia wandered, looking around.

“Cool? This is anything but cool. God, it's so hot in here,” Aura said the opposite as she looked around with a wary expression. Crudia, for her part, seemed to be just as excited about everything as always. Honestly, that's what I loved about her: she always made bad things seem a bit better than they were.

I began walking to the other side, and we made it there quickly. Well, too quickly. I half expected something to jump at us from the magma below, but nothing did. The monsters here either worked in a way I didn't understand yet, or the team before us killed them all before we could.

I wondered whether dungeons regenerated their monsters. They should, right? Otherwise, everyone would simply be competing for the XP on this floor, which, while it would fit the gold to watch us struggle to get any XP, I figured would kind of get stale after a bit. We would have to be careful when going back as well.

We entered the small cave on the other side, and here it looked exactly like the small room we first found ourselves in. There were indeed three players sitting around a small crafting table. They jumped up once they saw us come in, drawing their weapons. Why have them on your belts? Just materialize them, weirdos.

“New players? Now this is a surprise! Hello there, ladies and gentleman.” The centermost man smiled, showing off his two protruding teeth. His eyes and his neck were covered by scales. The other two players were not too dissimilar. One of them had enlarged eyes and slimy skin, and the last one was thicker than the other two combined. There seemed to be chitin on her skin, though it was duller than my colors. A poor imitation of me. I immediately filed the muscular woman as someone I disliked.

“Hello there! We saw you three players here and wanted to ask whether you want to join us on clearing out this dungeon! We're trying to get a gang together for going down to the next floor.” Crudia barked excitedly.

The snake man nodded just the moment I used analyze on all three of them.

All were levels 22 or lower, they didn't reach the max on the last floor. A snake, frog and a beetle. That told me nothing. Them not being level 24 was strange. I thought it was kind of too easy to reach. I actually would probably be on the current maximum level if the cap didn't exist. They must be hidden away for most of the first floor; there was no real need to gain XP to pass to the second.

“Clear the dungeon. Hmm, that's interesting!” He turned away from us and it looked as if he was sorting through his inventory. What was he doing?

“Yeah, join us! With you three, it would be seven of us, and we have two more friends. We would just need one more player, and then we would be ready to go to the next floor!” Crudia added.

Something was off. Mark gave me a look, and I nodded.

“Hmm, that works. We have lost two of our friends as we walked into this dungeon. Maybe we could share inventory and talk strategies? I see that you also have a guide with you somehow. How? All of ours disappeared.”

The two other players remained quiet. Looking at them from where they stood, they seemed almost prepared to strike at any moment. They didn't trust us. Fine; I didn't trust them either. This whole situation felt really uncomfortable. The man kept clicking something on his menu as well.

Something was really off. I switched my vision focusing on heat signatures. I didn't notice it before, but there weren't only two paths. I opened the menu and let the others know.

Shrimpie: I think there’s another path in front of us. Hidden by a spell.

There was a third one right where the man stood, and beyond it were more heat signatures, staring at us, holding something. They appeared as dots on the map then, showing as more players.

Mark: Lia, kill him!

Before I could register what happened, the snake man in front of me suddenly got impaled by dozens of feathers, all hitting him in the head, neck, or torso. He slumped on the ground, dead.

"It's a trap!" Lia shouted, and suddenly all the other players appeared from beyond the wall, their weapons drawn, attacking us.
