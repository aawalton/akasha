Next door, Ezra could hear Adonis going through books, obviously researching a bunch of different subjects preparatory to this mission. Over the past month or so, since Ezra had started working as Adonis’s guard, he had gotten used to being the person sent to the door for whenever snacks, new books, or whatever else were requested. Tonight, if Adonis asked for something, he’d bring a little bell in his room to make sure that Ezra was the one going to the door, just in case there was some danger presenting itself. However, Adonis’d said he had other things to do and wouldn’t be requesting anything. So, Ezra had the night.

Once his armor was placed on the mannequin, Ezra, with shaking hands, carefully tore open the envelope. Inside were two sheets, carefully folded and filled with Tilly’s neat handwriting. The fact that there was so much made Ezra smile, and he didn’t need to wait any longer.

Ezra,

It’s been so long since last we spoke. Honestly, since you’ve been so good (this fucking time around) about sending letters, it’s hardly felt like as long of a separation as it has been. I must apologize for my own delay in response. As I have no doubt you are aware, these letters are not a small expense to send, and though Mabel is a wonderful teacher, she’s an Acolyte and has precious little in the way of monetary wealth. In addition, this is an apprenticeship, not any sort of job, and I’m not paid except for when I take on extra duties.

As such, I’m now writing in the middle part of summer, where the season truly arrives. No longer is it merely summertime, but the scorching light constantly forces my face to flush. You always teased me about how pale I am, and how even a few minutes in the sun makes me burn, and I am sad to tell you that this has not miraculously changed in the past year.

Ezra couldn’t help but laugh at that, as the summers that he and the rest of his friends had complained about in Villy were blessedly mild. Here, however, many kilometers north and closer to the desert, the heat wasn’t just uncomfortable, it could be deadly. In the pressed streets of Brayton, the heat could literally choke the life out of the unfortunate. Ezra largely didn’t participate in patrols any longer, but he did have occasion to step out of the garrison and the Duke’s manor. When he did, he was reminded of how lucky he was to largely live in buildings whose construction had been careful to account for the heat of summer.

He continued reading.

Whenever possible, at the end of the week, I try to visit the New Dragon’s Toenail, to see the Madam and everybody else. They all ask after you, of course, and I share your letters with them. Adam has officially left the army behind, though he briefly considered joining. I can’t remember, had he begun speaking about that before you left? Well, regardless of if he had, he no longer cares to be beholden to the commands of the Count and the general. Is it the general? I can’t remember what the name is there. You hardly talk about them, and I never met the leader except for in passing. I only remember Georg, who I have seen a handful of times since your departure. He comes to visit Mabel, and asks after you.

He’s a good man, and I’m grateful you were so lucky as to be under his command when you were here.

She didn’t speak much more about Georg, but Ezra felt his jaw tighten as she spoke so highly of his old captain. His promises that he wouldn’t be jealous or upset if she had found a lover had already rung hollow in his heart, and now he couldn’t deny that, perhaps, he felt some measure of possessiveness towards her. What he wasn’t quite sure was if it was a romantic interest or simply a more fatherly or brotherly feeling. Regardless, he was glad that she spoke no more about Georg, instead mentioning everything that was happening in the New Dragon’s Toenail.

He skimmed past snippets of updates from each person back home, finding himself laughing as she explained that Adam had found a girl he was absolutely smitten with. At least, that’s what she said, and as he figured was at least half of the truth. Tilly had always had a greater interest in which person had greater romantic interests than the next, while Ezra wasn’t sure how much of it was just her making things up.

Evidently, the Madam was doing well, her business growing even beyond its previous stature. Something about the northern quarter of the slums was serving her bar well, though the south was no longer the entirety of the Blade’s territory. Now, they spread until their control encompassed over half of the slums in totality.

Ezra felt himself smile and worry through each new piece of information that Tilly gave. The first half or so of the letter’s two pages, front and back, was a reporting of what was happening back in Villy. This person had moved house, that person had gotten pregnant, and so on and so forth. The most interesting thing to Ezra as he read it was that, for the most part, he didn’t really consider Villy to be home. At least, his only home. Now, Brayton in general and the garrison specifically were at least as much home to him. He stopped, wondering, what exactly the reason for that was, instead continuing to read once Tilly moved on to what he could consider to be more interesting things.

The more time I’ve spent under Mabel’s instruction, the more I’ve realized how much of a damn genius you are. By the way, fuck you! Apparently, it took you about as long as I’ve been under Mabel’s care to become an Adept, and here I am, having not even entered the first circle. Then, you wander around, saying something along the lines of how you’re not too shabby, but nothing interesting. You made me think that the second I learned what a spell was, I’d be calling lightning down from the sky! Instead, I can barely light a candle from across the damn room! And half the time, I accidentally burn the entire damn thing up because, I guess, I’m more well suited to brute force than to delicate workings.

Fuck you again, for rubbing off on me like that.

Ezra laughed again, shaking his head as he shifted his position on his bed to continue reading. That sounded like a good thing to him, but Tilly spent a full paragraph explaining how, because of the way her connection to the world worked, she was better at pushing a large amount of mana around instead of moving it specifically. Apparently, that meant that she might be able to go further than other people but her path might be more difficult? As he wasn’t sure, based on her explanation, but it seemed like it wasn’t really a good or a bad thing, just her nature. He mentally labeled her “affinity” as brutish and continued reading with a smile.

I’ve been hopeful that I would have progressed to be a sorcerer of the first circle by now, to have been able to send you a message in my own voice. Unfortunately, that, too, is a delicate working that I lack the finesse to be able to do. I am, according to Mabel, just a few weeks away from truly establishing my first inner circle. I can’t imagine that you understand much about magic, but I guess you could call it very similar to the ascension that you needed to go through to become an Adept instead of an Initiate. Nobody around here has a particularly good grasp on how the path to Sainthood works, so maybe my comparison’s stupid. For me, it’s pulling the mana in the world into myself and inscribing a permanent circle on my heart. There’s quite a bit of technical mumbo-jumbo to make that work, but I can’t imagine that you would care to know the specifics.

For the first time, Ezra found he would have liked the “technical mumbo-jumbo”, but it would seem that he was out of luck. Tilly had a great understanding of him as an individual, but this was one thing in which his actual inclinations were different.

Finally, he drew to the final lines of her letter, a faint sense of disappointment coming over him as he reached the end of the final page.

I’ve been busy. It’s a good busy, not kind of busy a street rat gets into, where you work and you hustle all day, you nick something there, you fence something there, you buy some food, you take care of your own, and by the end of the day, you’re exactly where you were in the morning. No, I’m busy here, and at the end of the day, I can see how I’ve become better than the day before. It’s a glorious feeling, really, and I couldn’t be more grateful to you for it.

Thank you for listening to what I said about what I wanted to do. For looking to see if you could get me this apprenticeship. You’ve changed my life dozens of times, and I can’t help but feel like everything I’ve been through until now has brought me to this moment. And becoming a sorcerer? I feel like I’m… home. You know? Learning about magic, connecting with the natural world and feeling mana all around me… I’ve always felt small. You know, we all do, we ain’t shit compared to anything, the world will chew us up and spit us out and won’t even notice that it did. And being surrounded by mana, feeling it now, I still feel small. But I’m a part of it. It feels like maybe I matter.

But maybe I’m just being a poetic little shit. Who knows. What I do realize is that I’m swearing so much in this because I feel like I’m not allowed to swear here in the chapel and the Madam doesn’t want us cursing either. So, you’re getting all my “fucks, shits, damns,” and so on. I miss being around you. Feeling like I can be myself around you.

Fuck. This is a lot mushier than either of us were ever willing to be when we were together, but maybe I’m finally brave enough to say some of the things I’ve kinda always wanted to, now that you can’t laugh at me, or reject me. At least, not anytime soon.

Can’t wait to see you, whenever that happens. Mabel has gotten some premonition that that might be sooner than I think, but considering I was thinking it might be five years from now, that doesn’t mean a whole lot. Write me back soon, I can’t wait to read your letters. Sorry if I made it awkward.

Tilly.

There was something smudged like words that had started to be written and then blocked out, above her name. Ezra wasn’t going to try to figure out what that was, because that was a recipe for a world of more misunderstandings. Instead, he smiled, reread the letter, and carefully placed it in his pouch. Then, though he wanted to put it off, he instead turned to the small desk in the corner of the room. He’d written several letters there at this point, and he took a good hour to painstakingly write as neatly as he could, reporting that, unfortunately, this would be his last letter for the foreseeable future.

Finally, when his candle was burning low and his eyes drooping, he retired to his bed, prepared for the days that separated him from leaving on this newest assignment.
