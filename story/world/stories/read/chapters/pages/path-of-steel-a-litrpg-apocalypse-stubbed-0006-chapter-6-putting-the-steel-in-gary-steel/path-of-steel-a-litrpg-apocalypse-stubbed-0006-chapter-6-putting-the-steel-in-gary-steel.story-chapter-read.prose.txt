The jester stood a foot behind Gary. They were almost face-to-face. Its hand was buried in Goran’s back, and the man’s face as he stared at Gary was drenched of color. His eyes held only horror.

In Gary’s mind, that moment stretched forever. The jester was right there. He could take Gary’s heart in an instant.

Yet, the jester only pried Goran’s heart. It ran back to its cart, and Gary broke off the man’s death grip to sprint in the opposite direction. His feet thundered against the ground. He jumped over logs and rocks. So fast was he going that he almost tripped by himself.

At some point, when he realized he wasn’t dead, he dared look back. The jester stood by its cart a long distance away, wiping its hand clean. It stared at Gary, then at its cart, then back at Gary, and made a gesture as if wiping its tears. It then grabbed the cart handles, turned it towards Gary, and rolled it slowly.

The rest was a blur. Gary didn’t know for how long he’d run. All he knew was that, when he regained his faculties, he was on the ground panting so hard he thought his lungs would escape. He couldn’t hear the creaking or jingling. Looking around, he only saw trees.

He had escaped—but at what cost?

Gary ran across the street and dove into the coffee shop. “Clara!” he yelled.

The girl gave him an odd look. A couple people in line for coffee jumped in place. Gary felt awkward as hell.

“Yeah?” she asked.

“Sorry, I just…” He trailed off.

“Would you like some coffee?” she asked, her voice soothing.

“Uh… Yes, please,” Gary replied.

There was no line, so he walked to the counter and received his hot paper cup. It burned his palms. “Ouch,” he said, reaching for his wallet.

“No no,” Clara said, stopping him.

“What?”

“You don’t pay with money today.”

“How do I pay?”

She smiled sweetly. “Gary, can I have your heart?”

“Uh… Sure. I mean, you’re really pretty, and I—”

“Thanks!” she said, reaching into Gary’s chest and gouging it out. It beat in her hand. Gary looked down at the bloody hole and felt his body going cold. Crimson liquid fountained out. The only hot part were his palms, still burning by the coffee.

When he looked up, Clara was wiping her hands. She gave him a sweet smile, then her face warped into that of a jester. She grabbed a hammer from under the counter.

“Try to run,” she said. “I’m begging you.”

The coffee burned so fucking much.

Gary woke up in a cold sweat, finding that he lay on his belly with his hands death-gripping a mass of grass. The dirt dug into his palm, birthing a burning sensation. He rolled over and grabbed his chest, panting heavily.

“I think I almost had a heart attack…” he muttered.

The forest was eerily quiet around him. He thought it was sunrise for a moment before realizing it was actually the sunset. I slept through the afternoon? In the open?

He shot to his feet, clenching his fists in preparation, and the sudden gear shift made him double over and vomit on the grass.

“Perfect…” he muttered, wiping his mouth. Despite his readiness, the forest was peaceful as if trying to convince him everything that happened before was a lie. That monsters didn’t exist. That the jester hadn’t ripped out the hearts of three people.

“I can’t drop my guard,” he told himself, scanning his surroundings for signs of an ambush. Birds chirped on the treetops, while the soft breeze caressed his shirt. The grass blades swayed. Somewhere in the distance, he could hear bagpipes.

It’s the damn squirrels again. This is all real, he thought. Suddenly, despite his nap, he felt exhausted.

Gary sat with his back against a tree and opened the chat.

Gary Steel #00301: There are boss monsters, and they are overwhelmingly powerful. Do not approach no matter what. I ran into a clown who gouged out the hearts of my companions. I am alone now. I repeat, do not engage the bosses no matter what.

Crazy messages scrolled out his, but not immediately. The chat had slowed down. Gary hated to think what that meant.

Marla Jackson #87390: Gary, I’m sorry for your loss. Don’t lose hope. If you’re scared of that boss, just change zones. It won’t be able to chase you. Keep your head up, stay safe, and find a group to join. There are tens of thousands of us out here. Together, we are strong. We can survive.

He hadn’t expected a reply. Gary sat up straighter, sending his reply almost instantly.

Gary Steel #00301: Thanks, Marla. I appreciate it. What do you mean, change zones?

He waited, but no reply came. Maybe she missed his message in the fast-flowing chat. Maybe she was busy. Five minutes later, he threw back his head and sighed, then rubbed his palms all over his face.

“I mustn’t lose hope…” he muttered. “That’s easy to say. I want to leave this place. Take me back to Earth. The Tower, the Network, that robot in the white room—can anybody hear me?”

Silence was his only answer. Gary shook his head.

“I’m going to die,” he said with certainty. “Maybe not today. Maybe not tomorrow. But I am going to die, because there are monsters everywhere and I will eventually get unlucky or make a mistake. No human can beat that jester. Everything was in vain.”

He turned off the chat and just sat there. The breeze kept him company. There were no thoughts in his mind, only a tired resignation, as he closed his eyes and let the world take him. When he finally reopened them, the sun had fully set, and the forest was entombed in eerie shadow. A full moon lit up the sky.

“I don’t want to die,” he said, then got to his feet. Nothing had changed inside him—the tired resignation was still there, but in the silence, something else had also surfaced. The desire to do his best, even if it was pointless.

The desire to fight this.

Anything to get away from the damn clown.
