Our first hint that something had gone wrong came as we were passing the first tower that had collapsed. We had chosen to go along the right side of the lake, as we made for the northern shore of it. This was the half that both Bleddyn and Olag’s contingents had ventured along, with the latter presumably having vanished into the origin of the collapse. We had hoped that, against all odd, we would find Olag, Maria, and all of their soldiers waiting for us in front of it.

In a way, we did. Just…not how we hoped.

The walkway before the first collapsed tower was oddly clear, and even stranger, the actual doorway into it seemed to have been cleared. From what we could see, the actual inside of the tower was nothing more than shattered stone through which a path had been created. But that didn’t actually seem like a good thing.

Over a dozen different corpses were lying on the path before the broken remains of the spire, and I recognized them.

These were my people.

Members of Olag’s Raven’s Beak…and of my own Order. All of them were arranged in neat lines near the ‘water’line of the lake in oddly neat rows. They had fixed into resting positions with their legs held closely together, with their arms crossed over their chest. Even their eyelids looked to have been closed, and their faces settled into expressions of peace.

And yet, for all of that…

They looked to have died gruesome deaths. Each and every one of my soldier’s chests had been torn open, and their hearts ripped out.

I stared grimly down at the tableau before me, my companions at my side, while Renauld knelt next to one of them. One of the partisans of my Order, in fact. An older archer by the name of Yarrow who I’d shared a few watches with.

All I could do was stare at the infuriatingly tranquil expression on his aged, creased, blood-stained face, as Renauld examined his corpse with clinical efficiency.

For once, the chatter in my tripartite mind was silent. Almost frighteningly so.

Finally, the Gnoll stood up to face us with a frown on his matted, furry face. “It should be obvious, but that was the cause of death,” He said, nodding to the empty cavity in Yarrow’s chest. “It wasn’t done afterwards, and as far as I can tell, that’s the same cause of death for all of them. Just…bam. A single blow that ripped out their heart and killed them in an instant.”

As I suspected. The blank expression on my face didn’t budge, as I continued to stare down at the man who had chosen to venture north with me.

All in the insane hope that we could stave off the end of the world.

…he’d had a granddaughter, I remembered vaguely. That was why he had come with us. The last bit of his blood in the world, someone whom he had chosen to risk it all to save.

I’d…make sure she was taken care of, after.

…after.

Bleddyn broke the ensuing silence. I didn’t look at him, but I could hear the frown in his voice. “Did Olag do this? Set ‘em all up like this, afore movin’ on? It’s like they’re all sitting in a morgue, waitin’ for burial…”

I slowly shook my head. “No,” I said lowly. “This isn’t Olag’s style. I could believe he and Maria would have tried to give the fallen some dignity, but…”

“This isn’t dignity,” Sylvia continued, eyes narrowed as she considered the scene. “This is ritual.”

The words settled on us heavily.

Like always, my love knew my thoughts just as well as I did. I’d suspected the same thing.

Olag and Maria hadn’t done this.

Whoever they had run into at the spire had.

Presumably, another Rendarch, if they had been overpowered to this extent.

“They were captured,” Bella said bluntly. “Whoever did it, did this. Probably to cow the rest o’ them.”

Some of the listening soldiers shot her a dirty look at Bella’s tactless statement, but I understood. She wasn’t trying to be intentionally callous. I had known Bella long enough now to realize she wasn’t any less upset at this waste of life than I was. She was just…blunt, to put it kindly.

I think I surprised many people when a mirthless smile appeared on my lips, and I nodded. “Yes, that’s likely. Which means Azarus isn’t the only Florens we’re here to save anymore.” I shook my head and raised my voice. “Move out! There’s nothing more we can do for them but to avenge their memory.”

Quietly, my orders were followed, and our small army of soldiers, Classers, and warriors marched out once more. Whoever had captured Olag, Maria, and their men had cleared a path through the rubble, heading in the exact same direction we were going.

How kind of them to leave such intelligible tracks for us to follow.

I’d be sure to repay them for it.

……………………………..

Finally…we reached our destination. The journey was made in near complete silence. Nobody knew exactly what awaited us once we reached the central spire, upon which even now a titanic serpent was impaled. Its heart still beat, and sound was still obvious in the blood-soaked air of Karag Korvok, but it seemed…muffled, somehow, as we approached the actual source. Even beyond the lake area, it felt like the entire defiled Hold was somehow holding its breath.

I’m not quite sure what I expected our path to the central spire to look like, but it wasn’t the form it ended up being.

On the shore of the lake, there was what looked to be a single, blood-soaked plinth. The stone itself was nothing special; it was nothing more than a simple column. However, the top of it, roughly at eye level, had been hollowed out to leave a basin at the apex. Rising from the center of it was what seemed to be a replica of the very spire that loomed out of the center of the lake before us.

I frowned down at it.

This wasn’t quite what Serekh had told us. The path to the tower should have revealed itself to us when we simply reached the northern shore with one of the Rendarchs in tow. And according to Fade, the ‘Judgment’ he had placed on the archivist wasn’t something you could lie through.

It’s not like we didn’t have a Rendarch, either. The limbless torso of Draskul was sitting oddly still, from where Bleddyn had tossed him down before crossing his arms and standing off to the side.

I cut my eyes down to where Fade was sitting on the step below me, from where the dais rested. “What exactly did Serekh say about getting into the tower?”

The Spirit Wolf tilted his head to the side, visibly considering the question. “Well. You asked, ‘How do you get into the Heartspire?’ And he said, ‘My presence will bring forth the bridge’. Apparently, it should work even if he were dead.”

My frown only grew deeper. “Yes, but…” I trailed off. How? That was the question, I was just realizing. Mysticality had rules. Causes and effects. How was the ‘presence’ of a Rendarch being detected to create a bridge, even if they were dead? Presumably, it was being done through some kind of detection Ward. If it were an Aetherial signature acting as a passkey, then you would need to be alive for it to do any detecting.

Wait. There was one thing.

I examined the column one more time, lowering my head to stare at the replica of the spire rising from the bowl.

“Son of a bitch…” I muttered to myself.

That wasn’t some random column. This was a plinth. A Ward plinth.

And that stone spire was a Ward Stone.

The field it was emitting was weak. Very, very weak. In fact, I’d say it was localized to the stone itself, and probably only reacted to…

Touch.

I groaned aloud, standing up.

“Nathan?” Sylvia asked curiously. I shot her a tired smile to show I was alright, before looking over her head.

Meia Itzelan raised a single, curious eyebrow at me in response.

“Meia, get over here and take a look at this,” I sighed irritably, motioning her closer. “Hopefully, I’m wrong.”

All eyes turned to her, but the Half-Orc woman wasn’t fazed. Instead, she did as I bid, and once she was close enough to the plinth, she did the same thing I had, bending down to study it.

I wasn’t surprised when she noticed the same thing I did, only quicker. She stood back up, and I was probably the only one to see just how hard she rolled her eyes.

“How quaint,” Meia nearly sneered. “I see the solution, and see why this one-”

She lashed out with a kick directed right at the floating ribs of Draskul. The Rendarch didn’t even flinch from the impact.

Instead…it sounded like he started to laugh.

At least until I stepped in, aiming another kick at his jaw this time. I’d pulsed Might of the Wyrdwood just high enough for it to matter, so his jaw snapped from the force.

That stopped the laughing clean.

Meanwhile, Bleddyn made an audibly irritated noise. “Alright, what’s the problem? Quit keepin’ quiet about it. As much fun as it is to whale on this idiot, it don’t help us find the others any time soon.”

I grimaced and nodded. “Alright, I’ll explain. I don’t doubt that this, in some way, leads to the central tower. The problem is, the way is locked in a manner that the Rendarch we interrogated obfuscated. He was technically correct, in that all you need is the presence of a Rendarch to open the way…”

“But only technically,” Meia continued in a disgusted tone. “As far as we can tell, what actually opens this particular lock is a particular key. This type of Ward scheme is typically referred to as a ‘padlock’ type. You ‘key’ a stone to where its effects are only activated in the presence of a particular runic string. If the right sequence isn’t brought into contact with the stone, it doesn’t activate.”

“Some people inscribe the string on an object, some keep it on a piece of paper, and some, as I suspect…” I trailed off irritably, staring down at Draskul. “You can tattoo it on yourself.”

The people standing around, who weren’t as well-versed as Meia and I in Abjuration, followed my gaze down to the limbless torso of the Rendarch.

Who very notably did not have any kind of markings upon his hardened, waxy, exposed muscle pseudo-flesh.

Maybe it was my imagination, but I would have sworn that Draskul looked a bit smug. A very hard feat to accomplish with your lips literally seared shut, but somehow he was doing it.

I kicked the creature one more time for good measure.

“I could…” One, young brave soldier in the crowd took a hesitant step forward. “Look under his loin-”

“No,” Bleddyn said in a tone that brooked absolutely no argument. “Ain’t gonna happen. Nobody is demented enough to tattoo that.”

I wasn’t the only one to visibly ponder that possibility, judging by the sudden cringes all around me. All of them of the male persuasion.

“I’m not so sure of that…” Renauld muttered disparagingly.

Meia heaved a sudden, irritated sigh. “Cease your sniveling; that’s not where it would have been anyway.” She cast a disapproving gaze out at the gathered warriors, and more than one avoided it. “Judging by the wear on the spire replica, you’re meant to grasp the tower-”

Renauld snickered, only to yelp when Sylvia slammed a hard punch into his shoulder.

The Sorceress just ignored them. “-which implies the key sequence is meant to be on the owner's hands. Or even the back of the fist would be enough. Conspicuously, this beast has been relieved of his arms.”

Bleddyn held up his own hands to ward off her piercing stare. “Hey now, he was tryin’ to kill us wit’ those,” He said defensively. “I don’t think I saw any tattoos on those ham hands. Anywho, they should still be in our tower if we need ‘em-”

A rumbling echoed out over the lake, and every head around us turned in the direction of the noise. We all watched in silence as one of the very last towers that still stood around this grotesque lake suddenly cracked and began to crumble. The process really was interesting to watch from a safe place, I had to admit. The north shore was very far from the rest of the remaining towers. The way such a tall, thin structure gradually fell to pieces…

Well.

I wasn’t likely to forget it anytime soon.

It was only after the tower had fully collapsed into itself that I broke the silence. “Was that-”

“Yup,” Bleddyn said dully. He didn’t even look at me.

“And the arms were-”

“Yes,” Sylvia sighed.

I scratched my chin, wincing at the thick stubble I found there. “Well, damn,” I eventually said. “Guess there’s only one thing left to do now.”

I outright unbuckled the supply pouch I carried at the small of my back and brought it in front of me to rummage around in. It took me several minutes of sorting through various potions, alchemical pills, and even raw ingredients, but I finally found it.

A small, inscribed, runic bident.

Meia’s eyes lit up at the sight of it, and she tapped her chin in thought. “It’s a risk,” She said slowly. “But we’ll have to take it. Outright shattering something like this could have odd effects, but the possibility of bypassing the key is there. Plus, it’s not like there’s wild Aether in here to risk spawning monsters.” She shrugged. “I say go for it.”

In my hands was the original artifact I had based my design for the Bond Breaker on.

A Ward Breaker.
