Xavier flew high inside the large cavern within the Void Dungeon. Five Class C7 Ragatnis burned bright bellow him, flames engulfing them as they each readied a Lava Death attack. From the walls and ceiling, countless undead, grasping hands the size of a grown man would soon cleave through the stone and reach their dirty, bloodied fingers toward him.

The dragonkin grinned, activating a spell pattern he’d manifested inside his body.

A wave of temporal energy shot out from him, enveloping every enemy being in the vicinity bar one. Four Ragatnis stood frozen, their limbs, heads, and even their flames unmoving. The countless grasping hands never made it through the stone—he felt their energy there, beneath the surface, but if everything went to plan, they would never come out now.

The remaining Ragatni raged, stamping each of its massive hooves at the end of its six thick legs. As powerful as it was, it wasn’t able to make the walls of this place shudder. Xavier couldn’t hear the sound of those stamping hooves but he could see the soundwaves travelling through the air.

The instant he’d come around the corner to face these Void Dungeon-created beasts, he’d switched off both his hearing and his spiritual sense. No way was he going to suffer from the same mistake he’d made last time.

The Song of the Departed wasn’t going to get to him.

It angled its hammerhead shark-like head upward. A stream of flame flowed toward him. Xavier soared downward, easily avoiding that stream of flame. Energy pulsed from him in a wave, freezing the flame midair. He didn’t quite know how physics allowed for such a thing—the tongues of the flame froze and then fell toward to the stone, where they would shatter on the ground, but not before Xavier had reached his enemy.

He didn’t use a large bone spear, though he had in many of his other fights against the Ragatni, especially against that first one. They made for a good weapon, but they were more like a chisel. He still wasn’t strong enough to simply slam one of his bone spears through these thick skulls, he had to use a bone hammer to finish the job.

The ice wave that pulsed out of him slammed into the Ragatni, stopping it in its tracks, though he knew that wouldn’t last very long.

Xavier came to hover directly in front of the Ragatni’s large, flat head. He peered into the rage-filled eyes, burning with hate even through the sheen of ice that covered them. Xavier was getting a sense for how that hate felt, and he knew there was something behind it. It wasn’t merely the Void Dungeon beasts that hated him—it was the dungeon itself.

He wasn’t sure how he knew that, but he did. He wondered if his Ancestral Soul had anything to do with imparting that knowledge toward him, to the point where he’d tried contacting the soul again. It had been some time since he’d bothered to do that, as it hadn’t spoken to him since he’d discovered it.

At least, it hadn’t spoken to him in a way that he’d understood, a way that he’d heard. He was sure the Ancestral Soul was still influencing him in some way…

Wherever this knowledge—or perhaps hunch, he supposed—had come from, he wasn’t sure what to do with it. Did the Void Dungeon hate only him? Or did it hate every being who walked through its floors and killed the beasts it created?

But isn’t part of a dungeon’s purpose to lure people toward it?

Then again, he couldn’t say that all dungeons were the same. Natural dungeons had a certain level of sentience that artificial dungeons lacked. In fact, many thought they were just as much a “person” as any other sapient being out there.

Maybe this dungeon just wanted to be left alone—maybe that was why it had come to the void in the first place.

Unfortunately for the Void Dungeon, Xavier couldn’t leave it alone. And, honestly, he didn’t want to.

I want to defeat this thing. I want to find its core and feed off it. The power it must hold, considering how much each of these beasts holds… It would drag me so far forward in my Cultivator advancement, maybe even right to the end… I would return to my universe so much more powerful than I left it.

Hovering in front of the Ragatni, he grew a weapon from each of his arms, arms that were angled toward the Void Dungeon-created beast’s eyes. The weapons were… well, they looked like giant needles. Thin, hollowed out bone with incredibly sharp tips.

The tips of the needles easily pierced the ice, then reached the centre of each of the beast’s eyes. They kept growing, puncturing the rage-filled eyes of the Ragatni and pushing onward, through the eye sockets and toward the brain.

The first few times Xavier had fought one of these beasts, he’d killed it the slow way, ramming a bone spear with a massive bone war hammer straight into its brain. It had been effective, but… inefficient. And certainly inelegant.

While there was nothing elegant about inserting bone needles into the beast’s eyes and straight through the hollow part of its skull toward the brain behind them, it was certainly efficient.

The beast’s roar of agony was frozen by the ice that trapped it as the needle reached the soft tissue within its skull.

Simply inserting two bone needles into its brain wasn’t enough to kill it, however. It was powerful enough that it would be healing the wounds even as they were caused, over and over again. But this wasn’t all Xavier had in mind.

They were needles after all.

He activated another of the spell patterns he’d manifested inside himself. Energy flowed through the needles, straight past any defences the Ragatni had, and into its brain. The two streams of energy reached for one another, congealing together. This was the same type of energy that he used to grow his bone weapons in the first place.

It created a ball of bone.

He couldn’t see it, but he could picture it quite clearly in his mind.

The ball wasn’t very large, maybe the size of a walnut. It was lodged roughly in the centre of the beast’s brain. When it had finished growing and had received enough energy from the needles to do what he needed it to do, Xavier slipped his arms away, the needles sliding out of his skin and remaining inside the beast’s brain, sticking out of its eyes.

Three, two, one…

The walnut-sized ball of bone exploded inside the beast’s brain. It wasn’t a shrapnel explosion, but an explosion of spikes. There was a loud thud, coming from inside the beast’s head, as the spikes shot out from the centre of the brain and slammed into the insides of the skull where they were stopped. Some of the spikes weren’t stopped, however. Several came out of the beast’s eyes, while same came out under its massive jaw.

The light inside the beast dimmed and died. Energy flowed from the beast and straight to Xavier. He breathed it in, feeling the strength of it, and cycling it into his Reality Core. He rolled his head around his neck with a satisfying crack and looked at the first of the five Ragatni, dead and frozen inside the ice he’d conjured.

It had never even been a threat to him.

He didn’t stand there staring at it for long. Xavier thawed the beast and worked on removing the Void Dungeon Mini Core from within it. When he’d retrieved the mini core, he deposited both it and the beast into his Storage Ring. Then he moved onto the next Ragatni, freeing it from its temporal cage with but a thought, and freezing it in ice with another.

He repeated the process on every single one of the beasts until they were each dead and he’d retrieved each of their mini cores, depositing their corpses into his Storage Ring.

He walked out of the now-empty cavern with a spring in his step.

Maybe the Void Dungeon won’t be as hard as I thought…

Before he stepped around the next corner, he reactivated his spiritual sense and his hearing. He might be feeling a massive surge of confidence, but that didn’t mean he was dumb enough to stop being cautious.

His strategy might work perfectly well against the Ragatni, but there was a massive chance he would soon encounter something here that was far more powerful. Besides, though he hadn’t seen it yet, he wondered if the Void Dungeon would soon start adapting to his actions. It was intelligent, after all, and if the feeling he got from the beasts’ eyes was true—that the hatred was coming from the Void Dungeon itself—he was clearly being closely watched.

Though so far, he hadn’t seen any sign of adaption whatsoever. While that reassured him, it also made him think he might very well be missing something.

I suppose I’ll cross that bridge when I come to it.

Pushing out his spiritual sense, he could feel another cavern with another five Ragatnis.

Not exactly inventive.

As much as he was enjoying his time in this dungeon, he was beginning to wonder if it would give him something a little more interesting than fighting one beast after another.

At least the floors of the Tower of Champions had a lot of variation…

This still wasn’t quite what he’d expected when the ancient Wanderer Derrida had told him she would be introducing him to the wider multiverse.

He’d thought there would be… more.

Then again, he supposed this was just a test. Once he completed it, he was sure something more would lie on the other side. As long as he was able to survive the dungeon, of course.

Slaying the Ragatnis in the next cavern proved just as easy as slaying those in the first one had been. He didn’t employ the exact same tactic. Xavier had an unimaginably massive number of different options at his disposal, and while it would be far easier for him to use the same solution to the same problem twice, doing so wouldn’t exactly stretch his abilities, or his creativity.

If he was going to become a complete natural at altering reality around him, he needed to experiment. Besides, he was sure the approach he’d taken defeating these enemies wouldn’t be able to work on every enemy. There were few enemies so susceptible to being frozen in ice that he could simply stand there and insert needles into their eyes.

He also thought the attack, while effective, was a little too… well, unnecessarily gruesome.

There were a few attacks he decided not to try. He was fairly sure he could have figured out a way to blow up a Ragatni’s core, but he didn’t want to destroy the Void Dungeon Mini Core, he wanted to harvest it, which was a little difficult to do if it was exploding in his face.

Instead, he tried some ranged attacks.

There was no reason he needed to actually enter the next cavern at all. He could stay behind at a safe distance and fight these Ragatnis with spells. His spells could easily curve around the corner and reach them, after all. He’d considered creating drones and sending them through but figured that in this instance that really wasn’t necessary.

He still used the wave of temporal energy to freeze the Ragatnis he wasn’t attacking first—that was a no brainer. Yeah, it wasn’t as creative as coming up with something new, something that could stretch his mind and his skills, but sometimes being cautious was more important.

The final Ragatni noticed its brethren frozen and instantly got on the move. It surged forward through the cavern, pounding on its six thick powerful legs, flames bursting forth and burning around it. It was the first time Xavier had really seen one of these things move in a space it could actually fit inside.

Not that he could see the beast now. It was his other senses that had told him everything it was doing. He smelled the flames. Felt the slight vibration of its movement through the floor, and saw the soundwaves as they reached around the corner.

Just as before, his spiritual sense and his hearing were deactivated.

Xavier didn’t come here with a plan, though many possibilities surged through his mind, including a failsafe—or ten—should things go wrong. He wanted to hone these new instincts in battle. Wanted to see what he could come up with when he was under pressure—even if it was essentially artificial pressure.

A surge of energy flowed from him as he manifested and cast a spell pattern. He’d barely had to think of what he wanted to do, he’d just done it. Hell, his conscious mind wasn’t even 100 percent sure what was about to happen, it just had the gist of what he wanted.

Xavier wanted to see this with his natural eyesight, and so he stepped around the corner and watched as the energy coalesced roughly fifty feet in front of the enemy. The energy took the form of a large, transparent door. The door took up the entirety of the cavern, and there was no way the Ragatni was going to get to Xavier without passing through that energy first.

Xavier tilted his head to the side in interest at the Ragatni, watching as, without slowing its run, it reached the transparent door made from energy. The instant the Ragatni touched it, the energy brightened and the Ragatni disappeared.

Then the door shrunk, until it was the size of a normal door you might find on Earth. It solidified, looking as though it was made from nothing more than ordinary wood, and stood on the stone floor of the cavern.

There was a large thud from inside the door, making it shudder, but it didn’t budge.

Xavier smiled. He walked toward the door with his hands folded behind his back, giving it an appraising look. The Ragatni was still alive, of course, but now it was trapped inside a spatial storage device similar to the Roving Seed Base.

He touched a hand to the door and closed his eyes, connecting his mind to it. He was able to see inside the spatial storage device and found nothing more than a large, stone room with no windows. The only door inside the room was this one. The room was large enough for the Ragatni to get a small run up.

It slammed into the door again, making it shudder a second time, but it wasn’t powerful enough to break through to the other side.

Xavier grinned.

I can’t believe that actually worked.
