“Not that I’m opposed to helping by any means,” Shia said, looking around the room curiously. “But why exactly am I here?”

“Ovord believes that as a Druid of Bonds, you will be an important link in helping him, myself, and Lumel all connect with one another,” Vin explained, sweeping aside the large banner that had been laid out for him to work on as he prepared the large table for Alka’s body. “We need to utilize his soul magic expertise, my Beyond the Veil class ability, and her Divine Decipherer Capstone all at the same time. This is a lot of guesswork and a lot of hoping for the best, but I think we stand a serious shot. With my Friend of Mana Capstone, in theory, the mana itself should fill in any blanks in our plan with its sheer desire for something new to happen. This is probably the first time anyone other than the Gods has attempted to reconnect a soul that had once been a part of the System.”

“See, that makes a lot of sense,” Scule said, raising an eyebrow from his position atop Shia’s shoulder. “…But why are we here?”

Reginald squeaked his agreement, tilting his head curiously as he watched Vin prepare the table.

“The two of you have your Magical Bond passive,” Vin continued. “An ability that is literally designed to allow you both to share in the magical abilities of the other. That’s exactly what we need to replicate in order to allow Ovord to benefit from Lumel’s Divine Decipherer, so I figured giving Shia and the mana a clear-cut example of what we need to do wouldn’t be a bad idea.”

“It’s sort of wild that our team has all the pieces we need to potentially pull off something like this,” Shia said, beaming at all of them as she looked around the room. “If this works, this will be revolutionary. Reconnecting a soul back into the System without their flesh and blood body… I don’t know if something like this has ever been done before. At least not on my world.”

“It would certainly be a first for my people,” Lumel said, nervously wringing her fingers together while Vin finished up the preparations. “I’ll try and do whatever you need, Vin, but I don’t want to get anyone’s hopes up. Divine Decipherer is a complicated Capstone. I mean, it’s deciphering the workings of the Gods. I don’t know how much use Ovord will be able to get out of it with only a few minutes.”

“It’s alright, we don’t necessarily need to succeed today,” Vin said, giving her a reassuring smile. “That said, Ovord has been peeking behind the Godly curtain for a couple of minutes each day this past week in preparation. It sounds like he has a decent-enough grasp of what he’s working with. The problem now is fully understanding it.”

“Indeed. Should this attempt at joining your team’s various abilities work, I believe we will shave entire months off of this process. I am prepared when you are.”

“Ovord says he’s ready,” Vin said. Rubbing his hands together excitedly, he turned toward Alka. “Want to give it a shot?”

“Something about bringing the whole team into this makes it feel a bit weirder,” Alka muttered while she helped Epli down from her helmet and placed the schlime off to the side. That done, she turned and hopped up onto the table, her glowing green eyes dimming as she stared up at the ceiling. “Screw it, do your worst. I want to level again.”

“How exactly do we go about starting this?” Scule asked. “Do we all hold hands and start singing? Dance in a circle around Alka’s body and set it on fire? I’ll admit this is my first cult ritual, I’m sort of getting excited!”

“I was sort of hoping Shia might have an idea,” Vin admitted, turning to the Druid of Bonds. “Thoughts?”

“I think we’d start similarly to how I’ve already worked to make bound artifacts for some of you,” Shia admitted, directing Scule and Reginald to take up positions on the edge of the table. “So yes, we should all take hands. And don’t worry, Lumel, my progress on preparing your bonded artifact is almost done. Yours has been pretty tricky, but I think you’ll be pretty excited when you see the finished product.”

“I can’t wait,” Lumel smiled, taking Shia’s and Vin’s hands in her own. Vin took her hand and Reginald’s paw, Reginald grabbed Scule’s, and Scule completed the circle with Shia. The five of them stood in a ring around Alka’s prone form, and the Slayer tilted her head this way and that to take them all in.

“Alright. This is getting weirder by the minute. I feel like I’m about to be some sort of sacrifice.”

“She figured it out! Quick, get the knife!” Scule shouted, grinning as Shia rolled her eyes.

“As if we could even get a knife through her golem body,” Shia muttered before taking a deep breath and giving Vin a nod. “Holding hands is all I’ve got. We’re not all going to be imbuing our mana into Alka’s body as if she were a bonded artifact we were trying to create, so now it’s up to you and Ovord. Do your best.”

“I’ll certainly try,” Vin said, mentally preparing himself to try and do the impossible once again. “Ovord, you feeling ready for this?”

“Of course. The conditions are better than we could have hoped. You have put together quite the impressive team.”

“And I want to ensure none of them are injured or changed during this in the slightest,” Vin whispered. “I’m expecting to trigger my divine boon from drawing the mana’s attention on me again, but that’s fine. Just make sure none of the others are affected.”

“Your desires will be followed, my Lord.”

Vin paused, debating whether or not to put this entire ordeal on hold to address the word choice of the soul mage living inside him. After a few seconds he shook his head, deciding there would always be time later. Taking a deep breath of his own, Vin slowly began borrowing mana from around the room, doing his best to focus on the people gathered beside him.

He felt Lumel’s uncertainty around whether or not her Capstone would be enough to bridge the gap Ovord required to get Alka reconnected to the System. Shia’s eagerness to see just how strong their bonds had become after all these months of adventuring alongside one another. Scule’s curiosity toward what they as a team were capable of doing. Reginald’s love and desire to see his friend succeed in her wishes. Alka’s nervousness at being the center of attention for something unrelated to fighting for a change. He even felt Epli’s honest hope from off to the side that its newfound friend and protector would become what she always dreamed of becoming. The strongest warrior in all the world.

Emotions and feelings swept into Vin’s core while he breathed in the mana of the world around him, and he steeled himself as he accepted each and every one of them. Holding onto those different feelings for a second longer than necessary, he then turned them into pure mana. Everything was mana, and everything was connected. Each of them, regardless of race, class, or experiences, were unique, and yet they were also the same. Their entire beings could be reduced to nothing more than simple mana, and that meant they shared a second bond even deeper than the ones they’d formed as friends over the months of wandering Edregon’s surface with one another.

All Vin needed to do was tap into that bond.

“Ovord, get ready,” Vin managed through clenched teeth. Already he could sense the attention of the mana on him as he continued borrowing more and more from the world around him. “Here we go!”

With that, he threw control of his body over to Ovord, mentally gasping as he recoiled to the back of his head. Without missing a beat, Ovord released Reginald’s and Lumel’s hands and leaned forward, Vin’s own hands going ethereal right before the Arch Mage of the Soul plunged them both into Alka’s body.

Just like before, Alka’s green eyes exploded with light, filling the room with a blinding, ever-present green that seared into the back of Vin’s retinas. Yet even through that, he could see more than ever before.

Unlike the last few times Ovord had taken over and utilized his Beyond the Veil ability, this time, Vin was able to see exactly what the soul mage was looking at. Despite not having a body at the moment, Vin shook while his mind tried to piece together what stood before him.

An impossible tapestry of lights, sounds, and emotions sprawled out of Alka’s chest, somehow filling the entirety of town and yet managing to remain contained within the Slayers golem body all at the same time. And beyond that, as if hidden just below the layer of reality itself, was what could only be the new System.

Vin stared in awe at the planet-sized runic formation that extended outward in all directions as far as the eye could see. The Goddess of Benevolence had given him a peek at the corrupted old System many months ago during their first meeting, and the similarities were obvious. Other than the fact that nothing looked like it was corroded and falling apart, mana was actively flowing and powering the new System, running through trillions of runes all at once and controlling immeasurable calculations behind the scenes every second. The fact that they were trying to mess with such an impossibly vast working hit him all at once, and Vin felt despair like no other settle over him. He might as well have handed an infant the schematics to a nuclear power plant and told them to find and tighten a specific bolt hidden deep within.

But before that layer of despair could settle, Vin blinked when his neck was cracked for him, and Ovord flexed his fingers. He could feel his body smiling, and Ovord chuckled softly to himself.

“Oh yes. This I can work with.”

To Vin’s shock, the former Arch Mage reached deep into the tapestry of sensations and experiences making up Alka, isolating a single strand connected to the center of her entire being and nimbly plucking it out. It was only then Vin started as he realized that all of them seemed to have an identical strand. He, Shia, Scule, and Lumel each had a thick strand of mana stretching straight from their cores deep into the heart of the new System. Reginald had a smaller one connecting himself to Scule, which also seemed to almost be twisted in a way that didn’t quite make sense to Vin. It didn’t have to, however, as he realized there were identical twisting bonds currently connecting him and Lumel that either Ovord or the mana itself must have created at the start of all this in order to allow him and Ovord the use of her Capstone.

With Alka’s strand in hand, Ovord all but dove into the new System beneath them, somehow sifting through miles of runic formations without moving a single step as the magic and mana swirled around them. Certain runes glowed gold and shined brighter than others, seeming to make a convoluted path for Ovord to follow. It took him a moment, but Vin quickly realized he was seeing what could only be Lumel’s Divine Decipherer in action as the level 50 Capstone helped them make sense of the new System. Though even with that aid, it was only thanks to Ovord’s experience and the sensation of the mana itself pushing them along like a wind at their back that they managed to arrive at their destination.

A nucleus of souls. A glowing core of dense mana stronger and more powerful than anything Vin ever had or ever would experience. Millions of thin tendrils of mana stretched out from it in all directions, each one leading to someone up on the surface of Edregon. He somehow knew even just this faux proximity to the core of the new System was dangerous, and he willed Ovord to hurry up with what he was doing. Already the soul mage was on his third minute, and Vin didn’t dare to think about what would happen if he snapped back into control of his body in the middle of all this.

Thankfully, Ovord didn’t seem to have any intention of drawing things out for dramatic effect. The soul mage was as clinical and precise as ever. The moment they reached the nucleus, he stretched forward, tracing the very thread attached to Vin and moving to place the tendril of mana he’d retrieved from Alka’s soul directly into the new System beside Vin’s own. In that moment, brilliant chains of gold flickered into existence around Vin’s form, snapping taut and threatening to tear him apart. Yet before they could do so much as slow Ovord down, they dissolved, the dense and eager mana swirling around them in a brilliant storm devouring each golden link just as quickly as it appeared. With the last obstacle out of the way, Ovord gently placed Alka’s tether against the nucleus of souls, connecting her back into the System.

The moment he did, a flash of brilliant white light washed through the world, and then everything went dark.
