I looked at the three sisters, then at the strange landscape surrounding us.

"So, could you please explain what exactly you have done to me?" I asked, lowering my eyes to the translucent umbilical cord tying me back to my physical body.

I considered asking them how they had ended up as three people sharing a single body, but stopped myself. This couldn't be a normal thing that happened to Soulit people. Either this was some unusual condition they had been born with, or two of the three sisters had somehow lost their original physical bodies and ended up sharing one.

If it was the latter, that was probably not something they would casually discuss with a stranger. It was better not to derail the conversation when I barely understood the basics.

"We simply removed your soul from your body," Na'a said with a grin. "Don't worry. We can easily shove it back in."

"That is a very concerning way to describe it," I said.

Ri'i floated around me in a slow circle, studying me from different angles.

"I must say, your self-image matches your body impeccably."

"What does that mean?"

"Mostly that you are neither narcissistic nor depreciative of yourself, and you don't appear to suffer from any form of bodily dysphoria."

"I guess that's a good thing."

"Certainly," Ri'i said. "For your day-to-day life, it is one less thing to worry about."

"But when it comes to Soul Shaping," Na'a continued, "it could be a symptom of a rigid, unshakable soul."

As she spoke, her body suddenly changed.

She grew larger, stretching until she became a towering giantess. Then she shrank down until she was barely larger than a child. Her form twisted again, bones and flesh reshaping themselves until she became a small cat floating in the air.

Then, just as casually, she returned to her original shape.

"Wow," I gasped. "You can shape your soul into whatever you want?"

"Only Na'a can be that extreme," Ri'i sighed.

"Oh, don't be like that. We each have our strengths." Na'a pointed toward Ma'a. "Ma'a is a Soul Seer, and you are a Soul Dealer."

"aren't both of those talents something you are born with?" I asked. "While Soul Shaping is something every Soulit person can train?"

"The three of us went through the same training." Na'a shrugged. "Maybe their birth talents made it harder for them to keep up with me, while I wasn't born with any advantage."

"Or maybe your inborn advantage is simply a greater affinity for Soul Shaping," Ri'i said, turning her cheek haughtily.

"Ahem." Ma'a cleared her throat. "Back to the original topic."

The other two sisters quieted down.

"This is one of the Margin Worlds," Ma'a continued. "It is a simplified depiction of the material world and how it is perceived by the soul, whether living or dead."

She spread one hand toward the empty landscape.

"This is the lowest level, called Stillness."

My eyes wandered across the strange world.

The walls had no windows, the trees had no leaves, and the road had no people. Everything that should have been there was present, but only as the simplest possible representation of itself.

It was almost as if someone had taken the material world and stripped away everything that wasn't absolutely necessary to identify it.

"Why is it called Stillness?" I asked.

"Because this level contains what does not move," Ma'a explained. "The things that remain in place regardless of what happens around them."

That made sense. Before I could ask another question, her aura flared, and the landscape around us shifted, making everything blurry.

"The next level is Motion."

The walls surrounding us disappeared, replaced by a strange mist of colors and blurred shapes.

A cloud of green surrounded me where the foliage should have been in the real world. Marbled clouds of shifting colors occupied the spaces where the roads and buildings had been.

I blinked.

I looked toward where the door and windows should have been and saw semi-circular blurs exactly where those fixtures belonged. The door was represented by the space it occupied when it opened and closed. The windows were represented by the movements associated with them. Even the trees were no longer trees. They were clouds of shifting green, representing the constant motion of their leaves and branches.

"So the first level represents things that remain fixed," I said slowly. "And the second represents things that change position."

"More or less," Ma'a said.

I looked around again. It was fascinating. The world was still recognizable, but only if I stopped thinking about what things were supposed to look like and instead thought about what they did.

"The third and last level is Energy."

The space shifted once more. This time, the change was even more dramatic. The blurry landscape disappeared, replaced by a void filled with a haze of light, peppered with huge sources of energy spread throughout the environment, some originating from the road, others from inside the houses. Smaller lights surrounded some of the various objects that should have been scattered throughout the area.

Then I looked down at myself. My entire body was glowing. So was the bag of holding hanging at my side.

I stared at it.

"That's my mana, isn't it?"

"Among other things," Ma'a replied.

For a moment, the three levels seemed to overlap.

I could see the solid shapes of Stillness, the shifting patterns of Motion, and the glowing sources of Energy all at once.

Then Ma'a relaxed her aura, and the higher levels disappeared. The strange landscape returned to the Stillness environment, with its empty roads, windowless houses, and leafless trees.

My mind was already racing. Stillness, Motion, and Energy. The three levels were all representations of the same material world, just from different perspectives.

And the more I thought about them, the more familiar the concepts became.

Stillness and Motion were already representations of energy. Potential energy and kinetic energy. Potential energy described stored energy, while kinetic energy described energy associated with movement.

Could that be what I was looking at?

Three levels of Energy?

But if so, what exactly was the third level, the one Ma'a had just called Energy?

I thought about it for a second, and the answer jumped at me. It was painfully obvious.

"The three levels are all representations of energy," I muttered.

Ma'a tilted her head.

"Stillness represents potential energy. Motion represents kinetic energy."

"But Energy..." I looked down at my body, which had been glowing like a beacon only seconds ago. "Energy shows the mystical forces involved. Mana, souls, faith, and other manifestations of magic."

Ma'a's expression remained neutral.

Ri'i looked intrigued.

Na'a looked delighted.

"While I can see Motion as energy," asked Ma'a, "why do you subscribe to Stillness also being energy?"

"Because everything is energy," I shrugged. "Food is energy waiting to be eaten. Coal is energy waiting to be burned. Everything is energy; we just don't know how to access it."

"I like this one. Can we keep her?" Ri'i said, giving me a wink.

"I have read about the 'everything is energy' proposal, but I never heard it described in such plain words. I often see it as part of the Seven Axes of Creation theory."

"The Seven Axes of Creation. I heard about it being referenced in a few of the Mythic books I read, but it's not something they subscribe to, so I never found a book describing the theory in detail."

"The theory is Soulit in origin," Ma'a explained in her monotone voice. "It is about the building blocks of reality. To put it simply, the claim is that the physical realm is born from the intersection of seven flows of energy. While those energies exist in all realms, some realms are closer to one of the axes than others."

I blinked. There was an explanation for why each world had its own magical system.

"What are those axes called?" I asked, unable to hide my anticipation.

"The first is Ideal. That is the axis that governs the Soulit realm," said Ma'a. "The soul is an ideal representation of life. The Margin Worlds are an ideal representation of the physical world. Soul Scribes can access the ideal representation of a soul and modify it to create enchantments and Soulbooks."

I nodded slowly. That actually fit surprisingly well with what I had seen.

"The second is Alteration. That is the axis that governs the Kindred realm," she continued. "Kindred adaptation is a physical alteration to suit your environment. The Kindred realm has the most diverse fauna and flora because of the constant Alteration."

That also made sense. Kindred evolution was essentially reality rewriting living things to better fit their environment. If their entire realm was influenced by the Alteration axis, their biology being unusually flexible made much more sense.

"Next is Belief. That is the axis that governs the Mythic realm," she continued. "It is an undeniable fact that the concept of belief affects reality in the Mythic realm. Even public perception can make or break a system regardless of objective truth."

I raised an eyebrow. From my Mythic reading, I had heard most Soulit people despised the Mythic manifestation of power, especially when it came to the concept of divinity. Ma'a didn't share this inclination, though from her demeanor, I highly doubted Ma'a could despise anything.

"What makes it even more complicated is that those axes are not parallel or independent of each other. They intersect at multiple points. Belief could shift the Ideal, like a Soul Scribe, and alter a physical body like a Kindred Evolution."

I nodded in understanding as I remembered what happened when some of Damada's worshipers ascended in front of me. Some had their miracles expand without a change to their physical form, while others had their hair turn green with leaves or had their skin harden like bark.

If belief could influence the soul, then Mythic and Soulit magic would naturally overlap. If belief could directly alter physical bodies, then Mythic Ascension could also produce effects resembling Kindred Evolution.

It was more like seven different forces constantly interacting with one another, rather than seven neatly defined systems.

"Next is Materialization, the axis that governs the Elemental Bloodline realm," Ma'a continued. "This is where energy manifests in its most visible raw form as mana and its ten elemental affinities."

My eyes shifted to the side. Na'a was apparently bored with the lecture and had convinced her sister Ri’i to play a hand game that looked somewhat similar to Rock Paper Scissors.

I forced myself to look back at Ma'a. There was no way I was letting myself get distracted by Na'a's complete lack of respect for this important lecture.

"Next is Imagination, the axis that governs the Dreaming realm. As a part-Dreamer yourself, you must understand it better than me. It is the concept of Inspiration and how it interacts with dreams and creativity."

I nodded. Nina had done a good job explaining that to me. Imagination fed Inspiration, and it could be harnessed in the Dreaming world by expanding the Dream, or even in the Waking world via the rare and coveted Creation State.

"Finally, we have two last concepts that are the least understood, mostly because of the lack of observation of sentient people belonging to their respective realms."

"Though we have theories." Ma'a paused. "The Contested realm is suspected to be governed by Connection, based on the Telepaths' alleged ability to communicate and teleport."

That actually fit remarkably well.

"And the Sealed realm is supposedly governed by Doubt. Though how that leads to time magic is beyond me."

I doubted my curiosity about the Sealed realm would ever be assuaged. There was simply not enough information about that place. Maybe if I ever met Ion The Phoenix, I bet he was the only person who had a modicum of concrete information.

I looked down at the empty landscape beneath us. I had come here with a question, only to find myself saddled with a hundred more.

"All this long winded conversation was to say that, yes, everything is energy," Ri'i said from behind her sister, "but even energy has seven different flavors."

"Theoretically," Ma'a added with a nod.

My mind was already racing. If each realm was closer to one axis, what did that mean for Earth? Was Earth equally close to all seven? Was that the reason our magic was so weak? Or were we too far away from every axis for it to matter?

I didn't know. Besides, I was getting ahead of myself. I needed more information before I could start theorizing. Trying to construct a grand unified model of reality from one conversation with a Soulit person was probably not the most credible scientific methodology.

Still... the idea was fascinating.

For the first time since arriving in this world, someone had given me a framework that might explain why its different magical systems behaved so differently.

I had spent months treating Soulit magic, Mythic magic, Dreamer magic, Kindred Adaptation, and Elemental mana as separate phenomena, while being unable to clearly define my own strange teleportation ability.

Maybe they weren't separate at all. Just like time and space, they could be different dimensions of the same system.

I suddenly felt an almost irresistible urge to find every book ever written about this Seven Axes of Creation Theory and start doing research. But then I looked at the outline of my soul, linked to my body through an ethereal umbilical cord, then at the empty world around me, and then at the three sisters floating nearby.

That could wait. I decided to put the Seven Axes of Creation aside for the moment. I should not get distracted. I was floating inside a magical representation of the world with three Soulit sisters who seemed perfectly willing to explain how Soul Shaping worked.

"So, what can I actually do here?" I asked, looking around the empty landscape.

"You can first try moving around," suggested Ri'i.

I started walking and found myself flailing like I was in zero-G. I heard Na'a chuckling.

I am being stupid, aren't I? This is probably similar to something I have experienced before.

I started using my mind rather than my limbs and imagined myself swimming through the air. And just like that, my body responded, and I started drifting forward.

"Oh," I smiled. "That worked."

"Good job," Ri'i said with a smile.

I turned toward her and started circling her and her sisters.

"You are no fun," sighed Na'a. "You figured it out too fast."

"This is similar to Dream Flight."

I tried to zip faster, but I felt more resistance the harder I pushed.

"There are a lot of advantages to being a hybrid, it seems," said Ma'a thoughtfully.

"I guess." I stopped moving and turned toward the girls. "I feel like my speed is limited compared to Dream Flight, though."

"Your Will determines your movement," Ma'a answered. "And that metric is highly related to soul strength."

"Does this mean I can go faster if my soul gets stronger?"

"Yes, and no," answered Ma'a. "Your soul is already larger than average. You have not trained your Will enough to match it."

"You speak of Will as if it is a tangible thing," I said.

"Yes, it's what determines what soul comes up on top when a Soul Dealer is absorbing a soul," said Ri'i.

I remembered the Soulit teen who started neighing like a horse when absorbing that beast soul. Did he lose the contest of Wills?

"It also affects Soul attacks, aura suppression, and possession," added Ma'a.

"We shrugged off a Divine Mythic curse once," grinned Na'a, "just because our Will was stronger than the priest of Dorr we were facing."

I nodded.

Just one more thing to worry about... Mana, Inspiration, and now Will. There is probably a faith equivalent, as Vena seems to be limited in the amount of miracles she can cast, and has to recharge through fasting and other ritualistic commitments, though I had a feeling those resources weren't video game bars that you naturally ran out of, but more like stamina and physical endurance.

Wait... was Stamina the Kindred equivalent, or did they have some sort of Adaptation points they could keep track of?

My tangent was interrupted when I noticed something. I was moving very slowly toward the East.

"Am I drifting?"

"Yes," Ma'a said.

"Why?"

"The Soul Current."

I looked toward her.

"The what?"

I forced my Will to remain completely still, and it worked for a moment...

"The Soul Current is the natural flow of souls toward the nearest Soul Siphon."

...I got distracted by what Ma'a said and started moving again.

"The fuck do you mean by Soul Siphon?"

Na'a answered before Ma'a could.

"Soul Siphons are where lost souls are ground down and stripped of their useless parts before reincarnation," Na'a grinned, drifting closer.

"What useless parts? There is nothing useless about my soul."

"Oh, you know," said Na'a conversationally. "Stuff like memories, instinct, morality, and manifestations of powers."

"While it's true that the soul is immortal and invulnerable," explained Ma'a, "that only applies to the Ego part of the soul. The rest needs to be unburdened before reincarnation."

"Does that mean resurrection is possible as long as you get to a soul before it reaches a siphon?"

"Don't go there." Ri'i flew directly into my face and glared at me.

"It's a valid question," Ma'a said, shaking her head.

"Yeah, ease off, sis," added Na'a.

"Resurrection is indeed possible," explained Ma'a, "but it's rarely free."

"The Holy could do it with the virgin sacrifice," said Na'a. "But you know what that cost, don't you?"

"Twenty years of youth," I said.

"There are also the rebirth fruits in the Soul Realm, natural siphons in the Soul Realm that would sometimes revive a recently dead person. But that's more luck than sacrifice," Na'a said.

"The sacrifice is there, it's just paid for by the souls that lose the lottery," said Ri'i with a huff. "But yes, resurrection is technically possible."

I finally reached the give in my umbilical cord and stopped drifting. I glanced down at the translucent cord connecting my soul to my body. Then I looked east.

"So... if I were dead, I would just keep drifting until I reached a Soul Siphon?"

"Eventually." Ri'i nodded.

"And where is the nearest one?"

"Tree Gate."

I stared at her. "That's almost on the other side of the planet."

"Soul Siphons only naturally manifest in the Soul Realm," said Na'a.

"This is just speculation," said Ma'a in her monotone voice, "but the assumption is that before the Rift Gates, souls would have slowly atrophied in other realms over the course of decades before they were ready for reincarnation."

"Other realms, but the Mythic realm,” Corrected Na’a. “since their Afterlives are technically artificial Soul Siphons."

Holy shit. I didn't expect to be getting a lesson in soul ecology today. Though this was what Knight Edmand tried to explain to me after the war, when the band of mercenaries got slaughtered. Those souls were bound for reincarnation, whether it was through an Afterlife or Soul Siphon. It was inevitable.

"Am I moving east to Tree Gate because I claim no religion?" I asked.

And the three sisters nodded.

Yeah, maybe joining a faith wasn't the worst thing, though that highly depended on what those afterlives looked like. I did not want to end up in Valhalla, no thank you.

"You don't have to worry about Soul Siphons for now," said Ri'i with a reassuring smile. "The connection to your body anchors you, safeguarding you."

I followed the cord with my eyes. It stretched behind me toward my physical body, remaining taut and preventing me from drifting further.

"I am already at the limit of this cord," I observed. "That means I can't use Soul Shaping to safely explore the world or maybe scout the terrain."

"You might be having a misconception," Ri'i said. "The Margin Realm isn't safe. There are soul remnants of monsters or people resisting the siphon pull out of sheer resentment. You can also run into other Soul Shapers that might not want you to witness their passing, or even some environmental hazard that might not be coming to free-roaming souls."

"But yeah, you can definitely explore your environment or spy on people in this state," contradicted Na'a. "All you have to do is sever your cord."

With a swipe of her hand, Na'a cut off her own umbilical cord and started drifting east.

"Come on, Na'a," frowned Ri'i. "She definitely was not ready to even know about this."

"She is not a child."

"What just happened?" I asked. "Did she just kill herself?"

"No," said Ri'i.

"Technically, yes," said Ma'a at the same time.

"This is very dangerous, and we will explore it if you get competent enough as a Soul Shaper," Ma'a added.

"Yeah," sighed Ri'i. "That's step ninety-nine in Soul Shaping, and you barely know step two."

"Yeah," said Na'a. "First, you need to learn to resist the drift subconsciously before you even begin to consider doing what I just did."

She flew behind me and grabbed me by the shoulder. "But that's enough Soul Shaping for today."

She started pushing my back toward my body, and a moment later...

I gasped. I was sitting on one of the chairs back in the Waking World... or should I call it the Material World?

Marina was standing next to me, checking that I was fine.

Wait, was it Ma'a, Ri'i, or Na'a...?

I would have said Ri'i from their hair, but it's probably the three of them at once.

"What did you think about your first experience Soul Shaping?" said Marina... probably Ma'a, judging by the tone.

"I have more questions than when I started," I said, slightly overwhelmed.

"Lucky for you, we have a bit of free time as of late," smiled Marina charmingly. "Ever since Nada took over the Freelancer Guild reception, Ma'a is no longer the only competent bureaucrat at the helm."

"That means we can afford to teach you more," said Marina with an enthusiastic grin.

"Let's say once a week," she continued monotonically.

"Why are you offering to teach me personally?" I asked.

"I already told you," she smiled. "We want you as a friend and to keep helping the people of Hano."
