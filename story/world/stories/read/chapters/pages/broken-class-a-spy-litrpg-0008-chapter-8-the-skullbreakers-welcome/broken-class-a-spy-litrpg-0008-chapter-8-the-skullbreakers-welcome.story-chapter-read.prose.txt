Arthur’s consciousness flickered like a dying lantern.

Every time his eyes opened, glowing words pulsed across his vision.

Body reconstructed… Corruption detected… diverting energy… System initializing… Error… recalibrating…

He couldn’t read half of it. His brain felt like porridge and the parts he could read didn’t make much sense. It felt like a machine was booting up inside his bones with a migraine attached.

After what felt like hours inside a magical meat grinder. He blinked and this time, his awareness stayed.

He was lying in the moss-lit cavern, alone. Well, alone except for the storm of floating notifications swarming his face.

He’d read stories about this, but fiction had never described the sheer sensory assault. It was a machine booting up inside his bones with a migraine attached. The flashing lights, the strange words, the heavy feeling. It was all too much. The checklists he remembered from novels felt like a useless theory in the face of this painful, chaotic reality.

“Seriously?” Arthur croaked, his voice raw. “How am I supposed to see past all this?”

A voice, not his own boomed inside his skull.

“Another dumbass who doesn’t know how to mute messages. Why do I always get the clueless ones?”

Arthur bolted upright. He glanced toward the corpse of the Mana Nexus Beast. Still very dead. Definitely not the one talking.

Arthur went rigid, his breath catching in his throat. He didn’t speak, not at first. His gaze swept the cavern, cataloging every shadow, every stone. His spy training took over, forcing him into a state of cold, silent assessment. His senses swept the cavern, searching for any sign of an intruder. The air was still and cold, heavy with the scent of damp stone. No flicker of movement in the shadows, no whisper of displaced air. The silence was absolute. There was nothing here. "Where?" he muttered under his breath, the word barely a whisper in the huge cave.

"My, what a thorough search," the voice boomed, dripping with theatrical sarcasm. "Scanning for threats he can see. Adorable. I’m in your head, genius. I’m the remnant of Drogan the Skullbreaker. Yes, the Skullbreaker. You’re welcome."

Arthur’s mind raced. He’d read about this trope in a dozen isekai novels: the loud, overconfident, ancient mentor. An arrogant voice. Good. This was a personality type he could manage. The more it underestimated him, the more it would talk. He would play the part of the clueless newcomer, the perfect empty vessel for the ‘Skullbreaker’ to pour his ego into. Let him feel powerful. Let him explain everything.

Arthur blinked, deliberately widening his eyes with confusion. “Okay, Drogan. You sound... chatty. And inside my head. Why can I hear you?”

“Because I’ve been assigned by the System to guide you, genius. Congratulations! you’re now the proud owner of me. Try not to die too fast.”

Arthur decided not to react to the insult, continuing his act.

“Alright, oh mighty Skullbreaker,” Arthur said, laying the deference on thick. “How do I remove these floating messages from my face before I walk into a wall?”

There was a pause.

“...Was that flattery?” Drogan said suspiciously. “You trying to butter me up?”

Arthur coughed, playing the part of a nervous subordinate. “I just figured only someone as great as you could possibly train someone like me. I’m only a slave. But maybe. Just maybe, you can make something out of me.”

“Hah! Now we’re talking,” Drogan said, his voice swelling with pride. “Listen up, you walking corpse.”

“To clear messages, just think about clearing them. Hold the thought for a second.”

Arthur focused his will. The instant the instruction was given, the swarm of messages vanished. The speed of it was almost unnatural, a flicker of proficiency that went unremarked.

“Good. Now think ‘mute messages.’ That’ll stop stuff from popping up in your face mid-fight. You’re welcome again.”

Arthur did. Blissful silence.

“Now think ‘message console.’ That’s where you’ll find the full log. Read it. Or don’t. I’m not your mother.”

Arthur opened the console and this time, he could finally read what had happened:

Congratulations! You have unlocked the System. You have killed a Level 2183 Mana Nexus Beast.

Analyzing kill… Legendary Class eligibility confirmed. Legendary Class [Mana Lord] assigned. Beginning Class integration… Redirecting ambient energy to repair body… Corruption detected. Interference high. Redirecting more energy… attempting full reconstruction… Adaptation successful.

You have killed a legendary entity before unlocking the System. Reward Granted: Remnant Mentor assigned - [Drogan the Skullbreaker]. Title Granted: Infinite Wisdom Wisdom stat set to maximum. Grants absolute control over mana.

Arthur narrowed his eyes. Mana Lord? Legendary? What the hell does that mean?

“Hey Drogan,” he said aloud. “Now that I’ve unlocked this System thing… what now?”

“Now you give me access to your status screen so I can see what kind of disaster I’m working with,” Drogan said, far too eagerly.

Arthur hesitated.

But really, what choice did he have? He didn’t even know how to see his own stats yet. So he focused on the thought - grant access to Drogan.

A long silence followed.

Then Drogan broke into wild, echoing laughter.

“You - ha! You’ve got to be kidding me!”

“I, Drogan the Skullbreaker - slayer of Monster kings, breaker of towers, champion of four war realms. Only unlocked a Legendary Class at B-rank! And you, some no-name starving rat with a death wish, get it fresh out of the womb?!”

Arthur sighed. “I’m not a rat.”

“Unbelievable!” Drogan shouted. “What a Broken Class this is!”

Then, after a moment, he muttered with dangerous excitement:

“This… this is fate. I can already see it now. I will shape you, weakling. I will mold your pathetic bones into legend.”

“You will become a weapon, Arthur. A monster-slaying, kingdom-breaking legend. And when you stand atop the corpses of your enemies, the world will remember one thing...”

“You were trained by Drogan the Skullbreaker, greatest mentor in System history!”

Arthur, still lying in the dirt, groaned.

“God help me.”

“He’s busy,” Drogan said. “Lucky for you, I’m not.”
