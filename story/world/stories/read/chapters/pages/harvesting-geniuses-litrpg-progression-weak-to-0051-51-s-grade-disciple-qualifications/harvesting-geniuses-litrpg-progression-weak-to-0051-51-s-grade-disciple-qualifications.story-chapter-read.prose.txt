Dozens of Eltharok took turns standing guard around the Yang Na tree as though it were a priceless treasure. Jinyo couldn’t understand the appeal of smoking tree resin. He had no taste for smoking of any kind. No matter how much he loved plants and herbs, the thought of turning them into tobacco had never once crossed his mind—save for herbal decoctions, which he’d tried nearly every variety of, even though most tasted so foul he wanted to retch.

It wasn’t worth fighting a herd of blood-crazed antler demons alone. If this forest had a fully grown Yang Na tree, there had to be others of the same species elsewhere. The smarter move was to mark the coordinates in his notebook and return with reinforcements. There was a high chance they would launch an assault on any human they sensed nearby.

Jinyo crept past without alerting them. What had started as a search for Yang Na trees alone now expanded to include a second objective: mapping monster positions in the surrounding area. Thanks to his Nature Synthesis technique, Jinyo blended seamlessly into the environment. As long as he didn’t stupidly walk right up to them, neither the Eltharok nor the Cursed Villagers would pay him any mind.

Several minutes later, Bloodline Inference detected a tree whose properties matched the Yang Na. The moment he reached it, he spotted two Eltharok sitting beneath it—one standing 240 centimeters tall, the other only 220. The taller one had a higher level. The Yang Na towered three body-lengths above their heads. In both height and girth, it couldn’t compare to the one Jinyo had discovered before, but just two Eltharok shouldn’t exceed his current capabilities.

“Salty!” Jinyo gave the command. A plump potato-man furrowed his brows and burst from the bushes, charging the larger Eltharok with a roar that announced his presence. The Eltharok heard only the word [Potato!] before Salty leapt onto its chest.

BOOM!!

The blast from the Martyr Potato was violent enough to stagger one Eltharok. Its companion, less than ten meters away, couldn’t avoid the shockwave. Jinyo drew the Sonneteer from its sheath, used the Trampling Blossom technique to spring from tree to tree, then slid down from above.

Flower petals scattered in the wind. The Ether Blade glowed pink as he channeled power into it. Sword Spirit severed the head of the Eltharok that was still reeling from the explosion, unable to regenerate in time. Jinyo had to swing twice before the head finally dropped from its shoulders.

“Moo!!” The shorter Eltharok bellowed in fury. The antlers on its head glowed as it gathered energy. Its arm and chest muscles swelled; it kicked off the ground and charged its hated enemy. Jinyo simply spun mid-air and dodged with ease. Its massive body was both its greatest strength and its greatest weakness.

Jinyo, who could read its movements with perfect clarity, had no trouble evading it. No matter how fast the Eltharok was, its sheer size meant it carried the burden of its own weight, making it still not fast enough. Jinyo could have killed it easily with another Martyr Potato, but Salty wouldn’t be available again for another five minutes.

But killing a single Eltharok didn’t require that much time.

Trampling Blossom!

Jinyo used the moment the Eltharok lunged to redirect his momentum off a nearby tree, concentrating all his power into his legs and launching in two bursts.

Wandering Butterflies

Jinyo cocked his blade and shot straight at the Eltharok’s back in a beeline. When it turned around, Jinyo’s sword caught it across the throat. The cut wasn’t deep enough to sever the head in one stroke. Jinyo attempted to use his Planar-control technique to trigger a Punishment Wave, but failed. His Planar control wasn’t refined enough to achieve a 100% activation rate.

Jinyo didn’t wait for it to recover. The moment of regeneration was when its brain functioned at its slowest. He used Trampling Blossom to leap to the other side, then launched Wandering Butterflies again. It took roughly three rounds of the combined technique before the head finally came off.

[You have slain Eltharok Lv.22 — Received plant cultivation ticket: 11 hours]

Jinyo sheathed his sword and began using a knife borrowed from one of the survivors to carve out the Yang Na’s heartwood. Luckily, the Gardener’s Bag could store any agricultural product, and Yang Na heartwood qualified as one.

Jinyo spent hours felling Yang Na trees and shaving their heartwood into long-burning natural fuel. Vines were braided into small ropes to bundle kindling—branches and bark that burned just as well. He stuffed it all into the Gardener’s Bag, which grew heavier by the minute, eventually slowing his travel speed.

His first objective was complete. A Yang Na between five and eight meters tall should provide enough material for torches to last a while. In the middle of a fog-choked forest where day and night blurred together, Jinyo couldn’t see the sun, so he had no idea what time of day it was. All he knew was that he had to return to his son before dark.

During his exploration, the thick fog gradually thinned as Jinyo approached a structure. He spotted an old wooden fence made from small logs sharpened to points, bound and joined with rope and natural adhesive, its base coated in a white powder to repel termites and insects.

Jinyo’s eyes widened—this was a structure no monster could have built. He was excited to investigate but remained cautious. He pressed himself against the fence and peered through to the other side, though the white fog was so thick he could only see about a hundred meters.

He saw nothing—only the shadowy outlines of structures.

It wasn’t just a wooden wall. There were houses fit for habitation—not just one, but possibly three or four.

Everyone had been pulled into the Hollow Gate only yesterday. No one could have built houses complete with fencing in that time—unless they possessed construction-type magic or skills.

“Something that was already here?” Jinyo ran his hand along the fence. The wood had lost much of its life force, meaning it had been cut from the tree long ago. There were signs of weathering from sun and rain, dried and splintered. This fence wasn’t one or two years old—it was far older than that.

Jinyo climbed over the fence as quietly as possible. Salty was summoned to walk behind his master, carrying a staff. Jinyo drew closer to the structures until he could make out single-story and two-story wooden houses built side by side. He could say with full confidence: this was a village. There were more than a dozen houses—a large settlement.

No sign of living creatures. Jinyo took the liberty of stepping inside a house. He saw earthenware pottery, furniture made of wood and stone. Although the belongings were scattered, they told him that this place had once been full of life. Hundreds of houses like this, yet not a single resident. No—there had been residents once.

The Cursed Villagers… the pumpkin-headed humans who fought with farming tools. If not them, Jinyo couldn’t imagine where else the villagers had gone. He recalled learning that Hollow Gates sometimes contained their own history and lore. This village was the remnant of a civilization that had collapsed long ago for reasons unknown.

Jinyo hurried out of the filthy building that reeked of sawdust. There were no treasures, food, or useful resources to scavenge here—only structures that could serve as shelter for a more secure night’s sleep. He memorized the village’s location and planned to propose to Nephrin that they relocate and use it as a base. At the very least, it beat sleeping in the open forest with nothing at all.

Nightfall was approaching. Though he couldn’t tell through the thick fog, Jinyo estimated he’d left Gretel with Nephrin for several hours already. If he didn’t hurry back, he worried Gretel might feel uneasy. Then again, perhaps Gretel had already found his mother and was happier than Jinyo imagined.

“MOO!!!” A sound he’d heard countless times echoed through the air.

“Eltharok!” Jinyo sprinted toward the source. The sound was neither close nor far—just within earshot. Someone was fighting an Eltharok. Apart from the time he’d found Luciria, Jinyo had never had the chance to save anyone again. Every time he arrived, the other party was already a corpse. If he had the chance to save someone, he wouldn’t hesitate—it was his way of atoning.

Jinyo ran, panting, toward the source of the sound. But what he found was not the image of an Eltharok crushing a helpless human in need of rescue. Instead, it was a young man barely in his early twenties, wielding a red sword wreathed in flames, severing the head of an Eltharok that stood over 260 centimeters tall.

A neck thicker than a bull’s was cut clean through in a single stroke. The red blood smeared on the blade evaporated into steam. The headless body collapsed and attempted to regenerate—but failed again and again. The young man had white hair streaked with jet-black, and a pair of vivid ruby-red eyes that reminded Jinyo of Rina, someone he knew.

Behind the mysterious stranger, dozens of withered, headless corpses lay piled across the ground. He had eliminated every last Eltharok by himself.

Bloodline: Sun God (Unawakened)

Planar: Knight Tier 2

Divine Punishment: Heretic Inquisitor

Dominant Genes: [Sword ★★★] [Magic ★★★] [Fire Element ★★] [Vitality ★★] [Energy Burn Rate ★] [Iron Body ★] [Regeneration ★] [Learning ★]

Recessive Genes: [Hot-Blooded ★] [Food Allergy ★★] [Hunger Rage ★] [Insomnia ★★]

What kind of insane qualifications were these?! Since acquiring his system, Jinyo had never—aside from himself—seen Bloodline Inference rate anyone’s potential this high. Sword 3-Star, Magic 3-Star, Fire Element 2-Star—this man was born to be a Spell-Sword. Not to mention that every stat at its lowest was Grade E+ in psychic power, with everything else above C.

And that wasn’t all. The sword Esther held was forged from the same ore as the Sonneteer—a precious mineral that was the heritage of all humanity, crafted with the most advanced technology ever created.

Who the hell was this man?!

“Hey, you… a new species or something?” Esther fixed his gaze on Jinyo. Those ruby eyes looked as though they could incinerate Jinyo to ash. Dense crimson Planar poured into his blade, dyeing it a vivid scarlet. Heat radiated from the sword so intensely that even Jinyo, standing several meters away, was drenched in sweat.

“Wait—I’m human.”

“Lying demon. You think I’d buy that? If something like you is human, then I’m the Demon King.” Esther launched himself forward. His red blade unleashed a wave of flame. Jinyo raised his sword to block the overwhelmingly heavy strike. His wrist nearly twisted from absorbing a blow powerful enough to decapitate an Eltharok in one swing.

“Phew…” Esther inhaled deeply, then exhaled black smoke like engine exhaust. His slashing speed increased. Sparks from his swings scattered alongside the petals from Jinyo’s techniques, creating a scene as beautiful as a festival—but Jinyo wasn’t enjoying it one bit. It wasn’t just power; the sheer speed of Esther’s strikes was extraordinary.

Esther was like a machine that knew no fatigue—a killing engine that wouldn’t stop until its enemy’s head rolled.

Eye of the Sage!

Gather data and reveal the opponent’s weaknesses!

Jinyo held out for roughly twenty seconds. Eye of the Sage fed data directly into his brain—there were several critical flaws. And yet, Jinyo couldn’t find a single opening to exploit any of them.

The system wanted him to take this guy as a disciple?!

A disciple like this—what the hell was he supposed to teach him?!

“Just listen to me! I’m not a monster—I’m really human!”

“Human? Your aura is more vile than a hundred of those antler demons combined—pitch-black and utterly repulsive! And you still think I’ll believe you’re human?” Esther made no effort to hide the disgust in his eyes. Jinyo couldn’t understand it. He was genuinely human—there was nothing about him that resembled a demon. Yet Esther had snapped and was dead set on killing him.

Every single strike Esther unleashed—without the assistance of Eye of the Sage, Jinyo would have died two or three times over. These weren’t swings aimed at a creature of his own kind. They were the strokes of a blade meant to annihilate the enemy without a shred of remorse.

‘Talking is useless. I need to calm him down first.’

“Establish Domain.” Jinyo expanded his perception, channeling his focus into the spell.

Realm of Paradise!

The curtain of fog surrounding them was swept away in an instant. Esther’s eyes went wide as saucers the moment the Domain deployed. Though he stood in the exact same spot, it felt like he’d been transported to another world entirely. He could breathe easier. His body felt lighter.

Esther couldn’t comprehend why Jinyo would use a technique that made his opponent feel better. But when he turned his gaze on Jinyo once more, the sight made his blood run cold. He didn’t see a human being of flesh and blood. He saw a dark, writhing mass of something resembling countless roots, mimicking the movements of a human.

That terrifying aura intensified tenfold the moment the Domain was deployed.

“I knew it… you really aren’t human after all.”
