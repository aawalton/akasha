After Pvilycht was silent for a few seconds, Lexie raised an eyebrow.

“Well?”

“I’m not sure what you want me to tell you.”

“Did anything unusual happen between my father and Vacek while we were trapped in the pocket dimension?” she asked.

“I’m not sure what you would categorize as unusual.” He thought about it. “I could better show you what happened after you disappeared, and maybe you can decide what is and isn’t unusual.”

After Lexie nodded, he played the reel in her head, from his perspective. His senses were attuned to numbers running in his mind as he actively weakened the constraints of the dungeons and relayed the shifting constants to Aiden. He was doing his best to read the dungeon, while Aiden and Vacek were talking in the background, then arguing, then yelling. He couldn’t hear the words properly, since all his focus was taken up by what he was doing.

By the time he looked back, Aiden was standing there alone, and Vacek was gone.

As the entire pocket dimension inverted, Pvilycht disappeared, but not before he noted that Aiden collapsed that phase of the dimension.

Lexie knew what happened next. The rest of the heroes appeared. So did Lexie and Vulcan. The only one missing was Vacek.

She sighed.

Pvilycht didn’t know what had happened after all. He’d been too focused on analyzing the pocket dimension to pay attention to Vacek's discussion with her father. What were they arguing about? Was it about Lexie or Lara?

Did he hear any of what Vulcan had said to her?

Surely Aiden hadn't believed Vulcan? And he wouldn’t have truly gotten rid of Vacek with Pvilycht there, if he knew that Pvilycht could tell Lexie everything, would he?

It could just be that Vacek had gotten separated and the Alchemist had gotten rid of him before he was captured, since that might have been his main aim all along. Aiden had suggested as much. Maybe Lexie not killing Vulcan instantly had given the narrative time to push things in Vulcan's favor rather than Vacek’s, even though Lexie was pretty sure they had the numbers more on their side.

Then again, Tate had mentioned that Fate didn't really choose sides.

Or maybe the narrative didn’t acknowledge her at all, since she wasn’t part of the Chosen program. There were just so many angles with this, and the only way to know the truth at this point would be to ask her father.

Lexie sighed.

She couldn’t go say definitively that the Alchemist was lying, hence she couldn’t kill him before calling her dad again. And her dad was still incognito. She'd called him a few times today, but there was no answer, which told her that communications were probably still down on their end.

Oh well.

At least she’d taken out Vulcan. In a day or two, once the heroes discovered that Vulcan was dead – she'd put his body back in the pocket dimension so they wouldn't panic and think he'd escaped – they might try to move the Alchemist somewhere else for his own safety, but they couldn’t do that without Aiden’s approval and they couldn’t reach Aiden the same way Lexie couldn't reach Aiden.

So she still had time.

And even with that, they probably wouldn't move the Alchemist anyway, because honestly, where would they move him to? There was nowhere for him as safe as the pocket dimension. There was also nowhere for him to hide from Lexie.

The alternative would be to then try to imprison Lexie, but they probably couldn't do that either. So she was interested to know what penalty they would give her. Would they give her up to the Fae? Unlikely, if they hadn't already done that for the Alchemist.

Lexie knew the Fae had to have wanted him at some point, thanks to the powers he had, but Vacek had probably said no.

So they probably wouldn't give her up if they were smart. It was her first offense, and she was a child after all.

Now that she thought about it, the Fae did have an Alchemist now. Aiden.

Could they have planned to do something with him?

Aiden wouldn't have gone if he thought so, right?

Lexie was tired of being so filled with worry for him. She really wanted to kidnap her father, but she would settle for just checking on him again.

At least now she had some sympathy for how Aiden felt when Lexie would go off and do dangerous things on her own.

She blew out a breath. All that would come later. She needed to finish her mission first.

“What line is this dungeon a part of?” Lexie asked Pvilycht next.

Pvilycht sniffed the air. “I believe this is of the line of Zyraxis, Lord of Decay.”

“Fun. Now let's figure out how we get into this heart.”

He nodded.

Lexie was so lucky to have Pvilycht by her side. With his help, they didn’t even need to decipher any of the Fae poems in the dungeon. He simply laid his hand on the walls for a few minutes and said, “We need to arrange those rocks counter-clockwise.”

Lexie nodded, and did it, and then suddenly she was in the dungeon, in purgatory, observing in the distance a series of graveyards.

A ghostly wind blew across what was likely the first level, highlighting the faded headstones and rustling the crouched trees. The whisper of wind chilled Lexie's bones, and she felt like something was trying to crawl out of her skin.

Plus, it smelled like a dead body.

“Gross,” Lexie cupped her hand over her nose. She realized then that she’d lucked out getting stuck in her dungeon, which, while a little barren and a lot more dangerous, did not have the stench of corpses and rotten food.

If she'd been stuck in this dungeon for months, she wasn't sure she would have made it. She might have lost the will to live at some point just from the smell alone.

“This dungeon is all about endless loops," Pvilycht explained. "And each stage you're confronted with the undead. It could be a loved one, or maybe an old passion that you had. You must find a way to defeat it and break the loop before you advance."

“And this is supposed to be a level 4 dungeon?”

“Yes. The stages are pretty straightforward. Things are rarely confusing, and the dungeon is not really that sentient to make it more difficult for you.”

"Good." It was better for the dungeon not to have any awareness. In case whatever Yasycht was going to do to it hurt.

Lexie had a moment of hesitation where she thought about the possible repercussions of her actions. Most likely, this was more than just giving Yasycht dungeons so he could have fun playing with them.

There had to be something else he wanted.

But she needed him and couldn't afford to get on his bad side. So, unfortunately, the sacrifice had to be made.

"Okay, so now I just need to find a doorway, steal the Lord's essence, say the ritual words, and voila.”

“Yes.” Pvilycht nodded, then added experimentally. "And voila."

“Is there anything else about this ritual I should know?” She asked Pvilycht.

Pvilycht shrugged. “I was not around when Neqal did his.”

Of course.

"Can you dig up that grave for me?"

Pvilycht gave her a look. "Dr. Bossetti says I must invite others to meet me as my new self. I would like to invite you to make your requests with more courtesy."

Wow, he was really taking the book seriously.

"Please?" she added.

He nodded and set about doing it.

Lexie waited for him to finish, and when he was done, she figured the grave could work as a doorway.

A zombie tried to emerge from it, but Pvilycht decimated it with absolutely no courtesy. Only the bones and the disgusting stench of a corpse remained.

As Lexie lowered herself into the grave, she reached down and connected to the dungeon, and suddenly her mind was filled with rot.

It was decaying from the inside out, the stench worsening. Lexie could barely breathe through the fog of it, as it tried to rot her brain before she could act.

She used her soul to siphon the chaos, calming it, taking over the dungeon, and suppressing it.

She reached deeper and felt the tentative bond between the Lord and the dungeon. She tugged at it, siphoning some of the essence from it, even as the Lord snarled in her mind.

She ignored it and muttered the magic words.

Suddenly, she felt a presence fill it, imposing and suffusing the atmosphere while the dungeon screamed out. Lexie flinched. She thought the dungeon's lack of sentience meant that it wouldn’t feel it, but she felt its emotions relayed from their connection.

It hurt.

The dungeon was confused, its essence stretching to accommodate the amount of chaos that invaded it, dominated it.

She felt Yasycht's presence invading all transition points.

Once it was done, she breathed in and disconnected, but she still felt the vague imprint of the dungeon on her soul.

She felt a little bad for what she'd done. She figured the guilt would lessen with time, so she told Pvilycht, "Onto the next. Please."

Lexie managed to handle two more dungeons that day. One was from the Lord of Mania, which was a series of funhouse carnivals. The other was Balsycht again, which featured a heart that looked despondent and frozen in time.

While on the third one, she noticed that the impression that had been left on her from that first dungeon wasn’t dissipating.

She didn’t know what it was. It didn’t feel the same as a soul contract or a soul link, but it felt like something had been stamped onto her, and she couldn't get it out.

Once she was done giving Yasycht access to Balsycht's dungeon, she asked Yasycht, "What did you do to me?”

“You’ll find out later.” He didn’t laugh, but he radiated amusement regardless.

“Are we linked somehow?”

"You should be able to access me while in the human world now."

"Why?"

He didn't answer.

“Is that all you did?”

He didn’t answer that one either, and Lexie pulled back.

She was a little concerned about it, but there was no turning back now.

If she'd somehow made a mistake, she would fix it later.

When she was leaving the third dungeon, she got a phone call from her Uncle.

“Lexie, where are you?” he asked.

“Just finishing up." She checked her watch. "Actually, I have a meeting I have to attend first before I come home. Is that okay?”

“Just makes sure you get back before dark.”

“Okay.” Lexie figured she could get to the other two dungeons tomorrow. The process actually wasn’t hard and took maybe an hour at a time. It would be easy enough to finish it the next day.

She teleported to a boardwalk in a metropolitan city close to where Doyle lived in Old Moulding.

She was about to walk into the coffee shop, which was their meeting point, when an older woman gasped, pointing a finger at her. “Oh my God, it’s you.”

Lexie stared blankly at the woman, who sang, “You’re the card mage, right? The one who escaped from the dungeon?”

“Lexie Sparrowfoot!” Her companion slapped her shoulder in excitement. “That’s her name. She’s the Archmage’s daughter.”

"Yes, of course. I'm so honored to run into you. What are the odds? Can you sign something for me? It's for my granddaughter."

“Um…sure…” But the problem was that once Lexie reached for their pad to start signing, more people saw her, and they encircled her too, talking over each other and staring at her like she was a zoo animal.

Damn it.

She really should have remembered to wear her hoodie. She’d forgotten about the train incident too soon, forgotten how popular she was now.

The second she signed one pad, another pad was thrust toward her, and Lexie was too polite not to sign that too.

And then another.

And then another.

As the crowd grew, people asked her questions that she couldn't answer. Until someone finally gave her one that she wanted to.

“Do you think you can teach us how to get out of dungeons, too?"

“Hopefully,” she said, which led to more chatter as the coffee shop door opened and someone dragged her inside, before closing and locking it.

Lexie looked back and saw a strange man with curly brown hair and a gap-toothed smile staring down at her.

She was confused, but then saw Doyle step out behind him.

“Sorry,” the man said, instantly taking his hand off her. “I should have picked a less public place for our first meeting."

“It’s okay,” Lexie said as she brushed her shoulder. “I'm Lexie.””

“I’m Stanley Bucksworth, and I have to admit, I’m such a huge fan of you.”

“Why?” Lexie hadn’t actually asked anyone that question before. She’d just kind of accepted that she was a spectacle because she was the first person ever to escape a dungeon, and people were probably curious about her as a result. But she’d never really thought about what made people fans of her when they didn’t even really know her.

“‘Cause you’re awesome,” Doyle said plainly as he gestured to the people who were still standing at the doors, their heads pressed against the windows.

“Yes,” Stanley said. “Not only did you achieve something that not even the Fae can do, but your whole life story is kind of crazy. Down to the Archmage dad, who was both a hero and a villain, and then mom, who used to fight in the AFC, and then you were also in the AFC and dropped out after you injured a girl so badly she had to take a year off…I mean, your story’s pretty remarkable. Intimidatingly so. Sponsors would go crazy for you if they’re not already tearing down your doors.”

Lexie didn’t think they were, but then again, there would be no way for them to reach her. They couldn't get to her through system mail, and Aiden had put wards around their home.

“Will I have to do sponsorships?” she asked.

They shared a look. "I mean, yeah. That’s how we’ll make money."

Lexie didn’t need ot make money, but she could understand that they did.

"What if I pay you not to do them?"

That puzzled them, and Stanley cleared his throat.

“Let’s sit, and we can talk about all that,” he said. “This is my friend Mina's coffee shop, and there's a neat private booth where I typically have meetings."

With that, he led them to a door at the side of the counter, while at this point, everyone in the shop stared at Lexie and talked in hushed tones.

The private room was small but cozy with booths that gave off a diner feel.

“We’re going to record this,” Stanley said, setting a recorder on the table as they sat. “I hope you don’t mind. Of course, we won't let anything you say here get out; it's just for our own uses."

“Sure.”

“Okay, Lexie Sparrowfoot,” he said and cleared his throat. “Now there’s been much talk about you, but none of it seems actually to be coming from you. So I want to take this opportunity to ask: Who are you? Who do you want to present as, and what exactly is it that you want to do?”

Lexie thought about it. “I’m Lexie Sparrowfoot. I want to present as Lexie Sparrowfoot. And I want to change the world.”

“Ah, of course. So noble. In what way exactly do you want to change the world? Do you want to be a Superhero?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because I don’t want a job.”

He laughed, but when Lexie didn't join in, the laughter died. The room stilled for a moment.

“Heroism is a job," Lexie assigned. "One with far too much oversight and far too little control. You get assigned missions. You complete them. Someone tells you how well you did, and they decide whether or not the mission was successful enough. They decide if you acted appropriately, where you go next, and what you do. Constant supervision. Don't get me wrong, it's probably a good thing, and those safeguards are probably necessary. But I don't want that. I'm not noble enough for it."

"I'm sure you're just being humble."

“I'm not," Lexie said plainly. "I'm far too selfish to be a hero, and too protective of my own interests. I also don’t align with the mission of the association, one that states there should be order all the time. I think sometimes there’s necessary chaos. Sometimes stuff needs to go south so we can be better. I live by that, which is another reason I wouldn't be a good hero.”

“What do you want to do then?" Doyle asked. "The AFC?"

She shook her head. "I want to advance humanity," she said. “I want to create my own research team to push beyond our understanding of magic and redefine the system. I'm recruiting Developers, Engineers, Healers, Mages, and Heroes, all the roles. And mundanes as well. I want to work with just about everyone in every field to see how we can make things better for ourselves, with or without the Fae."

Doyle and Stanley blinked, then shared a look.

Stanley coughed again, a clear sign of discomfort.

“Well, erm.., we'll probably have to figure out a way to rephrase that last part. It's just that there was this guy...well, it's kind of before your time, but he was called the Alchemist. And he said a lot of the same things you're saying, but he was a total villain."

"I know."

"Yeah. So people would want to know what makes you different from him."

"My methods," she said. "Also, the fact that I don't plan on doing this as some secret, hidden thing without the input of the rest of humanity. I'm enlisting the expertise of other roles and those who exist on this Earth who are tired of the status quo. I'm also having mundane oversight. There will be no human testing until we're absolutely sure we've got it right, and even then, there will be a separate board that decides that. It won't just be me. Because I can't trust myself to make the decision." Until then, she would just keep testing on Tate and any other golems her father could make for her. Golem testing would be expensive, but Lexie could make it work.

"I see," Stanley said. "Ultimately, though, what's all this for? Do you have any idea what you want to research?"

Lexie thought about how it all had come full circle and smiled. "I want to make magic for mundanes."

Doyle froze, his entire body stiffening as he stared at Lexie.

Stanley seemed to be waiting for the punchline, and when he didn't get it, his jaw slowly dropped and stayed there for a while.

Eventually, they recovered and went back to taking their notes. Lexie had given them just enough to use and draw public interest, but she wouldn't tell them about destroying the system just yet.

Though the Alchemist had warned her that the Fae would kill her if they suspected what she was up to, they probably already did suspect. Or at least, they'd thought she might get to this point, and that was why the Ambassador wanted her gone from Earth.

Nevertheless, she couldn't be on the defensive forever, and she needed as much human support as she could get. After all, the oath was with all of humanity, and humanity probably needed to align to break it. Even if it didn't work like that, more powerful humans would act as a deterrent and would better resist the Fae if they ever did try to invade and take over.

Lexie mentally noted another thing that would differentiate her from the Alchemist.

I won’t be as easily stopped.
