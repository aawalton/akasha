Ferris had some free time before dinner, so he took a chance to relax. He had been working every day that he could to earn enough merit points for Acid Resistance. Now, he knew he would need at least fifteen more to purchase the materials, plus one more to transfer them to his soul. He could understand Brix’s frustration with being told to develop a new attack ability, and he was glad she had found a different way. Acid Resistance didn’t even directly help Ferris with the qualifications needed to delve, other than taking him a step closer to healing his leg.

Ferris and Jade grabbed an afternoon snack, then visited the bathhouse. Soaking in the water was relaxing, and Jade would awkwardly try to swim around Ferris. The slime was still not very coordinated in the water, even after all the practice in the slimespace when collecting qi, but they were definitely having fun.

That night, after Ferris and Jade had eaten dinner and finished harvesting qi from the Air Passage, they were standing in the slimespace at the Digest River. The river wasn’t full of qi, as Ferris hadn’t been able to give Jade extra food—something he planned to change the next day. Looking out at the qi that was there in the river, Ferris considered his current collection method using metal fishing chains.

Once he acquired the Acid Resistance ability, he could simply walk along the bottom of the river and pick them up himself. Unfortunately, he now knew that it would be at least a couple of weeks away, probably more like a month.

His current method worked, but he had to spend about twenty percent of the qi he collected making disposable chains. He wanted to do more experiments to see if he could create an acid-resistant material.

Ferris had been hoping that the Acid Resistance ability would help him, but he didn’t see any way to use it to apply it to the things he created. The ability would protect him, but not anything else.

He looked out at the ocean of acid surrounding the dome in the slimespace. If Ferris ever wanted to explore that place, he would need equipment that could survive along with him.

Most immediately important, Ferris needed a way to protect structures in the slimespace from the acid waves. Placing them up high only helped if he could build a platform that resisted acid underneath.

Ferris had seen four things in the slimespace resist acid: the clams at the bottom of the river, the translucent green material of the dome, the sandy yellow stone that made up the ant’s spires, and finally the chunks of solid qi.

He wasn’t willing to kill any of the clams, as they produced qi for him. If they turned hostile, he would lose all qi production from the river.

The qi chunk was an interesting case. After he converted it to liquid qi and then formed it into an object, the qi construct dissolved easily. It was only in this form that it appeared to be fine in the river.

Although… maybe that wasn’t the case. Ferris had always wondered how natural slimes collected qi from the clams. Maybe it was dissolving, just slowly? He had noticed that the chunks were different sizes, but he had never checked to see if a chunk of qi would disappear over time.

He worked with Jade to fish out a chunk of qi, and then set it back in the river near the shore. It didn’t dissolve away immediately, but maybe it would over time. He planned to check it the next day.

Even if the qi chunks were better resistant to acid, it didn’t help if he had no way to form them into a desired shape. Qi formed constructs melted immediately, after all, so something about that process changed the material.

That left the translucent green material of the dome and the yellow sandy stone of the pillars. The dome was easy to dig into. For the sandy stone… Ferris was still hesitant to break the ants’ pillars. He decided to leave the pillars alone for now and went to get his shovel.

He dug out a good amount of the dome’s hard, green material. It was rigid to the touch yet still kind of flexible. It didn’t crumble like stone. Digging into it was strange, as he would need to sort of cut out or shave off large pieces. It stopped glowing as soon as he removed it from the dome’s wall.

After he made a pile of the stuff, he placed his hand on it and imagined himself going back to the soulspace with the entire mound along with him. Uncle Winton had mentioned that the qi rope trick was a crutch to help learn how to take large objects between souls.

This… sort of worked. A bit of the stuff appeared with him in the soulspace, but only what was directly around his hand. Resigned, Ferris grabbed the qi rope and traveled back to the pile in the slimespace. This time, he laid the rope around in a circle, then used it to imagine returning with everything inside the rope. It worked better than expected, and he even got some green dirt from the ground underneath.

First, Ferris tried melting the hard, translucent green material down by itself—without qi. He remembered the pot he had lost to experimenting with the demon corpses, but he figured it was worth the risk.

Different than the demon corpses, the green material didn’t melt over the Heartfire. He left it there for a while, but nothing changed other than it becoming red and hot. After he pulled it off the heat and dumped it out, he watched it for a few minutes. It appeared the same as before, cooling and turning back to its normal green color.

Taking some more from his pile, he tried cooking it with an equal amount of qi added. This time, it melted down to liquid, much like demon corpses. The only difference he saw was how long it took. He estimated the qi with demon corpses liquified twice as fast.

After he removed it from the Heartfire and let it cool, he was relieved to see that it stayed a liquid. Reaching a hand in, it felt more… goopy… than normal qi. He focused his intent and created a chain, pulling it out fully formed in his hand.

Holding the translucent green chain, at first it looked correct, but then it fell apart in his hands. The material felt more like gelatin, and it sort of separated under its own weight. It was nothing like the hard, flexible material of the dome. He could squish it in his hands.

Ferris started over, this time trying equal parts qi, demon chains, and green material. The result was a hard, brittle, dark greenish material. It broke apart with a small amount of force, but it was at least an improvement.

He wondered if a different set of ratios would work better. Before, he’d always used qi for half of the mixture, but now it was only one-third. He might have to try a bunch of different ratios, and then see if he could keep both the strength of the chains and the acid resistance of the green material.

That thought made Ferris stop and reconsider. He wanted something both acid-resistant and hard, but the entire material didn’t have to be. What if he made a chain, and then coated it with an acid-resistant… paint? The green goopy gelatin had failed to make a hard material, but it might be better suited to forming paint with the correct intent.

Once more, he started over and quickly made one of his standard demon corpse chains.

Then, he cooked and cooled another pot full of equal parts qi and green material. Sticking a hand in, he imagined a thick, sticky paint that would coat everything it touched. He focused his intent on paint that could act as a lacquer coating, providing its natural ability to resist acid to anything it covered.

Pulling his hand out, it was now covered with shiny green paint. Looking down at this pot full of the stuff, he realized that there was likely no way to clean out the pot. Or his hand. Hopefully, it would revert to normal after he slept.

If the paint worked, then maybe Ferris could coat himself with it as well… he would need to test it with his hand at least. The idea didn’t feel truly practical as he wouldn’t be able to open his eyes, but it wouldn’t hurt to have it as an option.

Ferris grabbed the chain he’d prepared and dipped it into the paint. It came out covered in the same glossy green as his hand.

Lastly, Ferris created a new demon corpse chain using his normal method for comparison—only without the paint—and then took his experiments to the slimespace to test.

First, Ferris placed an unaltered chunk of the dome’s translucent green material in the acid. He knew it resisted acid, as it made up the dome’s wall around him… but it had stopped glowing when he removed it. He wanted to confirm it was still resistant afterwards.

After about ten minutes, he didn’t see any signs of it dissolving. He decided to check back on it over time, along with the qi chunk he had placed in the river earlier.

Done with the two ‘naturally found’ materials he was testing, he moved on to everything he had crafted.

Even though it was already a failure, Ferris tried the gelatinous remains of the goopy chain. He had to carry it in his bucket and dump it into the acid. As far as he could tell, it was as acid-resistant as the base material.

After that, he tried the brittle combination of qi, demon corpse, and green material. It dissolved quickly, only lasting about a minute.

Holding one end of the painted chain, Ferris tossed the other end into the acid. He had never been able to truly time how long one of his previous chains would last, but he knew it was longer than thirty minutes. Right next to the glossy green chain, he tossed in the unpainted one created with his normal method.

Now that they were both in the acid, he just needed to wait and compare them. Looking at his bright green hand, he sighed a bit dramatically before crouching down next to the acid. With a bit of trepidation, he placed a finger in the liquid. After a few seconds of nothing happening, he placed more of his hand in and waved it around.

Pulling his hand out, he looked over at Jade. He called the slime over, then spoke over the bond.

“Jade, I would like to test something. I want you to try to eat my hand.”

“No eat hand!” The slime started rolling away.

“It is okay, this is just a test. I have this paint on my hand that should protect it.”

“No eat hand, not again.” They shook a little, moving further away.

Ferris spent some time calming Jade down, but the slime still refused to test out the paint. In the end, Ferris went back to the soulspace, got a piece of demon corpse, and then dipped it in the pot with the green paint.

Back at the Digest River, Ferris asked Jade to eat the green chunk of meat. The slime considered it for a moment, as if trying to see the trick, but then decided to comply.

The birds circled above the Consume Pool, and then a duplicate chunk of green meat appeared in the air. It fell into the pool with a splash, and several nearby ants jumped in after it. For a moment, two identical green chunks of meat existed, one inside Jade and the other inside the pool.

An ant reached the green chunk, grabbed it in its mandibles, and brutally tore it apart.

The green chunk of meat dissolved both inside Jade and inside the pool, along with the ants that had jumped in. Watching it, Ferris was suddenly very glad Jade refused to eat his hand. He had wanted to see if a duplicate hand was formed… and if he could still control it, but the idea of his hand melting away made him shudder. Mental body or not, the pain was real.

Ferris suspected that the green paint was acid-resistant, but the magical effect added by the ants was simply stronger than it could handle. The green paint was holding up fine against the acid in the river so far, after all. This followed what Ferris knew about resistance in general. There was no such thing as immunity, just progressively stronger resistance that could be overwhelmed with a higher amount of power.

Ferris was also starting to see a pattern of enhancement for both the acid and resistance. The Consume Pool’s acid was magically strong, but the ants made the acid even stronger. For the Acid Resistance construction, the metal frame could resist acid, but the sacrificial rods made it stronger. Both of these enhancements had a price, either in ants’ lives or qi to make rods.

Sitting back down, Ferris checked on the two chains. They were still fine, which was a good sign for the paint’s use against the river’s acid. If it hadn’t worked at all, the green paint would’ve dissolved away.

It was starting to get late, but Ferris decided to settle in to wait. Jade bounced around exploring the area a couple of times, then settled into Ferris’ lap. The ants scurried around, traveling in groups everywhere they went, and the birds would fly overhead in swooping patterns. Ferris felt himself relaxing, just watching the acid flow by.

He’d been waiting for about half an hour when Ferris saw movement inside the river. One of the clams was gone, somehow sunken into the riverbed and out of sight.

He stood and walked to the shoreline, trying to see what had happened. The clam was definitely gone. There was a cloud of dirt floating around where it had been.

About a minute later, the clam returned—although in a slightly different place. It pushed up through the ground and then settled in as if nothing had happened. The clamshell was closed, although he hadn’t seen if it had been open before.

He’d previously noticed that the clams moved, but this was the first time he had seen it happen. Somehow, they went under the riverbed, then later emerged. Why did they relocate?

Unfortunately, it was just another mystery to add to his notes.

Returning to the chains, he settled back in to continue waiting. Every so often, he moved them around to check. The fourth time he tested them, the chain made with the old method fell apart, while the glossy green chain was still strong.

Ferris was satisfied. The new method was much better than the old. It lasted longer, and making the paint would cost less overall than replacing entire chains. He called over Jade, and they harvested all the qi in the Digest River with the painted chain.

At the end of the night, it was still strong, although he thought he could see parts where the paint was fading. It might need fresh coats every so often. If he waited too long to repaint, he would lose the chain.

Both the qi chunk and the raw green material that he had left in the acid appeared unchanged as well. He decided to keep watch over them each day going forward.

The goopy gelatinous chain had fallen apart. He thought he could still see small pieces, but most had been washed downriver.

Ferris went to bed happy with his progress. Things hadn’t worked the way he had hoped, but he had found a way regardless. He qi was back down to a dramatic low, less than a single full collection from the Digest River.

His original fishing chains had let him start collecting from the Digest River, but they cost qi to remake each day. He estimated that about a fifth of the qi gathered was used up making more chains.

The acid-resistant paint was much more efficient. He could go days without needing to make more paint, and he never needed to recreate the chain.

The experiments had definitely been worth the cost.
