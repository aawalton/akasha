Day 168, 07:00 AM

I’ve got two weeks to speed through the Archmage class, and little time to waste. I apologize to Lucy and Gila and tell them I will clear the dungeon on my own while they are studying at the mansion. The situation is a bit awkward as I head out, but I don’t fret it. I might redo the whole situation several times.

Instead, I focus on the task at hand. Assuming archmage is even more spell-focused than mage, a dungeon, with its increased mana density, is probably the best place for advancing the class.

Two miles away from the mansion, I catch a fish and along the way shape a small bowl of stone so that it can survive until I can use it in an experiment. Hopefully, Edna isn’t paying attention to me, but who knows, she might sense the spike in mana if I were to cast the spell outside the dungeon.

So, slowly and patiently, a goldfish in hand, I head to the dungeon. I descend to the first floor, where I capture a scuttling land lobster.

Edna naturally didn’t teach me the spell for fusing two lifeforms, but Hadriuse’s private grimoires contained several versions of it, along with a mind-numbing number of other spells. Hopefully, more than Edna knows.

I focus on the spell, the song and gestures included to reduce the mana cost. Entranced, I watch a completely different sight from the one I witnessed as a bystander. The fish is melting, portions of its liquid form flow into the lobster, slithering into the cracks between its armor, carrying with them the carp’s fishiness, implanting and spreading its genetic material through the lobster.

Hours seem to pass before the fish melts away completely, leaving behind an abominable lobster spasming on the floor. Winded, I check BSD and find the exact information I was looking for.

Archmage, path of life, level zero. To level up, cast five different grand spells.

Grand spells are archmage level magic. I know thirteen, Edna probably knows zero. The only problem is I have never cast them before because they are mana intensive.

I summon BSD, my mana has tripled, my control over life magic increased, and I’ve got six more attribute points. With all those added up, I should be able to cast the grand spells. I’ll do it with the song and gestures, to further reduce mana consumption.

Worst-case scenario, I have six unspent attribute points, should my mana reserve prove insufficient despite all my effort. As for everything else, I’ll redo as many times as it takes to cast the spells.

The first and simplest of the life-based grand spells forces a lifeform to mutate, mimicking natural evolution, with the caster controlling the general direction.

Ten minutes’ rest recovers my mana, and I capture another lobster. The paralyzed creature can’t move a muscle as I begin to sing of fish crawling onto dry land, struggling for breath. The second image is one of my accelerated crash from childhood into manhood, and all the bodily changes the wreck of puberty implies.

My concept of evolutionary mutation is probably something I should discuss with a shrink one day, but the scene fascinates me enough for the spell to complete with me not giving the lobster any direction to mutate in, so it remains the same.

With a sigh, I sit down for another half an hour to recover mana and try the spell again, this time intending for the lobster to become smaller and harmless. I complete the song and the gestures, over half my mana spent, but the paralyzed lobster still remains unchanged.

I try twice more with the same result and vaporize the lobster in annoyance.

“It’s not the lobster’s fault,” Blunt says for me, and I keep talking. “Think. Why wouldn’t a spell which promotes evolution work when it’s landing and mana is clearly interacting with the lobster.”

It takes an embarrassing five minutes for my massive intellect to process its massive stupidity. Lifeforms adapt to circumstances, not requests. They don’t want to be bigger or smaller, they have the conditions and the opportunity to improve their odds at survival, so they do.

I capture another lobster, and this time cast the spell thinking of food shortages and narrow crevices one needs to enter to escape terrifying amorphous predators.

The lobster shrinks, its armor growing thinner, lighter, focused on speed and agility rather than what one might loosely term impregnable defense. I feel like letting the lucky winner free, but I will need it for my next spell, clone.

A day and a half later, a level up notification appears, giving me the choice between Expert Awakened Consciousness and Initial Parallel Thinking. The choice is simple and I almost make it before realizing I might be making a mistake.

Edna once mentioned archmages could cast two spells at the same time thanks to parallel thinking and some other feats, probably Ignore Song and Ignore Movement, which I already have. Since this is an experimental run, I choose Initial Parallel Thinking and read my next level up condition.

Alter a total of fifty lifeforms.

Easy enough, if time consuming, and four days later, I meet the level up condition.

[You have leveled up.

Select a skill within sixty seconds or a random one will be assigned to you.

Advanced Biomancy - Greatly advances your knowledge of engineering life.

Intuitive Shaping - You have a knack for reshaping life.]

I stare at the screen. I don’t think I’ve ever seen a level up option to advance a skill by two tiers. Which begs the question of how powerful Intuitive Shaping is. I choose biomancy, hoping I might develop the initial tier on my own before selecting it next time.

Optimize a natural process in a living being without adverse effects. Well, I’ve never had a decision prove itself wrong quite as quickly. Then again, I have ten days to achieve the feat, so it might not be that bad.

I fail, but a redo later, I’m entering the dungeon again. This time, I’m a level one archmage two hours after stepping into the dungeon, and I modify fifty lifeforms in another thirty hours.

By knowing what I need to do, I achieved in a day what I took a week before. This time, I pick Intuitive Shaping, and after crashing for six hours, I head to the sixth floor to find a fresh monster. An unlucky tarantula falls into my hands. I’m already intimately familiar with the monster and its anatomy, so I get to work straight away.

Is my mind fraying?

“Probably not.”

“Thanks, Blunt. Talking to myself is really encouraging.”

Instead of further arguing with my skill, I focus on the tarantula. My new skill tells me the creature’s web glands are vestigial, merely useless lumps inside their bodies. The ways I could optimize this is to broaden the channels leading outside the spider’s body, letting it use its silk once more, or completely removing the organ.

While both should benefit the creature, the former requires less work and seems to fit the criteria better. I sing of puberty to force the paralyzed creature’s evolution to adapt for an unlikely scenario. It should prepare for a life in a world in which prey is abundant for half a year, then scarce for the second half. Storing it inside webbed cocoons should be an ideal solution.

The nudge works, and BSD notification appears even before I’m done with the spell. I keep at it, using my Initial Parallel Thinking to read the skill descriptions while still casting the spell.

The choice is so unbelievable, I nearly drop the spell and leave a half-finished tarantula.

Initial Mana Affinity and Expert Mana Ambivalence.

I’m intimately familiar with the skills, and I make my choice without hesitation, but the point is these skills appeared so much later for all my other mage classes, and here it’s a level three option. Better yet, the level up condition is simplicity itself - Reverse the biological changes you have made without consequences.

There is a grand spell for that, so I finish mutating the tarantula, and after recovering, start casting a grand spell designed to erase magical mutations. To save time, I sink all my available points into physique and cast the spell without song or movement. Otherwise, getting the proper casting right by following the memorized written instructions might take hours.

Mana flows into the paralyzed arachnid, and within moments, it starts disintegrating. Its body splits, then flakes and falls apart into a sizzling heap. I repeat the process three more times with different specimens, but all the bugs die the same, gruesome deaths.

“What am I doing wrong?”

Then it hits me. I capture a giant centipede, and purge it of its magical mutations without altering it, and the creature falls apart just like the other ones, proving my hypothesis. It’s a creature made of magic; erasing magical influence deconstructs its entire body.

At least I figured it out quickly.

I sigh and head outside into the torrential rain. A hundred yards away, a stream is swelling under the night’s downpour, the fish inside swimming happily. I grab one, and take it to the dungeon, where I evolve it to survive outside water.

Then I undo the spell which makes it a proto-amphibian and get another notification.

[You have leveled up.

Select a skill within sixty seconds or a random one will be assigned to you.

Spell Improvisation - You no longer require spells to perform magic.

Spell Mastery - You double the effectiveness of any spells you cast.]

Insane! Both skills are insane, and I want them! I read the descriptions over and over again, and I know how to heal Edna. It’s still too early, I want to continue my experiments, but Spell Improvisation is the key to disabling her and restoring her mind.

With Expert Mana Ambivalence, Intuitive Shaping, distilled mana, and superior stats, I can overpower her, undo the recent changes she had made to her body, and set her free. But another, potentially better possibility comes to mind.

Perhaps by becoming an archmage of another path, I could enjoy even greater benefits.

While a portion of my mind explores potential cures for Edna, another part reads the level up condition.

Help ten apprentices become mages. Yeah, that one’s not happening for a couple of months, probably not until we overthrow the church.

Still, it’s attainable, and the path forward exists.
