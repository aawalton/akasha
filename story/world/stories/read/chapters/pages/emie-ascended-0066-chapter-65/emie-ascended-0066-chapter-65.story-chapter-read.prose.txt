“So, you’re both done with the training, then?” Cora asked as we made our way from their new residence to Arkenton’s Dungeon Complex at the end of the week.

The weather was nice enough that we decided to walk instead of teleporting.

“Not entirely,” I answered. “I’m pretty sure they plan to continue training next week while we’re on call to respond to any incursions.”

With a thoughtful expression, Cora said, “Well, I guess that’s better than sitting around being bored. Do you think you’re capable of moving a dungeon on your own already?”

“Maybe, though I doubt I’d be able to do it without losing most of the floors.”

Though I wasn’t able to directly follow everything Veridia had demonstrated, I’d been able to ask plenty of follow-on questions after our little excursion, and I felt like I understood the process well enough to manage it in a pinch.

“Speaking of floors,” Mike interjected. “We should probably discuss what we’ll be facing when we enter the dungeon. We’ll almost certainly be entering on the sixteenth floor since that marks the beginning of the bronze-ranked floors for this dungeon.”

That was five floors earlier than Sorenguard’s Ascender Dungeon, but that was pretty normal. In fact, Sorenguard’s dungeon was probably the outlier in having a full twenty floors dedicated to beginners.

“What kind of information were you able to find about the floor itself?” Stephen asked.

“There’s actually quite a lot of information available,” Mike stated confidently. “Floor sixteen is actually a twilight floor, where the lighting is always dim, and there is no real day/night cycle. However, the lighting does change a bit, so it isn’t completely static.”

“How does the dungeon handle respawns?” Stephen asked. “Does it happen during one of the shifts?”

“New mobs are spawned twelve hours after the old ones are killed. There is no set renewal period, which is both good and bad. Luckily, there are safe zones spread throughout the floor, much like what we’re used to seeing.”

“What about instancing?” I asked. “Are we going to have to worry about running into other parties on the floor?”

“Yes. In fact, this dungeon allows up to ten parties on every bronze-ranked floor.”

Stephen’s grip tightened slightly on my hand, but he didn’t say anything at the news. Considering he’d just spent a few days shifting in front of some of our classmates, I was hopeful that meant he was finally starting to get comfortable with the idea of others learning about his shapeshifting ability.

We stepped through the door leading to the dungeon access point and stopped. The room was quite a bit smaller than what we were used to. To make matters worse,  the number of people gathered inside made it feel all the more cramped inside.

“I didn’t think there’d be this many people waiting to enter the dungeon,” Cora commented as she followed Mike away from the entrance.

Unlike the large, open room housing the dungeon’s entrance in Sorenguard, Arkenton’s Dungeon Complex had done things differently. Part of the reason the room that we’d entered was so much smaller than what we were used to was that it was essentially just a waiting room for those preparing to enter the dungeon.

From what I could tell, the actual access point into the dungeon was in a different room, which made a certain sort of sense. Most likely, exiting the dungeon also led to a separate room where all of the tax stuff could be handled away from curious eyes.

“Should we put our names on the list?” Stephen asked, pointing at a desk near a large door on the other side of the room.

“We might as well.”

After registering our party for the dungeon, we were given a slip of paper with the number ‘22’ written in bronze ink.

With as many people as there were waiting, I expected it to take a while before we made it inside the dungeon. However, I was pleasantly surprised when ‘bronze 22’ was called out just a few minutes later.

“They need to establish a similar process in Sorenguard,” I commented after we passed through the dungeon’s entrance.

“I agree. That was surprisingly well organized,” Cora said before focusing her attention on our new surroundings.

“There shouldn’t be much to harvest in or around the safe zones in this dungeon,” Mike informed us. “I believe there are some valuable herbs deeper inside, though, so we’ll all need to keep an eye out.”

“Do you know what kinds?” I asked.

“Some flowers called Sleeping Pixies and Prismatic Grass, if I recall correctly. There might be others, but those are the two I remember.”

I was vaguely familiar with the flowers – they were known to only bloom at twilight and dawn, so their appearance in a dungeon with such an environment wasn’t all that surprising.

Prismatic Grass, however, was completely new to me.

“Sleeping Pixies are supposed to look like a pair of fairies sleeping atop a bud,” Mike continued. “They aren’t as valuable as Prismatic Grass, but that’s partly because they are easier to find.

“Apparently, Prismatic Grass is extremely difficult to find. It’s supposed to be found somewhere near a waterfall, but I guess the mist makes it hard to actually locate the stalks.”

“Well, hopefully having two people with Herbalism will give us an advantage,” Cora said with a grin as she patted Mike’s arm.

“What about mobs?” Stephen asked.

“The most dangerous non-boss is probably the Night Stalker. It’s a feline of some sort, probably a panther or something similar. There are also bats with a confusion attack, a carnivorous vine mob, and something called a Cervidan.

“Cervidans are probably the most common and least dangerous of them all. They are a type of deer, I think. That’s what they look like, at least,” Mike added. “Their hooves and horns are metallic, though.”

“They sound like the perfect targets for Emie’s Lightning Bolt,” Stephen said.

“Anything else we should know?” I asked.

“That’s pretty much it. Is everyone ready to go?”

We’d entered the dungeon geared up and ready to fight, so after a quick round of last-minute checks, we headed deeper into the dimly lit dungeon floor.

“Something is following us,” Stephen said quietly. “My Danger Sense is triggering from behind.”

“It’s probably a Night Stalker,” Mike said, tensing up. “They like to stalk their prey from the trees, and I can’t sense that through my Earth Manipulation skill.” After a moment, he added, “It might also be a small swarm of bats. Either seems equally likely.”

“I’ll send a Shadow Clone to see if I can’t flush whatever it is out,” Cora said.

A shadowy copy of Cora split from her body and boldly dashed toward the trees behind us. While her clone wasn’t truly independent, it was capable of following basic instructions like ‘find the mob stalking us and attack it’.

Though the clone was mostly incorporeal, the skill’s most recent upgrade meant the clone could solidify long enough to attack, making it a truly valuable addition to the party’s arsenal.

“I’ve got a Space Bolt ready if it gets close,” Cora added.

A part of me was jealous that she’d picked up so many of my old skills. I sometimes missed using my trusted Gravity Bomb/Compressed Space Bolt combination.

However, I reminded myself that I could still use both; they just took significantly more focus and time to cast than they would if I gave in and slotted them.

Ultimately, though, I was happy with the variety of skills I had acquired during my time in Dia. I still loved space and time magic, but I also loved having access to so many other types of magic as well.

“Got it,” Stephen called out as he loosed an arrow toward the lower branches of a nearby tree.

The arrow flew too fast for the ambush predator to fully dodge, though it did manage to react fast enough to avoid an instant-kill shot. Unfortunately for the Night Stalker, Cora was ready to follow up with her own attack.

“Good job,” Mike said when the body of the mob fell lifeless to the ground.

As he’d suspected, the creature stalking us was a Night Stalker.

Looking at Stephen, Mike commented, “It’s a good thing you picked up that danger detection skill.”

“Yeah. We’ll need to get something similar if we plan to delve the dungeon without you,” Cora added.

“I believe this dungeon tends to produce similar skills pretty regularly – probably because it has ambush creatures of one sort or another on most floors,” Mike stated. “It shouldn’t be too difficult to acquire something for you that would work.”

“I’m still looking for Spatial Sense if the dungeon somehow produces it,” I volunteered.

“I don’t think that’s one I’ve seen mentioned, but if we happen to come across it, we’ll save it for you,” Mike promised.

Since she’d been the one to land the killing blow, Cora looted the corpse and handed me everything that had dropped.

Though we all had storage items or skills, I continued managing the party’s loot since it made more sense to keep it all together until we left the dungeon.

“Oh! Are those the pixie flowers you mentioned?” Cora asked, pointing toward a small outcropping of rock with colorful blooms peeking through the tall grass growing around the base.

Because of the lighting, everything looked like it had been overlaid with a sepia filter, but even then, it was easy to see the colorful buds from a distance.

“No. Sleeping Pixies aren’t that eye-catching,” I said as I moved a bit closer to identify the flowering plants with both Herbalism and Identify. “They are quite pretty, but don’t seem to have any value otherwise.”

“Then why are you collecting them?” Stephen asked.

“Because they’re pretty,” I replied with a smile before sending another cluster of flowers into storage. “I’ll have to figure out how to keep them alive since the sanctuary doesn’t exist in perpetual twilight, but maybe deep shade will be enough.”

For now, I’d keep them in stasis. But hopefully, I’d be able to plant them in my garden or somewhere else I could enjoy them soon.

“They might be fine in regular lighting as well,” Cora pointed out. “If they are, I wouldn’t mind a pot or two once you get them established. What did your skill say they are called, anyway?”

“Dusk Blossoms,” I said as I pulled the last few blooms into storage.

“Oh, then I doubt they’ll bloom in full light,” Cora said with a disappointed frown.

“Either way, I’ll be sure to save some for you if they survive,” I promised.

The flowers really were quite pretty.
