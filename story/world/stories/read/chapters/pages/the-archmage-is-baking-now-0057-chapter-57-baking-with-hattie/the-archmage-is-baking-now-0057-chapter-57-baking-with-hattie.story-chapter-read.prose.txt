The inn was quiet when they returned, though a few customers seemed to be filtering in for meals and drinks. This set Jane’s mind at ease. Since she and Bella were the inn’s only guests, she had felt a little guilty about skipping dinner.

Once in their room, they spent a few minutes making themselves presentable, which mostly meant changing out of their working clothes into some more of the freshly laundered clothes.

"Do you think this is formal enough?" Bella help up one of the simple dresses she’d worn on the journey. "Hattie seems laidback, but I don't want to insult her."

Jane soaked a rag in a basin of water, then used it to wash the worst of the dust from her feet. "I don't think she is the type to be insulted. By incompetence, maybe, but she’s not going to get her feelings hurt by a dress. Just be normal."

"Fair point. Normal it is."

When they came back downstairs, the common room was almost full. Men and women packed out the tables, eating, drinking, and laughing. It was a lively bunch. Everyone was at ease with each other in the way you only got from sharing an ale after a hard day’s work.

Jane smiled to see it. Trailor seemed like a nice town, if not exactly her nice town.

She caught a few interested glances as they passed through. People had heard that the archmage was in town, apparently, and wanted to see what such a person looked like. Jane tried to keep her expression neutral and friendly, the way it might be if she were walking through the streets of Glenfall on any normal day.

Thankfully, nobody stopped her or Bella, or tried to talk to them. They made it out the door without any more delay than a few friendly nods.

"That was interesting,” Bella said once they were outside. "I expected someone to say something."

"Maybe they're just polite. Or afraid of me, like people tend to be of archmages."

Jane knew it was probably a combination of the two, but there was no point in dwelling on it. She would either solve their problem or she wouldn't. Either way, she would leave eventually. What they thought of her didn’t matter much.

What did matter, though, was her effort. They deserved her best. And she intended to give it to them.

The walk to Hattie's house was surprisingly short. Though Hattie lived on what Dave had described as the other side of town, the actual distance was almost nothing compared to a walk across Glenfall. The whole settlement of Trailor was just a few minutes’ walk in every direction, mostly living spaces punctuated by a few necessary shops. It was more intimate than most places Jane had ever been.

Hattie's house was made of the same reddish-brown brick as everything else, distinguished only by a small herb garden growing in a painted window box. The herbs looked healthier than the other plants Jane had seen around town, though still not as lush as they should have been.

Hattie herself was visible through the front window. Her hand came up in a wave when she spotted them. A moment later, she was at the door.

"Come in! I hope you're hungry."

The inside of the house was warm, and it smelled of spices and baking bread. Jane's stomach, which had been pleasantly full after lunch, suddenly remembered that several hours had passed since that meal. It decided to voice its opinions loudly on the matter, which made Hattie chuckle.

"You are hungry. Good! I couldn't help myself." She pointed to the table. A plate of steaming meat buns sat there, alongside a bottle of wine and three glasses. "I made more during the day. Figured we could eat while we cook and make a whole evening of it."

Bella eyed the plate. "That's going to result in a lot of meat buns. Piles of them.”

"Yes." Hattie didn't seem bothered by this observation in the slightest. "They make a wonderful midnight snack, and I'll send some with you for your lunch tomorrow. Better than whatever you'd scrounge up on your own, I'd wager."

Jane couldn't argue with that logic. She sat down at the table with Bella, and they accepted the glasses of wine Hattie poured for them.

"Now." Hattie poured herself a glass and sat facing them. "Tell me about your findings. What did you learn today?"

Jane reached into her bag and pulled out her notebook. Opening it to that day’s first page, she slid it across the table.

Hattie picked it up and held it at arm's length, squinting slightly. "These are thorough," she said after a moment. Some of her casual country nature was gone now, replaced by what Jane recognized as competent professionalism. "I appreciate that."

Jane spent a few minutes helping her decipher the list of locations and measurements.

“I want to say that things get a little worse the closer we get to the mine,” Jane explained. “But there’s one farm that breaks the pattern so much, it’s hard to have confidence in that theory. The farm is near the mines, but the water level is higher there than anywhere else.”

"This one?" Hattie tapped an entry near the middle of the page, where Jane had recorded the first pump they visited outside town. "That's the Thornton place. It's uphill from the other farms. The whole property sits on a bit of a rise. Not steep enough to notice just looking at it, but I know from various surveys we've had done over the years.”

Jane nodded. "Taking that into account, the mine is clearly indicated as the source of the problem. Or at least the direction of it."

"The mine." Hattie set down the notes and picked up her wine glass. "I was worried you might say that."

"Why worried?"

"Because mining is complicated, dear. There are tunnels going every which way, some of them old and some new. If something in the mine is causing this, finding it could take a very long time."

Jane had been thinking the same thing. The mine represented an enormous amount of additional complexity. Instead of examining pumps on the surface, she would be diving into a maze of underground passages, looking for something that might be anywhere.

"We'll deal with that tomorrow," Bella said, reaching for another meat bun. "Tonight is for cooking."

Hattie stood up and moved to the kitchen counter. "Bella's right. Come on, you two. I'm going to teach you how to make these properly."

The older woman walked them patiently through every step of the process. First, they mixed the dough, then left it to rise while they prepared the filling. Jane carefully noted the proportion of different spices in Hattie’s signature blend, as well as the optimal cooking time for the meat. Once the dough had risen, they divided it into sections, rolled out each section into a circle, and began assembling the buns.

It was simpler than Jane had expected, though the simplicity was deceptive. There were small techniques involved that would have taken her weeks to figure out on her own.

"The dough has to be just wet enough,” Hattie told them. "Too dry, and it cracks. Too wet, and it won't hold its shape. And take care when you’re making the seal on each bun. You don’t want to press down too hard.”

Hattie demonstrated, pressing the edges of a bun together with practiced fingers. Jane tried to copy her movements and produced something that looked more or less correct. Bella did the same, her years of experience in food preparation not betraying her now.

"Not bad for beginners." Hattie lined up their efforts on a baking sheet. "One or two might have some leakages, but I’d say this is a mostly solid batch. And you'll just keep getting better with practice."

They made two full batches over the course of the evening, filling the house with the smell of spices and baking bread. Jane found herself relaxing. Letting the work occupy her hands, her mind wandered free of the focused effort that had occupied it all day.

I needed this.

As they worked, Hattie told them stories about the town, from its foundation to how it had grown over the years.

"The mine has been here for three generations now," she told them. "My grandfather was one of the first to work it. Back then, it was just a few men with pickaxes. Now it's a proper operation."

"How long has the water been a problem?" Bella asked. “A long time?”

Hattie shook her head. "No. That's relatively new. Started maybe two years ago, which we attributed to a bad year for rain. It got worse about six months back. Before that, the pumps were always reliable."

Jane wiped her hands on a cloth, opened her notebook, and jotted that information down. A problem that developed over time suggested something had changed, like the introduction of some new factor that hadn't existed before. Knowing roughly when the effect began would make it easier to find a cause.

By the time the second batch of buns came out of the oven, Jane's notebook had been supplemented with a rough timeline of the water troubles. Hattie had a good memory for details. Between her recollections and the official records Jane had studied before leaving Glenfall, a picture was starting to form.

She studied her last page of notes thoughtfully. "Tomorrow, we check the mine."

"Tomorrow, you check the mine," Hattie agreed. Taking the notebook from Jane’s hands, she closed it and set it aside. "But tonight, we eat."

She wasn't wrong about that. The fresh buns were even better than the ones from dinner. The trio kept snacking, chatting, and sipping wine late into the evening. Jane ate until she was full, then ate one more because Bella dared her to, then accepted a bundle of extras wrapped in cloth for the next day.

"Thank you for this," Jane said as they finally prepared to leave. "For the food, and especially for the baking lesson."

Hattie opened the door for them with a kind smile. "Thank me by doing your best to fix our water. That's all the thanks I need."

The walk back to the inn was quiet, the streets empty except for the occasional lamp burning in a window. Jane's mind was already jumping ahead to the next day, running through the mine and wondering what she might find there.

Bella's hand on her arm pulled her back to the present.

"Stop thinking about work."

"I'm not."

"You are. I can see it on your face." Bella grinned. "Whatever's in that mine will still be there in the morning. Just enjoy the walk. Remember the baking.”

Jane decided to take the advice. It wasn’t that hard, really. Tonight, she had learned a host of new baking techniques from a real master of a particular kind of dish. She had never been able to do that before.

True, she had once learned how to make keln from the former owner of her bakery, but that was different. Shelby had been a shade, summoned by magic. The echo of her memory had vanished once her knowledge had been imparted.

Hattie, on the other hand, was very much alive. And she had generously shared with Jane the best possible gift: her kitchen.

"Fine,” Jane said, savoring the pleasant sense of emotional fullness the evening had given her. “I'll stop thinking about work."

"Good." Bella picked up the pace. "Now let's get back to the inn and get some sleep. I have a feeling tomorrow is going to be a long day."

—

The ride to the mine was smoother than Jane had expected. Dave was clearly taking her comfort seriously, keeping the carriage at a pace that avoided the worst of the bumps and jolts.

It was still a short trip. The mine was closer to town than the outlying farms had been. Even at a sedate pace, they arrived only ten minutes after leaving the inn.

Dave pulled the carriage to a stop and hopped down to open the door for them. "Here we are."

Jane stepped out and stopped, subtly disappointed by what she saw. In her imagination, the entrance to a mine was a cave mouth, leading into darkness and perhaps centered over a track for carts. What she found instead was an enormous pit, open to the sky, with stepped walls descending in rough terraces towards a flat bottom far below. Men moved along the terraces, hauling equipment and working at the exposed rock face with tools.

"This is a mine?" Bella asked, echoing Jane's thoughts. "It looks more like a quarry."

"It's both, really." Dave tied off the horse's reins to a post and came to stand beside them. "It started as a search for ore, way back. They found some iron, enough to be worth digging, but what they really found was good stone. Gradually, the ore became less important, and the slabs became more the goal. Even before the iron ran out."

"So it's called a mine because it started as one."

"That's about the size of it. The proper mine is over that way." Dave pointed at a distant hillside. Jane could just make out what looked like a more traditional mine entrance, a dark opening in the rock face. "Coal and iron, mostly. No stone. Much more like what you are probably thinking."

Before Jane could ask more questions, a figure emerged from a small building near the quarry's edge and made his way toward them. It was Eustice.

"Good morning,” he said, nodding to each of them in turn. "I heard you’d be coming. Mother caught me up this morning. Said you think the problem might be connected to the mine somehow."

Jane nodded. "It's looking that way. The water levels get worse the closer we get to this spot. I’m hoping I can find the source either here or in the mine proper."

“Here’s hoping.” Eustice gestured toward the quarry's edge. "Let me give you the tour."
