Eric spent the rest of the day alternatively meditating to get mana back and pushing everything he had into Regeneration. Cy didn’t say anything or ask about his wellbeing, but he hovered close around Eric all day, clearly keeping an eye on him without “keeping an eye on him.” He slept soundly, and woke up bright and early the next morning to find Cy sleeping beside him, curled up next to his pillow.

Smiling, Eric stretched. It was nice to have someone else care about him for the first time in a long time, even if that someone was a small rabbit with delusions of lordship.

All the sore and tender things cried out as he stretched, and Eric winced and slumped back in bed. Ow. Today might be a wash, too. Regeneration had done work—the wound where the Trinotaur had gored him was a fresh pink scar, but it had closed over, and his ribs, while painful, didn’t scream every time he took a breath anymore. He was recovering, but he wasn’t recovered yet. Another day, maybe two, and he’d be in pristine shape. He didn’t need pristine shape, but it felt silly to push himself and risk his life on an aching body, so Eric closed his eyes and rolled over. Today would be another rest-and-train kind of day.

To be fair? He had plenty to work on when it came to training. Cy had identified that he couldn’t cast Fireball before the System warned him; there was something he was lacking that the rabbit knew about and could, perhaps, teach him. And that was overlooking all the things he already had on his plate, like training his aura, learning aura-hardening, and the like. It wouldn’t hurt to get a little physical training in, too, though his body was in kinda rough shape for anything serious. Still, some stretching never went awry.

When he got up an hour or so later, having long-since been abandoned by Cy, he wandered downstairs and found a note on the café counter. Rest up, kid. We need you in good shape.

Eric jolted. Cy caring for him was one thing, but Joe cared, too? Did he have a heart under that oily, slippery showbiz exterior?

The next line cleared things up. Congrats on featuring on tonight’s episode. You went to sleep early, so I recorded it for you. Enjoy.

Eric raised his brows. Damn, I featured? And on the one night I didn’t watch it! He wandered over to the television and turned it on. It hadn’t ever had a menu before, but today, it booted up to a smart-TV style menu. Rather like a hotel’s restricted smart TV, there weren’t many options. Livestreams, Free TV, Birdsong, Recordings, Settings, Options.

“Birdsong?” Eric wondered aloud. He stared at it for a while, then couldn’t resist. He clicked it.

RESTRICTED popped up on the TV in big bold letters.

“Well, okay. I didn’t really want to listen to birdsong anyways.” He picked Recordings.

There was only one, dated the previous day. Eric ordered an egg and bacon breakfast sandwich and a mug of coffee and settled in to watch.

The first half hour or so was the Tutorial drama he’d come to expect. There were new characters every time, and they always found the juiciest, most convoluted drama possible to showcase—which quickly became completely insane, with all of humanity to pick from. Despite being kinda mad about it and not a big fan of reality TV or drama to begin with, Eric found himself sucked in by the various plots despite himself. Where did the wine go? Did Esme drink it, or was Lisa just starting shit? And why were their respective husbands always getting found in lonely tight spaces together, all sweaty and disheveled?

I think anyone who isn’t blind can figure out that last one.

The screen split, and once more, he was unable to follow any of the dozens of individual scenes. At last, it wound down, and a bright red screen flashed. Jagged yellow font in the “cool but savage” kind of genre splayed Featured Fight across the red.

“Whoa whoa, what’s this?” the announcer screeched. “We’ve got a featured fight? And the Game hasn’t even opened properly yet! Prepare yourselves for a rare treat, ladies, gentlemen, and otherwise! Because here… we… go!”

The shot opened on the arena. It panned over the cheering goblins, getting a little too close to their filthy teeth and ragged loincloths, then turned, rotating outward, flying over the rest of the goblins. It showed the Trinotaur from overhead, hurtling down toward its big primary horn in a stomach-sickening lurch, then pulled back, showing its face, its big, rolling eyes, its mouth, dripping spittle, its muscular body, its enormous hoofs. And then, almost reluctantly, the camera turned. It showed Eric from afar, the angle and distance of the shot serving to make Eric look even smaller and weaker in comparison to the Trinotaur than he’d felt in the moment. The long snakeskin jacket wasn’t doing him any favors, either, not when he looked just as nerdy and not-warrior-like as he remembered. He hated to admit it, but he looked like he belonged behind a desk in a cubicle farm (or worse, an open office plan) aside from the awesome coat. He looked like he was a cosplayer who hadn’t wanted to commit to the wig, twiggy frame and nerdy face included. He looked like he was wearing the clothes of someone both cooler and hotter than him.

“Woof,” Eric said. Wonder if the Game eventually makes its star Players hotter, Hollywood style. Or at least photoshops us.

The first part of the fight was hard to watch. He barely scraped a dodge and tossed an ineffectual Firebolt at the Trinotaur’s back, then got thrown, the hit just as brutal as any throw at the rodeo. He winced as he watched himself go flying from outside. It was a weird sensation, knowing how it had felt, and knowing how it looked. It looks worse than it was.

The rest of the fight played out as he remembered. He took another hit, but traded it with a fireball, and then the last hit: him sitting there, smashing the sword into the dust, while the Trinotaur showboated. To the Game’s credit, the camera made him look better than he’d thought he looked. In his head, he’d looked like an idiot, sitting splayed on the ground and beating his weapon into the ground like a kid in a sandbox, but the way they showed it, he could feel the tension, the desperation, the last-stand nature of it all. The camera lingered on him, stretching the moment, cutting from him to the showboating Trinotaur and back, drawing it out. And then the final hit: the second Fireball blowing the Trinotaur apart. The camera cut to the exploding Trinotaur and got in close, showing the gore in shining detail over and over again.

Eric skipped ahead there. It made his stomach lurch, not least because he’d been the one to inflict such horrific destruction on another living being. When the Game finally stopped replaying the gore and cut back to him, he turned it back to regular speed.

“And there we have it! What a glorious victory. Who knew there was already a contestant out there and taking on the Game? Wow! How eager. What a go-getter! Now, back to the Tutorial…”

Eric cut the TV off. He sat there, folding his arms. On the second look, he’d understood why the System had only handed him Fireball on his second try. The first Fireball had been… well, a mess. The explosion was too small, and the fire and the explosion looked to be fighting one another as much as attacking the Trinotaur. He’d combined the spells, but not well. Not successfully.

That’s probably fortunate, he thought, remembering the wreckage of the Trinotaur after the second, much more successfully formed Fireball. If he’d hit himself with that version of the spell, he wouldn’t be alive right now.

It was something to remember. Spells were dangerous weapons that didn’t know friend from foe. He could easily kill himself experimenting with spells, especially if he did it on the fly. He’d really had no option there; the boss was way, way harder than the previous boss he’d fought, and if he hadn’t come up with Fireball, he wouldn’t have survived the encounter. But in the future, he made a note to try and do most of his experimentation on his own time, somewhere safe where he could deal with the fallout.

Might be something to add to the guide. He needed to come up with a name for it. Branding. Honestly, he was surprised Joe hadn’t brought it up yet, but then, Joe was kind of on the fence about the guide in the first place, so it wasn’t that shocking. The guide was his baby, at the end of the day. He had to take responsibility for it; he couldn’t expect Joe to step up.

And then he giggled. He’d done it! He’d been on an episode, and not just on it, but featured! Sure, it was the clip-show version of the fight; they hadn’t actually let the whole thing play out, but cut from highlight to highlight. Still! That was him. That was his fight!

I’ll have to ask Joe, but I bet I’ve just spiked in viewership. That was one way to take off: appear on some bigger show or established form of media, then grow from there. Now that he’d made a real appearance, he’d been given his one chance, his spark. He had to make sure he didn’t let that spark die.

Breakfast and a show was over. It was time to open some rewards.

First off, the rare reward. It popped onto the ground before him with very little fanfare: a pack of AA batteries, enough to power the Magical Wand for weeks. It was kind of weak for a rare reward unless you considered the power of the Magical Wand, in which case it was absolutely massive. “Let’s go! Big hit off rip,” Eric muttered to himself. He pulled out the Magical Wand and reloaded it, happy to have his last resort back in action. The rest of the AA batteries went into his inventory.

The ultrarare was next. With a glimmer of gold light, a pair of lightweight knitted gloves plopped down on the counter in front of him. He looked them over. They were black on the inside, but on their backs, a snake bared its fangs, knitted into the fabric itself. The outline of the snake was in white, with bright white fangs and gleaming red eyes. He tried them on, wiggling his hands. They fit… well, the way one-size-fit-all usually fit.

He raised his brows. The way things had been going, he’d expected pink bedazzled gloves with pretty ponies on them. Had the System noticed that the pink and powder blue didn’t bother him and swapped tactics once it had noticed him cringing at the snakeskin? Either way, he still didn’t care. Who was going to see him?

Well, I mean, the whole universe, but whatever. They can yell their mockery at their screens all they want. I can’t hear them.

Gloves of Nullify Temperature

These handy little gloves nullify temperatures. What’s the limit? That’s for you to find out. Try dipping your hand in lava! It’ll go great!

“That’s nice for the next dungeon, if nothing else.” He took them off and put them in his inventory for now.

That left one reward: the Linked Dungeon Reward. He opened it up, and a big, fluffy neon-orange puffer jacket popped out onto the counter, along with a big pair of boots in the same color.

Warm Fluffy Jacket and Warm Fluffy Boots

Can be equipped with other armor in the same slot

Cam be equipped without being physically or visibly present

+1 armor, +5 vs ice and cold

It’ll keep you warm. Good job! You figured out the super basic obvious puzzle! Did you make the right choice? The other way around, you would have received a weapon to trivialize the Trinotaur. So what do you think? Is it worth it? How’s that fluffy coat feel now?

“Oh, fuck off,” Eric muttered. His logic had been sound. He had no way of knowing ahead of time that there was a massively dangerous monster in the side he’d picked. With the evidence he’d had at hand, he’d made the best choice he could. Looking back, was it the best choice possible? No. But that was the way life went. Ahead of time, no one could know what would come of their choices. He, and everyone else, just had to pick the best they could and live with it.

Plus, was it the right choice to trivialize the Trinotaur? If he’d known, he surely would have picked it, but at the same time, it was his serious, no-kidding, no-tricks fight with the Trinotaur that had gotten him featured in the Game’s nightly episode. If he’d had that weapon, he wouldn’t have pushed himself to learn Fireball, and he doubted he would have gained 3 levels from the encounter. True, he also wouldn’t be wasting a day resting today, either, but on the other hand, he’d gained a lot from facing the Trinotaur. Calling his decision “the wrong choice” when it had also done so much unforeseen good was ridiculous.

Guess it goes to show. No matter how much information we have, we never have enough to know the fallout of our decisions. Good? Bad? At the end of the day, he was happy with what had happened, and he wouldn’t change a thing. That was all that mattered.

He checked the new clothes over, looking for any silly details the System might have added. As he’d suspected, there was a large cobra embroidered on the back of the jacket in neon orange thread just a little shinier than the matte jacket, and likewise, the boots had a snake embroidered on them, wrapping around them, then coiling back down so a snake bared its fangs on the toe of each boot.

“Cool,” he commented to himself. It was a lot of effort to embarrass him when the armor could be equipped invisibly, but whatever. Maybe the System was bored. It surely had a lot of time on its hands with everyone else in the Tutorial.

The coat and boots went into the inventory with the rest. They’d come of use later, but right now, he’d just be sweating for no reason.

Alright. Rewards opened. Time to check my levels.
