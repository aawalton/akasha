There are few things more foolish than committing a crime in broad daylight. Say, taking a dive off a mountain ledge. Or wandering into rough neighborhoods with nothing but prayers and a full purse of coin, the words 'I’m Clenn—Keep Your Filthy Hands Away' written across your back. It takes no brains to admit all that.

And yet there he was, late afternoon breeze tossing his hair as he crouched above the cellar of a wine shop, watching Cinder fumble with the locks of the trap door. "Any progress?" Kalsin asked, making sure to wrap just the right amount of sarcasm in his voice.

Cinder didn't bother to answer. Kalsin held back the sudden urge to put a finger in her ear. If actions spoke louder than words, his sister might just need to grow another mouth to compensate. Now that was a scary thought if you ever needed one. It was already difficult enough to shut her up most of the time.

Kalsin was supposed to be on the lookout, but their intel assured them the owners weren't going to be around today. "Come on," he said. "It's not too late to leave. Technically, we haven't done anything yet."

"And we'll keep it that way," Cinder said, still focused on the lock, but her silent treatment finally coming to a close. "If shops weren't open to customers, owning one would hardly be a profitable investment."

"That's weak, even for a cover story. We're customers who aren't looking to buy, using an entrance we're not supposed to go through."

Cinder took a moment to send a mildly annoyed look, then went back to work. "Do you really need a reminder? No stealing. Not a single drop misplaced, not a single bottle opened. And for gods' sake, let me do the talking."

"If there's anything to talk to." Kalsin reminded her. Someone had to keep his sister's expectations low, after all. "Which one of your friends was the source? Berley? Becky?"

"His name is Brock, and he works as a hoarder for The Blind Pelican. Likes his liquor nearly as much as his money, money which I paid for the information. He told me there was something down here, a harmless looking thing. Non-human. Too small to be anything else, he said." Cinder clicked her tongue, nearly making Kalsin believe she finally got the lock open. Jerk. "Anyway, I just want to take a looksie."

Kalsin shook his head, perfectly aware of Cinder's obsession with anything mythos related. You'd think a grown woman didn't have to rely on her little brother to act as the voice of reason. Even worse, Kalsin wasn't reliable enough to hold the leash, considering the situation they were in. Nope. Stubborn as she was, he even let himself get dragged along for the ride.

The lock fell away, feeding a spoonful to Cinder's already bloated ego. "Took you long enough," Kalsin said, trying to salvage the unsalvageable.

The trap door opened with a creak, dust falling off at the edges and into the dark hole, with only the first steps of a ladder visible from the outside. Cinder ducked away, blinking rapidly and probably holding back a sneeze. Served her right.

Taking advantage of the distraction, Kalsin quickly wormed his way between his sister and the opening. Cinder could make reckless decisions, fine. Didn't mean she had to take the front row seat in every risky situation.

Unfortunately, his efforts were in vain as Cinder surged forward, catching Kalsin under his arms and, with her enhanced strength, promptly lifted him and put him to the side. "Not in a lifetime, little guy," she said. She gave Kalsin a good natured pat on the shoulder, then she moved to descend.

Kalsin didn't have to wait long until there was a call to follow from below. A prodding of legs, old wood sliding under his fingers, the trap door lightly falling back in place, and they were both standing in the dim lantern lights of the cellar. The temperature had taken a dive down here, a welcome change from the city heat. Lining the cavernous walls, dozens of tapped barrels gleamed with the shine of polished wood, facing a collection of bottles on the opposing side. Their own breathing and a hesitant drip, drip, disturbed the silence.

That, and snoring. The body sprawled on the floor might’ve had something to do with that.

Their intel’s description about the mythos was accurate, if somewhat lacking in details. Like an island in a puddle of wine, a bulging stomach rose and fell rhythmically, with limbs and head joined to make up a creature no more than four feet in height altogether. His eyes, still closed, were too large to be mistaken for human, underlined by a row of pointed teeth peeking from his parted mouth.

Some kind of gremlin or gnoblin or another.

As Kalsin tried to get a closer look, his foot caught a half-empty bottle, landing a splatter of the purplish liquid on the non-human's face. The following curses that Cinder stringed together didn't help matters, and the mythos slowly peeled his eyes open. Cinder pushed her way between him and Kalsin, her short sword drawn and pointed at the blinking creature. Having trained with it hours on end daily, the blade wasn't just for show. Cinder was a good swordswoman. Great, even. One of the few positive results of her mythos-mania.

"Take it easy," Kalsin said. The creature looked harmless enough, but they've heard all sorts of stories about mythos. There was a reason why humans hunted and butchered the things without exception. "Alright, satisfied?" Kalsin whispered, bumping Cinder's shoulder with his hand. "Your first one, face-to-face. Applause, clams and rainbows, now let's get out of here."

He could've been talking to a rock for all the attention Cinder was giving him. "What the hell are you?" she addressed the mythos, sword steady. Not the most tactful way to approach things. Pointing a sharp stick in the general direction of your worries tended to mess with diplomacy.

"Thirsty," the little man said. He bent to pick up the bottle, frowning at the near empty liquid sloshing at the bottom. "Dreams bring the drink, drinks bring the dreams." His face lit up like a bonfire at new year's Fest-Eve as he finally noticed them. "Well met! Get that thing outta my face, no self-respecting clurican likes sharp toys, 'specially not when forced to play with 'em."

"Is that what you are? Clurican?" Cinder's face had never been home to so many different emotions at once. A raised brow of surprise, eyes of suspicion, flushed cheeks of awe, and the slight wrinkle of disgust upon her nose. Kalsin could practically feel the itch in his pocket, pen and paper ready to sketch this spectacle into an object of eternal mockery.

"That's what I said," the clurican said. Throwing back the bottle, he finished the drink off, licking his lips afterwards. "So what's two humans like yourselves came here for? If you want drinks, help yourselves. There's enough for everyone."

Slowly, Cinder lowered her sword. After a moment of hesitation, she sheathed it, but drew her skinning knife and passed it to Kalsin. Just in case. They didn't need to say the words. Carved with delicate lines, the knife's handle had the word vér on its surface. Blood, in an old language they’d heard in a tavern once. Kalsin had made it last year as a birthday gift for his sister, and Cinder had never parted with it. Kalsin flushed with pride holding it, the trust from his sister warming him from head to toe.

"Drink's all yours," Cinder said. "But we'd like to ask a few questions, if you don't mind.” She held up both her hands. “I'm no hunter."

The clurican shrugged. He walked up to the nearest shelf, and in a moment had a new bottle's contents draining down his throat, throwing it away seconds later to replace it with another one.

"Can you tell me about your kind?" Cinder must've had a list somewhere, labeled 'Nonsense To Ask When Confronted With Some-Such Miniature Alcoholic Mythos'. As the clurican kept drinking and looking at her sideways, she continued. "Are there other mythos nearby? Surely if one could hide in the city, there's bound to be others milling around, hiding among us. Where'd you come from? How long do you live? And where's all that wine going anyways?" she finished with a pointed look at the clurican's seemingly endless stomach.

The clurican finished his fourth bottle. "Well, well, aren't you a clurious one?" He then threw away the empty glass, watching it bounce and roll, but staying in one piece, to his apparent disappointment. "Boring. I'm bored."

The mythos closed his eyes, as if thinking hard.

"A deal. Let's make it a deal." The clurican scanned the ceiling as if the stone held liquor-dipped ancient knowledge. "Thirsty, thirsty, thirsty. You must be thirsty. Drink from the barrels, that's my price." He gestured at one of the barrels, its tap closed, then held out a stubby arm for a handshake.

Cinder glanced at Kalsin, making sure he had her back, but didn't look concerned otherwise. "Seems like a pretty one-sided deal to me. Could be poisoned."

"Could be, but it's not."

Cinder took a step closer until the clurican's extended hand was right in front of her. "If you're lying, my friend will open your throat."

Kalsin wasn't so sure about that, but a threat was a threat. He'd play along, as long as Cinder knew what she was doing.

The clurican nodded, the rest of his body still as a statue. Pearly white, pointed teeth grinned. "No lies."

Cinder met the waiting hand with her own. A simple request for a wealth of information. They shook on it.

When Cinder drew her arm back, her hand didn't come back with it.

Like a decaying doll falling apart, it simply detached from the wrist. Without struggle, without a sound of tearing or breaking or blood gushing out, her palm and fingers remained gripping the clurican's hand. Cinder stared at the stump, paralyzed.

Then she screamed.

The nightmare filled with Cinder's desperate whaling, but no matter how hard he tried, Kalsin couldn't wake. It's not real, it couldn’t be real. The clurican's bubbling laughter joined the scene, gleeful, shaking the detached hand maniacally up and down. "Drink. Drink up!" he gurgled between breaths.

Kalsin's body was numbed with fear. His thumb twitched, causing a cascade to shatter his frozen prison, and he lurched into motion. He lunged forward to swipe the knife at the mythos, be he dodged out of reach, backing into a dark corner of the cellar.

"Drink! Drinkdrinkdrinkdrinkdrink," he chanted, manic.

Cinder was on her knees, desperately trying to wrap her arm with the torn sleeve of her shirt, but it remained dry. She met Kalsin's eyes, tears streaming down her face. They were both helpless in the face of the impossible.

Drink, drink what? Kalsin willed his legs to move, staggering to the barrel the clurican had pointed at. Hands shaking, he opened the tap. The liquid that came out was a different shade of red. Not red like wine. Red like blood.

The thin waterfall splashed on the floor, sticky and thick, soiling the air with a repulsive metallic smell. Kalsin felt bile rise in his throat. And through it all, Cinder screamed, her voice bouncing between the cavernous walls.

Kalsin closed the tap, but the blood wouldn't stop flowing. He opened it again, closed it, as if force of will alone could make a difference, but nothing stopped the puddle from growing on the ground, washing away drops of wine and seeping into the cracks. 'Drink, drink' echoed in his head, even though the clurican had disappeared already, gone, his voice an imprint of the past. When Kalsin’s mouth was hovering only an inch away from the tap and a drop hit his tongue, his stomach gave out. He heaved, vomiting.

He wouldn't drink blood. He couldn't. Gods have mercy, he just couldn't do it.

He turned back to Cinder. His sister had quieted down, staring at her bare wrist in pure horror and disbelief. Her face was sickly pale. "It feels... feels like it's still there." Severed bone, flesh and tendons gleamed wetly on the smooth stump's surface. But it wasn’t bleeding. Cinder fell as soon as she tried to stand.

Kalsin rushed to her side, hooking an arm under her shoulder. "We need to get you help. You're..." He saw the tap was still leaking a thin line of blood, the flow ebbing one second, gushing the next. A drop fell from an adjacent tap, starting to patter. "You're losing blood."

His sister was heavy, all lanky muscle and not much else. Kalsin managed to get Cinder on her feet, but taking the first step, they both fell like a stack of cards. He tried lifting again, pushed and pulled like never in his life before, but he was barely moving Cinder. There was no chance of a young teenage boy like him getting her up the ladder.

He searched within himself, harder than he'd ever searched before. Those times when his schoolmates mocked him—get up, surge, just tap into it—had nothing on the desperation with which he now pushed deep inside. His father's disappointed gaze was a sapling compared to the giant oak he wielded as the battering ram, trying to burst through a door. But the door didn't exist.

He couldn't surge. He wasn't enough.

Kalsin took a second to rest his forehead against his sister's. He could hear nothing but the chorus of dripping blood. "Hold on." Her skin was clammy with sweat. "Anise, she lives just around the corner, she'll know what to do." The monster was gone, but still Kalsin locked Cinder's remaining fingers around the knife. "Please, hold on."

Kalsin ran.

Outside, the afternoon had turned darker. That's all he noticed. Every face, sound and smell washed together like a soggy oil painting as he went. He knocked something over. He barreled into people. Shapeless, faceless blurs passed him, a single purpose burning in his mind so bright it blotted out everything else. Except for the buildings. He saw the buildings. A smithy with a wooden sign billowing above the entrance, a hammer striking an anvil. It didn’t make sense. Why would you hammer a naked anvil? A half-moon painted on the wall of a tavern, framed by three yellow stars. Stars weren’t yellow. A small house, garden, colorful flowers and leaves. He threw the door open.

Anise was there, sitting at her mixing table. Just sitting there, or she might've been busy with something, or maybe not. He should've seen, but still it felt as if a tube had descended on the world, only showing glimpses of events unfolding.

He said something. A purple flower plummeted from Anise's fingers. It was beautiful, with an orange bud in the middle. There was more talking, just fragments of words that were muffled by the dripping in his ears. The wrinkles on her face kept changing forms, curving urgently.

Kalsin led the way back. Breathing took effort, his legs were on fire, but they carried him to where he needed to go. All the while his vision pulsed with red rivers as if it was his own lifeblood draining away, discoloring the skies and the city below. It was peaceful, in a monster-under-your-bed sort of way, tucked comfortably in a blanket that wouldn't do a thing if teeth and claws decided to tear into him. That comfort might've been the only thing keeping him from falling apart, but even that much was ripped away when they reached the cellar.

Cinder was sprawled on the ground. She was in the midst of wine bottles and blood, the red pool having crept all the way to her head from under the still leaking barrels. Kalsin watched as Anise touched Cinder's neck, held her temples between her fingers, turned her severed arm this way and that. Her other arm was closely at her side, holding the skinning knife in her hand. Kalsin crouched. He peeled away his sister's fingers and took the blade, feeling the smooth wood slippery with sweat.

He would care for it. He would keep it safe until Cinder got better.

It took him some time to realize Anise had a hand on his shoulder, shaking. "Kalsin? Kalsin, can you hear me?"

He looked into her eyes. They were damp, but she wasn't crying. He nodded, not trusting his voice. When he started turning back to his sister's still form, Anise stopped him, holding his head in place. "Here, now. Everything's alright. Kalsin, it's okay." She smiled tightly. "Cinder is going to be okay."

Kalsin looked from one eye to another, sometimes lost in the wrinkles in-between. Searching for assurance in those old, knowing eyes. "She is?" he asked.

"She's fine, love. Trust me. She will be fine. Your sister just needs some sleep."

When the first sob rocked him, the others followed and he couldn't hold them anymore. The nightmare was finally over. It was out of his hands now and Anise was smart, and a healer, and she knew what to do. If she said Cinder was going to be okay, then Kalsin knew she would be. He cried in Anise's arms until he was drained.

"Go, now," Anise held him at arm's length. "You've done well. Go home, I'll take care of her. Get some fresh air, clear your head, will you?"

"Shouldn't I get more help?"

Anise sniffed. She gave a smile. "I might be old, but I still have enough surge in me to carry her. Now go, love. Enough excitement for one day."

Kalsin stood with trembling legs, but steadier than before. He did all he could.

His sister would live.

A fresh breeze raked across the torch-lit streets. Kalsin wasn't sure when evening had set in. A handful of people milled around at this time, the peaceful quiet only broken by peddlers closing their stalls for the day. Somewhere in the back of his mind, he noted how this would've felt like just another nightly stroll if not for the accident. It was an accident, right? Blame luck, blame the circumstances. They were at the wrong place at the wrong time.

Gods, how's he going to tell father?

A dull pain in his hand commanded his attention. He still held a deathgrip on Cinder's knife, the tiny wooden handle hidden behind his clutching, whitened fingers. He slowly eased up until some of the numbness receded. Sharp things are no toys, he reminded himself. He could've easily cut himself in his carelessness.

He crouched, quickly unlacing his coin pouch from his belt. He took the excess drawstring, tying the pouch and the skinning knife together. He made sure that the single edge was facing outward, unable to cut open the fabric by accident. He gave an experimental tug. Best he could do at the lack of a scabbard. It would hold until he got home.

Kalsin couldn't help but gaze at the little trinket. How could something so cheap and insignificant take up so much room in his mind? He traced each letter of the carved runes, feeling out the incisions in the hard surface.

He had been so embarrassed that night, he remembered. The hall had been full and the atmosphere light, the innkeep laughing as he kept filling glasses the moment they emptied. The sweet smell of apple cider in the air. Cinder, surrounded by her friends, as careless as ever.

Eyes turned his way when Kalsin had approached, putting him under a weight that slowed his body and scrambled his words. He'd almost turned away just then, but Cinder stood, excusing herself with an effortless comment. She'd made it look so easy. Then she'd given him a lopsided smile, leading him down the bar some distance from prying eyes. Just like that, the weight lifting from Kalsin.

The words spoken that night eluded him. At first, when he had presented his gift, Cinder looked at the knife with a pleasant air of interest. Not dismissive by any means, but an expression as familiar as pocket change. Kalsin said something then. It threw Cinder off her game, making her give a more thorough look at the knife.

She then looked Kalsin in the eye, making some remark about amateur craftsmanship, then turned away to order some drinks. But not before Kalsin caught a shining glint in her eyes. The present wasn't just a cheap tool from a woodworker's discount shelf. He'd made it with his own two hands—and that made all the difference to his sister.

The memory scattered as something barreled into Kalsin from behind, lurching him back into the present and knocking him down on the road. Spitting dirt, he looked up to see a figure running away. He scrambled back up and felt for his coin pouch, but his hands landed on empty space. Thief.

He tried calming his breaths. It was fine, no need to panic. There wasn't much money inside, just a few coppers, only enough to buy some sweets or a pair of rolled breads.

And Cinder's knife.

Kalsin frantically looked around, just barely catching as the figure disappeared in a crack between a pair of buildings. He started running. For the second time that day, something descended on his mind, clouding his senses. This time it was different, though. It was anger that pumped his legs.

Of all days, of all people, why was it him that misfortune chose to mock? He hated Brock, hated the mythos, and hated the Scarrd that would try and get their dirty paws on his meager possessions.

Turning the corner, Kalsin spotted the thief farther down the alley, difficult to see with his eyes yet to adjust to the darkness. Dodging a pile of rotting fruits, jumping over a ditch in the ground, he drove himself as fast as he could. Each step brought him closer to his target. He could see better now, but the sorry state of the alley and the half-collapsed shacks with rotten window sills could've been invisible for all he cared. Only a couple of feet now.

The thief didn't have to look behind to know he was catching up. Footsteps were the only sounds in the night. Kalsin saw the thief's head whip around for escape. Started to slow, ready to take another turn, but that was all Kalsin needed, and a moment later they collided. He tried to get a hold on the thief, only managing to make a tear into the weak rags catching on his fingers. His momentum ended in an ungraceful roll, skinning his elbows, but he ignored the pain and scrambled back up.

The Scarrd was already standing, breathing heavily and pointing Cinder's knife at him with the coin pouch tied to it and dangling from the handle.

"I'll fucking gut you," she said, voice scratchy. In accordance with local regional laws, she had short hair, nearly bald and patchy as if shaved by a blunt razor. Her scar started at the bridge of her nose and crossed over to the corner of an accusing eye. It looked as if her skin had collapsed in on itself, leaving a tar-black incision. Like rot eating into the flesh of a fruit. Utterly appalling.

"You deaf, boy? Run before I hurt you." She pitched forward, thrusting towards Kalsin, a jittery and nervous move. Coins clinked.

Voice shaking with anger and maybe a hint of fear, Kalsin stood his ground. "Give it back. The knife, I need it back. You can keep the money."

"Think I'm fucking dense? Last chance to turn around in one piece."

"Please," Kalsin raised a hand, trying to calm both himself and the Scarrd. "It's barely worth anything. It's, it’s not mine. My sister had it ever since we were little. It means a lot to her. To me as well."

He was half successful. While he mostly regained his composure, the girl stalked closer, her disgusting face getting wilder, more frantic by the second. "Barely worth anything?" she said, whispering. "Another month to keep me fed. Barely? That’s how much you value the life of a dirty Scarrd?"

Watching the knife twitch in her hand, Kalsin swallowed. He took a step back. "I need… Please. You can still—"

With a scream, the Scarrd charged at him. He would've been cut open by her wild slash if it weren't for the training Cinder put his little brother through every week. Kalsin waited until the knife barely sailed past him, then stepped in, trying to knock her off balance with a punch. But even with the opening he had, just as she was drawing back her arm, he realized his fist would land right in her eye socket, right on her scar.

Nausea rocked him, hitting with a force more stomach-turning than the stale smell of the sewers. At the last second his aim involuntarily jerked away, just brushing past the Scarrd’s head. The awkward maneuver tipped his balance, sending his body forward.

A knee furrowed into his stomach, and would've doubled him over if his arm weren't still slung over the thief’s shoulder. He strained, but like a thousand times before, he was unable to surge.

Kalsin had never been so sure he was going to die.

He felt hands grabbing him as the Scarrd lifted him off his feet with surge imbued strength, throwing him aside. His back smashed into something heavy, the impact blacking out his vision. A scream tried and failed to escape his throat. Everything hurt. Helpless to force out sounds, his pathetic begging only echoed in his mind. He didn't want it to end this way. His cold body left in a forgotten alley all because of a series of his mistakes.

His breaths were still coming short as the world flickered in and out, only catching glimpses as a figure crouched in front of him. Fingers dug into his hair, sending another spike of pain down his back as his head was wrenched up. "Look at me," he heard the thief say. A slap sent the world reeling again.

The blur slowly sharpened into the Scarrd just an inch away from him. Her mouth was drawn into a cruel sneer. Her nose was covered with grime. One of her eyebrows was nicked in the middle. She hit him across the face again and he couldn't turn away, locked in place. Tears, blood or both dripped down the line of his jaw.

"Look at me, you animal, or I swear I'll gut you," she said, her grip tightening on his hair. "Look at the thing that marks me lesser than your kind."

Kalsin wanted to scream again, but couldn't let out more than a terrified whine. His body wouldn't stop shivering. He didn't want to die. Gathering the last threads of his strength, he focused his eyes. Maybe if he did as she asked, she would let him go. She had to.

The scar was even worse from this close. The black, uneven line stretching between the horizon of her eyes had an oily, unhealthy sheen to it. A sick quality he'd seen on festering infection, but subdued and dry instead of the weeping liquid around dying meat. The blackened veins bordering the scar were too small to notice from afar, but now they burned into his vision. Kalsin started struggling against the grip holding him. Bile rose in his throat again, bitter in his mouth. He swallowed it back.

"I disgust you, hm?" Her words grated like gravel, cutting a hateful, vicious edge. Her free hand locked on his wrist. "Rather put your face to the shit you take in the morning, yeah?"

Kalsin had no strength to resist as she forced his hand higher. He felt dirty just at the thought of touching that thing. The scar repulsed him on a level he couldn't comprehend, its horrid nature doctored into his mind. He remembered another boy in school, Willem. He'd been one of the popular ones, at least until he took a bet to lay his hands on a scar. The few coins he gained were not worth the years of mockery and beatings he had to endure for years to come.

Kalsin didn't know what to expect. An unpleasant, bone-dry texture. Maybe puking his guts out.

Certainly not the sudden wave of agony ripping through his mind the moment his fingers came in contact with the scar.

The sour smell of rot roused Kalsin from sleep. He slowly opened his eyes, catching the first rays of dawn cracking the sky with an orange line above the city. His neck gave an audible crack as he gingerly looked around. The alley was empty save for a cloud of buzzing flies feasting on something foul.

He pushed himself to his feet, aching all over. The planks had a slight dent where his back had smashed into the wood, with tiny pieces of cloth impaled on the occasional splinter. Looking down at himself, most of his outfit had suffered the same fate. Dirt clung to him, the smeared patches of brown demoting his clothes to nothing but rags. A bath. He wanted a bath, just a simple wish to soak, to feel normal again.

Kalsin trudged along the narrow road, his battered body obeying his commands for the most part. With pain and exhaustion as a friendly background noise, soon he found himself back in a respectable part of town. The morning held the promise of a sunny day, making his parched tongue that much heavier in his mouth. He attracted looks as he went, people giving him a wide berth. A stray dog came up to him, sniffing and following him for a while. His long, golden fur reminded him of freshly harvested wheat. With feeling steadily returning to his legs, Kalsin quickened his steps and the dog eventually fell behind. He wished the children pointing at him, tugging at their parents' sleeves would do the same.

Relief squeezed his chest when his neighborhood came into sight. He barely noticed the last threads of his shoe give way, a hole tearing open at his toes. He was home. He pushed himself to keep going, but it took effort to keep himself together for just another minute. Kalsin brushed his nose, sniffing. His hand came away with flakes of dried blood.

The door to their house was locked. Unsure if his voice still worked, he knocked instead. He waited, shifting from foot to foot. Once again he was at the edge of collapsing, the wait worse than the walk, but somehow he managed to stay upright. He knocked again, louder this time. It sent a sharp pain along his knuckles.

The door was suddenly wrenched open, his father standing in the entrance with a stormy expression, but his shout died on his lips as he looked down at Kalsin. His eyes were red and bloodshot as if he hadn't slept a minute. Kalsin shivered under the gaze that was filled with horror and disbelief. Faint crying came from deeper inside.

Guilt gripped Kalsin's throat. He averted his eyes. They must've heard about Cinder already.

"Father?" he whispered. Is she fine? Can I see her? He wanted to ask, but words refused to come.

Kalsin had to back up as Terrin stepped outside, pulling the door closed behind him. He regarded him, expression turning more distant by the second. His eyes closed for a breath. His wrinkles of indecision washing into certainty scared Kalsin more than any Scarrd last evening.

"Samuel Thguon," his father said, voice hollow. "He lives in Gulling, a merchant town south of here." Taking out a handful of yellow coins without looking, he motioned for Kalsin to take the small fortune sitting on his palm. Failing to understand, Kalsin reached out, but Terrin flinched. Coins fell, bouncing all over the stone paving.

"I'm sorry, father," Kalsin burst out. "I shouldn't have let her go down that cellar, it was a stupid idea, I wasn't thinking about Mom's stories about the mythos. I'm so sorry."

Terrin spoke as if he hadn't heard him. "Samuel owes me. He runs a business, personal guards for hire. Tell him Colonel Terrin Zares sent you. Tell him 'the Veiled have been driven back from the third front'." He turned away as if there was nothing left to be said.

Kalsin desperately grabbed his arm. "Let me see Cinder! Is she awake yet?"

With sudden violence Terrin pushed him off, sprawling him on the ground. "Don't you dare get your hands on me!"

"I don't understand, Father."

"No. Not yours."

Kalsin watched from the ground as the door slammed shut, his father's back the last thing he saw from the only home he had ever known. He laid there, weeping. Where would he go? Because of him, his sister was now a cripple and she would never be able to hold a sword. Never live a good life.

Father was right to punish him. To send him away. How could he ever think a freak like him without a sliver of surge should live under the same roof as normal people.

With shaky hands, Kalsin rubbed his eyes. But his skin felt all wrong. He gently prodded at his face, trying to map out the cracked skin under his fingers.

No.

No no no no no.

A cry broke out of him as reality dawned on his battered mind. Fingers curling, he started clawing at the jagged scar between his eyes, but it remained cold and hard as he only succeeded in tearing the skin underneath.

Kalsin was Clenn no longer.

He was Scarrd.
