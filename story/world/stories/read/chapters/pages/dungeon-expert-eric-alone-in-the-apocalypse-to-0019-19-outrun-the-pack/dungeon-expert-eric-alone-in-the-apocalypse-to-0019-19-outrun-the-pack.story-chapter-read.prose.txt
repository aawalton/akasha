He didn’t run far; that would’ve defeated the purpose. Instead, Eric ran laps, kiting the gnomes around the lakeside area and a little ways down the path he’d come from and the path the big gnome had left from. When he heard the distinctive crackle of a gnome about to burst, he whipped around, snatched it up, and lobbed it back at its fellow gnomes. He swapped between throwing the gnome high to try and land a hit center-mass on the gnome pack and rolling it at them like a vaguely cylindrical bowling ball in an attempt to blow the vanguard gnomes back and buy himself a little space and time.

They rolled poorly. Whenever their hats touched the ground, they hared off in that direction like a car with a broken wheel, circling the drain until they stopped moving. If he threw them flat enough, the hats typically stayed off the ground, but he was in a rush. A good chunk of the ones he rolled ended up in the bamboo instead of the horde, so a toss for center-mass generally did more damage than a rolled gnome, though hypothetically the rolled gnomes should have been better.

The horde slowly gained on him. He only had so much room to maneuver, and eventually, the horde figured out it could cut corners and catch up to him. More gnomes ran back from the clearing to rejoin their fellows in chasing Eric, and all he could do was huff and puff and keep running.

Thank goodness I did cardio back in the gas station’s clearing, Eric thought, but even then, his chest burned and legs ached. He’d been running all afternoon. He was on his last legs, and he knew it.

The gnomes, meanwhile, were tireless. They clinked and clattered after him, merciless eyes glowing the same murderous red, stubby little legs powering on. They suffered no exhaustion. Their bodies had no lungs to give out on them. They ran, and ran, and ran, chasing him forever.

Thump. Thump. The earth shook, and the bright red blaze of the giant gnome’s eyes beamed through the bamboo as it hurtled over. Rather than fear, the only thing Eric felt was relief. Finally. Finally, he could stop running! He just had to beat the giant gnome at one last sprint.

It lumbered through the last stand of bamboo and loomed over him, immense, heavy, unstoppable. Its eyes blazed like headlights, and hot steam emanated from between its doleful teeth. Eric swallowed. Why did I think I could outrun this thing, again?

It lowered its head and charged, pointing that conical hat directly at Eric. Eric instinctively ran sideways, out of its path, only to find a mess of little guys waiting for him. It was too late to change his trajectory. If he hesitated, he’d get destroyed by the huge gnome. He charged into them, kicking through them as he ran.

He didn’t have the time to stop and deliberately punt any of them; he just raced through, hoping none of their grabby hands caught ahold of his legs. They grabbed at his jacket, and cold hard porcelain reached for his legs, but fortunately, he wasn’t wearing pants, just shorts. Their hands weren’t big enough to grab his whole leg or nimble enough to pinch onto his skin. One clung to the tail of his coat, dragging the coat against his shoulders and the back of his neck. He reached back and snapped the fabric, and the limpet fell off moments before it exploded. It burst in the crowd of gnomes chasing after him and blew the frontrunners back.

Coming to a halt against the bamboo, Eric turned back. A field of shattered porcelain marked where the big guy had charged through the little gnomes without a pause, and he charged off still, hurtling into the bamboo. The giant gnome slowed, then turned. It lumbered about, not particularly quick on the spin.

“I see,” Eric said aloud, remembering he had an audience. “Right. It’s slow when it turns. I can work with that.”

He looked at the houses, then at the giant gnome. “If it’s over there right now, then…” Eric jogged, standing next to the lake. He stood to the side of the houses, not directly behind them or so close that his presence ignited them.

The gnome finished turning. Its eyes locked on to Eric once again. It lowered its head and charged.

The second it charged, Eric leaped out of its way, then jogged along the whole span of the houses, setting each one alight. They crackled and glowed, rapidly heating up until he could feel the heat from where he turned. The giant gnome finished its charge and turned, facing Eric once again. Eric didn’t wait. He ditched his boots and coat in the bamboo and charged into the lake, splashing into the shallows. The little gnomes all immediately turned and ran after him, eyes burning with vicious passion as they sensed his unobscured mana signature, but they slowed to a stop as the water reached their stubby knees. It looked as if they were afraid to get the brim of their robes wet, even though the robes were made of the same earthenware as the rest of them.

Eric kept going, slogging deeper into the water. Behind him, the giant gnome came to a halt at the water’s edge and roared, glaring at his back. The houses crackled and snapped, on the verge of exploding. Taking one last deep breath, he dove underwater and kicked his way into the deeper part of the lake, swimming as deep as he could.
