Link walked through the icy forest, feeling an increasing dread. The further up they went, the stranger the inside of the mountain became. It was covered in dirt and rock, but the area underneath it didn’t look like rock. It looked like bone, and the rivers veins.

Then there were the sinkholes. They looked like a Venus flytrap. It was like a pair of crusted lips. It was about ten feet across with a split in the center, leading down a shaft full of rows of…

Teeth. That’s how Lent would put it, but he wouldn’t speak it aloud. It was too disturbing—considering that there were dozens of them in all directions.

Ella stood after examining one on her haunches. “Where are the spheres?”

Lent let out a sigh of relief, glad she wanted to get away from the thing. He pointed ten feet up. “Right there.”

Her team set to work to dig up whatever lay beneath the area, but the shovels kept hitting solid ground. Even their weapons weren’t cutting it.

“Unbreakable…” Ella muttered. “I wonder…” She turned to her team. “Stand back.” After they moved, she put her hand on the ground. “Come out, Aphna.”

A conduit spirit shot into the ground, sending a cruel shockwave fifty feet through the ground—

And that’s when the terror began.

The ground split in half above the sphere, and to their horror, what was underneath it was an eye. Its iris was the size of a hot spring, and it bounced around aimlessly before snapping on them.

“What the fuck!” a man screamed. He tried to run, but the entire mountain suddenly shook, knocking over the team.

“W-What the hell is this thing?” Ella cried as she stumbled backward. She hit the ground, but that was fine—she needed her hands on the ground anyway. “I guess it doesn’t matter,” she muttered. “You’re dead!” She released her fog, but before her fog spread three feet, the ground split underneath her.

It was the sinkhole they had just left—and it was filled with jagged teeth. She screamed and scrambled out, but the mountain shook again. She slid backward, leg falling into the sinkhole—and then the maw snapped shut.

Calid’s eyes expanded as the “mountain” before him suddenly opened hundreds of eyes and mouths. The ground rumbled as it slowly rose, gaining hundreds of feet. It was ominous and deadly—but it didn’t get real until a tidal wave of frost spread over it—and it didn’t even blink.

There was a moment of silence as they processed that. Then, it opened its mouths in unison—and it released a blood-curdling screech.

The ground shook as Kalia overlooked the eastern cliffside, staring at the mountainous beast rising toward the heavens. Hayden would later refer to it as Magenta’s Maw—a mythical beast with thousands of eyes and mouths. Mythical was an understatement. It was larger than the largest skyscraper she had ever seen, and twice that across. The thing wasn't a beast—it was a geographical landmark, a permanent high point on a topographical map—and it was hideous. Just staring at it made her sick, and the thought of fighting it gave her bone-slicing anxiety.

A blast of icy fog suddenly spread over it. It spread for what felt like a quarter mile—a truly massive area, even from above. It made the beast billow like a smokestack as it screeched and slammed the ground with its massive feet. The forest shook, and trees collapsed as the ground buckled and quaked.

The Frost Witch’s famed Crystalline Fog spirit was a brutal spirit—but it didn’t do serious damage to the creature. The attack only reached one-twentieth of the creature’s body at most, and her team wasn’t providing her with serious cover fire.

Kalia studied the ground and saw thirty sickles struggling to escape from the endless earthquakes. That’s when the situation finally made sense. She looked at the cliff they were on, and then back at the beast. There was a direct line of sight, and their attacks would reach it.

“You were going to wake it up from up here…” She turned to Hayden. “Weren’t you?”

Hayden didn’t respond. Instead, he turned around and back. “Hurry up. We need to get past the Maw before the teams tailing us start attacking it.”

Photel turned to him in horror. “Get past it as in…”

“We’re using this chaos to get to our destination.” Hayden pointed slightly southwest. “Fringe, Yaka, and I are going to that smaller mountain. You all are going to move southeast…” He pointed to a distinct gray rock. “Toward that rock with absolute stealth. It’s a full speed grind—no breaks. Do you understand?”

Fringe laughed. “No, I don't ‘understand.’ Are you out of your goddamn mind?”

Suddenly, the colossus stomped its feet, and the impact was so devastating that the cliff they were on started to crumble.

“Fringe,” Hayden said. “Wrap us in a barrier and take us down the mountain.”

“Wait!” Tibbi cried. “You took us all the way up here just so we could leave?”

“No, I led the Sickles to this position so they would lure the Maw away from our goal. Now stop questioning me and gather around Fringe. “

Kalia’s body ran to Fringe, but her mind remained frozen after Hayden’s plan clicked in her brain. Even if the Sickles didn’t reach Magenta’s Maw, he was leading Carian’s team to a place where they could attack it. Then, he would wake it and force them to fight, allowing their team to slip past to their desired destination.

It was a brutal plan—but he didn’t need to execute it. The Crimson Sickles showed up—and summarily killed themselves.

Hayden gave them a warning—and they didn’t listen. So she surprisingly felt no empathy or remorse as she entered Fringe’s bubble barrier and jumped with the rest of them, plummeting down the cliff to the forest below. Things were about to get messy—and she had to keep focused on the plan ahead.
