The first thing I do is go and check on those who have been fighting. There are many who were injured, whether by the ballistas, the magic thrown from Kyrian’s ship, or sections of the final wave that crashed onto the deck despite our best efforts to divert it.

I’m relieved to find that there have been remarkably few deaths considering the intensity of the fighting at times. Of course, even one death is too many in such a senseless clash, and my ire rises at Kyrian once more. Still, I’m guiltily glad that I don’t personally know any of the lost.

The good thing about the magical healing of Moriax is that if someone isn’t killed immediately, they have a very good chance of pulling through with help. Gangrene and infection of wounds only occur when no magical healing techniques or materials are available – I have no intention of limiting either for my fighters.

What I do find, however, is that our sole trained Healer is rather swamped with cases that are too serious to be dealt with by a simple poultice or potion – they don’t work as well on those without Classes which is why Healers are still so much in demand. I have no intention of losing more so I roll up my sleeves and get to work, practically putting my hands in someone’s chest as I heal their shattered ribcage.

Very few of my Bonded are among the wounded – only a couple of Maxen’s squad who were originally the slaves I bought in Azaarde – but since the vast majority don’t have Classes, their ability to resist my intervention in their bodies is incredibly limited.

It’s still tiring and there comes a point when I don’t even realise that I’m just staring blankly at an empty bed until a hand lands on my shoulder and makes me jump.

“You need to rest too,” Healer Thessalanor scolds me lightly, a frown on his swarthy face. I give him a wry smile.

“No rest for the wicked,” I reply.

His frown just deepens.

“Well, you’re no good here, this tired. Go – the worst cases are dealt with, and I’ll get to the others once I’ve rested myself.”

I glance over the mess hall that’s been repurposed as a temporary infirmary – food is being served up on deck instead, and lots of it to help people replace what they’ve lost in their healing – and have to admit that he has a point.

When I walked in here, it was a crowded hall that stank of blood and echoed with cries of pain. Now, only a fraction of those who had been here remain, and they are all calm and either lying down in temporary cots set up for them, or sitting with white bandages wrapped around the injured areas.

I nod at Thessalanor gratefully.

“Thanks,” I tell him quietly.

He nods in response, his intent regard letting me know that he recognises my words aren’t only for his consideration of my own needs, but for his calm and unhurried approach that has turned chaos into order.

“Thank you,” he returns seriously. “There are few lords I know who would expend themselves so greatly to heal the common folk who have fought for them.”

I shrug, feeling a little uncomfortable with the praise. While I know he’s right, I see that more as a failing on the part of the other lords than a particularly saintly quality on mine.

Pushing myself to my feet, I nod at the Healer and then head up to the top deck.

There, I find Sarran poring over a load of boxes, a nervous-looking man standing nearby. I don’t blame him – the frown on Sarran’s face is rather forbidding. Before I reach them, Sarran rounds on the man.

“Your tribute is short,” he accuses sternly. “By fifty gold coins.”

“Are you sure?” the man asks, visibly sweating. “Check again – I’m sure it must be right.”

“What’s going on?” I ask as I step closer. Both men turn to look at me and the stranger blanches as he takes me in. I think at first it’s because of my identity, but when Sarran’s eyes linger pointedly on the deck behind me, I realise that I’m leaving bloody footprints everywhere I walk. With the dusky red of my armour and the frequent patches of dried or still-wet blood that decorate it, I probably look as though I’ve walked out of a charnel house.

Controlling a strand of magic, I collect the blood on myself and the deck and then send it flying over the edge of the ship. When I return my attention to the stranger, I see that my use of magic hasn’t exactly reassured him. But if I’ve read the situation right, that might be a good thing.

“This is House Bladestrike’s representative – Ser Yuna.” A Classer, then. I can only guess that he’s low level or not a combatant, based on his reactions to me. “The market value of their tribute is fifty gold coins short of the thousand you demanded.”

I step next to Sarran and peer into the boxes one by one. I see herbs, materials for crafting, even a few Cores…and a small pouch of gold coins.

I don’t ask Sarran if he’s mistaken – that would be an insult I don’t want to levy. Anyway, as Nicholas’ manservant and my Expedition Steward, I trust his judgement. Instead I look at Ker Yuna expectantly.

The man blanches again, looking away and swallowing. I notice his fingers shaking slightly even as he tries to hide them behind his back.

“If you’re considering lying to me, don’t,” I warn him casually and watch as he pales a shade further.

“I…my lord, forgive us, we didn’t intend to give insult,” he says finally, his voice taking on a begging tone. “We have nothing left! All that my lord has to give would be our food stores. Please, Lord Titanbend, please have mercy on us!”

This has a ring of truth. I wish I could confirm with Lathani or Kalanthia, but Kalanthia is still supervising Kyrian and Lathani is somewhere else. I’ll have to trust my own judgement that indicates he’s not lying.

Which then presents me with a dilemma. Arguably, they could sustain themselves on the sea – this ocean is full of fish. However, I’m not keen on the idea of literally taking food from people’s mouths.

“Has he brought the contracts and the slaves from their ship?” I check with Sarran. My steward nods.

“He has – and signed by Heir Bladestrike. I have signed them in your stead in my role as Expedition Steward.”

I nod slowly, my eyes once more flicking over the crates of goods that Heir Bladestrike sent. Apart from trying to inflate the value of the goods he’s passed over, he’s dealt fairly with me. I’ll give him a chance.

“I will accept a promissory note,” I decide finally, “as long as your lord delivers it to me within the next candlemark. The debt can be paid either with resources on the Lost Continent when we next meet, or with gold passed between House Bladestrike and House Titanbend back on the mainland. The limitation of the debt would be until the end of the next calendar year.” That gives him a year and a half – more than enough time.

Relief crosses the emissary’s face and he bows deeply.

“I will inform my lord, Lord Titanbend. I am sure he will be grateful for your patience.”

That remains to be seen. But I answer the representative with a nod and he quickly scurries back to the boat that brought him here with the goods in the first place.

I nod at another pile off to the side.

“House Siren’s tribute?”

Sarran inclines his head.

“All present and correct. There were no problems with Lady Siren’s representative – and before you ask, yes, I have taken possession of her slaves. Lord Torrent’s manservant has passed over the contracts for theirs too.”

His tone is faintly disapproving.

“You have a problem with this?” I invite as neutrally as I can.

Sarran eyes me more carefully.

“Our food stocks may not suffer more mouths to feed,” he points out.

“We have plenty of food stocks – especially if we fish and supplement them,” I argue, using the same point I considered with Bladestrike’s emissary. “Worst comes to worst, our mages can grow some easy fruit or vegetable plants.”

Sarran nods slowly, reluctantly.

“They are also security risks. Several of them have sentences for murder or repeated assault, some sexual. We will need to keep a closer guard on the noble ladies.”

I hold his gaze.

“We can certainly pass on the warning, though I think Tiria and Keshaka are both capable of defending themselves if they must. Anyway, they can’t be that much of a risk or the other lords and lady wouldn’t have brought them along. Consider another point: they might also be helpful for us. More hands to help defend the ships; more people to colonise our new settlement.”

“I hope you are right,” Sarran acknowledges after a slow breath.

I smile at him, though part of me agrees with his caution. “As for House Siren’s tribute, is it in similar goods? Herbs, and crafting materials mainly?”

Sarran sends me a long look telling me that he knows what I’m doing, but he goes with me for now.

“Half is in gold – I suspect Lady Siren’s steward deemed that less valuable for the Lost Continent than the resources. The other half, herbs, yes, and crafting materials that will be particularly useful for enchanting.”

“Good,” I reply. “That will help us repair the damage the fight did to the various ships.”

“On that note, the carpenters and enchanters have come back to me with a report about Lord Torrent’s ship – would you like to hear it?”

“Sure.”

Sarran takes that as his cue to launch into a dispassionate but faintly condemning report about the poor state of Kyrian’s ship. I might have been a touch too enthusiastic with my fire – it seems that many of the enchantments that assure the strength and impermeability of the boards have been compromised and the deck itself is eaten away enough that it’s dangerous to walk on in patches.

I don’t exactly regret winning the fight in the way I did – winning was the most important thing and if I’d held back, that might not have happened. At the same time, I probably could have been a bit more restrained without risking the outcome.

“Can we salvage the ship?” I ask Sarran after he finishes relating the litany of problems the experts have discovered.

“Oh, certainly,” Sarran replies, surprising me a little – it had sounded so serious that I was wondering whether we should just cut the ship loose and be done with it. “Master Mirena, Hunter, and Alyna will have to spend a good few marks repairing or replacing the most important enchantments, but we can then set off without concern that the ship will sink as soon as we start building up any significant speed. The rest of the repairs will take a few days to accomplish, but those can be done while we’re travelling.”

Well, that’s a relief. I exchange a few more words with Sarran, getting a better picture of our logistics and an update about Kyrian. The main cause of this mess is still sulkily unpacking his Inventory under Kalanthia’s stern gaze, but he’s almost done. He has certainly pushed the boat on uncooperative and Kalanthia’s had to use her domain a few times to remind him of his place in this – since his Willpower isn’t high enough to resist her influence, it’s worked as quite a good stick.

Finally, my head spinning a little with figures and logistics, I leave Sarran and descend below decks to speak to another group of people who desperately need more clarity on their future.

For this purpose, I send a message for Loran and Maxen to come and to bring with them Alyna, Rory, and all those on my ship who are still collared.

They don’t need much explanation – not when they’re faced with a group of close to a hundred people currently filling the main crew’s open dormitory. The slaves won’t be able to stay here, but for now, with the mess hall being used as the infirmary, this is the only large enough space to hold everyone.

I’m pleased to note when I enter that someone has thought to give everyone food and water. From how clean most of the bowls are, the meal was very welcome.

Wary gazes fix themselves on me as I step in through the door and those closest to me edge away, one woman drawing her child close. My lips thin as my eyes land on other diminutive forms despite my attempts to keep a blank face. Sarran had warned me about that – children can’t technically be slaves, but when the mother is a slave and a child is born without a non-collared father who acknowledges and supports them, they end up coming along as a package deal. At least there’s a law that prevents children from being forcibly separated from their mother before they come of age at fifteen – that’s more than I was expecting from what I’ve learned of the Moriaxar judicial system.

Of course, there is a catch – in exchange for the child staying with their mother and being fed, housed, and clothed by the mother’s owner, the child is considered an unpaid employee and is therefore obliged to work to the extent of their capabilities if the ‘employer’ demands it of them. Which is probably why they were brought on a journey this dangerous – for their previous contract holders, the mothers offered a two-for-one deal that is more than open to exploitation.

There aren’t many of them – nine children in total, from five different mothers. But I’ll have to figure out what to do with them regardless. Though the five who are ten or older should probably start learning a trade for their own benefit, the four who are still very much small children need to have other arrangements made.

But those are questions for later.

For now, I just introduce myself and give an overview of what treatment they should expect that is starting to become far too familiar. I can tell from the expressions I see that few of the slaves are convinced by my words. They’re guarded and wary – for good reason.

Not only am I a lord, but I’ve just demanded them in ‘tribute’ after winning a battle. At best, that makes me someone who has a purpose for slaves; at worst, that makes me someone who goes through so many of them that I constantly need a new supply. They wouldn’t believe my main motivations so I don’t even try to convince them.

Instead, I send my companions to talk to them, those who are currently my slaves – as uncomfortable as that thought always makes me – and Loran who is walking evidence that I’m willing to free those who distinguish themselves. And then I leave them to get on with.

Fatigue nags at my mind but I force it away – until the last of Torrent’s fleet disappears over the horizon, I won’t feel able to rest.
