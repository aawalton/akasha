The fear on Sakura’s face told Edge the situation was deadly serious.

The crew ducked into an establishment where rooms featuring powerful magitech privacy devices could be rented by the hour—a common precaution for people who wanted to conduct sensitive transactions in a settlement filled with prying eyes and curious ears.

When the protective dome was in place, Sakura turned to the crew and said, “There is a monster in World’s End. The traces would have blended into the background before, but they are clear as day thanks to Gift of the Magi and Monster Slayer. The trail was old, but we crossed it multiple times in just a few blocks. The creature has been here for a while and can mingle with the crowd without drawing attention to itself.”

“It must be an Ancient,” Edge spoke the words everyone was thinking. “The System said the rift was artificial, and the Ancients have been lurking in the shadows for centuries.”

“World’s End is one of the most important settlements on the planet.” Sasha pinched the bridge of her nose. “It’s an ideal place to use as a base of operations.”

“It sounds like the monster was in the city at the same time we were,” Jumo said. “If it could sense scions or was looking for us, we would have found out the hard way already.”

“This is bad,” Trapper said. “Until we learn more, we can’t do anything that might attract its attention. Don’t talk about the rift, mention the Ancients, or discuss the dimensional overlap without a privacy barrier in place.

“They can possess cored creatures, and while the monster could be concealing itself via magical means, it seems far more likely that this Ancient is inhabiting a person. For all we know, it could be anyone, even someone we consider a friend. If we let it know we’ve discovered its presence or had anything to do with closing the portal, I doubt we will live through the day.”

Everyone sat with that grim revelation for a while, wondering if any of their acquaintances were monsters in human form and what the creature was plotting.

“It could be anyone,” Sakura said eventually, “but it’s most likely someone in a position of power. One of the factions’ leaders or someone who can manipulate events behind the scenes. We need to find out who it is and what its objectives are without tipping our hand, then figure out what we can do about it.”

The hunters had been planning to inform the Hunters’ Association about the rifts and impending dimensional overlap but revealing that information the wrong person would be placing their necks in a noose. While they needed to find allies powerful enough to help deal with the threat, the crew had no choice but keep the situation to themselves for the time being.

“We never made it to the Haunted Vale,” Trapper said after mulling the matter over. “We ran into the monsters we were contracted to kill before we reached the sub-biome and spent the last few days hunting them down, encountering dozens more along the way. It’s the best subterfuge I can think of, since accepting that mission is already in the society’s records. Edge can swing back by the sub-biome to pick up the parts we left behind later, but we can’t afford to be associated with the rift more than we already are.

“While we get a handle on this mess, we will deal with the Coalition as planned. Once Widow’s crew returns from Puppet Town and Edge evolves, we can shift our focus to dealing with the Ancients. In the meantime, we will follow the monster’s trail, figure out who we can trust, and learn what it’s planning without giving ourselves away.

“After we rent a room, everyone should check in with the sources we’ve been cultivating over the past few weeks. We need updates on the situation within the free city, including any recent activity by the Wardens or Coalition. We will keep hunting to advance and earn Credits, coordinate with whoever Dialla sends from Puppet Town, and continue cultivating alliances that will benefit our people back home.”

With that, the crew left the establishment and continued walking down Sword Street. They rounded the inner ring road that took them past the arena, passing beneath a network of arcing ramps that connected to all six terraces and cast long shadows upon the mixing bowl below.

Despite the developing dilemma, Edge and Mel shared a smile when they heard the roar of the crowd. They had enjoyed their date at the arena and were eager to enter the ring themselves when they had time to spare.

As they approached the far wall of the butte, the Hunters’ Association came into view. The Association was one of Ord’s major factions. While they weren’t in the same league as the Wardens, thousands of high-stage hunters were numbered amongst their ranks, and the Association had branches in every major city.

The crew crossed into a sprawling plaza featuring five massive towers protruding from the rocky wall. They were the headquarters for each hunting society, although the contents of the bounty hunters’ tower had been razed to ash in the aftermath of the anomaly—when the bounty board was broken and the most prolific manslayers were either murdered or driven out of the free city.

It was a lucky break for Sakura and Edge, since someone had placed an astronomical bounty on Unique cores shortly after the System went on lockdown. They still had to watch their backs, since the planet’s major forces were all searching for scions, but if the board was still intact, every person in the settlement would have been a potential enemy.

While these thoughts crossed his mind, the crew made their way to the shadowkillers’ society. They parked the wagon beside the tower—under the watchful gaze of a stage-three hunter who worked for the Association—then made their way inside.

Edge’s eyes widened when he saw that the lobby was packed. Normally, this society was the least frequented of the four. As he listened to the chatter, he learned the Haunted Vale wasn’t the only area where a swarm had been sighted, although nothing on the same scale as the invasion Trapper’s crew had thwarted.

He didn’t know if the Ancient that had created the dimensional anchor was responsible, but given the timing, it seemed unlikely that the dramatic uptick in monsters was merely a coincidence. His suspicions were confirmed when he heard that many of the creatures had bizarre appearances and were poorly suited to the terrain where they had been discovered.

No one mentioned encountering corrupted magicytes, receiving a quest from the System, or discovering anything that sounded like a rift leading to another dimension. If other portals were the source, they had closed on their own far faster than the rift his team had come across in the Haunted Vale. The general consensus was the phenomenon was somehow related to the death-race dungeon, which should open sometime within the next few months.

While these developments were deeply unsettling, it wasn’t all bad news. The monsters had appeared around the same time and there hadn’t been any new sightings since. Whatever was going on, the Lakelands didn’t seem to be in danger of being overrun, and flying beast riders had been deployed to scout the biome on a rotating basis.

The crew waited in line until they reached the front. When they arrived, the attendant had them step into an adjoining room with a table running along one wall. A senior staff member joined them shortly after, at which point, Trapper shared the story they had fabricated and produced the remains of the monsters they were contracted to kill, which they had found splattered across a ravine in the Vale.

While they hadn’t technically killed the creatures, the hunters had wiped out an infestation that was far more severe, and it was only fair that they were compensated for their efforts. As they were a crew in good standing, the evidence was sufficient for the society to record the job as successfully completed.

“This mission was posted by the Magisters,” the woman said, “and I’m authorized to issue payment immediately. While I transfer the Credits into your leader’s account, have your Guides interface with the pillar.”

They had known this was going to happen. While it was far from ideal, their cover story should hold up and refusing would draw even more attention.

When Edge confirmed a prompt from the System, his Guide detached from his chest before sinking into the magitech pillar filling one corner of the room, updating the society on his recent accomplishments. The rest of the crew did the same, except for Sakura, who had to go through a different process since she was a prisoner and the System didn’t acknowledge freelancers as official Association members.

When it was finished, his hunters’ badge, which had an emblem for each of the five societies, began to change. The shadowkillers’ symbol went from gleaming gold to sparkling gemstone, which meant he was now officially a diamond-rank monster hunter. The rest of the crew shot straight up to gold, which was insane considering that none of them had killed many monsters prior to the disaster.

“Holy hells,” the attendant swore. “That mission must have been way harder than our assessors believed. Your crew can kick some serious ass.” Edge was curious what benefits came with his new diamond status, but under the circumstances, learning the details could wait a little longer.

Before they left, the woman updated Sakura’s ranking too. The scion was a jailbird, not a tourist, which meant she had been sentenced to life on Ord and was still in her original body. Before the disaster, prisoners hadn’t been eligible to join the hunting societies, but World’s End had changed the rules now that everyone was trapped here for the foreseeable future and death was the end for them all. The attendant promoted Sakura to gold and painted that corner of her hunters’ badge by hand.

That just left returning the floating wagon and selling the monster parts sitting in Edge’s vault. He volunteered to handle both tasks while his friends went to the Lost Traveler to see if there was a room available and find another inn if there wasn’t. He retrieved the wagon and made his way over to an assessment station shared by all four societies.

It wasn’t uncommon to request an enclosed room and a privacy device, and the employees were sworn to secrecy by the Association’s rules. The assessor’s eyes went wide when Edge removed the tarp covering the wagon’s contents. “That is an insane haul for a single hunt. I can’t believe you came across so many stage-three monsters and lived to tell the tale.”

“You don’t know the half of it.” He sighed. The man had to bring in a senior appraiser given the value of the materials—a no-nonsense woman with silver hair. When she was done sorting through the contents of the wagon, he started pulling materials out of his vault, which were even more valuable than the rest.

Her eyes twitched as she used her skills to evaluate their grade. Edge didn’t show her the chunk of shell he’d recovered from the lobster, and what little the crew had been able to salvage from the blob. Given the recent surge of monster sightings, their cover story should hold up even if this information was leaked, but there was no way to explain away defeating multiple stage-fours, and he was planning save those materials for his team regardless.

The crew wasn’t in dire need of Credits, so he asked the appraiser to have the Association put the parts up for auction. The next one was still several weeks away but selling these materials to highest bidder would yield the best profit. After receiving his receipt, he thanked the staff and left the station. He returned the floating wagon to the warehouse they’d rented it from and made his way up to the third terrace where the Lost Traveler was located.

Everyone was waiting in the lobby when he stepped through the door, sharing a nod with the guard on duty as he entered the establishment. Lanna, who owned the building, walked over and shook his hand, followed by Astil, a petite blond teenager with serious brown eyes.

Edge was caught off guard when Astil threw her arms around him while wiping away tears. He had grown fond of the resourceful young guide during his time in World’s End, and it seemed the feeling was mutual. Rue refused to be left out of the fun and teleported straight into Astil’s arms—stomach up and ready to be petted.

Rue was the size of a German Shepard after evolving to stage one, and the fox was advancing faster than any beast Trapper had ever seen. However, Astil was cored too. Even at stage one, she was strong enough to hold the adolescent beast with one arm while administering the scritches he’d requested.

It turned out that Lanna had reserved the floor the crew had been renting for the last few weeks, in hopes of their safe return and continued patronage. Since it seemed like the team would be working in the free city for a while longer, they agreed to the same arrangement as before.

The hunters promised to join Lanna for dinner in the inn’s common area, but they were filthy, in desperate need of relaxation, and eager to head up to their rooms. Edge took a shower and slipped into bed, planning to spend some quality time with Mel when she finished washing up.

I’ll just close my eyes for a few minutes, was the last thought to cross his mind before he drifted away.
