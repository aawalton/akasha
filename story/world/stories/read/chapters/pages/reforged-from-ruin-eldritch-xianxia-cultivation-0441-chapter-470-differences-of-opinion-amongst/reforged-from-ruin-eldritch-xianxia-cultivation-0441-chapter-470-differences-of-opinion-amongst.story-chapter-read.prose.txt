Citizens of the Empire, be at peace! As we approach Founding Day for this grand home of ours, we once again are reminded of the glory of our mutual progress, the safety we all enjoy, and the bountiful benefits of our place in the world! We honor the founders who came before and the leaders who follow in their footsteps, working together with our divine Emperor of Emperors to create a world best suited for all the Empire’s people, from our smallest villages to our grandest metropoli.

But that is not to say that nothing may yet change! As we approach this festive day, many peoples are grappling with the loss of loved ones, as the turmoil at our fair Wall has demanded the lives of many promising youths and talented heroes. We mourn their loss, and honor their sacrifice and victories. Be certain that all of their names are recorded on the steles of the Division of War, that honorable institution which protects our borders and empowers our safety to remain ever steadfast. In spite of our losses, the Empire holds firm against the advances of the mindless savages from beyond our borders. This surge of ravenous beasts, demented sorcerers and hateful terrorists is surely the result of desperation, as the Divisions of Exploration and Research approach further truths and yet greater tools with which to neutralize these enemies of our homeland! Their desperation has cost us the lives of many youths upon the bastions and fortress-cities of our great home, but their violence has remained, as ever, insufficient in the face of our loyal forces and divine providence.

So rejoice! We stand once again tested by a cruel, uncaring world, which seeks only to annihilate all that we are and all that we hold dear, but the Empire stands unbroken! We stand above the churning horrors at the gates, protected, educated, fed, and given long, healthy lives by the golden life offered to us by the great powers that be. On this day, let us not forget our grief, but embrace it with the joy and comfort of our safety, honoring what is lost while celebrating all that we have. Glory Be, my fellow citizens! Glory Be!

–Weekly pamphlet delivered to every Imperial Post station throughout the Empire as a prelude to Founding Day

Seven seats for seven manifestations of Raika. Seven different forms of the same person, or of the person-who-was. They are not made equal, nor made alike, but each seat holds them identically to every other.

The first chair is made of black wood, bits of obsidian threaded stylishly into the grain to lend it a sparkle in the midnight of its color. Seated upon it is a woman with skin shaped of colored thread, silken strands making up her pretense at humanity, but well-visible beneath through slits and open sections, there is a sea of black worms, infested by acidic beetles that squirm and eat. In her hand is a knife, which is a flower, which is a cut of perfect Black, and she, of all the seats, seems the most at ease.

The second chair holds a flame, shaped only vaguely in the form of a person. It eats away at its seat, a charred thing of simpler fare, and flickers, dozens of different faces and the vague outlines of bodies flashing in and out of being from that central figure of blazing, multi-hued heat.

The third and fourth chairs come as a pair, close enough to each other that the hand of one of the women upon it reaches over to rest itself on the arm of the other. In one, a stylized obelisk of black metal, its insides pulsing and occasionally venting heat, is planted upon the seat. In the other, a woman made entirely of that same metal, a churning fire in the back of her throat and a series of sharp-edged shapes, the hints of weaponry, shifting in and out of the world as she sits perfectly still, like a statue.

The fifth and sixth seats too come closer than the others, though not by as much as the third and fourth. In one, Rai Ka sits in the form of a goddess of gold and purple. Her hair glows and writhes like tongues of plasma growing from a sunrise, her eyes glimmer with stars from the night sky, her skin armored in strange metals and stranger flora.

Beside her is a seat that holds… nothing. An empty shape, reversed from what comes before it, like a funhouse mirror. The nothingness is large, larger than is necessary, a thing of empty and inverted tendrils and teeth and hunger, but she, too, sits quietly, smiling in the ways that a nothingness can smile.

And then there’s her. Raika, again. Raika, with a thousand scars. Raika, with a body that had to recycle itself to stay alive when suddenly cut off from infinite Qi. Raika, with a missing arm and a bum leg and an almost-human shape to her.

Her chair is also very uncomfortable. It’s fucking annoying.

Reaching out into the mind-space, she pulls a pipe, followed by a packet of herbs ready to be packed into it. “I mean, really. The declarations? Every time? It’s a bit much, no?”

The demi-divine Raika smiles a bit sheepishly, shrugging. “Maybe a bit. It’s pretty fun, though. Plus, we’ve been building up to this for a while, what’s so wrong with being a bit dramatic about it?”

“Words have power,” says the one who calls herself Kai Ra, her smile softer than her divine counterpart’s. “Big words can have big power. If it were someone else, I’d say we should use them sparingly, lest they lose their weight, but we’re us. They’re gonna have weight no matter what.”

“Calling us fat?” the mortal Raika jokes, packing her pipe with a thumb as she holds it one-handed.

“We’re perfectly healthy for our size,” Kai Ra jokes back. “Except maybe the tits. Those have always been a little dramatic.”

“Fuck off, I like the tits.”

“We all like the tits.”

A series of nods and signs of approval from all around.

“I’m more of an ass persons”, says the burning effigy of Raika.

“Ok, we also all like the ass, obviously.”

More nods all around.

“I’m actually more into feet,” says the nothingness shaped like her, wiggling a pair of seven-clawed, armored, digitigrade examples.

Six other figures turn to stare at her, while she smiles all the wider.

“Moving on,” says the demi-divine Rai Ka, “War. Big topic for a big day. We’re almost done with preparations on our end. We’ve rebuilt an internal space, and negotiations are… progressing. Morae’s still a fucking mess, which is finally only sort-of our fault. Turns out a shitload of high-tier cultivators were stuck as border guards, and got pissed off enough to join together in a sort of military revolution. Apparently, enough of their sects were annihilated during the Beast Tide that they have nowhere else to go, and they’re refusing to work with the Peaks in most things. They’re even less excited about working with the Pack, but… not by much. Most of them see beasts as too stupid to properly hate, so they’re not good at working together, but they don’t start fights on-sight as often.”

“I’m having a similar set of issues with the dead,” Kai Ra says. “Most of them are still passive. Most of them refuse not to be, actually, even after I END them, which is a headache in and of itself. Our senior brother is enough to make up for a lot, but without the Church as a whole, we’ll be missing a lot of force. I’m in negotiations with the Holiest of Holies, but it’s more of a ‘come help or I’ll keep nuking your territories’ type of negotiation. Progress, but slow.”

“Pillar? How about you two? How’s progress?”

“Progressing quickly,” intones the genderless braincase. “We have consolidated 99.2% of all previously lost Qi generators and the resulting terraformed zones generated thereof. Additional spatial warping with the aid of Raika 5 has allowed for continued growth and expansion. Increasing the Pack’s dependency on our production of resources has consolidated control over many and has allowed for significant increases in useful biological material.”

The statue of weapons and flickering embers beside Pillar creaks slightly as its mouth opens. “We are… building… better… tools. We… have been… successful.”

“Right,” Raika says, finishing packing her pipe at last. “So… not ideal, but good progress all around, right? What makes this meeting so important, then? Who called it?”

“That would be the second issue. First issue is talking about the coming War, so we’ll get to the other shit.”

“I can’t fucking contribute to this big war,” Raika interrupts. “I’m not involved in that side of things, and I don’t want to be right now. I can keep sending messages around, keep us grounded, but I’m not going to sit here for three days while we discuss a billion little details. When are we going to war, then?”

Silence in the chamber for a moment.

Eventually, it’s broken by the creaking of metal.

“Seven. Months.”

Raika blinks, turning to look at the war-machine.

“She is the expert,” Kai Ra says with a shrug.

“Interfacing with Concepts relating to the coming conflict and analyzing patterns of behavior across 182000 miles of terrain indicates seven months to be an accurate timeframe,” intones the Pillar. “Based on troop movements, conversations detected through infiltration methods, and updates on the progress of other fractionals, approximately seven months, six days, three hours, plus or minus sixteen days, fourteen hours, appears to be the best time to initiate higher conflicts.”

“And that’s certain, then?” she asks.

“No. Nothing is certain. Calculations are based only on the data we possess, not the data we do not. However, based on the progress of Raika 1, Raika 5, Raika 2, myselves, and Raika 4, we believe this is the time that will likely have the best overlap of functional alliances to Imperial weakness.”

“Why would– oh. Right. Founding day.”

“Indeed. A grand celebration and show of force. The distraction it will cause will likely be minimal, due to the enhanced security inherent to such an event, but a minimal advantage is better than no advantage, and the calculated psychological effect of such an assault grants additional benefit.”

“And I’m definitely going to need a few more months,” Rai Ka says. “Even beyond repairs and refitting people, we’re not exactly trusted. Being an existential infestation does tend to spook people, but we… we’re still making up for a lot of hurt. It’s not impossible, but getting people to trust us at all, much less work together, hasn’t been easy.”

“Luckily they’re greedy,” says the nothingness with a glib little grin.

“Right,” Rai Ka sighs. “We’ve got shit no one else has. The Heart-realm has only gotten larger, and there’s a lot more Souls in it now. Between offering enlightenment and resources, acting as instantaneous travel across the Jiang’Hu, and charity work, we’re making progress, just not enough to get people to blindly agree to march, much less not try to kill each other.”

“An additional six-hundred and forty seven neural nets can be made available to facilitate with political analysis,” the Pillar intones.

“That’s way too many. We’re pretty crowded over here as it is. If you can develop maybe ten, but keep them non-sentient?”

“Confirmed. Sentience is only developed when requested. Neural tissue resources do not require intrinsic self-awareness or identity to be functional tools of processing.”

“Appreciated.”

Raika leans all the way past Kai Ra, who nods politely and leans back out of the way to let her. All the way around to “Raika 2”, she stretches her arm towards the flame-effigy. An arm made of purplish-green fire reaches out of the many burning shadows, turning blue at its fingertips and lighting her pipe.

“Thanks,” she whispers, and some of the flame-heads nod.

“Still doesn’t answer my other questions,” she says, taking a puff of the imaginary herbs in her imaginary pipe and enjoying the cool smoke that enters her imaginary lungs. “Seven months, fine, big question answered, y’all can go back to discussing it after I’ve fucked off back to bed. What’s the other shit? Who called the meeting in the first place?”

Rai Ka, her inverse nothing, Kai Ra, the effigy, all turn to look at each other or shrug.

“We did,” intones the flames.

Raika raises an eyebrow at that. “You? Really? I don’t think I’ve heard you say more than six sentences in the last year.”

“Has something happened?” Rai Ka asks.

“Yes.”

There’s a moment of silence as everyone waits for clarification.

“Please inform us of the pertinent details of the event or change,” Pillar eventually offers.

“We have been met by new things. They are immune to our burning, for now. They seek dialogue.”

The room gets a little quieter at that.

“Someone new?” Kai Ra asks, raising a brow of black silk. “That’s interesting. Do they have names?”

“Yes.”

“Please tell us their names,” Rai Ka says, half amused, half annoyed, all focus.

“They call themselves the Speakers of the Sun.”

A few Raikas lean a bit further forward at that.

“Are they an Imperial faction? Someone from the Overgrowth?”

The many-faced effigies of flame shake their head. “No. They are from further south. They are touched by burning wyrms. They are blessed of sand and glass. They seek to commune.”

“And with you spread all across the southeast, makes sense they’d go to you,” Rai Ka says, hand on her chin as she thinks. “Alright. You want one of us to come and negotiate? Meet with them, get a better feel for who they are?”

The Raika of flames nods their heads.

“I can do that,” Kai Ra offers. “Would make for a nice break from arguing with corpses all the time.”

“That’s fine, but I can make a new detachment of us for it. I’ll leave most of me here, working in the Morae, but we can do a little joint operation, work with you, Pillar, and big fire over there.”

“That should work. Would be nice to make something a little joined together, work hand in hand for once.”

Raika sighs, taking another puff of her pipe and exhaling it into a big smoky cloud around her. “All grand and good. Happy to help, but if that’s all…?”

“Actually…”

She frowns, turning to Kai Ra, who’s smiling softly.

“I’ve been spending a lot of time here in the Fallen Kingdom, and then in the Overgrowth. And some of us were talking, reviewing, discussing and all, and…”

Raika frowns harder. “And?”

Kai Ra, Rai Ka, the Daemon-nothing in the shape of some of her, the flames, and even the broken statue of war all smile at her.

“Well, we were thinking that maybe you could do some babysitting for a while?”

Her first reaction is worry. Thinking of a euphemism, think what it might actually mean, worrying about West Laketown and how it might go wrong–

But her second reaction is to stop. Evaluate. Breathe.

In spite of everything, she’s spent years here. Reconnecting with herself, experiencing her own memories from other eyes, feeling what’s happened and sharing her own perspectives on it.

She trusts herself. All of herselves, in spite of a few hiccups here and there. They wouldn’t do something just to fuck with her.

“Define babysitting.”

Kai Ra and Rai Ka both shrug simultaneously. “Well. You know. We’ve got some people a bit on the younger side that could use a break from all this violence and messiness, and Laketown seems just fine for that. So… I was wondering if you might want to spend some time with Jin, one on one.”

Her heart almost stops at that. Her breath catches in her throat. She feels her chest grow tight.

“Oh, and also some others.”

She blinks at that. “Wait, what others?”

“Don’t worry about it. It’ll be great.”

She could just experience their memories. Could just reach past her mind into their minds, conjoined as they are by the astral concepts that bind them to a single identity, a single cohesive, messy, half-concrete whole.

Instead, she just sighs, and takes another pull of her pipe.

“Fuck it. Lemme just clean the house first.”
