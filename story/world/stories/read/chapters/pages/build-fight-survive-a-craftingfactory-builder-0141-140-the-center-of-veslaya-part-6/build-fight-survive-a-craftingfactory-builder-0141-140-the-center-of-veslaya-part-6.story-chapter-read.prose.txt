The thing in the corridor was built wrong and covered in Red Resin.

Blood-red tendrils wrapped its body from the neck down, thick as cables at the torso and thinning to vine-like threads along the arms and legs, the same slick crimson growth that had consumed Doctor Miro from the inside out. The tendrils pulsed with a wet sheen that caught his headlamp and threw it back red. The limbs beneath were human-proportioned but moved wrong, jerky and too fast, the joints bending at angles that looked dislocated until he realized the Resin had restructured the connective tissue into something more flexible than bone and ligament were supposed to allow. The eyes were ruby-red, the irises gone, replaced by boiling crimson.

It wasn't alone. Behind it, filling the corridor in a formation too tight and too synchronized for anything natural, more of them came. Four, six, eight. Each one wrapped in the same Resin growth, each one moving with the same inhuman fluidity. Ruby eyes reflecting his headlamp like an animal's.

Ethan squeezed the detonation trigger.

The first fragmentation charge blew with a concussive wall of pressure and copper shrapnel expanding from the blast plate at a velocity that turned the air white for one full second. The shapes staggered. The turrets opened up behind the blast wave, all six barrels cycling in staggered sequence, penetrator rounds punching through the dust and debris. Bodies went down. The Resin-wrapped bodies were human-sized, human-shaped underneath the growth, the targeting calibration close enough to hit center mass. Ethan's hands were already on the second detonation trigger before the first blast had finished echoing.

The second charge fired. More shapes fell. The corridor filled with noise and the sharp chemical smell of copper shrapnel meeting something wet, and what came out of the wounds wasn't blood. It was the black-red sap he remembered from the forest, thick and dark and smelling of something mineral and alive. But more kept coming. They moved through the wreckage in perfect synchronization, identical strides at identical spacing, stepping over their own dead without hesitation. Faster than the Resin creatures on the surface had ever been.

The third charge blew. The fourth. The turrets cycled through their magazines. The corridor was a slaughterhouse and the shapes kept coming and the shapes kept dying and the ones behind the dead ones kept stepping forward at the same stride, same speed, as if the ones on the ground had never existed.

The first one reached the barrier plating.

Ethan drew the sidearm and fired point-blank into its chest. The round punched through Resin and the body armor underneath, matte-black composite, and the thing dropped backward into the corridor. He stepped forward to the barrier and looked down.

Underneath the Resin, there was a face.

The tendrils didn't cover everything. They wrapped the skull from the temples back, threaded through the hairline, crawled down the jaw in thin red lines like veins drawn on the surface of the skin. But the face between them was intact. The bone structure and brow line. The jaw and the small scar above the left eyebrow.

Ethan stopped.

Everything stopped. The turrets were still firing behind him and Maria was still breathing behind the turrets and Frederick's heartbeat was still pulsing through the chamber floor and none of it reached him because the face on the corridor floor, under the Red Resin, under the ruby eyes that were fading to glass as the body died, was his face.

Julian's face. His twin brother's face.  The same face, identical beneath the crimson threading, down to the scar at 10 when they'd been wrestling in the backyard and Ethan's elbow had caught him wrong and their father had been furious at both of them for a week.

The eyes were open. Under the fading red, they were Julian's eyes, but they were empty.

A second shape hit the barrier plating. Ethan didn't fire. It vaulted the plating and landed two meters from him and he saw the face again under the Resin growth, Julian's face, the scar above the eyebrow, because whatever process had copied him had copied everything before the Resin was threaded through. A third came behind it. A fourth. Same face under every crimson mask.

CelestOS: Ethan. Move. Move now.

But he didn't move. The second clone closed the distance and swung at him with a combat strike that was fast and precise, thrown from a center of gravity he recognized because he'd grown up watching that body move. Julian had been the athlete. Julian had been the one whose feet always knew where the floor was, whose balance never broke, who'd made varsity in two sports with a certainty that came from somewhere deeper than practice. The Resin had made it faster. The muscle memory was in the copy. The enhancement was on top.

The strike connected with his shoulder pauldron and spun him into the barrier. Pain bloomed down his arm. The third clone was on him before he'd recovered, Resin-wrapped hands going for the sidearm, and underneath the tendrils the hands were Julian's hands, the same long fingers, and Ethan couldn't fire.

CelestOS: Genetic match confirmed. Julian Cross. 99.97% concordance. Multiple instances. Red Resin integration at cellular level, not surface contamination. These are clones, Ethan. Cloned and engineered with the Resin deliberately. They are not your brother. Shoot them or they will kill you.

The words didn't land. He was looking at Julian's face two feet from his own, the face he'd shared a womb with, and the ruby-red eyes were locked on his and they contained nothing. No recognition. No memory. Just the Resin and whatever it had made of the body it occupied.

“Julian,” he said.

Nothing. The grip tightened. Red tendrils from the clone's forearms began creeping toward his suit plating, feeling for gaps. His vision started to narrow.

Maria's voice hit him from forty meters away.

“ETHAN!”

She was on the birth platform in active labor and she was watching her husband get strangled by a Resin-wrapped copy of his dead brother on the HUD feed that ran through both their suit systems. His name was the only thing she could throw at it.

He shot the clone in the chest. The hands released. Julian's face fell away from him trailing red tendrils and hit the floor and he shot the next one and the next one and each time it was easier and that was the worst part.

The third came over the barrier low and fast and he shot it in the throat and the clone went down in a spray of black-red sap, making a sound in Julian's voice that Julian had never made. The fourth vaulted the plating and took a round through the shoulder and kept coming, the Resin tendrils around the wound already contracting, trying to seal it, and he had to fire twice more to put it down.

The turrets ran dry. Four of the six magazines emptied, the cycling barrels clicking on hollow chambers. The remaining two had partial loads. CelestOS redistributed fire to cover the gaps. It wasn't enough. The clones were past the barrier plating now, moving through the turret line in pairs, the Resin giving them speed and resilience that Julian's body alone wouldn't have had, absorbing rounds that should have dropped them and staying upright for two, three more steps before they fell.

Ethan fought in retreat. The sidearm kicked in his hand with every shot and every round that penetrated put Julian on the floor with the same empty ruby eyes staring at the ceiling. He was backing toward Maria, three meters at a time, firing and moving, and there was no rhythm to it.

One got close enough to grab his sidearm arm. He headbutted it. The helmet met Julian's nose through a lattice of Resin tendrils and the crack was something he felt in his own face because he'd broken that nose once when they were sixteen and the memory came up like bile and he shoved it down and fired through the clone's sternum at contact range. Black-red sap sprayed across his visor. He pulled the weapon free and kept moving.

Maria was still pushing behind him. He could hear CelestOS's voice between the gunshots, talking her through the contraction. The two soundtracks played over each other, CelestOS coaching his wife through labor and Ethan killing copies of his brother in the space between the platform and the arches, and neither soundtrack stopped.

[CHAMBER DEFENSE STATUS — CRITICAL]

[Heavy turrets: 4 depleted | 2 partial]

[Frag charges: 7 of 8 spent]

[Sidearm ammunition: 14 rounds remaining]

[Hostile casualties (chamber): 11]

[Remaining hostile contacts: 8–10]

[Classification: Julian Cross clones | Red Resin enhanced | Celestitech bioweapon]

A clone caught him from the side. It had come through the secondary approach, the lighter turrets on the far side killing one or two before the rest flanked. The hit took him across the ribs, a Resin-enhanced strike, and something cracked under his suit plating. His breathing went shallow on the right side. He turned and fired twice and the clone dropped and Julian's face hit the floor six meters from the birth platform where Maria was pushing through a contraction with her eyes locked on his HUD feed.

CelestOS: Two fractured ribs, right side. No lung puncture. Pain management available through the suit's medical system but it'll reduce reaction time by eight percent. I'm recommending you decline because you need every millisecond you have.

“Decline,” Ethan said through his teeth. He put his back to the birth platform. Maria was behind him and the crib was behind her and everything that mattered was in a three-meter radius at his back. The remaining clones were spreading through the cathedral, using its width, flanking through the arches, and the Resin had made Julian's muscle memory faster, and Julian had always been better at this than Ethan.

He killed two more. Twelve rounds left. The sidearm was warm in his hand.

The clones stopped.

All of them. At the same instant. Mid-stride, mid-advance, every clone in the chamber froze in place. They stood where they'd stopped, Julian's face repeated six times in the amber light of the cathedral, Resin tendrils pulsing faintly along their bodies, ruby eyes fixed on nothing. Six copies of his brother standing at attention among the arches as if someone had hit a switch.

Someone had.

Footsteps. One set. Slow and deliberate and unhurried, hard-soled boots on stone, coming through the primary approach. The pace of a man who had never once been in a hurry he hadn't chosen.

Patel walked through the corridor of his dead clones without looking down. His armor was Celestitech corporate military, a full combat suit in matte black with resin-composite plating across the chest, shoulders, and thighs, heavier than anything the clones had worn, built for a single user with a single user's budget. His helmet was open. His face was visible. He stepped over Julian's Resin-threaded body. He stepped over another four meters later. He didn't look down either time.

He stopped at the edge of the chamber where the corridor opened into the cathedral space. The gold veins in the floor pulsed once with Frederick's heartbeat, the light washing over his boots, and he looked down at that. He studied it.

He looked at Ethan.

"You look just like him," Patel said. His voice carried the same register Ethan remembered from the Perseverance, unhurried, precise, pitched for a boardroom that wasn't there. "The resemblance really is remarkable. We had to use older samples, of course. Before the degradation set in. But in his prime?" He stepped into the chamber. The gold veins dimmed under his boot and brightened when it lifted. "Beautiful specimen. Celestitech's finest."

"Celestitech's finest. And you used him for corridor clearing." Ethan's voice came from somewhere outside himself.

"I only used thirty-seven copies of him for corridor clearing. I have plenty more, it makes no difference." Patel took another step. "Julian understood what he was signing up for. He volunteered the samples after you stole his mission slot. Signed the consent forms the week after the Perseverance launched. Called it his legacy, actually, since you'd taken the one he was supposed to have." The smile was small, precise, boardroom-polished. "The Resin integration was his idea too. He saw what it did to the local fauna and he asked us to try it. Your brother believed in the mission you stole from him, Cross. More than you ever believed in anything."

Ethan's ribs ground against each other. Julian's face stared up from the floor between his feet. His brain caught on the word Patel had used twice now, the one that didn't fit with the bodies on the ground.

"You said degradation. What does the source material lose per cycle."

Patel tilted his head. The smile changed, sharpening into something that had been waiting behind it.

"You didn't know." Flat, almost satisfied. "They didn't tell you. Julian was a Celestitech asset, and assets have confidentiality clauses." Another step forward. "The cloning process has a cost. Genetic samples degrade with each generation. The source body deteriorates in parallel. Julian knew that when he signed. He knew what it would do to him."

The chamber pulsed gold around them. Frederick's heartbeat, steady and indifferent to the man explaining how he'd turned Ethan's brother into a production line.

"Deteriorates." The word sat wrong in Ethan's mouth because it was present tense and the bodies on the floor were past tense and the gap between those two things opened up under him like a fault line. "He's alive."

Patel stopped walking. For the first time, something crossed his face that wasn't performance.

"Alive. Lucid. Decorated." He let each word land separately. "Julian Cross is the most effective field operative Celestitech has produced in a decade. He ran the Resin integration program from the source side. He selected the enhancement sequences for every clone you just put on the floor." Patel's eyes didn't move from Ethan's. "He wanted to come himself. I told him no, too valuable. But he was very clear about his preferences."

Ethan's hands were shaking around the sidearm grip. He couldn't make them stop.

"Your brother considers you a thief, Cross. You took his name, his clearance codes, his seat on the mission he'd trained two years for, and you got caught before the ship cleared dock." Patel took another step. The gold veins flinched under his boot. "Julian didn't volunteer for the cloning program because he believed in Celestitech. He volunteered because you left him with nothing else. You took his legacy, so he built a new one out of his own genetic material, and he wants it back. Every copy you killed tonight was built from a man who hates you more than I ever could."

Ethan fired. The round hit Patel's chest plate dead center and flattened against the composite and fell to the floor like a coin dropped on stone. Patel looked down at the scuff on the matte-black plating. He looked back up.

"Eleven rounds left," Patel said. "Save them. You're going to need them more than I do."

The six frozen clones took one step forward in perfect unison.
