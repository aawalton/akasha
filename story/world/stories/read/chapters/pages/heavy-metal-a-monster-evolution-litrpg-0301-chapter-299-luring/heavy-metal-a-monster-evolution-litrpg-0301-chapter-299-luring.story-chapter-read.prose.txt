“Report!”

A man riding a horse, draped in expensive-looking white armor, approached the ruined fortress. His armor bore a crest depicting a shielded knight wielding a silver sword. Behind him stood a disciplined column of around a hundred heavy cavalrymen and a similar number of heavily armed foot soldiers.

“Commander Valdemar! The compound has fallen!”

A bloodied scout knelt before the armored commander, his voice shaking violently.

“The warden is dead. The entire guard force was slaughtered, and the main keep is burning!”

Valdemar narrowed his eyes as he stared down at the ruined fortress before him. Clouds of black smoke rose high into the pale gray sky, carrying the smell of charred wood and burnt flesh.

“What of the prisoners?”

“The elves freed every last one of them, Commander.”

The scout kept his head low, seemingly afraid to meet the knight leader’s eyes.

“They took the slaves? All of them?”

“Yes, Commander. They carried many emaciated survivors with them. Their trail leads into the nearby forest.”

The scout answered quickly, his voice clearly tense.

“Those bugs... They dare go against us. Take your men and move into the stronghold. Salvage whatever you can. Prioritize the warden’s research above all else.”

He barked out the orders, but the scout finally gathered enough courage to ask another question.

“What about the guards inside the prison, Commander?”

A brief silence fell over the scorched land. Valdemar stared down at the scout, his expression turning cold beneath the shadow of his steel visor.

“If any guards are still alive, they can dig themselves out. Weakness is not tolerated in the army of the kingdom. Those who failed to hold a mere prison have already proven their irrelevance. Collect the research, secure the underground vaults, and prepare the remaining foot soldiers for pursuit.”

Valdemar spoke in an unforgiving tone. The scout swallowed hard and bowed his head even lower until his forehead nearly touched the wet dirt.

“At once, Commander!”

Valdemar turned his horse toward the dark tree line of the ancient forest. The thick trunks already swallowed much of the fading light, leaving only sparse patches of sunlight between them. It was as if the prison break had been precisely planned, timed so that the escape would take place under the cover of night and leave his forces at a disadvantage.

The mounted knights were powerful but almost useless in close combat within the forest, so Valdemar did not even consider ordering them forward. Instead, the foot soldiers would have to flush the elves out. Once the elven bastards were forced into the open, his knights would slaughter them all and show everyone what happened to those who dared rebel against the humans.

“They won’t get far. The subhumans are carrying wounded and starving prisoners as dead weight. They cannot move quickly. We will hunt them down, butcher their soldiers, and show them the true cost of defiance.”

Valdemar signaled to his officers. Multiple captains immediately shouted out orders, sending columns of heavily armored foot soldiers marching into the gloomy shadows of the forest. The heavy cavalry remained behind, fanning out along the tree line like a wall of iron. They waited patiently, ready to cut down anyone who tried to double back toward the open plain.

Deep within the dense woodland, shadows lengthened beneath the trees. Leaves rustled quietly as the night air grew cold. Rusty stood motionless on a high branch, blending into the darkness in his Wraith armor.

His life detection skill mapped out the forest around him. On the forest floor below, he could sense living beings approaching. Human foot soldiers were spreading out in a sweeping formation, moving through the underbrush with spears lowered and swords drawn.

Beside him, Teral crouched low on a larger branch that could support his weight. Lethira was no longer with them. She had moved away to tend to the injured and lead them to safety.

“There are a lot of them coming from roughly the western side. It seems like they’re fanning out to cover a larger area. We could probably use that to our advantage.”

The forest had grown dark, making it difficult to see. The human soldiers carried several torches and what appeared to be magical lanterns. They were probably using detection skills as well.

“We won’t need to do that, Rusty. We’ve already planned something.”

Teral shook his head, his expression focused on the mission. It was somewhat surprising to see him like this. It was a stark contrast to the young elf Rusty had met during his recent visit to this world. Teral was certainly more mature now, especially compared to someone like Gwen. Even after half a year, Gwen was still very dependent on Rusty, while Teral seemed far more independent.

“So what’s the plan?”

Rusty did not ask too many questions. This was not the time to reminisce about their old times. They had a job to do.

“We just need to lure them to a certain location. Keep them busy long enough for everyone to get out of the forest safely.”

Teral unfolded a map and pointed to their current location. He then traced his finger farther west, stopping at a small clearing.

“We need to get here, but it would be better if we took our time. Otherwise, it won’t work.”

Although the young elf maintained a confident expression, Rusty could see the concern beneath it. A bead of sweat ran down Teral’s forehead, as if everything was still too much for him. Perhaps the confidence was simply a front he had put up for his own sake. If Rusty had not arrived, Teral might have been able to escape on his own, but keeping the armies occupied would have been far more difficult.

“Don’t worry. I’ll keep them busy. See my current form? It’s quite good in an environment like this. Though, you’ll have to keep up with me within the tree line.”

Because this was a summoning mission, Rusty still needed to remain close to his summoner. Otherwise, he could move independently. Fortunately, Teral was a wood elf, accustomed to maneuvering through forests even at night. He was especially skilled at moving through the treetops, something he had already demonstrated by easily keeping up with Rusty’s Wraith form.

Teral nodded and leaped silently onto the next branch. His elven agility proved useful as he moved like a shadow across the treetops. Rusty jumped alongside him, his twilight steel Wraith form blending seamlessly into the night.

Below them, the soft glow of magical lanterns cut through the darkness. The crunch of heavy boots against damp leaves announced the arrival of the first human squad.

Five soldiers walked in a tight formation, spears raised and eyes nervously darting toward every rustle in the bushes.

“I will get their attention and make them chase me. This should give your people enough time to escape.”

This was the current plan: make the soldiers focus on him and him alone. The forest gave him plenty of cover, and his wraith form allowed him to slip through the dense branches without making a single sound.

“I was planning to use my constructs to make a lot of noise… but perhaps this is a better strategy. Great spi… I mean, Rusty.”

Rusty did not argue or question how he planned to escape while running through the forest with living armor constructs and humans surrounding him. It was clear that this part of Teral’s plan was not the greatest. It probably hinged on him supporting multiple living armor constructs.

Yet, he was somewhat depleted when it came to mana and also seemed rather tired, judging by the dark circles under his eyes.

“Don’t worry. Leave it to me and try to recover some of your mana instead!”

He said this before jumping straight down, his clawed hands already reaching for one of the unsuspecting soldiers. They dug into the iron pauldron of the lead soldier, tearing through metal and flesh in a single fluid motion. Before the soldier could scream, Rusty slammed him onto the damp forest floor, driving the air from his lungs with a heavy thud.

“What is that thing?!”

One of them gasped, backing into a patch of thick shrubbery.

“It came from the trees!”

Before the soldiers knew what was happening, strange chains erupted from the ground, locking their legs in place. Rusty spun around, his clawed hands striking toward the gaps in their armor. He aimed for their necks and joints, targeting the vulnerable points where iron plating met leather.

Dark claws tore deep into their throats and chests before they could even react. Blood splattered across the damp underbrush as two more men collapsed into the mud, their spears clattering uselessly against the exposed tree roots.

The last remaining soldier panicked. He dropped his weapon and scrambled backward, reaching for the horn at his belt to signal the others. Rusty let him.

A blast of sound echoed through the quiet woods, alerting every search party in the vicinity. Only after the horn call finished did Rusty step forward, sweeping his clawed hand across the soldier’s throat and silencing him permanently.

The loud signal worked exactly as intended. Heavy footsteps, shouting officers, and the flickering light of distant torches instantly shifted direction, converging on their location from all across the forest.

Now in his D-Plus rank form, Rusty was more than equipped to handle them. As soon as they arrived, his body vanished, fading into the shadows with the help of one of his skills.

“Thanks to ‘Basic Elemental Meld’ evolving, I can even move in this state.”

He was using an old skill of his, one that allowed him to become more or less invisible while surrounded by darkness. Previously, he needed to remain perfectly still for it to work. But now, ever since the basic skill had advanced in grade, he could move slowly while maintaining the effect.

It was limited to walking for now, but that was enough to move through the darkened forest without being discovered.

‘That’s good. Keep chasing me. Keep looking for me.’

He thought to himself as he led the soldiers on a wild goose chase through the dense, dark forest. Torches flickered wildly through the woods as panicked men rushed toward the sound of the horn call, only to find the bloodied bodies of their fallen comrades lying silent in the mud.

Rusty moved without making a sound, an intangible specter forged from twilight steel, a living shadow. Whenever a human squad grouped together, he created a sudden distraction by hurling a heavy stone into the brush or snapping a thick branch somewhere in the distance.

The moment the soldiers turned their magical lanterns toward the noise, Rusty dropped silently from above. His dark claws tore through armor plates and leather padded coats, silencing commanders before they could shout their orders. He never stayed to engage in drawn-out battles, and whenever things got tough, he used his darkness element to conceal himself and keep the humans weakened.

Terror spread through the human ranks like wildfire. Heavy boots squelched through the damp earth as soldiers huddled together in tight circles, spears raised and eyes wide with fear. They swung their weapons at empty air, startling at every rustle of leaves.

High above in the leafy treetops, Teral moved along with him. The young wood elf leaped from branch to branch, keeping pace with Rusty while remaining hidden. Watching Rusty single-handedly dismantle the enemy search parties left Teral in awe. It also gave him time to recover.

“S-strong…”

“Of course I’m strong! And I’m going to get much stronger!”

Rusty sat nearby, proud of his progress. This was not the time to celebrate, though. It seemed it was also time to continue with Teral’s plan.

“This should be enough, Rusty. They are tightening their formations. We can lead them to the clearing. Let’s go.”

Rusty gave a silent nod, adjusting his posture within the trees. Beneath them, the flickering torches of the human force began to merge into tight, defensive clumps. The soldiers were terrified, yet their commanders kept pushing them forward with shouts and threats of harsh punishment.

“Lead the way,” Rusty said through their connection.

“I will make sure they stay right on our heels.”

Teral leaped effortlessly from one thick branch to another, moving deeper into the woods with grace. Rusty shadowed him from below, shifting into his Goliath form and increasing his weight so that his feet thudded loudly against the ground.

“There it is!”

“Follow the monster! The summoner must be close!”

The soldiers had no trouble finding him. The noise was far too obvious. At first, Rusty thought this obvious trap would never work, but it seemed these humans were not too smart.

Perhaps their hubris made them incapable of seeing elves as anything more than inferior subhumans who could only flee. Perhaps their terror of the dark was overriding their common sense. Regardless of the reason, the heavily armored infantry stomped after him like blind beasts charging toward a cliff.

The tree trunks grew sparse as the dense woodland opened into a wide, shallow clearing. Moonbeams filtered through breaks in the leaves above, illuminating the open expanse. Rusty slowed his heavy strides, letting the thud of his boots ring out across the open ground. He looked ahead and saw something he was not expecting. There was not much there.

“Are you sure this is going to work?”

“Yes, Rusty, don’t worry. Just lead them into the middle of the clearing, then run as fast as you can.”

Teral said this as he jumped down from the treetop and started running toward the open meadow. Rusty waited for a moment and only moved once he saw the torches emerge from the forest.

“I see the bastard elf! It’s him! It’s that elf criminal!”

The soldiers shouted as they saw Teral, seemingly identifying him as some sort of criminal. Teral did not wait to ask about it. Instead, he continued running forward, with his summoner up ahead but still within range.

Once the soldiers spilled into the clearing, they started charging after them, firing arrows, throwing spears, and chasing them in a maddened rush. Soon, Rusty reached the middle of the meadow, and that was when he noticed something.

The flowers were gone, replaced by nothing but patches of grass. It had been a long time since he had seen anything like it, but he could still feel faint traces of nature mana in the ground, as if the grass had been grown by it.

“I see, so it’s like that…”

He did not glance down, but continued running. That was when he noticed that Teral was not the only elf nearby. Lethira was there too.

She was holding her bow, aiming at something buried in the ground, something Rusty had just passed. The soldiers ran straight through that spot, pouring into the clearing behind him. Then she fired. The flaming arrow struck the ground, and a giant explosion tore through the center of the meadow.

It was similar to the device they had used to collapse the laboratory. More of them had been buried beneath the ground, and once the reactions began, the resulting explosion was tremendous. Rusty stopped in his tracks, momentarily forgetting that the flames were also coming toward him.

This was not something he had expected the wood elves to be capable of. Explosions. Fire. Burning the very forest they had lived in for centuries. But perhaps, in these troubled times, they too had been forced to change. To become more ruthless, just like the humans hunting them.
