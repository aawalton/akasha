“Are those… death slugs?”

Ace didn’t answer Marcus’s question.

He and his squad knelt behind an outcropping of bushes deep in the Gloomvale Woods. After an uneventful night—thank God—they had headed out on their first focused hunt together. Mid-morning sunlight filtered through gaps in the canopy, casting the forest in a hazy amber glow.

Just as Rachel had said, they’d been able to hunt down a group of slugs not far from their camp. A cluster of giant slugs lay still beside a stream down below.

Per usual, the Identify ability kicked in, and a screen appeared in front of Ace.

———

Corrosive Slugs

Core Racial Trait: Acidic secretions. Burns on contact.

———

“Corrosive slugs, apparently," Tara whispered from several feet behind them, her voice barely audible. “Looks like their acidic secretions are really powerful. See the iridescent sheen on their skin? That’s probably what burns you if you get too close.”

“Yay,” Marcus muttered under his breath.

Ace shifted his weight, careful not to disturb the underbrush. Below them, in a shallow depression, a dozen slugs the size of small dogs glistened in the fading light. Their bodies pulsed with an unnatural shimmer. In the rare moments when they moved, they glided across the ground as if on ice. Any vegetation in their path blackened and curled, dissolving on contact. Their trails of slimy ooze sizzled against outcroppings of stone embedded in the dirt, and the acidic aftermath left narrow paths etched in the rock.

“Perfect targets,” Tara said, positioning herself beside Ace. She already had her bow in hand, and her fingers tested the string tension. “Low level, minimal threat if we coordinate properly.”

Marcus rubbed at the patch of bark-like skin on his forearm. "Yeah, well, everything in this place wants to eat us, so I’m not sure I agree.”

"That's why we're being careful," Ace said. He studied the terrain, mentally mapping the ideal approach and a couple of escape routes. "Everyone remember their roles?"

Tara nodded. "Basic long-range support and healing. Use my bow where I can."

“And don’t hit us,” Ace added with a tilt of his head. “I know you’ve been practicing, but try to only aim for targets that aren’t near us, okay?”

She nodded.

"I'll distract and draw attention," Marcus said, tapping his temple. "Mental manipulation should work well on these mindless things. I’ll stir them up and get them scared enough to scatter.”

“Good,” Ace said. “Olivia?”

Olivia, who had been silent all morning, twirled a long stick she had found. It wasn’t exactly a staff, but it was thick enough to do damage, and it would suffice for the interim "I'll engage directly to keep them scattered and vulnerable. I’ll kill what I can."

"Excellent," Ace said with a satisfied nod. "I'll clean up whatever gets through you, Olivia. We hit them fast, we hit them hard, and we don't get cute. These things are corrosive. It looks like one touch could melt your skin."

“Rachel said they’re easy to kill,” Marcus interjected. “Apparently, the System really wanted us to come this way. She wouldn’t stop talking about them.”

Everyone paused and looked back at her in stunned silence. The soft squish of the slugs inching over the ground below filled the air, alongside a quick gust of wind through the canopy.

“You think maybe you should’ve told us that sooner?” Ace pointed out.

“Why?” Marcus asked. “The System told Rachel everything else about the landscape. Why would this be any different?”

With his thumb, Ace gestured over his shoulder at the slugs. “Because the System is constantly trying to kill us.”

Marcus frowned. “Should we not do this, then?”

Ace rubbed his jaw and, with a glance around at the others, eventually shook his head. “Everyone, be extra careful. This is probably a trap of some kind.”

In answer, a disembodied giggle floated through the air.

Everyone flinched and glanced upward, but the swaying canopy above remained empty.

“God, I hate her,” Tara muttered under her breath.

“Alright, let’s get our heads in the game,” Ace said with a glance back toward the slugs. “Anyone have theories on the best way to approach these things?”

“Rachel would know,” Marcus said gently.

A hush settled across the group as Marcus's words hung in the air. Ace's jaw tightened. He stared at the space where Rachel should have been—their researcher, their analyst, the one who had an uncanny understanding of the world around them and the patience to piece together aspects of the environment that everyone else had missed.

Her absence felt physical, like missing a limb.

Tara sniffled and, a moment later, let out a soft chuckle. “She would already be listing three different approaches and telling us which is the most likely to get us killed.”

The rich, earthy musk of decomposing leaves mingled with something sharper—the acidic tang that surrounded the slugs in the distance. No one moved. No one spoke. They all simply shared a moment of silence for the fallen.

Marcus cleared his throat, the sound unnaturally loud in the stillness. “Sorry. I didn't mean to—”

“Don't apologize,” Ace cut him off, his voice rougher than he had intended it to be. He inhaled deeply, and the damp forest air filled his lungs in a warm rush.

Somewhere nearby, a branch snapped—perhaps from an animal, perhaps from something worse. The sound broke their moment of silence, and Ace jolted back into the present. This was a mission, and these woods were dangerous.

They had to focus.

He glanced toward the distant corrosive slugs, their gelatinous bodies glistening wetly in patches of sunlight. “Any ideas?”

“Hmm.” Olivia’s eyes narrowed as she inched closer to the bushes and peered over their prickly branches. “Look at their heads. Can you see that subtle pulse of light? It’s almost like the Behemoth’s nodes. Maybe we should aim for those.”

In unison, everyone leaned forward to get a better view. Through the translucent tissue of the slugs' bodies, pulses of bioluminescent light rippled in a hypnotic rhythm—amber and pale green light flowing like liquid heartbeats beneath their slimy skin. Each time the light surged, the surrounding vegetation recoiled, and leaves curled inward as minuscule droplets of acid scattered from the slugs' undulating bodies.

“That’s disgusting,” Marcus said flatly.

“Can’t argue with you there,” Ace admitted.

Ace took a deep breath to brace himself against what was about to happen. This was their first carefully planned hunt, and they all needed it to go well. No more fumbling through fights or relying on brute strength and luck.

They needed to operate as a unit.

"On my mark," he whispered.

The team tensed, readying their weapons and summoning their power with the grim efficiency of survivors who had learned that hesitation meant death. They weren’t soldiers, yet, but they were getting there.

Tara's fingers glowed with crimson light as she prepared her healing magic, the unnatural hum casting jagged shadows across her determined face. Marcus's eyes turned obsidian-black as psychic energy built behind them. Olivia stiffened, the fingers of her left hand pressed into the soil at her feet as she prepared to spring forward.

Ace closed his eyes for a moment, surrendering to the familiar cold rush of shadow magic coursing through his veins. It came easier each time. The darkness flooded his system, temporarily swallowing his humanity as he allowed the monster within him to take over, and each breath pumped the cold energy further into his system.

When he opened his eyes again, his vision had sharpened with unnatural clarity, and the world before him shifted. Colors faded. Auras sprang to life along the slugs, pinpointing weak spots along their squishy bodies.

This crystal-sharp, inhuman perception still wasn't fully comfortable. It was almost like wearing someone else's skin stretched over his bones. It reminded him of targeting systems he'd used in combat, except now the hardware was his altered body, and the software was whatever the hell the System had done to his brain.

A shiver of dread shot through his core—not because of the monsters, but because of how natural it felt to become one.

With a firm gesture, Ace signaled for them to move out.

They shot forward and fanned out with a synchronization that would have been impossible just days ago, operating as a killing unit forged from necessity and trauma. Four former humans, moving like a single predatory organism. Tara remained on the small overlook above the river. Meanwhile, Olivia fanned out to one side while Ace and Marcus circled to the other side of the river.

This wasn't just teamwork—it was a collective survival instinct channeled into raw violence.

When everyone was in position and still hidden from the riverbank, Marcus struck first. His eyes flashing with psychic energy. The man stepped forward, his usually jovial face hardening. His eyes darkened, and his eyes went completely black.

Instantly, the slugs convulsed. Their pulsing lights sputtered and faltered. Marcus’s hands shook as his magic flared to life. The invisible tendrils of psychic energy pulled something intangible from the slugs’ bodies, and their heads bent backward in silent screams.

"Got them," Marcus hissed through clenched teeth. "They're... God, it’s disgusting in there. It’s like listening to static.”

Sweat dripped from his forehead as he maintained the connection. The shadows between Marcus and the slugs thickened, condensing into small orbs that orbited his head. Three fragments hovered there—not much, but enough.

The affected slugs writhed, and acid began to leak from their bodies like oil from a car. Small ribbons of bubbling goop leaked from them and burned through the forest floor.

“Try Psychic Rupture,” Ace suggested. “Better to use that before the rest of us get into the range of fire.”

“Good idea.”

Marcus flung his hands forward. Instantly, the air between him and the slugs cracked and warped. A concussive wave of mental energy blasted outward, bending the surreal sunlight as it expanded.

When the wave hit them, the slugs contorted. Five of them—the closest ones—twisted and writhed in unnatural arcs. Two exploded, spraying acidic remains that burned into nearby tree trunks. The others froze, their lights dimming to pinpricks.

Marcus's eyes burned ever darker as he targeted the next group of slugs. Instantly, they recoiled, their gelatinous bodies contracting like fists clenching in pain. Their shimmering skin rippled with involuntary spasms, their primitive nervous systems evidently overloaded with artificially induced panic. They scattered in different directions—exactly as planned.

It was nice, really, for a plan to go exactly how he’d intended it to.

"Not bad for a streamer," Ace said with a grin.

“That’s it for today’s livestream,” Marcus said, chuckling. “Comment, like, and subscribe.”

Ace laughed.

He couldn’t help it.

Acid trails sizzled behind them as the slugs fled, eating into stone with audible hisses that sounded disturbingly like panicked whispers. The ground steamed where their corrosive goop made contact, releasing toxic vapors that caught in the throat and burned the lungs.

The initial maneuver had worked perfectly, creating vulnerabilities where moments before there had been only a unified threat.

But success came at a cost. Marcus swayed slightly on his feet. Blood trickled from his nose in thin streams.

The man caught Ace’s eye and waved away any concern. “I’m alright.”

“Hang back,” Ace ordered. “You did good. Offer us cover, but don’t push yourself too hard.”

“You got it, boss.” Marcus said with a weak salute.

From opposite directions, Ace and Olivia surged forward as one. With each step closer to the surviving slugs, they dodged acidic puddles that smoked and bubbled. As Ace charged the nearest slug, Tara's bow sang. Three arrows launched in rapid succession, and though two hit tree trunks with hollow thunks, the third found its mark in one of the slugs' sensory organs. The creature writhed, emitting high-pitched squeals that sent birds flapping from nearby trees.

Olivia sprinted forward like flowing water, her power carrying her between targets with hypnotic grace. Her makeshift staff whistled through the air, and each hit landed with perfect form.

"Ace, three are moving toward the riverbank!" Tara called out from the high ground.

He didn't hesitate. Shadows enveloped him as he activated Shadow Realm Immersion, his body phasing into the other realm. The world around him turned grayscale, and the sound of their battle muffled. Without a moment’s hesitation, he propelled himself through the space between worlds and reappeared behind the fleeing slugs.

Ace materialized, and as he stepped back into the real world, two shadow-forged daggers solidified in his hands with a flash of smoke. He aimed and sent them both flying. To his delight, both landed perfectly in their targets. The blades sank into two of the slugs’ heads, and the fallen monsters hissed and bubbled before collapsing in on themselves.

The hunt continued with mechanical efficiency, a ballet of coordinated carnage that transformed four desperate survivors into a whirlwind of murder. Ace and Olivia darted through the killing field with a synchronicity earned through bloodstains and lessons learned the hard way.

Once he had recovered, Marcus herded slugs with targeted bursts of terror. Each blast of psychic energy carried a different shade of fear that rippled briefly through Ace before slamming into the herd of slimy beasts head-on—first the primal terror of being trapped and consumed, then the suffocating panic of drowning in acid, and finally the existential horror of dismemberment. The slugs responded to each wave with different patterns of retreat, their nervous systems lacking the complexity to process such varied threats. Their confusion created exploitable gaps.

"Channeling another cluster your way, Ace!" Marcus called out, his voice strained.

Ace smirked as another of his daggers flew through the air. It landed perfectly between one slug’s eyes, and the beast shrieked as it dissolved into a puddle of smoking goop.

He was enjoying this perhaps a little too much.

Olivia danced between the retreating slugs with elegant flips and spins. Each step placed her exactly where she needed to be three seconds before she needed to be there, and her makeshift staff extended her reach to the perfect length for deadly blows to the creatures’ heads. Her Blood Echo Skill manifested as faint crimson afterimages trailing her movements, creating an illusion of multiple attackers striking simultaneously that sent the slugs into full-on panic mode.

Tara's arrows whistled through gaps in the combat zone. She improved with each twang of her bow, and her arrows hit their target more often than not.

Ace's shadow-forged daggers sliced through the air, each blade trailing wisps of darkness like spectral contrails. The weapons left his hands at carefully timed intervals—not thrown but released, as though the shadows themselves were guiding them home.

“That’s it!” Ace shouted amidst the fray. “Almost done!”

Tara's arrows sang past Ace's head, close enough that they sent a ripple of wind through his hair.

“Hey!” Ace said sharply. “Watch out!”

“Sorry!” Tara called out from above. “My bad!”

Ace sighed.

Wave after wave of Marcus’s magic flowed across the battlefield, and the rhythmic slap of Olivia’s makeshift staff against the stone created a deadly drumbeat that echoed through the forest.

This was it.

They were really doing this.

As Ace phased through the Shadow Realm once more, he skidded to a stop and stood with his back to a steep dirt cliff overlooking the battlefield. Arrows rained down on him from above. Two missed, but one hit a slug’s beady eye. The monster recoiled in pain.

This one, however, wasn’t dead—and it charged him with surprising speed. Two more survivors flanked the one with an arrow through its eye, and all three came barreling toward him.

Effortlessly, Ace phased into the shadows. The creatures skidded to a stop as he charged through them, immune to their acidic attacks now that he was in the Shadow Realm. In an instant, he was behind them. In the span of a few seconds, they were trapped between him and the steep edge of the overlook, oblivious to where he had gone.

Yet again, he ignored the rows of humanoid figures in his periphery so that he could focus on the fight.

Once again, he phased back into reality, boxing them into a corner with nowhere to go. He summoned three Duskblades and threw them one by one, releasing the final volley of daggers with ruthless accuracy. The last three slugs burst as the daggers hit. Where their acid splashed across the ground, tendrils of smoke coiled into the air.

In a matter of minutes, the riverbank had become a slaughterhouse.

One by one, the team regrouped. Marcus emerged from the trees, while Olivia hopped over the stream to Ace’s side of the water. Tara slid down the overlook’s steep embankment, and in an instant, they were all together again. It was natural, like instinct, and he didn’t even have to give the order.

The ease of it all disturbed Ace more than the killing itself. This fluid transformation from individuals into a murderous unit hadn't required years of training. Practice and the System's twisted guidance had reshaped them into something optimized for this blood-soaked reality.

The most efficient predators weren't born—they were forged through necessity and natural selection.

As Ace scanned the acidic aftermath, however, he couldn’t help but feel like something about this whole fight had been off.

Easy, really, when he paused to think about it.

Maybe a bit too easy.
