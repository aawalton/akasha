I tried to pull my hand away from the scythe, but my fingers were wrapped around it like a vice glued shut. The metal was cold and unnatural.

The shaft of the weapon shimmered and distorted. Black flakes peeled off it and crawled along the length toward where I held. They moved rigid and mechanical, clicking like a machine, self-assembling into some sort of crude armor around my hand and wrist like Iron Man’s suit, but far less cool and far more terrifying.

I pulled up my sleeve and discovered more of the black material leaking out from the scar on my forearm. Instinctively, I swiped my left hand along my arm, trying to wipe it away. The stuff was hard, textured like obsidian. It ignored my attempts, undeterred from its mission.

My fingers to elbow were completely encased now, and I could feel it crawling up the rest of my arm like an army of ants.

Is it gonna take over my entire body? I had a fleeting fear that this was some sort of parasite about to consume and control me, but then it stopped advancing as it hardened around my shoulder.

Beneath, my skin burned.

The armor cracked, releasing steam that smelled of iron. It morphed, forming ridges and spines like an insectoid exoskeleton. The surface shimmered, faintly breathing like it wasn’t just armor but alive. I couldn’t look away, as horrifying as the sight was. Then I was forced to as the burning transitioned to within my right eye.

Vision snapped off. Then blinked, reappeared, and held steady.

A rectangle containing text appeared in the center of my vision. It looked like the glitches I'd already seen, but corrected. Completed.

[Initializing integration…]

The words stayed there, pulsing for a few seconds.

[Integration complete.]

[Assigning affinities based on Vitrial life experiences…]

[Affinity assimilation complete.]

[Rank: Amber.]

[Affinity 1: Astral.]

[Affinity 2: Death.]

[Welcome to Penumbra.]

Pen—what? Death? Affinities?

I had no clue what any of that meant. Or why I was SUDDENLY GETTING TEXT OVERLAYS IN MY EYES. The memory of the skull-being injecting a viscous black sludge into my eye immediately came to mind. The phantom sting of the needle prickled my pupil. Did that have something to do with this?

Was that… real? Is this actually real?

As the box faded, I realized my vision had changed as well.

At the top of my field of view, a slightly curved red bar hovered. Below it, a similar blue bar. They remained stationary in my sight, following the movement of my head and eyes, like the HUD in a video game.

And then there was the monster.

It had been strange before, sure. But now it was on a different level.

An orange aura now emanated from the creature, coming from a cracked web of lines across the creature’s body that glowed like rivers of molten lava. Those hadn’t been there before. And even more odd, the image of a black skull hovered over the beast, silhouetted within an orange glow. The skull itself was partially orange as well, like a glass half full with orange soda.

I closed my right eye—the injected one. The orange rivers and skull disappeared, the beast appearing exactly as it had before I grabbed the scythe. Opened and they returned. Closed, gone. Open, back.

Well, that’s not weird at all…

As I examined the beast, an odd sensation washed over me. An otherworldly presence connecting to my own. My breathing and heart rate slowed involuntarily to match the rhythm of the throbbing orange web. Each pulse vibrated in my ribs.

Text popped into my vision again.

[Identity: Sin

Rank: Amber

Affinity: Zeal]

I gaped at the grotesque hybrid creature. This was a sin? The angel dude had talked about ending sin. Was he talking about… the monsters?

The carapace on my right arm hissed like a steam engine, distracting me, and finally stopped releasing the last faint wisps of smoke. Layers and joints clacked into place. Armor plates settling. My arm felt… stronger. The pain was gone too. Whatever transformation had occurred… it was complete.

And not a moment too soon.

The reptilian monstrosity groaned as muscled limbs lifted it to a standing position. The surrounding dandelions shook with the motion and released their red seeds into a light breeze like a bloody mist.

It towered over me like an SUV tipped onto its tailpipe. Awake now. Saliva dripped from two-inch long serrated teeth, sizzling faintly as it hit the grass. Though its mouth and snout extended out like a crocodile, the eyes and ears, even the hair, were distinctly human. Even notably feminine.

The salamander critter, which had somehow found its way to my shoulder when I wasn’t looking, evidently decided this was the moment to peace out. It hopped off and dropped, before bolting away through the flowers, squeaking in alarm.

He had the right idea… but it was too late for me.

Why the hell didn’t I tiptoe away and run while it slept when I had the chance? Oh right, because I’m an idiot.

The beast roared, spittle flying through the air in warm droplets that stank of decay. Then she barreled toward me, a clawed hand pulled back, ready to swipe once it was in range. I rolled out of the way and felt a rush of wind up my back as the sharp uppercut narrowly missed slicing me in half.

This isn’t gonna end well.

I dodged two more strikes, one of which should have ripped my cloak to shreds, but instead passed straight through it without so much as a scratch. The reptile left no time for me to wonder about how that was possible, and chomped her jaws at my head.

Instinctively, I raised my arm to block—which would’ve been a really stupid thing to do in normal circumstances. Teeth chomped down on my forearm. I braced for the biting pain of a dozen-plus knife-like teeth to sever my skin and muscle, but instead there was nothing. The carapace armor encasing my arm held like an impenetrable shield. The teeth grated against it, bits of yellowed enamel chipping off.

I laughed with relief and renewed hope. Well, that’s a mighty neat trick.

Unfortunately, that feeling was short lived. The monster still had a monstrously strong clamp on my arm. A giant fist pummeled into my stomach, knocking the wind out of me. The impact sent a burst of bile up my throat as my feet flew out from beneath me. Then she swung her head, flinging me up by the arm. And suddenly the world was whirling around me as I tumbled through the air.

I landed and rolled several feet, dirt and flower petals sticking to my sweat-slicked skin. Miraculously, I still held the scythe. The punch and tumble hurt far less than they had any right to, though the red bar atop my vision had decreased a small amount.

Health? A real-life health bar?

I rose into a ready stance and gripped my scythe with both hands. It was shockingly light, balanced like it belonged there.

“Alright. Clearly only one of us is walking away from this. And it sure as hell isn’t gonna be you.”

The alligator charged again, but this time I was ready for it. Armed with a weapon and shield-arm-thing.

Aaaaand had no freaking clue what I was doing.

I swung wildly, the scythe awkward and bulky. My strike connected, but the handle slipped in my grasp on impact and hit with the side of the blade more than the edge. The beast bowled straight through, unimpeded, and shouldered me to the ground, knocking the air from my lungs.

She loomed over me, looking far too smug. Saliva dripped from her jagged teeth and dribbled over her lips onto my shoes.

The hybrid held up a clawed hand. Not to strike, but… Something flickered and tendrils of smoke swirled in her palm like a school of minnows trapped in a globe of water. The air sizzled. Then the smoke burst into a ball of flame.

You’ve gotta be kidding me. Monsters that have magic fireballs? Really? As if things couldn’t get any worse.

The fireball hurtled toward me, but I froze in panic. Not eaten to death, not murdered in surgery by skeletal demons, not cut in half by gator claws. Burned alive. That’s how I was going to go out.

My body finally reacted. I rolled left at the last possible second.

Air whizzed past me. When I slid to my knees, I was somehow a good ten paces from the burning hole left in the grass by my foe’s blast.

I stared blankly at the gap between where I should have been barbecued alive and where I currently crouched. The blue bar atop my vision had decreased by a small chunk—maybe a fifth? So then, was that magic?

If I’d performed that roll like a normal human, I would still be within the radius of fire. Yet here I was, unscathed—and more importantly, behind my enemy.

The enraged creature glanced around her feet, as confused by my sudden disappearance as I.

I didn’t hesitate any longer though, my legs pumping as I ran toward her back. I lifted the scythe over my head and plunged the blade directly into the nape of her neck with all the strength I could muster.

Blood sprayed from the wound, hot and metallic, splattering across my face and burning in my nose. The alligator-human crumpled to the ground in a heap. I released my scythe and let it fall with the beast.

She lay motionless. Not even the subtle rise and fall of breath. The orange veins and aura around her slowly faded away. The skull image, which had remained the entire battle, disintegrated into triangular shards that blew away in the wind.

Meanwhile, my chest heaved from the adrenaline and exertion. My hands shook as I let myself sink to my knees.

I… killed it… Somehow.

The reality slowly resonated.

“I killed it.”

I stared at the scythe still sticking out of the creature’s neck and recalled the text that appeared after I first grabbed the weapon.

Affinity 2: Death.
