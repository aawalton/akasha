It was early morning as Hadrian walked down the mountain trail toward town. He was heading to work like every day, forcing himself to take slow steps. It grated on him to walk this way; he wanted to run, to leap along the winding mountain path as he usually did, but he couldn’t, not today, not with a kid trailing in his shadow.

He glanced back. The girl kept a careful distance, her eyes downcast, small darkened hands hidden inside her tunic.

Oupire.

His jaw set ever so slightly at the pointed tips of her ears, barely visible through her auburn hair. That was fine. He could handle that. What bothered him was—

She stumbled over a rock.

Hadrian turned back, his hand shooting out on instinct to catch her before she fell. She flinched at his touch, but so did he. Hadrian let her arm go quickly—perhaps too quickly—and it was obvious she noticed when she took half a step away from him.

“Watch your step,” he muttered, turning away before she could see his face. His palm still tingled where he’d touched her arm, not from her, of course. She’d done nothing to him. It was from the memory of what he’d done to her kind instead. Screams. Fire. The smell of—

No. He shook his head, forcing his breathing to steady. That was decades ago. Hadrian was a different man now, living a different life. He noticed that the girl hadn’t moved. She was staring at him with those too-large eyes, waiting for… what? Permission?

“Keep up,” he said, clearing his throat and continuing on.

She kept eyeing his uniform as it clinked softly with each step, the golden-black leather of the Cay Blessen Division unmistakable even from a distance. Usually, kids stared at it with wide eyes and pestered their parents with questions.

“Is he a real Blessen?”

“Can he fight Grulls?”

But this girl looked at it with suspicion, perhaps fear. What had she gone through? He didn’t have all the details, but apparently she had fought an Emanant in the forest. Why? This girl could be of importance if that Emanant turned out to be who he was thinking of.

Glancing at the girl, he thought about how loud hers and his daughters’ laughter had been last night, as if they were long-lost friends who hadn’t seen each other in ages. Humans and daemons acting like that… if you were to tell the Hadrian from twenty years ago, he wouldn’t have believed it.

Strange how quickly kids bond.

Stranger still, how quickly they’d trust each other. Part of him envied that. However, it troubled him to see how this child carried herself, with shoulders tight and eyes lowered but alert, like someone who didn’t expect kindness.

They were nearing the town when she pulled out a silk headscarf and wrapped it around her head, making sure to hide her ears, then put on some black gloves, maybe silk as well, nothing fancy, not that Hadrian would know.

“So,” he began, his voice low, trying not to sound too intimidating. “Do you want to tell me who you were fighting and why?”

The Oupire stared at the ground, deep in thought. Hadrian could tell her silence wasn’t stubbornness—it was concealment, the careful construction of a lie, as if she were debating whether telling him or not would endanger her or perhaps even him.

She’s a smart girl.

“You know I’m from the Blessen Division. It’s my job to deal with cases like this,” he said, trying to reassure the young girl. “If that man was doing some unsavory business, I’m the person you should inform.”

“You probably work for him,” she muttered under her breath, her voice as cutting as a whetted blade. She was an Oupire alright.

Hadrian’s brows knit. What is that supposed to mean? An accusation? Then he pondered for a second. So that man was related to the Blessen Division. That was why she’d been glaring at his uniform all this time… hmm. He needed to press her.

Pointing at his plated chest, he asked, “I suppose you’re implying he was wearing a uniform like mine?” This could be a bigger deal than it seemed at first.

She shook her head hesitantly. “He wore a tunic on top of it while I fought him, but I’ve seen him wearing it before.”

“So you’ve seen this man somewhere before?”

She nodded.

“Was he a bit short? Stocky?” Hadrian’s eyes sharpened. It was a long shot, but still worth a try…

“Yes…” She gave another hesitant nod while her gaze barely rose to him, as if expecting a rebuke.

Hadrian stopped for a moment. If this is who I think it is… Then this could be the lead Lord Venrie has been searching for. “I… see,” Hadrian said after a moment of musing.

They kept walking along the dirt path, which was slightly muddy from the overnight rain. They reached the coastal town of Aura Cay, where stalls were opening and many vendors chatted as the twin suns rose ahead on the horizon, heralding another day.

Hadrian had mapped Aura Cay years ago—every stall, every alley, every blind corner, and of course every inhabitant. He could feel the eyes of vendors and passersby shifting toward him; they waved to greet him, and some offered treats, drinks, pastries. He nodded gratefully to all, not accepting their gifts. Although it had been hard when he first started as a Division member to say no, now it was second nature.

“They act different around you, sir,” the girl said suddenly.

“They’re just being good folks. I have helped most of them once or twice,” he said with a shrug. “So that is their way of repaying, although they don’t need to at all.”

All he could think about was how to confirm the identity of the man. He had a clear suspicion of who it may be—the one they had been trying to catch all along but couldn’t because of a lack of proof. However, her testimony wouldn’t be nearly enough to get him behind bars. He needed to gather more before trying anything.

Rubbing his chin and pushing the suspicion aside for now, he focused on the far easier but still necessary duty. “It’s time to take you home. Where is it?”

“Umm… sir, I can take it from here. I don’t need to burden you anymore—”

“No, that’s not going to work. I promised my wife I’d take you straight to your parents and explain everything.”

“But…” she wavered.

“I’m not changing my mind, so lead the way,” he instructed. She looked away quickly, then clicked her tongue in annoyance. “Okay… It’s in the shard market,” she muttered.

“Oh, your family works there?”

“Yes, my father is a blacksmith and has a small shop near the end of the marketplace.”

Back in his youth, the shard market was a place Hadrian visited regularly. Throughout all citadels, and even in different countries, there had always been a shard market one way or another. There were countless different layouts, a variety of stuff people sold in those markets, many smithing styles, or crafting mechanisms, and even the shards that were dealt. The market in Aura Cay was… well, smaller than most.

“It’s over there.” She pointed at a small stall in the corner, clearly one of the worst-selling ends of the market. By the time a client arrived, they more than likely would’ve already found what they needed elsewhere. The man behind the counter had a rugged beard and slightly disheveled, long, braided hair with streaks of blond, and… he was snoring like a horse. Hadrian knocked firmly on the counter. It came out louder than he intended, but it served its purpose as the man slowly opened his eyes.

“Huh?” He grunted, disoriented, looking at Hadrian with tired eyes. He rubbed them, and then gasped loudly, his eyes widening as soon as they fixed on the girl.

“Selene!” He jolted up from his chair, nearly tripping on his own feet, trying to rush around the counter. Once he managed, he hugged her tightly, his arms trembling as he wept. “Where’d you go, my little girl?” His voice was muffled by his face pressed against her shoulder. “You had your mother and me so worried.”

Her father’s human? Interesting… Also, had I seen this man before? Maybe. Aura Cay wasn’t big. Faces crossed paths all the time. Then the faint odor of alcohol the man emitted made Hadrian frown slightly.

Not a drunk… just a man drowning in worry. Hadrian had been there before; that was the image of a father who thought they had lost a child.

“Dad, stop! I’m fine,” the girl said, trying to escape from his embrace.

“Excuse me, sir,” Hadrian said. “I’m going to assume you are the father of this girl?”

Her father immediately tensed up when he saw Hadrian in his uniform. Blessen Division members carried weight in society, but not the kind that should make a man react with fear. He put himself in front of his daughter and nodded reluctantly. “I-I’m sorry if my daughter caused trouble, please—”

“Do not worry, she did nothing wrong,” Hadrian cut him off, raising a palm placatingly. “However, we do need to talk about why she was missing.” He looked at the girl, then back at the man. “In private… that is.”

The father looked panicked for a second, but eventually nodded and gestured for his daughter to wait in the back of his shop. She twitched her fingers at this, clearly wanting to be part of the conversation.

Hadrian noticed the uneasiness in the girl’s shoulders; there had to be a reason she did all that, one she wasn’t willing to share—at least not with him. But perhaps her father might have had a clue, and Hadrian would make sure he talked.

Don’t worry, child. I promise you I will catch that man.

Hadrian crossed his arms, posture rigid, as the talk went on over the counter. The father tried to disguise his nervousness with an overly straight posture, not daring to appear too relaxed, but Hadrian could see through the masquerade. After the father introduced himself as Roger, Hadrian began to explain in detail all that had happened to the best of his knowledge.

Roger’s eyes darkened, and his color went pale as he heard of the precarious condition his daughter had been in when brought to Hadrian’s house—that if it weren’t for Anadis, she might not have made it.

“So, do you have any clues as to who that man may have been?” Hadrian asked after finishing his explanation. “And why was she there fighting him?”

The man rubbed his thumb along the side of his index finger, and sweat gathered on his forehead. He hesitated for what felt like too long—like he was hiding something.

“For the gods’ sake,” Hadrian’s voice rose, hands firm on the counter. “She is your daughter. It’s your duty to protect her. So what’s holding you back so much?”

Taking a deep breath and wiping his sweaty forehead, Roger looked Hadrian in the eyes with resolve. “About three weeks ago, my youngest son went missing…” He paused as if the words cost him. “We haven’t seen him since. As soon as it happened, we informed the authorities, and for some reason, the Blessen Division got involved.” He kept going through gritted teeth. “They told us not to worry, said they’d do everything in their power to find our boy… but that it would take time. So far, we haven’t heard anything.”

“I’m sorry about your son. I was not told of this by any of my colleagues.” Hadrian put a hand on his shoulder. “However, why would the Blessen Division get involved in anything like this? And also that does not explain why your daughter decided to risk going to Grull territory and fighting such a dangerous man.”

Once again, the man seemed reluctant to say anything. “Kids get things twisted… she probably misheard.”

“What did she mishear?” Hadrian’s voice was cautious.

Roger fell silent again.

The patience was wearing thin. Time to push. “Listen, that man saw your daughter’s face.” The words left a bitter taste. Lying to a father wasn’t something he did lightly, but he’d rather swallow the guilt than let this chance slip. “He’ll be looking for her. If you don’t tell me anything, I can’t catch the fucker.”

Roger’s breathing hitched, afraid of what was coming. His jaw worked, as if he were chewing on the words before swallowing them whole. “But if she’s wrong… I could lose my shop, my home, my—”

“Your son…”

The word landed like a stone in still water. Roger went perfectly still.

“If you don’t tell me, you’ll lose him,” Hadrian reiterated, holding his gaze. “You can trust me. I’m not like those who came to your house.”

“I know you’re not…”

Hadrian blinked. “What?”

Roger stared at him, something cracking behind his eyes. Then, unexpectedly, a faint smile tugged at his lips. “You probably don’t remember, but you saved a friend and me almost twenty years ago…”

“I did?” Hadrian pointed a finger at his own chest.

“We were on the same boat to the island. You were with your wife, mother, and little child.” Roger’s gaze softened, as if seeing it all again. “You don’t remember?”

“Wait…” Hadrian finally realized. “I remember now.” He searched Roger’s face. The years had changed him, but the eyes were the same. “Yeah, that was you. That’s why you seemed familiar. It was you, another man, and a woman, right?”

“Yes… you gave all of us a chance all that time ago. I never knew how to repay you, and even now through your son saving my daughter, you help me again…”

“There’s no reason to mention that.” Then Hadrian thought back to the girl and her heritage. “So is she…” He didn’t need to say it out loud.

“She is… but… they are no more.”

“Oh, I understand. I’m sorry.”

“Well, leaving that behind. I’ll tell you everything I know.” Roger swallowed. “After some Blessen Division members came to our house, my daughter followed them without my knowledge. She swore she heard one of them say that the child—our kid—should already be on the way to the hole. I just assumed she misunderstood what they meant.” He looked up at Hadrian, voice shaking. “There’s no way… right?”

Placing a hand on his shoulder, Hadrian said, “There’s a way. And the fact that she even fought someone out there proves she heard right. Do you know the names of any of the Blessens who visited you? Or can you at least describe them?”

His gaze hardened. “I know the name of one very well.”

“On my honor, I’ll take care of this. I promise,” he said, looking the man straight in the face. “Just tell me the name.”

“Truss Conti.”

The name prompted veins to bulge on Hadrian’s fists, skin taut around them. That fucker… this is the lead we’ve been looking for.

“Roger, for your security I’ll need you and your daughter to follow me…” He glanced around—there was no one suspicious. “You’ll need to stay somewhere secure until we can figure this out, so close shop for now.”

“I understand,” the man said, nodding at once. “Selene, come on, we’re closing shop for the day,” he shouted at her.

Later, when everything was said and done, Hadrian headed out of the shard market, followed by Roger and his daughter. He felt steadier than he expected knowing that he had found someone he had helped so long ago. It was rather difficult to explain, but it felt satisfying, as if in a way the gods were telling him he was walking the right path. And yet, what that man Conti had done to this poor family, to their kid… Hadrian would make sure he paid for it.

“What is that you’re carrying with you?” Hadrian asked as Roger fiddled with something wrapped in a leather mantle.

“Oh this,” he smiled. “It’s a gift for someone.”

A gift…

Hadrian huffed slightly. “Roger, I promise we’ll find your son.”

Be it alive or dead, we’ll find him.
