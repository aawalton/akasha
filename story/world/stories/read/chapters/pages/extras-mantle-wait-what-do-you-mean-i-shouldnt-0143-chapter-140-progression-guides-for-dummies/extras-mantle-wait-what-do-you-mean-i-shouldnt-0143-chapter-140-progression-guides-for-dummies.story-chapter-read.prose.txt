Joe’s room was significantly nicer than it had any right to be.

The man had apparently found enough downtime between whatever shady nonsense he got up to and the missions to play interior decorator. As Jin wandered in, he immediately recognized the brown leather sofa… it was the same one from the safe houses out in Vienna.

« Someone’s casually making trips. »

Yeah, well, that’s Joe for you.

He turned to get a better look at the space, finding it now boasted a worn leather armchair, the aforementioned sofa, actual carpet, a functioning lamp, and a small kitchen setup.

At least now we know from where he got the sandwiches, Jin thought, chuckling to himself as he carelessly dropped his weight into the armchair.

« True. »

It was a mistake.

"OWww!"

His ribs immediately lodged a sharp, stabbing protest, reminding him that casually dropping rocked his body and that was very painful for him. He swallowed the undignified noise that tried to claw its way up his throat, instead settling carefully against the backrest. Dr. Campbell had strictly forbidden him from using any essence, leaving him feeling as fragile as glass.

So, he did what he did best: he put on a smug face, leaned into his deflective humor, and pretended everything was perfectly fine.

« You are not fine,» Angel observed.

I know, Jin thought back. But if I look fine, fewer people ask me annoying questions.

« Reyana is already looking at you. »

Jin glanced over. Reyana was standing by the door, arms crossed like she pissed off at him. On the other side of the room, Rudy was already sprawled across the sofa. He looked offensively comfortable, radiating the cheery, oblivious energy of a golden retriever without a single care in the world.

"Grab a seat, Reyana," Jin offered, gesturing vaguely. "Hovering by the door is terrible for your posture."

She responded with a glare that could peel paint, but let out a long-suffering sigh, closed the door, and claimed a spot on the sofa next to Rudy after hitting him in the shin to sit properly.

"Start talking," Reyana demanded, her voice cold and sharp.

Jin held up his hands in a placating gesture.

"Alright, alright," Jin started, his voice coming out rougher and far more exhausted than he intended. He leaned forward, ignoring the dull throb in his sternum. "You wanted to know about the system. That notification that both of you saw."

"Oh, yeah! What is that thing?" Rudy chimed in, sitting up slightly with an eager grin. "I’ve been too scared to mess with it. Knowing my luck, I’d accidentally push the wrong glowing button and do something stupid and permanent."

Reyana’s stony expression didn't shift, but she gave a slow, deliberate nod. "Z.A.C. Branch Initiated. That’s what the text said. It doesn't look or feel anything like a normal Mantle Reflection. And frankly, given the dubious sources it came from, I’m not inclined to trust it."

"Look, Reyana, whether Trish—or whatever her actual name is—happens to be a highly suspicious person, a terrifying monster wearing a people-suit, or just a misunderstanding, is entirely beside the point," Jin said, waving his hand dismissively. "The point is Z.A.C, which stands for Zenith Ascendancy Continuum.”

"Right, let's start with the basics," Jin continued, shifting his weight to ease the throbbing in his chest. "You know how we use our Mantle Reflections to peek at ourselves and glean information about our mantles?"

"Oh, yeah," Rudy chimed in. "It's how everybody sees their stuff... well, not everyone, I guess."

"Yes, the Mantle Reflection is basically the discount, knock-off version of Z.A.C. It doesn't give you much unless you possess a dedicated information-type skill," Jin explained. "The Mantle Reflection is just a localized interpretation generated by our planet's developing consciousness. By the way, our planet's official name is Eldamar-IX. Just in case you were wondering."

Rudy blinked, his relaxed posture suddenly stiffening as he leaned forward. "Eldamar-IX? I thought it was called... Aurileaniya? After that first explorer dude? I always forget his name. Arthur something?"

"Yeah, well, the official name is Eldamar-IX," Jin said. "The universe is a hell of a lot bigger than we thought. Out there in the cosmos, there’s this thing called the Universe's Will. It’s not a god. It’s more like a cosmic mandate—a fundamental law of physics that grants all life the capacity to grow, to interact with essence, and to evolve."

Jin paused to let that sink in. "The problem is, raw cosmic mandates are impossible for mortal minds to properly process."

He gestured vaguely with his right hand. "So, a long, long time ago, some absurdly advanced civilization decided to fix that. They built a framework to translate that Will into a system users could actually comprehend and interact with without going insane. That systematic interpretation is Z.A.C."

Reyana finally uncrossed her arms. "And how, exactly, do you know all this, Jin? Even with a quest... obtaining that level of information shouldn't be possible."

Jin had been waiting for that question. He braced himself for the reaction. "I have Angel with me; he can access deeper documentation and logs from Z.A.C when conditions are met."

Reyana's eyes narrowed dangerously. "Angel? The imaginary partner you talk to?" She glanced toward the door. "Should I go get Dr. Campbell? Because you might be—"

"No!" Jin scowled. "I'm perfectly lucid. Well, mostly. Look, do you remember me telling you about picking up a cognitive-based skill? Back when we were appraising the loot?"

Reyana paused, her expression still icy, but she gave a slow, measured nod. "Yes. I remember."

"Right," Jin nodded. "Well, my skill, along with a gift Rudy and I gained from our sponsor—" Jin held up one finger before she could interrupt. "—Which is a whole other terrifying conversation that we'll come back to, just bear with me."

They both nodded, though Reyana looked like she was already regretting it.

"My skill grew and evolved," Jin explained, his voice softening, "until it gained a voice. An alter ego, essentially based on me, sitting right inside my head."

Both Rudy and Reyana stared at him, their faces a complicated mix of emotions.

Jin chuckled, shaking his head. "Look, it’s not as bad as it sounds. I’ve had him since the awakening of the quest. Honestly, the constant company is probably the only thing that kept me from having a complete mental breakdown and going totally crazy."

Reyana gave him a flat, deadpan look.

Well, Jin thought, giving her a weak, lopsided smile in return. Maybe I did go a little bit crazy. But considering the circumstances, I figure a little crazy is perfectly acceptable.

« … You are impossible. »

Hey! Don’t be like that; we got more info about things. Jin mentally shrugged.

"You're making my head hurt, bro," Rudy groaned, aggressively rubbing his temples. He still radiated the cheery, uncomplicated energy of a golden retriever, an image only slightly undermined by the crimson-white aura still merrily wreathing his knuckles. "So what does this Z.A.C. thing actually do?"

"It gives us access to the real system," Jin said. He settled deeper into the armchair, careful not to aggravate his ribs again. "We can look at our stats in detail, track any status effects, and actually communicate with our sponsors. They have their little errands and quests, and we get paid for it if we complete them."

Jin held up a hand. "It also issues quests directly. And just to clarify, unless it specifically says the quest is issued by the World's Will, it's not the kind of quest that makes everyone in our world panic."

"That's not very reassuring," Rudy muttered.

"Eventually, when the Veil over Vienna lifts, we'll be able to access the Zenith Ascendancy Shared Space." Jin waved his hand vaguely. "I'm assuming it's essentially an intergalactic auction house and trading hub. It connects Z.A.C. users across vast cosmic distances."

Reyana looked down at her hands, then back up, meeting Jin's eyes.

"When I woke up after getting your gift... I got a notification," she said softly, her violet eyes losing focus as she stared at the invisible screen in her vision. "It said I had one sponsor offer available."

Jin smiled, a sharp, knowing expression creeping onto his face. "A sponsorship offer is a massive deal, Reyana. It means some vastly powerful cosmic entity looked down, nodded to itself, and decided to invest some serious capital in you. What does it say?"

Reyana swallowed hard, reading the floating text aloud for the room.

•••

SPONSORSHIP OFFER — 1 PENDING

Nyx, the Lady of Night and Death, extends an invitation.

"Child of the mantle, you walk the edge between the living and the ended. I have watched your steps. I would watch them further, if you permit it."

ACCEPT / DECLINE

•••

"Should I accept it?" she asked, her voice tight. The prospect of binding herself to an outer entity clearly sat uncomfortably with her.

"That," Jin said, pointing a finger directly at the heavy oak door of Joe's room, "is a question you should absolutely direct at Joe. He knows a hell of a lot more about outer entities and cosmic stuff than I do. Sponsorship is a huge commitment, so take it very seriously before you forward. The only reason I accepted was that Rudy and I met ours and only survived thanks to his intervention."

Jin let out a weary chuckle, a spark of mischief lighting up his eyes. "Make sure you corner Joe just like I did. Back him into a wall and aggressively demand answers."

Reyana’s serious facade broke into a tiny, reluctant half-smile. "On second thought, I think I'll let you join me for that."

"Oh, me too," Rudy chimed in, nodding enthusiastically as his flaming fists bobbed up and down. "I love demanding answers."

Reyana gave a firm, decisive nod, carefully filing the task away. "I will."

She had absolutely no idea she was cheerfully marching into a conversation Joe would rather eat ground glass than have. Jin felt a small, tired pulse of profound satisfaction.

« That was deliberately cheeky, » Angel noted. « You know how Joe is with that kind of information.»

Completely.

"Right. Next thing on the agenda." Jin reached into his spatial ring and retrieved two slim digital tablets, handing each to Rudy and Reyana. "I had Illiana rig up a localised network of interconnected systems. It's basically a knock-off internet. These two are keyed directly to your essence signatures and logged with your details."

Rudy picked it up and started scrolling through, eventually landing on a shopping app.

“What’s this?” He muttered and tapped on the icon. Lines of text and holographic images bloomed above the screen—weapons, armour, skill cores, alchemical reagents, ordnance—and his eyes went wide, reflecting the glowing text.

"We have all this?"

"We do," Jin shrugged. "You forget how much random junk we indiscriminately looted before rolling into the bastion. Plus, I gave Joe hidden directives to loot the outpost we hit tonight and also collect the corpses of higher-ranked cultists."

Reyana nodded. "Corpses for your mantle, I presume?"

"Yeah, I have plans," Jin said, then hurriedly added, "Once I’m recovered. Then I’m going to use them to forcefully pull up the ranks of our people."

Reyana’s icy expression shifted into something thoughtful. "Like that time you pushed information directly into my head?"

Jin smiled. "Exactly like that."

"Wait, hold on," Rudy interjected, leaning forward and almost dropping his tablet. "We can push people to a higher rank? Just like that?"

"Yes," Jin said, his tone turning serious. "But it's terrible for your foundation. It's the equivalent of making a deal with an evil god from the cult instead of training; it completely severs your chances for natural progression. Not to mention the extensive list of absurd, highly specific conditions I have to meet to even pull it off."

Rudy nodded slowly. "Still. If it's a choice between a ruined foundation and being dead, it definitely increases the odds of survival."

"Anyway, browse and requisition whatever you need for the loot table," Jin said. "For now, only the core circle has access." He pointed a very deliberate, warning finger at Rudy's tablet. "One extremely important thing. One of the spatial rings in the system is designated exclusively for cultist corpses. It is clearly labelled. Do not accidentally requisition from it."

Rudy’s hand froze mid-swipe, hovering over the glowing interface. "Why would anyone accidentally—"

"People get curious," Jin interrupted seamlessly. "The label is very clear. Read the labels."

"But what happens if you—"

"A corrupted cultist head materialises directly into your lap," Jin said deadpan.

"Boys!" Reyana snapped at both of them.

"Right, moving on," Jin said breezily.

Rudy set the tablet on the cushion beside him very gently, as though it might detonate.

"Next up, marked skills," Jin continued, "I need you both sitting at five out of five slots. We have enough skill imprint cores now to be selective about it, but I don't wanna hear 'Oh I'm saving for something' nope! I want you both fully built before our next operation." He turned his gaze to Reyana. "I know for certain you were saving your slots."

"I was," she said, her tone guarded.

"Fill it. And like I said before, a cognitive augment would be a very good idea." He said it flatly, without a hint of apology. "We don't have the timeline to hold out for the optimal, perfect build. If we find something better later, it's fine. I have a removal mechanic now."

Rudy’s head snapped up from where he’d gone back to cautiously scrolling the tablet. "Removal? bro, I thought marked skills were permanent."

"Turns out the answer to most 'is this permanent' questions in this world is 'no, but the alternative is going to make you wish it was,'" Jin said and shifted carefully in the oversized leather armchair.

"Removing a marked skill means literally ripping the runic matrix straight out of your Essence Heart," Jin explained. "I’m the only one who can do it, using my mantle. But doing so completely resets your progress on that slot and triggers a period of severe physical backlash while your essence pathways try to recover from the shock. Which makes it a measure of absolute last resort, not a convenient build optimization tool."

"How severe is severe?" Reyana asked.

"Somewhere between 'you'd be a sobbing liability in combat for a week' and 'absolutely do not try this during an active siege unless you enjoy being a stationary target.'"

"Clear enough," she said, her violet eyes giving nothing away.

"Oh, right, before I forget. I made some videos," Jin said, gesturing vaguely toward both of their tablets. "Recorded them while you two were off playing hero on your mission. You can find it through an app in there."

He leaned back. "Dr. Campbell was aggressively clear about my training ban. No essence manipulation whatsoever. And since I can't use essence right now, I improvised to better use my downtime."

Reyana looked down and tapped the screen of her tablet. A neatly organized interface bloomed into view. She navigated past the Bastion's localized armory network and paused.

There, sitting on the screen, was a specific app icon featuring a stylized image of Jin giving a cheesy, overly enthusiastic thumbs-up.

The app was proudly titled: Progression Guides for Dummies.

She slowly raised her head, locking her eyes onto his and delivering a thoroughly disappointed look.

Jin just offered a shameless shrug. "Efficiency, Reyana. Efficiency is key. We would soon have hundreds of confused new recruits pouring into the upper levels, and I really didn't want to explain the exact same things over and over again to every single one of them. My throat would give out."

"Classic Jin," Rudy chuckled from the sofa.

Reyana let out a long-suffering sigh, shaking her head as she tapped the screen and clicked on the first video in the directory.

The display flickered to life. A brief security overlay popped up, indicating a tiered clearance system—showing that certain advanced videos were strictly restricted to specific individuals with higher Bastion clearance. This first video, however, was unlocked for general access.

On the screen, the recorded version of Jin stood dead-center in one of the Bastion's brightly lit halls. He was wearing a ridiculously long, stark-white doctor's coat that looked at least three sizes too big for him, swallowing his slender frame.

"Welcome," the recorded Jin announced, spreading his arms with a dramatic, overly enthusiastic flair. "This is the first introductory video for 'Sorcery for Dummies I'. Why the name, you ask? Because if you follow these steps and still blow your own eyebrows off, the title is completely accurate."

Rudy let out a loud snort, leaning over the sofa cushions to get a better look at Reyana's screen. "Jin, where the hell did you even get a lab coat down here?"

"The medical bay," the real Jin answered without missing a beat. "Maya wasn't thrilled."

On the tablet, Video-Jin began pacing back and forth like a seasoned university professor. "Today, we're going over the absolute basics of sorcery. We will cover how to safely cycle your essence, how to align your mental intent, and how to utilize basic verses."

The recorded Jin stopped pacing and held up one hand, his palm facing the camera. "By the end of this introductory course, if you've paid attention, you should be able to successfully cast the most basic, one-verse light orb sorcery."

Video-Jin paused, a smirk creeping onto his face as a small sphere of soft, white light materialized above his palm. "If you reach the end of this module and you still can't cast it... Well, I'm sorry to inform you that you don't have a single magic bone in your body, and you should probably just stick to swinging a very heavy stick."

Reyana reached out and tapped the screen. The frozen frame captured Jin's brightly lit face.

"You made tutorial videos," Reyana stated flatly. "And what’s with the persona… It’s like we have—"

“Joe all over again?” Jin smiled.

Rudy let out a booming laugh from the sofa.  Reyana, conversely, just glared at Jin with the cold intensity of a striking viper that shut both boys up.

"It's a highly scalable system," Jin defended, though the corner of his mouth twitched. "And it frees up my time to focus on the actual war. You can study the theory while you rest, and practice it when you're back on your feet."

"It's brilliant, is what it is," Rudy chuckled, already tapping his own tablet to pull up the video. "I can't wait to see what you wear for the advanced classes."

"Oh, I have some really interesting things planned," Jin said, leaning happily into the bit. "Joe had an entirely disturbing array of costumes hidden away in his inventory."

"Ugh... boys," Reyana groaned.

“Guy. On an important note,” Jin picked up his jacket from where he'd draped it over the oversized leather armchair, moving carefully. "How to merge skills, basic one-verse sorcery, a few practical combat applications. Watch them while you rest. Request anything you need from the supply. I have a meeting to get to."

He made it three steps toward the door.

"Wait!" Rudy bolted upright, and Jin heard the sofa protest the sudden movement. "I wanna know what happened between you two!"

Jin stopped.

He turned around, slowly. His face cycled through the expressions of a man running through multiple possible interpretations of what he'd just heard, finding absolutely all of them baffling. "Between whom?"

Rudy gave him a look. Then he gave Reyana a look. Then he turned back to Jin, giving him the look again.

The temperature in the room dropped by about ten degrees.

« He certainly has a cheery outlook on life. »

Reyana was looking at Rudy. The expression on her face was very quiet and very flat. Anyone who had survived knowing Reyana for more than a week understood this to be significantly more dangerous than visible, screaming anger. The death essence in her mantle hadn't actively leaked through her suppressors yet, but it was hovering right at the edge, hungrily tasting the air.

Jin turned back to her with a bright, perfectly pleasant smile.

"All yours," Jin told her. "Please try to beat some sense into him without ruining the carpet. It really ties the room together."

"Gladly," Reyana said. Her tone carried such a chilling, murderous promise that Rudy reflexively reached for Jin for help.

Jin shook his head and left the room.

Stupid Rudy.
