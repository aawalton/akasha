Fine clothes and hasty healing followed after I made it back behind the lines.  Even though we had clearly already won, there was still plenty of fighting left to do.  Knots of the enemy were fighting to the death, having been surrounded and with no way out.  Others were running, being chased down by squads of bunnies or the dragons.

Flexing my injured left hand, the green light from my magic knitted the flesh back together, but a faint scar remained.  Stumbling onto an enemy who could actually hurt me had been a shock.  Big Kenny had been a one-off in my head, but the pointed reminder that there were others out there like him was probably needed.

The zombies had been totalled, but they’d done their job, soaking fire and scaring the crap out of their former mates.  Groups of bunnyborgs were stopping to make their terrible art in between gathering up destroyed chassis and lugging them through portals for recycling.  Never let it be said that Bob doesn’t care for the environment.

“Woof!”  Heartflash issued her traditional greeting as she reined her horse to a stop next to me.  “The day is ours.”

“I can see that.”  I straightened the cuffs of my silver dyed tunic and sat down to pull on a pair of boots.

She dismounted and plonked down on the grass next to me, leaning back and staring at the sky for a moment.  Her horse bent down and began to nibble at the taller stalks.  “You’ve got eyes on you.”

“Only the usual pair.  I’ve never tried shapehifting one into the back of my head.”

“Meow.”  She made the cat noise sound like a laugh.  “I meant other people's eyes.  You’ve made enemies in every faction today.”

“Hopefully, my enemies don’t include your boss, or Armand.”

“Noooo…  my baron wouldn’t have sent me if he were against you.  Tonight is going to be difficult for you.  Don’t expect much gratitude.”

“If I did it for the thanks, I’d be broke.  They’re all going to want the portal tech, and that means new customers.  If they don’t particularly like me, that’s fine.”  The second boot was laced, so I rose to my feet and looked down at her.  “You coming?”

“It’s just the way I’m sitting.  Baaa.  Pratnip has your back.  Whatever happens, just remember that.”

We walked to the pavilion.  It had been set back up behind where the Bulb-affiliated forces had formed up, far enough back that the smell of blood and shit wasn’t too noticeable, but close enough that the aftermath of the battle was still visible.

“Why are the walls rolled up?” I asked as I took a glass from a passing waiter, threw the contents on the grass and produced a bottle of Golden Jack.  I refilled the fluted glass and took a sip.  I offered the bottle to Heartflash, who chugged her own cup, then held it out for a top-up of the amber liquid.

“The Madson wanted to make a point.”  She nodded to where the Emperor, a handful of men I didn’t recognise, and Armand were talking quietly.

The other nobles were spread out in small groups.  I’ll be the first to admit that I’m not always good at reading the room, but the tension in the air was obvious even to me.

A woman approached and gave me the mammalian equivalent of a level three glare.  It was quite impressive, but spoiled by her mousy good looks, like Jerry squaring off against Tom face-to-face.

“You have brought shame to us all, sir,” she hissed.

“Woof,” added Heartflash.

“By winning?  Who the hell are you?”

“I am Lady Jeanette von Shlurmbaggen.  I commanded the west flank.  The one you didn’t support.”

“Am I wrong, or did the middle and rear of their ranks getting bunnyborg’d not help you out as well?  And Jace was bombing the shit out of everything.  Did the presence of dragons drawing all the magefire and missiles not help you out?”

“You made a farce of the entire process.  There was no honour here.”

“Moo.  How many of your soldiers are still alive when they otherwise might have fallen without the addition of the Fidler’s Mill forces?”

“Colonel, Baron Pratnip has spoiled you.  You should know better than to speak when your betters are present.  You are a mere baronet, be silent.”

I flinched, anticipating a cat fight in the peace tent.

“Bob, do humans have different flavours, or are we all just the same to you?” Heartflash asked in a worryingly sweet voice.

“I’ve only eaten evil ones, but they did taste delicious.”

Von Shlurmbaggen paled.  “How are you the Champion of the Light?  No wonder there’s a schism in the faith.”

“I didn’t ask for it.  Bulb just took an interest.”

“Disgusting.”  She turned and stalked away, heading for the Emperor’s group.  Lozenge noticed me and waved me over.

With a sigh, I made my way to join them, Heartflash taking my left elbow like we were on a date.  Three men, all tall and dark-haired, glowered at me.  I had to assume this place was crawling with goddamn ninjas.  My shoulder blades itched at the thought of invisible guards creeping along behind me.

“Bambi, this is the one!  Settled the Skuldrak problem for us.”

The tall, dark gentleman inquisition leaned forward slightly and looked me up and down. “Abusing a dungeon to power a portal network is a novel idea.  I’ve got certain interests that could benefit from rapid transport.  Baron Bamblebunt, a pleasure.”

I eyed him suspiciously.  His head should not still be attached to his shoulders.  Vik had said it would take a week, and that was seven days ago.

“I’m more than happy to extend any legal enterprise access to the network.”

“What’s the point of power if one doesn’t use it to get around the edges of the law on occasion?”  He smiled broadly, and despite the fact that I needed his head, the man was annoyingly charming.  His tone was warm and gentle, and his bright eyes seemed to show he was genuinely paying attention.

“I’m sure the monster will have no problems with shady dealings,” von Shlurmbaggen added.  “Have you seen the sculptures his machines are making?”

“That is an odd thing, Bob,” Lazenge said.  “Is it meant to terrify any survivors should they face you again?”

“It's a bug, not a feature.”  I shrugged eloquently.  “The god of Music seems to be creeping into them, hence the cheery tunes they sing as they slaughter.  I’m not sure the origami is part of MJ, though.  Best guess, the process changes the, ah, pilots in some way, and it’s the unibunnies expressing themselves.”

“Monsters enslaving monsters to slaughter good men and women,” von Shlurmbaggen sniffed.

“Like Heartflash said, how many of your guys aren’t dead because of my monsters?”

“Undead, nightmarish creations that reek of orlic modjick, monstrous dragons.  Why not just ally with an ancient evil and pretend you’re still on the side of the Light?”

“I don’t know why you hate me, von Shlurmbaggen, but it would be a good idea to be polite if I’m such a villain.”

I wasn’t a villain, I knew it in my heart.  Even if I’d only done it for the sake of making more shinies, I’d made the lives of people in the Mill, Longbottom and Dampcut better.  The transport network had revolutionised trade, and it looked like it had done the same for warfare.

“If Bulb wants to take back his blessing, he’s more than welcome.”  I clapped my hands, a shower of golden sparks exploding as my palms met.  “You ever tried to sneak away for a quickie with the missus when you look like a sparkler?”

“Control is what matters, Bob.  You need to be cold inside.  I’m surprised you’re so emotional.”

“Bambi, I’m not that emotional.”  He blinked at my mimicking of the familiar name.

“I hear you have a portal in the Brokebone Isles?  I could find a use for that kind of thing.”

“No.”

“The trade—”

“In raccoon girls, it's over.  Besides, you wouldn’t want to fuck with Alicya.”

“She is Moon Shiver then?” Bamblebunt asked.

“Yeah… You’re pretty well informed.”

“I keep my ears open.”  Bamblebunt issued another level nine smile of charm.  I’d bet he had an ability for it.

“There will be a Conclave of Nobility to settle this civil war.  I’d like you to attend, Bob,”  Lozenge said.

“I’m a noble, aren’t I?  Why wouldn’t I attend?”

“There are conventions around someone like you having access to the very centre of power, with every leader in the Empire gathered together,” Lozenge said softly.

“Some slime thing that ate a bunch of dukes a few hundred years ago?” I asked.  I vaguely remembered the story.

“And a reigning Emperor.”  Lozenge reached out and took a glass from a tray held patiently by a waiter.  The waiter winked at me.

“Sure.  I’m not going to do that.”

“There is now one less person who might pose an actual threat to you in the world.  Courtesy of Duke Smith’s intervention.”

“You don’t sound happy about that.  This place must be crawling with invisible bastards with daggers aimed at my back,” I growled.

“Not at all!  I mean, there are a dozen Immortals discreetly watching over our little chat, but that’s always the case when I talk to anyone.”

Bamblebunt gestured to the waiter, who moved around to offer him a fresh glass.  “It poses a challenge to the social order, Bob.  We’ve had petitions from elves and dwarfs on the borders who want to join.  And some more, ah, exotic species.”

As he finished speaking, the waiter stumbled.  Glasses fell to the ground, spilling booze across the imperial party.  Bamblebunt stepped backwards, cursing everything from the waiter's parentage to his sexual prowess in one fluid stream of invective.

“Apologies, lords!” The man spluttered.  Bamblebunt stepped forward and slapped the man across the face, knocking him to the ground.  He brought a foot back to put the boot in, but before he could strike, the tray blurred out in an arc and sliced through his other leg.

Red sprayed out, and everyone was locked in place.  I tried to move, but couldn’t do more than track the waiter with my eyes as he flowed back to his feet as though bones were optional.

“You should always treat your servants with respect.”  The man stepped over Bamblebunt and peered down at him as the noble clutched at his severed limb.  Blood sprayed, changing the waiter’s white shirt to crimson.

“Bastard!”  Bamblebunt snarled.  “Immortals!  Where the fuck are the immortals?”

The waiter waved a hand, and four heavily armoured soldiers appeared in my line of sight, frozen mid-lunge.  “They won’t be of any assistance, I’m afraid.”

“I can give you whatever you want,” Bambi begged.  “Wealth, women, power?  Name your price!”

“I want a new home, somewhere a bit more interesting than the city.  Fresh air is good for you.  Now stop squirming.”  The tray flashed again, and Bamblebunt’s head rolled away, the mouth continuing to open and close soundlessly.

The assassin picked it up by the hair and took the glass from the Emperor's fingers.  He drained the booze as he ambled over to me.  Stopping, he looked me up and down and winked.

“As promised, Bamblebunt’s head.  I don’t think I like the idea of the wilds, Bob.  I’d rather be a bit closer to civilisation.  The Mill has some land to the south that would be a nice place to plant my heart.  I think you’ll need some new friends on hand as well.”

I could move again.  But it wasn’t me moving my body.  My hand came up and took the Emperor’s literal bosom buddy's head, then the waiter collapsed into a pile of offal at my feet, and everyone unfroze.

They immediately refroze, staring at me holding the dead man’s head.

“Look, I can explain, I promise.”
