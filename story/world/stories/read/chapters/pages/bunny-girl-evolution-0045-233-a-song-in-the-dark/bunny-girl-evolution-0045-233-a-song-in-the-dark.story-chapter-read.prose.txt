With the elite draugr before them, and more draugr coming crashing in from all sides, Elise knew that she couldn’t afford to mess around. Immediately, she reconnected to the System, and used her strongest {Power Word Kill} on it.

Nothing happened.

Elise was so shocked that the Skill failed that she barely noticed the other approaching draugr in time to dodge its attack. The Skill had failed to kill before, but it had never just utterly failed like this before. It wasn’t that the elite draugr’s soul was resisting the Skill. It was just completely unaffected. The Skill hit, and its soul didn’t waver in the slightest. It was like it was immune.

Shoot!

There were already dozens more draugr in the area, and Elise could sense more crashing in from all around. Charlotte had torn a few of them apart, but she was moving a little bit sluggishly. She hadn’t weathered the draugr’s scream attack as well as Elise had. They needed to leave.

“Charlotte!” Elise shouted, flying upward. “Let’s go!”

Charlotte finished decapitating one of the attacking draugr and summoned her wings to fly upward and join Elise, but before she could get airborne, the elite draugr screamed again. Elise almost fell out of the sky, and Charlotte stumbled. Elise watched as another draugr took a flying leap, landing on one of the arachne’s legs and wrapping it with its tentacles.

Elise’s eyes widened and she started to panic. She sent a {Power Word Kill} at the draugr on Charlotte’s leg, as well as the ones that were close enough to attack, and that solved the initial issue, but it was unsustainable. The Skill used too much aether, and there were far too many draugr for her to handle them all that way.

Swooping down, she grabbed Charlotte under the arms, struggling to pull her up. Charlotte recovered a moment later, and her own wings assisted the climb, but the draugr screamed again, and the both sunk. Elise barely kept them afloat, strengthening her soul defenses as she tried to drag Charlotte out of danger.

For a few seconds, it seemed to be working. The draugr were still pouring in from all around, including a few airborne ones, but Charlotte was working on her own soul defenses, and Elise was picking up speed. Then, below them, the elite draugr doubled over as its back erupted in a mass of flesh. The flesh rapidly formed itself into wings, and the next moment, it was airborne as well, chasing after them. It screamed again, and while Elise fully blocked it, the scream seemed to empower the landbound draugr, and at once, two dozen of them all leapt up, flying straight at her and Charlotte.

For a split second, Elise’s mind went blank. There were too many of them. They had more than enough stat points to leap this high, and if even just a couple of them landed, she wouldn’t be able to stay in the air. And even if she could stay airborne, she couldn’t outrun the elite. What could she do?

Then, she remembered.

“Stop!”

Her voice rang out across the ruins as her aether erupted from her body. All activity on the ground immediately stopped. The draugr in midair leaping toward her continued on their path, but when they hit Charlotte, they just stopped and fell to the ground. Elise and charlotte were buffeted, but unharmed. Even the elite draugr stopped, flapping its fleshy wings in midair as it stared at her.

Elise used the brief reprieve to take a deep breath and calm herself down. She had been down here for so long working on single target mana and aether attacks that she had nearly forgotten that was not where her true specialty lay. Between {Royal Presence} and {Royal Decree}, even a horde of hostile monsters as powerful as this one could be kept at bay.

Below her, Elise felt Charlotte regain her senses and start holding herself afloat, so she let the arachne go and instead turned to face the elite draugr. It was still hovering there, staring at her with its mismatched eyes.

So this affects it… but not {Power Word Kill}?

“Kill yourself,” she commanded.

The draugr’s still-human arm rose towards its face, then stopped, then lowered itself again. She had some degree of control over it, but not enough to kill it. And she didn’t dare risk an aether spike, in case it broke free, and then screamed to break the other draugr free. Elise was burning through enough aether as it was without adding another crisis to the mix.

She would have loved to stay and experiment more and figure out why her other aether Skills worked, but not {Power Word Kill}, but she didn’t have the time. They needed to get to safety before her aether ran out and the horde resumed their attacks. There were well over a hundred of them now, all staring up from the ground with their many eyes. It would not go well if they stuck around. Before escaping, though, she made sure to {Inspect} it, hoping to glean something useful.

[ Abyssal Draugr Singer, lvl ??? ]

Sadly, it didn’t help much. She already knew it used its voice to attack. It was good to have a name for the monster, though.

“Fly away,” she commanded the Singer.

It turned and started flying the opposite direction, and at the same time, Elise grabbed Charlotte by the arm and took off toward their shelter. When she disabled {Royal Presence} all the draugr on the ground started moving again, but they were not slow or observant enough to follow. A few airborne draugr attacked, but they were too few in number to slow them down much, and they were very vulnerable once Charlotte took out their wings.

Whether the Singer broke free and turned to pursue, Elise never knew. She and Charlotte flew at top speed back to the tunnel, and immediately locked themselves back behind Charlotte’s webs. Because they had not been at all stealthy, nor had they bothered to clear the path back, a few stray draugr wandered in and tried to attack, but they ended up killing themselves on Charlotte’s webs, and a few minutes after they returned, it was peaceful again.

“That was scary,” muttered Charlotte, twisting around to inspect her leg.

Elise looked as well and saw that thankfully, she was largely uninjured. There were a few scratches, and some of the hairs on the leg had been severed, but it was otherwise unharmed, and there was no blood. With Charlotte’s physical stats, she would be fully recovered in a few hours.

“Yeah,” said Elise. “We’ll need to be more careful from now on. And make sure we’re doing our soul defenses when we’re traveling.”

“Mhmm,” agreed Charlotte. “Why was she crying?”

“Probably because she was sad,” said Elise. “Her home got ruined and all her friends died and turned into draugr. And she got turned into an even worse one. Wouldn’t you be sad if that happened?”

“Yeah, I think so.”

“That’s why we need to make sure that they all stay dead. Because what happened to them is very sad. They all had friends once, but now they’re mindless monsters. Some of them could have been Erun’s friends.”

Charlotte nodded, then went back to inspecting her leg, feeling at the scratches in the chitin. Elise took the silence to think about the strangeness of the engagement. There was the obvious, with the Singer being immune to {Power Word Kill} somehow, but there was also the less obvious, like how she was able to command all of the draugr like that. It felt normal in the moment, but looking back, she shouldn’t have had enough aether for that.

Like everything else in these caves, the draugr had a disproportionately high amount of aether. They did not have their human souls anymore but the monstrous souls were still powerful, and should have been more than able to resist both her presence and her commands. Sure, she had gotten stronger since she last faced a group of monsters like that, but she hadn’t gotten that much stronger. Just a few levels and some soul development. There was something else going on.

Whatever it was, though, it was much less pressing than the {Power Word Kill} issue. The other at least worked in her favor, but not being able to use {Power Word Kill} on the Singer was an actual problem. She and Charlotte had spotted a few other unusual draugr from a distance, and if they were all like that, things could get dangerous. They got away this time, but what if there were two of the elites the next time? Or what if the next elite wasn’t as easy to sway as this one?

At the very least, she was pretty sure she knew the reason for this one. She pulled up the System description of the Skill, just to double check her memory, but it was as she thought.

[ Power Word Kill: Speak the True Name of the Rune of Death to a creature and impose your will upon them to activate the Rune ]

While it had a similar effect, {Power Word Kill} was fundamentally different from the aether spikes she had been practicing. The aether spike was just a raw aether attack, while {Power Word Kill} was channeled through a rune. And for some reason, the Singer was immune to that rune.

If not for the fact that the Skill worked on the other draugr, she might have thought it was something related to the fact that it was undead. It would make sense for undead to be somehow related to the Rune of Death. And she couldn’t entirely rule out the possibility, since the Singer was higher in Tier than the others, but it, at the very least, wasn’t purely due to the undead part.

She had a sudden, terrifying thought that the Singer might also have {Power Word Kill}. It was a vocal monster with some relation to the Rune of Death. Even if it hadn’t used the Skill in that engagement, it didn’t mean it didn’t have it.

If it did, she thought she could probably survive it. She knew from experience that it didn’t work well on those with powerful souls, and her own soul was more powerful than any of the draugr. If she added her own soul defenses on top of that, it made survival more certain… but she really didn’t want to test it. And she definitely didn’t want to test it out with Charlotte. Even if Charlotte’s soul defenses were enough to survive, it was far too risky.

“Hey, Charlotte,” Elise said.

“Huh?” asked Charlotte.

“We’re gonna be really careful from now on.”

The possibility of the Singer and other higher-tier draugr having {Power Word Kill} was worrying enough that she almost wanted to say they were going back to Erun’s cave to work on their soul defenses for another month before trying again. But she didn’t. As dangerous as it was, she also recognized it for what it was: an opportunity.

For a year, she had been growing, but she had not truly tested herself. During these past few days alone, just pushing her mastery of aether in a more practical, combat setting had done more for her ability than two weeks of calmer training. It was also a good eye-opener. {Power Word Kill} had always been her fallback option in case things got bad, but in a way, she had become dependent on it. By always having it in her back pocket, she was never truly in danger, no matter how bad things looked. She could always just pull it out and kill whatever she was facing.

But here, that was no longer an option. There was at least one monster that wouldn’t work on, and a possibility of more. There was real danger, and real pressure, and failure had real consequences. It was scary, but perhaps also exactly what she needed. An opportunity to grow stronger, and to potentially move further from the System as she built an alternative to falling back on {Power Word Kill} whenever things got iffy.

Over the next few days, she and Charlotte took things much more slowly, partly out of caution, and partly out of necessity. The Singer’s call had brought hundreds of draugr to the area, and while they were no longer as densely packed as they were when they swarmed, there were still far more than usual, and their usual progress took more than twice as long to make.

The whole time, Elise fervently practiced her aether spikes, and Charlotte practiced keeping her soul defenses up while moving. A few times, Elise sensed more powerful presences in the distance, and whenever she did, she immediately steered them in the opposite direction. They were making good progress, but she didn’t want to face the Singer, or any of the other elites until she was sure they were ready.

On only the first day, her aether spikes reached the point that she could instantly kill any of the ordinary draugr, so she started practicing doing it to two at once. At the same time, she also tried to study {Royal Presence}, both to see how it affected the drow, and just to see how it worked in general.

How it worked was fairly simply. It was mostly just her increasing her aether output in all directions, while simultaneously filtering it as it left. The filter part was a little bit complicated, but she could see generally how it worked. It was definitely something she could do on her own with enough practice.

How the draugr reacted remained a mystery until the fourth day, though. Every time she used the Skill, they all reacted the same way. They completely stopped moving and just stared at her. She sensed no subservience or devotion or anything like she had felt from other monsters when using the Skill. The undead didn’t seem to have emotions, generally. But they still stopped, for some reason. She had a few theories, mostly relating to them being undead and not having any willpower of their own, but those vanished when she saw what was pretty sure was the truth.

She and Charlotte were skirting around the edge of the city, practicing and killing and melting bodies, when they came to a larger, relatively intact manor. Just like many other manors in the area, this one had a room with an old, faded portrait of Queen Ber’Isel. There were four draugr waiting inside, all staring blankly at the portrait, and when Elise saw them, she realized that the way they stared at the portrait was identical to the way they stared at the portraits.

Is it because it’s a “royal” presence, or…?

As she and Charlotte melted and buried the draugr, her mind was a mess of different theories, but there was one in particular that she latched onto.

Whatever had happened in these caverns, it all revolved around Ber’Isel and Ur. Everything Erun remembered supported this, and so did all of her own dreams. Every single dream she had featured Ber’Isel, and at least mentioned Ur if it didn’t feature him as well. The familiarity Elise had felt when looking at the painting of Ber’Isel wasn’t something she could ignore, and neither was the fact that the teleportation scroll had sent her here. Ber’Isel and Ur were important to this place… and so was she.

What if… I am Ber’Isel?

She didn’t fully buy into the idea, but once she had it, she couldn’t let it go. Her dreams, the familiarity, the missing memories, the past life before the past life, the way the draugr reacted… It all fit. And while she had other theories, none of them made as much sense as this one. Even Erun, with the way he never believed her when she told him her name. Was he recognizing her as someone else?

Another week passed as she trained, and the idea sunk deeper and deeper, until she decided to tentatively believe it. She had two more dreams during that time, and while neither had been particularly important, both had once again featured Ber’Isel front and center. Ber’Isel seemed to be the main character of her dreams, which would make perfect sense if they were not random visions, but her own memories. Until she found something that proved that theory wrong, she would treat the assumption as the truth.

And if that was the truth, what was the real reason she was here? Was it just to grow stronger… or had she left something behind for herself?

Whatever the answer, she needed to get to the central city, and to do that, she needed to evolve, and strengthen her soul. Her goals hadn’t changed. If anything, they had been reinforced.

With that in mind, the next time she sensed a powerful presence approaching, she chose not to run. She and Charlotte had grown quite a bit since the first encounter, and while she wasn’t 100% confident in victory, she felt they were at least ready for a second round.

“Charlotte,” she said. “Remember what I said about the crying draugr and her Skill?”

“Power Word Kill!” said Charlotte. “Yeah!”

“Well, get your soul defenses ready. I don’t know if it’s her, but there’s an elite approaching right now, and we’re going to fight it.”
