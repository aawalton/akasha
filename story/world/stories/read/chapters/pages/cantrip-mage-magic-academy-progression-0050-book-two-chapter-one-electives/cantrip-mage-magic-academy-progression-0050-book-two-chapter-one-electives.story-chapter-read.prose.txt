1 - Electives

Eola Lemiene was getting good at magic.

Not the actual casting—the few other students who’d stayed behind during the three-week break between Varin’s Academy’s terms were miles ahead of her. Some had reached Journeymage halfway through last term, and only a handful hadn’t. Their attunements all helped them, though, while hers…

At least it wasn’t actively harming her learning anymore.

But the theory behind magic? That, Eola was very good at. She probably had a better grasp on how Ordered Magic worked than anyone else at the academy. How it was put together, what the different components of each rune array did, and how they interacted to produce a spell—all of that was Eola’s wheelhouse.

Right now, though…

“Atta, what do you think?” she asked her yellow-eyed, blue-furred familiar. The cat was, as usual, sprawled across her desk, lying on top of one of the two books she’d spent the three-week break between terms studying. That book was still in pieces; the Great Mage Varin had been fond of secret codes, dead languages, and riddles as an apprentice, and just getting his journal’s pages in the correct order was an exercise in frustration for Eola. She was close, though. Soon, she’d be able to start translating some of it. The Old Alemic, Forsooth Script, and Emerald Cursive would all be possible, but there were other languages mixed in, too. Add the ciphers on top of the languages, and she had her work cut out for her.

But that wasn’t the book she was poring over right now. On the far corner of her desk sat a letter, with five lines she’d underlined and circled.

Advanced Mana Studies - Madame ReyannaSpellsword Tactics - Maestro Yarrowbloom and Lieutenant-Librarian FringeCombat Cantrips - Professor D’LeonidePatrol Duty - Lieutenant-Librarian FringeApprenticeship - Instructor Tarik

Her preliminary courses had come in this morning, and she had concerns.

“I have concerns,” Atta said after she picked herself up, stretched out, and arched her back into a reversed ‘U’ shape. “Even if you insist on the combat track, I don’t see why you need three courses on fighting. I’d try to get out of Advanced Mana Studies, Combat Cantrips, and Spellsword Tactics.”

“Really?” Eola asked. “I was thinking Spellsword Tactics would be important, and Patrol Duty was the drop.”

“Won’t work, silly girl. All of Varin’s combat-track students get practical experience in the library or the Spine Mountains. You can ask Instructor Tarik if they’ll make an exception, but I doubt it’ll happen. I’d plan on taking two of the three classes, but choose two replacements.”

“He’ll let me drop Advanced Mana Studies,” Eola said. She flipped the page of the thin book that had arrived with her list of classes. It was only twenty pages long, with a simple binding of thicker parchment covering inexpensive paper. The ink was almost as cheap; instead of a crisp black color, it was a dark-ish gray.

Varin’s Academy Course List: Second Term.

The combat track at Varin’s Academy was a little more strict in terms of which courses a student could take. Her apprenticeship with Instructor Tarik was non-negotiable; even if she’d wanted to drop it, she was stuck taking it along with her fellow students. Every student in the entire school completed a two-and-a-half-term apprenticeship as part of learning Ordered Magic, and just because Eola wasn’t learning Ordered Magic didn’t mean she was an exception.

She thumbed through the pages, looking for a few interesting classes.

Extinct Languages - Instructor TarikGeneral ElectiveThis course delves into the ancient languages of Alemia, covering Coastal Elven, Orantic Dictation Shorthand, and Southron, as well as an introduction to Gnomic Cipher. Library Floor: Second, Third (guided)Magic Danger Level: Minimal

Advanced Ideograms - Instructor TarikGeneral ElectiveThe study of ideograms becomes increasingly complex at the higher Orders of magic. This course teaches students how to identify Second and Third-Order spells, how to safely sketch them, and how to cast them consistently and quickly. Library Floor: SecondMagic Danger Level: Moderate to High

Introductory Ensorcellment - LannithGeneral ElectiveEnsorcellment, both temporary and long-lasting, can greatly increase a mage’s effectiveness as their chosen profession. In this course, students will complete projects of their own choice from proof of concept, physical creation, and magical ensorcellment. Library Floor: ThirdMagic Danger Level: High

Combat Medicine - Instructor ClearanceCombat ElectiveWarfare is bloody and messy. A basic knowledge of healing spells is wise for any spellsword, battle mage, or siege mage looking to survive against monsters, duelists, or enemy armies. Library Floor: FirstMagic Danger Level: Minimal

Atta rolled her eyes. “You don’t actually need Combat Medicine. It’s a Combat Elective for a reason.”

“How do you know?” Eola asked. She stared at the cat, eyebrow raised. “You never explain how you know any of this. You just do.”

“I do because I’m smarter than you.”

“Rude. Assuming you’re not wrong, that still leaves three choices. I’m not sure which to—“

“Drop Ensorcellment from that list.” Atta licked her paw.

“Really? You’re sure?”

“Yes, really. Ensorcellment is unnecessary in every possible way. You shouldn’t even be trying to be a spellsword, or even in the combat track. If you were smart, you’d drop everything but your apprenticeship, pull out of the combat track, and—“

“Pick up as many classes on runes and languages as I can. I know, I know,” Eola said. “You’ve been trying to convince me to do that for almost two weeks solid, and you haven’t given me a single good reason. I know that my new mark’s something special, but I don’t see why I should abandon the combat track, especially when I’ll need to fight to rescue Mom and Dad anyway.”

Atta yawned and lay back down, covering the book. “Fine. Don’t listen to me, even though you know I can’t tell you anything. But Ensorcellment is going to be a waste of your time.”

“I’m not so sure about that,” Eola said. She circled the three classes—Extinct Languages, Advanced Ideograms, and Ensorcellment—in the cheap book, then stood up. “You’re obviously trying to hide something, and I want to find out what.”

Eola’s room was quiet.

Her whole suite was fairly quiet, honestly—especially with both Patrice and Colin gone for the three-week break. Roth kept to himself, and the only noise he made was when he played music and his songbird familiar sang with him. But Eola had picked her room because it was out of the way, small, and butted up against the dormitory’s outer wall. It was narrow, with only enough room beside the bed to squeeze by to the desk, and the wooden floorboards creaked a little if she stepped on them wrong.

But it had a window, and the loft overhead gave her plenty of storage space for her mother’s breastplate and smallsword. As she pulled her gear on and the self-fitting ensorcellment hugged her chest and ribs snugly, she relaxed. Every time it worked, it meant her parents were still alive. Every time it worked, it meant she’d be protected for another trip to the library.

Varin’s Academy, like every academy in Alemia, was centered on its dungeon. The flow of Mana gave practicing mages a much easier time refilling their souls between casting sessions, after all, and no magic school would turn down the ability to make their students learn more. But only Varin’s dungeon doubled as a part of the school.

Specifically, its library.

Eola hadn’t been to the library since she and her friends had left it after the Library Exam at the end of their first term. She’d had her hands full with Varin’s notebook—and it wasn’t wise to go to the third floor without her friends, anyway. But for a simple query like what she needed, going alone wouldn’t be a problem. In fact, there was every chance that she wouldn’t even need to leave the first floor.

Atta hopped onto her shoulders as she left her room. The cat stared at the side of her head, and she studiously ignored it until they made it out into the shockingly green school grounds. “What are you thinking?” Atta hissed in her ear.

“I’m thinking we find a basic manual on ensorcellment and dig into it as a field before I make a decision. I don’t know enough about what it can do, and until I do, I’m not making a decision like the one you want me to. Now be quiet. Someone might see you talking to me.” Eola hurried across the grounds. It was late summer, and the abbreviated fall that heralded the long winters northern Alemia faced was on the wind. Still…she’d enjoy not having to wear her cloak every time she left her room.

“You’re not as stupid as you act sometimes,” Atta said. “Fine. But while you’re there, check for any treatises on—“

She hacked and coughed in Eola’s ear, then pulled herself together. “Never mind. Do what you want.”

Eola scratched her familiar’s head as they entered the library.

This time of year, there were surprisingly few students, which only made the visiting scholars stick out more. The first floor was divided into several sections of shelves to hold the most common and least dangerous books; a half-dozen armed and armored librarians patrolled or stood near the silvered gate to the second floor, including Tagg. The old man’s spear leaned against the gate. He waved, and Eola waved back.

The catalog books sat on a shelf near the entrance. She skimmed through them, looking for any information on ensorcellment. Then, after a few minutes, she nodded. “Second floor. That’s not ideal, but not the worst. Row Eight, Shelf Four.”

Atta blinked, then shut her eyes as Eola headed past reading nooks and study desks. A few visiting scholars sat nearby, but most ignored her. The only one she caught looking was a robed woman with the symbol of Y’aer’s Cult hanging from a thick chain around her neck. She looked away as soon as Eola made eye contact, turning back to the thick, heavy tome on her lap.

“Haven’t seen you in a while, Miss Lemiene,” Tagg said as she approached the gate. “Second floor, or third?”

“Second today, Tagg. I’m just after one book.”

Tagg hadn’t gotten much balder in the last three weeks, and his beard hung down to his chest. He nodded, raised an eyebrow appraisingly, and waved at the gate. “You’re armed, and since you’re a second-year combat-track student, you have access up to the fourth-floor gate. Good luck, Miss Lemiene.”

“Anything I should know about in there?” she asked.

“No.” The old librarian scratched his head. “There’s a group of Y’aer-worshippers in there. Shouldn’t be an issue. They brought a Truemage spellsword with them. If you see them, make sure they know you’re not a threat. They were twitchy, and the spellsword’s the only one who knows his way around a fight.”

“Thanks,” Eola said. She tapped her badge against the center of the gate, and it creaked open. The long, unlit tunnel crept upward to the second floor, and when it opened, the second floor was surprisingly bright. The main aisle angled upward, dozens of Bright Balls floating in the air like a classroom full of eight-year-olds had just learned the spell.

“At least you won’t have to worry about monsters,” Atta said dryly. “These idiots are attracting every danger on the whole floor, the way they’re moving.”

Eola nodded, but kept her mouth shut and her sword and wand at the ready. Row Eight, Shelf Four was a short trip, and she covered the distance quickly but cautiously. The tall shelves blocked her vision like they were walls, and Y’aer only knew what might be behind each of them. Feral books or unweeded sections? Monsters? The Scholar’s Cult—an illegal but apparently tolerated cult, in contrast with Y’aer’s Cult? She doubted they’d be this low down, but it was hard to say for sure.

When she arrived at the tall shelf, her book was at the very top. A rolling ladder was nearby, and after some whispered discussion, she wheeled it over while Atta stood watch. Then she tucked her dad’s wand under her arm where she’d be able to grab it quickly, sheathed her smallsword, and climbed to the top shelf.

Ensorcellment Projects for Emerging Mages: One Hundred and One Easy Ensorcellment Ideas for the Budding Practitioner, Fifteenth Edition. It was a massive book; Eola wouldn’t be able to tuck it away in a pocket, and that meant either no sword or no wand. On balance, the sword could stay sheathed. Safer that way.

She clambered down and waited for Atta to hop back onto her shoulder. “Let’s get out of here.”

The cat nodded. “Now’s as good a time as any. Those idiots found something a few dozen shelves down.”

“How do you know?”

“The sounds of battle. The magic in the air. All the Mana on the second floor shifted about forty-five seconds ago.”

“Should we help?” Eola asked.

Atta cocked her head, sniffed the air, and slowly shook it. “No. Their spellsword’s casting like a madman. He’s breaking out the Second-Order spells, so whatever they’re fighting, it’s not fast. If he’s really a Truemage, nothing on the second floor’s a threat to him. But they’ll kick up more trouble if they aren’t careful, and we don’t want to be part of that. Get moving.”

When Eola returned to her suite, it was to the sound of harp music and birdsong.

She nodded at Roth’s open door. The dark-haired, darker-eyed boy scowled back, but Eola ignored it; he had a permanent scowl, but as far as she was concerned, he was one of her closest friends—as well as the only other one of Tarik’s apprentices who’d stayed over the term break. He didn’t stand from his seat in front of the harp, and she didn’t interrupt his practice.

Instead, she headed to her room and opened the gigantic tome to its table of contents.

Truthfully, Eola had no interest in the specific projects. Their detailed instructions and ingredients didn’t mean anything to her right now. She’d grabbed Ensorcellment Projects for Emerging Mages for one reason, and one reason only: to get an idea of what was possible.

Her finger worked its way down the list, past sharpening spells and spells that temporarily heated or cooled tools, self-repair ideograms, and even a variation on her breastplate’s fitting ensorcellment designed for robes and jackets.

She was halfway down the list when she found it.

“Atta, I’ve made up my mind,” she said, pointing. “I can talk Instructor Tarik into letting me drop Advanced Mana Studies with this for sure.”

“Efficiency ensorcellments, huh?” Atta asked. Then she shook her head. “Let’s see what this book says about them.”

Eola flipped to the page, licking her finger as she did. Her face fell when she got there. “That’s…a lot of steps.”

“And a lot of ingredients. And this book says the benefit is minimal.”

Eola stared at the page. There were at least a dozen different steps, maybe more, and the ingredient list went all the way from rooks’ feathers to a stomach stone from one of the Spine Mountains’ most dangerous predators. “I have no idea how we’d even get a Lurking Bloom’s gastrolith.”

“Probably at a shop in Varin’s Town,” a girl’s voice said from the door.

Eola leaped to her feet. “Patrice! When did you get back?” She only came up to the tall redhead’s chest, but even so, she gave her a quick hug.

“Colin and I just got in. The Clerk servants are bringing up our luggage, but he got called away to Instructor Tarik’s to talk over his classes for next term,” Patrice said. Eola noted the letter in her hand, sealed with Varin’s Academy’s wax seal. Patrice didn’t seem particularly curious about its contents.

“Well, I’m glad you made it. The term starts the day after tomorrow.”

“Thanks. Just enough time to recover before I start all my remedials.”

Eola shook her head. “I doubt you’ll be in too many. The combat track was over half of my schedule. It’ll be the same for you.”

“I hope so,” Patrice said doubtfully. Then she brightened up suddenly. “Listen, Colin wanted me to tell you he’ll be at The Chubby Saber as soon as he’s finished with Tarik. He said you’d be starving yourself to death, and judging by that hug, he wasn’t wrong. Also, he definitely misses you.”

“I have research to do.” Eola’s face heated up.

“You’re being stupid, Eola. Is he your first suitor?”

“Yes.”

“Thought so,” Patrice said smugly. “You’re missing an opportunity if you stay here. Also, I’ll pin your arms behind your back and march you out there myself if you say no.”

Eola sighed. “Fine. I’ll go.” She grabbed her cloak—the evenings were getting chilly already—and shut the massive open book on her desk, then carefully stacked up all three tomes in a pile. “Ready.”

“Great. Oh, one more thing. Roth, you’re coming, too!” Patrice called into the room.

A string twanged. Eola winced. And a moment later, Roth’s scowling face emerged from the door. He stared for a moment, then nodded. “Fine. I could eat. But you’re buying.”

Patrice groaned. Eola laughed.

It was good to have friends at Varin’s. The year was already starting better than her first one had.
