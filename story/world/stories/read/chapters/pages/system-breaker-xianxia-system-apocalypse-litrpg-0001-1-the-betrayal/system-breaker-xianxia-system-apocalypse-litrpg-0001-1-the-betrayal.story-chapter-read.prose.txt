"Weakness is the highest sin."

-Wei An Wei

Not nearly good enough.

Wei’s eyelid twitched as he stared at the second cut that now marked the tree in front of him—just a hair's breadth away from the first. But a hair's breadth was a hair's breadth too much.

It took quite a bit of willpower to stop himself from punching a hole through the trunk. The same two cuts lined dozens of other trees all around him.

It was technically a good day. The sun was shining, people were going about their tasks with reasonable levels of happiness, birds were singing, all that stuff that would make people say “Hello, young master Wei, such a wonderful day isn't it?”

About thirteen people did in fact say slight variations of this sentence to him while he stalked through the village situated around the entrances to the mountain, doing his best to smile and suppress his urge to strangle someone.

He had been training his spearmanship for hours—flowing through his forms over and over. Compared to a month before, he moved both much faster and with more force; his power had doubtlessly improved.

But that was the issue. His power mattered little when his skill was shakier than ever. His focus was as lacking as his temper today.

It didn't matter that he was the only child of the Drowned Sky Sect’s patriarch and matriarch. It didn't matter that he had just recently reached the Nascent Spirit stage, an incredible achievement considering he was merely 16 years old. None of that mattered. What mattered was execution—victory. And right now, despite all his efforts, he was performing about as well as his eldest cousin after eight drinks too many. Not that this was saying something, considering the man in question was seven times Wei's age and still behaved like a rowdy teenager.

The official celebration of his breakthrough—the very reason for his recent underperformance in the skill department—was just a day away. Being lauded as a genius and prodigy was nice, especially rubbing it in the faces of his brainless cousins and those obnoxious outer disciples who always made fun of his height, but he needed to settle his foundations properly and avoid embarrassing himself. He spent the days following his advancement doing nothing but training, to get a solid grasp on what this new stage offered him, but he had barely made progress.

And of all times, it was today that his father gave him this meaningless task. For whatever reason, instead of focusing on his training, he was to visit this village near the peak of the mountain that housed the Drowned Sky Sect and settle some meaningless conflict between lesser members of the sect. Just thinking about it made Wei grind his teeth. This was both beneath him and a waste of his precious time.

Not that he was avoiding it. He wouldn't dare to disobey his patriarch. He could settle whatever matter had caused his father to personally send him here and return home in a matter of minutes. But first, he would devote some time to something much more meaningful.

The future was coming, always coming. If he was ever going to be the patriarch of this sect when his parents ascended, then imperfection was unacceptable. He lifted his spear once again—

"Come on, Wei. It's enough for a day," a voice suddenly sounded behind him. Wei closed his eyes and breathed in deeply.

He didn't have to look to know the man who had just addressed him had a long white beard and skin as rough as the bark of the trees that surrounded them. He held a gnarled quarterstaff marked with countless nicks, yet pulsing with a subtle spiritual power. Wei greeted the man with a slight nod but then turned back to the tree.

"I think I'll stay a little while longer, Master Gang Mu," Wei murmured as he readied himself once again. He had no interest in humouring the old fool. Gang Mu was many things, but certainly not one of his favorite teachers.

He focused intently on the two cuts he had left, determined not to stop until his execution was absolutely perfect.

Gang Mu regarded him for a moment, a knowing look in his eyes. Then, he bowed his head. Whether it was a gesture of acceptance or resignation, Wei couldn't be sure. But the elder acquiesced and turned to walk away. It didn't matter. Wei was going to be perfect, whether he had help or not. Living as a fool beneath the heel of the heavens was not something he had an interest in.

Fast breakthroughs were meaningless if his foundations were shaky, if his focus was lacking. What kind of immortal would he become in that case? How could he ever meet the eyes of his mother and father?

Wei resolved to spend at least a few more hours honing his skill. He was sure his father would understand. And if not, he'd take the chewing-out with his back straight.

Unfortunately, what he wanted, what he intended, and what the heavens willed were rarely ever in alignment. For at that moment, as Wei readied himself to deliver another strike, a cataclysmic detonation slashed through the woods he was training in and flung him off his feet.

The young master cried out. The foliage above combusted instantly, showering him in a rain of ash. The wave of heat seared his skin and shrapnel scored shallow cuts across his flesh. Instead of his spear slicing through the tree, his skull impacted right where he left the second mark earlier, punching clean through the trunk and causing it to topple over.

When he finally came to a stop, his vision was spinning, his head ached, and the sky above was laid bare. Gone was the overgrowth. Now, he witnessed a scar expanding across the sky. A line of pulsing red was burrowing through the brilliant arrays that surrounded the atmosphere of his realm—arrays bound to the enormous branches of the Everblossom trees that protected his world.

And from the scar came small shapes. The first few Wei could barely make out, but then came a roar. A thunderous bellow that belonged to a thing of nightmares. A creature pushed its way out from the fiery portal thereafter, its twisted snake-like body looming as large as a river. As it descended, Wei watched as mystical techniques rose into the air and impacted its body, the cultivators of the sect wasting no time to greet the invaders.

Demons. They were being invaded by demons. Demons… They were right at his doorstep, near his home.

Shaking off nausea and pain, Wei jolted to his feet, tripped, fell, and scrambled up again. After grabbing his spear, he broke into a disoriented sprint, rushing through the falling ash. When he finally reached the village, he almost froze in place.

The world was burning, and young master Wei An Wei could do nothing but watch.

The skies tore apart like paper, opening up into more and more vast rifts of glowing red. The portals dispersed the clouds, and the seemingly endless hordes of demonic invaders they released into the world blocked out the light of the sun. Horned creatures clad in burning armor and wielding swords of brimstone rained fire upon the earth, cracking the defensive arrays that protected the sect.

High above, beyond the clouds and even the ruptures in existence, Wei glimpsed at a gargantuan, indistinct shape that was drawing closer to the planet every second.

Neither the junior disciples nor the elders of the sect had been prepared for this scenario—it should have been utterly impossible. The protections woven around their world, Evernest, should have exorcized any demons that dared enter its atmosphere, thanks to the vast and sprawling branches of the Everblossoms that surrounded the planet. Especially here, at the mountain fortress of the Drowned Sky Sect, where the spiritually awakened trees first sprouted from and were at their most powerful, no infernal creature should have been able to set foot. But here and now, the legions of hell had emerged, at the core of their greatest defense.

Cultivators all around the village fought alone or in formations, directing animated weapons to obliterate swarms of screaming insects and spearing through demonic leviathans trying to squeeze through the ever-expanding rifts. Even unprepared, a cultivator remained a cultivator; they were already set upon defying the laws of heaven. They held no fear for the forces of hell.

But where virtue shone brightly, the reality was that they were like a sinking island trying to fight off an encroaching sea. Wei watched as cultivators fought, desperately holding their pockets of resistance until the swarms inevitably swept over and devoured them. Some, however, used their final moments to deliberately invoke tribulation, reaching beyond the expanse of their physical reality—nakedly trying to steal power from the heavens one last time.

In response, primordial lightning descended from the sky, carving swaths of reality away into black-and-white nothingness and taking the cultivators that dared to transgress beyond their station along with countless demons with them.

The demons simply kept coming.

Wei sprinted back toward his home at the heart of the mountain, pushing past fleeing disciples and families. Shortly before reaching the decorated entrance to a hallway, he saw Master Gang Mu being thrown to the ground by a humanoid demon wielding a burning sword. As the creature raised its weapon to deliver the killing blow, Wei flung his spear through its head, causing it to explode in a shower of sizzling gore. He didn't have the time to stop and help any more than that, reaching his parents took precedence over all else. He jumped over another demon blocking his path, tearing the creature's head off with his bare hands and landing back on the ground in one smooth motion.

He finally passed through the cave entrance into the depths of the mountain, watching as Essence-forged runic symbols covering every surface throughout the tunnel went out one after another, showing the last layers of protections crumbling away under the demons' assault.

The infernal creatures hailing from a different realm were a known threat to the people of Evernest, sometimes managing to enter through rifts in places where the banishing power of the Everblossoms was weakest, but a large-scale invasion such as this was entirely unprecedented. He needed to get home, to his parents. They would know how to strike back against these invaders.

Soon, Wei reached the incredibly vast cave at the heart of the mountain. At its center stood his family's towering manor, stretching all the way to the ceiling of the cave. Surrounding it were the smaller estates of the Elders of the sect, as well as a spiraling town formed by the homes of the Hundred-Names—a traditional group of Pathless mortals that had served Wei's family faithfully for centuries.

But even here, Wei could see indescribable shapes hunting the people of his sect, laughing creatures made of shadows and ash pulling helpless people off into the dark. The demons had found their way into the heart of the sect through its various tunnels. Between the streets, the smaller spiritually awakened Everblossom trees growing under the surface of the mountain were burning—screaming. Their radiant petals of violet and red were consumed by fire and turned to ashes, a result of their larger siblings above ground being destroyed by the demons. The artificial waterways passing between the houses to supply the trees were still flowing, but now red with blood.

Bile rose within Wei's throat as he witnessed the atrocities, but he faced the world as his parents had taught him; he accepted the horror as a cultivator should, and pressed on.

In his urgency, he became a blur of violence, smashing through walls and demons alike and leaping over collapsed bridges. Only one goal ruled his mind: he needed to reach the mansion. Staying to help these people was a doomed prospect.

The alchemical lights at the ceiling of the cave were beginning to wink out, the Essence that supplied them fading along with the burning trees.

With each passing second, his heart, the muscle containing his Nascent Spirit, ached worse than before. He was drenched in sweat, his long black hair sticking messily to his face. He hadn't had enough time to properly set his foundations yet, his Essence was unstable. He would need to meditate and center himself when he reached the mansion. He passed the final threshold of houses, leaving fleeing mortals and shattered demons in his wake. His heart swelled with relief as he laid eyes upon his home.

The joy in him faded, however, as a few houses away, along the pearl-inlaid pathway leading to his home, he saw massive abominations tearing into an old man. Wei barely recognized him as the elder tried to fight back against the many-legged hounds ripping him apart.

It was his beard that Wei recognized. Flowing and white, with a fashionable curl at the end. "Master Mou Ze!"

Before Wei had a chance to act, Essence rose high from Mou Ze's body, reaching through the mountain, past the arrays, past the titanic branches of the Everblossoms winding through the sky, past even the demonic rifts looming above them.

A bolt of unfathomable monochrome energy lanced down from a place unseen. Wei sensed it before it even arrived. It smashed into his senses like an impossible weight and pierced clean through the mountain as if the ancient stone wasn’t even there. Lightning the colors of deepest black and pure radiant light struck Mou Ze, consuming him and the hellhounds in a flash of brilliance.

A shockwave of force swept out across the cave and sent Wei crashing into and through the wall of a nearby house. The young master somersaulted out of the hole and hurried back to where he had stood just a moment ago. Gone were the hounds. Gone was Master Mou Ze. Only a crater remained.

Wei stared at the hollow depression dotting the ground for a heartbeat and repressed the tearing inside of him.

Grief was a weakness he had neither the time nor the want to indulge.

Springrise Mansion, home of Wei's family, was named so because of its composition. Created by Wei Jing Quan, Matriarch of the Drowned Sky Sect and Wei's mother some seven centuries ago, it was a living building formed by the original and oldest Everblossom trees in the world having been molded together, shaping walls and rooms with their trunks while the branches of the ancient trees climbed upward, spearing through even the stone of their mountain stronghold itself. Upon the wood burned defensive scriptures infused with Essence that invoked commands directly upon one’s will.

A creeping question gnawed at Wei, then. Why hadn’t his parents reacted if their home, the core of the banishing arrays, was still intact? He could still sense the Essence rippling from the ancient Everblossoms, flowing defiantly across the root network connecting them to the rest of the world. Why didn't they shrink the perimeter’s defenses before expanding them again to forcefully banish the scourge?

The moment he plunged into the inner courtyard, he knew something was wrong. The mansion was silent and empty. Wei had never seen it this way in his entire life. He considered calling out but thought better of it. He didn’t know what threats lurked ahead, despite how intact everything seemed. Taking a moment to center himself, he planted a hand against a branch of wood and exhaled.

All cultivators knew how to meditate. It was how one centered themselves in the turbulent sea that was the world, drawing in the currents of discord before refining them, nourishing their spirits by purifying chaos into order. Regardless of his youth, Wei’s highest blessing by birthright, furthered by extensive training, was his unshakeable focus.

His mind emptied with a thought, and he found himself hovering in a miasma of chaos. Streams of flowing Essence washed through the Everblossoms around him, and their voluminous presence, Essence shaped by his parents, made the gray haze that colored his spirit seem little more than a contaminated swill.

His masters often told him that he was gifted—a generational talent. Wei didn’t know how that could be true when his mother and father could produce such purity through their cultivation. Though his spirit thirsted, he controlled himself and internalized the Essence into his Nascent Spirit.

Moments of calm presented instances of greatest danger to a cultivator, for they were refiners of existence. Many disciples had reached beyond the expanse of this realm before they were ready, attempting to steal spiritual Essence from the world beyond—a world where the Ascended and other higher beings lurked.

Time and time again, Wei felt lured to claim more than he should. Time and time again, he resisted it.

Where most disciples his age were two stages behind, struggling to set their first foundations, Wei had awakened his Nascent Spirit just days before the invasion—and taken his first true step on the climb to ascension.

His mother had even granted him one of her rare smiles. Never mind tomorrow's celebration, right now, he would settle for getting out of this mess alive.

Drawing Essence from the Everblossom, he ignored the feeling of guilt as the complexities of his parents’ spirits dissolved upon greeting him, colors vanishing into a dull blue. Filled, he exhaled and returned to the world.

The mansion was still silent, still empty. A string was pulling up at his guts, and a drum of dread hammered in his chest.

He strode forth through the hallway. The wings to his left and right had their doors closed. Wei frowned as he wondered where the animated constructs meant to guard the place were, but he pressed on, seeking the place where he was most likely to encounter his parents.

The door leading to the innermost part of the mansion, a small enclosed garden where his parents spent most of their time, was halfway ajar, its brass, ring-shaped handle twisted slightly as if released mid-pull. Gathering his courage, Wei pushed the door open and stepped forth.

He stopped dead after a single step.

Before him, on the ground, lay a severed head. Jade-colored eyes, the same shade as his, staring sightlessly ahead. Mouth slightly ajar, as if interrupted mid-speech. Hair still tied in neat tassels, with a crystalline hairpin holding it in place—something his father had gifted her during their courtship.

Seconds passed, and Wei stared on, mind unable to process the scene before him. Somewhere deep inside, he knew he was staring at his mother’s decapitated head, and a trail of blood leading but a few steps away where her body lay unmoving. Behind it, facing away from Wei, stood his father, one of the towering man’s palms pressed against the ancient trunk of a sprawling tree, channeling the will of his spirit through the Everblossoms.

Willing them to lower their arrays.

In his other hand shimmered his Shapeless Blade, only made visible by the blood coating its length.

Blood.

His mother’s blood.

Reality slammed down on Wei like a hammer greeting an anvil, breaking him from his stupor. He went down to his knees, taking his mother’s face into his shaking hands. Absurd as it was, he reached into her using his spirit, yearning to find something there.

A shade of color.

A semblance of life.

But there was nothing.

Just emptiness.

Just death.

A noise escaped Wei before he could stop it. There was no coherence to the note, no meaning besides pain. His mother would never watch him train again, instruct him, smile at him, chide him for bullying the outer court disciples, or slap his hand for forgetting decorum when they ate together.

His mother was gone.

And father killed her.

Something inside Wei shattered. What was a cry of grief broke into a note of incomprehensible rage. "Why?" he said, barely whispering the word. "Why?"

He watched his father reel back from the tree, spinning to bring his blade to bear. The man froze as he faced Wei, his face an ashen mask of misery, eyes red from weeping.

For a passing heartbeat, they stood there, father pointing his blade at his son, son holding the remains of his mother in his arms.

People often said he resembled his father more than his mother. Aside from inheriting the hue of her eyes, father and son shared most other features—strong jaw, pronounced cheekbones, and wild, flowing black hair. Somehow, that made this all hurt so much worse.

"Why?" Wei managed again, a growl seeping into his voice this time.

The man before him sagged with exhaustion as he… looked upward and away from Wei? "I did all that you asked… Not him too! You said you would—I did everything you fucking told me to! This wasn't part of our deal—" His father suddenly flinched as if slapped, and bowed his head.

Wei stood frozen. His father—a legendary cultivator defined by calmness and focus—was gnashing his teeth and cursing at the heavens, speaking like a commoner who'd received faulty wares.

His father crumpled in on himself and clenched his eyes shut. "You said I wouldn’t need to make this choice. You said he would never lay eyes on me again."

It took Wei a second to realize his father was referring to him. "What? What are you talking about? What did you—""There was no choice," Yu Wu Wen repeated, all expression vanishing from his face, focus reasserting itself. "This was an outcome demanded by a will beyond our own. I'm sorry, son. I did not want this. But it doesn’t matter. Nothing I want matters."Wei’s confusion only grew with each passing word. He didn’t even realize that he had placed his mother’s head down on the ground as he staggered closer to his father. His head was spinning, red creeping in on the corners of his vision. His fists clenched and unclenched, tightening in pace to the thundering in his heart.

"I will not make you suffer," Yu Wu Wen said, stifling a quiver. "I will not. That is all I can give you. I didn’t—"But Wei was on him first, seeking to take his father's life to avenge his mother; a Nascent Spirited boy facing a master on the verge of true Ascendance—the eighth and final step before one became a true immortal.

Wei blurred, faster than any ordinary human's eyes could perceive, channeling every bit of his Essence into his speed. He strode and pivoted right, seeking to deceive his father. But Yu Wu Wen was no outer disciple fool; skill was the least of his many advantages over Wei.

The boy might as well have been standing still when his father drove his Shapeless Blade through his heart.

Wei lurched. His mouth filled with a wet coppery tang, and his heart—the muscle containing his Nascent Spirit—shrieked as it struggled to beat. His father’s face contorted in repressed horror as he banished his blade, stumbling back as he took in his deed. Blood welled out from Wei’s chest, painting the whites of his robes a deep red and pouring down his body. He coughed a spray of crimson phlegm, mind dangling at the precipice of madness, spirit punctured and leaking Essence.

"Oh, god… I’m sorry! I'm sorry..." Yu Wu Wen muttered over and over, offering his worthless apologies to the son he had just mortally wounded.

In that moment, Wei learned what it truly meant to hate someone. He didn’t know if it was his burning rage or the last bits of his ruptured spirit that gave him the strength to keep standing, to take another step toward his father. All he knew was that he needed to kill him.

And on the verge of insanity, a plan formed in his mind. A desperate plan that would see their family reunited in death.

Wei cast a glance at his mother’s head one final time.

Forgive me, Mother, for what I am about to do to your Everblossoms. What I will do to our world.

It was a mad plan, but with his mind drifting, one final acknowledgment of his filial piety seemed appropriate to him.

Clenching a fist, he lurched forward, ostensibly seeking to land a blow on his father. The man just sighed as he dodged, going to his knees and reaching out as Wei crumbled with a choked gasp, embracing his son one final time. "It will be over soon. It will be calm soon. Go. Go be with your mother. She should not depart alone."

Wei vomited blood over his father’s back and gripped the back of the man’s neck with his left hand. Pushing feebly, he forced his father to meet his gaze, hatred matching sorrow as he spoke: "I will kill you for this… No matter how many…. I will come back… No matter how far you flee… No matter how high you ascend… I will find you… I will make sure…"

With each word of loathing he spat, his father's expression wilted further and further, until he was on the brink of tears once more. As the man was distracted by his own torment, Wei reached with his other hand and grabbed the nearest branch of the Everblossom just behind his father.

For one last time, Wei mastered himself—against all odds, he silenced his mind.

No more did his mother’s death matter.

No more did his impending end concern him.

All that was in existence was his wounded spirit spilling free into the Everblossom, and the siren call of tribulation beyond.

This time, unlike all the times before, Wei didn’t resist. Instead, he reached, groping deeper as he ignored all there was left to refine himself in this realm, and sank his will into the heavens beyond.

Faintly, he heard a rising cry of alarm someplace distant. But that mattered no more. Deeper still he rose, deeper still he drank, deeper, until he felt a presence reach back and seize him.

And then it struck.

He opened his eyes and grinned widely as he beheld his father’s disbelieving gaze. He clenched the back of the man’s neck tighter. "A Patriarch," Wei spat, "should not abandon his family. Wei on the other hand found himself being devoured in totality. His spiritual Essence crumbled under the shattering force of tribulation, while his body was being eroded away, though, strangely, much slower than the instantaneous annihilation experienced by all other cultivators he had seen claimed by the same fate.

Adrift in this sea of chaos, Wei strained and struggled, trying to resist the end. His father merely gazed at him, eyes burning with despair. He reached out for Wei, but then pulled his hand back, mouthing something, perhaps an apology, perhaps a last request.

It didn’t matter. It was worthless. Wei didn’t want to hear him speak, he wanted to watch the man die. Why had the heavens protected him at this very moment? Why were they denying Wei even this? His final vengeance.

Just then, the ocean of light and dark parted behind Yu Wu Wen, and Wei was momentarily blinded by a flare of the sun that emerged behind his father. Uncomprehending, Wei tried to orient himself. Whatever effect had preserved him from death until now, perhaps a side effect of whatever the demons had done to drag his father to this place, was coming undone.

No.

No!
