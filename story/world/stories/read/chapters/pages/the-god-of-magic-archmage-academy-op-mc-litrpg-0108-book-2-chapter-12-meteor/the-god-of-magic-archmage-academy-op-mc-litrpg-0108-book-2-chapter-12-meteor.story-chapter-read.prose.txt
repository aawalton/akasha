There were five of them.

According to Tutor, three of them were high-noble class, in the upper five hundred levels.

Two of them were high-lord class, which would put them in the upper six hundred levels. But even among the two strongest, one of them stood out. Sen’s mana senses and instincts were in agreement. The aura of an exceptional mage was approaching. It was the aura of someone with the potential to reach the highest mage ranks.

They were close now.

Sen and Drussan positioned themselves on a rooftop a short distance away from the gateway. Sen shifted his spells so that the mist and rain avoided the area directly around them. The remnants of the escaping dreadbeasts still filed through the portal leading outside of Vitree. As it was, Sen could maintain the gateway for a while still. But allowing mages as strong as this to get too close was dangerous. If the gateway was tampered with or destroyed, Sen wasn’t fully sure he could restore it. The act had exhausted Xochi, and without the mana axolotl anchoring the far end of the gateway, it would be nearly impossible. Despite his breakthrough in creating it the first time, he still had work to do before mastering this spell completely.

Their only choice was to fight. To delay their pursuers long enough for the rest of the dreadbeasts to escape town.

Drussan stood several feet to Sen’s left. He was quiet.

“Are you alright, Drussan?” Sen asked. “If you don’t want to fight, you don’t have to.”

Drussan had few qualms about the death of slavers. But he still carried reservations about harming others. He shook his head. “I must fight. It’s true I’ve developed both a talent and preference for solving problems with my words over the years. But I have always kept myself prepared for the inevitable day when words weren’t enough.” He turned and stared Sen in the eyes. “I’m going to fight to protect my people.”

Sen nodded. “As will I.” He turned and stared ahead.

Their guests had arrived.

Their leader, the strongest of them, was the first to appear. He appeared in an instant on the far end of the very rooftop on which they stood. He was tall and scanned them each for a moment in silence.

“I’m Solomon, leader of Meteor Haven,” he said simply. He wore a black tunic and his feet were bare. Plumes of smoke rose off them as if he had walked through fire to get here.

Drussan gestured towards Sen. “This is Gosen, a mercenary I’ve hired as well as an honored friend. I am Drussan, a dreadbeast Khan. Why are you pursuing us?”

Solomon raised a brow. “A Khan introducing a mercenary before himself? You must think highly of him.” He turned and scanned the rooftops around them for a moment. “A dreadbeasts committed murder within the walls of Vitree. Someone important to my client. I’ve captured the dreadbeast in question, but I believe he had help considering his…general cowardice. I’m aware there are more of you. Until I’ve completed my investigation, I can’t allow you to leave.”

Sen’s eye twitched. Did this Solomon presume to tell Sen what he was allowed to do? And what was this about a dreadbeast committing murder? It didn’t sound like they’d found the bodies of the slavers. That was more of a massacre than a murder. No, Solomon was talking as if a single murder had taken place. What was going on?

Before he could respond, three of Solomon’s companions arrived. The first was a large man with broad shoulders. He had a thick mane of red hair and carried an enormous battle axe in one hand. He was clearly the second lord-class mage based on the mana radiating from him. On the opposite side of Solomon stood two women. One woman had long, silver hair that fell down her back. She wore all-black armor and had a silver sword sheathed at her waist. The other woman had a thin face and a bluish-gray braid that hung all the way to the back of her knees. She looked only a few years older than Sen.

Solomon motioned to the red-haired man with the axe. “Allow me to introduce you to Bartholomew.” He turned and motioned to the silver-haired woman with the sword next. “This is Agatha, and next to her is—”

“Theodora.” The woman with the long braid answered in his place. “What’s with the introductions? We here to fight or dance?”

Bartholomew cackled. “Aye, Thea, we’re of one mind.” His eyes focused on Drussan. “The beasty is mine. The other two were a disappointment. This one seems stronger.”

Drussan’s face hardened. “What other two?” he demanded.

Sen raised a brow as well. There shouldn’t be any other dreadbeasts in the city. Had something happened they’d missed? Or was this some odd tactical deception before a fight?

Theodora smirked. “You should keep better track of your friends, dreadbeast.”

Drussan glared at her. “I’ll say the same of your limbs in a moment.”

The smirk slowly faded from her face, replaced by one of anger.

Solomon held up a hand. “The dreadbeast responsible for the murder claimed his name was Mi. The other was named…Slari, I believe.”

Drussan didn’t appear familiar with the former, but at the mention of the latter, his eyes widened. “Rearguard Slari? Where is she? Where are they?” he demanded.

“Surrender, and I’ll take you to them,” Solomon offered.

Solomon looked past them as a new man arrived on the rooftop behind Sen and Drussan. Sen glanced over his shoulder. The newcomer had shaggy brown hair that resembled the mane of a wolf, and an eyepatch covered his left eye. “I’ve confirmed the existence of a portal. A large number of dreadbeasts are gathered there, using it to escape. Should I put a stop to it, boss?” he asked.

Drussan exchanged a look with Sen. Sen held a hand up to urge him to wait. The portal was fine for now, and his senses told him the dreadbeasts were still moving through it uninhibited. Besides, all five members of Meteor Haven were now gathered here.

Solomon closed his eyes for a moment, and Sen felt a wave of mana rush past him as the man scattered his mana senses in the direction his subordinate had indicated. When he was done, he opened his eyes and turned to stare east. A small, blazing bright circle appeared in front of his right eye, and he stared into the dark horizon. Whatever it was he saw satisfied him. “No, that won’t be necessary. They are still well within our reach. We’ll catch up to them after finishing up here.”

Drussan gripped a fist. A soft laugh escaped Sen.

Solomon raised a brow at that.

“I don’t know why cult hunters are working for a man like Thessus. But I do know you’re on the wrong side of this. These dreadbeasts are fighting for their freedom. How can you stand against that?” Sen demanded.

Solomon frowned. “Whether that’s true or not, one of them killed Lady Orianne’s fiancé. Until we get to the truth of it all, I won’t let even a single one escape. There are forces at play beyond your understanding, mercenary. Besides, I could ask the same of you. Why are you fighting to help the kin of murderers?” he asked.

“I’m on the right side of history,” Sen spat. He reached for his sword and gripped its hilt. “Before you make this mistake, let me be clear about something. If you try to stop them, if you get in our way, I am going to take something from you today. It may be the lives of your friends, it may be something else entirely. But crossing me will have a price. Ensure you’re prepared to pay it.” Sen stared him down as he spoke.

The subtle amusement faded from Solomon’s face. “Go,” he ordered, and his guild members responded accordingly.

Bartholomew was the first to move, and he was fast.

He exploded forward toward Drussan in the blink of an eye. “You’re coming with me, beasty!” The red-haired man barreled into the large dreadbeast and sent him flying. Drussan tumbled backward, skipping over the roofs of buildings like a rock on water, kicking up dust and debris as he went. When he slid to a stop, he stood up and roared defiantly. Bart charged after him.

“Drussan—” Sen called out. But his senses warned him that he was under attack as well. Theodora and Simeon struck in unison.

“Meteor Aspect - Speed!” Theodora said as she shot towards Sen.

“Meteor Aspect - Impact!” Simeon yelled from behind him. “Beast Claws!”

Sen responded with a spell of his own. “Sword Twin Maxim.”

The spectral swordsman shot out of Sen’s back to block Simeon’s attack from behind with a matching katana. Sen caught Theodora’s punch in the palm of his hand. She threw another.

The battle began in earnest.

The spectral sword twin’s katana clanged against the claws Simeon had grown on the end of his fingers with the second of the two spells he cast. Sen still wasn’t sure what the effects of the meteor aspect spells were, but he would learn soon enough. He glimpsed a glowing symbol in Simeon’s left hand, but whatever it was, the man couldn’t get close enough to use it. The sword twin was keeping him at bay.

Theodora, on the other hand, was ramping up her own attacks. It seemed each new strike came faster than the last. Sen alternated between blocking them with his palms and swatting away her wrists to deflect the attack. For a while he was able to match her speed, but eventually he found himself forced to use Encapsulation.

It was as good a time as any to try out the enhanced version of it on an opponent. His own speed increased rapidly. At the same time, Encapsulation found Theodora within range and struck. The same wind that sped up Sen’s movements slowed her down. It made deflecting her attacks more manageable. For a time. Just as before, with each punch her strikes came faster, until eventually she’d regained her lost speed.

Sen’s brow furrowed. Her strikes were outpacing his defenses and finding his barriers more and more often. He reached for his katana and drew it. In a single motion, he swung it outward toward her. Theodora jumped back out of range and slid several feet from the momentum of her movement. She rapped her knuckles against her thigh the entire time. Knocking on them as if knocking on a door. Her knocks moved just as rapidly as the punches she’d been throwing.

Was it some odd habit, or did it have something to do with her spell?

He looked up and saw Solomon staring at him with crossed arms. He was all alone.

Sen quirked a brow. Where was Agatha?

His eyes widened, and he turned to his right just in time. She fell out of her leap with a sword held high above her head. “Meteor Aspect - Ablation!” she cried out like a deadly warrior. He raised his katana to block her strike. Theodora shot forward to attack in unison.

Agatha’s blade crashed into his. The moment it met Malleore, the sword twin spell dissipated. He pushed her back, sent a pulse of mana toward Theodora, and whirled around to block Simeon. Simeon landed a punch on Sen’s chest just as he turned around.

The glowing circle in Simeon’s left hand transferred to Sen’s chest.

Simeon smiled, revealing extremely sharpened canine teeth, and jumped back. At the same time, Theodora retreated as well and rapped her thighs again. It left Sen facing Agatha on his own. Swordsman versus swordswoman.

But why had Simeon and Theodora relented?

Simeon moved to Theodora’s side. Then she punched him.

Sen blinked. “What’s going—”

His barrier underneath the glowing mark pulsed as if something had struck it. Sen looked down in disbelief. But Agatha shot forward. He lifted Malleore, and the two exchanged blows.

The clash and clang of their blades filled an otherwise quiet night.

All the while, Theodora kept pummeling Simeon. Yet somehow, each strike seemed to land on Sen’s barrier. Theodora’s braid swung wildly as she punched. She was drenched in sweat, but she didn’t stop. Her movements continued like an unrelenting whirlwind, blurred by speed. His barrier threatened to crack under the pressure.

[We need to deal with them, master. Repeated strikes on a single point will eventually break even a barrier as strong as yours. Perhaps we should utilize more of your magic abilities?]

Sen smirked. Sure, he could handle them more easily in that case. But he was playing the role of a swordsman. Surely he could pretend a little longer. He had a rough idea of what Theodora’s and Simeon’s powers did now. He just needed to better understand Agatha’s. After that, he’d end this fight.

But for now…

He blasted raw mana at Agatha’s feet to force her back and followed through with a slash of his katana. With the room he created, he cast a spell. “Sword Pulse Maxim.”

Malleore thrummed in his hand like the beating of a heart. Metallic veins formed along the blade, pumping mana instead of blood.

Ba-dum.

Ba-dum.

Ba-dum.

Agatha rushed forward again, and they exchanged a few more strikes before the spell was finished. Malleore thrummed in his hand again. This time the heartbeat was much louder.

Ba-dum.

It was ready.

“Sword Pulse Heart Attack Maxima!”

Thousands of metallic sword slashes formed around Sen like a cage and shot outward. Agatha was forced to fend off the rapidly expanding cage with her sword to keep from being eviscerated. Her feet slid against the rooftop as the cage grew outward.

Theodora still pounded away at Sen’s barrier using the impact spell. But the cage reached them, and they were forced to separate. Theodora leapt backwards and slid back again, rapping her thighs in the meantime. Simeon backed away, losing his footing and tumbling off the rooftop.

The cage stopped expanding once it reached Solomon, as if some invisible force repelled it. But that didn’t matter. His technique had done its job.

He’d disrupted Theodora and Simeon’s attacks, and learned something about Agatha’s power. She couldn’t cut through all forms of magic. That meant she’d be no match for what he had in store next.

He gathered his mana.

Agatha struck the cage over and over, casting her spell again.

“Meteor Aspect - Ablation!” Even bolstered with it, her sword failed to pierce the cage.

Theodora grimaced. Her knuckles rapped against her legs so rapidly that they bled. Her thighs quivered, and she cried out in pain. “Simeon, hurry!”

His hand gripped the side of the rooftop, and Simeon pulled himself up. He ran to get close to her again. But before he reached her, her legs gave out. She fell, catching herself on the ground. Theodora cursed and slammed a fist into the rooftop. “Gotta start over.” She huffed in deep breaths of air. Her body was on the verge of exhaustion.

Sen smiled.

Now he understood. Theodora required momentum. Each strike made her faster, but as soon as she stopped, she lost it all. It was why she kept striking her own thighs when he forced distance between them. Simeon’s power allowed one to redirect all damage sources to the point of impact once it was placed. Agatha could cut through certain spells with her ablation technique. But force her out of range, and her options were limited. Unlike him, she was a true swordsman.

She gave up on the cage and shot towards Simeon. “We’re ending this now!” Agatha roared.

Sen tilted his head at that. “What’s she—oh.” Simeon held his arms out and exposed his body to Agatha.

[Master…]

“Right, I still have the impact spell on my chest. She’s going to cut me.”

His barrier was weakened as is. A technique like ablation would certainly cut through it, and it seemed she was confident this would end the fight. He wasn’t so sure about that, but why let them put it to the test?

Sen sheathed Malleore quickly and concentrated on the spell in front of him. “The best swordsmen have a way to deal with magic such as this.”

They had to, otherwise Ragmár would’ve been outmatched against mages on the battlefield. This technique was a favorite of his.

“Sword Sever Maxim,” Sen whispered a moment before Agatha struck Simeon. To an untrained eye, it would appear as if he’d barely unsheathed Malleore before re-sheathing it.

But Solomon noticed. He turned to look at Agatha. “Agatha, wait!” he yelled.

Too late.

Far too late.

Simeon’s impact spell shattered into pieces milliseconds before she struck. Without it, he couldn’t redirect damage. When Agatha brought her sword down across Simeon’s chest, it sent blood splattering into the streets below.

Simeon looked down in shock. His hands reached toward the open wound that stretched diagonally down from his shoulders to his waist. “What?” He fell backward, plummeting from the roof again.

Agatha stared at the blood on her sword in confusion and silence. She’d froze.

Theodora screamed, reaching out as if she could grab a Simeon that had already tumbled off the roof.

“I told you, Solomon. I told you that you were going to lose something,” Sen said.

That part wasn’t over, actually. “Black Wolf Howl, Spirit House.”

A portal of shadow burst into life behind him, and the black wolf pack leader crawled out. It pressed its snout against the fabric on Sen’s shoulder, and the disguised cloak welcomed the creature within.

One moment the giant wolf stood behind him; the next it was housed within his cloak.

[The black wolf pack leader dwells within the cloak. You may borrow its powers for a time.]

Sen smiled. He’d try out the technique the wolf had shown him in the ruins of the kingdom.

“Forest of Shadow Maxima.”

The world around them turned into a void, robbing the eyes of those around him of the light within. Blinding them and their senses.

“I told you there would be a price, Solomon.”
