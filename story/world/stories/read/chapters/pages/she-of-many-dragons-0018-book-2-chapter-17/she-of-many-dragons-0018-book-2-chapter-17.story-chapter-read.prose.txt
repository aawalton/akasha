Alice walked into the room and stopped short upon seeing one of the people she had hoped least to encounter in the Protectorate Guild: Samantha the Sorceress. Alice had what could be considered an uneasy relationship with the other woman, and she had the distinct feeling that it would not be made easier anytime soon.

The Sorceress gave her a bare nod, no smile, and looked immediately to Oliver, who was also there.

"This is a waste of my time," Samantha said.

"These are your orders, Sam. Helping new inductees get stronger is never a waste of our time.”

Samantha snorted. “From what I understand, we're not helping her grow stronger at all. Her little pets are the ones who are to be trained."

"They're not pets," Alice said. "They're my familiars."

"Which happen to be dangerous magical beasts," she snapped back. “Typically, one slays beasts for experience, does one not?”

Iggy hissed low, threads of smoke drifting from his muzzle.

Oliver looked distinctly uncomfortable. "Alice has shown time and time again that her dragons are completely under her control. Though I will admit that her class is a little bit unusual—"

"Yes, she has her familiars," Samantha dragged out the word, "do all the work for her. I would say that that class is rather convenient. Wouldn't you?"

Alice had quite enough of this. "If you object so much, what are you doing here?"

"As Oliver said, I was ordered."

"Ordered to do what?" Alice said, then inwardly marveled a little at herself that she was daring to question someone of such higher rank.

Alice from a year ago would have never done that. Never would have even considered it.

Samantha must have been thinking along the same lines because she all but snarled. "Since I am of a high rank, I can provide illusions for your beasts to attack, and hopefully level up since it seems that they are in dire need of help."

That stopped Alice short, and she exchanged glances with Prim, who, as usual, sat on her shoulder. "You can grow through illusions?" she asked. "No actual fighting required?"

"Of course they can," Samantha snapped. "You think that noble parents would send their children into actual danger? Think, girl!"

"Samantha," Oliver said repressively, and to Alice's surprise, the woman snapped her mouth shut and turned away. Oliver looked to Alice. "That does help explain a few things, and it is admirable that you have apparently come by your levels honestly," he said with a quirk of a smile. "But you did hear the administrator. He would like for you to be at least at level 10." He paused. "That is typically the bare minimum we require for induction into the Protectorate, but of course, seeing as you have quite the unusual—"

Samantha muttered under her breath, "Dark class,"

Oliver finished with another quelling look at Samantha, "Unusual class, exceptions were made."

"I see." Alice thought hard. If she had known that illusions were all that were needed, she could have had Prim creating them for the others.

Or perhaps not.

"Not all of my dragons are combative," she said. "And I think that I will need to level each of them in order to level myself."

Oliver frowned. "I assume every time you gain another familiar, it starts at a low level?"

"Yes."

"Well, that could be quite the bottleneck."

Prim straightened. "I don't believe that will be the case.” Something in the tone of her voice told Alice that she was speaking for her ears alone. "I do believe your levels are a culmination of experience and do not require every single dragon to level again before you do."

Alice nodded again. She thought about relaying her words to Oliver and Samantha, if only to strip away the smug look on Samantha's face. But a quieter voice—one left over from her General Laborer skill—told her that it was better to be underestimated by people of power. She didn't care much if she was a source of derision, just as long as she was not seen as an actual threat.

Samantha was not on her side, and she didn’t need the woman to think that she was stronger than she actually was.

"Well, as I was saying," Alice said, "only a few of my dragons will grow through combat."

"And what of the others?" Samantha asked, with a sneer.

"Gardening," Alice said sweetly.

"Not technically gardening, but plant enrichment does help," Natura said.

Samantha did a double take at the dragon, then noticed her bark-like scales and snorted. "Well, there are several courtyards full of plants. Hopefully, your dragon will not put the gardeners out of business."

"If all goes well," Natura said, "I will give them more work than ever before."

Samantha opened her mouth, and by the look on her face, she had something cutting on the tip of her tongue. She was in an awful mood, even worse than Alice remembered.

But Oliver raised a hand. "That's enough, Samantha." Then he looked at Alice. "For this exercise, we will only use your dragons that will gain experience in combat. We’ll work with the others later."

One of those dragons was Bella. She currently sat on Alice’s other shoulder, opposite Prim. Alice reached to touch her dark hide, conveying silently that she was to stay out of this.

She wanted them not to think of Bella at all. Not even to notice her, if it could be helped.

"Iggy, Spark, Aconite," she said, "see if you can fight the illusions."

Iggy gave her a long look, as if he were reluctant to leave her side—it was plainly obvious he did not trust Samantha. Then his gaze fell on Bella.

Something passed between them, almost as if Iggy were silently commanding her to watch Alice.

Bella bristled a bit at being ordered but then bobbed her head once in a nod.

Samantha huffed and then twiddled her fingers in the air in a complex pattern that Alice thought might be a spell. Her other hand held a glowing white stone. Then, bringing her palm to her lips, Samantha blew across it, and monsters formed in the air.

This was a high-ceilinged room, and the monsters were… odd. More like shapes made out of spikes with wings than anything natural. As soon as the illusion spell was released, the winged, spiked creatures flew high into the air. None were as elegant-looking as her dragons, yet under Samantha's direction, they seemed to rise more effortlessly than a dragon ever could.

That's not fair, she thought, but outwardly she only gave a nod.

"Take them down," she said calmly.

Iggy led the charge with Spark and Aconite flying after him. Out of all of them, she was worried for Aconite, who didn't have a long-range option and had to bite to attack his victims. But he charged bravely forward, just like the others.

Spark threw out an arc of lightning that went through one of the spiked creatures. There was a pause, as if Samantha were unsure how to react, before the creature faded from view.

Spark chittered angrily, and though he didn't use words, Alice could tell that he was little disappointed with the outcome. He would rather it explode.

Iggy breathed fire, but the spiked creature dodged in a way that was practically teleportation. Definitely unfair. Iggy’s gout of flame went wide.

But Iggy had become a wily hunter during his time in the forest and immediately flipped over himself to swim through the air after it, his narrow wings making him extremely deft at maneuvers.

The spiked creatures were good at dodging left and right, and so Aconite had the hardest time of all three to get a hit. But when Spark chased one nearby, he immediately folded his narrow purple wings and struck down at it, claws and fangs out. He bit, and the illusion faded.

Within a few minutes, Spark had killed two, Iggy three, and Aconite had one. There were only two of the spiked creatures left, and they flitted around the open air for a while, seeming unusually deft.

Oliver stepped close to Alice. "They are good independent hunters, but can they work together?”

That was an excellent hint. “Prim, tell them," Alice said.

Prim was Mother of All, and she was the one supposed to relay her orders. Alice couldn't be seen bellowing commands—she didn’t know much about the people in the Protectorate Guild, but she sensed they would think that undignified.

Prim raised herself on Alice's shoulder and let out a sharp call that sounded like an eagle's cry.

Alice didn't sense any words to it, but Iggy, Spark, and Aconite acted as if she had just laid out a plan for them. Immediately, the three converged with Iggy at the front, Aconite on one side, and Spark on the other. A basic arrow formation.

They aimed for one of the creatures that was flapping in a corner. It darted to the side as if to escape, but Spark sent out an arc of lightning to chase it back. Aconite went under it, harassing it from beneath to ensure that it did not think to dive down. And Iggy closed in, bathing the area wide with his breath of fire. The illusion was caught and faded out.

Then they turned on the last one, and using similar methods, made short work of it.

At the end, Aconite gained another level.

Spark and Iggy did not, but they were much more leveled than the newer sub-aspect.

Oliver brightened when Alice told him that there had been some process with their levels. "Excellent. We believe that part of what helps gain experience is increasing the knowledge around fighting. They had to use several different tactics to defeat their enemies, and that is powerful to practice. That means they can advance, even when there is no danger. Not to mention," he said with a laugh, his eyes tracking Aconite, who flew back to Natura, panting heavily, his sides expanding and contracting, "it's also good for their physical health."

"Well, I have an idea of their baseline capabilities, which isn't much, but I suppose it's done you good enough so far," Samantha said. "This might be helping them, but it's a waste of my time."

She reached deep into her pockets and brought out the small white stone, which was now dull and cloudy, and tossed it at Alice.

Alice caught it out of reflex. The stone was incomprehensible to her, with a rainbow of coin-sized dots scattered in a circle around it. Several lines denoted levels, but there were no words to explain any of it. "What is this?"

"That is a basic illusion training stone. I modified it and set the levels low for your dragons," she said. "We were working on the purple simulation. Touch the green."

Alice did. The stone lit up a brilliant white, and an illusion of a terrible monster that might have been a bull appeared in front of her. It stood on hind legs, had a man's chest, and carried a spiked mace. Alarmed, Alice backed up instinctively, but the bull-man did not move.

"Now you can see the difficulty level," Samantha said. "See how the little bars go up? That means it's more intense. I would suggest trying for the least intense before moving forward, but they're not my beasts—ah, familiars." She nodded, half-bowed to Oliver, then swept out of the room.

“It is brave of her to be so rude," Bella said. “She doesn’t know who she speaks to.”

"She may be a bit... brusque," Oliver said, "but what she just gave you is worth several blue steel coins."

Alice looked at it in a new light. "Truly?"

"Yes, so, ah,” he shifted uncomfortably, “it would be best if you did not take it out of the Protectorate building. Or tell people that you had it."

Alice nodded.

“I am not mage-gifted, so I’ve used these before to augment my own training when I was young." He carefully took it from her hands. "You see this button with the arrows? This sets it in a loop, so it will replay the simulation once the last is complete. Let's do the flying combat simulation again," he touched the purple dot, and those spiked flying creatures appeared once more.

Oliver did not have to do any of the same finger twiddling or blowing into his palm as Samantha had done, Alice noticed. Had that been just an illusion as well? Something for her to pretend like she was more important than she actually was?

"I'll set it for the next difficulty rating up. It looks like we are on the bottom. Ten would be the most difficult,” Oliver continued, then added quickly, "though it is still not something that would endanger your dragons. Remember that this is what we use to train noble children."

So she had basically been given a children’s primer. An expensive one. Alice couldn’t be angry, though she wished it had not come from Samantha. “If it is that safe and can be set on a loop, I assume once the illusion is set, we do not have to stay here to watch the fight?”

Oliver agreed with a nod.

“Then let’s see what we can do to train my other dragons,” Alice said.

Prim spoke up, "I'd like to stay here and observe."

Alice was startled for a moment, but then she remembered illusions were Prim's forte. Of course she would be interested in the illusion stone and all it could do.

"Of course.” She looked at Oliver. "Samantha may have been joking, but I think I would like to see those gardens."

Natura gave a squeal of delight, and Alice smiled at her.

With all luck, she could find something for Bella to level herself up with as well.
