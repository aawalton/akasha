Broken foundations cause rough waters.

The ferry kept moving and Harker sat and watched the city pass by. Smoke gathered in the air as the streets grew dimmer, both from the chimneys and the torches that soon replaced the tidal gem lamps. Elegant, infused stone walls were also gone. In their place were walls of stacked fieldstone or, more abundantly, wooden planks. Those were sourced somewhere upriver and were a far cheaper alternative to excavating the mountain for its precious green stone.

“Or so the citylord claims,” said the white haired Talented. She waggled her thin fingers at her friend. “But who owns the lumber and the sawmills? That’s right, the citylord and his pack of sycophantic hounds.”

“They own the rights to mine the stone around the city as well.”

“Exactly my point! It’s all theirs.” The old woman scoffed. "Back in my day, all the buildings were stone."

"Our day, love."

"That's what I said. Back then, it was a crime to hoard a common resource. The citylord even sold it all for cost! ‘The price of growing the community,’ she said. Where’s that spirit now?”

“It was a different time, I fear.”

“No, it’s greed. It's all greed." The white-haired woman clucked her tongue. "Everyone is gobbling up, everything on the plate, leaving nothing for those of us that can't fight our way to the table."

Her iron-haired companion leaned back, studying the distant sparkle of false stars. "What can we do?"

"Anything is better than nothing."

"Is it? Or is it motion without meaning?"

The two faded into a companionable silence as the canal slapped against the wooden hull. A few other conversations bubbled up around Harker. But none grabbed him quite as much as the two old women nearby. He found him liking them for all of their endless complaints. Perhaps they'd be safe to ask.

"Excuse me," Harker interrupted.

The two women turned to look at him, both with kind smiles. "Oh, hello, dear. What do you need?"

"I'm sorry to bother you, but do you happen to know where the Charterstones are in the city?"

"A traveler!" The iron-haired woman with the heat Talent leaned closer. "Or a Chartermarked?"

Harker considered obfuscating. Lying was, of course, off the table thanks to his Stature. But Harker just felt tired. "Chartermarked.”

The woman traded an excited glance with her white-haired friend. "That is lovely! Taking up the mantle of the Aspirants. You know, I wanted to Descend the Spires in my day, but never had the gumption."

The white-haired woman sighed. "You and me both, Greta."

"Sadly, my Warming Aura isn't much use against monsters, is it, Clarice?"

"Neither is my Woodbind,” Clarice said. “But they've both proven excellent for our coopering business, eh?"

"Too true!"

They dissolved into cackles, and Harker watched, helpless. "What about the Charterstones?"

"Oh, of course. I'm sorry. Clarice here tells me that if I wasn't careful, I'd laugh my own head off.” Greta clapped her hands. “But the Charterstones, of course, yes. They are down by the oldtown market, easy to find."

"And where's that?" Harker asked.

"Near Grummel and Ovalon's. Big dock-side inn. Impossible to miss. Look for the enormous gold and blue banners.”

Clarice sighed. “It's grown ever gaudier in the last decade or so."

"Do you remember their 100th anniversary?"

"Oh, yes. They had a parade."

"Incorrigible old man, that Grumble."

The old woman kept talking, relaying the most inane details of the inn, the market, and several acquaintances that had long since passed. Yet he couldn't help the smile as he listened.

The Charterstones were closer to the docks than Harker expected, but it worked for his plans. It certainly makes things more convenient.

When the woman came up for air between mad gales of giggling and gossip, Harker quickly stepped in. "Thank you both. I appreciate the directions."

Greta reached out, patting his hand. "You're quite welcome, dear. But you must know, most of the Chartermark have left the city.”

“You're running a bit behind," Clarice said with a bobbing nod.

Harker grimaced. "I know."

Greta and Clarice traded a look.

"And one other thing," said the iron-haired woman. "Our clients have told us that the forests around the Southern Reach are…infested."

Clarice leaned closer. "Aberrants. True ones to boot."

Harker clenched his teeth. True Aberrants were like Bestials like sharks were to pike. "How many?”

“They didn’t mention. Only that the city was on lockdown except the Chartermarked passing through toward Antrim.”

Antrim. That was the most direct route to Vodaten and the Travelspire. “That is good to know.”

“Just make sure you find someone to watch your back, dear." Greta held on to Clarice. Their hands wrinkled as they deformed into a tight, comforting grip. "You need a team. Going it alone is asking for trouble."

The old woman got off the ferry, moving just as nimbly as someone half their age. Others climbed on, some bundled more than Harker thought necessary for the mild evening. The ferry moved on and conversation sprang up around him again. It washed over Harker like the slap of water against the ferry’s hull, and they sped onward, quickly losing sight of the old women.

Harker toyed with the coin purse. A team, huh.

Harker endured three more stops before he disembarked himself. In the span of a half hour, he found himself negotiating the trash-strewn crowds at the riverside docks.

The road was rough beneath his heels, and the air smelled of fish and sour sweat. Criers dotted the crowd, extolling the virtues of cheap ale, loud music, and the sizzling scent of burnt food. The crowds were heavy despite the late hour—back home, cheap ale or not, they would have long since headed to bed. Here, they were just getting started.

Harker tried to keep himself separate from the press of half-drunk riverboat sailors, dockhands, and warehouse workers that squeezed into great halls and taprooms. Traffic was thinnest near the water, where the river sloshed against large stone pylons, but the footing was slick and uncertain.

Thaumic lamps here were non-existent. The few posts that still stood had long since been broken, their tidal gems torn free by opportunists. Even the guards were few and far between, and those that were around leaned sullenly against corner posts, unwilling to do much more than grumble amongst their own kind. Oily tallow candles were mounted on a few posts where others were simply dark. Only the false stars above and a heavy proliferation of torches gave any illumination at all.

It didn't matter to Harker, in any case.

Sovereign Sight hung around him, feeding a flurry of impressions into his head with every step. He focused mostly on the feel of folks around him, and on what he could see. While five feet was the limit of his sphere, his eyes could still pick out distant details better than before. His Boon had improved them immensely, and his Talent only served to sharpen that advantage.

Years back, Garon had a spyglass in stock. Within hours, it became the most sought after item in his store—at least to every preteen boy within a ten mile radius. Harker had stared at it for days, unable to do so much as touch it, but one day Garon had been feeling particularly kind; he let a gaggle of them all look through it. Not touch—just put their eyes up against one end. Harker still recalled the feeling of seeing a distant tree leap toward him, so close it was like he could take it in his hands.

His Talent and improved eyes worked in a similar fashion, except that they offered him a new measure of control. With some effort, Harker discovered he could focus the distance. He peered at a sign swinging from a wrought iron post. It was three blocks away and nothing more than a blurry blob in the distance—yet just as easily as focusing his eyes, he could also focus his Talent. The sign drew closer, sharpening as it did so, until a slightly fuzzy picture of a bubbling tankard and fish loomed before him. At the same time, his Talent refused to let him lose awareness of everything else in his sphere. It was a confusing sensation, to be sure, but one that Harker found immensely useful.

Each step he took was an attempt to grow used to the sensations bombarding his head. Over and over, Harker focused on objects near and far while keeping aware of the crowd around him. Once or twice, dizziness gripped him, but it never lasted for more than a second. At regular intervals, in fact, his senses would overlap. They joined together in new combinations that made his thoughts spark and scramble more than once. Scent and sight, taste and hearing, even touch and sight combined into one. They mixed, until the air was a smoky blue, stabbed by the rusty sharpness of passing unwashed bodies. He could taste the slippery, sweet sound of bells ringing on nearby buoys, even feel the press of greasy tallow light as it spread amongst the crowd.

He could hear the cold breath of the creature that loomed over him.

Harker jerked back, losing grip on his Talent as it sputtered out in his tributaries. He blinked, and realized that he stood in the center of a square. To his right was a large inn, covered in enormous banners displaying a golden badger on a field of blue. Other shops filled the space amid the flagstones, and people crowded close to all of them. Everywhere except around Harker…and the trio of enormous, looming rocks.

The Charterstones.

Harker frowned. He was the only one within at least ten feet. “Sovereign Sight.”

His Talent engulfed the standing stones and immediately he felt it again—a cold, heavy presence that felt like nothing so much as a looming, breathing creature.

No. That’s…they have neither mouths or lungs. No air is being inhaled or exhaled. Yet despire his refusal, he couldn’t deny that the stones felt aware.

Even that sensation fled though as a deep thrum passed through Harker’s body. His shoulders tossed back and a creaking stretch fluttered through his chest and extremities. His Stature thickened, and he tracked it all with his Talent—it coiled around his reservoir, Soul Lock, and the spiraling profusion of his tributaries. A pain he’d barely noticed anymore eased away, and the relief was like taking a deep breath for the first time in weeks.

The rest of the Stature thickened around his tributaries, and was especially noticeable around the newly cut complexities where he’d cut deepest. The pale potency soaked him, crown to toes, reinforcing his spiritflesh like rafters beneath a roof. It wasn’t as good as making a Bargain, especially with someone as strong as Ironwine, but it helped. Harker felt steadier. More himself, if that made any sense, and he wasn’t sure that it did.

His soul might have been broken, but the patch held strong.

Okay. First task taken care of. He licked his lips and reached out toward the stones. Next up is checking the leaderboard…and finding out where I stand.
