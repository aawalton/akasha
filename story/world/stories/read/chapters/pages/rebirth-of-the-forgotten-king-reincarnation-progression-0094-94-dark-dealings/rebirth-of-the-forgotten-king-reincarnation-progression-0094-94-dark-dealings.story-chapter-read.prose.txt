Natasha’s expression didn’t shift. If anything, there was something colder beneath her usual composure. "Elias did this to himself."

She turned away before Adrian could respond, walking toward a nearby desk. Afterwards, Natasha pulled open a drawer, reaching inside carefully, and when she turned back, something small rested between her fingers: a crumpled brown bag no larger than a coin pouch.

The moment she lifted it up, Adrian caught a glimpse of what lay inside. A fine, glittering powder clung to the fabric, shimmering in the dim glow like crushed stardust. Even without knowing what it was, his instincts screamed at him to keep his distance.

"Don’t get too close, even inhaling the residue can have effects." Natasha warned.

Adrian took a cautious step back. His gaze flicked to Zuri, who had stiffened at the sight of it.

The moment she saw what was inside, her expression darkened. "That’s… Duskrime."

Adrian frowned. "You know what this stuff is?"

"It’s made from Nyx Shade, a herb that only grows in the deepest parts of the Umbral Forest back home. The plant itself is harmless until it’s refined into this. It’s... potent. A small dose heightens your senses, makes you feel like you’re untouchable. But it doesn’t last, and the crash is brutal. Overuse leads to... well." Zuri's gaze flicked to Elias. "This."

Adrian looked at the boy again, his stomach twisting. "And how is something from your homeland showing up here? Nyssalor isn't exactly close to Mistral."

Zuri shook her head. "It shouldn’t be. The export of Nyxshade is tightly controlled. Even back home, it’s rare. For someone to smuggle it out, process it, and get it all the way here means that some very well connected people must be involved."

Natasha snapped the bag shut before she tucked it back into her coat. "Which is exactly why I wanted you both to see this firsthand. A lot of students have gotten into trouble they shouldn’t have, just to get their hands on this stuff."

"And has the academy done anything to stop the proliferation of Duskrime?" Adrian asked.

"Obviously not, though I strongly believe it's not because they aren't aware of the issue."

"You think that some of the academy staff might be involved in the spread of this stuff?"

Natasha nodded. "Some of them, yes. We’re still piecing everything together, but there’s a pattern forming. Some students go missing for a night or two and come back looking like Elias. Others return unharmed but refuse to say where they went. And then… there are those who don’t come back at all."

Zuri crossed her arms. "And is the academy just going to keep quiet about this?"

"The principal is well aware of the situation. So is the student council. But whoever is bringing Duskrime into the academy knows how to cover their tracks, and they know exactly who to target." Natasha then gestured to Elias. "The desperate, the reckless, and the foolish ones looking for a cheap thrill."

"So what’s our job in all this?" Adrian asked.

"Your job is to help us find out who’s responsible. We need to know how Duskrime is getting in, who’s distributing it, and who’s behind all of it." Her fingers tapped against the edge of the desk, a quiet rhythm against the heavy silence. "The student council can only do so much from our position. The disciplinary committee, however... you’ll have more freedom to investigate directly."

"You mean we’ll be doing the dirty work."

"Semantics, Adrian, besides, my hands are tied here as is."

Zuri folded her arms. "I don’t mind getting my hands dirty, but why rely on us? Why not bring the faculty into this? Or better yet, the kingdom? If Duskrime is as rare and dangerous as I know it to be, then this isn’t just an academy problem."

"Yeah, if this stuff is being smuggled all the way here, someone higher up has to care, right? Or are we pretending this is just another ‘student issue’?" Adrian added on.

Natasha sighed, rubbing her temple before lowering her hand to rest against the desk. "You think we haven’t considered that? The faculty knows, at least, some of them do. The problem is, they can’t act openly. If word got out that students were using Duskrime, it would be a significant stain on the academy's reputation."

"What, so they’re just turning a blind eye?"

"No, They’re observing the situation. But they can’t do anything until we give them something concrete. If the staff steps in too early, the real culprits will disappear, and all we’ll have left are the students they used as pawns. And if that happens, we lose our chance to cut this off at the source."

"That’s a dangerous game."

"It is, but it’s the only one we can play. The faculty has rules they have to stick to, and they can’t go storming through the underground networks without the academy board breathing down their necks. In contrast, the disciplinary committee isn't bound by the same red tape. And you'll be more excused for 'overstepping' your bounds."

Adrian exhaled sharply. So the school cares, but not enough to do anything themselves. Typical.

Zuri, however, was still watching Natasha closely. "And what about the kingdom? If this is an exported drug, especially coming from my homeland, then this isn’t just an academy problem, it’s a crime that crosses borders. Are you telling me the crown wouldn’t care?"

Natasha’s jaw tightened slightly before she answered. "They’d care, but they’d care too much. If the kingdom gets involved, this turns into something much bigger than an internal investigation."

"This academy is more than just a school, it’s a place where heirs of noble houses, royal envoys, and foreign dignitaries send their children. If word got out that Duskrime was circulating within these walls, there would be political fallout, among other potential ramifications."

She paused, letting that settle before adding, "And if that happens, no one will be looking for justice. Just scapegoats."

Zuri exhaled through her nose. "So what you’re saying is... if we don’t deal with this quietly, a lot of innocent people are going to get caught in the mess."

"Exactly, and not just students. If the wrong noble house gets implicated, if the wrong heir is caught in this mess, the consequences could spread far beyond these walls."

Adrian blew out a slow breath. So this isn’t just about keeping the school’s reputation clean. It’s about stopping something before it turns into a disaster.

Zuri tapped a finger against her arm, considering. "And if someone else forces the kingdom’s hand?"

Natasha’s lips pressed into a thin line. "Then people like Elias?" She nodded toward the boy on the cot, still lost in whatever his mind was trapped in. "They’ll be the least of our concerns."

Silence stretched between all of them. Adrian ran a hand through his hair, letting out a dry chuckle that held no humor. "Great, so we’re not just investigators, we’re also glorified damage control."

Natasha’s smirk returned, though it was tinged with something grim. "Now you’re getting it."

Zuri exhaled, rubbing the bridge of her nose before dropping her arms to her sides. "Fine. I get why we’re doing this in-house. But we need leads."

"And," Adrian added, his gaze flicking back to Elias, "we need to know what he knows."

Natasha nodded. "Then let’s wake him up."

She stepped toward Elias, and then rolled back her sleeve, exposing the pale skin of her forearm. Her hand lifted, fingers sketching a slow arc in the air before pressing down against her own flesh.

A sharp hiss cut through the stillness. Adrian’s breath hitched as a thin crimson line bloomed against her skin, fresh blood welling at the surface. But it didn’t fall. It moved.

The liquid defied gravity, twisting into tendrils that coiled around her fingers like sentient threads. It pulsed, glowing faintly, shifting from deep red to something almost molten, like living fire coursing through her veins. Adrian watched, entranced and unsettled in equal measure. Blood magic, it's been a while.

Beside him, Zuri inhaled softly but kept still, her arms folding tighter across her chest. She didn’t flinch, but Adrian noticed the slight tension in her shoulders.

Natasha knelt beside Elias’s cot, her blood-streaked fingers reaching toward the boy’s temple. The glow around her hand intensified the moment her fingertips met his damp skin. A pulse of energy rippled outward.

Adrian swore he saw something shift beneath Elias’s flesh: a tremor, a ripple, as if the magic were threading through his very mind, digging deep. Then, Elias convulsed.

A ragged gasp tore from the boy's throat as his entire body jolted, his muscles locking up in a spasm before releasing all at once. His chest heaved, his pupils shrinking to the size of pinpricks before blowing wide. He sucked in a breath like a drowning man breaking the surface, his ribs rising and falling in quick, panicked bursts. His fingers clawed at the sheets beneath him, nails scraping against fabric as if searching for something solid to ground himself.

"Wha—what—?!“ His voice cracked, raw and frantic. He twisted, trying to sit up, only to freeze when he felt Natasha’s hand still pressed against his forehead.

"You were lost in your own mind, I pulled you back." Natasha said.

Elias let out a ragged breath, still shaking. His fingers curled into the thin mattress, gripping the fabric like a lifeline. Sweat slicked his forehead, rolling down his temples in glistening trails.

His bloodshot eyes darted toward Adrian and Zuri, panic clinging to him like a suffocating fog. "Who—who are you? What do you want from me?"

Zuri crouched beside the cot, leveling him with an intense gaze. "We’re not here to hurt you. But we need answers."

Elias shook his head violently. "I—I don’t—"

Adrian leaned forward. "You do. You know why you ended up like this. You know what you took. And you definitely know where you got it."

Elias flinched. His fingers curled into fists, knuckles white. "I—I wasn’t supposed to tell anyone."

"You don’t have to want to tell us, you just have to do it."

Elias trembled, his face pale, lips pressed together so tightly they almost disappeared. But the moment his gaze flicked to Natasha’s bloodstained fingers, his resolve shattered.  "They… they said they’d kill me if I talked."

Adrian’s stomach clenched. Who is ‘they’?

Natasha’s hand lifted from his temple. "You don’t have to be afraid of them. They don’t have power here—we do."

Elias let out a shaking breath, shoulders slumping. He closed his eyes for a moment, as if trying to push through the terror clinging to his bones. When he opened them again, he spoke in a whisper. "Falkner. He’s the one who gave it to me. He gets it from someone else."

"Full name," Zuri pressed.

Elias exhaled, running a hand through his damp hair. "Falkner V—the—"

Suddenly, Elias's voice cut off. His entire body went rigid, his pupils dilating once more. His breath slowed, turning eerily measured, the frantic fear draining from his expression so suddenly it sent a chill down Adrian’s spine.

"Elias?"

No reaction. Elias blinked once. Then twice.

His hands, once trembling with fear, loosened their grip. He looked around the room, his expression unfocused, as if he had just woken up in a place he didn’t recognize. "What…? What’s happening?"

Adrian’s pulse hammered against his ribs. "Elias, you were just talking to us. Do you remember what you were saying?"

Elias frowned, shaking his head. "I—no. I don’t— what… why am I here?"

Zuri’s expression darkened, her hands tightening at her sides. "What’s the last thing you remember?"

Elias blinked again, slow and sluggish, before his brows furrowed. "I—I don’t know. I was… I was going to meet someone, I think? But I—I don’t remember who."

His breath quickened as panic crept back in, but this time it was rooted in pure confusion rather than fear of exposure. His hands ran over his arms as if trying to ground himself, trying to recall something that simply wasn’t there anymore.

Natasha’s jaw tightened. "It’s gone."

Adrian inhaled sharply. "What?"

"Someone cut his memories, though I'm not sure by how much."

Zuri straightened, her expression hard. "You mean wiped them."

"Same thing." Natasha pulled her sleeve back down, eyes narrowing as she studied Elias with renewed scrutiny. "And if someone was willing to use memory-altering magic inside the academy, then we have a bigger problem than just smuggling."

Zuri’s arms remained crossed, her fingers pressing into her sleeves as she studied Elias. “If someone erased his memories, then that means they knew we were here. Either they were watching him, or there’s a failsafe that activates if he gets too close to saying something important.”

Like a leash around his mind. Adrian thought. The idea of someone having that much control over another person made his skin crawl. “That kind of magic shouldn't be common, right? Not at this level?”

Natasha exhaled, stepping back from Elias’s cot. “No. It’s not. But this means all of this is much bigger than I had initially anticipated. For now, we should just standby."

Zuri’s head snapped toward her. “What?”

“You heard me. we wait.”

“Wait for what? We have a lead.” Adrian said.

“We had a lead,” Natasha corrected, nodding toward Elias, who was now rubbing his temples in a fog of confusion. “And now we don’t. In a few days, once you’re both officially part of the disciplinary committee, you’ll have more authority. You’ll be able to investigate without raising too many questions. Until then, you do nothing.”

"But what about Falkner? Elias mentioned that name at least."

"There's quite a number of Falkner's in the academy, it's not an uncommon name. So either way, we'll need more time."

Zuri scoffed, shaking her head. “And if more students disappear in those few days? If more people end up like Elias?”

“Then we make sure we’re ready to deal with it when we do move.”

Adrian clenched his jaw. Damn it. Every instinct screamed for him to do something, but Natasha wasn’t wrong. If whoever was behind this was powerful enough to wipe minds, then charging in without a plan would only get them nowhere.

Zuri exhaled sharply, eyes narrowing. “Fine, but when we are officially part of the committee? I’m not holding back.”

Natasha chuckled, but it didn’t reach her eyes. “I wouldn’t expect anything less.”
