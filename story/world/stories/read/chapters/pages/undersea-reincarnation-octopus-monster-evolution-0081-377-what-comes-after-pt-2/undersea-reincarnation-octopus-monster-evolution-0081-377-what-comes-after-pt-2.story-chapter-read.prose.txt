As the beam faded and as the light and colors of day bled back into his vision, Henry watched what had once been the parasite’s body drift away with the wind. The small cloud of blooming ash dispersed, some of it hitting the water while the rest was carried away. Of the parasite, nothing was left, as far as Henry could see. Just dust.

1x [Puppeteer of Thorns and Flesh (A) - Level 31] lesser core fragment was collected.

Whatever Attal had used, it had completely annihilated it. Henry didn’t know A-ranks could be one-shot like that. The parasite might not be the most defensively minded creature he’d encountered, but that was still a sobering reminder that Henry and Maurice shouldn’t piss off the wrong creature.

Henry stared from above, feeling the wind on his arms for only a couple of seconds before the first thrashing limb broke the surface, soon followed by the four remaining arms, one of which was missing a good length out, though it was already regrowing. It seemed like the starfish hadn’t appreciated losing its master. Or maybe that was some sort of failsafe the parasite had left it with?

It was hard to tell. Though to be honest, Henry couldn’t care less. The two creatures had been too dangerous to have this close to the island, and the parasite hadn’t been that friendly to begin with. Both could move on to the next life, as far as he was concerned.

He had more important things to worry about.

“Can you manage the thing that’s left?” Attal floated down next to him, stopping a few paces away from Henry’s right eye.

The man was practically the same size as his eye. Probably lighter, too.

“I want to get started on some failsafes,” Attal continued, nodding back toward the island. His features were mostly blank, but there was some clear tension in his shoulder and jaw. “The barrier between us and them might have been weakened. Somehow. I don’t need that thing to try and crawl through again.”

Henry tried to nod, but when he had no neck and was hundreds of times larger than the man he was speaking to, it was a little awkward. Plus, now that the body-snatching creature was gone, he could get in there and do some damage himself. “[Sure. Go for it. We’ll talk later,]” Henry sent back while a few of his clones went to check if there was anything left of the parasite. As for the rest of his army, they were swimming down, going after the starfish. Henry wanted to have eyes on its core and its limbs, in case something unexpected happened.

Attal floated lower and seemed to be ready to shoot away, only to hesitate for a second. He glanced up at Maurice, then back at Henry. “Those copies you sent after her… do you think they made it?”

Henry eyed the man for a second, then he began floating down toward the water. Attal seemed to be conflicted about something. There might even be a sense of guilt there, but Henry didn’t want to delve into that just yet. He wasn’t one to throw stones considering he might have contributed to the creature tracking them back. “[I’m not sure,]” he sent back. “[The simpler clones should survive the trip, but unless I’m able to send some more clones through a rift and bring them back, I won’t know. So hopefully they did survive. And hopefully they can listen to her orders.]”

Attal simply listened, then nodded. There was a little bit of hope. But it was a slim thing, and both of them seemed to agree on something: right now, it’d be better to do something productive instead of contemplating remote possibilities.

Attal flew off toward the island, and Henry plunged down below the waves.

“[Do you think we could open up our own little rift to test your clones? To see if they’d survive the trip?]”

Henry glanced down at Maurice, and found him wringing his hands, much like he would be doing if he’d been in his natural form. And while he agreed wholeheartedly, and wanted to have some concrete answers himself, he knew they couldn’t.

“[It’d give us answers… but it’s probably too risky for us to attempt. Who says we can close it once it’s open?]”

Maurice had no answer to that, and neither did Henry. Velistraine had thrown herself out of the world to give them a chance at closing the rift. To give the System a chance. This had been likely important and delicate enough that the System intervened and even it has had issues.

No, it’d make no sense for them to open the rift back up. But there was one aspect of the skill’s description that might eventually give them some answers.

Henry watched the giant starfish kick up a storm, reaching up for him and his clones, but he could easily dodge it. “[Trickster’s Shadow’s description says that as long as we’re on the ‘same plane of existence’, the clones would survive. I might not be able to communicate with them, but they should still be fine. The question is then: is where Velistraine has gone and this world are considered part of the same plane of existence? And what does that even mean? How many planes are there?]”

“[The System wrote the skill. It would know,]” Maurice pointed out.

Henry smiled at that, recalling those golden threads and the prompt the System had thrown his way. “[It would know, wouldn’t it? Means we keep going. We need the rest of the orbs. It did say there’d be answers once we do, so maybe we can get it to spill the beans, then. Those orbs must be the key to lift some of its restrictions, I’m betting.]”

With that settled, the two really got into dispatching the starfish. It was resilient. It was massive, and it regenerated somewhat quickly. But after a few minutes of consistent, relentless venomous bites, it was growing lethargic. And Henry wasn’t leaving the task to just his clones. He’d hitched himself right atop of its center, and through his beak, he was continuously converting the mana he was stealing from it—both through the mana-venom and through his bite and contact draining abilities—into venoms, of which he had plenty.

Of course the starfish was fighting back. But it wasn’t anywhere near the level of the parasite or the seahorse they’d fought before, and without its master, it was mostly a wall of spikes and muscles, neither of which could do much damage to him. She he latched on to it and kept poisoning it. The best the starfish could manage was puncture a few inches into his flesh, and once Henry wrapped his arms around its center and formed a bone-dome above his head, it could do nothing to get him off of it. Not even whacking him with its massive limb did much but break its spines. As for its venom… well. Henry had experienced stronger. It seemed to be some sort of mana-burning, paralyzing thing, but against his high Constitution and his own resistance to toxin, it merely itched. As for Maurice, he was focusing on its core, detonating it bit by bit, and eventually, some ten minutes later, the notification came through.

1x [Unending Acanthaster Vassal (A) - Lvl 9] greater core fragment was collected.

“[Unending might have been a little pretentious,]” Maurice chimed in as Henry detached himself off of the creature. Though before he swam up, he eyed the thing warily.

What were the odds this thing had parasite eggs? Likely. They’d probably be born weak, but why take the risk? The safe thing would be to chuck it in a volcano or something, but the corpse was massive.

Could I fit this?

Wrapping a tentative arm around one of its limb, Henry pulled, activating his Maw—and the starfish disappeared, kicking up wide stretches of sand and dust in its wake.

“[Whoa! Did you do that?]”

Henry was still blinking. He hadn’t expected to be able to fit the damn thing. It was huge. Then again, the storage capacity of his Maw had been growing pretty aggressively lately. “[I guess it fits. We’ll see about finding someplace safe to destroy it.]”

With the starfish dealt with, Henry now had to decide. The island first, or start working on contacting Zerathstra. He’d have to re-establish the network, and he’d better start on that right away.

And after that… I’ll see about the walls of notifications waiting for me, he thought to himself, looking up.

It wasn’t even noon yet.
