The guards and adventurers holding the wall greeted us as heroes, crowding around, shouting, and calling out questions. They wanted to know of the dragon, the white bear, and the lightning cat.

“Thank you for slaying those siege beasts!” An archer with blood down one side of his face said as he caught my forearm and helped me up. Another stopped herself just before clapping Eryn’s shoulder, then settled for saluting the clearly wounded adventurer instead.

“They honor the King appropriately,” Roq said. “An intelligent crowd of bipeds who understand who has saved their lives.”

“It’s all part of a Central Command field test,” Mara shouted over them. “And if one more of you keeps us from the healers, I’ll throw you off this wall myself.”

That shut them up and got them moving.

Two guards worked to lift Knut, but had to call for a third. They carried him and Lan down first, then helped the rest of us from the wall. Many of the guards on the battlements sported wounds, and the blood on the stones didn’t all look like monster blood.

We were taken to a healing station inside a stone storehouse near the wall. Every cot was occupied, with more wounded lying on blankets between them. Upon seeing us, the head healer started triage, and soon after said to three patients on cots, “take your blankets and find a spot on the floor, no.”

As soon as the cots cleared, she had Knut, Lan, and Nabeeh laid down on them, and they got to work.

I waved a healer toward Eryn, but he planted himself in front of me and pointed at the blood running down my legs.

“I’ll be fine, healer,” I said. “Special… er… central command magic.” Taking a page from Mara’s playbook, I opened one of the cuts in my armor wide enough for him to see. Blood Forge heated the skin beneath until the edges pulled together.

The healer watched it seal, ran a finger across the new skin,  then grunted and shook his head before turning to Eryn.

That bought me the freedom and time to sit beside her and hold her hand while the healer did his job. She had cuts across her arms and face, but they could be healed. Nabeeh had the same type of cuts, in addition to two broken fingers. Mara had lost two teeth.

Once Roq decided I was far enough from death not to complain, he stopped healing me.

“What in the cracked bell happened to my healing? Are we running out of carcasses?”

“The king’s steed is now stable, and—”

“I’d prefer healed.”

“And I would prefer to know what memories are stored in the insane membranes of Illadrox’s brain before they decay beyond use!”

“I still think you should be able to do both, as much as you brag about being the most outstanding and awe-inspiring dragon King of all the worlds and all that.”

While being actively healed, I was perfectly fine dealing with the pain, because I knew it was temporary. But once the heat of Blood Forge disappeared, all the wounds begged for my attention. That might have made me a tad grumpy despite our victory.

“I could, of course, do so, but I am not risking missing out on invaluable information that nobody else in the worlds can get ahold of. If you are in so much pain, then call for a healer!”

I looked down at the blood dripping to the floor below me.

“The healers are needed elsewhere, and you know it.”

“What I know is the worth of the enemy’s knowledge to my court, and how it far eclipses your minor repairs.”

“Riftrot.”

“You’ll be fine,” Eryn whispered, squeezing my hand.

Roq triggered Memory Drain.

“He had a penchant for black stone,” Roq said after a moment. “No, of cliffs. It seems a core memory of his revolves around great pillars of stone, surrounded by storms. So large there are entire cities inside them. Oh.”

“Is it Illadrox’s home world?”

“I—No. Oh, that’s a lot of killing.” Roq made a disgusted noise inside my head. “The worms made him to conquer it.”

Roq told us of a civilization of bug-like beings who had built their cities into the sides of huge black mesas with storm-filled chasms dividing them.

The Hive Mind empire had created winged minions to attack the cities from above, and Illadrox had joined the assault as a new variant. He was found to be good at killing, and even followed the defenders inside the cliff once the retreated.

“He served on two other planets, but after helping to conquer the third, he didn’t see another war,” Roq said. “But instead, he was… sleeping?”

“Dormancy,” Greenrot said. “Some variants gather self. Self creates wants. Wants interfere with purpose.”

“So variants can break free from the empire?”

“Impossible.”

“Then what’s the problem? Why put him to sleep?”

“Self increases cost of use. Inefficiencies in carrying out orders.”

“They feared him,” Roq said. “So they stored him until needed.”

“So the Empire stores the variants that grow too powerful to discard and only wakes them when it needs something special destroyed?”

“Correct.”

“Does that mean he has a soul gem inside?”

“Negative,” Greenrot said. “Soul trimmed during sleep, then new fragments injected. Prevents one soul taking over again.”

“Greenrot. Did you just admit to making a mistake, and that you shouldn’t have created variants with so much of one soul in them?”

“Correct. This Mind now sees the danger of doing so. Without the fragments, the wielder would never have succeeded.”

“I would have found a way to break free, no matter…” Roq began, then broke off. “The foul-smelling creature slept through Foraxia’s invasion.”

I let go of Eryn’s hand and put it on top of Roq’s head.

“He slept while the Hive ripped our people and cities apart,” Roq said. “And now they work him again?”

“Their loss,” I said, patting him.

Roq stopped talking long enough that I almost asked if Memory Drain had failed.

“Stone passages,” he said. “Breeding pits on every level. Storage halls stuffed with everything Hungerkeep stole from the territories it crossed. Cageheart’s Mass. Sleeping champions.”

“There are old variants in the dungeon,” Roq said. “Some are damaged. Some are undergoing changes. Others…”

“Have too much self,” Greenrot supplied.

“…are too powerful for cowardly worms to control,” Roq snarled.

He also found a memory showing the direction Hungerkeep had taken after leaving the territory around Hillock’s rift. Just as we had expected after what we’d seen riftside of Riverdam, the keep left immense tracks, discarded breeding residue, and stretches where every monster had been swallowed up, so the trail would have been difficult to miss anyway. But the memory confirmed its next target was Calamour.

Cageheart had ordered one army through Hillock’s rift, then traveled east across Noros with it. Illadrox had been left behind to destroy the city if possible and stop anyone from retaking the rift.

While the wandering keep was gone, plenty of breeding pits remained riftside. Those pits had produced the siege beasts, and from Illadrox’s memories, Roq estimated we had a few days before they could breed replacements.

After he had squeezed every useful memory he could from Illadrox, Roq restarted my healing.

With the current status of my party, we wouldn’t be going anywhere anytime soon. The soul items needed time to heal as well. But for the moment, a manned wall stood between us and the monsters, and we weren’t the ones who had to hold it.

I took Eryn’s hand once more as a healer worked on fixing her wounds.

* * *

About an hour later, Eryn was cleared to leave, and Roq had patched me up sufficiently that I walked without a limp. An ornate horse-drawn wagon was summoned for Mara, Eryn, and me, and we were whisked away through the city. Knut, Lan, and Nabeeh remained at the healing station for treatment.

“I have seen plenty of caravan horses and the fatter one Riverdam gave us, which you re-gifted to the refugees,” Roq said. “These are larger. Better fed. What silken coats they have.”

“A King does not eat a duke’s horses,” Arclight said.

“If they are not for eating, then why make them look so deliciously edible?” he complained, but dropped the topic.

While Eryn and I sat in silence, enjoying each other’s company and safety, Roq and Arclight spent most of the journey comparing Hillock to their old capital.

Hillock dwarfed Dawnwatch. Broad streets were lined with stone and wooden buildings. There were even walls that split the city into districts, so it could be defended separately. It was a beautiful city, with architecture that would have Ma rambling for hours and Pa grumbling they were “too pretty to be functional,” then stealing ideas for his next projects.

That beauty also made the damaged parts harder to ignore. There were collapsed roofs from where boulders had made it inside, and twice we passed areas where they were combating flames. And everywhere we looked were people gathered, cramped together in a city which had let thousands of monsters march straight past.

The castle sat on the highest ground inside the city, was protected by its own wall, and had enough armed guards to hold a small town. An ostentatiously dressed man met us in the courtyard and looked us over, taking in the blood, damaged armor, and weapons as if he wanted to tell us to go have a bath before stepping onto his floors.

Then he spotted Mara. His eyes widened, and he bowed so low a silver chain around his neck swung forward.

“Guild Official, what an unexpected honor. If we had known…”

“Take us to the duke,” Mara said. “Now.”

We met Duke Halvar, Guild Leader Merek, three high-ranking adventurers, and the captain of the guard in a sitting room larger than my childhood home. Halvar’s blue velvet coat pulled tight across his stomach, and his silver beard ended in a square beneath his chin. Merek’s shirt was ironed stiff.

The duke took both of Mara’s hands in his. “Hillock owes you its life.”

Mara let him finish before pointing to me.

“Not really. These are Adventurers Ash Aldrich and Eryn Whitcroft,” she said. “Adventurer Aldrich led the party that attacked the siege beasts and killed the leading variant. They are presently not affiliated with Central Command. I arrived, together with a group of Hillock adventurers, just in time to help with the retreat.”

Merek stared at me, then crossed the room and gripped my forearm. “If Hillock has it and you need it, ask.”

“We’ll get to that,” I said. “But first I would like to know how Hillock’s rift was lost.

Merek released my arm. Duke Halvar’s thumb found the edge of his signet ring and began turning it.

“The attacks had been worsening for months,” Halvar said. “Three weeks ago, they surged.”

“Any reason?” Mara asked.

“None we could find. We held, but every day cost more mana, bolts, and men. Every gem-gram said the other rifts were under pressure too. We did not know whether reinforcements would come.”

He spent several minutes explaining how difficult that uncertainty had been, and I wondered if he could make any decision without giving a speech first.

Merek cut in. “Then an army hit the fort. We could hold it, but not without bleeding ourselves dry. We triggered the traps and withdrew through the rift.”

“To the city walls,” Halvar said. “Which are stronger. It has worked before. Let the monsters follow us through, break themselves against Hillock, then retake the fort.”

“But they didn’t attack Hillock?” I said.

Merek’s jaw tightened. “They marched east. We sent guards and adventurers after them again and again. The monsters turned, swarmed each force, and drove them back inside.”

“So you just let them go?”

“We preserved our forces so we can hold the city,” Halvar said. “And we have called Central Command for relief, multiple times.”

“And got us.”

“More monsters came through while we waited, but most marched east, towards where the army would gather.” His fingers stopped on the ring. “But Illadrox stayed. He brought in the siege beasts, and gathered an army big enough to smash our defences if we don’t have walls.”

I still thought they had made the wrong choice, but I understood it. Their walls still stood.

Mara waited until the duke finished.

“And what of your losses?”

The captain answered before the duke could. “Forty guards dead. Near eighty wounded. We began with four hundred.”

“Twenty adventurers dead,” Merek added. “A hundred and forty remain, after we sponsored several scavenger breakthroughs.”

“We did everything we could,” Halvar said. “Kingsworth, Calamour, Riverdam. We sent warnings every day and received confirmation.”

I looked at Mara. “What did Riverdam say?”

“Nothing,” Merek said. “Only confirmation that our messages were received.”

“Riverdam’s gem-gram was smashed over a week ago,” I said. “And if you haven’t heard about their breached rift, that means the message never got sent. But who is confirming your messages then?”

Mara’s expression did not change.

“Finish your account,” she said. “Then Aldrich comes with me.”

“We will keep our forces behind Hillock’s walls until Kingsworth marches,” Halvar said. “When the army calls for us, we will ride out.”

Once there was a break in the meeting, Mara led me to the castle’s gem-gram room, leaving Eryn to visit their quartermaster for supplies.

The operator’s office was thrice the size of Sverre’s office back in Dawnwatch, with gems covering nearly every surface.

“Alright Aldrich, there’s something you need to know,” she said after ordering the operator out of the room.
