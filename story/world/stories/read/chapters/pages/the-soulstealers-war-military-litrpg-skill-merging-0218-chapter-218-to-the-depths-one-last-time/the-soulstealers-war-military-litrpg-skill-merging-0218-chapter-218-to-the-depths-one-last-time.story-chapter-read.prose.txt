With only three remaining lieutenants, our evacuation wasn’t orderly. Still, I helped where I could, packing our tools and supplies onto wagons, attaching horses to the carts, and helping fill wicker amphoras with the castle staff’s belongings. We loaded up all the scrap and in-progress armour onto a wagon-train for the blacksmiths, making sure nothing got left behind.

As we worked, I hunted for Habit. Even just a moment to try to gauge his intentions would be enough.

He was nowhere to be seen. His squad didn’t know where he was. It was a pressing concern, sure, but there was nothing I could do about it at the moment. Like Moonhand said, I would focus on what I could change.

By the time midnight arrived, I found Lord Ceonwin hurriedly giving orders to Sir Sidal and his messengers. We didn’t know the fate of Sir Aldhelm or any of the other thegns who had fought with us at Oschester, save for Sir Sidal.

I waited until Lord Ceonwin dismissed his men, then said, “My Lord, may I have a word?”

“Yes, Levi?”

“We were expecting to have a few more weeks to run the Labyrinth. However, if we’re leaving, we won’t get another run…” I wouldn’t get a chance to attack the Keeper Topaz Dragon properly. We may not have been ready, but we had to try. “Sir, if you let us, our squad needs one last ingredient to complete our armour. We could delve into the Labyrinth and take down one last monster, then emerge as quickly as we could and rush to join you.”

He narrowed his eyes, then nodded quickly. “Agreed. With what is likely to transpire, you will need all the strength you can get. But don’t take too long. The last thing you want is to face the approaching orc horde alone. You have until sunrise.”

“Yes, sir. Thank you sir.”

“Gather your equipment and go. We will leave your squad’s horses in the stable.”

Frythlid had been sifting through reports all afternoon. The front line was crumbling in the south, and orcs were pouring across the Thelea River at multiple crossings, unopposed. She had too many problems to deal with. Although she was no commander, as a middle-ranking Subtle Man, her main duty was to act as a messenger between the battalions in times of crisis. It was a glorious, honourable role, and a great act of service to the Warlord.

Her nation would thank her for this.

At the moment, she had a mobile headquarters set up in the hills near Castle Urcia. A circle of wagons and lower-ranking Subtle Men scurried about, preparing weapons and readying their wagons to evacuate.

Frythlid herself was busy stacking up her notes of all the reports she’d received tonight and in the prior days. She’d been hoping to find clarity in the conflicting messages, but with such limited time, she would receive no such luxury.

As she hoisted the stack of parchment onto the back of the wagon, a Subtle Man guard shouted, “My lady! We have a visitor!”

The guard hauled a Dupe toward the center of the camp, holding him under his arm. She didn’t recognize him, and he had no defining features. It didn’t matter.

“What is your identification number, Duplicate?” she demanded. At the same time, she triggered her Art, analyzing the Dupe’s mind. Truth and lies became obvious ripples in the Path. His memories rippled in the back of her mind as disturbed water.

“D5982,” the soldier replied stoically, his expression plain.

“He is called Habit by his companions,” the guard added.

“That name means nothing to me,” D5982 said. “I am yours to command. I am a number among the Warlord’s ranks.”

“Excellent. Your number is all I need. Why are you here? Is Lord Ceonwin not evacuating the castle?”

The Duplicate swallowed. “My lady, I have reason to believe that Sir Levi is conspiring to commit treason against the Warlord.”

Sir Levi. D5333. D5333 was a thorn in her side, but he’d proven himself harmless in the multiple interrogations she’d put him through.

Still, he had always been suspicious. His rise to thegnage was a peculiarity, but she was in no position to contest the princess’ orders. No matter how foolish that princess may have been.

“How dangerous is the threat?”

“He seeks to become an Ealdorman. He is directly opposing Lord Hinlaw, and plans to kill him. I suspect he plans to attack the Warlord after.”

Frythlid shut her eyes. She examined his memories. D5982 hadn’t seen who D5333 was speaking with, but he had heard someone whisper, “If you do your duty and listen to my wisdom, Hinlaw shall die. Then, next, the Warlord.”

Then D5333 had stepped out of the shadows.

It was as credible of a threat as any. Though D5333 was still a tiny fish in a provincial well, he was becoming more and more of a threat by the day. This could not stand, and would warrant a disproportionate response to snuff him out.

“You are certain of this?” Frythlid asked. “If our report is true, we will be rewarded. If it is not…we will be hanged on the suspicion of the very charges you are bringing against D5333.”

D5982 hung his head. “I know what I saw.”

Frythlid stared intently at him. He believed he was telling the truth. He believed what he had seen.

There was no thought of individuality inside him. His training had broken that part of him. He only saw a duty to the Warlord, their creator.

If this was a true threat to the Warlord, Frythlid had a duty. Her service to the nation was more important than any potential accusations of being a traitor. If she did nothing and D5982 was correct, the Warlord would die.

She glanced at the Subtle Man who held D5982’s arm. “Release him, and get me two flash-ravens.”

In the chaos of the evacuation, no one gave a second thought to me or my squad gathering our equipment and rushing to the Labyrinth entrance.

We delved down alone. Inside the caverns, it was painfully quiet, and it felt odd to be the only squad down here.

When we reached the elevator, we had to belay ourselves. We dragged the ropes down to the dish, then Brawl and I slowly released them, letting the elevator slide down to the depths of the second level.

“It’s almost like the monsters know something’s coming, too,” Sage breathed.

“It’s your imagination,” Anchor said. “It’s just quiet because there’s no other squad.”

“It doesn’t feel that way,” I muttered. Our elevator reached the bottom. “It feels like the last day of high school, wandering the empty hallways late in the afternoon as you get ready to move on to the next phase of your life. But I don’t think you guys really understand that feeling, do you?”

Anchor and Sage shook their heads.

“It is likely the last time you will see this Labyrinth, brother,” Brawl said.

I shrugged. “Perhaps—”

“You’ll have a throne in Steelshore soon, dustbrain,” Sage said.

“Don’t jinx it,” I replied. “Let’s make this quick. We have a dragon to slay.”

We stepped off the elevator, then raced through the depths of the city until we reached the plaza that hosted the Keeper Topaz dragon. It may have just been luck, but along the way, we only encountered a pair of skeletons.

When we arrived, I stopped at the edge of the plaza. The dragon was curled up in the center, its skeletal wings folded over its crystalline back legs. Its nostrils flared as it snored.

“We’ve got a chance to hurt it as it sleeps,” I said.

“That is a little slimy, is it not?” Brawl asked.

“We don’t have time to worry about giving monsters a fighting chance,” I said. “Stick with me, and stick close. We’ll have to hit it hard, and hit it fast.”

When it came to piercing a Gold’s armour, my resonance Skill wouldn’t cut it. It was still effectively rated for Titaniums. We’d have to break the armour first.

That was my job and Brawl’s. His resonance Skill was Advanced-tier, now, but it was still meant to go against other Silvers. Not a Gold, with the massive attribute difference between us. It wouldn’t pierce. (While the dragon didn’t have direct numbers and a connection to the System, it had an equivalent strength to a regular Gold, otherwise it wouldn’t be projecting a Gold’s pressure. That meant it had similar attributes.)

Anchor, though, had a resonance Skill rated for Golds—Expert tier. It was still limited by him being a Silver, but it was the best we had for piercing.

“Brawl, stay by my side,” I whispered. “We’re going for its throat. Hit it hard, then duck and let Anchor put an arrow in its weak spot.”

We approached as a squad, walking shoulder to shoulder. Our feet glided over the the paving stones of the underground plaza, and we barely breathed.

When we were within striking distance, I held up my fingers. Starting with three, I counted down. Brawl reached up, preparing to strike. His Presence shuddered as he prepared to use a resonance Skill.

Finally, he slammed his flail down. I added a gravity well around it, pulling it down faster and harder. It impacted the sleeping dragon’s scales with a boom that could rival thunder. Crystal dust flew off the impact point, choking me and creating a fog of orange mist around me.

I rammed my spear into the same point a moment later, for what good it would do. The power of the impact would help damage the amour—that much, I guaranteed.

Before the dust cleared, an arrow cracked over my shoulder, booming through the air with the speed it travelled. When it struck its target, it collided with a satisfying thwack.

I couldn’t see the result. As the dust cleared, I backed up. The ground shuddered, and the dragon rumbled. It used its wings to help push itself up, and a hazy shadow of it loomed beyond the cloud of dust.

Limbs shuddering, the dragon took a step forward. I waited for it to collapse, but every second, it grew more and more steady on its feet.

“Get back!” I yelled, and we moved away from the dragon, pushing ourselves back across the plaza.

When the dust cleared, the dragon’s eyes locked with us. Its throat rumbled, and it tilted its head as if it recognized us.

Maybe it did.

We’d left a patch of damaged crystal on the side of its neck, though. Anchor’s arrow had penetrated in a few inches, and still hung there. It hadn’t done any fatal damage.

It wasn’t quite strong enough to pierce anything important.

Still, it just needed a little nudge, and we’d have defeated a Gold.

“Sarge, what’s the plan?” Anchor hissed.

“You get your wish,” I told Brawl. “We’re going to fight it toe-to-toe, it seems.”
