“No matter what you offer, I’m not lowering the price. The production costs are enormous.” Jinyo lied with a straight face. True, raw materials like Celestial Earth Fertilizer required ingredients worth millions. But if he could sell Exploding Potatoes at this price every month, he’d cover all expenses and still turn a massive profit.

“You’ll definitely want this. It’s information that matters to you—and everyone close to you.”

“???”

“The thing is, a few days ago, the 8th-ranked core disciple of the Venomous Fang Sect vanished without a trace. Nobody knows where he is. Most assume he’s already dead. The Sect Master is furious and has issued investigation orders. In case you’ve forgotten, the Venomous Fang Sect is a demon sect with its headquarters in the neighboring city.”

“And what does that have to do with me? I’m minding my own business.”

“Oh, that’s exactly the point. Because the 8th-ranked core disciple of the Venomous Fang Sect was blinded by someone. And before he disappeared, he loudly declared he was going to burn his rival’s mansion to the ground.”

“…”

“Sounds familiar, doesn’t it? That’s right, my dear friend. The person you blinded is the 8th-ranked disciple of a demon sect master. Exciting, isn’t it?”

“Exciting my ass! How could a demon sect master’s core disciple have such pathetic skills?” Daikyo’s name had never even registered in Jinyo’s mind. Jinyo, with less than a thousand units of planar, had defeated Daikyo as easily as flipping his palm over. It was almost too absurd—that he’d stumbled right into a hornets’ nest by pure accident.

Demon sects were schools that practiced dark or unorthodox techniques. In the modern era, the old distinctions between righteous and unrighteous martial arts had been abolished from official doctrine. So long as they operated within the same legal framework, “demon sect” was merely a label. Still, the fact remained that most criminals happened to practice demon sect techniques—because those arts tended to be more lethal, more bloodthirsty, and more prone to corrupting the practitioner’s mind.

Demon sects were steeped in assassination and violence. Their disciples were taught with less emphasis on morality and empathy than those of other schools. In the current era, the truly evil demon sects had been eradicated. Those that remained were the ones that had adapted to globalization. But being marked by a demon sect was no small matter. They may have stopped committing brazen crimes like in the old days, but breaking the law while evading the authorities? That was still very much alive.

“I didn’t kill him. And we haven’t crossed paths since.”

“Who knows! In their eyes, Daikyo came to take revenge and then vanished. They simply assume you killed him. Sure, there’s no evidence you did—but there’s no evidence to clear your name either. Tell me, do you have an alibi for four days ago?”

“No. None.” Four days ago, Jinyo had been consumed with his territory upgrade quest. He had no alibi whatsoever—nothing to argue with, and no need to either. Jinyo was innocent. He had never killed another human being in his life. No matter how little he thought of Daikyo, the man was just a stranger he’d had no prior grudge against. Jinyo had no intention of getting Daikyo killed, nor any desire to pick a fight with a demon sect.

“Nice gift, right?”

“So nice I’d love to return it twofold.”

“No thanks—I’ll pass. Life’s busy enough trying to make money. The last thing I need is being targeted by a demon sect master. The future-millionaire lifestyle I’m cultivating doesn’t call for that level of excitement. Now, a VIP seat at the edge of the arena to watch you squirm? That I’d find interesting. Especially the look on your face when you’re losing—it’d make my champagne taste ten times better.”

“William…”

“Oh, sorry—lost the battle against the voice in my head for a moment there. What were we talking about? Right, yes—the deal. The deal.” William propped his chin on one hand with theatrical thoughtfulness. A knife-edged smile surfaced in his fox-like eyes. Sharp enough to tilt every line on the negotiation table squarely in his favor—not Jinyo’s. It struck Jinyo then, with piercing clarity, that he was not the one holding the better hand. Worse still, he’d invited the devil through his own front door. He sometimes forgot that William wasn’t some noble, trustworthy friend—but a middleman merchant who was simultaneously the ally and adversary of every farmer in the world.

“I could help you, you know. Make it so the demon sect drops the matter entirely. You and your family would be safe. But there’s nothing in it for me, so… pity.”

“If you’re angling for the Exploding Potato cultivation method, I’m not telling.”

“My, you saw right through me. But this is a matter of life and death, you know. The sect master could send assassins—or dispatch another ranked disciple to avenge their junior. People marked by demon sects never live easy lives. You know that, Jinyo Southerncross. Tell me what I want to know, and we stay allies. I’ll make your demon sect problem disappear.”

If Jinyo weighed William’s proposal on pure logic, it wasn’t a bad deal at all. Just hand over the method and escape the crisis barreling toward him. But Jinyo would sooner die than reveal it. Even if he did, William wouldn’t be able to replicate it anyway. Jinyo himself still couldn’t make any headway growing Exploding Potatoes outside the Garden of the Sword. He racked his brain for a way to squeeze out maximum leverage from this negotiation.

“If I die, nobody will ever sell you Exploding Potatoes of this quality again. Is that really what you want?”

“They’re just Exploding Potatoes. Regrettable, sure—but I won’t lose sleep over it.” William didn’t flinch.

‘That didn’t work. What now? This isn’t going the way I planned.’ Jinyo’s mind raced for an exit. His hand was weaker than William’s. William had nothing but confidence—he stood to lose nothing at this table. A merchant always calculates the most profitable outcome. The profit from Exploding Potatoes was real money, certainly—but it wasn’t worth the risk of making an enemy of a demon sect master.

‘The profit I make from buying and selling your Exploding Potatoes is just the tip of the iceberg compared to what I’d gain if I had the production method. I’m not going to stake my neck on those petty margins, genius botanist.’ William was waiting for Jinyo to fall into his trap. He had to corner Jinyo, drive him into a pit so deep he could never climb out. No matter which way Jinyo turned, it was his own fault for inviting the ravenous beast through the front gate.

“I can’t tell you,” Jinyo said.

“No matter what, I can’t tell you. Telling you would be pointless. Pressuring me for the method is worthless.” No lies. No tricks. So blunt and direct that William’s expression actually shifted.

“You’re just going to fold? Not an ounce of creativity at the negotiation table!”

“I’m telling the truth. I can’t share it. You want to rob the gold from my hands, but I never had any gold to begin with.”

“Says the man sitting on a golden goose. That’s truly amusing.”

“This particular golden goose happens to be rather temperamental and mysterious. So what’ll it be? Do you want to do business with me, or let the demon sect kill me and watch your chance at wealth slip right through your fingers? That’s not how you want this to end, is it?”

“Hmm…” William had invested in expensive goods and traveled all the way here to find Jinyo. Going home empty-handed was not in his vocabulary. This William simply could not spell the word ‘failure.’

“I have one final option. If you promise to sell Exploding Potatoes exclusively to me—no other merchants—at a purchase price of 300,000 per 100 kilograms, same as today, I’ll buy you time to prepare. By then, you should be strong enough to handle the demon sect on your own, Father of the Genius Swordsman.” The last rope William tossed down for Jinyo to grab was a repulsive trade monopoly. The first batch of high-quality Exploding Potatoes already released to market served as advertising—a magnet that would draw wealthy merchants to Jinyo, all routing through William. Future purchase prices wouldn’t stay at 300,000 to 350,000. By William’s calculations, he’d be ready to adjust prices to 400,000 to 500,000 down the line—provided Jinyo’s produce kept improving in quality.

“Isn’t that a bit excessive? My produce is far superior to anything on the market, but you’re only willing to pay market rates. That’s highway robbery. Where am I supposed to turn a profit?”

“Come now—I’m offering to extend your life expectancy. Besides, if you can’t survive this, you can’t be a long-term partner for me, can you? …Don’t you think that makes things more interesting?” William dealt out a test nobody wanted. He was the sort of man with a rotten disposition who relished watching others suffer—who got a thrill from witnessing lives turned upside down. Jinyo was stuck in an impossible position, nothing going as he’d planned.

“Not once—not even by a fraction.” He couldn’t reveal the cultivation method. Accepting the deal meant being disadvantaged in the long run. The other option was to reject both and seek out a different middleman who’d offer better terms.

But would there really be anyone who’d give him a better deal than William?

‘No. Absolutely not.’ Even William, who he’d known for a while, had fangs that dragged along the ground—peeling away Jinyo’s skin until only bare muscle remained. Try negotiating with the pack of demons out there, ready to devour Jinyo until not even bone was left? The future would be just as bleak.

“This cell phone has the number of one of my mother’s acquaintances in the World Government.” Jinyo placed the phone on the table. William’s gaze locked onto the outdated model—a relic from decades past. He was genuinely surprised; he hadn’t expected Jinyo to carry something so ancient everywhere he went. Or was this part of his preparation? It was fascinating to see how Jinyo would wriggle free from his grip.

“What’s this? Going to go cry to Mommy’s friends?”

“Something like that. And besides, I have Rina—who works for the World Government. One word from me, and she’ll move heaven and earth to help. At the very least, the demon sect master wouldn’t dare lay a finger on me.”

“And you think that’ll work? World Government officers don’t intervene in petty disputes in small towns like this. They have their own ironclad regulations. And would that young lady really be willing to help you?” William smirked. Jinyo was like a bird being shoved into a cage, frantically trying to fly free. The World Government only intervened in matters of significance. Their officers didn’t have personal freedom—they were bound by strict rules and even stricter enforcers. Violations meant severe punishment.

“Rina will help me. She’ll throw away everything and come handle this, even if it means being punished herself. And when that happens, your value drops to zero—because you couldn’t protect me. I’d have no reason to do business with you anymore.”

“…” The smile vanished from William’s face. He leaned in close to Jinyo and whispered:

“What makes you so certain she’ll help you?”

“Because she loves me.”

“Oh… that stings, right in the heart.” William clutched his chest. Twenty-eight years of single life cried out in anguish. Jinyo’s expression didn’t waver, but inside, he was drowning in guilt. He dug his nails into his own flesh to keep his composure from cracking. One card had disappeared from William’s hand. If Jinyo used his mother’s contact to reach someone in the World Government, he could escape the demon sect’s clutches. And even if that plan fell through, he had Rina as his trump card.

Rina would absolutely come to his aid. Jinyo was certain of it. But he didn’t want to drag her into a mess he’d gotten himself into. Taking responsibility for his own actions—that was the way of the gentleman Jinyo aspired to be.

“Sigh… fine, I understand. Here’s the deal: you sell Exploding Potatoes exclusively to me. No contacting anyone else. I’ll purchase based on quality and quantity, referencing the market price at the time. Agreed?”

“And you’ll also help conceal my identity. Keep the demon sect from finding me for as long as possible—months, if you can manage it.” Jinyo needed time. He had no desire to clash with the demon sect, especially not now when he lacked both connections and power. The only strength he could lean on was Rina’s—and hers came with the constraints of her position. And there was still the matter of Norn, who still needed help. In the end, he had no choice but to rely on his own growing strength.

“Understood. But I’ll warn you now—even with my connections, I don’t know how long I can stall them. Demon sect people don’t operate on normal human logic. They don’t even need reasons. But… that’s what makes them fascinating.” William laughed. The pressuring expression he’d worn was, in truth, rather entertaining. He pulled out a sheet of paper and began drafting a contract.

The terms were as follows: Jinyo would supply William with 1,500 kilograms of consistent-quality Exploding Potatoes per month. The minimum purchase price would be 300,000 per 100 kilograms, fluctuating based on quality and market demand. Jinyo was prohibited from selling Exploding Potatoes to any other middleman—William’s agency would hold exclusive distribution rights.

In return, William would be responsible for Jinyo’s security infrastructure and the protection of his personal information. He would handle all distribution and export to maximize profit. Additionally, William would pay Jinyo a 10% bonus on total revenue whenever seasonal sales exceeded 20 million.

William left the gift basket behind, humming a tune as he kicked dust on his way out of the mansion—as elated as if he’d just closed a hundred-million deal. Jinyo, on the other hand, let out a long, exhausted breath. Not only had the Exploding Potato sale gone poorly, he’d been locked into an exclusive middleman arrangement. And on top of that, he now had to worry about a demon sect coming after him for a crime he never committed. William had come prepared, and Jinyo had been led by the nose from start to finish.

But there was one thing William had overlooked.

No—it was something Jinyo had kept hidden so well that no one could have seen it.

He still had two more cards up his sleeve.

Jinyo’s true ace wasn’t the Exploding Potato. Exploding Potatoes were the crop with the lowest medicinal value in his garden. High volume, yes—but difficult to sell due to the market’s limited understanding of them. The product Jinyo planned to use as the ladder to the Southerncross family’s success wasn’t potatoes at all. It was Immortal Rice and Happy Carrots—crops that, while easier to grow, yielded far less per harvest. But they possessed medicinal properties the market craved, backed by high demand.

And then there was Immortal Rice—something no one in recorded history had ever discovered before.

“Oh, William… if you hadn’t played me, I might have actually considered you a trustworthy ally.”

Whether the two of them would become reliable, long-term partners—only time would tell.
