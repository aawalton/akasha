Ferris paused. How did Serena expect him to ‘cheat’ while building Acid Resistance?

She’d stopped cutting the carrots, to Jade’s dismay, and reached into her sack. She pulled a long metal rod out of it, which Ferris immediately recognized as step #1 from the jade slip.

“The correct method is to learn all the spiritual forging techniques to create everything you need from qi. The shortcut is to have someone else make as much as possible in the real world, and then transfer it into your soul.”

She set the metal rod on the table, then pulled a couple of other things out of the sack. There were different large metal balls, some joints and brackets, as well as more rods of various lengths.

“Later on, this doesn’t work. Some things can only be forged in the soul, and some parts need to be made from your own qi. Still, this is much faster. I will warn you,” she said, picking the knife back up and pointing it at him, “the crafters are all warned to reject young Adepts asking for custom-made parts. I was able to get these, and the rest you need, because Elder Harper allowed it. It still won’t be free, as you need to pay for the forging and then transfer it all into your soul, but it won’t cost qi at least.”

She returned to chopping carrots and feeding Jade, who rippled in happiness.

“The only real cost in qi should be gluing the parts together. That is the spiritual technique I mentioned you would need, and I can give it to you along with the forged components.”

“How much will it all cost?” Ferris asked.

“Let’s see,” Serena said, “the merit store charges a base price of three points for resources with magical potential between 16 and 31, six points for 32 through 63, twelve points for 64 through 127, and twenty-four for anything else up to 255, and then you get into tier two territory after that. Of course, harder-to-find types or special properties raise the price. Luckily, the two primary resources for Acid Resistance are pretty common and should cost six merit points each.”

“What about anything with less than 16 mp?” Ferris asked. He bet things like the berries from the slime ranch fell into this category.

“They are usually sold in bulk,” Serena shrugged. “Depends on the item and its magical potential. Something with only a couple mp is barely magical and not worth much, but 15 mp would still sell well. There is a qualitative change that happens at certain levels, which is why they are more expensive.”

“Then, you will need to pay for the mundane materials and forging,” she waved at the metal components on the table, “here you are in luck. They don’t require an Adept to create, so the cost is lower. The clan charges three merit points for the entire lot of it, which is a steal compared to the qi you would spend creating it yourself. Honestly, three merit points is overcharging for the mundane work and materials, but they want to discourage abuse and set a minimum price, on top of requiring special permission.”

Ferris was once again struck by the gulf between Adepts and mundane people. Three merit points represented just three days of work for him, and it would purchase him everything to make the large construction he had seen in the jade slip. He could understand the base metal ore being cheap, as mundane resources like that could often be found in bulk in low-tier rifts. The problem was that the work involved processing and forging; much more work and expertise than Ferris’ three days of effort would equal. Yet Serena called it ‘overcharging.’

There just weren’t enough Adepts. The nature of life in the bastions made them the sole providers of resources and food, which left the rest of the population with more time to pursue other crafts. There was no room for slow farming, so magic was needed to grow crops faster. There were no safe natural resources inside the small area of the bastion, so delvers needed to go out and collect things like wood or ores. That left a large part of the population unable to help with basic survival. Additionally, delving was dangerous, and many would die—although cultivators did gain a longer life span if they could survive the dangers.

“Then,” Serena continued, unaware of Ferris’ musings, “we will need to transfer both the magical resources and the pre-built components to your soul. There are many techniques that can do this, but all of them require some setup or ability in the soul. The quickest way to move bulk goods of this amount is to rent a spiritual device from the clan. They cost one merit point per hour, but you shouldn’t need it longer than that. Of course, if you have your own way to get things into your soul, then that is preferred.”

Ferris wondered if the Consume Pool could be modified to do so. Probably not anytime soon, but the concept felt related enough.

The ability made everything appear comically larger, and then fall into acid. What would happen if Jade ate a bar of metal, then Ferris somehow caught the copy before it dissolved? He suspected the yellow scarab had somehow fully moved into the slimespace this way…

If he figured it out, would he then have a massive bar of metal, or would it be weaker in some way? Cultivation broke conventional rules, but the first tier had limits. There had to be a cost to the size increase.

At least for this purchase, he needed to rent the spiritual device, bringing his required total to sixteen merit points.

“Once it is all in your soul, you construct the building as described in the technical manual and place the pattern stone and magical resources inside it. Unfortunately, after that is a waiting game,” she finished cutting the last of the carrots and tossed them to Jade. “Your soul has everything it needs for the ability, but you need to wait and see if your Core Tree will produce a matching fruit. If it takes too long, then the advice is to add other resources, and perhaps re-construct the building.”

Ferris had begun to understand this was the heart of how abilities were shared between cultivators. The Core Tree would typically give abilities that a soul could already support or had the resources to build. This wasn’t a hard rule, but it was uncommon to be given seeds that the soul had no way to build into abilities. Ferris had seen this in the simple nature of Grow and Jump—just plant a seed and provide qi—compared to the complexity of Assimilate Object, which appeared after Jade had bonded.

If Ferris had already constructed the building, then he would more likely get the seed that matched it.

But then, there was also the pattern stone. The instructions from Acid Resistance Ability Construction claimed it held a ‘resonance’ of the ability. How was that created, and why did having a resonance in his soulspace affect the Core Tree?

The magical items apparently had a type or nature that had to match. How did they know what kind of resources worked? Were they required, or did they just make it more likely?

For Ferris and Jade specifically, he had no idea how Adaptability would change this, as he didn’t fully understand the ability. In fact, it worried him, as Jade had no reason to adapt an ‘acid-resistant’ ability.

“One more thing,” she said, “the alternatives are useful if you can’t find the primary item, and sometimes they are even preferred. There would be a note if an alternative was significantly better. But,” she added a dramatic pause, “you can improve your odds by collecting them all, instead of just the primary items. Duplicates don’t really help, but any additional item—listed or not—that shares the nature can help.”

Ferris snorted. Better odds at the low price of thirty-six more merit—more if the alternative items were uncommon. Still, it was good to know, and Ferris could try saving up for more of them over time if he got stuck.

Serena noticed his response to the idea, but thankfully ignored it. “Any other questions?” She asked, instead.

“Yes,” Ferris said, “it is about the concepts behind Acid Resistance. I understand how sacrifice applies, but I am confused by connectivity. My, admittedly recent, understanding of the chemical reactions caused by acid doesn’t directly pull things apart.”

Serena looked up for a second, then back at Ferris.

“What did it say about connectivity, specifically?”

“It said it follows the ideal of stability through cohesion and unity,” Ferris recited. It had taken a moment to recall, but he had read it several times just that day.

“Think of it this way,” she said, “the best defense against romantic suitors is to already be married.” Serena looked off to the side, “Though, that metaphor only goes so far.”

“So…” Ferris said, a bit thrown, “the idea is to reject the bond by already having one?”

“Now you get it. You aren’t holding things together, you are connecting them. Sometimes it doesn’t even matter about the strength of the connection, just that something already exists. The structure of the pyramid matters here. Alright, speaking of bonds,” Serena said as she picked up one of the metal rods from the table, “let’s review how this stuff all goes together.”

They spent the rest of the time discussing the construction plan for Acid Resistance’s strange half-sphere porcupine-like structure. The rods, balls, and other parts were designed to be assembled together in different ways. Serena showed him a couple of them, as well as discussing the overall design.

The ‘connectively’ of the ability was represented by two connected metal frames of triangles. Each triangle had three large metal balls as points, connected to each other with rods. Then, each triangle had another point added further back inside the dome to form a pyramid. Inside the half-sphere, these points were once again all connected to form more triangles. Altogether, this created two layers of metal framing that formed the walls of the ability’s structure.

Sticking out through the center of each triangle was a metal rod with another ball on its point. Inside the half-sphere, these rods connected to another smaller structure, which held them in place. It even had special holsters built to release the rods after a certain amount of force was applied.

“One thing to note,” Serena explained, “the sacrificial rods don’t magically come back. You have to make new ones to replace them.”

“But…” Ferris said, “I won’t be able to, as I will have never learned how.”

“At that point,” Serena shrugged, “you will already have the ability, so you have time to learn.”

She leaned in closer, and lowered her voice.

“In fact, there are a couple of known ways to improve the ability. One is with a magical item called a qi intent forge. Find one of those in a rift, or just purchase it, and then integrate it into the structure. Afterwards, the rods will magically replace themselves. Of course, they still need qi each time.”

Ferris considered the idea. He knew that abilities could grow and be improved, especially with resources from the rifts. The technical manual for Acid Resistance didn’t mention this at all.

“Thank you for the hint,” Ferris said with a small bow of his head.

Serena leaned back and waved her hand.

Ferris couldn’t help but contrast the Acid Resistance’s instructions with the information from the fruit for Assimilate Object. The Core Tree’s fruit had given Ferris general instructions of intent and form, while the plans for Acid Resistance were much more detailed and specific. He didn’t know if it was related to the ability, or if both had a lot of flexibility. Perhaps the plans for Acid Resistance were just optimized by the clan. He would learn more once his Core Tree produced the fruit for the Acid Resistance.

As Serena described the different parts, she talked about how they related to the concepts listed at the front of the book on Acid Resistance: connectivity and sacrifice. Ferris realized that he had a strong conceptual source of connectivity in the oath statue. What was the oath, if not something that bound things together? Serena described the metal rods as providing connections between the large balls. Ferris thought he could place the oath statue inside the metal frame and then feed a white chain up and into each ball. This would, conceptually, strengthen Acid Resistance.

Only… Ferris didn’t like what the idea would imply. The ‘connectivity’ aspect of Acid Resistance was trying to keep his body together. Feeding the white chains into the metal balls would be feeding them into something that represented him. That was exactly what he was trying to avoid, and why he liked the original idea of using it on Jade’s assimilated organs. There was a degree of separation there, and he preferred the concept that the Assimilate Object ability had of exerting control on the objects inside the ability. Ferris wanted to control the oath, not the other way around.

Eventually, the scheduled time ran out. Their next meeting would mark one month into the research project. Ferris and Serena would have to report to Elder Harper on their progress. They spent a few minutes discussing how they would make the report, then ended the meeting.
