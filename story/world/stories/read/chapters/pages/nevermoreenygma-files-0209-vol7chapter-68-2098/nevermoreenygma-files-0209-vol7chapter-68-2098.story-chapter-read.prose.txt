Chapter 68

2098

Of all the summers of her life, that one could be said to have been the coldest.

It was a direct consequence of the main portal. The closer one got to the epicenter of the dimensional disturbance, the harsher the cold became. Even on a global scale, average temperatures had dropped by several degrees since the beginning of the conflict. That, too, had become one of humanity's greatest fears: a worldwide decrease of ten degrees could bring consequences almost as devastating as the Fractus themselves.

By the time May and June arrived, most of the snow had melted, yet the days remained cold. Only around midday did temperatures climb to a comfortable twenty-five degrees Celsius, while at night they could plunge to ten degrees below zero—or even lower—in many regions. In fact, the Arctic summer had become far more pleasant than the battlefields of Siberia. That explained why blankets and winter gear had become commonplace during a season that, in previous years, had been defined by heat waves and swarms of insects across the tundra and around the countless lakes.

Yet, beyond the cold, the skirmishes and battles against the Fractus never ceased.

The situation worsened toward the end of July, when the squad was deployed to Novosibirsk. The southern outskirts had become a patchwork of military formations, with countless squadrons and units operating side by side.

The city itself—a vast expanse of industrial districts and residential neighborhoods built over the previous twenty years—had descended into absolute chaos.

An invasion of Clyx, Griven, and Aurex Fractus was spreading rapidly, and the pattern of their advance was anything but random. Their collective movements resembled those of an enormous insect swarm, coordinated with the efficiency of an ant colony, allowing them to occupy streets, buildings, and open ground with unsettling precision. The Fractus had, in every sense, replaced the insects of summer. Ground units poured through barricades and collapsed structures without slowing their advance, while every movement of the airborne variants seemed to probe the density and vulnerability of the defenders' positions.

Amid the chaos, operations of every kind continued around the clock. Joint offensives were carried out day and night, while military camps repeatedly entered high alert whenever Fractus managed to approach their perimeter.

By then, everyone had grown accustomed to sleeping poorly. One small relief came with the arrival of reinforcement units composed of androids and combat mechs manufactured through Russo-Japanese industrial cooperation. The use of fractium had drastically reduced production costs, making large-scale deployment increasingly feasible. Experiments in biomechanization were already underway, but heavy mechanized units remained the preferred choice for front-line operations, despite their lower mobility. For all the technological advances achieved during the war, biological soldiers equipped with powered exoskeletons continued to be the backbone of every major offensive.

Flesh, steel, carbon fiber, and nanopolymers had kept the Fractus at bay until then, and military planners hoped that would remain the case, even as the production of androids and mechanized units accelerated month after month.

Stepnye Volki was merely one small squad among the forces assembled there. As preparations neared completion, the order was finally issued: the operation to reclaim Novosibirsk and eradicate every remaining Fractus within the city will begun the following morning.

The snow-leopard fey commanding the larger Silver Fang unit—and an old friend of Natalya's—proposed dividing the forces into three groups. One would carve safe corridors through the streets. Another would contain and slow the Fractus' direct advance. The third, led by Stepnye Volki, would locate and mark the generation nodes, forcing the creatures into designated kill zones where several ships waited to sweep them with railguns. The operation required assault teams to descend deep into the city's underground network, which had become just as heavily infested as the surface. Dangerous or not, there was no alternative.

Together with another group of mages, Jiho summoned echo elementals through enchanted origami, raising powerful gusts of wind that cleared rubble and swept fractium particles from the air. The airborne Aurex relied on those microscopic fragments as a vast detection network, and dispersing them opened routes toward the northern industrial complexes, where intelligence believed the largest concentrations of Fractus were gathering.

The advance was slow and exhausting, lasting two full days, but it was steady—and, remarkably, free of fatalities. Aside from a handful of lost limbs, one gunship destroyed by a swarm of Aurex, and numerous damaged exoskeletons, the assault continued uninterrupted, clearing nearly five kilometers of urban terrain.

Ludovic held the central line of the third group while Sasha remained behind the front, supervising the deployment of heavy artillery and continuously adjusting the electric cannons according to the density readings Mai collected alongside a small reconnaissance team.

As they approached the northern factories compasses started to work oddly, and Mai sensed something unusual too.

Between the chaotic movements of the Fractus, the readings from drones and surveillance aircraft, and her own moth-like senses, she detected a distortion that disrupted her echolocation. It was from within that disturbance that new waves of Fractus emerged with unnerving precision. Reconnaissance drones were dispatched, fearing the presence of another portal, but none was found. Instead, they discovered something equally troubling: railgun fire and every other heavy weapon aimed at that location had almost no effect.

It was not the result of conscious control, but rather a form of gravitational resonance that diverted attacks aimed at the central structures within the industrial complex. Electrical discharges from railguns and fractium rifles dissipated harmlessly into the ground before reaching their targets. It was as though the energy itself instinctively shielded the horde, allowing the Fractus to operate with near-perfect efficiency.

The flanking strategy appeared to be working... until another Fractus variant entered the battlefield.

The Orbiter.

At first for someone else, it seemed like a disaster waiting to happen.

But instead of that, it was the Fractus themselves who began to scatter.

It was one of the strangest variants ever recorded—a hemispherical organism that remained at extremely high altitude and never descended to the ground. Yet wherever it drifted, it left behind an invisible trail across the landscape. Ground-based Fractus appeared to hate—or perhaps fear—it, never crossing any area over which an Orbiter had passed.

Some scientists theorized that the Orbiter possessed legs impossible to perceive within three-dimensional space, yet perfectly visible to the Fractus themselves, causing them to instinctively avoid any terrain touched by its passage. While air units focused their efforts on intercepting the Orbiter, ground forces and droid units were forced to adapt their plans on the fly to accommodate the creatures' rapidly changing behavior.

Ludovic temporarily joined a larger unit tasked with repositioning the heavy weapons. He was ordered to establish defensive lines at carefully calculated angles, allowing projectiles deflected by the gravitational field surrounding the energy source to be redirected through improvised barriers built from the architecture of the industrial complex and the surrounding buildings.

Jiho, working alongside another unit of mages, led the diversion operation, using magic to draw the Fractus' attention.

Natalya struck from the flanks with her team, taking advantage of her speed and the cover provided by dust and smoke stirred up by the wind. She sliced through Griven filaments while distracting the Aurex circling overhead with newly developed cutting weapons. Made from fractium and electrically charged, these nanofilament projectiles unfurled after being fired, expanding into razor-thin blades capable of bisecting a Fractus even in midair.

Soo-ah and Eun-ji coordinated with the support units, moving troops through collapsed buildings and establishing interception points that exploited every bottleneck the city offered. These same units also carried out the dangerous task of extracting wounded soldiers—a mission that often had to be performed in the middle of active combat.

At the center of the formation, Mai never stopped moving. Thanks to her echolocation, she could anticipate the swarm's movements beyond the distortion and determine which route each Fractus would take to inflict the greatest possible damage. She identified the most direct path toward the energy structure and began guiding Sasha's and Ludovic's advance, steering them around ambushes while keeping casualties to a minimum.

At midday the Clyx roaming the streets were pinned down by coordinated crossfire. Grivens became trapped inside fractium snares, while the Aurex were met by mobile barriers, coordinated gusts of wind from the mages, and concentrated shots from weapons emplaced on rooftops and elevated positions throughout the surrounding area.

Under the sun every action became part of a carefully choreographed tactical dance. Gunfire, defensive barriers, the gravitational distortion surrounding the energy source, and the squad's coordinated maneuvers meshed together like the gears of a clock. Their coordination transformed the city into a giant chessboard where every instinct-driven movement of the Fractus could be anticipated and countered. At last, Mai located the weakest point within the central structure—a region where the disturbance was concentrating unevenly.

Before the squad could begin studying it, however, a transmission came over the tactical air command network.

"All units, hold your positions. The bait has been deployed."

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then a shadow slowly drifted across the sky.

The Orbiter emerged from the east, gliding silently above the few low clouds as two fighter aircraft and several drones carefully guided it using electromagnetic emissions and fractium decoys. They weren't trying to shoot it down.

Only to lead it.

"Those crazy bastards... I can't believe it..." Sasha muttered, his voice carrying across the shared communications channel.

Less than three minutes later, the entire operation changed.

It had only been an experimental plan, which was why none of the ground units had taken it seriously when another air force squadron had proposed testing it the day before.

The creature slowly passed over the industrial complex.

And then it happened.

As though a silent command had spread throughout the city, thousands of Fractus began pouring out of the factories. Clyx burst through concrete walls, Grivens slipped through them like ghosts, while Aurex rose into the sky in massive spirals. Not a single one remained beneath the Orbiter's flight path.

At the same time, the gravitational distortion began to collapse and Mai's readings returned to normal almost instantly.

"The disturbance is disappearing."

Several test shots from nearby railguns tore straight through the industrial complex without being deflected.

The formations wasted no time.

From several kilometers away, the ships commenced the final sweep. Dozens of electromagnetic projectiles ripped across the city through corridors that had already been cleared by the ground forces. The impacts obliterated the infested structures while the Fractus, forced from their shelters, became trapped inside the kill zones that had been prepared over the previous two days.

The weapons of the android units and the soldiers fired ghostly bursts, one after another.

The Aurex were brought down by the anti-aircraft electric batteries.

The Grivens were shredded by overlapping crossfire.

The Clyx were annihilated before they could reorganize.

In less than an hour, organized Fractus resistance had ceased to exist.

When the final shot echoed away, silence returned to Novosibirsk.

The midday heat now had everyone drenched in sweat, while the air carried the sharp smell of ozone left behind by countless electrical discharges.

It was no longer the city that had existed before the war. Entire districts had been reduced to skeletons of concrete and steel. Yet for the first time in weeks, there was no sign of enemy activity only the small fractium cores among the streets and buildings.

The units began regrouping. And the wolves were together once again.

Soo-ah was the first to collapse onto a supply crate with a long sigh. Natalya returned covered in dust and black fractium residue, while Ludovic coordinated the withdrawal of the last defensive positions. Eun-ji made one final pass across the battlefield, ensuring no wounded soldiers had been left behind.

Mai looked up.

High above the clouds, the immense ring of artificial asteroids surrounding Earth shimmered faintly beneath the summer sun. The orbital belt now seemed like a silent reminder of the sheer scale of the war.

Far above, the Orbiter continued drifting northward, floating through the clouds like a chubby whale swimming across a white ocean.

Free.

"I wonder what it eats..." Mai murmured, absentmindedly rubbing her stomach.

A specialized reconnaissance squadron would soon take off to shadow it from a safe distance and study it more closely.

The preliminary conclusions of the operation were clear. For only the second time since the beginning of the conflict, it had been observed that certain Fractus swarms could generate a collective gravitational disturbance capable of deflecting attacks and protecting a strategic objective. However, the colony's unexpected reaction to the Orbiter suggested the existence of a biological hierarchy—or at least an instinctive behavior—far more complex than anyone had imagined.

No one knew why it happened.

Only that this strange creature, without firing a single shot or showing the slightest interest in either the humans or the Fractus below, had made the recapture of an entire city possible.

As the transport engines began to roar in preparation for the journey home, Mai remained watching the small black hemisphere disappear slowly into the far away clouds.

A chill ran down her spine.

They had better be ready.

In a few hours, the temperature would begin to fall once again.

The October wind in the Siberian Arctic cut like knives, and the snow fell in heavy sheets over the shadows advancing toward the abandoned research station.

Stepnye Volki, wrapped in specialized cold-weather suits, moved cautiously while leading a section into one of the most inhospitable regions on Earth. Reports indicated that a group of fractus had taken over the station, establishing a fortress from which they threatened to push deeper into critical territories.

Among the forty members of the section, Nikolai, the werewolf who had recently joined the squad, proved to be one of the most complicated additions. Mai knew him well—she had worked with him on a couple of missions in Nevermore. She was glad to see someone from the island after all these months, but Nikolai didn't have much news about the others either—not even more than she already knew. Yet integrating him into the team had not been easy. Although his ability to deploy into the form of an imposing werewolf gave him a tremendous edge in combat, his mental state was another matter entirely.

Nikolai suffered from a deep depression that left him silent for long stretches of time, a trait that clashed with the squad’s usual energy. While the werewolf was extremely efficient on the battlefield whenever necessary, the rest of the time he seemed lost in his own thoughts, often isolating himself from the others. During the first days, Mai tried to reach out, but he always kept his distance.

Despite his emotional state, Commander Sasha did not hesitate to assign Nikolai to the mission to secure the station. “When all this is over, I promise we’ll have a serious talk about your condition—but right now, we need everything you’ve got,” he told him, placing him alongside Ludovic and Soo-ah at the team’s vanguard.

The mission was difficult. Eun-ji and Jiho remained in the rear, providing medical coverage and tactical support via reconnaissance drones. It was in this harsh setting that Mai, Natalya, and Sasha took charge of the flanks, advancing while the portable railguns—provided by ZAIEN—were deployed across the combat formations. The new fractium-based railguns were marvels of engineering: lightweight, precise, and extremely powerful, capable of piercing heavy armor of fractus at long distances. Ludovic, with his field experience, was the first to fire them—his shots so precise that the Empusa-type fractus disintegrated before they could even attempt a counter.

The arrival of support drones also changed the mission dynamics. Equipped with advanced camouflage systems and armed with EMP device, they helped disarm traps and scouted the station’s corridors before the squad entered. The aeon assigned to the mission, Mikail, oversaw the entire operation from a remote location, coordinating multiple units across a hundred-kilometer area with real-time intelligence. Stepnye Volki was only one section of a much larger strategic movement.

The battle inside the research station proved far more complicated than expected. Just as the squad secured positions, a group of rolling-type fractus erupted from deep within the facility and others hidden beneath the snow—catching the humans and feys off guard, who had been expecting only Empusa-types.

The advance of these rolling fractus—officially known as Tornrulle—forced the squad to improvise.

These creatures moved at high speed, forming a spinning crown of thorns capable of ramming through barriers with relentless force. Each fractium core seemed protected by an outer mass of constantly reconfiguring filaments, making direct attacks risky and ineffective. Ludovic ordered angled defensive lines, using debris and abandoned vehicles as improvised cover points so that the spinning crowns would glance off the flanks instead of striking the soldiers directly.

Jiho coordinated with the drones, unleashing EMP bursts toward the densest clusters, to slow them down, while the paper golems reinforced mobile barricades and diverted the assaults of the spines toward cleared zones. Each defeated didn’t fade quietly, it transformed into crystal claws that sprouted from the fractal mass, scraping at the fabric of reality as though it were a wall. It was a phenomenon that forced the squad members to stay in constant motion, avoiding any prolonged engagement with a single target so they wouldn’t get caught by the spatial tears that floated in the air—cracks born from fallen cores.

Fortunately, they tended to vanish after a few hours, but entering the affected area while the effect lingered was hazardous. Many in Norway had lost limbs in the previous years, back when this type of fractus was first discovered.

Natalya, with her feline speed, was responsible for flanking the rollers, slicing filaments and redirecting their path toward cleared sectors, while Soo-ah moved the support platoons to carve safe corridors through the rubble. Ludovic held the center steady, blocking the most direct charges and ensuring that no crown of spines could break through the firing line. In other team Mai analyzed the rollers’ movement patterns through her echolocation, anticipating each charge and directing the artillery to the weak points where concentrated fire would destabilize the cores.

Their coordinated work turned the battlefield into a three-dimensional board of strategies in constant flux: the rollers spun, rammed, and burst into crystal claws, while the squad shifted positions, fired, raised barriers, and released bursts of air and fractium to contain them. Precision and synchronization between drones, railguns, and fighters became essential, a single mistake could mean being trapped beneath a spinning crown. Little by little, the rollers dwindled in number, dispersing under the combined pressure of crossfire and defensive maneuvers that turned each charge into a calculated risk.

It was then that Nikolai, in his wolf form and clad in his metamorphic armor, unleashed his full power. The werewolf’s bestial shape attacked with unprecedented ferocity, tearing through enemy lines. Though his emotional state remained an enigma, the battle placed him once more in his element: fighting alongside his comrades, like an unstoppable war machine.

The squad managed to secure the station and eliminate the fractus, though the cost in equipment was high—the rollers had destroyed the vehicles during their charges. They would have to wait for another team to arrive before they could leave, maintaining control of the area in case new threats emerged.

At the end of the mission, Mai noticed Nikolai retreating to a lonely corner. She approached him, carrying a self-heating soup can, and in a soft voice said, “You don’t have to fight alone, you know? We’re here for you—like a team.”

He looked at her for a moment, his eyes filled with an unfathomable melancholy. “I don’t know how to fit into this world, Mai,” he said, voice hollow, clenching a fist that transformed in a big hand covered in fur and sharp claws. “But I’ll do it, because this… this is the only thing I know how to do. I still am useful.”

“What a pain,” she said and smiled.

Mai placed a hand on his head as a gesture of comfort.

According to Dr. Kohinata, that was something that genuinely soothed him. Nikolai’s depression surely stemmed from something that had happened before he arrived on Earth—something on the other side. Despite the memory loss, certain chemical states in the brain lingered even after extensive mental training. Who knew if Nikolai would ever be fully free from those deep currents of sorrow.

As he sipped his soup, he smiled a bit. “Thank you.”

At the end of December, the Stepnye Volki squad was deployed to Irkutsk, where the situation had rapidly deteriorated. Several abandoned weapons factories scattered along the industrial outskirts had been occupied by fractus whose bodies resembled giant dragonflies and mosquitoes. The specialized research centers must have been running out of ideas, since they were simply labeled 46-A and 67-D. Sasha suggested calling them simply “mosquitoes” and “dragonflies.”

These facilities, twisted now under the creatures’ influence, served as collection centers for materials: corroded metals, scrap, vehicle remains, machinery—anything they could assimilate into their fractal masses. The logic behind this behavior was simple: originally of minimal density in their native dimension, they likely needed to increase their mass in order to exist and move stably within the three-dimensional reality. Every fragment of metal or concrete they absorbed reinforced their bodies, allowing them to maintain their shape and speed, turning the factories into mechanical-organic hives.

The squad had to advance with extreme caution. These fractal war machines had no consciousness, yet their collective movements were precise: they acted like swarms of insects defending their hive, reacting instinctively to any threat. In fact, rather than mosquitoes and dragonflies, they reminded everyone of how bees kept predators away from their hives, whether they were wasps or ants. In this case, Stepnye Volki were the ants.

Each fractium core was protected by layers of filaments that reconfigured constantly. When damaged, these cores generated crystalline fractures in the air—extensions of the mass—just like the phenomenon seen with the rollers. Approaching a downed fractus was as dangerous as fighting one, for the fractures projected micro-distortions in space, altering projectile trajectories and forcing the squad into continuous maneuvers to avoid being trapped.

Amid all that, they would need the support of those fast enough to fight at close range while engaging the Fractus with their weapons. Natalya had always been one of the squad's finest close-quarters fighters, but Nikolai possessed greater mobility, and his powered exoskeleton gave him the extra protection needed to bring down any Fractus that swooped too close.

The problem was that Nikolai's melancholy and depression could cloud his judgment.

As they advanced through rusted corridors and snow-covered yards, memories of his days as a wild werewolf roaming the forests could came crashing back. Sometimes Nikolai would look out the windows at the snow outside with a strange expression. Mai remembered him saying, long ago in the island, that he had spent years wandering through mountains and woodlands as a wolf before finally reclaiming his human form.

For a brief moment, he hesitated before throwing himself into the vanguard.

"Nik."

He turned his head just enough to look at her. She simply smiled and gave a small nod.

"Trust your instincts."

Sasha's voice cut through the frozen air.

"Left flank! Move!"

Without even looking at him, Ludovic checked the charge on his railgun. "We'll cover your advance, Nik."

Soo-ah finished adjusting a condenser on her electric arc and flashed him a crooked smile.

"Besides... if you don't charge in first, who's supposed to make the rest of us look brave?"

Natalya spun one of her blades between her fingers. "Just make sure you leave me something, big guy."

Jiho folded another paper construct in a matter of seconds and let it drift beside the squad. "The barriers are ready. You won't be alone."

Nikolai lowered his gaze for a moment.

“Just be careful around metal surfaces, or I’ll have to give you my special treatment for people who’ve been electrocuted,” Eun-ji smiled.

Sasha raised a fist. "This is your moment. Don't waste it."

That was enough.

A faint smile crossed Nikolai's face as he let out a slow breath.

At the same moment, Natalya twirled her twin blades and lunged toward the nearest Fractus.

"Whoever brings down the fewest is the rotten egg... and no drinks from the Captain's stash!"

"That's just cruel," Sasha said.

"Seriously?" Ludovic muttered as he raised his railgun.

Soo-ah burst into laughter.

"Deal."

Nikolai was back into focus.

His wolf form unfurled, metamorphic armor crackling with fractium, and he charged toward the fractus line with renewed fury while the others deployed an electric net to restrict their movements—something akin to a mosquito trap, but using the factory structure itself as a conductor.

Violence, it seemed, could help Nikolai regain control over his emotional states. Perhaps, after all, his animal side simply demanded more movement and exertion than he ever allowed himself.

Ludovic and Mai opened breaches with their railguns, piercing cores and diverting filaments toward areas where Jiho could offer fire support. Natalya moved between columns of metal and machinery, slicing filaments and driving the mosquito-like fractus into improvised traps—polarizing parts of the structure to attract them, and then using her rifle to concentrate the electricity and produce a charge strong enough to destabilize the swarms. As the fractus assimilated pieces of the factory, they grew larger and more resilient, but also slower and more predictable, allowing the squad to deploy dynamic tactics: angled defensive lines, safe corridors carved through debris, coordinated bursts of air and energy that rattled the fractal masses out of their rhythm.

Meanwhile, the support drones flew over the rooftops, marking targets and detonating weak points. The synergy between was flawless, every shot, every barrier, every maneuver was perfectly timed, because a single mistake could mean being caught between a spinning crown of spines and a fractium core releasing crystal claws.

After a couple hours of fighting, the last Fractus finally fell.

The factories, damaged and still crackling with residual electrical discharges, had been secured. Mai, Natalya, and Nikolai still had their hair standing on end from the static, though none of them had suffered so much as a scratch.

Since the vehicles had been destroyed during the battle, they would have to wait several hours for the relief team and reinforcements to arrive.

At least the main building still had an old break room, although the cold seeped through every shattered window.

An improvised campfire burned in the middle of the room, fed by planks torn from abandoned offices. They would wait there while heating canned food and boiling water for coffee. Surveillance drones patrolled the surrounding area in case of an emergency, but for the moment they could finally relax.

Sasha opened a small metal box and pulled out a flask of vodka. "Well... a bet's a bet."

Natalya grinned immediately. "I knew the Captain had a secret stash."

"Had."

Ludovic chuckled quietly as he accepted a small metal cup.

"So that's why you were always guarding that box in your pack. Here I was thinking you kept your porn collection in there."

Jiho accepted only a sip before returning to the notebook resting on her knees. She began sketching the Fractus cores they had recovered. It was something she always did. Instead of taking photographs, she preferred filling notebooks with drawings and observations, alongside ideas for new origami designs. She always insisted that drawing helped her process new discoveries far better than photographs ever could. To her, drawing—as much as folding paper and imbuing it with magic through carefully handwritten spell circuits—was an art.

Meanwhile, Mai slowly heated a few cans of instant soup over the fire while Eun-ji worked on braiding her now much longer hair. Mai had no intention of drinking anything before eating.

"It's grown a lot over these past months," Eun-ji said.

"I've actually been thinking about cutting it again. It's getting too long."

"That would be a shame. You have beautiful hair."

"Static electricity makes it a nightmare unless I put hair oil on it."

"That explains why you always smell like cake," Ludovic said.

Eun-ji lifted the end of Mai's braid and sniffed it.

"He's right. Coconut and almond."

"That's thanks to Soo-ah. She's the one who gave me the oil."

Natalya raised a hand.

"Can I borrow some?"

Sasha took another sip from his cup.

"You're going to end up eating your own tail thinking it's a slice of cake."

Natalya immediately tried to kick him.

"Maybe we should order a whole bottle."

She glanced toward Nikolai.

"I mean... for Nikolai."

Nikolai nearly choked on his drink.

"Please don't. My sense of smell is sensitive enough already. Besides, the fur only shows up when I'm in my wolf form. It's not like I have to brush it."

Mai couldn't help smiling as she imagined Nikolai starring in a hair oil and shampoo commercial for werewolves.

"There."

Eun-ji admired the finished braid with obvious satisfaction.

"Thanks," Mai said, handing one of the steaming soup cans to her.

"It's nice to eat something that wasn't heated by a self-heating pack for once. I was getting tired of those."

"Food is food," Natalya shrugged.

"No," Mai replied. "Food heated over a fire always tastes better."

Ludovic took another drink and sighed contentedly.

Soo-ah watched everyone receive a little vodka and let out an exaggerated sigh. "So unfair..."

Natalya raised an eyebrow. "Rules are rules."

"I only lost by two."

"You still lost."

Soo-ah puffed out her cheeks before reluctantly accepting a mug of coffee from Ludovic.

Nikolai watched the scene for a few seconds. Then he quietly slid his cup toward her.

"Here."

She looked at him in surprise. "What about you?"

"I don't really like vodka."It was an obvious lie.

Soo-ah smiled softly. "We'll share."

She took only a small sip before handing the cup back.

"Otherwise this spotted cat will say I cheated."

"I was just about to."

Nikolai let out a faint smile as he lowered his gaze toward the fire.

“You feel better, now?” Mai asked.

Nikolai nodded.

His tail moved from side to side, sweeping the dust from the floor.
