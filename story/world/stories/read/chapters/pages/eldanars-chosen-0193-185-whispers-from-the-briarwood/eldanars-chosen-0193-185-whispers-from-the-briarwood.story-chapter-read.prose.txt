The late afternoon sun was already sinking behind the high timber walls of Ashenfall when the party finally reconvened in the crowded common room of the Bull's Head Inn. The atmosphere in the tavern was just as sombre as the morning, but the party themselves looked entirely revitalised.

Josh stood near the hearth, adjusting the newly repaired straps of his breastplate. The human smith had done a phenomenal job. The heavy steel plate sat perfectly against his chest, the terrible acid scarring pounded out and polished away, while the thick patch of hardened troll-hide stitched into his gambeson offered a reassuring, dense layer of kinetic protection over his ribs. He rolled his shoulders, pleased to find his mobility completely unhindered.

Across the table, Brett was meticulously inspecting his hands. Gone was the polished wooden staff he usually leaned upon. Instead, his long, elegant fingers were encased in a pair of stunning, deep red drake-scale gloves. The thick, heat-resistant leather was intricately embroidered with glowing, raw fire-silk thread that formed complex runic arrays across the knuckles and palms. Brett snapped his fingers, and a perfectly spherical, intensely bright spark of pure plasma danced effortlessly between his thumbs, casting a warm glow over his sharp features.

"The mana conductivity is absolutely sublime," Brett murmured, admiring the dark red scales. "I feel as though the thermal energy is drawn directly from the air rather than channelled forcefully from my core. It is a monumental improvement."

Bhel simply sat with a pot of heavy oil, humming a low dwarven war tune as he worked a fresh, high-quality diamond whetstone along the curved edge of his right axe. The iron gleamed with lethal, renewed promise.

Only Carcan and Perberos seemed untouched by the restorative power of a good shopping trip. When the elven twins stepped through the heavy wooden doors of the inn, their spatial sacks figuratively bulging with freshly purchased arrows and tightly corked mana potions, the mood immediately shifted.

Perberos looked as rigidly stoic as ever, his pale silver eyes scanning the room with his usual predatory detachment. But Carcan was a completely different story. The elven healer looked incredibly pale. Her grip on her amber-topped staff was white-knuckled, and her golden eyes were fixed firmly on the floorboards. She walked with a stiff, unnatural posture, entirely lacking her usual serene grace.

"Are you alright, Carcan?" Josh asked softly as she approached the table, his brow furrowing with sudden concern. He looked her up and down, searching for any hidden injuries. "You look completely exhausted. Did you run into trouble in the market?"

"I am fine, Josh," Carcan replied quietly, her voice lacking its usual soothing resonance. She did not meet his eyes, carefully arranging her robes as she sat down on a wooden stool. "The crowds were simply overwhelming. It is difficult to see so much suffering and be unable to help at all. I just need a moment."

Josh accepted the excuse, knowing the immense empathy she carried for the vulnerable, but he caught Brett shooting him a highly sceptical, knowing glance across the table. Something else was clearly bothering her, but pushing the issue in a crowded tavern would do no good.

"We have our gear, and we have our supplies," Josh stated, taking charge of the group once more. He checked the short sword at his hip. "We should head to the guild hall. Mich promised she would have a clearer picture of the local bounties by this evening. We need to figure out where we can do the most damage."

They left the quiet misery of the inn behind and stepped back out into the freezing twilight. The walk to the Adventurers' Guild was a silent affair. Carcan stayed near the back of the group, walking incredibly close to her brother. The bustling, panicked noise of the city seemed to wash right over her as she wrestled with the terrible, impending choice Perberos had laid at her feet. Abandon her home, or abandon her friends.

When they climbed the wide stone steps and pushed through the heavy oak doors of the guild hall, the interior was just as chaotic as it had been the night before. The air was stiflingly hot, packed with the scent of sweat, stale ale, and the sharp tang of old blood. Groups of heavily armed fighters argued over maps spread across sticky wooden tables, while clerics rushed between groups handing out minor healing salves.

They wove their way through the throng, cutting a path toward the row of administrative desks at the back of the hall.

Mich was sitting in her usual spot, completely submerged behind two towering stacks of official parchment. The blonde rep had dark circles under her bright eyes, and her ink-stained fingers were flying across a fresh bounty ledger. She looked up as Josh's heavy boots clanked against the stone floor, and a tired, genuinely pleased smile broke across her face.

"Look at you all," Mich greeted them, leaning back in her wooden chair and letting her quill drop. She eyed Josh's pristine armour and Brett's glowing new gloves with an administrator's appreciation. "Fully repaired and geared up. I am guessing you didn't come back just to cash in your Kobold cores."

"We are looking for work, Mich," Josh said, resting his gauntlets gently on the edge of her desk. "We spent the morning upgrading our equipment. We reached level twenty-five during the siege, and we want to put the third tier to use. Where can we help the city the most?"

Mich’s smile faded into a tight, professional line. She pulled a large, hastily drawn map of the surrounding frontier out from under a stack of papers and spun it around for the party to see. Red ink stained almost every single marked dungeon location.

"The situation is incredibly grim," Mich explained, her voice dropping to a serious, confidential whisper. "The city guard is completely focused on fortifying the walls against the rumoured undead horde approaching from the Crypts. That means the local perimeter is entirely exposed. We need competent parties to step out there and start settling the minor dungeons that have broken before they spit out enough beasts to become another threat."

She tapped a finger on a red circle situated to the east of Ashenfall. "I have a group of mid-level adventurers preparing to head back to the Goblin Caves. Toby brought us word that the horde overwhelmed the exterior camps, but if a strong party can push inside and slay the monsters around the portal, and then clear the dungeon enough times, that should solve that problem. It will give us a secure spot to train new adventurers again, and with the current threat, I think there will be a lot of them soon. I also have open bounties for culling any undead in the northern woods, escorting supply caravans to the river outposts, and hunting down displaced native apex predators on the southern roads."

As Mich listed the terrifying array of continent-wide threats, Carcan felt the breath catch in her throat. The sheer scale of the disaster was impossible to ignore. Every word Mich spoke seemed to hammer another nail into the coffin of Briarwood’s safety.

Carcan looked away from the map, her chest tight with a suffocating panic. As her eyes scanned the chaotic, crowded guild hall, she spotted a flash of brilliant sapphire blue through an open archway leading to the senior training rooms.

Standing in the centre of the adjoining room was Caistina Tharistrar.

The elven mage was a striking figure of absolute, unyielding power. Her tall, willowy frame was wrapped in elegant sapphire-blue robes adorned with complex silver arcane sigils. Her long, moonlit-white hair cascaded down her back, and she held a pristine white staff etched with intricate runes that pulsed softly with latent magical energy. She was currently speaking in sharp, commanding tones to a group of terrified novice mages, her piercing amethyst eyes glowing faintly with authority.

"Excuse me," Carcan whispered suddenly, interrupting Mich's briefing. She stepped back from the desk, her hands clutching her amber-topped staff. "I need to speak with someone. I will be right back."

Before Josh could ask where she was going, Carcan turned and hurried through the throng of adventurers, practically running towards the archway.

She stood nervously near the edge of the training room, waiting with uncharacteristic impatience as Caistina finished dressing down a young human warlock who had apparently mismanaged his mana reserves during a recent patrol. When the chastised novices finally scurried away, Caistina turned, her sharp amethyst eyes locking immediately onto Carcan.

The older elf’s stern expression instantly softened into a look of profound, patient warmth. She tapped her white staff gently against the stone floor and gestured for Carcan to approach.

"Carcan, child," Caistina greeted her, her voice carrying a melodic, ancient wisdom that instantly commanded respect. "I heard you had returned from the Kobold territories. Your survival is a testament to your faith and your skill. But you look deeply troubled. It is not just exhaustion that weighs on your shoulders today."

Carcan stopped in front of the powerful mage, looking down at the stone floor. She felt incredibly small standing before the woman who had guided their first magical steps in Ashenfall.

"Caistina," Carcan began, her voice trembling slightly. The emotional dam she had built up all morning finally cracked. "I am entirely torn. Perberos... my brother believes we must leave the city. He believes that with the safe zones collapsing and the massive portals surging across the continent, Briarwood is in imminent danger. He wants us to return to the deep woods to protect our kin."

Caistina’s expression grew deeply solemn. She leaned slightly on her runic staff, the silver sigils on her robes catching the candlelight. "And you do not wish to go."

"I love my home," Carcan said quickly, looking up, desperate to be understood. "I love the silver-leaf trees and our family. The thought of them facing these horrors makes me physically sick. But..." She swallowed hard, her golden eyes shining with unshed tears. "I cannot abandon my party. I cannot leave Josh, Brett, and Bhel to fight an undead legion without a healer. They are my family now, too. And... and I care for Josh deeply. More than I should. If I leave him here to die, my heart will simply break."

Caistina listened in perfect silence, her piercing eyes reading the absolute, crushing sincerity in the young healer's face. The older elf let out a soft, understanding sigh and reached out, resting a cool, comforting hand on Carcan’s shoulder.

"I empathise with you more than you know, little one," Caistina said softly, her gaze drifting toward a high, frosted window. "When the Shattering began, when the first reports of the surges reached the guild, my very first instinct was to abandon this city and walk the long road back to my own ancestral glades. The pull of our blood is incredibly strong. It is the natural response of our people to seek the trees when the world burns."

Carcan nodded miserably, wiping a rogue tear from her cheek. "Then Perberos is right. We must go."

"He is right to be afraid," Caistina corrected gently, her hand squeezing Carcan’s shoulder. "But he is acting on blind fear, not intelligence. You are operating under the assumption that the deep woods are blind and helpless. They are not."

Carcan blinked, thoroughly confused by the mage’s calm tone. "But the safe zones are collapsing. The beasts are fleeing."

"Yes, the outer perimeters are in chaos," Caistina agreed, lowering her voice and gesturing toward the main guild hall. "But the core territories are holding. The Adventurers' Guild is not just a tavern where mercenaries collect coin, Carcan. It is a highly sophisticated, continent-spanning network. We have communication arrays."

Carcan stared at the older elf in absolute, unadulterated shock. "Communication arrays? You mean... towns can actually talk to each other across the wilderness?"

Caistina offered a small, patient smile. "Of course they can. Did you truly believe the guildmasters relied on horseback couriers to coordinate monster bounties across thousands of miles? We possess deeply embedded scrying crystals and magical relay points in every major settlement and allied territory on the continent. The network requires immense magical power to maintain, but it allows us to monitor the global situation in real time."

The revelation hit Carcan like a physical shockwave. The idea that information could travel faster than a running horse was entirely alien to her understanding of the primitive, sprawling frontier.

"So... you know what is happening out there?" Carcan breathed, her hands tightening on her staff. "Right now?"

"We know the broad strokes," Caistina nodded, her amethyst eyes turning serious once more. "And I can tell you this with absolute certainty. We have lost contact with Verentide entirely. The relay crystal there went completely dark yesterday morning, likely overrun by the same undead surge that Vane’s party barely escaped. But Briarwood?"

Caistina paused, holding Carcan’s gaze. "Briarwood is currently safe. The deep elven woods have reported minor skirmishes on their outer borders, but their rangers are holding the line. There have been no major portal tears within their territory. They are not calling for aid, Carcan. They are actively fortifying."

Carcan felt the ground sway beneath her feet. The suffocating, crushing weight that had sat heavily on her chest since the damp alleyway that morning simply evaporated. A massive, overwhelming wave of pure relief crashed through her body, leaving her feeling light and entirely breathless.

Her home was not burning. Her family was safe. She did not have to make the terrible, heartbreaking choice. She did not have to leave Josh behind to die.

"Thank you," Carcan whispered, her voice cracking with profound gratitude. "Thank you so much, Caistina."

"Do not thank me for the truth, child," Caistina smiled warmly, releasing her shoulder and taking a step back. "Your place is here, with your party. This city needs every ounce of light you can provide in the coming days. Now, go tell your brother to put his fears to rest, and focus on the battle in front of you."

Carcan offered the powerful mage a deep, incredibly respectful bow before turning and practically sprinting back towards the main hall. She wove effortlessly through the crowded room, her silver eyes searching for the familiar, muted leather of her twin.

She found Perberos standing near Mich’s desk, his arms crossed tightly over his chest, listening to Bhel loudly argue about the best way to decapitate a goblin.

Carcan grabbed her brother by the arm and unceremoniously pulled him away from the group, dragging him toward a relatively quiet spot near a large, stone pillar.

"What has happened?" Perberos asked immediately, his hand instinctively dropping to the hilt of his curved blade, misinterpreting her frantic energy for a physical threat.

"Briarwood is safe," Carcan blurted out, unable to contain the massive smile that was breaking across her face.

Perberos froze, his pale eyes narrowing in deep confusion. "What are you talking about? How could you possibly know that? No messengers could have made it here yet."

"The guild has a magical communication network," Carcan explained rapidly, her words tumbling over each other in her excitement. "Scrying crystals and relay points connecting the settlements. Caistina just told me. They are monitoring the entire continent. Verentide has fallen, but our home has not requested aid. The border rangers are holding the deep woods. There is no active threat to our family right now."

Perberos stared at her, processing the incredibly complex information. The tension in his lean, lithe frame was entirely visible. He looked down at his boots, his mind running through a hundred different scenarios, weighing the validity of magical communication against his own deeply ingrained primal instincts.

"A magical relay," Perberos murmured, his voice laced with heavy scepticism. "Magic can be blocked. It can be manipulated."

"Caistina would not lie about this, Perberos," Carcan insisted softly, placing a hand on his arm to ground him. "She felt the same pull to return home as you did. But she trusts the network. Our kin are strong. They are holding their ground. We do not need to abandon our post here."

Perberos took a long, slow breath, closing his eyes. The immense, protective burden he carried for their family was warring violently with his innate pragmatism. Finally, he opened his eyes and looked down at his sister. The rigid tension in his shoulders dropped a fraction of an inch.

"If the mage is sure that the woods are safe," Perberos stated quietly, his tone measured and deeply analytical, "then we hold our position here. We stay with the party."

Carcan let out a joyful, entirely un-elven squeak of relief and threw her arms around her brother’s neck in a tight, crushing hug. Perberos grunted softly in surprise, awkwardly patting her back, wholly unaccustomed to such blatant public displays of emotion.

When they walked back over to Mich’s desk a moment later, Carcan looked like a completely different person. The colour had returned to her cheeks, her posture was tall and serene, and her golden eyes sparkled with renewed, brilliant determination.

Josh looked up from the map, catching her eye and offering a questioning smile. Carcan simply nodded back, her expression assuring him that whatever demons she had been wrestling with were entirely conquered.

"Right, we are all present and accounted for," Josh announced, turning his attention back to the grim map spread across the wooden desk. "Mich has given us a few options. We have the undead to the north, supply escorts, or the goblin dungeon to the east. We need to pick a target and hit it hard."

"I say we take the Goblin Caves," Brett suggested immediately, leaning forward to tap the red circle on the parchment. He held up his hands, the deep red scales of his new gloves catching the ambient light of the hall. "Toby said the horde overwhelmed the exterior camps, meaning it is a high-density, low-level target. It is the absolute perfect environment for us to field-test our new capabilities without risking a high-level elite wiping the floor with us."

"The spell-slinger makes an excellent point," Bhel agreed enthusiastically, hefting his newly oiled axes. The dwarf cracked a fierce, terrifying grin that pushed his braided beard up his cheeks. "The Goblin Caves is where we started as a proper party. It is only fitting we return there to settle the score. Let's see how those little green runts handle tier three iron."

"It makes sense," Perberos added smoothly from the flank. "We are familiar with the geography of the caves. We know the choke points. It will allow us to coordinate our new skills efficiently."

Josh looked around the circle of his friends. They were battered from the road, but they were also incredibly powerful, heavily armoured, and deeply united. They had crossed the threshold of level twenty-five. They were no longer the desperate, scrambling novices who had nearly died in the dark a few months ago.

"The Goblin Caves it is," Josh decided, a confident, slightly arrogant smile touching his lips. He looked down at Mich. "Sign us up for the cull, Mich. We will clear the exterior camps and push inside to break the Chieftain."

"I will mark you down on the ledger," Mich said, pulling her quill back out and scribbling their names onto the parchment. She looked up, offering them a small, cautious smile. "Be careful out there, though. Even low-level hordes can be deadly if they swarm you."

"Do not worry about us, Mich," Brett scoffed lightly, adjusting his heavy fur cloak with a flick of his wrists. "We have faced down a much more dangerous horde in the past three days. A disorganized horde of basic goblins is barely going to serve as a warm-up exercise. The worst bit will probably be the walk."

Josh nodded in agreement, feeling the latent static electricity humming comfortably in his core. With their new gear and their devastating new class advancements, returning to the first dungeon they had ever cleared felt like a guaranteed, incredibly easy victory.

They turned away from the desk and marched out of the guild hall, walking confidently toward the inn for one more night. They were entirely convinced that the coming battle would be nothing more than a simple stroll through the woods.
