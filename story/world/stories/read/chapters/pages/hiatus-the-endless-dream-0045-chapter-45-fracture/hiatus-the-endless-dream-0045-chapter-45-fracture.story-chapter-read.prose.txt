Jeren wasn't exactly naive, just hopeful, and with different values. Honorable might be a good word. His word was as good as a contract or a genuine desire. I didn't know if I'd be able to pass through his barrier after coming to an agreement, but I was an opportunistic person. If it wasn't there, it would've been somewhere else down the line, but it was there and I didn't miss the chance. I couldn't at that distance.

I plunged the wooden stake into the third Visitor's neck. The traitor hadn't even hit the floor before I was violently shunted out of the golden light. I balled myself up, covering myself in the [Shroud of Nothing]. My vision blurred as the room streaked around me. Safe in the bubble of the Void, I slammed into Rend, carrying it to the wall where it then teleported to the other side, bringing me to rest.

I looked back at the golden veil only to see Jeren and the other one staring at the dying Visitor with wide, shocked eyes. Jeren turned to look past Rend and at me, as if part of him didn't believe I had done it. That was the thing. Jeren was so focused on the parts of me hurting that he failed to fully consider the lengths I would go to hurt others. At the end of the day, he saw me as some fragile animal or child. It's why I couldn't trust him to make the right decisions.

Rend continued to pace from side to side. Jeren dropped his barrier, pulsing it whenever the wolf moved forward. His eyes flicked from me to the wolf to the dying traitor. "Dorian! Heal him!"

"No." My voice was sharp, but sounded distant, even to my ears.

The blond man's hands were pressed into the back of his own head. They slid forward, covering his face, only broken by the occasional scratch of his scalp. Even so, he never let the wolf get too close. "Fine, you can take him with you! Just use the Boon."

I stood up, leaving most of my body outside of the Shroud. Rend didn't leap at me, long since having learned how to interact with my stolen Boon. "There's not charg—"

"I'll give you the Presence. I'll pay it!" Jeren cut me off.

I laughed in his face. "You're insane." That was a thousand Presence. For what?

"Why are you doing this? Dorian please," he continued.

"None of you get it." The Visitor wouldn't even die permanently. He was a Visitor. "Nobody owes us their saving. And we sure as hell don't owe a traitor anything!"

"How?" Jeren spoke and my frown deepend. "You broke an Instance over fairness one moment, the next you gut a kid? He's not the enemy. It's the Instance Holder! Why can't you see that."

I flinched, the twisting knot that had been forming in my stomach finally snapping. Cold shame—and the brief thoughts of Krevith, Illia, and Talin—were crushed by images of Nico, Alder, Orlan, and the third Visitor. "They are the Instance Holder! They're doing what he wants. I'm getting rid of threats!"

"He's not a threat." Jeren screamed back.

I gestured to the bleeding out Visitor. They were clutching their neck, their eyes already going glassy. They wouldn't pass out until they died, courtesy of the Instance Holder's [Cruel] title. "Yeah, thanks to me."

The man's face twisted in disgust. "I'll give you anything. Boon. Item. Whatever you want. Just heal him!" He changed his angle, and it filled me with more frustration.

"Why do you insist on trying to kill yourself and everyone else?" I screamed, unable to keep all my feelings bottled in.

Orlan's quiet voice, picking up volume as he continued, cut through our argument. "I'll tell you, God's— The Instance Holders' secret! If you heal him, I'll tell you how to hurt the Instance Holder!"

My head snapped towards the man, and all my apprehension faded away. I wanted some of them to survive. I really did, but this. This trumped all of that. "Okay," I looked to Jeren, "the Presence, the information, and I take the Visitor."

I began to step forward, but in Orlan's world, Rend entered a frenzy. He burst out. Slamming into the golden veil, causing the whole ship to shake. It howled in rage and turned to the wall. It tore through it, destroying as many supports as possible before disappearing into the ship. The sounds of destruction and the steadily increasing splintering let me track its movement, despite having no line of sight. I hoped the others had left.

"We need to go." Jeren intoned, dismissing [Wayfarer] before stepping towards me with an outstretched hand and static in his eyes. I took it.

[Jeren has gifted you 1000 Presence].

I dumped it all into Identity and turned to Orlan.

"Info." It was short and sharp.

"God puts part of himself in mortal bodies. It's why he was so angry at Maerin for killing the high priest. It was one of his vessels, in part."

A section of the roof, a few rooms off, fell. Jeren made for the door, but I held up a hand. "Just launch me through the wall to make an exit." I turned my attention back to Orlan. "How many vessels and where?"

He swallowed hard, looking across the shaking ship. "He has four at any given time. It takes a year to make a new one. I only know of one other."

"Where." I stepped forward, staring down the man.

He looked up and pointed to the ceiling, but I could tell he meant past it, "The moon."

I nodded and positioned myself near the wall of the ship. "Launch me," I told Jeren.

"Heal Theon."

I scowled. "Once we get down."

"He won't survive the jump."

I sighed. Fair point.

"He comes with me." I pushed. I wanted his agreement explicit.

Jeren stayed silent, clenching his teeth as if he could escape this, but he couldn't find one. "Fine."

I reached out, removed the stake from the Visitor's neck, and simultaneously activated [No Good Deed]. While major, the wound was relatively small, so the flesh appeared and knit itself together quickly. Jeren activated [Wayfarer] before I was fully ready and shot me out of the ship. I landed on the dirt outside in a sprawl.

Outside, the light was no longer pale in the slightest. The air looked thick, and a constant heat haze acted like a fog. More than twenty feet away, the landscape was a swirling mass of barely decipherable information. I could tell there was a treeline, but the exact distance or where the ground and trees separated was impossible to parse. The only thing at a distance I could see was the dark, red wine moon looming above in perfect clarity. It shimmered, and the craters on its surface seemed to float across its surface in small spirals.

I took a nauseous step forward, then another, and slammed right into the bark of the tree. That shouldn't have been possible. I didn't get launched that far, but in two steps I had traveled more than thirty feet. The shade of the tree blocked out the miasmic light, letting me see a little clearer.

I saw Jeren and the others had made it down. Orlan seemed to be limping slightly. As he stepped, it looked like he had left his body behind. His legs dragged forward, but his torso stayed in place. His arms, disconnected, pushed down on nothing, yet supported his hip. I could see the muscles and joints hanging in space. Then all at once they snapped together and he was at the treeline, panting and out of breath.

Jeren came next, holding the still unconscious Visitor in his arms. I was content to let the man carry the traitor for now—unwilling to waste any energy on him.

I finally glanced further into the forest to see the others already out. I looked over the group. There were five in total. Nico was nowhere to be seen. Krevith, or rather the parasite puppeting his unconscious body, stood next to a series of shattered stone pieces. The others stared at us with relief, worry, and fear. There were only six Survivors alive then.

I couldn't afford to kill Alder and Orlan now, not unless we got to the last moment and were guaranteed to win … but it wasn't my business anymore. Maerin had asked me to leave, and I doubted her feelings had changed in the past few minutes.

I directed my words towards Jeren. "You need to take everyone underground." I didn't elaborate. I felt like the reason was self-evident.

Jeren, still holding the Visitor, hesitated. I let my annoyance wash over me, more disbelieving that he was still trying to find a way out of this. I was tired of arguing, though. No amount of reasoning, of stating that they were a Visitor and wouldn't actually die, or that I wouldn't kill them, or anything else, would ease his mind. An empty threat would have to do.

"If you don't, I'll use [Open the Path] to create a portal to the moon and kill everyone here."

Jeren's eyes narrowed in on me, and his lips thinned. "You wouldn't."

"Jeren," I said, coming dangerously close to believing my own words. "I just want to go home at this point. Win. Lose. I'm so tired. Me trying is a courtesy."

The man stared at me for a long moment before nodding. The Visitor shivered, clinging tighter to Jeren, but then changing tactics and grabbing onto Orlan.

"I'll come with you," Orlan said, and I shrugged.

"Sure. Do what you want," I said and gestured further into the forest, off the path towards the spire. "That way. Start walking."

They did as I asked, and I left Jeren and the other Survivors behind without a proper goodbye.

I had a lot to think about. My mind was too much of a mess. I felt like I was grasping for anything and everything I could to stabilize myself.

Technically, I had accomplished everything I wanted.

I had secured the Survivors' safety as much as I reasonably could and removed the third Visitor as a threat to the Survivors.

For some reason, the whole thing just felt bitter. The outcome. At the end of the day, it was what I wanted. I should've been happy about that, but all I felt was regret instead. I couldn't parse why. Krevith, Maerin, and Paltra were still alive. That was what my goal had developed into.

I wanted to push it all down for later. But it was constantly fucking later. It was endless. It never stopped. Even when I went home to my world, I was sure it would never stop. It would just keep going, and I would let it. The Dream had promised me I could see my brother again, and now that I had that knowledge, there was no going back. It would just eat at me, keep me awake night after night, knowing that I could've done something.

I stopped walking and just stared up through the canopy. The red light burned my eyes as if I were looking at the sun. I needed a new goal. Just one to keep me sane long enough for the Instance to close, and only one made sense. I needed to figure out how to shatter the piece of this Instance Holder held inside the moon. I'd kill that part of him before I left the Instance.

I focused on that. I'd just have to know I did everything I could to set the Survivors up for survival.
