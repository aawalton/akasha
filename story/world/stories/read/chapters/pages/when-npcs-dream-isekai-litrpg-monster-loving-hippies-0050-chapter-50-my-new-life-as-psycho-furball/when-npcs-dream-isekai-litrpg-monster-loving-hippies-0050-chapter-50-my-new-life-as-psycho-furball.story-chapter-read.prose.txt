“Come with me,” said the flying, talking furball—Uul.

I didn’t think twice before complying. Because this creature scared the absolute shit out of me.

It wasn’t actually that hard—to scare the shit out of me. But Uul did it on a whole other level. I was absolutely petrified of him.

He gently brushed against the snakes, whispered something to them, and each snake grabbed one of the jerks who’d tried to kill me—they were lying unconscious on the ground. Then, Uul floated toward the forest, and the snakes followed.

I followed, too. I think I was supposed to.

As I walked numbly behind them, staring at the terrifying furball, the monstrous snakes, and the unconscious idiots being dragged like potatoes, I realized how truly bizarre this situation was.

I tried to think—tried to make some sense of it. The fact that I was dumb wasn’t helping much either.

Let’s deduce some basic facts here, I thought, as we entered the treeline. There’s a flying, talking furball. He controls giant snakes. He technically saved my life. And… something tells me he might not be a nice guy.

That’s about it.

Oh, and I should probably do whatever he says, because of the whole snake-control thing.

Great. That seemed like a solid enough assessment of things. Now I’d just see where it all goes, and decide what to do next.

We stopped in a clearing not far from town. The snakes dropped the two jerks and coiled peacefully on the ground, like deadly pets resting after a walk.

The tall ugly guy and the other one — Linguth —started to wake up. Slowly, they blinked stupidly, looked around, saw the snakes, saw Uul, and promptly lost every drop of color in their faces.

“What… What the fuck is this!?” the ugly guy yelped.

Linguth looked at me, eyes wide with horror. “Is this your doing, Shooya?”

“No…” I mumbled, looking down. “It’s really not…”

“It was my doing,” said Uul softly, hovering in front of the two jerks.

“Shit, it talks!”“What is that thing!?”

“You are clearly so very confused,” said Uul, sounding vaguely bored. “And that is tiresome to me. Shooya, please explain the situation to them.”

“This is… Uul,” I said quietly, keeping my head low. “He… he controls the snakes.”

“Very good, Shooya,” said Uul with a smile. “Short and to the point!”

As he said it, one of the massive, red-orange striped snakes slithered closer, its fangs glistening, almost brushing against the terrified men. I think things started to become very clear to them at that moment.

“What… what do you want?” asked Linguth, his voice trembling, on the edge of tears.

“I need you to bring me things,” said Uul. “From the town.”

“Okay…” Linguth stammered. “Like… what things?”

“I need a wheeled vehicle,” said Uul. “A wagon, I believe is the term? Large enough for me, two of these snakes, for Shooya, and for supplies. I also require provisions—food and water for a two-week journey. Ropes. A map of the region. And funds.”

Then Uul turned to me. “Shooya, how much would be sufficient for long travel? Enough to purchase necessities along the way?”

“I don’t know…” I stammered. “Maybe… five gold?”

“Make it ten,” said Uul calmly.

“We don’t have that!” cried the ugly one. “Not even close!”

“You’ll manage,” Uul said softly. “Shooya will accompany you to ensure things are done properly. Now go.”

The two exchanged a look — the kind that meant they were already planning to bolt.

“Sure,” said Linguth. “We’ll do as you say. We’ll get the stuff.”

“I already said go,” Uul replied in a voice so cold it scraped your soul. “Don’t make me repeat myself.”

We started walking toward the town—but then Uul’s voice rang out again.

“Oh, I almost forgot,” he said casually, brushing a wing against one of the snakes.

Before we could blink, the snake lunged — impossibly fast for its size — and bit Linguth and the ugly guy each in the arm. They screamed.

“Don’t worry,” said Uul as they stared at him, wide-eyed and clutching their arms. “It’s a special delayed snake venom. You’ll likely feel nothing beyond a slight headache. But in a day, it will begin melting your intestines. You’ll die horribly and slowly over several hours.”

Their faces turned white.

“Bring me what I asked for, and I’ll give you the antidote. The poison will then… neutralized. But don’t wait too long—it works best within the first eight hours.”

Uul floated toward me then, hovering uncomfortably close to my face.

“But of course, there’s no need for such methods with you, Shooya,” he said in his calm voice. “After all, I saved your life. And you are my dear and loyal friend.”

My eyes welled up with tears. “Am loyal… friend…” I murmured quietly.

“Good! On your way, then!” Uul said cheerfully.

The three of us—me and the poisoned jerks—started walking toward town.

“I don’t want my intestines to melt,” the ugly one whimpered softly.

“It… it might be a lie!” Linguth said, trying to muster some courage. “He might just be trying to scare us!”

The ugly one grabbed Linguth’s shirt in a panic. “Do you want to wait and find out!? Do you really want to!?”

Linguth coughed. “Okay, okay. We’ll do what he said. I know where Alerzath keeps most of the band treasury. Should be twenty gold at least. I can take fifteen—five for supplies, and ten for… for it.”

“Listen, Shooya,” Linguth said, turning to me. “All the stuff I said before, the knife… I was just trying to scare you. I would never actually hurt you badly.”

Yeah, sure, I thought. Dumb as I was, I wasn’t that dumb.

But I nodded, because that’s what I was supposed to do.

“It’s just… all that Empire stuff,” he said. “But, you know, that’s old world stuff. We can leave all that behind us.”

“Yeah… sure.”

“That… thing,” said the ugly one, “he seems to like you. So maybe… you can tell him not to hurt us?”

Oh… I knew I needed to say something here.

Long, confident speeches were hard for me without theatrical flair, but I clenched my jaw. This was important.

“Sure, I will,” I said. “But… don’t try to bullshit Uul. He… he’ll know. And he’ll kill you. You get him everything he asked for, yes? Then he’ll give you the antidote. I… I’ll tell him not to hurt you.”

“Yes! Yes, of course!” they both said at once.

“We’ll get everything!” Linguth added. “You can count on us.”

They looked… like they were afraid of me. That was a first. And weird. And I hated it.

“Also…” I added. “Don’t… don’t tell anybody.”

They nodded quickly.

That’s right. I was working for the psycho furball now. Because he terrified me, and because I had no one else to turn to. Not really. So now, I had to think about his interests.

“I’ll wait here,” I said quietly. “Bring everything back… here.”

That way, if they tried to return with guards, I could run ahead to warn Uul. Not that I thought they’d dare. They were just as scared of him as I was.

I waited alone, sitting on the damp grass.

Hours passed. Darkness crept in.

And I was scared. Really scared.

Scared the whole town would come with torches and pitchforks to kill us. Scared Uul would lose patience and order the snakes to eat me. Scared of my own fucking shadow.

I hated this. I hated being hungry, poor, cold, hated, afraid… and miserable. All the goddamn time!

I was a dumb and useless Empire girl, but I didn’t deserve this. I didn’t do anything to deserve this.

Somehow, sometime—I would change it.

Then I saw it. A wagon. Two figures. It was them. It looked like they’d pulled it off.

I was… a little surprised. They were dumb people, too. I guess the intestine-melting threat really did work magic on dumb people like us.

“It’s all here!” said Linguth. “Bread, salted meat, even fruits! Surely enough for two weeks—maybe more!”

He handed me a small sack.

“Ten gold in here,” he said. “They’ll kill us if they find out.”

“…Good,” I mumbled, counting the coins. “Where’s the… uh… map?”

“Oh. Right.” He handed me a folded paper, which I checked.

“Let’s go,” I said, and we began hauling the wagon back toward the clearing.

When we arrived, Uul gave the wagon a casual glance, then turned to me.

“Well?”

“It’s all here,” I said.

“Very good,” Uul replied. He hovered over, and with his tail, placed a small, round, brown beads in my hand.

“The cure,” he said simply.

I brought it to the two jerks. They snatched it and popped it into their mouths immediately.

“I thank you for your cooperation,” Uul said. “But before you go… one small matter.”

They stiffened.

“I don’t need you anymore,” he said calmly. “But you did try to kill Shooya. And Shooya is my friend.”

“No, no, no,” they stammered. “We were joking! Please—”

“We’ll let Shooya decide,” said Uul, turning to me. “With a single command, this horned snake will bite off their heads. It will be swift. They likely won’t feel pain. Would you like that Shooya?”

“No…” I mumbled. “Please… don’t.”

“Hmm.” Uul considered this. “Oh well. Then you two are free.”

“Th–thank you!” they muttered, slowly backing away… then sprinted like lunatics.

“Now,” said Uul, “tie the wagon to two of the horned snakes. The other two will ride inside. When the front ones get tired, we’ll switch.”

I did as instructed, then joined Uul and the snakes inside the wagon.

It was… very crowded. I ended up crumpled awkwardly on top of one of the horned snakes.

But I must say—those snakes had incredibly soft, fuzzy fur. Warm. Comfortable.

“Is the cure… real?” I asked after a moment.

“The cure?” Uul mused. “Just some seeds I picked up. But the ‘special snake venom’ was a lie too.”

He made a low growl that sounded disturbingly like a chuckle. “Intestine-melting… really.”

“So… they’ll be fine?”

“Unless those seeds I picked up happen to be poisonous,” he said and chuckle again. “Now, give me the map. Let’s plan our route.”

I passed him the map. He grabbed it with the tip of his tail, examining it with interest.

“Hmmm… your town is called Riluan?”

“Yes,” I said.

“And south of here… Merezeth?”

“Yes. Our capital.”

“Ah,” Uul murmured. “I don’t wish to visit another of your towns. If we head southeast, we’ll eventually reach Sin-Irsh Kingdom. That’s… the bird-people kingdom, correct?”

“Ah… I don’t know. I’m sorry.” There was a limit to how useful dumb little me could be when it came to general knowledge—and it wasn’t exactly hard to hit that limit.

“I believe it is,” said Uul. “From what my masters learned, bird-people inhabit that region. I wish for us to travel there. Unlike your species, I can control them. And so, advance our plan.”

“Okay… but isn’t that far?”

“If the terrain is not too inhospitable, and my snakes maintain pace, we will arrive in four to five days.”

“Oh. Okay.”

I was exhausted. The snake fur was like warm velvet. The wagon rocked gently along the road.

I drifted into sleep.

We rode for days.

Uul liked to talk — a lot. And with no one else to talk to, he talked to me a lot.

He went on and on about species, psychology, evolution, mental control, and—of course—the superiority of whatever ancient species he came from. I didn’t really listen. But I got the gist. He thought everyone else was trash.

But the journey itself? Not bad. The weather was fine, the terrain not awful.And most importantly: for the first time in forever, I wasn’t hungry, wasn’t cold, and wasn’t being hated by everyone around me.

In fact… I actually started to enjoy my travel companions.

Obviously, I mean the snakes.

I named them, talked to them theatrically, told them about my tragic life in long, overdramatic monologues. They were wonderful listeners — truly a perfect audience for my dramatic talents.

So yeah. Despite everything, I was… kind of okay.I even got good at ignoring Uul unless he gave an order or asked something important.

After five days, we arrived at a small bird-folk town.

We couldn’t bring my snake friends in with us, sadly.They were considered monsters here — NPCs would attack them on sight. So we built a little makeshift camp just outside the town. They looked at me like puppies when we left.

I promised to come back. Gave each of them a dramatic forehead touch goodbye.

Then me and Uul headed into the town.

It was pretty.

Small, but clean. Not too different from my old town, though the buildings here had a different architecture — lots of smooth stone and twisting metal arches.

And, weirdly enough, every NPC here—the guards, the shopkeepers, the young and the old—were bird people. Not a single normal human with carapaces in sight. It was truly a sight to behold!

An understanding slowly hit me. The players here… they weren’t my world at all. They came from entirely different planet—with its own nations, technologies, histories, and empires.

No one here had even heard of my planet—let alone the stupid Zorthican Empire.

No one here would hate me… well, not until they actually got to know me, at least.

I could… maybe… make friends here.

I hoped that whatever Uul had planned, it wouldn’t bring pain to the bird people.I didn’t want to help hurt others. Not like my family probably did.

And in that moment, I wondered: when the time came — if it came — would I find the courage to stand up to him?

I hoped I would.

“NPCs from all species are fundamentally the same,” Uul explained. “They simply look different. They treat players uniformly, regardless of origin.”

“Oh,” I said. That made a weird kind of sense.

“What we are looking for,” he continued, “bird-person player. I cannot take control of an NPC. Their minds are too… rigid. Different from what I was trained to interface with.”

“Okay.”

“My masters could likely manage it,” he said. “Given time and practice. But even they required direct experimentation to develop the proper control spores. So they could not grant such an ability to a mere agent like myself.”

“I see…” I said, pretending to understand.

“The challenge,” Uul said, “is that identifying player bird-folk is not straightforward.”

Basically, he didn’t have a clue how to find one.

The solution was simple. Uul was dumb sometimes.

“Just walk… in crowded places,” I mumbled. “The player will talk to us.”

Uul paused. His floating puffball body made a small, thoughtful spin.

“Such an obvious insight,” Uul said, smiling. “Impressive — for someone of your mental limitations.”

“Thanks…” I muttered.

“Listen,” Uul said, “we’ll pretend I’m your tame monster. NPCs are usually tolerant of tamed creatures, especially harmless-looking ones. They won’t suspect I’m a player from another continent. If asked, say you’re a merchant surveying shops for a bug-people trading group from Merezeth.”

“I’m… not good at lying,” I mumbled.

“Oh, just do as you’re told,” he said flatly. “Don’t worry — I’m releasing calming spores as we speak. It will make any nearby players more relaxed, more trusting, and less… subspecies-minded.”

I didn’t know what “subspecies-minded” meant.

We wandered through the bustling market. NPCs ignored us, but after a few minutes, my plan worked.

A group of three bird-people approached — and with them was a creature unlike anything I’d ever seen.

It stood upright, soft-looking. No fur, no scales, no feathers — just naked pale, pinkish skin with clothes. Only its head had long, bright yellow fur. Its limbs were bare, with strange club-like hands and many tiny, wriggly fingers.

It looked so fragile, like something that had just shed its shell. I actually blushed — it was the cutest thing I’d ever seen.

“This is the naked builder species,” Uul whispered, his tone surprisingly excited. “My masters were very interested in them, as they resemble the builder species that once lived on their old world. They believed these might be even better builders than the bird people. Finding one here is a great stroke of luck!”

“It… is?” I asked. It looked way too cute for building stuff.

“Yes,” Uul said with a wide, toothy grin. “Our plans can progress even faster now.”
