She who had once been Endless Templar was perhaps no less impressive in humanoid form: cat-like golden eyes, a striking reptilian face, green-blue scales framed around rippling muscles, a tail — which was new, as Samantha remembered it — and a head full of wriggling, writhing black snakes. Despite what she was, no one was looking away.

In the spirit world, the gorgon appeared very similar to the natural, but the serpentine elements were more pronounced and resonated with a blatant aura. Samantha didn’t have much to compare it to, but it felt dragonish. She didn’t have a conventional ‘third eye,’ but instead the eyes of her snakes were a kind of unique amalgam of one. Through them, Samantha understood, the entity could gaze into many places at once, and even portray a separate face.

She’s like a hydra.

Nyomel seemed paralyzed by the new arrival, eyes as wide as they could go. She looked as if she wanted to throw herself at the being’s feet, but instead, she simply fell to her knees as she stared on, in awe, shaking.

The others also seemed a bit stunned.

Keinauz was perhaps the least put off, though he remained quite guarded. He called softly, “Hail and well met. Firstly, I must ask, are you friend or foe? That was quite an entrance, and leaves us all somewhat unsure, you see.”

The gorgon paused and blinked at him. “Friend and ally, but if you want to spar, I’m always willing.” She grinned predatorily. “And have much to teach.”

“Hmm. And your name?”

“I don’t have one yet.” She looked to the goddess above. “She who made the way and called me down has that answer.”

Slowly floating down until she was in front of the gorgon, Samantha first said, “I don’t remember a tail.”

The gorgon gazed up at her, tail flicking around, and replied, “It would’ve been unfair in the duel.”

Samantha chuckled, then declared loudly, “This is Six Moons Falling.” After a pause, she muttered, “Six for short.”

The newly named being smiled rather rapturously. Somehow, there was even more of that heat in her eyes, which burned more in the spirit, fully-formed. “Indeed. A perfect name to mark a vengeance long in the coming.”

“Now you’re speaking my language,” Keinauz said.

“Vengeance for whom?” a minotaur asked.

“No mere clan or people, Child of the Mountains,” Six answered, “nor even those purely of this world, as corruption has touched all things. I seek vindication for every soul degraded by the Destroyer, until he is utterly spent and defeated. I also do so in honor of my father, Avenging Leviathan, whose vendetta is more specifically for our kind — serpents, dragons, naga, and like kin, almost all of them wiped from the world or devolved from glory.”

Samantha felt the Ring of the Cheater’s naga-face do an emphatic ‘okay’ hand sign to these words.

There were many respectful nods among the gathered, and something more like bows from the minotaurs, as well as whispered prayers. Samantha figured the minotaurs had her pegged as a demigod, which perhaps made her holy to them.

Being deemed holy is always a good icebreaker. I have experience there!

Dax asked, “Can you do the meteor thing again, by the way?”

Six asked in response, “Can you return to the womb?”

There was no further reply.

Turning to the group as a whole, Samantha commanded, “Let’s get to work if we have something useful to do. If not, we can find something. Scavenging decent gear off the battlefield, for instance. And I know we need to make some sort of camp. Let’s fall back and build around the structures made for the healers. Attention to defense, with full precautions. We don’t want to get caught slacking if our enemies send new strike teams from somewhere.”

“There are ancient catacombs uncovered in the crater,” Six commented. “You’ll probably want to check those out.”

Wearily, Samantha shook her head. “Later. Or I’ll just leave it to Azure entirely.”

“What about them?” Dax asked, nodding out across the field.

Samantha turned her mirrors, focusing on the great mass of undead. Most remained idle. A few seemed to be fighting each other, but it wasn’t catching on except in meager pockets. “Hell if I know. Destroy them, maybe?”

Ash’s voice popped into the gestalt. “If I may be so bold, Mother, could we cordon them off behind fences for now? They should behave like animals without any directive governing them. We may be able to utilize them akin to golems.”

“How would that work?” Dax asked. “There are tens of thousands of them out there, and we don’t specialize in necromantic control.”

“Well, the control mechanisms are still lying in the dirt, are they not?”

Everyone turned to see the sword and the handle of the whip that the wraith general had been using, still lying there, along with various pieces of armor.

Samantha scoffed. “Fine. Do as Ash says regarding them, and we’ll make use of them if this shit works for it somehow. They’re just meat. I imagine the spirits that moved on wouldn’t mind serving the one who killed all their traitorous masters. Except for the truly stupidest of loyalists, anyway.”

Odysseus said, “I’ll throw my support behind this, if it counts for anything. You would be correct for most of the men I knew. They didn’t sign up for this, and would cherish spitting in the eyes of the ones who stabbed them in the back. Aside from that… these remnants remain soldiers. Let them serve as their whole selves once did. Perhaps you could use them as sentries and guards to free up the living, especially for out-of-the-reach zones that need coverage.”

“Sounds good to me, Oddy. Consider your wishes granted. They should become less and less recognizable, but if anyone has a problem being around a particular one or the other, because they knew them or something, we’ll deal with it case-by-case.”

Everyone slowly got into motion. Someone brought Six a robe, and she wandered off, looking around rather wide-eyed in all directions (at once) and deeply curious, like an amnesiac who just woke up might — or an adult newborn.

Shaking her head at the continual oddities that came into her life, Samantha floated up above it all and finally was able to turn her attention to her waiting notices and completed quests, and the gains therein.
