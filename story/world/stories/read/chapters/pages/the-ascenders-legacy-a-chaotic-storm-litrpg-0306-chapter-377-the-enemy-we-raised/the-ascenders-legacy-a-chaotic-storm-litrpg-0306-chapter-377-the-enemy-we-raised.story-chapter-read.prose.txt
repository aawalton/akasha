Quinghe Arena.

Central Kingdom. 2275.

Daruk stood atop one of the Quinghe towers, looking down at the millions gathered below— people from every corner of Lutia who had come to watch the continental tournament—hundreds of the continent's finest awakened battle for supremacy.

He looked over the vast arena, at the suffocating concentration of energy pooling in the air, and felt nothing stir within him.

No excitement. No fear.

He breathed deeply and closed his eyes, feeling the wind curl softly around him.

The continental tournament had begun two weeks ago, and for the last three months—ever since its announcement—it had grown into a continent wide obsession.

Unlike the usual annual academy tournaments, this one wasn’t just a competition among academies, but kingdoms. Academies from all over Lutia—Ragnarok, Unoros, Calodan, and Sunstone had all sent their best students to represents them.

This time, however, the brackets weren’t divided by class year, but by sigmas.

Sigma 25-39. Sigma 40-59. Sigma 60-74.

Each academy had been tasked with sending six students—two from each sigma—and when the call first came, Daruk had thought he had finally found something capable of rousing him again.

After all that had happened with Abyssos and the cultists, the 5th Academy had been desperate to prove itself. To do that, they needed victory. And victory required their absolute best.

But with Aodhán gone, the hierarchy of the first Sigma had been ripped apart.

Cyrus had taken immediate advantage of the vacuum, forcing his name to the top of every available list—the tower rankings, forge points, and most recently, the simulation leaderboards—until Daruk had knocked him down on all of them.

He had only managed to hold onto the position for a day before he too had been knocked down. Not to second, or third, but fifth.

Andrew, Yurin, Cyrus, and Cruella Vaelys.

Those four had shoved him to fifth place, and in those first few weeks, Daruk had felt something close to excitement.

Competition had driven him the same way it had driven Aodhán, setting a fire beneath his feet.

With single minded focus, he had trained, meditated, fought against his friends and beside them, refining himself until, inevitably, he became too strong for any of them to defeat.

And when the selection trials had come again, he had fought his way up, even through the second years—his strength having grown colder and sharper in the last few months.

Even working together, none within his sigma could pull him down.

That was when the thrill had died.

Because with every victory came the same quiet certainty: He was unrivaled because Aodhán wasn’t here.

He knew it in his bones.

Wherever Aodhán was right now, he had left him behind.

He was second best. Yet here in Lutia, they hailed him as the strongest.

Even now, they chanted his name, their roar shaking the arena as the announcer called his match.

"Daruk Brystion of Ragnarok against Lorelia Vermont of Sunstone."

It was the last semi-final battle in the Sigma25 bracket. If he won, he would advance to the finals and face Herod Descarpes, grandson of the Chief of Calodan himself.

And if he won that…

It would be a great achievement, the greatest of his lifetime. He would be bestowed with a continental title.

A Hero of Ragnarok.

But Daruk just couldn't bring himself to care.

He had no desire to stand in that ring and pretend all was right with the world, or that making Ragnarok proud was something he held sacred.

Nothing drove him anymore.

And so he remained where he was, coat flapping in the wind, waiting for the officials to disqualify him for failing to appear.

His loss would disappoint so many people—Principal Zatya, the 5th Academy, and even his own parents.

The last of those he regretted. The rest, he found he could live with.

"Daruk?" A voice echoed from behind him and he turned to see Cruella, her raven-dark hair dancing in the breeze. No doubt she had come to do what she had done so often these past weeks—drag him back toward the ring.

Daruk exhaled slowly. "I'm tired, Cruella. This kingdom… this continent… it's lost whatever appeal it once had."

Cruella said nothing for a moment, then nodded. "You want to leave. To join Aodhán."

"It's less about joining Aodhán and more about growing. I'm not growing here."

“That’s a lie,” Cruella shook her head. “You’ve grown so powerful these past few months. Far more than you ever did when Aodhán was here. You cannot deny it.”

Daruk opened his mouth, then closed it again.

How could he explain what had no shape? How could he explain this feeling of error that came over him every time he advanced? How could he explain the looming sense of a limit despite doing everything right?

How could he explain knowing without doubt that if Aodhán was here, he would have advanced even further? Grown even stronger?

Eventually, he shook his head and sighed. "You don't understand."

"But I do," Cruella said softly. "I hear your thoughts, Daruk. I feel your fears. And I'm telling you—they're lying to you."

She stepped closer.

"You are not failing. You are not walking the wrong road. You are not in danger of the limit."

Daruk closed his eyes.

He wanted to believe her. He simply didn't know how.

Cruella moved behind him, her hands sliding up his back before curling around his chest to rest on his heart.

He leaned into her touch before he could stop himself.

"Daruk," she whispered. "You're on the right path."

His shoulders loosened.

Then she sighed. "But even if you aren't, you'll never leave Lutia by standing up here."

He went still.

"You need to finish this tournament," she said. "You need to win. That is what will put you before the Calamities. That is what might open the gates you want opened."

Daruk's jaw tightened. "I know."

"Then why hesitate?"

He looked down at the arena below. "Because I hate this."

Cruella said nothing.

"I hate the cheering. I hate the praise." His mouth twisted. "I hate the way they treat me, knowing how quickly they would have crucified my brother."

His gaze hardened. "To go down there and entertain them… to reward them with a spectacle after what they are…" He shook his head. "I can't."

Cruella's expression sobered. "And yet sometimes you must dine with the devil to get what you need."

She turned his face to hers and slowly pressed her lips to his.

Daruk kissed her back, melting into the softness of her. He felt his anger thaw, his chest ease, and when she finally pulled back, he was panting.

"Come with me," he breathed. "When I leave… come with me."

Cruella smiled. "If that's going to be possible, you have to win first."

Daruk stared at her for a long moment, then turned back toward the arena. They were still chanting his name.

His opponent, Lorelia, looked visibly irritated.

Daruk felt the same.

Taking a slow breath, he stepped off the tower, a sleek ice armor forming around him even as he fell. He used no platform. He didn't brace for impact.

He simply dropped.

His feet slammed into the battle-ring an instant later, a cascade of Memory-Ice erupting outward in a glittering burst.

The crowd roared. His opponent spun.

"Fight!"

Lorelia lunged immediately, a river of bladed shadows rushing to engulf him.

Daruk barely moved. Barely breathed.

He simply blinked, and the rushing darkness faltered to a sudden halt as Memory-Ice drained every battle memory from Lorelia’s mind in a single, merciless instant.

She staggered, her eyes growing vacant as every instinct and hard-won reflex was ripped away so suddenly that even standing became foreign.

Her knees buckled. And an instant later, she collapsed.

The crowd erupted instantly into thunderous cheers, mistaking his cruelty for spectacle.

Daruk sighed, then placed the memory crystal gently against her chest.

What was he doing here?

***

High above the arena, atop the Quinghe tower Daruk had just vacated, Principal Zatya stood a few feet behind Cruella, a tired expression etched across her face.

For a moment, neither of them spoke as they watched the crowd roar and scream in excitement below.

Then, slowly, Zatya shook her head. “Do you think you can encourage him to the finish line?”

Cruella sighed. “I don’t know. He’s very hard to... convince.”

Zatya grimaced, but before she could speak, Cruella continued.

“One thing, however, is certain.” She glanced at Zatya. “The longer Aodhán stays gone, the more of himself Daruk will lose.”

Her gaze drifted back to the arena below, where Daruk stood with his head bowed while thousands chanted his name.

Her expression tightened.

“In the end, we may not have groomed a champion at all, but an enemy.” She paused. “An enemy who knows far too much.”
