The sun had already passed its highest point when Riley gave the order to move out. She walked at the head of the march with a hatchet attached to her belt and a small pack tight against her shoulders. Behind her stretched two neat lines of soldiers: forty archers carrying tall bows and full quivers, and forty infantry soldiers gripping shields and short spears. Their boots struck the hard-packed dirt road in a steady rhythm that carried far across the open fields as they closed in on the tree line.

Four horses followed on long leads; their saddles empty except for the large canvas bags lashed to each side. The bags hung limp and flat, waiting to be filled on the return journey. At the rear of the column, two bound raiders trudged along under guard, wrists tied in front of them and ankles linked by short ropes that forced them to take careful steps. Their eyes full of fear wondering if this would be their last day.

Riley kept her gaze fixed on the path ahead and the HUD map that pointed her toward the raiders’ compound.

The weight of the day's final checks still sat heavy in her chest. The archers carried buckets of the thick black tar mixture she had prepared. Every bucket had been tested before they left the gates. Now the only thing left was the march itself and what waited at the end of it.

No one spoke much, the soldiers saved their breath for the road, their expressions set with the determination to rescue their brothers held captive and to settle a score that was long over due. Riley's face showed nothing but stoic determination with her thoughts on her men being held by the raiders that made their living spreading misfortune. She adjusted the strap of her pack and lengthened her stride, matching the pace she had set for the army she led.

The afternoon light filtered through the forest canopy, stretching long shadows across the leaf-covered ground as they continued forward. The horses plodded along with their empty saddlebags swaying gently at their sides.

The infantry soldiers carried long pikes balanced across their shoulders and large rectangular shields strapped to their left arms. The archers walked with buckets of the thick tar mixture swinging from their hands, the dark substance inside giving off a faint sharp smell that drifted on the breeze. Every soldier wore a hard sword at their hip, the blades ready for the close work that might come once the fighting grew tight. Several men and women near the front held lit torches low at their sides, the flames flickering steadily as they moved.

Thirty minutes from the raider's compound, Riley raised her hand and the entire group halted without a word. She turned to face her people and motioned them closer until they formed a tight half circle around her. The soldiers leaned in, their faces serious under the late afternoon light.

"Normally I would give this address just before we would charge into battle," she said, her voice low but clear enough for every ear. "But we need to approach quietly."

She paused and met their eyes one by one.

"These are the raiders that attacked, and wounded or killed many of you. You had no weapons and they had the element of surprise. Inside the compound they are holding Valrik and another of our brothers. You have waited too long to right this injustice. When I give the signal after the gate is breached, we enter with the roar, a roar of vengeance, because today it will be ours."

The soldiers nodded, their grips tightening on weapons and shields. A few exchanged glances that carried grim understanding. Riley watched the resolve settle deeper into their postures before she gave a single sharp nod and turned back toward the road. The soldiers reformed to march in silence and began the final approach to the raiders' compound.

The army halted again ten minutes from the compound beside a small stand of young trees. Several soldiers stepped forward and stripped the branches from one straight trunk with quick strokes of their knives then sawed down the remaining trunk. They lifted the bare log between them and carried it along as they resumed the march. The added weight slowed their pace, but the trunk would serve as a battering ram against the main gate. Gathering it this close to the target had saved them from hauling it the entire distance from the settlement.

Riley glanced at her HUD and sent a silent command. Three pairs of soldiers slipped away from the group and melted into the underbrush ahead. The rest of the force waited in the lengthening shadows, breathing quietly. When the scouts returned a short while later, they delivered their report in low voices.

Riley pulled Thorne aside and spoke close to his ear. "We need two groups. One large and the other smaller. The smaller will circle until they reach the west side of the compound to cover the small door and the large group stays at the main gate. Tie off those two idiots behind us." She pointed toward the gagged raider prisoners who shuffled at the rear of the group.

She could have assigned the formations through the HUD, but she wanted Thorne to gain experience giving verbal commands to the army. In a larger battle one day that skill might prove vital if the system ever failed or if silence became necessary.

Thorne gave a firm nod. He turned to the waiting soldiers and began issuing quiet orders, dividing the force with clear gestures. The soldiers moved efficiently into their new positions while the horses stood patiently with their empty saddlebags still swaying gently at their sides.

The soldiers adjusted their positions under Thorne’s quiet direction. Riley stepped closer to him again and spoke in a low voice that carried only to his ears.

"The large group at the front gate will have the bulk of the archers fan out and use the trees as cover. Line the bulk of the infantry in a V formation with their large shields in front in a defensive stance."

She paused briefly as she scanned the area, then continued.

"The remaining group of archers and infantry will use the testudo formation you have been drilling. They will approach the gate in formation and do what we talked about."

Thorne nodded once more, his expression focused.

Riley pulled him in close so that only he could hear her next words. "This will be our first battle together. Make it glorious so we all can honor the great hero you are afterwards."

Thorne looked down for a moment. Then he raised his gaze to meet Riley’s eyes directly. "The glory will be yours. You are our leader."

With that he broke off and moved among the troops, organizing them with crisp hand signals and low commands. The soldiers shifted smoothly into place. Archers slipped behind tree trunks and low bushes, nocking arrows but keeping their bows lowered for now. Infantry formed the wide V at the front, shields locked edge to edge in a solid wall. The smaller group gathered into the tight testudo shell, shields overlapping above and on every side like the plates of a turtle, ready for the order to move in.

Riley stood back and watched the final adjustments, her hand resting lightly on the head of her hatchet. The compound lay just ahead, its wooden walls visible through the thinning trees. The two bound raiders remained secured at the rear, their gags tight and their eyes wide with growing unease. The horses waited calmly a short distance behind the lines, saddlebags still empty and expectant.

Riley and the army moved forward through the last stretch of trees, their steps careful and quiet. They set the heavy tree trunk down on the soft ground and broke apart into their assigned formations, shields and weapons held ready. The soldiers stood poised to advance the moment the order came.

Riley gave a quick hand signal to the group of archers positioned closest to the manned watchtower at the front gate. Those ten archers drew their special arrows; the shafts lightly wrapped in fabric soaked with the thick black tar mixture. They touched the lit torches to the wrappings until the tips caught flame, then raised their bows and took careful aim at the tower.

All at once they released. The arrows flew in a tight volley and landed with rapid, soft thumps against the wooden structure. The raider standing watch in the tower screamed as three of the arrows struck him squarely in the chest. Black smoke began to pour from the tower as the flames took hold and spread across the dry wood. The sharp smell of rotten eggs drifted strongly on the air.

A bell rang out inside the compound, its urgent clang cutting through the late afternoon quiet. Shouts and alarmed voices rose from within the walls as the raiders reacted to the sudden fire and the scream from their lookout.

Riley kept her eyes fixed on the gate ahead, her grip tightening on her hatchet. The testudo formation stood ready beside the makeshift battering ram, while the main force waited in their V shape behind the tree cover. The first blows of the attack had landed, and the compound was waking to the threat.
