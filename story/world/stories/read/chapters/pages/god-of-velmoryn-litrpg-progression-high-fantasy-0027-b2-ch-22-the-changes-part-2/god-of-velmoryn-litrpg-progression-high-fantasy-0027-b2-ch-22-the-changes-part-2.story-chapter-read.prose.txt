The first thing that caught my attention was my new Rank, but since I already knew its name from the Guidance after looking into the Goddess’s memory, I chose not to linger on it. Raising Ranks was important, but I doubted its name was something unique to gods. It seemed more like a measure of development, a way to show how much strength and experience a deity possessed.

What mattered far more were the Divinity Points and the Authority. But before I could move on, I noticed that my [Status] had changed. It no longer said I was weakened or recovering. For the first time, my condition was listed as normal.

Is that why I feel so much better? I didn’t realize I had been exhausted all this time. Compared to before, I feel so much more energized…

Perhaps that improvement came not from the change in [Status], but from the fact that my form had finally moved closer to an actual body instead of a drifting mass of mist. Either way, I was certainly better off than before raising my Rank.

What wasn’t better, however, were my Divinity Points. Before losing consciousness, I had 10 000 of them. That was how I managed to raise my rank in the first place. But after awakening the Angel, I had less than five thousand left.

Well, at least this confirms that dropping below the required threshold doesn’t cause demotion.

Losing my new Rank would have been catastrophic, especially because it might have prevented me from gaining complete Velmoryn Authority, which was the only thing that could ensure no god would ever tamper with my believers again.

Also, it wasn’t like I had wasted those 5000 Divinity Points. I had gained Authority and Aurelion in exchange, and while I wasn’t yet certain whether he was worth that cost, I believed that even in the worst case I hadn’t lost much.

Let’s see if my complete Authority feels any different.

I closed my eyes and tried to focus on the Velmoryns closest to the Crimson Guardian.

I sensed Mel immediately. She was happily playing hide and seek with the other children, hiding behind one of the giant roots that rose from the earth and easily covered her entire body.

Then I extended my focus farther, toward the Velmoryn with whom I shared the strongest connection. That link was always the easiest to notice. Whenever I concentrated on the bonds between me and my believers, I felt countless threads. Among them there was always one that stood out, the one that felt the most solid and shone the brightest.

But this time there were two.

One thread led to the temple, where Tekla was already busy with her daily work. The other stretched deep into the forest and connected to Lyle. She walked among the hunting party beneath the towering trees. But instead of carrying meat from a kill, a line of strange beasts followed behind them, bound by ropes.

Only then did I realize I was seeing into the places where my believers stood without relying on the Window. I couldn’t see everything, only a small portion around them, but it was still an impressive new ability.

This is interesting.

Not only was it convenient that I no longer needed to constantly shift the Window as before, but I could now check on my believers with nothing more than a thought, and nothing could be faster than that.

Still, that was not the only advantage. The Window had always been limited. I could observe only two places at once, and since the skill was already at its maximum, I doubted it would ever improve. But now, with the ability to observe the area around my believers directly, if I created more clones similar to Red Grima, I would be able to monitor all of them at the same time. It would be invaluable in the future. Not just for making sure they were safe, but for creating the best scouting network anyone could hope for.

Wait… what if I try to connect with the Blue Tribe members? Some of them should still be Velmoryns. If I can see where they are and what they’re planning…

I focused on the threads again. Despite having complete control over them, I had no way to filter them by specific criteria. And before trying to sense the Blue Tribe, I needed to confirm whether this new ability even worked on Velmoryns who were not my believers.

To my surprise, identifying the nonbelievers was easy. Their threads were thin, dull, and fragile. Just looking at them made me think they could snap at any moment.

I went through each one. With the process relying only on the speed of my thoughts, it took less than a minute. Besides, there were not many. In total, only around two thousand Velmoryns were not my believers.

And among them, none belonged to the Blue Tribe. Or rather, none who had followed Akrion.

As expected. It wouldn’t be that simple…

The result wasn’t surprising. What I didn’t expect, however, was to find Velmoryns who had been living outside the forest. Unfortunately, there were only five and two of them seemed to be a couple.

One was sleeping soundly in a building constructed from finely carved white stone. The other cleaned a small cabin aboard a ship, and judging by the situation around him, he was either a low-ranking member of the crew or a simple passenger. The couple, when I focused on them, were tending the soil in front of their home, which stood at the edge of a small village. I didn’t see anyone else nearby, so I couldn’t tell whether they lived among Humans or another race entirely. But based on how lively they looked, they must’ve been enjoying their lives.

And the fifth one… he was a prisoner in a dark dungeon, and in terrible condition. He was chained to the wall. The collars on his bony wrists and ankles glowed with silver runes. Countless scars covered his body, some fresh and bleeding, others hardened into old marks. His long, messy silver hair was smeared with mud and grime, hanging over a body where every rib could be counted. Beneath his bushy beard I could make out pale lips and hollow cheeks.

If he survives long enough, I might try to save him after the Night God issue is resolved.

They weren’t useful to me at that moment, but I was glad that my believers now allowed me to see into four regions beyond my forest.

Then I returned to my Status Window, and only then did I notice that the stat which had been covered before, showing only strange symbols, now displayed [Radiance]. Without a second thought, I activated Guidance.

And it didn’t even take Divinity Points from me…

Not only had Guidance become free, but the information it offered was far more detailed than before. Until now it had usually given me only what the system wanted me to know, nudging me toward a direction of its choosing. Now it finally felt like a skill worthy of its name, even if it was still trying to influence my decisions.

So why is my Radiance pure? Did it have something to do with the Goddess? Or was I simply that lucky?

I tried using Guidance again.

[Apologies, Verde. Such matters can only be explored by deities themselves.]

Well, I already knew I was asking too much.

The system clearly wanted me to enter the Pantheon, and I fully intended to do so, but only after I gathered more information and confirmed that it was absolutely safe.

Before anything else, however, I wanted to check my new skill - the one the system claimed had been chosen specifically for me and apparently was also unique.

[Astral Rebirth (Passive) – Unique]

Every deity is born with a trait that distinguishes them from others, yet a rare few awaken abilities no god has ever possessed. Astral Rebirth was one such anomaly - originally allowing you to split and regenerate your astral body. After your Rank increased, the skill evolved into its final form.

You may now transfer your main consciousness into any of your astral bodies, provided all conditions are met:

• The chosen astral body must be capable of containing a deity’s consciousness. • It must already hold 10,000 Divinity Points. (Its Rank cannot be below Awakened Divinity.) • The consciousness residing within must willingly perish.

Limitation: Astral Rebirth may be used once per year. After each transfer, you will enter a year-long slumber.

So other gods can’t regenerate their bodies the way I do? Is that why it took the Goddess years to create Avenor’s body, while all I need is to split a part of myself and that alone is enough?

The first part of the information answered more questions than I expected. I had always wondered why gods didn’t have many vessels, especially those who had existed for centuries. Why hadn’t they created hundreds of them and taken over the mortal realm with overwhelming force?

Now I had my answer. They simply couldn’t.

The realization made me proud of my skill, especially the part that allowed me to transfer my consciousness from one vessel to another. But I wasn’t sure whether any of my vessels would willingly die for me if it came to that. Unless I created a new one with limited thought and a single purpose - sacrifice. That would be simple enough to arrange.

It would serve as an escape route, a way out if my existence was threatened and running was the only path to survival.

This skill might save my life one day.

I was truly pleased with this gain, but it also brought a new source of worry with it.

If I had a skill that seemed overpowered, then other gods likely had their own equivalents. And the worst part was that I couldn’t anticipate any of them.

I will have to enter the Pantheon and learn more about deities. Now I understand that acing the Night God without understanding gods better is far too dangerous... Maybe I can even find an alliance there.

The idea of entering the Pantheon no longer felt so undesirable. Still, before stepping into that place, I needed to settle the matters at hand, and one of those was finally understanding what my Angel was capable of.
