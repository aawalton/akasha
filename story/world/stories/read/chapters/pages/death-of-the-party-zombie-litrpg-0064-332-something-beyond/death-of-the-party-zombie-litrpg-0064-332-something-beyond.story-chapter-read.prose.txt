In a small cottage in the goblin village, Sally paced around her kitchen, a stormy scowl on her face. “This wait is killing me,” she huffed.

Theo, by contrast, sat leaning back in a chair by the table. His eyes were closed, hands behind his head. After exhaling through his nose, he opened an eye to address the frustrated zombie. “Nothing is happening. Archie will call us in once they’ve read through the data. Best you occupy yourself with something else.”

She turned on him as if she were going to snap back at him, but instead clicked her fingers. “Fine, Fangs. I’m going to go look through our shared Storage to see if we’ve got anything useful.”

Before he could object, she had already stomped out of the room. His eyes followed the sound of her movement as she went up the stairs to their bedroom. With a slight grimace, he held his breath and waited for the inevitable-

“Theo!”

The vampire sighed and reluctantly left the table to join Sally upstairs. He found her crouched at the end of the bed, with the lid of the wide metal chest cracked open an inch so that the System let her see the contents. She gestured wildly.

“Look, I know I'm not exactly on top of Inventory management, but what is this?”

Theo leaned against the doorframe. “Well, anytime I wanted to take a stroll and find a new item, or test my skills, I'd just dump most of my loot in there without really thinking about it once I got back.”

“Pup, you’ve done that every other day. For almost three years.” Sally gestured again at the case. “I'm surprised it didn't hit a stack overflow error or bug out. There are thousands of consumables here. Enough to outfit an army.”

“What a coincidence, given our circumstances. Time for a yard sale, maybe?”

Sally moved her jaw from side to side as her mind turned over. “We have a lot of calls to make. No more saving for the sequel.”

The first character to prod was Bully. After taking stock of everything, all potions and potential ingredients went to the Alchemist’s Guild. Not only were they in charge of the distribution of most consumables, but they’d have a few days to craft whatever the world could provide them.

With the call to arms now in effect, every capable soul was out doing their part for the war effort.

Mundane weapons and armor went to every barracks and guard station across the world for outfitting anyone who needed them. Various craftspeople could break down anything not useful for material parts.

“Half of this stuff is either inert or has changed function since storage,” Sally complained. “Thanks to several System changes. I have a bow artefact here which just says ‘this is a bow’. Who knows what it used to do.”

“Makes me a little sad that Chuck is unable to develop firearms, even with Ren’s as a blueprint.” Theo leaned back against the bed. “If… STAR+ worlds become common, some will surely progress in technology.”

“I’m on the fence.” She wrinkled up her face. “If they’re a novelty on specific worlds, then it might be a nice change of pace. Being common throughout the Sea would really harsh this fantasy theme we’re accustomed to.”

“Max said he fought a guy with chainsword arms, from the Sci-Fi Othea. I think our immersion is doomed to be broken, the more we…”

Sally closed the lid of the Storage so that she could address the vampire directly. “Now that you’ve had some time to decompress, how do you feel about the future?”

“After the war? Same thing as we always do. Go get into trouble with the excuse of protecting people.”

“Not ready to settle down and start a family?”

Theo tilted his head. “I think there might be a day when I want to retire, when our work is done. Are you having second thoughts?”

“One day, maybe when the burning desire for adventure is over. Even though the STAR+ makes babies possible, I don't think they're possible for us.”

“Because we're undead, or because we're genocidal and insane?”

“Yeah.” Sally grinned and stuck her foot out to prod at the vampire. “Adopting might be the best bet anyway, as a child born of the Aspects of Life and Death would either be a total Mary-Sue, or a Gohan.”

“My impeccable foresight doesn't help there.” Theo grinned. “We'll just have to see what trouble is in store for us.”

The zombie sighed and relaxed. It was probably a good thing that the war loomed in the background. Having Theo back reminded her of the good times and made her soft. Without the pressure of death and destruction, the offer of a simple life gave her more pause than usual.

Getting older and growing more mature was terrible. The threat of an eventual old-age death was both frightening and comforting. Even a resolute mind like hers wasn't built to withstand immortality.

“Do you think the other versions of Sally also found their Theo?” she asked.

“An interesting thought exercise revolving around fate. Technically, we died to travel to the Sea, so what if death is the destiny of all versions of us?”

Sally ignored his answer, smiling to herself. “I hope the other two Sallys are badass and not wasting away in a diner. We deserve the best.”

She sat back and allowed her imagination to take over while Theo returned to rummaging through Storage. It wouldn't be possible, but she enjoyed the idea of teaming up with the two other versions to fight against evil. The other Sallys probably weren't zombies, though. Would they think she was weird? Maybe their brains would taste the best. There was probably some kind of law against that.

Theo grunted, and a message came through her System at the same time as his. Sally read it briefly before shooting the vampire a tired glance.

“Looks like Archie is ready for us,” she said.

The round meeting room in the tower looked rather empty with just five occupants. An effect further compounded by the fact that other than Archie, the others sat in pairs. With a sigh, the wizard gave them a tired smile.

“If our next recruits are a blonde and brunette married couple, I might start to question how this reality works.”

Sally pulled a face. “I figured the next to join the Outsiders would be Hunter. Or… are you hiding away a long-lost flame who could be your opposite Aspect?”

“Magic is the only love in my life.” Archie furrowed his brow. “That aside, I’m not sure Aspects have to come as an opposite pairing. We’re just as likely to find someone with something unrelated to mine, I assume. As for Hunter, we will have to see.”

The zombie gave him a brief nod. Their shadowy ally hadn’t even made it to Sanctuary. She assumed he would turn up fashionably late, with a dramatic entrance to the war. Whether he wanted to become an Outsider was another question for later.

Archie cleared his throat and stood. “I will reiterate that our role against the Radoch invasion is preventing as many ships from deploying troops as possible. That means boarding their ships, being grossly outnumbered immediately, and dealing with the most powerful aliens in the fleet so that our ground forces won’t have to.”

Max rubbed his chin. “Fair to say they won’t just let us do that.”

“There are many ways to skin a cat, trust me.” Archie winced. “If they block teleportation, we will assault manually. We have tried to consider every scenario and have devised solutions. In a moment, I will take you to a secure location with several Storage lockers. One for each of you with everything we are able to provide you.”

More Inventory clutter. Sally tensed up just at the thought.

“You said the ships had vulnerable power sources,” Ren asked. “Perhaps my skills might be better utilized from ground level?”

“Chuck has converted the top of this tower into a ‘sniper’s nest’. We don’t know how long that method of attack will remain viable, but it’s an option for you if required.”

“Ah.” Theo tapped the table. “Elephant in the room, but Chuck said there were more vessels than he anticipated. Do we have any numbers to work with yet?”

Archie sucked his teeth and looked between the gathered group before responding. “Based on what little data we had originally, expectations were around a dozen to twenty ships. Now… there may be just over one hundred.”

Sally furrowed her brow. Each of the ones she had fought had thousands of Radochs on board. They could be facing more than a million bugs, outnumbering the forces on Sanctuary immensely. “Are they… all like the ones on that other world?”

He shook his head briefly. “The data isn’t solid yet, but about half are around the same size. Most of the rest are smaller, around Omen size. There are a couple that are larger. One in particular… dwarfs the size we previously encountered.”

“A mothership?” she offered.

“Given what we know of them, it could be a storage ship. A living museum, as it were.”

It wouldn’t just be a treasure vault. The Radoch collected interesting people, powerful monsters, and odd technology. Pieces of land and monuments. A massive ship full of everything could be a problem, combat-wise.

“Realistically,” Archie continued, with added weight to his voice, “up to 70% of Sanctuary’s forces will die during the first wave of Radochs. That’s with Chuck restricting airspace.”

Max grimaced. “We have some aces up our sleeves, though?”

“Several, but he is keeping them close to his chest. We wouldn’t want to promote false hope.” The wizard reached into his robe. “Although there is one I can reveal.”

He placed a slim wooden box on the table and pushed it so that it slid toward the middle. Once it slowed to a stop, the lid popped open, revealing four keys. They were a bright silver with a cut emerald inlaid at the top.

Archie gestured to the domed ceiling. “The moon is now a tangible place. Quite an impressive feat, if I do say so myself. The first and only instance of an orbiting secondary bubble of reality. It is also now the Outsiders Headquarters.”

Sally reached forward and took one of the keys.

[Lunar Fortress Key][Teleports you to the Lunar Fortress. 10 second cast time]

“You couldn’t think of a cooler name?” she asked.

“It’s a secret. If you are wondering why Chuck doesn’t even have a projection here, it’s because… we are Outsiders. It’s a step on the path toward our separation from Sanctuary. Before you ask, if things go badly on the main world, the moon also suffers. It is more just a disjointed area where we can regroup, restock, and redeploy to the battle.”

Theo took his key before pushing the box closer to the other two. “This Sea-faring mercenary group is really becoming a thing, huh?”

“I already have a mission for us once the war is over.” Archie smiled. “Assuming we survive, of course. With that in mind, please use your keys now.”

There was an awkward lull in sound as the five of them used the keys and waited for the ten seconds to pass. In short burst of pale blue light, they vanished from the room almost at the same time.

Sally turned in place in her new surroundings. A room of polished marble with soft lighting. Three short benches sat in the middle, while the left side was entirely tall lockers. Her name was plastered over the top of one, and she hopped over to it while the others still got their bearings.

The locker had three sections. One full-length metal door, and two half-length ones on top of each other. These latter two were System Storage - the top for consumables and bottom for equipment. Her eyes swept over them briefly before she turned her attention to the larger door. She grabbed the handle and popped it open, assailed by the smell of something similar to waxed leather.

Her eyes widened as they ran over the contents hanging within.

“A new uniform,” she whispered in awe.
