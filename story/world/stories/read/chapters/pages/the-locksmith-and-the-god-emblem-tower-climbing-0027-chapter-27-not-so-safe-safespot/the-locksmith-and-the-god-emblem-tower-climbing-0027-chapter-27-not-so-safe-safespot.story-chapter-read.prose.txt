Not even a lifetime of preparation could Harry feel less shocked to see it. It's a child. That was his only thought as he dragged a weeping Petrikov across the snow. But how...? And not only the questions of the mechanism behind the bodies swirled around his mind. The why was the most important. Why bring children to this wretched Tower? Why let innocent lives be ruined for what seemed to be a purposeless action. But Harry knew it wasn't the military's conscious decision. Yes, they were the ones who led them here, but following the professor's account of things, it seemed to be an accident. Still, it was hard to accept.

If the Locksmith was having a hard time, one could only assume the tide of melancholy that took over Petrikov's spirit. His tears continued to be swept away by the raspy wind of... nothing. For this Tower could only replicate or create the lands of the outside. This mountain was nothing but a junction of rocks and stone, carved by devilish hands in order to betray the universe's orders. That's how Petrikov perceived it, anyways.

"Watch out!" Goldman yelled once again as he used his Emblem as a shield, blocking incoming debris that could hurt the group. Although it was working for now, the young thief could only imagine how long it would take for his Emblem to not work properly anymore.

Clack! The sound of meat being squished as Harry took a step almost made him throw up. That's why the terrain was weird. We never stepped on the actual ground, but the bodies on top of it. It explained the sound and irregularity that Madeline was complaining. But how could the bodies be trapped in the loop? Harry then looked at the snow closely, finally understanding the shape of the material; it was a Meta. In fact, millions of them, tiny as a real flock of snow.

Like bacteria, they covered the mountain. As people kept dying, they got covered in the creatures who solidified them into the next cycle. Harry questioned himself how much of the mountain's mass was made out of bodies. The thought brought shivers down his spine.

"Don't think about it!" Harry yelled to Petrikov who had stopped. "Do you want to live!?"

"I..."

"Answer me!" Harry yelled, furious not at Petrikov, but at the world. At the Logos Tower.

"I do!" Petrikov managed a weak smile that was covered by sentimental water. "I want to keep going!"

"Then we will go!" Harry yelled once again as he embraced the man and helped him, once again. "We will survive and get you out of here, okay? Don't think about them; think about the journey. Hug the cold and the wind. Focus on the patterns in the sky and rigid nature of the falling rocks. Do you understand?"

"Yes, of course..." Petrikov replied. "One step at a time"

"Yes!" Harry gave a sincere smile. "One step at a time, Petri. Slow and steady."

And soon they could see the group once again. They had taken shelter in a minuscule cave, spacious enough for the group to fit but not the ideal place. In the center was Vela's candelabrum as each member surrounded it, viciously discussing something. As soon as Madeline saw Harry, she called their attention. The group embraced them both.

"Fuckin' hell..." Harry put his hands on his knees. "Just... just tell me why."

And even though the why wasn't specified, they all knew what he meant.

"The Metas." Brun's confirmation made Harry slightly proud of himself for having figured it out, but not enough. It was never going to be enough. "They freeze the bodies in time as the cycles kept going and the loop restarting. It covers one third of the mountain's surface."

One third? Harry though he heard it wrong. Because it couldn't be that much, could it? He almost asked if she was joking, but she wasn't the type.

"Of course, that only accounts to the top of the mountain, which is what this floor contains." The old woman added. "I assume the number of bodies will decrease significantly as we keep on going since most prisoners wouldn't have made it this far following this... harsh climate."

"Prisoners?" Petrikov grimaced and took a step forward. "How wicked has humanity turned for us to see dead kids and view them as prisoners? This isn't hell, for even hell has a ruler. This Tower is the absence of... everything. We call ourselves jailers and bear witness to humanity's greatest sins. We're nothing but shells, aren't we?"

The man looked at each and everyone of the group, his eyes drowned not by tears, but guilt. Remorse. Pain.

"I'm..." The old woman grimaced as she closed her eyes. "I'm sorry, Petrikov. I'm sorry, but this isn't the time to discuss it."

"Brun's right." Goldman said as he used his Emblem almost as a force field. "Although it pains to say this, but there's a more urgent matter..."

"...What?" Harry asked as they waited for Brun to answer. "What could possibly be worse?"

"My Emblem has told me one last thing. It told me that the rocks that keep falling are made of the same material as the rocks originally found in this mountain."

"Which means?" Harry asked, anxious.

"Which means this mountain isn't alone." She simply said. "It has company."

Company? What could that possibly... And then it struck Harry. He went wide eyed as he took a step back in denial. Looking up, not afraid of being by any passing objects, he saw a small, almost imperceptible shadow of something. As if tearing through the sky, the thing fell slowly while the stones grew larger with each passing minute.

It was another mountain that fell from the sky.

The snow that kept on falling proved to be a huge obstacle as time went on. Sure, the group had Vela, who could use his candelabrum to provide a strong source of light. What would happen if the object was lost? What if Goldman failed to protect as a lateral rock took the monk's hand along with the wind? At the moment, it didn't seem like it could happen, but a fucking mountain was falling. So yes, it was safe to assume that they were in deep waters. And the snow didn't help.

Even though it was sheltering them for the duration of the discussion, they had to resolve what to do very fast.

"I will go first!" Goldman declared. "We just maintain the same formation as before. I am sure—"

"We can't!" Brun was the first to reply. "It is time to use the Emblems, otherwise we will lose too much time. Time we don't have."

"You think it is easy?" Daniel inquired. "We've been having trouble without the use of our powers and you think that using them will make it less confusing?"

And although the viking had phrased it in an odd way, because in no point had Brun stated that it would be less confusing, Harry had to agree. The Manuscript Emblem was strong in regards to information snatching, for sure, but its effects on Brun could barely be felt. Since it was a slow and constant process, she wouldn't feel the acute and devastating pain other, more dynamic, Emblem users felt.

Harry had brought the group down with him to the hotel, but it was one at a time. That situation, however, did not provide such a luxury.

"We can use it," Harry partially agreed to the old woman, but voiced his concerns. "But I can't guarantee anything with my doors. Without Goldman's protection, we will be in constant danger, and I'm sure that the door hopping won't make it easy on him. And that's not even talking about Emblem Exhaustion..."

"We can use my ink." Madeline suggested. "Creating a path along the snow whilst also providing shelter above. When I got tired, someone would just take over."

"And then what?" Daniel replied. "After all of you are drained in the snow, vomiting your intestines and dying, what are the rest of us supposed to do? We won't have the strength to carry all of you. With this fucking storm, we don't even know how long did we have until the top, and that's not considering the hatch problem. It could be in any spot around the summit."

For how long had they been in this discussion? How long until it was too late to make it? The Locksmith didn't intend to find out.

"Fuck this." He blurted out. "Goldman can lead us like we've been doing. We don't have time left to argue about this. If it turns out it's too dangerous to keep going on foot, we use our Emblems. I can provide for Petrikov. Madeline can help Brun. Daniel can help Manes and Goldman can help Vela. Does this work?"

The group, still a bit surprised by the sudden and definitive answer, nodded their heads.

Before they could get out, however, the storm got more severe. Crack! A huge piece of the mountain had just clashed with the shelter. Goldman tried to contain the piece so it wouldn't fall on the group.

"GO!" He yelled. "GO AND KEEP CLIMBING!"

The only thing he felt before being plunged into the mountain's wrath once again was Madeline's warm hands. The cold, however, quickly took care of the heat.
