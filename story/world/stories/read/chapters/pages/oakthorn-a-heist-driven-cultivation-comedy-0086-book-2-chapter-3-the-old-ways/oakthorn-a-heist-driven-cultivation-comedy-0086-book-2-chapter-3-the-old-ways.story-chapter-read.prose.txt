The wind carried the scent of terror—sweet, metallic, and pure. Destruction inhaled deeply, savoring how it tickled her nostrils and danced across her tongue. It had been millennia since she’d tasted true, unadulterated fear. The sensation became her intoxication, a narcotic draught after centuries of abstinence.

Destruction stood atop the alabaster cliffs of Syarthia, her crimson gown writhing flame against stark white stone. The world spread before her—small, fragile, and painfully ignorant. Her world. Or at least, it had been, once upon a time.

A bitter smile twisted her lips as she surveyed the landscape below. Cities and towns dotted the rolling hills and valleys, their inhabitants scurrying about, blissfully unaware of her gaze. How quickly they had forgotten. How eagerly they had erased her from their histories, their prayers, their nightmares.

“They think themselves beyond my reach,” she whispered, her voice honey drizzled over shattered obsidian. “They believe the old ways are dead.”

Lightning fractured the sky behind her, illuminating her silhouette in stark, terrible glory. Her shadow stretched across the cliffside, elongated and monstrous, the physical manifestation of darkness that pulsed within her chest where a heart should have been.

With practiced grace, she lifted her hand and examined the elaborate network of glowing red veins that crisscrossed her alabaster skin. Power thrummed beneath her flesh, hot and eager—tamed destruction awaiting release. She had been patient. So very patient. And now...

“THE TIME FOR PATIENCE HAS ENDED,” she declared, thunder punctuating each syllable.

The earth trembled beneath her feet, acknowledging her declaration. She closed her eyes and allowed her consciousness to expand beyond the confines of her physical form. It stretched across plains and mountains, valleys and oceans, until she could feel the entirety of the world’s ley lines pulsing against her mind.

They were stronger than she remembered—brighter, more vibrant. In her absence, they had grown wild and untamed, a network of power awaiting her command. A pleased hum vibrated in her throat.

But there was more to check—her precious investments, scattered across the continents, dormant seeds of her will.

With a thought, she dissolved into crimson mist, becoming one with the westward winds toward the ancient forests of Eldermere. She reformed beneath the gnarled branches of an oak tree so old it remembered her reign. Its bark wore her symbols—protection runes, loyalty sigils, and at its center, a pulsing hollow where something precious once rested.

Destruction approached the tree and pressed her palm against its weathered trunk. The oak shuddered at her touch, branches creaking in recognition.

“You’ve kept my secret safe, old friend,” she murmured, reaching into the folds of her gown to withdraw a small, glowing red object. It pulsed in her palm, a living heart ripped from its chamber.

The Remnant—her Remnant—sang to her, a high, keening note that only she could hear. It recognized its creator and yearned for reunion. But that was not its purpose.

Gently, she pressed the Remnant into the hollow of the tree. The wood sealed around it eagerly, leaving only a faint crimson glow to suggest anything unusual lay within.

“GROW DEEP, MY SWEET,” she commanded, stroking the bark tenderly. “SINK YOUR ROOTS INTO THE VERY MARROW OF THIS WORLD. I’LL NEED YOU STRONG FOR WHAT COMES NEXT.”

The oak’s branches swayed in acknowledgment, the air perfectly still around them.

She traveled next to the frozen wastes of the north, where another Remnant waited within a glacier of ancient ice. Then to the scorching deserts of the south, where she retrieved a third from the belly of a dormant volcano. In the forgotten depths of the ocean, a fourth pulsed within the ribcage of a leviathan long thought extinct.

Each Remnant returned to its hiding place, each guardian reminded of its sacred duty. With each placement, her design grew tighter and stronger. Soon, so very soon, her final project would be ready.

But as she worked and traveled unseen through villages and cities, indignation crystallized within her.

They had forgotten her. Not simply in their histories and prayers—that she had expected—but in their daily lives. The altars stood abandoned, the offerings ceased. Communities that once gathered to perform rituals of appeasement now housed marketplaces and taverns. Sacred grounds that once ran red with sacrificial blood now flowed with ale and idle chatter.

No one spoke her name in reverent whispers. No one taught their children to leave gifts for her at midwinter. No one remembered the proper ways to plead for mercy when her wrath descended.

They’d abandoned the old ways. Her ways.

The shadows behind her deepened and coalesced into robed figures. Her Endwalkers—seven faithful souls who had kept her memory alive through the long dark years. They knelt before her, heads bowed in perfect submission.

“MY FAITHFUL,” she addressed them, voice soft yet commanding. “WHAT REPORT HAVE YOU OF THE WORLD’S DEVOTION?”

The eldest Endwalker, Tyric, face obscured beneath a hood of midnight blue, raised his head slightly. “Great Goddess, we have tried to keep your teachings alive, but few listen. The new gods hold sway. Your altars crumble. Your rituals are forgotten.”

“We have walked among them for centuries,” said another, her voice barely above a whisper. “We have reminded them of the price of forgetting. They laugh. They mock. They call us madmen.”

Destruction’s fingers curled into fists, her nails drawing blood from her palms. “AND WHAT OF VALENDEL? THE VILLAGE IN THE VALLEY—MY LAST TRUE STRONGHOLD.”

The Endwalkers exchanged glances, hesitation palpable in their silence.

“SPEAK,” she commanded.

“A festival begins there today,” the eldest said carefully. “A celebration of... abandonment. They burn your effigies and dance. They call it the ‘Freedom Day.’“

A cold smile spread across Destruction’s face. “FREEDOM. HOW... PRECIOUS.”

She turned to her servants, her eyes twin voids of promised pain. “YOU HAVE TRIED REASON. YOU HAVE TRIED WHISPERS AND WARNINGS. IT SEEMS A DEMONSTRATION IS REQUIRED.”

“We have tried before—” the female Endwalker began.

“THEY WILL HAVE MOTIVATION ENOUGH WHEN I AM FINISHED,” Destruction interrupted, her voice rolling across the landscape. “GO. PREPARE THE OTHERS. WHEN I CALL, YOU WILL TEACH THEM THE OLD WAYS ANEW.”

The Endwalkers bowed deeper, then faded back into the shadows from which they’d emerged, their unease a tangible miasma in their wake.

Destruction stood motionless for a moment, then dissolved once more into crimson mist, flowing down from the cliffs, across the plains, and into the heart of a verdant valley. There, nestled between towering peaks crowned with eternal ice and snow, lay Valendel. Great glaciers loomed above, gleaming blue-white in the sunlight, their frozen rivers creeping ever closer to the village below.

Destruction paused, appreciating the stark beauty of this place where her fiery domain bordered the realm of ice. She had always cultivated this boundary—her volcano hidden within the mountains kept the valley warm and green even as winter’s grip strengthened on the surrounding peaks. The delicate balance between fire and ice pleased her aesthetic sensibilities. The mortals below benefited from her artistry without ever acknowledging its source.

The village bustled with activity. Colorful banners hung from every eave. Children ran laughing through the streets, their faces painted with symbols of the new gods. In the town square, a large wooden structure stood draped in flowers—an effigy of her, she realized with cold fury. It was crude, monstrous, nothing reflecting her terrible beauty.

They meant to burn it at sundown.

Destruction reformed at the edge of the village, invisible to mortal eyes. She walked among them, studying their faces, their joy, their utter ignorance.

A young mother sat outside her modest home, nursing an infant while two older children played at her feet. Her face bore the lines of hard work but also of laughter, of contentment.

“Mama, tell us about the bad goddess again!” the older child, a girl of perhaps six, called out.

The woman smiled, adjusting the baby at her breast. “It’s just stories, love. Made up to frighten children into behaving.”

“But Granna says she was real,” the younger child insisted. “She says we must respect the old ways.”

The mother’s face hardened slightly. “Granna remembers things that never were. The old gods were inventions of a more fearful time. We’re better now.”

Destruction crouched before the woman, reaching out a hand toward the nursing infant. The baby’s eyes widened, focusing on something the mother couldn’t see. It released her nipple, a small fist reaching toward Destruction’s outstretched finger.

“YOU TEACH THEM TO FORGET,” Destruction whispered. “YOU TEACH THEM DISRESPECT.”

The baby gurgled, a bubble of saliva forming on its tiny lips.

“What is it, little one?” the mother asked, following her child’s gaze to empty air. “What do you see?”

Destruction stood, leaving the family to their false security. Elsewhere in the village:

An old woman—this “Granna” perhaps—leaving small offerings at her doorstep, glancing fearfully over her shoulder lest she be seen.

A baker placing the first loaf of the day beneath his counter rather than in the window—an old superstition he couldn’t quite abandon.

A young couple making quiet devotions in their bedroom, a small stone altar hidden beneath floor planks.

Not all had forgotten. Just most.

Destruction climbed the hill overlooking the village, watching as preparations for the evening festival continued. She could wait until nightfall, until the effigy burned. But patience had never been her virtue.

“THEY CELEBRATE FREEDOM FROM FEAR,” she said to the empty air. “LET ME REMIND THEM WHY THEY FEARED.”

She raised her hands to the clear blue sky, fingers splayed. Power surged through her veins, burning bright beneath her skin. The clouds came first—rolling black thunderheads that blotted out the sun in moments. The villagers paused, glancing upward in confusion. Some hurried to gather laundry or children, worried about a sudden storm.

They had no concept of what approached.

Lightning struck the center of the village square, splintering the wooden platform bearing her effigy. Thunder followed, so powerful it shattered windows and knocked people from their feet. And then came the rain—not water, but tiny droplets of flame that sizzled against skin and ignited thatch.

Panic erupted, beautiful in its purity.

Destruction watched, unmoving, as chaos consumed Valendel. She saw the young mother clutching her infant to her chest, screaming for her other children. She saw the old woman—Granna—standing in her doorway, head bowed in acceptance. She saw the baker crushed beneath a collapsing wall, his hidden offering dying with him.

Then she walked down the hill and into the heart of the nightmare she’d created.

A man stumbled past her, clothes aflame, shrieking for mercy from gods who couldn’t hear him. A young woman knelt in the ash-strewn street, cradling her lover’s broken form against her breast. Her eyes reminded the goddess of vacant stars, frozen between beauty and horror. She whispered poetry to unhearing ears, promises of tomorrows that would never come, her tears carving glistening rivulets through the soot on her cheeks. Fires spread from house to house, unstoppable even if there had been enough people left to fight them.

Among the pandemonium, Destruction found the young mother again. She had gathered her older children, shielding them with her body as her home collapsed before them. The infant wailed in her arms as they watched from the street.

“Please,” the woman sobbed to the universe. “Please, not my children.”

Destruction became visible, standing over the cowering family. The woman’s eyes widened in recognition and terror.

“You—you’re real,” she whispered.

“I HAVE ALWAYS BEEN REAL,” Destruction replied, her voice drowning out the crackle of flames. “EVEN WHEN YOU CHOSE TO FORGET.”

“We didn’t know—”

“YOU CHOSE NOT TO KNOW. YOU TAUGHT YOUR CHILDREN I WAS A FABLE.”

The woman clutched her children tighter. “Please. They’re innocent.”

Destruction crouched down, bringing her face level with the mother’s. “INNOCENCE IS A LUXURY AFFORDED TO THOSE WHO REMEMBER THEIR PLACE IN THE WORLD.”

Around them, the village burned. Screams faded as more succumbed to flame and smoke. The woman’s oldest child stared at Destruction with eyes too knowing for one so young. The child saw her—truly saw her—and in that moment understood what adults could not.

“The bad goddess,” the child whispered.

Destruction smiled, touching the girl’s cheek with one burning finger. No mark appeared, but something passed between them—knowledge, perhaps. Or doom.

“REMEMBER THIS DAY,” she told the child. “REMEMBER MY FACE. REMEMBER MY POWER. TELL OTHERS, WHEN THEY ASK WHY YOU FEAR ME.”

She stood, surveying what remained of Valendel. The destruction pleased her, but it was merely the beginning.

“WITNESS MY RETURN,” she proclaimed to the dying village, her voice carrying beyond the valley, beyond the mountains, to every corner of the realm. “WITNESS AND REMEMBER.”

With that, she turned away from the sobbing family, from the burning ruins, and ascended into the storm-black sky. She became the storm, spreading across the continent, visiting her wrath upon town after town, city after city. Mountains crumbled at her whisper. Rivers boiled at her glance. The very air turned acid where her attention lingered too long.

For seven days and seven nights, she raged across the world. And in her wake, the dying remembered the old ways. Hastily constructed altars appeared in town squares. Crude offerings—food, wine, valuables—were left at crossroads and shorelines. Desperate prayers, half-remembered and clumsily spoken, filled the night air.

On the eighth day, exhausted from her fury but satisfied with its results, Destruction returned to her sanctuary deep within the Shattered Mountains. Her palace of obsidian and bone welcomed her, rebuilt by her will from millennium-old ruins. From her throne of interwoven ribcages, she surveyed her handiwork through a pool of liquid fire that showed her any corner of the world she wished to see.

The imagery pleased her. Fear had returned. Respect had begun to blossom anew. It wasn’t enough—not yet—but it was a beginning.

As dusk settled over her dominion, something caught her attention—movement on the alabaster cliffs that had once been her favorite viewing place. She leaned forward, focusing the fiery pool on that location.

A family climbed the treacherous path to the clifftop. A man, a woman, and three children, the youngest clutched in the father’s arms. Their clothes hung in tatters, their faces gaunt with hunger and grief. They carried nothing save a small, cloth-wrapped bundle.

Intrigued, Destruction left her throne and journeyed to the cliffs, arriving just as the family reached the summit. They did not see her, standing against the darkening sky. They saw only the edge of the cliff and what lay beyond—a world forever changed by her wrath.

The man stepped forward, unwrapping the bundle with trembling hands. Inside lay five locks of hair—one from each family member—tied with a red ribbon, resting atop a crude wooden carving of flames.

He placed it on the ground, then backed away to join his family. Together, they knelt, foreheads touching the earth.

“Goddess of Destruction,” the man began, his voice cracking, “we offer ourselves to your mercy.”

Destruction materialized before them, suddenly visible to their mortal eyes. The woman gasped. The children whimpered. The man continued, though his entire body shook with terror.

“We have forgotten the old ways,” he admitted. “We were not taught. Our parents were not taught. The knowledge was lost to us.”

She circled them slowly, studying each face, each trembling limb.

“TELL ME,” Destruction said softly, dangerously, “WHAT BRINGS YOU HERE, TO OFFER YOURSELVES SO COMPLETELY? WHAT CALAMITY TOUCHED YOU THAT YOU REMEMBERED WHAT WAS NEVER TAUGHT?”

The man swallowed hard. “The mountain... the one they called Grayspire... it—”

“SPEAK PLAINLY,” she commanded. “SAY WHAT YOU MEAN.”

“You brought the mountain down upon our village,” the woman whispered. “We lost... everyone. Everyone except us.”

In her mind, Destruction painted their tragedy with exquisite detail—their little village nestled in Grayspire’s shadow. The man’s parents, the woman’s three brothers, the children’s friends—all obliterated when the mountain surrendered to Destruction’s whispered command. Their home, spared by mere chance. Their desperate search for survivors, calling names into swirling dust. The silence that answered. The grief. The terror. And then, somehow, the remembrance—a half-forgotten tale of appeasing the Goddess of Destruction at the alabaster cliffs.

Such a perfect, tragic offering. It pleased her beyond measure.

A smile curved Destruction’s lips, terrible and beautiful. “YOU HAVE REMEMBERED.”

They trembled before her, hope and terror warring in their expressions.

“OTHERS WILL FOLLOW,” she continued, her voice the rumble of distant avalanches. “THROUGH PAIN, THE WORLD WILL REMEMBER THE OLD WAYS. MY WAYS.”

The middle child—a boy of perhaps seven—looked up at her with eyes rimmed with red.

“Will you bring down more mountains?” he asked softly, his accent thick with the mountains.

Her smile widened, and it was the visage of a predator scenting blood. “ONLY IF I MUST.”

Destruction turned toward the setting sun. The sky blazed crimson and gold—fire made manifest, endings given form. In the shadow of an ancient oak, a long-hidden Remnant pulsed, sending a signal to its siblings across the world. Beneath the glacier, within the volcano, in the leviathan’s ribcage—all answered in kind. Only one was missing, but she knew that upstart, Cade Stormhollow had it inside of him. That was fine. For now.

Soon. So very soon, the goddess thought.

The final project was almost ready.
