Another week had passed. Not in a daze, but in a focused blur of intense, at times mind-numbingly monotonous activity.

In an arena warded for privacy, Gio stood panting, covered in sweat, allowing the potent magic to slip from his fingers after a long testing session. A reflective sheen of water across the ground dissipated as his domain receded. It had been a wild week, consisting of a repetitive cycle of drafting, testing, feedback, theorizing, rewriting, and retesting, ad nauseam. There was even a panic-inducing few days before Gio figured out that he wasn’t properly implementing the water aspect of his spell in his envisioning, where the whole spell was dangerously unbalanced, and he could not get it to function. He’d promptly fixed that, though.

And the results spoke for themselves.

Jean rose from the spot where he had been sitting against the wall, throwing water bottles to Chandrika and Gio.

“Well?” Jean asked, looking at Chandrika expectantly.

“It’s… impenetrable. I can’t find a way to destabilize it, never mind defeat it.” Chandrika said, turning to Gio. “You… did it. You have a domain. A good one, by my measure.”

Gio bashfully rubbed the back of his head. “It’s not perfect. I could still adjust the balancing array. I think that the refractive index of the conjured water-”

“Gio.” Jean chidingly interrupted, “It doesn’t have to be perfect. In fact, I think perfection would be insane to aim for.”

“The goal is passing,” Chandrika said. “And if you wouldn’t pass with this domain… then I would be frightened for the rest of our classmates.”

Gio frowned.

Are they right? It feels… strange, to make it to this point and accept a ‘good enough’ score. Maybe there’s still time for some-

“Shoot! Look at the time!” Jean yelled. “We’ve got class!”

Chandrika’s eyes widened as she raced for her bags. “And we’re having a special visitor today.”

“The headmaster, right?” Gio asked, gathering his things quickly.

“Don’t let her know that I told you, but… yes,” Chandrika said, holding the door open for Jean and Gio. “She’ll be making it to all four first-year classes, and I think we’re the last one on her list.”

The trio made a dead sprint for the Manse entrance nearby, nearly bowling each other over as they each asked their personal interfaces to transfer them to their lecture hall.

Rushing through the astral corridor summoned by his mentor’s magic, Gio appeared alongside his companions as some of the last students to enter the hall. Already, Professor Anaximenes was standing in front of the gathered class, just about to begin addressing the class as a whole. He curiously eyed the three students, bidding them to quickly take their seats before continuing.

“I will excuse cutting it close today, as nobody is late… but tomorrow, every single student in my class shall be no less than fifteen minutes early. You do not want to be late for an official examination. Do I make myself understood?” Professor A lectured.

“Yes,” responded the class in unison.

“Hmmm?” Professor A hummed.

“Yes, sir!” The class responded louder.

“Quite right.” The professor smiled. “And now… speaking of keeping people waiting, our guest today is someone whom I do not like to displease. Madame Headmaster, the floor is yours.”

Murmurs erupted from the class before quickly quieting down as a series of clicks echoed out from the heels of Headmaster Vespertine’s shoes as she made her way down the aisle from the back of the class.

“Thank you, Professor Anaximenes,” Eudoria said.

The ever-stylish administrator of the Crystal Ring Academy represented a striking figure, choosing to wear a more informal version of the robes she was usually seen in when performing her official duties. Over a stately black dress, the school's signature colors were represented in the form of a small triangular brooch that held three colored jewels: white, silver, and blue.

“As you are all aware, tomorrow marks the day that you shall each be subject to a test. I will not mince words… this test is designed to be the first major hurdle on your path to wearing the title of [True Mage].” She spoke.

Eudoria seemed to glide across the teaching platform, making eye contact with every student as she personally addressed them. She had the full, undivided attention of the 257 students in Gio’s class.

“This test… is important.” The headmaster continued. “It has also changed many times over the years, at the behest of both the human faculty on my payroll… as well as the collected voices of the spirits of knowledge that make a home in this dungeon- some of which would rather not accept mortals as students, if you gather my meaning. There is much that my staff and I cannot say about this school… about the nature of the challenges that each student will no doubt face during their time here. As instructors, we have the pernicious job of skirting a… challenging boundary- toeing a line drawn between competing ideals; of what it means to teach, versus what it means to represent the world's largest and most prestigious hoard of magic and knowledge. Of this test you face tomorrow, I wish to say many things... especially the things that I am not allowed to say. Know this- as a former student of this academy… as a mother… and as a mage… I am going to tell you… as much as I can get away with telling you.”

Her gaze turned serious. Gio intently studied Headmaster Vespertine’s features.

What… isn’t she allowed to tell us?

Eudoria spoke in a gentle yet serious voice, causing the entire class to hang on her every word.

“Tomorrow, you shall each wake at an early hour and make whatever preparations for the day that you need to make. Bathe. Eat. Exercise, if it behooves you to do so. I recommend trying to go to bed as early as you can, if you can stomach the extra sleep. Do whatever you must to ensure that you are prepared to face the day bravely. At the allotted time of 0700, you will congregate in the examination hall with the other three first-year classes. There are over a thousand of you, so please remember to find your seats as soon as possible. At this point, you will be greeted by your proctor, a mage who will serve as the singular representative of the spirits, and shall serve as the final say in the event that the examiners cannot reach a consensus on a student’s score. Your proctor this year will be a special friend of the academy- a very powerful [True Arch-Wizard] as well as a Saint of magic.”

A murmur swept through the class.

A Saint? Which Saint, I wonder?

“Aside from the Proctor, the four first-year guidance professors shall serve on the board of examiners. Your professor and his peers shall serve as a sort of filter that will protect you from the other faculty.”

Other… faculty?

“This brings me to my next point.”

Eudoria stopped momentarily, looking out to the class and heaving a comically emphatic sigh.

“Despite all of the honeyed words or threats that I can muster, and despite years of campaigning to change the culture of the upper echelons of academia… We have a small population of individuals who enjoy the benefits of holding the occupation of [Professor] without doing the actual work of teaching at our school, as they view fostering the next generation of mages as a waste of their near-infinite time. I try to make life very difficult for these parasites… but they don’t seem to mind as much as I would like. One of the ways these so-called ‘professors’ are able to maintain their relationship with the spirits of the school is by participating in testing ceremonies like the first-year domain examination. In practice, what this means is that there will be a crowd of faceless mages present tomorrow that will refuse to give you their names while offering little to no useful advice. Do not interact with these mages. They are not worth your time. If you must address them for some reason, simply call them ‘professor’ and move on with your day. If they approach one of you with an offer of mentorship, as is their right to do so… carefully consider their offer. I won’t deny that there might be some among this number who are genuinely looking to pass their legacies down to promising young mages… but I would also add that some of them might just be looking for cheap labor or help with unsavory experiments. Use your judgment.”

“Sounds like a great way to end up with a bunch of extra work for no reason,” Chandrika whispered to Gio. “I think I’ll stick to just the mentor that I already have… even if she can be rather challenging to learn under at times.”

Headmaster Vespertine cracked a discreet yet dangerous smile at Chandrika’s words before continuing.

“So… that is the ‘who’s who’ element of what you should expect to meet tomorrow. A proctor, a panel of four judges, and a peanut gallery of useless academics.” The headmaster said. “Next… you shall be summoned to the stand in groups. Those of you with… special circumstances shall be among the first few groups chosen.”

She looked directly at Gio as she spoke the words ‘special circumstances’.

So… I’ll be going first. Great. I guess making your own domain isn’t something that usually happens, so I should have probably expected as much.

“Additionally, those of you who are going to be utilizing the time-honored tradition of casting the spell [Wizard’s Domain] shall be summoned last.”

More than a few groans echoed out among the class.

“Yes, yes. It’s horrible, I know. The nerves will be dreadful for whoever has to go last. It’s only that more than a third of all students every year choose to use that spell for obvious reasons, and it can be rather… monotonous to sit through.” The headmaster said.

A rush of whispers broke out in the class. Professor A cleared his throat, silencing the room.

Eudoria smiled. “This brings us… to the test itself. After you are called to the stage, you will be asked to demonstrate your domain. This is a relatively straightforward process. You shall wrest control of a portion of the school, albeit temporarily. The spirits shall put up a token defense against this incursion upon their territory, testing your ability to maintain control over the space you have claimed. Depending on the spell that you have chosen to use for this process, you will be called to demonstrate its relevant capabilities and questioned by the examiners.”

Eudoria paused for a moment. She clasped her hands behind her back.

“Let me be utterly clear.” She said.  “At this point in the examination, you will instantly be made aware of whether or not you have failed. Every year, we have roughly a 7% attrition rate for this test, meaning that I expect that about 70 students will be leaving campus tomorrow. I don’t say this to discourage you- in fact, I would like to remind you that there is no shame in failure! As I said to each of you at the start of the year, this school will kill you if you let it. Do not argue with the judgment of the professors, nor the spirits. Do not question, argue, or fight the results you are given. Failing out of school doesn’t mean that your life is over… but doing something irrational and emotional might.”

Gio hung on every word. While he wasn’t planning on failing tomorrow, his mind still bent towards the possibility of failure like a plant facing the sun.

Seventy students.

Gio near-unconsciously looked to the rest of the class, feeling some level of sympathy and wondering who among his classmates might fail… and found several sets of eyes looking his way, before they quickly darted away so as not to meet his gaze. He tried not to feel indignant, knowing on some level that his struggles with obtaining a domain had made the rounds.

What would his life look like if he failed? Gio wondered what would happen to his class if he were forced to evolve it away from true magic before graduating. Would he join the Ring Guard? Or would he try to find a way to continue learning magic through other means?

He tried to shake such negative thoughts from his mind, but found it hard to do so. Gio looked back at the Headmaster.

Death… and failure. What point is there in telling us any of this the day before the exam? It’s… almost like she’s trying to sow some amount of uncertainty into us.

The expression on Headmaster Vespertine’s face was difficult to read. Like her husband, she was a master at controlling herself, and she only ever let emotions show when it was convenient for her to do so.

But at the same time, I… think she genuinely cares about her students. I simply couldn’t believe that she would be deliberately trying to sabotage us. So… what is her goal? What is she trying to accomplish here?

“I can see that I have left you all with a great deal to consider. I will finish on a lighter note so that I might send you off to the realm of dreams tonight with a lighter burden to carry.” She spoke. “Tomorrow will mark the greatest challenge that most of you will face this year. In a certain lens… it is also a portal, of sorts. A portal… to the future. Historically, after students demonstrate their domain… and experience the first real hurdle of their academic careers, it can cause a sort of metamorphosis. You will see a change in your classmates, and perhaps yourselves, as you start to come into your own as mages. You will build confidence in your ability, and perhaps, some may feel the heat of ambition- or the heat of a fire lit under your seat, should you find that your studies until now have been lacking. One of the sublime joys of being an educator is that I get to watch you all mature and grow into such interesting young people. I beseech you to dare to dream about who you will be after tomorrow. Where will your futures take you? I truly hope that we will be able to find out together. I hope you each have a pleasant evening… and I wish you success in your endeavors tomorrow.”

As the last syllable rang out over the class, Headmaster Vespertine faded away. Where she had been standing, a black cloud billowed up to the ceiling, billowing out like smoke until a glittering night sky hung overhead for all the class to stare at.

Professor Anaximenes began applauding, which, after a brief stunning pause, the rest of the class took up as well.

“Even after all these years… I cannot help but feel inspired by her.” Professor A sighed. “I also cannot begin to fathom what I could say that would add to what the Headmaster has encapsulated… so I will simply release all of you to the rest of your evenings. I shall see each of you tomorrow. Good luck, and have faith in yourselves. I am proud of each of you.”

As the professor dismissed the class, Minette quickly gathered her things and began to speed towards the entrance. Gio took note of this, quickly banishing his notebook back to his room using his student badge and bolting after her.

“Minette!” Gio yelled.

The blonde girl tucked her head down, folding her books to her chest and sprinting forward.

Gio called after her.

“Min-”

Without a word or a glance spared to his direction, she vanished into the Manse.

“-ette?”

A jingling sound to the side caused Gio to look away from the entrance to the classroom, where other students were already starting to filter out of the class. A familiar face approached Gio from the second row of desks.

“I’m not surprised she ran away from you… She’s been avoiding Papyrus and I for the past few days.” Vito said, wearing a grim expression.

“I… just wanted to see how she was doing. Maybe offer to help, if I could.” Gio said, through a lopsided frown.

“Minette is… not doing well,” Vito said. “Her domain… isn’t like any that I’ve seen. It’s like… a living organism, in a way.”

“I don’t mean to be insensitive, but can’t she use a different domain?” Gio asked.

Vito raised a pierced eyebrow at Gio.

“I would think that you, of all people, would know that domains don’t grow on trees,” Vito said.

Gio cocked his head to the side, momentarily stunned by the uncharacteristic words from Vito.

“Sorry. That was rude.” Vito sighed. “It’s… her dad. He won’t let her use anything else. It’s something to do with being a [Shaman], I think.”

Gio sighed to match Vito’s. “That’s… tough. I’ve been so absorbed in my own problems that I don’t think I really noticed that she had been having a hard time until last week… and I haven’t been able to spare much time-”

“I don’t think you’re being fair to yourself.” Vito interrupted. “You can’t blame yourself for not being able to solve her problems on top of your own. I’m worried about her, too… but we can’t help her if she won’t even speak to us.”

“That’s… true. Thanks, Vito. I appreciate it. But… Is there really just nothing we can do for her?” Gio said.

“I’ll… I don’t know. I guess I’ll try to send her a message through the Manse, or something. I know Papyrus has been messaging her, and he’s been getting no reply.  In any case, try to have a good night, Gio. See you tomorrow.” Vito replied.

“Okay… see you tomorrow,” Gio said.

Gio watched as Vito left the room. Chandrika and Jean approached from behind.

“Is everything alright? You look sad.” Jean offered.

“I’ll be fine,” Gio replied. “I don’t know if I can say the same for Minette.”

“That would be most unfortunate. I’m not particularly close with her, but she seems nice.” Chandrika said.

“I don’t know if I would say that I’m close with her either… but I can’t help but feel… sympathetic? I don’t know.” Gio wondered.

“With it being the day before the test, I don’t know that there’s really much that anyone can do for her. All we can do is focus on ourselves.” Jean sagely noted.  “Speaking of, are you going to try to clock in any more time at the testing arena? Or are you going to listen to reason and grab dinner with us?”

They were nearly alone in the class now, with only a small gaggle of students waiting in line to ask last-minute questions to Professor Anaximenes.

“Food sounds nice.” Gio agreed. “I think I’m going to try and take the Headmaster’s advice and go to bed early. I doubt that I’ll be able to sleep much, but I guess I can try.”
