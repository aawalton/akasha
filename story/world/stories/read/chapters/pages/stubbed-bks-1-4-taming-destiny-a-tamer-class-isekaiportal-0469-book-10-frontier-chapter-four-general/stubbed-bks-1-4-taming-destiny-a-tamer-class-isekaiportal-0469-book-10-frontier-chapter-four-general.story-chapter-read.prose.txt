“Another nunda?” I wonder audibly as I see the image of the leopard-like being currently approaching us. This one looks more like a snow leopard than Kalanthia’s African leopard appearance, though its tail isn’t as long and fluffy as a true snow leopard. Is that enough to make it a different species or is it like how Lathani changed colour on Evolution?

“A nunda?” Layton asks sharply, making me blink. “Like your Bonded?”

I nod.

“From what I can see, yes.”

“That’s troubling,” Valence contributes, his eyes fixed on the moving trees. I agree silently. Kalanthia is powerful for her Tier and has indicated that that’s common among nundas. A Tier four of the species is surely something to be feared.

I clench my jaw as I realise that this is not going to be anything like the fight against the parasitic controller. That one had had an advantage in numbers, but few of its beasts had been truly powerful, and once they were eliminated, the Tier four itself was a relatively easy target. This is more likely to be similar to the Wind Roc – a single, powerful beast. I remember how many deaths that particular creature caused and my stomach sinks.

“Prepare the defences,” I order just as the Tier four breaks through the treeline. Below my feet, I see several flashes as the defensive scrolls are deployed, enhancing the enchantments already present.

“We’re going to fight?” Layton asks tensely. Valence reaches to hold the hilt of his sword, his expression focused.

“Only if we have to,” I reply grimly.

Now able to see the beast in the flesh, the automatic elements of my Inspect kick in. I immediately get a sense of the beast’s general threat level – high, too high. Definitely more powerful than the roc.

With that in mind, I use only the most subtle form of my Inspect, directing it to abort as soon as there’s even the chance that the Tier four might feel the probe.

The Tier four inadvertently gives the probe time to work – he’s paused at the edge of the treeline. He scents the air in a very familiar gesture, scanning the land ahead of him. I take a moment to compare him to my Companions.

I’m increasingly sure that he’s indeed a nunda – apart from his different colouring, his body shape is identical to theirs. He’s also bigger, though not by as much as I might have expected – if Kalanthia is equivalent to a female African elephant in size, this nunda is a big bull elephant. His canines are like longswords, and I don’t doubt that his claws are long and sharp to match. Like Kalanthia, this isn’t a beast I want to face in physical battle.

Then my probe returns. I prompt its impressions to appear as a table before me, too focused on what the beast is doing to properly make sense of the sensations.

Prime Nunda

Tier 4 Beast (Immortal)

Special abilities: Lightning, ?

Health: ?

Mana: ?

Minimum Willpower recommended to Dominate without other impacting factors: 308

Open to a Bond: No

Impacting factor: ?

Nundas are apex predators, known for both physical and magical prowess.

I’m hardly surprised at the little information that populates the field – even if Inspect has gone up significantly in rank, this is a Tier four I’m trying to survey.

Prime nunda…I’ve heard that somewhere, I think, frowning at the screen. At least it’s confirmed the species, though I would guess it’s some sort of evolution of the base kind. Maybe that’s what the colour indicates.

The information that it’s proficient in lightning is both good and bad news. Lightning is certainly dangerous and could allow it to reach inside the cavern despite our defences, but at least it isn’t earth-magic. I consider what might work best against lightning – probably metal-magic.

Maxen, find out if anyone is capable of creating long metal rods and keep them nearby, I order. Then, after a moment’s thought add, And see if anyone has Skills that are particularly effective against lightning. After all, with magic in the mix, I shouldn’t only rely on solutions from Earth.

Unfortunately, one of my go-to options is decidedly off the table. I’m unsurprised that the nunda isn’t open to a Bond, and had a feeling that forcibly Binding a Tier four would still be out of my reach, but seeing the minimum Willpower required written here so blatantly is a little disconcerting. Almost a hundred more points than I currently have….

Negotiation should definitely be our go-to strategy here.

Then the nunda lowers his head and gazes directly at us with glowing white eyes, a thunderous snarl ripping out of his throat, echoed by a rumble of thunder in the quickly-gathering clouds above his head. At the same time, his domain blasts out and hits us like a blow of predatory intent, only slightly blunted by our defences. Though he hasn’t moved forward an inch, it feels like he’s looming over me, like his claws are about to scythe through my body.

I stagger backwards a step in primal fear; there are several startled cries as others back away enough to fall off the wall.

“It will be well!” I shout, turning my attention from the nunda to my own people. “Hold your positions!” Bastet, send out your own domain! I urge and, though I sense her own primal fear, she hurries to do just that. Her domain isn’t nearly as large or as powerful as this Tier four’s, but it’s enough to push away the worst of the attack domain’s influence.

But it’s not enough, and it’s weaker than usual due to her own feelings of intimidation. Though people have stopped actively backing away, and healers are coming to help those who have fallen off the wall, I still see far too much fear on the faces of those around. If they do not believe that we can win, we will have lost the battle before we start.

I turn to Captain Ilas.

“Do you have anyone with Skills capable of encouraging people to have faith and stand together?”

The captain blinks and turns to me, the fear written on his face taking on a thoughtful mien.

“Do you mean like Encouraging Shout?”

“That sounds like the kind of thing, yes.” I’m not familiar with the Skill, but its name is clear enough.

“I believe we do, my lord.”

“Then have them help everyone to stand fast,” I order. “We can’t have them breaking so soon.”

“Yes, my lord,” the captain replies and bustles away, seeming grateful to have something to do.

My gaze returns to the Tier four and my heart clenches as I see he’s started prowling towards us, every step predatory and intentional.

“We should strike,” Valence urges, his eyes tracking the beast padding towards us, his mouth set in hard lines and his sword hilt starting to glow with runes under his hand. “The closer it gets, the more danger it poses.” The nunda pauses. “Look! it’s stuck behind the wards – it’s an opportunity! We still have a few explosive artifacts – an a fire cannon scroll. ”

I hesitate for a moment, weighing up the options – of what I know of the Tier four and our own capabilities.

And then we jump as a bolt of lightning flashes down from the sky above, the accompanying crack of thunder deafening. Half-expecting the lightning to be sent at us, I’m surprised when it instead strikes the ground just ahead of the nunda. Then, as golden runes flare brightly at the contact point for a moment before they go dull again, I realise what’s happened.

If the curses are anything to go by, so do the other lords. Bastet presses hard against my shoulder, seeking and giving comfort in equal measure.

“First layer of defences gone,” the mage who’s standing near the enchantment control panel confirms shakily

“Destroyed, or temporarily out of action?” I snap back at her. She consults the panel for a tense moment that seems to last forever even as the nunda resumes his steady prowl forwards. Strains of music and shouts ring up from below, those with Skills meant for encouragement employing them. We’re outside their area of effect and so feel little benefit.

“Overloaded,” she concludes. “As long as there’s no structural damage, they’ll be online again in a halfmark.”

I hiss a breath through my teeth. A halfmark is too long – and that’s even if they weren’t damaged. If this Tier four decides to attack us, we could all be dead in that time.

“I need to speak to him,” I say quietly, my tone grim.

Layton and Valence stare at me incredulously.

“Markus…he’s clearly not looking for a peaceful resolution,” Layton points out.

“It’s that or face a slaughter,” I snap, though regret my tone a moment later. It’s not Layton’s fault. I take a moment to calm myself with a breath. “He’s capable of overloading our wards and I hardly think the wall itself will cause him much trouble. After that, we all know what the next defences are.” I look meaningfully at them. Layton swallows; Valence looks away.

I nod and call up a current of air.

“Stop where you are and state your business!” I shout, using the air current to send my words to the nunda.

He snarls loudly in response, continuing to step forward without pause.

Bring her out! His telepathy snaps like a whip across our minds, making several people recoil. I flinch – the sharp pain of his communication reminds me of when Kalanthia ransacked my mind after I Bonded with Lathani. Still, unlike then, this one is short-lived.

As the pain fades, I find myself bewildered by the content.

“Bring out who?” This time, I don’t bother to use air to take my words to him – if his telepathy stretches this far, he’ll hear my projected thought.

Bring out the female Enlightened! the nunda snarls, the lash of his thoughts even stronger this time.

I glance down at Bastet and she looks up at me, my fear reflected in her gaze. It seems far too likely that this is indeed the territory king, looking to meet the newest Beast Lord. Perhaps he means to scare the newest incumbent into submission, but his rage seems all too real. I find I’m unwilling to take any risk with Bastet’s life.

“What are your intentions with her?” I ask the Tier four with as much confidence as I can. “I will not bring her out if you wish only to attack or kill her.”

The nunda snarls with even more rage, proving it possible despite my doubt.

How dare you question me, Binder!

I wince at the most painful mind-lash yet. It isn’t helping my thought process at all. This is starting to sound all too familiar – confusingly so. Are all nundas so familiar with Tamers? But I cannot think too deeply on that – right now I have to deal with the crisis at hand.

“Swear you do not intend to attack or kill her!” I insist.

I am the last one to wish her ill, the nunda snarls. Now bring her out or I will destroy your den and take her! As he speaks, his lightning flashes down and overloads our second ring of defences. One more to go and then he’ll be at the wall.

I exchange another glance with Bastet, one filled with resignation. We both know what we have to do. Yet Bastet is currently helping keep my forces intact. If she moves, her domain will no longer protect them.

“Withdraw your domain and she will come out,” I demand of the Tier four, my mind working busily to figure out what we can do to increase her chances of survival if the Tier four is lying about his intentions.

The nunda in question snarls wordlessly, but I feel his domain lessen its pressure. Not gone, but it feels like the predator has backed off a couple of steps and is no longer looming, poised to kill. I take a shaking breath, the deepest one since the domain struck me. Around me, I notice several people’s postures shift – no longer leaning backwards like they would run if their feet were not rooted to the floor.

Alright, Bastet, I sigh. Make sure your shield is up, and take from Health Share if you need extra health.

I feel her acknowledgement as she heads towards the gates. At the same time, I head to the edge, flaring my wings in preparation to leap down to the ground floor.

Valence steps between me and the wall edge.

“Markus, you can’t mean to go out there?” he asks with horror. “You’re powerful, yes, but you can’t think you can overcome a Tier four! And we can’t guarantee to be able to defend you in time.”

I turn my head and look at him steadily.

“I know,” I agree, “but I am Bastet’s packleader. I will not leave her to face this alone.”

Valence’s expression hardens.

“Then take me with you too. We can defend her together.”

I’m touched and reach out to place a hand on his shoulder.

“I appreciate the offer,” I tell him quietly, but sincerely, "but if this Tier four decides to attack, we’ll need a rescue.”

Valence searches my face for a moment that feels long in the context. Then he nods and steps aside.

“Then we shall be ready.”

I offer him a humourless smile.

“I hope it’s not needed.”

With that, I leap to the ground, using my wings to cushion my landing. The gates crack open and Bastet and I slip through them.

Outside, I feel immediately more vulnerable. Although I am aware that we were hardly more protected behind the wall, given what this beast had proven himself capable of, it feels significantly different to be on the open ground, facing a large elephant-sized leopard without anything visible between us.

“We’re here,” I tell the nunda, proud my voice doesn’t shake despite how much I’m quaking inside. Bastet stands to my side, her head level with mine, her posture proud and confident – if not for the way her wings are so tightly pressed against her back that they’re trembling. “What do you want with Bastet?”

The nunda glances at the raptorcat beside me and then snarls again. This close, it rips through me with as much impact as a crack of thunder.

I have no interest in the larnatis! the nunda snarls, sounding impossibly more enraged. Bring out the female!

“What female?” I demand, frustration briefly overcoming my primal terror. I gesture angrily at Bastet. “Don’t you want to meet the new Beast Lord?” Unless…. Could it be…?

What care I for that as long as she does not challenge me? the nunda demands impatiently. I want my mate!
