I wish the only thing I felt right now was pride at just how quickly my forces had organized themselves. It felt like we had gathered and readied ourselves in record time, before the gates of Karag Varla. In reality, it was to be expected. Everyone had been anticipating our arrival at the Hold for days now, and not only had been fully armed and armored, but had been impressed upon about the time limit we were operating on.

They were ready, and I was proud of them…

But mostly I was just frustrated and focused.

I don’t know what was happening here at Karag Varla, but I was positive that whatever it was, it was about to become my problem.

I didn’t have time for more problems. And while I knew I needed to have more sympathy for whatever might be happening to the people of the Hold, I couldn’t afford it right now.

I needed to push, and that meant I needed to identify the problem and fix it. I had no idea what I was getting into, but what else was new? I was good at fixing problems.

Oftentimes in a permanent manner.

For our formation, I made sure that Olag’s platoon was at the forefront. My theory was that if this missing legion was on the other side of the entrance tunnel, they would be less likely to attack what was mainly a group of fellow Dwarves.

Plus, he and his Raven’s Beak were the heaviest armored of us.

The tunnel was only wide enough for two of our formations to fit shoulder to shoulder within the length of it, even if it was many times that in height. I made sure that my platoon was one of the two marching right after Olag, if only to set an example to the men, while Bleddyn’s was directly to our right. It set my teeth on edge just to step foot in the tunnel. It was far darker in this stretch of immaculately quarried and set stone than it had been before the gates. There weren’t any of the gigantic light crystals in here, with the light of the last one only barely stretching past the entrance.

This place was a goddamned death-trap, if I had ever seen one. Not only was it too narrow for a conventional army to fully march through, but it curved. The entire tunnel twisted back and forth like the undulating of a serpent. Not to an insane extent, but enough that you couldn’t see the end of it. Like the winding of a great old river, I could see the practicalities of it. Furthermore, even through the darkness of the tunnel, my high Perception score and mutated eyes could see the faint depressions in the wall, where the stone could pull away to reveal surprises. The entire top half of the entryway into Karag Varla was lined with murder holes.

Maybe it had been a bit arrogant of me when I had thought we might be able to force our way through if the Jarls denied us entrance. I think it would have been easier to siege Blutstein than this narrow corridor. You’d pay a blood toll of thousands for every inch gained in here, and I didn’t have thousands.

I had hundreds, and I had to hope they would be enough.

I smelled it before I saw it. The scent drifted down the tunnel before we even reached the end of it. At this point in my life on Vereden, I had grown familiar with the sickly sweet stench that assaulted my nose. I wrinkled it, and cast down my eyes to the form of Fade stalking to my side, at the head of my platoon. The Spirit Wolf had flattened his ears against his skull and scrunched up his muzzle. At my look, he nodded his head with bared teeth.

Death. This was the smell of death and rot, and it didn’t bode well.

And then we passed beyond the next curve of the tunnel and reached the end of it. Before us lay the portal into Karag Varla, and the entirety of the Mountain Hold enfolded before us.

The sight of it was so contrary to my expectations, and so awe-inducing at the same time, that I felt my breath hitch in my chest. I was far from the only one, as well. Low murmurs of amazement rolled through the ranks, backwards and forwards, and I had to almost run into the back of one of Olag’s armored warriors in front of us. Our Dwarves weren’t immune to the sight either.

In a strange way, I could see the influences that had created the Dwarven, or perhaps I should say Velancian, sense of aesthetics within the greater country. Velancians were fond of low-angled, steepled roofs clad in clay-fired shingles, atop multi-storied buildings. Residential and business, it was often hard to tell them apart, because often they were used for the same purpose. Domes and bell-towers were common in Velancia as well, often denoting more important areas in the city, to easily denote from a distance that governmental services could be found there. Nothing like the endless towers of Blutstein, of course, but still noticeably well-built. Just in a more…horizontally inclined manner, rather than the vertical building of Herztal.

Varla was much like that, just…more so, if that made any sense. I think the Dwarves of the hold might well have hollowed out the entire base of the mountain, from just how much the city before me stretched out. It was, by far, the single widest city I had ever seen in my life, and I could see the whole thing as it stretched out before me. The base, and only level of it, lay below the entrance into it from the mouth of the gates, like a great built upon plain. But where the Velancians seemed to favor clay and brick construction, the Dwarves of Karag Varla very much preferred stonework. Specifically, what could only be marble. Very polished, precisely cut marble, different from the plain stone of the gates. All of it was lit by the single largest example of a light stone I had ever laid eyes upon, embedded in the faraway ceiling of the hollowed out mountain, it’s five great spokes snaking through the cloud cover that had formed across the roof, blocking the bulk of it from the eye.

All of that was very eye-catching, to be sure…but it wasn’t what was so amazing about the city.

No, it had to be the water.

High upon the walls of the enormous chamber, arranged in a half circle upon the back wall of the mountain, were waterfalls. Five of them, to be precise. Massive, placid sheets of crystal clear water, each nearly as tall as Kyronkar itself, flowing from precisely cut indentations in the walls. What was most astonishing about these waterfalls was just how calm they were. The only reason I could tell they were actually water, and not just monumentally carved panes of glass, was because of the gargantuan reservoir that lay between the walls of the mountain and the city itself. The flow of the water as it cascaded down was so quick and so smooth that the effect was similar to something I had seen once upon a time, back on Earth. Laminar flow, if I remembered right from my old classes, in the before years.

The amount of water in that awe-inspiringly huge Dwarven made lake-no, inland sea, was more than I had seen anywhere but upon the ocean. There were even ships floating upon it, and not just small row boats either.

I don’t know how they had done such a thing, but they had certainly taken advantage of it when building the Hold-City of Varla itself. The entire thing was threaded with canals. There were so many snaking their way through and along the streets of Varla that they almost appeared to be veins, carrying what could very well be the lifeblood of the Hold. There were so many bridges, and docks, and jetties all around the Hold that it was just…genuinely awe-inspiring. Not what I had expected from the Holds at all.

However…something was tainting the magnificence of Varla. Something I had smelled on the way here, completely befouling the scent of fresh water, I could tell that tried to linger in the nose.

Instead, the air was filled with the smell of blood and death…for a very obvious reason.

There had not only been a battle here, and recently at that. But I think it was still in progress.

The exit from the tunnel led into a long series of stairs stretching out into the city itself, along a beautiful pathway interspersed with fountains.

The entire thing was littered with the dead bodies of Dwarves. Two particular kinds of Dwarves, to my eyes. The corpses were so numerous that a number had even fallen into the massive ringed reservoir that surrounded the Hold. They floated, face down, in the water with streamers of crimson blood flowing from them like bright pennants flying in the wind. They littered the stone, as well, and in the distance, what I assumed was a normally immaculate ‘skyline’ for Varla was littered with curling fingers of smoke, rising from fires below to mingle with the misty clouds creeping along the ceiling.

The distance was too great for sound to reach us, and I couldn’t see into the city from where I stood, but I was certain that there had to be a battle raging through those streets at this very instant.

For a moment, I felt a sharp, painful flashback to the maddening battle through the burning streets of Rhoscara…and my failure within them.

I would not allow that to happen again.

I grit my teeth, and when Olag glanced sharply back at me from his place at the front of the column, I gestured forward.

We weren’t stopping here.

My forces carefully marched down the steps that led into the city, nearly slipping on the blood that stained their marble surface. Almost unconsciously, the soldiers of the new expedition fell into a defensive position, while the leaders huddled together. I hunched down alongside Olag as we inspected two dead examples of the differing forces, here on the bridge that led deeper into Varla. One was what I could only assume was a member of the Hold Legion, a stout example of his race clad in what almost looked like bronze-seeming armor, of a cut and style not unlike that of what the statue at the gates had worn. Oddly enough, he had a crest upon his helmet, with an almost broom-like shape. Normally, I think it would have been white in color.

But it had been dyed a deep, sickly crimson from his own blood. It looked like a blade had found a gap in his armor at the throat and opened it up. I was almost grateful that I couldn’t see this Dwarf’s face.

The other one, though…he couldn’t be farther from the other.

At first, I wanted to attribute his downright disgusting and disquieting appearance to his own battle wounds. Unlike the first, this Dwarf was clad in nothing but an odd pair of trousers. No armor, no shirt, not even any armor for the battle he had died in. He was bald as well, just like every other example of his faction I could see around us, but that was the least of his differences.

He had no nose. No lips. No eyelids. He didn’t even have ears. Everything that could have been a fold on his body, or even something beyond the most extraneous, skeletal covering extensions, had been cleanly sliced off in an expressly deliberate manner. Gingerly lifting one broad, limp palm, I could see that this thing, this mockery of a Dwarf, didn’t even have fingernails. It was…disturbing to consider, but even his nipples had been cut off.

Even more, though…as part of my examination, my hand accidentally brushed the only pair of clothing he had on, those strange pants.

…too soft. That leather was far too fine-grained for something as simple as trousers. A light tan in color, they were strangely simple in design, in comparison to the apparent quality of the materials. He…almost looked naked, like some kind of deformed, sexless doll with only those pants on, almost the same color as his…

…skin.

I grimaced as I looked away from them, making the connection. It was only reinforced when Olag snarled from his place next to me.

“Korvoks,” He sneered, spitting off to the side. “Where did they come from? These mad creatures…It’s hard to imagine them overcoming the Varlayan Legion so easily.”

“Considerin’ there’s nearly four of ‘em here for every dead Varlayan…” Bleddyn said distantly, standing above us. “I’d say that’s how. They swarmed ‘em. It ain’t pretty, but there’s a damned lot of strength in numbers.”

“Honorless curs…” Olag muttered under his breath, shaking his head and standing. “Marshal, we cannot allow the Korvoks to wipe out the Varlayans. I will not allow more Dwarves to fall to the adherents of madness. Not again.”

“If they’re not already all dead…” Sylvia whispered, standing off to the side. She ignored the foul look that Olag sent her in response.

I think he was surprised when I stood from my own crouch and nodded sharply. “And it won’t. We do this fast. We get in, we kill every Korvok we see, and then we make for the Spirit. Hard, brutal, and quick. No wasting time, and no mercy. It shouldn’t be hard to distinguish the Varlayans from the Korvoks, when you encounter them, considering…that.” I waved a dismissive hand at the disfigured Dwarf lying on the bridge. “Link up with whatever Varlayans you can, and if they don’t have a safe place to retreat to, send them back down the tunnel to our reinforcements. Otherwise, we’re breaking up. All forces are to advance into the city and kill any Korvok you encounter. This will not be a second Rhoscara.”

I met every pair of eyes I could, standing in a circle around me. Captains and regular soldiers.

“Move out, and show no mercy.”
