Aodhán hadn't shared his knowledge of familiars with the sect to get something in return. In fact, up until this moment, the idea hadn't even crossed his mind. But if Zarxan was offering, how could he say no when there was still so much he needed?

To be honest, Aodhán felt he had gained a lot already just from the conversation they'd had about the pendant—no, the interface. The conversation had opened his eyes to the true nature of chaos in the Origin Plane and even raised questions in his mind about how to proceed now that he could enter the Origin Plane of Storm without being affected by chaos.

More than that, it had opened his eyes to the gravity of what Az'marthon had done for him back in the Tower of Ascension, opening his mind to the Origin Plane without any inspiration or meditation.

The conversation alone had provided him with so much understanding and insight—not to mention the free appraisal—that Aodhán would have been more than content with it. But if Zarxan thought he deserved more, then Aodhán wouldn’t argue.

With a smile, he answered, "I have a list of things I need. Do you want me to write them down or simply call them out?"

Whispers erupted behind him as Sophran and Seamus began suggesting a few things they thought Aodhán needed to ask for, but as important as they were, Aodhán had bigger plans. He fixed his gaze on Zarxan, waiting, a bit nervously, as the man regarded him intently.

After a long moment, Zarxan finally spoke. "You may call them out. Luseph will jot them down."

Elder Luseph glanced at Aodhán, not particularly enthused about the task. Grudgingly, he pulled a notepad and pen from his robes. "I'm listening."

Aodhán nodded. "I want three major things. A direct contact line to High-Elder Zarxan, a hundred thousand sect points, and a position on Elder Gumi's team for the duration of the project."

A stunned silence settled over the hall the moment he finished speaking. Elder Luseph blinked at him in disbelief. Elder Vasmos shook her head in resignation. Behind him, the other specials stood open-mouthed in shock.

As huge as a hundred thousand sect points was, it wasn't so huge that the sect couldn't afford it, nor was his last request that big of a deal, either.

No, what held everyone in stunned silence was his first request.

A direct contact line to a High Elder?

It was tantamount to having a Calamity at one's beck and call—an achievement that would no doubt skyrocket his reputation into the stratosphere, opening doors to opportunities he didn't even realize existed.

But as great as that was, a greater reputation wasn't the reason Aodhán had requested Zarxan's contact. The High Elder was simply the only person he fully trusted in the sect—a man who could cross a continent to save him was a man he could trust to have his back if things ever went south.

And if such a time came—as it almost always did—having a direct line to one of the most powerful beings in the sect would most definitely come in handy.

Zarxan seemed to sense his intentions in that moment, and after another pause, he spoke. "I'll keep your request in mind, Special Disciple Brystion. And as soon as the other High Elders approve the project, I'll get back to you." His gaze softened as he continued. "As for my contact line, I fear I cannot give that to you. I can, however, give you the next best thing."

He glanced at Elder Luseph. "My aide will give you his contact. Once you reach him, you've reached me."

Aodhán glanced at Elder Luseph, who gave him a wooden smile in response. It wasn't exactly what he'd wanted, but it was better than going through the cogs of sect bureaucracy.

Aodhán smiled. "Thank you, High Elder."

Zarxan nodded. "You are welcome, Aodhán. You may all go."

The door swung open as they all turned to leave. However, they'd barely taken a step before Elder Vasmos's voice rang out. "Apologies, High Elder, but I, too, would like to make a request."

Zarxan raised an eyebrow, impatience leaking into his tone. “What is it, Vasmos?”

"I'd like to work on the interface project, too. Elder Gumi will need as many hands as she can get."

Zarxan nodded. "I'll try to persuade her. But you must know that Elder Gumi can be a bit… difficult."

"I know that." Elder Vasmos bowed. "All I ask is that you try."

Zarxan sighed. "I shall. Now you may go."

They left the hall a moment later, and as soon as the door shut behind them, the other specials converged upon Aodhán with excited chatter.

"Dude!" Mirelle exclaimed. "You took your request to the moon."

Sophran shook his head, his expression a mixture of pride and shock. "We thought our ideas were greedy, but yours? That's next level, man." He frowned. "I have a lot of questions, though. There are so many things you need to explain."

"And I'm sure Aodhán would gladly explain it all when you all return to your residences." A voice came from behind them, and they all turned to see Elder Luseph. He raised an eyebrow at Aodhán and stated. "I believe you forgot to get my contact."

"Oh," Aodhán realized, and he stretched his ringed finger toward the Mythic. Elder ThunderWind had explained the process to him.

Elder Luseph glanced at his outstretched finger for a moment before, very hesitantly, clinking his own against it. "There. Now you have my contact."

Aodhán smiled. "I promise not to call you at every inconvenience."

“Oh, don’t be a stranger, Aodhán.” Elder Luseph stated with that same blank expression. “I need good reasons to pluck that number out of that ring. You being rational isn’t going to help my cause at all.”

He stepped back into the hall without a backward glance, and after the door shut once more, Darien muttered. "What an enigma; I'm never sure whether he's a friend or foe."

"He's not related to any of you." Elder Vasmos scoffed as she created a platform of smooth, ordered electricity and gestured for them to climb onto it.

They did, and as they descended back to the Special Sect, Aodhán decided to voice a question that had been gnawing at him all morning.

“Aren’t you guys scared of the war?”

The question must have seemed out of the blue to many, but for Aodhán, it wasn't. That question had roiled in his mind ever since he'd seen the chanting crowd that morning, each one so easily accepting of the war as if it were a simple shift in the weather.

In fact, he'd almost expected them to resent him. If this were Lutia, that wouldn't even be in question. But rather than ask if they resented him, Aodhán settled for the safer question.

Fortunately, his question had the intended effect, and everyone sobered. Aodhán wasn't sure how he would have felt had they brushed the topic aside. Darien was the one who responded first.

"Of course, we're scared. Survival is never certain in times of war, and regardless of whoever wins, people will surely die. But we are a family, and if another sect threatens one of us, then we are not going to take it lying down."

Mirelle nodded. "If we won't go to war to protect one of our own, then what would we go to war for?"

Those weren't the responses Aodhán had been expecting. These people could have avoided this war by chasing him out of the sect or killing him, but instead, they were ready to fight to protect him.

Their words pricked his heart, and despite his earlier hesitance, Aodhán asked, "And you guys don't despise me for it?"

"Oh, we despise you." Seamus replied while slicking his hair back. "With your familiar, your aspect, your status as a transmigrant, and storms know what else, you're pretty easy to despise."

Elder Vasmos chuckled. “We are family in the Storm Sect, Aodhán. We are bound by the affinity thrumming through our pathways. But the main reason why we don’t resent you is because, despite how it seems, you’re not the cause of this war.”

The specials nodded, but Aodhán frowned in confusion. “My presence here is the reason for the war.”

“It is,” Elder Vasmos agreed. “But you’re just unlucky to be the fourth transmigrant. It could have easily been Sophran, or Nymera, or Ashscar.”

Sophran nodded. “So, you see, in essence, you’re not the cause of the war. And if you think, “Oh, the sect shouldn’t have accepted you, then you’d be wrong too, because the sects are obligated never to turn away one of their own. So, in the real sense, this war is sort of inevitable. If you hadn’t been the fourth transmigrant, someone else would have been, and the war would have happened regardless.”

Aodhán wanted to argue, to point out that he was still the catalyst, but the logic was sound. The sect hadn't chosen to make him the fourth transmigrant—fate had.

And suddenly, he felt less guilty about the whole thing and wondered why Zarxan hadn’t explained it this way.

It would have made him feel a lot better about the war if Zarxan had explained it this way. It was a fresh perspective to look through, and though it didn’t quite eliminate his guilt, it certainly eased it greatly.

Nodding, he asked, “How terrible do you think this war is going to be?”

There was a slight beat of hesitation before Elder Vasmos spoke. “We don’t know. We can never accurately predict how things will turn out. But no matter how it does, you should know that you have absolutely nothing to fear. From the outer sect to the elder sect, we will fight as one, and we will protect the sect with all that we have.”

Aodhán glanced at the others, and Sophran smiled. "Every one of us."

Aodhán regarded them all for a moment before nodding. "Well, I don't intend to sit around while I wait. I need to grow stronger. Can you guys help me?"

Seamus grinned. "It will be our utmost pleasure."

Far, far away from the mountains of the Storm Sect, hundreds of miles distant, lay the Serenity Sect—a paradise of perfect stillness nestled within an array of luscious green mountains.

Unlike the Storm Sect, there were no clouds in the sky above the Sect of Serenity. Rather, it was perfectly blue, with the sun shining down at just the perfect, serene, little angle. The winds blew gently, birds and insects chirped, brooks bubbled, flowers bloomed, and a blissful aura of perfect contentment reigned.

The sect was a sanctuary of perfect stillness and peace, like a memory trapped in time—unchanging, unchaotic, and serene. Or it would have been, save for one oddity.

A single jagged line of crimson streaking across the sky like a scar.

Bloodlust—a reminder of the brutality of their most recent war against the Sect of Entropy. They had won, of course, but despite their victory, they had lost a significant number of disciples and talents and were only just recovering.

The next few years should have been a time to lay low and bask in the peace of restored balance. But for a people so peace-seeking, it seemed the Sect of Serenity was intent on living in direct contradiction to its ideals.

Deep within their grandest temple, in a hall so steeped in the essence of serenity that even the air seemed afraid to move, a trio of Calamities sat atop their meditation mats, their gazes serene as they waited for the First Seat to arrive from her seclusion.

Nothing moved while they waited save the Calamities themselves, their chests rising and falling with each slow breath. Time passed slowly, crawling forward at a perfectly serene pace, until finally, a bell chimed—soft, airy, like the chirping of a bird.

As one, the Calamities rose to their feet and bowed as the First Seat arrived—a woman so beautiful and powerful that her entrance sent a flutter of fear and admiration through the hearts of the Calamities.

Yet, her entrance created not even a ripple in the space as she moved to her own mat and gracefully sat down. Her serene gaze fixed on the Second Calamity, and in an almost musical tone, she asked, "Who is the culprit this time?"

"The Storm Sect." The Calamity's voice was steady. "It happened just this morning—an imbalance so drastic it's like nothing we've ever seen."

The First Seat grimaced slightly. "What could it be this time? A new Calamity? Several?"

"No, First Seat," the Calamity responded, his bald head gleaming in the sunlight. "I fear the current situation is far more delicate." He exchanged a glance with his silent partners before proceeding. "We think it's a transmigrant—a very powerful one."

Anyone else might have gasped in shock, but the First Seat was too serene for that. Instead, her eyes widened slightly, and she whispered, "This makes it the fourth transmigrant in their possession, and if he's as strong as you say…" She trailed off and shook her head. "How bad is it?"

"Very bad, First Seat. Despite being a force of change, the Storm leans too much into destruction and chaos. Its very structure opposes us, and with their recent addition, their Origin Plane has not only expanded—it's caused its counters to shrink. Us and several others, First Seat. It is a great imbalance, and it must be righted."

The First Seat exhaled. "We just concluded our strife against Entropy, High Elder Haorin. To go on another campaign for balance now, when our people are still recovering…" She shuddered. "My entire being is against the idea."

"As are ours, First Seat, but our corner of the heavens is already small. We have only two transmigrants among our ranks, and the Storm Sect has double. Even as we speak, I can feel the Origin Plane of Serenity shrinking."

The other Calamities nodded, feeling the same thing, and even the First Seat grimaced.

High Elder Haorin continued, "The Storm Sect is on the rise. They will not stop here, and neither will their Origin Plane. If we do not pull them down to maintain balance, their influence will spread across the heavens and choke our order with chaos. It won't be long before we find ourselves rotting in obscurity."

A tension descended upon the hall immediately, but the First Seat dispelled it with a single flick of her fingers. "What other sects were affected by this expansion?"

"The Sect of Earth, the Sect of Silence, and the Sect of Illusions, among a host of others."

"Then we must reach out to them in preparation for another campaign. I will be glad on the day when we can finally rest, but until then, it seems there is no rest for the peaceful." Her gaze hardened. "Bringing the Storm Sect down is now our topmost priority. With four transmigrants in their possession, it's only a matter of time before they subjugate the entire continent under their heel and crush us all to oblivion. And by Serenity, we cannot let that happen."
