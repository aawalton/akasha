Against all the signs of his body, Harry walked out of the church, no longer needing the help of Vela, who was willing to offer it. The sky was dark already as the Locksmith and the former monk walked alongside each other. Harry found a heavy stick he was using as a crutch, but what kept him going wasn't this.

Should I really? He added inwardly. I mean, they could have fun without me, right? Everything around me hurts...

"I know what you're thinking." Vela said in a calm voice, his bronze skin appearing through the light of his candelabrum.

"Yeah, right..." Harry said with a chuckle. "You do."

"You're thinking of bailing out or just staying in the church, recovering."

"...You do." Harry replied after a moment, surprised. "Wait, do you think I was going to just run away?"

"So the thought didn't cross your mind?" Vela said as they kept walking, slowly.

"I... Yeah, it did. But come on, you can't expect me to just be okay with everyone and be all talkative after I nearly died."

"In the monastery," Vela began, looking at the stars, "We were forced to stay days without food and shelter. It was a test, not obligatory, but I chose to do it either way. People think they are hungry after a day or two of skipping meals, but that's not hunger, that's merely an inconvenience compared to true famine. You stare for minutes at a rock, forcing yourself not to put it in your mouth just in case there's something squishy inside it. You grasp at flies like a drowning man clings at a straw. Nature is unforgiving. It is irreducible.

"After a while, you start to interiorize the facts that led you here. You leave the experience a different man or woman. After you've talked with your primitive self face to face and survived, you know that pain is but an evil we can control. It isn't nature who defines us, nor the perception of others. It is hardships. It is life. And it is death.

"And when I ate the first meal I had in weeks, I cried desperately. I didn't even know there could be so much water inside a human being, but there was. I hugged all of my friends and didn't let go for about ten minutes. My family was there. Everyone was there. My point is: you've already been through the worst. Don't treat your mind as an independent entity from your body. Even if you don't feel like it at the moment."

Harry stared at the fake stars, a meek copy of the beauty of the universe. His eyes then focused on the illusion of the trees and the outrageously false flame that came from the ground. The church behind him with a weird white mask on top of it, which was all also an illusion, stared at him, forcing itself not to blink. In front of him, however, came a heat wave stronger than the mother flame; it was a real one. A warmth from connection.

"...So you're saying you ate a rock?" Harry finally answered.

"That's all you've got from my monologue?" Vela chuckled. "You know what? Maybe that's for the best."

"No, no!" The Locksmith stopped him and gave the man a warm smile. "I get it. I know, um, I see what you mean. It's just... It's a lot, Vela. This Tower really isn't what I was imagining."

"What were you imagining?" The former monk said as they were almost arriving at the party.

"Definitely more blood and guts." He replied. "Dozens of crimes being committed all at the same time. And maybe something involving poison."

"Oh, that's because you haven't tasted the beer!"

"And you did? Isn't that, like... blasphemy or something?"

"I am a former monk, Mr. Harry. I am entitled to have a couple of... escapades from time to time."

"Fuckin' hell, Vela!" Harry cheered the man. "Now I want to see you drunk—"

"Locksmith!" A hoarse voice broke Harry's vibe as he snapped at the direction of said voice. Daniel was standing still, with such a sharp frown Harry thought he'd get hurt if he came too close.

Gulping, he took a step forward. "...Yes?"

After a few seconds of pure, awkward silence. Daniel raised his left hand to greet him. "Come and have a few drinks with us. Now."

Vela was right, the beer was tasteless. As if the person who created it had no idea what beer even meant and just grabbed a bunch of water from the nearest lake and colored it orange. An orange water with bubbles and a slight taste of beer from back home. At least it's something... Harry added inwardly as he ventured into another sip.

Daniel and Goldman were sitting and, as one would expect, discussing the morality of combat. The pirate, with a smile in his face — which made Daniel even madder — defended the innate goodness of mankind and how the existence of civilization is an evidence. The viking, however, trusted in humanity's knack for cruelty. And how if we were really all that good, there wouldn't be laws or prisons. Although Harry didn't exactly agree with Daniel's position, he was surprised in how much thought the man put into the conversation, which didn't seem like him.

Near him, a frowning Manes was trying to reach the beer out of Vela's hands. "Give it to me! I'm probably older than you!"

"I am doing this for your own good, Mr. Manes. Time has told us that—Ouch!"

The boy kicked his ankle and... growled? Maybe he had spent too much time around Daniel.

"Hey, kid." Harry came closer to him and Vela. "You alright?"

"I should be asking you that." He then grabbed Harry's right stump. "Does it hurt?"

"Yes!" Harry took a step back and prepared to kick the kid but... Yeah, that wouldn't be nice; even if he really wanted to. The young thief then sighed deeply and continued. "So you decided to help us, huh? Really makes you think."

"That brute has been tormenting people for about... I don't even know for how long, actually."

"You've been inside the tower for long?" Harry asked.

"A bit, yeah..." The kid looked to his dirty shoes. "Although time passes differently here, I feel like a thousand years have gone by. Like, I should've gotten bigger and all grown up. Everyday I have the same dream: I wake up in a stormy day, only to find a beautiful mirror where I can see how much I've grown, only to see myself. I'm a forever kid, man."

"...You're not just a kid," Harry added. "You're a survivor of the Tower. The Logos Tower. Do you realize that? That's not just some random achievement."

"I guess you could say so..."

"How did you end up here, anyway?"

"Emblems, Harry." Manes said in a tone that matched a rainy day and funerals. "It's always Emblems."

"...I know." Harry said, thinking about his own troubles. "I know."

"Hey!" Goldman grabbed Harry's shoulder with a tenth of his strength. "How we doing? Is the beer okay? I made it!"

And as soon as he said it, the group kept quiet.

"HAHAHA!" He broke into a guffaw. "I'm just kidding, aye. I would never do such harm to my fellow crew. Now I need you guys to just help me and Daniel in a—"

"Fierce verbal battle." The viking completed.

"Um..." Harry, glancing to his side, saw a red haired woman sitting on a log, alone. "Hey, do you guys know where Madeline is? There's something I would like to talk with her."

"Oh, right, she's, um..." Goldman looked around, landing on the same spot as Harry's. "Right over there."

"Okay, thanks. I'll be right back..."

Harry still held onto his crutch but walked in a way as to not make any sound.

“Lacoste!” He shouted in her ear. As a result, Madeline turned around at the same instant, grabbed him by the arm and threw him over the bench. Her vision was then covered by Harry as he groaned in agony.

“I...!” She covered her mouth with her hands, dropping the brush. “My god, Harry! Are you okay? I didn’t think—”

The young thief, between groans of agony, let out some laughs. When Madeline noticed this, she made a blank face, picked up her brush from the ground and crossed her arms.

“You know what.” She said. “You deserved it.”

He kept laughing while hugging his leg. What he really wanted to do was scream in pain, but he didn’t want to draw attention to Madeline, who clearly moved away from the group for a reason. Instead, he held it in and prepared to get up with his crutch. He asked for her hand while fixing his hair.

“Come on. It was funny...” And when he saw that she didn’t react, he changed his approach and tone. “...I’m sorry.”

A few moments passed before she gave in and offered a hand. He then put his crutch aside and sat next to her, both looking at a gigantic mountain in the background. Harry turned his head a little and saw the beer by her side.

“Why are you drinking this? It sucks.” He pointed to the mug.

“That's a different one, I grabbed it in one of the cabins...” She said with a slight smile on her face. “They are way better, you know? Like, they encapsulate the taste so good it almost makes me—

Harry took a sip.

“Fuck!” He opened his eyes in surprise. “What’s the alcohol percentage in this? I think if someone drinks more than two sips, they get intoxicated.”

“No need to exaggerate.” Madeline added, “But yeah, it’s pretty bad... Just like the others, but somehow worse. ”

The two then stayed quiet for a few seconds. Madeline picked up her brush and kept playing with it while Harry thought about what to say.

“...Lacoste?” She then turned to him, curious. “Where do you know his works from? You always seemed a bit ignorant about that to me.”

Ouch.

“Just because I didn’t create an entire identity around stealing art that I can't—”

“I didn’t steal as much as I transported them to a safer place. There’s a difference.”

“Whatever...” Harry continued, “Art has its place in the world, and some of the nobles I was after were obsessed with that guy. I always thought he was a bit pretentious... Besides, I had a hunch you would show up because of him, so I did my homework.”

Taken aback, she didn’t know what to answer for a few moments, which made Harry wonder if he had said something wrong.

“Were you stalking me?” She finally said.

“Wait, what?” He turned his head to the right. “No, of course no. I just... Some days, when I had already done my tasks for the semester, I didn’t have much to do. I couldn’t go out or even drink. In those moments, I remembered how fun it was to trade lines with you. That’s all. It seemed important, you know? You had a great passion for what you did, which made my work seem much more executive.”

It was funny how Harry talked about his work as if it didn’t literally involve stealing important documents and books around the capital. His heart told the truth, despite that; his thefts had a motive, and that motive led him here, to this moment.

“You know what pissed me off the most?” Madeline grabbed her brush and started making shapes in the air as she spoke. “They spent hundreds of thousands, some even millions of Chimes per piece, just to leave them sitting in the middle of a hallway that at most two people walk through per day. I’m sure the painters didn’t make art just for it to be thrown away by the world. Someone needed to appreciate them...”

I’m also sure the artists were definitely very happy with millions of chimes in their pockets... Harry added internally, holding himself back from saying it and making her even more upset.

Madeline evidently had a great appreciation for art and what it meant. Maybe she looked at those framed paintings left to be forgotten and thought that, if she were to engage in the same business, her art would also be left to rot. Art was something to be remembered. It is the celebration of the human condition! She thought triumphantly. She wasn’t lying. Not at all, but what right did she have over that art? Maybe the paintings were exactly where they should be.

Despite that, one knew better not to contradict thieves. While most carried out their acts out of greed, the few, those with ideas behind their actions, like Harry and Madeline, were stubborn. Kleptomaniacs with purpose were the most dangerous types of thief. Those were the ones who never gave up.

Thump! Thump! His heartbeat doubled as he touched her without meaning to. Before the moment became awkward, he smiled and asked the first thing that came to his mind.

“How'd you end up here?” He asked. “In the Logos Tower, I mean.”
