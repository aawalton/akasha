Sen

The rain, the mist, and the darkness of night all worked together to create the perfect cover for their escape. Each moment brought them closer to the city’s east gate. But somehow, the closer they got, the more tension there was in the air.

When they were close, Drussan held up a hand, and their procession came to a halt. Khan Vlay scanned the rooftops. “Something wrong?” he asked.

Drussan shook his head. “Gosen and I will go on ahead to ensure the path is clear. Keep the pace and stay vigilant,” he instructed the others.

Ebron nodded.

Mau gave him a thumbs-up.

Drussan and Sen exchanged a look, and then they set off. It was strange seeing Drussan move so quickly in Malachi’s body, but there weren’t any other people around so maintaining the appearance of a frail old man didn’t matter. Between the heavy rain and the thick mist, the streets were empty.

With just the two of them, progress toward the gate was quick. They used a building’s corner for added cover and surveyed the gates. The rain and mist were products of his mana, and when he peered to see through their veil, they obliged. The effect extended to Drussan as well.

There were dozens of men on top of the wall. They held up shields, spare clothing, and whatever else they could find to protect themselves from the rain while remaining at their post. Most nights, the guards on duty would focus their attention outside the walls. But tonight, the guards were evenly split, scanning both sides of the wall in equal measure.

Something was off.

The guards looked on edge, and not just because of the weather. Sen turned to Drussan to check his expression.

Drussan’s face hardened. “There are too many of them. They’re nervous. I don’t like this, honored one.”

“Have we been betrayed? Do you think your contact sold you out?” Sen asked.

Drussan shook his head. “I gave the lieutenant over this stretch of wall more gold than he could earn in a lifetime, and that was just half upfront. There wasn’t any reason to betray us.”

Of the two of them, Drussan was far more experienced with bribes. When Sen wanted something, he used power to get it. He’d trust the dreadbeast knew what he was talking about. But if he was right, and they hadn’t been betrayed…what was going on?

The answer came in the shape of a man.

Someone shouted something. The guards dropped the various objects they were using to shelter from the rain and stood in formation. A man with bright red, long hair that hung down his back and a spear in hand approached. Sen recognized him from when he and his friends had first come into town.

Captain Fulcrae.

“What is he doing here? Could he have learned of the bribe?” Sen asked.

Drussan shook his head. “I…I don’t know,” he said.

Fulcrae shouted orders, and the men hurried into position. Suddenly it wasn’t such an even split of men watching each side of the wall. No, now most of the men had turned their attention inside the walls. As if they were waiting to intercept someone.

[Like a group of dreadbeasts, perhaps?]

An alarm blared loudly into the night, and the insides of homes all around them lit up as people woke up wondering what was going on. “Any ideas?” Sen asked.

Drussan turned to look back the way they’d come. “We need to return quickly. If the others panic, they may act rashly.”

Sen nodded. Drussan didn’t need to warn him of what a dreadbeast could do when threatened. It was best that they return now and think of solutions as they went.

They returned to find the Khans in heated deliberation. Mau held his arms up to calm Ebron. “We just need to wait for Drussan. He’ll know what to do,” Mau said.

Ebron scoffed. “The plan has failed! We need to prepare for the worst-case scenario. It might take a fight to escape the city now.”

Vlay nudged Ebron and pointed at Drussan and Sen as they slowed to a halt in front of the group. “Any news?” Vlay asked.

“Captain Fulcrae is guarding the east gate. That path is closed to us now,” Drussan said bluntly.

Ebron shook his head. “Closed? That was our way out! If bribing our way out of the city won’t work, then we’ll fight. Those walls were built to keep people out, not to keep dreadbeasts in. We concentrate our forces and break through where they least expect.”

Sen eyed the crowd of dreadbeasts behind them. They were anxious, and many of them clearly found appeal in Ebron’s plan. They wouldn’t simply abandon their freedom. It was now or never, and they knew that. Tonight was the night for escape. If Drussan’s plan wouldn’t work, then many would turn to Ebron unless given a better alternative.

Drussan stepped forward. “These aren’t slavers. The city guard is full of innocent men and women who had nothing to do with our fate. We will find another way. One that doesn’t risk lives. Ours or there’s.”

Ebron shook his head. “What way, Drussan? What way?”

Drussan scanned the ground while his mind worked. He wanted a peaceful solution. “I’ll talk with Fulcrae.”

“He doesn’t like Malachi,” Vlay pointed out. “You said that yourself.”

“No, he doesn’t,” Drussan said. He activated the bracelet on his wrist and exploded in size as he returned to his dreadbeast form. One moment he’d inhabited the body of an old man, and the next he was a hulking dreadbeast that towered over Ebron by half a foot. “That’s why I’ll go as myself.”

“You can’t take that risk, Drussan. Besides, if they captured you, we’d have to fight anyway! To get you back!” Mau said.

“If they capture me, you leave me behind and follow Ebron’s plan. With minimal loss of life,” Drussan said.

“Don’t do this. Don’t waste yourself on the humans. My plan is good. Come with us,” Ebron said.

Sen sighed. Drussan really was a noble soul. It was inspiring, really. He wouldn’t allow Drussan to risk himself like that, though if anyone would be open to listening, it was probably Fulcrae. At the same time, Sen also wouldn’t allow Ebron to lead some reckless charge that would lead to casualties of the town guard and dreadbeast alike. No, he’d find a better solution. He’d create a path to uncompromising victory.

Drussan’s plans were freeing his people all around the Isle of Magic. He’d done so much for his people, and now Sen had an opportunity to take some weight off his shoulders. He turned away from them and scanned the street. His eyes pierced through the mist until he found what he was searching for.

“Leave it to me. I have something in mind.” Sen placed a hand on Drussan’s shoulder and walked toward the alleyway he’d spotted.

Tutor, Xochi, I’ll need your help for this.

[I know what you’re thinking. This will be difficult.]

Xochi emerged from the fabric of Sen’s shoulder. Sen had transformed his cloak into garments more appropriate for his swordsman disguise, but Xochi could still inhabit it thanks to the Spirit House passive. Though Xochi wasn’t a spirit, the cloak apparently considered him ‘spirit adjacent’ to enough of a caliber that Xochi benefited from the ability. The mana axolotl looked up expectantly at Sen at the mention of his name.

Sen smiled at that. He turned the corner and walked toward the end of the alley. It seemed that at one time this path had allowed people to cross from one side street to the other, but it had since been boarded up. It didn’t matter; this place was perfect for Sen’s purposes.

The dreadbeasts followed him. Ebron scoffed as he turned the corner. “A dead end, Drussan. Your human’s plan is a dead end.”

Sen ignored the others and focused on his work.

His progress with temporal magic was limited. He could force his way into the temporal world and even manipulate portions of it to create his own space. He’d also created the Time Crawl spell, which was an invaluable part of his magic arsenal. But other than that, he hadn’t managed to create any other spells that manipulated the flow of time. However, he had seen something in the past that might be useful to him now.

The three temporal gateways the Cult of Harpis had used to transfer their forces to and from Harpis Castle. Sen had experimented with something similar when he made his most recent temporal world. He’d created a similar gateway that would one day, in theory, allow his friends to visit his temporal world freely. But those anchor points had been simple. One was within a temporal world where he had ultimate authority, and the other was wherever he was standing at the time. It was also a smaller-scale spell designed to transport a handful of people at a time.

Here, he had hundreds of dreadbeasts that needed to get out of the city, and time was not on their side. One day, he might be able to create a temporal gateway between two anchor points all on his own. But today, he’d rely on his allies.

“How about a special mission, Xochi?” Sen asked.

The axolotl tilted its head at him.

Sen condensed a massive amount of mana into a small, snack-sized ball for his companion. The mana marble snack floated an inch above Sen’s pointer finger, and he moved it within Xochi’s reach. As soon as it was within striking distance, Xochi leaned forward and gobbled it up. Xochi shuddered with delight as the huge influx of mana coursed through his body.

“I need you to travel as far and fast as you can, Xochi. To the east. You’ll have to help me establish an anchor point for a gateway spell.”

Xochi stared blankly at him.

Sen sighed. He formed another mana marble over his finger, pointed it toward the eastern sky, and fired it off at high-speed.

Xochi didn’t waste a second. Mana axolotls were capable of amazing things when fed mana. With their abilities being heavily influenced by the power and volume of the mana they consumed. At Sen’s side, Xochi’s potential was endless. He proved that when he sprouted wings. Long, moist wings emerged from Xochi's back. They were the same pink color as his body and were each about a foot in length, much larger than Xochi himself. The external gills on the back of his head grew larger, flowing as if they’d been affected by the mana as well. With one last look at Sen, Xochi shot backwards into the air to test his new wings.

Backward and directly into Ebron’s face.

“What the—” the horse-faced dreadbeast sputtered.

Xochi collected himself and then shot up into the air after the mana projectile.

[I’m fairly certain he did that on purpose. Good boy!]

Focus, Tutor. We need to maintain the connection. Keep an eye on him.

Ebron scowled. “Are you sure he’s an ally, Drussan? He’s cornered us. If we’re attacked now, then—”

Drussan stepped dangerously close to Ebron and glared at him. “You test my patience, Ebron. Do not question either of us again. Let him work.”

Ebron stared at him for a moment before looking away. “My apologies.”

Sen shot a subtle but approving look towards Drussan before turning back towards the wall. He placed his hand on it and focused on his connection with Xochi.

Xochi was bound to him, so maintaining the link was easy enough. Now it was a matter of creating and stabilizing the gateway itself. It would be easier, far easier, with a temporal magic essence sphere. But unfortunately, he didn’t have access to one of those. Though he’d received a huge number of more common elemental spheres from the ruins of the Kingdom of Magic, temporal spheres were far too rare. He’d have to do without. Failure wasn’t an option here. The last thing he wanted to do was strand hundreds of dreadbeasts within the void of time itself.

[That’s reserved for misbehaving demons and flesh golems!]

And interfaces that distract me while I’m trying to concentrate.

[My quips are wasted on you. But I can take a hint, shutting up now.]

Xochi was moving surprisingly fast. In only a few minutes he’d traversed miles. The temporary wings he’d grown plus the potent mana coursing through his body were doing wonders for him.

[For what it’s worth, I still think we should’ve chosen the magma gorilla.]

I thought you were shutting up?

Sen focused his mana. “Temporal Gateway - Maxima!” A small, but quickly expanding silver rectangle appeared in his hand and slowly expanded. In the plains well beyond the city, Xochi latched onto the link between them and joined Sen’s efforts. Even from all this distance away, Sen could sense a second silver rectangle forming under the mana axolotl’s guidance. The creature was acting purely on instinct, mimicking its master.

But they weren’t done. Sen concentrated on the gateway and fueled it with his mana. Something resonated with the spell from within his temporal world, and he willed it forward. His God-Grade grimoire suddenly appeared beside him and flipped open to a blank page. He scanned it as words appeared within.

Temporal Gateway - Maxima.

The grimoire was chronicling the spell. In theory, it could make future attempts to cast it easier. More words popped up, but Sen couldn’t spare any more attention on them now. With the help of Xochi and Tutor, as well as the stabilizing power of his cloak and grimoire, Sen wove his mana to reinforce the structure of the gateway. It was the perfect example of just how far his arsenal had come. He was improvising his own version of a temporal spell even without using magic essence spheres to fuel its creation.

He was improvising, and it was working.

The rectangle stretched until it covered the entire wall of the alley. The walls’ corners became the gateways’ corners. There was a magic click as each end of the gateway locked into place and created a stable pathway. Each portal was wide enough for four or five dreadbeast to enter side by side.

This was their escape route.

For a moment, Sen thought of stepping through himself to test it. But before he could, Xochi flew through the portal with the mana marble in his mouth. He nibbled on it and shot back inside Sen’s cloak to rest.

Sen chuckled. “I guess it works. Good.” He was lucky he’d been able to interact with and modify the gateways to Harpis Castle in the past. If not for that hands-on experience, this might’ve been impossible without magic essence spheres.

Still, recreating a spell of this level and element without spheres was an incredible feat. He’d be sure to properly appreciate that once everyone was safe. Unlike the portals leading to Castle Harpis, this one wasn’t self-sustaining. Until he had the proper tools, creating a permanent gateway like this would be highly unlikely for now. But for their purposes tonight, that wasn’t a problem. The portal would fade as soon as Sen dropped the connection to it. It meant the dreadbeasts couldn’t be followed.

Ebron frowned. “What magic is this? It’s unfamiliar to my senses.”

Dreadbeast instincts were good. Even though they often couldn’t, or didn’t use magic, the stronger of them could still recognize and evaluate it. With some even being capable of identifying the type of magic their opponents were using. There was a reason the war between their people had been so intense a thousand years ago. Dreadbeasts were incredibly well-suited to fighting mages.

Sen didn’t have time to explain the intricacies of temporal magic. Nor was he willing to. As far as he was concerned, this was one type of magic best kept secret. “This is your path out of the city. It leads three or four miles east. No fighting. No bloodshed. This is your new east gate.”

The dreadbeasts stared at him in silence. Ebron’s eyes flickered to Drussan, and then back to the portal. “Lead the way. This is your victory, Drussan.”

“No.” Drussan shook his head. “No, I won’t leave until all of our people have. Only then is my work finished. You three go ahead. I want you there if any monsters show up. I’ll be right behind you,” Drussan said.

Vlay frowned. “Garvhadda sent us to ensure your safety. At least one of us should stay.”

“Protect the plan and our people. I can handle myself,” Drussan declared. He turned to his people and raised his voice. “This is your way home! Garvhadda awaits! Through this portal lies the path. Walk towards our new home and know that the homeland we’ve been promised is just ahead. We’ve broken our chains. Now we make the trek to the future that act has earned us!” Drussan pointed at the portal as he spoke.

The dreadbeasts cheered.

Sen sighed. He wished they’d escaped the city before giving speeches. At least he’d thrown up a silent sphere spell when he realized what was coming. No one aside from the dreadbeasts would’ve heard anything.

Drussan’s words were infectious. A wave of inspiration washed over the dreadbeasts, and just like that the despair that had fallen on their shoulders at the sound of an alarm had lifted.

Ebron and the other Khans relented. They led the way through the portal. Mau and Vlay each nodded toward Sen as they passed. Ebron paused for a moment and lowered his head. “Forgive my rudeness. I only wished to see my people free.”

“All is well. I’ll guard Drussan’s back in your absence,” Sen told him.

“Thank you,” Ebron said before stepping inside the portal.

[Ugh! Why’d he have to apologize? I was going to suggest smiting him, but now we’d look like the bad guys!]

One by one, row by row, the dreadbeasts escaped Vitree through the gateway.

About half of them were safely on the other side when Sen felt it.

His mana senses cried a warning.

Drussan noticed it too. He turned and called out to his people. “Hurry! Pick up the pace, get through as fast as you can!”

The dreadbeasts redoubled their pace, but the alleyway was crowded as it was, and their people were large.

Still, the urgency in his voice roused them.

Sen kicked off the ground and landed on the rooftops. Drussan jumped up and grabbed the edge of the roof. He pulled himself up, and part of the roof cracked under his grip.

“What is it?” Drussan asked.

Scan scanned through the rain with both his eyes and his mana senses.

Distortions in the rain. Powerful mana signatures.

“Remember those adventurers Thessus hired? The cult hunters?” Sen asked.

Drussan nodded. “Yes. What about them?”

“I think they’re here.”
