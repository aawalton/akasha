I teleported back to my team feeling better than I had in a while. The night spent dreaming with Nina had done wonders for my mood.

It was a little over an hour past dawn. The team was already on the road, riding in a cart pulled by animals that had gone extinct on Earth twenty million years ago.

Shingo and Kan were running alongside the cart as part of their morning exercise, while the rest of the team rolled along the overgrown dirt path. The terrain was gradually becoming rockier as we approached the Kurco Range, whose jagged, year-round snow-capped peaks were now visible on the horizon.

After I appeared, Calr, who was driving, pulled on the reins to stop for me.

"Your soul has expanded again," Ja'a said immediately.

I smiled as I climbed aboard.

"I expanded my dream sanctuary again."

"Your soul is stronger now than Raik's was when you first met him," Ja'a said, narrowing her eyes.

"What?" Raik asked. "Really?"

The fire-blooded boy didn't look offended or annoyed, only surprised.

I couldn't help feeling a little proud as well.

Building my sanctuary had become one of the few forms of progression that felt entirely mine. The tarot aspect gave it a level of uniqueness that was different from learning lightning magic or training with a spear. Only my teleportation power came close, but I had yet to learn how to grow my soul through it.

"If Raik hadn't received that surge in soul growth after discovering his cold manifestation," Ja'a continued, "Alice would currently possess the strongest soul on our team."

"That is precisely why soul strength is not a measure of true power," Katar said from the rear of the cart.

The swordsman was busy cleaning his blade. Despite the bumpy ride, his steady hands moved with practiced confidence, showing no fear of accidentally cutting himself.

"Soul strength isn't entirely without merit," Raik replied.

Katar looked up, but Raik continued before he could argue.

"Both she and I have similar mana capacity. Mine only seems endless because I mostly use my innate bloodline powers, while Alice relies on Soulbooks."

"If we both picked up an earth manipulation Soulbook and started throwing rocks at each other, we'd run out of mana around the same time," he added.

"Exactly," Ja'a said.

Katar snorted.

"You trained your instincts through combat," he said, shaking his head. Then he pointed at me. "You buried your killer instincts beneath years of study."

"A warrior and a scholar."

His words might have been harsh, but I wasn't offended. They were, after all, accurate.

I was under no illusion that I could win in a fight against Raik.

I was an anthropologist. I had spent years studying people, cultures, and history. My first instinct when encountering something unfamiliar was to study it rather than kill it.

No, what interested me wasn't what he said but why he said it.

I suspected I had lost a great deal of respect in his eyes after losing my cool in the aftermath of the battlefield. After spending a day sorting through the dead and injured, I had lashed out at Edmund.

Katar probably viewed my compassion as weakness, and whatever respect I had earned by killing the troll had likely vanished by now.

Oh well. He can think whatever he wants. I'm not craving his validation.

The cart rattled over a bump, and Ja'a decided to break the silence.

"Out of curiosity, how large is your sanctuary now?"

"Not large enough to have a gate to the Great Dream, but it's getting close," I said with a smile.

"Can you invite us in? I would love to visit," Calr said.

"Not without Nina's help, I'm afraid," I replied with a sheepish smile. "Maybe my next expansion should focus on that aspect."

"Too bad," he said. "I've only been inside a dream sanctuary a couple of times, and the experience was always fascinating."

"I've never been to the Great Dream," Raik said.

Vena gestured that she hadn't visited one either. Today should be the last day of her vow of silence, and she would be able to speak again tomorrow.

"Neither have I," Kan added. She was still running alongside the cart but had moved closer to listen to the conversation.

"I've been a few times," Ja'a said. "My half-sister is a dreamer and would sometimes take me to the Melody of Gossip, where we'd barter for news with the bards."

"I've only been to the Crafters' Paradise and the Madhouse," I said. "Maybe I should visit the Melody of Gossip next."

"You should," Calr said with a laugh. "That's probably where the rumors about a new saint are spreading."

My eyes widened.

"No way. I never even considered that. That's why the rumors spread to random villages so quickly."

"Yep," Calr said. "Rumors about a new saint are exactly the kind of story the bards would eat up."

I groaned and buried my face in my hand.

We traveled for a few more miles before stopping to let Shingo climb aboard. The giant Kindred had run enough for one morning. He hauled himself onto the rear of the cart with surprising grace for someone his size, causing the axle suspension to creak in protest. Five seconds later, he was asleep.

I used the interruption as an excuse to jump down and start my own cardio exercises, joining Kan, who, unlike Shingo, was still running. Out of everyone on the team, she had the best endurance. Shingo might have been physically stronger, but he could never keep up with her.

The road wound steadily upward throughout the morning, and the weather grew colder.

We passed several villages scattered across the foothills. They were mostly hunting and herding communities, with each house having its own vegetable garden instead of dedicated farmland.

A few people, after learning we were freelancers, pointed us toward a minor problem.

Apparently, a small pack of what I assumed were velociraptors had been harassing the livestock.

The birds were no larger than turkeys, but their beaks were lined with sharp teeth, which was rather uncanny since birds weren't supposed to have teeth. They also had tails as long as a cheetah's, which helped them balance while running.

And run they did.

As soon as they saw us approaching, they abandoned the goat they had been feeding on and bolted.

"Get them!" Raik shouted.

Katar and Kan sprinted after them, but there was no chance of catching up. Calr, on the other hand, whipped out his crossbow and managed to hit one in the shoulder. By the time he finished reloading, however, they were already out of range.

I summoned my spear back into my hand.

"I wish I were in their way."

A second later, I was standing directly in front of the murder chickens.

I raised my spear.

Zap!

I understood why the village needed our help. While monsters were harder to kill, beasts were smart enough to run, and I doubted a normal farmer could ever catch up to them when even Kan couldn't.

Since they were beasts rather than monsters, there were no cores to collect, and the meat wouldn't taste very good either because it was too gamey.

Still, helping the villagers earned us goodwill and a free meal.

By early afternoon, we reached the final settlement before the mountain crossing.

The village sat at the mouth of a broad pass that cut through the Kurco Range, connecting the northern territories to the southern plains where the Sunless Reach awaited. Even from a distance, the geological formation looked bizarre, as though a mountain had been sliced cleanly down the middle to create a passage between two towering cliff faces.

That was hardly the only remarkable thing about the area. No, that honor belonged to the herd of mammoths grazing on fig leaves and fruit.

The adults fed directly from the treetops, using their trunks to strip entire branches, while the juveniles picked through fallen limbs beneath the watchful eyes of their mothers.

There had to be at least fifty of them, massive shaggy beasts roaming the grove surrounding the village. Their curved ivory tusks gleamed in the sunlight while their thick brown fur rippled in the mountain wind.

For a moment, I simply stared.

Back on Earth, entire museums had been built around the skeletons of these creatures, and yet here they were nothing more than livestock.

"Everyone here is stronger than the average peasant," Ja'a whispered beside me.

Her eyes scanned the herders scattered among the beasts.

"The weakest adult I can sense is around fifteen SB."

I blinked. That was more than double the average villager's strength.

Ja'a continued.

"I can sense a pair in their thirties."

Now that was unusual. They were almost as strong as veteran freelancers.

We continued riding until we reached the village square. There, a crowd had gathered around a fenced training area. Two enormous figures were sparring beneath the watchful eye of a rugged older man.

At first, I thought they were trolls or something similar.

Then I realized they were people.

Well, sort of…

Both fighters possessed human features mixed with mammoth characteristics. Thick fur covered their bodies, massive tusks protruded from their jaws, and their noses had elongated into muscular trunks.

Most shocking of all was their size. Each stood nearly as tall as Shingo.

I watched in fascination as the two giants exchanged blows.

They wore only loincloths and fought unarmed, if you ignored their natural weapons.

Punches, kicks, and clashing tusks. Shoulder charges and the occasional trunk grab. The entire fight resembled a sumo match if the wrestlers had been allowed to gore each other with ivory tusks.

"Is that a Kindred transformation?" I whispered to Ja'a.

She nodded.

"Most likely. Almost everyone here is Kindred."

The match continued for several minutes before one combatant managed to catch the other's leg and drive him into the dirt.

The crowd erupted into cheers, and the older man raised a hand to declare the winner.

As if that were their cue, both giants rapidly shrank. The fur receded, the trunks vanished, and the tusks withdrew into ordinary canine teeth. Within seconds, the two warriors had returned to being perfectly ordinary teenagers standing in the arena.

Neither looked older than sixteen, and neither stood taller than one meter seventy-five.

"Where did all that extra mass come from?" I asked, staring.

"Beats me," Calr said.

"No, seriously."

"Maybe it's stored in their soul," he suggested.

Screw the law of conservation of mass. Who needs it anyway?

Now that the spectacle was over, we decided to park the carts at a stable. Raik paid a boy to look after the Burdrases, and then we entered the inn through a side door.

Once inside, our team leader spotted the innkeeper, an older man with the muscular build that seemed to be the norm in this village. The two exchanged greetings before discussing the road conditions and whether the mountain pass was still safe.

The old man burst out laughing.

"As if any bandit would dare set up camp near us."

The confidence in his voice was impossible to miss, but after watching the local youths fight, I was inclined to agree. Any criminal foolish enough to attack this village would probably become a cautionary tale.

Raik split off to gather information, with Katar acting as his bodyguard.

One of the barmaids, a dusk-skinned girl of maybe eighteen, began openly appraising Shingo before circling him.

"Want to spar, big guy?" she asked with a toothy grin.

Shingo pointed at himself with a bewildered expression.

"Yes, you," she said. "Who else would I be talking to? You're taller than me, even in my mammoth form."

"Careful," Ja'a said. "Her soul is bigger than yours. Fifteen to your ten."

Shingo shrugged, then nodded to the barmaid. The pair headed toward the training area.

Calr hurried after his friend, explaining Shingo's condition and translating his gestures as the girl asked where he was from and what beasts he had slain.

Vena followed as well, just in case someone needed healing.

Ja'a wandered off to inspect the local trade goods, with Kan tagging along in case she got into trouble.

That left me alone, and I decided to see if there was anyone knowledgeable about the mammoths.

A few questions directed at the local youths, along with a bronze coin, led me to an old woman sitting at a table with a drink in hand while watching the fight that was about to begin between Shingo and the barmaid.

"Hi."

I noticed her mug was nearly empty and reached into my bag of holding for a bottle of Ectamel mead.

I leaned over to refill her glass, then hesitated.

"You drink mead, right?"

She nodded.

I smiled and filled her mug.

She took a sip and raised an eyebrow at the sweet drink.

"So, what do you want, girl?" she asked.

"Tell me about the mammoths. How are they this docile? Is the gestation period still two years like it is for elephants? If so, how did you manage to turn them into livestock when it's that long? To maintain a Kindred transformation, you have to eat a few pounds of mammoth meat each week, right? Does that mean you slaughter a beast that often?"

I fired off my questions one after another.

To her credit, the woman just laughed and started answering.

Apparently, they had bred the beasts for docility and a shorter gestation period. Since the mammoths were also Kindred, they adapted quickly from generation to generation, evolving to meet the needs of the people. The calves were born smaller and matured faster, yet still reached their full adult size as long as the herders cared for them properly.

The downside was that the current herd would struggle to survive in the wild. They had become too specialized and would need several generations to readapt if left on their own.

And yes, one beast was slaughtered every week, with the meat distributed throughout the village to sustain their transformation magic. It had become a communal tradition where the entire village gathered to roast and eat a mammoth together.

As we talked, we watched Shingo and the now-transformed barmaid exchange blows. Both had stripped down to their underwear before the match began.

The girl clearly had more formal training in unarmed combat, but Shingo possessed far more experience fighting dangerous opponents.

Even so, the match remained close.

In the end, Shingo managed to bait her into goring his shoulder with one of her tusks, creating the opening he needed. A powerful kick to her knee sent the girl sprawling into the dirt.

Shingo was declared the winner and immediately started putting his plate armor back on while Vena healed his injury.

Ja'a eventually returned carrying a bundle of mammoth fur, with Kan following behind, carrying a pair of carved ivory ornaments.

"I bought coats for all of us," she announced, tossing a bundle my way before digging through her bag of holding to retrieve another for herself. "Can you believe it? They even had one in Shingo's size."

I slipped mine on and was immediately enveloped in warmth.

The shaggy fur felt surprisingly soft to the touch. The garment reached almost to my boots and looked vaguely like a qashabiya, the camel-wool cloak I had seen in North Africa before being isekai'ed.

It wasn't particularly fashionable. It was, however, remarkably effective against the cold.

Meanwhile, I had Ja'a negotiate the purchase of a few pounds of fresh mammoth meat from the woman I had been speaking with. As the local veterinarian, she had the authority to sell it. After agreeing on a price, she sent a boy to fetch the order.

Raik rejoined us a few moments later and sat down beside me.

"So, do we stay here for the short night, or do we push on for another couple of hours before camping?" he asked.

"What's the rush? We won't be able to reach the Sunless Reach by Waterday anyway," Ja'a said.

With Raik and Ja'a heading back inside to secure rooms, I decided to spend some time walking among the herd.

The mammoths looked healthy. The calves played energetically, running between the adults and tugging on tails with their trunks while their mothers watched from nearby. The beasts didn't seem particularly wary of people and were clearly accustomed to human interaction. Several even allowed me to pet them, lowering their massive heads so I could scratch them between the eyes.

After a while, workers began guiding the animals toward fenced pastures for the evening. The villagers clearly knew what they were doing.

Back on Earth, the sight might have horrified some conservationists.

Here, however, this was simply animal husbandry.

The mammoths were thriving. Their population appeared stable, if not actively growing. Even the larger bulls didn't seem particularly aggressive toward one another. There was no visible competition for territory, food, or mates.

And the villagers depended on them for everything: food, clothing, transportation, and trade.

As an anthropologist, I couldn't bring myself to object.

If anything, I found it fascinating.

Back on Earth, humanity had spent thousands of years domesticating animals. Here, Kindred adaptation seemed capable of shortening that process tenfold.

By the time darkness began to settle over the village, I returned to the inn.

I found the team already seated around a long table, waiting for dinner to arrive.

As I sat down, the barmaid who had fought Shingo earlier began serving large portions of something that looked similar to shepherd's pie. Vena, however, was still fasting and had to settle for a warm glass of milk.

Once everyone had food and drinks in front of them, the barmaid turned toward Shingo.

"Once you reach your limit," she said, "if you can't earn an evolution, consider retiring here."

Then she hurried away before anyone could ask what she meant.

"What was that about?" I asked.

"Kindred tradition," Kan said. "In many Kindred clans, you're not supposed to have children until you reach your soul's natural limit. Once you get there, you either settle down or keep risking your life trying to evolve."

"The tradition is sound," Calr said. "A child's limit is influenced by the current strength of its parents. If you have children before reaching your own maximum potential, it's almost like wasting part of the strength your parents passed down to you."

"Maybe that's why the average person here is stronger than elsewhere," Ja'a said.

"When the weak are allowed to breed, you get people with limits of six SB," Katar said.

Ja'a immediately pointed at him.

"Your limit is six SB."

"Hey, that's not fair. He's Mythic, not Kindred. He can't grow without faith," Raik said, coming to his friend's defense.

"And yet faithless he chooses to remain," Ja'a replied with a shrug.

Katar merely continued eating as though the conversation had nothing to do with him.

After that, the discussion gradually died down as everyone focused on their meal.

Still, I couldn't stop thinking about what had been said.

Was faith and religion truly the only way for Mythic people to grow?

There had to be another path.

After all, the oldest gods had somehow ascended in the first place. Someone had to become the first god before worshippers even existed.

If they had found a way, perhaps there was still a path to power for someone like Katar to follow.
