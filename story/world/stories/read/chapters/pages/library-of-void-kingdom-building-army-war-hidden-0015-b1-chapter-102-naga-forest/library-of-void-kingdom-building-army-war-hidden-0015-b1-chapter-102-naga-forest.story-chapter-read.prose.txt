A sharp growl tore through the silence.

It echoed low and feral, cutting across the forest air just before the first hint of twilight—those fragile moments before dawn when night still ruled, but the world had already begun to stir. The sound pulled Vector from the depths of the Simulation Space, where he had been locked in virtual combat. A System notification flashed before his eyes, warning of an outside disturbance.

Vector's eyes snapped open.

Around him, most of the group remained asleep, their bodies wrapped in shallow rest after the long march. Even the few soldiers assigned to night watch—figures standing like shadows at the cave's mouth—seemed unaware of whatever had triggered the alert. Only Vector had received the warning through the System, and as he moved silently toward the cave entrance, he understood that only Sebastian and he had sensed what was coming.

Sebastian was already there.

He stood at the edge of the cave with his posture relaxed yet alert, his gaze fixed on the forest beyond. The guards who were awake watched the two of them quietly, sensing only that something had changed in the air without understanding what had drawn their leaders here.

After approaching the entrance, Vector felt it—presences moving through the forest, approaching fast. Their movements were scattered yet fluid, making it difficult to determine their exact number. Still, one thing was certain: there were more than fifteen of them. After observing their vitality, he confirmed something else. Their strength was at the First Stage. He double-checked his assessment, then triple-checked it.

Once he was certain their strength and that he could handle them, he turned his head slightly toward Sebastian, keeping his expression composed rather than reckless.

"Let me deal with them first," he said quietly. "I want to test my strength."

Sebastian's eyes narrowed almost imperceptibly.

"Young Master," he replied in a low voice that was measured but firm, "there are twenty-five monsters approaching."

Vector understood his caution, but his confidence was no longer impulsive. After attaining Basic Mastery in Archery, he no longer felt like a warrior grasping at strength he could not control. He wanted to measure himself—not as he had been before, missing arrows and relying on brute force—but as he was now.

"There's no need to worry," Vector said calmly. "Let me engage first. If there's any real danger, you can intervene."

He continued to sense the approaching figures, though they remained invisible through the dense foliage and the low visibility of pre-dawn darkness.

Then—

From the dense brush ahead, shadows began to emerge.

"Redmaw Hyenas…" came a low voice from behind him.

Vector ignored the quiet shock that rippled among the guards and fixed his gaze upon the pack.

A pack of Redmaw Hyenas stepped into the faint pre-dawn light. Their mangy fur stood in coarse spikes along their backs, and their jaws were stained with a deep, unnatural crimson that gleamed faintly even in the minimal presence of Somira. These were nocturnal beasts that hunted in darkness and twilight, and they were infamous within the Naga Forest—not because of overwhelming individual strength, but because of their coordination. They hunted in tightly organized formations, rarely alone, attacking in calculated waves that overwhelmed prey through timing and discipline rather than brute dominance. The deep crimson hue that stained their jaws—whether a natural marking or the permanent evidence of bloodshed—had earned them their name and reputation alike.

A faint crease formed between Vector's brows—not because of their strength, but because of their presence. They should not be here.

He had studied the Naga Forest extensively before embarking on this journey, and the Redmaw Hyenas were creatures typically active within the Inner Region of the Naga Forest. In fact, they could be termed kings of that region. He was also certain that he and the others hadn't even ventured near the Inner Region yet.

Yet here they were—in the Outer Region.

The forest was divided into four zones: The Periphery, where common beasts roamed freely. The Outer Region, inhabited by common beasts and others whose strength rivaled Blood Energy awakened warriors. The Inner Region, where beasts matched Blood Energy awakeners and First Stage warriors. And finally, the Core Region—the domain of Overlord Beasts, where most creatures possessed strength equivalent to Second or even Third Stage warriors. Rumors persisted of occasional Fourth Stage beasts appearing within its depths, though few dared venture far enough to confirm such claims. The population within the Core was not sparse; rather, it was dense and terrifying—hundreds, perhaps thousands, of beasts whose strength exceeded the Second Stage.

Beasts also improved their strength through body refinement, though not like humans did by awakening Blood Energy. They were innately strong, and combined with medicinal herbs, elixirs, and rare flora found within the forest, they refined and strengthened their bodies. Though the effect was not as effective as it appeared on humans, since they didn't have a way to guide the medicinal essence properly.

Vector gauged their numbers as he drew a blade from his waist. Estimated count… twenty-five or more.

The Redmaw Hyenas didn't attack immediately. They instinctively sensed something—Sebastian's presence. The aura of a Third Stage expert pressed down on the pack like a physical weight, keeping them at bay for now.

Grrr…

Low growls rumbled from their throats as their formation tightened gradually, gauging distance and threat. But they did not attack—not yet. Because they sensed Sebastian. The oppressive aura of a Third Stage warrior pressed outward like an invisible weight, heavy and suffocating, forcing instinctive hesitation into the pack's movements.

But Sebastian's oppression was overcome by their greed and hunger for the prey ahead.

The first Redmaw Hyena lunged without warning.

Its body blurred through the dim pre-dawn light, jaws parted wide enough to crush bone, crimson-stained teeth glinting as it aimed straight for Vector's throat.

Vector did not move backward.

His hand moved to the bow. The string was already drawn before the beast completed its leap.

Release.

The arrow traveled so fast it seemed to erase the distance entirely. It entered through the open mouth and exited through the back of the skull in a spray of bone fragments, embedding deep into a tree behind with a dull, violent thud.

The hyena's body dropped mid-air, dead before it touched the ground.

The rest of the pack reacted instantly. Growls deepened. Claws tore into earth. Four more broke from formation and charged.

Vector's bow swung toward the charging beasts.

Draw.

Release.

Draw.

Release.

Every shot was lethal and precise. Every arrow pierced the head of a hyena, moving so fast that a normal human couldn't even track them with the naked eye. Each motion flowed into the next with seamless precision—one pierced through an eye socket, another shattered a shoulder joint mid-stride, a third struck cleanly through the throat of a beast that had attempted to flank from the left.

He was fully demonstrating the newly learned martial art—the Silent Arrow Art.

Bodies fell in rapid succession.

But the hyenas had encircled the entrance and were now closing in, charging from multiple angles. Vector's releases didn't stop. He dealt with another six of them with his destructive arrows, his breathing slowing as his shoulders relaxed. The world narrowed to lines of movement and predicted trajectories. He fired not at where they were—but where they would be.

One managed to reach striking distance—

Vector sidestepped and drove his knee upward into its jaw.

Crack.

Bone splintered. The beast spun sideways and crashed into the dirt. He loosed another arrow before its body stopped rolling.

"By the ancestors…" Rhea's voice echoed somewhere behind him.

The commotion had awakened the others.

But Vector ignored them, focusing ahead. The hyenas had stopped trying to encircle the entrance and were now closing in directly on him. He put away the bow and reached for his knife.

He drove the blade toward the eye of the first hyena, then with a swift movement kicked another one in the head, shattering its skull.

"Crack!"

Brain matter scattered across the ground. The other two hyenas showed fear in their eyes upon seeing this and tried to flee from the battle. But Vector's speed was already more than six times theirs, so he caught up with them and ended them before they even had the chance to turn and run.

In a very short time, with his explosive strength and speed, he had cleared all the hyenas. None remained in sight anymore.

Guards moved ahead to gather the beasts' bodies. But as one of them reached to collect the first hyena—the one Vector had shot first, whose body lay near the bushes—a Redmaw Hyena burst from the foliage and attacked him.

Even before it had time to leap on the guard, an arrow made its presence known through the beast's skull. The arrow's speed was so fast that it instantly pierced the hyena's head and embedded itself in a tree behind it. The arrow's impact also destroyed a chunk of the tree trunk.

Vector had already been prepared for its attack. Though he couldn't see it, he could sense it. He had known one of them was hiding behind that bush—a last desperate struggle to take down at least one enemy.

…

The group gathered the beast corpses carefully. Nothing would go to waste. They packed them for transportation, knowing that every part would be used, whether for crafting weapons, tools, or other essential supplies.

Bones of the beasts were ideal for crafting arrows, weapon handles, and reinforced grips. Hides could be treated and fashioned into leather armor, boots, or bracers. Claws and teeth served as sharp arrowheads, knife points, or were kept as raw materials for crafting deadly traps. Tendons and ligaments made for excellent bowstrings, binding ropes, and tension-based weapon parts due to their natural strength and flexibility.

They were happy that they had gotten so many 1st stage Beasts, which will be useful in forging and most importantly it could be used in elixirs to awaken Blood energy. They simply watched as Vector returned to the path ahead, his pace measured, his hands folded behind his back, like nothing had happened. But they knew the strength of their young master better now.

⚜

After packing their things and camp gear, they resumed their journey, heading northeast through the Naga Forest.

As the group moved through the dense woodland, beasts occasionally attacked, but none posed a real threat. The guards dispatched them without much effort, maintaining the same movement strategy they had employed the day before—non-combatants remained in the center while the three teams led by Marcus, Tristan, and Rhea surrounded and flanked them in protective formation.

Around mid-afternoon, with the sun shining steadily over the treetops, the team approached a lake surrounded by vibrant greenery. The scene before them looked like something from a fairy tale. The perimeter of the lake was alive with activity—various herbs grew in abundance while beasts occupied the periphery, as if guarding precious treasure from any intruders.

Vector and the three, Marcus, Tristan and Rhea, exchanged glances, their eyes narrowing. After witnessing so many medicinal plants gathered in one location, they immediately became alert.

As Vector's group approached near the water's edge, the party's herbalists and scholars immediately noticed something extraordinary. Wild herbs and medicinal plants were growing along the perimeter of the lake, watched over by the various beasts stationed there.

One of the herbalists exclaimed, "These are medicinal plants! Is that a BloodMoon plant? And over there on the western perimeter—that's BloodRoot! And that's… yes, it's Heartleaf!"

"This place is a treasure trove," a scholar said, his voice filled with awe.

Vector looked at the beasts that surrounded the lake. There were mix of beasts like bears and wolfs. And they surprisingly working together not driving each other’s group.

There were around thirty beasts with strength of 1st Stage warrior and approximately sixty common beasts with Blood Energy awakened—most probably subordinates to the stronger creatures. Yet most of them dared not approach the group, held back by a powerful and terrifying presence they could sense.

Their instincts screamed a warning: If you attack the group ahead, you won't even know how you died. Your presence will be erased from existence. And the source of this terror was Sebastian.

Vector issued orders in a serious tone. "Rhea, you protect the people here."

His gaze moved to Marcus. "Marcus and Tristan, your team is going to deal with the beasts."

He emphasized his next words carefully. "There are also some beasts having First Stage strength. I'll handle them. You take your teams and deal with the common beasts."

Sebastian, as usual, remained still and didn't participate in the battle.

Marcus gave a short nod, and his team moved forward to clear the beasts with weapons drawn.

Vector also drew his bow from his back and began firing arrows at the beasts. Within four to five seconds, he had felled one First Stage beast with a powerful and precise arrow aimed at its head. Occasionally, these arrows pierced directly through the creatures' eyes, dropping them instantly.

Good thing I practiced archery first, Vector thought to himself. He was genuinely glad that he had learned the Archery Arts yesterday. With his keen aim and rapid fire, he eliminated multiple First Stage beasts from a distance, each shot delivered with lethal precision.

Marcus's team also dispatched the beasts efficiently, moving with practiced coordination as they cut down their targets.

After around fifteen minutes, Tristan and Marcus's team had killed all the beasts. Under Rhea's instruction, a small team of herbalists and scholars spent some time collecting and harvesting the medicinal plants.

These herbs were valuable—many of them played a vital role in helping individuals awaken their Blood Energy and supported the process of Body Refinement.

BloodMoon plants were used to make elixirs that could help awaken Blood Energy. BloodRoot vine was even more valuable, used for making healing elixirs. The Heartleaf plant was used to create elixirs that could enhance the vitality of a person who had reached the Third Stage—a very rare herb indeed.

There were few herbs that could enhance vitality, especially at the Third Stage. Enhancing vitality meant that a person gained a longer lifespan. While only one or two of these herbs would help Vector personally at his current level, they were priceless to his soldiers and craftsmen. Even if the boost they offered was small, every bit of strength mattered, and he intended to make full use of these resources.

The collection took nearly an hour. By the end, the area had been so thoroughly harvested it looked barren, as though not a single blade of grass remained.

Around early afternoon, with several bundles of valuable herbs secured, the group resumed their march through the forest toward Vantara Village.
