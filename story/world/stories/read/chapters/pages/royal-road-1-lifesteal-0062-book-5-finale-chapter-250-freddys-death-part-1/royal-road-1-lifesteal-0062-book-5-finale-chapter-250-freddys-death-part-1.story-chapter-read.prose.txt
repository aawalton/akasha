Harold woke without waking, coming to in a world of billowing shadow and shadowy figures, made of bargains to be made.

“Have you decided to accept the terms?” The silhouette roiled with the words, its indistinct form vibrating as it spoke.

Harold snorted. “Sure. I’m about as ready as I’ll ever be. But remember!” He grinned. “Once I’m done, you better deliver on your promise.”

“As I’ve said before, Harold. Once the deal is made, there is no going back. For either of us.”

“Oh please, you know damn well that I have no clue how this voodoo nightmare shit of yours works.”

“And yet, you know enough of the Absolutes which rule the world to know that my words must be true, nevertheless.”

“I know enough to be willing to take the risk.”

“Do you accept?” the figure urged.

He hesitated for the briefest of moments. This creature, whatever it was, was right. Ether followed rules, and some of them were Absolute. But telling Absolutes apart from things that were just very difficult was tricky, and little was known of the nightmare affinity. Still, he knew the world well enough. He knew the nature of trade well enough, too. Just enough to be willing to take the chance.

And, well, frankly, he had been intending to kill Freddy Stern eventually anyway, so this was just two birds with one stone.

“Yes,” he said, and a bargain was struck, its stain flowing over his very soul.

“Go on, then.”

He woke up, this time, in the world of the wake.

The apartment he’d purchased in Starhold was a small, low-key thing. Just for the time being. His funds were better served elsewhere.

As a newly re-appointed Lord, he was currently without fief. And getting one would be a serious pain in the ass. As one of the old Lords, he’d been lucky enough to hold a territory directly under the empress. Now that he’d lost it, he had a year to establish himself somewhere else, under some jackass Overlord who’d be all too eager to screw him over.

He was in no rush to do so.

A wide grin spread across his face.

Well, not before he delivered on his end of the deal, and received knowledge of how the world would move next.

He didn’t feel any different. The world didn’t look any different, either. And yet, as he got up and took a look in the bathroom mirror, an unfamiliar sight greeted him, nevertheless. Leaning in, he examined the square pupils. Looking down, he located the lenses he’d prepared beforehand. Carefully picking one up, he slowly placed it on his eye. “Argh!” he winced as pain shot through the entire side of his face.

The lens he’d placed flew off as if repelled by a magnet, clinking off the mirror and shattering into pieces that scattered into the sink. “Son of a bitch…” he muttered, blinking the sting out. Turning, he picked up the pair of sunglasses he’d prepared, instead. Those went on perfectly fine.

He sighed. “This is going to be tricky.”

Absolutes ruled the world, indeed. The nightmare affinity forced some kind of easily-identifiable mark upon whoever made a deal, for as long as that deal wasn’t completed. There were layers to this, though. Mark had been able to get away with lenses somehow, while his tagalongs couldn’t, just like himself.

What was the difference? Hell, why were sunglasses allowed in the first place? Was it because they weren’t subtle enough? What about people without eyes? Do they get something else?

Eventually, sunglasses would surely become a suspicious thing to be wearing, and people would likely immediately suspect that something was off. But for the time being, he was probably safe. Hopefully.

Sighing, he moved back into his bed, kicking his feet up, and turning the BC on.

D-day was tomorrow. He’d already prepared everything.

The only reason he’d accepted the deal today rather than tomorrow was because he tended to get a bit too excited when big stuff like this happened, and just like a little kid, couldn’t fall asleep. He’d rather not risk missing the chance to take the deal in the first place.

For today, he would simply rest and relax. It went just about as any other day off. He ordered some food, watched some arena matches—that Inquisitor guy intrigued him. There was a good chance he had made a deal with some of the performers to pull all of that off, but even then, remarkable planning and execution.

The power of whoever this was was just simply off the chart. Meaning—

“Another severed powerhouse, eh…?”

There were many who collected rare treasures and then severed back at the first star. Nobody’s first ascension went perfectly. Thus, it wasn’t uncommon for Lords and Ladies to collect countless resources then sever back at the first star, taking a second shot at ascending.

Harold had thought of doing the same thing many times, but… eh. Just as it wasn’t uncommon for four-stars to take a second shot at starting from scratch, it wasn’t particularly rare for them to die before getting back to the fourth star, either. There was no such thing as a safe ascension, and regardless of how good of a start one has, making it to the fourth star was always going to be a brutal slog.

The day passed him by quickly, in a blur of flipping channels and letting his brain rot. Eventually, the clock ticked 9 p.m., and he sighed, getting out of bed. “I guess it’s time.”

He walked into the kitchen.

There was a chess board on the table. It was an automatic thing, programmed by some of the brightest minds in the empire. He pulled the dial to the max of 3000 ELO, and then left it there, turning to the kitchen sink.

Several dirty dishes waited to be washed. He could just put them in the dishwasher, but it was fine.

Mom would take care of it.

With a thought, a thing of stone appeared, shaped like a woman, hunched over the sink. He blinked, and Mom turned to face him, a radiant smile on her face.

She said something.

“Yeah, I’m done with the preparation.” He rolled his eyes as he gave her a quick kiss on the cheek. “Dad’s waiting for me.”

She nodded, and went back to whatever it was she was doing, while he sat at the table.

He waved a hand—

Dad, seated on the chair before him, quirked an eyebrow. He asked a question.

“Yeah, yeah, you cheeky fart.” Harold snorted. “I’ll show you who’s too young in a minute.”

They played a round of chess, as they often did, chatting about the weather, and the day.

His dad asked another question.

“College? Are you senile? Do you have any idea how old I am? Or what year it is?” He snorted, moving a piece forward. “I hope your skills aren’t going the way of your memory.”

As if to contest his words, the cheeky old man forked his bishop.

“Ah.” He chuckled. “I should probably pay more attention.”

His dad gloated.

The match went on, a battle for the ages. It was a gruesome, protracted thing, blood spilling for every square gained. But his earlier blunder had resulted in a devastating series of events, and ultimately, he lost.

Filled with frustration, he reset the board.

Dad teased him, and he jokingly flashed the middle finger at him.

Mom chided him, and he flinched. “Sorry, he’s… He’s just so annoying.”

Dad laughed.

They played another match, this one, in silence. The board clacked with the movement of pieces. The fight raged on. And then, finally, an opening—

He reached for his queen, his index finger and thumb coiling around her neck, the warmth suffusing his fingers and her sweat making his grip slippery. His breathing had gotten heavier at some point. A bead rolled down his forehead. Something itchy was stuck in his throat, and no matter how hard he swallowed, it remained there.

Before he could change his mind, he moved her, right where she needed to be; right in the way of an enemy pawn. A brilliant move.

His father yelled at him.

“Shut up,” he muttered. “I had no choice.”

Dad said something else.

“There’s no game without the king,” he repeated. “What else am I supposed to do?”

He turned, and his mother was nowhere to be seen. As expected. He turned again, and his father was gone, too.

“Fuck you both,” he slammed an open palm on the table, causing the pieces to tumble off the board. “It was mate in six, anyway.” He left the board and the scattered pieces just as they were, while he got ready for bed.

As expected, he couldn’t catch a single wink.

Opening the balcony doors, he stepped outside, right in the plain view of the city. He sat on the chair, letting the light gust of wind blow over him, the dilute hum of distant dance and song carrying on it. The weather effects inside the Starhold Dome were impressively done, and the air was clean.

It was a long way from what pioneer cities used to look like.

The stink of carrion, from the mountains of flesh surrounding the settlement. Fog in the air from any number of sources. Sewage.

He smirked.

Let alone having clubs and luxury resorts, a single clean place to eat would be a miraculous find.

Somewhere deep down, he couldn’t help but feel like he missed such places. Perhaps it was simply for the noise of the messy outside silencing the noise of the messy mind. Or maybe he was just being nostalgic. At any rate, these kids were way too soft. And paranoid.

It had been an absolute pain in the ass to find a place with a real balcony. The paranoia within this city ran too deep, and nobody dared walk out into plain sight, where someone could snipe them with a well-aimed attack.

What happened to that iron-clad camaraderie forged in the fires of progress? To the sense of community, and mutual responsibility? To the respect people gave each other as they gathered to tackle the front lines of the interspace?

Shaking his head, he scoffed, his mood souring further.

He simply sat there, relaxing, letting the tingles run their course, letting the terror of failure wash through its well-worn banks.

At some point in the middle of night, he got up. It was the dead of night in Faralethal, and the brink of dawn in Pittersville, where people were waiting for him.

It was time to go.

In the middle of the vast nowhere stretching between almost every permanent settlement in the empire, there waited over a hundred people. Thousands, really, but those many thousands were cannonfodder—run-of-the mill nobodies, just some bastards with crossbows.

Not that one should underestimate a bastard with a crossbow, or thousands of them.

Hell, Harold had pulled off some hilariously high-profile assassinations by sending a bastard with a crossbow down the right alley.

There was Harold himself, wearing large sunglasses and yawning lazily as the minutes passed by.

And all around him were powerhouses he’d scouted out for the mission of the day.

During his time in Starhold, he’d released subtle feelers, getting a sense for who could be persuaded to try and kill Freddy Stern. And well before he could even make a move, several people reached out to him first, having smelled what he was cooking.

This was David Severe, Indigo’s brother, and a man who had recently ascended to the third star. Harold didn’t know why exactly he was here, except that he had some kind of personal grudge against their target. Regardless, he was still a highly talented fighter, and Harold didn’t mind having extra fodder to throw at the flames.

The trio was absolutely not connected to several large rival factions who were conspiring against the Umbra clan, and they were definitely not here to take out the competition before it could become a problem. Wink wink.

Either you surrender, and we give you a quick death, or you fight back and a lot of people die first. The mouths along the black sword in his arm suddenly released deathly screeches as a flood of blood essence coursed out the wound and into the weapon. And then, he retaliated.

Georgie strained as she tugged, but she managed to successfully reel the man in. The wound on his chest, where his heart had just been skewered, was gone, the healing from blowing an entire man up granting him just enough lifesteal to all but undo the injury.

Harold blinked. Huh? He blinked again, the smile on his face growing ever-wider. You’ve got to be kidding me! Seriously!?

Other than that, there were some other knick-knacks inside, including some kind of cursed necklace, and a three-star satellite, but… There was nothing truly… special. He had expected rarer stuff than this.

On a second look, this looked like a ring with all the things he’d need for a final push to the third star when he was done with the tower. Odds were, he had probably feared losing the ring in the tower, so he had only taken the bare essentials.

And a lot of money, probably to stock up on supplies once he reached Nova York.

The fifteen mysterious keyholes around it gave Freddy a rather obvious hint for how it was supposed to open.

He approached one, slotted the key into it, and turned it. The keyhole lit up with a strange golden light, and the key was fully jammed in there. He nodded. “Fourteen more keys to go.”

Turning down another path, he started running, activating Hydraulic Flex. The slick stone surface made it hard not to trip, but redirection saved the day, helping keep him going in the direction he was aiming for. He turned left, right, right, left, right—

And stumbled into a three-meter tall monster that had been shambling through the hall. A bladed arm slashed out at him belatedly, but he was already dashing back, startled. He got a better look at the thing.

An absolutely horrid undead creature blocked the path forward, with long, bladed arms, hollow eyes, and a dessicated form.

He scoffed. “You scared the Bajesus out of me, big guy.” He lunged forward and swung Sanguine Avenger down. The sword embedded into the creature’s body, but only a little. And before he could react, the beast shrieked and swung a clawed arm at him.

Clearly, it had been as surprised as he had when Freddy had stumbled into it a moment ago, and that first slash had been a clumsy, reflex swing.

This one wasn’t.

It was an attack of such absurd speed and power that it left him thoroughly rattled. The bladed appendage tore through his flimsy armor and left a large rent down the front of his chest. He gritted his teeth as absurdly potent miasma seeped into the wound, making it fester.

Sanguine Avenger screamed bloody murder, demanding a price in blood for the strike, and Freddy obliged, swinging it down. The weapon sank into the creature’s side, but it did shockingly little damage, even as omnibleed got started with sapping it of false blood. He paled. “What the hell is this!?” he shouted, mind uncomprehending.

It shrieked, landing another attack, this time, across his left arm, which he’d just barely raised in defense. His forearm shield was torn apart like tissue paper, and the attack ripped across his skin, leaving only a shallow cut, but imbuing enough miasma to make his entire arm go numb.

“What the…!?” He retaliated again, leaving another shallow wound. Shouting, he ducked the next attack, and then threw a double-starred Tsunami Strike at the monster. The front of its body exploded as it shot back, slamming back-first into the wall, sending cracks rippling through it.

He received absolutely no lifesteal from the attack, given his target’s thoroughly undead body had no life left to give. But it did still have some fight in it, since it started getting back up.

He shivered at the sight of the creature struggling to rise. “What the actual hell is this thing…?”

He’d grown notably tougher since the alchemy department juiced him to the moon, but double-starring Tsunami Strike was still punishing. His fingers had been severely damaged, skin torn, bones cracked. He needed to end this fight before it got back up. If it started chasing him down, he was in serious trouble.

Lifting his arm, he double-starred his Blood Javelin, and then, he quickly charged up an attack with Hydraulic Throw. The javelin flew forward, its twin following right after it, both slamming into the creature’s head. But neither penetrated. They bounced off the thick skull, leaving only a shallow wound, even if the sheer whiplash sent the monster tumbling back on its ass.

Rather than let it return to its feet, Freddy rushed it while it was down, throwing a series of slashes at it, gradually chopping it up. He cautiously evaded the swings, trying to stay out of reach as he slashed at the creature’s arms. His attacks were only leaving minor nicks in the creature’s flesh, but the omnibleed gradually sapped its body of its durability.

His attacks started making more of a difference, gradually whittling the creature’s arms down until the bladed appendages were finally dulled enough to be of little threat, all while working to ensure that the creature couldn’t get back up.

He had spilled a good deal of his blood across the ground, then solidified it, effectively gluing the monster to the floor, which worked well enough to keep it in place. It could probably easily break the binding if it wasn’t distracted by the relentless attacks.

Once its arms had been neutralized, he got started on the neck.

Freddy could deliver some truly ridiculous damage with the Sanguine Avenger, especially when the Cycle Scythe was attached to it. The sheer weight and speed of the attacks alone was enough to blast through the defenses of most targets.

And yet, it took like twenty swings to behead this damned thing, and it would have taken far, far more if it weren’t for the omnibleed.

Once the creature’s head was finally detached, he brought the sword down on it until he finally received the ether confirming that the creature was killed. The sheer volume of ether he received for the kill was ungodly.

Hell, he’d compare it to how it felt to kill Nahar!

No, it was more than that!

In sheer durability, that was the single most powerful opponent he had ever faced in his life, and it was miles ahead of anything else he’d fought in the tower so far! And this was a blue door! A puzzle! What the hell was a creature like this doing in a—

He winced, his expression turning ugly. “Ooooh… I’m a fucking idiot.” He looked down at the creature’s lopped-off, caved-in head, noting the hollow eyes. It had been a slow, shambling thing, and obviously, it was blind.

This was an obstacle monster, not an actual enemy. This was a puzzle trial after all. He had never been meant to fight this thing in the first place. He was supposed to avoid it as he navigated the labyrinth to collect the keys.

Given its blindness, it could probably only track him down through hearing, meaning he was supposed to move quietly and cautiously. Obstacle monsters weren’t supposed to be fought. A good number of people actually believed that killing them wasn’t even possible.

He burst into a sudden fit of laughter as he realized what he’d just done.

It would have been a pretty damn brag-worthy accomplishment if it weren’t for the whole part where he was a total dumbass for fighting it to begin with.

He wiped a laugh-induced tear out of his eye as he pulled out the spark of undeath to heal from the rather devastating damage he’d received. “Well… At least this’ll make the puzzle a bit easier, I guess.”

And it did. Although it was a bit time-consuming, as was to be expected from a puzzle trial like this, it wasn’t hard in the slightest. Gosh, he sure wondered why it was so easy. It was almost like something critical was missing.

It took him only about ten minutes to wrap up the trial, and then, the passage went from going nowhere—quite literally—to the familiar pitch black of an active passage. He stepped into the next room.

43 doors. He was still in the lead. Another mish-mash, and this time, there was a purple door. Smiling, he leaped forth, tackling yet another trial.

He had been ready for anything, but in hindsight, there was nothing to be surprised at. No matter how many trials he went through, the doors always ticked down by just one. There was no chance of anyone catching up to him, not unless something truly unfortunate happened.

People had limits.

Be it stamina, essence, or just the need to catch a breath and relax for a moment—it was normal for people to need to pace themselves.

And Freddy wasn’t a normal person.

He bulldozed his way up to the twentieth floor, landing mostly purples and a handful of reds.

Purples were the fastest since they were up against just one opponent, but every kill only granted 1% essence in turn. He could get away with being more frugal and letting his satellite do the heavy lifting, but even that ultimately overflowed his reflux essence, effectively causing the same problem. Thankfully, some of the reds sent a literal horde of weaker opponents at him, letting him stay topped up without having to sacrifice too much time.

The twenty-first floor was another variant—half blue, half green. He went for green. He was made to ascend a mountain. It paled in comparison to the Northern Spine, which he had braved when he’d been far weaker. With Hydraulic Flex active, he made a joke of the trial, practically sprinting all the way up to the peak in only a handful of minutes.

It was smooth sailing again up to the 29th, where he found another golden door. It was a red potion this time—upon drinking it, he felt a bit stronger. Again, not a huge effect, but given how far he’d already pushed his physique, it had a greater impact than it would have otherwise.

The 33rd floor presented another bizarre variant—half black, half gold. Basically, it would utterly shaft the slower half of the remaining participants. Freddy wasn’t going to complain about another reward. It was a blue orb again. This time, it gave him 3% progress to Hydraulic Flex, bringing it up to Stage 3—5%.

It was smooth sailing up to floor 41, where he hit all blues.

The trial put him on the top of a mountain, inside some kind of temple. It was cold as hell, and there were gargoyles flying around. Gargoyles were basically just golems that could fly. As if regular golems weren’t enough of a pain in the ass.

There were these four pedestals. Upon them, there were runic orbs. Basically, just large, spherical boulders with runes inscribed into the surface. But uh oh! One of them had rolled down the side of the mountain.

He knew runes well enough to decipher what the challenge was. The runework was inscribed with all kinds of unity, synchrony, attraction, mutuality and so on and so forth. Clearly, he needed to find a way to somehow alter the runes to make the three boulders pull the fourth one back up. It wasn’t even that hard to figure the puzzle out.

But nobody had time for that shit.

So, he simply leaped down the side of the mountain, picked up the boulder, and started hauling it up. Even for him, it was insanely heavy, and as expected, he was instantly beset by the gargoyles.

This time, he didn’t bother trying to fight back.

The gargoyles were unreasonably fast. Even if he didn’t realize they were just meant to be an obstacle to prevent cheesing the trial, he wouldn’t have been able to kill them. Their airborne agility aside, their attacks were precise and lethal. The combination of speed and precision put even him on a timer, and he focused on hauling the boulder up, trusting his body to last long enough to make it back up.

Crimson Dragon Scales were already up across his skin, just under the armor, giving him enough added durability to be able to resist getting torn to shreds. It only took him a few minutes to climb back up, but in that short time, he got utterly ravaged by attacks, leaving him a bleeding, lacerated mess.

There was a steep wall leading up to the platform upon which the rest of the stones were waiting. There was no way to climb back up there while holding the boulder.

Thus, he would have to throw it.

Once he reached just below the temple, he braced himself, double-starring Hydraulic Throw. He charged it up for a couple of long seconds, and then threw the boulder. The throw tore his upper-body muscles apart, but it had worked, and thus, he finally left the range of the murderous creatures of stone and re-entered the temple.

There, he first healed from his wounds, and then, he put the boulder back on its pedestal.

The mirror in the middle of the temple turned black, and he ventured forth, deeper up the tower.

Floor 43 granted him another golden opportunity. This time, it was a red orb that pushed Sanguine Avenger by 4%. It was a bit weird that the orbs targeted specific abilities, but at the very least, he was happy to see it was targeting those he relied on the most.

The number of the doors was starting to rapidly dwindle. At 44, there were only 7 doors left.

45 threw out 5 blues and a single lone green door. He didn’t have to think much as he rushed into the green.

Unfortunately, this choice turned out to be a mistake.

He was forced to waddle through a noxious marsh. He had never bothered to develop Waterwalk, and now, it was biting him in the ass. Quite literally, something kept biting him in the ass, and he couldn’t quite catch it no matter how quickly he reacted. It was driving him crazy.

His feet were constantly stuck in mud. There were coiling vines and other growths he kept getting tangled up in. There was absolutely no tree life, so he couldn’t use those to jump from one to the other. Swimming with the help of raw water essence manipulation was marginally faster than wading, given all the plants he had to navigate through, and given he had no ability for it, it was gobbling up too much essence to be worth it.

He tried jumping to maybe make it several tens of meters in big leaps, but his feet simply slipped on the mud, and the water sapped his jumps of all their energy.

Thinking himself a genius, he unleashed Perished Water into the surrounding swamp, filling it to the brim with killer fish phantoms. The idea was to slaughter whatever lurked in the water and hopefully gather enough blood for a Blood Sacrifice.

Bloodshed could probably throw him a good deal across the swamp, and if he got lucky, it might even be able to carry him across.

There was just one minor problem.

Clearly, the ghost fish were killing something as he was definitely receiving a lot of lifesteal. But Blood Sacrifice was silent—no blood was being spilled.

Upon closer inspection, he discovered the problem—they were tearing up the plant life, rather than hunting down whatever lived in the waters. “What the hell!?” He had never encountered this problem before. Well, this was his first time ever using perished water in a swamp, he supposed, but still, he didn’t remember the affinity behaving like this out in the ocean.

Soon enough, he discovered the problem.

The waterlife phantoms of the perished water affinity were effectively blind. They were simply following the closest source of life force, and in this swamp, the plantlife was the dominant source of it, vastly overwhelming the fish, and making it basically impossible for his affinity to “see” anything that wasn’t a plant.

To make things worse, the water was dense with all kinds of microorganisms, further intensifying the “haze” of life force in the water.

He tried submerging himself and using Ghosts of the Drowned to control the phantoms, but it was hopeless. He still had to see the target to command the phantoms to go after it, and in the muddy, algae-ridden bog water, he was as blind as the phantoms themselves. Vague orders to “ignore plant life” weren’t cutting it, either.

Leaving the water, he released a frustrated sigh.

He had no easy way around this one, thus, he simply suffered the indignity of wading through waist-deep bog water full of butt-munchers until he reached the finish line.

It took him agonizing days of work to make it to the end. He had tried and tried, but there was no way to make it go any faster. By the end, he was beginning to panic, feeling like someone must have caught up to him by this point.

But when he finally made it to the exit, he found that he was still in the lead.

On floor 46, there were 2 reds, 1 green, 1 purple, and 1 black. He planted his stinky, swampy ass right before the purple door, and sat down, turning to face the gaping portal on the other side of the room, waiting for it to spew out a competitor. No such thing happened. He merely sat there, eating his food pills, drinking some water, and just taking a breather.

Three straight days—according to the watch in his storage ring—of speed-wading through water was one hell of an exercise, even by his standards.

But it had really only been like four full days since he’d entered the tower to begin with.

Now that he was finally taking a moment to think about it, it wasn’t at all strange for nobody to have caught up to him.

That was an absurdly fast time to get to where he was, but even by his standards, that had been way too much action. But alas, he was still Freddy. After about four hours of idling, he was back on his feet, diving through the passage.

The bosses were starting to get pretty rough at this point. He faced a dark-affinity snake that had some rather nasty bindings. It wasn’t a hard fight per se, but he really didn’t want to be struggling at this point. He wrapped the fight up neatly enough, and proceeded forward.

And then, on the next floor, disaster struck.

Four doors. All black.
