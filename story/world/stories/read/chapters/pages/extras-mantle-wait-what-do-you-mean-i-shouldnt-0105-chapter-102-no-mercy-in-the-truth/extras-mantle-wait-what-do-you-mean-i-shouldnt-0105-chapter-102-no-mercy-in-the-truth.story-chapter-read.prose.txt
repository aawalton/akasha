Just ten-twenty minutes since the “Trish” incident, and Jin had already lost count of how many cultists he'd put down. Twelve? Fifteen? The bodies were starting to blur together, leaving Jin to wonder just how many damn cultists had infiltrated the Bastion?

The wounds Trish had dealt him were healed now, mostly, courtesy of the various blends [FAI]—the artifact [Field Alchemy Injector]—had injected in him. But there was a stiffness in his chest that went deeper than being wounded, a tightness in his throat that no amount of essence cycling could ease.

Exhaustion, his body informed him helpfully. The kind that came from running on fumes and spite for hours.

He rolled his neck, feeling vertebrae pop one by one, and tried very hard not to think about how much he missed having Rudy by his side.

The unshakable wall. The immovable object that had stood between Jin and a thousand deaths since the day he'd woken up with Ryujin's memories screaming through his skull like broken glass.

Ever since that moment—since he'd opened his eyes in a body that wasn't quite his anymore, drowning in knowledge of a world he shouldn't possess—he'd been fighting. Clawing his way through trials and ambushes and desperate last stands against enemies that should have killed him a dozen times over.

And Rudy had been there. Always.

Then Reyana had joined them. Then Joe. And somewhere along the way, between dungeon runs and coordinated strikes and plans that actually worked, Jin had gotten... comfortable. Had fallen into patterns that only functioned when every piece of the machine was in place.

Bad habits. The kind that got people killed when circumstances changed.

Standing here now—alone except for two exceptional fighters who weren't his team—Jin was becoming wildly, viscerally aware of just how fragile that machine had been.

How fragile he was.

Jin knew he could cause devastating damage, no doubt. He could punch well above his weight class with the right setup, the right positioning, and the right support; he could kill things that should have been untouchable for someone barely clinging to Order II.

But if something got past his defenses? If he couldn't maintain range? If he ran low on essence while the enemy kept coming?

He was done.

The past twenty-three minutes had made that abundantly, brutally clear.

"Bossman?" Brocki's voice cut through his spiral, gruff and laced with concern that the big man was trying very hard to hide. "You good?"

Jin glanced over at the twins.

Brocki's heavy kite shield was a mess of dents and scoring; the design of a crest was barely visible under layers of blood spatter and what looked like acid burns. His armor wasn't much better.

Flocki was worse. A shallow cut ran from his temple to his jaw, still weeping blood despite the hasty bandage he'd wrapped around it and the healing potion Jin had given him. His shield arm shook slightly—just enough to notice if you were looking—and there was a hitch in his breathing that spoke of at least one cracked rib.

But they were standing. Weapons ready. Eyes alert despite the bone-deep weariness Jin could see in every line of their bodies.

Brocki and Flocki were good. Exceptionally good. Veterans who knew how to hold a line, how to cover angles, how to read a fight and adapt on the fly without needing orders for every little thing. Jin was genuinely impressed—and deeply, profoundly grateful—to have them at his back.

But they weren't Rudy.

They couldn't absorb punishment the way Rudy could, couldn't lock down multiple enemies at once while Jin orchestrated the killing blows. And without that safety net, Jin had been forced to fight differently.

Closer than he liked. Messier than was safe. Trading blows instead of avoiding them entirely, burning through essence reserves to compensate for gaps in their defense that shouldn't have existed.

It was working. Barely. And the cost...

"I'm fine," Jin said, then immediately regretted it when his vision swam slightly, and he had to plant his feet to keep from swaying.

Definitely not fine.

«You are objectively the furthest thing from 'fine.'»

The Narrator cut in, his voice sharp with an exasperation Jin had been hearing on loop for the last eight minutes.

«Essence reserves at 31%. Cognitive processing strain at critical thresholds. The backlash from your harvesting attempt is still accumulating damage. I cannot stress this enough—attempting to drain an entity of unknown Order while already operating at a deficit was catastrophically stupid.»

Yeah, I got that the first twelve times you mentioned it.

«Clearly not, or you wouldn't have made the attempt. And against my explicit advice, I might add.»

Jin let out a weary breath through his nose, careful not to talk with the narrator out loud. Already the situation was worse, and them thinking Jin was talking to himself… yeah, no way he would be able to convince them.

The gains from that desperate gamble with Trish had been incredible—he could feel the raw power churning in his core, could sense how close he was to breaking through to the next rank. But the cost...

His head hurt. Every time he tried to pull on Overdrive, every time he leaned on the Narrator's analytical processing for more than basic commentary, it hurt. Like someone was driving nails through his temples from the inside out.

According to the narrator, he'd pushed his mental strength well past safe limits. His entire cognitive and essence system was in what amounted to a cascading failure state, held together by willpower and spite in roughly equal measure.

He needed sleep. Desperately.

«Sleep deprivation, essence exhaustion, cranial strain from Overdrive abuse, structural damage to primary essence channels... You need rest, Jin. Actual rest. Not 'close my eyes for five minutes' rest.»

"I know," Jin muttered under his breath, low enough that it wouldn't carry. "Not exactly swimming in options here."

"Bossman?" Flocki glanced over at him, one eyebrow raised. "You say something?"

"Nope." Jin straightened despite every muscle screaming protest, schooling his expression into something approximating alertness. "Just catching my breath. How are you two holding up?"

"Solid enough," Brocki rumbled, checking the straps on his battered shield. He caught Jin's eye and offered a tired grin. "Took a few dents, nothing serious. Though I gotta say, boss, these floors are way too empty. Almost feels like everyone packed up and moved somewhere else."

"Noticed that too," Flocki added, his voice tight with pain he wasn't quite hiding. "Haven't seen a single guard or civis since we cleared the third group… 'cept the corrupted ones trying to gut us." He paused, then looked at Jin with something that might have been concern. "You need us to take point for a bit? You look like you're about three steps from falling over."

Jin opened his mouth to decline—because he was fine, he was handling it—but before the words could form, a new sound cut through the blood-scented air of the corridor.

Footsteps.

Multiple sets. Heavy boots on stone, moving fast, coming from the corridor ahead and slightly to the right.

All three of them froze, hands going to weapons.

Jin pulled on his Essence Sight—one of the few abilities that didn't make his skull feel like it was splitting down the middle—and immediately felt his stomach drop.

Eight signatures.

Oh, you've got to be kidding me.

"Contact," Jin said quietly, the word coming out flat and cold despite the spike of adrenaline flooding his system. He shifted his grip on Iron Howl, brought Essence Edge up into a ready guard.

"Positions," he continued, voice steady even as exhaustion pulled at the edges of his awareness. "Guys, take point."

"Aye!" Brocki and Flocki grunted in unison. Shields came up, stances widened, bodies angling to create an overlapping defensive wall that would funnel enemies into Jin's firing lanes.

Good. They were good.

«Confirming hostile count. Eight signatures detected.»

«Do you want me to perform deep scans for individual status readouts?»

No... I can't handle the strain. We do this manually.

«...Acknowledged.»

Jin took a breath—shallow, careful, not enough to jostle the ache in his ribs—and raised Iron Howl. His hands wanted to shake. He didn't let them. Just focused on the corridor ahead, on the sound of approaching boots getting louder with each passing heartbeat, and waited.

The world lost some of its color the moment eight figures rounded the corner at a dead sprint.

All of them were wearing Bastion uniforms with the same twisted black eyes. Jin’s senses could feel the darkened one's presence from the essence that writhed around them in chaotic spirals.

Just like the others we’ve faced so far… no one’s talking, just acting like beasts.

« Means either they're disposable, or someone doesn't care if they survive. »

Heh… What else could we expect from bloody cultists.

«True. Ten seconds to contact. »

"Bossman!" Flocki asked. "Orders?"

Jin's expression went flat as he fixed his gaze on the approaching hostiles, watching the way they moved and felt everything else fade away.

The fear. The uncertainty. The bone-deep exhaustion from hours of fighting through this nightmare.

All of it bled away like water through a sieve, leaving only the path forward.

"We show each and every cultist hell," Jin said softly.

"Yes, Boss!" both brothers chorused, grinning fiercely and bloodied as they braced their shields and dropped into defensive stances.

«Eight seconds to contact. »

Jin tickled small amounts of essence through his system as he fixed his gaze on the approaching enemies, taking each and every one of them using essence sight and pulling on his [Inevitable Doom] marked skill.

Jin carefully channeled essence into his body, into the marked sigil of a skill he had only started using ten minutes earlier.

Can’t believe I was sleeping on this. Jin said to the narrator.

« You were, and I did tell you about the higher skill ceiling of this one. »

Faint shimmers of gold appeared around Jin as the skill [Thermokinetic Engine] hummed to life.

Unlike other skills, this one was an active concentration-based skill that he had learnt the hard way, almost shredding his leg muscles.

Fortunately, that had an easy fix.

Asking one of his three Echoes—of the [Omni Reader viewpoint] skill—to permanently manage the skill's output had done the trick.

« REGULATOR, set to 10% conversion output. »

« Five seconds to contact »

Jin fixed his gaze on the enemy—five ORDER IIs and three ORDER IIIs—Jin focused, knowing this would be a tough fight.

« Three seconds to contact. »

Jin triggered [Inevitable Doom]. The marked skill ignited with a sensation like ice water flooding his veins, and he felt his essence reserves bottom out to dangerous territory as eight thin threads of pale light—visible only in his mind's eye—lashed out, latching to each of the enemies.

Now need the hit. Jin prepared himself just as the front wave hit Brocki and Flocki's shields like a battering ram.

CRASH!

"Gah! Heavy bastards!" Flocki grunted, voice strained but not panicked, even as three cultists tried to force past him.

"Hold! HOLD!" Brocki barked back as he absorbed the charge of the other two attackers. His boots scraped backward an inch—just one—before he dug in and pushed.

Jin aimed [Iron Howl] at the space directly ahead of the twins' feet.

«O sound, be crushed to silence—then break the world with your return!»

His marked skill [Skillshot Synthesis] activated, imbuing the sorcery directly into his bullet.

"Back!" Jin snapped.

Both twins disengaged immediately, shields coming up to cover their retreat as they put three steps of distance between themselves and the cultists.

BANG!

The bullet left Iron Howl's barrel glowing pale blue in the area’s dim lighting. It hit the stone floor directly between where the twins had been standing and where the cultists were still pressing forward.

WHOOM!

Shockwaves exploded outward, catching all eight enemies mid-charge. The force sent bodies flying backward in sprays of blood and startled screams. Some tumbled head over heels. Others slammed into walls with sickening crunches that echoed off stone.

And with damage registered across all eight targets, Jin's Mark of Doom triggered.

The pale threads connecting him to each enemy suddenly burned as [The Mark of True Doom] seared onto each of the cultists, and along with it, curses and debuffs snaked their way into the targets.

Five of them dropped to the ground screaming, their bodies jerking like broken puppets as they clawed at their own faces with fingernails that drew blood.

Dark fluid poured from their mouths, their noses, their eyes as necrosis and whatnot affliction tore at them.

« They won’t be surviving the afflictions, focus on the ORDER IIIs.»

Jin's gaze snapped to them immediately as he barked orders. “Form up!”

Two of the Order IIIs had staggered backward from the blast, yes, but they'd recovered, and so had their aura, which was shimmering around them.

And final cultist…

He stood at the far back, with black eyes, and everywhere on his face were visible dark, blood-red tattoos. In his hands were dual dark-gold sickles.

How in fuck did no one saw that and said bloody cultist! Jin cursed in his mind, then cursed again as he realized there was no [Mark of Doom] on him.

That one's the problem. Probably also an elite.

"Brocki, Flocki—left and right!" Jin commanded, "Take the two shakier ones. Keep them busy. I'll handle the dual sickles."

"On it!" Flocki surged forward with a battle cry, shield raised as he made straight for the cultist on the left, a woman whose aura kept flickering like a dying light.

"Right behind you, brother!" Brocki moved a heartbeat later, his massive frame interposing itself between Jin and the cultist on the right, a younger man.

Which left Jin and Sickles facing each other across ten feet of blood-stained stone.

For a single breath, neither of them moved.

Jin's fingers tightened around Iron Howl's grip, feeling the accumulated kinetic charge from [Thermokinetic Engine] building in his legs. The faint golden shimmer around his calves intensified slightly.

Sickles’ head tilted fractionally, those too-dark eyes tracking the movement with predatory focus.

Yet none of them have spoken till now. It’s weird! Why is it weird?

Then Jin blurred.

Essence flooded through [Thermokinetic Engine] as the Regulator opened the floodgates, converting stored essence to kinetic force in a burst that launched him forward like a stone from a sling.

The world streaked past in smears of color and sound, and he closed the distance in less than a second, [Essence Edge] coming up in a rising slash aimed at Sickles' exposed throat.

The dark-gold sickle came up to meet him with inhuman speed, edge catching edge with a sharp CLANG that sent sparks flying. The impact jarred up Jin's arm, and he felt the strength behind that block—far more than an Order III should possess.

Blessed, maybe. Definitely not standard.

Jin twisted mid-motion, using his momentum to pivot and brought [Iron Howl] around for a point-blank shot at center mass.

Bang!

Sickles’ second sickle swept up in a blur, batting the gun barrel aside. The shot went wide, punching a smoking hole in the wall instead of a skull.

Too fast. Way too fast.

Jin disengaged immediately, shadow-stepping backward.

Narrator?

«Analyzing. Opponent reaction time: 0.14 seconds. Weapon proficiency: expert minimum, possibly high elite. Aura manipulation: peak Order III. Still calculating exact blessing effects, but revised threat assessment: extreme.»

This was the compromise they'd found after the Trish incident—after trying to use Appraisal directly on an enemy had not only nearly gotten Jin's mind torn apart by the backlash but she'd also noticed—so instead of attempting to read status Jin had assigned his second Echo—Observer, he'd started calling it—to monitor and analyze anything he could perceive with normal senses. It kept mental strain minimal and essence expenditure negligible.

And right now, Observer was telling him he was in deep shit.

Behind him, Jin heard the sounds of Brocki and Flocki engaging their targets… grunts of effort, curses, the clang of steel on steel as the twins worked to keep the two ORDER III occupied. But his attention stayed locked on Sickle-Blade, tracking every micro-movement.

Can't afford a drawn-out fight. The twins can hold for maybe a minute, maybe ninety seconds if they're lucky, before those Order IIIs overwhelm them through attrition. Need to end this. Fast.

«O force, narrow your fury—become the point that nothing may deny.»

Sickles didn't wait for him to finish the cast. The cultist surged forward with that same unnatural speed, sickles spinning in a pattern that left zero openings—high-low-middle, alternating angles designed to prevent counterattack.

Jin fired anyway.

CRACK!

The bullet screamed through the air, essence trailing behind it like a comet's tail as it rocketed toward Sickle-Blade's center mass—

The cultist twisted, and the round missed by centimeters. Jin watched it streak past.

He smiled as he yanked on the essence tether connecting him to the spellshot.

The bullet detonated the sorcery contained within.

BOOM!

Surprise flickered on Sickles' face as he was caught mid-dodge, taking the full force of the blast at near-point-blank range.

The concussive wave picked him up and hurled him backward into the corridor wall with enough force to leave a visible dent. He slid down in a spray of blood and dust, his right side a mangled mess of torn cloth and scorched flesh.

Got—

"GAAAAH!"

Brocki's scream cut through Jin's moment of satisfaction.

The cultist Brocki had been fighting had gotten past the twins' guard. His blade was buried in Brocki's shoulder, driven deep enough. Brocki was still on his feet—barely—but his shield arm hung limp, useless.

No. No no no—

"Target left first!" Jin barked. He pulled three talismans from his ring—binding seals, expensive as hell but absolutely worth it—and hurled them at the downed Sickles. "Flocki, cover your brother!"

The talismans struck true, flaring with golden light as they activated. Ethereal chains erupted from nowhere, wrapping around Sickles’ arms and legs with enough force to pin him flat against the stone.

Not permanent. Maybe a couple of seconds if he was lucky. But it would have to do.

"Already on it!" Flocki roared, abandoning his own fight to bull-rush the cultist, tearing into Brocki. His shield came around in a devastating horizontal slam that caught the younger cultist in the chest and sent him flying.

But that left the woman he was fighting… free and unengaged.

Damn, Ironhowl overheated from the last shot…

Jin gritted his teeth and threw [Essence Edge] with every ounce of enhanced strength [Thermokinetic Engine] could provide, the golden shimmer around his arm flaring bright as Regulator dumped maximum conversion into the motion.

The dagger whistled through the air like a falling star, trailing vibrant blue essence in its wake, ripping through the unstable, flickering aura—

And punched straight through the back of the woman's skull.

She dropped like a puppet with cut strings, dead before her body hit the ground.

One down.

Jin turned towards where Flocki and Brocki were both pressing the younger cultist now, their combined weight and skill finally overwhelming the advantage of higher rank through sheer coordinated brutality. The cultist went down hard, Flocki's sword finding his throat in a spray of arterial blood.

Two down.

Which left—

Jin's gaze snapped back to where he'd bound Sickles, and to his surprise, the cultist was smiling.

Not screaming. Not struggling against the binding chains. Just... smiling, that expression wrong and too wide on a face that should have been twisted with pain.

Dark-gold essence was coiling around his right side—the side Jin's blast had torn open—healing flesh back together.

And felt those black eyes locked on him.

"Something's wrong," Jin muttered, voice barely audible even to himself. His gaze swept the corridor, taking in the scene with fresh eyes and feeling that familiar prickle of wrongness crawling up his spine.

Eight cultists. Seven down—five still screaming on the ground as corruption ate them alive, two dead by Jin and the twins' hands. And this one, Sickles, bound and healing and smiling as none of it mattered.

The hyper-aggression. The complete lack of verbal communication from any of them. The numbers—way too many infiltrators.

And not once had Sickles had looked at his supposed allies when Jin had killed them. Hadn't even flinched when the woman had gone down with a dagger through her brain.

Like he'd been... waiting for something.

Narrator, check if my mind is messed with in any form. Check my memories, check my thoughts…

«…»

Narrator?

«...Anomaly detected.»

The narrator's voice was different. Careful. Almost... worried.

«There's a subtle influence affecting emotional responses and decision-making processes. Similar in structure to what you used on Nourma, but far more sophisticated. It's been... amplifying aggression and fueling certain emotions.»

Jin went very, very still. How long?

«Unknown. At least since the Trish encounter, possibly longer. The effect is mild enough that none of your mental defenses flagged it as an active attack; it's more like... background emission. Constant manipulation.»

Fuck.

The feeling of wrongness crystallized into certainty, sharp and cold in his gut.

"Get me full status readouts," Jin interrupted, his voice flat and hard as stone. "All the eight cultists. Pull from their essence signatures, their surface memories, whatever you can access.”

“Disregard damage… Now!"

« Processing. Accessing ‘mantle reflection’ status for the… »

A pause.

A very, very long pause.

«...Jin.»

The Narrator's voice had changed again. Lost all that clinical detachment, all the dry exasperation it usually carried. There was something in its tone now that… felt like dread.

What is it?

«Seven of the eight we just killed... they don't bear blessings from any god and their minds show severe signs of forced corruption, external manipulation consistent with high-level mental magic. Diving deeper into accessible memories and recent behavioral patterns...»

"In other words…" Jin started.

«Yes… Jin, they're civilians. Bastion guards and support staff. The corruption is artificial. Imposed. Best estimates suggest eight to twelve days since initial infection. They... they weren't in control.»

The world tilted sideways.

Jin's grip on Iron Howl went white-knuckled as the words sank in, each one landing like a physical blow. Memories flashed through his mind—the past twenty minutes of combat, bodies dropping, blood spreading across stone floors—the casual efficiency with which he'd marked and executed threats.

Threats that hadn't been threats at all.

«Additionally... remember the second essence signature I detected from lower levels when Trish revealed herself? The one I couldn't identify?»

Yes.

«We have a mind mage, Jin. Possibly Order IV, based on the sophistication and range of the mental manipulation. And they're still here. Still active.»

"And the one in front of me?" Jin's voice came out flat. Dead. Carefully controlled despite the sick realization spreading through his chest like poison. "Sickles, what about him?"

« That one... that one is a true cultist. Peak Order III with the genuine blessing of the darkened one. He's real.»

The cultist—Sickles, the only one who'd been actually trying to kill them—threw his head back and laughed. The sound echoed off stone walls, high and manic and wrong, and Jin watched blood spray from the man's lips as his body shook with mirth.

"Oh, this—" another wet laugh, more blood "—this is just perfect!" The cultist's grin split his face ear to ear, teeth stained crimson. "Did you—hah—did you figure it out yet, boy with a gun? Did you finally notice?"

Jin said nothing. Couldn't. His throat had locked up tight, and something cold and terrible was spreading through his chest like frost across glass, making it hard to breathe, hard to think.

Seven innocents. Seven people who'd been controlled, puppeted, forced to attack while screaming inside their own heads.

And he'd killed them. Efficiently. Without hesitation.

Because he hadn't checked.

"That was faster, no… I bet you are the only one to figure this out till now," the cultist continued. "Seven little lambs out of so many, and you butchered them! Didn't even look! Didn't even care!"

He laughed again; the sound scraped against Jin's ears like broken glass.

"You have a fancy eye-skill, didn't you? What was it—Observe? Appraise? Essence Sight?" The cultist's grin widened impossibly further. "You could've felt them, could've known they were innocent, but you didn't check. Too confident. Too sure. Too certain they were all enemies." His black eyes glittered with malicious delight. "How's it feel, hero? Knowing you just murdered innocents while they screamed inside their own heads, begging you to stop?"

«Jin—»

"When the hierarch sent us here," the cultist continued, rolling his shoulders like this was the most fun he'd had in months, "we were honestly disappointed. Infiltrate this old Bastion? when we could have stormed and killed everything? Please. But this—" he gestured at the corridor, at the bodies, at Jin "—this is awesome! Watching you realize, watching it sink in—"

Something inside Jin's chest cracked.

He'd killed innocents.

Because he'd been arrogant.

Because he'd assumed.

Because he hadn't taken five seconds to verify what his eyes were showing him, hadn't spent the essence to check, hadn't—

Every thought Jin had been clinging to vanished.

Not suppressed. Not pushed down. Just... gone, like someone had flipped a switch and turned off everything that made him human.

The cultist was still laughing, drunk on the horror in Jin's eyes, on the sick realization painted across his face. "Oh, you should see yourself right now! Priceless! Absolutely—"

The anger came first… so overwhelming it should have consumed him, should have detonated in an explosion of rage.

Jin felt it rising like a tidal wave, felt it rushing up to drown him in fury and self-hatred and guilt—

And then something else rose up to meet it.

Cold.

The world drained of color in an instant. The corridor's dim lighting flattened to shades of grey, red blood becoming black smears against lighter stone. Even the cultist's mocking grin faded to a monochrome nightmare, teeth and eyes standing out like pale spots against dark skin.

Jin's expression went completely, utterly flat as his Eternal Sovereign stars roared to life inside him, burning bright.

Every emotion he had—the guilt, the fury, the sick realization of what he'd done—funneled directly into a single, crystalline purpose.

Make this right.

AND START WITH HIM!

Behind him, he heard Brocki mutter something low.

Flocki's response was quieter, barely audible: "Boss... Brocki..."

"Yeah." A pause, then softer: "I see it."

Then they moved—both twins stepping forward despite their injuries, despite the blood still pouring down Brocki's armor. They placed their hands on Jin's shoulders, one on each side, and squeezed gently.

"We're with ya, Bossman," Brocki said quietly, voice steady despite the pain. "Whatever happens next."

"Whatever it takes," Flocki echoed.

Then they backed away, giving Jin space, putting distance between themselves and whatever was about to happen.

Jin's gaze never left the cultist. For the first time in his life, he understood what it meant to truly focus. Not the tactical assessment he'd been running this whole fight, not the combat calculations or the Overdrive-enhanced processing.

This was something else entirely.

This was what it felt like when every unnecessary sensation, every irrelevant thought, every shred of doubt or hesitation was stripped away and fed into the only thing that mattered.

Execution.

The cultist's laughter faltered. Just slightly. Just enough to show he'd noticed the shift, noticed something in Jin's eyes that made even a blessed cultist pause.

"Oh," he breathed, and for the first time, genuine interest entered his voice. "Oh, that's new."

“Too bad I’ll have to kill you,” the cultist said. “Can’t have the secret out just yet.”

Jin offered no response as he pulled his mask from his ring and settled it over his face. For a moment, nothing happened. Then a faint silver aura flickered into existence around Jin.

It didn’t roar or flare. It simply existed as a thin, trembling sheen clinging to Jin’s outline as aura surged.

Iron Howl came up, barrel leveling at the cultist's center mass with the inevitability of a falling blade.

When Jin spoke, his voice was cold. Utterly, inhumanly calm.

"You're next."

And the aura responded.
