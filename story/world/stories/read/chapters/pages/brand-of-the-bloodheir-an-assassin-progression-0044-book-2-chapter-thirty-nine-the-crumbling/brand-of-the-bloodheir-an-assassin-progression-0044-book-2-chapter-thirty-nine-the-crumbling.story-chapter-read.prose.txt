Risens relaxed his grip on the Raven Talons, folding his arms over his chest as he watched the truant with poorly constrained annoyance.

Aleth worked diligently in the tiny, dead, abandoned garden. Ignorant of the filthy water, she dipped her hand in the placid pool, cupping a small measure of the liquid to nourish her planted seed.

Risens had no desire to reveal his presence to her at the moment. Other tasks in Windwake demanded his attention, and they were far more pressing than a little girl’s trespass.

Whatever fates were involved to drive her back into his path, he was now certain this would not be the last time. She had not merely stumbled on the Raven’s Court. She had been led here for a reason, though it was one that he was yet to understand.

Following the peak of the wall, he circled the area, stopping as he reached the crumbling gate. A final glance confirmed that she remained oblivious to his presence, still worrying over the garden. With a shake of his head, he leaped down into the deep shadows of the alley. Tucking and rolling, he disappeared into the dirty corridor without a sound.

The skyline of Windwake brightened as he snaked through Broad District. The unnatural glow of the fire would soon be overwhelmed by the coming sun. The continued distant ring of the fire bells, however, confirmed that the blaze was far from contained.

The hour was still late, but Risens cared little for the time. Tawny had provided a crude map to the loose-lipped male healer’s discreet clinic. Owing to the secretive nature of his labors, Risens expected that he would not find the man disposed to any private ventures. However, he couldn’t rule out the presence of any of the courtesans whose company he clearly preferred. He would pay the healer a visit. After all, the man was a liability now.

He couldn’t be sure if the healer was connected to other details yet to be found within the Dreamcatchers Tome. If so, it would be a race to see who would reach him first. Whether it be Risens’s stealthy approach or the thudding boots of the king’s troops on the healer’s doorstep, Risens suspected that after the visit, the man would be unable to offer further information.

His lips would be forever sealed in death.

Risens had stuck to the terrestrial pathways of late, skulking through the refuse of the filthy alleyways. With daylight rapidly approaching, his decision to abandon the surface and the soon-to-swell population turned his sights skyward. Clambering up the face of the nearest weathered building’s facade, he spotted his intended route with ease.

Overhead, the elevated aqueduct system floated above the city like a serpent swimming through the air.

Leaping from rooftop to rooftop, he steered toward the nearest point of ascension. Like with many other buildings and walls throughout Windwake, scaling one of the massive pillars that held the winding gutter aloft was accomplished with ease. Decorative features embellished the sides of the support, attempting but failing miserably to disguise the structure’s inglorious purpose. Beyond the carvings, handholds in any of the countless and ageless pitted holes made climbing easy for one with the proper skills.

Risens swarmed up the side of the aqueduct—a task even an amateur could accomplish with little fear of falling. It was only a matter of minutes before he peered cautiously over the weathered edge of the rounded channel that carried water to the city. In times not so distant past, passage along the great aqueduct would have been fraught with danger. The flow of water was fierce, running chest-deep as it spread its vital liquid throughout the sprawling city and the surrounding lands. However, it was nearly bone-dry now. The drought that plagued Halthome was fiercer.

More than half a year had passed, and nary a drop of precious, life-sustaining water had fallen from the sky. The rivers had sunken far below the level where they were of any true benefit to the city, and the various springs that dotted Windwake were heavily guarded, like dragons hoarding gold. Those who were too forceful in their demands were met with force. Those who tried to steal were met with death. The lucky few who controlled the springs did not suffer for want, nor did the king’s castle. The palatial complex Risens had once called home had several springs at its disposal, with vast reserves. Still, they amassed a great quantity of water—enough to bathe while the citizens’ lips bled.

Keeping the vast green spaces lush and flowering was more important to Lathrenon than the thirst of those who struggled beneath his iron fist.

The raised aqueducts had been built for function rather than ease or convenience of travel. The true date of construction was chronicled, though immaterial. It was rumored that it was only with the assistance of the gnomes—the reclusive people whose impressive control over magic had powered the sentinels—that the stones reached their final resting place high above the buildings of Windwake. However, he had no confirmation of the tale.

Few, if any, traveled the elevated pathways, but he still proceeded with caution. During these early hours, it would be surprising to encounter any workers, and if they were present, the noise they generated by their efforts was generally loud enough to wake the dead.

At the intersections, branches broke off from the main channel to supply all districts. At each, a large gate stood open. In need, the heavy stone panels could be shut to stymie the flow of water for purposes of repair. Lathrenon, at times, had even commanded the closure for no other reason than to punish one district or another.

Risens had ascended using one of the pillars near the finger that supplied water to the Central Ward. The main channel continued south through Learners Row and to Quayside and beyond. He would follow the offshoot here toward where the healer was known to haunt the taverns and to where his private clinic was concealed amid the unknowing denizens of the district.

Risens moved through the shadows of the trough, ignoring the narrow walkway built for the workers whose perilous job it was to control the water’s flow and maintain the aging structure.

The sudden stab of warning pierced through him as he neared the next intersection.

Screaming bloodlust erupted in his mind, and he slowly pulled the Raven Talons from their sheaths. The clear, taunting words echoed through his mind.

“Perhaps today will not be the day he sees his wings clipped.”

“Maybe he is not too blind to see the threat standing before him.”

Risens gritted his teeth against the weapons’ insults. Their blades had undoubtedly sharpened since their evolution and quenching in the Tears of the Under; so too had the intensity of their verbal assaults.

Their unquestionable lethality was an impressive boon to Risens’s arsenal. The bloodlust, the madness that seemed to inhabit their very cores, however, seeded vile, blackened thoughts and doubts within him.

Shadows separated from the wall, moving purposefully against the wavering glow of raging fire and the brightening morning horizon.

Five distinct, featureless shapes resolved into the unmistakable outline of human torsos.

Reflection of the moon overhead flashed off the naked steel held in the hands of each.

Risen’s anger swelled. The attempts on his life had already been confirmed to be more than mere coincidence. As if unseen eyes had watched his movements, all paths thus far had been chronicled in disturbing detail. It appeared the aqueduct would follow suit.

The Hunt had hinted at their preparation, though how could any have known his mode of transit this evening? How many teams lie in wait, clogging these arteries? How could this organization, whatever it was, maintain such a dramatic reach?

He seriously doubted the possibility that this encounter was pure chance as soon as it formed in his mind.

Their purpose was clear. It was his death.

Risens understood implicitly that, as prepared as they were, they would not fight fair.

Neither would he.

Risens stopped, forming a dam in the center of the dried aqueduct. They’d sprung their trap a moment too early. They’d waited for his appearance at the intersection where their numbers could be used to their advantage. He would not afford them that luxury.

The whisper of the Raven Talons slipping from their sheaths was drowned out by the screaming bloodlust in his ears.

“Dinner time!”

“It’s morning.”

“Is it? Who cares? Blood is blood!”

Risens’s eyesight was keen, honed over years of training. Even from several meters away in low light, he could tell that each wore a mask over the lower portion of their face. Such coverings weren’t necessarily uncommon, especially in his line of work, but the quality was apparent even from a distance. The similarities between these and those who had ambushed him in the confines of the hedge maze were poignant. As with the others, he expected they were all mageEnhanced to some degree. It was the feature that covered the rest of their faces that drew his attention. Thick goggles covered their eyes, each emitting a dull yellow glow. He suspected that the unorthodox augmentations imbued upon the wearer the ability to see even when darkness hid the natural details of their surroundings.

“Suppose you don’t want to make it easy on yourself, do ya?” The call came through the darkness from a gruff, gritty voice with an undeniable tone of magical alteration. His attackers fanned out across the narrow intersection of the aqueducts. His rapid scan confirmed that no magi were among them.

Risens recognized the decoy immediately, however. The distraction of conversation, drawing his attention to the thrown voice, was purposeful, meant to divert his gaze from the movement of the newest shadow stealthily along the aqueduct’s walkway. To their credit, they had not entirely overestimated their numerical strength, adding strategy to their trap. Still, they doubted his focus. They questioned his abilities. They would soon find out that that assumption was wrong.

“We’re not here to hurt ya, Rightmaker,” the man continued. “Put away your blades. We’re not here for a fight. We only want to talk.”

“Don’t listen to him.”

“He lies!”

“Perhaps chances for a conversation would be better started if one was greeted by naked blades,” Risens growled as he stalked a step forward.

Out of the corner of his eye, he traced the slow, steady shadow along the edge of the walkway, which crept ever closer.There was no sense in delaying the inevitable. Their words were hollow. They would find his daggers far less impotent.

The assassin sneaking along the edge of the aqueduct was only a few meters away when Risens threw himself into action. With Talons still in hand, he darted forward and caught the man in the face with a wicked punch. The cracking of bone resonated through his fist, and he followed up his attack with a precise and strategic hammering of his other fist into the man’s temple. His strike narrowly avoided the solid rims of the mageEnhanced goggles. Unconscious, the man plummeted off the side of the raised aqueduct.

“What are you doing‽”

“All that blood, wasted!”

With a screamed command from their apparent leader, the other waiting assassins sprang into action.

Risens caught the slight glint, the flash of the moon’s light on the mageVial as it twisted in the air. The solid, meaty slap of the man’s body reaching the avenue below was perfectly timed with the crunch of glass against the aqueduct’s wall.

MageVials were notoriously tricky to combat as their effects varied wildly, limited only by the devious intent of their creator and the materials at their disposal. Having dealt with this crew before, he anticipated the result. In their first two meetings, their first attempt had been to incapacitate, thus lessening the burden of physical assault of one whose skills they undoubtedly knew. Or at least they thought they understood.

Even in the darkness, the cloud of gas escaping from the shattered glass was clear, their attack predictable. The expanding fumes were far too familiar to his nose: Cimmerian Calcify.

The potent gas was a pointless trap for him. He grinned as he stalked steadily through the cloud, the Shadows Shroud protecting him from the toxin’s disabling effects.

His thoughts soured as he cleared the smoke. He knew the hand that had carefully cultivated the required, potent herbs for this assault.

He could only make out the eyes of the assassins, though surprise was made evident there as he lunged forward, slicing through the poison they thought would incapacitate him.

Risens parried the first defensive slash with relative ease. The blade in his right hand quivered in ecstasy as its edge sliced through flesh into bone. The immediate wail of pain was silenced as the second blade neatly severed the man’s throat. Spinning quickly as he ducked under another vicious thrust, he landed his fist square into the face of the second attacker. Pain shot through his fingers as his closed fist met with steel and glass, not bone and flesh.

The unmistakable whistle of a blade splitting the air followed. Shifting quickly, Risens latched onto the attacker’s arm, wrenching him closer, diverting his swing to the side. The strike that had been meant to end his life ended up impaling the assassin whose face he had just bloodied. The howling protests that erupted from the Raven Talons for Risens using his fist again were silenced into mumbling delight as the affront was rectified. Their bloodied tips punched through the back of the man who’d leveled the strike at him. The glowing symbols in the corners of his vision both ticked up another digit, as the cackles of delight shifted to an insatiable demand for more blood.

Risens’s sudden attack had brought him a span of several meters as the assassins involuntarily backed away from the lightning-fast wreckage of their companions toward the opportunities presented by the widened terrain of the intersection. The trio of soldiers that remained fanned out, two remaining in the main channel, while the other stepped into the offshoot of the aqueduct. Disgorging his blades from the man he still held aloft, Risens lunged forward, closing the distance in a single stride. Before his second boot could reach the ground, he disappeared in a fluttering of rapidly beating wings.

The air whistled in his ears as the Dull Wind carried him a step behind the pair blocking the access to the main channel. No particular skill or fancy movements were needed to finish the bloody task. The Raven Talons felt no need to correct his strikes as he drove the blades through the back of each man’s chest, punching out directly through their hearts.

The commotion that had started so quickly seemed to fade just as fast. The final assassin growled as he realized his fatal plight. As a fighter, Risens couldn’t help but admire the man’s response to the deadly affront. He stood his ground instead of fleeing. He didn’t beg for mercy even though many in his position would have. The shifting of the mask over his mouth and nose hinted at a grin.

Risens groaned as he realized the man’s intended actions.

The moon’s shining light reflected off the trio of glass vials gripped at his side. Risens needed no investigation to provethat the Cimmerian Calcify would not be found within.

Immobilization had failed. These would be meant to kill.

“For the glory of the Hunt!”

The glass shattered on the aqueduct’s floor before the man’s tongue finished the words.

There was nowhere for Risens to run. The steep, rounded walls of the aqueducts provided little avenue for escape. A fall from this distance would be entirely debilitating, if not lethal. There was no shelter among channels. The heavy, stone panel meant to divert water was raised, towering over the assassin a few meters beyond where he stood. Entangled as he currently was, Risens chanced that at a full sprint, with the Dull Wind at his command and feathers in reserve, he would reach the cover in time. But would it be enough?

The assassin, it seemed, had accepted his fate.

Cursing his limited position, Risens ripped the blades from the dead men’s bodies. Calling on the aid of the Dull Wind in a flutter of feathers, he flashed forward. Blinking back into space, he found himself short of his target—a little less than a meter remained between him and the assassin-turned-martyr. It was an inopportune and compromising time to discover the limitation to the distance the Dull Wind would propel him. In addition, to Risens’s chagrin, no time remained to consider recovering the feathers left by his movements.

He now saw but one chance at survival. Without further hesitation, he grabbed the unsuspecting assassin. Gripping him roughly by the tunic, Risens heaved him off the ground, the motion driving them both forward. Rotating, he shifted to use the man’s body as a shield.

It had only been a moment since the shattering of glass had sounded through the aqueduct. In the silence that followed, he’d crossed several meters, yet his position was still precarious. The momentary lull was ruptured by a thunderous explosion that ripped through the air like a violent peel of thunder. A heavy weight and thick pressure slammed into the pair.

Even with the added protection of the assassin’s body, the force was crippling. Jagged chunks of mortar rebounding off the stone of the aqueduct screamed past him, yet the deafening boom of the explosion had drowned out his hearing. The exposed skin of Risens hands and upper portion of his face bore the brunt of the devastating heat expelled by the detonation. The sky flashed with the brightness of noon.

The two found themselves soaring violently away. Pain lanced through Risens core as his back slammed into the edge of the now crumbling aqueduct, and he toppled bodily over the rounded edge.

His would-be assassin offered no complaints as they fell. He simply smiled and pushed himself away as they plummeted toward the stone avenue below.

Breaths were now the measure of Risen’s remaining life. The filthy cobblestones raced toward him at a dizzying pace, and his thoughts were scrambled. Marlaine. Tawny. The King. Mother Raven…

Desperation fueled his actions. Waving his right hand in a wide arc in front of him, he begged the Quillkey for assistance. Less than a meter from death, the atmosphere peeled open, and he slipped into the blackness beyond.
