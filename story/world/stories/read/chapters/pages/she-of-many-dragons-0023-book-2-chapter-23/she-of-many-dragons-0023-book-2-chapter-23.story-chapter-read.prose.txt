Alice could tell that Numi was practically bursting with excitement, eager to share what had happened during her and Prim's adventure. However, Alice’s primary concern was Bella and ensuring the safety of all her dragons.

She gave Numi a look that clearly said, “We will talk later.”

As a group, they moved out of the park and back to the street. Alice carried the sleeping Bella. By this time, it was so late that it had become early. Some cooks, a few maids, and other basic serving classes were out on the streets. Prim could and did cloak their vision and, according to her, some of their sound, but they still had to be very careful to avoid notice. Every time somebody like a baker passed by, they moved to the side of the street and quietly watched them pass. Sometimes that person would glance right or left as if sensing they were being watched, but could not quite pinpoint where that feeling was coming from.

It seemed to take forever to reach the Protectorate Guild. Alice didn’t want to be noticed going in and out, so she made her dragons wait until they could follow behind somebody who was entering or exiting. Luckily, the guild was busy at all hours. Prim's illusions were quite complete, and Alice's fears that there was anti-illusion magic were put to rest. If the higher-ranked individuals noticed what they were up to, no one raised an alarm.

They reached the elevator and Prim let her illusions fall. Her head was drooping with exhaustion and she could not even hold up her wings from scraping along the ground as she walked. Alice would have picked her up, but her arms were quite full with Bella. And it seemed that her dark dragon was growing heavier and heavier by the minute.

She blinked and looked closer. It wasn’t just that her arms were tiring, Bella was gaining both weight and length, practically before her eyes. Her long dark tail draped off Alice's arm and reached halfway to the floor.

Alice rested against the side wall as the elevator ascended, and when it opened again it took all of her strength to walk down the long hallway to her room. Numi opened the door before them.

Once inside, Alice settled Bella on the bed and stepped back to give her a good look. There was no doubt her dragon was growing. Alice would have questions for her once she woke up. But the important thing was that she was fine. Bella, semi-conscious, rolled over and curled into a tight dark ball.

Finally, Alice flopped into a nearby chair and looked to Numi, who had taken a seat on the table right beside it. "Okay," she said, "what did you find?"

"So many things!" Numi did a little circle before settling back down again. "The thief ran all sorts of double-back paths around the city. I think he realized he was being followed because he went down alleyways I wouldn't have noticed if I hadn't been practically at his heels."

"It was difficult," Prim agreed, "but we were able to keep up. Just barely."

"I think I might be offered a movement skill next," Numi added. "Anyway, he eventually slowed down and I realized that we were in that terrible part of town where I first hatched. Remember that place?"

Alice nodded. "I will never forget it." Sometimes she still dreamed of the men who had been searching for her and how she had been trapped. Numi had saved her life that day by getting her into a locked room to hide.

"Yes, well, eventually he went into what I assume was his hideout. It was partially underground and it was even dustier than that other building. There were a couple of coppers and even a silver coin hidden, but I couldn’t search it closely with him in there. We thought it was a dead end and we'd have to go trick another thief, but then"—Numi's eyes brightened—"someone else came to visit him."

"Someone else?" Alice asked. "Who?"

"I didn't get his name, but the thief called him a fence. He was really angry that the thief didn't have any jewels for him today."

Prim, probably feeling left out of the story so far, quickly took up the thread. "The thief said that he wouldn't pay the fence. Then the two of them got into a really bad argument and started accusing each other of different things. They were pushing each other."

"I think the second man was the one who sells all of the stolen goods," Numi added. "He accused the thief of holding out on him, and the thief told him that he nearly got away from a trap by law enforcement, but the fence didn't believe him. Then they started pushing each other again. Anyway, I stole from the fence."

"Wait." Alice held up her hand. "How did you manage to do that?"

Numi half bounced in excitement. "Prim had me cloaked under an illusion and I was hanging on to the wall. When the thief pushed the fence back, I just reached into his jacket. It was easy."

She reached into her mouth and pulled out a slightly wet bag. "Anyway, after that, we decided to get out of there."

Alice shook her head. No wonder Prim had received an illusion skill-up. It was a wonder that Numi had not received more levels, but then again, pickpocketing was not one of her current skills. Why was it that her dragons had such dark abilities?

"As we were leaving, the fence noticed that his bag was gone and he started to blame the thief," Prim said with amusement.

Alice was surprised at her own thoughts. She would’ve felt sorry for the man for being set up like that through no fault of his own, but... he had been willing to kill the illusion of Alice, so she did not have much sympathy for him. "So what did he have in his pocket?"

"I don't know yet. We wanted to find out together." With that, Numi eagerly pushed the bag to Alice.

Alice took it and, setting aside any moral quandaries, loosened the knot on top and dumped out the contents: a handful of silver coins, two gold coins, and a couple of rings spilled onto the table. One of the rings was topped with diamonds.

"Oh, a wedding ring," Numi said, picking it up and holding it close to her eye for examination. "The rest of the rings are cheap costume jewelry."

"The fact that he had all these together suggests that they aren't his," Prim said quietly.

Alice nodded and looked sadly at the wedding ring. The best case was if someone was out there missing it and the thief had not murdered for it. But she did not hold out much hope. "Will any of these stones help you advance?"

"No, but I think something in this box will. I can feel it."

With that, Numi opened up her mouth wide and pulled out a slim wooden box from her stomach storage space. The box looked longer than her body, which was a little ridiculous.

Alice stared at it. "Dearest, where did you get that?"

"Well…," Numi said, drawing out the word, "the thief and the fence kept fighting, and eventually it spilled outside of the hut and onto the street. They were being very loud about it. And then the fence was stabbed."

Numi said this while ignoring Alice's horrified grimace.

"Prim wanted to leave at that point because she could sense that you were upset about something, but I told her to wait outside the hut for a few minutes while I went back in."

"Were you under an illusion spell?" Alice asked sharply. This sounded like quite the risk.

Numi shook her head. "The two of them were outside the house at that point, and I knew I would be quick. So I snuck back in, looked around, and found this box hiding under the floorboards behind a lock." She held it up proudly. "It turns out that the thief was actually hiding stuff from the fence after all!"

Alice felt a few complicated emotions about that. Prim must have picked it up because she said, "Yes, it seems that the thief is a murderer, twice over, so of course he was hiding something away from his business partner."

She sounded a little grumpy, probably because Numi had proven to be right and Prim had been wrong.

Numi spoke up excitedly, "I think there might have been other hiding locations too, but the thief had just got done stabbing the guy and people were yelling and I think law enforcement was coming. So at that point, Prim and I had to get out of there."

Alice wasn’t sure what to say. She was a little horrified that her dragons did not seem to be bothered that they had witnessed a murder. They were just tired and proud of themselves for doing a good job. Then again, tended to only care about people once Alice showed her own sympathy for them.

"You said murderer twice over," she said slowly, not really sure if she wanted to know the answer, but she had to ask. "Did he...?"

"Yes," Prim said firmly. "You saw how he broke my illusion. He would have murdered you if he could, and he definitely killed that fence. There was blood everywhere. He’s done this at least once before."

"The crowd was really upset with him," Numi said. "I think some of them were just annoyed that they got woken up, but others didn't want law enforcement to come in and investigate. Everybody was yelling at one another, and it was very loud."

Mob justice. Alice wondered if the thief got away. That was a rough area of town, and if people were angry, annoyed, and fearful enough of a murderer in their midst, he probably hadn’t.

Well, perhaps it made her a bad person, but she would not mourn someone like him. "Let's see what he had."

It took almost no effort at all for Numi to unlock the clasp using the claw of one foot. She seemed rather put out that she did not receive yet another lockpicking level for the easy job.

Alice opened up the slim box and stared. It was filled with loose jewels, rings, stones, necklaces, bracelets, and other jewelry she could not readily identify.

Everybody stared save Bella crowded close and stared down with amazement. In the strengthening light from the window, it seemed that some of the jewels actually glowed.

"Why was he hiding all this?" Alice wondered. "He could have bought anything with it. He could have lived in the rich part of town. There’s so much!"

Numi cocked her head. "I'm guessing he had to sell these pieces slowly, especially if he killed the people that he was stealing from. That's probably why he was working through a third party like the fence."

She immediately started picking through the jewels, tossing a fair bit away as cheap glass and setting some aside. Then she held one up triumphantly. "Oh, Iggy, what do you think about this? It's a tiger eye stone and my assessment skill says it has magic."

Iggy picked up the stone in his stubby claws. "Possibly," he grunted, "but I feel like I am still digesting the pearlescent stone."

Meanwhile, Natura was showing marked interest. To Alice's surprise, she picked up the diamond wedding ring that Numi had set aside earlier.

"May I take this?" she asked.

"Why?" Numi asked. "It has no magic."

"It comes from the earth, and I believe that it may help me focus my earthen and stone power."

Natura effortlessly popped the diamond out of the ring and swallowed it down. Alice waited for a notification, but there was none. Nevertheless, Natura looked extremely pleased with herself. Finally, as if the System were trying to decide what to do, the notification arrived.

Alice glanced at her dragon, and of course Natura was amenable to it. She nodded, and Alice gave her silent agreement as well.

There were no further level-ups, but Natura looked content.

"That’s quite enough for now," Alice said. She was exhausted, and the worst thing was that they needed to be up early that morning in a couple of hours to train. It would be very suspicious if word got out of a brawl in the city, or if someone thought something unusual had happened in the park, and Alice and her dragons were suddenly seen as very tired.

With that thought in mind, Alice tucked herself around Bella and fell asleep, still over the covers.
