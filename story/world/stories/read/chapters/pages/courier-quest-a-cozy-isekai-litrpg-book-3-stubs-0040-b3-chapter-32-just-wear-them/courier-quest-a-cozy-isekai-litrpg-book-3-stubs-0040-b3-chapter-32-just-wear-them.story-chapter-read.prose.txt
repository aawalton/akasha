“That was, without hyperbole, one of the best massages I had ever received in my life,” Adabelle said as she sat down on the couch in their room and leaned heavily against her husband.

“Getting a professional massage was a new experience for me, but, yeah. I feel ya,” Trevor replied. He had all but fallen into his seat, relishing in the relaxed feeling permeating his mind and body. “I feel like I could just drift away to sleep right here and now.”

Aivy and Garlett had been wonderful, and the newlyweds had been the only ones in the ship’s spa for a half hour afterwards. They had rested in a scented bath, sweat in a sauna, and cooled off in the provided shower. Both of them had come out of there relaxed, refreshed, and wearing the fluffiest, softest robes they had ever put on.

They were color coded for him and her, but they had traded off. Trevor knew that the blue one was traditionally meant for him, which was something cultural that an Earth Summoned probably brought with them, but it was his wife’s favorite color. Since he didn’t have any qualms about wearing pink, he was wearing that one instead.

Adabelle placed her head against her husband’s chest, careful of her horns, and slid a hand into his robe to rest against his skin. “We still have parasailing,” she reminded him.

“Then they can come and bring this couch along with us,” he replied, not opening his eyes.

“I’m not sure the furniture is structurally sound enough for that, nor do I believe their parachute would be able to hold it,” the demonkin mused.

“Braidley will just have to give it a little extra juice, is all. If he can keep the Red Dream speeding through the air like this, he can do the same thing to a couch.”

She giggled at his statement before looking up at him and kissing his cheek. “I’m going to go get changed, and then we’re going to leave. Does that sound fair?”

“Yeah, I guess,” Trevor sighed. His fingers trailed down her back and tail as she stood up and walked away.

Clay jumped onto the couch and into their master’s lap. They put their front paws on his chest as they looked up at him with literal sapphire eyes. Trevor opened his own, smiling at his familiar as he began to pet them.

“I know you’re interested in parasailing, but I don’t feel comfortable taking you up there,” he said before cradling the earth elemental in his arms. “Here on the ship, even out on the railing, is one thing, but there would be no recovering you if you fell into the ocean. Sorry, little buddy.”

A feeling of stubborn insistence came across their bond as Clay didn’t seem to want to accept that answer.

“I said I was sorry,” he repeated, frowning that he was letting his friend down. “Remember when we did all those experiments in the river? With the shapes and the pumice? From this height, nothing is going to help you even if you do turn into a boat.”

The familiar crossed their arms and turned their head away. Trevor recognized that pout.

“No, don’t act that way,” he soothed. “You know I would take you if I could.”

“Why don’t you just wear them?” Adabelle asked from the bedroom.

The Summoned and his familiar both paused at that. Trevor frowned and shifted so he could see the door. “Like, a hat?”

Clay became very excited about this prospect. They were ready to give it a try right then and there.

“You have a high enough Strength score that a stone hat probably wouldn’t strain your neck, but I don’t think that will help much with all the wind we’ll be dealing with,” she answered.

“Yeah, they’d still get blown right off,” Trevor agreed.

“I meant something like an armband, or a bracelet, or a glove,” Adabelle continued. “Clay’s core is a bit big for something like a ring, but even a necklace would do.”

The courier looked down at his familiar, who was watching him with hope in their bond. Not only that, but they were wiggling in anticipation.

“Can you show me your core, little buddy?” he requested.

With a nod, Clay’s body went still as a black sphere appeared from between their ears. Trevor picked it up and their stoat husk went inert. The familiar’s core was solid and shiny, clearly taken care of with the same process that the familiar used to polish coins. They were only about the size of an ordinary marble. Definitely too big for a ring without making it look gaudy, but there were other avenues to explore.

“I know you can see through your core like you do through your sapphires, but I still want this to be at least a little bit comfortable for you,” Trevor said as he thought things through. “A bracelet would work, but I’m almost always moving my hands around. An armband would be better but still not great.”

Clay rolled out of Trevor’s palm and fell back onto their stoat husk. They moved to their own forehead before pointing with their claws.

“What, like a circlet or something? I don’t think I can pull one of those off,” the courier said, rubbing his head. “It would add some stability, though. I’m almost always moving my head but it’s not as chaotic as my arm.”

Adabelle approached from behind and leaned over to wrap her arms around Trevor’s neck. She scratched Clay’s chin, causing them to preen at the attention.

“Maybe a brooch, or a pen,” she offered. “You’ve got some shirts with pockets, right? They can sit inside, little marble sticking out while clinging to the fabric.”

“You’ve got the best ideas, love,” he replied, tilting his head to kiss her. “Though, now that I’m thinking about it, Clay could also become a band around Tanazi’s hat whenever they want to go incognito. It’s not what I would traditionally call a pirate’s hat, so they could sit atop it and swivel around in all directions. All I’d need is your permission to wear it.”

“If you want to wear that raggedy thing, be my guest,” Adabelle said as Clay continued to nod and wiggle. She giggled at the familiar. “Seems like someone is excited about it, at least. Now, come on. I got dressed, so now it’s your turn.”

“I suppose,” Trevor said with a half-hearted, pitiful whine.

His wife was unimpressed with his forced woe.

He passed his familiar to Adabelle before standing up. The stretch he performed afterwards felt amazing. While he was still reaching towards the ceiling, his body turned dark as he changed into something both comfortable and had a pocket on the chest. The button-up shirt was something similar to what he’d buy back on Earth. It had proven to be a staple in two worlds now.

“Alright, Clay. Time to turn into something vaguely pen-shaped that will fit here,” Trevor said as he tapped his pocket. “Try not to make it too heavy, though.”

The familiar nodded as they jumped down from Adabelle’s arms and onto the couch. They bounced slightly on the plush cushion before laying out flat. All at once and with surprising speed, a thin bar of gold and marble ejected itself from underneath Clay’s core, which had still been on their forehead.

Their form was almost exactly like that of a pen, complete with pocket clip. The black sphere was situated on top of it, which would ensure that they would stay high enough that they could see. In their excitement, Clay inched towards Trevor like a worm, ready for the pocket.

“Someone’s eager,” the courier laughed as he reached down and picked his familiar up. They crawled around his fingers, testing out the new body, before they were unceremoniously dropped into his pocket. “There you go. Feel free to clip yourself on. Let me know how it feels, yeah?”

Clay straightened out and swung the clip over the side of the pocket, pulling themselves up. Their body was slightly too short, so they fixed that by shifting their mass to be a little thinner. After that, they had no problems. Trevor could feel their emotions through the bond as they got used to their situation. To help, he started walking around the suite.

The pen-shaped familiar flopped with each step before transforming slightly. One clip became two in a V shape to help with stability and their body flattened and spread out like a spatula, taking up more of the pocket. Two little arms went in either direction, resting snuggly against the sides. They nodded as best they could, happy with the new arrangement.

Trevor smiled as he felt Clay’s emotions through their bond. They had always been fairly independent, wanting to stay as mobile as possible, and he had always assumed it was because of their time stuck in their own husk. The earth elemental had been overjoyed to be free again, so much so that they had become his familiar almost immediately after.

Now, the Summoned found it heartwarming that their little buddy trusted him enough to be mostly immobile again. They could move around a little bit, sure, but this body was very limited in comparison to their stoat form. Trevor reached down and sent the now-inert husk into his inventory.

“You make for a very dapper accessory, Clay,” Adabelle complimented, patting them both gently. “I dare say this is a better solution than simply pocketing them when you go places where you can’t bring what looks like an animal with you.”

“That’s only happened a couple of times, but I feel a little silly now,” Trevor admitted as he scratched the back of his head. “All this time we could have been accessorizing in style. Well, better late than never, I suppose. Are we ready to go?”

“Yes, we are,” the noblewoman replied before kissing her husband gently. Then, not so gently, began pulling him towards the door. “Which is good, because I am not going to be late for this, Trevor Anderson.”

“I got it, I got it,” he laughed as he matched her pace. He managed to pull away just long enough to lock the door behind him, but then they were off.

While Trevor allowed himself to be led by his wife, he kept his attention on Clay. The familiar was used to riding on his shoulders, but this was something else entirely. Their view was different and they were far more exposed than usual. Their core was a literal one, their entire being, and here they were in the open air.

But, even now, he could feel the trust and admiration his familiar had for him. Trevor smiled, looking down at Clay, before squeezing his wife’s hand tightly.

“I love you two,” he said, vocalizing his immediate thoughts.

Adabelle looked at him and matched his expression. “I love you both, too.”

Clay’s body began warming up and vibrating, causing the courier to laugh. “This little guy clearly feels the same.”

They made it to the main deck of the Red Dream. There were even more people here than yesterday, enjoying the fresh air and comfortable weather, but they ignored them to head towards the back of the ship.

“That was so exciting!” a child laughed and yelled as they ran from the parasailing area.

“Never doing that again,” their mother said, dabbing a cloth against her damp forehead as she followed her kid. “That is not for me.”

Trevor put on a thin smile, feeling for the woman. In a world without roller coasters or thrill rides, something like this could be a daunting experience.

A pair of crewmates, including one of the rare non-demons, were cleaning the gondola. It was a metal bar with straps and harnesses underneath to create seats for two riders. The parachute was stylized to look like a rose and had been bundled up so the wind wouldn’t catch it.

“Lady Adabelle and Trevor?” the human man asked as he spotted them.

“That’s us,” she answered.

“You’re a couple minutes early, but no worries. We’ll get this cleaned up and ready for you in a jiff,” he said with a smile.

“No rush, take your time,” Trevor replied as he led his wife a good distance away. They looked over the railing at the boundless ocean. The airship was moving quite fast, and there weren’t any islands in sight.

“Are you ready for this, son of Ander?” Adabelle teased quietly, making sure they didn’t hear her.

“Psh, of course,” he said as he puffed out his chest and thumped it, careful not to hit Clay. “I come from a world where we drive death traps at nearly a hundred miles per hour, attach ourselves to zip lines and cross over deep canyons, and scare ourselves silly for the thrill of it. This is child’s play.”

“Oh? Is it?” she challenged, leaning against him. “You’re so sure of yourself.”

“Somebody told me that was a sexy quality to have,” he replied, wrapping an arm around his wife. “But, if I’m being real, I’ve got three parachutes, a magical inflatable raft, several days worth of food, and a familiar who could become a propeller if they really needed to. Plus, we still have the wing suit feathers that Drelik got us. Even if we fall, which are slim odds, we’re going to be set.”

“That’s my pragmatist,” Adabelle giggled, tapping his chest with her fist. “I’ll be counting on you if something goes wrong.”

“I’m sure nothing will,” Trevor said with a smile. “Everything else on this ship has been great, so I imagine this is just as tried and true as the rest of it. Plus, I kind of miss some of the thrills of my old life. I was just thinking about roller coasters.”

“Wayne’s talked about those before,” the noblewoman replied. “Long, twisting paths that go high into the air. You ride along it, doing loops, spirals, and more, right?”

“That’s right. They’re a lot of fun, and there were a lot back on Earth. Tall rides, fast rides, twisting rides, upside down rides; we had thrills galore. I’m hoping that Aletander has at least one amusement park. Might be worth breaking out of our regularly scheduled programming for it.”

“Perhaps,” Adabelle agreed before leaning in and whispering. “Or, we could be bad.”

“I’m listening.”

“We could abuse a couple of Full Night’s Rest and pull some all-nighters so we can do both.”

Trevor considered that before nodding. “We have been good about using them and I have plenty. So long as it doesn’t become a habit, I’m down.”

“Good. Then, if they have one of these amusement parks, it’s a date.”

“Now I'm really hoping they do, lady.”

“We’re ready for you,” the human said, waving them over.

Adabelle was the first to move, and Trevor smiled at her palpable excitement. Parasailing would be a new one for him, but he knew he was going to enjoy it, too.
