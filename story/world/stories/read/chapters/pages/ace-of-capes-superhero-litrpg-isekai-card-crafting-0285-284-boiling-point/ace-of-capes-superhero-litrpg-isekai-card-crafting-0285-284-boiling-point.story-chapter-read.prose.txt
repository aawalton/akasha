Aiden's earthquake had split it open, but a sigil formed underneath the Lightlark's feet, and he leapt back up at the speed of light.

He was fast. But Lexie wasn't about to let him escape.

She turned the ground into miniature Earth golems, their limbs shooting out and grasping for the man that was slippier than his girth would suggest.

Lexie created a web lattice using airlinks and trapped him in place, as Aiden targeted a tiny dot of red right at his chest.

An explosion instantly hit him, and he was launched back by the force of the blow.

And somehow so was Lexie.

“Lexie!”

It had been a leash around her neck that attached her to him and now dragged her along. She tried to sever it with VOID, but she couldn't touch it, not even with metaphysical VOID.

The light repelled the VOID. Fantastic.

She needed Pvilycht for backup and to figure out this jerk's weakness.

She summoned him, but it felt like hot coal on her veins. And Pvilycht didn't look much better.

In fact, when he saw who they were facing, he did something she'd never seen him do.

He paled.

She guessed she could see why. The other man had somehow landed on his feet, unscathed from Aiden's miniature bomb that had cratered the ground.

Oh, and he was glowing.

Slices of shadows fell on them.

Aiden floated in the air, his hand held up to the sky. Red-searing spears had multiplied several fold above their assailant's head, and Aiden pointed at him, with his eyes blazing with rage.

“Let her go,” he demanded.

The man looked unimpressed. “I did not spend my entire life protecting creatures from calamities like her just to let Earth get overtaken by her kind. Not on my watch."

Aiden didn't respond. He barely let the man finish his sentence before he sent the array of blades his way.

In a flash, Lightlark did something. He bent the light from the sun towards Aiden, and Aiden winced, blocking with a darkened force field against the blinding light.

The bending also served another purpose. The light seemed to have eliminated the shadows and somehow trapped the weapons in place.

Lightlark made an X with both arms across his chest, then swiftly tore them apart.

The weapons flew in every other direction but at him. Lexie's heart damn near skipped a beat.

His light bending left him in the dark, and Lexie took advantage of that, attaching his feet to the endless shadow. Meanwhile, Pvilycht attempted to gain control of his body, but the Light from his body exploded in all directions, hitting Lexie and Pvilycht again.

They both screamed.

Lexie's head pounded as she summoned rain, fire, and lightning, surrounding him, shooting at him from all angles.

A part of her enjoyed the chaos, but most of her hated the fact that he was unscathed right now.

"What the hell?” Lexie heard someone exclaim.

Terry most probably. Their ruckus had probably eaten into her property. They had already destroyed Lexie's home and were bound to destroy even more if this continued.

But Lexie didn't think about that right now. She would leave it to Aiden to protect them and fix things later, while she focused on killing this asshole.

Her mind sifted through cards quickly, hitting him with everything she had, attack after attack, without giving him any downtime. More golems exploded from the ground. She summoned her creature cards and sent them at him. She trapped him in webs that he couldn't escape, using all the elements she had in her arsenal, including sound, against him.

He finally bled from his ears, but he didn't go down. Didn't stop resisting.

Pvilycht stuck out his hand, and the man’s arm twisted a little, but the light snared out, and Pvilycht screamed like a girl when it touched him. He instantly disappeared, and Lexie could only stare in disbelief as one by one, her creature cards were vanquished by a swipe of light.

What the hell was this guy? Why was he holding his own against her?

Sure, he hadn't landed any more hits thanks to her excellent shielding, but he should not still be standing. She was the freaking King of the Eldritch. He was just a human. He should have fallen by now!

As he deflected more of her father's attacks, including more bombs, Lexie realized.

Shit. She might actually be in trouble here.

She drew up her most recent cards, the ones that didn’t need her mana. Unfortunately, most of them weren't really attack cards, but one of them was a mind-scrambling card, and the other, if used properly, could trap him in a puzzle box.

She also had a card that caused decay.

She activated, dazzling him first, then sent the decay at his feet.

It didn't reach him in time. He was too fast. Too fast for the puzzle box, as well.

Well fuck it. She was fast, too.

She turned up <Lightfoot> to the max, activated her slime attack, and Windbreaker to trap and torpedo him.

She used the mindscrambling card again, as she got in his face, landing a solid punch that broke his nose and added enough force to drive him back.

Nice. That felt really good.

Next time, she should tear his head off for good measure.

But suddenly, light snaked around her arms and wrapped around her neck, dragging her to the ground.

Exhaustion crawled over her.

She wasn’t actually tired; it was the light beam that made her so. It was suppressing her Eldritch mana, making it such that she could only draw from her human magic, which wasn't nearly plentiful enough to spam attacks like she was doing. Not to mention it hurt.

Trying to get up hurt.

Breathing hurt.

But she was used to pain, so she focused instead on beating him.

Her mind worked through it, figuring out a strategy. The light was his strength, both the one within and the one outside. She couldn't beat him here, in the daytime, surrounded by sunlight. She needed to drag him to her domain.

The dungeon.

She just needed him to get close enough first.

She would lie here and let him think he had her. But the minute he touched her, she would teleport them both out of here.

At the same time, she wanted him to think she was still fighting. So he didn't see the trap coming.

She sent a lightning bolt his way, and predictably, he reflected it. Lexie made it vanish before it hit her.

"Everything the light touches is mine," he said, and as he swept to her, Lexie translated to Aiden through mindspeak: Don't stop him and don't activate second Aiden yet. I have a plan.

Lexie hoped her father listened even as he used a blackened forcefield to block Lightlark, knowing fully well the other man could break through it.

Lexie didn’t want Golem Aiden revealed yet. He was their secret weapon in case this went really left, and she didn’t want him to get confiscated, too.

But things went awry in an unexpected way when they heard a familiar whooosh of teleportation and a familiar scream:

“Uncle Leo, No!”

“Stop,” Xena cried out. “Don't hurt her!”

“Take her out of here, Lionel." The man stopped to yell up at them.

“No, please! You said you would help her.”

Ah. Lexie felt a sting of betrayal.

So Xena had told her Uncle what Lexie was and had asked for his help. She had exposed Lexie's secret.

That hurt almost as much as the light bolt in the chest.

Then again, it didn’t even matter. The Ambassador could have told him, too.

But the fact that the reveal came at least partially from Xena was what made Lexie feel crushed like a heel on the ground.

She hated that feeling.

“I will help her,” Leo Lightlark said. “If she comes with me calmly. I didn’t come here to kill her, but she appears to be too far gone for reason."

Too far gone for reason? When had he even attempted to reason with her?

Luckily, during the intermission, Aiden swept close enough to Lexie to touch her shoulder, and they both disappeared into pitch darkness.

Thank the system. A pocket dimension.

"Lexie?"

She held out her hand to her dad as she choked, the lasso of light sending her to her feet, dragging her into darkness as she cried out, "Dad!"

“Lexie!” He put out his hand, probably to teleport their pocket dimension somewhere else.

But she still wasn't far enough away.

Lexie’s heart raced. That meant that she needed to go somewhere that the light couldn’t reach.

"Dungeon." She choked out to inform her dad, as she teleported there, and the leash finally loosened.

She couldn't feel it anymore, the tension, even though her body was still reacting to everything that had happened.

She collapsed on the red dirt, finally drew a deep breath, the scent of chaos without the sharp tang of light.

Lexie stared up at the red sky.

“Home sweet home,” she breathed.

Then she started to laugh, in earnest this time. She didn't mean to; it just kind of bubbled out of her.

That had been horrific and humiliating.

But it was also the most fun she'd had in a while.

She felt exhilarated.
