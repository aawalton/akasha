That was pretty fun. Laying down goals, boundaries, and displaying my power all in one go? Pretty neat, if you asked me. Scaring the kid had felt a little weird, but she needed to know where I stood. I’d helped them plenty, going above and beyond our agreement to share resources and magical knowledge, and so far she’d barely fulfilled her end of our deal.

As for attacking the nation to the South, that was something that had been on my mind for a little while at this point. Not only had they attacked me, but that was also where that weird Esheth guy lived. I’d been warned that he was strong, but if I took the fight to him then I’d be facing far less risk.

I was hoping to find some way to translate the common folk of Yalten to undeath, rather than killing them and then resurrecting everyone. While the upper brass deserved what was coming for them, the average person hadn’t played a role in the assault of my tower. They were simply… misguided. Under poor management. While I wouldn’t call that type of takeover passive, it would hopefully turn out much better than what I'd made it seem like I’d be doing.

That is, if I could get it to work.

It would also probably be a little while off. The math said I had two months– a bit less, really– to prepare for Esheth, and while I often got super distracted and did things without regard for time, I could get a lot done in that amount of time.

Kelemnion had said that I wasn’t ready for Esheth before. Since then, I’d gotten an extra Enhancement in each category, but that most likely wasn't all I’d need.

He’d told me to specifically focus on abilities that were mine, as well as my Conceptual manipulation.

I’d already worked quite a bit on the latter– I’d improved my skills in stripping and replacing intent in spells. As for the former, it sort of depended on what he meant. The closest things I could think of were the abilities I’d developed personally, the ones that allowed me to punch so far above my level, and those that were characteristic to my being, such as my Repository and Sovereignty.

Runework was one of the areas I was most confident in. I had obviously obtained a lot of resources for it from my specialization, but actually using them had been all me. Programming complex inscriptions always made me feel particularly smart, and I was fairly confident in my ability to conquer most threats– using that– or at least the ones I could see coming and didn't have a way to ignore my rune effects.

The trouble there was that it required an amount of preparation. I knew Esheth was supposed to be strong, but I didn’t know how he was strong. There were so many different types of advanced energy that I couldn’t possibly prepare for all of them. I could go investigate in Yalten, but that would require spreading my domain into their territory, which would tip my hand before I was ready to tear down their government.

If I couldn’t figure out his energy type, and I couldn’t prepare for every possible contingency, then I’d have to make wider preparations that would be more versatile, and I’d have to be prepared to use some of my personal strength.

There was a good chance that I’d be able to counterspell some of his attacks, but my own attacks were meh at best, so I’d need to ready my troops for battle as well. I’d want a bit of everything, and diversifying was probably a good plan considering that I didn’t know exactly what I’d be facing. In game design, I remembered, there was a triangle of damage, speed, and tankiness, and each monster was usually given up to two of those, with only a few rare exceptions. Following that strategy seemed wise, giving me a better chance of countering Esheth at the risk of wasting some mobs.

Creating more variants of my Antigo creatures was an easy choice. They were sort of standard soldiers for me at this point, and I already had a version that had potent defenses and damage, though I’d likely want to further reinforce the archetype if I were creating more versions.

Another good move would be delving into some more realms. The risk of encountering a god was enough to make me hesitate, but so long as I didn’t enter personally I could probably stay safe. Like Kelemnion had said, and as the death of Icarios had demonstrated, these dimension gods couldn’t leave their own worlds.

The process would likely be slower for the other dimensions because of this, and I’d be less capable of handling the foes within, but as long as I didn’t pick something that countered my own energy as thoroughly as Empyrix had, it should turn out fine.

I could also potentially ask Kerenth for some support against Esheth, but after the stunt I just pulled I figured asking for anything else would be a bit much. When I’d first learned about this situation I’d figured that Azrael would be there to help me out, but she still hadn’t returned from her “quest.” Her gear was still drawing power, so I figured she was alright, but I was starting to understand why she’d always been so upset about me spending weeks locked away working on my magic without any communication. It was… concerning.

Without her, my chances were a bit lower, but part of me was glad that she wouldn’t be drawn into the crossfire. And I’d be fine. Probably.

I felt the urge to work on my tower, but if things went the way I wanted them to, it wouldn’t actually matter. My previous justification of needing it to be tough enough to weaken him didn’t matter if I was going to take the fight to Esheth personally.

Though… I suppose that making a new boss wouldn’t be too bad? The question is, what would I want to make? I had a dragon, a werewolf mage, a rabbit warrior, and a… slug librarian. What else was there?

I mean… I suppose I could make a water level?

I smirked to myself. Everyone hated water levels. That was what made them fun.

And instead of water, I could use ink, and the final boss could be a leviathan or something?

Now that was an interesting idea. Maybe I could drop Esheth into a portal with a giant snake in it, or open up portals around him so it could eat him?

So far all of my bosses were rather refined. Carnic didn’t talk much, and Drachma’Uban slept all of the time, but they didn’t really feel… primitive, even as monstrous and aggressive as they were intended to be. I wanted something like that. Violent. Hungry. Something that loved the destruction it could wreak with its own might.

I made a pocket dimension where the boss floor would eventually be and filled it with water that I then used my Concept boons on to give it something of a mind of its own. I’d give it the consistency of honey, the volatility of a storm, and the toxicity of mercury.

Once enough intent had been applied to reach an acceptable level, I started work on the boss monster itself. I wanted it to be a massive serpent, bigger than any of my creations so far, but for that work it would need to be strong enough to swim through this new liquid.

A lot stronger than it would need to be otherwise, actually. After dropping an Antigo in the black water and seeing it struggle to maintain even a little bit of forward momentum, I knew that my new creation would have to be the physically strongest thing I’d ever made.

Which had been the plan in the first place, but at least now I had a good excuse.

I ended up expanding the pocket dimension significantly, realizing that the size and sheer physicality I’d be going for would need a bit more room to move around, and people fighting him would also need room to evade. Once I made the space big enough to support a large-scale naval battle, I decided it was good enough and started making a sky-scraping spine.

I spent time creating detailed musculature for the skeleton– I usually didn't have to focus too much on this, but I wanted this particular monster to be different. Studying the way my other creations naturally worked, how their muscles pulled and twisted, helped me decide which parts to reinforce, and all the while I infused my intent into the forming beast, instructing reality to make it a monolith of might.

Slowly, a snake-like body took shape, but that wasn't enough for me.

I focused my mind on the boss monster's ribs and made them stretch and deform, slicing through the forming flesh and growing. I shaped joints into them and filled them with structures like those in spiders– muscles to pull inwards, and some of the liquid I used in place of the water to replace the hemolymph hydraulic system.

It took some messing around and a couple tests, but by delving deep into the anatomy of a few random spiders I'd kidnapped from the edge of my border and taking advantage of the shenanigans my magic allowed me to do, I managed to get it working.

The giant meat-snake now had eight giant skeletal legs. Now I just needed to add some more details, like senses and perhaps magical manipulation, and some natural incentives for people to kill this thing.

Maybe this time I'd make its scales out of carbon fiber? That would be nasty.

Then again, I wasn't sure I wanted people to have access to a lot of that stuff. At least, more than they could already access in the dungeon. While anything formed from my personal energy would be beholden to my power, if anyone figured out its creation there was a small chance it could be turned against me.

Yes, so far Kerenth’s inscriptions were trash, and that would prevent them from doing the detailed and magic-intensive work required, but I couldn't guarantee that the same would be true for Yalten, and they were the problem.

I started scanning around my domain for anything interesting, but came up blank. I mean, there were several materials infused with concepts, but nothing groundbreaking.

Whatever, it was probably fine to go ahead with it. It wasn’t like anyone from Yalten would be getting a chance to kill this thing.

I started by making a single thick scale, then copied it and layered it over itself on a dimensional level. I then replicated the scale and fused them onto the serpent.

This took a little while, and I felt a bit rushed considering how much time I had left until I was attacked, but this felt necessary.

A few more bits and bobs were added to the new creation, and then after a bit of practice piloting the body and making sure it worked how I wanted, I created a wisp to serve as its mind. This one was much simpler compared to all my others, so it didn’t take as much out of me, but I still tried to instill it with a sense of self-awareness.

I was making a monster, but it was beneath my standards to make a boss incapable of having its own interests and interacting with its brethren if it so chose.

Sure, it’d probably end up being a total savage anyways, but I wanted to give it the capability to be more, even if it never made use of it. As for a name… I decided I’d call him Jormun, to match with Fenrir.

Once that was finished, I slipped some instructions into the new creation’s mind and then left it to become accustomed to its own body. I couldn’t commit it to a Named Belonging slot since I didn’t have any free, but that was alright. I had a perfect memory with my Repository, so those slots had lost most of their utility to me. It’d probably make recreating the boss a bit quicker in the future, but I wasn’t expecting it to be taken down frequently.

While it had seemed quick to my senses, I knew it had been at least a few days since I’d started working on the floor, and I needed to prepare my other things.

And we’re back to the question of what Kelemnion meant when he told me to focus on what’s mine. He was clearly referring to things that weren’t given to me by the system, but when it comes down to it everything I am now comes down to that power.

Again, my mind went to runes and inscriptions.

I’ve literally invented my own runes. Surely that’s what he meant?

In that case, I’d need to make more runes, new runes. Maybe I can use that to translate the people of Yalten as well…

I started gathering my intent within myself, but paused. What do I actually need?

Obviously, some way to cancel out whatever nonsense Esheth was going to do, and some way to make the people of Yalten into undead without scrubbing their memories.

Is it that simple?

I focused on the desire to convert living flesh into undead– focusing heavily on thoughts of liberation and enhancement, rather than destruction or control, as I wanted these people to ascend into undeath, rather than become mindless zombies– condensing it into a liquid and pouring it over a thick sheet of gold. There was probably a better way to actually make the rune, but I wasn’t worried about it at that moment.

I was more worried about finding a test subject.

While I was most concerned about how the rune would work against a human, I figured it would be best to start on an animal, so I instead teleported several rodents from the edges of my domain into a control room that I carefully purged of all hostile energies.

It would be difficult to test how effective the conversions were on less intelligent creatures, but it would be good to make sure my rune actually worked before using it on people. Not to mention, I’d want my first test subjects to be consenting if at all possible.

I wasn’t going to be able to make everyone in the entirety of Yalten agree to become undead, but I felt that forcing it upon them would be fine. After all, they’d be getting free meals, healthcare, dental, and effective immortality courtesy of my Occult Sovereignty. They’d unlive, and they’d get over it.

My rune was going to take a little while in the golden oven, though, so in the meantime I observed my newest boss monster. He’d been thrashing around in the water when I left, and I felt a pang of guilt at not introducing him to existence as carefully as I had with the others.

How are you, Jormun? I sent to him.

The massive beast paused in the water for a moment, then sent back, Strong.

Strong, huh? I supposed that was what I’d made him for. How strong?

Jormun flicked his tail smugly, causing the entire ocean of ink, the one thick enough that an Antigo could barely move through it, to whip into a tidal wave. Very strong.

Admittedly, the ink had been created with chaos in mind, but it was still impressive. Honestly, I wasn’t sure the physics even checked out on the act, but when working with Conceptual materials physics were less rules and more vague suggestions.

I was about to concentrate back on my products, but then I paused, feeling another flash of guilt. It wasn’t like I didn’t mostly leave my creations to their own devices, but being so lackadaisical with another creature, one that was effectively a child as well, was wrong.

Hey… I sent over, Is there anything you want?

Jormun sent over a feeling of confusion.

Like, I don’t know, something to do? Or… I don’t know. Anything at all?

Power?

I chuckled at the serpent’s response. I think you’ve got plenty of that for now.

Jormun seemed content at that, going back to playing around in the water, so I checked over my other creations.

Drachma’Uban was, predictably, asleep. Fenrir was busy working on his massive lore carvings– effectively fanfic of me. I avoided reading it for now, knowing that it would probably be awkward for both of us.

Carnic was, unexpectedly, wrestling with some of the other rabbits from his section of the dungeon. He was being gentle, so it seemed lighthearted enough.

The Librarian was teaching some of the wisps to read, which I maybe should’ve seen coming but didn’t. I sort of just hadn’t thought the wisps possessed much free will at all, but then again, all my bosses were really just highly intelligent wisps stuffed in meat suits.

I did a brief overview of the rest of my creatures, and found that they were all in good health, save for a couple that were locked in combat with that kid from earlier. I left them to it, and instead turned my attention to my brand new rune.

Time to see if this thing works.
