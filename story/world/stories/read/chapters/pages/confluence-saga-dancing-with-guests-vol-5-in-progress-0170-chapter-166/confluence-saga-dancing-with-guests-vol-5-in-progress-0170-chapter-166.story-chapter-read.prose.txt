Bern is angry. I am almost always angry but Bern helps keep me calm. Except when we fight. Then I can be angry. I can't be angry when we duel or spar, I must not hurt others.

It is too easy to hurt others, too easy to hit them and then they don't get back up. Except the ones who make Bern angry. I can't hit the man, he doesn't get close. When he does the music makes me miss, lets him dance away. The woman though, her I could hit. Her I want to hit, and not for Bern. She hits me, she faces me, she keeps fighting even when I come close to hitting her.

One day I will hit her, one day she won't hit me back.

I'm not supposed to hit Royals. My blood knows that, my spirit knows that. I must not hit her, even if I want to.

Bern wants me to hit her, wants to hit her himself.

Bern hasn't faced her. He talks loudly, he tells me to fight. He says he will win.

Bern is angry. That doesn't mean he's right. He doesn't know what I think, he doesn't ask.

"What did Valene want?"

Fay looked up from her dinner. Most nights she and Briana ate alone, but they'd agreed to let Alex join them so that they could update him on what they had learned while visiting Scattered Creek. The fact that he'd asked about Valene rather than Anya or Seb caught her by surprise.

"He had to address some Seer Council concerns," she said.

"Yet he came to see you rather than summon you to see him?"

"I did something that triggered an alert that he had to acknowledge. He was explaining the details and giving me access to knowledge to implement those things properly."

"All the more reason for him to have had you go to him," Alex said.

"Alex," she said. "He was never here, he did not officially visit despite officially granting me access."

"But you just said he was here," Briana said.

"No," Alex said. "I understand. I won't ask unless you're allowed to explain further."

"I can't, at least not right now. Anya and Seb are allowed to know, but beyond that it deals with information even most Seers are never taught about."

"Like Anya's cousins?" Briana asked.

"Cousins? She's related to students at the hall? But that wouldn't be a secret unless…"

She saw Alex trail off, his eyes widening as he considered the implications of what he was saying.

"I'm not supposed to know enough to ask the question I want to ask. I'm pretty sure Velli didn't realize they had me read an un-redacted report calling them second rate Fae."

"I'm not sure they are, even if implications and hints are true. They don't Name anyone or anything," Fay said.

"Gwen isn't anything like what I've heard you say about the Velli though," Briana added.

"You mean she is? But how are you telling me this, how does Briana know this?"

Fay explained how Anya seemed to be a part of one of the Royal families, how a senior Royal had agreed that core Holding personnel who might need to deal with that heritage were allowed to know.

"Briana isn't a core member, she's not even a retainer," Alex said as she finished. "Not that I don't mean you aren't important."

"No," Briana said. "You're technically right, I'll just remember that when I'm making coffee tomorrow."

"Briana!" Alex said. "I'm sorry, I didn't-"

"One day, after that you're forgiven."

Fay thought about his words and frowned. How many times in the last few days had Briana's status as not part of the Holding come up? How often had she had to justify the fact that Briana deserved to know, should know, was safe to tell?

She wasn't a Retainer and Fay knew she wasn't looking to become one. Briana was here because of her, was in this life to be with her. She was part of the Holding because Fay was, part of everything because Fay was.

"Bri," she said, interrupting the playful banter. "I don't want to ask you to marry me-"

"What!? Fay-"

"Wait. I don't want to ask you that and ask you to wait for years for the marriage. I want Anya and Seb here for that and they won't be until school finishes. I want that, once we can have the ceremony. But I want you to be part of the Holding, part of my life officially."

"Fay…"

She could see Briana blinking back tears, see them gleaming in her eyes as she failed.

"I'll marry you today, tomorrow, in a century. I'll wait for everything to be right for both of us. And yes, I would want those horrible, horrible people who called me for you and then ran off to be there."

Fay felt Briana's arms wrap around her and realized she couldn't see through her own tears. She hadn't realized she'd started to cry, she'd been so focused on the words that it hadn't mattered.

"We'll still need to wait," she said. "They still need to approve-"

"No. They don't," Alex said.

She turned her head to look at him past Briana's shoulder.

"What do you mean? They're still the Holders."

"They approved it before they went to the Hall."

"That was a year ago," Fay said.

"Who won the bet?" Briana asked.

"Bri?"

"Seb," Alex said. "He said over a year but less than eighteen months. I think you missed by under a week."

"Bri? You bet on when I would."

Fay felt Briana kiss her forehead before answering.

"Fay. I love you. So much. I would have said yes a long time ago if you'd ever thought to ask. Everyone has been informally treating me as though you had asked for a few years."

"But…"

"I couldn't have asked you. And I wasn't going to pressure you."

"I'm done eating," Alex said before standing. "I'll leave you two some privacy. If there's any Holder related information I need to know, tell me tomorrow."

"Goodnight Alex," Briana said.

Fay stared at him in silence before looking back at Briana. She wasn't clueless, she was excellent at figuring things out, at finding details others had missed. But she'd needed Anya to call Briana after the convention, she'd-

"Next time I miss the obvious, tell me," she said.

"No," Briana said and smiled. "Your not realizing is part of what I love about you. It's who you are."

"Please? You were all placing bets on me," she said. "If I'd known I could have made sure you won."

"That's cheating. What would Anya say if she caught you."

"That I'm playing favorites? Which is true."

She could feel Briana chuckling at her words as she rested against her. She couldn't explain why it felt so good to hear the happiness in the sound, but she'd do whatever she could to make sure it lasted.

"So what did you do that Valene had to scold you about," Briana asked.

"Bri. I can't," she said. "I really can't."

"This is more secret than the Fae?"

"Different. The Fae are their own secret. This is a Seer secret."

"If you ever can, you will."

"I promise," she said.

Briana pulled away then stood, turning to walk away.

"Good enough. I think I'm ready for bed."

"It isn't that la-"

She kept her smile hidden. She wasn't completely clueless. But Briana's laughing explanation was worth pretending.
