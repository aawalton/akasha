The Gilded Fang had lost its spark. More than two weeks into the Great Hunt, the initial frenzy had soured. The air was thick with smoke, and a feeling of bitter doubt had settled over the room. The wild cheers for Arthur's first-day massacre were a distant memory, replaced by the grim clinking of coins over resigned bets. Nobles and guardians watched the enchanted hookah illusions with tired eyes, their gazes fixed on a leaderboard that was no longer a source of myth, but a tedious reflection of a slow, bloody grind.

At the center of the room, the mana-crystal display still glowed, but the names on it had become a source of frustration rather than excitement.

LEADERBOARD - DAY 17

The name at the top hadn't changed in over two weeks. "Arthur the Ghost," they called him now. A flash in the pan. A legend who had burned bright and vanished, leaving the rest to a slow, bloody crawl. The bets had shifted from wild speculation to grim mathematics.

"Rowler will pass him by the end of the week," a portly merchant grumbled, tapping his ledger. "I've got fifty gold on it. The boy is a machine. Consistent. Ruthless. Arthur was just a fluke."

"A fluke that soloed a hundred beasts in a day?" a sharp-eyed woman in Guardian leathers countered, swirling her drink. "No. He's waiting. Or he's injured. And what about Ysara? She was keeping pace with Rowler, and her team has the best healer in the Hunt. For her to just stop... something's wrong."

The merchant scoffed, waving a dismissive hand. "Whatever her reasons, she has fallen behind. That's what matters. Rowler, on the other hand, treats this like a war, clearing entire territories with brutal efficiency. That's not just aggression, it's dominance. That's why he's winning."

A tense silence fell over the table. The merchant's words, though arrogant, held a certain truth. Rowler's relentless pace was undeniable.

The tavern's bookie, a wiry man with eyes that missed nothing, scratched a quick note in his ledger. "Odds are shifting," he announced to no one in particular. "Rowler is now the favorite to win. Arthur's odds of finishing in the top spot are dropping by the hour."

A cheer went up from the corner where Baron Drelmor's men were drinking heavily, their laughter loud and grating. They were already celebrating their scion's inevitable victory.

Then, the leaderboard flickered.

A single, violent pulse of light washed over the room, making the enchanted smoke shimmer and die. Every head snapped toward the crystal.

The air went still.

The board refreshed.

And a line of blood-red light slashed through the second entry.

LEADERBOARD - DAY 17

For a full second, there was only silence. A thick, suffocating disbelief that pressed down on the room. Rowler Drelmor. Dead. The update was absolute, a final, brutal truth displayed in glowing runes that left no room for hope or doubt.

Then the dam broke.

"NO!" The merchant roared, slamming his tankard down so hard it cracked the wood. "IMPOSSIBLE!"

A chair scraped back violently as one of Drelmor's men shot to his feet, his face pale with shock and fury. "A trick! It's a system error!"

The tavern erupted. Shouts and curses flew across the room. A table was overturned, sending coins and half-empty mugs clattering to the floor. The bookie ducked behind the bar as a bottle shattered against the wall behind him.

"My wager! I've lost a fortune!"

"Who could have done it? A monster? Another team?"

"His party was handpicked by the Baron. For them to fail, for Rowler to fall... what in the hells could have done that?"

The mood had shifted from cynical boredom to raw, panicked chaos. Rowler wasn't just a top contender, he was the son of a powerful Baron. His death wasn't a simple loss on the leaderboard, it was a political earthquake. The implications were staggering.

Amid the uproar, a lone, ecstatic shriek cut through the noise. A scrawny man in gambler's robes, who had bet a long shot against Rowler, was dancing on his table, waving a fistful of betting slips. "I TOLD YOU! I TOLD YOU HE WAS OVERRATED!" he crowed, as the bookie reluctantly pushed a pile of gold in his direction.

His joy was short-lived, drowned out by the rising tide of paranoia.

The woman in Guardian leathers slammed her empty mug down, her face a mask of cold realization. "Think about it," she said, her voice cutting through the din. "First, Garran Drax and his entire team are eliminated a week ago. Then Princess Ysara, another top contender, stops hunting entirely for no reason. And now Rowler, who was about to take the top spot, is wiped out? This isn't a coincidence."

A new, darker rumor began to snake through the crowd, a whisper passed from table to table.

"What if... what if Arthur never stopped hunting?" a young noble murmured, his eyes wide. "What if he just got bored of the monsters?"

The implication hung in the air, heavy and poisonous. That Arthur, a ghost at the top of the leaderboard, had spent over two weeks not resting, but hunting a different kind of prey: the other participants.

The tavern split down the middle. One half erupted in outrage, calling him a cheat, a fraud who was manipulating the System. They claimed no solo hunter could take down entire elite teams.

The other half, however, embraced the chaos. The legends grew wilder, more ridiculous. Arthur wasn't just a prodigy, he was a specter summoned by the Hunt itself. He didn't use weapons, he used the shadows to devour his rivals whole. He wasn't hunting for points, he was culling the weak.

The bookie finally emerged from behind the bar, holding up his hands for silence.

"All bets on Arthur are now closed," he declared, his voice flat and final. "No more wagers for or against him. The house will not participate in this madness."

No one argued. Betting against Arthur now felt less like a gamble and more like a death wish. The rules of the Great Hunt had just been rewritten, and only one man seemed to know the new game.
