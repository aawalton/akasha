I sat there, staring at the unmoving corpse. Dark blood still oozed from the severed neck and spinal column. I’d finally extracted the scythe and stuck it into the ground beside me, red still dripping from the blade.

I’d never killed anything before—aside from, of course, various insects and the occasional apartment-dwelling mouse. This felt different. The weight of the act pressed on me like an invisible hand. But it attacked first, right? It was self-defense.

I expected to feel guilt. I didn’t. I expected to feel sorrow. I didn’t.

Was that… wrong?

The orange salamander thing trotted over from wherever it had gone to hide, its tiny feet squelching in the blood-soaked grass. It sniffed the corpse’s head, lifted a leg, and peed on one of its still open eyes.

“Way to kill the mood, buddy.”

My hand snapped to my mouth. Is that… my voice?

I hadn't noticed when I'd spoke in the heat of battle, but just like my body, my voice sounded nothing like the one I'd known the majority of my life.

The realization caught me off guard. I felt like a foreigner in my own body. Could I even claim I was myself at all. My looks were different, my voice, magical skills, and I'd just killed a living creature…

I shuddered, a memory crawling up from the dark depths of my mind that I didn't want to see. Maybe I was closer to my true self than ever. Maybe—

I shook my head. No. That was a long time ago. It was nothing.

But the little lizard was right. I was justified here. This creature was a raging monstrosity, not dissimilar to the one that maybe-killed me by the lake. And who knew what it did to my friends after I was gone. I hadn’t even considered that yet… The thought clawed into my chest, and I shuddered, trying to shove it to the back of my mind.

This beast didn’t deserve remorse, let alone guilt.

I still didn’t know anything about this place. Penumbra. If it was even real. What I was doing here. Answers to those questions first, then worry about the rest. I needed to find that angel being. He clearly orchestrated all this, or at least could explain what was going on.

The sound of someone slow-clapping pulled my focus away. Each hollow clap echoed across the hillside. The salamander scurried up to my shoulder and hid under a fold of my hood, its body trembling against my neck.

I turned to see a boy similar to my age sitting on a rock jutting out of a rise in the hill nearby. He also wore an orange metallic cloak, his hood down, revealing perfectly curled blonde hair. He stood, raising a pointed chin in my direction, then slung his scythe over the back of his neck and shoulders, resting his forearms on the shaft.

I didn’t know how long he’d been watching.

As he approached, I noticed something off about his right eye. Instead of white, the sclera was black, the iris a shimmering orange, almost glowing, unlike the normal blue iris of his left.

I brought a finger to my own eye—the same one that the skull-faced tormentor had injected.

Does mine look like that too?

“First kill?” he asked, his voice like a truck driving over cracked gravel, rough and resonant.

I nodded.

“You’ve got a slug or something on your shoulder.” He pointed to where the salamander peeked out.

“Yeah, it won’t leave me alone.”

“Adorable.” He walked closer, then twirled his scythe down and stuck it in the ground. The blade hissed as it bit into the earth, sparks of amber light briefly crackling across its edge.

“You’re the first person I’ve seen,” I admitted. “I only just got here. Wherever… here is.”

“Ah, well, then let me be your welcoming committee. Name’s Zeus.” He didn’t extend a hand.

“I’m M—.” My voice stopped working mid-word. “I’m M—” I tried again with the same result, my tongue locking like it'd been snared by an invisible hook.

What the…?

Zeus quirked his lips and chuckled. “You can’t talk about stuff from your other life here. Quirk of the System. You’ll have to assign yourself a new name. Took me a little bit to come up with mine as well. ’Til I discovered I could do this…” He extended his fingers and a crackle of electricity jumped between them, filling the air with the scent of ozone. “Then it became pretty obvious.”

That’s… Magic?

I nodded, trying to hide my awe. I had done that dodging sprint thing during the battle—without which I’d likely be dead. Could I juggle lightning too?

“You must have a million questions,” Zeus said.

“At least. What is this—”

“So, you gonna harvest that Will or what?” Zeus interrupted, not allowing me to actually ask any of my questions.

I looked down at the dead beast.

“Harvest… a what?”

“Right, no one’s taught you yet.” Zeus smirked. “Let me show you how it’s done.” He stepped up to the body, scythe now in hand. “It’s simple really. You know how to use your Second Sight?”

“You gotta stop with all these names, man. I literally have no idea what you’re talking about.”

He pointed to his dark eye and closed the other one. I mimicked the gesture.

A somber shadow bathed the world. I could still see everything, but it was like I was looking through a black veil. Colors muted, edges blurred. Only… there were things visible now that had previously been hidden, just like the glowing veins on the monster and skull floating over its head had only shown up after I accepted my scythe.

I looked at Zeus and text popped up again.

[Identity: Reaper

Rank: Amber]

Now, a churning ball of orange light, like a miniature sun, hovered in the center of Zeus’ chest, radiating heat I could almost feel.

Why the hell is everything around here orange? Oh, wait… Not orange. Amber. This all has something to do with that rank I’ve been assigned by… the System. That’s what Zeus called it, right?

Zeus pointed to the dead body beside us. “Now look there.” For some reason, I expected his voice to be muffled or distorted like my vision, but it came through perfectly normal.

The monster had a similar Amber sphere in its chest cavity, but this one appeared solid instead of gaseous, though whatever comprised it still swirled within, like it’d been contained. A symbol orbited around the sphere like a satellite. I looked closer, trying to discern what it was. A flame?

Well, I guess that makes sense, considering it almost burned me alive. But what does it mean?

“Watch and learn,” Zeus said.

He levered his foot against the body and rolled it onto its back. The carcass thudded, releasing a foul, meaty stench. Then, he slid his scythe through dead tissue, straight into the sternum, as if it were a knife into butter. When the point finally hit the dormant orb of light—a Will he’d called it—shining cracks shattered out across the enclosure originating at the scythe’s tip.

The orbiting flame symbol sped up until it was going so fast it looked like a ring around the orb. Then it vanished, resulting in the same symbol burned into the orb’s surface amid the glowing cracks.

For a moment, I worried the Will was reawakening and I was about to find myself face-to-face with a reincarnated monster. Instead, the shimmering in the cracks flowed across the surface of the orb and into Zeus’ scythe. The Amber light crackled and swirled along the blade and handle, giving it an aura like the beast once had.

Then the remaining husk of a Will inside the monster’s body disintegrated into ash and the aura around the scythe dimmed until it was barely visible.

The corpse began crumbling in on itself, as if it were aging thousands of years in seconds. Skin shriveled, bones blackened, until it mummified and curled into itself. At last, it was nothing more than dust scattered in the grass.

Its existence now trapped within Zeus' scythe.

He knelt down and picked up something that remained in the burnt grass, right where the Will had been. It looked like a small ceramic bowl, faintly glowing with residual heat.

Zeus clutched the object in his carapaced hand. It shrunk and the strange exoskeleton seemed to consume it. Then he stood and turned to me.

I stopped cold. A nervous chill ran up my spine, like when you’re alone at night and spot two feral eyes watching you in the dark.

I made contact with the feral eyes watching me now. And Zeus’ sly smile beneath them.

The salamander—still on my shoulder—huddled closer to my neck, shivering.

“It’s not personal, mate,” Zeus said, raising a hand that buzzed with electricity. “But I’m gonna be the fastest to ever reach the Spire. Thanks for the Will.”

He duped me. I killed that beast and he stole the Will right under my fucking nose. Clearly whatever was inside the Will was important here. I’d earned it. And I just watched him steal it. How could I be so stupid?

I raised my own hand to stop him, as if that would do anything.

A zap of lightning struck me in the chest. My whole body seized, the world flashing white as if my nerves had been turned inside out. I flew backwards and landed on my back. The salamander tumbled off into the grass with a frightened squeak.

I lay there groaning, the scent of charred flesh and fabric overpowering all else, smoke rising faintly from my chest.

Then my vision faded black.
