Tressa looked around in shock. How had he found her? They were getting closer to Falidorn, but not that close. Was it just bad luck? It seemed impossible that he would happen to show up here.

Aldmern faced down the minotaur and the statiki, arrow drawn back. All he would need to do was loosen his fingers.

“W-Wait! Stop!” Tressa tried to yell from the ground, Lisha and Briadon around her protectively.

“I’ll say again, put down your weapons,” Aldmern ordered in the common tongue. He stood there in his dark blue uniform, silver hair standing out in the night.

“Ha!” Briadon laughed, hefting up his club. “You think those weak little arrows can stop me? I’ll flatten at least half of you damn elves before that puny shit even slows me down!”

“I imagine a single arrow will suffice.” Aldmern responded calmly. “Or perhaps two. One for each eye.”

Briadon scoffed, clenching his teeth in a menacing smile. “Hope your aim’s good enough to back that up! Cuz I’m flattening you first, little elf!”

What were they saying? Tressa had no idea, but it didn’t sound like they were clearing up the misunderstanding. If anything, it seemed to be getting worse. She looked around in a panic, seeing more and more elves move into position just outside the firelight.

“Aldemern don’t! Don’t hurt them!” she yelled at him in Elvish.

“They are poaching in our lands, they’ve brought harm to you, and they refuse to lower their weapons!”

“They didn’t hurt me; they saved me!” Tressa insisted.

“Even if that is so…” Aldmern said, focusing his aim, then switching back to common. “I said drop your weapons, or we fire!”

“Hell no!” Briadon said. “Call off your damn archers first!”

Lisha’s eyes were in constant motion as she walked a slow half circle around Briadon’s back. Her tongue flicked as she spoke in a low voice. “This looks bad, Briadon.”

“Really? I almost didn’t notice.”

“Should we surrender?”

“Then we really will be defenseless, and I don't trust them not to shoot us anyway.”

“Yes. I feel the same,” Lisha agreed. She then raised her voice. “What do you want with this girl?”

“What I want,” Aldmern said, each word sharp and controlled, “is for you to drop your weapons and step away from Lady Tressa before I order my men to empty their quivers into you. We have thirty archers trained on you at this very moment. Now. DROP YOUR WEAPONS!”

Briadon spoke low once again. “Thirty, huh? What do you think, Lisha?”

“He may be bluffing.” Her tongue flicked. “I’m not sure I smell so many.”

“Either way… we might be able to take them.”

Tressa couldn’t understand all of their deliberation but thought this definitely didn’t seem like it was heading towards a peaceful resolution. "Ah—Lisha! Briadon! My Friends! Um, sharp down!” she tried telling them to just put their weapons down, but she didn’t have the right words.

They didn’t seem to pay her any mind anyway. They didn’t trust that they wouldn’t be attacked no matter what they did.

“Aldmern!” Tressa yelled in Elvish. “I said they didn’t hurt me!”

“But they’ve broken our laws anyway by being here.”

“No, they didn’t! I’m the one who led them here! They were trying to help me.”

“They’re poachers, Tressa. I guarantee they knew better when they came out here at all.”

“Y-You're wrong! They’re from the hunters guild!”

“Then they should know better! If they don’t surrender, we have no choice!” Aldmern shot back.

“If I get them to drop their weapons, will you stop!?” Tressa asked. “Give me your word!”

“If they lower their weapons and step away from you,” Aldmern said, his eyes still locked on Briadon, “Then no arrows will fly unless they attack first.”

“Promise me!”

His jaw tightened. “You have my word.”

Tressa sucked in a breath and looked up between Lisha and Briadon. “Lisha! Briadon! Uuuh, sharp down! No… no hurt! No break! Down. Down! Please, friends! Okay?”

Briadon didn’t even look at her, his massive hands only tightening on his club. “Not happening.”

“No arrow!” Tressa insisted. She pointed at Aldmern. “Ummm. Contract promise! Arrow not! Aldmern promise!”

Lisha finally asked, “Tressa, are you sure?”

“Yes! Friends!” Tressa insisted.

“As Tressa says,” Aldmern spoke up once again in Common. “If you drop your weapons, we will not fire. You have my word.”

Briadon snorted. “An elf’s word doesn’t mean shit to me.”

“Briadon,” Lisha said.

“What?”

“Tressa’s the one asking us.”

“Tressa’s not the one pointing arrows at us.”

“You're right,” Aldmern said. “I have no reason to make promises to one of you barbarians. But this promise is to my lady. I wouldn’t break my word to her.”

“Lady…” Briadon said under his breath.

“That’s the second time he said that…” Lisha muttered.

“Elf!” Briadon glanced down at Tressa. “Are you a damn princess!?”

“Uh?” Tressa blinked. “Sharp down?”

“Last warning!” Aldmern yelled over them.

Tressa pleaded one last time. “Sharp down! Friends! Please!”

“Briadon, I’m dropping mine,” Lisha said. “Even if we can beat them, which I’m not so sure of, we can’t afford the injuries.”

“Like hell I’m surrendering to some damn elves!” Briadon growled.

“Unfortunately, we don’t have a choice. We can only trust Tressa. But do what you will.” Lisha finally tossed her Halberd to the side.

“Lisha!” Briadon growled at her.

There was a shuffling amongst the archers as most who were aimed at Lisha turned their attention to Briadon instead.

“Don’t be a fool. We can’t fight this,” Lisha said once more.

Briadon gritted his teeth. Then, finally, with a heavy, furious breath, he dropped his club to the ground. Several elves gasped as it practically shook the ground.

“I swear, if they start shooting me, I’m at least dying on top of that bastard,” Briadon muttered under his breath, his glare never leaving Aldmern, who just smirked back.

A few elves quickly ran towards them, carefully picking Tressa up and guiding her away, despite her struggles as she looked around. Every single bowman still had their arrows nocked and pointed at her friends.

“Aldmern!” Tressa yelled. “Tell them to lower their arrows now!”

Lisha looked towards him as well. “We kept our side of the deal.”

But Aldemern nodded his head to the side. “Take ten paces to the right, away from your weapons, and get on the ground.”

“We had a deal!” Lisha hissed at him.

“I said we wouldn’t fire. But I still have questions for you.”

“You damn liar, you know what we meant,” Lisha spat at him.

“Fucking hate to say I told you so,” Briadon muttered under his breath.

But, seeing they really had no choice now, Lisha and Briadon did as he said, stepping away from their weapons and then lowering to their knees.

“Aldmern!” Tressa yelled once again. “What are you doing! You promised me!”

“Worry not, my lady,” Aldmern assured her. “We simply have a few questions for them. If they are truly from the hunters guild, and truly have made an honest mistake, then I see no reason we can’t let them on their way.”

“And if they’re not?” Tressa yelled.

“Then I don’t see why you would object to me upholding the law.”

“Because they saved me! Aldmern, I swear if you don’t release them this instant, you are going to regret it!”

He locked eyes with her. “I don’t believe you’re currently in any position to make such threats, my lady.”

Tressa’s mouth opened in shock at hearing those words from him. But as the elves helped her sit down and began checking her injuries, she looked around and realized something. They were not treating her like a criminal. Did that mean Aldmern didn’t sell her out about her rune crafting after all?

As for Lisha and Briadon, they had no choice but to sit on their knees and put their hands behind their backs as ordered. Two elves broke from the crowd, first pulling Lisha’s arms behind her back and binding them with rope. As for Briadon…

The giant bullman let out a snort as they realized they couldn’t get his hands to go very far behind his back, and as they began to tie rope around his wrist, they just stared, confused about how exactly they were supposed to go about this.

“Having a little trouble there?” Briadon grinned.

“Quiet. Damn minotaur,” one said with a thick accent.

Briadon’s grin widened. “Oh, so you do speak common. I thought it was just the pretty one over there,” he said, nodding over to Aldmern, who was finally coming towards them, bow lowered at his side.

“Just keep a few arrows on the minotaur while I question them,” he said. “If I decide we have to make this a formal arrest, we’ll figure out what to do from there.”

Lisha, already bound and kneeling, glared up at him, her tongue flicking past her lips. “If you don’t release us, you’ll be making an enemy of the hunter’s guild. We came here searching for Farron Moths and were fully within our rights to be doing so as requested by an official guild order.” She nodded towards Briadon. “You can find our contracts and tokens in Briadon’s satchel.”

Briadon began to reach for it, and the elves training their arrows on him tensed at his movement. “Whoa, now,” he grinned, wiggling his fingers. “You’re not afraid I’m going to pull a weapon out of this little bag, now are you? Sorry, but that’s not my style.”

“Just show me,” Aldmern ordered him.

Briadon pulled out a couple leatherbound booklets, one of which had their contract folded and stuffed inside.

As Aldmern began looking it over, his eyebrow raised so high it made Briadon laugh.

“What’s wrong? Like what you see?”

“You’re silver-ranked Hunter’s Guild members?” Aldmern asked.

“Damned right we are,” Briadon answered. “And we damn well earned it too.”

Lisha’s tongue flicked. “Now that you’ve seen it for yourself, release us.”

But Aldmern didn’t respond yet, instead scrutinizing the contract. “Even still, the Hunter’s Guild had no right to send you to our lands.”

“And as we said, and Tressa confirmed, we were not hunting your lands. The closest we came was many miles south of here, where we killed a Farron Moth, and we only even came that close because one of your own people, Tressa herself, guided us there,” Lisha explained. “Either way, if you take issue with our actions, it should be taken up with the Hunter’s Guild. We only did as our contract said.”

“And technically we didn’t even kill that moth. Tressa did it herself,” Briadon added.

Lisha perked at the realization. “Yes! That is correct! So you can not possibly call us poachers.”

Aldmern called over to Tressa in Elvish. “Tressa. Who killed the Farron moth?”

“I did,” she called back, pushing away the elf that tended to her leg as it was fine. She then grabbed her staff from another who’d retrieved it for her as she forced herself back to her feet, using it as a walking stick. “They saved my life when I was attacked by a Terragan Bird. I’d be dead if not for them. And so, I offered to help them finish their hunt, and they were going to be on their way in the morning. They even insisted on paying me a gold coin for my assistance, despite me attempting to turn it down.”

“What’s a Terragan Bird?” Aldmern asked.

“A large monster. I’d fallen, and my leg got pinned under a tree. It surely would have killed me, but these brave hunters showed up just in time. It was like a godsend. And now, you have my saviors bound as lowly thieves! So I’ll say it again, release them at once!” Tressa ordered, flexing her rank as a noble, which is something she’d rarely do. With so many witnesses, no matter how stubborn Aldmern wanted to be, he wouldn’t risk assuming authority beyond his station. He would be the one breaking the law at that point.

Finally, he bowed respectfully to Tressa and said, “Very well, my lady. I apologize. I just had to be sure of the situation first.”

“It’s alright, Aldmern,” she said.

And then Aldmern motioned to the guards as he handed the papers back to Briadon. “Lower your weapons and release them.”

Briadon scoffed, “About goddamn time.” He snatched their papers back and got up as they untied Lisha, then stomped over to his club, hefting it one-handed onto his shoulder and grabbing her halberd as well. “On my knees to a damn elf,” he muttered, “Fucking embarrassing.”

Lisha looked curiously at Tressa, who had guilt written all over her face.

“Sorry… friends…” Tressa said.

“It’s alright.” Lisha stepped over to her. Several elves tensed, but they stopped when Tressa smiled and let Lisha place a clawed hand on her shoulder. “Friends.”

Then, the statiki looked over to Aldmern and couldn’t help asking, “You keep calling her lady. Does that mean Tressa here is a princess?”

“Yeah, that’s what I want to know,” Briadon agreed, handing Lisha her halberd.

“Not quite,” Aldmern answered. “Tressa is the daughter of a high council member. Their power, I suppose, might be comparable to a duke in a human kingdom.”

“I see…” Lisha said, tongue flicking as she looked at Tressa in a new light.

“Can’t believe this crazy elf is a noble,” Briadon said, tugging on his beard braid.

Tressa grinned sheepishly, not sure what they were saying or why they were looking at her like that all of a sudden.

She turned to Aldmern and said, “Before we leave, I’d like to speak to them alone for a moment.”

“But Lady Tressa—” he began.

“I was not asking permission, Aldmern,” she said sternly, once again flexing her rank in a way she would rarely do. “These two have been dragged into enough trouble because of me. They saved my life, and I will thank them and apologize properly. Anything less would be beneath me, and beneath the Windshade name. Or would you have me disgrace my house by not showing proper appreciation to those who have done us a service?”

She added that last part, knowing it completely boxed him in. He would simply not be able to refuse her when put that way.

Aldmern tensed, giving her a look that said he knew exactly what she was doing. However, having no choice and with so many of the guard nearby, he bowed respectfully before her. “Of course, my lady.” He gestured to the others as he walked away. “Let us give Lady Windshade her privacy for a moment.”

Once they were alone Tressa grinned sheepishly at Lisha and Briadon, each staring at her like they were seeing her for the first time. Briadon stroked his braid. They had just watched the chaotic girl dressed in torn, mud-stained clothes, leaves in her hair and bandages and scratches all over her arms and legs give orders to a man who looked like a military official, then commanded his soldiers with a few brief words. It didn’t match at all. Nothing about Tressa looked like someone who should have that kind of authority. The way she grinned up at them only made the contrast sharper.

“I uh…” she made a pinching gesture with her fingers. “Sorry. Much sorry.”

Lisha and Briadon gave each other a side eye, Lisha’s tongue flicking past her lips.

Now that Tressa knew Aldmern hadn’t sold her out, and she didn’t have a jail cell waiting for her while she underwent a trial, Tressa knew what she wanted to do.

She had to complete the dragon’s trial and make a new spell. Whatever it is, it would have to be something truly significant. Something that her people didn’t already have. She did not believe she could achieve this under the strict rules and constantly keeping her research under wraps. She needed to leave and see the world.

She had thought of running away, but it didn’t sit right with her. She wanted to tell her parents her plan, and only hoped she could make them believe in her and leave with their blessing. But she wouldn’t just run without saying a word.

“Lisha… Briadon. I come.” Her eyes lowered as she let out a breath, pounded her chest and pointed. “I travel. Monster hunters. See Guild. See other city.”

“You’re saying you wish to come with us?” Lisha asked.

“Yes!”

“What do you think, Briadon?” Lisha asked.

Briadon huffed, stroking his braid. “I think it’s crazy. Considering we almost got executed by the elf princess’s personal army, I’d say we’d have to be completely out of our minds.”

Lisha cracked a toothy smile. “Is that not already the case? And she is not a princess. Try listening when others speak.”

He huffed. “Well, whether she wants to go or not, I doubt they’re just going to let her walk off with us at this point. And we need to rest. I still plan to camp here tonight so long as these elves are backing off.”

Tressa put up three fingers. “Three… uh…” she tried to remember the word. “Days! I come!” She pointed back. “Must say to mother and father. And I find.”

“So you’re going to ask for permission first?” Lisha asked.

“Yes.”

“We can’t hang around for three days waiting for her,” Briadon said. “We don’t have the resources, our bounties are due, I want to get paid, and frankly, the sooner we get out of this damn forest, the better.”

“No… you are correct,” Lisha agreed. “Let me into your bag.”

Briadon turned so Lisha could reach inside. She shuffled around a little, and then she pulled out a round token and showed it to Tressa.

“This is a token of the hunter’s guild. Specifically, it is one of our tokens.” She turned it over to show two silhouettes on the back that looked like a Statiki and a minotaur back to back. “Show this to anyone at the hunter’s guild and they’ll know that you got it from us. If you ask for us, you’ll be pointed in our direction. When we go on hunts there is always a paper trail left behind that the guild can follow, so if they don’t know where we are they’ll know of someone who does, or at least where we had been seen last.”

Tressa blinked as she took the token and turned it over looking at it. She didn’t have to understand Lisha’s words because she knew all about these. The merchants guild had it too and the one she used to talk to told her all about Guild Tokens.

“I can have?” Tressa asked. “I find you?”

Lisha nodded, tapping the token in Tressa's hand with a single claw. “If you want to find us. Show at Hunters Guild. Then find us.”

Tressa smiled. But, she had an even better idea than that. Her staff gave off a slight glow as she cast a tracking spell. Since this belonged to Lisha it would easily be able to find her.

Lisha tilted her head, not sure what Tressa just did, but then Aldmern called to her. “Lady Tressa, are you finished? The horses have arrived and I’d like to get you back to your parents as soon as possible.”

She let out a breath and responded in elvish, “Very well, I’m coming.”

“Goodbye now, Tressa,” Lisha said. “I’m happy to have met you.”

Briadon smirked. “Yeah, you’re not too bad for an elf.”

But before leaving for good, she looked back at the two monster hunters one last time. A mischievous smile spread on Tressa's lips as she pointed at her butt and then held up three fingers.

Lisha smirked, instantly knowing what she meant—three days.

She hobbled back to where a couple of horses waited, and as Aldmern helped her up onto one she asked, “So, how did you find me anyway?”

That is something she was very curious about, but figured it had to have just been bad luck. Maybe his branch of the search party just happened to spot their firelight. But a subtle expression crossed Aldmern’s face that grabbed Tressa’s attention.

He quickly covered it up with a smug grin and answered, “You know I’m the best tracker in all of Falidorn!”

Tressa blinked.

Yes, he was good, and he always was able to find her so quickly whenever he’d be sent to find her before.

The only problem is, no matter how good, he couldn’t have tracked her in the sky and not from so far away. And she had come from the complete opposite direction. What kind of trail could he possibly have picked up? Not even advanced tracking magic quite worked that way!

But before she could question him on it further, he jumped up behind her onto the horse. She sat between his arms as he grabbed the reins and called to his men, “Alright! Let’s get back!”
