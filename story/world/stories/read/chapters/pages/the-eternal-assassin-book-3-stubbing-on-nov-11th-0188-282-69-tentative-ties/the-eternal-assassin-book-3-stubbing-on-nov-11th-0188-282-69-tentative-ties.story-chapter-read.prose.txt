“Asher!” Brian shouted, laughing in surprise as Asher popped into existence right beside him. Brian and Samantha were back in their room in the palace, looking like they were getting ready for something. Brian was already wearing some form of small, child-sized suit, and Samantha was in the middle of tugging on a simple but beautiful black dress that looked fantastic on her. All but tackling him, the twelve-year-old grinned as he looked up at him. “Sam told me you were busy and wouldn’t make it!”

“Turns out I have a few free minutes before I need to head back,” Asher said, spotting Samantha beaming at him. “You don’t mind, do you?”

“Of course not,” Samantha scoffed, leaning over Brian and giving him a tender kiss. “I’m glad you were able to make it, even if only for a few minutes. Everything okay?”

“Yeah, the ceremony preparations are underway. I should get my shards and all that within the hour,” Asher confirmed.

“Does this mean now we can buy a castle?” Brian asked, still clinging to his waist. “I just think it would be cool to have a castle.”

“I think we’re all well aware of how much you want a castle by now,” Asher drawled, shaking his head as Samantha giggled. “But no, no castles just yet. Your sister and I are going to need a ridiculous number of shards soon enough, so these are going to be sitting pretty until then.”

“That’s boring,” Brian said, letting go of him as he put his hands on his hips. “You should have more fun! A few shards won’t hurt you when you have so many!”

“Brian, what have I told you about letting people make their own decisions with their own money?” Samantha asked, raising an eyebrow.

“Don’t bug them about it,” Brian sighed. “Anyway, Sam and I were just about to go watch a play. I’ve never actually seen a real play on a stage before, but apparently they’re doing a story about an archer, and I thought that sounded cool!”

“I thought it sounded cool too,” Samantha shrugged, blushing slightly. “We’re washing up before we head over. We were shooting targets earlier, and I figured showing up covered in sweat was a bad idea. I also thought it would be a fun excuse to dress up.”

“Probably for the best,” Asher chuckled. “Brian, would you mind giving me and Samantha a minute alone? I need to talk to her about something really quick.”

“Fine. But just a minute!” he said, giving them both a hard look. “…No kissing!”

“Brian!” Samantha snapped, shooting her brother in the face with a stream of cold water from her finger as he ran from the room screaming and laughing. Shaking her head, she sighed. “I swear, I remember being much more mature at his age than he is!”

“Girls tend to mature quicker than boys do,” Asher said, clearing his throat as he tried not to laugh and shift Samantha’s ire from her brother onto him. “Give it another few years.”

“I guess. Anyway, what did you need to talk about?” she asked, pulling her hair out of the way as she turned around to reveal her bare back. “Also, could you zip me up? I haven’t worn anything like this in a long time. It feels beyond weird to go from killing undead monsters one day to cleaning up and attending a play of all things the next. As if I’m living a double life like one of the characters in a book I used to read.”

“It’s the country of Nunait,” Asher said, carefully doing as asked and taking a moment to admire her figure as he tried to work out how to explain everything in only a few minutes. “Long story short, they want to establish something like an alliance with me. Apparently The Reaper is turning into a bigger deal than I originally thought. Hence the Shadow Squad that came after me. The Order of paladins in charge of Nunait is concerned that with the Deadlands gone, they might be attacked from the outside for a change, and they want powerful allies backing them to try and prevent that from happening.”

“I get that you’re something of a big deal, but you’re just one person,” Samantha said, turning to face him once more as she frowned. “Is The Reaper really that big a deal?”

“See, that’s exactly what I said,” Asher laughed. “Long story short, yeah, it sounds like he kind of is. So, what do you think? I didn’t want to make any big decision that would affect our entire team and possibly our mission without at least checking in with you first. Rulfar’s off sleeping somewhere so I can’t really ask him, and I guarantee you Moxy doesn’t care one way or another.”

“I guess my question is what exactly do we get out of this? This is an Order of paladins who have trained to fight against the Deadlands. Would they agree to bring the fight to the liches if we asked them to help?”

“I had the same thought,” Asher nodded, watching her as she slipped on some delicate shoes that were rather different from her usual footwear. They looked like they were more strap than shoe, and Asher gave her a curious look. “…I didn’t even know you had fancy shoes.”

“I don’t,” Samantha admitted, her face heating up slightly. “Or, I didn’t. While Brian and I were walking back up to the room to get ready, Donvath came out of nowhere and told me I should be ashamed of myself for wearing my usual shoes with such a dress. Then he shoved these into my hands and walked away.”

“Glad to see the High Prince of the entire kingdom is using his Time element wisely,” Asher said, shaking his head. “The most powerful person in this entire kingdom messing with time to fix your wardrobe issue aside, it sounds like we’re on the same wavelength regarding the alliance. I agree, with the condition that they help us attack the other liches when the time is right?”

“I think that’s a good idea,” Samantha confirmed, tapping her feet on the ground experimentally before giving him a shy smile. “So? How do I look?”

“Like I wish Brian hadn’t set a ‘no kissing’ rule,” Asher sighed dramatically, getting a giggle out of the hunter as she wrapped her arms around him and whispered in his ear.

“I can think of plenty of things we can do later that don’t involve kissing…”

Before Asher could respond, Samantha broke away, giving him a teasing wink as she grabbed both a quiver of arrows and her bow, slinging them over her back. The contrast between her fancy outfit and normal weaponry was enough to break Asher out of the spell her words had just put him under, and he laughed at the sight.

“Donvath didn’t tell you not to bring those as well?”

“I think he knew there wasn’t any point in trying,” Samantha admitted, giving him one last smile. “I’m about to see a play about an archer, after all. The rest of the audience will probably just think I’m a big fan!”

Asher stood on the edge of a raised platform in the center of the city square, doing his best to look stoic as the cluster of musicians off to the side finished playing what he could only assume was Nunait’s national anthem. The song was much more fiery and fast-paced than he would have expected, but he supposed that made sense for a nation literally designed to act as a shield against an undead threat. As soon as the music finished and the applause settled, Amanda turned to address the gathered crowd before them. She wore the same white armor as always, with the only addition being a bright, golden cape clasped around her neck.

“My people!” she began, literally beaming at the people as light radiated off of her, just below the level that would require one to look away. “I’m sure by now most of you have heard the news one way or another, but allow me to make the official declaration. After one thousand, five hundred, and thirty-one years of plaguing our lands with their undead forms, as of yesterday… The Deadlands are no more!”

The wall of noise that slammed into Asher was unlike anything he’d ever experienced, as thousands of people cheered as one at the top of their lungs. Tears flowed freely and people clung to one another in celebration, and Asher couldn’t help but wonder as he looked around at the sea of tired and excited faces.

How many of these people have stories similar to Amanda’s? How many of them lost loved ones to the Deadlands over the years?

The boisterous cheering and celebration actually carried on for a full five minutes without any signs of stopping, and eventually, Amanda had to raise a hand to get the crowd to calm down. The fact that such an action was all it took to slowly get everyone to focus back on her and wipe the tears from their eyes told Asher all he needed to know about how these people felt about Amanda as their leader. She had their rapt attention, and as far as he could tell, their full support.

“This goes without saying, but I am just as excited as the rest of you that the threat we have been fighting to contain all these years is no more,” Amanda said, smiling out at the crowd. “However, before we let the ale flow freely and lose ourselves to a night of celebration, it is important we honor the one who has made such a miracle a reality. Ladies and gentlemen, I would like to introduce you to the man standing before me. Whispers of his deeds have swept the lands over the past few months, and I am both proud and honored to reveal that he is not only the one who cleansed the Deadlands for us, but has agreed to an alliance with our people should we ever need to call on him again. He may work primarily in the dark, but his actions bring light to us all, just as our Order has strived to do since our very founding. I give you… The Reaper!”

Asher had expected a sea of gasps or looks of disbelief when Amanda finally got around to announcing his name. Because of that, he nearly recoiled at the second wave of cheers that was almost as loud as the first. As the people shouted and punched the air in excitement, Asher turned to raise an eyebrow at Amanda.

“…Not really the response I was expecting to get from your people at the announcement you’d thrown in with an infamous assassin.”

“You cleansed the Deadlands,” she snorted, as if he were being ridiculous. “You could have come up here drenched in blood and guts and these people would have viewed you as a hero. And not to shine my own sheath, but the simple fact that I am vouching for you as a friend goes a long way. I know I was the one to request this potential alliance, but it does go both ways. You want to become more than just an assassin known for slinking around in the shadows? Befriending the Falling Sun and wiping out the Deadlands was an excellent first step.”

“Good to know,” Asher said, giving an experimental wave to the crowd and flinching at the surge of noise he received in exchange.

“As promised, I would now like to officially give The Reaper the reward our people have slowly grown over the many centuries spent battling the undead menace,” Amanda said, holding out a hand and manifesting five glowing, violet orbs one after another. Each and every one of them was a perfect sphere, without a single flaw to be seen. Asher knew a million was the limit to how many shards could be shoved into one orb, and he nodded his thanks as he happily accepted each of the five spheres before pulling them into his core.

Shards: 9,723,676

“There will be many new trials and tribulations headed our way before long,” Amanda told the crowd once they’d finished cheering again. “This is an untold time for our people and our nation, but one we will get through together. Even so, that is a problem for tomorrow. Today is a cause for celebration! I will not keep you all here with boring speeches and pointless rabble. The sun has set and our lands are rid of undead for the first time in our history! We have prepared a feast and some entertainment, so please, before you head home, take at least a few minutes to celebrate with your neighbors and enjoy this sensation. We are free, now and forever!”
