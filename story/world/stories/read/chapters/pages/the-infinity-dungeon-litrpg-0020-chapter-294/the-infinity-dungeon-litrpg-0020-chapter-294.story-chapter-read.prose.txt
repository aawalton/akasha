Chapter 294

The airship hovered in the sky, a black shape above the wispy clouds. From there, the landscape below looked like a tropical paradise, a little corner of civilization between a lake, mountains and the sea. There was green all around, and a quaint dock had a few boats still floating on the muddy waters.

The idyllic sight was marred by a huge scar, upturned earth, rivers of dark red blood, bones jutting out of the ground, widespread destruction that had twisted and blended things together, remaking them in the unholy imagery of the Netherworld.

Michael jumped down, straight into the crater. The air was thick with miasma, PAC energy turning the sky into syrupy amber. The city of Mataoleo, a few clicks beyond the hills, was in upheaval. People were crying, screaming, throwing themselves into the dark polluted waters of the lake, where mounds of pulsing flesh were all that remained of their loved ones.

“What a mess,” he said as he landed.

Under his feet, a gravel pathway flanked on both sides by the dark waters had melted into reddish glass, sinking below the water level and spilling oily red blood in the lake. Operators were deploying shields, pushing the water away, revealing mud and rotten boats, but also bodies of men, women and devils. In the distance, the mangroves were wilting at a visible pace.

It was there that he saw Travis fiddling with a Domain emitter device. He flipped a switch, and the Domain connected to his spiral and began to suck the corruption away from the world, into the forever hungry maw of the black hole. The wilt stopped spreading before it reached the dome of the shield they had deployed, a few miles wide, cast at maximum range to make sure no rogue devils or pockets of PAC energy could escape.

“How’s the situation?” Michael asked.

“Dire,” Travis said. Around them, specialized operators were using a skill to talk to the locals in their language. “But under control. We managed to contain the corruption before it spread too far, and kill all the devils.”

Michael looked up, eyes unfocusing and refocusing. “You missed one. I marked it for you.”

Moments later, a squad had been dispatched to hunt down the devil. Travis gave the commands with practiced efficiency, triggering a Quest, and the devil was dead only minutes later.

Meanwhile, as the PAC retreated, the mounds of flesh kept alive by its corrupting power dissolved into rotting heaps of meat. The rivers of blood slowly dissolved, the domain breaking them down and taking all proof of the disaster away. The dead trees remained. The holes in the ground and the broken buildings remained.

“The veil is being repaired,” Michael confirmed. “I want you to leave the Domain here a bit longer, though. There’s a lot of pollution around. Might as well get rid of it.”

Travis nodded, and an operator quickly stepped up and began recording and taking pictures.

Even though to Michael's eyes they were all just operators, the System helped him see that this man was from the PR branch, here to carefully curate and shape the narrative following the event. Preparing the world.

The dead trees would be cut down, the buildings torn apart and rebuilt. It was going to take time but it would show the lengths Unity Corporation was willing to go to protect the planet. President Terris had been alerted and instructed on what to do.

“We also found the perpetrator,” Travis said, his voice a growl. “Dead, no shard on his body.”

“Reeks of Faith,” Michael said after he examined the man’s body.

He was a local. There were devil scales on his arms, and his blackened fingertips were in the process of morphing into claws. His pupils were yellow slits.

“He is one of the priests who did not respond to the pope’s summons.”

“Are we tracking them down?” Michael asked.

“We are trying, but we don’t have enough men.”

“I see.” In the distance, he noticed a peculiar stone formation. “David has been here. Did he leave already?”

“Off to chase more tears in the veil,” Travis said. “Again, Michael, we don’t have enough men to deal with everything that’s going on.”

Michael looked at the lake. The water was slowly getting clearer thanks to the Domain, and he could see in the distance where it spilled into the sea, fresh water mixing with salt water.

“Give the Origin System to more people, then.”

“How about the US military?” Travis proposed. “It’s about time we used them.”

Michael nodded. “Yes. And look around, Travis,” he said. “Some of these people have lost everything. Do you see the fire, the anger in their eyes? Let’s give them a way to fight back.”

Travis crossed his arms. His eyes took in the sights. There was a mother, holding a shard of bone thicker than a torso to her chest, cradling it like it was her child. It had been her child, before the devils came. Michael’s domain had not dissolved it, sparing it and a few other things so that people could mourn.

“It’s a lot of people, Michael. And unlike with the military, they are not trained.”

“You can train them. There are skills to ease the process. The Quests and their rewards will make the process smoother and faster as well. It’s time to use the System to the fullest.”

Travis frowned. “I was under the impression that we were still trying to save energy.”

“And this is what happened. It’s time to pull the stops. I’m going to the dungeon next, to power up so that I can keep up with all of this. I want the whole world to be under a Domain, Travis, the sooner the better.”

This made the man grin. “Now we are talking,” he said, patting Michael in the back with enough force to cause a sonic boom. Of course none of the two felt any pain, but heads turned in their direction. Travis met the confused gazes of the locals, and his grin widened, showing pearly white teeth. “Now we are talking,” he said again, muttering to himself.

David plopped down on the ergonomic chair, making the magically reinforced thing groan with strain due to his immense weight. Not only from his height, but his whole body’s density was increasing, starting from the muscles and bones, due to his prolonged usage of Blackstone.

Before him, holograms and flat projections created a half sphere of densely packed data, where he could see everything that happened around the globe. His eyes drifted toward the world map, colored in green, yellow and red. Most of the world was green–the veil strong enough not to be a source of concern–but patches were slowly becoming yellow, and red dots kept appearing all around. He had just been to one such red location with one of the teams, where they had managed to deploy a Domain before the veil broke down completely.

The most frustrating thing was that there wasn’t even a shred of PAC corruption there. The veil was simply… giving up.

Fucker, he thought angrily. He suppressed the urge to bang on the reinforced steel table, although small dents in it showed that he was not always successful in doing so. He thought about that little city in Indonesia, and the loud bang that resounded through the room told him that he had lost control.

Lifting his head up, above the screens, he saw the many stations of the other squad coordinators, a myriad of desks scattered about the immense empty underground space that looked more like a hangar than a bunker. Nobody had reacted to his outburst. Then he noticed the shield around him.

Icarus’s doing.

He felt the stone around him. Below his feet, several yards of reinforced concrete and steel. Same thing around and above. Then, pure compressed Blackstone–his handiwork. The only way in and out of here was a service tunnel, leading straight to Candle Light’s deepest parts. No portals, no space magic.

The reason was behind him.

He turned around, spinning in his chair. A whole third of the hangar was empty of people. In the middle of the empty space was a spherical shield, overloaded to the point it shone a bright electric blue and its light had to be dampened by another layer of shield. Inside were the seven shards in Unity’s possession. Their PAC emissions had spiked again recently, but that was not the reason they were here.

He had personally asked for the coordinators and the personnel to be made to work here. Travis had not agreed at the beginning, not until David had explained his reasoning.

The shards were a Damocles sword. Just slightly out of view, behind everyone’s back, not visible save for the faint humming sensation of immense magic keeping the shield active. Should the shield fail, be it because the shards overpowered it or because the Origin System supplying it with energy through the Domain collapsed, they would all immediately die.

The weight on their backs kept them focused, sweating, motivated. Perhaps even more so than the XP and the Quests and the gamification, although David was not a fool and knew of its power over the human mind. He himself was not immune to it, and was coming to enjoy it despite his attempt to resist it.

Travis had laughed and agreed to the idea immediately. After all, should the shield fail here, it would also fail at the Vatican. And should that happen, then nowhere in the world would be safe from the corruption. The devils that would come after wouldn’t even be a problem, everyone would be long dead.

Since it did not matter where the shards were, he was allowed to have them here, the motivator, the hanging sword. He even understood why Michael was not prioritizing these shards over the Vatican ones for disposal. It made sense, and truth be told, David was coming to like the sensation of his ass clenching every time his focus slipped and he once again heard and felt the hum of the shield. He liked the strange tingling in his back every time he realized what sort of atomic bomb he was sitting on.

The screens all turned red, beeping. Around him, the others were too.

WARNING: RIFT TEAR DETECTED

Already he could see the Origin System spinning up its gears, preparing to give Quests and whatnot.

“I’ll take it,” he said, sighing and already regretting it. He had just gotten back from one such mission.

“Daedalus,” he said in a low voice. “Tell me about the event.”

“Of course,” the AI said softly. David knew it was actually Icarus talking to him, but for the sake of everyone else who was stuck with the other, public persona called Daedalus, he kept up the pretense. “There was a sudden weakening and subsequent tear of the veil between worlds at these coordinates.”

The map updated.

“Shit,” David said. “It’s a city center. Is there any Netherworld energy?”

“None that we can detect,” not-Daedalus said. “I am, however, sensing a powerful mana signature. If you confirm that you will be the one handling it, I will create the Quest for you.”

David nodded, inhaling sharply. He looked around. Each squad coordinator was responsible for a team of highly trained men. Men who had braved the dungeon. Men who dealt with anomalies. Good men, motivated men.

Some of the men, he had chosen himself.

He did not trust them to do a good enough job. That was the fact of the matter.

Thus, given that Travis was out, still dealing with the aftermath of a PAC outbreak, it had to be him.

“I confirm,” he said.

He had plans for the afternoon. He wanted to spend time with Liff, who was in the Valley with Michael’s sister Maggie and that strange psychic girl Travis had sort of adopted. They were still dressing up the golems and having tea with them. Hardly normal for kids.

You know what? He thought as an idea came to mind. Fuck it.

“Daedalus, am I seeing this right? There’s no devils?”

“Indeed. There is no PAC corruption.”

“No monsters either? Just mana coming out of a rift in a guy’s basement?”

“Yes,” the AI replied, still acting like Daedalus. “You are seeing it right, David. The signature might indicate a dungeon entrance in the process of forming.”

Good, he thought. Let’s see if I can use this Origin System of ours to do the work for me, then. Just like Travis and Michael always say. Turn everyone into an operator.

“Daedalus, show me some information on the guy who owns the house. What’s his personality like?”

Data appeared on screen. Among the various lines, stats flattening a whole personality into traits and numbers, there was one that made him smile.

ORIGIN SYSTEM COMPATIBILITY: HIGH

“Prepare a portable Domain emitter and a wearable sub-Domain emitter, please. I’m going to pay him a visit.”
