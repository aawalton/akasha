Luke stepped into the room with a sense of trepidation, his eyes sweeping across the spacious interior. At its center stood a long table surrounded by high-backed chairs. Seated at its head was a broad figure garbed in a pristine white robe, a wide smile stretched across his face.

Ming Hu rose to his feet and opened his arms in welcome.“Lucas, I trust Melanie took good care of you in my absence?” he asked, throwing him a wink.

The implication was difficult to ignore, but Luke remained composed. He cupped his fist respectfully.“She was very attentive. Thank you.”

“Wonderful. Please, take a seat. Little Qin, Hilda—join us as well.”

Hilda nodded silently and moved to comply. Fairy Qin, however, still wore a furrowed brow, her confusion evident. Though she said nothing, she took the chair to her father’s left, her gaze resting on Luke with a faint frown.

After a moment, Ming Hu’s smile widened.“I must admit, I’ve been eager to hear more about this new product you wish to bring to market.”

“Of course,” Luke replied, offering a polite, if slightly strained, smile. The stares from both father and daughter weighed on him—though for entirely different reasons. It was... uncomfortable.

“But before we begin,” Luke continued, “how should I address you? Though we are business partners, this is our first meeting. I would hate to be presumptuous.”

At this, Fairy Qin straightened in her seat.“You will call him my lord,” she declared sharply. “Do not think yourself special just because my father allows you to conduct business in Ralis City.”

Silence fell like a blade. Tension rippled through the room. Luke arched a brow at the woman, puzzled by the intensity of her disdain. Whatever grudge she held, it seemed deeply personal—and entirely unprovoked.

Ming Hu’s smile froze. Slowly, he turned to his daughter.

“You may call me Ming, Lucas,” he said, voice deep and deliberate.

Qin flinched. Her eyes widened as she turned to her father, disbelief painted across her face. Clearly, that wasn’t the answer she’d expected.

“I apologize for my daughter’s tongue,” Ming said, still watching her. “I had assumed she would want to be part of this meeting, especially after enjoying your last product so thoroughly—but it seems I was mistaken.”

Without waiting for a reply, he turned to Hilda.“Take Little Qin to her room. We have business to discuss.”

Hilda stood at once, as if she had anticipated this outcome. Yet the moment wasn’t quite over.

“F-Father!” Qin cried, rising abruptly. “Why are you being so considerate to this—this outsider? He’s just a merchant’s son! We deal with his kind all the time!”

Ming’s expression darkened, thunderclouds gathering behind his calm facade.“Leave. Now,” he growled.

A flash of betrayal crossed her face. She let out a frustrated huff and stormed toward the door like a petulant child. For someone lauded as the Fairy of Ralis City, her tantrum felt oddly juvenile—but Luke couldn’t help the flicker of schadenfreude that warmed him.

As she brushed past him, he caught another trace of that familiar scent—Jade Mist—lingering in the air.

“You know,” Luke said off-handedly, sparing her a glance, “I would’ve thought you’d wear Silken Petals rather than Jade Mist. It would probably suit you better.”

He held her gaze. For a heartbeat, her frown lingered—then gave way to a look of dawning shock. He could practically see the cogs turning in her mind as realization struck.

She parted her lips, likely to speak, but was cut off by a thunderous voice.

“Leave us!”

Ming Hu’s command rang out like a whip crack.

Hilda immediately ushered Fairy Qin toward the door. As she passed Luke, the older woman cast him a brief, concerned glance before stepping out and pulling the door shut behind her. The resulting click echoed sharply in the sudden quiet.

A heavy sigh escaped Ming’s lips as he dragged a hand through his beard. In that moment, the fearsome figure transformed—no longer a gang leader or merchant lord, but simply a weary father. The shift was startling.

“I apologize for my daughter’s behavior, Lucas,” he said, his voice softer now, though still rich and resonant. “I’m sure she didn’t recognize you. At least... not until that final moment.”

Luke’s thoughts raced, trying to adjust to the abrupt change in tone. This gentler, more human side of Ming Hu was unexpected.

This is the leader of the Black Tiger Gang?

“It’s no matter,” Luke replied evenly. “I’m not so easily offended.”

Truthfully, he just wanted to move on. Ming Hu was dangerous—and the fewer dealings Luke had with him, the better.

“Good, good,” Ming nodded. His demeanor shifted again, sliding back into his earlier assertive persona. “Now then—tell me about this new product of yours.”

And just like that, they were back to business.

Luke launched into his pitch: soap—once again targeting the nobility. While the product had universal value, he lacked the infrastructure to mass-produce it, at least for now. In the future, he intended to offer it at affordable prices for the general population, but for the moment, nobles were the ideal market.

Ming Hu listened silently, fingers stroking his beard, his expression carved from stone. If he had thoughts, they were unreadable. But Luke pressed on. This product—like perfume—was both practical and desirable. Cleanliness in this world was poor, and poor hygiene led to disease, even death. Soap was more than a luxury—it was a necessity.

Whether it was Luke’s confident tone or the success of his previous product, the large man eventually nodded.

“Bring me your first batch,” Ming said finally. “I’ll have my women test it before deciding whether to bring it to market.”

He leaned forward, resting his elbows on the table, the corners of his lips tugging into a grin.

“We already have scented baths. I don’t quite see how this soap of yours is as lucrative as you claim. As for profits... how about 30–70?”

Luke nearly cursed aloud but bit it back. Instead, he returned a tight smile.

“I apologize, Sir Ming, but I won’t accept less than fifty percent,” he said, voice calm and measured. “These two products are only the beginning of my innovations. If we’re to remain partners, we should build goodwill—not breed resentment.”

Though Luke’s words remained polite, the underlying message was clear:If no agreement was reached, he wouldn’t hesitate to walk away.Or at least, that was the implied threat.

Ming studied him in silence, his grin slowly fading. A few tense heartbeats passed before the man suddenly threw his head back and laughed—deep, resonant laughter that shook his broad chest.

It went on longer than Luke expected.

Finally, Ming wiped a tear from the corner of his eye, still chuckling.“Sorry, sorry,” he said, regaining his composure. “You’re absolutely right, Lucas. I shouldn’t prioritize short-term gains over what could become a long and prosperous partnership.”

Then, his smile twisted—something darker flickering in his eyes.“But you see, I am like a beast. Whenever I smell weakness, I pounce—tearing out the throats of my prey.”

He let the words hang in the air, idly inspecting his well-manicured nails.“Those nobles… they hide behind sophistry and status. Many have come to me, alone, believing that their titles grant them immunity. That their names, their bloodlines, mean something. That they are untouchable.”

A chill crept down Luke’s spine. The hairs on his neck stood on end.

“But here’s the thing about beasts, dear Lucas,” Ming continued, his voice unnervingly casual. “They don’t waste time planning their next meal. Either they’re too stupid to care… or confident that they can hunt anything—and anyone—who steps into their territory.”

His eyes locked onto Luke’s, and the silence that followed was suffocating. A heavy pressure settled over the room, making Luke feel as if he were prey under a predator’s gaze.

He fought the instinct to fidget.

The message was clear.This city—this market—belonged to Ming.Anyone who dared to sell without his blessing would face one outcome.

Death.

“I am that beast, Lucas,” Ming said evenly, the threat laced in velvet. “Don’t fool yourself into thinking you’re indispensable. Even without your little products, I will continue to rule within these walls—until the day another beast rips the crown from my head.”

A grin spread across his lips again, this one far more sinister.“But that day won’t come. Not for a long time.”

Luke swallowed the dread rising in his throat.Hilda was right… this guy is bad news.

Still, he nodded, forcing a respectful expression to his face.

“Indeed. That’s exactly why I came to you, Ming,” he said, voice calm but deferential. “I wouldn’t dare to peddle my wares without first consulting you.”

He paused, then added with subtle care,“While you may be a powerful beast, I am but an eagle—passing overhead. If we cannot strike a deal, I’m content to fly elsewhere. But I give you my word: I will not challenge your rule, nor will I sell my products within your domain without your blessing.”

Luke cupped his fist and offered a slight bow.He had no illusions about who held the power in this room.

This wasn’t about profits anymore.It was about survival.

The tension in the room crept higher, teetering on the edge of detonation. For a brief moment, Luke found himself paralyzed, his breath caught in his chest, as Ming’s presence seemed to swell—an invisible force that loomed larger than life, threatening to consume the room entirely.

Not good.

Just as it felt like the man might lunge, a sudden, frantic rap at the door shattered the silence. The sound rang out like a gunshot.

Both men turned toward it, expressions starkly different.Luke’s eyes narrowed. Ming’s eyes flared.

“We’re busy,” Ming growled, voice low and dangerous.

Despite the warning, the door creaked open. A pale-faced woman stood in the threshold, her expression stricken with panic. For a heartbeat, Luke feared Ming would leap across the room and crush her where she stood. But instead, something shifted in the man.

His fury vanished in an instant.

“What is it? Is Qin okay!?” he demanded, leaping to his feet.

The woman hesitated, glancing nervously at Luke, clearly reluctant to speak in front of him.

“Tell me!” Ming thundered, gripping her shoulders with both hands. The sheer size of him compared to her was alarming—it looked as though he could snap her like a twig with the barest effort.

“T-The Second Prince…” she stammered, nearly collapsing under the weight of the moment.

Luke’s blood ran cold.The Second Prince? Had they found him? Was he discovered downstairs?

If that were the case, the entire plan could unravel in an instant.

Ming’s eyes burned with fear—but when she didn’t mention Qin, he seemed to calm slightly. Still, he pressed her, voice low and urgent.

“Well?! Out with it!”

“He’s… dead.”

Silence.

The word struck the room like lightning.

Ming froze, blinking in stunned disbelief. His grip loosened, hands falling limply to his sides. It was as though the weight of the news knocked the wind from him. His shoulders sagged.

Luke exhaled a breath he hadn’t realized he was holding.So it wasn’t about the plan. Not yet.The misinformation about the returning army and the supposed deaths—his and the Second Prince’s—would take time to circulate. But Ming, with his reach, had clearly caught wind of it early.

Still, the worst seemed avoided—for now.

And then, pain.

A sharp, violent snap struck Luke’s cheek, and the world turned sideways.

One moment he had been resting his elbows on the table.The next, he was face-down on the wooden floor, ears ringing, eyes dazed.

What…?

His vision swam as he looked up—and saw her.

The same pale-faced woman who had brought the message now lay twisted on the ground in front of him, her eyes wide in eternal shock. Broken pieces of the once sturdy table lay strewn on the floor.

Luke blinked again, struggling to process it.

And then he saw it.

The sunken side of her skull. The bright red smear of blood.Her neck was bent at an impossible angle—wrenched violently to the side, though the skin still clung desperately to the torn structure beneath.

His stomach flipped. A wave of nausea surged up his throat, bile rising.

“FUCK!” Ming roared.

He brought his massive foot down on the woman’s leg.A sickening crunch echoed through the chamber.Then another.And another.

“MOTHERFUCKER!” he howled, stomping again and again.

Luke scrambled upright in a daze, his hands slick with sweat, mind racing.

The man was beyond reason now—a frenzied beast released from its cage.

He turned to the still-open door.

I have to get out of here.

He didn’t know if Ming would turn on him next, but he wasn’t about to stay and find out.
