I didn’t read much, did I?

Now that I had perceived all the information one of my threads had gone through, I couldn’t help but feel disappointed. Apparently, I knew next to nothing about warfare. Most of what I had came from fantasy novels and shows, and unsurprisingly, none of it held any real value here. Even in a world with magic, knowing that something like a sun ray spell or a cannon that shot magic existed meant nothing without understanding the logic behind it.

Without that knowledge, almost everything I had once found impressive was reduced to nothing more than imagination.

Still, not everything was useless.

I did remember a few practical things. The simplest among them was a pulley.

The velmoryns could make use of the massive trees nearby, and with something as basic as a pulley system, they could significantly reduce the manpower needed to move materials while expanding Eriavel. The settlement was far from complete, and anything that improved efficiency had value.

But no pulley would help in the coming battle.

What would help were traps. Traps, and the way they could be combined with Amon’s ideas.

The outline of a plan began to take shape. If I properly prepared a section of the forest that Noah’s forces would have no choice but to pass through, I could control the battlefield. That alone would give me an advantage, a big one at that. Fighting closer to Eriavel also meant Orrvyn could intervene if needed.

If I make the velmoryns start digging a new channel from the lake and create a dam there, maybe I can even use that to my advantage and flood Noah’s forces…

As the plan expanded, another problem surfaced.

As I planned the traps, I realized that if Noah’s Window could see inside the forest, or if one of the demons managed to spot what I was preparing, it would all fall apart—and perhaps even be turned against me.

Guidance.

[God’s Sight]

Every god possesses the ability to perceive the mortal realm. The extent of this vision depends on their influence and Radiance.

No deity may pierce another god’s dominion with their sight. However, they may observe through their followers, seeing the world through borrowed eyes.

The region surrounding Eriavel, guarded by your first Guardian, falls under your dominion. No god with inferior Radiance than yours may observe it directly.

The rest of the forest remains outside your full control. Until the neutral creatures within it accept your mark or resettle under your influence, it will remain a neutral territory.

I need to convert velthwyn…

I was already used to Guidance subtly nudging me in certain directions, and I was fine with it. I was fine with it as long as its suggestions remained beneficial to me.

Relieved that Noah couldn’t see what I was preparing in Eriavel, I considered a few new weapons the velmoryns could use against the Night God’s army.

Avenor’s bomb idea was completely fine, but not only could the projectiles be blocked, some creatures - like the ones the Night God had unleashed in the nest of the spider mutants - were simply immune to physical damage. However, they were weak to magical damage.

I needed to be prepared for every scenario.

Karla gave some great information on light magic, but creating explosives that trigger spells is beyond my understanding of magic.

Noah was called the Night God for a reason. All his subordinates I had seen so far resembled goths, and their summons were all dark as charcoal. Even his divinity was as dark as night.

So to counter that, I would need to use the light element. But the problem was, none of my velmoryns knew anything about light magic, and I was almost certain they didn’t have an affinity for it either.

I am overthinking this. First I should focus on what I can do…

Refocusing, I decided that setting up traps that would trigger Amon’s bombs would be quite effective. However, just that one type of explosive wasn’t enough. To make the attack more lethal, I would need to hide another attack beneath that one.

The projectiles I had in mind would unleash poisonous gas.

If we mixed the two types of explosives, the enemy would think that Amon’s projectiles were the only attack they had triggered, while in reality, they would be inhaling poison.

Creating such explosives was definitely possible. The forest was filled with extremely poisonous beasts and plants. All the velmoryns would need to do was create a liquid that turned into a toxic cloud when triggered.

Maybe when heated…

Another thing I considered was crossbows. If I gave them to mages, they would remain useful even while waiting for mana potions to restore their spent reserves…

But as I went through all those theories, I realized the biggest issue I was facing. I had no way to implement them. Was I truly supposed to teach velmoryns how to create those explosives myself? Even if I did, what about the dam? Or the traps?

It’s the least godly thing to do. But what choice do I have?

Just as I was beginning to accept that I might actually have to lead the velmoryns myself during this war, I realized there was a far simpler solution. I could choose one of them and teach them how to make explosives, even a crossbow, through a vision. I would not even need to tell them it had come from me.

Hadn’t artists, engineers, and plenty of inventors claimed their best ideas came to them in their sleep? Why could the same not happen to my velmoryns?

I’ll wait to see who the council chooses as leader, and if they prove competent enough, I’ll teach them about traps and the dam…

“Verde,” Amon’s voice rang in my mind. “What should I do with this guy?”

Whenever he called me by name, it felt like someone was shouting directly into my ear, the exact opposite of my believers’ prayers, which sounded like a soft whisper.

I abandoned my plan to look for the next velmoryn Da Vinci and instead shifted my Window to Amon.

He was inside prison cell with two other velmoryns. Lucas stood in front of the one who had nearly killed him. The man was chained to the floor, though unlike Eralon, the restraints were long enough to let him move around, and someone had even given him a mat.

“Hey, the tension in here is a bit much for me. Could you calm down?” Amon frowned and stepped in front of Lucas, cutting off the murderous stare he had fixed on the prisoner.

“Be grateful High Father is merciful,” Lucas hissed, then turned and left the cell.

“Don’t leave me alone!” Amon shouted, but it was already too late. He clicked his tongue and looked back at the chained velmoryn. “Damn it. First they tell me to make sure Lucas doesn’t kill you, then they leave me here alone. This is my last day in Eriavel. I wanted to enjoy it and learn more magic. Is that too much to ask? Do you even understand me?”

The velmoryn took a big sniff.

“How can a human be a velmoryn?” he asked.

“Huh?”

“I can smell your blood. I know you are half velmoryn, but how? We cannot reproduce with humans.”

“Maybe that’s because you’re too small down there,” Amon said with a smirk. “Why did you attack Lucas? All he did was save your ungrateful ass.”

“You speak strangely. No velmoryn talks that way. Nor does any human.” The man’s face remained completely neutral, even when Amon was deliberately mocking him.

“Because I’m different. Was that not obvious?” Amon spread his hands. “Now why did you attack Lucas? I answered your questions. It’s only polite that you answer mine.”

“If you are going to kill me, then kill me. I have nothing to say, nor do I intend to resist.”

“You are a velmoryn. And you were shown mercy by your Lord. Don’t you have anything to say?” Amon stared at him for a while, then sighed and leaned forward.

“If the hard way is what you wish, then the hard way it will be!” Amon sneered and pulled a wand from his sleeve. The small smile vanished and his face twisted, anger written all over it.

“Listen. I know you are terrified of the angel, and that is why you have not used your powers. I know those chains cannot hold you. But guess what. This place is full of people who can erase you from existence.”

The wand began to shimmer red as he poured divinity into it.

“Now you will learn what it means to defy Father’s will.”

The moment the words left Amon’s mouth, he waved the wand, and his divinity surged forward, covering the velmoryn’s head.

The man collapsed onto his side. He twisted on the floor, chains rattling wildly as he clutched his head with both hands, trying to squeeze it.

“Three,” Amon said, calmly watching the writhing velmoryn.

The man groaned through his gritted teeth, holding in the pain.

“T-ttwooo.” He drew the word out slowly, very slowly.

The velmoryn began screaming, curling in on himself, his knees reaching his head.

“One and a half.” Amon tilted his head as he noticed blood running from the velmoryn’s nose.

The prisoner began banging his head against the stone floor.

“One and a quarter,” Amon said, crouching beside him.

He watched as the man stopped hitting his head, convulsing.

“One and one tenth.”

More screaming, more pain.

“One and…”

“Please…” the velmoryn cried out, cutting him off.

The red shimmer vanished. The screaming stopped and turned into groans, but the man still forced himself upright and leaned against the cold wall.

“I cannot sell my soul. Not until I receive the promise I seek,” he hissed, glaring at Amon with eyes full of hatred. For the first time, the emotion on his face was genuine.

But genuine was not what Amon wanted.

He began channeling his divinity again, yet before he could cast the spell, I stopped him.

“Enough. Bring him to me.”
