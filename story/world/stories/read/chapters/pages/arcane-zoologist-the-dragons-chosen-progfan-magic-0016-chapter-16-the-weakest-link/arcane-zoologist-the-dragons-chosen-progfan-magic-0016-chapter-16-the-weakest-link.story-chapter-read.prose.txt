With everyone relieved at Mom's recovery, it was time to return to their daily activities.

"I'm heading to the smithy. The work must have piled high while I was gone. Callie, after you're done here, you come straight to the smithy," Dad said.

"Why is Callie going there?" Mom asked, glancing suspiciously at Callie.

Callie shrank back. Right. Mom didn't yet know about the whole incident with Aurelia and the punishment she received.

"I trust you can tell your Mom what happened and why you are being punished while you're here," Dad looked at Callie sternly.

"I-I can," she replied in a shaky voice.

"Good. Callen, you're with me. I have to stop by Mister Blackthorn and tell him to give the girls a special lesson."

Callen nodded and threw a relieved glance at Mom before leaving. It was just Granny Ethel, Callie, and Mom now.

"So what was that about punishment?" Mom looked at Callie with a stern gaze.

"It wasn't Callie's fault, Maeve. No need to look so harsh," Granny Ethel said, urging Mom to lie down.

Mom lay down reluctantly, all while keeping her gaze on Callie. "Then why is she being punished?"

"Because this kind-hearted child told a lie to protect another life. Not the best approach to the situation, but her heart was in the right place. She'll do better next time. This village as a whole could do a bit of learning. Everyone is so jumpy. One mention of arcane beasts and you all reach for your swords."

"This isn't a joke, Ethel. I nearly lost my arm because of one. If it's something dangerous, it needs to—"

"Oh, hush! This attitude is the reason Callie was forced to lie in the first place. Assess the situation rather than try to solve everything with force," Granny Ethel said, gently pushing Mom down and covering her with a linen blanket. "We're not talking about glauxis here, although it coming here is a strange sign."

"Why is it strange?" Callie jumped eagerly at the chance to change the topic.

"Because glauxis live further north, where snow rarely melts. Their fur is pristine white, specifically to blend in with the snowy scenery. They can appear in these parts during winter, but one appearing here during summer is unheard of," Granny Ethel explained.

"It's certainly unusual. But figuring out why it came here won't help us solve the issue. I'm hoping that after our ambush, it'll feel threatened enough to leave, but if it doesn't… I'm afraid we'll have to call for, I don't believe I'm saying this, but we might have to call the dragonbloods."

"Don't you say such a thing! Calling dragonbloods to our village," the elderly woman tapped the wooden table with her knuckles three times. To ward off bad luck. "Shouldn't you consider the rangers first?"

"I don't like it any more than you do, Ethel. But a monster that strong could be a serious threat. I just narrowly escaped with my life. I can't risk putting my children in such danger. I wish we could have rangers dealing with this. In fact, one of our hunters did send them a message, but their numbers are few. Unless a group happens to be nearby Greyfen, we might be waiting for months until they show up," Mom said and let out a groan of pain.

"There, there. Rest for now. No need for rash decisions. Callie, bring riaru tincture. She needs to rest," Granny Ethel instructed.

Callie nodded and hurried away to the medicine room. As she bustled about, searching for the riaru tincture among the many other tinctures placed on the shelf, Callie couldn't help but think about the glauxis. She desperately hoped that it was just lost and would soon head home.

But what if it didn't? What if it came closer to the village? What if Mom called the dragonbloods? What if they somehow found out that Callie had the dragon's mark? She would be taken away. Not to mention that they would undoubtedly kill the glauxis.

If it comes closer to the village, then I'll have to search for it. Maybe I can talk to Glauxis just like I can talk to Wiggles and Snowglow and understand why it's coming here. Maybe if I explained the situation to it, the glauxis would leave.

The healer's hut didn't stay quiet for long. Other hunters who had gone with Mom but hadn't sustained such horrible injuries came back to get their injuries cleaned and rebandaged. Callie had to run around, fetching Granny Ethel things and boiling the old bandages so they could be used again.

After her work with Granny Ethel was finished for the day, sometime in the late afternoon, Callie hurried over to the smithy to help Dad. She felt completely out of place in this stone building filled with metal, tools, and a burning hot furnace.

It was so hot in there! Callie couldn't stand it. The urge to take off her tunic to walk around in just her undershirt was intense. But she couldn't. Dad would see her dragon's mark then, and he already looked tense whenever Callie appeared in his view.

Frankly, Callie didn't understand how Dad could stand this. All she did was carry and clean the tools, but all the heat and smoke were steadily getting to her head. It didn't take long for her to feel nauseous and dizzy.

"Callie, are you alright?" Dad asked with a worried expression.

Callie realized that at some point, she had slumped down to the ground feeling very faint.

"I… I don't think so…" she admitted.

"Oh… Damn! Maeve will kill me for this," Dad muttered and picked Callie up. "Let's head home!"

Thankfully, it wasn't anything serious. After drinking a lot of cold water from the well and washing up, Callie felt much better. To her relief, Dad announced that she wouldn't have to work at the smithy anymore; instead, she would have to focus on her chores at home.

The next day, when Dad explained what had happened to Callie in the smithy, he got an earful from Mom. Apparently, she didn't think Dad would actually make her work there, saying it was madness having a young girl do any work next to the furnace in the middle of summer.

Chastised, Dad slunk away to work while Callen hurried to Mister Blackthorn, doing his own apprenticeship under the man. For Callie, it was yet another busy day, with them preparing more tinctures and bandages, as well as the ointment for Aurelia and Gerda. However, Callie prayed that the time would stop.

She didn't want to go to her survival lessons. Well, she did, but also didn't. She wanted to be given permission to leave the village. But she didn't want to see Mister Blackthorn or the village girls. There was no way they would look at her favorably after what happened. Not that they ever did.

Nonetheless, Granny Ethel dismissed her ahead of time, so Callie could head to her survival lessons. Tristan followed her with an envious gaze, but didn't dare to say anything in front of Mom.

Arriving at the training grounds, Callie was met with intense glares from all the village girls. If looks could kill, then Callie would already be dead from Aurelia's stare alone. Thankfully, with Mister Blackthorn present, nobody dared to say anything to Callie.

"Calluna has arrived. Good. We shall begin. Today we'll have a special lesson. It appears that the children in this village had lived peacefully for far too long and have forgotten that nature is not to be trifled with," Mister Blackthorn said, and the village girls once again turned their gazes to Callie.

She felt herself shrinking under their intensity. Did the whole village now think that Callie had gone to the Deep Forest? It was better than them knowing about her encounter with Snowglow, but it was still far from pleasant.

"This isn't just Calluna and her brother I'm talking about. This whole new generation is far too careless, wandering the forest like it's their backyard. Especially now. It has been confirmed by our hunters that a glauxis has been found prowling near the village. For the next few lessons we're going to learn how to traverse the forest and avoid monsters such as glauxis."

Aurelia's hand shot up. "What kind of monster is a glauxis?"

Although Callie hated the term monster, she was definitely curious about what glauxis looked like. All she knew was that it had white fur and that it resembled a lynx in some way. It was actually exciting to find out more about it.

"It's a predator, large and white. You'll recognize it the moment you see it. There's nothing else like it. Its claws secrete a deadly poison and it hides up in the trees, silently waiting and watching its prey," Mister Blackthorn said in a grave voice. His hand had shakily gone down to his hip, grasping at something. Only after a moment did the man seem to come to his senses and clench his fists.

"You're going to be put in pairs and work your way together through an obstacle course. Aurelia, you're going together with Calluna."

"NO!" Callie whined.

"No way! She's only going to hold me back," Aurelai shouted, then glared at Callie.

Mister Blackthorn glared at Aurelia, making all the girls flinch back from his horrifying expression. "In the wild, the group is only as strong as its weakest link. As the most experienced student, it is your duty to teach and protect your juniors. If Calluna fails, then it'll be your fault."

"Not fair…" Aurelia muttered angrily, but not loud enough for Mister Blackthorn to hear. When the stern man turned to the other girls, Aurelia glared at Callie. Well, even more than she had before. "If you drag me down, I'll make sure that you're never passing Mister Blackthorn's final test!"

Callie couldn't muster an answer. What even was this test? Thankfully, Aurelia didn't persist, just turning away from her with a derisive snort. She exhaled, but inwardly couldn't help but also be angry with Mister Blackthorn. Why pair her up with Aurelia of all people? Better yet, why even pair them up at all? She would have preferred to work alone.

"Teacher, I have prepared the obstacle course," Callen, along with a few other older boys, came running into the training grounds, covered from head to toe in mud.

"Brother, what happened to you?" Callie couldn't help blurting out.

Callen gave a reassuring grin. "It's just a bit of dirt. Don't worry."

"Good. You're coming with us, too. I don't trust myself to be able to help should something happen…" the man said, his hand inadvertently clutching at his leg, where his old injury was.

"Where are we going, Mister Blackthorn?" Aurelia asked in a pleasant tone, all of her previous anger gone. Was she batting her eyelashes at Callen? Bleh!

"The obstacle course, obviously. Make haste. We don't have all day," Mister Blackthorn said.

Callen led the way, and Callie followed after him, sticking close to her brother. Sadly, she couldn't talk with him to ask what this obstacle course was, since Aurelia was sticking annoyingly close to them. Callie wasn't sure what she disliked more—Aurelia glaring at her or making those honey eyes at Callen.

After walking a good distance through the forest, in the area beyond the training grounds, Callie noticed fewer bushes and more moss on the ground. Before long, mud appeared, and she realized that they had entered the swampy part of the forest. It was also eerily quiet. Birds were quiet and Callie felt like she was being watched.

No, that has to be my imagination. Right?

Among the trees, there was a trail marked out with branches set out in deliberate lines, carved markings on tree trunks, and deliberately-placed rocks. The worst part, however, was that the laid-out trail clearly led into the swamp.

"Mister Blackthorn… Is the obstacle course… in the swamp?" Callie raised her hand feebly and asked.

"Very observant, Calluna. You are correct," Mister Blackthorn confirmed.

All of the girls gasped.

"But that's dangerous!" Gerda objected.

"Hence why I have Callen here on the standby in case anything happens. This swamp over here is nothing more than a baby swamp when compared to the monster lurking in Deep Forest. Your goal today is to make it through the course and back without requiring assistance from the boys, losing your partner, or being marked."

"Being marked?" Aurelia cocked her head.

As a demonstration, Callen picked up a piece of moss dipped in mud. "Being hit with one of these."

"The boys will be throwing these as you proceed through the course. They will only throw them if you do something that would attract the attention of a monster. Think carefully as to what that should be," Mister Blackthorn explained.

Callie swallowed hard, her palms turning sweaty. This sounded hard. Could she actually make it through this course? Together with Aurelia, no less? Not to mention that the heavy feeling that she was being watched never left her.

I have to! If I don't do it, then I won't be able to head into town and learn more about magic from the library.
