As we entered the Telvan Outpost, the world froze. The small door shut seamlessly behind us, as I stumbled to a halt.

Hundreds of robed Hiscariat stared right at me. Standing stock still, they were a bright, vivid tapestry of different colors frozen into a painting. The only movement came from their tentacles and the vibrant flags and shade sails fluttering softly in the wind in the background.

Victoria groaned. “Carajo, that’s a lot to take in.”

I nodded softly. The mental pressure! It felt like taking off on an airplane, that same sensation of constant pressure inside the head. Only here, if I focused on part of the pressure, I thought I could almost isolate a single part, comment or thought. It was wildly overwhelming and uncomfortable. For me, at least.

Victoria giggled. “Somebody thinks I’m pretty.” She yelled. “I can’t spot you, but you’re not too bad yourself!”

“This’ll kill me. Either the pressure or the embarrassment. Can we get going, please?”

“Aw. I’m sure somebody out there thinks you’re pretty too, with those cauliflower ears.”

“Thank you so much.” I said drily.

“One of those Tavungor you go on about, perhaps.”

“Die in a fire.”

Mind-Aloft started walking, ignoring our banter, for which I was truly grateful. The closer we got to the crowd, the less I was able to focus on the back-and-forth. I focused on closing off the constant wave of pressure, which grew stronger the more we entered the press of people. Then, suddenly, something changed, like my ears popped, only it was inside my fucking brain, and the pressure eased off markedly.

I blinked to see the notification. Then I started laughing. “I fucking love this world.”

You have earned a new defensive skill:

Mental Resistance

Passive skill

Mental Magic is, beyond comparison, the most subtle magic type there is. Where other types loudly announce their presence, Mental Magic whispers in your ear. You have been influenced by Mental Magic. Every level in Mental Resistance will marginally decrease the influence Mental Magic may exert on your mind and improve your control over what you allow to enter your mind.

I quickly shared around what I’d gotten and the others confirmed in short succession that they’d gotten it as well. Truth be told, I was happy with it. Not just in knowing that it was possible to actually turn off all the goddamn chatter at the edge of my conscious hearing, but because it was one of the skills I’d wanted to gain the most. Why? Because the mere idea of somebody being able to go in and use me mentally as a sock puppet scared the living crap out of me.

To my amazement, I soon learned that you could use the Mental Resistance skill gradually, as a sort of volume control. I played around until I could hear the constant mental chatter, but was able to move around without being overwhelmed.

We gawked. Oh yes, we gawked. Steve turned around himself with wet eyes and wide smile on his face. Victoria just walked and didn’t say much, but her eyes were everywhere. I… did the tourist thing. I might’ve even walked around with my mouth open for a while.

The Telvan Outpost was… overwhelming. Too much. I hadn’t been sure what to expect from entering the home of a different species, but with Last Chance in the back of my head, I hadn’t known what to expect. Definitely not this.

There was one damn building in the entire city. This wasn’t hyperbole either. The edge of the city was elevated along with the wall and battlements, allowing us the chance to take in everything at a glance. The single erected building lay straight at the center of the city, a circular, multicolored creation with a tall kaleidoscopic dome on top. Within the city itself were… sails. Sails or flags were everywhere, in a riot of color and movement. No exceptions for other buildings, though. I actively ignored the question about what they did about privies.

The city wasn’t pure chaos, though. There was a system here, once you took in the scene. Stemming from the palace at the center, ropes holding flags spread out in every direction, like spokes on a wheel.

Oh, there were groupings, too. On our left, a group of purple and red-robed Hiscariat were seated in a circle below a purple shade sail that rotated ever so gently, held aloft by nothing that I could spot. This was repeated everywhere, colors coinciding, defining groupings and entire sections of the city.

Then there was the magic.

It was everywhere! Back in the Ever Steady, I’d gotten used to seeing some amazing things. Workers carrying loads that should be physically impossible. Farmers sprouting plants from nothing. Humanity starting to work together and pull as one! But it didn’t hold a candle to this place.

As I watched, a section of the outside wall actually crashed outward in a loud rumble. Nobody reacted to it, though. A second later, I saw why, as the whole tear in the wall repaired itself, as if nothing had ever happened.

A group – not just one person, but five Hiscariat, gathered in a closed circle – casually floated from the northern wall to the south-eastern, at a speed that Victoria would be hard-pressed to match even with her Running skill and talent activated.

A Mage stood idly at the edge of the crowd, a dozen brilliant flags weaving in and out in the air above him in an elaborate dance.

Magic was everywhere. And it was glorious.

We didn’t speak for a while. Mind-Aloft seemed to catch our mood and drifted ahead of us, content to give us the chance to soak it in.

We slowly approached the palace. I tried to commit everything to memory. There was so much I wanted to know, but I didn’t want to stop and risk missing something else I could be taking in. Only once did I pause and ask a question. “Hey, Mind-Aloft. Is that a market?” I pointed in a specific direction.

That section of the city was, as far as I could tell, special in that the flags stemming from the center represented every single color present in the city. In that direction, Hiscariat lined up everywhere in uneven ranks, crafting, showing off items, sometimes just standing stock still as they, supposedly, were mentally touting something or… singing, for all I knew. One Hiscariat close to me, wearing eye-searing orange robes sat cross-legged on the ground while, in front of him, a blaze of fire stemming from nothing I could see lovingly caressed a dozen pieces of meat as they floated about in the air.

“Is that food… magic? I want to try the magic food. Can we?” Victoria asked.

She smiled. “No, that is not magic food. It is, however, extremely spicy. The human metabolism might not agree with this level of spice. Also, yes, Liam, it is a market, of sorts. I would love to show you around after your introduction.”

We resumed our walk as Mind-Aloft expanded on exactly who we were meeting. Apparently, they were a motley mix of the actual leaders of this joint, along with a blend of what I understood to be representatives of specific opinions or movements, along with a few citizens of note. That’s the way I understood it, at least. Either, she was being vague or I was overwhelmed by the day’s proceedings. Probably a mix.

A notification gave me a mental jolt. Seconds later, I saw Steve react the same, blinking and pausing mid-step, before shaking his head in amusement.

I brought mine up to see what was funny. Then I laughed out loud.

Congratulations. You have unlocked the higher rarity of a skill:

You have activated Secrets of the Self more than 25 times within a 10-minute period

Secrets of the Self evolves to Higher Secrets of the Self.

Higher Secrets of the Self

Rare Passive skill

Everybody has secrets. Everybody. Yet, not all care to spend the energy needed to guard them. This skill, when triggered, will help you guard the details of your level, class, health and whichever other details that others might divulge through the use of Identify or similar talents. Every level in Secrets of the Self will be added to your level when calculating whether Identify or other skills are successful, and potentially warn you of anybody using Identify on you.

Cost: 5 stamina per trigger

In addition, once a day, you may double the effect of Secrets of the Self for ten minutes.

I tried out the upgrade straight away. Instantly, the skill kicked off, giving me a very solid indication that, yes, a kid in a blue robe had just attempted to use Identify on me. As had-

I ignored that part of the skill, letting it run unobstructed in the back of my mind for now. Clearly, by now, they knew everything up to and including my shoe size. But having something that could entirely hide my details from strangers, if only for ten minutes, could be extremely useful.

“Hijole.” Victoria paused, swaying for a moment. “Give me a second. I just need to deactivate… whew. There we go. It was sapping my Stamina too fast for me to keep up.” She wiped sweat from her eyes before grinning weakly. “I really am popular!”

“This is our L’Oreal moment.” I nodded. Then I chuckled and considered the insanity of the situation. I, Liam Berg. Child of the system, he-who-must-not-be-adopted, punching ball in life as in profession, was about to go speak to a new species, as a sort-of representative of mankind. Yeah, no. I wasn’t even going to comment on it. I just hoped I wasn’t going to ruin it.

“Never thought I’d get to say something as cheesy as this, but I’ve got to admit, it feels good.” I turned to Mind-Aloft and intoned. “Take me to your leaders.”

She raised one confused eyebrow. “You were the ones who stopped. But of course.”

A minute later, we crossed the threshold of the Hiscariat palace, on our way to act as the first ever introduction between our two species, and hopefully the first step on the path to future friendship and co-operation, as opposed to Cuilcis’ suggestion that we slaughter them for XP.

No pressure. Everything was fine.
