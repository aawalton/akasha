“How was your delve?” Cora asked when we visited after our trip to the dungeon.

Because they wanted to start a family soon, Cora and Mike had decided to rent a small apartment in a residential neighborhood near the crafters district instead of staying in delver quarters.

Residing within the Dungeon Complex would have likely been cheaper and came with perks that living within the greater community did not. But since children were not allowed to reside in delver quarters, they’d opted for something they would be able to remain in long-term.

A nice house would have been ideal, but because space was at a premium, such residences were out of reach for most who were below steel rank.

“It went fine,” I replied before filling the other couple in on the details.

“I picked up two new skills during the delve as well,” Stephen offered. “I tried to get Emie to take one of them, but she insisted that I should slot both.”

“Really?” Mike asked with wide eyes. “They must have been good skills, then.”

It wasn’t unusual for us to find a skillbook during a delve, though it didn’t happen every time. We usually sold the skillbooks we acquired to the Dungeon Complex since we were all pretty particular about what skills we slotted.

We had to be, given the finite nature of skill slots. The very fact that Stephen had filled both of his remaining skill slots also said a lot about the value of the skillbooks we’d acquired.

“They were. Danger Sense and Vortex,” Stephen informed them. “Danger Sense basically does what it says and warns me if I’m about to encounter something dangerous. I was able to sense nearby mobs and the fact that a knife one of the gnolls was using had poison on it, so I feel it was a worthwhile addition.”

Continuing on, Stephen said, “Vortex is an area of effect skill that creates a huge rotating windstorm. It’s basically a larger version of the skill Tornado, and does a ton of damage.”

We’d tested both of his new skills before leaving the dungeon, and after seeing the damage output of Vortex, a part of me actually regretted giving it up.

However, I didn’t mind giving up useful skills to Stephen since I had every intention of staying with him indefinitely. And if things ended up not working out… well, losing access to Stephen’s skills would probably be on the lower end of my concerns.

I listened and contributed occasionally as the group discussed some of the applications for Stephen’s new skills. So far, Mike and Cora’s move to a new city hadn’t really changed much about our relationship, aside from the fact that the couple couldn’t just stop in.

For now, at least. Stephen and I still planned to relocate to Arkenton before too long.

The fact that Mike and Cora’s lower-grade communicators didn’t work when we were in Sorenguard or performing our Porter duties outside their immediate area was also a bit of an issue, but that was something we’d already gotten used to.

They still got the messages that we sent, but we wouldn’t see their responses unless we traveled within range of their devices.

It made planning a bit more important while Stephen and I were still based out of Sorenguard, but that was a temporary thing.

“Hey, so how is the rest of your training going?” Cora asked, shifting the topic of conversation. “You said the equipment they gave you is effective, but do you think it’ll be enough to keep you from being discovered by higher-evolution demons?”

Cora had expressed concern about our participation in what was considered to be a fairly high-risk group. I’d explained that until we reached silver rank, we’d mostly be recovering any nearby baby dungeon cores.

While there was some risk involved since there would be an active incursion nearby and demons tended to target nearby dungeons, it wasn’t nearly as high as those directly working to close the breaches.

“It should be fine, especially if we’re both using our own stealth abilities,” I answered confidently, though I did feel quite a bit of trepidation at the idea of being near a higher-evolution demon regardless of the circumstances.

“You slotted a stealth skill?”

I shook my head. “No. With a bit of preparation, I can manually cast Stealth using the magics that I have access to. I used an Arcane version of Stealth in my old realm, so figuring out how to do something similar wasn’t all that difficult.”

Cora stared at me blankly for a moment before sighing. “The training you got before ascending was so unfair. The fact that you had access to pretty much every element through Arcane magic is also pretty broken.”

She wasn’t wrong, and this wasn’t the first time that the rogue had mentioned such a thing. Thankfully, I didn’t get the sense that she was truly upset at my advantages.

“Totally broken,” Stephen agreed with a grin, keeping the mood from dipping. “And we’ll get to see just how effective the combo really is next week since we’ll be stationed in Portal to respond to any incursions that appear.”

“Oh, really?” Mike asked. “They’re having you take the field already?”

“Yes, though Emie and I will be shadowing a more experienced pair instead of operating on our own,” Stephen replied. “I’ll be serving as her guard for now, though I suspect they’ll assign me a different role eventually. It just doesn’t make sense to have her guard be another bronze-ranker when everyone else is at least two evolutions stronger than us.”

“Stephen plans to tell them about his shapeshifting ability since it seems relevant to the mission,” I added, surprising both of them.

“Really?” Cora gasped. “I thought you didn’t want anyone else to know.”

Stephen shrugged. “I didn’t, but Emie’s safety comes first. I realized during our last delve that having me on oversight from the sky makes far more sense when she’s trying to sneak around, especially if we work out some form of communication.”

“It’s too bad you couldn’t acquire a Telepathy skill,” Mike stated.

“I agree. That would have been helpful. Unfortunately, those skills are a lot like the Portal skill – those who find them tend to keep them, and the few that hit the market sell for high prices.”

They weren’t quite as expensive as a Portal skillbook, but fell somewhere between Teleportation and Portal. Regardless, it made purchasing such a skill beyond our current means.

“We should visit the dungeon in Gremora sometime,” I suggested. “I’m almost positive the guild offers a transfer there.”

“Let us know if you do,” Mike said. “If the timing works out, Cora and I would love to explore another dungeon.”

‘If the timing works out’ was another way of saying ‘if Cora isn’t already pregnant.’ I was fairly certain they were holding off on trying for the moment since they both wanted to get situated in Arkenton before Cora took an extended break from the dungeon, so we probably had a bit of time.

“Speaking of exploring new dungeons,” Cora interjected, “are the two of you up for delving the Arkenton dungeon this week?”

I exchanged a look with Stephen. He didn’t seem to mind.

“Sure. We can do that. It’ll have to be a short delve since Stephen and I have classes all week, and we’re scheduled to spend next week in Portal,” I stated.

With a smile, Mike said, “That’s fine. Cora and I can always go back on our own if we feel the urge. The Builder Guild doesn’t have a lot of work locally at the moment, and Cora can basically work at her own pace with the Alchemist Guild. We were actually asked to tutor the City Lord’s children after he learned that we are both Ascenders, but we haven’t given him an answer yet.”

“I remember you both expressing interest in teaching youths when we first met. It makes sense that you’d lean in that direction now that you’re planning to decrease the amount of time you spend in the dungeon,” I said.

“Yeah. It’s something we both enjoy, though I’m not sure how tutoring a couple of noble kids will work out for us,” Cora said with a frown. “It seems like the City Lord is more interested in our titles than our actual abilities since neither of his children has expressed any interest in fighting like a rogue.”

“Well, whatever you decide to do, I hope you enjoy it.”

“I’m sure we will,” Cora said before waving her hand. “Enough of that. Let’s go get something to eat. There’s this little hole-in-the-wall restaurant that I found a few days ago that I absolutely love.”

“You still haven’t learned to cook after all this time?” I asked jokingly.

“I mean, I can cook you something if you really want,” Cora countered with a wicked smile.

“Nope! Eating out is fine!” I said with an exaggerated look of panic. “Don’t put yourself out for our sake.”

The truth was, while Cora wasn’t a bad cook, per se, she also wasn’t a very good cook unless it involved roasting something over a campfire. Strangely enough, the meals she could make over a campfire were actually pretty darn good.

However, joking about her lackluster ability to cook using anything other than a campfire (and her completely non-existent ability to bake) had become something of a fun pastime for me.

“That look makes me want to cook for you,” Cora countered. “I’ve actually been wanting to practice my baking recently. Perhaps I’ll prepare a few treats to take with us on our next delve.”

I couldn’t help but give an involuntary shiver at the threat.

“Again, there’s no reason to put yourself out on our account. How about I pick up some pastries and cakes from that bakery in Sorenguard that you liked so much?” I offered.

Cora’s eyes shifted from playful mischief to genuine excitement at the thought, and she quickly agreed.

“Great, now that we’ve settled that, let’s head out.”
