The silver blade sheared through the champion’s armor. A cheer broke the tension in the family room of the New Wighthelm pocket dimension. It erupted from George. The young cadet was on his feet, hands in the air, caught in a surge of raw adrenaline.

Then he realized where he was. He looked at my parents, the blood draining from his face, and dropped back onto the edge of the sofa. He clutched his teacup, knuckles white. My father didn't scold him. He simply offered a slow, grim nod of approval.

On the woven rug, Kaelus moved with preternatural speed. The moment the blade shimmered on screen, the dragon unfurled his massive wings. He wrapped them tight around Lyra and Ela, blocking their view of the execution.

Lyra pried at a wing, her muffled voice full of righteous indignation. "Eggy! Let go! I want to see the boom!"

"No booms for hatchlings," Kaelus projected. His mental tone brooked no argument.

The immediate threat of the duel was over. The machinery of war demanded my attention. The pause in the fighting was a window that was rapidly closing.

"I need to return to the CIC," I announced, standing up from the couch. I looked toward my parents. "I should be back before dinner. The Hegemony’s command structure will need a few hours to process what just happened."

I turned to Nyxia. She stared intently at the frozen image of Bob standing victorious in the ash. "I could use your assistance, Nyxia. If you're willing."

She blinked, startled by the direct request. She stared at me blankly for a second, then nodded. The sudden, eager movement earned her a raised eyebrow from my mother. Nyxia caught the look. Her pale cheeks flushed a vibrant crimson. She stood up quickly, smoothing her midnight-blue gown.

"Of course, my Lord," she said. Her voice was stiff, reclaiming her professional composure. "I am ready."

We walked back to the command bridge in silence. The hum of the ship’s engines thrummed beneath our boots. When we arrived, the CIC buzzed with activity. The tactical map updated in real-time, showing the chaotic shifting of the Cinderfall lines in the wake of their champion’s death.

I bypassed the main holotable and moved to a secondary, secured console. "Tes," I commanded. "Bring up the formalized Rules of Engagement document. The draft we finalized last night."

A long, glowing parchment materialized in the air between Nyxia and me. It was dense with legal and tactical parameters, written in the archaic, formal syntax required for continent-wide declarations.

"Read it," I told Nyxia. "I need your political expertise. I want no loopholes they can exploit later to claim we acted outside the bounds of sanctioned warfare."

Nyxia’s eyes scanned the document. She read quickly, her sharp mind processing the strategic implications of each clause.

"Article Four," she murmured, tracing a line of text in the air. "Authorization of sustained, twenty-four-hour combat operations. Nullification of the sun-up to sun-down armistice." She nodded approvingly. "You're pressing your logistical advantage. They rely on mages who need sleep to recharge. Your machines don't."

She scrolled further down. "Article Seven. Authorization of long-range, over-the-horizon artillery and aerial bombardment without prior visual confirmation of enemy combatants." She looked up at me, a cold smirk touching her lips. "This effectively legalizes the carpet bombing of their supply lines. Brutal. Efficient."

Her eyes reached the final addendum. She stopped reading. Her brow furrowed. She read the paragraph again, slower this time. She looked up at me, confusion warring with suspicion in her crimson eyes.

"Article Twelve," she read aloud. Her voice carried a note of utter disbelief. "'Any combatant who formally surrenders their arms shall be immediately escorted from the active theater of operations. They shall be provided with adequate sustenance, medical care, and secure lodging. Upon the cessation of hostilities, all surrendered personnel shall be granted amnesty and provided with arable land within the newly secured territories to resume civilian life.'"

She paused, looking at the next sentence. "'Furthermore, any civilian population center, town, or city that formally declares neutrality and ceases all support for the Hegemony military shall be immediately designated a protected zone. No aerial bombardment, artillery strikes, or hostile ground actions shall be permitted within its borders.'"

Nyxia let the hologram fade. She crossed her arms, staring at me as if I had just sprouted a second head.

"This is a joke, right?" she asked. "A propaganda piece you intend to circulate to lower their morale, but have no intention of honoring?"

"It is the law of the Aegis Legion," I replied. My voice was dead serious. "Any commander caught violating Article Twelve will face a court-martial."

"But why?" she demanded, stepping closer. "You have the power to annihilate them. You erased fifteen million elves in a heartbeat. You dropped a sun on Wighthelm to make a point. And now you're offering amnesty? Land? To the soldiers marching to kill your people?"

I sighed, rubbing the bridge of my nose. The disconnect between my modern sensibilities and the brutal reality of Aethelgardian warfare was a constant hurdle.

"Nyxia," I said, gesturing to the tactical map showing the massive red swarm of the Cinderfall army. "Look at them. Fifty million men. Do you honestly think King Theron has a standing army of fifty million professional soldiers? He doesn't. Ninety percent of that force consists of conscripts. Farmers, blacksmiths, bakers. Men dragged from their homes at spear-point, handed a rusted sword, and told to march or their families will burn."

I pulled up a live feed from one of our high-altitude drones. It zoomed in on a Cinderfall encampment. The image showed exhausted, emaciated men huddled around smoky fires, chewing on hardtack.

"They don't want to fight," I continued, my voice softening. "They are starving. They are terrified. If I simply slaughter fifty million men, what do I win? A kingdom of widows and orphans who will hate me for the rest of their lives. A continent of ash with no one left to rebuild it."

I turned back to the console and pulled up a heavily encrypted file.

"Tes, display Project: Renaissance."

The tactical map vanished. In its place, a series of stunningly detailed, three-dimensional architectural and infrastructural models bloomed into existence.

Nyxia’s breath hitched.

"I don't just want to conquer Aerthos," I explained, walking her through the glowing blueprints. "I want to rebuild it. But not as it was. Better."

I pointed to a sprawling agricultural model. "Automated irrigation systems utilizing localized, low-power water magic. High-yield crop rotation schedules calculated by the Oracle network."

I swiped to the next model. A network of wide, paved roads. "A unified highway system constructed with magitech-infused brick. It will connect every major city. And here..." I zoomed in on a sleek, metallic track running parallel to the roads. "High-speed mag-lev rail lines for mass transit and heavy freight. We can move grain from the southern valleys to the northern borders in hours, not weeks."

I brought up schematics for massive, shielded container ships designed to brave the Maelstrom Sea. They would establish a safe, permanent trade route between Aerthos and the Obsidian Dominion. I showed her blueprints for sprawling, automated industrial complexes. They could mass-produce basic necessities, freeing the population from grinding, manual labor.

"We will establish a global teleportation relay network, based on the Lumina ruins Varuna discovered," I said. The plans flowed from my mind to the screen. "And this..." I highlighted a series of large, open-campus buildings. "Free, mandatory education for all children up to the age of sixteen. Voluntary higher education in specialized academies will follow. We will implement a universal healthcare model, managed by automated diagnostic units and staffed by specialized healing mages."

Finally, I showed her the defense grid. A networked, modular system utilizing the Colossus-class Crawlers as mobile anchors. The Vindicator carriers and orbital kinetic strike platforms would support them.

"We will offer optional military enlistment," I concluded. "Not conscription. Citizens can choose to serve, to become Aegis officers or engineers, earning a living wage and learning advanced skills."

Nyxia was silent. She walked slowly around the holographic display, her eyes wide, drinking in the sheer scale of the vision. She was a scholar of magic, a woman who understood the intricacies of political power. She was looking at a utopia that defied the fundamental laws of their brutal, feudal society.

She stopped, looking at me across the glowing models. "If these projections are true... and knowing the absurdity of what you have already built in the Obsidian Dominion, I believe they are... this is an economic revolution, Alarion. In five years, Aerthos wouldn't just recover. It would be the richest, most technologically advanced nation in the world. It would dwarf the Lumina Imperium."

"That is the plan," I nodded. "But to build all of this, I need a workforce. I need farmers to learn the new irrigation techniques. I need builders to lay the mag-lev tracks. I cannot afford to slaughter fifty million potential citizens just because King Theron forced them into uniform."

I brought the tactical map back up. "I plan to distribute fliers along with the food drops. We will offer them an alternative. We will promise them that if they lay down their arms and defect, they will be spared. They will be fed. We can save millions of lives from meaningless bloodshed."

Nyxia frowned, her tactical mind engaging. "It’s a noble goal, Alarion. But it's dangerous. You are inviting an army of fifty million desperate men to walk toward your lines. If even a fraction of them decide to attack instead of surrender, they could overwhelm your automata through sheer mass."

"I know," I said, my expression grim. "That is why we must balance the equation. We cannot effectively manage mass defections if the enemy maintains overwhelming pressure. I need to keep the active, hostile Cinderfall army at a manageable number. No more than fifty million at any given time."

I looked at her, my eyes cold. "I will utilize the Shadow Order and localized, surgical strikes to ensure the enemy forces do not exceed that threshold. We will continuously cull their reinforcements and leadership while actively prompting the frontline conscripts to break ranks and run to us."

It was a delicate, terrifying balancing act. A war fought with one hand offering bread, and the other holding a sword.

"Send the terms," I ordered. "Let them know the rules have changed."

General Valen stood in the center of the ashen valley once more.

The sun was beginning its descent, casting long, bloody shadows across the scarred earth. The Cinderfall lines were dead silent. The execution of their champion still hung heavy in the air.

This time, Sir Dumaric Farthallow did not ride out to meet him.

A new representative approached on foot. He was an older man. His armor was practical, devoid of the ostentatious gold filigree favored by the high nobility. He moved with a stiff, guarded caution.

He stopped a few paces from Valen and bowed stiffly. "I am Lord Gravex. Due to unforeseen complications regarding his composure, Sir Dumaric has been stripped of his noble title and rank by decree of the King. He has been reassigned to the frontline infantry." Gravex cleared his throat, trying to hide his apprehension. "You will find him in the vanguard."

Valen offered a predatory smile. "A tragic loss for diplomacy. But I suppose we must make do."

Valen unrolled the heavy vellum scroll bearing the seal of House Wight. He didn't hand it over. He read it aloud. His magically amplified voice ensured that not just Gravex, but the thousands of Cinderfall soldiers shivering in the trenches behind him, could hear every word.

As Valen read the demands—the authorization of twenty-four-hour combat, the nullification of safe zones, the threat of continuous, over-the-horizon bombardment—the silence from the Cinderfall lines turned to a murmur of crushing terror. They were officially trapped in a nightmare with no pause button.

Then, Valen reached Article Twelve.

He read the terms of surrender. The promise of food, medical care, and land.

The murmur died instantly. The Cinderfall soldiers stared at the elven general, their minds struggling to comprehend the words. Take in their enemy? Treat them as their own? It was unheard of. In the history of Aethelgard, the fate of a conquered army was slavery or the sword. The concept of amnesty, of being given land by the conqueror, sounded like a cruel, elaborate trap.

Gravex stared at Valen, his face pale. "This is an insult. You expect us to believe you will spare the men who marched to burn your home?"

Valen slowly rolled up the scroll. He stepped closer to Gravex, dropping the theatrical amplification. He spoke in a voice meant only for the old lord.

"I like you more than the last representative, Gravex," Valen said softly. "So, I will give you a word of advice. Start digging trenches of your own. Deep ones. Our Lord Commander despises the mindless loss of life, but he despises a threat to his people even more."

Valen turned and walked back toward his waiting carrier.

"And Gravex?" Valen called over his shoulder. "Tell your men... the food in the camps is much better than boiled leather."
