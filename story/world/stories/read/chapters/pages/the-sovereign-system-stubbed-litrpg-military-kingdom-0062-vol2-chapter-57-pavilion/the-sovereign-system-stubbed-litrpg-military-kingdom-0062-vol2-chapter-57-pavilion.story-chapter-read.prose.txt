Luke walked among the crowd of soldiers pressing through the streets of the capital, his gaze sweeping across the familiar landscape. Not much had changed since his last visit. Families still huddled in the back alleys, reminiscent of the homeless who once populated the metro streets back on Earth.

He found himself wondering about their stories. Judging by the absence of homeless men, it wasn’t hard to guess—these women and children were likely the widows and orphans of soldiers who had fought and died for the Marxx Kingdom.

The realization turned his stomach.

How many more would be driven to the streets now that over twenty thousand men had perished in a fruitless war with Qin?

His eyes shifted to the prince walking beside him. Lyon’s expression had stiffened, his gaze fixed on a dirt-covered woman cradling a child tightly to her chest. Luke didn’t need to guess—this was likely the first time the young man had truly seen something like this.

“Why are they sleeping on the streets?” Lyon asked, unable to tear his eyes away from the woman.

“They probably had nowhere to go after their husbands or fathers were killed in the war.”

“But don’t we give them compensation?” the prince countered. “Shouldn’t that be enough to find lodging? At least enough to keep them off the street?”

Luke shrugged. “Assuming they even receive the compensation, it’s probably not nearly enough to survive in the capital. Everything’s more expensive here… for a variety of reasons.”

He didn’t say it out loud, but one of the main reasons was the Black Tiger Gang. Every merchant in the city needed the gang’s permission to sell, which eliminated competition and allowed for rampant price gouging—especially on necessities like food.

That, combined with the fact that Ralis was home to many of the kingdom’s noble families, meant the cost of living had inflated beyond reason.

If things continued like this, Luke wouldn’t be surprised if the Marxx Kingdom struggled to field an army in the future—crippled by poverty and declining birth rates.

“Take it all in, Lyon,” he said quietly, placing a hand on the prince’s shoulder. “These are your people—the ones you’ll be expected to protect. Not just from enemies, but from the very people you consider allies.”

The weight of those words caused Lyon’s head to lower—perhaps in shame, or perhaps in dawning responsibility. Either way, it was enough. Luke was satisfied.

If he’s going to be king, I need him to understand his purpose…

They walked in silence for a time as more soldiers peeled off from the main road, drifting toward their homes and waiting families.

Luke, however, was headed toward a large wooden building illuminated by a glow of lanternlight. It wasn’t just him—many men were moving in that direction, drawn to it even after enduring the chaos of their campaign.

A grand wooden sign hung above the doorway, its words etched in elegant calligraphy:

Jade Silk Pavilion.

“Wait… are we really going to the Pavilion?” Lyon asked, freezing mid-step.

“Yes. That was always the plan.”

“H-Hold on,” the prince stammered, “won’t they recognize me? I used to go there every few days to see Fairy Qin. Most of the staff know me…”

“Calm down. No one will recognize you unless you announce yourself,” Luke said confidently. “What time does Fairy Qin usually perform?”

“Huh!? You want to see Fairy Qin!?” Lyon’s voice cracked. “No—I can’t let her see me like this!”

He turned on his heel at once, nearly colliding with a few soldiers behind them. Instead of reacting with irritation, the men gently held him in place—almost as if they had expected this outburst.

Lyon squirmed for a moment before giving up, his shoulders slumping. Luke let out a small sigh and shook his head.

“We won’t be meeting her directly, so stop jumping to conclusions,” he said, trying to keep his patience intact. “I have a friend inside who can help us find a place to lay low until the time is right. All you need to do is trust me. Surely, you can manage that much?”

With help from a few of the nearby men, Luke was finally able to get the second prince under control.

“Now listen,” he continued. “I need you to keep your identity hidden—even from my friend. I don’t want her getting caught up in all of this. She’s done enough.”

Lyon gave a reluctant nod, allowing Luke to breathe a small sigh of relief. He didn’t blame the prince for his hesitation—after all, the plan was flimsy at best.

I just hope she’s still working for the Pavilion, he thought, resisting the urge to rub his temples.

“Fairy Qin should be performing very soon, General,” one of the soldiers whispered beside him. “You’ll need at least two gold to get a VIP seat, though—it’s usually only nobles who can afford that.”

Luke thanked the soldier, only briefly scolding him for using his title. It was already remarkable that the ten thousand men had agreed to this plan. A minor slip like that could be overlooked.

A wry smile tugged at Luke’s lips as he reached into his knapsack, already knowing what he’d find.

Nothing.

Even after pooling their resources back at the academy, they had only managed to gather thirty gold coins—and most of that had been spent on ingredients and materials to create and package perfume. He didn’t know whether the product was selling well, but even if it was, Luke had no way to contact Kayson or Sebastian to find out.

I’ll have to find another way, he thought grimly.

He fished out the few silver coins he had left, then grabbed the prince by the arm and made his way toward the entrance of the establishment.

Two large guards in tight-fitting earthen robes stood posted at the door, collecting payment from the flood of patrons entering the building. They looked like bouncers pulled straight from the nightclubs of Earth—thick-necked, broad-chested, and distinctly unamused.

“Good evening, gentlemen. Entry for two, please,” Luke said, stepping forward.

The jingle of a few silver coins in his palm rang out loudly despite the crowd. He hoped it would be enough.

The guard said nothing. He merely held up the silver coin to the light, inspecting it closely as if suspecting it was a fake. His expression remained stony and unwelcoming.

Beside him, Lyon stood rigid—his entire posture tense under the weight of the guard’s scrutiny.

Luke didn’t fare much better, but his Dragon’s Heart skill pulsed quietly within him, keeping his composure firm. The pressure washed over him like wind against a mountain—felt, but never swaying.

“Get in,” the guard finally grunted, scrunching his nose before turning away and waving them through.

Luke didn’t wait for a second invitation. He grabbed Lyon’s arm and stepped quickly through the threshold of the Jade Silk Pavilion.

The moment they entered, the atmosphere hit him like a wave.

The rich scent of ale and damp wood filled his nostrils, tinged with sweat. Lanterns hung from the vaulted ceiling above, casting a warm golden hue across the room. Wooden tables sprawled in chaotic clusters across the floor, filled with men laughing, shouting, and singing off-key.

Some raised mugs in drunken celebration. Others had their hands buried in the laps of courtesans perched on their knees, fingers wandering without shame.

Women weaved between the tables with practiced grace, balancing trays of drinks while narrowly avoiding the occasional hand reaching from beneath a table. Most dodged these advances smoothly, without even breaking stride.

Luke inhaled instinctively—then instantly regretted it.

The stale taste of fermented drink and sweat clung to the air. He coughed into his hand, trying not to gag.

“You said you came here all the time?” Luke shot a look at Lyon, half-incredulous.

The prince scoffed. “I had a private room upstairs. What do you take me for?”

Luke gave a dry nod and let the matter drop, though his expression said everything. He turned his gaze to the stage built into the northern wall. It adjoined a winding staircase that likely led up towards the VIP balcony.

At the center of the stage, a dancer moved gracefully beneath the lanternlight. She wore little more than a scrap of fabric, her curves accentuated by every twirl and sway of her hips. The men in the front rows leaned forward with eyes wide and mouths parted, captivated.

Beside her, a smaller figure sat cross-legged with an instrument perched in her lap. Her robe was modest, in stark contrast to the dancer’s attire, and her fingers moved expertly over the strings.

It took Luke a moment, but recognition clicked into place.

A pipa.

The instrument’s sound filled the room with elegant notes—sweet and sultry, playful and precise. Each strum carved through the noise, matching the dancer’s rhythm in perfect harmony. The melody seemed to hang in the air like a spell, tightening the hold over the drunken audience.

As the final note rang out, a burst of applause erupted. Silver coins showered the stage, accompanied by slurred declarations of love and drunken howls of approval.

The dancer cast a lascivious smile toward a few select patrons before sashaying off the stage, hips swaying like a pendulum.

The musician, still seated, then stood quietly and produced a woven basket. She began collecting the coins strewn across the floor with practiced, almost mechanical movements.

Luke squinted, taking a closer look.

His breath caught.

She’s… young. Not even a teenager…

His skin crawled.

What the hell was a child doing in a place like this? Was she only here to play music? Or were these sick bastards forcing her into something far worse?

His fists clenched at his sides.

“Luke, calm down.”

The harsh whisper cut through the noise of the Pavilion, barely audible above the drunken ruckus. Only then did Luke realize his jaw was clenched, his fists tight at his sides, teeth grinding in silent fury.

“I’m okay…” he growled, forcing down the surge of anger boiling inside him.

Now wasn’t the time. He couldn’t afford a crusade—not when thousands of lives were depending on him.

Trying to redirect his focus, he asked, “When is Fairy Qin performing?”

But before Lyon could respond, a towering man stepped onto the stage. The air shifted.

Chatter vanished in an instant. A silence rippled across the room, palpable and reverent.

The newcomer was massive—broad-shouldered and tall, even larger than Richard Boyd, one of the biggest men Luke had ever encountered. A thick spiked beard streaked with silver framed his square jaw, but it was his bald scalp and piercing gaze that struck fear into those who looked his way.

Were it not for the pristine white robes that clung to his bulky frame, Luke might have mistaken him for a barbarian warlord. In truth, he wouldn’t have been surprised if the man suddenly pulled out two greataxes and carved through the crowd like wheat.

“Welcome, everyone,” the man boomed, his voice deep and resonant—easily carrying through the hall without need for amplification.

All eyes snapped toward him. The entire Pavilion seemed to hold its breath.

“It is a special night tonight,” he continued, arms spreading wide, revealing a barrel-like chest. “Our mighty soldiers have returned from their campaign deep into enemy territory. These fine men deserve our respect and admiration.”

A few murmurs of agreement echoed, quickly swallowed by the commanding presence he exuded.

“Allow me to extend my sincere thanks for your sacrifices to the kingdom. Ale will be free for all soldiers tonight—so drink to your heart’s content!”

The cheer that erupted nearly shook the lanterns above. Glasses clanged, fists pounded on tables, and the atmosphere surged with raw, joyous energy.

But just as quickly as it came, the celebration fell silent as the man raised one hand—like a conductor halting his orchestra.

“But first,” he said with a grin that revealed two rows of perfect white teeth, “allow me to introduce our next performer…”

He paused for effect.

“…The gem of the Jade Silk Pavilion. The angel among mortals. Our very own—Fairy Qin!”

He swept an arm dramatically toward the side of the stage.

From behind the curtains, a woman emerged in a flowing robe of deep green silk. She ascended the stairs with grace, her steps featherlight, her poise exquisite. Her skin was pale as moonlight, her features delicate—accentuated with just enough makeup to elevate her beauty without masking it.

She was young, but not a child—easily of marriageable age, and every inch of her poised elegance commanded the room.

Luke felt his skin prickle as silence reclaimed the Pavilion. Every pair of eyes locked onto her as if nothing else in the world existed.

Even Lyon stood frozen beside him, his body rigid, gaze utterly transfixed.

Luke turned away.

He had seen enough.

His eyes began scanning the area near the stage, searching for someone else. Someone important.

And then he spotted her.

The long, golden hair caught the lanternlight, glowing like sunlight pouring through stained glass. It spilled down over a wide, muscular back clothed in a simple green robe.

She turned.

A strong, scarred arm brushed back her hair, revealing a striking face—pretty, proud, and fierce.

A smile crept across Luke’s face.

Hilda. I finally found you.
