The quality of a silence could be determined by all that it contained that should destroy it, and yet failed to do so. How robes rustled but were swallowed whole. The breathing of twelve Cultivators adding to the oppressive quiet. Birdcall flattened by wooded walls till it died a meaningless death.

A meeting room that contained ten suppressed elders, one suppressed lord, and the woman doing the suppression was utterly quiet after the last pronouncement. Silence needed to linger for audacity to be appreciated. And so it was that despite all the noises that should have broken the silence, they were all in awe of the sheer madness it took to say the words Hua uttered.

“Perhaps I misheard you, but did you say you mean to seize every piece of gold and silver within Liao territory?” asked Elder Shenhou, foremost elder of the Liao Clan, to his new Lord, the daughter who claimed the name Weilong.

“Just the taels. The mortals can keep their trinkets.” Looking down on him from her father’s raised throne, she grinned with all her teeth. “I am not so cruel to take away a lover’s gift.”

Grandmother cackled. This now broke the silence. Her suppression faded and finally, ten elders, and one lord, could breathe at last. Her laughter was a deep, chortling thing. The unabashed nature of it only highlighted her deep, raspy voice.

“You are audacious, grandchild. Perhaps the most audacious I have seen in a century.”

“I learnt it from my greatest teacher.”

You, she did not say but all knew it.

“We’ll take their gold and silver. Give them paper in exchange. If they know their wealth is protected by the Liao Clan, then they can trust it and still use it for transactions.”

“Or rise up in rebellion and try to murder us,” a younger Elder muttered.

“They were going to do so anyway. If it must happen, let us force it to happen on our terms. But no, I do not think it will happen. Mortals can adapt to changing circumstances. So long as we give them a reason to believe in this deal, they will abide by it.”

“What reason could be great enough?”

“The authority of the Dragon Throne and the lightning of Liao. There were requirements for the Magistrate’s support. Namely, that the Elders of Liao support him,” she lied. “And so, Honoured Elders, I ask who amongst your number is willing to go forth and project the strength of Liao. To remind everyone in this province that the name Liao is closest to heaven.”

“Madness,” Shenyu hissed. “This is madness.”

“I will go with the magistrate,” Grandmother said over him, offering her support. Weilong resisted the urge to smile. “We will have much to discuss on the matter of currency on the way to Seagate City. Does anything remain to be covered by this meeting? No? If not, then this old lady will take her rest.”

Hua rose with her. “This Lord will accompany you. I need to examine the treasury before we leave. Honoured Elders, sort amongst yourselves who will lead. I expect the seniormost amongst you will have the most success in the more distant regions. You are excused.”

And with that, Liao Hua left with her grandmother, the only person who could keep her alive in a room full of vipers.

***

Hua and her grandmother walked in amiable silence down the clan grounds. If the sky wasn’t yellow, it would have been a truly beautiful day.

“If I hadn’t found out about that meeting, would you have told me?” she asked pleasantly.

Grandmother gave her an equally pleasant smile. “Once I had made certain those children would be reasonable about their decisions for the clan. I would not have permitted them to bring us to ruin, but I will not fight your battles for you. No lord Liao will be so helpless they cannot corral their council of elders.”

“You’ll watch me sink just to test me.”

“You’ve always known I have higher standards than most.”

“I hate you sometimes.”

“Careful, that’s how your father got started. You don’t want to spend your last days cursing me.”

“Father will live.”

“Perhaps,” Grandmother said with the ease of someone who had already buried three sons and many daughters.

Hua shook her head. She didn’t like facing that inevitability. Father had led them to glory. He should have risen to the realm of myths and legends. It was Hua who would have to take on that mantle if he died. Lay down the steps to heaven and ascend them so that she could cut down the gods that had taken Qing from her.

“How do you reach the Foundation Establishment?”

“Don’t worry, I will be there to teach you when the time comes.”

“And if you are not?”

Grandmother waved away her concern. “You won’t truly need me. In a way, it happens naturally. All at once, a moment of enlightenment will guide you to make the choice that will define you for the rest of that realm. It can be a wrong choice as may learn, but you will know it when it comes. I can aid you every step of the way, but ultimately, you must walk those steps. Lay down your own foundation to immortality.”

“Could my father have broken through to whatever lays beyond if he hadn’t… if he’d avoided his deviation.”

“Yes.”

“So certain.”

“I know my son’s potential. He was an entire flock of potential. And now he will join all others who flew too high and sink into the same grave.”

Hua shook her head. “I still have faith.”

“Would that the world cared for faith and not truth. I hope faith serves you better than it ever did this old lady. Now, where are we going?” Grandmother asked as they made their way to a more removed part of the clan grounds.

“The armoury,” she said, grateful to move past that topic.

“Why?”

“I need a new sword since you broke mine. If it had come down to a fight with Shenyu, I would have only had my lightning.”

“For someone who isn’t a physical Cultivator, you certainly go through swords faster than they could.”

“Maybe because these swords are little better than scrap iron. Half of them feel like they’ll deform just from holding them too tight. I’d rather just use my fists but everyone else has a sword or spear.”

“Truly, I have failed to raise a lady of elegance and high standing. My husband’s foolishness spread to my four idiot sons and now I find my only worthwhile grandchild has rocks for brains.”

“The commonality is you.”

“No, your idiocy is unique. Tell me, where do you mean to store all the newfound wealth you plan on acquiring?”

“I had hoped the vaults would be large enough.”

“Child, do you know how much space a tael takes up? Let alone the millions you plan on seizing. Don’t be absurd.”

“We could build it where the Temple of Winter Dragons used to be,” she suggested.

Her grandmother paused and when she saw Hua was entirely serious, made a warding gesture. “That is bad karma, girly.”

“I make my own karma. Besides, it has a lovely lakeside view now. Truly prime real estate. And I think the priesthood would be grateful for an influx of cash if we bought that land off of them. It would be a quick way to offload some of our paper currency. Practically free real estate.”

“This is the kind of bad karma that gets you sent to the Ten Hells for ten lifetimes. How many times can you cheat a priest before you get punished?”

“Well, I did kill one, and the mortals became extra annoying, so my karmic debt is paid off.”

Grandmother gave her a look that said she was reconsidering every piece of praise she had ever given Hua.

The vassal on duty at the armoury frowned at her, confused perhaps by the golden eyes and the mantle she wore, until he connected the dots and realised the lord he now served was also the same girl who visited every few months requesting a new sword. Reconciling this fact took up his entire mental capacity.

Hua walked past him and dismissed everyone in the armoury with a gesture. She was rather enjoying that ability to have everyone leave when she wanted some privacy.

She grabbed three swords from the same area where she had picked her last one. Hua checked them for weight and general balance. There was almost no difference that mattered to her. All three took her Qi just as readily as the others and so all three would do the job.

Completely at random, Hua chose a new sword and strapped it to her waist.

“Why did you decide to go to Seagate City first?” Hua asked.

It was the second-largest city under the direct control of their clan. It was named after its purpose as a gate to the sea and was built where the Liao River fed into the greater sea.

“If we can successfully conduct a seizure in the second largest city we have then it will prove to those who live here how viable it is. And the consequences if they don’t comply. I am known across the province, dearest child. When they hear my sword sing, they will remember just who I am.”

“You don’t want me to conduct the seizure here but neither do you want it done first,” she realised.

“Correct. You will go secure our resource base in the Western Reaches while I and the other elders spread the story of you. They may not like it, but the imperial document they carry will have your name on it. By the time you return from the west, the story of Lord Weilong will have spread.”

“You’re sending me to the mining communities? Those are backwaters. I don’t think they even have teahouses in the west.”

“Good, no distractions on your part as you learn how to command without the politicking of the elders distracting you. Without whatever manoeuvring the Dragon Throne is doing with all these soldiers.” Grandmother shook her head. “When you’ve completed that duty, you will return and consolidate power here. It should hopefully break up the power bloc the eldest Shens have made. By proving yourself knowledgeable, powerful, and competent, you’ve put a chink in their stranglehold over clan politics. It was foolish to challenge Shenyu without even a sword, but you survived, and that is all that matters in the end. You showed he could bleed, even if all he bled was pride. You proved to the other elders your strength. The older Shen Generation might generally dislike you, but there’s enough bad blood across that entire generation that they will happily use you to jockey for influence.”

“And if you send them to regions remote enough, they’ll return to find me in control.”

“As much control as you can manage, yes. Do not make the mistake of thinking it will be unshakeable. That is how you get stabbed in the back. Assume whoever you think loyal has a price and at least two elders have paid it.”

“Yes, Grandmother.”

“Have you selected the members of your retinue yet?”

“I don’t need one.”

“Don’t be a difficult child. Your retinue will define your rule. They will speak in your name and project your power. Act where you cannot or will not.

“Father doesn’t have any retinue.”

“Your father has an entire clan at his beck and call. Besides, his retinue from youth and the Yellow Cap War are all long since buried. He simply outlived them.”

“Liu Xin, certainly. He’ll serve well in many roles. Spy, assistant, scribe. He’ll do well with dealing with the mortals for me.”

“Never overload a single person with too many responsibilities. Makes it too easy for something to slip past their attention. Cripples you if something happens to them. And something will, that much is certain. Always have some redundancy.”

“Weiji, then. He can be my redundancy—no, he’ll need to be my second in command. He’s the grandson of Elder Shenyu and he was being trained for the role under Brother. A degree of continuity will pacify some of the Elders. Give them a lever to control me through.”

“And reassure others of your generation that you aren’t completely enamoured with a foreigner. Have you found Weiji to be of any wisdom? He can be cunning, that boy, but I worry he lacks true patience.”

“I feel like I can trust him to do any task I set. He was trained by the elders directly for this. Why are you worried?”

“I said wisdom, not intelligence. He has the smarts, I just need to know I can entrust you to him. As your second, he’ll be the biggest point of failure. If he falters, it will mean more than just his death. With each year, replacing his institutional knowledge and gut instinct will be an increasingly difficult task.

“Sometimes, I almost think you care about my welfare.”

The dragon that was her grandmother smiled. “Don’t count on that to save you in battle.”

“Weiang.”

“Perhaps in the future. I’ll do you a favour and take him with me. See if I can polish him into somebody useful.”

“I’d like Ming.”

“No, the priests will need to continue cleansing this city and Seagate. Do you want a Gu outbreak because taking Ming is how you get a Gu outbreak.”

“She’s just in training. The clan can do without her.”

“You severely overestimate how many spiritual prodigies we have lying around or you are incredibly underestimating her talents. I’m worried it’s both. No, leave her here to train and acquire knowledge. Should anything happen to the other priests, we’ll have a line of continuity. And, if you mean to go with your plan of acquiring the Temple grounds, you’ll want someone trained in Taoist arts to make sure ghosts don’t murder anyone working there.”

“Fine, Ming can stay but just know I’ll be cursing you if I have to deal with ghosts.”

“Every moment, you behave more and more like your father,” Grandmother chuckled. “Take Captain Yao as well. He’s respected amongst the soldiers. A third son of the ruling line of a vassal house. He’ll be able to cover for any of your strategic shortfalls.”

“Excuse you, I have no strategic shortfalls. You trained me personally.”

“In small unit tactics organised around Cultivation battles, not battlefield command or grand strategy. Stop being stubborn, I know you agree with me.”

“You still should have taught me.”

“Had we more time, you would be learning those lessons now. What I gave you will have to be enough. I had you read the history of great battles. So long as you know some of the bigger mistakes, your seconds can shore up your deficiencies. When you return, I’ll be working on you with that.”

“As Lord, I refuse to sit for another lesson.”

“When you’re strong enough to refuse me, that will have more meaning. Eventually, you’ll need to find someone from the branch lines. Can’t have them getting too uppity by feeling dismissed. That’s how you build another power bloc against you. Favouring them too greatly is also a weakness.”

“Anyone else.”

“An ally or two almost entirely unaffiliated with our clan would round out your retinue. You’ll find roles you need to fill. And even if you don’t, spreading the burden among more people reduces the chance for failure when someone inevitably dies.”

“That’s four, maybe five people I’ll need to screen and assess. Sifting through rice for spiders sounds less tedious.

“That’s what the flooding is for,” Grandmother said slowly.

“Oh. I thought rice just needed all that water to grow.”

“How do you not know anything about rice farming?”

“We’re named for our wheat fields, not rice. If we were called the Silver Sea, then you’d have a point.”

“My granddaughter is an idiot. Lovely. What were your tutors doing your whole life? Rice heads are yellow and brown—they’re just as much a part of the amber fields we’re named for.”

“I don’t know, Grandmother, what were you doing as my primary instructor? The failings of the student are reflective of the teacher. Oh, and there are no bad students, only bad teachers. You only have yourself to blame.”

“Just get out of my sight before I decide to kick you across the training field again.”

“That would be threatening if it wasn’t the only time I’m allowed to hit you without consequence.”

“You say as if you’ve managed to land a hit on me once.”

“Grandmother, I think it’s best we settle this on the training grounds.”

“Grandchild, I’ll show you mercy and avoid breaking any bones.”
