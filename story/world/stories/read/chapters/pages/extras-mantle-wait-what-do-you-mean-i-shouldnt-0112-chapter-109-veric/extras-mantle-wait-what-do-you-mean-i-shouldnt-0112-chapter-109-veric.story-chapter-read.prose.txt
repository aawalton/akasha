The corridor on the thirtieth floor felt different from the one above.

Jin couldn't immediately put his finger on why. The same strip lighting, the same reinforced walls, the same faint chemical undercurrent that seemed to saturate every level of this place below ground. But something in the quality of the… silence was different.

« You okay? »

Yeah… It’s just something isn’t right. Jin replied mentally. It’s been what, 2-3 hours since the cultists were revealed, and why aren’t there any large-scale attacks? We would have surely felt it if that had happened. So why? What are these bastards thinking?

« There’s so much we don’t know. It’s a pity we don’t have any access to mind magic, or else we could have gleaned something out of the cultists. »

Yeah… wait. Jin paused, and then a slow smile formed on his face. Say, Angel, it’s been some time since we harvested someone, no?

« The way you say that feels… »

Heh. At least we know how to get around one problem. Jin said and focused back on the path and where Brocki was taking him.

Brocki walked half a step ahead, favoring his good shoulder slightly, his boots making soft thud-thud sounds against the stone floor.

He did something with his boots?

« Likely some modifications to suit his combat style. »

Jin watched him for a moment and then looked away. Good for him. I really like the twins.

"Tell me about Veric, Brocki," he said. "Beyond the basics of what everyone says."

“Veric?” Brocki was quiet for a moment, his stride not breaking.

"Boss, it’s Hard to get a read on him," he finally said, voice pitched low enough it wouldn't carry. "He's well into his forties, and Flocki thinks he's ex-military or something, maybe, based on how he moves. The guy isn't a top-tier combatant..." He paused, tilting his head slightly. "But he shines when it comes to planning. The man's got a mind like a steel trap. Tactics, schemes, reading people... and if he says something's off, I listen."

"I see," Jin said.

"Regardless, he's a good fellow." Brocki shrugged with his good shoulder, the other barely moving. "He managed to find and convince some really capable people to join our group. That alone's worth something down here."

Jin nodded. "How did you find him?"

"He was with Maya and the young boy… I forgot his name." Brocki said. "When I started rounding people up after the collapse, he stood and got others to join as well."

The man's expression shifted—something almost like respect threading through his features. "Which I thought was interesting, considering I'm sure he doesn't like attention."

Jin smiled faintly. "The kind who doesn’t like fame and limelight?"

"Yup." Brocki's tone carried a hint of dry amusement. "Doesn't care who gets the credit as long as the work gets done. Rare trait, that."

"That does sound like someone interesting." Jin agreed.

They turned the corner, and the corridor opened into a wider space, and Jin saw three figures standing at the end of the corridor guarding a heavy, plated metal door.

He felt them before he saw them properly.

His aura—still settling after the integration—brushed against their core. It was more of an involuntary action, and he knew he would have to spend time honing and mastering his aura, or else he would be telling everyone who is able to sense aura that he is here.

« I’ll come up with basic exercises and a plan. »

Thank you. Jin said mentally and focused on the feedback from his aura.

All three of them were almost at the peak of Order II. And they were wearing the looted sets.

« At least it's getting put to good use. »

True. Jin shrugged. Those raids on the cultists' outposts were well worth it.

« Indeed. Though I still think you should've kept more of the gear for yourself. »

Maybe. But I don’t think those would be of any use.

He focused on the men as he drew closer.

The one nearest the door had the build of someone who had carried heavy things for a long time—thick through the chest, thick through the neck, with a face like quarried stone—a heavy metal gauntlet adorned both hands.

"That's Barsta," Brocki whispered, barely audible. "He was in the imperial army before retiring and settling here in Vienna. The man's silent as a rock, and I heard he used to run a small smithy on the outskirts before all this went to hell."

"Looks strong," Jin murmured back.

"Aye, he's one of the stronger combatants we've got, and people listen when he talks." Brocki's voice carried approval. "He's been quite helpful in getting certain things done. Especially the heavy lifting no one else wants to touch."

"I can only imagine," Jin added with a slight chuckle, picturing that mountain of a man simply picking up problems and moving them elsewhere.

The second guard was leaner, with short, messy green hair and a scowl marring his face as he glared at the third man.

The third guard—stationed at the other end of the door—had a short but well-kept beard going white at the jaw and a seemingly pleasant expression as he ignored the glare being thrown his way, arms crossed, looking faintly amused by the whole situation.

"The glaring one is Gleeward," Brocki whispered as they drew near, his voice dropping even lower. "And the bearded fellow is Jack."

Jin felt the mountain's eyes shift to them—Barsta's gaze, heavy and assessing—and he nodded to the man in acknowledgment before turning his head back to Brocki.

"Why's Gleeward looking sour?"

"Ah, that..." Brocki hesitated, and Jin caught the edge of suppressed laughter in his voice. "The man's excellent with a spear—no doubt a top-tier combatant when he's focused—but he's got a foul mouth and a ridiculous addiction to drinking."

Jin quirked his eyebrows.

"Jack most probably hid his alcohol reserves." Brocki finished, grinning despite himself.

Jin shook his head, letting out a soft exhale that was almost a laugh. "Quite a company you've assembled..."

"Indeed," Brocki's grin widened slightly. "We all have our quirks."

Jin distantly felt he was being included in that group of crazies, which was fair enough. He'd probably earned it by now.

"Sir!" Barsta snapped to attention first.

"Sir!" The other two followed Barsta's lead. Gleeward's salute was sharp and military-crisp despite the scowl; Jack's was more casual but still respectful.

"At ease." Brocki nodded, his tone shifting, and Jin watched the dynamics shift as the one called Jack stepped into his role. "How's the situation?"

"Veric is still inside," Jack said, taking the lead without hesitation. "Apart from that, we heard loud explosions and essence discharge coming from the west wing about forty minutes ago. Miss Elenor went looking thirty minutes back, and we haven't had contact with her since then." He glanced at the heavy door behind them. "There's still thirty more minutes before the time she gave us to expect her back."

Brocki nodded, then briskly stepped back, giving Jin the floor with a small gesture that said Your turn, boss.

Jin smiled slightly and shook his head as he turned to face the three guards properly.

"Well," he said, keeping his tone light, conversational. "I'm assuming you're all well aware of who I am?"

"Yes, sir!" Three sets of eyes shifted to Jin, each one assessing him in that particular way soldiers did when meeting a new commanding officer for the first time.

Jin had expected Barsta to stay silent and Gleeward to ignore him entirely, based on Brocki's description.

But he saw—or rather felt with his aura—something else.

Fear.

A faint tension in Gleeward's jaw, a slight widening in Barsta's eyes, the way Jack's hand had unconsciously moved closer to the short blade at his hip.

Huh?

« Seems like your fight with Orodain was seen by quite a few people. »

Yeah, but still, it was just a fight... what's that got to do with this?

« If only you could see a replay of exactly how you fight, you would have your answer. »

Haha, very funny, Angel.

« I'm not joking, Jin. You went against an Order III. A peak Order III while you were still Order II. You stood in a tornado of flames. You stood while Orodain had sliced your body open to the bone and kept fighting. »

« To most people watching? You looked like something that refused to die. You are even crazier. »

Whatever.

Jin forced his attention back on Jack and noticed the man had a raw scar that ran from his left eyebrow down to his jawline.

"Sir Winters," the man said, offering his hand.

Jin shook it.

"These two are Gleeward and Barsta," Jack continued, gesturing to each in turn.

"Hmm. Brocki filled me in on the details," Jin said, releasing Jack's hand.

"I hope for good things." Jack's weathered face cracked into something that might've been a smile, and Jin chuckled a short, genuine sound that seemed to defuse some of the tension threading through the space.

"All the good things," Jin nodded. "Pleasure to meet you all. Not many would stand and fight, given the mess we're in."

"No one would," Jack said flatly. "But if this is how fate allows us to punch those damned cultists in the face? I'm all in."

"Totally with you on that." Jin agreed.

Jack tilted his head toward the reinforced door behind them. "Veric and Alden are inside. They've been at it for about an hour now." His expression shifted… something darker threading through his features. "With Miss Elenor's help, we managed to snag one of the cultists alive before he could bite down."

"A cultist?" Jin frowned. "Not one of the mindwashed civilians?"

"No, sir." Jack's expression darkened further, and Jin saw Barsta's jaw tighten in his peripheral vision. "This one knew what he was doing. Fought like a professional. We barely caught him alive after Miss Elenor knocked the bastard out cold, and even then, we had to pry open his mouth and remove the poison capsule from his teeth before he woke up."

"They're killing themselves?" Jin asked.

"Yes." Jack's voice went flat, cold. "But only if they feel threatened that any sensitive information might be compromised." He spat to the side, a sharp sound of disgust. "As for the mindwashed civs, they're in the other cells further down."

"Most of 'em are knocked unconscious," Gleeward added, speaking for the first time.

"That's the holding section." He nodded toward doors further down the corridor, partially visible in the dim lighting. "We had to bind them and knock them out, or else they'd hurt themselves trying to break free. Some would even die trying."

Interesting.

"Had two tear their wrists open on the bindings," Jack said grimly, meeting Jin's gaze steadily. "One dislocated his own shoulder and kept going, trying to wrench the arm out of the socket completely. Knocking them unconscious was the kindest option we had."

He hated mind magic.

"Alright," Brocki said. "I'll leave you to it, boss. Got to get back and make sure the rest of the base doesn't fall apart.”

Jin nodded. "Appreciated."

Brocki offered a half-salute—more habit than formality—and headed back the way they'd come, his heavy footsteps echoing down the corridor until they faded entirely into the background hum of ventilation systems and distant machinery.

Well, Jin turned his attention back to the door. Let's see if our cultist friend would offer us some answers.

« Agreed. His status should tell us something useful. »

If not, didn’t say my gear needed a little harvest?

« … »

"Show me," Jin said to Jack.

Gleeward pushed the heavy bolt aside, and the door swung inward with a low groan.

Jin followed Jack inside.

◈◈◈

Jin glanced through the first window as they passed, glimpsing the holding cells Jack had mentioned.

Inside, a man lay on a stone bench, arms bound behind his back with a thick rope that had already chafed the skin raw, head lolling to one side in unconsciousness. His face was slack, peaceful in a way that felt wrong given the circumstances.

"Assuming these are the mindwashed?" Jin asked, keeping his voice low.

"Yes," Jack's voice came out rough, threaded with something that might've been guilt or just exhaustion. "All of them in this section. We really had to knock them unconscious to keep them from hurting themselves." He paused, then made a swinging motion with one hand. "Don't have much sedative left, so we went the old-fashioned way."

Jin chuckled. "I hope you didn't cause any long-term injuries to them... Can't handle it if they all sue our asses when this is over."

Jack let out a surprised bark of laughter. "That's what you're here for, Boss. The paperwork and the politics."

"Wonderful."

Jin stopped at the next window. A woman this time, maybe mid-thirties, tied down the same way. Her face was slack, peaceful even, like she was just sleeping off a long day instead of being forcibly unconscious in a makeshift prison.

But Jin could see the raw skin around her wrists where she'd fought the restraints.

Deep abrasions. Some still bleeding sluggishly.

"As I said earlier," Jack continued, stopping beside him and following his gaze, "they don't really care about their own health once the eyes go full black. No self-preservation instinct at all." He shook his head, disgust threading through his tone. "We had one try to bash his own head against the wall until we got him down. Kept going even after the first impact split his scalp open."

He paused.

"Mind magic." The words came out flat, bitter. "Hate the stuff."

"Can't blame you," Jin said quietly, pulling his gaze away from the window.

They moved on.

The cells grew darker as they went deeper—fewer light sources, more shadows pooling in the corners like spilled ink—and Jin counted six more unconscious civilians before they reached the end of the corridor, where a final door stood slightly ajar.

Pale light spilled through the gap.

And the smell hit him.

Cigarette smoke.

Sharp and acrid, cutting through the stale underground air like a knife.

Jack gestured toward the door. "That's where we're keeping the cultist. Veric's in there with him now."

Jin nodded, and Jack pushed the door open fully.

◈◈◈

The room was small, maybe four meters square, with a single overhead light strip that threw everything into flat, uninflected white, which made the shadows look darker by contrast.

A metal chair sat bolted to the floor in the center.

And on it sat the bloodied form of the cultist, back facing the door.

Bound. Arms tied behind his back with rope, ankles lashed to the chair legs with what looked like steel cable. The cultist was a young man—mid-twenties, maybe—with dark hair matted with sweat and blood from a cut above his left ear that had crusted over hours ago.

But the second thing Jin noticed wasn't the prisoner.

It was the interrogator.

A middle-aged man sat on a wooden stool positioned directly in front of the cultist—casual, almost relaxed—with one leg crossed over the other in a pose that suggested he had all the time in the world. He was well into his forties, with greying hair slicked back from a high forehead and a face that looked like it had been carved from old leather. Deep lines bracketed his mouth and eyes, and a long scar ran diagonally from his jaw to his right ear.

The man—Veric, most likely—had a cigarette between two fingers, a thin thread of grey smoke rising straight up in the still air before curling against the ceiling. The faint smell of tobacco overlaid everything else in the room, mixing with sweat and fear and something metallic that Jin recognized as old blood.

Jin noticed a third person in the corner.

A younger man—Alden, presumably, based on Jack's earlier mention—sat on an overturned supply crate, watching the proceedings with misty green eyes that tracked every movement the cultist made. Early twenties. Lean build, not quite athletic but still fit. He was twirling knives between his fingers—two of them—spinning in opposite directions.

Show-off.

But an effective one, based on how the cultist's eyes kept flicking toward the spinning blades.

Jin was sure Veric had noticed them enter—the man was too controlled, too aware not to—but he didn't acknowledge their presence. Didn't turn. Didn't break his focus on the prisoner.

Jin caught the dynamic immediately and gestured to Jack—a small motion, barely visible—to quietly close the door and just observe.

Jack nodded and eased the door shut with barely a sound.

Veric raised the cigarette to his lips, took a slow drag that made the ember flare bright orange in the flat light, and exhaled through his nose. The smoke drifted toward the ceiling in a lazy curl.

He said something—too low for Jin to catch from this distance—and the cultist's bound hands twitched.

"I'll ask you one more time." Veric's voice came out calm. Too calm. The kind of calm that was more threatening than shouting would've been. "What are the orders for the infiltration?"

The cultist's jaw worked, muscles tensing under bruised skin, but no sound came out.

Just silence.

Veric sighed—long, like he'd expected exactly this response—and flicked ash onto the stone floor between them.

"You're making this harder than it needs to be," he said, still in that same deathly calm tone. "I've got all the time in the world. You, on the other hand..."

He tilted his head slightly, studying the cultist's face.

"Well. We both know how this ends."

A pause.

"For you and your cult."

The cultist began to laugh.

It came out wrong—too high, too sharp, edging into hysteria—and Jin felt the hairs on the back of his neck stand up.

"You would end my cult!" The words came out between gasping breaths, laughter threading through them like broken glass. "Hahaha! You think—you think you can—hahaha!"

Veric said nothing.

He just leaned back slightly on his stool, still holding the cigarette, still watching with those sharp, calculating eyes that missed nothing.

Waiting.

The laughter cut off abruptly.

"Sure," Veric said slowly, letting the word hang in the air. "Maybe not your cult. Maybe that's beyond us." He took another drag, exhaled. "But you and your brethren here in Vienna..."

He let the implication settle.

The cultist stopped laughing entirely.

"You do know what Commander Mathew would do to you all," Veric continued, his voice dropping even lower. Quieter. More dangerous. "I'm sure you've heard about his changes... haven't you?"

The cultist's eyes widened—just a fraction, just for a heartbeat—but it was enough.

Jin saw it.

Veric saw it.

And based on the slight smile that tugged at Veric's scarred face, he'd been waiting for exactly that reaction.

Fear.

The man knows exactly what he's doing, Jin thought, impressed despite himself.

« Indeed. The more skilled people we have in our employ, the better. »

Agreed.

"There it is," Veric murmured, almost gentle now. "You know what happens if you talk. Don't you?"

The cultist's face went white. Like all the blood had just drained out of it in one go.

"Well." Veric stood smoothly, cigarette still between his fingers, movements unhurried. Controlled. "Think on it."

He turned toward Jin and gave him a small nod—acknowledgment, respect, maybe both—then gestured toward the door with his free hand.

Jin held Veric's gaze for a moment, reading the exhaustion threaded through the lines around his eyes, the frustration barely visible in the set of his jaw.

Then he let his eyes drift to the cultist.

Angel. Pull his status.

« Understood. »

Jin took one long look at the bound figure and felt the familiar tingle of essence as Angel activated his appraisal ability.

The panel bloomed open in his vision.

•••

Corren Vasht [Cultist—Rite of the Veil Faction]

[ORDER II] E8 ENTITY

Status: Bound | Mind-Sealed | Fear [Moderate] | Essence Channels Suppressed

Mantle: Mantle of Varan, spirit of darkness.

Blessing: Minor Blessing of the Darkened One

Notable Traits:

└─ [Mind Seal]: Subject bound by high-grade compulsion magic. Automatic mental lockdown triggers upon approach to restricted information. [Tier III Construct]

•••

Jin held the information for three seconds, noting the rank, the faction designation, and—most importantly—that mind seal.

Well, harvest it is then.

He followed Veric out into the corridor.

Jack came with them, and Alden stayed behind, silent, watchful, keeping eyes on the cultist while still twirling those damned knives.

The door closed with a soft click that seemed too loud in the sudden quiet.

Veric was silent for a long moment, and Jin could feel the edge to his emotions even without reading his aura. Frustration, definitely. But also weariness. The kind that came from spending an hour getting absolutely nowhere.

"We're dealing with fanatics, Veric," Jin said, keeping his tone light, trying to ease some of that tension. "We need to keep moving forward despite no visible success."

Veric spat to the side and took another drag from his cigarette before responding.

"That's what we're doing, isn't it?" His voice came out rough, threaded with bitter humor. "And let me tell you, it isn't working." He exhaled slowly, smoke curling from his nostrils. "How long can we even hold on? How long can we survive against the never-ending forces of these bloody heretics?"

He sighed heavily, shoulders dropping slightly, and shook his head.

"Forgive this old man's rambling."

Jin smiled. "Nah, it's cool. Sometimes you just need to say it out loud."

"Gets it out of your system," Jack added from behind them, his rough voice carrying unexpected warmth. "Better than keeping it bottled up until you do something stupid."

That Jin felt was sound advice; he could do much better by remembering that.

Veric let out a short bark of laughter, and some of the tension bled out of his posture.

"There’s a Mind seal on our hostage," Jin said, pulling the conversation back on track. "Probably an Underlord’s work."

Veric's expression shifted immediately—back to business—and he spat again, the disgust returning to his features.

"Figured as much." His jaw tightened. "The moment I get close to something real—names, locations, operational details, anything actually useful—he locks up completely. Goes absolutely silent." He took another drag, the ember flaring bright. "I threaten to kill him, threaten torture, offer clemency... doesn't matter. The mind fuckery kicks in first."

"How much have you gotten?" Jin asked.

"Scraps." Veric's jaw worked, frustration threading through the single word. "Enough to know they're organized and that they've been planning this infiltration for months, not weeks." He paused. "But specifics?"

He shook his head.

"Nothing that leads anywhere. Every time we get close to operational intelligence, the magic triggers, and he can't speak."

Jin thought about that.

Mind seals weren't uncommon—plenty of organizations used them to protect sensitive information from interrogation or casual telepathy—but to get around that would be difficult using conventional ways.

Still, placing such a well-thought-out seal would not have been easy, Order IV or not. Which meant either Corren knew something really important...

« Or whoever placed the seal was paranoid enough to lock down everyone, regardless of their actual knowledge. »

Jin wasn't sure which option worried him more.

"You tried breaking it?" he asked, already knowing the answer but wanting to hear Veric's assessment.

Veric gave him a flat look that would've been funny under different circumstances.

"I'm good with people, Winters. Not magic." He gestured with the cigarette, ember tracing an arc through the dim corridor. "I can read him, pressure him, find the psychological cracks and exploit them. But breaking something like that?"

He shook his head.

"That's not my area of expertise. You'd need a specialist, and with our comms cut off and this place in such a mess, no way to get them."

Fair enough.

Jin glanced back at the door, thinking.

“Unless you are one?” Veric asked.

Jin laughed. “No, I’m not proficient in the mind magic field… but…”

“But?” Veric asked, hope crawling into his voice.

“I have one way, but it would most definitely kill the cultist.” Jin shrugged.

“But you would get the info?” Veric asked.

“Yes. I can not guarantee full knowledge, but most of it, anyway.”

Veric studied him for a long moment—eyes sharp, assessing, cataloging Jin the same way Jin had seen him catalog the cultist earlier—then nodded slowly.

"Alright. The bastard had long served his purpose." His voice carried careful neutrality. "What do you need?"

"Just time," Jin said. "Some quietness and I’d prefer to be alone."

"You've got it." Veric nodded.

Veric pushed the door open and gestured to Alden, who exited the room.

And after sharing a nod with them, Jin stepped through into the flat white light of the interrogation room.

Corren's head lifted slightly at their entrance, eyes tracking Jin's movement.

Jin met his gaze and held it.

"Let's try this again," he said quietly, as silver chains manifested around him.

"Shall we?"
