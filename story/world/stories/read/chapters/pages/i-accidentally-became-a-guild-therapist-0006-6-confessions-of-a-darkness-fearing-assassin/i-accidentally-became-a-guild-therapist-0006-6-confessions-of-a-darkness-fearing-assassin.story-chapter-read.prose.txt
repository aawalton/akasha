ARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia Marcelline Quinn, Mental Supporter Lv. 2 and reluctant internet celebrity, is forced out of her comfort zone when Nyx Shadowmint, the Ghost Rogue, asks for a private therapy session. Behind his absurd phobia—a shadow killer afraid of the dark—Livia finds herself facing deep trust and a potential crush that makes her NPC life far more complicated than it should be.

Livia Marcelline Quinn, Mental Supporter Lv. 2 and reluctant internet celebrity, was starting to accept that her life as Emotional Buffer 2.0 was a cosmic joke with no punchline in sight.

Her Therapist’s Log was a battlefield of scribbles:

Bron: Existential dread. Resolved (provisional). Watch for relapses.

Phina: Slime love. Ongoing. Possibly escalating.

Alaric: Gambling. Unresolved. He bet on his own recovery odds (3:1 against).

Nyx: TBD. Probably weird.

The Bloodbath & Beyond base camp buzzed around her in its usual state of aggressive existence. A slime near the supply tent was attempting backflips. It had been attempting backflips for forty minutes. It had not yet succeeded. It showed no signs of stopping.

Livia perched on her crate, slightly-less-itchy robe a small mercy, and watched the slime.

I am their mascot now, she thought. Flunked psych, memed across worlds, eight million ASMR plays, and currently watching a slime attempt athletic feats. This is my life.

[Achievement Unlocked: Resigned Celebrity. +10 Coping Points (Barely Functional)]

“Gee, thanks, system,” Livia muttered. Her progress bar read Mental Supporter Lv. 2: 200/2000 EXP. 1,800 to go. The new Charisma +1 made her words fractionally more persuasive, which had so far accomplished nothing except making Alaric slightly more invested in her opinions on his betting strategy.

Her brooding was interrupted by a faint pink glow bobbing at the edge of her peripheral vision.

She knew that glow.

Everyone in the guild knew that glow.

Nyx Shadowmint, the Ghost Rogue, materialized from the shadows between two storage tents with the practiced silence of someone who could go undetected in any environment—and who was currently failing completely, because the Hello Kitty flashlight he carried everywhere cast a steady pink beam that announced his presence from approximately fifteen meters away.

He stopped about three meters from her.

He looked at her.

He looked away.

He looked back.

He’s been standing there for a while, Livia realized. He’s been working up to this.

She waited. In her old life she’d learned, the hard way, that the worst thing you could do with someone who was trying to say something difficult was make them feel rushed. So she waited, and watched the slime fail another backflip, and let Nyx have his moment.

She was beginning to understand something, sitting there watching a rogue work up the nerve to ask for help. Therapy wasn’t always knowing what to say. Sometimes it was knowing when not to. Sometimes the most useful thing she could give someone was enough room to arrive at their own words.

He shuffled forward another step. Stopped again. His hood was up, which it always was, and his free hand was doing something complicated in his pocket.

“Uh…” he started.

Stopped.

“Hey.”

Stopped again.

Livia kept her face neutral. “Hey.”

Nyx stared at a point approximately six inches to the left of her face. “You’re not… busy?”

“I’m watching a slime attempt gymnastics,” Livia said. “So no.”

He almost smiled. It was a very small almost-smile, visible for maybe half a second before his expression went carefully neutral again.

“Can we…” He stopped. Started over. “I need to…” Another stop. He looked, briefly, like a man who had rehearsed this conversation and found that the rehearsal had not prepared him for the actual version.

“Talk?” Livia offered, quietly.

A pause.

“Yeah,” he said. “If that’s okay.”

[Achievement Unlocked: Brave the Awkward. +5 Social Points (Questionable)]

They found a corner near the guild’s storage tents, tucked between a supply crate and a wall where the rune glow was dimmer. Not dark—Nyx had angled himself so the flashlight covered the shadows directly ahead of him before he sat down, a small automatic motion that he probably didn’t notice he was doing.

Livia noticed.

She opened her notepad: Patient: Nyx. Pre-session observation: positions himself relative to light source before sitting. Flashlight grip: white-knuckle at rest, loosens slightly when talking. High vigilance. Treat carefully.

[HUD: Patient Stress Level: Elevated. Low-Light Sensitivity Detected.]

They sat in silence for a moment. The camp noise was muffled here—distant shouts about loot drops, the periodic sound of something exploding that nobody seemed concerned about. The slime was still attempting its backflip somewhere to their left. Still failing.

“So,” Livia started, keeping her voice even. “You mentioned the dark. Before, in the camp. You want to tell me more?”

Nyx didn’t answer immediately.

He looked at the flashlight. The pink beam cut a steady line across the ground between them, Hello Kitty’s cartoon face serene and unbothered on the casing. He’d had it for a long time, Livia guessed—the paint was worn at the grip, the edges softened from handling.

“It’s stupid,” he said, finally. “I know it’s stupid.”

“I didn’t ask if it was stupid,” Livia said. “I asked what it was like.”

Another pause. He was deciding something.

“It’s like…” He stopped. Tried again. “You know when you’re in a dungeon and there’s that moment before you cast a light spell? That half-second where you can’t see anything?”

“I’ve never cast a light spell,” Livia said. “My magic stat is zero. But I know the feeling.”

He glanced at her. “In that half-second—” His voice was quieter now. —“something’s in there. Looking back. And it knows you can’t see it but it can see you.”

Livia didn’t write anything. She just listened.

“I’m a Ghost Rogue,” Nyx continued, and there was something like shame in it. “I’m supposed to use the dark. Be the thing in the shadows. Every other rogue in this server thinks darkness is… home. A weapon.” He looked at the flashlight again. “I can’t even sleep without this on.”

The camp noise filtered through the gap between tents. Someone laughed loudly at something. An NPC looped its sales pitch.

“How long?” Livia asked.

“Always,” he said. “Even before this world. Even before…” He stopped. “Just always.”

Always. Not an acquired phobia. Not a specific incident. Always meant it was built into him somewhere deep, part of the architecture.

Livia wrote: Not situational. Foundational. The dark isn’t the problem—the feeling of being unseen while something else sees you is the problem. Hypervigilance. Threat detection stuck on.

Out loud she said: “Does the flashlight help because it gives you light, or because it gives you something to hold?”

Nyx blinked. Like the question had landed somewhere he hadn’t expected.

“Both,” he said, after a moment. “Is that… weird?”

“No,” Livia said. “That’s actually very sensible.”

He blushed. It was visible even under the hood, a warmth creeping up the visible lower half of his face. “You’re… good at this. Listening. Nobody else really…” He stopped himself.

“Does that, uh.” He was looking at a point to her left again. “Bother you? When people say that?”

Oh no, Livia thought. He’s not just making conversation. He’s checking. He wants to know if it’s okay that he said it.

He’s definitely crushing.

She kept her face professionally neutral. “It doesn’t bother me. I’m glad it helps.” She underlined the word professional in her head approximately four times.

—————————————————

The interruption arrived in the form of Miles Vexley, who appeared around the corner of the storage tent with the timing of someone who had developed a finely tuned instinct for the exact worst moment to arrive.

“Yo, Buffer!” His smirk was already at full deployment. “Hiding with Nyx? Got a therapy queue forming out front. Bron found another body pillow and he’s having feelings about it.”

Livia shot him a glare with enough force to dent armor. “I’m working, not hiding. And stop calling me Buffer.”

Miles held up both hands in a gesture of theatrical innocence and retreated. Nyx had pulled his hood lower.

Livia turned back. “Sorry. Where were we.”

“It’s fine,” Nyx said. His voice had closed slightly—the small opening from before had narrowed. Livia recognized it: interrupted vulnerability had a texture, and it usually required careful re-approach.

“We don’t have to rush,” she said. “This isn’t going anywhere.”

He looked at her. Really looked, for maybe the first time since they’d sat down.

“Okay,” he said. “Yeah. Okay.”

—————————————————

They tried the visualization first.

“Picture the dark as a quiet friend,” Livia said. “Not an enemy. Something that’s just… there. Neutral. The flashlight’s your teammate, not your only lifeline—backup, not survival.”

Great metaphor, Livia. You sound like a motivational poster.

But Nyx nodded, and his grip on the flashlight loosened, fractionally. “A teammate…” he repeated, like he was testing the weight of it. “Yeah. Okay.”

“You want to try something harder?” Livia asked.

His jaw tightened. “How hard?”

“Ten seconds,” she said. “That’s all. Hold the flashlight—keep holding it—but turn it off. I’m right here. You can turn it back on the second you want to.”

Silence.

The backflip slime failed again in the distance.

“Ten seconds,” Nyx repeated.

“Ten seconds,” Livia confirmed.

He looked at the flashlight. His thumb found the switch. He’d done this motion ten thousand times in the other direction—always turning it on, never off. His hand knew the switch by muscle memory. This was using that memory against its own grain.

He turned it off.

The corner went dim—not dark, not truly, the camp’s ambient glow still filtered through the gap, but noticeably darker, the shadows deepening at the edges. Nyx’s breathing hitched once, sharp, and then he held it.

“You’re okay,” Livia said, keeping her voice even. “I’m right here. The dark’s just quiet. Nothing in it but us. Count with me—one.”

“One,” he said. Strained.

“Two.”

“Two.” Slightly less strained.

“Three.”

He counted. His breathing evened out, increment by increment—not smooth, not easy, each number its own small negotiation with the thing his nervous system believed was in the dark. But he counted.

At ten, the flashlight came back on.

The pink beam swept the ground. Hello Kitty smiled up at them, serene as ever.

Nyx exhaled.

“I… did it,” he said. Quiet, like he wasn’t sure yet if it counted.

“You did it,” Livia said.

A small smile broke through—genuine, unguarded, the kind that happened before a person remembered to be careful. It lasted about three seconds. Then he looked away, and his expression settled back into its usual careful neutrality, but the smile had been there. Livia had seen it.

“Thanks, Livia,” he said. His eyes came back to her, soft and undefended for just a moment. “Really.”

Her face heated.

Oh no.

He’s definitely crushing.

He’s also seventeen years of dark phobia and he just turned the light off for ten seconds because she asked him to.

She was not equipped for any of this.

[You helped a shadow killer face the dark. +1200 EXP. Skill Unlocked: Phobia Breaker Lv. 1. Mental Supporter Lv. 2: 1400/2000 EXP.]

[Phobia Breaker Lv. 1: Reduces patient fear response by 10% during sessions. Note: Does not reduce therapist awkwardness. That one’s on you.]

Livia opened her notepad and wrote, very carefully: Nyx: Dark phobia. Foundational, not situational. Progress: significant. Ten seconds. Possible crush. Approach: Help with phobia. Absolutely do not address the crush. Deny everything. Maintain professional distance. Do not think about the smile.

She underlined do not think about the smile twice.

Then crossed it out.

Then wrote it again in the margin.

Miles reappeared as they walked back into the main camp, falling into step beside Livia with the ease of someone who had been waiting for exactly this moment.

“Nice work, rookie,” he said, and for once his tone was missing its usual edge. “Got Nyx out of his crate and his comfort zone. Both. Same session.”

“Ten seconds,” Livia said. “That’s not exactly a breakthrough.”

“For Nyx it’s a breakthrough,” Miles said. He wasn’t smirking. “He’s been carrying that flashlight for as long as I’ve known him. He doesn’t talk about why.”

Livia processed that.

“How long have you known him?”

“Long enough,” Miles said, which was not an answer. He glanced sideways at her. “Careful, though. Kid’s got it bad for you.”

Livia’s face went immediately warm. “Not my problem,” she said. “I fix phobias, not feelings.”

“Phobias are feelings,” Miles said.

Livia opened her mouth.

Closed it.

He’s not wrong, her brain supplied, unhelpfully.

Miles’ smirk had returned by now, softer than usual. “He trusts you. That’s not nothing, for him.” A pause. “Don’t screw it up.”

For a second, Miles didn’t sound like a guild master. He sounded like someone who had already watched someone he cared about leave once.

Then the moment passed, so quickly Livia almost doubted she’d heard it at all.

“That’s not an encouraging thing to say,” Livia told him.

“Wasn’t trying to encourage you,” Miles said. “Just being accurate.”

He walked off before she could respond, which she was beginning to suspect was a habit.

The guild’s chaos had reached its usual evening intensity: Bron wrestling something near the supply tent, Phina composing an ode to shadows that Nyx would never know was about him, Alaric having already opened a betting pool on the outcome of Nyx’s therapy session.

[Achievement Unlocked: Heartbreak Dodger]

[System Note: Current status: dodged. Future status: unclear. The system reserves judgment.]

Livia stood at the edge of it all and watched Nyx find his usual crate, settle into it, and angle his flashlight at the gap in the lid with the automatic precision of someone who had done it a thousand times. Pink light spilled out through the crack.

He looked over at her, once.

Looked away.

The flashlight stayed on.

I fix phobias, not feelings, she had said.

Phobias are feelings, Miles had said back.

Livia closed her notepad.

“I’m a therapist,” she muttered, to no one in particular. “Not a rom-com protagonist.”

The system, wisely, said nothing.

She was not entirely sure she believed herself.
