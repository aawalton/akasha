Ezra quickly inserted himself into the group meditation when he returned. As he had promised, he immediately set about helping Dirk with the aspects of moving aura to his inner world. It was interesting that Dirk, who was considered the least talented member of the trainees, at least among the commoners, was markedly more competent than even Leah, who’d been the best of the people in his elite squad back in Villy. Part of that was definitely the amount of time that the short, stocky man had spent training in sensing and using aura, but he was, in truth, only asking for advice. He was more than capable of doing what he was asking for help with.

After detailing the way that someone would have to strip the native affinity of aura before then shaping it once more into the affinity that suited their inner world, Ezra was mostly done for the night. When the other commoners pressed him for information about his new assignment, Ezra pushed them off for now, promising to tell them at breakfast the next day. When he retreated back to his room, he saw his armor and weapon, and happily set about making sure they had been perfectly maintained. From one day to the next, he had little opportunity to use the fancy equipment, since he wasn’t fighting enemies. Because of that, the armor and axe were both in perfect condition, and it was only a few short minutes' work to check and see that there was nothing that needed his attention.

You still didn’t tell Chloe about anything, Jormun said as soon as Ezra settled onto his bed.

“When was I supposed to?” Ezra shrugged, staring at the ghost. “In the middle of receiving a new assignment, just say, ‘Did you know that there’s a ghost in my head and he knows that the aberrant that we fought is actually a demon from another world, and if we leave them alone, they’re going to destroy everything’?”

It would be better than nothing. I do not envy your position of attempting to justify the reasoning for how you know something, but the fact of the matter is that you need to do something. There’s nobody else who does know what you know, and if you are too scared of potential social consequences to do what you need to do, then you’re a coward.

“It just doesn’t make any sense how I would be able to convince anybody of anything!”

As soon as he finished the complaint, though, a thought crossed Ezra’s mind, and he slapped his forehead.

A thought has occurred to you. Tell me.

Jormun seemed to sit down on the chair on the other side of the room as he said it, and he stared at Ezra expectantly.

“The compel truth spell. I could use that to begin a conversation. Even if they don’t take me completely seriously, I can give enough information for people to begin to do things. I don’t know if maybe people will think that I’m going crazy, but at least it’ll be a start.”

As he spoke, Ezra couldn’t hold still, standing up and beginning to pace back and forth in his small room. He could only make maybe ten steps in either direction before having to turn on his heel, and with how much his emotions were spiking, he found himself accidentally drawing small threads of aura into his legs. His steps lengthened, and he fought to keep from rushing out just to get some of his energy out.

That’s a good start, Jormun agreed. You do need to plan out what you’ll say, though. I don’t know what the limitations are for the spell, but you’ll want the Duke to know exactly what he’s asking when you’re there to make sure you don’t waste it.

Ezra agreed with that, and together, the two prepared a question for the Duke to ask to be confirmed as truthful when they met up on the next day. That took about twenty-five minutes before the two were prepared, and then, as always, they went into sword practice. Moving so slowly through different forms, having to stop himself when Jormun raised his own blade and would have blocked Ezra’s movement, was more exercise than the Ezra from a few months ago would have expected. He still teased the old ghost about his unwillingness to have done this half a year ago when they first met, but after half an hour of training, Ezra retired to his bed, sent aura circulating around his body several times to recover, and then fell asleep.

***

The morning came quickly, and Ezra, as he’d become accustomed to, woke well before dawn. As the summer came, he wondered if the morning conditioning would still come before sunrise while he dressed himself then followed everybody else down the halls. The ringing of a bell, loud enough to wake people up but not so piercing as to disturb everybody outside of the garrison, summoned the trainees for their conditioning. The other career soldiers had their assignments to train as well, but the trainees were so “lucky” as to have an additional half hour of training.

When Ezra hurried along into the square to join the rest of the trainees, Chloe caught his arm. When he stopped and looked at her, she shook her head with a laugh.

“Like we said,” she smiled, “you will be exempted from the conditioning training. That remains true, because today’s the day where you’ll have your first one-on-one with Dominick. Since you’re up, I have some ideas about what you should do, but we can figure out which in a minute. First, I need to reiterate—you need to be ready for this training today.”

“I was already nervous enough,” Ezra laughed. “Do you really need to terrify me like this?”

“I don’t know if I need to make you afraid,” Chloe shrugged, her expression not at all playful, “but I genuinely need you to take this seriously. Dominick’s not a young man anymore, but he’s going to push you hard. And, on top of that, there’s nobody whose opinion I care about more than his, so work hard. You’re getting this opportunity entirely because I’ve vouched for you, and if you don’t push yourself until you’re puking blood, I’m going to be, at the very least, disappointed.”

From some random guy, Ezra would’ve laughed in their face. From Tilly, he would’ve argued back. From people in the Dragon’s Toenail or from the army, he could’ve ignored it. Other than the Madam, Ezra wasn’t sure if he could say that there was anyone whose disappointment he could give a rat’s ass about.

Today, he learned that Chloe was one of those people whose opinions he treasured enough to actually change the way he acted due to what she thought of him. Instead of snarking back at her, he nodded and met her gaze.

“What was it that you wanted to do with me?”

She cocked her head to the side, staring at him. Evidently, she’d noticed at least part of his own realization and was evaluating him. Even so, only a few short seconds passed before she shrugged.

“You haven’t done hardly any training with polearms,” she said, gesturing for him to follow her. “The simple truth is that, whenever you’re going up against Dominick, he’s gonna kick your ass. There’s no maybe about it. You’re going to lose, and you’re going to lose bad. That’s fine, but having no idea how to even deal with somebody who has a much longer reach than you is going to be frustrating, to say the least.”

“I did see that he has a halberd yesterday,” Ezra replied, holding himself upright and following her. “What’s the difference between fighting somebody with a halberd versus a spear?”

“The reach is typically just a little bit better on the spear, but it’s more limited in only being able to thrust. The addition of the extra weight makes a halberd an axe, and its strikes are damn heavy. That goes for any stabbing as well, but mostly chopping. A polearm can be an armor breaker, and the extra length combined with the heavy weight means it can crack through defenses more than you might expect. You’ve already used your axe to good results cracking through defenses. You use one hand, Dominick uses two, has more experience, and is a better fighter than you. He’s absolutely going to bully you every time that you fight. He’s going to teach you well, but don’t have any illusions of being able to get an upper hand on him anytime.”

Ezra laughed a little, but thinking of how he’d seen Michel fight, he knew he wouldn’t have a chance of landing a hit on the man with the sword and shield.

“So what do I need to do?” Ezra asked as they entered a separate training pit.

“Be patient but aggressive,” Chloe explained, walking towards a weapon rack and pulling out a practice halberd. “He doesn’t mind taking a minute, or even two, to allow somebody else to expose a small opening. You don’t have enough experience to make sure that you’re flawless, so what I’m going to train you on right now is how to approach somebody using a polearm without immediately losing the fight. Just for a half hour or so, I’ll let you use your shield, but then after that, I’m going to take it away.”

“Why?” As he asked it, Ezra was strapping a shield on and pulling out an axe.

“Because he’s going to let you use the shield only part of the time,” Chloe replied, walking forward to stand in the center of the ten-meter-diameter room. She left footprints in the sand and invited him to warm up before beginning to practice.

“Nobody’s gonna let me take the easy way out, are you?” Ezra laughed.

“Nope,” the Pseudo-Knight confirmed. “He’s going to let you, and in fact force you, to continue to use the shield, but he’s going to make sure you get exposure to every weapon. Once you’re a Pseudo-Knight, his idea is that people can start to have a favorite weapon, but the duty for an Initiate and Adept is to try everything. Even an Adept’s inner world, though established, isn’t particularly stable, and, depending on how long somebody is stuck before becoming a Pseudo-Knight, there can be huge changes within their inner world before they’re anywhere close to ascending.”

“So is he going to be pissed that I have my yooperlite axe?” Ezra asked, though he couldn’t help but notice that Jormun had an approving tilt to his head as he listened to Chloe.

“No,” she shook her head. “It was his idea to conduct the tournament that he did. But that’s enough time talking. Come toward me slowly, and I will show you the usual polearm methods of attack.”

Ezra obliged, and was first treated to an overhand swing. As they were going at a slow pace, he raised his shield against the attack, the metal-banded edge striking the wooden haft of the weapon. No sooner than he had done so did Chloe aggressively push down and pull back with her weapon. The shield was yanked away from his body, and Ezra grunted as Chloe then shoved the halberd forward. It thumped against his chest, sending him stepping back from his approach.

“Of course, you’re raising your shield to protect yourself,” Chloe explained. “Make sure that it doesn’t land with the blade just behind your edge. Otherwise, they can pull you like that.”

“If I smash it up,” Ezra wondered as he stepped forward, “wouldn’t I just break the weapon and turn the battle in my favor?”

“Yes, but,” she cautioned him, “on real weapons, not practice ones like this, the haft of a halberd is usually reinforced for a ways down below the head for that exact reason. If you can hit your shield into the center of the haft, between the head and the person’s hand, that might be possible. Otherwise, flatten your shield so the blade doesn’t come close to you. Plus, once someone’s a Pseudo-Knight, breaking their weapon through the shroud is nearly impossible without exhausting their inner world, at which point you’ve already won the fight.”

He nodded in understanding, stepping back and repeating the motion as they came forward. Instead of just raising his shield directly, he curved it over his shoulder to instead let the weapon’s head sink against the shield’s wooden edge. He continued on towards Chloe, and she stepped back, shifting her grip to swing the butt of the weapon towards his right side. He tried to get his axe in position to parry, but compared to the easy grace with which she moved, his was rushed.

“Both sides are dangerous,” he muttered, more of a reminder to himself than anything else.

The practice continued on like that, with Chloe exhibiting thrusts, overhand blows, and wide swings. Each different opening to his approach required different defenses, defenses that weren’t too difficult with his shield but seemed basically impossible once he was left with just an axe, spear, or sword. It seemed like every time he was left without a shield, the poleaxe was perfectly made to screw him over. It could smack aside his spear, outreach the sword and axe, and he couldn’t ever quite get in range like he needed to be able to strike her back.

Regardless, she gave him patient instruction, the entire practice going slow as Chloe patiently showed him what needed to be done. By the end of about an hour of practicing, he had worked up a good sweat, but was mostly frustrated at the prospect of fighting against somebody at full speed like this.

“You’ve got the general idea of it down. Just keep learning and be patient with yourself.”

Chloe didn’t offer him any further instruction, instead simply giving him what was effectively a pat on the back before turning and walking away. He made sure to give her his thanks as he made his way towards the mess hall, where his fellow commoner trainees were waiting for him.

Telemachus was the first to catch Ezra, and as he caught the leader of the little squad, his demands for answers were enough to, perhaps for a short while, take most of the nervousness that was twisting in Ezra’s stomach away.

Only most. Chloe’s efforts still stuck with Ezra, and he wondered just what kind of training he was in for.
