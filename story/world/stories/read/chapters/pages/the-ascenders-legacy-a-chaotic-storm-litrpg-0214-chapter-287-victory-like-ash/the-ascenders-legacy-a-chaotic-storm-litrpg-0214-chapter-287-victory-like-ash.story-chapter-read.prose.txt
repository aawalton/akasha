Aodhán stepped into the battle ring against Mirith, heart pounding.

He had known Mirith was powerful and had even calculated the tier gap between her and Monica, but it was beyond chilling to see her abilities in effect. She hadn't lifted a finger. She hadn't even blinked.

And yet, she had ended what could have been an epic battle in a single second.

It was scary to see someone be mind-controlled like that, and if one factored in Aodhán's preexisting phobia of telepaths, then the right adjective to describe him in that moment was terrified.

Aodhán was completely terrified.

He couldn't even imagine how many of his secrets the telepath already knew just by being near him. She hadn't said anything yet, but her knowledge was obvious in how she called people by their full names without any preexisting relationship.

And who went around thinking their full names?

No, the telepath was digging, and more likely than not, she had already dug into his mind. His secrets had been plundered. His status as a transmigrant and, more importantly, his status as Az'marthon's legacy.

Those were his two biggest secrets, and—shit! He had just thought of them.

He glanced up at Mirith in sudden panic, but the telepath gave no reaction whatsoever that would hint she had heard or gained any new knowledge. Aodhán began to let out a sigh of relief when their gazes suddenly locked.

Mirith smiled. A smile so chilling it seemed to scream, I know all your secrets.

His expression tightened into a grimace, and he scowled. "Stay out of my head, telepath."

"I'm not in your head," Mirith responded, her smile widening. "But I can still hear… things. Bad things."

His grimace deepened as he remembered how easily she had captured Monica's mind. A mind nearly ten tiers above his own.

If Monica had fallen so easily, how much more him?

The familiar weight of dread settled in his chest, but Aodhán pushed it down with practiced determination. Unlike Monica, he wasn't going into this battle completely blind.

He was going in with a plan. And that plan hung on his neck.

He fingered the lightning pendant, not caring whether Mirith noticed or not.

She did, and her gaze narrowed into a frown.

Aodhán smiled. If the pendant could protect his mind from the whispers of chaos in the origin plane, then it should be able to handle one advanced class telepath. And if that didn't work, well, he also had more than enough willpower to spare.

He had come this far. The only way he was leaving this mountain without those pins was—well, not in a body bag, but he was certainly going to fight for it.

"Are you ready?" Baxter asked quietly, his gaze darting between Aodhán and Mirith cautiously.

Aodhán nodded. "I am."

Mirith gave a small nod in response, and after a short moment of hesitation, Baxter finally made the call. "Fight!"

The word cracked like thunder on the silent mountain, and yet, neither Aodhán nor Mirith moved. Instead, they watched each other warily, waiting for the other to make the first move.

When it became obvious that Aodhán wouldn't be falling into that trap, Mirith’s expression shifted, and then she moved—a blur of azure essence. So fast that Aodhán was flying off the mountain floor before he could even blink, his jaw cracked from the force of a blow that would have blown a weaker man to pieces.

His body slammed against the invisible shield like a bullet, ears ringing and bones rattling. Groaning in pain, he opened his eyes in shock, only to find Mirith floating before him like a specter of azure energy. She yanked him by the throat before he could even think of dodging, and before he knew it, he was flailing in the air once more.

Pain slammed into him as he crashed onto the mountain floor with the force of a meteor, the stony earth cracking underneath him. His ears rang, and his mind raced in panic, but amidst it all, one thought was crystal clear.

How was this possible? How was a telepath this strong? This fast?

"Common sense, Aodhán." Mirith smirked down at him, arriving before him in an instant. He tried to teleport, but a vise grip around his neck banished that thought before it could even fully form.

"Oh, you poor child," Mirith tutted with an amused smirk as her hands tightened around his throat. "You're not the only one who has learned to embody your affinity." Her smirk widened. "Regardless of your special attributes, you seem to forget that you and your brother aren't the only ones with any common sense in any given space."

Her fists clenched tighter around his neck, and Aodhán immediately phased, sinking into the mountain floor before she could seize him completely. However, just as his head descended below ground, he felt a sudden, near-overpowering impulse to go back up. To fight recklessly. Or to simply give up.

The thoughts entered his mind like the whispers of chaos, appealing to his ego and trying to manipulate his emotions. However, just as the impulse began to feel overwhelming, Aodhán felt a sudden warmth bloom on his chest, banishing it immediately.

Eyes wide, he glanced at the lightning pendant and sighed, grateful that it had worked as it should. He couldn't afford to hide here forever, but he needed a second to think.

For some reason, he had expected Mirith's combat abilities to be limited to mind control alone. She had even implied it at the end of her battle against Monica. But of course, she had been lying.

The woman had embodied the concept of thought itself—moving at a speed that was impossible for him to follow. And still, he couldn't help but think she was holding back.

He wasn't a threat to her at all. As if an eighteen-tier gap in advancement wasn't enough, the woman could take him out blindfolded. And in that instant, Aodhán realized he hadn't been thinking straight at all.

What in all the world had made him agree to battle the telepath after calculating their eighteen-tier gap? How could he have been so foolish? And why had everyone so easily agreed to it?

The answer came to him like a lightning strike, and he scowled.

Mirith had manipulated them all, nudging them toward a decision that most favored her. And they hadn't even sensed it. For some reason, they had all believed the woman would only use her abilities in combat, not during the planning and decision-making that was where any battle actually began.

They had all been played like a fiddle, and Aodhán hated it. This—this exact reason—was why he hated telepaths.

They were too unpredictable, too cunning. Aside from the fact that they knew all your secrets, it was near impossible to tell which decisions a person was making for themselves when they were near.

However, he had begun this battle. He had to finish it.

Lose or not, one thing Aodhán was sure of was that he was taking himself far, far away from her the moment this battle was over. He couldn't give up his autonomy for anything.

With that thought and determination burning in his mind, Aodhán rose back up to the surface, ready to fight the telepath with all he had. However, the moment he surfaced, he was met with the strangest sight.

Mirith knelt motionless at the center of the ring, arms at her sides. And as he emerged, she did something so absurd it sent ripples of shock through everyone present.

She bowed.

And in that moment, Aodhán realized he couldn't win no matter what.

Mirith was playing a game only she understood. And he hated it.

She looked up at him from beneath a dense coat of raven-black hair, an amused smile on her face.

Aodhán tensed, his lightning body morphing from silver to red in an instant. He was ready for an attack—expecting it even. However, as the seconds passed without any move from Mirith, he realized she had changed the entire game while he'd hidden within the earth for a breather.

"What is the meaning of this?" he gritted out, the sight of her bowing figure sending warning signals through his mind. "What tricks have you come up with this time?"

"None." Mirith's expression remained placid. "I have no tricks. Only a proposal."

"No," Aodhán shook his head. He wanted nothing to do with the woman. Whatever proposal she had, she could shove it up her—

"I surrender from this battle, Aodhán Brystion." Mirith's voice cut through his train of thought like a knife. She raised herself to her full height. "Or at least I wish to do so on the condition that you let me into your little group. I love your survival plan far more than that of Sebastian Rune."

"What?" Aodhán and Sebastian chorused at the same time and then glanced at each other.

"What plan is she talking about?" Daruk asked, but before Aodhán could even utter a word, Baxter growled out.

"No, Aodhán, this is a terrible idea."

Brennon and Destra echoed his words with their expressions, and Aodhán nodded. His friends were right. He needed the telepath far from him, not as a constant companion. Having her so close to him for the next twenty to thirty hours was a terrible idea—

Or was it? He suddenly frowned. The telepath had proven herself to be incredibly strong and powerful. Having her fight alongside them would drastically improve their chances of survival. Not to mention, it would also mean he got all ten abyssal pins.

Greed welled up within him, and his frown deepened. Was he putting his bias over his survival? Was it not better to work with an enemy if it would ensure his survival? Was it not—

His mind ground to a sudden halt, and he glanced up at Mirith, a new train of thought blooming. Was he the one currently generating these thoughts? Or was she implanting them? True, Mirith would be an excellent addition to the team, but could he risk living like this for the next few hours? Could he afford to keep second-guessing every thought, every impulse, and every decision?

Wouldn't that just drag him down?

Making up his mind, Aodhán shook his head. "No. I forfeit the pins. I want nothing to do with you."

"Then you leave me no choice but to present harder conditions, Aodhán Brystion." Mirith grimaced. Her azure gaze hardened, and when their gazes met, Aodhán felt a spike of fear suddenly skewer through him.

What other conditions was she offering now?

She was in front of him before he could blink, and in a whisper no one else heard, she answered his mental question. "An oath to keep your real identity secret forever."

His breath hitched, but Mirith didn't smile. For the briefest instant—so quick he almost missed it—her gaze flickered toward the horizon, toward whatever was coming. Fear. Genuine, bone-deep fear.

This wasn't a victory for her. This was a negotiation for her survival.

"If you accept me," she continued, her tone hard. "I will make the oath, right here, right now. If you do not, I will proclaim it to the entire realm to enact my vengeance."

Aodhán shuddered at the prospect. His breath caught, and for the barest instant, his face betrayed his anger.

Oh, she was ruthless.

Proclaiming his status as a transmigrant to everyone she met would ruin him, literally. He couldn't afford that. He couldn't afford to take the chance that people had bigger problems to deal with than burning a transmigrant at the stake.

If Mirith proclaimed his identity to the whole realm, then it would be him and Daruk against the world once again. And though they had survived this far, Aodhán would much prefer not to have every single person gunning for his life at every turn.

Still, the thought of spending more time with Mirith made him feel physically ill. It was torture, pure and simple.

The more time she spent in proximity, the more she knew. The more she knew, the stronger her influence became. Barely an hour in her presence, and she already had him by the neck. How much stronger would that influence be when she had spent a full day around him?

His jaw clenched in a grimace as he felt the curious gazes of people come to rest upon him.

Mirith's lips curved. "You're thinking only of the bad, Aodhán. I promised to swear an oath, remember?"

"I would have appreciated an oath that frees me from your searching shackles more."

"And yet, this oath is all I can offer." Mirith shrugged. "It is your decision. Now, decide, Aodhán. Time is running out."

He groaned mentally. An oath to never reveal his secret was a great one. But would he have to make this same oath with every single telepath he encountered in the future? No wonder transmigrants never lived long.

A growl escaped his lips, and he snarled. He had two real options here. Deny her entry into his group and let her proclaim what she would. Too many people in the world outside already knew. He would lose said group and any other future groups, but at least he would have his freedom—

"Wrong!" Mirith cut into his thoughts with a scowl. "I didn't just find out about your status, Aodhán. That is old news. In fact, I doubt I've learned anything new since your arrival on this mountain. Whether you run far, far away or let me join your little group, I truly doubt you could escape my sphere of influence. And I swear I'm not even trying."

His thoughts stuttered for a moment as he contemplated the meaning of her words. If what she was saying was true—and he doubted she was lying—then his secrets had never been safe. He'd been avoiding her for no reason at all.

Anger simmered within him at the thought, and with a guttural tone, he asked, "When? When did you know?"

Mirith raised an eyebrow at him. "If I tell you, will you say yes?"

He glared at her, lightning sparking in his eyes, but Mirith refused to be cowed. She glared back at him coolly and said, "Decide, Aodhán. We are making our audience very impatient."

He sneered at her, then glanced at the floating pins, eyes hard. His jaw worked in silence as his anger and frustration mounted. His fists clenched tightly by his sides, and when he finally made a decision, his voice was raw with fury.

"You are a vile woman, Mirith. But you've left me no choice."

Her expression brightened. "I gave you two choices, Aodhán Brystion."

"None that free me from your influence." Aodhán nearly snarled. Closing his eyes, he exhaled deeply. "Fine! I accept your surrender. Welcome to the team."

Baxter muttered a curse from behind him, and Destra glared daggers at him, but he had been left with little choice. Daruk was the only one who seemed to understand what had just happened, and when their gazes locked, he smiled sympathetically.

From the sidelines, Sebastian and Monica glowered at them, while Seojun simply looked relieved that the battle was over.

Mirith, however, wore an expression of pure, unadulterated satisfaction as she bowed and began reciting the oath. "I, Mirith Vaelys, do swear upon my core and spirit that I shall never reveal Aodhán Brystion's true identity to any soul, living or dead…"

Aodhán felt the weight of the oath settle into place an instant later, but the victory felt stale.

He had kept his identity secret for now. But he doubted this would be the last time he would be bending to someone's will to keep it so.

He was tired of it. The constant bending. The constant strong-arming. One day, he was going to snap and say to hell with it all.

And in that moment, that day didn't seem very far at all.

Sagging under the weight of his stale victory, Aodhán levitated upward to grab the ten pins. They hummed in his hands, notifications pinging in his mind as three hundred and fifty points were added to his total instantly.

But the victory brought him no joy. It tasted like ash.

Shoving the pins into his pendant, Aodhán began to speak, but his unformed words were cut off when Mirith spoke instead.

"How about I make myself useful—and share with everyone the little escape plans you've been keeping locked in that stormy head of yours?" She raised an eyebrow in question. "The more who know, the better, right?"

Aodhán hesitated, and then he cursed.

Somehow, he just knew this woman was going to give him a storm of problems.

And the worst part? He still didn't know if that thought was his own—or if she'd put it there.
