Chapter 72 – Fireborn

The Grove was quieter by morning, but no less tense.

A pale mist clung low to the ground, curling around thick roots and gnarled trunks like lazy smoke. Every breath came out in faint puffs of fog, and the air tasted of damp bark and pine. Light filtered through the dense canopy in silver threads, piercing the gloom in scattered shafts that barely touched the mossy forest floor. It was still early, but the world felt like it was holding its breath, waiting for something to shift.

Ezra stalked through the underbrush, footsteps careful and deliberate. His black cloak rippled slightly with each movement, frayed at the edges but impeccably clean, a contradiction that matched the boy himself. The morning light kissed the hilt of his dagger as he shifted his grip, the metal gleaming faintly. His face, sharp and pale, was focused, every feature taut with concentration. His dark eyes flicked from shadow to shadow, never resting, always scanning.

Marcel moved beside him, his longbow already in hand, string taut, a single arrow resting lightly between his fingers. A quiver bounced against his back with every careful step. His hair was tied back into a neat tail, face smudged with a line of dirt from earlier fighting, though his calm demeanour never wavered.

They didn’t speak for a while. The trees were dense here, tangled with hanging moss and vines like a net suspended between trunks. Birds called in the distance, short, chirping warnings rather than song. The forest seemed aware of their presence, watching.

Suddenly, a low growl broke the quiet.

Ahead, in a small clearing framed by broken stumps and thorny brambles, a group of orcs had gathered. Seven of them. They were ugly things, bulky, with gray-green skin and broad tusks jutting from under thick lips. Some wore crude armour made of stitched leather and scrap metal, others had no more than bone necklaces and crusted paint smeared across their faces. They argued in loud, guttural barks, their language rough and sharp like cracked stone.

“Not exactly graceful,” Marcel muttered under his breath. His voice was dry, with a wryness that only half-masked his readiness. “Barely worth the effort.”

Ezra crouched beside a fallen log, the bark damp and flaking under his glove. “Still counts,” he murmured. “Every mark adds up.”

Marcel twirled his coin along his knuckles. A nervous habit. Or maybe a lucky one. “You want loud or quiet?”

Ezra gave a brief glance sideways, the corner of his mouth twitching upward. “Just don’t dawdle.”

He moved before Marcel had time to respond.

Ezra shot forward like a knife loosed from a sheath, a blur of black and steel. He ducked beneath the low branches, boots skimming across the wet earth, closing the distance before the orcs even noticed.

The first one fell without a sound, its throat opened by Ezra’s curved dagger, blood spraying in a dark arc before the body collapsed. A second spun with a roar, swinging a rusted axe—but Ezra was already behind it. A flick of his wrist, and the blade sank deep between vertebrae. It toppled forward, twitching.

A guttural cry went up as the remaining orcs rallied.

Marcel stepped into the open. His bow rose smoothly, almost lazily, and loosed with a sharp twang. One arrow, then another. Each found its mark. One orc jerked backward, a shaft through its eye. Another dropped to its knees with a bolt lodged in its throat.

“I’m starting to feel guilty,” Marcel muttered, notching a third arrow.

“They’d do worse,” Ezra said, dancing around a wild swing and cutting deep into an exposed leg. “Think about all we’ve seen, all those people. That was monsters, plenty have died to their hands. No guilt from them, why should we be any different.”

Within moments, it was over. Orc corpses littered the clearing, steam rising from their cooling flesh. Blood soaked into the dirt, turning the moss to dark slush. The silence afterward was almost jarring.

Marcel lowered his bow, breathing steady. “That was disappointing.”

He wiped his blade clean on the orc’s tunic. “Let’s keep moving. Nothing special here.”

But before Marcel could agree, the entire forest shook.

It wasn’t wind. It wasn’t thunder.

It was a deep, resonating roar.

The sound came from the west, splitting through the trees like a rift in the air itself. Every bird scattered. The mist recoiled. Even the forest seemed to pause, as if the trees themselves feared what had just announced its presence.

It wasn’t like the howls of wolves, nor the guttural cries of orcs. It was something deeper. Wilder. Not an animal’s voice, but a creature of fire and sky.

Ezra froze.

Marcel’s head snapped toward the sound. His eyes were wide now, more curious than afraid.

“You thinking what I’m thinking?” Marcel asked, voice low but taut with tension.

Ezra’s answer came in a breath. “Big one.”

They didn’t wait.

Marcel jogged beside Ezra as they sprinted west, winding through the trees with practiced ease. Their boots crunched over scorched leaves and frost-bitten roots. The mist thickened as they neared the ridge, but so did the smell.

Smoke. Burning wood. And beneath it… something sweeter. Metallic. Like scorched copper.

“Smell that?” Marcel said, wrinkling his nose.

“Burnt blood,” Ezra replied. “Like a bonfire full of the stuff.”

As they reached the edge of the ridge, the trees began to thin. The snow was gone. The ground blackened. The trunks of trees were charred halfway up, branches melted into twisted shapes like clawed hands. The air shimmered with heat despite the early hour.

Then they saw it.

In the centre of a scorched clearing stood a creature that did not belong.

It was no larger than a horse, yet its presence dwarfed everything around it. Fire clung to its body like a second skin, flickering along the curve of its wings and the arch of its back. Feathers, not scales, adorned its form, each one glowing faintly gold and orange, as if spun from molten glass. They shimmered with every movement, catching the light and bending it, giving the illusion of motion even in stillness.

Its beak was long and sharp, tinged with a deep crimson at the tip. Its eyes, twin golden orbs, burned with something far beyond animal instinct. There was a mind in those eyes. Young, yes. But powerful.

And hungry.

Ezra’s gaze drifted downward, where the creature hunched over a body. A student. What was left of one.

The Phoenix tore a chunk of flesh free, steam rising from the wound, and swallowed it in a single motion. Blood sizzled where it hit the ground. Bones snapped beneath one clawed foot.

Marcel gagged softly, one hand flying to his mouth.

Ezra’s hand tightened around the hilt of his blade. “No…” he whispered.

The creature looked up.

It saw them.

And it did not flee.

Instead, it lifted its head fully and opened its beak wide.

A roar ripped through the clearing. Not a cry of warning or threat, but announcing its arrival. Fire poured from its mouth in a cone of heat, singing the air but stopping short of the treeline.

The forest behind Ezra glowed faintly orange in reflection.

“That’s not just any monster,” Marcel breathed, his voice hoarse. “That’s…”

“A Phoenix,” Ezra finished. His throat was dry. “A baby Phoenix.”

The young creature shook ash from its feathers and spread its wings. Fire crackled at the joints, dripping like liquid from the tips. It took a single step forward, talons sinking into scorched earth.

And it watched them.

Curious.

Evaluating.

Like a predator deciding if its next meal would fight back.

Ezra’s stomach twisted. “This shouldn’t be here,” he murmured.

Marcel, still staring in awe, shook his head. “A Phoenix... In Erundal? During the hunt?”

“It’s just a child,” Ezra said. “There are only meant to be a few in the whole world, how’d it end up here?”

The Phoenix tilted its head. Fire flickered from the corners of its eyes, not in malice, just an innate sense of both wonder, and hunger.

Marcel stepped back, getting ready to run and said, “A better question would be who managed to kill it. If it’s here as a baby, it must’ve been kept weak to prolong its rebirth until now.”

It took another step.

Ezra felt the threads of Essence pulling taut around him. The world grew quiet.

The fireborn had noticed them.

And it was still hungry.

Smoke rolled low through the grove, thick and acrid, curling around the roots of scorched trees like the breath of something ancient. The once-lush forest had fallen silent again, as if stunned into reverence. Nothing moved, no birds, no wind, no whispers, only the crackle of embers and the slow sizzle of flesh on blackened bark.

Ezra stared across the clearing, barely breathing.

The Phoenix crouched over its kill, golden feathers slick with gore, its flames now a quieter simmer, content, momentarily, in the aftermath of carnage. The body beneath it had already been reduced to indistinguishable pieces, bones peeking through seared muscle, clothing reduced to ash. A student, once. Now a warning.

Ezra’s mind raced.

They couldn’t win this. Not as they were. Not alone.

He grabbed Marcel’s arm and yanked him back, running for about a hundred meters before resting behind a towering oak, whose bark was blistered but not yet aflame. The tree groaned under its own weight, one of the last untouched sentinels of the grove. Here, veiled by mist and shadow, they had a moment. Just one.

Marcel was still wide-eyed, shaken but calculating. “We can’t fight that.”

“Not like this,” Ezra said, voice hushed but firm. His eyes flicked over the tree, the canopy, the space around them. His thoughts were moving faster now. “But we could… even the field.”

Marcel blinked. “You’re not thinking—”

“I'm saying we could Meld.” Ezra interrupted, his gaze steady now. “It would at least be a proper fight this time, unlike with those orcs.

Marcel hesitated. They'd only melded a few times in practice, and the hardest monsters they had faced were mere orcs. A phoenix was another story, even with it just having been born.

But the Phoenix... it wasn’t something they could run from. Not now.

He nodded. “Fine. But I swear, if we die, I'm putting that on you.”

Ezra cracked a crooked smile. “Wouldn’t be the first ghost on my back.”

They knelt, shoulder to shoulder, hands pressed together at the base of the oak. Ezra reached inward, threading magic along the channels of his mind. The connection opened with a shiver, first cold, then blindingly hot, as his awareness began to stretch.

Marcel’s mind brushed against his, wary but steady. A current of thought, emotion, and instinct passed between them like twin wires drawn tight. Their senses tangled, vision overlapping, reflexes aligning, breathing syncing into rhythm.

It wasn’t perfect. Not with all this stress. But the lines blurred just enough for the two of them to become something more.

One pair of eyes. One pulse.

Ezra’s body was the vessel. Marcel, the anchor.

Ezra swayed slightly as the last threads of the Meld solidified, the weight of two consciousnesses settling into his frame. The effort always left him winded, his body resisting the extra presence, like lungs expanding too far, but the power that came with it...

Focus sharpened. Every leaf, every ember, every flicker of movement in the distance came into crystalline clarity.

Marcel’s voice whispered through his thoughts. "Still hate this."

Ezra grinned faintly, already reaching for ferns and loose moss around the roots of the tree. “Just stay alert, and don’t start peeking around in my memories.”

“Can’t promise anything.”

He worked quickly. Covering Marcel’s now-limp body with brambles, leaves, strips of bark, anything to hide it from eyes that burned hotter than sunlight. It wasn’t perfect, but it would have to do. Time was a luxury they didn’t have.

Ezra turned back toward the scorched clearing, the Phoenix still feasting in the distance. His steps were silent, faster now, fuelled by the Meld. The edges of his vision pulsed with light, the colours more vivid than they had any right to be.

Every muscle felt coiled, every thought hyper-aware. Marcel’s combat instincts hummed beneath the surface, calculating angles, estimating distance, tracking escape routes. Ezra layered his own precision on top of it, and together, they moved as a single force.

The last stretch of forest was barely standing. Branches drooped under the weight of heat. Trees leaned in awkward, melted directions. The ground itself was raw, stripped of undergrowth, seared and smouldering. The mist had evaporated here, giving way to open air and acrid smoke.

He stepped through the treeline.

And froze.

The clearing had changed.

What had once been scattered corpses, blackened trees, and smoking roots had now been obliterated. A shallow crater of ash and bone stretched wide across the glade, the earth scorched to glass in places. The phoenix’s heat had flared in a short-range explosion, incinerating everything within a thirty-foot radius, trees, bodies, even the brush. Nothing remained but soot and molten stone.

The student’s remains were gone.

So were the other corpses.

Ezra’s breath caught. That much fire, from a creature that small… It shouldn’t be possible. Though then again, no phoenixes had been seen for at least the past decade, and even then, none had been fought for even longer, at least not that the public knew of.

The Phoenix was crouched at the centre of it all, wings folded now, flames dimmed to a faint aura. It looked... satisfied. But as Ezra took another step, the ground beneath his boots crunching against crystallized ash, its head turned.

It saw him.

Its gaze locked, eyes golden and alive. It stood with slow grace, feathered flames igniting once more, and turned fully to face him.

The fire rose behind its eyes.

And then, it stepped forward.

The moment stretched, time threading tighter around Ezra, Marcel’s thoughts sharpening in tandem.

The Phoenix had chosen its next prey.

Them.
