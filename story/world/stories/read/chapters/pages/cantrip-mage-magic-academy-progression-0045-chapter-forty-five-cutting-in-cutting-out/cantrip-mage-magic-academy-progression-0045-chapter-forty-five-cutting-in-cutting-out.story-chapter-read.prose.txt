Two minutes later, Patrice was awake and armed, Colin was mid-sketch, and Eola’s smallsword was in her hand, a Held Lighting Blade readied, and her wand prepped and ready to cast her new Cutting mark.

“Ready?” Patrice asked.

Colin nodded. “Fifteen seconds.”

“Ready,” Eola agreed. “Ten seconds, then stop, Roth.”

Roth nodded. He wouldn’t be joining their attack; he’d spent all his Mana keeping his attention-warding song up and buying Eola, Colin, and Patrice the time to prepare, plan, and recover themselves, and his soul-burned veins looked, if possible, even worse than they had before. Eola winced just looking at him, but they hadn’t had a choice. They’d moved the barricade blocking the door, and all that remained was to pull it open.

Patrice clutched the doorknob. “We’re going now!”

The door flung open. Roth’s song stopped. And a massive gout of fire ripped across the open study room. It scorched through Safe Shields, blew past wards, and jetted toward Eddarn—who rolled off his chair and threw himself behind the thick oak table. All around the room, the library’s spells activated, pulling air from the shelves and snuffing out any fire that got close.

“Y’aer’s curse!” Eddarn shouted as the fire died down. He drew his rusty sword and shoved Patrice’s hammer off-course. It crashed into the oak table with a horrible cracking sound. Behind her, Colin started to ‘purify’ Mana for another big cast. His sword was in his hand, but he hung back, keeping well away from Eddarn.

So did Eola.

The plan they’d come up with was simple. They’d force Eddarn to use his magic, and she’d disrupt it. While he was distracted from casting—and from whatever Eola was doing to his spells—Patrice would try to take him out of the fight with her pole-hammer. If that worked, they’d turn and fight the book monster. And if not, Eola would join the melee. Either way, they’d go fast, hit as hard as they could, and try to clear the room so they could get Roth to safety.

It was a good plan, but it ran face-first into two problems. First, Eddarn himself. He whirled and spun, rusted smallsword flicking out to draw the tiniest bit of blood from the gap between Patrice’s pauldron and breastplate before redirecting her hammer into a chair. The chair exploded. The hammer stuck in place for a moment, and Eddarn laughed, then coughed. “Oh, Y’aer curse it, the cult needs new blood with your fire, girlie!” His attention seemed locked on Patrice.

Eola wasn’t fooled. His eyes kept flicking between the three of them like he was constantly analyzing their moves and deciding who was the most dangerous. Right now, that was clearly Patrice, but when Colin’s Mana recovered, it’d probably be him again. Eddarn might’ve been a bit mad from his time in the library’s upper floors, but he knew how to fight.

Patrice’s hammer wasn’t enough pressure to force him to make a mistake.

And then there was the second problem. The book monster was moving, high up on the floating room’s fourth floor. The only reason it hadn’t joined the fight already was the wooden framing surrounding the highest floor, but it’d be past that soon.

Eola had to do something. If Patrice’s hammer wasn’t enough, maybe her smallsword would be. “I’m cutting in!” she called as she ran past wrecked furniture and shattered tile.

“Hold on!” Patrice shouted back. She swung her hammer, and Eola ducked as it whipped through the air. The shaft bent as its head slammed forward, and Eddarn whirled to the side to dodge it. More tile cracked.

Eddarn stopped laughing. His blade snapped down into Low Guard as Eola thrust at his face; her blow went off-center and caught nothing but air. Atta hissed from the room’s far side, unable or unwilling to close the gap.

“So, Tabby, you’ve got claws after all,” Eddarn said. He started casting—Binding Knot. If it finished, it’d ensnare one of them, and they’d be easy pickings. Eola focused on the spell, trying desperately to see the modifying marks. But just because she could identify it didn’t mean she could break it into parts in just a handful of seconds.

The Binding Knot finished. Mana poured into it. Eola threw herself to the side, slamming her already-bruised side into the broken table and bouncing off of it. When she rolled to her feet, she was still free.

But Patrice was down, rope wrapping around her arms and legs like a cocoon around a caterpillar.

“Y’aer curse you!” the tall girl swore as she struggled and squirmed.

Eddarn moved fast, and Eola threw herself between his sword tip and Patrice. It glanced off her breastplate, leaving a bright scratch on its silvered surface. Binding Knot didn’t last forever. Already, the magic was fading, leaving inanimate tangles of rope behind—but even that was enough to keep Patrice down for the count.

“Incoming!” Colin’s pain-tinged voice echoed across the room. Another gout of fire ripped across the wrecked study floor a split second later; Eola’s hair curled and smoked from the heat, and Eddarn had to retreat. The ropes smoldered around Patrice as she got an arm free.

“Get him!” she growled, shaking in fury. “No one does that to me! No one!”

Eola closed the gap. Her feet moved carefully through the smoking rubble as she tried to press against Eddarn. But he was already casting. She froze in place. Her wand came up as Eddarn’s next Binding Knot formed mid-air. Her eyes locked on it. Mana poured into it.

“Not going to dodge?” Eddarn mocked.

There! A single modifying mark appeared: Reaching. Good enough. Eola’s wand moved. She sketched her own mark, and part of the Reaching fell away. The spell wavered as Eddarn’s control over it slipped for an instant. Then, before Eola could shove her own Mana into the rune, he finished filling it himself.

Rope exploded outward toward Eola. She braced herself, bringing her smallsword up to cut through as much as she could. But instead, it fell to the floor, writhing and coiling feet from her outstretched blade. String Summon, not Binding Knot. Child’s Magic.

“What did…what’d you do to my magic, girlie!?” Eddarn stammered. The confident fighter he’d been a moment before disappeared.

Eola smiled. They’d as good as won—if Eddarn was an eighth-bar mage like he’d claimed, he had to be burning through it fast.

Then something slammed into her, and she found herself on the ground, two lines of pain across the back of her neck.

Eola’s breath left her lungs for the second time since the Exam’s beginning as the book monster slammed into her. It wasn’t heavy, but she hadn’t been braced for it, and the impact crushed her against the wreckage of the chair Eddarn had been sitting in.

They’d done everything right. She’d pressured the Scholar’s Cultist. Patrice had done her best with her hammer. Colin had used his Soul bleed to refresh into a second ritual cast more quickly than he should have been able to. They’d done everything they could to beat Eddarn.

And it hadn’t been enough.

She pushed the book monster aside and sprinted for one of the ladders. “Patrice, Colin—you’ve got him! I’ll take this thing!” It wasn’t a good move, but it was the only one she had.

Patrice shrugged off the last of the rope around her and gripped her hammer in both hands. “Be careful!” she yelled back. Then she was on Eddarn—who, for his part, recovered from the shock of having his spell Cut quickly. The two traded blows.

Eola only saw a moment of it. Her hand wrapped around a ladder rung, and she dragged herself upward. The second story’s wooden walkway looped all the way around the room, with a wide, open space right above the clustered study rooms; she headed there first as the book monster flew up to chase her.

Then she spun. Lighting Wing erupted from her wand and hit the thing dead in its book cover face. The leather curled slightly, but the burst of light had no other effect. It didn’t even slow the thing, and a few stray pages floated down as it surged toward her.

“Y’aer's curse,” Eola muttered. Her smallsword flicked up into High Guard.

She couldn’t beat it with her sword, though, and she knew it.

So did Eddarn. As the paper claws slammed into her blade, he coughed and laughed. “Tabby Cat, put that claw away and give up! It ent going to hurt my book, and your friend ent going to hurt me!”

“We’ll see about that!” Patrice shouted. The hammer whipped through the air loudly enough that Eola heard it, and metal rang against metal.

But Eola had other things on her mind. The book monster pinned her blade against a bookshelf with one claw and started to cast with the other.

Frost Lance.

The icy missile slammed into her breastplate. The ensorcelled, silvered steel held, but the impact right below her breast still hurt. She gasped for breath—she’d only just caught it again—and jerked her sword free. The blade cut across the monster’s throat, right below a hanging chain.

Nothing. No damage. No recoil. It hardly even got the living, half-man-shaped whirlwind of pages’ attention. Eola spun. Her smallsword punched into the monster again, just above the glowing purple ball of Mana. That got its attention, but it didn’t look hurt. Just angry.

She stared at it. It stared back.

Then she broke into a sprint. Distance. She needed distance. Her sword couldn’t kill this thing, and Colin’s best spell had barely scratched it. Maybe together, they’d have an answer for the monstrosity Eddarn commanded, but alone?

It started to cast again. This time, Eola recognized the Silenced modifying mark, and she quickly Cut it away. She expected to fight for the spell, then have to dodge the modified Ice Touch as the monster took control of it.

Instead, her Mana pushed into the spell, and it cast in place.

Ice Touch wasn’t a combat spell by itself. Even with three modifying marks, it wasn’t something most spellswords would use for one simple reason. It required a target to touch. Without that, it only created a cold zone around the rune.

But in this case, that was exactly what Eola needed. The paper monster’s pages slowed and cracked as snowflake patterns grew across them, the ice stiffening and transforming the pages below into a brittle mass of frozen liquid and mulched wood.

Eola turned, sprinted, and lunged. All her strength went into the one-handed blow, and it slammed into the small, frozen circle just above the swirling mass of Mana. The monster shook itself. Ice and cracked pages rained down onto the floor below, showering Patrice and Colin with freezing cold sleet. Eola spared a second to take stock of their fight. Colin was down, but he didn’t look injured—at least, not more than Soul bleed would cause. Patrice, though…

She had one hand on her hammer. The other was in front of her, and she was…sketching.

Patrice was using magic. The situation down below must be really bad.

“Oh Y’aer curse it,” Eola muttered. Then she started running as Patrice’s Bright Ball finished. But what actually cast, of course, wasn’t Bright Ball.

Thorned vines erupted from every corner of the ruined study room. They converged on Eddarn; his sword lashed out and cut through one, but another tangled around his foot and pulled him to the ground. They grabbed at Colin, too, and at Roth. Only Patrice was unaffected, and she looked horrified—and furious.

But before Eola could watch Patrice finish the fight, the book monster reengaged.

This time, it approached almost cautiously. Its claws probed, and it didn’t try to cast again. Eola clenched her teeth as she blocked its attacks, losing ground. A rolling set of wooden stairs led up to the next floor. She stepped onto them, counterattacking furiously even as she gave up step after step. Nothing she was doing had an impact.

But she wasn’t just fighting. Most of her attention was on the gap in the monster’s paper body—and on the spell she’d just identified behind it.

Three rings in Old Alemic—each with a modifying mark. Another mark, connected to the diamond core at its center. Delayed. Peacebonded. Personal. Permanent. The paper monster wasn’t a monster at all.

It was Eddarn’s familiar, and at its core was Call and Calm.

Eola laughed. Her dry throat couldn’t take it, and the laugh turned into a cough as she stepped onto the third story’s walkway and the monster followed.

She sheathed her sword and raised her wand as it lunged toward her, and, with a quick motion, drew a single mark.

Cutting.

On the Permanent modifying mark.

The day Instructor Tarik taught Eola and the other first year students how to cast Call and Calm, he’d said two things that had stuck with her.

First, he’d told her—inadvertantly—that First Order spells could be overloaded with Mana.

And second, he’d described the backlash Colin would experience if his attempt to bond with his yellow weasel failed.

Eola had figured out the rest in a surge of, to put it humbly, genius. One of the four modifying marks she’d used to recreate Call and Calm was Permanent. That was necessary because the spell and the bonding process that followed created an unending magical connection between familiar and mage. In effect, the spell was ongoing from the moment it was cast until either master or familiar died.

None of her books had explained this. She hadn’t even realized it until she identified the spell powering the book monster as Call and Calm. But it gave her a weapon, and she used it.

Her Cutting mark replaced the Permanent modifying mark on the Call and Calm spell in the paper monster’s core, and the Call and Calm ritual failed. When she did it, a number of things happened very quickly.

First, the modified Child’s Magic overloaded. Mana exploded outward, and the Bright Balls lighting the floating room winked out. So did Patrice’s Wild Roots spell, a dozen different Safe Shields still littering the mess of a room two floors below Eola, and, most importantly, the Binding Knot spell Eddarn was in the middle of casting.

Second, Call and Calm’s bond failed. A wave of backlash rippled through the air, and Eddarn screamed in the darkness.

And third, as the spell powering the whirlwind of paper failed, the monster looming over Eola lost cohesion, and the tightly-packed paper body fell apart. Parchment covered the floor, bouncing off of Eola’s armor and filling the air as it fluttered down toward the fighting below.

She breathed deeply, arms shaking. The back of her neck hurt from where the monster’s claws had ripped into her skin; when she reached back, her glove came back bloody. But she’d won. The paper monster was gone.

She turned to finish the fight with Eddarn; with all three of them together, they’d be able to beat him easily.

A Bright Ball blossomed below, and Eola caught a glimpse of a thin, pale-skinned figure dashing for a stairwell, rusted sword reflecting the nearly-white light. Then Eddarn was gone. A small part of her wanted to chase him down, but her stomach rumbled in protest, and when she checked her soul, she had nothing left. She began to ‘purify’ Mana, working her way down to the lowest floor as she did.

Halfway down, a bolt of bright blue fur zoomed past her. “Where are you going, Atta?” she asked without expecting a response, and sure enough, her familiar only stared at her for a moment, then kept running.

The fighting was over. They’d won. But when she got to the bottom, her sense of elation vanished.

Colin was down. His veins were almost as blue as Roth’s—the angry, dark-haired boy leaned against the wrecked table and stared at the chaos—and neither of them was in any shape to find the last two books. Patrice’s eyes met Eola’s, and she shook her head slowly.  She didn’t care about passing the Library Exam anymore. All she cared about was getting Roth and Colin to safety.

As for the other consequences? She’d figure those out, too. The combat track would be helpful, but she could find a way forward without it. After all, she’d gotten this far without Ordered Magic.

Eola nodded once and threw her arm around Colin’s waist, letting the boy lean on her for support. Patrice lifted Roth and slumped him across her shoulders, arms and legs dangling. Nera climbed onto the boy’s back, and Eola and Patrice started their retreat from the library’s third floor.
