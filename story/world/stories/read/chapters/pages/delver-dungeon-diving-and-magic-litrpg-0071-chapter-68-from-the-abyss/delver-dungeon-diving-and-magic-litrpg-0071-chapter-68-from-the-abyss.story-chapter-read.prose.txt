The first ritual of the Patari, The Blood Oath, is a simple, yet powerful magic. Two people swear upon their blood, then mix it in into a rune. This rune can be carved into the earth, into metal, or into skin. The container does not matter, all that matters is both people understand the depths of their vow. This rune is one most mages would not recognize, for it does not come from the standard language of magic, but something far older.

Blood Rituals Of Patari by High Mage Baramal

“Do you see anything?” I dismount the shadow mare, giving her a soft pat in thanks for carrying me. Her nose nuzzles my hand, and I scratch her ear idly as I look over to our scout.

Daron’s eyes stay focused on the distance, seeing something I can’t. “Nothing close.”

I give a sharp whistle, and moments later a black form flies out of the large gap in the earth, swooping down and landing on my outstretched arm. “A Worm rests deep down, but it’s deep in its sleep.”

I shudder. Yeah, I’d rather not deal with a Worm.

“Eyla, I think you can start.” I call out to the others. “Daron, keep an eye below us.”

The ice witch nods, then she approaches the gap, starting her magic. Grildor stands practically on top of her, ready for anything that might try to come after our mage. Daron’s gaze keeps that distance to it, somehow watching below us.

My gaze moves to the sky, where something large swoops through the clouds. A light rain, almost a mist, starts to fall, illuminated by the sun that somehow still shines through the clouds.

“Shar.” I call.

The bird turns to me, then follows my gaze up.

A wing cuts through one of the clouds, leaving a wake behind it. “That’s as big as I think it is, isn’t it?”

Shar hops to my shoulder, “It’s far from the mountains.”

“What is it?”

“One of the Leviathan Eagles.” She answers. “I didn’t know they came this far South.”

I watch the edge of it, hidden by the clouds, as it flies northwest.

“We’ve got something moving!” Daron’s calls.

Immediately, blades are drawn, and Eyla hurries away from the edge, Grildor covering her.

Shar hops to one of the trees, while I draw my own sword and activate Hide Presence, my left hand reaching for the wand in my pocket.

The whistling of nearby birds goes quiet, and Lady Kala’s people surround her. She raises her own claws, a faint shine covering them.

We wait in tense silence.

“It’s hard for me to see. They’re moving in the darkness.” Daron breaks the quiet, a slight frown on his face as his eyes flicker. “They’ll be here, in three, two, one…”

Nothing.

My increased perception sees a shimmer moving across the ground, and Shar screeches as she suddenly dives in front of Tanya, her claws drawing blood from seemingly nothing as the girl suddenly steps back.

“Camouflage!” I yell, swiping forward with my sword.

A limb falls to the ground, turning white as its blood leaves it.

Libby grabs me by my cloak a second later, pulling me away from something I can’t see.

Grildor slams his shield to the ground, a field of shields surrounding him and Eyla, frost covering the ground as she focuses, several spell circles surrounding her.

“Vishten Warrior! Make as much fire as you can!” The Training Golem shouts, and the Vishten warrior doesn’t hesitate as his tattoos ignite, his dual swords bursting into flames. He steps forward and disappears, a trail of fire following him as he extends blades of fire from his swords.

The fire catches on limbs that have surround us, burning the camouflaged skin so that we can see it.

“Everyone! Back away!” Eyla calls, and the whole group listens, as a storm of ice blades fill the air behind the mage.

Her eyes glow bright with mana as she finishes the spell, and the blades fly forward, cutting through and stabbing the limbs to leave only bloody and dead things on the ground.

A screech echoes from the earth below as a mouth of many teeth rises from the ravine.

Keve dashes, appearing on top of the thing as his swords stab down, flames extending from him in waves. Then he’s gone, a trail of fire following him as a large, barely visible limb swipes through where he’d just been.

Eyla forms another spell, and Ice Spikes stab up from the ground into the creature’s limbs, holding them in place for precious seconds.

“Commander!” Libby yells.

Vakar seems to understand, signaling forward with his hand.

Then the Dralk hit the thing.

Their claws cut through it with ease, their swords cutting it into pieces as they dismantle it in one fell swoop.

The creature falls, its limbs cut further as its weight drags it off of Eyla’s ice.

Several thumps echo out as it hits the earth multiple times on its fall, rocks following it into the abyss.

Then it stops.

A rumble shakes the earth.

My eyes widen, as I run to Jasper, where the Angel Statuette rests. “Everyone to me!”

No one hesitates as the group collapses on me. I activate the statue.

The earth continues to rumble, our bodies shaking as my eyes stay on the ravine.

The earth erupts from our side, throwing our group backwards as Grildor’s large flickering shield appears above us, catching several rocks before they can crush our group.

Silver hairs cover the creature, sensitive things that sense every single movement. Its mouth opens wide as a roar shakes the forest, the Worm’s terrifying visage showing far too many teeth designed for destroying the earth below us.

Then it dives back into the earth, a portion of the forest disappearing to its maw as it dives back into the depths. Its hairy body flows past us as the earth shakes with its movement.

I hold the statuette tight as it disappears, not daring to make a movement until its gone.

The earth continues to shake as it moves below us, returning to whatever depth is natural for it.

“Mata!” Lady Kala’s voice turns my gaze to one of the Dralk who hadn’t made it under Grildor’s shield.

Immediately, Libby is beside them, holding their hand as her power flows through their body.

I step closer, noticing how their lower half is crushed under a large rock. It’s the same Dralk Scout who’d been talking so comfortably with Daron just a few days ago.

“Shar, keep an eye out.” I say quietly, and the bird takes to the sky.

Commander Vakar and Grildor lift the rock off the body, as the Dralk gasps for breath.

I watch for a long second as Libby does her best to bring the man back from the brink of death.

Then I’m running to one of the horses, all of whom had avoided the catastrophe somehow, aside from a little bit of dust.

I know exactly where it is, and I find it quickly.

Identify Artifact confirms my thoughts.

Pill Of Reinvigoration

This potion boosts the soul and body of a being, helping them resist various afflictions.

I slip my glove off, and put on a ring I keep in my pocket, as I run back to the man. Tanya is already having him drink one of her potions.

Healing magic isn’t something I’ve had the chance to study too intensely. It’s an aspect of magecraft that the books say require a lot of study, understanding the human body and the spirit from years of learning.

I don’t have that.

I take a pill, and plop it into his mouth, Libby looking up at me. Immediately, his body starts glowing.

I reach for the wand in my pocket, but Libby’s hand on my arm stops me.

“The pill will be enough.” She says, then turns back to the man, the wounds across his body closing as bones snap back into place. “I can guide it.”

I look up from the man, and immediately meet the gaze of a woman behind a golden mask.

Lady Kala tilts her head, looking at where my hand grasps something under my cloak.

I let go of the wand, and pull my hand out.

“Daron, is there anything else coming from below?” I call.

There’s no response for a moment before, “No, the worm scared everything away.”

I stand up, and motion to Eyla. “We need to get moving, the blood will call scavengers soon.”

The presence of the Worm likely scared away most things, but they will return.

Eyla immediately moves back to the scar in the earth, Grildor watching her as they start to build over it once more.

I wipe my bloodied hands on my shirt, then make my way to the hole the Worm had come from. Another wound in the earth, leading deeper than I’d like to know.

Some time later, Eyla announces she’s done, and I hurry everyone across the gap, before having Eyla destroy the bridge of ice she’d made.

It’s good timing too, as creatures start to emerge from the forest, feasting on the severed limbs of whatever had crawled from the earth on our first attempt.

I don’t let us camp there, instead forcing us down the road some ways until we find a clearing I remember from our last trip.

There, I set up the statuette, and hide us from the world once more.

The Dralk scout who’d injured his legs has some trouble walking as he makes his way to the fire. But he refuses the help of his companions, instead forcing his way through the pain until he’s sitting next to Daron. The young mercenary offers him something that definitely isn’t water.

Normally, I’m against drinking in the wilderness, but I’m definitely not going to say anything to the two of them.

“I’ve heard stories.” Lady Kala says, drawing the group’s gaze. Her gaze remains on the injured Scout, her claws biting into the log she sits on. “But it is different seeing it. The wild.”

I pick up a log, and feed it into the fire, causing several sparks to fly as I push the coals under Tanya’s pot of soup. “The forest can be the most beautiful thing you’ve ever seen, giving you food, water, shelter without asking anything in return. The next second, it’s terrifying, dragging you down into the darkness, where diamond-bears wait to tear you limb from limb.” I let out a small laugh, “There’s a reason a lot of Explorers are seen as mad.”

“No.” Lady Kala shakes her head, “We- I have great respect for Explorers, the camadar who leave our home.”

I smile in thanks. I turn away to accept a bowl of soup from Tanya.

“I am just… unsettled.” She looks to Commander Vakar.

“The Forest protects us from wild beasts.” The Commander says. “They stay wary of the mushrooms. And the Great Tree commands those under its roots.”

“The Great Tree?” Eyla asks, sitting down beside me and accepting a bowl herself.

I pause in slurping some of my soup, looking up at the Dralk. I hadn’t heard the term before, although I can guess what it’s referring to.

“Yes, the tree watches over our forest, living in harmony with the plants within. It guards our home, thanks to the sacred druids who speak with it.”

I remember seeing it myself, when walking with my Master not too long ago. It was larger than any of the other trees around it, but other than that, it had been a… well, a tree.

A hand finds mine, and I look over to Eyla as she listens to the Dralk, her fingers curling around mine.

Her eyes might look cold to another, but I see the warmth underneath, and that fact makes me smile. My smile fades as I think about the life I live, and the danger it puts both of us in. Forget evil wizards inside the Stronghold. There are forces of nature out here that could take either of us away in a moment.

I squeeze tight, and look to the fire. But that’s life, isn’t it? Sometimes things disappear in a moment, and that’s why you have to appreciate them while they’re here, in your hands.

I snort to myself, drawing her attention for a half-second before she looks back to the Dralk, asking another question.

I continue to eat with one hand, steadying the bowl between my legs.
