“What is happening?” a woman asked in a hushed voice as she squeezed through the rows of velmoryns gathered before the Crimson Guardian.

“Haven’t you heard?” a man replied, covering his mouth with one hand as he leaned closer. “The Vael of the Silver Tribe was captured last night. Apparently, she killed someone.”

“Killed someone? High Father have mercy on us…” the woman exclaimed, tracing the symbol of a tree across her chest. Then she frowned, her attention settling on a familiar figure near the front. “But why is Freya there?”

“Freya?” The man rose onto the tips of his toes, trying to see past the rows of velmoryns between him and the captives. He was shorter than the woman and had nowhere near as clear a view.

“Yes, Freya. Dariel’s wife.” The woman’s frown deepened. “Don’t tell me they both…”

“Shush. High Vael is about to speak,” an impatient voice interrupted from behind them.

Indeed, Othrien slowly stepped away from the other council members. Usually, it was Tekla who addressed the people on occasions like this.

“I am certain many of you have already heard some version of what happened,” Othrien began, his voice carrying over the crowd. “Yesterday, we were forced to capture several velmoryns who had been plotting against the Priestess and the order of Eriavel. We believed their desire for greater power had led them down a path toward ruin, that they had betrayed us.”

Othrien paused, allowing whispers to spread through the gathered velmoryns as he carefully studied their reactions. Some showed clear shock and disbelief. Most looked angry, while a few seemed… disappointed.

Yes. Disappointment was probably the best way to describe their tightly pressed lips and slumped shoulders, as though they felt helpless before what had happened, or had simply resigned themselves to it.

“That was our initial conclusion,” Othrien continued. “We assumed they had chosen a path against our Lord and betrayed our people. However…”

His gaze shifted toward Aria, who stood a few steps away like a pale statue, staring strangely at Freya and Nelya.

“Lady Aria confirmed, after examining their minds, that their intentions were more… complicated than we first believed.”

That was Aria’s cue to share what she had discovered.

She stepped forward, though only after giving a faint shake of her head, her gaze lingering on the two captives.

The rope binding Freya’s hands was held by Dariel, whose forehead was still lined with bulging veins. The burly man looked exhausted and furious, but more than anything, he looked ashamed. Once the council had confirmed that Freya would be judged publicly and made their minds on the punishment, his only request had been that their daughter be spared from witnessing her mother in such a state.

Nelya, on the other hand, knelt in front of Lucas.

Unlike Dariel, whose face was a map of conflicting emotions, Lucas showed only disgust, betrayed by the faint curl of his lip whenever he looked at her.

“Our Lord’s Sword granted me a vision and showed me what they were scheming,” Aria began. “When I confronted them, they swore they had never intended to betray High Father. They swore they wanted nothing but what was best for our people. And after examining their minds, I can say with certainty that they never intended to act against our Lord’s will…”

Aria paused.

Her lips parted as though she intended to continue, but no words came.

She looked conflicted.

Until now, no matter what happened, Aria had always seemed certain of herself and her decisions. She always knew what was right, or, at the very least, what she believed was right.

Not anymore.

“If they never intended to betray You, if they never meant to act against Your will, then why did Your Sword show me that vision? Why did he order me to capture them?”

Her thoughts began to spiral as she stepped back behind the high-ranking velmoryns, all of whom stood together as if to emphasize the gravity of the moment and to make it clear that none of them stood beside Freya and Nelya.

“Now that I have exposed them before everyone, simply letting them go is no longer an option. But is death not too harsh a punishment? Does Your commandment not teach us to cherish our brethren and protect their lives? Is this… empathy? Why did You make me understand something like this now? I need Your guidance, High Father…”

Before moving fully behind the others, Aria glanced at Dariel once more.

He still held the rope binding his wife, his gaze fixed on the ground with such intensity as though he wanted to dig a hole with his eyes and disappear into it.

Aria was worried about what Dariel might do if the council truly decided to execute the rebels.

She was about to find out.

Tekla stepped forward to address the velmoryns next. She still wore the same clothes as the previous night, dark circles beneath her eyes betraying the fact that she had not slept.

“You must have many questions,” Tekla said in a soft voice, though exhaustion weighed on it more heavily than usual. “I cannot answer all of them right now, but in time, everything will become clear.”

She paused, giving the velmoryns a chance to voice their disapproval if they wished.

No one dared speak against the Priestess.

“The punishment for betrayal is death,” Tekla said firmly. “Those who harm our people, and those who stray from the path High Father has set before us, shall not be forgiven. But are these velmoryns traitors? Have they truly betrayed our Lord? Have they betrayed Eriavel?”

“They have!” someone shouted from the crowd.

The accusation was quickly joined by others. Soon, most of the gathered velmoryns were demanding an execution. Some merely shouted in anger, while only a very few found the courage to call upon the Priestess for mercy.

Tekla’s brows furrowed as she raised one hand, silencing the crowd.

“I hear your anger. I also felt that way. But I do not doubt Aria’s magic. If she says the captives are telling the truth, then I trust her.”

Her words caused surprised glances to pass through the gathered velmoryns. Even Freya and Nelya looked taken aback.

“I cannot forgive them, because I called them my sisters and they schemed behind my back. I cannot forgive them because they were indeed seeking more power for themselves. But not every crime should be answered with blood. Not every crime should be answered with hatred or banishment.”

She gestured for Othrien to stand beside her.

“All five of them shall be stripped of their standing and wealth, save for the homes they live in. From this day forward, they shall take on the duties no one else wishes to perform - cleaning the canals and minovar dens, and carrying food and supplies to our scouts and workers near the new lake…”

I am all for not killing them, but isn’t that too lenient?

The velmoryns had seemed almost disturbingly harsh when I first saw how easily they executed a man for blasphemy. But now, they seemed far too forgiving.

Even if Freya, Nelya, and the others had never intended to betray me or go against Tekla, they had still planned to remove Othrien and seize control over Eriavel. That alone seemed serious enough to warrant a harsher punishment, if only to make an example of them.

Well, letting Tekla and the others handle this was my idea. Now I have to trust them enough to deal with the consequences of their own decisions. In the end, whatever happens, no velmoryn can ever disobey my will. If things ever go too far, I can always intervene.

“But,” Tekla continued, “this is not the only reason I gathered everyone here today.”

She turned around.

A young boy, the only apprentice Gundir had accepted, hurried toward her carrying a crown.

It had been shaped from one of the Crimson Guardian’s roots, its surface a vibrant scarlet, with six sockets carved around it for gemstones.

Five were already filled.

I recognized them immediately.

Each gem represented the highest authority of the old tribes, the stones once carried by their Vaels. One of them was the gem I had accidentally acquired from Cellia’s corpse. She must have stolen it from the Yellow Tribe and had intended to sell it to Akrion.

Only the Blue Tribe’s gem was missing.

Tekla had apparently left the sixth socket empty on purpose, to make it clear that one day, even that final stone would find its rightful place.

“I had planned to hold this ceremony after the war was over, when we could finally enjoy peace and prosperity,” Tekla said. “But what happened recently showed me that everyone needs to be reminded that High Vael Othrien has my blessing, and, more importantly, our Lord’s.”

Tekla stepped beside Othrien and slowly placed the crown on the old velmoryn’s head. She had to rise onto the tips of her toes to reach him, which made the moment look slightly awkward. Then again, asking the old man to kneel probably would have looked even stranger.

But to me, the scene held an entirely different meaning.

As I watched through the Window, I noticed a faint connection between the gems.

I had seen each of them countless times before, yet not once had I sensed anything unusual from them. Now that they had been brought together, however, I could definitely feel something.

It was divine energy. Extremely faint. But undeniably there.

More importantly, it was familiar.

Guidance.
