While he still intended on this being only a spar, Henry channeled all of what he had left in the Predation Aspect and empowered one last injection of his personal venom. He was sure the lobster could still take it. He could have used Dancing Shadows and Thaumaturgy as well but… that might be overkill, and the fight wasn’t over. But once it was, one way or another, he couldn’t afford to be left empty handed, with only the stashed essence in his Maw. That stuff was fine when he was working on projects, but it wasn’t the most practical in the heat of the fight.

The lobster screeched, enraged, and it finally dawned on it that keeping Henry’s shapeshifted lance in its guts was a bad call. As it angled its larger pincer down to tear into him, Henry braced and activated his teleportation. He’d delivered enough venom. He was good to go.

The world flicked around him as he appeared a short distance away, but the change of scenery was unfortunately coupled with sudden sting and weightlessness as his improvised spear failed to leave along with him.

Looking down, he saw the leviathan teeth peeking out of the lobster’s back. But on the other side, dark blue blood bloomed, coloring wafting up and around the lobster as if a chimney was under it.

It had managed to sever the arm.

Oh well. It was worth it, Henry thought as he vaguely sensed the malicious toxin he’d left in the creature. Already, he could sense some of its stamina and mana leeching back into him. Looking down at his burnt and severed arms, he gave the green Octomind some more of his Lifeweaving essence, helping it counter the foreign essences that still floated around his body. His use of mineral-rich coating had prevented most of the damage from the electricity, but it had still come with some essence, and the improvised Faraday shield couldn’t do much about that.

But… this is far from over, Henry thought as he sensed what his toxins were telling him. While the lobster did not look in great shape, Henry could sense it still had plenty of mana and stamina to continue putting up a fight and well… maybe it was time to pull back? Henry had already proven what he came to prove. He could stand up to the weaker an A-ranks. Trade blows. Land solid hits. Sure, the lobster was still standing. But it was hurt, and Henry could have done much more damage if he’d wanted this spar to be lethal. But he honestly didn’t, and he was happy with how it had gone. He'd gained a mountain of Aspect levels. Got some loot. For all intents and purposes, his mission was accomplished. Killing it would just be... overkill. Literally. While I wouldn't say no to the core, Henry was still the aggressor here. He didn’t that on his consciousness.

Eying the lobster below, Henry decided to extend an olive branch. “[Hey! Do you wanna call this a draw? No one needs to die, you know? We could call this a spar. It was fun.]”

The lobster seemed to still for a second, then it clicked its pincers, its form glowing. Coiling its tail, it sprang up toward Henry, the sand exploding behind from the violent push, and Henry just teleported away, summoning a couple dozen more clones for safety.

Being stubborn I see, Henry mused. Fine, let’s keep going for a bit.

Henry looked down calmly at the lobster as his limbs finished regrowing and this time, he didn’t create a stabbing instrument. Instead he fused not two, not three, but four of his arms together to create a club nearly as large as he was. Coating with multiple inches’s worth of thick, heavy bone, Henry pulled the arm back and waited.

“Speed up my perception,” he called out to his Octominds. Henry began layering the Evershifting Strike in the arm as he watched, and when the lobster’s tail began to curl, Henry pushed more mana and Dancing Shadow essence into the skill nexus, both to empower the impact and make the swing even faster.

The lobster blurred, body crackling, and Henry swung.

Henry’s club moved as if there hadn’t been any water to slow it, swinging up and to the right in a diagonal as the lobster quickly closed the distance, and with his improved cognition and Octominds-empowered vision, Henry had the time to see the A-rank slowdown as it raised its pincer to parry the club. But its own momentum and Henry’s swing—just like the lobster’s dash—had been incredibly fast, and as everything happened in less than a tenth of a second, there was just no time to abort or change course.

The die had been cast.

So it was no surprise when Henry felt the mighty shockwave rippling up his arms and body, soon followed by the impact that might have killed all the nerves extending down that specific arm. His club hit the shell and crunched into the lobster’s legs with enough power that the water superheated around the impact. The crunching of the lobster’s shell was too loud and short to be able to tell the extent of the damage, but the cloud of blood, bones and blue chitinous fragments told him that neither Henry’s armored coating or the lobster’s side had come out unscathed.

As the numbness slowly twisted into ever-growing, dull pain, Henry teleported away. With the eyes of his clones, he waited, panting as he helped his healer-Octomind re-knit his torn muscles. He also recreated the thick bones around the club, just in case, and sensing his dwindling mana, he summoned one of his healing orbs and popped it in his mouth.

As the stored mana and health flooded in his system, Henry watched the cloud of blood part, and the lobster was once more revealed.

It did not look great.

Ouch, Henry winced as he noted the crushed legs and the gaping, exposed innards where the large claw must have impacted. It looked like it had tried to stop Henry’s club with its left claw, only for the claw to turn into a weapon itself, blasting and cracking the shell it was supposed to protect. The pincer itself was deformed, but not cracked, while the left antenna had a nasty bend to it.

I have a feeling it won’t be charging me in a straight line anymore… unless it’s a lot dumber than I thought, Henry thought. More of the lobster’s strength flowed into Henry, but the effect was dwindling as the A-rank’s natural resistances slowly broke down the foreign influence, though with this impact, he could feel the flow pick up. The lobster’s attention must have been taken away from Henry’s malicious toxin.

Just gotta give it a few more doses, Henry thought. And now that the lobster was injured, it was time to press his advantage.

***

Over the next few minutes Henry hounded the lobster relentlessly. He sent exploding clones. Peppered it with Toxic Rays that he always aimed at the bits were its shell had broken down, and whenever he distracted or stunned it enough, one of his smaller clones would sneak in and bite right into its exposed innards, delivering another dose of venom.

Henry was growing exhausted, and while his attacks weren’t the most effective at breaking through the lobster’s innate defenses, it was still growing slower. Its lightning became less bright. Its charges a little slow. And as its health dwindled, Henry’s was stabilizing. Between his own regenerative abilities and his venoms wreaking havoc within the crustacean’s system and appropriating some of its resources, Henry was doing well. Unless the lobster had some last resort, hidden ability, this fight was Henry’s.

I still don’t want to kill you, though, he grumbled as he dodged the half-hearted dash. He’d gotten plenty of practice against the monster. He’d gotten Aspect levels and he was pretty sure a few of his skills were ready to step into the next rank, like Trickster’s Fortitude, Spiteful Detonation, and a bunch of others. He also… well, there was no way of sugarcoating it. He stole from the lobster. So he felt bad killing it as well, while he was the one who came and bothered it in its home. That A-rank bird was worth killing more than this guy. And maybe it was Henry’s bias toward crustacean, but he really didn’t want to kill it.

Taking stock of his clones in the ruins, he ordered them all to rally so he could store them and began planning his retreat and while he dodged the monster’s weakened strikes, blinking on its back to work the leviathan teeth out of its shell whenever he could. Above, one of his clones was ready to switch places with him as he waved an arm toward the huffing lobster.

“[Hey! Good spar. Let’s do this again sometime!]”

The lobster clacked its pincer, annoyed, and its shell crackled as it readied another charge.

“[No seriously! Stop it. I’m leaving!]”

The lobster blurred, but Henry had plenty of time to shove himself out of the way with Telekinesis as he channeled mana into his teleport. “[Go rest! We’ll do this again soon, alright?]”

The lobster seethed, its mandible twitching, and its shell lit up again to charge, but Henry was ready to peace out.

“[Laters!]”

The world twisted and darkened around him, and a beat later he was far the rock formation that housed the ruins. Most of his clones had finished arranging themselves back in his Maw, and only a couple were still in the ruins. Dismissing them, Henry swam up and away, finally sensing his own fatigue creep up as the flow of energy he’d been stealing from the A-rank cut off.

Right… that has a limited range, I guess. At least it’s not so limited that it messes with the fight when I’m around, he mused as he eyed the levels he’d gotten. This had been a very good use of his time, all in all. Sure, he missed out on a few tokens, but he’ll be facing plenty of them soon. It wasn’t as if he could earn achievements anyway. That whole reward system was gone, as far as Henry could tell.

Reaching into his Maw, he popped the familiar, blue metallic orb in his arm and grinned. What were the odds that one of these thing happened to be in these ruins? Was that a coincidence, or was this some sort of System-arranged coincidence? Henry might never know. But he wasn’t going to look a gift horse in the mouth. Henry popped the mysterious orb in his Maw, then pulled the notifications as he made his way back to Thalis.

This had been a productive day.
