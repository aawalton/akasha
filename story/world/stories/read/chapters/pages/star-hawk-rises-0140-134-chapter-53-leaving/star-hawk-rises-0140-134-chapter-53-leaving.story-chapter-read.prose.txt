I wasn't more than seven feet off the landing pad when I heard confirmation that I had left the Ceremony Protection Rights area, and an alert blared informing me of an incoming missile that had been fired at my ship. I let out a hiss of annoyance as my sensors instantly began tracking the trajectory of the missile and where it had come from as I added more power to the engines to push me forwards. I was hoping that by doing so, the missile would miss as I moved out of the way.

However, that hope was dashed when the missile's trajectory altered to follow me. Gritting my teeth, I tossed away all notions of planet side speed limits and safety precautions, deciding that if I was going to get shot, then I’d do it while doing everything I could not to get shot.

I shifted most of the power to my engines as I made sure the shields were operating at a hundred percent. I felt the ship lurch at the sudden increase in speed as I pulled back, heading for the atmosphere. I was also a little surprised when sensors picked up more missiles coming from multiple directions.

I quickly traced them as I tracked their trajectory, making sure to stay ahead of all twenty-three incoming missiles. I was about to dive and head for the ground in hopes I could shake them in the mountains, when my sensors finally identified where the first missile had come from.

“What the fuck do you mean it was fired from Alistar Talindarson’s personal ship!” I screamed at the sensors, knowing it would never answer me.

I rolled the ship and banked hard to the port side as I slowly grinned. ‘Fine, you want to shoot at me. Well, guess what’ I thought as I mentally slammed down on my imaginary big red button.

Dozens of missiles suddenly flew away from my hull, as the laser defence grid activated shooting at the incoming missiles, my sensors now tracking my own missiles that were on what I called a ‘return to sender’ mission.

The next second, I had rolled the ship all the way over and was slowly heading for the ground as I performed a large loop until I was aimed at the sky again. Then I grinned like an evil villain as I dropped the shields and added the power to the engines.

I knew this was suicidal and a terrible idea, but I combined it with an insane plan. The moment the power was added to the engines, I also launched every missile I currently had loaded into a tube. At the same time I deployed a dozen A-22 burst-field missiles, designed to explode around the ship, creating a thick smoke screen to block visual, radar, and missile lock-ons. The last thing I fired was a catalyst particle bomb at the rear of the ship; I then used one of the defence lasers to ignite it.

“I’ll see you in hell while I’m regrouping!” I shouted, thinking of an old motto I’d heard once back on earth. I couldn't remember it exactly, but it was something like ‘marines don't die, they just regroup in hell.’

The catalyst particle explosion acted like an initial burst to a rocket, effectively adding its power to my already overextended rear engines and launching the ship skywards at rapid speeds, causing the hull to creak and groan.

“Come on, big girl, you got this,” I whispered as I quickly instructed the automatic functions to reload the missile tubes.

I rapidly hit the atmosphere, punching through it at hypervelocity and nearly had a panic attack when the sky caught fire. It wasn't real fire. It was plasma. A sheath of incandescent blue, electric and furious, peeled across the ship like the world itself was striking a match. My cameras were washed out in a blinding flare as the air ahead compressed into a screaming wall of light.

For a heartbeat, I actually thought the atmosphere really had ignited. That I’d just done the stupidest, most brilliant, most catastrophically explosive thing of my entire short life.

Then the shockwave hit. A violent, concussive WHUMP slammed through the ship, throwing me body against its restraints as the ring of superheated plasma rippled outward, shredding the lingering smoke behind me like a paper under a blowtorch. The sky turned the colour of molten steel. I felt the ship buck like a living thing, desperate to tear itself apart.

“Come on, I said you got this, hold it together!” I gasped.

Another flare tore across the bow. The plasma sheath crackled, spluttered, then stabilised into a shimmering cocoon of light, like an angel of fire wrapped around my hull as I clawed my way higher, faster, beyond the burning edge of the world.

For an agonising second, the ship felt like it was made of liquid metal. Flexing, warping, threatening to shear open. Then, with a gut-lurching snap, the surrounding pressure vanished.

I grinned as we broke free. The plasma cocoon tore away like a dying star shedding its last flare. Darkness flooded the cameras as I observed deep space. It all looked a little too calm after the fury I’d just torn through. My engines were howling in protest, before settling into a strained, uneven hum.

“Altitude transition confirmed,” someone croaked. They sounded a little dazed to my ears.

I felt a little rattled as I slowly shifted my attention back to the sensors. Many ships were following me up, but at a greatly slower pace. Including the ones slowly converging on my position from low orbit. That’s also when the alarms lit up in my head again. Thousands of ships fleeing and fighting had suddenly stopped. It took me a while to figure out why.

They’d seen the flare. They had all seen it. The plasma bloom hadn’t just been bright. It had momentarily lit half the hemisphere like a false sunrise. Anyone on the ground with eyes, anyone with sensors, or the faintest hint of curiosity now knew exactly where I’d broken through and was quickly identifying my ship.

That wasn't the worst part, though. A new indicator had started blinking that I instinctively knew meant, hull-stress telemetry. Thermal fatigue. Microfractures were spider webbing across the ship's spine.

The overdrive, the catalyst blast. The atmospheric punch-through. All of it had consequences. I winced as I understood that any continued high-velocity manoeuvring may lead to catastrophic failures.

I angled the ship, wincing at every groan of the hull. Ahead lay the cold reach of open space. Behind me, the enemy pushed their way through the atmosphere.

A final consequence of my actions appeared. One, I honestly hadn’t accounted for, the catalyst burst had supercharged the engines, over feeding the power grid. I felt my stomach twist. I could sense the reactor’s energy cycle was spiking, it was cycling too fast, too hot, I was unable to bleed off the excess.

“Crap, crap, crap!” I cursed as I tried to think of a way to slow down a ship I had effectively used as a slingshot’s ammunition.

I swallowed hard as I whispered to the big girl. “Okay, it's okay, we had a fun trick up our sleeves and if we think about it for a moment, I’m sure we can pull off a second.”

I glanced around the ship, trying to find an optimal second trick. Something that would bleed off this speed and create an escape route, that wouldn’t tear the ship apart.

”fuck it!” I screamed as I powered the thrusters, maintaining the ship's forward momentum. I then reached down with my mental hands and grabbed the warp drive ring and began pushing down on the left as I pulled the right, trying to lift it.

I glanced back at the approaching ships when the ring didn’t spin, and quickly shifted my attention back and stomped on the left side. I nearly shouted in joy as it started to slowly spin. I used my mental hands to get it spinning faster. A moment later, I mentally stepped back and watched it spin faster and faster as it began to blur into a single ring, and the next second a portal appeared in it. I felt a tug, it was gentle, almost inviting. Remembering that feeling, I smiled and threw my arms wide as I leapt into the portal head first.

The feeling of being the size of the Milky Way again returned, I held my wings out as I glided on some currents, this time I couldn't feel anyone under my wings. But on the other hand, I was moving so fast I wasn't even sure if I was flying on currents or making them.

I could feel the damage to the ship like it was my own body and was terrified to try and lean, in case it ripped everything open. I closed my eyes for a second, trying to feel for anything I recognised from the first transition through the void. I kept coming back with nothing, and a little warning was now starting to niggle at the back of my mind. ‘People get lost in the void, without a guiding animal to safely see them through,’ I groaned as I was starting to suspect my guiding hawk ass was not going to get out of this one.
