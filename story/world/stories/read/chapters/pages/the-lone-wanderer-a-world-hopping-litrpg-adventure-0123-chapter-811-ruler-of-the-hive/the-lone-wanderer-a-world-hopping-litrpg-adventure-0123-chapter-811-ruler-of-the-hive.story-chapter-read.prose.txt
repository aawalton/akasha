Percy and Nephthys had stuffed her late mother’s soul into the pyramid-shaped gem before processing her physical remains, in the hopes of eventually granting her a new body. They certainly hadn’t expected to revive her mere months later.

The fallen wasp was the universe’s foremost expert in managing her descendants, which was the very skill that Percy and the Amenthei goddess needed to hone the most right now. There might be powerful deities capable of directing far larger armies that the White-cored bug, but they wouldn’t be nearly as familiar with this particular species of wasps, nor their unique method of communication and rigid hierarchy. If the Ruby Empress couldn’t help Percy improve his control over the hive, nobody could.

‘This body is smaller and less developed than I’m used to, but I guess I should be grateful to be occupying any vessel whatsoever,’ the revived creature said upon having her situation explained.

‘Apologies, Your Majesty,’ Percy replied, scratching the back of his human head in embarrassment. ‘We’re currently a little short on royal bodies to place you in, as I’m sure you’re aware.’

The Starry Commander’s flesh was admittedly a major downgrade for the Empress, but still the best they could have done under the circumstances. Unless they snatched the second Princess back from the Great Houses, that was unlikely to change anytime soon.

‘At least it’s not without its upsides. The lower mana requirement to sustain myself is nice. I almost forgot how it feels to be young and not have to spend every moment of my day eating or conserving my stamina,’ the Empress admitted with a mental shrug. ‘I don’t know if I’ll be able to help you with these tiny antennae, but I’ll do my best.’

Without wasting time, the revived wasp tried to reach out to every bug in her host’s neighbouring tunnels. She overlaid a second signal on top of the Commander’s to greatly amplify its strength, while also broadcasting it far more proficiently than her granddaughter could have managed by herself. Percy and Nephthys were able to follow her mother’s actions closely through their intimate connection, since she was still technically operating from inside the Princess’ head.

‘I can reach wasps located a dozen times farther away and convey my instructions to about a hundred of them at once. That’s quite a feat for my current body, but even a regular Green Princess would be able to do much better,’ the deceased bug concluded after a few tests.

Percy frowned, thinking that it was a damn shame.

They were clearly on the verge of making his familiar’s evolved mutation useful, yet there was something still missing. This amount of additional control would only be a drop in the bucket compared to the tens of millions of wasps living in the hive.

In theory, Percy could provide Nephthys with enough dream mana to awaken the rest of her subjects and project each mind into the head of a different Commander, but that would require months of accumulating fused mana if he wasn’t willing to interrupt his enriched cords. Even then, it wouldn’t make much of a difference.

‘What if you only act as a relay for my own signals?’ Nephthys asked. ‘If combining your power with the Commander has such a potent effect, adding a third mind would be even better. A fourth too, if we include Percy. Not to mention that the other wasps will probably be more responsive to my direct commands.’

Percy and the Empress promptly agreed that it was worth a try, though figuring out the logistics of their new experiment took a couple of hours. Percy and Nephthys had to focus their instructions into the silver dot glinting within the crown, while the Empress merely had to propagate, amplify, and accelerate the signal instead of generating her own. The Starry Commander was thankfully also intelligent enough to understand what she had to do upon receiving the signal.

Ironically, their greatest bottleneck was Percy’s teamwork with his familiar. The others only had to preside over a linear pipeline, while Percy had to synchronize his signals so that they reached the Empress at the same time as Nephthys’.

Fortunately, they had grown quite good at working together over the years, so it didn’t take long for them to solve the issue. The key was to simplify their instructions so that they could be transmitted periodically. The mental messages started off slightly out of phase, but it wasn’t very difficult to align them.

The only drawback was that their orders had to be as short and generic as “click your mandibles”, because that type of message was easier to convey repeatedly than a detailed battle strategy. They would have to practice a lot later to improve the scope of their commands, but it was better to tackle one problem at a time.

Once everyone was ready, Percy adjusted his signal until it arrived alongside his familiar’s. The eight prongs jutting out of their glowing crown vibrated audibly as the messages resonated with one another, and the oscillation grew even more pronounced once the Empress joined their efforts. The vibration was intense enough to disturb the colourful motes dancing around the magical jewel, scattering them in every direction.

Percy knew that the Starry Commander had also done her part, because Micky informed him of the tens of thousands of bugs that had just snapped their mandibles within the creature’s vicinity. The affected wasps seemed to be limited within a kilometre of the house-sized proxy, though the Huehuan hadn’t been able to scan the area properly without access to his sentries and with so many obstacles in the way of his sensory domain.

Not long after hearing his friend’s report, Percy received a separate confirmation of their success from his Status.

[Congratulations! Your familiar’s spell has evolved: Wild Art: Heir of the Hive – Masterful -> Wild Art: Ruler of the Hive – Masterful!]

‘That’s an encouraging sign,’ Nephthys said upon listening to Percy’s findings and reading her notification. ‘From my perspective, this felt no harder than commanding a single wasp. If we do this for each of my subjects, I’ll be able to delegate my authority to a few hundred Commanders and control countless bugs simultaneously.’

Percy nodded. Passing some of the mental load to the Empress and Commander had helped a lot, and this new method of transmitting the signals was also more efficient. The message only had to cross the bulk of the distance separating Nephthys from her subjects once, before branching out to lots of neighbouring wasps.

As massive as the hive was, its total energy capacity was finite and linked to the accumulated nectar reserves. This hierarchical approach to delivering instructions would allow the creatures to consume their collective reserves far more slowly.

Despite these promising results, there were several obstacles that Percy and his familiar would have to overcome to make the new spell reliable.

Firstly, the slumbering mortals lacked the Empress’ experience, so they’d need a lot of coaching before they became useful. The Amenthei’s ability to practice would sadly be restricted by Percy’s pitiful dream mana output. Assuming that he could spare a droplet per day without affecting his cords, that each droplet could only awaken a handful of souls, and that the mortals would remain awake a little over a day – a safe bet if this ability worked like Dream Harmony – Percy and Nephthys would have to train their subordinates in lots of small batches over the following months.

Secondly, it would be best if they could personalize their instructions, as ordering thousands of wasps to perform the same action might not always be a good idea.

‘Do you think that the signals can be tweaked so that individual bugs within the same group will act upon them differently?’ he asked the royals.

‘In some cases, yes,’ the Empress replied. ‘For example, if you command them to “spread outwards”, the ones to my left will fly farther left, while those to my right will move the opposite direction. Alternatively, ordering them to “attack anyone within reach” will cause only those closest to an enemy to engage, and telling them to “block or avoid the incoming spell” will leave the exact response up to their circumstances and personal discretion.’

Percy mulled over the implications. Conditional instructions like those were indeed the key to having his cake and eating it too, as they would allow him and Nephthys to control millions of wasps with a certain degree of variance while only sending a single message to each cluster. However, choosing the best possible phrasing for every command while managing hundreds of aerial platoons would be difficult in the heat of a large-scale conflict.

‘Would another mindset help?’ he asked Nephthys.

‘In part,’ she replied. ‘Thanks to my latest promotion, I should be able to assimilate the rest of my memories and get rid of Structured Amnesia entirely. We’ll fill my capacity up with a weaker version of Supercharged Cognition, so that I can keep up with you a little better. We can also introduce another simple mindset in each of our minds to automatically synchronize our signals.’

‘It doesn’t sound simple,’ Percy protested.

‘It is,’ Nephthys assured him. ‘It’ll do the same thing we were doing manually just now. When one of us sends out a signal, their mindset will delay it slightly to give the other’s mindset a chance to generate an identical echo. This should also allow us to send longer and more detailed commands since we won’t have to worry about timing them ourselves.’

‘That’ll make things easier,’ Percy acknowledged. ‘But that’s still a lot of information to process in real time, even with my mental pockets and cognitive acceleration. Is there a way to take advantage of my Command trait as well?’

The Ruby Princess considered his question for a couple of minutes before replying. Her people hadn’t known about spectral fiends and traits, so she’d clearly never had the chance to experiment with them while she had been at the height of her power. That said, she’d learned a lot about the topic in recent years from Percy and Zoris, and could likely speculate on how a person’s traits would interact with their mindsets.

‘I’m confident that it’ll automatically help you select the best possible instructions to convey to the wasps. That’s literally one of the Command trait’s primary functions. But if you want it to work even better, we can try building the instincts it grants you directly into the mindset,’ Nephthys explained.

‘How would that work?’

‘The trait is a part of you, just like your physical senses and limbs. There should be a star inside your mindscape that represents its functions, or we can just create one if we can’t find it. By linking it to the new mindset, we can filter the signals through the trait so that they get modified automatically by your intentions before you even transmit them. That way, you’ll only have to decide what you want the swarm to do, and our brains will feed the right signals to our proxies to achieve the desired result.’

Percy sighed, feeling exhausted just thinking about all the work that lay before him.

Though he shouldn’t be complaining. Ever since the rest of the Great Houses had joined his enemies, he’d known that Nephthys and the hive would be the keys to winning the war. He hadn’t figured out how exactly to weaponize the wasps, but the details were finally starting to become clearer.

The new mutation and spell would allow the Ruby Princess to control vast numbers of bugs efficiently, while Percy’s spectral trait, upcoming mindset and excess brainpower would help him decide how to actually use the enormous swarm during a large-scale conflict.

The only thing missing would be the ability to gather information from the chaotic battlefield in real time. Micky’s sentries and sensory domains should make up for that once they finished upgrading the spell on Mapel. Then, it would just be a question of putting everything together. Percy was already labouring to improve Supercharged Cognition under Nephthys’ guidance, so adding another mindset or two into the curriculum would only help him explore the subject more thoroughly.

After discussing and approving of the plan, the Ruby Empress retracted her mind to the dazzling tiara and waited patiently for the effect of Percy’s dream mana to expire. Unlike the Amenthei, she wouldn’t need much training, so there was no reason for her to keep hogging her descendant’s body or staying awake within the crystalline prison.

Before delving into their new project, there was something that took precedence. Gifting the rest of the pink droplet to his familiar to use as she pleased, Percy withdrew his attention from her body as best as he could without actually leaving it, so as to give her some privacy.

After millennia of suffering and working hard for the sake of others, Nephthys certainly deserved an afternoon to catch up with her son.
