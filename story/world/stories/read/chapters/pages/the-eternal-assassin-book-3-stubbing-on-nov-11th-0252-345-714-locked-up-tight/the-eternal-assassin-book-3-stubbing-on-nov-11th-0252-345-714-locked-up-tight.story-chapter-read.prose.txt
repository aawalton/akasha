Just as Asher had expected, Donvath was of the same view as he was that they should leave the issue of him being attacked alone for the moment. The High Prince promised him he’d take a personal look at the scene of the attack in the morning, and with his Time element, he should be able to figure out exactly what had happened without too much difficulty. With that taken care of, Asher and Samantha turned in for the night, getting some much-needed sleep after their eventful evening.

However, it wasn’t long before Asher was jerked back to consciousness as he felt a familiar vibration within his soul.

Sitting up in bed, Asher rubbed the sleep out of his eyes as he pulled his sending stone from his rift, noting how the fifth and newest rune he’d only recently added was now glowing. Tapping it, he held the stone up to his ear.

“Amanda? Did you guys get the lich?”

“Not quite… but we could still use your help,” the paladin admitted. “Sorry, I know it’s late, but any chance you have some time?”

“If it helps take down the Council of Death, I’ve got nothing but time,” Asher said, yawning as he stood up and pulled on his armor. “One sec.”

Turning off his stone, Asher focused on the mark Amanda had given him permission to place on her kite shield. Deciding to let Samantha keep resting, he gave her a quick kiss on the forehead before triggering Recall and warping across the continent. He didn’t even know where the other team had been sent or how they got there in the first place, but that didn’t matter to Recall.

Using his skill did put a slight bit of unpleasant pressure on his soul, but it wasn’t pain, which was a good sign that his soul was just about healed up again. Even so, he knew he should be taking it easy until he didn’t feel anything off when he used his skills. With that thought in mind, he popped into existence in the center of a dark, arid field, and his eyes widened as he took in the smoldering remains of a creature that put the four-story-tall monster he and his team had killed while hunting down Jormon to shame.

“You weren’t kidding when you said ‘one sec’,” Amanda said as she grinned at him. The paladin wore her full set of bright white armor, and was currently keeping the glow low enough that looking at her wouldn’t ruin anyone’s night vision. “Consider me jealous about that ability of yours. I can move fast, but not that fast.”

“How did you guys even kill something like that?” Asher asked, still staring at the monster that had to have been at least seven or eight stories tall. Just as Lanra had mentioned, the creature looked to be some form of horrific abomination, little more than a shapeless blob constructed from thousands of corpses being stitched together. It didn’t have anything resembling a head or any other vital point, which means Asher wouldn’t have even known where to start in terms of taking it down.

“They don’t exactly call me Falling Sun for no reason,” Amanda pointed out with a sly smile as her armor glowed brighter for a brief moment before dimming back down. “It’s not an exaggeration when I say I am probably the strongest warrior on our entire continent when it comes to killing undead. My entire Order was created with fighting the Deadlands in mind, it’s what we train for and what we devote our lives and foundations toward. Also, it’s not actually dead. Not completely. Groln and his team and the handful of members from my Order I brought with me are currently down there, sweeping the monster and finishing off the corpses that are still moving.”

“Got it,” Asher nodded, not missing the fact that a certain name hadn’t been mentioned. “What about Hounth?”

“According to him, something came up and his promised soldiers weren’t prepared to make the trip with us,” Amanda frowned. “He is definitely plotting something, but I don’t know what. He did send a team of mercenaries to help, but the poor saps were in over their heads. I did what I could to help them, but they were killed almost instantly. If I didn’t know any better I’d think he was trying to ensure the coalition failed in taking out the Council of Death, but that seems like a rather stupid thing to do. The Lambit Republic already officially joined the coalition. If we don’t wipe out the Council, their country will be on the receiving end of a few angry liches just as much as the rest of ours.”

“What if he’s not trying to make us fail, but just to slow us down?” Asher asked, an idea slowly forming in his mind. “If you asked anyone in the coalition which country has helped out the least so far, we’d all say the Lambit Republic, right? In that same vein, who would you say has helped out the most?”

“The Noala Kingdom,” Amanda said, her brow raising as she realized where he was going with this. “You think the Lambit Republic wants the Council strong enough to retaliate. To wipe out the Noala Kingdom before the coalition finishes them off after the fact.”

“It sounds like an insanely risky and stupid plan, but people do stupid things,” Asher said, feeling more confident in his idea as he thought it over. “Not to mention straight up evil, seeing as it would result in hundreds of thousands of deaths.”

“We should discuss this more in depth later, along with the rest of the coalition,” Amanda decided. “Right now, there is the matter of why I called you here in the first place. We managed to take down the lich’s most powerful creation, along with most of her other undead. However, we’ve discovered she has something of an impregnable bunker that she’s retreated to, and we can’t crack it.”

“A bunker?” Asher repeated. “Where?”

“There,” Amanda said, pointing down at the small mountain of corpses. “The abomination had been created around the bunker. As far as we can tell, the lich was actively empowering it from within the entire time we were fighting it, making it far harder to take down. If not for Groln and his skilled team, I’m not afraid to admit I’m not sure my Order would have managed to defeat it.”

“So the monster is down, but the bunker remains,” Asher concluded. “And you can’t crack it?”

“At first, we were worried about risking our most powerful attacks, as we didn’t want to shatter the bunker and accidentally destroy the lich’s body inside,” Amanda explained, motioning for him to follow her as she began walking toward the abomination. “However, even as our attacks scaled up in intensity, we quickly realized there was nothing we could do to even put a dent in this thing. I don’t know what elements the lich has, but whatever they are, they must include some powerful skills regarding durability. It would explain how she managed to create something so powerful and hard to put down from thousands of individual corpses. Something of this size should have exploded to pieces the moment we attacked it.”

“Whatever she did to her bunker, it’s nothing my void dagger can’t handle,” Asher said, wincing as he realized that was probably a bad idea. “Though I might need to ask one of you guys to do the actual cutting. My soul took a little damage during our own mission, and it’s still healing.”

“You took soul damage?” Amanda asked, halting atop one of the corpses as she turned to stare at him. “And you’re up and about?!”

“Don’t worry about me, I’ve got it covered,” he said, giving her a reassuring smile. “Just show me the bunker and we’ll deal with the lich. After you cut a hole through the wall, I’ll pop in and finish her off.”

Amanda looked like she had more she wanted to say regarding him just shrugging off soul damage, but after a moment, she simply nodded and continued walking over the collapsed monster. Trudging up a small mountain of burnt corpses wasn’t exactly a pleasant experience, and Asher did his best not to look down or breathe in too deeply while he followed the paladin toward the center. He could hear undead groans and snarls coming from here and there, often followed quickly by the sounds of blades slicing through flesh as the remaining undead were finished off. Eventually, they crested a small mound and spotted Groln standing atop a large, black rectangle roughly twenty feet long and half that wide. The bottom was hidden within the corpses, and the demonic man frowned as he stood there with his arms crossed.

“I’ve got the backup!” Amanda called out with a wave.

“Took him long enough,” Groln said, nodding toward Asher before looking down at the bunker once more. “I’ve never seen anything like this. As far as I can tell, it’s some sort of incredibly resilient material combined with an enchantment that the lich herself probably can’t undo. I think it was a failsafe designed to make the bunker impossible to open for the next year. My Demon element gives me a unique understanding of what tritessence does just by looking at it, and that’s the vibe I’m getting. Nothing is getting in or out, not via teleportation or any other means.”

“Unfortunately for her, I happen to have the world’s deadliest can opener,” Asher grinned, stepping off the corpses and onto the top of the bunker alongside Amanda. “Do we have any idea if she might put up more of a fight after we break inside?”

“Not a clue,” Groln admitted. “We chose this lich because we knew where to find her, and we knew we could take down her undead army easily enough. Your inside man didn’t have any other information, nor any idea about this bunker.”

Out of curiosity, Asher tried slipping into the astral to see if he could just fall through the bunker’s outer wall. But just as Groln had warned, the material was just as solid in the astral realm as it was in the material. Letting his skill drop, Asher nodded as he handed Amanda the abyssal sheath with his void dagger. “Would you care to do the honors?”

Freeing the dagger, Amanda recoiled as the hungry blade instantly began consuming her tritessence. “That is a horrible sensation,” she muttered, staring at the sliver of void held in her hands before shuddering and crouching down. Asher prepared himself for anything, but as she slid the dagger into the bunker, nothing happened. As easily as if she were dragging a hot knife through butter, she cut a large square hole into the top of the bunker. The moment she finished, the chunk dropped inward, crashing onto the floor below with a loud bang, as if it were made of metal rather than stone. Not wasting any time, Asher gripped Crescent in one hand and Nail in the other as he dropped in after it.

Landing in what looked like a posh bedroom complete with a shelf of books, a comfortable bed, and a writing desk, he found himself face to face with a lich with soft blue fires burning in her eye sockets. Based on the intensity of the flame and how hard they were flickering, he didn’t think she’d been expecting anyone to manage to break into her bunker anytime soon.

“How—”

Asher ignored the pressure on his soul as he slashed through compressed space with Crescent, separating the lich’s head from her neck and snuffing out her undead life in an instant. As curious as he was as to her final words, his experience with Hylm had been all the warning he’d needed that letting any lich with an unknown element use their skills was a recipe for disaster. As the latest lich fell to a crumbled heap onto the floor of her bunker, Asher smiled and waved up through the hole in the roof at Amanda and Groln who were peering down.

“Six to go!”
