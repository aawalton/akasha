It was an interesting experience for Henry to be awake during the evolution process.

Well. Technically.

His mind was much slower than usual, but he was still cognizant. He wasn’t sure why he hadn’t conked out like usual, exactly. Maybe it was because his mind had truly been elevated, or alternatively, divided between his hundreds of projections, so much so that as long as one of them was aware and active, he couldn’t be rendered unconscious. Whatever the reason was, for the first time ever, he had the opportunity to sense the evolution process, and he was a little surprised by what he was sensing.

Simply enough, Aspects were involved. But they weren’t his own. Henry could tell, as surely as the sun rises, that these forces were beyond him. He felt them alter his body and soul, and he knew that there was no resisting the process. That any attempt at directing them would be as effective as telling a rock not to roll downhill. It was… like some sort of natural, universal rule was being followed, fueled and empowered by the energies of the world and its Aspects, and there was just no negotiating with such power.

Also, obviously, why would he try and hinder such forces anyway? The laws of this world were working on his evolution. He had nothing to gain by sticking his grubby arms in that. For now, anyway. It was just interesting to learn that while the System had eased a few of the early processes, the world itself knew what needed to be done. So Henry just let his mind drift while he loosely observed the changes. His soul was affected first, slowly but surely growing a little more resilient as the mixture of spiritual energies infused its ephemeral tissues, giving him an odd sense of stability. It was like he’d suddenly wrapped his arms around this moment in time. As if his presence in the world had gained a little more weight, and as the power spread through his soul, it also spread through his flesh, though there its effect was much easier to sense.

It was simply painful. But it was such a varied tapestry of pain that Henry, with his awareness thankfully somewhat removed from the process, found himself admiring. His nerves burned. His muscles spasmed, and some he was pretty sure tore from the strain before they were healed and were submitted to the same strain once more. His skin tightened painfully and pulled, cracking in some parts, as if he was being desiccated. It was all highly unpleasant, and those were the main things he could sense, but the changes didn’t stop there. All of his organs were subjected to the process, and frankly, Henry didn’t know how long it had all lasted, but eventually, the pain slowly became a background noise, until it was fully gone and in its stead, a nourishing balm was applied to his body and soul. Henry luxuriated in the feeling for a while, and soon enough—too soon, even—he felt his awareness slowly return to him as the transformative process ebbed. At first, the sights and smells from his Avatars, like a mosaic of scenes from the hundreds of clones he had connecting him to the surface and beyond it, hidden in trees, floating in the air, masquerading as rocks. Then the shouting reached him, in his hidden cove, along with the smell of acid and melted rock.

Henry cracked an eye open, and he realized that he wasn’t as hidden as he thought he’d been.

“Do not move, kraken, or I will be putting this through your brain,” called out the whistling voice, and Henry, while he simultaneously looked through his notification, eyed the bone-spear and the gold-masked figure pointing it at him. Behind him, it was mayhem. Ants, some crawling and some flying, were throwing themselves in droves at the little squad of masked Merfolk surrounding his alcove. Most were C-ranks, but there were also plenty of B-ranks, with a few larger forms further away leading the swarm that might even be A-ranks. But the ants didn’t bother him. Instead, it was the man pointing a weapon at him that piqued his interest, so just for the fun of it, he cast Identify.

[Merfolk (A) - Lvl 35]

That would put this individual slightly above the puppeteer Henry and Maurice had faced a few weeks ago. But as he saw the notifications waiting for him, even if Henry was to face that disgusting creature again, he didn’t expect the battle to go the same way.

In the end, the evolution was not as small as he thought it might be.

Veilwatch Kraken (B+) evolved into Thousand-Eyed Sovereign of Shifting Reflections (A).

All base stats increased by 30.

“That’s a mouthful,” Henry muttered, mostly to himself as he ignored the spear jabbing him between the eyes. The man was saying something about him coming quietly with them, but Henry just eyed his new species which, oddly enough, got changed into a title. He guessed it was hard to be a species when one was so unique. That might explain why most A-ranks—at least those that didn’t hide the detail—had such long and poetic names.

“Gar’Dak, stop that,” hissed the one wearing a pearlescent white mask, and Henry recognized her voice. This was the one he’d spoken to near the Merfolk settlement, the one that wanted him to talk. She had been a little busy with the ants, but now that her companion was preparing something, she seemed to grow a little alarmed.

Henry finally gave the man some of his attention, and he noticed that something was building up in his chest. A skill of some sort. Henry could see the sun-like mote of energy travel up from the stomach, slowly gaining a shadowy-sheen as it reached the throat, and then, the man spoke, and his voice seemed to permeate flesh, soul, and even the stone around them, as all of the ants suddenly went still.

“Submit,” the Merfolk ordered, his attention focused on Henry, and while the man’s only contact was the spear that tried and failed to puncture through Henry’s dark and white-dotted skin, the voice that reached his ears was overflowing with power, the concepts within urging him to let go and just listen to the man who’d spoken to him. To give him his attention and focus, and to follow his order lest he suffer for it. Just like that puppeteer, the order wanted to find a spot to plant itself and remind Henry that he’d always need to listen to the man’s instruction. Henry’s arms went limp, and he distinctly sensed an aura of smugness emanating out of the figure for a brief moment, before his arm whipped out and cracked the golden mask against the cave’s wall, punting him through rocks and into the swarm of ants that, now that the mental compulsion had been shunted away, raged madly once more.

Henry let out a snort as he unfolded his limbs out of the stone pocket, flushing away the remains of the man’s essence that made their way into his spirit. Psychic Resistance had done most of the work already, but there was no scenario in which Henry would let himself fall under this clown’s sway.

With a simple application of Flight, Henry levitated above the crowd, which caused some of the ants to shoot their pale-yellow acid at him. He wasn’t much larger than any of these people, having shrunk himself to hide while he evolved, but they certainly felt small to him. Still, small or not, they’d come ready, and as Henry felt for his clones, he sensed something interfering with his ability to switch with them. He hadn’t triggered it yet, but it felt like there was something just waiting for him to try.

Some sort of anti-teleportation measure? Henry hadn’t known such thing existed.

“He shouldn’t have done that, Sovereign. I apologize on his behalf,” spoke the female Merfolk before dropping to one knee, which had the odd effect of making her entourage freeze for a moment before they turned their attention back to the swarm of ants, at which point, a clump of them went flying as the one Henry had, well, sent flying swatted a bunch of ants with his spear in his effort to make his way back.

Henry stared down at her, his skin rippling with ominous blacks and yellows as his aura spread out. By now, it felt almost easier to let the pressure of his Aspect push out than it was to restrain them, and it made all the dark shadows in their surroundings flicker and deepen, as if there were shapes in the dark, watching and waiting.

The ants definitely did not like his aura, and now most—if not all—of their ranged assault was flying at Henry, but well… acids and darts couldn’t do much to him. Instead, he spoke down to the Merfolk.

“What is your name?”

“I am Sel’Veth, Changing One. A Guardian here on a mission for my Depth Lord.”

“And this mission concerns me, I’m guessing?”

It seemed she was about to respond, but Henry just raised a hand.

“Let me make myself clear, Sel’Veth. You kidnapped my friend, put a collar on him, and I didn’t raze your city to the ground. Because even when I’m pissing mad, I can still discern the guilty from the innocent,” he said, punctuating his words with three Apostles who appeared out of thin air. The trio froze Golden Mask who was almost upon him with an Unfettered-empowered Telekinesis, then one of them punted the moron back to the other side of the cave with an enlarged, empowered arm that detonated on impact like the crack of thunder. Henry never looked away from the Guardian. “Now you chase after me, and I wake up after my evolution with a spear pointed at my head. So here’s what I’ll tell you one time, and one time only. I want nothing to do with you or with whatever it is you’re planning. I care not for your Depth Lord. I have bigger fish to fry. You might be able to contribute to that, eventually, but for now, I want you gone. Is that clear?”

Sel’Veth and her entourage stood still while Henry hovered above them, shielding all of them from the onslaught of ant-attacks, then slowly, the representative let out a whistling sigh.

“I’m afraid I can’t do that just yet,” she said, raising a glowing hand. All exits suddenly shone with similar teal-colored lights, and water poured in, washing the ants down the chasm in the middle, while a worrying reddish-light shone from where Golden Mask had landed. “You must give me a chance to rectify our mistake,” she continued, voice whistling and low, and suddenly, Henry didn’t think she was as reasonable as she seemed.

Still, he wondered… were there actually anti-teleporting magics at play, or was he imagining that? And if so, how resilient would they be?

A smile pulled on Henry’s lips. It had been a while since he’d had such a complicated scenario to extricate himself from. He wondered how well he’d do.

First things first, let’s see what we’re working with, he mused, activating his first skill, and the cave exploded with lights.
