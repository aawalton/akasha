Zaiden brought me to the deepest part of the forest outside Alterus. The moonlight filtered through the canopy of leaves, dappled silver patterns pouring on the forest floor. The songs of the night were more animated in fae country, unlike anything I’d heard since arriving in Etheryn. Night crickets chirped, birds sang their sweet songs, and frogs crooned in melodic harmonies that flowed through the evening breeze. A sound like tiny bells emerged from within the hollow of an old oak—I spotted foxes, their calls echoing in perfect counterpoint to the deeper hoots of moon owls nesting in the higher branches.

It almost sounded like they were all singing in a choir.

“You hear that?” Zaiden asked. “Pretty awesome, isn’t it?”

‘I feel like I’m in a Disney forest….’

“What’s Disney?”

I smiled. “Nevermind. Sounds beautiful, though.”

“We’re almost there,” he promised, ducking under a low-hanging branch.

‘You know, most kidnappings start this way,’ I quipped as I continued to follow him.

He chuckled. “I’m pretty sure I’d have better luck defeating a tier 5 dungeon solo.”

‘Very funny,’ I replied dryly, though I couldn’t help but be curious about where he was leading me. The forest grew thicker around us, trees towering overhead with trunks so wide that three people couldn’t wrap their arms around them. The air felt different here—older, charged with a kind of magic I hadn’t encountered before.

‘Zaiden, seriously, where are we going?’ I pressed as we descended deeper into a field of  interwoven branches.

“You’ll see,” he said, pushing aside a curtain of hanging moss.

We reached a circular clearing where the canopy opened just enough to let moonbeams cascade down like silver waterfalls. In the center stood what looked like the remnants of an ancient shrine—weathered stone pillars carved with intricate runes. But what truly took my breath away were the flowers.

Hundreds of luminescent blooms carpeted the ground, their petals glowing blue. They pulsed gently, like sleeping heart beams, opening and closing in a hypnotic rhythm. As I stepped closer, they responded to my presence, their glow intensifying slightly.

‘What are these?’ I whispered, mesmerized by their beauty.

“Moon lilies,” Zaiden said softly, kneeling beside one of the flowers. “They only bloom during the full moon, and only in places where old magic still runs deep. And by old magic, I mean godly magic.”

‘Godly magic?’

“This is one of the many hidden temples of the gods. Underneath this temple are ley lines connected to the divine network that spreads throughout Etheryn. Most people don’t even know places like this exist, but they are special remnants of our gods who disappeared a decade ago….”

Zaiden’s voice took on a somber tone. “One day they were answering prayers, blessing harvests, protecting the faithful. The next….” He gestured around the abandoned shrine. “Silence. Complete and utter silence.”

‘I remember Nolan telling me about the disappearance. I’m sorry Etheryn is going through this,’ I said, settling down among the glowing flowers. ‘It must be terrifying to lose that connection.’

“It was,” Zaiden admitted, running his fingers along one of the carved pillars. “My folks were devout followers of Amossira, the goddess of creation and fertility. When she vanished….” He trailed off. “Let’s just say it left a lot of people feeling abandoned.”

‘And this place? It makes you feel connected to her again in some way?’

“Somewhat. I recently discovered this. This façade in ruins, it brings me back to when times were better for Etheryn. Where we didn’t have to fight every year for survival as a planet. These tournaments, they’ve changed the composition of everything. They’ve turned our realm into an arena for others’ entertainment, and I’m tired of it.”

‘Why bring me here, Zaiden?’

“Well, you did say that you were lost. When we are lost, we seek guidance from our gods and goddesses. They aren’t here, but their ley lines remain. Those ley lines connect to the realm of the Infinity Tree, or The Tree of New Beginnings. And there, maybe they could hear us.”

‘Have you tried praying in this temple before?’

“No.” He smirked. “Want to pray together? Combined strength yields far better results.”

I smiled. ‘What are we praying for? You know, besides guidance and clarity?’

“A brighter future,” he said. “And the strength to face whatever comes next.

I walked toward him on the path rimmed with moon lilies, their gentle glow warming my scales. The idea of praying to gods who might not even be listening felt strange, but there was something peaceful about this place that made me want to try.

‘How do we do this?’ I asked. ‘I mean, I’m not exactly from around here. I don’t know the proper way to address your gods.’

“There’s no wrong way,” Zaiden said softly as he prompted me to follow him inside. “Just speak from the heart.”

As soon as I stepped foot inside the ruins, it came to life. The runes along the stone began to blue, the same brightness of the moonlilies.

“Hey, I guess someone’s listening up there, huh?” Zaiden said with a big smile on his face. But I wasn’t so convinced. This wouldn’t be the first time I touched or stepped into something and got a reaction from it. Like the time I’d activated a dungeon creation, or that time I somehow summoned The Dark One from its slumber by just being in its cave. This time was no different, or maybe, it was….

Unlike the other times, I felt something underneath my feet trying to connect to me on another plane. As Zaiden knelt by my side in prayer, I closed my eyes and let whatever force was reaching out find me.

Suddenly, the world around me shifted. I wasn’t in the temple anymore—I was standing in a vast space filled with swirling galaxies and floating islands connected by bridges of pure light. The forest clearing faded, replaced by a vast expanse. I found myself standing on what looked like crystalline ground that reflected the cosmos above. In the distance, I could see an enormous tree where its branches stretched impossibly high. They disappeared into points of light that might have been stars or maybe something else entirely, its leaves shimmering with every color imaginable.

‘The Infinity Tree?’ I was too afraid to move, taken aback by the sheer magnitude of what I was witnessing. The tree pulsed with life, each beat sending ripples of color cascading through its leaves. It was magnificent, breathtaking, and utterly terrifying all at once.

Finally finding my courage, I took a tentative step forward, drawn to the cosmic tree like a moth to flame. But as I tried to move closer, an invisible force pushed against me, like walking into a wall. The more I pushed, the stronger the resistance became. It wasn’t painful, just... immovable. Like trying to press against a law of nature itself.

‘What is this?’ I murmured, pushing with all my might but making no progress. The tree remained tantalizingly out of reach, its cosmic magnificence just beyond my grasp. I couldn’t understand why I was being shown this wonder only to be prevented from approaching it.

Then, cutting through my awe, came the voices.

They were scrambled and low, like little angry whispers. I tried to concentrate on them, but I couldn’t make out what they were saying. I swear I heard the name Nyx, the man who’d destroyed my old life with a flick of his warlock’s magical staff. But hearing his name here, in this cosmic place, didn’t make sense, did it?

All of a sudden, the vision before me began to fade. I was irritated. I was teleported to a divine plane hoping to get some answers, but I was booted as soon as I came. I didn’t understand how I got here, so trying to stay was out of my control. I shouted, hoping someone in the cohort of voices could hear me, but nothing called back out.

‘Hey! Someone, anyone!’ I screamed. ‘Can you hear me?!’

I was feeling déjà vu. Being ignored as a mysterious entity stood there, almost like when I was first introduced to my codex, except it wasn’t barking orders at me.

‘If anyone is listening, my name is Freya! Freya Raseni! I don’t know how I got here, and I don’t know why, but I need help! I need guidance!’ I begged desperately. ‘My codex doesn’t have the answers I’m looking for. I just want to know what my Infernal Bloodline means! I want to know why I’m so special! I want to know why I can summon dungeons and awaken evil spirits, and see all of this!’

The whispers grew louder, more urgent, but still remained frustratingly incomprehensible. It was like trying to tune into a radio station that was just slightly out of range—I could catch fragments, emotional undertones, but no clear words.

And then, an answer….

Again, it wasn’t coherent, but that voice… I’d recognize that voice from anywhere….

Dad?

Then, just as suddenly as it had begun, everything went silent. The cosmic vista dissolved around me like mist, and I found myself back in the temple ruins, gasping as if I’d been holding my breath underwater.

“Hey, are you all right?” Zaiden asked me.

‘Yeah, yeah… I’m fine.’

“You know, you can breathe when you pray. It isn’t supposed to be a taxing experience!” he jested.

‘No, I’m just… overwhelmed is all,’ I said, then shook my head and got up on my hind legs.

“The power of the hidden temples can do that to you! I felt something too. I’m not the religious type, but after they disappeared, you could say I became more aware. You don’t know what you got until it’s gone, an ol’ wise man’s saying. They might not be around, but something is happening. I know one day, they’ll come back to us, Freya. You can’t lose hope.” He got up on his feet and dusted off his knees. “I’m hoping this wasn’t a complete waste of time, and that you got something out of it.”

‘Oh yeah….’

“Heh, great! Let’s head back. It’s getting late.”
