Sancus and the Brain Trust as a whole drafted a contract for Palthennius to sign to restrict any potential betrayal on his part. He could break it, but it would dish him a nasty permanent curse for defying multiple deities. It was defying a whole Pantheon, after all. On top of this, even thinking about it would warn them. The closer he got to doing it, the more intensity would be felt, even by him. This extreme degree of assured good behavior (mostly related to harm upon her people) was only for the duration of the war. He could optionally refresh it or ask for re-drafting to something lighter and more appropriate, at that point.

Samantha wanted this partially because Zadkiel would have the same thing happen, and she wasn’t certain exactly what would happen with him and the Naugites afterward. It might not matter in the end, but the principle of things always mattered.

In any case, Palthennius read it without saying a word, and then signed it and spoke the oaths that fully triggered it all. He knelt before his new goddess and swore to serve her to the best of his ability, and she swore in reply to treat him fairly as his liege.

He was given a new alias and an expensive, discreet, cosmetic shapechange, linked on a trigger he could flip when the time came. Samantha suggested the name ‘Odysseus’ and it was readily accepted. The story would go that he was a mercenary champion from the Southlands. He kept his real armor hidden in a dimensional pocket, and he wore a new set. His sword would be returned to him when the time was right and not before.

He told them all he knew about the intended undead transmutation magic — which wasn’t much — and then all he knew about Matrazere. This included things they’d already gleaned, but he still enriched their knowledge. He confirmed reports about the details of Matrazere’s fortress gate: it was included in the rune of indestructibility, and when it was closed, there was no ground path inward. Three other technical entrances existed. One was from underground connecting to extensive tunnels, and two were up top in the tower, accessible only by air. Each one was no different than the gate: eternal and indestructible. No one actually knew if the doors could be forcefully pried open, sadly. If it had ever been tried, or known, it was lost.

Another rumor got confirmed: magic attempting to cross the threshold inward would fail absolutely. As such, even if a door was open, methods to push inward had the complication of no good means of clearing space. Until you crossed the threshold, those inside were utterly protected. In practice, this was basically a death trap, because spellcasters and sharpshooters could unload by line of sight from within, with impunity, behind specialist ‘doorman’ tank defenders.

It was worse than that, though. Matrazere had ‘observation orb’ emitters at numerous points all the way around, from which magic could be cast out. Rumors went that these could be dispelled, and this was true… but the wizards had specialty-charged artifacts that could endlessly refresh the spell. Thousands of charges. So, any dispelled orb was only temporary. Even if you managed to pull off a method of widescale suppression, the wizards inside had sequences of contingencies to continue working. The full list was unknown, but one thing Palthennius pointed out was that, despite the lack of obvious windows, numerous sections could be made one-way transparent. They could see even if magically blinded.

Despite being perhaps a simple thing, they had not known this, likely because this fact hadn’t been relevant for all the aeons of time Matrazere had stood. They’d just been using their scrying methods instead.

Ultimately, these things are just what they expect us to be worrying about. Funnel us to the doors to try prying them open and getting in, losing lives by the hundreds, and failing. No. We have to make them think that’s what we plan, and then take out the damn rune. Everything must be sacrificed for that, because if we succeed, the unbreakable will be made breakable. The unbreakable will be broken. That’s the key. It’s more important even than getting inside.

Did she even want to? To be inside that place, to ‘take’ it? No. It was like occupying Chernobyl. She’d rather turn it to dust. Bury it.

Before they even got to the fortress, they’d fight an army. It was disconcerting that the enemy was planning on making that army undead, as they would then become thoughtless meatshields with zero chance of routing. The prospect of dealing with the attrition necessary to wade through hundreds of thousands of zombies before even reaching the fortress was just depressing. They’d lose a lot of men. Too many. She couldn’t bypass them, either. There was no swiftly gained target behind them. They had little chance of taking down the rune if they didn’t have control of the battlefield. If they weren’t laying siege.

Matrazere was not going down quickly, so shenanigans to get to it quickly were worthless. They’d get flanked on top of everything.

There was the city they could attempt to fortify, but the enemy would expect this. Almost certainly had mechanisms in place for centuries to punish this idea. From what information could be gained, the buildings weren’t special, nor enchanted. The place wore down even faster than a typical city — likely due to the energies battering it all the time — and reconstructions were common. It was laughably non-viable to bother with. Literally the only smart thing was to demolish it as the deathtrap that it was.

But what about the undeath spellcraft? Could they avoid so much misery and pain by interfering?

I have to do it, then. I have to try. There’s no choice…

Samantha unleashed what she’d been holding back until the last minute, if ever: a blitz of spies. They still had sleepers in the city to activate, to risk for some final rare nuggets, and others were poised to come with the continual flow of people flooding to the place to bolster numbers.

These spies were well-paid independents for the most part, who knew precious little. She tried not to think too much about how she was sending ‘expendable’ people to their likely doom. They were taking a risk they’d had stressed to them, in any case — Samantha fooled none of them. It didn’t change how shitty it made her feel to enable the desperate. People with ‘beneficiaries’ if they failed. She should just give them the damn money and release them. But she couldn’t; she wouldn’t. She’d use them like pawns in a game of empires.

I’m doing it for everyone, and so are they. Each soul. To break the cycle. No more of this! No more war and pain and misery and death! No more torture. Never again. One last time to wield the wicked hook, to carve this disease from our lives for good.

Telling herself this didn’t help much. It was hard to believe. It felt like a naive girl’s dream, not reality.

Whatever could happen with the spies, they had to keep moving. The march would continue. Beyond their efforts, Samantha discussed the feasibility of countering the heinous spell with the Brain Trust.

Ash immediately, soberly, pointed out that it was far different than at the lake. There, they’d been able to interact directly with an almost totally abandoned source, interfacing and investigating the spell while effectively able to ‘touch’ it. This was not the case with what was slated to happen in Matrazere. There, the spell was almost certainly focused inside the fortress, even if it would bloom outward. Likely, it would operate by Sympathy anyway, making countering it directly almost impossible. It could even be some sort of artifact activation, in which case they were doubly screwed. This further tracked with numerous things that already existed as technology in respect to the fortress.

Samantha fielded the idea of being there, or perhaps having Antimages there, and this was almost violently opposed by nearly every other member of the Brain Trust. For one thing, they didn’t know when the enemy would do it. Perhaps it was feasible, if, as Palthennius had suggested, they’d do it at the last moment. Then, the armies would already be there.

But by no means could they park valuable members anywhere near Matrazere. Samantha didn’t even suggest this directly, and yet she got scolded anyway by the likes of Dax and Zephyr.

Dax asked rather saucily, “And if I could wave a fucking wand and counter it, would you put me in reach of hundreds of powerful wizards, without the support of our own hordes of specialists? Without matching forces to avoid getting overwhelmed or trapped? Can you say you would, Madam?”

Samantha had no answer to this. All she could do was pout.

The truth was, even if she did it, and even if she remained hidden, it might be for nothing. She had precious few means of countering artifact activations, and she’d have to somehow know a Sympathetic spell was going off, and reach the source to actually target it. Could she see all the targets being affected at once? Could she interact with a spell under those circumstances? So many question marks.

Ash didn’t bother pointing them out. She met her mother’s eyes with sympathy, knowing the truth was already clear.

They didn’t know enough, and the people capable of countering couldn’t be risked or spared to lurk around one of the most dangerous places on the planet, on nothing more than a prayer. Just like with the lake spell that unleashed the undead, this required investigation first.

It required time.
