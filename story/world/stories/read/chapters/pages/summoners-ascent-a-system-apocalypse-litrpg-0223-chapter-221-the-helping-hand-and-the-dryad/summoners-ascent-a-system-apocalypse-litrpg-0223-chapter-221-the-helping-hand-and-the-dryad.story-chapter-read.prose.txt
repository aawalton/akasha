Ashton drew a contract circle for the Helping Hand on a sheet of paper. The ink that he was using was basically the same ink that he made for the Salesman. The general offering for the Helping Hand spirit was also things with actual 'value'. The Helping Hands were originally, well... thieves.

They would go around and steal things from other clans or people from the material plane if they were already powerful enough. So, in order to convince the Helping Hand to come here and forego the things they could otherwise be stealing, Ashton needed to offer things like jewellery, coins, precious metals, or gemstones. Just anything with value. That was why Ashton could use the Salesman's gold ink.

Usually, it would need to be a little different, and Ashton did shift the mana pattern a little bit and thin the ink down a bit more, but since this was a low-grade ink being used to summon a lowest-grade spirit, it could skip a lot of the usual restrictions that came with an ink that didn't quite fit right.

Ashton drew the contract circle, placing a few pieces of jewellery onto the cornerstones, and then put both of his hands into the centre of the pattern while he was wearing the ring-gloves.

He cleared his throat, and filled his voice with magic. Ashton could see the shifts in the veil forming above him as the magic circle was already forming a connection to the spirit realm. It seemed like Millie was also taking a break from the work that Ashton had given her, and was just eagerly staring at him.

"I call on you, oh thief of thieves, oh right hand of kings. I ask of you, come to my side and extend my reach. Let me stretch my hand toward the sky and grasp all that I wish. The rewards I offer will be plenty, more than you may ever carry."

As the magic from his voice intertwined with the magic being gathered and changed by the contract circle, Ashton could feel that shift in the veil grow larger, as something came through.

[You have formed a contract with a [Helping Hand]!]

[[Helping Hand] is being registered in the [Spirit Summoning] Category]

[Skill | Summoning - Level 13 -> Summoning - Level 14]

Through his connection to the spirit and the view of the world through the wisp's eyes, what Ashton saw in front of him was a mass of mist that would be invisible to everyone else. A living cloud of pure magic, with two tendrils extending from it. At the end of each tendril, Ashton saw a glove.

They were white gloves that looked like they would fit Ashton's hands absolutely perfectly.

Ashton watched as the mass of mist mimicked his body and stood in front of him. The tendrils turned into arms, and the spirit held out one of their hands toward him.

With a smile, Ashton shook it. "Glad to be working with you."

The mass of mist immediately turned back into a more undefined form, as the two hands moved around the rooftop, curiously inspecting everything around them. What Ashton knew with this spirit was that they could be taught certain 'behaviours'. Specific motions that they could mimic, which was particularly useful to pass on certain parts of potion-making. Hell, maybe Ashton could even teach the helping hands to harvest magic stones. He wasn't entirely sure how much he could teach them exactly.

The Summoner only ever used them as direct extensions. They had dozens of limbs, but of course a limited range, and used the Helping Hand to reach every tiny corner of their miles-long channelling range.

The biggest benefit of the Helping Hand was that Ashton could directly channel his magic through them, like they were a true extension of his body. And figuring that he should try it out and see how exactly it worked, Ashton quickly channelled his newly contracted spirit.

Instantly, the hands stopped moving on their own as the shapeless mist was pulled into the ring gloves. The only trace that was left of that mist were the thin threads that connected to the floating white gloves.

Every motion that Ashton was doing, the spirit mimicked perfectly. All the small, minute twitches of his fingers were translated perfectly. Ashton used the gloves to pick up something in the tent. He could feel the pressure of actually holding the small cub press onto his fingers and palm, but there was of course no weight.

The Helping Hands couldn't carry anything that was heavier than what Ashton could already carry himself, of course. Ashton could move the helping hands around his channelling range the same way he could move the elementals, and since he already had a lot of practice, he could do so with a fair amount of precision and speed.

While on the other end of his channelling range, Ashton started forming a spell circle. It was just the 'silence' spell, nothing special, just something that let Ashton test out this channelling ability.

"Perfect," Ashton said with a relieved smile. There were still a lot of things that he would have to figure out with these, and he would learn how to plan ahead to make proper use of the Helping Hand, but this would be a wonderful addition to Ashton's arsenal.

And with that being the case, Ashton quickly moved on. There was more that he had to summon.

"That's so cool!" Millie exclaimed excitedly, grasping their fist tightly. "What's this one called?"

Ashton smiled. "They're the Helping Hand spirit. And remember, this one is a spirit; giving the Living Abode a nickname is fine, but names are a lot more special for spirits, so just actually only call them 'Helping Hand'."

Millie nodded. "Okay! So the Helping Hand is just the pair of gloves and that mist?"

"Mm-hmm. That's basically–" Ashton responded, a little absent-mindedly, before realising what Millie actually just asked. The Helping Hand seemed similarly surprised. "Wait, what did you just say? You can see that mist?"

Millie tilted her head confused. "Yeah. Why?"

"Hm... well, usually, that mist shouldn't be visible... and from what you told me, you can't see mana normally..." Ashton pointed out, focusing on the spirit. "But maybe you can see the mist this time because it's the Helping Hand's body? Interesting."

Ashton smiled. Did this mean that things like invisibility spells were useless in front of Millie as well? There weren't many spells that could hide the soul itself, just ones that hid the physical body while masking mana signatures. In that case, Millie would have a huge advantage later on. It wouldn't be possible to sneak up on her.

Monsters would only get stronger from now on, and their magic more potent. That also included monsters who were built around stealth, obviously. Those were often the most dangerous ones. After all, how were you supposed to defend against an opponent you didn't even know was there?

This ability just on its own would increase Millie's survivability considerably.

But even without that, Ashton would help her survive to the end of this all. He was her guardian now, so there was no way that he would let her fall victim to the council.

And to do that, he had to summon a few more beings.

First was one more elemental. The different inks were done by now and could be used to draw the two contract circles, which Ashton drew one after another. He would wait to actually contract for the carbuncle until later, but the dryad could come along already. To summon it, Ashton used a small sapling that he had dug out with its roots and all.

[You have formed a contract with a [Lesser Dryad]!]

[[Lesser Dryad] is being registered in the [Elemental Summoning] Category]

[Skill | Veil Mastery - Level 5 -> Veil Mastery - Level 6]

As for the Dryad, what Ashton used was an oak sapling. It took on the form of a serpent made of the sapling's thin trunk, with the young leaves hanging off the sides of its head, giving it an appearance similar to a cobra.

Ashton had already prepared something to test out the dryad's power. A small bag of seeds. The sort that you could get at a grocery store.

He emptied it out onto his hand and channelled the lesser dryad's magic, immediately taking control of the seeds. They sprouted immediately, soon growing into a bunch of intertwined flowers. Their roots were intertwining themselves with the chains of Ashton's ring glove, holding onto them for support.

These flowers were pretty, but they wouldn't last long as they were. They would be fine as long as Ashton continued to provide mana to them, but a level one lesser dryad wasn't capable of letting plants stay permanent. Maybe in the future, with an advancement. Or, at the very latest, once this cute little snake reached the stage of a regular dryad, this should be in the realm of its abilities.

But well, at the very least, Ashton should be able to use the dryad to skip a few stages in a growth cycle, making sure that seeds laid their roots in the right soil. This was one of the reasons why Ashton contracted the dryad in the first place. He would need to mass-produce a lot of certain plants if he ever wanted to make enough money to buy that staff from the Salesman in a reasonable amount of time.

Of course, the dryad was incredibly useful in combat as well. They were the best elemental when it came to trapping and restricting opponents, and if Ashton got his hands on the right seeds, he could use them to spread poison through monster camps as well.

The serpent moved up Ashton's arm and coiled around his neck this time. It was a bit tight, but not exactly in a bad way. Poppy seemed to be getting along with the dryad as well, and leaned onto its wooden body with a smile.

But now, there was a third contract that Ashton had to form right here on the rooftop. And while he felt a little bad 'wasting' this, Ashton got incredibly lucky that he got his hands on an Uncommon Settlement Box earlier. Otherwise, he might have had to make his own.

But knowing what type of things there were in the Common Settlement Box, Ashton figured that this one would have exactly the right objects ready to be used.

And then, scrolling through the selection screen, Ashton found it. A large amount of soil-filled clay vases. Specifically, ones like these were used, at least in Lumia, when a people needed to grow specific medicinal herbs under particular conditions. Magic pots that could control soil temperature and moisture easily. Nomadic tribes often had these, and he also often saw similar ones in desert tribes where the soil they had access to could only really be used for one or two types of plants, if for anything at all.

Ashton didn't know whether or not these were the exact sort of pots that he knew, but from the written description in this system window, they seemed similar.

And the being that Ashton would contract now would be in charge of actually managing all these pots, and maximizing their efficiency.
