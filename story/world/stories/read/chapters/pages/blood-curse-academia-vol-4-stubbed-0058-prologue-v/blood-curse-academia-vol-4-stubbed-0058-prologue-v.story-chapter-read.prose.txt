Prologue

Chiame exalted in her freedom. Plenty of conditions had to be made in exchange for this artificial body, and yet she still believed those who granted it to her were absolutely nincompoops. Death held no sway over her now. Her new body lacked many of her original’s senses. She no longer could smell or taste. The trade was that she now required no nutritional substance or air in her lungs. Only an occasional resupply of the goopy black ichor that the artificial golem used. And she knew the recipe for that, far from a complex concoction for someone of her skill. Very few obstacles stood between her and immortality. An eternal existence for her to play with.

She skipped down the passages of The Great Labyrinth Sekai. She required no map, for an innate sense pulled her closer to her love. Kizu. Not the bratty one that existed up on the overworld. Her Kizu. The piece of the soul that was the true successor of the crone. The greatest witch to ever exist. He embodied every ideal that Chiame admired. He stood alongside Calamities and acted as an advisor to the greatest powers in the world. Just thinking of Kizu sent shivers though Chiame’s cold, unfeeling body.

And he’d chosen to bond to her. Their souls united in a tangle only ever known between witch and familiar. A forbidden link. Even the Dragons feared bonds like hers.

Chiame had half a dozen irritating restrictions placed on her by the weaker Kizu. But those would all pass in time. Eventually, the world would be hers. And she had plenty of interesting activities to keep her occupied for the next century. This was a far better outcome than she could have dreamed of.

Her body flickered, invisible as she passed a denizen of the treacherous dungeon. A creature made of magma and stone slowly turned in her direction, but upon seeing nothing, it returned to wading around in its magma pool.

Avoiding detection with ease, she stepped around the corner and flickered back to a visible visage. This body was excellent. It would serve her very well in the centuries to come.

How long it took her to reach the Blood Lord’s lair remained a mystery to her. She simply continued down the winding tunnels, following her soul’s bond, until she found herself at the gates of the underground palace. Time mattered little while piloting an unrelenting body.

Rather than deal with the bloodspawns’ annoying questions, Chiame went invisible and strolled by the guards. She had no patience for servants and lackeys. These people had forfeited their souls to the Blood Lord in exchange for power. Now they were little more than fodder with the capacity of thought.

Half of the lair was a sunken castle keep, designed for defense, while the other half was a luxurious palace. Both had been absorbed into Sekai thousands of years ago. The halls melded the two styles together, often switching between soft carpets and hard stone in a seemingly nonsensical combination. Torches lit the walls, glowing blue. In previous discussions, Kizu had explained to her that these fires exuded a coolness rather than natural heat. But this new body lacked the capacity to feel those sensations. Even as she passed by a room coated in ice, her body remained utterly uncaring of temperature.

However, the frozen room still put her at unease, regardless of the cold’s inconsequential impact on her. It was the first thing to ignite her nerves a bit since her freedom from the summoner girl’s body. Her pact required her to avoid any interactions with the Calamities. One word to the wrong individual could result in her soul extinguishing in an instant. She needed to proceed with caution when passing near Otochi’s resting place.

But those worries evaporated from her mind as she exited the room and followed her soul’s link down stairs into a dungeon. She smiled. A dungeon within the great World Dungeon. How fitting.

Kizu, her love, sat on a table crusted with old blood, his legs crossed and back to her. His long green hair, shaggy and unkempt.

“I’m surprised your friends haven’t licked that clean,” she said, dropping her invisibility.

“They’ve tried,” Kizu said, unmoving. “The blood doesn’t suit their unique palettes."

Chiame giggled. “Kitsune blood? Then you have the Harbinger here?”

In response, Kizu twisted his head around to face Chiame and beckoned her over with a hand. Once close, he spun himself around and leaned into her from his position on the table. He ran a finger over her new body, pausing at several nooks and joints. His eyes lit up with a mad greed.

“Yes. Yes. Yes. I can put this into use. You’re now exactly what I need.”

She shivered under his scrutiny. Every morsel of her being craved pleasing him. Well, nearly every morsel, at least. A small natural instinct still craved survival above all else.

“Concessions were made in exchange for this new body,” she reluctantly admitted. “I can’t knowingly assist the Calamities in their objectives. Should I fail to uphold this pact, my soul is forfeited to nothingness.”

Kizu leaned back and considered. He didn’t look disappointed, but rather intrigued. Then he gestured over to the side. She followed his pointed finger.

There was a cage on the side of the room. Inside was a curled up fox, its size far larger than a normal fox while still smaller than a true dire beast. Patches of the fox’s fur missing, with the rest of the fur matted down with blood and grime.

Chiame crouched down next to the cage and her golem face smiled at the creature within. A half-breed Kitsune. This was her Harbinger. Hon and Tross shared this creature. Brought down and caged by Otochi. The Kitsune didn’t even raise her head to meet her scrutiny. Her bloody snout rested on crossed paws. One eye was too swollen to see out of, but the other tracked Chiame with an obvious disgust. Much to Chiame’s amusement, that eye also held a hint of fear as well.

“I just saw her son and daughter earlier,” Chiame commented. “They bargained the girl’s life to the Tross witches in exchange for their protection from my old coven.”

That got the Kitsune to stir. She opened her mouth to bare her teeth. But instead thick blood dribbled out of her lips. Most of her teeth were missing. It probably made snacking quite painful.

Kizu chuckled. “Quite the adventure up there! I heard the Dragons failed their little conquest. Hubris! They really thought they needed just three members of their race to conquer Shinzou Island?”

“Otochi is only one,” Chiame commented. “You believe he can accomplish what three Calamities failed to do?”

Kizu sprang to his feet and slammed both hands into the cage that held the Kitsune, rattling the bars. His prisoner flinched and hunched her shoulders. He smiled at the overt fear in her eyes then leaned back, his body loose as he spun back to face Chiame.

“Otochi may be the only full Blood Lord in existence. But, unlike the Dragons, he’s not committing all his actions on a whim. Unlike them, we decided not to work with Necro after his failure in Hon.”

“But you do have a plan?”

“No, no. Darling.” Kizu waggled a finger at her. “You said yourself, no work talk. My experiments with this fox must remain a secret.”

Through their bond, Chiame felt a wave of ecstasy. He truly cared about her. The witch melted under his caring sneer.

“I have a mission for you. You’ll need to travel far to the west. Can you do that for me?”

“If it lets us be together, then anything.” Chiame truly meant the words too. She’d sacrifice all for this green-haired visionary.

“Excellent. You’ll be tasked with locating my dear sister.”
