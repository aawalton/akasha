One single toll of the bell was usually to summon Cuthward or Lord Ceonwin to the great hall. Three tolls of the bell were meant to gather all the city officials for an important meeting.

The bell kept tolling, five times, then ten times. Something was happening beyond a simple request for a meeting.

I tugged on my cloak and pulled up my hood, then ducked out of my tent and rushed to the great hall. Already, the new portreeve had arrived, along with Lord Ceonwin and a whole host of other city officials. We had two Dupe commanders, and a regular human commander appointed as the head of the mercenary companies. Besides them, the entire squad from the 294th that I’d left here over a year ago was now in command of the militia. All of them were lieutenants, and the old sergeant was now a commander.

It was a bit of a mess, but they all reported to Lord Ceonwin for now.

Lord Ceonwin stood at the end of the hall. Everyone was hushed as they entered. So far, we only had commanders and lieutenants here, but there were still over twenty of them in total. It shouldn’t have been this quiet unless it was really important.

I snuck into the hall with the swell of hurried people, keeping my head down while looking like I belonged. One of the commanders recognized me, but he said nothing—he just gave me a quiet nod.

“Our scouts report that the orcs are moving,” Lord Ceonwin announced. “They are attacking sooner than we thought. Right now, they are forming battle lines, and they will be marching within the hour.”

They must’ve noticed us picking up the pace on the wall. They were trying to attack while we still had an obvious weakness.

I cursed under my breath. My squad hadn’t gotten back yet, and I was going to have to enter battle without them? Against a Hangman? They were supposed to be high-up in the Gold tier.

“We have civilians scattered across the countryside,” Ceonwin continued. “I sent riders to evacuate them and rush them to the walls, or tell them to flee farther west with all of their most important possessions. However, we will not have time to get them out if we don’t stall the orcs. Worse, the wall is still an hour away from completion. I did not want to do this, but we must meet them in an open field to bye time.”

Cuthward, who stood near the edge of the hall, pulled me aside into a dark corner. There were no braziers here, and I was completely shaded by a banner. He whispered, “Lord Ceonwin wishes to appoint you as a temporary commander of a cavalry unit. He won’t say it now, but he needs you.”

“Is he trying to reveal me?”

“He has a spare set of armour,” Cuthward says. “The plan is simple; you don’t need to be here for the entire meeting. Get in his spare armour, and get ready. He expected this eventuality—for deploying you in the field. Most of the men either know you, or are under the impression that we also have a Commander Lemming with us.”

“Me, as a cavalry commander?”

“You learned some formations and cavalry tactics during your time with the sappers.”

“I never had time to practice anything,” I hissed.

“You are the best rider we have, aside from Lord Ceonwin himself,” Cuthward said. “That’s what Ceonwin claims, at least. The Drillmaster acknowledges that you have at least had a bit of riding experience, with jousting, and with the ambushes that you led two winters ago.”

“That was one ambush,” I whispered. “But yes. If it must be done, then count me in. Have someone find Burr, and show me where Ceonwin’s spare armour is. I’ll lead our riders out to the field and face them.”

“Come with me, my lord,” Cuthward said. He led me around the edge of the hall, then to a back room—one of the residence rooms that Lord Ceonwin had staked out. I slipped inside with Cuthward, and he pointed me to a set of armour near the back of the room. “I believe you have already jousted in his armour before.”

“He told you that story, did he?”

“Yes. This spare helmet also lacks a plume—you will not be identified as a lord or Ealdorman, especially if we put you in an orange cloak.”

“If you can, I’d appreciate a hand getting ready. It’ll make this quicker.”

~ ~ ~

Fifteen minutes later, coated in Lord Ceonwin’s spare chainmail and scale mail, and wearing a plume-less helmet, I rushed out of the hall. A pair of Dupes held Burr’s reins, and I swung up onto her saddle.

Cuthward pointed to the gate, then said, “They are gathering in the eastern fields, just before the valley slopes up. Lord Ceonwin wishes for you to attack and disrupt them before they finish their formation. He doesn’t expect you to win—you just need to hold them until we can get everyone inside the walls.”

“Understood.” I gripped Burr’s reins and gave them a snap, then took her through the city at a gallop. We passed the inner palisade wall. Already, horns bellowed in the cooling night air, and soldiers cleared the streets. Archers rushed to the walls, and city officials rushed to gather the civilians, instructing anyone able-bodied to help gather water and prepare to put out fires, or to help the most vulnerable get to safety inside the inner wall.

I didn’t expect this to be a long siege, but we couldn’t afford to take chances. We’d best get them inside the wall, just in case.

Burr raced to the gate of the outer wall. Soldiers rushed through the center, and civilians passed through the edges, doing their best to scramble out of the way.

The soldiers parted for me as they prepared to mount a defense outside the gate, and Burr trotted up to the edge of the valley, where the cavalry was gathering. In total, we had about two-hundred mounted troops.

I rode to the head of their formation, then said, “I need two lines. The orcs are forming up into units, so we can expect a Black Captain. We also know there is a Hangman. We are not there to kill them. Our goal is to delay them. I will lead the first line ahead. We believe they have fell-fox riders. The first line’s goal is to lead the riders away, and the second line will attack from the side, flanking their foot soldiers and avoiding any pikemen they have.”

A pair of lieutenants rode up to me, carrying their shields and spears at the ready. I didn’t recognize them immediately, but like most of the Dupes here, they did know who I was. I pulled off my helmet.

“Commander…” one said, then whispered, “My lord.”

The other gave me a knowing nod.

The point wasn’t to hide my identity from the Dupes. They’d all been sworn to secrecy already. The point was that, for the reports, it wouldn’t show that I led the charge, and the regular militiamen or civilians wouldn’t see me around.

“You two understand your orders?” I asked.

They both bowed their heads.

“Get the men moving. Tell them the plan.” I pointed at one of the lieutenants. “Bring a quarter of your men with me. We don’t need a large number to distract the fell-foxes. Just enough to pull them away.” I pointed at the second lieutenant. “Once we sound our horn a second time, you charge up the hill.”

“Yes, my lord,” they both whispered, then trotted their horses back to the lines. Shouts rose up as they rearranged into their formations. I positioned myself at the front of the smaller group.

Riding in front of the crowd, I pulled my helmet on and raised my spear. Though I wasn’t wielding my standard shield, I hoped I wouldn’t need something that durable. If we saw the Hangman, we were to run away, not engage.

“There’s no time for a speech. Do your best, watch each others’ backs, and kill what you can. If we’re lucky, this will all be over by sunrise.”

I pointed my spear. A lieutenant sounded a horn, and the first group was off. We had fifty riders, though it looked like more with how spread out we were. A banner trailed behind us, and the riders’ helmets’ plumes floated behind them.

I took a diagonal route up the valley’s slope. Instead of charging straight up, we would hit it at a sideways angle, so we could take stock of what we were up against. As a bonus, any orc that saw us would estimate our numbers to be much higher than they actually were.

When we reached the top of the ridge, I stopped. There was a slight slope on the opposite side, where the orcs had set up an encampment. Tents made of pelts and furs dotted the land, and rudimentary defenses surrounded them—wooden stakes angled outward to dissuade an attack from a group of riders.

Orcs poured out of the camp. High-pitched, woody horns sounded, ordering them around. It reminded me more of a clarinet than a war-horn, but at least the orcs were clear about whose horns were whose.

I couldn’t say how I knew, but something tingled. It wasn’t the same as the Warlord’s presence, and it couldn’t have been a single person. It was more like a web stretching over the land ahead.

Was that the impact of a Black Captain? And I was just strong enough to sense it, now?

Holding my chin high, swept my gaze over the forming ranks. Orcs held mismatched weapons, but those at the front did all wield spears and some kind of shield—anything from tower shields to bucklers. They were ready to intercept a mounted charge from the front.

If my riders moved right, we could convince them that we were the only riders. They would focus on us. One of the ways orcs dealt with cavalry, according to Moonhand, was this exact play. Keep the horses back with pikes and spears, then intercept them with your own riders.

Once we drew all their attention, our other group of riders would sweep across their flank before they could prepare lines.

I firmed up my expression, then pointed my spear and yelled, “Forward!”

We raced toward the orcs. It was near impossible to get horses to willingly charge into a wall of spears, though, and I didn’t even try. We angled to the side before we hit them, holding up our shields to catch a spattering of arrows. The orcs didn’t have a full volley ready, but it was enough to cause us problems.

We galloped in front of their line, as if we were looking for an opening, all while aiming north. We’d move as if we were trying to hit their flank, and that would—

Something ahead of us yipped. In the dim light, I strained my enhanced vision.

A swarm of fell-fox riders bounded around from the northern flank of the army.

“Here they come!”
