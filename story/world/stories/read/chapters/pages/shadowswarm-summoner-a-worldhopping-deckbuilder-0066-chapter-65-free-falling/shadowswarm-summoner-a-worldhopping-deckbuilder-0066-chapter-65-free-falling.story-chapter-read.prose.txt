Chapter 65: Free Falling

As Ambrose fell to his death, he couldn’t help but laugh at the absurdity of it. [Cloud Cushion] still had ten seconds left in it, but what would another ten seconds do? There was nothing but death beneath him. And worst of all, he wouldn’t even get to enjoy going out in such a fiery, explosive way. The rising heat would kill him before he got to splash in lava.

Round 2

Cantrip: [Vampiric Kiss], Spell: [Assassin’s Strike], Spell:[Shred Mind], Creature: [Festering Wretch], Creature: [Night Terror]

His back was to the lava, so during the Pause, he got to stare at the bottom of Bedrock Spire, just out of reach. Soon to be just a dull, metallic splotch in the distance. None of his creatures could save him now, though he briefly thought about trying to get his imps to fly him out of this. It gave him a good chuckle, at least.

Ambrose thought of his family. This was really all their fault, if he thought about it. If his father hadn’t been a craven idiot and bad gambler, he wouldn’t have pissed away the fortune after Grandfather died. All that pressure, coming down on him for being born gifted in a way his father wasn’t. Bastards, all of them.

Even his siblings. Cynthia was just old and bratty enough to be interesting, and Barabas…it wasn’t too late to stop him from becoming like their father. When time sped back up and the fall resumed, Ambrose deeply wished he’d been a better brother. Now, because of him (well, their father, really), everything would come crashing down on them. He couldn’t save them anymore. Maybe he never could.

His stomach dropped, and the urge to cry and laugh hit him. How long did he have to fall? The wind around him was hot, and would only get hotter, but the freefall almost made it soothing. For now.

What could he do? Falling like this, it had the incredibly unfortunate dichotomy of him not wanting to die, but also wanting it to be over, because the wait, the fall, was infinitely worse than the landing could ever be.

Things had been so weird all year, and now he’d rebelled with his friends. Gods, his friends. They were probably going to get killed. That damned mole was beatable, but he’d correctly identified the biggest threat and took him out. He almost respected the move.

What could he do? The answer had always seemed to be better cards, more power, more resources. He had new cards in his pocket, but what good would they do him?

Wait.

There was a chance. A small one, if he acted now. There was ten seconds left on the timer.

Ambrose activated [Cloud Cushion] and pulled out the remaining cards they’d bought. Two of his, and three for Jessica. He pulled the correct card out and examined it.

[Devil Form: Rare. Shed your mortality and spread your wings to embrace your inner darkness.

Special: Avatar. User transforms into a full demon for 3 rounds.

Special: Flying

Special: When your creatures die, grow in strength

Special: Requires the sacrifice of a creature each round to stay active

Strength: A, Defense, C, Speed: A, Magic: C. 5 Mana]

It was Affinity Level 5, just out of his range to equip, and probably would be for another year. And then he’d still need to draw it in order to use it. There was no telling when combat would officially end, but his fear and adrenaline seemed to be keeping it going. He hoped.

What could he do? If he equipped a card he wasn’t ready for, he’d fall into a coma like his grandfather. But then, was that such a bad thing? It would be a mercy, not feeling the heat get worse and worse.

And there was no telling what it would become when he equipped it. What if it did something stupid and turned him into an imp? Ugh.

But it was all he could do. So why not?

Ambrose removed [Night Terror] from his deck and let it fly away. [Shred Mind] was now better anyway. He took a deep breath, and equipped the card.

Images flashed through his head, the way they did with every level up. Cards flashed in front of his eyes, rocketing past him and falling into nothing. Each one had a laughing face, a horrible little imp cackling at his predicament. All of them except for one, moving slower than the others. He reached out and grasped it and watched as the imp’s simian face sharpened into something more or less human. Two small but regal horns grew from the demon’s crown.

[Imp King: Unique. Hybrid. Embrace your new role as a rabblerouser and nuisance, a thorn in the side of authority. Take this power, Ambrose, and break things.

Special: Avatar. Transforms into a demon form for 3 rounds.

Special: Flying.

Special: Disposable Subjects. When a creature you control dies, it explodes, activating any abilities or spell-like effects it possessed.

Special: Usable out of combat. Using this card immediately starts combat and costs 1 charge. Charges replenish every week.

Strength: B. Defense: C. Speed: A. Magic: A. 5 Mana/3 charges remaining]

Ambrose blinked, not believing what he saw. He’d never seen or heard of a card naming the user before. And it gave him exactly what he needed. Almost like it was a second Wildcard. But how? Why?

That didn’t matter just now. [Cloud Cushion] ran out, and once more he plummeted to his death. But not for long.

Cackling like a madman or fool, he cast [Imp King].

Heat buffeted him from below as he fell, rising in temperature until it was like standing next to an oven. The spell took its time to kick in, but when it did, Ambrose gasped. It hurt, but in a primally satisfying way, like cracking your back or peeling dead skin. His body bulked up with muscle, limbs elongating. The horns were the best part, he had to admit, and feeling them sprout from his temples made him remember Raeva’s words to him just a few weeks before.

“The more I think about it, the more a [Hateful Imp] sounds perfect for you,” Raeva had said. “It reflects your rotten, twisted soul.”

Well, the wild magic recognized his rotten, twisted soul and gave him a gift. Wings sprouted from his back, last but not least, and he pumped them. His flight wasn’t reliant on them, but they helped him maneuver. He rose, faster and faster, back towards the city, just barely out of reach.

Round 1

Cantrip: [Vampiric Bite], Spell: [Shred Mind], Spell: [Thought Tap], Spell: [Assassin’s Strike], Signature Creature: [Hateful Imps], Creature: [Festering Wretch]

Equipping that card had indeed risen his Affinity Level, and it advanced his cantrip, giving him a bigger deck size and hand size, and an incredible rise in his mana. This was it, what it was like to have unlocked the proficient level of Shadow. This was the point where he decided whether he would continue down that path or try a second affinity.

But it could wait a little longer. For now, he had as many imps to cast as possible. They were in some serious trouble.

Funny enough, Luthor had expected one of the most logical possible deaths to be by execution. It came with the whole rebellion territory, and his mother had made it very clear what awaited them if anyone understood what they were doing. Well, Vanderborn knew, and he still allowed them to operate. But then, Vanderborn was a small fry in the grand scheme of things.

They were a couple levels down now, almost to the bottom. There wouldn’t be much time before they were thrown off. Oddly enough, Luthor was handling it well. Not Raeva.

“Let me go! Let me go or I’ll kill you. I’ll fucking kill you all, just let me go!” she screamed and fought the entire time. No matter how many times someone cuffed her or slammed her into a wall, she kept fighting. “I want my brother, right now!”

This time, the captain of the group slammed his knee into her stomach. It cut off her screaming into a pained wheeze. Then, just because, he backhanded Raeva, smiling smugly the whole time. Until he got a look at the way Luthor stared at him.

“What the hell is your problem? She’s at least acting reasonable for someone about to die. You’re just staring. Do you think someone is coming to help you? You still have some hope left?”

“Yes, actually,” said Luthor, smiling serenely. He hadn’t given up on Mulch. Dexi had reminded him to rely on the molekin. Things were bad, but they wouldn’t be for much longer.

Or they would, and it would all be over. Either way, there were only two outcomes to worry about now, and both left him feeling mostly okay with it. Luthor found himself wishing he’d dropped and done more in the field with his mother’s forces. Rentan Academy had been almost nothing but poison.

“You’re a fool,” the man sneered.

“Maybe,” Luthor admitted, “but at the very least, my other friends are going to live for now. That’s worth hoping for.”

That earned him a fist to the stomach. It hurt plenty, but it also bought them time. And the pain was a reminder that he was still alive. He’d gladly welcome it and whatever else the captain here had to give.

“You people make me sick. You know how many good men you killed?” He pulled out a small leather strap.

“N-none,” Raeva wheezed.

“Yes,” Luthor agreed, forcing his breathing to be steady, “None of you are good people, given what you’re doing to the people here.”

He welcomed the pain that followed, and the precious time it bought them. Without it, things could’ve turned out differently. Beaten and bloody, Luthor grinned at the captain and looked past him.

“You should probably look behind you.”

“Do you really think I’m going to fall for that?” he sneered, brandishing the strap again.

“Ancestors,” Raeva managed, now laughing.

At that, the man turned around. “Oh.”

A horned and winged form hurled relentlessly through the air, leading a pack of nine demented looking imps. Their eyes radiated malice and hunger, visible even from a distance. The winged figure stopped just short and said, in a deep but silky voice, “Pathetic worms. Let them go and run…while I let you.”

The captain recovered and pointed his strap Ambrose’s way. “Get him!”

Ambrose laughed maniacally and pointed a sharp clawed fingertip at them. The swarm of imps descended on the group of guards, almost equalling them in number. Luthor and Raeva were dragged to the side just in time for the forces to collide.

The guards were armed and ready for violence, but the imps were vicious, fast, and had no regard for their own life. Luthor saw one cackle and even turned its head, presenting its neck to a man with a spear. He took the opening and jabbed, only for the creature to explode a moment later with an irritating pop. It hit another imp, which also exploded, and then...

It was a chaotic mess of colors, noise, and spells going off. Pops and cracks, screams rising and then cut short. The end result was that almost all of the guards were quite dead. Only the two holding him and Raeva remained.

Ambrose pointed his finger at their captors. “Run, or you’re next. You have five seconds.”

They didn’t hesitate. The man holding Raeva threw her down and booked it, and the one behind Luthor followed. Part of him longed for the chance to get him back, but that would have to be later.

“Just in time to save you all, as per usual. You now address a king, incidentally. I’ve got some weird and important things to share, later.” Ambrose hovered in place for a few seconds longer, before his demonic features faded away like ash on the wind. He then dropped a few feet onto his ass.

“Ambrose, you jerk,” Raeva cried, launching herself at him. She hugged him for all she was worth, while Luthor stood and stretched.

“Just in time to see you almost be cool, and then ruin it. Great job, Ambrose.”

He didn’t seem to care, as he hugged the Bedamin princess back just as fiercely. Luthor let them have it for a few seconds longer before he broke the bad news.

“Jessica and Raeleq were taken by Boss Parl, up to the top of the city. We can’t help them now.”

“Oh, I don’t know about that,” said Ambrose, parting and standing. He wore a cockier grin than usual. “I just got even stronger, and I don’t think any of them can take me now. Let’s do this.”

“No,” Luthor insisted. “We can’t help them now. We have to trust that they’ll be okay for a little while longer. We need to regroup and come at this from another angle.”

“Like hell we will,” Raeva protested. “I am not leaving my baby brother behind.”

“Your baby brother is six foot five,” Ambrose said, almost by reflex. He shut up when she shot him a dirty look, but Luthor didn’t let that anger build up.

“We have to. Our way out is here, and I don’t think they signed up for a fight.”

Ambrose threw his arms up in the air. “What are you talking about?”

Luthor pointed, and the others turned. Off in the distance, ten flying figures came their way, getting bigger by the second.

“Bats?” Raeva said, screwing up her eyes.

“Bats,” Luthor confirmed. “Courtesy of our good friend Mulch.”
