The party was in full swing when they made it back. Luke sort of wanted to practice more with the ability to follow healing echo threads for teleportation. If he got it to a place where he could use it without almost bleeding out, it would turn into a more than powerful ability. The possibilities would be endless. But, as was so often the case, the trip back was not the time for it. Curtis didn't have anything like that skill, and leaving him seemed like an asshole move after what he'd just been through.

Once back, Milla invited the Reaver into The Band before handing him over to Tiffany and Triss, who pulled him onto the part of the main hall they'd designated as the dance floor. Luke had never seen anyone quite so uncomfortable before.

"What happened?" Milla asked him.

Once Luke had explained the Intruders, the strange object Curtis found, and the countdown, his sister looked worried.

"What do you think it's counting down to?"

Luke shrugged. "System event, maybe?"

"System, what?"

Another session of explanation followed, throughout which Milla kept giving him a strange look. When Luke was through, he returned the look. "What?"

"You're getting into a lot of mischief, aren't you?"

"Mischief?"

Milla chuckled. "Sorry, I've been talking to Lemira a little, and her archaic way of speaking has rubbed off on me. What I meant to say is that you're a shit-stirrer of the highest degree."

"I just keep healing people, and it keeps working."

"Mhm," Milla said. "Any other monumental events or pieces of information I should know about?"

"Don't think so."

Milla was about to say something else, but Luke interrupted her. "No, I've got one more thing. Or two, maybe."

He launched into an explanation of what he saw as the system weave, Smudge and his disappearance, the Singers, meeting Guide again, the fact he'd killed Alan, and how he'd done it.

His sister looked a little pale afterwards. "No wonder you don't want to be guildmaster. You don't have time with all that crap you keep unearthing."

"Right?" Luke said. "Better have you do the hard stuff."

"Yeah... That's why Dot isn't here? The Alan business?"

That sobered Luke a little. "It's part of it."

Milla shook her head. "I told you to go for Hannah. You never listen."

"Um, what? I doubt Hannah sees me as anything other than a friend."

"Oh, little brother. How naïve you are."

"I'm not," Luke said, looking out across the throng of people partying to catch Hannah's gaze. She sat on a wooden bench, one foot propped up beside her, arm resting over her knee. She raised her chin to him in greeting before taking a sip from a bottle of beer.

"See?" Milla said.

"Shut up."

Milla let out a teasing laugh. One he'd heard a lot of times when they grew up together. She had always treated him like a small child, no matter if he was five, fifteen, or twenty-five. "Hey, I've got a question for you," he said, trying to change the subject.

"Mm?" Milla hummed, her expression showing she was on to what he was doing, but that she allowed it.

"How's Mom and Dad?"

He hadn't been the best son for a while. Not visiting was one thing, but he hadn't even given them a thought. They were always just there, bickering in the house, but that must have changed now. With all the upheaval the world was going through, nothing could stay the same.

"They're the same."

Luke coughed and turned to her. "You must be joking. You're joking, right?"

"Well. Some things have changed. They're Integrated, for one."

If Luke had been drinking, he would've spit it out. "What the hell, Milla! Since when?"

"I more or less forced them through it about a week ago. That way, I can give them some credits, and they'll have food and water no matter what happens. DIA is spearheading an operation to get those in need through the Tutorial Dungeon so they can set them up in guarded places and provide for them."

"That's pretty far from 'the same', dude!"

Milla laughed. "Maybe you're right. Anyway, they're safe and provided for. They're at Level 1, so they won't attract monsters even if some happen to get close."

"That's a thing?"

"DIA tested it while I worked there. It's a thing."

"Damn. Well, I hope they're doing well."

"You could go visit them. I'm sure they'd love that."

"I will. Once there's a little more time."

"There's never enough time, little brother. We have to make time for those we love."

He saw her look across the room to Lana, who was in deep conversation with Priyah.

"Did you find out what the DIA is doing with those people we rescued from the ants?"

"Yeah. They're being treated well as far as I can tell. They've got them learning English. I think the DIA will keep going even if the rest of the government crumbles. There are a lot of people needing to be kept safe, and they've amassed quite a lot of credits."

"Cool."

They sat in silence for a while, letting the music wash over them. Luke saw a whole bunch of people looking over at him, as if they wanted to speak with him but didn't want to interrupt.

"I'd better go mingle," Luke said. "We'll need to take a moment tomorrow so I can take another look at your lungs and mana channels."

"I'm fine."

Luke shook his head. "You're not, though."

"Go mingle, little brother. Don't forget to dance with Hannah."

"Shut up."

Time. He needed time to think. But his sister was right. Spending time with the other guild members was important as well. To that end, he headed over to Lemira and Hannah, who'd gone up on the dance floor. Luke's moves were... not great, but at least no one laughed in his face.

A little later, back in his own shelter, Luke couldn't help but think about that countdown. Lemira was the one person who had any sort of experience with system events, even if her knowledge had been passed down through Ezezal.

"Lemira?" he called out, before remembering she had been offered and accepted her own room in the guild headquarters.

Luke: "Have you heard of countdowns for system events?"

Lemira: "Countdown?"

Luke explained what he and Curtis had seen.

Lemira: "No. That does not match what I know of system events. My father never spoke of intruders."

Luke: "Damn, okay. This is something else then, probably."

Lemira: "The object you described is not something I have heard of, either, I'm afraid."

The messages ceased for a moment, and Luke got into bed. If he went any longer without sleep, he'd start hallucinating.

Lemira: "Thank you for bringing me into The Band. It will be an honor to fight for your system."

Luke: "Of course. I promised your father I would make sure you're safe."

Lemira: "Is that the sole reason for the invitation?"

Luke: "Of course not. You're a friend."

Lemira: "I consider you a friend as well. Tomorrow, we will fight together and grow stronger."

Luke: "Milla posted the assignments already?"

Lemira: "Indeed, she did. Tomorrow, then?"

Luke: "Tomorrow."

Going through the guild interface, Luke saw that his sister had indeed posted the different parties that would venture forth the next day. His own was without new faces. Hannah, Curtis, Elijah, Ray, and Lemira. She'd decided to put most of the high-level members into a single party. It made some sense, since a too-wide level difference would mean no one received experience unless the highest leveled member entered the portal first, but that meant putting the lower leveled people in danger. That was not Milla's style. Perhaps the teams would change once the others caught up a little, or they might not. Learning to work together was an important part of forming a party, after all.

Even though he was tired, Luke found he couldn't sleep. Ever since becoming Integrated, his need for it had lessened to a significant degree, and as he gained in levels and power, that need had almost disappeared. Almost. Luke wanted to sleep. More than anything, he wanted to sleep, even if the need wasn't quite there yet. Turning off his brain would be just the thing at that moment, but no. It wouldn't come.

"C'mon," he muttered.

After another couple of minutes of trying, Luke gave up. "Screw it!"

He got up again and stood, surveying the small shelter that was now his home. Adding the extra room had been premature. Since he no longer hosted guests, that bed wasn't needed. The question was what he should do with the space instead. Luke had never been one to need a lot of room.

"I've got it!" he said after giving the matter some more thought.

There was one activity he did where it might be helpful to have a dedicated space. Enchanting! He removed the bed and added a simple table, a chair in front of it, and a row of storage containers along the wall where he could store stuff. After giving it some thought, he added a small standing lamp to the desk and a fake, but real-looking, bonsai tree.

"Interior decorator extraordinaire," Luke said, nodding to himself in satisfaction.

Now, all he needed was to gather things he could use to train the skill.

The air was crisp, and the night silent, except for the occasional sound of monsters in the far distance. Alone time had been lacking, and he found the solace soothing. Not that Luke minded company or the many new friends he'd made, but before all that happened, he spent most of his days alone. The new circumstances were an improvement, of course, but the occasional moment on his own worked as a nice little breather.

A calm spreading through him, Luke went on the prowl for items to use when training enchantment.

Trash Bag of Health: A slight boost in maximum health.

When he first started out, he figured the only objects he could use were those that might work as armor, since weapons were a no-go. That theory went out the window in a hurry since he'd been able to enchant pebbles. Wrapping the trash bag around his arm gave him the bonus, but how the hell was he supposed to wear a pebble? In his pocket, maybe?

Small Rock of The Mind: A slight increase in mana regeneration.

Yep. In his pocket.

Strolling around the deserted street around his Shelter, Luke gathered some things he planned to use for testing the new enchantments that became available when he reached Level 3 in the profession, but the peaceful task was soon interrupted.

To get a small measure of distance from the guild headquarters, he'd placed the Shelter around a corner from it. When he rounded that corner to pick up what looked like a discarded coffee mug, muttering to himself about having turned into a trash collector, Luke saw them.

A group of people huddled in the darkness around the plaque that served as the door to The Band's headquarters. At first, he took them for party-going guild members, but there was something about how they stood and how quiet they were that made him take another look. No, these people were not members of The Band.

"Hey! Who the hell are you?" Luke shouted at them.

The four of them turned as one, and any notion Luke might still have had that they didn't mean harm disappeared. First, they looked shocked at having been discovered. Then their expressions turned to a mix of irritation and determination. Determination to do violence.
