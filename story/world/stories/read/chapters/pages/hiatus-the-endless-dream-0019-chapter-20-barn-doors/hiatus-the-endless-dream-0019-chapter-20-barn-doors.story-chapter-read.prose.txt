My mind spun in circles as I walked through the cornfields, my elongated arm/leg dragging painfully behind me. I couldn't look at it, but I didn't need to. The hairs on the appendage were sensitive to texture, like how your tongue knows how something would feel to lick without having done so before. When I was younger, I often imagined what a completely different sense would be like, but when I was thinking about that, I was thinking about psychics or mantis shrimp eyes. I hadn't considered texture and pressure-sensitive hairs.

The only saving grace was that my wounds had healed when the chimera's leg merged into my arm. Even though the changes were centered on my … limb, they rippled through my entire body; my previous bleeding wounds had scabbed over and sunken into my skin.

I hated how the arm felt. Unwieldy. Clunky. Itchy. It was like wearing someone's overly stretched skin. When my bones and muscles moved it felt like the rest of me lagged a split second behind. At points, I focused on the sensitive hairs just to stop thinking about the skin, but then that became too overwhelming, and I focused back on the skin.

On and on, the cycle went unbroken.

I was being silly. This was temporary. It shouldn't matter. I could bite my tongue off now and wipe my hands clean of all these feelings and changes, but the thought rang hollow. I wanted to win. It was selfish and foolish, but gods, I wanted to win, and if that took dealing with being the most discomforted I had ever felt in my own skin—so be it.

My bag was heavy, holding two large jugs, several canteens, shears, and a sheet I used to dampen the sound of metal on metal. The sheet had come from the house, but it was only polite to leave something behind when you stole, so I had left one of the gas canisters in the oven. As I approached the barn, I took one of the prepared canteens, unscrewed the lid, and chucked it in the opposite direction that I was walking, still near the barn. I almost chased after the canteen, but controlled the impulse

Despite the lack of wind, the cornfield burst to life. Rustling seemed to resound from all around me as the stalks shook violently. I pressed myself into the ground, not daring to breathe until I heard the noises move further and further away. Quiet as a mouse, I slunk to the side of the large red building

The power lines didn't go directly to the barn. Instead, it looked like they led to a small unattached shed at the back—not even far enough for me to lie down between them from head to toe. I tried the door—naturally, it was locked. I peered in through one of the two windows. The moonlight illuminated a large breaker switch, which no doubt would power up the telephone somewhere around here. I didn't go for it. Instead, I found the back entrance of the barn and began to carefully retrieve items from my bag.

This was the dangerous part and the thing I was betting my life on. If there were a lot of monsters in there, this would be a bad time. I managed to get both Peeler and my last canteen of blood in one hand. I opened the door. In the brief moment before I entered, I could catch faint glimpses of open barn stalls lining the walls and a ladder near the entrance. Then I was in.

Almost pitch blackness. No windows. Two doors existed but only allowed meager light, which only sought to illuminate the nearest dirt patches. The building may have well as been hundreds of feet underground for all the difference it made.

I produced the canteen and, with shaking hands, twisted the cap. My mouth watered.

I heard movement immediately—the shifting of hay—the smell shouldn't have been able to even travel that fast.

Amidst hurried fumbling, I barely managed to toss the container away. There was no aiming and certainly no precision.

Ting.

A torrent of sound, of scrambling, of bodies slapping against wood, the trickles of fallen dirt, and the vibrating of creatures. Gods. I hadn't even heard it before, I was always too busy trying to survive to notice, but the vibrations made an audible sound in the air—like crickets or cicadas. Individually it was quiet, but there were so many of them they melded together into a wave of noise.

I had hoped there wouldn't be any monsters. Thought that if there were, it'd just be one, two, hell, maybe even three creatures. This sounded like so many more than that.

I paled as I remembered the scientist's notes. I had taken the liberty of skimming through them before I left. He hadn't started his journey with humans—that was "the logical end point," according to him. He had, instead, started with bugs.

The plan was a bust. It was time to leave. I creaked the door open and was instantly slowed. My eyes darted around wildly, trying to find where the creature was. Shit. My bag was still outside too. There was a small rustle of corn over by the shed, stones—rapidly and progressively closer, bounced off the ground to my feet.

As fast as I possibly could I tried to hook the end of Peeler one one of the backpacks loose straps. The movement was slow, and my body only listened to my commands from two seconds prior. The door was shutting, but unable to correct for my momentum the crowbar was briefly trapped in the doorway. I looked up through the small crack and tried to twist my body out of the thin blade of moonlight. Suddenly, half my vision was gone. I reeled back, the door finally shutting. Banging. Slamming from the other side, but the door didn't open for the creature.

Cutting pain dragged from my cheek, across my eye, and across my scalp in a thin line. There was wet, sweet warmth there, and my heart sank.

I was bleeding.

The sounds of buzzing continued. I frantically stripped the Hawaiian shirt I had taken from Nate—now completely shredded— and pressed it against my head. For only the second time in my life, prayed to the gods.

They didn't answer. Buzzing was drawn in by the overwhelming scent of blood—I felt tiny legs begin to land on exposed skin. In bursts. They'd land, step, bite, and then dart off back into the air.

I spasmed and whipped my body around, but I couldn't use my hand to slap them off as my only good one was holding both Peeler and the shirt keeping my blood from pouring out.

I didn't get any time to think as the air pressure shifted ever so slightly, a brief wind, and to my amazement and horror, I realized I could estimate where things were, even in the dark. It's how I knew the commotion had drawn one of the plant monsters and that it was staring straight at me, not more than a strides length away ... but I wasn't slowed? How was that—

Oh. Oh! HA! They couldn't see in the dark either!

The bugs began nipping at my neck, making it higher and higher to the source of heat. The plant creature took another step forward.

This wasn't going to work. Something had to give.

I dropped the shirt.

I hobbled forward, the horrid leg dragging behind me. I misjudged the downswing and felt an impact lower than I expected. It didn't matter where I hit, though, because I was on it now. Dropping Peeler I tucked low and kicked out with my knee, driving it between the creatures legs and slipping my leg around its. With my free hand I grabbed at the creatures waist and let gravity and forward momentum take us both to the ground.

The fall was more painful than I expected as my new appendage crumpled at an odd angle like I had just landed with locked legs. I hissed out in pain but used the sheer weight of the appendage to pin one of its arms down. I shimmied to the creature's chest and suppressed its remaining arm with my knee.

Peeler was too far away for a clean kill, so instead, I felt for the creature's eyes. Its head whipped from side to side, evading my attempts to find what I was looking for. I pinched at the squishy flesh hard, but my hands kept slipping. I grabbed the top of the head to keep it still, brought my own back, and aimed my forehead to the center of its face. There was a disturbing lack of resistance. These creatures didn't have bones like humans after all. The creature was stunned for a moment, and that's all I needed as I shoved every finger into its eye—and unlike last time, I could use my full body weight to push down

It convulsed in death throes, but I had already gotten to my feet.

The barn was silent. Not a vibration, not a sound. Even the bugs currently biting at my cheek were quiet.

I stumbled back to my bag. Finding the zipper in the dark felt impossible. What seemed like thirty seconds passed before I finally got the fucking thing open and grasped onto the gas jug. I quickly slung the bag around my shoulder and turned my attention back to the jug.

I unplugged it, set it on the ground, hesitated, and reached down to hold the prize that was-. NO. No. That was the integration talking. I reeled my foot back and kicked as hard I could toward the dead monster.

The silence broke into a frenzy.

I ran for the nearest stall. With one arm, I jumped, trying to pull myself up and over, but I couldn't get enough power in my legs. The second time I tried, I got one of my shoulders over, but the new leg weighed me down.

The hairs told me a tide of creatures was approaching. The third time got me over, and I didn't bother trying to balance as I threw myself to the other side.

I panted, my lungs heavy, my muscles aching, and my face still in pain as bugs tore away flesh.

I slapped at the bugs. They died easily, bursting like mosquitoes against my palm. The wooziness hit me as I tried to stand.

From the other side of the stall, I felt and heard them. They tore and ripped in wild motions as they splurged themselves on the ichor. One found the jug and began drinking directly from it, which prompted the others to become agitated and aggressive. I bit into my thumb. A trickle of blood made its way into my mouth. I bit harder. A little more. It calmed my nerves. I needed that bag none of this would work without it.

I sat quietly in the stall, constantly batting and slapping at the buzzing creatures.

I "watched" the feeding pile intently through the hairs, studying every movement and change that happened to their body. And change their bodies did, becoming more bestial than a plant with every successive swallow of gore. They vibrated as they changed, the limbs turning on their own bodies as they rolled in place. Yet still, in all of that pain, they kept reaching out and drinking the tainted blood. Even the bugs, vibrating wildly on top of various mutating monsters, were getting their fill.

Good. Good. Apparently, the scientists' whole textbooks about balanced humor had some basis in this world. I had assumed as much, but it was still good to confirm it.

I recalled the ladder I had seen when first entering the bar and began stumbling towards it. The monsters were too busy gorging themselves on the blood to notice me.

I climbed to the top of the loft and collapsed in a leap. Safe. I was finally safe. Now, all that was left was to wait.

Plan A was to kill Maggie, the scientist, and all the trapped Visitors.

Plan B didn't exist.

The how mattered here. At least for Maggie. She needed to die by a plant monster, not one of the weird hybrids I had just created.

A crash resounded from the back of the barn. Snarling, whimpers, a yelp before silence. Then chewing. It was outside the range of my hairs, so all I had was the wet sound and crunching. It started off with a few isolated yelps, then it grew and spread until it was all growls, impacts, and the sounds of chewing.

Having one of the plant people kill Maggie might be more difficult than I thought, but it was a requirement.

Ten minutes passed, and the sounds of cannibalism had long since faded. I heard noises outside. Muttering, a latch being undone, and then the creaking of the doors opening. I saw the silhouette of an older woman projected onto the ground by moonlight, and I smiled. A flood of shadows poured in behind her, but I was more focused on the two remaining beasts feasting on their fallen brethren.

"What the hell!" Maggie's voice shook, and I could hardly contain my impatience.

Come on. Come on. Step forward. It's all I could think about. I brought something out of my bag.

She spoke again—this time, her voice was layered with something strange, "Sit."

The hybrid monsters struggled and fought against the command in a desire to keep eating, but something twisted, straining against them. Only one ended up sitting. The three other survivors locked onto Maggie let out strangled howls as they darted forward.

"Kill!" Maggie shrieked, and in a moment, there was a thunderous crack of nineteen whip-like tendrils coming down and pulping the charging monster. Then the other arm of the plants were brought down. Then the original. All in the span of as many seconds. I gulped. I couldn't even see the attacks, just the aftermath. I had been hit by the whips before, most recently in the eye that was still bleeding, but by and large those had come when I was close to the creature, interrupting their movements. Nineteen of the creatures, with all the space in the world to get their arms up to top speed? I looked down at the hybrid monsters and saw only gore where creatures once were. I realized. It had never been these creatures goals to kill me, only capture.

Before I could think much more on it Maggie stepped forward, and my mind switched gears.

I brought up my last jug of chimera ichor and dropped its contents straight onto her head.

"Hello Maggie," and I couldn't help but keep the smile from my voice. "Just so you don't hold a grudge. I did warn you."
