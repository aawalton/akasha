Golden Jade City. Population: roughly 1,000,000

Huge marble tiles stretched out across the foyer, and gigantic columns held a mosaic covered ceiling some twenty or so meters up, giving the room an almost unimaginable grandess.

The chancellor approached King Camayor who’s bulbous form was sprawled across a lounge, watching veiled women dance. Several bodyguards and junior clerks flanked the chancellor, but stopped short of following him through the silky veil that curtained the king’s lounge area.

Dropping to his knees, the man dressed in silk robes awaited his king's command in perfect, bowed stillness.

“Rise,” King Camayor commanded with a lazy flick.

“Great King of the Fertile Plains, ruler of the seven great rivers, and divine avatar of the Immortal King, I thank you for having me.”

“Out with it, Chancellor! I’m feeling restless today.”

“Of course, of course, my lord. The scouts have returned from the fallen city of Lydrus.”

“Oh? Is that the little ankle-biting town that fell to the hordes? What interest is such a pathetic little place to me?”

“Yes, my lord, exactly,” the chancellor swallowed. “But the thing is… the scouts… they couldn’t enter the walls as the presence of demons was too strong. They have confirmed the city and surrounding lands have fallen, but what concerns them is the gathering hordes.”

“Oh well, things happen. Is this what you disturbed me for? News of demons dancing in the ruins of some backwater?”

“No, sire. It is the horde. Please, it’s quite concerning. They gather in those lands, more and more funneling down from the wastelands that they come from. We fear they may choose to turn their attention toward our great kingdom.”

“Do you know? What does this demon horde number? How many demons does it take to seed such fear in you, Chancellor?”

“The scouts estimate around two hundred thousand warriors.”

“And can our army not deal with this?”

“Sire, with all respect, our current forces number only around a hundred thousand.”

“Well then, hire more! Do I need to do everything?”

“Green recruits will hardly stand up against demonic warriors. This problem isn’t so easily solved.”

“I don’t want to hear problems, I want to hear about solutions, Chancellor! Hire as many as needed. If you need half a million soldiers, then do it and get out of my face!”

“Yes, sire,” the chancellor bowed. “I will see it done.”

“Was that really so hard? Did I really need to be bothered over such an obvious solution? Now get out of my face! I have catgirls to entertain.”

“As you wish, sire.”

“Wait, one moment, chancellor,” the king raised a hand and, for the first time, looked away from the voluptuously dancing girls. “This land, Lydrus, was it?”

“Yes, sire. That is correct.”

“An idea for you, as you seem to lack creativity. If a competent commander can be found, one who can drive the demon horde away. Then let them rule over this broken land. Such an offer should entice some powerful adventurers looking to become rulers.”

“Yes, sire. That is a great idea. I’ll send word out. We’ll have champions from all across the world coming to Golden Jade City with such a promise on offer.”

The people of Lydrus scrambled for cover as the roar shook the cavern. Meanwhile, Carmack and Lydrus' magical advisors studied the crystal ball. Aiding this, his senses were attuned to the chamber above.

Through the phylactery, they watched the dragon spit acid at the walls, melting even stone before its boiling anger calmed slightly, and it turned to sniff for evidence.

Swinging its head toward the exit heading out into the enormous adjoining cavern, it sniffed again and rushed forward. Reaching the gored flesh, feathers, and blood, the dragon roared again and shook the ground as it slammed against the floor.

Flapping her wings, the carrion wyrm flew erroneously out of its nest, smashing against the entrance walls and loosening rocky debris, and then fell and crashed to the ground.

Jumping back to its feet, it took off again, flying well out of sight and passing out into the next cavern, headed for the beast’s lair.

So, that confirms it. Dragons are as powerful in this world as they are in Avalock.

Carmack’s senses could follow it somewhat, but they grew weaker as the wyrm traveled further.

“Is everything going to plan, hero?” Managra asked.

“So far,” Carmack nodded.

“She’s definitely aggravated,” Madrix muttered. “She’s also very much alive; hopefully, we’ll remain that way too.”

Carmack raised a silencing hand. Mostly because he didn’t want to hear Madrix’s thin little lips squeaking, but also because he focused his mana on the wyrm.

Several very long minutes passed before he sensed something heavy collapse to the floor, and a chilling wave of deathly mana passed through the cavern, confirming something had died.

Knowing something had died didn't make the wait any less nail-biting, and knowing that only one creature had died increased their anxiety.

Hours passed, and Carmack still couldn't sense the dragon, and it certainly hadn’t returned to the nest above them. However, he wasn’t about to risk running into an irate dragon.

“So, hero?” Madrix said. “Are you going to check on our lizard problem? Or do even heroes get scared?”

“No, not yet. We’ll wait. No need to take unnecessary risks. In a day, I’ll head up.”“Wise decision,” Managra said.

“Agreed,” Master Tibus said.

“The church stands with your decision,” Head Maiden Octavia said.

“Then it is agreed. The hero will head up and investigate in one day’s time,” High Priest Managra said.

“No need to rush. It’s not like a dragon threatens our lives or anything,” Madrix snarked.

Although the people were undoubtedly tense, no sign that the dragon had returned or even survived the encounter surfaced over the following day.

Carmack prepared himself to head up, but this time, he went alone. If the dragon were there, he would have sensed it. Still, there was always a chance he was wrong. And if he was, he had far more chance of escaping alone.

Creeping carefully and keeping to the craggy edges where he could find cover, he made his way through the deserted nest and around to the monster's burrow.

As he reached its mouth, he sensed no life in the tunnel but proceeded with the utmost caution. A stench of death permeated the chamber, tainted with the heavy, foul smell of an ancient creature whose hatred had stained the earth.

I guess it didn’t like being used as a tool.

Monsters with cores were capable of many communication methods, even if they couldn’t talk. A foul stench of hatred this strong was beyond simply becoming a larger creature’s prey. The dragon had likely made it known why it was here, and the mutated Chimera had likely figured out that it had been tricked.

The stench of rage and hatred lingers heavily here.

But there was no space for compassion. The beast served its purpose, and Carmack’s responsibility was to the people of Lydrus, not random beasts.

Squeezing his nose, he stepped into the chamber. Inside was a graveyard of bones and corpses, which had been completely strewn around and desecrated. Blood and monstrous, shredded limbs were spread across the ground.

Carmack noted the severed furred arms, legs, and scorpion-like tail, but there was no sign of the dragon.

If it didn't die, then where did it go?

Walking across the lair, he realized the tunnel turned down, delving into the earth. Thick, dark red blood stained the rock, and Carmack could smell dense mana seeping into it.

It seems that the dragon went this way.

He tried to look down into the hole, but neither his dark-attuned sight nor his senses could find anything besides a blood trail.

Wherever it went, it’s gone now, hopefully for good.

A shiver traced his spine. Knowing that they had potentially made a mortal enemy of a dragon and did not know where it was did not sit well with Carmack. But he wasn’t about to go chasing after it; that was a step too reckless.

Here's to hoping you don't decide to come back, wyrm.

Walking back to the torn-apart corpse, Carmack realized that the Chimera, as big and powerful as it was, wasn’t a match for even the weakest dragon.

“That is concerning,” he muttered to himself and approached the corpse.

He could sense the mana in the monster's core, still at the heart of the beast. Not to mention, the scaly carapace shielding the back and tail looked particularly strong. Even the leathery underbelly was tough.

Turning his hand into a spike of stone, he stabbed down into the beast's chest between already torn flesh and removed its core. He'd send people up to collect the corpse, but the core was too valuable to risk, and his people were too desperate to risk wasting such a valuable win.

Wait, this gives me an idea, Carmack mused as he held the marble sized core between his fingers. It pulsed with power, and he knew the potential it possessed.

Opening his skill screen, Carmack scrolled to the Enchanter tab. Opening the skill tree, he flicked across until he found Greater Core Enchanting. Without supporting skills, its uses would be limited. However, it was still a high-level skill that would allow him to use powerful cores like the one between his fingers to enchant items.

There were no powerful enchanters mentioned during the population census… Carmack smirked. At least in Avalock, a good enchanter was hard to come by and worth their weight in gold. If this world were similar, he could use a skill like this to bring much wealth to their fledgling settlement.

Grunting in satisfaction, Carmack confirmed his skill purchase and turned away from the corpse.

I will have to find a use for this core. Carmack thumbed the monster core.

Walking back to the wyrm nest, he stopped to think for a moment. No doubt others within the Undercavern would notice the wyrm’s absence, and eventually, someone might move into its empty lair. This was not acceptable. The lair had vantage over his entire settlement, and a hostile settlement or beast was too great a risk. However, there was always a chance the wyrm would return, and he didn't want to be caught lounging in its den. Which meant fortifying it and making it a part of their territory wasn’t viable yet.

For now, he decided to close the lair off from the larger cavern and seal the holes that gave access to their cavern. It wouldn’t stop anybody determined to dig into the chamber, but at least it’d make squatting harder.

He planned to use the massive cavern and passageway to the surface for trade routes, but for now, they would plant their roots and grow stronger in safety and secrecy and arise once they were stronger.

Before closing it off, he moved the phylactery into the huge cavern. Carmack placed it on a little ledge of rock high above the entrance and formed a wall of rock to close them off from the larger cavern.

There were many places he could use the phylactery, but between the dragon and the sheer vastness of the adjoining cavern, this seemed like the most useful spot for now.

Looking back on his work, Carmack forced himself to move on. There was no perfect way to account for a dragon, and he had so much work to do.

This will have to do for now. If only we had spies and informants throughout this Undercavern, we could learn more about this place.

Not knowing exactly what threats were around them kept his nerves on edge, but there was nothing more to be done about it until they had better settled.

Returning to the settlement, Carmack did his best to forget about the dragon and went back to work. Right now, they needed the infrastructure to put Lydrus’ surviving craftsmen back to work, especially now that less additional manpower was required for digging and carving out rooms.

It didn't take long to hollow out a smithy and several simple smelting furnaces. Through his geomancy powers, Carmack had some ability to control metals, but it was limited. With his former strength, he could draw out minerals from rock and even shape them, but it was mana costly, and his mana was in such short supply in his current state.

Blacksmiths and other craftsmen were absolutely required.

To get the smiths started, Carmack created molds of stone and clay for an anvil and other tools. He could easily make more molds if they wanted them, but he would leave them at this unless requested. Forged tools and weapons would be stronger than molded ones, and he didn't want them to become reliant on weaker alternatives.

Although they lacked the materials at present, he also hollowed out workshops for tailors, carpenters, and many other professions. He figured it would still be worthwhile even if none currently worked in these crafts where materials weren’t available. This way, the craftsmen could set up the workshops so they were ready to go when materials were available.

A calm started to spread across the population over the following days. Immediately after the mission, people were divided about the hero’s plan to slaughter a dragon’s spawn and blame it on another monster, some even calling for his removal. But since the dragon had disappeared, most had dropped the subject and were happy just to feel a sense of peace.

The Rykals were no different. Marie and Todd stood in the long, seemingly never-ending line for their rations like every other family.

They had been fine with this arrangement until recently. Survival meant rationing, and everyone understood that. It was better than dying in Lydrus, and things seemed to be getting better from all accounts.

But the ration lines were only getting worse. Everyone knew there was corruption. All it took was a couple of silvers to skip the line.

There were plenty from Lydrus who had stolen away with their savings or even those of others—the flight from the city being as chaotic as it was, few knew who genuinely owned what. And without a proper market or outside trade, there wasn’t much else to spend it on.

As more people paid to skip the line, it got slower. It also seemed obvious that those taking bribes were intentionally slowing things down to encourage their own shady business.

The chatter of many voices sounded behind them, and Marie and Todd turned to their rear. It was Priestess and Head Maiden Octavia, trailed by four of her understudies. Suddenly, the line started to move quicker, and the guardsmen responsible for keeping order and taking bribes began to shoo away those who approached them to cut in.

“Finally,” Marie said. “They won’t dare take bribes with Maiden Octavia here.”

“About time, I’m starving,” Todd said. “It feels like we’ve been here all day.”

Walking toward the head of the line, Octavia stopped as people begged for her blessings, which she gave with a gentle smile.

“Bow your head,” Marie whispered as Octavia neared, and Todd did as he was told.

“Blessings to you, children,” Octavia elegantly waved her hand over them as she passed.

Ahead, the guardsmen stiffened as Octavia and her entourage approached.

“How goes your watch, sergeant?”

“It’s an honor to receive you, Head Maiden,” the guard sergeant bowed low, and his men mimicked. “Our watch goes well, thanks to the church’s blessings.”

“Good to hear, Sergeant. Though I worry about the people.”

“Keep your mind at ease, priestess. Me and my men watch over these people and see to their needs.”

“Thank you for your service,” she smiled. “Sergeant, if I’m not mistaken, you are the nephew of the young lord, Cecil Jalaxidus, are you not?”

“I am, Head Maiden. Sergeant Rudy Jalaxidus, at your service. Can I help you with anything?”

“No, it’s fine. I just find it interesting that the relative of a prominent lord is given one of, if not the, most covenanted guard stations. Especially with all the rumors surrounding excessive line waits.”

“Yes, I suppose some might think it is a bit of a coincidence,” Rudy nodded. “Luck has always run strongly in the Jalaxidus line.”

“Has it now. Carry on, then. Oh, and you don’t mind if my sisters remain here to share blessings with the people, do you?”

“No, of course not,” Rudy said, fighting back the snarl curling across his lips. “I’m sure the people would greatly appreciate their service.”

“Fantastic,” Octavia said and turned to walk. “I do hope these unsavory rumors cease. It is quite troublesome to hear of people focused on their own material gain in such troubled times. Oh, and the sisters, bless their souls, have a penchant for gossip. As much as I try, I don’t seem to be able to weed it from them.”

Sergeant Rudy understood the threat well as he watched the beautiful woman leave, almost forgetting to avert his eyes from her curved shape and reddening as he turned away.

Damn her, he thought. If they care so much about the people, perhaps they should start sowing the fields!

The bribes Rudy had paid for his station had been expensive, and he had greatly indebted himself to his uncle Cecil. If he couldn’t retrieve bribes from the people thanks to the maiden’s sisters being on watch, he wouldn’t be able to repay his debts.

And no doubt, Cecil would find an alternative way to reimburse himself if he failed to do so.

I won’t just roll over, you damned self-righteous maidens! This is my mark, paid for in full, and I intend to profit from it!

“Are you alright, Sergeant? You look a little flustered.”

“Shut up and get back to work!” Sergeant Rudy hissed at his men, who all suddenly straightened and returned their focus to the queue.

Patting his brow, Rudy realized he was sweating profusely and scowled. His clothes were damp, and people were looking at him.

Is this that damn Ocatvia’s magic? Curse her! These fools will see. They’ll all be made to bow before House Jalaxidus soon enough!
