The Spanish Moss overhead grows ever thicker. It gets to the point where it starts to block out the sun, and the ACVs have to turn on their headlights to see where they’re going. Second Squad grows even more nervous now that they have trouble seeing. The snakes were fairly loud when attacking as a horde, but they continue to trickle in, usually alone or at most in pairs. With so few of them at once, they’re deadly silent. Thor manages to spot a couple without my assistance somehow. Most of the scouting now left up to me.

Virtually all of the drones we had were destroyed by the snakes in the first massed attack. While they didn’t intentionally target them, they destroyed anything in their way during their haste to get to Second Squad. Fortunately, even with the magical interference, my multi-spectrum sensor suite is more than enough to detect the snakes before they get within range. They might be quiet, but they’re not silent. When it comes to passive hydrophones and the world’s best signal analysis software, that’s more than enough. I can even use the propellers as a source for sonar.

Eventually the muddy lakebed turns into peat, and I grab a few handfuls of the stuff. It was often used for heating, fertilizer, and power generation before the modern age. If Morgan wants to experiment with the concept of magical fertilizer, there’s nowhere better to start. I wish we had more time to really salvage a Fracture. A single one of this size could feed a whole city’s hunger for building materials on just the wood alone.

A shape lunges out of the darkness from under a fallen long. Jaws wide, it clamps onto my arm before I can even react. It’s a massive Alligator, easily as large as the ACVs.

“Got something down here!”

The Alligator—Alli…graver? No, needs work—rolls violently, ripping me out of the peaty lakebed and then slamming me against another fallen log. With my free arm I slug it in the eye. It pops with a meaty squelch and the Alligraver—damn it, I don’t have anything better—roars in agony through a clenched jaw. It refuses to let go and slams me against a log again.

With no leverage, I can’t easily throw the monster away from me, not without causing a powerful enough shockwave to pulp Second Squad. I almost summon my sword in my free hand with the intent to use it as an anchor when I get an idea.

I have to force my sword to form inside the monster’s mouth. It takes far more effort than the normally near-effortless flash of will. Now it feels like I’m lifting something truly heavy. Still, it heeds my desire. My weapon is me and I am my weapon. It bends to my command, not in spite of my effort but because of it.

The twelve foot long blade of stars forms inside the monster with a shockwave, muffled by the fleshy body of the Alligraver. The sword suddenly lodged in its brain instantly kills it, and I let my sword return to the ether.

“Where?!” Tom cries. The ACVs are breaking out of a line-ahead formation and their remote weapon station sensor mounts whirl around. It’s been less than ten seconds since I was first ambushed.

“Never mind, it’s dead.”

I quickly scan about, looking more actively with less stealthier sensor systems. I don’t see anything, but that’s not exactly proof. At the very least, I’ve cleared all the nearest dark corners.

“What was it?”

“Giant alligator. Stabbed it in the brain.”

I pry the jaws open and pull my arm out. I actually break the jawbone in several places. The muscles apparently clench even tighter after the monster died.

They don’t call it a death grip for nothing.

“Are there anymore?”

“Not that I can see. I’ll start using active sensor systems. I was hoping we could stay undetected longer without them, but that’s clearly not worth the risk.”

Sonar pings can travel for dozens of miles through water. If I start actively pinging, it’ll put every monster in the Fracture on alert. That being said, they likely won’t be able to pin-point the source unless we get close, but they will be looking.

The ACVs regather back into a line-ahead formation when Eric comes on the radio.

“Hey, Mach, what funny name did you give this monster?”

“…What makes you say I gave this one a funny name?”

His brother, Jericho, snorts. I can hear it through the ACV’s hull, despite the vehicle’s thick metal armor.

“Because you always do? Come on, spill the beans.”

“It needs work.”

“Oh, come on,” Eric teases. “Now I’m too curious.”

“Alligraver. Alligator and gravely dangerous?”

“Wow. You’re right. That does need work.” Eric says, barely holding in a laugh.

“I get no respect around here,” I jokingly grumble.

“Not even a little, sir,” Callisto says dryly.

By the time we get into what we’re pretty sure is the center of the Cypress dome, the trees have graduated from gigantic to mountainous. Each one is the size of a skyscraper. Even I can’t see their tops. A combination of magical inference, bad angles, and spatial distortion characteristic of a Fracture’s internal boundary eliminate any chance to get an accurate reading. Even an estimate is impossible.

The true center is a massive island, seemingly made up of dozens of these monolithic trees felled by something and pulled together. They were then crushed into something approximating an island about three hundred yards in diameter. Thick moss, grass, and even other trees grow on the pile of decaying trees. The convoy smoothly drives up onto them, and though the island creaks and bends under the ACVs’ weight, it holds them up. Water drips off all of us as we haul ourselves out of the lake, now nearly forty feet deep.

There’s a hum in the air. There’s a subsonic heartbeat, one Second Squad probably can’t hear, but might be able to feel. They exit the ACVs and start to rummage around the island.

“Do you hear that?” Thor asks to my surprise. “It sounds like a heartbeat. It is very quiet.”

“How can you hear that? It’s in the subsonic range.”

He shrugs. “The headsets, perhaps. They amplify some sounds while damping ones that are too loud. Maybe mine is of higher quality? Or maybe newer?”

“Well, whatever it is, you’re right. There’s something down underneath us.” I put my hand on the island and tap a staccato rhythm on some exposed wood.

“Seismic mapping is a no go, the structure is too varied to make anything out. Let me try something else.”

I use powerful directional radar pulses to map out the interior of the island. The lower overall density helps ameliorate the general jamming effect of the Fracture. Despite being able to overpower the interference, it’s still slow going.

While I carefully scan the island, Second Squad starts to tear it apart. Jericho and Eric rip a tree out of the roots and all. After setting up and launching the drone, Sandra and Dillion start quickly stuffing handfuls of moss into a few containers we brought with us. While not a scientific mission, we still brought a few crates with us, just in case. Now that Second Squad is dismounted, there’s no reason not to take everything not nailed down.

After nearly twenty minutes of painstaking scans, I finally find something. Deep in the center of the island is a hollow cavern, though whatever is inside that is impossible to determine from the poor returns I receive.

“There’s something beneath us, maybe twenty feet down. I can’t tell what it is,” I report.

“It must be important,” Thor says while looking around. “This is the center of the Fracture, is it not?”

“As best as I can tell, yes.”

Callisto kicks a thick green vine with a metal-tipped boot. The arm-thick vine barely budges.

“The hell is this?”

I pull at a similar one next to me. The vine, or others like it, are wrapped around this island. It’s almost like a web.

Or maybe roots.

It comes unspooled as I pull on it, and it just keeps coming out. It reminds me of the endless handkerchief gag item clowns and magicians tend to have.

“Not sure, but it goes deep into the island.”

The vine grows taught, either running out of room or snagging on something. It easily gives way, ripped apart by my immense strength. I wasn’t even trying to rip it out. A reminder of just how fragile this world is to me, even when magically reinforced.

I pull the rest of the length out, nearly eighty feet long. Greenish ichor pours from the snapped end. With a rumble, the entire island shudders. Second Squad stumbles but most of them remain on their feet.

“Uh, what was that?” Eric asks nervously. I send another pulse of directional radar down towards the center of the island. The hollow beneath my feet is rapidly expanding.

“Don’t know, but it’s burrowing up!”

“Back into the vics! Now!” Tom orders, and Second Squad scrambles back into the ACVs. They come to life with a mechanical roar, and charge back into the water. I lose my footing as the island comes apart, the very same vines pulling it apart like a broken shell.

Roots and vines shattering wood and rip apart the moss. Several go for the rapidly retreating Second Squad, and more go for me. I lunge for the ones gunning for Second Squad, blade held high, ready for a slash. I cut the questing tentacles apart with a brutal chop. The ones I ignored wrap around my leg and torso.

I slam my sword into the ground right before they pull taunt, and I resist the immense strength in them. Instead of flying into the pit that’s rapidly consuming the island, I tear a gash in the wooden ground with a roar.

Wooden splinters and torn up moss go flying in the maelstrom. Chunks of rotting bark shatter and bounce off my armor, and I hear even more splashing into the swamp all around.

I plant my blade in what little remains of the island and grip the mass of tentacles with both hands. My boots dig into the ground as whatever is down there tries to resist me. With a final roar of effort, I haul it out of the rapidly growing pit.

A giant white flower flies up out of the darkness. It has five pointed petals and a red center. What draws my attention are the five pearlescent buds, each of them visibly pulsing with Potentia. I only have a few moments to get a good look at them, which is more than enough. Each one is so full of life it’s physical form can’t contain it.

The Heart of Growth!

The buds themselves fold away deeper into the flower’s main body, if it could be considered to have one. The Carnivine’s—a much better name than the Alligraver—main flower body is easily the single largest portion of the monster. Despite being about the same size as an ACV, it’s at most maybe a third of the monster’s total mass. Dozens of long tentacles make up the rest.

When the buds fold away, in their place comes multiple layers of grinding teeth, reminiscent of a leech. Tom fires a Javelin at the monster, and its lightning quick reflexes give it enough time to interpose tentacles to block the missile. Pulped plant matter and viscous green sap splatter all over me.

“Cease fire, cease fire! Don’t shoot the main body!”

“Say again, Mach? You don’t want us to shoot it?”

“*It’s the Heart of Growth! The ATGMs could penetrate too deeply!”

“Roger. All units, switch fire to the roots. Mach will deal with the main body.”

The Carinvine lunges towards me, grinding teeth buzzing. I grab its mouth, just barely holding it back. More tentacles wrap around me, straining to pull me in. The ground beneath my feet is giving way before me. The ACVs’ 30mm rounds explode into shrapnel on my back, chewing up tentacles. There’s always more to replace the ones destroyed.

I have only seconds before I’m likely pulled in the buzzing maw. I’m sure it’d grind up anything stupid or unlucky enough to find itself consumed. Anything, but me, of course. It reminds me of a jet engine’s turbofan. If a turbojet was hungry for people.

Oh, that gives me an idea.

I jam my arm into its primitive maw, which is little more than a pocket for the counter-rotating disks of teeth. They snap down on my heavily armored arm and I feel a pressure, just for an instant.

With a crack they explode into thousands of tiny fragments. Tiny fragments of rock and stone burst out in a fan. It shreds the Carnivine’s closest approximation of a face. Sickly sweet green sap coats my entire body and it writhes in agony. I pull it down to the ground, pull my sword out of the ground where I left it, and sever it from the rest of the tentacles with a single swipe.

The island stops shuddering and falling apart. The tentacles stop moving and go limp.

“Cease fire! Cease fire!” I hear Tom call out over the radio.

The exit and reward orb form an instant later, but I ignore them both for the far more important prize. After ripping away chunks of plant-flesh and splattering even more sap everywhere, I have the five pearlescent buds in hand.

“What are they?” Tom asks over the radio.

“The Hearts of Growth, I guess. I don’t really know. But we did it. We found the first piece.”

A cheer breaks out, one I can hear even through the armored hull of the ACVs.
