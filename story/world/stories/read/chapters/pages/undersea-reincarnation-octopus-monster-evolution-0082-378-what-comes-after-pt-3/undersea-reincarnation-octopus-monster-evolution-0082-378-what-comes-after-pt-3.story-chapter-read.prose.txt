The news draped on Zerathstra’s like a particularly heavy blanket, hunching the guardian forward as if he was suddenly feeling his age. Next to him, Arisia’s reaction to the news wasn’t much different, but it wasn’t as pronounced as the sad smile Zerathstra was showing. In his gaze, and in the small shake of his head, Henry saw something unexpected, but in retrospect, it made sense.

Familiarity. This was likely not the first time Thalis’ guardian had lost people. Velistraine had never given him exact numbers, but the ape had been around for a while.

Still, Zerathstra and Arisia quickly rallied, especially once they’d heard there was a little hope. Even with Henry downplaying the odds of his clones surviving and providing Velistraine with any support, they still hung to the possibility. Like he and Maurice were doing, frankly. And sure, even if his clones could provide a meager support, there was still the whole thing of Velistraine finding herself in the creature’s turf and the danger that represented but… well. So long as her odds of survival weren’t zero, a miracle was possible, and when Henry asked about the “plane of existence” thing mentioned in his skill, neither of them had answers, but they promised they’d look into it. To see if their library had any mention of such.

With that part done, Henry and Maurice turned their attention to the crew and instead of addressing all of them—which would have implied him rounding them up from all over the island, Henry opted to address Ray, who wasn’t too difficult to find. Velistraine’s first mate listened gravely, the ship swaying under their feet. While the rest of the crew were still staying on the island, Ray had been running some errands and so Henry had found him conferring with the quartermaster on the ship, and once he’d recounted everything—which was still as unfamiliar to Henry as it had been the first time—he told Ray that he could take them all to Thalis, as Velistraine had asked. He wouldn’t force them, though. Henry didn’t feel he had the authority for that. He’d only been a guest on the ship. As for Ash and Fabian, Maurice had stayed back in Thalis after they’d spoken to Zerathstra, and the crab would be telling them what had taken place.

Leaving Ray to digest the information and relay it to the crew, Henry flew toward the horizon, then dropped underwater. He would let the news sink in, then he’d see with Ray if they needed transport, after which he’d check in on Attal again. A few fish—some of which seemed to be looking for a meal—came to check on the small human that dropped in their midst, but when the large arcane arms manifested and shoved them away, they quickly dispersed.

Henry wanted to get moving. He and Maurice have had a good break, but the danger around them and around this world seemed to be continuously growing, and he needed the levels. He needed to be doing something that’d make it so they were more ready the next time such a monstrosity happened to peek through the curtains. And he needed them to be capable of handling their own A-ranks, and not have other come in to help.

They had needed Attal’s help against a mere level 31. The difference in power every ten level provided was just too much. Henry believed if it had been safe for him to touch the parasite, he could have won, but that wasn’t even on the table. It was certain loss if he had let it touch them. It wasn’t as much of a fighter when compared to the Stinglance, but it was much, much more dangerous in a whole different way.

It was clear that they needed more levels. Attal had one-shot the parasite, but he had seemed pretty powerless against the alien mantis.

It’s time, Henry thought to himself. They’ve consolidated enough. Now was the time to get back to the grind, and with the two bad-news-bearing tasks accomplished, Henry could turn his attention to something else. He wanted to move on to testing the limits of his clones, but he needed Maurice for that, and the crab was a little busy.

So he might as well look through his notifications, though before that, Henry decided to feel up his skills and see what was ready to upgrade. God knows he’d been slacking on those and so, he closed his eyes, then started going one by one, writing down everything on his mental whiteboard as he went until nearly an hour later, he had a clean, clear list ready.

He was looking forward to increasing the potency of his venoms, now that he was relying on them so much. He would just need to be careful about which one to push to the next rank first, considering that his Aspects were also advancing fast, and would likely be much more expensive to upgrade.

I should also look into synthesizing some new toxins. Would sampling some inorganic poisons allow me to create them? There must be more I could do, there, he thought as he loaded the shorter list of Aspect notifications.

Dancing Shadows has seen the most progression, and Thaumaturgy has finally made it to level 10. It would have likely gained a level if it hadn’t hit the limit of the rank, though he thought he had some time until he needed his Aspects to finally step into A-rank. Not to mention, he didn’t think he even had the tokens or the equivalent energy to push the upgrade anyway. Still, who could have guessed that today would be the one he’d look an outer-world monster and fight two A-ranks at the same time?

To think he had thought the world would be safer outside of the Current.

Levels. We need more levels, he thought again, and so without further ado, he consumed the two core fragments he had at his disposal, and the fish around him immediately dispersed as an intense golden glow suddenly emerged out of his chest, basking him and his soul with energy while the notifications filled his vision.

So long as he could get to it, of course.

Continuing his leisurely swim, Henry joined a school of black and white-dotted rays for a few minutes, thinking of the day he just had, but after a while, he just shook his head.

We had a little vacation, at least, he thought glancing down at his palms. He’d recreated his human form, since he had to talk with Velistraine’s crew and whatnot. Plus, he was going to go meet Attal later, so he might as well keep it for now. Control Water made it easy enough to match the fish swimming around him.

Closing his fist, Henry drifted away from the school and broke the surface, transitioning smoothly from Control Water to Telekinesis-empowered flight as he headed to main island. While Maurice was still in Thalis, he wanted to have a talk with Attal and start planning their next move. Henry would like to get moving toward the Reach within the day.

“I will not be going with you.”

Those were Attal’s words when Henry had arrived in front of the man. He was in his lab and already, fully-geared workers where laboring on excavating and reinforcing the elevator shaft. Attal was still wearing the same damaged attire he’d been sporting since the rift incident. His face was pale, and as Henry watched the man pull out a little device that cut off their surroundings’ sound, he’d even go as far as the man was twitchy.

“I’ll give you my orb. I want to stay here and make sure nothing else crawls out of… That can’t happen again. It can’t, ” he muttered, trailing for a bit before blinking himself awake. When he looked back up, his gaze was tired, but sharp. “I wouldn’t be doing you any favor if I were to go with you anyway. You got potential, but it’s worthless if you don’t earn the levels that will be needed. You’ve seen what we’ll be dealing with. Collect the orb, see what happens, and get us some answers.”

Attal reached into his side pouch, then retrieved his gold-tinted orb, which he lobbed at Henry.

“We need a way to evolve past A-rank. It’s not enough for what’s out there…” the man said, drifting off as he stared toward the elevator shaft for a moment, and Henry sensed a flash of… guilt? Not exactly, but Henry could tell he’d been feeling the same thing, though he didn’t want to voice it.

Had they led the monsters to them by studying it’s progeny? Had they been playing in its hands?

Attal just stared in the distance for a moment, then pulled himself together once more as he faced Henry. “The Reach is just as dangerous as the waters of the Pillars. Potentially even more. Be ready before you step onto its soil. But if you play your cards right… you’ll level fast. And be careful of the people there. Watch your back. Don’t trust anyone and just… try and keep me updated. Especially if you learn anything new about Velistraine or the System, please. I’d appreciate it.”

With a few more warnings and a couple more spacing-out incidents, Attal bade him goodbye, and Henry flew away, this time heading toward the docks. Speaking to a local navigator, he got himself a compass and a vague direction toward the Reach before he flew again once more. Between the map and his own Pathfinding ability, he didn’t need much else to find his target.

Now he just needed to pick up Maurice. Though before they left, they had to do one final stop.

The wait was unbearable.

After a quick chat with Maurice, the two decided that the best test would be to completely put the crab and a handful of clones fully out of Henry’s reach. For an hour. In that hour, Maurice would try to get the clones to do things for him. To communicate, put things in storage and retrieve them, cast some defensive ability, so on and so forth. The storage and telepathic ability were the most important to Henry. Those would likely affect Velistraine’s survivability the most. But anything else useful Maurice could think off, he’d add to the list. After which, Henry would re-establish the relay system, and they’d see what had happened.

It wasn’t the exact conditions Velistraine was in, but this—they felt—was the closest and fastest test they could come up with to get some answers and by now, it should have been long enough, so Henry began re-connecting his network.

“[Henry!]” Maurice’s chipper rang in his head. “[They work! Kind of… Well, they don’t have your stuff, but they can store a few things that I give them and they can retrieve them just fine. We tried storing me but um… that killed one of them. And it killed the second that had to pull me. But I can fit inside! Also, they can teleport me without too much difficulty between themselves. They can use the skills I ask of them. They’re smart enough for that. Um… what else?]”

Maurice went on, explaining every little test he’d ran on the batch of clones Henry had left with him. It was unfortunate that the backlash of storing a B-rank was enough to dispel them, but it was good news that at least they could be used for storage. It was interesting though how they still could use the ability but not access his stored items, though now that the network was reconnected, Henry could see every addition Maurice had done.

It’s like a secondary storage gets established over there, then when we reconnect, the content gets transferred, Henry summarized. So technically…

“[So if Velistraine hides in their Maw,]” Maurice began, following the same train of thoughts, “[then they hide somewhere, she can be safe until we retrieve the clone!]”

Henry wanted to agree, and he did, a little. But what were the odds of his weak clones surviving out in the void? Henry didn’t want to think about it too much. And how big was it out there? Plus, there was still the question whether Velistraine was still on the same plane of existence as they were.

Lots of ifs and buts. But once they got some answers, they’d know a little more. Plus, there was one thing Henry could do to improve everything.

He just had to get stronger. Pushing his Dancing Shadows to the next tier might give it the necessary oomph to peer past the limits of this world. It might also give them enough resilience, if the evolution properties were to propagate to them, though that might be unlikely, considering the distance. In any case, it wouldn’t hurt to get stronger, and on that note, Henry and Maurice took a few moments to say goodbyes, then Henry pulled them off the coast of the Emerald Isles.

They’d be able to talk to the crew or Zerathstra for a day or two at least, as Henry’s network kept growing longer and wider as with the jump in power of Dancing Shadows, he could now field a little more than six hundred clones. But it was time to get moving. Reactivating the whales’ divination anchor along with the ring of the orbs, Henry watched as the golden lines drew themselves up in his vision once more, starting with the orb that was supposed to be in the Reach, then the orb lost at sea and finally, the location of the Bahamut whales. Though when the latter appeared in his sight, Henry couldn’t help the amused chuckled that came out of him.

“[What? What is it?]”Maurice asked, having also shed his human form for this stretch of travel.

Henry just waved an arm, refusing to look down at the tiny claws tapping his arm. “Nothing,” he simply said.

Maurice froze for a moment, then tapped with his pincers even harder. “You’re lying! You won’t even look at me. And your Mind Shield’s still weak. I can feel you having fun. What is it? Tell me!”

Henry just grinned, and he decided to dangle the truth for a few extra minutes as two swam below the waves toward the Reach, Control Water carrying them fast under the fishing and scouting boats.

Henry would tell him. Eventually. Still, he wondered if it was a coincidence that for some reason, the path to the whales had moved toward the Reach.

He wondered if they knew something he didn’t.
