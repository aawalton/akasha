The first thing Aodhán felt as he stepped into the rift was the blistering cold.

It engulfed him even before the world finished spinning, biting hard like the cruelest edge of winter as it slipped past skin and bone to gnaw at his spirit.

His mind slowed immediately, but a quick burst of willpower chased the chill from his spirit before it could spread any further.

"A debuff," he muttered, surprised. He would have to keep channeling willpower through his spirit just to remain here.

That alone would have been a deterrent for many. Fortunately, willpower was a resource he possessed in abundance.

Shuddering slightly as a frigid wind swept past him, he opened his eyes to take in the world around him.

It was beautiful.

Despite its biting chill, the rift stretched as far as the eye could see—an endless expanse of snow and ice, broken only by jagged rises of blue that looked less like mountains and more like frozen waves halted mid-collapse.

The sky itself was dark as night, but there were no stars within it. Instead, ghostly veils of light hung overhead, rippling across the darkness like luminous curtains unfurling in shades of emerald, violet, and pale blue. They drifted and twisted like silk across the heavens, casting a gentle glow just bright enough to see by.

Ice crunched behind him as Lilian appeared, and he turned to her with a faint smile. "Beautiful, isn't it?"

Lilian grimaced. "I wouldn't be fooled by its beauty, Crown of Ruining Storms. This is a special rift—bound to house especially dangerous monsters. Even the air itself is hostile. We must be careful."

Aodhán frowned. "We?"

Lilian froze, her eyes widening for the briefest instant before settling back into a mask of calm. "That was an error. I meant you—you must be careful."

Aodhán shook his head with a chuckle. "You don't like this rift, do you?"

Lilian shrugged, her expression intentionally vague. "You did not choose it for me to like, Crown of Ruining—"

"Just call me Aodhán, Elder Lilian." He cut her off. "That alias is a mouthful."

Lilian stared at him for a moment, then nodded. "As you wish."

She turned her gaze to the snow-covered expanse below and grimaced. "This is a horde rift, Aodhán. Are you aware?"

Aodhán nodded. He could already sense the dense pockets of ice cores in the distance, ranging from Tier 25 all the way to Tier 100. The nearest pocket had begun to stir, moving in his direction, and a sharp sense of exhilaration rose within him.

Some of it came from Varéc. Aodhán grinned.

"Would you like to join me?" he asked mentally.

Varéc snorted in response, content to watch from his spirit until the creatures were worth challenging.

Aodhán chuckled. As much as he would have liked to do the same and skip every creature below his tier, that wasn't how horde rifts worked. He had to work his way up—literally.

He didn't mind much, though. He had skills to test and a power limit to determine. Better to do it against creatures who couldn't hurt him if they tried than against creatures who could.

The cold pressed in again, sharper this time. Another flare of willpower pushed it back.

Lilian did the same. She glanced at him. "Are you ready?"

"I am."

She nodded, studying him carefully. "I'll observe from here. If I think you're in danger, I'll step in. Otherwise… you're on your own."

Aodhán's expression softened. "Then I'd better give you a show. Make sure you don't get bored."

Lilian regarded him a moment longer, her brows furrowing slightly. "I was told you were more reserved. Serious—like Darien. This…" She gestured vaguely at him. "…recklessness. The thrill-seeking. It could be the twisted effect manifesting differently."

Aodhán opened his mouth to dismiss it, ready to say he was merely excited to test his new skills—but he paused. She could very well be right. The twisted effect typically amplified a person's emotions toward the extreme end of the negative spectrum—hence the anger, the rage, the murderous intent. It was like being a passenger in one's own mind while chaos took the wheel.

Aodhán knew the feeling well.

But he wasn't feeling that way now. He didn't feel different, or out of control.

Just excited. Maybe too excited—but he'd just gained five tiers from witnessing an ascension and a wealth of new skills. He had every right to be.

He gave her a reassuring look. "I'm fine."

"Okay," Lilian said with a nod, though she clearly didn't believe him.

Before she could continue, a howl echoed in the distance. Then another. Then a chorus.

His targets had arrived.

Aodhán turned immediately, his gaze snapping toward them as they came into focus.

White. Shaggy. Big.

Wolves.

Five of them.

These were no ordinary wolves, though. They had crimson eyes and fangs the length of daggers. A frosty haze shimmered around their bodies, and jagged icicles bloomed around their paws with each step they took.

Above their heads, text floated.

[Rimefang Scout—Tier 34][Rimefang Scout—Tier 34][Rimefang Scout—Tier 36][Rimefang Scout—Tier 36][Rimefang Scout—Tier 37]

The wolves were too weak to pose any real danger to him, but their titles gave Aodhán a clearer picture of what he might be dealing with here—and it couldn't have suited his path of authority better.

If there were scouts, there would be warriors. Commanders. Shamans.

Maybe even generals.

A hierarchy. An army waiting somewhere out in the frozen expanse—and an opportunity to test exactly how far his authority could reach.

A slow grin spread across his face. With bubbling excitement, he stepped off his platform, energy humming gently around him.

It had been five months since he had done anything like this. Five months without real combat. No strain. No release.

His body had been starving for it. And now, anticipation hit him all at once—sharp and intoxicating, like cold water to a parched throat.

He stretched a hand toward the five wolves, drawing their attention with the simplest motion.

The foremost wolf snarled, fur bristling, and broke into a charge. The other four followed close behind, paws crunching hard against the snow.

Aodhán felt the thrill surge through him. He inhaled deeply—the air suddenly crisp in his lungs—and with a simple burst of energy, tested his first skill.

Ruinous Matrix—Lightning Spear.

Energy surged from his core at once, flooding through his pathways like viscous plasma. But it wasn't just energy. Chaos poured into the flow, layering and intertwining until the two became indistinguishable.

No longer energy. No longer chaos.

Something else. Something inherently his.

Liquid ruin.

It coursed through him, shaping itself into a precise matrix even as it left his core—as though his very being had become a forge, and chaos the hammer that shaped it. He wasn't just forming a construct. He was crafting it.

The spear manifested in his palm a heartbeat later, glowing tendrils still faintly tethering it to his fingers. It was beautiful—sleek, deadly, and impossibly refined—emitting a pressure that far surpassed anything he had ever created.

But more than that, it was stable. Solid in a way his constructs had never been.

That alone would have been upgrade enough. Yet it seemed Ruinous Matrix allowed him even more freedom than he had imagined.

Tightening his fist around the spear, Aodhán whispered. "Thinner. Sharper. Lighter."

A grin split his face as the spear obeyed, its matrix adjusting with fluid precision. The wolves were closer now—barely a dozen meters away—yet Aodhán barely spared them a glance. His gaze remained fixed on the spear as he realized he didn't have to vocalize his commands at all.

He simply had to think it.

He raised his gaze to the charging wolves, and the spear shifted again—this time not in response to words, but to intent.

Elemental Lightning—Violet.

The shift was seamless, white-gold bleeding into violet like dusk swallowing the last light of day. Space undulated immediately, curving around the spear as its power pressed upon the world.

Half a dozen meters.

Aodhán's grin was impossibly wide. He met the gazes of the five wolves and, without hesitation, launched the spear.

It skewered through them in an instant, ripping savagely through hide, tissue, and bone before any of them could blink.

But it didn't stop there.

It punched through the fifth wolf and slammed into a towering ice ridge behind them, the force of the impact so thunderous it shattered the ridge to pieces, sending jagged shards scattering across the frozen expanse.

Aodhán stared wide-eyed at the destruction a single spear had wrought—amazed by its power even without the boost of quintessence or willpower.

Behind him, Lilian had gone completely still, shock evident on her face.

But the real shock came when the snow settled—and the spear remained. Floating in the air, unbroken and untouched.

Real. Persistent. Just like the armor Calamity Ghaltris had worn at his ascension.

Aodhán's breath slowed.

The spear wasn't just energy or chaos. It wasn't even the liquid ruin he'd named it. It was something else entirely—a construct that persisted as long as its connection to his core remained.

It was…

He had no words. He stared at the floating spear for a long moment before suddenly bursting into laughter, excitement overtaking him entirely. Oh, the miracles he could work with a skill like this. The possibilities.

How much more powerful would it be if he added willpower? If he added quintessence?

A sharp howl cut through his thoughts like a knife. Aodhán turned once more, his gaze snapping toward the new wave as their chorus rose.

There were more of them this time. Over a dozen.

And yet, they too were skewered in an instant—the same spear ripping through their ranks like a knife through paper.

And yet, it remained undamaged. It didn’t explode.

Aodhán couldn’t believe it.

Excited, he created three more spears and launched them at the next wave. Then the next. And the next.

For each new wave, he added a single spear.

It didn’t matter that the waves now numbered in the hundreds. It didn’t matter that the scouts had become warriors. It didn’t even matter that the weakest Rimefangs had reached Tier 50.

His spears ripped them apart all the same.

Bodies dropped to the earth in dozens, painting the snow crimson.

The spears were too fast. Their movements, too clean.

Each was a line of violet death cutting through the storm of white fur and frost. The coppery scent of blood filled the air, sharp and pungent against the sterile bite of the frozen wind.

His reign of terror was absolute.

His authority, unquestioned.

Until the eleventh wave arrived with a howl so piercing it tore through his eardrums like a knife.

Aodhán turned toward them immediately, his attention sharpening as he took in the Rimefangs in the distance. There were nearly two hundred of them, each easily three times the size of the scouts.

Their shoulders stood nearly as high as Aodhán’s chest, their bodies thick with dense white fur layered over ridges of blue-white ice. Jagged plates of frost had formed along their spines and flanks like natural armor, while their crimson eyes burned brighter than blood against the storm.

Above their heads, floating texts shimmered through the snowfall.

[Rimefang Warrior—Tier 58][Rimefang Warrior—Tier 60][Rimefang Warrior—Tier 63]

And at their center—larger than the rest, its mane stiff with long, spear-like shards of ice—stood something stronger.

[Rimefang Vanguard—Tier 67]

Aodhán grinned as he raised all twelve spears and aimed them at the wolves.

But unlike the others, these ones didn’t charge wildly. They spread across the snow with practiced precision, their movements low and controlled as they circled wide around the field of corpses.

Aodhán sent the spears flying, violet streaks ripping through the air fast enough to turn the falling snow to vapor in their wake.

The spears descended—

And then the Rimefangs moved.

They weaved through the attack with terrifying coordination. Those that could not dodge raised shields of ice from the frozen earth, forming thick, jagged barriers that took the impact head-on.

Aodhán smiled widely.

Finally, a challenge.

He called the spears back, and they returned to him in a blink, violet lightning crackling in their wake.

The wolves charged then. Aodhán could have easily taken them out with another skill, but he wanted to see just how far he could push this one.

He aimed the spears once more, and this time as he sent them flying, a single thought followed: Stealth.

He hadn’t known it would work, but his grin practically split his face when the spear vanished and an instant later, blood bloomed across the snow.

Bodies dropped. One after another.

Hundreds of wolves—dead before they could dodge. Dead before they could even see.

Aodhán laughed, excitement flooding his veins as Varéc burst out of his spirit to join in the fun.

Lilian barely spared Varéc a glance. Her gaze remained fixed on the bloodied expanse ahead, following the bodies as they fell without any visible cause.

And despite herself, she shuddered. Because there was only one thing worse than an attack you couldn’t block—

An attack you couldn’t see.

And she, a Mythic, hadn’t seen Aodhán’s attack at all.

Lilian swallowed as a horde of hundreds barreled across the white expanse, the weakest among them already well into the upper seventies.

It had been two hours since Aodhán began fighting, testing each skill to his heart's content before moving on to the next. Lilian had watched it all in quiet shock, unable to fully process how a person could be so overwhelmingly strong at such a young tier.

After the invisible spear attack, he had created a host of other constructs, methodically stretching the limits of Ruinous Matrix—layering in more power with each iteration, pushing his own ceiling as far as it would go.

After that, he had activated a skill he frankly should never have possessed.

A skill that gave him dominion over time.

Lilian had felt her entire body grow cold the instant it activated. It had barely lasted more than a few seconds—three, to be precise—before Aodhán deactivated it, complaining that it was too willpower-intensive.

Lilian wasn’t sure whether to cry or be grateful at the sight of the damage he’d wrought in those three seconds.

Time had ground to a halt and every living creature within his domain had been forced to a standstill.

Everything except he and his familiar.

The two of them had moved through the frozen domain like phantoms, slicing and ripping the wolves to shreds.

These were creatures in the late seventies—creatures who should have posed at least some hindrance.

And he had decimated them in seconds.

The next wave had come after that—stronger, faster, and larger than any that had appeared before. Yet they posed no hindrance.

Instead, Aodhán had activated a third skill, one she had first assumed was defensive.

Oh, how wrong she had been.

The skill had manifested as an ephemeral domain of storm almost as thin as smoke, barely encapsulating a dozen meters in width.

The Rimefangs had launched attacks at it—beams, spears, and spikes ripping easily through it only to be slammed back down with nearly double the force with which they had been launched.

Aodhan had used the skill in tandem with the spears for the last few minutes. However, as this new wave approached, he changed tactics once more.

He dismissed the shield, summoned a massive storm cloud, then turned to glance at his familiar.

He said something Lilian couldn’t hear, but she didn’t need to.

The familiar roared an instant later, and several vortices descended as it dove into the fray, fangs bared while Aodhán sat back to watch the show.

Lilian glanced from the familiar to Aodhán, then back again, trying and failing to understand how one could be so powerful, so confident.

Aodhán was too strong. Too powerful…

The thought saddened her for a moment, but then she smiled. She raised her head high, and when he defeated them all again in a few minutes, she cheered.

Because Aodhán’s strength was her strength.

The stronger he became, the stronger the Sect became.

It did not matter that, should they clash in a duel now, he would most likely bury her beneath a pile of snow. It did not even matter that, in the next few months, he would be so far ahead of her that she would fade into irrelevance.

Because he was a transmigrant.

He was the Crown of Ruining Storms.

The most powerful special in the Sect.

But even as she cheered, as she smiled, a small part of her wondered if perhaps this wasn’t the end for her.

Wondered if perhaps she, too, could one day feel this powerful.

Feel this ruinous.
