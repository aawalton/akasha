"Take care! I’ll probably be back by nightfall!" Archie shouted over his shoulder as he closed the door of Henry’s shop, “Woods R Us”, behind him, the latch clinking against the busted frame before swinging loose. “Oh, and you should probably fix this door!”

After popping the kinks in his back, Archie decided that he would head down the main stretch of the Workshop District, boots hitting the cobblestones in an easy, unhurried rhythm.

Creavenboor’s industrial heart was nothing like it had been earlier that morning. Six hours ago, it was quiet – eerily so, due to the festival in the more central areas of the city. But now, everyone in the Workshop District was alive and very much active.

The air was pungent with various scents of oils, hot leather, sawdust, and hundreds of other scents that all blended in with one another. … I could see myself loving this place if I chose to do a Profession Questline… Who knows, maybe I’ll open up a small shop here and pop in every now and then to sell my wares.

Archie could see tailors sitting on their chairs and bent over their tables behind wide glass windows, with three or four sewing needles in hand and sewing three separate items at once. Leatherworkers stood knee-deep in their vats, pulling up steaming hides and slapping them across wooden racks to dry. A sawmill somewhere to his left gave a sharp whirring noise as it cut through logs, followed by multiple people barking out chains of orders. And the oh, so delightful sound of hammering from smithies off in the distance – he would have to check out their wares when he had the time.

Archie weaved around a man balancing a crate of gears and brass pipe, then sidestepped a swarm of kids darting past with sticks they were pretending were swords. He caught himself grinning – Now this is a Workshop District, he mused to himself.

Compared to the one he’d seen in Neo-Eden, he much preferred this one due to it feeling alive, compared to Neo-Edens, where it felt a bit too modern-esque in a sense where it wasn’t loud and a lot more reserved.

Workshops should be like this – loud, Deacon nodded in agreement with his own thoughts before feeling very nostalgic. …Man, this reminds me of whenever I’d visit Michael’s Camp… I hope he also got out of stasis and was able to meet with his son.

He was halfway past a weaponsmith’s shop when a large crowd appeared from behind a curved street corner and headed in the opposite direction as him. Seeing how shoulder-to-shoulder they were, as a proper human being with etiquette, he stepped aside, letting the mass pour past, catching only scraps of their chatter over the din.

“…we really should head towards–”

“How was the Hollowed Redwood Dungeon here?”

“–those amber golems were a pain in the ass, like I think I still got some of their amber on my–”

Archie’s ears perked; the word dungeon cutting through the noise like a bell. He slowed just enough to catch a gravelly laugh from a thick-bearded dwarf near the back of the group.

“Hehe, well, I guess I might go there meself – get a couple jars o’ that amber for me wife’s sister so she can keep ‘er damn wig on her head instead o’ in the ale! Gehahaha!”

Archie bit back a smirk, filing the name away. Hollowed Redwood Dungeon… sounds like a fun afternoon. He mentally tacked it onto his growing Creavenboor to-do list, right between figure out how to get money without using his card and his Side Quest.

"Now, where can I find info on this Baron Alric Velstrome guy?" Archie muttered to himself as he wandered through the commercial streets of Creavenboor, hands crossed above his head, having already filed away the idea that he should experiment on figuring out how to add pockets into his armor.

"...There doesn’t seem to be a library here," he went on under his breath, glancing at the faces passing him by. "Because everyone I asked when looking for one looked at me like I’m some sort of idiot for thinking such a place was open to the public."

He exhaled through his nose. Guess I’ll have to figure this out the old-fashioned way…Either go and mug some rich-looking person and get answers from them, or go to taverns.

The smell of frying batter and roasted meat drifted past, catching his attention. Across the street was a massive, timber-framed tavern that, in contrast with the other shops he’d seen, was the size of five to six shops. Its swinging sign read “The Burrowing Badger”, and just beneath it, a small wooden stand listed the breakfast menu in thick black paint. This is the place that the guard suggested, Archie mused to himself.

Archie slowed, eyeing the chalky lettering. "Hopefully this place has some good hashbrowns and eggs," he muttered as he read the combo price, 10 Half-Copper Coins, not bad, considering he’d seen a couple of shops selling food for at least double that on his walk through Creavenboor.

Pushing open the door, he stepped into the candlelit interior and saw that the tavern seemed to be just opening – waitresses wiping down tables, a broad-shouldered man behind the counter giving a booming, "Welcome! Find yourself a seat, and one of these beautiful ladies will be over in a minute to sort you out."

Archie gave him a nod and scanned the room before choosing a corner table, three spots in from the entrance. Close enough to catch the hum of conversation, yet far enough not to be in anyone’s direct path, and with the final benefit of not needing to be wary of someone looking over my shoulder. But then again, with my Perception stat being 1008, I probably don’t have to worry about that, I guess.

As Archie sat down on the wooden chair, he was slightly taken aback by how comfortable it was. Comparatively, the one he’d looted from the Shaman Dungeon felt like it was carved from the bones of someone who’d died angry. Guess I’ll be furniture shopping soon, he mused, thinking back to the sad excuse for a table-and-chair set he had in his Basic Workshop – Blacksmith.

Oh. The thought hit him like a tap on the shoulder. My Explorer’s Tent. Practically empty. That mental image of the bare floor and that one crooked lantern in the corner made him exhale through his nose. Yeah… I’m going to need a lot of money. Not exactly something he was confident about, considering the only viable currency he had on hand was whatever he’d swiped from Tallen.

His mind drifted back to the earlier overheard chatter. I could hit that Hollowed Redwood Dungeon those people were talking about… or the Primeval Forest of Creavenboor from the I.F.O. Mission Quest. Both had potential. Herbs and odd plants could go to alchemists, beast meat to the butcheries, hides to the tanners – it would be easy gold to be made.

Considering he was acting as a spy, he wasn’t sure if it would be wise to use his bank card that has his personal information on it in the very same city he registered himself as someone else. Worst case, if he ended up broke and or needed the money before his mission ended, he could always just pop into another city and get his money out from there.

He was halfway through figuring out the logistics of carrying an entire bear pelt in his Spatial Storage without making it smell like wet dog forever when movement at his side pulled him out of his head. A waitress slid a menu onto his table with the ease of someone who’d done it ten thousand times. She dropped off several others around the room, then leaned toward him with a quick smile.

“Sorry, but we still need about five minutes to finish up readying the morning meals for the guardsmen before we can do yours,” she said with a warm smile, “but we can do drinks – is that fine with you?”

Archie nodded. “It’s no problem. The guards protect the city, who am I to deny their breakfast?” he said as he flipped the menu open, scanning the drinks list, then glanced up without hesitation. “I’ll take the lemonade if that’s no problem.”

“It’s no problem at all,” she said as she scribbled his order into her notepad before slipping both into her bosom and giving him a wink that could’ve meant anything from, you’re cute to I expect a tip, before moving on to another table without missing a beat.

Archie leaned back against his seat as he tossed the menu back atop the table, eyes already scanning the tavern for the kind of conversations worth eavesdropping on, as he saw that after he’d entered, quite a few other groups walked into the tavern and settled themselves in.

After she got a couple tables away, Volos and Arsenic popped out of Archie’s Soulscape like they owned the place. The twin centipede hatchlings crawled up onto the table and immediately demanded he give them head pats and scratches.  Acceding to their demands, Archie couldn’t help the faint smile as they nuzzled into the tips of his fingers, their segmented bodies curling and uncurling in that oddly soothing way.

While absentmindedly scratching the ridges between their plates, he sent a pulse of mana into the Agent XVI tattoo beneath his left armguard and opened the Spatial Storage linked to it. He drifted through the items within it for a bit – ignoring the wrapped bundle of herbs, his communicator, and what looked like a loaf of bread that was three weeks old before pulling out a black leather-bound journal.

It was worn, edges scuffed, and corners curling in just enough to suggest it had been carried around for years. Maybe this has something about the person I took over’s past or something.

In the background, Volos and Arsenic had already taken over the menu, both sets of antennae flicking rapidly as they leaned over the pages. Every few seconds, one of them would give a sharp little click and or chitter and tap a claw against a certain dish and or drink, only for the other to make a dismissive twitch and point at something else entirely.

Archie glanced around the tavern; no one seemed to be paying him any mind as they looked lost in their own conversations – good. He cracked the journal open to find the first page blank, not a single stroke of ink. Flipping to the next yielded the same result, and the next after that – still nothing.

“…Ha,” he muttered under his breath. I guess the guy I took over used this as a coaster or something. He let out a sigh, thumb still idly flicking the corner of a page as he placed the dirty-looking journal atop the table.

The sound of approaching footsteps barely registered before the waitress from earlier appeared at his side. What happened next was a blur – her eyes flicked down at the table, and the next instant she let out a sharp, glass-splitting shriek, her tray lurching upward as the lemonade she’d been carrying arced into the air.

The glass full of lemonade flew up into the air before shattering against the wooden ceiling and raining down lemonade over the table and those nearby; himself, the waitress, the startled Volos, and Arsenic.

She was standing there frozen, finger outstretched, the tray clattering to the floor. “B-B-Bugs!” she stammered, pointing straight at Volos and Arsenic.

The twins had turned their heads in perfect sync, giving her the tilted, confused look of creatures that had just been falsely accused of some heinous crime.

Archie raised a hand in what was meant to be a calming gesture. “They’re fine, they’re–” He stopped mid-sentence.

His gaze flicked to the journal in front of him, where a single droplet of lemonade had splashed onto the top right corner of an otherwise blank page… and in the spot it touched, faint ink began to curl into existence, as though it were being written in right that moment. “Log 324.”

Oh… Who’ve thought that a spy would use invisible ink to hide their notes?
