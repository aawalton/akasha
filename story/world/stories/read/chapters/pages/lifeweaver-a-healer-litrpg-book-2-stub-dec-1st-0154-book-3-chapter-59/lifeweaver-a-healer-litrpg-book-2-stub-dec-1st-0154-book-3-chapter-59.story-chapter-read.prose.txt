Healing mana flowed from his Meta-Heart and into his physical body, racing to repair the damage even as the muted sound of a gunshot somewhere far away echoed off the tall buildings. A bullet struck the ground next to him. The next one tore through his leg. Another into Luke's chest. One missed, then another lodged itself in his hip. The pain was nothing, far away and unimportant, but his limbs wouldn't work. Breathing was hard, and he coughed up blood, trying to remain conscious as he heard footsteps approaching.

Another sound of that gun firing, and Luke's head snapped to the right. All went dark, but just for a moment, before at least some of his vision returned. Despite being more than a little dazed, Luke forced his slow thoughts to form threads of mana. Someone spoke right next to him, but he didn't hear a word. They'd shot him in the head. Twice! Without Guardian Weave, those bullets would have gone through, because they looked huge to Luke's senses. They'd still gone through his skull and into his brain, but not far.

Like Relian, Luke had been on the receiving end of two headshots and survived. The problem was, he didn't have much mana left for healing, and the healing mana in his Meta-Heart was all but used up. The other wounds would have to wait. If he couldn't think, there wasn't much he could do to fend for himself. To that end, Luke pushed the two bullets out. Once the metal was gone, healing mana took care of the damn holes in his head.

Vision brightening, Luke worked to get control back of his limbs. Blood still poured out of him. He should do something about that at some point, too. Whoever stood over him reached down and picked one of the bullets up. Lashing out, Luke struck with a thread of mana, intending to drain the bastard and get the sweet mana needed to heal himself, but something sheared through the thread a mere moment after Luke gained access to the mana channels.

Laughter. They were laughing at him.

Turning a little, he saw someone raise a sword. Damn. Was he about to be decapitated? Gunshots were one thing, but Luke didn't think he could survive that. Weaver's Perception told him it wasn't just a single person, but four of them, not counting whoever had shot at him from a distance. Luke recognized one. Lee. The Torturer had returned with new friends. Perhaps more of those Luminous Larks idiots.

Rather than being afraid, Luke found himself getting pissed off. The anger warmed him, and he attempted to use health and mana potions, but found them on cooldown. He didn't remember drinking them, but he must have done so on pure instinct.

"Fuck," he tried to say, but it just came out like a wet gurgle.

"Be careful," he heard Lee say.

Another voice broke through Luke's half-lucid state. "He's dead."

"He's not dead until he's dead. Take the head. That was the deal," Lee whined.

"You're a weird fucker, Lee," the deeper voice said.

"Just hurry up. His friends could come any moment."

Through the hot rage building in Luke, a sense of calm settled over him, like a cool blanket of detachment. Being angry was a fine way to die. Luke didn't want that. One good thing about being half-dead and almost out of mana, he needed only to reach out with his mind to touch the weave.

Bracing himself for the world of pain that was coming his way, Luke grabbed the thread he had identified and weavestepped out of harm's way. If he stayed, he was dead. Going might end with the same result, but it gave him a chance.

Luke slammed down onto what he thought was a table, blood splattering everywhere. His health points, useless as they most often were when gauging a person's actual health, were at nine. Nine! No, eight. Seven. Shit.

His vision was gone again, but the glimpse he got from Weaver's Perception told him he was in a room full of people. What little mana was still available to him only allowed for a single thread of mana. Luke reached out with it toward the person he'd braved the weave of the system to reach.

Hannah didn't even flinch when he latched onto her and drank from her soothing, full pool of mana. It wasn't much, and he was careful not to draw too much on her, like last time, but it was enough to remove the worst fog from his mind. Focusing on his brain, Luke repaired the worst bits. More, he needed more.

"Milla," he rasped, head still resting against the table.

"I'm here, little brother."

Luke extended another thread. With the mana he pulled in, Cascading Threads followed. It took a lot, but it connected him to every single individual in the room, and there were a lot of them. Twenty, at least. It was about then that he realized he'd crashed the meeting between guilds, the DIA, and whoever else they'd found to invite. Not in the figurative sense, either.

They didn't sound happy. A few rebuffed his threads, and one or two cut through them, but most heard the plea of Milla and allowed Luke his fill. In mere moments, his health and mana were back to full.

Luke brought out a bottle of water and used it to clean blood off his face, then drank whatever remained. He'd survived. Like Relian, he'd gone through something that would have killed almost all other Integrated, but he'd made it through to the other side, still breathing. That reminded him, and he went into the left lung and pushed the bullet out, healing the wound left behind by the now crumpled piece of metal. Many pairs of eyes followed it when it bounced off the table and onto the floor.

"Sorry about the interruption," Luke said, clearing his throat. "I'll be going now."

A storm of questions and shouts from the other guild representatives gave him pause, but he ignored them in favor of Hannah's question.

"Not a dungeon, I'm guessing?"

"No," Luke said. "Ambush. Lee again. Those Luminous Larks people. Right outside the guild."

Milla broke in. "I'll get the word out. Don't want anyone going there by themselves."

"I'm going," Luke said.

"No, you're not."

Drawing in even more mana from everyone in the room and converting it to healing mana to store in his Meta-Heart, Luke ignored his sister's order and threw himself through the weave again. Through his near-death experience, he'd held on to the simmering rage and used it to keep himself conscious. Now, having nursed himself back to health, it was time to let that anger flower into something beautiful.

"Dammit, he's doing it, isn't he?" he heard Milla say before he disappeared from the room less than ten minutes after ending up there, having weavestepped using Hannah's warm and inviting thread. To his senses, it had contained the promise of a glorious future full of warmth, laughter, and power. There had been shadows there, too, but those they would deal with when they arose.

The thread Luke used now was one he didn't even want to touch. A coward's thread. He'd found it and singled it out by yanking so hard he thought it might break. The one on the other side of that wouldn't feel it, but it felt satisfying nonetheless. Luke didn't know who it belonged to, but he sure hoped it was Lee's.

When he'd attached a thread of mana with the hopes of draining mana, Luke started out with the tiniest amount of healing mana. It wasn't enough to heal anything, but it did give an echo for that person. After the Singer who portrayed an old woman and the fairy, this was now something he'd decided to do in all fights with anyone who wasn't a dungeon monster. You never know when you might need to find someone again.

Milla: "Luke! Get back here!"

It was much too late to go back, because Luke was standing right in front of two guys his own age sitting on a couch with open cans of beer in their hands and looks of surprise so thorough it was like seeing something out of a cartoon with their wide eyes and mouths hanging open.

"Let's have a little chat," Luke said, letting go of the one he'd weavestepped to. Lee fell to the floor, unconscious but still alive. "Let's talk about Luminous Larks."

These three were not all of them. Weaver's Perception picked up three more people in the apartment Luke had just arrived in. All three were already coming after Lee's cry of alarm. The asshole shouted in total fear when he saw Luke and had done his best to flee, but it was far too late for the Torturer at that point.

The ruckus alerted the others, though. It meant Luke didn't have to go in silent and play at being Ray skulking through the shadows. Good, because that had never been his plan.
