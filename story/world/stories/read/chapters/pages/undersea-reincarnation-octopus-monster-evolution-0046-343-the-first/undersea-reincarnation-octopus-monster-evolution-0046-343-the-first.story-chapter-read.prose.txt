It was getting harder and harder to see much of anything as the kicked up sand filtered into his beak and bounced off of his eyes. Thankfully, unlike his previous life, the sand might as well have been absent. It didn’t bother his eyes at all though. But Henry couldn’t see crap. Even with the Shadows spread out all around, it was difficult to see his opponent with how murky the area had gotten.

Good thing I’ve latched on to this guy, he thought. There was no way it could get rid of him. No matter how hard it bucked and slammed into any reef or crevices it could find.

Henry was a little to resilient for non-empowered damage to do much to him at this point.

Still, the A-rank was rampaging so much it felt like Henry was riding into a sandstorm. It must have looked somewhat impressive to see from the outside, but right now, he was too busy overseeing his Octominds and theorizing about how he could make Lightning Discharge—and most of his active skills—stronger, while his Octominds continued to observe how Venom Production did its thing.

Giving the skill more mana just makes the output larger, Henry thought. But not stronger. It also makes it more difficult to control and handle. That’s not exactly what I want. I want the Lightning Discharge to be more intense, not to just have more of it. Like what I’ve just done. Like the lobster’s…

Intensity was the main difference he could think of between his own Lightning Discharge and the lobster’s. Of course, the whole shtick of that A-rank was lightning so it made sense that it’d beat him in both quantity and quality. But that didn’t mean Henry couldn’t try to match. He just needed to upgrade and raise the quality of his own skill. Once it was powerful enough, he should be able to match the intensity of the lobster’s offense, though it would have a different angle to it.

Or he could improve on what he’d just done. But with how his soul still felt from overloading the skill nexus, he didn’t think it was a super-healthy method of increasing the power of his skills.

Maybe I’ll just focus on the pressure instead of the quantity? he theorized as he glanced down at the trembling the crab. Henry was pretty sure the crustacean’s fury was slowly turning to panic and fear. Especially considering the copious amounts of venoms coursing through its system. It wasn’t as susceptible to them as a being of Henry’s rank, but the sheer quantity of the stuff that must be circulating in its bloodstream must be wrecking havoc.

But it was still taking time, and Henry was glad he’d spared the crab the painful stuff. He only focused on the tranquilizing and paralytic ones. And his own, of course, which was robbing the crab of its strength and lifeforce more and more as time went on. He didn’t use any of the stuff he’d gotten from the lionfish or from that ass of a seahorse he’d fought with Maurice before the duel with Tevarius.

Ugh. Didn’t want to think of that prick, Henry cursed under his breath. He was glad the man would be shipped on another boat toward his exile.

Focusing back on a new instance of Lightning Discharge, Henry channeled only half the usual mana into it, holding it still and preventing it from pulling any more mana. This time, he took it slow. Henry spread his senses around the skill nexus, as if wrapping both of his hands around the crackling orb that just wanted to lash out, and then, he flexed, pressurizing the instance of the skill as he visualized it growing smaller, condensing into a more powerful version of itself.

It wasn’t as responsive as he hoped. In fact, it was even a little painful.

Henry groaned in the back of his throat as something in his soul strained, as if he’d just strongly exerted a muscle he hadn’t worked in… well. Ever. Come to think of it, Henry didn’t think he’d tried condensing skill instances in the past. He’d modified them, infused them with essences, tried to fuse them with other skills, but he’d never just… overloaded one then tried to condense it, and while it was apathetically responding to his vision, barely obeying his will, it was growing a little smaller. But the process was taking all of his attention. Unless he wanted the pressure to be uneven.

Henry also had the feeling that he shouldn’t let his focus slip. He felt that if he lost control, he might actually do damage to himself. And he wasn’t willing to confirm that intuition. So slowly, with the help of two of his Octominds, he pushed through the discomfort and minute by minute, the bowling ball-sized orb of crackling lightning grew smaller until by the time Henry couldn’t condense it any further, it was the size of a golf ball. Which he then finally freed from his grasp and let it burst down toward the crab.

Seeing its potency, Henry would have celebrated. If he wasn’t dealing with an equal amount of shock and pain.

It was as if a bolt of real lightning had just been shot out of Henry’s beak. There was a flash of intense light that turned the sandstorm white, and from one of his Shadows’ point of view, it had been like looking up at a dark cloud that had suddenly flashed with the wrath of nature. The attack bore into the crab’s innards and stunned the A-rank for a second. Even Henry’s arms were twitching as the powerful attack managed to affect him as well.

I guess I know what kind of technique I’ll be exploring next, he thought, taking a moment to both heal his arm and flush some Life Surge into his soul. The healing magic alleviated the pain, but he still felt a little sore.

He needed to finish this soon and go get some rest.

Below him, the A-rank was swaying as the damage was proving to be too much. It wasn’t just the lightning, but everything Henry had been pouring into its shell for the last twenty minutes was taking its toll, and while Henry wasn’t A-rank yet… he wasn’t that far either.

It was only a matter of time until the crab fell. And it did. Some five minutes later. At which point the notification finally rang in Henry’s mind.

1x [Moss-Shell Ravager (A) - Level 4] core was collected.

Henry soberly detached himself from the crab’s back, and as the sand and debris finally began to set, he could see the scorch marks and burnt blood seeping out of two foot-wide hole he’d used to inject and burn the monster. Swimming forward, he came to a stop in front of the monster’s face and already, a few scavengers had begun hovering around the plane-sized carcass.

Henry took second, staring up at the massive crab he’d just taken down.

He’d just killed his first A-rank.

Henry’s mind flashed to the first A-rank he’d seen. The giant turtle. He still remembered how utterly terrifying the experience had been. Back then, the weight of A-rank was nearly a physical thing. Standing up to them was not even a consideration. And now, he just downed the first one. It was on the weaker side, sure, but this was just the first of them.

Man… Maurice’s gonna be pissed, he thought with a chuckle. Henry eyed the massive creature as a nosy, flat shark poked its head between its mandibles. He was considering detaching a claw or two from the crab to eat later, but then he paused.

He could just… stuff all of it in his Maw, couldn’t he? Henry would bet the shell would be worth something. Then letting out a slow breath, he pulled on his Octominds and focused.

Summoning an instance of Maw right in front of the giant crab, Henry grit his beak as the strain from the earlier exercise made itself known, but within a few seconds, he managed to grab on to the giant crab and stuffed it in his storage. He didn’t need to enlarge himself or wrap an arm around it. That was the advantage of using a separate instance of Maw. And with that done, he began shapeshifting once more to regain his human appearance.

I’ll have to ask Velistraine about this condensing thing. I bet it has a name, he thought as he shrank his vital organs before he encased it all in a thick layer of bones. Then Henry got started on the outside body, and when he was done a few minutes later, he finally swapped with a couple of clones until finally, he landed with a splash atop the deck.

Henry stretched his arms above his head, eying the notification with a grin. He was about to consume the A-rank core when a cough caught his attention.

Turning around, Henry found both Maurice and Velistraine glaring at him, arms crossed.

Crap, he thought, swallowing. Then plastering an ingratiating smile on his face, he waved. “Hey… guys? What’s up?”

Velistraine and Maurice shared a look, gave him a withering glare, then turned with a huff and left in lockstep.

Henry watched them leave, then his shoulders sagged. Maybe he should have insisted on inviting Maurice. But he was sure he’d be able to bribe him later. As for Velistraine, she was being dramatic. She’d gotten way more A-ranks than he had done. He didn’t need to share his first win with her.

“Oh well, I’ll find something for me and Maurice. As soon as I get some rest and eat this bad boy,” Henry mumbled. Then, with a deep inhale of the fresh, salty ear, he consumed his first A-rank core.
