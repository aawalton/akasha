The world wasn’t ready for the first great conflict. There was no time for the combatants to prepare. There was no room for preparation or planning. The End of the First Era descended on the entire cosmos.

It is said that such Ends were always foretold to occur. That every Era would end with a violent overturn of the established Order. Such prophecies are etched on the core of ancient planets, on the hearts of the beings as old as Time. Yet, most remain unaware, and many who hear tell of it consider it blasphemy and fear mongering.

Yet, that was how the first of the Great Gods rose to prominence. The First End was what established the Overrealm.

Only Time and Fate can tell when the Second End will arrive.

Forgotten Compendium

Elphas T.

Ryland didn’t need too long to finish constructing the spell and start accruing the accretion matter. The issue here was one of storage. While he could try coming up with the right spell circle that would allow him to store what was one of the most volatile substances in the entire cosmos, he didn’t want to go to such lengths or such trouble.

Instead, he would rely on the Ivail Trade Guild. They were the ones who dealt with such cases, and since they more or less owed him a great deal, he wouldn’t need to even convince them to assist him.

He recalled well how he had fought the Calamity of Drought on Abarosk. The gargantuan pit of a creature radiated heat while sucking away much of the moisture and liquid from the planet, causing a decades-long drought to strike Abarosk with civilization-ending ferocity. The Mastitians would have perished if word of the Calamity hadn’t reached Ryland.

Well, word of it, and proof that it had been born from one of his former pieces.

A simple lymph node. That was all that was required for an actual Calamity to erupt into being, feeding on Abarosk’s thick quantity of mana and growing into a fearsome creature of legend. It had wiped out a great deal of species on the planet already.

That Ryland had managed to end it without further issues was a tremendous blessing, at least, according to the Mastitians. He had just ended up regretting that it had gotten to such a state.

Ryland wasn’t quite sure if that was what had influenced him, but he had ended up assisting Abarosk yet again with their Astral Invasion. Their homes had been assaulted, their people once again placed in desperate danger, and while the Mastitians had fought with determination and valour, they really might have fallen.

Except, Ryland had been in the vicinity and had decided to lend his assistance. The invaders had been beaten back and the Inscription Ryland had set back then had repelled any further attacks. Aggression was pointless, so the invaders had never returned.

So, it was no surprise that a portal of rippling blue-green energy opened up about a few dozen armspans from Ryland, from which a large, kelpie-drawn carriage emerged.

Well aware that the Inscription keeping the carriage and its contents safe against the cosmos was only going to last so long, Ryland made sure to cast [Oneself World] around them all moments after his allies arrived.

“Sir!” Viren said, poking his head out of the carriage. “We’re back.”

Ryland greeted him with a smile, which widened slightly when the Mastitians disembarked from the carriage just behind him.

“Glad to see you back, Viren,” Ryland said. “And you as well, Guildmaster Khetezo.”

The leader of the Mastitian group, Khetezo, moved towards Ryland. Watching others move in the cosmos with no flat ground was always strange. Odd enough to see Viren “swim” without any water to flow through. Weirder still to observe the Mastitians use their powerful tails to propel themselves forward with them coiling like springs.

“Great Lord Esper,” Khetezo greeted with a bow of his head. His retainers behind him followed his motion. “We’ve come to render you our aid in whatever endeavour you desire. Name it, and it shall be done.”

Ah. Ryland always found it refreshing to deal with the Mastitians and their absolute lack of understanding regarding small talk. Their entire species just didn’t compute it.

That said, Ryland got the impression they were aware of what he intended and were still honour-bound to assist him anyway. It spoke well of their character. Not that Ryland needed any further proof. Mastitians in general prided themselves on their dedication and justice.

“I don’t get it, sir,” Viren ventured. He looked like he was regretting speaking up, perhaps wondering why anyone would bother explaining what was going on to him anyway. But Ryland appreciated how he voiced his question regardless. He could never fault anyone for simply trying to understand things better. “Why do we need the help of a trade guild?”

Like how the Mastitians were potentially aware of Ryland’s real goals, it sort of looked like Viren had already begun to formulate answers to his own question. But Ryland answered anyway.

“The Ivail Trade Guild is one of the most powerful and accomplished consortiums in the entire cosmos,” Ryland said.

Viren nodded slowly, his eyes professing that he had started thinking about what that meant and implied about what was to happen next.

Khetezo and the other Mastitians all bowed, even lower this time. “You honour us greatly, Lord Esper.”

“One of the ways they excel is through their ability to store and transport all sorts of volatile and difficult-to-handle substances almost anywhere across the cosmos.”

“Across anywhere in this quadrant of the cosmos,” Khetezo said modestly.

Ryland smiled. “Which is more than good enough for me. I aim to collect the accretion matter, as Viren has probably explained to you. I need you to store it and transport it to Rhel.”

Khetezo scratched the scales on his arms contemplatively. “That should be no problem. Thy will be done, Lord Esper.”

“What does that mean, sir?” Viren asked. “What does Lord Esper mean?”

“It’s the title we bestow to the greatest among us,” Khetezo said before Ryland could explain properly. “Those who have accomplished the most, those whose achievements shine the brightest, those who have engraved their mark on the annals of our history. We honour them with that moniker, in favour of the first ascended Lord Esper who blessed Abarosk with his bounty.”

Viren fell into another contemplative mood, probably wondering what exactly had happened in those ancient times.

“Alright, then,” Ryland said. “Now, I know you have no intention of questioning what I intend to do, and you’re likely entertaining your own suspicions, but I’d like to make things quite clear. I intend to use the accretion stream matter as part of a ritual Inscription that will breach the Overrealm itself, in order for me to take on the Great Gods and rescue a student of mine.”

The stillness from the Mastitians convinced him that they were rather shaken by that announcement. But the general lack of response proved that he hadn’t really surprised them, just confirmed their worst fears.

“Your reasoning is just, Lord Esper,” Khetezo said, as ever the spokesman for not just the Mastitians with him, but for most of Abarosk as well, as far as Ryland had dealt with the Realm. “We stand with you against this grave injustice. To kidnap a mere child is no godly or divine act.”

“Even when the First Lord Esper is a Great God too?”

Viren gasped a little. “Oh, so that’s what they meant by ascended.”

Now Viren appeared extremely contemplative. Ryland could almost see the gears turning in his head as he tried to fathom just how a Mastitian could have gone on to become a Great God.

“The Great Gods are not all the same,” Khetezo said with calm insistence. “We have faith that Lord Esper, wherever he may be at this moment, would never partake in such a heinous act.”

Hmm. Ryland wasn’t quite so certain of that. Plus, the Great Gods weren’t actually the ones holding Acrypha against her will. They were merely sheltering her captors, for what reason Ryland was yet to glean. He’d find it out eventually, however.

“I appreciate your assistance,” Ryland said. “Perhaps we can begin? You can prepare your storage and transportation. I don’t intend to remain here and collect the stream matter myself, but the spell I’m leaving behind will be enough. Now, we can focus on the other important matter—payment for your services.”

As one, the Mastitians raised their hands as though to forestall any altercation or disagreement.

“Please, Lord Esper,” Khetezo said. “We don’t expect anything in return. After all you’ve done for us, this is the least we can do. The least we can offer. You mustn’t think of it as a service we are rendering in return for some sort of remuneration. We will be glad to help you in any way we can.”

“I understand,” Ryland said. He had been afraid of something like this, so he had prepared countermeasures already. It was a little bit manipulative, but in the end, it wasn’t really harmful. “Regardless, you wouldn’t want me to feel indebted to you for something you’ve done for me for free, would you?”

That made Khetezo and the rest of the Mastitians blink. They understood Ryland’s perspective, and they really didn’t wish to make him uncomfortable in any way.

And that was enough to make them accede to Ryland’s proposed deal.

“Is there anything overwhelming bothering you at the moment?” Ryland asked. “Something that you need assistance with and are willing to defend, but would need far stronger power to properly stop?”

That was such a vague and somewhat awkwardly phrased question, Ryland immediately started wondering how he might have phrased it better. Thankfully, the Mastitians didn’t appear to think much of it. Now that Ryland had relayed his request, they were left with no choice but to answer him truthfully, regardless of their feelings on the matter.

“Well, we are facing another invasion,” Khetezo eventually said after much consideration.

Ryland raised an eyebrow. “Another Astral Invasion?”

“Indeed, Lord Esper. We’ve spotted scouts in the far distance of the cosmos, all thanks to the wards you placed all around Abarosk, Lord Esper. Then their outriders reached us a few months ago. Since then, we’ve contended with miniature forays and assaults, though nothing that we would call a true invasion just yet.”

“Yet, you suspect there will be a true invasion before long.”

“We do indeed, Lord Esper.” The Mastitians behind Khetezo all straightened, as if the mere mention of an invasion had them mentally preparing for an assault. “I suppose it is fortuitous that you’ve come to us again before anything truly disastrous could occur. Fate smiles on us all.”

Ryland rather doubted Fate was smiling on anyone or anything, but he didn’t argue against Khetezo. Rather, since he had learned that they were facing a potential invasion in the future while also constantly suffering random attacks from the Astral Raiders, it was time for Ryland to take care of the matter. He would have even without the Mastitians helping him.

“Rest assured,” Ryland said to Khetezo and the rest of his party. “I will defeat the Astral Raiders and ensure Abarosk is safe again.”

They all bowed low to him.

Khetezo was the first to rise. “On behalf of all of Abarosk, we thank you greatly for your tremendous blessing, Lord Esper.”

Ryland smiled at them and turned to Viren. “Are you ready to leave, Viren?”

The boy nodded. “Yes, let’s get going, sir.”

Ryland realized Viren had absolutely no idea about what they were going to face. Viren knew it too. Yet he had so much faith in Ryland, he was more than willing to embark on this next little adventure without much foreknowledge.

Maybe that little shakeup where Viren had been disappointed upon Acrypha’s kidnapping was finally turning around.

So, Ryland started preparing for another battle and cast another [Translocate].

It was a few days before the next assault occurred. In the meantime, Ryland took Viren to Abarosk where they enjoyed much of what the city had to offer. Its variety of seafood, the strange public art it possessed, the way the Mastitians venerated their dead, and so on.

Basically, Ryland immersed Viren in the civilization and culture of the Mastitians.

Because simply showing up to battle wasn’t enough. Ryland possessed the power he required to repel the invaders, so all that was needed of Viren was just the willingness to understand why this was necessary.

“These people,” Viren said. “They’re not even part of the Accorded Realms, are they sir?”

“No, they aren’t. The Mastitians are one of the most isolated civilizations in the entire cosmos.”

“No one to rely on except themselves. And except you, sir.”

“To an extent. But you were right the first time. Their self-reliance is tremendous. Their willingness to confront any situation with their own prowess and skills, regardless of how it matches up against their situation, is what makes them such a hardy people.”

Viren nodded. His eyes were looking far-away, but his expression was one of determination.

Soon enough, word of the next assault came up. A few Mastitians whispered that this was the beginning of the next invasion, that they would all need to step up now.

Ryland spared them the need to do so and simply [Translocated] away into the cosmos.

With [Oneself World], they were once again embraced by the darkness of the void studded with stars. But upon closer look, some of the stars were flickering brighter and faster than the others. And upon even closer inspection…

“Sir,” Viren said with rising concern. “Are some of the stars growing bigger?”

“No,” Ryland answered. “The ones that are growing bigger aren’t actually stars. They’re the invaders.”

“The what?”

Ryland just waited until the first “star” blitzed into their quadrant of the cosmos. Until what was actually approaching Abarosk with blinding power and speed finally became visible, especially since Ryland’s barriers were protecting them against their eyes being burned out by the light.

“Are those…” Viren struggled to push out his own words thanks to sheer disbelief. “Are those people riding on meteors?”
