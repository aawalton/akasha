[Time]: Summer Break, Day 47 — 6:15 PM[Location]: White City · The Wisteria · Wei Changqing's Suite

The post-dinner suite had achieved the settled lethargy of a roster with nothing left to prove until morning.

Victoria sat in the high-backed armchair by the window, a thick volume of arcane ward theory resting open on her lap.

Across the coffee table, Cecilia occupied the centre of the plush sectional sofa.She was not occupying it alone.

Maria had claimed the sovereign territory of Cecilia's lap.

She was sprawled at full extension, eyes closed, radiating the aggressive contentment of a very large, very dangerous predator digesting a heavy meal. Cecilia held a strategy dossier in her left hand.

Her right hand moved in a slow, hypnotic rhythm through Maria's spilled pale white-gold hair, with the unhurried, automatic cadence one reserves for a particularly dangerous, high-maintenance cat.

Victoria turned a page.

Two months of continuous cohabitation had permanently calcified her sensibilities.

She had long since accepted the logistical inevitability that she was going to accumulate multiple, terrifyingly lethal sisters-in-law, filed this under Inevitable Geopolitical Realities.

Keeping her eyes firmly anchored to her textbook remained the only viable strategy for her continued cardiovascular health.

On the glass coffee table, Victoria's terminal emitted a single, brief vibration.

An unknown number. She reached for it with the mild distraction of someone expecting a municipal spam alert.

She tapped the screen.The colour drained from her face with the speed of a severed artery.

Across the coffee table, Cecilia's hand stopped mid-stroke.She lifted her eyes from the dossier. They settled on Victoria's face and stayed there.

What is it?Victoria raised her head.This was, definitively, the longest two seconds of her entire life.

Her mind catalogued and rejected at terminal velocity.A family emergency? They were her family.A sudden illness? Wei Changqing would have her inside a diagnostic array before she reached the door.A supply run? Flandmira would send a construct.Something left at the arena? Karula would insist on accompanying her.

The alternatives collapsed one after another, each destroyed by the suffocating competence of the four Arch-Witches currently digesting dinner in her immediate vicinity.

Victoria opened her mouth. Her vocal cords bypassed her prefrontal cortex entirely.

"My girlfriend," Victoria said, her voice completely flat, "is going to break up with me."

The suite stopped.Cecilia's expression simply ceased to update, the way a screen goes dark when the signal cuts entirely.

Girlfriend.The word occupied the air with the specific, unstable density of something that had no correct container.

Who? Your roommate? The Ludwig girl?Cecilia's lips parted. No sound emerged.Why do I not know about this? When did it reach the breaking-up stage?Has she grown up?

At the edge of the room, Flandmira's fashion magazine slid from her fingers. Both hands flew to her mouth, her eyes wide with dynastic horror. "Oh, by the Stars — Victoria."

On the sofa, Maria bolted upright."That girl?" Maria's amber eyes narrowed to dangerous slits. "Is she completely blind? I will personally —"

Maria looked at Victoria's face.She swallowed the rest of the sentence.

Victoria sucked in a sharp breath and stood, the leather volume sliding unnoticed to the carpet. She did not look at her sister.

"There's no time," Victoria said. "I have to go right now."She moved toward the entryway.

Wei Changqing was already there. The captain asked no questions. She lifted a dark coat from the rack and draped it over Victoria's shoulders as she passed.

The heavy suite door clicked shut.

Karula turned from the window. She crossed the room and stopped beside the sofa, looking down at Cecilia, who was staring at the empty armchair.

She placed a hand on Cecilia's shoulder.

"It will be fine," Karula said. "If that Ludwig girl truly intends to break it off..."

Cecilia closed her eyes and took a slow, careful breath. "When she returns," she said, "I am going to have a long talk with her."

[Location]: White City · Pedestrian Promenade

The heavy door of The Wisteria sealed behind her, cutting off the warmth and the voices of the suite.

Victoria stepped out into the White City's evening.

The sun had dipped behind the crystalline spires. The promenade was still crowded: tourists, broadcast screens running ceaseless pre-Final analysis on an endless, looping drone, souvenir stalls still open in the last light.

The air smelled of festival food and ozone.

Victoria stood on the pavement.She had no destination.

She knew, in the abstract, what a person in a crisis was supposed to do. Find a bench. Call someone. Initiate a coping mechanism. But her mind wasn't occupied by the crisis she'd just come from.

It was entirely consumed by something she'd said three minutes ago.

My girlfriend is going to break up with me.It had simply fallen out of her mouth, the first exit available: immediate, undeniable, and requiring her physical absence from the room.

The problem was that after the words left her mouth, they had not registered as false.

They hadn't tasted like ashes. They had felt, with an unnerving precision, like pulling a lever that had been wired and waiting in the dark for some time.

Victoria stared at the cobblestones.She did not know what that meant. She had no intention of finding out right now.

Liandra von Milan'thirskaya had only come out for a walk. She hadn't been back to the White City properly in months, and the evening air suited her.

She saw Victoria standing on the pavement. She walked over, slid her hands into her jacket pockets, and stood beside her, facing the same direction, staring at nothing in particular.

Silence."Your face is wrong," Liandra said.

Victoria did not answer. She pulled the terminal from her coat pocket and held it out.

Liandra took it. Her eyes moved across the image: the silver spoon, the ring, the convex reflection of the Eastern Courtyard. She handed it back.

"Two of us isn't going to be enough," Liandra said. Flat. Immediate. "Is there anyone else you can trust?"

"Hathaway von Ludwig."

The name came out before Victoria had finished the thought. She registered the speed of her own answer with a faint, unguarded blink.

Liandra looked at her.

A Wellington, the expression said, with the quality of a person encountering information that defies every geopolitical law they were raised on, and the person you trust first is a Ludwig.

She filed it under things she did not have time to analyse and gave a single nod. "Then we go find her now."

[Location]: White City · The Wisteria · Wei Changqing's Suite

Wei Changqing walked back from the entryway. She exhaled softly and crossed to sit beside Cecilia.

"I am certain," Wei said, her voice its customary, grounding warmth, "that it is simply a misunderstanding."

She exchanged a brief, perfectly calibrated look with Karula.

On this question, their positions were entirely aligned. Even if it were not a misunderstanding, they possessed the necessary resources to forcibly reclassify it.

Flandmira had already taken up her position at the centre of the Persian rug.

"It is the Ludwig family," she declared, "applying political pressure. Why else would a relationship simply collapse the night before the Final? This kind of archaic dynastic interference should have been outlawed in the last century: it is barbaric."

"Is it?" Maria said flatly. "That doesn't feel right."

She lay back down, but this time she didn't sprawl. She rested her head carefully on Cecilia's knees, her eyes fixed on the ceiling. Something in the room's frequency was wrong. She couldn't name it.

Cecilia placed her hand back in Maria's hair.The lazy, automatic petting was gone. Her fingers moved slowly, deliberately, searching for a grounding point in a room that had quietly lost its gravity.

Wei Changqing's gentle smile faltered by exactly one frame."...Professor Mihaye?" she said quietly.

The atmosphere in the room inverted completely.Mihaye Grün was standing in the hotel lobby, waiting with the patient, entirely unapologetic entitlement of a former coach.

The Greed Umbrella dismantled their own kill-zones with synchronised, practised speed.Flandmira crossed the room in three strides and threw the door open.

Mihaye strolled through the threshold. She cast one sweeping glance at the room, registered the temperature and composition of the silence, and dropped onto the largest sofa with the brazen, unselfconscious sprawl of someone who has never once been asked to justify their presence.

Wei Changqing rose. The lingering tension in her shoulders softened into a warm smile of deep respect. "Professor. To what do we owe the pleasure?"

"Here to mooch dinner," Mihaye announced. "I went bankrupt."

Flandmira stared at her, hands on her hips. "That is a personal failure of your own making, Professor," she said, the corner of her mouth already giving her away. "If you had simply wagered on us to win like a loyal coach, you could purchase a private floating island by now."

"The ingredients are in the kitchen," Karula added, rolling her eyes with practiced, theatrical contempt. "Cook it yourself. We have the Grand Masters Final tomorrow. The sheer audacity required to come here and demand we cater for you is extraordinary."

Mihaye pointed a tragically wronged finger directly at Karula's face. "You have a lot of nerve. This is entirely your fault. I placed a serious, significant wager on you winning against Paddy."

Karula's retort died instantly in her throat. A violent, catastrophic blush detonated upward from her collar to the tips of her ears.

"Well," Karula mumbled, eyes averted with considerable effort, "that still shouldn't have bankrupted you completely. There was still the match against Lin Zhaojun after that..."

Karula stopped.Her brain caught up with the timeline. She looked at Mihaye."Wait. Did you bet on me to lose that match?"

Mihaye looked at the ceiling. Mihaye looked at the far wall. Mihaye said nothing.

"I abandoned my aesthetic for that match!" The aristocratic composure cracked clean through. "I gave up the static bombardment paradigm! I threw my pride into the mud and fought her in a sweaty, undignified, high-speed dogfight, the exact kind of meta-adaptation you've been screaming at me to do for three months, and you bet against me?!"

"I acknowledge and respect your personal growth, Jackdaw!" Mihaye argued, holding her hands up defensively. "But the statistical spread on that matchup was a nightmare, and a responsible coach has to hedge her financial portfolio!"

"You are an absolute parasite!" Karula said.

Wei Changqing was already walking toward the kitchen, rolling up her sleeves with a quiet, resigned sigh.

On the sofa, the tight, load-bearing stillness in Cecilia's posture eased by a fraction. The corners of her mouth moved, barely perceptibly, in a direction that was not quite a smile but was adjacent to one.

Maria sat up."Where is Katalu, Professor? If you are bankrupt and begging for dinner, who is feeding the cat?"

Mihaye waved a dismissive hand. "Don't worry about her. There are plenty of people perfectly willing to throw food at her right now."

She settled further into the cushions, radiating profound self-sacrifice. "I deliberately left her behind. Do you know how much a Greater Lantern Cat eats in one sitting? I saved your entire pantry. You should be thanking me."

From the kitchen doorway, Wei Changqing's smile melted into the long-familiar helplessness of someone who could never out-shameless her former coach. "Yes, Professor. We are overwhelmingly grateful for your mercy."

After dinner, the table was cleared for cards.

Mihaye announced her intention to use her former students as biological ATMs.

Flandmira dealt the first hand with the theatrical, half-joking menace of a casino dealer preparing to fleece a mark. "You are bankrupt, Professor. We are not playing for free. What exactly are you bringing to the table as collateral?"

Mihaye rubbed her chin thoughtfully. "Hmm. If I lose, I come back as your coach."

The entire suite went dead silent.Greed Umbrella did not have a registered head coach. The administrative slot on their roster had been deliberately, stubbornly left empty for months.

The casual, after-dinner atmosphere evaporated. It was instantly replaced by the terrifying, synchronised bloodlust of a world-class competitive roster.

From the other side of the room, Cecilia walked over.She pulled out a chair, smoothed her coat, and sat down at the table.

Her expression had smoothed back into her unreadable composure. Her tactical execution was suffocating and exact, steadily dismantling Mihaye's imaginary capital with quiet, efficient contempt.

Maria, sitting across from her, played her hand with ruthless, uncompromising exactitude.Between hands, in the brief pauses while the next round was being dealt, Cecilia's gaze would drift toward the closed front door.

Maria caught it every single time.

Deep into the night, the suite grew quiet. The cards sat abandoned. The others retreated to their rooms.

Cecilia stood alone by the floor-to-ceiling windows.

The White City spread below her, neon and broadcast light spilling into the dark. The pre-Final coverage was still running on every outdoor screen, bright and ceaseless.

She pressed her forehead against the cool glass.

She was not thinking about Evangeline. She was not running simulations for tomorrow's match against [Golden Iris].

She was tracing a timeline.When did Victoria start looking at the Ludwig girl differently? What were we talking about during that time? Did she try to say something? How did I miss it?

The glass was cold against her skin.I should have asked her sooner.
