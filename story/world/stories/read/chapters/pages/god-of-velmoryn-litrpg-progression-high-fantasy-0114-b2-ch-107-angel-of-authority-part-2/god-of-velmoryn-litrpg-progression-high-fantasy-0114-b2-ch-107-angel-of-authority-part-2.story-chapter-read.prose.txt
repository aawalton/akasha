Aurelion frowned, his jaw clenched tightly as he once again tried to break the invisible shackles and force himself free, but the harder he struggled against whatever held him in place, the greater the pressure bearing down on his body became.

Once he admitted to himself that he could neither free himself nor activate any of his skills, he finally spoke.

“Who are you to question me? This is no duel. This is no test of our strength or will,” he muttered through clenched teeth.

Noah’s angel allowed him to speak, then waited a little longer as though making certain Aurelion had nothing else to add. Only when the silence stretched did the head marked with J respond.

“We… Are… The… Embodiment… Of… Justice…” it rasped.

“And you are mistaken, accused,” the head marked with P continued. “This trial is the ultimate test of your strength and will. If one is born with a larger body and greater strength, does that grant them the right to take the life of someone less fortunate? Does power alone make one right?”

Aurelion faltered.

Not because he believed he had done anything wrong, but because he had been asked something he had never truly considered. Angel or not, Aurelion had only been alive for a few months, and there was still much about the world he had never experienced, much less stopped to question.

“We, the four representations of Justice…” the head with the letter P continued, “…were born to uphold the law. We exist to bring justice into this world. We are the shields that protect the victims and the swords that cleanse the wicked.”

No matter what Noah’s angel spoke, I could never believe that the servant of a god who enslaved other deities through twisted contracts truly cared about justice.

No. Those words had to serve another purpose.

But what?

“Victims? Wicked?” Aurelion hissed. “You speak of bringing justice, but whose justice? Who will bring justice for my Lord’s children whom you and your god came here to slaughter and enslave? Who will answer for the velmoryns who died when that god descended?”

Anger surged through him, his scarlet eyes shining brightly against the darkness surrounding this strange place. He pushed against the force restraining him with everything he had, and for the briefest moment, his body actually shifted forward, as though whatever had been pressing him down had loosened its grip.

The head marked with P spoke again.

“None of what you have said justifies your crimes against mortals, accused. Your attempt to deflect from your own wrongdoing will not work against us. We are the embodiment of Justice, and should it ever be proven that we ourselves have acted against justice, even we would not be spared punishment.”

Then P turned toward the head marked with J.

“I request that we proceed with the first phase of punishment. The accused refuses to take this trial seriously, and therefore must be shown the weight of Justice.”

“DEATH! DEATH TO THE ACCUSED!” the head marked with E shrieked.

“Granted,” J rasped. “Proceed… With… Scourging…”

The moment the final word left its mouth, Aurelion’s entire body tensed. His teeth clenched hard enough for me to hear them crack, while veins bulged beneath his skin as the pressure bearing down on him intensified.

Watching my angel suffer, I felt completely helpless.

In theory, I could use my divinity to tear this strange space apart before the punishment went any further. At that point, I did not care about whatever penalty might follow, nor about exposing the fact that I was awake.

But I still could not intervene.

Why did you have to make that idiotic promise?

The thought came almost like a scream, anger overwhelming me for a moment before I forced it back under control.

If I saved Aurelion now, if I interfered and pulled him away from Noah’s angel, it would count as an admission of his defeat. And because of the stupid oath he had sworn after losing to Reest, that alone would kill him.

Aurelion’s roar tore me from my thoughts.

Needles of brilliant light pierced through his arms and legs, passing cleanly through his flesh and emerging from the other side. They did not seem to be made of any tangible substance, instead resembling narrow distortions in space, white, dazzling, and almost transparent.

At the same time, the veil covering the head marked with E disappeared.

A woman’s face was revealed beneath it, long eyelashes casting shadows over strange emerald-yellow eyes. Her lips were darkened to an ugly, unnatural shade, and when they parted in a wide grin, they revealed rows of yellowed teeth beneath.

“DEATH!” the head marked with E screamed, spittle flying from its lips.

“Now, now,” the head marked with P said, calming it. “We must continue the procedure according to the rules…”

“Rules… Are… Absolute…” the head with the letter A rasped.

“You call these rules?!” Aurelion hissed. “You call this justice? Free me and face me if you dare!”

“I believe the accused has yet to learn proper respect for Justice,” P said before turning once again toward J. “Shall we proceed to the second phase?”

“No. Wait,” Aurelion muttered solemnly.

He had finally realized that demanding an honorable duel was getting him nowhere, and instead of continuing to struggle against the invisible force restraining him, he raised his eyes toward the four heads.

“You asked why I did what I did? Fine. I will give you my answer.”

His scarlet eyes grew clearer as he stared at them.

“I shall not justify myself, for I have done nothing that requires justification. I was born to protect my Lord and His kingdom, and I shall gladly die fulfilling that duty,” he said, pride filling his voice. “You ask why I killed those creatures? Because they came as enemies. You do not wait to measure the strength of someone who comes intending to bring suffering and death to those you protect. If they arrive prepared to inflict pain, then they should be prepared to receive it in return.”

The pressure around him remained unchanged, but Aurelion did not lower his gaze.

“I do not regret what I did. My only regret is that, if this is truly how I die, if I am no longer destined to stand before my Liege again, then I wish I had vanquished more of His enemies before the end…”

“I believe we have heard enough,” P said, interrupting Aurelion and somehow forcing my angel’s mouth shut.

Until now, that particular head had spoken with such perfect emptiness that I could barely detect any intonation in its voice, much less emotion. But this time, I could have sworn there was anger beneath those words.

It turned toward J.

“I request we proceed with the next phase.”

If this world truly operated on the principle of equal exchange, as I had come to believe, then overwhelming power should always carry either a price or a limitation. A space capable of completely suppressing Aurelion, preventing him from using his skills and even sealing his glaive, should have some weakness hidden within it.

Something he could exploit.

How can Noah’s angel possibly be this powerful? If even I managed to fight Noah directly, then Aurelion should have at least some chance against his angel. Unless… has he been hiding his true strength all this time? No, that can’t be it…

“Granted,” J rasped, pulling me from my thoughts. “Proceed… With… Exaction.”

Aurelion screamed.

The invisible pressure around him intensified so violently that veins burst beneath his skin and bones twisted inside his limbs. Almost immediately, more needles of brilliant light pierced through his body, far more than during the first punishment.

Before, they had merely caused pain, but now they were tearing him apart.

Golden blood streamed from his wounds, only to vanish the instant it touched the brilliant needles passing through his flesh.

At the same time, the veil covering the head marked with P disappeared.

I froze.

I knew that face. I had seen it before, inside the memory the Goddess had left behind.

It was Noah.

The God of Justice himself.
