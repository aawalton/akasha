The King’s command rang out, filling the air and reverberating like an impossibly large bell had been struck by the hammer of some long-lost divinity.

A charge began to build in the air like the anticipation between lightning strikes.

“Do you feel it?” Saint Baphelus asked, with a dangerous glint in his eye. “Your authority, clashing against mine? Ages of contracts and justification are warring like tectonic plates shifting just underfoot. Earthquakes come and go with less fanfare than the choice that you’ve just made.”

The King waited. He dared not speak, lest he surrender a measure of his power in this moment.

The Lich of the Manse, however, was free to move about his lair. He sauntered forth, palms raised to the sky.

“Do you recall the tale of how the previous regime fell? Of how the Diamond Collective lost control of the Ringed City?” The lich asked. “It all started with a moment like this… an overgrown cabal of oligarchs that thought their right over this dungeon could somehow supplant mine. Did you stop to think what would happen if I were to disobey your command?”

Actual lightning struck the dungeon seal from the skylight, tendrils of actinic mana lashing out and coiling around the two contenders.

Meanwhile, inside a single black cat hair, Prince Arnault was casting a flurry of spells on himself as Gio watched in shocked horror.

“[Mind-wrack]”

“[Torture]”

“[Debilitate]”

Arnault clawed at his own head as a torrent of blood flowed from his nose. Against his will, he began to crawl toward the boundary, one aching spasm at a time.

Wink’s massive claw batted the air in front of the prince as the titanic beast growled a warning. As his massive paw passed by, Gio caught sight of something he hadn’t expected.

A single, perfect, golden thread.

He walked forward.

“Chandrika’s family magic? But… how? And why?” Gio asked.

Arnault was too preoccupied to respond, and Wink wasn’t one for words, so Gio’s question went unanswered.

As it expanded in his sight, Gio followed the golden thread to its two ends. The receiving end of the golden thread was anchored deep inside Prince Arnault, snaking through his head and heart, and coiling around all that he was. Its source, however, was the crown on top of the King’s head. Gio hesitantly reached out to touch it.

As his hand approached, the surface of the glowing silken strand flashed and bubbled, growing miniature offshoots that seemed to taste Gio’s presence. A chorus of justifications sang through the link, breaking through Gio’s mental resistances and erupting into the forefront of his mind’s eye with all the subtlety of a flailing animal. Gio winced at the sensation.

BIRTHRIGHT. CLAIM. ROYALTY. OWNERSHIP. AUTHORITY. KINGDOM. LAW. RESPONSIBILITY.

Part of the strand clearly wished to recognize Gio as something subject to its authority, but those meagre tendrils were burned to their stem by whatever residual chaos bled through his link to his reflection.

That’s odd… Chandrika’s magic doesn’t normally feel so… possessive of me.

A shot of clarity jolted through Gio as he made a striking realization.

“This isn’t Chandrika’s magic!” Gio gasped.

This is Arnault’s… I don’t really know what to call it. ‘Destiny’ seems kind of dramatic, but it might not be far off. It feels almost like this thread is the sole thing tying him to his father... and maybe the Ringed City as a whole? Does… does this mean that Chandrika’s golden strands are just her family’s version of this magic? Some kind of link based on royalty?

“Maybe Chandrika is more of a princess than she thinks she is…” Gio whispered.

“Mao,” Wink growled, bringing Gio’s attention back to the suffering man on the floor.

“Right.” Gio agreed.

He knelt down to face the bleeding man.

“Prince- I mean… Arnault. I think that I might be able to do something to help you… But even if it works, I don’t know what could happen. It could even be particularly bad for you.” Gio said.

Arnault looked up at Gio with determined eyes.

“Anything,” the man spat. “But returning to the palace. I don’t care what happens. Do it.”

Nodding, Gio quickly sketched a well-practised targeting circle on the ground that would allow him to return from the mirror dimension.

“[Mirrorwalker]” Gio invoked.

With a flash of silver mana, Gio appeared in an identical copy of Wink’s domain inside the Mirror dimension, with the only visual indication of his spells’ success being Rio standing there waiting for him to speak.

“I want to help him,” Gio said.

“Okay.” Rio handily agreed.

Gio staggered.

“Wait, really? That’s it?”

“It is simply not my job to be the voice of reason. Besides, I’ve been here watching the whole time. I get it.” Rio answered.

“I would have thought you’d have a harder time accepting this, being that you’re the one who saw him… torture me,” Gio said.

“He’s doing worse to himself, by the looks of it,” Rio said, examining Arnault’s bloody reflection writhing on the floor between them. “Hard to hold a grudge seeing him like this. If I could ask, though… why involve yourself? You don’t owe him anything, and this is certainly not our fight. Do you have any reasons besides just general altruism?”

“Hmm… a few reasons, really. Firstly, I can’t say I’m a fan of how the king is forcing him to return. I don’t know why he doesn’t want to be a prince anymore, but I’d have a hard time believing that he’s just running from his responsibilities… which is stupid anyway, right? Who says that a person born into royalty is ‘responsible’ for being a ruler?”

“Clearly, the spirits.”

“Besides the point. I don’t like that something has been decided for him just because of something he was born into. It’s kind of a mirrored situation from ours, in a way.” Gio said.

“Did you really just say mirrored situa-”

“Shut up. I’m trying to answer your question in good faith.” Gio deadpanned.

“Right, right.” Rio assented.

“I’m a commoner trying to insert myself into a space where mostly only nobility live… and he’s literal royalty who is fighting to continue working as a faceless servant. It kind of speaks to me in a divine irony kind of way.” Gio hummed.

“Well, consider me easily sold. Let’s do it.” Rio said. “Just… let’s try not to spend too much time. This will be the first since… well, you know.”

Gio nodded. The last time they had fused, their combined form had lost control, only stopped from seriously hurting or even killing their friends by the introduction of Trio, who could not currently leave Telchine’s Garden to come help.

“Just a quick trip. There and back in a second.”

“Don’t jinx it…”

Rolling his eyes, Gio pulled on the formless bond that they shared, as Rio did the same.

With a brilliant flare of prismatic colors, Gio became the Aspect of Chaos. Instantly, his flesh crystallised and cracked apart, and pure chaos began leaking from his pores.

“Let’s see if I can make the return trip less… dramatic this time.” Gio hummed to himself.

The last time he had broken into the material world in this form, he had lacked a targeting circle.

“[Mirrorwalker]” Gio invoked.

It wasn’t nothing, but the spell fell short of activating fully. Gio nervously looked toward Arnault, who was quickly losing his fight with the King’s command. A speck of shattered flesh flaked off of Gio’s head to form a full-body clone of himself, as [Fragmented Persona] activated on its own.

“We don’t have time for this. We need to figure this out now.” Rio said.

Gio nodded. He flipped open his spellbook to [Scrying Glass], a spell that his reflections had figured out. They had been getting more practice with it than he had recently. The enchantment flowed freely from his finger as he scrawled it onto a conjured circle of mirror with a bead of mana.

The spell failed, as the chaotic mana leaking from his being created too many cracks in the mirror, disintegrating it into a fine black powder.

“C’mon…” Gio groaned.

A tear in the mirror dimension opened despite Gio’s failure.

“I thought that might have been you. Little piles of shattered, chaos-leaking mirror appearing on my research table isn’t my idea of a funny- wait, are you both fused right now?” Trio incredulously asked.

“It’s for a good reason, I promise.” Gio and Rio replied simultaneously.

“One voice at a time. It’s creepy when you guys talk in sync.” Trio cringed.

“Fair,” Gio said. “Look, I’ll explain later, but I need to get to the real world right now, and Mirrorwalker isn’t working. I need ideas… Do I just do what I did last time and try to get a tiny fragment through? It worked in the dungeon…”

“That was a last resort, and you were nearly lost in the Between from that attempt.” Trio reminded him. “I think that you’re dealing with reality dampening.”

“There’s that term again.” Gio sighed.

“Well, you're currently part reflection. [Mirrorwalker] isn’t designed to take reflections from the mirror dimension. It’s designed for a real person to get there and back. You should know this- you made it.” Trio replied.

“So if the ‘reality dampening’ part is because we’re trying to use a spell to circumvent ‘the rules of reality’, which in this case is ‘Reflections don’t leave mirrors’ or something… how do we get around that?” Rio asked.

“Do you remember what that cloaked professor said to you during the exam yesterday?” Trio asked.

“The solution for most people in your predicament is to add a lot more mana to grease the wheel.” Gio parroted. “I’ve been replaying it in my head ever since.”

“Maybe try that?” Trio postulated.

“So what… we just pump the spell full of mana?” Rio asked. “It’s already our most mana-hungry spell… I don’t even have that much mirror mana to begin with. I’d have to convert some from chaos mana.”

“Who said anything about converting it?” Gio added. “Why wouldn’t chaos work?”

“I like the way you think.” Trio smiled.

Gio would later learn that his mentor was made aware of his entrance to the material plane from the very instant that the spell started working.

A wave of pure, undiluted potential erupted from a strand of cat hair strewn oh-so haphazardly on the floor of the dungeon seal, nestled next to a colony of dust mites, and neighboring a mostly-erased scuff mark from where a wheeled serving table that Baphelus had brought for a private event approximately 30 years prior had made a sharp turn.

“What was that?” Queen Sarissa asked.

Baphelus waved her off, curiously regarding a particular spot on the floor. “Hmm? Oh, probably just one of my students doing something ill-advised.”

The King just silently stood, awaiting the result of his command.

Wink hissed a warning at Gio, a rasping roar due to his enlarged size. The great cat coiled around the space with renewed vigor, as black fur all but blotted out the light beyond.

“Sorry!” Gio sheepishly apologized. “I uh- I must have used too much juice.”

The Aspect of Chaos giggled. His eyes swirled with color as he giddily toyed with the sparks arcing across his fingers.

“It worked, though! I’m here- oh! Right! I have a job to do!”

Gio skipped over to the suffering prince.

“Whatever you’re going to do… do it, please.” Arnault pleaded. “I’m losing the fight. He’s taking me over.”

“Ooookay!” Gio replied in a singsong voice. Arnault - just barely - still had the wherewithal to look at Gio like he was crazy.

And then, Gio grasped the thread, and his head flew back involuntarily as the lightning coursed through him. The fibers of the translucent cord began to unwind as a flow of something that felt like a cross between honey and molten glass flowed into his soul, wriggling its way into his spiritual capillaries and sizzling against the chaos that already occupied that space.

“HAHAhaHaHAA- That feels sooooo weird!” Gio yelled. Golden light dripped from his fingers, staining all that it touched in gilded chrome like concentrated ink.

Simultaneously, Arnault’s hair began to change colors, his golden locks dimming to a lustrous silver.

Outside of Wink’s imperceptible domain, King Arawn flinched back, gasping as if he’d been struck in the chest. Sarissa flew to her husband, supporting him with a concerned expression.

“What’s wrong?” She asked.

“I- I can’t feel him,” Arawn replied.

Baphelus laughed.

“What have you done? What have you done with my son?!” King Arawn demanded.

The lich kept laughing. It was a deep, hysterical belly laugh, like he’d just heard the funniest joke.

“ANSWER ME!” The king ordered.

Baphelus blinked away genuine tears, still laughing. “Me? Nothing. I’ve not lifted a finger at all. I swear before the spirits that I have had no part to play in keeping any of your children from you on this day. May the spirits challenge me if I dare speak any falsehoods or mistruths.”

“I see… then… that’s that. Come, husband. Let us depart this accursed place with some measure of dignity.” Sarissa said. She placed a gentle hand on her husband’s side, helping him walk.

“I…” King Arawn meekly huffed. Using so much of his authority had clearly drained him.

“Shhh…” Sarissa hummed, placing a hand on his cheek. She turned to face the lich once more.

“We will concede,” Sarissa proclaimed. “But…”

Baphelus raised an eyebrow.

“I would ask you one final thing.” Queen Sarissa asserted. “Not as a Queen… but as a mother, my child… he is alive? That… flash, whatever it was, was not his final breath?”

The Lich of the Manse paused.

“Words are… tricky things.” The lich said.

The Queen’s features hardened. She turned, facing the exit.

“But… it is always a good day when the forces of the Crystal Ring Academy suffer no casualties,” Baphelus remarked. “Today is one such day.”

The queen paused mid-stride. She didn’t turn, but she tilted her head to the side, nodding.

“An auspicious day, indeed. Be well, Saint Baphelus.” She said, resuming her march toward the door.

“You can come out now,” Baphelus said to the air.

The lich now looked more composed, sitting at his table and with a healthier flush to his skin. He was wearing the fashionable clothes that he was usually seen in instead of the dramatic black cloak that he had conjured, and his lantern sat on his tea table, missing any sign of the phantasmagorical inferno that it had just been wielding. For all intents and purposes, the menacing Lich of the Manse now appeared to be a suave older gentleman sipping tea, as he preferred to be perceived.

At Baphelus’s urging, a cat, a former prince, and a sparkling madman emerged from the shadows.

“Mrow.” Wink purred, now back in his diminutive form. His massive bushy tail twirled through the air as he nuzzled against Saint Baphelus’s chin.

“Being cute now doesn’t change the fact that you have been a very naughty boy.” Baphelus chided. “You’ve allowed a student to see a side of me that I would rather not have revealed.”

“I thought you were badass!” Gio slurred. “You were all- whaa! You shall not disrespect me! And the king was all ‘Give me my son!’ I felt like I was watching a play!”

The aspect of Chaos mimed cool action poses, punctuating his outbursts.

“Ahh… Gio. You’re as subtle as a battering ram, you know that?” Baphelus smiled.

“Thanks. Do you really mean it?” Gio proudly grinned. A trickle of golden mana was still splattered all over him and was now forming odd concentric circles over his skin.

“De-fuse now,” Baphelus ordered with a pleased nod.

“Okaaaaay.”

A crack formed from the top of his head, running all the way down to his left leg as two copies of Giorgio deGloria flew apart from each other.

Rio was near-instantly sucked off the side of the platform toward the massive, crystalline, and reflective walls of Baphelus’s chamber.

“AAAAaaaa-”

The aspect was shunted back into his home dimension, falling into the abyss below.

Gio stood up, grasping his head with a golden hand. “Ugh. Chaos hangover. Where’s Rio?”

“He’ll be fine.” Baphelus waved him off, a massive smile creeping across his face. “The important part is that congratulations are to be had! You’re a father!”

“WHAT?!” Gio exclaimed.

“Ugh…” Arnault sighed.
