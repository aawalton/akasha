Dax, wearing thick, nondescript armor and sitting atop a now almost ubiquitous Pegasus, watched the final legs of the army’s march. ‘Watch’ was perhaps inadequate to describe all that she was doing in her head. Absorb, collate, process, tweak, adjust, modify, distribute, command, synchronize.

These days, she felt like the nexus of a hivemind. There was Samantha, of course, who provided the raw stuff for it to even exist, but she was like an ‘over-brain,’ not the functional, logical operations center. She was the queen, and Dax was her executor, often knowing what she needed better than her. Samantha was one of the handful of people she still got into arguments with.

Linnore was another, of course — her perpetual, blessedly trustworthy second in command, who could handle the delegation of anything and everything she needed. In this battle, she handled about a quarter of the army on the left flank, while Carlisle led the right. Soon, all her carefully crafted orderly plans would turn to chaos and require more management than she could solely keep up with. They would try to do as they would. They-

‘She,’ damn it! Me! I’m Dax. Daxerris Stoneboro.

It was generally not easy remembering that, between the constant existence of the gestalt. Without the help of the one she argued with the absolute most, she’d be lost already.

Canmore was not nearby — an issue of argument not long ago, but Dax insisted the enemy would believe she’d be where he was, and they’d deliberately let them believe it to attack a carefully cultivated Faerie Dragon actor when they inevitably attempted an assassination.

The argument had happened half a dozen times, because Canmore needed to know early. At first, he’d tried offering clever ideas, like another double of him to go along… but she’d refused, citing that his skills were impossible to emulate.

Progressively, his arguments were just roundabout pouting about the injustice. She loved him for it, but she’d channeled the cold generals within her and refused again and again to budge. She knew this was the play. The enemy was no longer going to be inclined to try offing Samantha. This enemy knew the weakest link for the most serious damage dealt, and that was the mastermind of the general army, Dax.

It’s your own fault, oh great City of Heavenly Order. You exiled me all those years ago because I wasn’t corrupt enough for ya. If you hadn’t, who knows? Perhaps I’d be on the other side of this.

Another memory surfaced, of leaving a city in disgrace because of a botched order. She’d become a deep-sea fisher, and by an odd series of situations, a sea captain embroiled in war, and then on and on down the typical path to a war general.

A mere voice no longer; foreign memories blurring with hers, less and less of the seams stitched between being visible with each passing day. Dax didn’t even bother rejecting them anymore, wearily accepting it as part of her. Drinking no longer helped, and she needed sobriety.

I won’t have to resist much longer, anyway. Do you lot see the truth, too? I’m too keenly aware of the raw, exposed bone beneath the surface of things not to.

A slurry of acknowledgements came from the whispers in her head. One finally said, The truth is apocalypse. It always is. We always end, we always come again.

Fight. | Yes. | Agreed. | Kill. | Slaughter. | Efficiently make war. | Manipulate Fate. | The tumble of dice. “Daxerris Stoneboro.”

It took Dax a moment to realize it was an outside voice, even as it was glaringly different. It was from Ash, as a specific, personal communication. The concept of ‘sister’ and ‘individual communication’ helped her crawl out of the well she was falling into. Collecting herself quickly, Dax replied, “Yes?”

“Don’t poke that bear.”

Blinking a few times behind the armored visor, Dax swallowed hard. “Right.” It was something Dax had told her sister to say when she sensed her identity slipping. There was perhaps no one else in the world who had the right combination of affinities with Dax to do so. Ash was a psychic, had experience with mindsharing, and was an intimate relative.

“No time for more. Focus on the battle. Good luck.”

“You too.”

As prescribed, Dax focused on the battle, from dozens of shifting angles, processing it like a mixed-up panoply. Layers overlapping each other, making alien patterns she read like code, from uncanny, inhuman instincts.

She tried to focus on the images, instead.

Enemy pot-shot ranged fire was doing minimal damage to their ranks, and any dealt was quickly healed. A bit of resources were drained, but they had plenty more where that came from.

Just before the two groups clashed, Samantha unleashed her curse, [The Unseen Animus], on the entire enemy army, debuffing the vast majority, which had no chance to resist her, and showed zero signs of even noticing. It wouldn’t matter if they did, as it was not easy to remove.

Dax, with her preternatural sense of the tides of battle and the Fate related to them, could sense not only the curse itself propagate far and wide, but also the significant sway it made in things. Not only was it a generic EL penalty, but it also made any sort of luck-based ability give a goose egg for a bonus, too. That would hurt a lot of their mid-level types, particularly marksmen, who often tried to stack those things in.

We’ll take it. And now it begins.

The true start of the battle was as simple as it was brutal: two gigantic armies were crashing together, vanguards full of heavily-armored foot-knights charging each other, under the beat of the drums and beneath the aggregate patterns of hundreds of raised banners of every land in the civilized and semi-civilized world. They were not the elites, but they were a step down. The hardcore veterans, the immovable-from-positions, the True Believers.

All were buffed beyond anything done before. From the looks of things, the two sides seemed even on that front.

And the elites have yet to enter the picture.

The beginning triggered thousands of secondary responses reliant on it on both sides. Spells, ranged fire, new short-duration buffs, and so on. Dax almost breathed a sigh of relief when she realized their ‘surprise’ arrays of buffs eclipsed the other side and showed they had the advantage.

She held in a sigh when she saw the results of the enemy having vastly better range due to the limitations of approaching the capital: their own magical effect potency was limited to the front lines, exposing magi closer to the danger. The enemy used that from further away, taking advantage of their lack of similar restriction to strike at the more exposed magi.

The Counter Crew nonetheless blocked it all and almost eliminated casualties. They were more buttressed than ever thanks to Glae’s new elite contributions — and they were even promised relief later, when Samantha got to the point she’d summon those insane new support beings.

If there was one resource the alliance was low on, though, it was mana. They couldn’t waste it like the other side. Rotation was important, pulling magi back to regenerate their energy.

Dax made adjustments, pulling back around a third of the arcane users and moving in more clerics and Servitors, Samanthian and otherwise, to fill the gap; one of a thousand shifts she’d make over the duration, but aiming to conserve what was more precious. Faith Energy was something they had in abundance.

She made her next move to seize the advantage in the stalemate: gave the order for the flyers to sweep in.

Blessed Commander, Surghassa Breaking Heart, swept his greataxe through the ranks with contemptuousness, as enemy weapons bounced off of his enchanted armor. Armor that had taken the likes of drake jaws, giant’s clubs, and nega troll claws, armor that had been forged and reforged with each spine-tingling battle for the glory of Den Wa Koharra, the Zedwar of The Forge, invested with new spirit each time and hardened the same as its wearer.

Not long ago, he’d wondered, in fascination, what exotic equivalency he’d find among the accursed sagemen as their final showing before the end of all things, to become his greatest challenge ever faced.

Instead, he’d found nothing but weaklings, little more relevant than the minnows he’d swallowed up as a toddler. Ignorant flesh simply waiting around to die before comparative titans.

To his left and right, his mighty brother minotaurs, his supreme champions, were doing much the same as he, as the lines of the enemy were torn into ribbons. Meanwhile, the enemies’ pathetic arcane witchery pulsed and tickled the flesh of all but their weakest, coming up against the buff Surghassa provided to the vanguard and completely floundering.

The spellcraft did not meet the standards of a challenge, just as the foes in front of them were unworthy and pliant. It was pointless and infuriating to simply have their time wasted like this.

They are not giving their best here, but rather stacking it on the other side. How dare they insult us, how dare they not meet our might! We will punish them for this!

He let out a war cry, a bellow of offense and rage taken up by his fellows. He did not need to bandy words to communicate his feelings or intentions. Now was a time to act like one’s horns. To be like that animal not left behind, but merely put aside for a moment.

His call was answered by a thousand and magnified, and they all collectively were fueled into the stampede of the herd. A frightening thing, when the whole herd was composed of bulls.

They ripped through the ranks in front of them with renewed vigor, axes, hammers, bladed spears, and mauls swinging with greater and greater bloodlust, tearing the enemies limb from limb. Surghassa and his immediate companions broke through the harder vanguard and found even weaker foes being driven before them.

Pathetic.

If they will not take Minotaurkind seriously at their walls, we will make our way through them! Glory will follow us to the end!
