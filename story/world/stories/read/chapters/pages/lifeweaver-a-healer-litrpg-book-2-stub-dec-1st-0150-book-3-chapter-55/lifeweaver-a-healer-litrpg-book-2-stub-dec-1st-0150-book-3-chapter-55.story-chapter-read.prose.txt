With Alan, Luke had been able to sense the intrusion of skills trying to manipulate his mind. Not so with Lara's. He hadn't noticed a damn thing. It wasn't just anger bubbling up inside him, Luke realized as he got the hell out of there. It was a good helping of fear as well. After all that had happened, all the strength and power accumulated, helplessness was not something he'd expected to experience again. Not at the hands of another Integrated. Not on that level.

"Dammit," he muttered, exiting the building. The cool air of late afternoon made for a somewhat soothing alternative to the stifling ambiance of the abandoned building, and he stood there for a moment, gathering himself. Several more questions about Lara's abilities still nagged at him, but even with the help she'd provided, Luke found he couldn't trust her. The inherent deception in her abilities meant he couldn't risk getting near her, not without some way of counteracting them.

Luke: "Lara. Your portals. When you get monsters coming out of them, where do those come from?"

The way to get answers without getting near her wasn't a tough nut to crack, as it turned out.

Lara: "You'll forgive my mistake if I give you more information?"

Sometimes you have to lie. Yes, Luke realized the hypocrisy in that, but this was different.

Luke: "Yes."

Lara: "There is a sense telling me locations where I might find different types of monsters. Connecting a portal to that sense provides a link to that location. It's not straightforward, so please excuse the lackluster explanation."

Luke: "That's fine. Thanks. You made The Gauntlet back in Chicago."

Lara: "I did."

Luke: "So you have abilities with physical space as well."

Lara: "Like I explained, once I have melded an area with the system attributes you refer to as a dungeon, then I am able to change it to my liking."

Luke: "But The Gauntlet was huge."

Lara: "It took a long time to craft. Making the space permanent is time-consuming, and I would not have been able to have monsters spawning or anything like that."

Not wanting Lara to come out and join him, Luke set off running again, wanting to keep practicing touching the weave while not in full meditation mode.

Luke: "Thanks. One last question. Can you make portals between different places you’ve dungeonized?"

Lara: "Yes, but please stop calling it that. Why do you ask?"

Luke: "Just curious. Do you know of any other Architects?"

Lara: "I don't. ISG hasn't been able to find any others, but new Integrated can't pick the class. We checked that."

Luke: "Sounds like a powerful class. Not surprised there are no free spots. Thanks. Talk to you later."

Lara: "Luke. About the danger I sensed."

Luke: "I am the danger."

Lara: "Seriously, Luke."

Luke: "You don't need to concern yourself with me. I can heal almost anything."

Lara: "It's not you I'm concerned about."

That last message, Luke didn't reply to, and she didn't write again. By himself, Luke zoned off as he ran, extending threads of mana from his feet to push off and increase his speed while training for more reach. As always, he needed more reach. Luke ran. And ran. Working himself into the state needed to see the weave proved difficult, since he couldn't stop thinking about the Architect class and its possibilities and dangers, but after about an hour of trying, he managed it.

The many threads making up Luke's system appeared fragile and indistinct, like the other times when he wasn't either half-dead or in a state of deep, concentrated meditation. By sheer numbers, they appeared infinite, and he wouldn't be surprised if that was the case, or close to it. People were represented, every single one on Earth, Luke was sure, even if he couldn't pick out specific individuals if he didn't have an echo to go by. That, however, wasn't all. Things had threads as well, and he thought he sensed emotion in others, and a connection between things, people, emotions, and many other things. It was enough to make his head spin. Pulling back, he was about to call forward Hannah's thread while turning into a residential street with three-story red brick buildings, when he spotted the telltale shine of a portal near what he thought might be a school.

There were just too many portals for all of them to be dealt with, hence the occasional roaming monster, but this one had people outside of it. People who, of course, were crying out in pain. Sighing, he let his concentration waver. His sense of the weave disappeared. Without it before him, the world seemed a little paler.

Approaching the small group of four, Weaver's Perception provided all the information needed. Their injuries needed attention, or they'd bleed out right there on what, judging by the metal hoops in the air, was the schoolyard basketball court. Rounding the fence to get in, he wondered how many Integrated had bled out on the streets of New York since they didn't have a healer just happen to run past. A lot, he guessed, but there was no way of knowing.

"Hey," he said as he got closer. "What's going on?"

Four people. Three women, one man. The man was a tank, judging by his sturdy appearance and the even sturdier knight plate armor covering him from head to toe. He would be dead within moments without intervention, since he'd been pierced straight through the heart. Well, the side of the heart. The women were injured as well, two of them with vital wounds, but they'd hold for a couple of minutes, at least.

The one closest to Luke was a frail-looking girl with hair so blond it bordered on white, wearing a thin dress that glittered in the waning light. She was on her knees in front of the knight, but when she turned, he saw that she might not just look like a fairy. She might even be one. A pair of butterfly-like, half-transparent wings jutted out from her back. "Help!" she cried, trying to raise an arm that looked as if it had been crushed.

Her two other female friends were unconscious, both bleeding from similar wounds as the man, but neither had been hit through the heart. Even so, Luke used Weaver's Mercy to keep them stable while he worked to repair the man's heart.

"Thank you, thank you, thank you," the fairy said in a high, panicked voice, rising to her feet.

"A monster in there did this?" he asked, nodding at the portal while he worked, stitching the heart back together with Needle of Life. He could have used healing mana for it, but Luke needed the practice. There would be times ahead where he didn't have the luxury of full mana stores. Best to get back into the mindset of mana being a precious commodity before it was too late. This was no time to get sloppy.

"They were going in there," she said, her voice sounding out of breath, almost like she was getting excited.

"Huh," Luke asked, finishing up when searing pain struck him from behind. Something sharp punched into his back and penetrated all the way to his heart in a flash. If not for Guardian Weave, it would have gone through. "Guh," Luke grunted, turning as another puncturing stab thrust through, this one at a downward angle that sliced through a spleen and his stomach. Ouch.

He didn't allow her a third strike.

The Weaver's Moment stopped time, and before anything else, Luke healed himself. This time, he felt using healing mana to be warranted. The pool in his Meta-Heart raced out and did its job. Pinpoint wounds like the ones he'd just received didn't require much mana to heal, in the grand scheme of things, but Luke found his Meta-Heart depleted afterward. He'd forgotten to fill it back up after his last round of injuries. Not good.

It wouldn't matter in this fight, he thought, but it was a careless mistake, and those were unacceptable. They were the opposite of what he was going for, damn it. Unable to turn during the time freeze, Luke still got a good sense of what was happening behind him through Weaver's Perception. The woman's arm hadn't been crushed before. She'd changed it to look the part. Now, both her arms looked more like spears with mana channels branching throughout, concentrating to the tips, which he thought might be made of bone. Nasty stuff.

Reaching out to her, he considered what to do. Not because he thought there might be options to consider, but because Luke wanted to at least think about it for a second before taking the life of another Integrated. Making a habit of killing without consideration would lead to a dark place. But there was nothing to it. Someone like this could not be allowed to walk or fly around and kill others.

To make sure she never got the chance again, he reached out with a thread of mana and moved along her mana channels until he reached her Meta-Heart. He was about to cut the main mana channel when a flash of glittering light blinded his senses and destroyed the thread.

The fairy stumbled back as Luke turned, unsure what had just happened. Not only had she rebuffed his thread, but The Healer's Moment had also ended prematurely.

Getting to his feet, he weavestepped toward her and reached forward with a thread of mana, connecting himself to her again. The woman's eyes widened in shock before her entire body blurred, wings beating. Before he could stop her, she took to the air. Luke pushed off in a powerful jump aided by threads of mana, but she was too fast. Another blur, and she was gone.

"Dammit," Luke grumbled after dropping hard back down to the asphalt of the basketball court. He was saying that word a lot these days.

Looking into the air, he saw no trace of the fairy. The two injured women groaned. Right, he wasn't done healing yet. While he patched them up, the man came to his senses. Luke was just about to ask what the hell had happened when the knight pointed at the portal and wheezed. "Help them."
