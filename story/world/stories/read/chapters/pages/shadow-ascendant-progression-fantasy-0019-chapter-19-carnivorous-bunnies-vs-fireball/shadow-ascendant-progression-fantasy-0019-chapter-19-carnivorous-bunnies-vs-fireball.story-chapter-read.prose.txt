My eyes turned to the gate, torches lined its wall, similar to the other gates. The plan we made was simple. Mabel would attract the monsters; I’d be the attacker. Nothing too complicated, it was a plan even a child could follow, and certainly something we could do.

"Yeah…" I nodded.

Mabel turned around, stepping into the gate. This time I didn’t waste any time doubting myself, and walked in, with her by my side. The fear of the deeper floor had already begun fading.

"Mabel," I spoke up as we walked down the stairs.

"Yes?"

"What’s the deepest you’ve descended?"

It was a question I wanted to know from the start. The team she was a part of, with Princess Emily and Marcus, was most definitely the strongest in our class. Which made me curious to know how far they are from everyone else. From me?

"Thirteenth floor," Mabel answered, "we had to team up with Seraphina and her minions to go there. Not a pleasant experience; she was bickering with Marcus all the way." She paused, skipping a step, "not something I’d recommend."

"Is it so…"

They have reached the 13th floor, huh? That was certainly deeper than I’d expected.

"Wait, that reminds me, you said Marcus refused to go deeper than the eighth floor without Emily, right? Why’s that so?"

"It’s harder to navigate, and he doesn’t really trust my navigation… Also, the ninth floor is another labyrinth floor."

"Is Emily good with navigation?"

"Yeah, she’s even better than I am."

My eyes narrowed, "Yeah," I murmured, the thing about her being better than Mabel in navigation left the whole claim wide open. I mean, navigation was certainly not Mabel’s strong suit, considering all the coin flipping she did in its name.

"We are almost there." Mabel murmured.

I lifted my eyes slightly from the floor. The gate was in view, just a few more steps.

We entered the fifth floor through the gate. The familiar popping sound hit my ear, though it didn’t bother me.

My mouth hung open. I was amazed for the second time that day—something I hadn’t expected. The floor itself was aglow, not the ceiling or the walls, but the freaking floor! with the lush grass that shimmered bluish-green, illuminating the ambience.

I looked around, the grass field extended in a circular field, with a large spherical stone dome enclosing it, leaving only a small pathway at the centre, to go deeper into the floor.

The fifth floor was like a large grass field, where one would graze their cattle. And it was certainly pleasing to look at, and that’s when I was yanked out of my daydream by Mabel.

"Oi… bunnies hide in the grass, I suggest you stop gawking as if you have never seen it before;" she said.

"I have never seen it before." I yelled back in a whisper-like voice. Attracting bunnies before the plan begins was certainly not something I wanted.

Ignoring my reaction, Mabel turned to look at the pillar, "well then, you wait inside, keep an eye behind and one in front," she suggested.

"I’m not a monster," I wanted to shout, but understanding what she meant, and knowing the grassy field wasn’t as forgiving as the labyrinth above, I just nodded. Then, I stepped back into the gate, eyes peeled for any movement and ears straining for sounds from the stairs. I stood at attention, wand at the ready.

The silence stretched, broken only by the faint rustle of the grass, and the water dripping from the ceiling above. I kept waiting for Mabel, my eyes occasionally turning to the corridor, until she entered.

"That was, haha," someone’s voice broke the silence.

I wasn’t too sure whether it was a single person’s voice or more, but with the slow, multiple footsteps clicking in my ear, I believed there was more than one person descending the stair.

I glanced again at the corridor. There was no sign of Mabel, and so I stepped out of the gate, my boots crushing the glowing grass as I moved.

I stood at the side of the gate, with half my back exposed. I would have preferred to step aside rather than block their path, but startling them was hardly a clever idea.

"Did you hear someone’s footsteps?" The voice was clearer now.

It was familiar.

"Not too sure, maybe it’s a goblin?"

"Hey," I spoke up, looking behind. The group of adventurers walking with the lantern were people I recognised; they were Alfie the elf, Nozel Sereista, and Maya Cevires.

Fuck! I yelled inwardly. It just had to be him. I bit my lip, grimacing as I turned my face away.

"Ohh… Is that you, Dirtbag?" Alfie spoke up as the group stepped out of the gate.

Giving a slightly awkward smile, I nodded, "fancy seeing you here, Alfie," I said, giving an awkward chuckle.

A crooked smile plastered his face, as if he were happy to see me here. Was he going to warn me again? I wondered.

"What are you doing here? Isn’t it unsafe for someone like you? I mean, you find slimes on floor two, right?"

I fought the urge to narrow my eyes and give a sarcastic reply; the odds were against me.

"I…" what should I answer!?

"Haah…" Maya gave out an exaggerated sigh. "Is there really nothing better to do than chat with him? We came here to hunt monsters, right, or are you still pissed about him walking in with Noelle? That’s childish Alfie, she’d probably hate you if she found out."

Alfie’s green eyes widened with visible fear. He turned to Maya with both his hands raised defensively; "I was just chatting with him, is all. I wasn’t threatening him or anything." He lied!

"Isn’t that right, Arnos?"

Woah, so he can call me by my name? "Yeah… he was just a-s-king," I murmured, for some reasons emphasizing the asking part.

"He was, huh…" Maya repeated, certainly not amused. A small smile spread on her face as she was about to speak, but a scream cut her off.

"ARNOS!!!" someone yelled.

My eyes turned to the corridor. It was Mabel dashing through, with a cloud of dust behind her.

The plan had succeeded, or at least it felt like that.

"What’s that chasing her!" Maya exclaimed, her eyes wide.

And that’s when I realised how fucked up our plan was.

According to the plan, I was supposed to cast a massive fireball from the gate… but with Alfie and his group’s presence; I had lost that privilege.

"Fuck!" I groaned, and not wasting any time dashed forward with fire propelling me forward.

Mabel’s eyes widened slightly; she seemed to have glimpsed Alfie and others. With another Quick-step at my highest firepower, I closed the gap between me and her. The bunnies were approaching, and Mabel stood behind me.

I pointed my wand at the opening. "Fireball!" I yelled, pouring every drop of mana into a single, blazing orb. The globe of fire hissed to life at my wand’s tip, hotter than I had expected. Heat seared my palm—but that was good. It should be enough.

The bunnies froze, eyes wide, staring at the Fireball as if deciding whether to fight or flee. But it wasn’t a choice they were ever meant to have.

The ball of fire surged forward, its massive body tearing through stone and grass as it moved. It filled the entire corridor—I bit my lip, it should work, it must… my head throbbed.

My breathing grew uneven, even standing hurt. My mana was drained, just like before, when I fought the Minotaur

I kept my eyes wide open, my ears pricked, the fireball had moved far enough… no scream came out, did my spell fail? I gulped.

The Fireball, having consumed all the mana, dissipated in the middle of the corridor. The smoke still clogged it, and I coughed in my sleeve as my vision began blurry.

I felt someone’s hand on the back of my shirt, "Good work," that person whispered. My eyes turned to the voice. I couldn’t see though; it was Mabel, most certainly.

And then the voice of Celoris band rang in my mind.

[You have gained 36 credits for slaying eighteen Red-eyed Bunnies]

[Your total credits are now - 138/150]

And even though I was tired enough that all the muscles in my body refused to work, my lips curled in a smile as everything around me grew darker.
