After the first burst of lightning exploded, it was followed by three others. Mom just barely managed to pull Callie away to a safe distance, the power of the outburst making their hair stand on end.

As she was dragged away, Callie saw that there were four creatures in total. Her breath hitched as she admired their appearance. The fox-like creatures had a distinctly light underbelly and incredibly fluffy, large tails, much larger than those of a normal fox. Their fur was standing on end, and there were glowing streaks patterned on their tails and back, which seemed to light up occasionally.

Callie really wanted to draw these beautiful creatures. In essence they weren't that much different from foxes, but at the same time very different. They were clearly made to control the lightning magic, and also much smaller than a regular fox.

Smaller? No, they aren't just smaller. These are babies.

The lightning seemed to be crackling erratically, and their crude manner of speech suggested that these foxes weren't fully grown yet. Where were their parents?

Danger! Danger!

"We mean you no harm!" Callie called out to the foxes, but they didn't listen to her words at all.

Mom withdrew her sword, readying to attack them.

"You can't!" Callie shrieked.

"Stay back, Callie! These creatures are dangerous," Mom said, her eyes locked onto the little kits, cold and merciless.

Callie wanted to grab Mom to stop her advance, but Callen grabbed her and pulled her back. "You mustn't approach them."

"Mom, don't hurt them!" Callie called out. "They are only scared."

Mom glared at them. "A scared monster is more dangerous. This is why Mister Blackthorn was against your going. You are far too reckless."

Despite her harsh words, Mom's jaw clenched. Callie could see that she was hesitating. Perhaps, she could make her listen. But then one of the foxes released another lightning charge. Mom clutched her sword firmly and stepped closer to the foxes. Just as she was about to jump forward, the foxes released more charges of lightning, forcing her to back away.

Callie's mind was reeling. What could she do? She had to stop her Mom from hurting the kits. But they were frightened and lashing out at everyone who approached them. Callie reached for the magic within her, desperately trying to convey to foxes that they should not be scared, that they should remain calm.

But with her emotions roiling within her like a storm, the magic wasn't inclined to obey. It almost felt like it would burst out forcibly. That was the last thing they needed right now, so Callie had no choice but to suppress her magic.

"Stay back!" A fierce female voice called. A moment later, an arrow lodged itself in the ground between Mom and the kits.

Mom jumped back, extending an arm protectively in front of Callie and Callen while Silas tried to keep Gale calm and slowly retreat from the frightened fox kits. Their fur crackled as a new group of people arrived, four in total.

They were dressed in leather armor, their cloaks worn and boots muddy. In what felt like the blink of an eye, they had positioned themselves between Mom and the foxes.

"Back away!" A woman, clearly their leader, called firmly.

Callie felt a cold shiver run down her spine when she saw the woman's face. Her eyes were as cold as steel, a scar that was clearly the shape of a claw adorning her chin. Her black hair was cut short with a single grey lock running down her left side. The woman was unusually tall, about the same height as Dad.

Mom, to Callie's surprise, instantly obeyed, forcing Callie and Callen even further back from the scene. Callie tried to push her way forward, horrified by the crossbow the woman was holding in her hands.

"Don't hurt them!" Callie cried out, but Mom would not let go any further.

"Neutralize them!" The woman barked out, and the large man reacted instantly.

He pulled out some kind of flask from the belt on his hip, which seemed to be filled with such vials, and threw them at the foxes. The glass shattered, a cloud of smoke exploding from it.

"Do not breathe that in under any circumstances," the woman warned, and Mom pulled Callie even further back.

"Don't hurt them!" Callie repeated desperately, tears welling in their eyes. As soon as the smoke cloud cleared, she could see the crackling in the kit's fur subside. They staggered on their feet, clearly disoriented from whatever it was in those smoke clouds.

The group of strangers ignored Callie's words completely, instead running at the fox kits with practised precision. They pulled a black cloth from the belt and threw it onto the confused kits.

The woman in particular appeared brutal as she pressed the little creature into the ground with her gloved hand and tied the black cloth around its tail, restraining it. The little ones whimpered and cried as, in less than a few minutes, all of their tails were wrapped in black cloth, and the strangers held them firmly by the scruff.

Callie watched with horror, fearing what they would do to those helpless creatures. The tall woman let out a shrill whistle. Her whistle was answered with a loud neigh, and before long, four horses came running in their direction. Two of them were pulling a wagon which was similar to Silas's wagon, except larger, while two were running free.

Once the horses with the wagon came to a stop, the strangers carried the foxes into them.

"Wait, what are you going to do to them?" Callie called, trying to follow them.

Mom held her back firmly. "Don't. Rangers know what they are doing."

The tall woman stopped, the little fox still struggling in her hands, yipping miserably. Her cold, steely gaze met Callie's, and the girl just inadvertently flinched. She wanted to open her mouth to protest, do something, anything to get the little ones to safety. But she couldn't.

What could she say to make this cold, merciless woman listen? She was clearly like Mom, a hunter, not even more than that. Her every movement was precise, practised. If Callie spoke out against her, she could likely end up on the receiving end of that horrifying crossbow, which was strapped to her back.

Callie bit down her lip in frustration, but she couldn't withhold her tears. She and the ranger woman stared at each other for a moment until she turned away and headed for the wagon.

The other rangers had already placed the foxes into the cages, their leader being the last one. The foxes trembled and hissed, but they were unable to produce any lightning anymore. What had these people done? Was it the cloth that suppressed their magic or that weird smoke cloud? Callie had no clue.

She felt so utterly useless. Had she been faster, had she been able to use her magic properly, perhaps she could have calmed the fox kits down and convinced them to return to the safety of the forest. But since she couldn't control her magic at all, Callie could only helplessly watch.

"Thank you very much for your help," Mom said, bowing politely in front of the tall ranger woman.

"You were lucky. We were heading to Greyfen to investigate a case of a glauxis appearing nearby," the ranger said.

"The glauxis left!" Callie yelled, surprised by how loud her voice had come out. But she didn't want these horrible people to follow the poor glauxis, when he had only just gotten his egg back.

"Callie, mind your manners!" Mom said firmly, then turned back to the ranger woman. "My apologies. This child is easily excitable. The glauxis was indeed near the village, but it has since left. We believe it is unlikely to return."

The ranger woman nodded. "We'll confirm that later, but assuming it is not an emergency, we're going to deal with these thunderfoxes first."

Callie glowered at the woman. She wanted to scream at her to release the little kits, but didn't dare. Instead, she watched the rangers get on their horses and gallop away from the same direction they came from.

"To think that we would actually run into rangers. We got lucky! I don't remember the last time they appeared in these parts," Silas noted, watching the group leave.

"We were very lucky indeed," Mom said, then glared at Callie. "What were you thinking? Approaching monsters so carelessly? You could have been seriously hurt!"

"But they were merely scared…" Callie tried to defend herself.

"I've already told this, but a scared monster is the most dangerous. Mister Blackthorn was right. It is too early for your travel. We were extremely lucky that the rangers were nearby. Extremely!" Mom repeated for emphasis.

"More like extremely unlucky…" Callie grumbled, thinking of the poor foxes. Would they be skinned and turned into fur coats for dragonbloods? Their meat sold on market stalls? The thought made her stomach churn.

"Callie!" Mom chastised.

"There, there. I believe there's no need to argue. It's only normal that a child wouldn't want to see cute creatures get hurt. After all, these monsters don't look much different from regular foxes, do they?" Silas said, trying to end the argument.

"My apologies, Silas. My daughter's recklessness nearly got us hurt," Mom apologized.

"Please, don't worry about it. Nobody got hurt in the end, and that's all that matters, isn't it?" Silas said, smiling at Callie.

She just barely restrained herself from saying "everyone but the foxes". But it was clear that these people didn't share her opinion at all. Callie would have hoped that Mom would understand arcane beasts a bit better after what happened with the glauxis, but she had gone back to calling them monsters.

"Come, Callie. Let's continue on our way," Callen said consingly, then whispered in her ear. "I'm sorry. There's just nothing we could have done against the rangers."

Callie looked at the road ahead of them. The rangers were nothing more than tiny specs in the distance. "I thought rangers never came around these parts."

"Yeah, I thought so too," Callen nodded.

"Oh, they do come," Silas said, motioning at the wagon. Callie and Callen both got in the back, followed by Mom, who still looked angry at Callie. "But there's a distinct lack of rangers. Unlike hunters, only the very best people can become rangers. Those who can deal with problems relating to monsters with ease. It's what they've been trained for."

"But since they are such an elite unit, they are very much limited in numbers. They try to mitigate this by travelling from region to region and solving the issues that pop up along the way, following rumors of the most dangerous creatures. But as you can imagine, this little part of the woods is far down their priority list. If it weren't for the glauxis that was recently seen in Greyfen, they likely wouldn't have come here at all."

"How did they find out about that?" Callie muttered.

"Mom, did you send a message to the Rangers?" Callen asked.

"I did," she confirmed. "When the glauxis appeared, I figured it was worth messaging them. Given how far away we are from their headquarters, I had little hope they would arrive on time. I feared we would have to call for dragonbloods instead."

"Good thing it was resolved by our lovely little Callie here," Silas said cheerily. "Tell me, how did you manage to find that egg?"

The question caught Callie by surprise. Chief Aemon had declared that Callie and Tristan were just lucky to stumble upon the egg while playing in the forest. The reaction from villagers was… mixed. Some seemed to treat Callie more kindly, while others regarded her as an even bigger pest and troublemaker.

But nobody had outright asked how she managed to find an egg, likely accepting that it was nothing more than a happy coincidence.

"I-It was an accident. Tristan and I were playing when we found it," Callie replied nervously. After all, any mention of magic was strictly forbidden.

"I see. That must have been quite a surprising find," Silas said, and a loud crack suddenly came from the front of the wagon.

"What was that?" Mom asked.

Silas chuckled. "My apologies, my hand slipped by accident and I broke off a piece of the bench. The thing must have started rotting. I'll have to replace it."

Callie stared at the man's back for a while. He seemed to be acting odd. Perhaps the incident with the rangers had shaken him more than he was letting on. Even now, Callie couldn't help but worry about the little thunderfoxes. If only they could escape from those cages somehow.
