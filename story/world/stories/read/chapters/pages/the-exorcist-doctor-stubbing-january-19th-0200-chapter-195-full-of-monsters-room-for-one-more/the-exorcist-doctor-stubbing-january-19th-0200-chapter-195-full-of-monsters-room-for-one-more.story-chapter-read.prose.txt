The Breeder hadn’t seen the sky in eight months, which, when he occasionally remembered that fact at all, struck him as less a deprivation and more just an accidental experiment in habit.

Once upon a time, he may have thought people required certain things simply because they were accustomed to requiring them—sunlight, open air, conversations, weather—but he’d been pleased to discover, over the long years of his research, that most of those ‘necessities’ were more sentimental rather than biological. He breathed filtered air perfectly well. He slept beneath steel ceilings without difficulty. As for sunlight, the reinforced glass dome above his surgical chamber provided an acceptable substitute whenever a luminous deepwater fish passed overhead and briefly painted the chamber in strange colors. If anything, living under the sea was superior. Nobody ever knocked on his doors to interrupt him.

At present, the only thing interrupting him was the periodic vibration of the outer dome shell as another powerful underwater current rolled into the glass. But he’d learned to ignore that quickly too.

While he continued operating on the unconscious dockworker strapped to his surgical table, he glanced at the ten enormous metal tanks looming around the edges of the circular laboratory. He was less admiring the pure size of them and more observing their pressure gauges to see if they were still holding, but yes, he was still admiring them. For the last nine months, he’d done nothing but create more artificial Myrmur Hosts, release them out into the wilderness of Wraithpier, and allow them to acclimate. He’d allowed them to feed on the giant fish of Wraithpier, fight each other, consume each other, and drain their Hosts to death. Then, once the C-Rank Wretch-Class children developed into C-Rank Blight-Class adults or so, he had his obedient Myrmur Hosts ‘recover’ them and grind them down into pure bioarcanic mass—all of which, of course, was stored inside those ten massive tanks.

Over the months, he’d come to admire Wraithpier for reasons its residents would almost certainly misunderstand. They might enjoy its markets, its fishing culture, its canals, and its endurance beneath stormy weathers, but those were all just superficial traits of the ward. What mattered was that Wraithpier was an enormous, uncontrolled bioarcanic growth chamber. Its waters were so saturated with mutated organisms, and its deep lakes so full of aggressive prey that his released artificial Myrmur Hosts flourished extremely well here. His Hosts developed much, much faster than when he’d released them to grow stronger in Bleakhearth, Ironwych, and Blightmarch, so by extension, he’d been able to collect more tanks of pure bioarcanic mass than he’d been able to with his old lab.

… The downside was that stronger, faster-developing Myrmurs attracted more attention, and that led to its own troubles.

His gaze drifted toward the thousands of newspapers scattered across the chamber floor. The papers had all accumulated over the last three months until they formed several ugly heaps, all stained at the edges with moisture and surgical dirt. His obedient Myrmur Hosts brought them down whenever they delivered medicine, new supplies, and new Hosts to him. And he read almost every issue while he performed his operations.

The same words kept appearing.

Heartcord Clinic… Saint’s Hands… Gael Halloway… Myrmur numbers dropping in over three dozen deepwater lakes…

His mouth tightened as he read the headlines again. He hadn’t seen Benedict in nine months. And he hadn’t heard a single peep from the man either. All he knew was that Benedict was probably moving on with the final stage of his preparations up in Vharnveil, and was extremely ‘busy’ to the point even a simple letter would take too much effort to write, but there was an old saying that time could not be found, only created.

He could lament the fact that Benedict hadn’t done good on his promise to deal with the Heartcord Clinic, but the truth was, he’d known Benedict for decades. The man had no qualms with killing for the sake of his research, which meant his inability to decisively destroy Gael and the Heartcord Clinic came not from a barrier in his heart, but rather, it was part of his research. Benedict wasn’t going to kill Gael because he needed Gael alive for something… and that was going to be his downfall.

The Breeder knew it. And he understood it perfectly even if he found it maddening. Benedict was a more intelligent man than he was, which meant if Benedict wanted to rationalize not killing his own son, he could do it easily.

So what was the Breeder to do?

Let Gael and the Heartcord Clinic continue to get in his way, and, by extension, disrupt Benedict’s further and further?

… No, he thought. I’ll do what your heart cannot, Benedict.

I’ll kill your son for you.

Back in his Bleakhearth lab, every artificial Myrmur Host of his was given memory suppressants, hallucinogens, confusion agents, and sensory disruptions in carefully staggered doses throughout the entire operation process to reduce the chances of someone being able to trace them back to him. However, since he had to evacuate from his Bleakhearth lab in a hurry, he’d been forced to abandon most of his stronger compounds. What remained here in his backup Wraithpier lab was functional but inferior. His artificial Myrmur Hosts here probably remembered a great deal more about his lab than his artificial Myrmur Hosts back in Bleakhearth, so considering the Heartcord Clinic had managed to rescue over fifty of them over the past three months…

It was more than likely they’d already interrogated the Hosts and figured out a general idea of what his lab looked like. Pale, they might’ve even figured out where his lab was already—but it wasn’t likely they had the technology and the knowhow to actually breach it even if they had the information. That was probably a safe assumption to make. The Heartcord Clinic had only recently arrived in Wraithpier. It’d take them at least another month or two to develop the technology to get this far out onto the sea, let alone dive this deep under the sea, which meant if Gael wanted to speed up the timeline, he’d have to ask for local help.

Specifically, Gael would need… the Fishermen’s help. They were the only ones who ever sailed this far out onto the sea. In that case, he could still buy himself a bit more time to prepare to evacuate everything out of the lab.

After all, wasn’t there some sort of massive fishing festival coming up very soon?

He turned mid-cut into the dockworker’s abdomen and glanced at his most obedient Myrmur Hosts—his bodyguards’, he liked to call them—who were all sitting motionless before each of the giant tanks of bioarcanic mass. Even shrouded in shadow, their Blight-Class Myrmurs enveloped most of their limbs and appendages, making them look more half-bug than half-men. One had mantis armor fused across his torso. Another had spiky beetle plates layered over his shoulders and spine. Two carried massive crab claws for arms, while the largest of them crouched near the reinforced loading doors, her entire body wrapped in the heavy segmented shells of a deepwater crab.

They were his most powerful Hosts. They were his most obedient Hosts. They were his most intelligent Hosts—and they were his pride and joy.

“... I feel like seeing a leviathan up close today,” he said, grinning madly at all of them. “Mind catching one and dragging it into the loading bay for me?”
