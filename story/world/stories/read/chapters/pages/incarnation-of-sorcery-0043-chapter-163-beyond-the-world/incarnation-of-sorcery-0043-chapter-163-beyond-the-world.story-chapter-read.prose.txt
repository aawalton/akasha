Viren wasn’t the only one alarmed when the shaking started. They were underground, for crying out loud. Being buried alive was not on his to-do list. As such, when the first tremor nearly threw Viren to his knees, he looked around in terrified alarm.

“Ryland hasn’t come back…” Academ Jules said rather insightfully.

Viren was pretty sure Ryland wasn’t causing the commotion. He knew they were all inside, and he wouldn’t willingly put them in danger. Something else was going on.

“We haven’t found Acrypha yet,” Viren said. “We can’t leave.”

Because he saw it in their eyes. This sort of shaking was dangerous, and they’d do well not to remain inside the Crypts for any longer than they needed to. But how could any of them think of leaving without bringing Acrypha along with them first?

“You have to consider the possibility that Acrypha isn’t even here any longer, Viren,” Linsby said. The graduate student at least looked remorseful about the prospect of leaving.

Viren felt like arguing. They didn’t really have proof that Acrypha was elsewhere. But more tremors assaulted the Crypts. That just compounded the intention to leave the area before it was too late, especially when several priests and priestesses came running too.

They didn’t linger for long. Just like Academ Jules and Linsby, they were frightened about the prospect of everything collapsing upon them.

“Let us at least head out to see what’s going on,” Academ Jules finally said as a compromise. “Right now, staying here frightened isn’t going to help anyone. If nothing truly dangerous is occurring, then we can return and continue our search.”

Viren grumbled but had to accept. Maybe he was being a little stupid, but his heart panged at the thought of potentially leaving Acrypha behind. How in the world would she be feeling if she was indeed here and was buried alive to boot? She was already traumatized thanks to her family. Viren really didn’t want to add onto it.

But he followed the Academ and Linsby in silence. One of the priests guided them to the closest exit, which was distant from the entrance he had used with Ryland. It took them to the top of a hill not far from the geographical centre of the Crypts.

Whereupon they could finally stand witness to whatever madness was going on.

“What in all the Realms…” Academ Jules murmured as he stared upwards.

Viren didn’t say anything still, but he certainly agreed with the Academ’s reaction. There was a firefight going on high, high above them. Violent magic clawed its way through the world, sending out shockwaves that pounded into Viren’s ears and made him feel like he ought to be flattened to the ground.

Things would probably have been a whole lot worse without Ryland’s barrier keeping him safe.

“Is… are there people attacking Ryland?” Linsby asked. “But…”

There were probably a lot more questions crowding the graduate student’s head, but he was probably too awestruck by the sheer intensity and wonder of the magic on display. So was Viren, even after all this time, even after seeing Ryland in action so often, he could hardly fathom everything happening far above them.

Especially when it looked like Ryland himself didn’t particularly care about the Crypts’ safety. Not when he had pulled out that gargantuan meteor from one of the largest rifts Viren had ever seen.

“I really think we should be moving now!” Linsby said.

He was almost instantly proven wrong when the meteor simply stopped descending. The fight continued in and around it, the mages flinging spells and throwing attacks far too quickly for Viren to note everything that was going on. The endless collisions of their sorcery threatened to overwhelm Viren’s mind, so he just tried to focus on the big picture.

Like how the meteor was now reshaping into a monstrous maw, spewing a gargantuan torrent of deadly magic and seeking to swallow the offending mages whole. No. Viren focused on one specific figure he barely felt like he recognized.

“Those are Guardians,” he said. “Why are they attacking?”

Neither Linsby nor Academ Jules had an answer, but they could all fathom the implication. The Guardians wouldn’t act, not in such a concerted effort, if their master hadn’t bidden them to do so.

Demiurge Mavar Val Arona was the one indirectly assaulting Ryland.

What for? Because they were getting close to discovering whatever secret lay within the Crypts? That sounded a little stupid because the way they were conducting this battle almost suggested they didn’t care what happened if stray shots from their fracas against Ryland ended up damaging or destroying the Crypts.

Then again, when that enormous meteor had appeared, a glaive wielding Guardian had done her level best to hold up that enormous ball of rock.

Right up until Ryland had summarily blasted her to the Crypts. The impact of her body striking the earth, from which she didn’t rise, made them all quake. Viren had to pick himself up off the earth.

“What kind of magic was that?” Linsby asked in muted shock.

Right. Viren couldn’t recall if even he had seen that spell before. The one where Ryland turned into a comet to take out one of his opponents permanently.

“We must get away,” Academ Jules said. “This location isn’t at all safe.”

His tone brooked no argument, and in all honesty, Viren couldn’t blame him. The longer they stayed here, the longer they remained in danger of potentially getting caught in the crossfire.

“[Smoke Wings],” Academ Jules cast.

In moments, the smoke billowing from his spell circle quickly formed a bird large enough to carry the three of them. Even with the unbelievable cocktail of magic crackling above them with unstoppable fury, Viren couldn’t help but be impressed by the Academ’s sorcery.

It was Viren who took the first seat, with Linsby and Academ Jules taking their positions in single file behind him.

They soon shot off across the hilly ground, straddling the valleys between the mountains to get away from the danger zone. Ryland’s latest spell had cracked the entire world apart, and while they were distant enough to not get caught in those deadly serrations in reality itself, the effects wouldn’t cease chasing them so easily.

“Hold on!” Academ Jules shouted.

Viren gripped the strangely solid smoke with a white-knuckled grip, determined to not fall off as they soared over the landslides and broken rocks blanketing the mountainsides.

“They’ve stopped,” Linsby said.

Viren twisted around as his companions did, trying to make out what was going on. Linsby wasn’t wrong. Ryland and another Guardian were frozen at their locations, surrounded by those distortions in reality that almost felt wrong to look at. Far above them, the other Guardians were desperately trying not to get swallowed by Ryland’s impossible magic.

Whatever conversation occurred between Ryland and the Guardian didn’t go well. Whether it was working or not, it was cut short. Blue portals swallowed up the Guardians.

So there was someone else with spatial magic besides Ryland. Was it that mage he had attacked beside the spot where the glaive wielder had fallen?

Whatever the case, the Guardians were free from the reality-breaking trap. For his part, Ryland was apparently done with the entire rigmarole. Just as the Guardians were preparing one final magical burst, so too was Ryland’s manasoul turning into something Viren couldn’t even look at any longer. It was star-bright now, the impression of his form searing into Viren’s eyelids.

The avalanche of magic that emerged from both sides made Viren forget momentarily that he was supposed to be flying away to safety.

Dozens of rifts opened at the same time, pushing out mana in forms that Viren would never grow tired of seeing. Storms that could end worlds, a rain of volcanic eruptions, lightning that left whatever it touched petrified, demonic arms lined with crimson eyes that tore apart everything they touched.

The Guardians responded with a combination spell the likes of which Viren had never seen before. A spell circle as big as the meteor Ryland had summoned went up. Each of the Guardians formed a luminescent point, lines solidifying from figure to figure until a colossus was floating in the air before them. A colossus that broke through the blast of irrepressible magic from Ryland.

Viren could hardly believe what he was seeing. That thing shattered through the storm, through the dozens of arms tearing away at its golden mana, through the rain of furious power from an Incarnation of all people. And—

And it finally fell to a dragon.

Just the existence of the unstoppable, unfathomable magic erupting in the distance was making Viren feel like he was fainting. His heartbeats were muted, sounds were distorted, and his lungs struggled to draw in the required amount of air.

But the arrival of the skeletal dragon head, of a rift that blanketed out the entire mountain range sitting atop the Crypts, put everything else into an extremely harsh perspective.

Ryland had still been holding back against these prideful Guardians.

The maw’s appearance distorted space itself, its enormity making Viren veer this way and that. Linsby and Academ Jules cried out as they desperately held onto the smoky bird.

On the one hand, there was the rush of wind from something so gargantuan entering reality so suddenly. On the other, that huge rift was sucking away much of the local air too, acting like a whirlpool dragging them towards a sinkhole.

But that was fine. Even if Viren was vibrating like a struck spoon, he was stuck in more or less a singular location. This allowed him to note just what in all the Realms was going on. Not that he would have missed it easily. For the giant dragon’s head had opened its enormous maw, which made Viren think it had been about to swallow that golden colossus whole.

Instead, it blasted out dragon fire the likes of which just broke Viren’s ability to comprehend.

A stream of energy that could have swallowed the entirety of Arcoryx City easily climbed into the sky like it had been launched from the rockets famous on Kuldor. Viren’s eyes were shocked by the brilliance, so much so that he could feel the pain in his orbits, his ears screaming in protest alongside the violent shrieking burst from that titanic dragon.

“Madness,” Linsby said, somehow loud enough for Viren to hear. “Utter madness.”

The stream of cataclysmic dragon flames, so compressed together that it appeared like a singular beam, crashed through the distant clouds. It continued rising and rising. The atmosphere broke, warped, distended until the flaming beam was shooting into the cosmos.

Headed straight for the moon.

Something odd occurred. Something shocking enough to pull Viren back to the present, overcoming how overwhelmed he was by the blast from that skeletal dragon.

The beam from the dragon got deflected.

He was a little stupefied by not just what had occurred, but the fact that he himself had actively seen it. Was it Ryland’s barriers still allowing him to witness it all? Both Linsby and Academ Jules were crying out at the pressure unleashed by that blistering magic, by the dragon fire that was rapidly turning the world into an oven, turning the daylight into a scarlet nightmare.

But what in all the Realms was up with that moon? How was it simply pushing aside magic of such an awestriking calibre? Who or what had cast a barrier that could rival even Ryland’s power?

It took a while for some semblance of normalcy to return. Viren hadn’t seen what had happened to the Guardians, besides seeing most of them plummet to the ground. That their bodies were still somewhat recognizable as having once been living creatures spoke volumes of their innate magical strength.

Academ Jules had stopped his flying mount at the cessation of the battle, and it wasn’t long before Ryland appeared before them through his rift.

“Apologies for the debacle,” he said, once more looking like a normal mage and not some sort of godly being made of pure mana. “I tried to end it quickly but there were too many of them.”

He spoke like he had just finished dealing with unruly birds that had somehow gotten trapped inside his house, not several mages who were Empyrean-ranked at a minimum. Viren opened his mouth, then promptly closed it as he failed to form a correct reply. His mind was buzzing with too many questions, with too much of everything that had gone on.

“I’m… glad you’re safe, Ryland,” Academ Jules said a little haltingly. “But uh…”

“Worry not.” Ryland didn’t even spare the fallen Guardians a second glance. Viren looked closely and spotted that a few were actually still alive. Presumably, the one stuck inside the cage of void atop a cloud had survived too. “If anything happens, the blame will fall squarely upon me. Though, I suggest you depart sooner rather than later.”

Viren finally found his voice. “But sir. What about Acrypha?”

“Acrypha is on the moon, Viren.”

“W-what?”

That was Linsby, because the revelation had left Viren dumbstruck.

“I managed to get one of the Guardians to reveal her whereabouts,” Ryland said. “Unfortunately, the battle continued before I could ask for any further details.”

“But is it really true, sir?” Viren asked. “I mean… it’s the moon…”

Ryland looked up. “Considering what we just saw, I intend to at least check it out.”

“Then I’m coming with you, sir.”

“I’m afraid it’s too—”

Viren leaned forward on his seat, so much so that Linsby had to hold him back from falling off. “It’s not too dangerous, sir. I’ll be with you, won’t I?”

“That is true.”

“So I can go, right?”

Ryland paused to deliberate. Viren needed to go. He knew that Ryland could handle basically everything by himself, but that didn’t mean Viren could just sit back. What sort of a friend would he be if he didn’t help?

He was also aware that, due to the differences in their capabilities, he might just end up being a liability for Ryland. A part of him was besieged with niggling doubt. What if he was the reason Ryland ended up failing in his endeavours? Just the thought of something happening to Acrypha because Viren had held Ryland back, unwittingly or otherwise, nearly made him faint.

Maybe he was a little too stupid for wanting to go. Maybe he’d be better off simply putting his faith in Ryland.

“Sir,” Viren said shakily. “Maybe I shouldn’t—”

“It’s alright.” Ryland smiled at him. An encouraging, faithful smile. “If my suspicions are correct, I believe we aren’t done facing opposition, Viren. As such, I might just have need of you.”

“Is that really wise, Ryland?” Academ Jules asked.

Linsby looked like he wanted to protest too, but he never mustered a real word. Something stopped him. Maybe it was the catastrophic magic he had just seen being thrown about so casually.

“I trust Viren, Academ,” Ryland said, his smile never wavering. “Fear not. When all is said and done, we will return with Acrypha. Together.”

Viren took a deep breath. “I won’t let you down, sir!”

“I know you won’t, Viren.”

Academ Jules eventually agreed with Ryland. Like Linsby, he seemed to recognize that he was fairly outclassed by Ryland, whose judgment couldn’t be unsound after his displays.

Linsby and Academ Jules promised they would warn the rest of the Arcoryx contingent to get going. They parted, and soon, Viren was with Ryland by himself.

“Sir,” Viren said, swallowing a little. He was looking at the ground again. “They attacked you in broad daylight, not even bothering to mask their intentions, unlike last time.”

“Indeed. So you understand the seriousness of the issue.”

Viren did. Especially since Ryland’s demeanour had changed once Academ Jules and Linsby were gone. He had grown more serious. Almost angry.

“[Oneself World],” Ryland incanted. “Now, let’s get going. [Translocate].”

Viren watched a familiar bubble of stable atmosphere wrapping around himself and Ryland before they were whisked away via another one of Ryland’s rifts. This one deposited them in the middle of the cosmos, right in front of the moon with a glowing barrier.

Where the Demiurge himself was awaiting their presence.
