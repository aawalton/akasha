I plummeted through the air.

If my guess was right, the Warlord wasn’t used to fighting people as weak as me. It meant he wouldn’t know the difference between my weak signs of life and no signs. Besides, not even his physical senses could reach that far. There was no way he’d be able to hear my heartbeat over the crashing waves and rushing wind.

I didn’t know about his arcane senses, but those didn’t matter. If I didn’t have any Presence, he wouldn’t be able to sense much of me.

There was no way I was getting out of this alive. He had to think I was dead.

The water rose up to meet me. Wind whipped around me, but my stomach was surprisingly calm. I expected it to rise into my stomach.

Adrenaline powered me.

If I hit the waves like this, I would die. All spread out, the impact would shatter every bone in my body, no matter how strong I was.

I straightened myself out, forcing my body into a diving position, no matter how badly my wounds cried out in pain as my body stretched.

My fingertips hit the water and shattered on impact. My helmet saved my skull, but my shoulder dislocated.

But I survived. The water caught me, icy cold, and surrounded me. It took my breath away, no matter how high my Vitality was.

Flailing my legs, I thrust myself back to the surface. The metal armour dragged me down, but it couldn’t overpower my enhanced stats. Even without Presence, I was as physically strong as before.

The Warlord would be watching. He might see my head bob up to the surface.

I held my breath and stayed underwater, flailing my legs and kicking. The shock of the cold water and the will to make it out alive gave me one more burst of energy.

For five minutes, I swam as quickly as I could, getting as far from my impact point as I could. The rush of the waves ripped me toward the coast and the spikey rocks. A swell rose, trying to bash me into the coast, but before it could, I gripped onto the bollards of a pier and lifted my head out of the water just enough to take a breath.

Then I waited for the sun to set completely, shifting myself under the pier until I was out of sight. It would be easier to slip away during the night.

Yes, I was bleeding. I moved as little as I could, trying to minimize the blood loss, and I hoped my Vitality would do the rest. It would have to keep me alive. The saltwater ate into the wounds, cleaning them as my armour sealed back together repairing itself. Everything stung, but it wasn’t just physical. A bone-deep pain etched through my leys. I had no idea how it would feel if your blood vessels frayed, but this was what I imagined it would feel like.

Minutes turned into hours. The sky darkened. I listened. Patrols of soldiers raced along the docks. I shifted myself, holding onto the netting beneath the pier, hidden completely from sight.

Next came the Subtle Men. So they were back out again, now that their one true master had arrived?

I only caught glimpses of them. They rushed about, their masks glimmering in the torchlight. They sailed boats toward the impact point, trying to search the waves, but I was too far away.

An hour after the last wisps of twilight faded, I heard a new kind of footstep. It was far lighter than any of the others, and it had an organic rattle, like someone was walking on twigs and leaves.

Sage. There was no one else it could be.

I let myself look around the edge of the pier for a brief moment—long enough to spot the rest of my squad marching down the pier. They had taken off their armour and wore regular civilian clothes, and they patrolled nonchalantly. Anchor and Brawl wore hats to hide their faces.

Smart.

I waited hoping to catch their eyes. But I couldn’t stay out in the open for too long. The Subtle Men were also searching. I had to be faster.

My pride told me I needed to pull my own weight. My desperate brain told me I needed their help. I whistled softly. My voice could be mistaken for anyone’s whistle to an average ear, but I knew my squad would recognize me.

Anchor and brawl bundled up a burlap tarp, then rushed over with purpose. When no one was looking, they lowered it into the water. I crawled on. As soon as they had me in their grasp, they sealed it up, hiding me from sight. A Subtle Man demanded to know what they were doing, and Sage told him they were just gathering up driftwood. I stayed as still as I could, not even breathing. If the Subtle Man sensed anything, it would be over.

After a few seconds, he let us go. My squad carried me up the pier and into the city.

In the darkness, my limbs gave out. I fell perfectly still, and before I knew what was happening, I passed out.

~ ~ ~

I awoke on a wagon. The sky was overcast, and everything was blurry. Sage had healed most of my physical wounds, but a deep pain still burned beneath my flesh. My leys weren’t healing on their own.

A mixture of shame, anger, and frustration boiled inside me. I whispered, “I’m sorry.”

I’d promised them I wouldn’t get us in trouble. But the Warlord had found me anyway. I wanted to say there was nothing I could have done. How could I have done anything about it? Should I have killed Habit as soon as he had noticed me? I didn’t think he was that far gone. I didn’t think he could betray me like that, after all we’d done.

It didn’t make me want to blame myself any less.

Sage, who sat on wagon’s cart beside me, said, “Save your strength—”

“Is Hinlaw dead?” Anchor asked me, butting in.

“He is,” I gasped. “What about his son?”

“We got him, brother,” Brawl said.

“It won’t matter now,” Sage grumbled. “The Warlord thinks you’re dead at the bottom of the sea.”

“They haven’t found his body.”

“And?” Sage snapped. “They never will. Even an Emerald can’t swim to the depths off the coast of Steelshore. They’ll assume he sank to the bottom, with no way to verify. Everyone thinks you’re dead. Your squad has gone missing. They won’t appoint a dead man as Ealdorman.”

I clenched my eyes shut. “But—”

“There are no buts,” she said. “It just won’t work.”

~ ~ ~

I faded in and out of consciousness for a few days. We rode east again, trotting back to Hyrdford. It was slow progress, but we had our three horses attached to the wagon—they had found Burr, and she was doing most of the work.

Even when I was awake, I spent most of my days in silent contemplation.

What now?

Everything I’d worked so hard for this year was gone in a blink.

Worse…the Warlord was probably right. I didn’t have a plan. I didn’t even have an idea of how to rule a country.

Could I just give up?

I could give everyone else hope, it seemed. Not myself.

I glanced at Anchor and Brawl, who sat at the front of the wagon in their plain clothes. Next, I glanced at Sage. She stared blankly out the back of the wagon, watching the empty countryside roll past. Our armour lay in heaps, resting against the walls of the wagon.

I had promised them no surrender. But where were we supposed to go after this? Not only were my political ambitions gone, we were facing the largest orc attack we ever had. The entire south would likely fall before the next winter.

What if Anchor’s fears had been right? It was almost a year ago now, when he’d pulled me aside in Hyrdford and warned me that things would seem different when I had a truly impossible task ahead of me.

I didn’t know what to do.

I was just a normal guy from Earth. Just a normal guy with normal fears. No amount of armour and attributes and Skills would change that.

The orcs were coming. No one was there to stop them, and we were all going to die.

~ ~ ~

I didn’t speak a single word until we arrived at Hyrdford. Lord Ceonwin rushed out to meet us, then quietly rushed us into the great hall. He pulled us into a room near the back—the room that supposedly belonged to me, as a thegn—and begged me to sit. I said, “My Lord, I’d prefer to stand.”

“Sir Levi—”

“I’ve been sitting the past four weeks on a bumpy wagon,” I said coldly. “I’d prefer to stand.” I paused. “Am I still a thegn?”

“Perhaps not. You are supposed to be dead. We heard the news a week ago. The Warlord had found you and branded you as a traitor. He slew you.”

“He may as well have.”

“Sir Levi…”

“I advanced my Art,” I said. “I had to. It was the only way to survive. Three uses, and I don’t think my Leys can’t withstand a fourth.” I pulled my reading slate from my kit bag, then activated it. The letters were etched shallow in the slate’s face, barely legible. I looked at my list of Skills, and I could barely make out a new set:

Native Skills: Soulstealer’s Will (Advanced), Unseen Bolt (Advanced), Art: Mass Hammer (Expert), Memory Modification (Apprentice), Memory Palace Creation (Apprentice), Memory Palace Sorting (Apprentice), Perception (Apprentice)

I showed it to Lord Ceonwin. He grimaced and said, “I suppose you would like time to yourself?”

“Yes, please, my lord.”

~ ~ ~

A week later, I hadn’t made any progress, save for stewing. I transitioned through shame and embarrassment. How could I blame myself for what happened? I needed to heap as much of it on myself as possible. I could have changed something, couldn’t I?

Perhaps I had been arrogant, like the Warlord said. I thought I could do it. I had risen too quickly, and hadn’t convinced the right people of my loyalty. I had seriously thought I could be a great man, the likes of Aelfwine the Great?

The feeling of sorrow soon numbed into a smear of grayness. I ate once a day, and drank only when I had to. I expected myself to cry at some point, but nothing came.

One morning, though, something clacked at the window of my room in the great hall. Talons clicked on my windowsill, and a beak tapped on the glass.

Croak. He had found me. He stared directly at the ribbon on my wrist.

I hesitated.

Had Alis sent me a letter, reporting on some good news, thinking the Witenagemot had successfully appointed me without knowing the full truth, and it would only hurt more to read it?

For a few minutes, I stood perfectly still, half hoping Croak would fly away. He just kept tapping on the window.

Then my gaze drifted over to my spear.

That’s what separates the great from the mediocre. That’s the moment. It’s when you’re at your most desperate, lowest point that you have the choice. There’s greatness in all of us, if you choose to nurture it.

Hope was overrated, anyway. I needed willpower.

Clenching my teeth, I forced myself to move. Just enough to open the window. Just enough to pick up Croak’s letter and unfold it.

My eyes drifted down the letter:

Dear Levi,

Say it is not so. I am begging you. The news has reached Eraen-Kalora—someone outed you as a traitor, and the Warlord slew you. Please, please, please, please. I fear I pushed you too hard and too far. Perhaps someone grew suspicious because of my interactions with you?

I hold out hope because there was no body. It is likely that your armour pulled you to the bottom of the sea, they say, and you will never resurface.

To think we came so close. The Witenagemot appointed you before they heard the news, and I have ensured the proper paperwork was filed. You were an official Ealdorman for the briefest time.

In truth, if you live, you still are. To change the holder of the position, the previous Ealdorman’s body must be burnt. You technically seceded Hinlaw after his death, if only for a matter of minutes, and since your body has not been burnt, you are still the legitimate Ealdorman. His Domain is now yours.

I am begging you, Lord Levi, if you still live, return my letter as soon as you can, tell me you live. The Warlord is insisting on a foul man as my suitor, and I do not know how much longer I can hold out.

I hoped you could be my king. I still hope, but it dwindles by the day.

With a hopeful heart,

Alis

I tapped my foot inside my boot and gulped, then glanced over at my armour. I was still technically an Ealdorman, then?

I inhaled sharply, and a pang of warmth shot through me. I tightened my fists. That meant there was still a chance.

The other thegns had been curious about me. They had wanted a difference. If I, the true Ealdorman of this domain, could rally them to my cause, I could take it back. I could still make a difference. Maybe I could get them to follow me.

First, we’d fight the orcs back. We could return to Steelshore and close the Labyrinth gates. Then, I could rescue Slowbend and the 294th, and take back our lands. I could move in the shadows, guiding the southern domains, building an army to rise against the Warlord without suspicion. Dead men couldn’t raise armies, and he wouldn’t suspect me.

First, I’d defeat the new Ealdorman. I’d defeat whoever was in charge of the Subtle Men down here and seal the Labyrinths.

Then, with a host of battle-hardened troops, I could march on Erean-Kalora. I could beat back the Warlord and seize control.

For the survival of this land. It might not have been my true home, but it was the closest thing I’d get.

I knelt beside my armour, then laid my finger down on one of the scales. As usual, the Keepers avoided my flesh, but…but if I could fix Shatter’s Leys, then I could repair mine. This wasn’t over.

I had to keep fighting.

No matter what, I wasn’t going to be the same person as I was before this defeat. I’d have to be a little more cautious and a little more ruthless. I’d come back stronger, and I’d slay the Warlord where he stood.

I could use that power to not only save Gate’s people, but to bring them freedom.

But before I could try anything else, my reading slate trembled. It nearly shook itself off the table I’d left it on.

I rushed over, placed my hands on its edge, and examined it.

A faint message appeared on it, completely different from any previous message it had left me.

You have completed the necessary conditions to advance to Gold.

Please select a new Class:

Scarred Outlaw:

A roguish skirmisher who strikes from the shadows. Improves the rate that you gain Agility and Perception, and improves the rate that you gain ranged weapon Skills. Immediately grants you [Forestman (Expert)].

Monarch:

He who selects this class must never turn back on his path. He seeks to rule, and failing that, he will fall in battle. Improves the rate that you gain Strength and Focus, and improves your ability to gain Skills relating to your Art. Immediately grants you [Art - Will to Power (Expert)].

Select your class by speaking its name aloud.

Was it even a question?

I narrowed my eyes, extended a push of focus, and, with certainty, I stated, “Monarch.”
