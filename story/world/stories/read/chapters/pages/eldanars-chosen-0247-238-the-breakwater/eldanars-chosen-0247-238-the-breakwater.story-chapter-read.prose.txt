The business of culling the passive horde had settled into a steady, entirely unnatural rhythm. Josh stood fifty yards beyond the shadow of the Ashenfall timber walls, his heavy boots planted firmly in the churned, freezing mud. He kept his dark steel heater shield raised, watching as a pair of local blacksmith apprentices methodically surrounded and dismantled a stationary, bloated Crypt Ghoul. The creature offered virtually no resistance, its milky eyes staring blankly as iron spears pierced its rotting chest.

A moment later, a faint, translucent blue glow washed over the two apprentices. They let out a ragged, breathless cheer, the intoxicating rush of system-granted experience temporarily washing away the trauma of the morning.

"It feels wrong," Carcan murmured softly, standing just behind Josh's right shoulder.

The elven healer had her staff resting against the mud. Her pristine pale green robes whipped around her legs in the freezing winter wind. She looked out over the vast, silent sea of monsters stretching towards the northern horizon.

"It is a slaughterhouse, not a battlefield," Carcan continued, her silver eyes filled with a deep, complex sorrow. "These were innocent people once. Striking them down while they stand utterly defenceless feels like an execution."

"It is a necessary execution, Carcan," Josh replied, his voice a low, heavy rumble. He did not lower his shield. "If another boss monster wanders down from the mountains and claims this horde, every single one of them will become a weapon again. We have to thin the field while they are docile. I also kind of see it like we’re letting them escape whatever torment they’re suffering now.”

Nearby, Garrik swung his heavy iron broadsword, cleanly severing the spine of a wandering skeletal hound. The tavern landlord wiped a smear of black sludge from the visor of his dented greathelm. He was fighting with grim, silent determination, using the cull to physically work through the suffocating guilt of surviving the earlier breach.

The pale winter sun offered no warmth, but the sheer exertion of trudging through the thick, freezing sludge was keeping the civilian militia warm. The atmosphere was almost light, the tension bleeding away with every easy kill.

Then, the world shattered.

It did not begin with a sound. It began with a sudden, violent drop in ambient temperature that pierced straight through Josh's heavy plate armour. The freezing wind died instantly, leaving a dead, suffocating stillness over the plains.

Then, a voice echoed directly inside their skulls.

Did you truly believe, the voice hissed, carrying the cold, ancient malice of the grave, that I was that easy to kill, weak mortals?

The psychic intrusion was physically painful. It felt as though a jagged shard of ice had been scraped against the inside of Josh's brain. He winced, dropping his longsword slightly to press a heavy steel gauntlet against the side of his helmet.

Behind him, Carcan let out a sharp, terrified gasp. She dropped her staff into the mud, her hands flying to her temples.

"Perberos," Carcan whispered, her voice trembling with sudden, absolute panic. Her luminous silver eyes widened as the horrific reality of the telepathic taunt set in. "Josh, he is alive. The General is still alive."

Before Josh could offer a word of reassurance, the vast, silent sea of monsters surrounding them violently awakened. The mindless stupor vanished instantly. The milky, lifeless eyes of thousands of rotting villagers, ghouls, and skeletal warriors suddenly flared with a sickly, malevolent green light. The dark magical tether had been forcefully re-established.

A deafening, unified shriek of pure fury erupted from the horde.

Josh looked towards the northern horizon. The rear half of the massive army, numbering in the thousands, had turned away from Ashenfall. They snapped their heads towards the distant eastern ridge and began to sprint wildly towards the dense, corrupted forest, confirming Carcan's absolute worst fears. They were actively hunting the assassins.

But the front half of the horde, the thousands of monsters standing directly on the plains outside the city gates, locked their glowing green eyes onto the fragile, exposed culling squads.

"Shield wall!" Josh roared, his voice cracking with absolute, desperate urgency. "Form a line! Right now!"

The order was entirely lost in the sudden, catastrophic explosion of panic.

The civilian militia, who had grown bold and reckless on the promise of easy experience, completely broke. The illusion of safety shattered into a million pieces. They were no longer fighting passive, helpless targets. They were standing in the open plains, surrounded on three sides by a shrieking, coordinated wave of death.

A young farmhand standing twenty feet away dropped his crude iron spear, letting out a high-pitched scream of pure terror. He turned his back on the horde and bolted blindly towards the distant open gates of Ashenfall.

He took barely five steps before a corrupted skeletal hound, its speed magically enhanced by the General's fury, bounded out of the mist. The beast leapt onto the boy's back, its fleshless jaws clamping brutally around the back of his neck. The boy was dragged screaming into the freezing mud, instantly swarmed by a dozen rotting ghouls.

"Do not run!" Garrik bellowed, raising his heavy broadsword and stepping forward to cover a terrified baker who had frozen in shock. "If you run, they will cut you down! Stand your ground!"

But the panic was a physical contagion. A squad to Josh’s left broke rank, throwing their weapons into the sludge and sprinting wildly for the city walls. The disciplined, orderly cull devolved instantly into a chaotic, bloody rout.

"Josh!" Carcan cried out, her serene composure entirely gone as a group of four purple-skinned zombies lurched toward her.

Josh did not hesitate. He took a massive, heavy step forward, placing his towering, steel-clad bulk directly between the elven healer and the advancing monsters.

He called upon the deep, humming reservoir of his mana core. He triggered Stormforged Adamantite, pouring raw power into his evolved body. Blinding blue lightning erupted violently from his dark steel plate armour, arcing through the freezing morning air.

He swung his electrified longsword in a brutal, sweeping horizontal arc. The crackling blade sheared cleanly through the torsos of three charging zombies. The extreme heat of the electrical discharge instantly vaporised the dark magic animating them, turning the rotting meat into glowing ash before they could even hit the mud.

"Stay behind me!" Josh commanded, his voice a low, heavy rumble. He turned to the right, spotting the tavern landlord fighting desperately against two skeletal warriors. "Garrik! Form up on my right flank! We are moving backward!"

Garrik smashed the hilt of his sword into a skeleton's faceplate, scrambling through the mud to join Josh. The older man was panting heavily, his eyes wide with fear, but he locked his shoulder against Josh's dark heater shield, creating the foundation of a two-man barricade.

"We retreat slowly!" Josh yelled, projecting his voice over the shrieking horde. "Rangers and mages, cover the flanks! Heavy fighters, anchor the line! Walk backward! Do not turn around!"

The scattered, terrified remnants of the culling squads heard the human Guardian's booming voice. Drawn by the blinding blue light of his electrified sword and the solid, unyielding presence of his heavy armour, dozens of fleeing civilians and low-tier adventurers scrambled towards him.

They formed a crude, desperate blob of humanity in the middle of the plains.

Josh acted as the tip of the spear in reverse. He battered ghouls away with his heavy shield, using sweeping, controlled strikes with his longsword to carve a safe path backward through the freezing sludge. Garrik fought with grim, silent determination on his right, while a heavily scarred beastkin mercenary anchored his left. Carcan stayed directly behind Josh, her amber staff pulsing brightly as she fired quick, desperate bolts of holy light over his shoulder to blind the approaching monsters and erecting shields to block errant attacks from striking the young adventurers.

They moved at an agonisingly slow pace. Every single step backward was a bitter, exhausting fight. The quality of the enemies attacking them was incredibly low. They were fighting farmers, weavers, and fragile skeletons. But the sheer, impossible quantity was completely overwhelming. For every zombie Josh cut down, three more threw themselves wildly against his shield, trying to use their dead weight to drag him down into the mud.

"Keep moving!" Garrik shouted, severing the reaching arm of a ghoul. "We are halfway there!"

They merged with another retreating squad, their numbers swelling to nearly a hundred desperate survivors. The massive, huddled blob of fighters slowly fought their way back towards the shadows of the towering timber walls.

But as Josh risked a quick glance over his shoulder towards the main iron portcullis, a cold, sharp knot of absolute dread formed in his stomach.

The entrance to Ashenfall was completely blocked.

Hundreds of panicking, screaming militiamen and adventurers had beaten them to the gates. The thirty-foot-wide archway was entirely choked with a frantic, surging crush of bodies. Men and women were trampling each other in the freezing mud, desperately trying to squeeze through the bottleneck and reach the safety of the inner staging area.

Above them, the defenders remaining on the timber walls were trying desperately to cover the retreat. Heavy iron-tipped arrows rained down into the advancing horde. A few scattered fireballs and shards of jagged ice exploded amidst the zombies.

But the magical support was painfully weak.

The high-tier casters, the ones capable of unleashing truly apocalyptic destruction like Brett and Caistina, had been rotated off the wall to rest their depleted cores, or had collapsed on the wall, left to their slumber. The lower-level mages currently manning the parapets simply did not have the raw firepower to stop a tidal wave. Their spells blew small holes in the enemy lines, but the gaps were filled instantly.

The retreating blob finally reached the outer edge of the bottleneck. They slammed into the back of the panicked crowd, their backward momentum completely halting.

"Move!" a terrified merchant screamed, shoving a young woman aside as he tried to claw his way towards the iron gates. "Let us in!"

Josh locked his boots into the freezing mud, his heavy backplate pressing against the surging crowd behind him. He raised his dark heater shield, taking a brutal, jarring impact from a leaping Crypt Ghoul. He deflected the monster, driving his sword through its skull, but he was instantly forced to bash a skeletal hound away.

They were no longer retreating. They were trapped against a wall of their own panicked allies, facing an endless ocean of teeth and claws.

Josh looked at the sheer volume of monsters pressing inward. The flanks of the defensive blob were already buckling. He saw a young, low-level adventurer on the left side of the formation get dragged down by three rotting villagers, his screams muffled as they tore into his leather armour. The civilians behind Josh were entirely focused on pushing through the gate, completely abandoning the defensive line.

They were going to be overrun in no time.

Josh lowered his electrified sword for a fraction of a second. He turned his heavy steel greathelm, looking directly at the tavern landlord fighting beside him.

"Garrik," Josh said, his voice entirely devoid of panic, carrying a cold, absolute finality.

Garrik deflected a rusted spear, turning his head. He saw the grim, unyielding look in the Guardian's storm-grey eyes, and the older man instantly understood exactly what the towering human was about to do.

"Josh, don’t do it," Garrik pleaded, his voice cracking. "The crush will clear in a minute. We can hold them."

"No, we can’t," Josh corrected softly. He reached out with his left hand, grabbing the thick iron collar of Garrik's tunic and forcefully pulling the man in front of Carcan. "Take her. You keep her behind you, and you use your shoulders to push her through those gates. You do not let anything touch her."

Carcan heard the exchange. The elven healer stopped casting, her silver eyes widening in sheer horror. She reached out, her pale, blood-stained fingers grabbing desperately at the heavy steel plating of Josh's arm.

"Josh, no!" Carcan cried out, her melodic voice raw and frantic. "You promised me we were leaving together! Do not step away from the line!"

Josh turned his head, looking down into her luminous, terrified eyes. He felt the heavy, iron anchor in his chest pull tight. He wanted nothing more than to step back, to wrap his arms around her and carry her through the gates himself. But he knew the brutal reality of the battlefield. If he stepped backward, the horde would wash over them all.

"I am keeping my promise, Carcan," Josh murmured softly, his voice trembling just slightly with the weight of the moment. "I am making sure you survive."

He gently pulled his arm free from her grasp. He did not look back again.

Josh turned his back entirely on the safety of the open gates. He raised his dark steel heater shield high, pointing his electrified longsword directly at the endless, shrieking sea of the dead.

He triggered Juggernaut's Tread.

Blinding blue lightning flared wildly around his heavy steel boots, granting him unstoppable, explosive momentum. Josh let out a deafening, gravelly roar that echoed across the bloody plains.

He charged directly forward.

He hit the front line of the advancing horde with the force of a runaway siege engine. The concussive impact shattered the brittle bones of a half-dozen skeletal warriors, throwing them violently backward into the crushing ranks behind them. Josh did not stop to fight them individually. He pushed relentlessly forward, wading ten yards deep into the absolute thickest, most suffocating concentration of rotting meat.

He stopped his forward momentum, planting his heavy boots deep into the freezing sludge. He was completely surrounded on all sides, standing alone as a towering, electrified island in an ocean of rot.

He had successfully created a breakwater. He then activated his Lodestone Taunt skill, physically drawing the enemy towards him and away from the fleeing townspeople.

The mindless, shrieking horde instantly reacted to the massive, bright blue threat standing in their midst. Hundreds of zombies and ghouls abandoned their push towards the gates, swarming entirely over the human Guardian. They threw their bodies against his heavy shield, their jagged claws scraping uselessly against his thick steel plate armour.

"Mountain and stone, you stubborn fool!" a deep, familiar voice roared from the crowd near the gates.

Josh deflected a rusted broadsword, glancing over his shoulder, a smile appearing on his face even with his situation so dire.

Pushing his way violently out of the retreating crowd came Torin. The heavily scarred veteran carried his heavy iron mace, his face twisted in a mask of furious, battle-crazed determination. Right beside him was Korg, the dual-wielding beastkin, and a half-dozen other high-tier, heavily armoured adventurers who refused to hide behind a human roadblock.

They charged forward, carving a bloody path through the mud to join Josh in the meat grinder.

They formed a tight, heavily armoured circle around the Guardian, turning the single breakwater into a jagged, lethal reef.

"You didn't think we were going to let you take all the glory, did you?" Torin grunted, smashing his mace into the skull of a leaping ghoul.

"Just keep them off my back!" Josh yelled back, sweeping his crackling longsword through the torso of a forest troll.

The quality of the enemy was entirely irrelevant. The veteran fighters could kill the lesser zombies with their eyes closed. But the numbers were infinite, and the physical weight was suffocating.

Josh felt the heavy, rotting bodies pressing against his shield, trying to physically crush him. A ghoul managed to slip past his guard, its jagged teeth snapping uselessly against his steel shoulder guard, its rotting fingers desperately trying to find a gap in his visor before Torin crushed its spine. His sword arm burned with sheer, agonising exhaustion. The blue lightning wrapping his blade flickered erratically as his mana core scraped the absolute bottom of its reserves.

He fought blindly, mechanically, entirely consumed by the brutal, desperate grind of survival.

He did not know how many minutes passed. He did not know how many rotting bodies piled up around his boots. He only knew that every single swing of his sword, every single bash of his heavy shield, was buying another second for the terrified civilians packed into the bottleneck behind him.

He swung his blade through the neck of a skeletal warrior, his vision swimming with dark spots, and offered a silent, desperate prayer to whatever gods were listening that Garrik had managed to push Carcan through the iron gates.
