Returning to the alchemy station, Carmack greeted the shop assistants. Their gatherers had provided them hundreds of mushroom and moss samples since they had last spoken, and he had received word that the head alchemist wanted to speak.

“One moment, please. He’s on his way,” a shy, red-headed, and freckled girl behind the counter said.

“Sunflower, was it?”

“Yes, that’s me,” she squeaked.

“No rush,” Carmack waved as he perused the tonics and potions. “Take your time.”

A door creaked open, and he turned to see a little bald man with a magnifying glass seemingly glued to one eye and a fuzzy, salt-and-pepper beard.

“The hero,” the man threw up his hands. “We finally meet. I have been waiting for this moment!”

“Batholomew?”

“Exactly,” he said, scuttling across the room and shooting a hand out before Carmack. “Head Alchemist and Herbalist Bartholomew Beetroot.”

“A pleasure,” Carmack said, easing his grip as the little man grimaced and turned red. “Sorry.”

“No, no need. I’ve heard a lot about the Great Hero’s vice-like grip. A pleasure to experience it,” he said, shaking and massaging his hand.

“So, Bartholomew, about those mushrooms… anything promising?”

“Ohohohoh, you are going to like this. Come, follow me into the back room. And you too, Sunflower, I might need your help. You need to thank those gatherers, they really did a great job. So many pristine samples.”

“I’ll make a note to do that.”

Passing into the backroom followed by the girl at the counter, the mad alchemist raced around his crowded workshop, ducking underneath drying mushrooms hanging from strings and swirling around ominously bubbling cauldrons.

“Quite the setup you have here.”

“Yes, isn’t it? Put a lot of work into this—almost on par with what I had in Lydrus.”

Picking up a vial between two fingers, he raised it up to the crystal embedded in the ceiling and flashed his crooked smile. “This here is what I call chameleon's blood. One vial and your body will start to mimic its surroundings. We even managed to get it working while the test subjects were wearing light clothing. Quite an accomplishment. You can ask Sunflower how awkward it was testing in the nude.”

Carmack turned to Sunflower, who reddened.

“Ah, don’t worry. It’s only a little flesh. We’ve all seen it before.”

Batholomew’s words didn’t help, and Sunflower shrank back behind a potion-filled shelf.

“That is impressive. Are you going to be able to produce a lot of this, chameleon blood?”

“With enough mushrooms, sure. With what we have? Barely a dozen vials.”

“Hmph, we’ll need more. Make sure to mark down what you need on a priority list so I can ensure the gatherers are focusing on the right resources.”

“Ah, yes, sure. Will do. But we’re not done yet,” Bartholomew raised a grimy finger.

“Oh?”

“This here is what I call Cat’s Eye,” he grabbed another vial. "Luckily, we have a little more of this. About fifty vials already.”

“What does it do?”

“It allows the consumer to see in the darkest of dark,” Bartholomew grinned.

“Now that is useful.”

Carmack obviously didn’t need the potions for himself, but something like that would be invaluable to scouts and forward formations that might have to fight without light.

“And finally, Serpent Moss extract. This stuff is nasty. It won’t kill you, but it will put you in a horrible state. Vomiting, and horrible, horrible diarrhea. Seriously, shit everywhere. Truly foul. Sunflower can attest to that, can’t you, girl?”

Carmack looked over at her. The red on her face practically glowed in the low light now, and she squealed as she hid further behind the shelf of herbs and potions.

“And how much of this stuff do you have?”

“Almost a hundred vials. The moss is quite prevalent around the rivers. It is by far the easiest potion for us to produce.”

“Good, make as much as you can.”

“It’s nasty stuff, but if you’re sure.”

“Perfect for our enemies. I must go, but please inform me if you discover anything new.”

“Will do, hero.”

“Good luck to you as well with the potion making. I hope you don’t feel ill again,” Carmack bowed slightly toward Sunflower.

“You too,” she squeaked.

Despite his best efforts to avoid it, Managra was no amateur when it came to politics. Wanting to warn Carmack of the trouble brewing, he requested a meeting in his private residence.

If those within the court were daring enough to voice their contempt openly, there could be no doubt their scheming went far further in the shadows.

Managra strolled through Carmack’s home at the Hero’s side as they spoke politics. His home was quite large, with a fountain in its foyer, bordered by little glowing mushrooms and fed by the aqueducts. Above it was a stone statue.

“A fountain, impressive. So, you’ve finished the waterworks, then, have you?”

“I have,” Carmack nodded. “This was a little reward for myself. Every home, business, and establishment now has access to clean, running water.”

“Marvelous. That should keep the pesky complainers talking about the surface quiet for a while. Even they will have to admit we never had running water back in our homes in Lydrus.”

“I hope so. We need unity.”

“Haha, glad you're excited about the title.”

“Oh, that’s only half of it. You’ll see. I have big plans for this place.”

“Good, good. I’ll take your word for it. These old bones need their rest.”

“Let me see you out, High Priest,” Carmack said and walked him to the stone door, which he opened with a magical flick of his wrist.

Outside, the High Priest’s guards awaited him, and several figures walked down the crystal-lit corridor toward them.

“Commander, I wasn’t expecting you,” Carmack said.

“High Priest, Great Hero,” Gorn bowed and continued toward them.

“Good to see you, Commander. He’s all yours.”

“And as always, it is good to see you, High Priest,” Gorn bowed again as Managra’s guards led him away.

“Err, the note you sent me?” Gorn’s expression twisted.

“Oh, right. Sorry, I’ve been busy. Please, come on in.”

Walking across his marble floors, Gorn took a seat in Carmack’s strategy room, which was adorned with maps and tabletop sculpture of their spire home and its surroundings, with little pieces to depict assets.

“It’s about our militarization,” Carmack said. “The armor and training your men are currently undertaking will provide us with a starting point. But men who were used to guarding roads and buildings against bandits and goblins will need a little more help against the terrors of the Undercavern.”

“I have procured some items that should help us.”

People who are fast on their feet and perceptive. As long as they can avoid the webs, they should be able to sneak around with relative safety.”

“Safety?” Gorn scratched his chin dubiously. “That doesn’t sound particularly safe to me.”

“And have these people rotated in and out. I want them training between their missions below. Killing monsters appropriate for their level and getting stronger whenever possible.”“Yes, hero. I’ll make sure of it.”

Timber will bring us one step closer to satisfying the needs of the people and add arrows to our stocks.

Carmack was feeling increasingly confident. Gradually, his plan to become an exporter of magical items grew nearer. Once these rogues were trained up, one obstacle would be completed.

We just need to find a reliable source of gemstones.

Carmack had searched for them with the use of his mana, but either there were no nearby deposits, or they were hidden by some means. For now, he would focus on what was within reach. Gemstones might require going off on a little adventure, which he wasn’t in the position to entertain right now. The settlement still had plenty of jobs that needed doing, and the rumors that they were being spied on tugged him back to their urgent needs.

Unfortunately, he was still too weak to access most of his spells. Golems and other advanced geomancy magic would have to wait, and he would have to bide his time.

Be patient, Carmack told himself. The pieces are falling into place.
