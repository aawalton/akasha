As soon as our strip of rainbow light portaled us, I searched around, and soon, they began to gather. The group of rescued fae were huddled around the forest area, and they were damn happy to see us!

“You’re alive!” called out the elderly fae woman who had seemed to be their leader. She struggled to her feet, her weathered face creased with worry. “We weren’t sure if we should wait or try to find help. That terrible man’s magic kept us from venturing too far.”

“We’re fine,” Nolan assured her. “How are you guys holding up?”

“Spirits shattered, but not defeated,” she said with a warm smile, her thin eyes but thick lashes reminding me of my grandma. “We are so very grateful to you all. Some of us are just now regaining our strength, but we didn’t want to risk everything traveling by ourselves. Besides, we don’t know where we are.”

Suddenly, a woman and a man stepped out of the shadows of the forest, the man carrying the woman over his shoulder. It was Elara and her son.

“There they are,” she said as she cracked a dry smile. “You came back, as promised.”

Nolan walked over to her and took her hand into his. “Hey, how are you feeling?”

“I’ve seen better days, but I’m still free.”

“And that’s what matters most,” Nolan replied, squeezing her hand gently. “We’ll get you all somewhere safe.”

I watched the interaction with a strange feeling in my chest. There was something in the way Nolan looked at Elara…. Maybe it was just compassion for a fellow survivor, but something about it felt personal. He didn’t know her, yet, that expression on his face, that gentle concern mixed with something deeper, suggested otherwise.

One fae suggested we find the closest settlement where they would have access to resources so they could recover, but Nolan left our destination to Elara. He remembered that she wanted to spend her final hours in her town, where she could die in the comfort of her home, surrounded by her family. I then thought about the legendary base elixir that we had, and that maybe Esme could fix a potion to restore Elara. But Nolan didn’t suggest it, and I understood why.

We were comparing saving two worlds against saving one individual….

As much as it stung, we needed that special vial to cure King Khunum’s curse.

When I gave my rainbow feather to Elara, she was able to teleport us to the last seven places she’d been to, her home town being one of them. There, the friendly gathering of fae tended to the wounded and traumatized survivors. The town was small but welcoming, nestled between rolling hills with thatched-roof cottages and gardens bursting with colorful blooms. The locals walked out of their homes with blankets and food when they saw us arrive with their injured kin.

Elara seemed to be the only prisoner from this town, the rest of those taken along with her long gone. I felt terrible for leaving her, but she confirmed she was more than happy to be back home. Her traveling son would be staying here until her passing, making sure to tend to her during her last moments on earth.

After we said our farewells, I passed the feather along. Everyone went on to their respective villages and towns, and I was shocked to see how far King Leech’s operation spread. We finally reached Zaiden’s temporary home, the fallen town of Alterus, a place where the vast majority of the prisoners were also from. Reunions spread around us—tearful embraces, cries of joy and disbelief. Children ran to parents they’d thought lost forever. Spouses collapsed into each other’s arms. The weight of what Zev had done, the families he’d torn apart, became painfully clear.

Once everyone was situated, the night grew still. Alterus was still repairing itself from Zeverus’ attack. Most of the fae there didn’t want to leave, and had plans on reinforcing the perimeter. Shield magic wasn’t easy, especially the real potent spells, but this time around, the town was hopeful, outsourcing their resources.

It was Lun’s idea. He had connections, and he was going to hook up these pleasant fae folk.

I get that they refused to be chased from their own home, but I feared for their safety. Regardless, their decision held firm, some of those fae also itching for revenge. With their powers coming back to them, they were prepared to defend their land, and their families at any cost. I was a realistic juvi dragon, and I’d seen first-hand what that rotten organ-harvesting fae could do. While I wanted to be hopeful for them, I also wanted to be realistic. Nolan was also against it, but it wasn’t our place to force them to evacuate.

I guess it was a good thing that Zaiden was willing to stay and help, too.

I was away from my party at the high hour of midnight—Typha was asleep, Nolan was out collecting items for our trip, and Lun was busy hustling a few gullible fae men in some dark alleyway, playing a card game known as Kazathan. As for me, I was standing along the edge of a cliff, being creative with my new dragon wings. I dove back and forth in all sorts of formations, enjoying my new mode of traveling around in Etheryn. When I became big enough, I planned on riding with Nolan, and we’d be able to get around without relying on a teleporter item.

“Having fun?” I heard someone say behind me, and when I turned my head around, I saw Zaiden.

For a second, I forgot how to fly, clumsily flapping my wings to regain my balance before landing gracefully on the cliff’s edge.

‘Don’t sneak up on me like that!’ I scolded, though my tail was already wagging despite my attempt to sound stern.

“Couldn’t sleep,” he admitted, walking closer to where I perched. “Too much has happened. My mind won’t quiet down.”

‘Hmm, you’re right on that one.’

He chuckled. “It’s funny, talking to you aloud while your voice is running through my head. It looks kinda crazy from this side, almost like I’m a nut-job talking to myself.”

‘Well, you wouldn’t be completely wrong,’ I teased.

He laughed. “You got jokes.” Zaiden sat beside me and gazed out at the sprawling landscape below. The lights of Alterus twinkled like earthbound stars, and beyond that, the vast wilderness of Etheryn stretched endlessly into the darkness.

“You know,” he said quietly, flexing his shadow-wreathed fingers, “I keep expecting to feel different. Like having this arm should change who I am fundamentally.” He paused, watching the wisps of darkness curl around his knuckles. And then, he turned his entire arm black, like a sleeve of ink. “But I still feel like me.”

‘That’s because you are still you,’ I replied, settling beside him.

“I haven’t gotten the chance to properly thank you for everything back there.” He turned to me. “I’m not exactly sure what a dragon like yourself would need from a hybrid fae like me, you know, besides the unyielding servitude of being your thrall.”

I rolled my eyes.

“All jokes aside, I owe you my life, Freya. Thank you.”

‘I say it’s all in a day’s work.’

“It’s more than that. You risked your life out there for me. I don’t… think anyone else, beside my sister, would have done that for me. To you, I was a complete stranger.”

‘I wouldn’t expect you to do that for me, if that’s what you’re getting at. I just couldn’t live with myself knowing I’m leaving someone out there to dry. You were in… pretty bad shape. I saw what Zev did to those other prisoners… torment and torture, harvesting organs, stealing life force…. I couldn’t let that happen to you.’

Zaiden was quiet for a long moment, his shadow arm returning to its normal fleshy appearance. “My parents died a few years ago,” he said finally. “Typha was so damn young. I’ve been taking care of her ever since, trying to be strong enough for both of us.” He looked down at his hands. “But in that place, when Zev took control... I felt so helpless… like I’d failed her all over again.”

My eyes softened on him.

“I make it a priority to keep moving. We were just unlucky that day. We were evacuating, just like the rest of them. The village was burned down, and a lot of them were slaughtered just for the hell of it. When my sister and I reached the forest-line, the chase was over. I did away with the orcs, but when Zev came… his magic. It was like nothing I’ve ever faced before. I was no match for it.”

‘And now?’ I asked. ‘Do you think you can face him now, with the dark prince’s powers? You’re planning on staying in Alterus, right? You need to have a game plan if he decides to pay you a visit again.’

“It’s not a matter of if, it’s a matter of when,” he said, his confirmation making my stomach knot up. “I have no doubt that the son of a bitch will come looking for us. For me, especially. But I’m not the same fae he captured. This power... I can feel it growing inside of me, making me stronger.”

‘That’s both reassuring and concerning,’ I admitted. ‘That same darkness consumed Theron. It promised him riches, power, immortality, you name it.’

“It could promise me the world, I’m not turning dark,” he said confidently. “I’m a dungeon raider, Freya, and I’ve spent years building my willpower. Typha’s klepto ring negates the effects of high charisma and willpower, so she’s got it easy. And I’m happy it has that feature for her. But me, I had to climb the ranks to get this strong. They say strength and endurance are the most important attributes, but all of that doesn’t matter if a baby dragon can walk up and induct you into her thrall.”

I deadpanned him and he laughed.

“Hah, seriously, I let you take me in. Just for the record. I was desperate. But, I digress. Strength, agility, and everything in between means nothing if you can’t control it yourself.”

‘Oh yeah? If your willpower is so high, how did the dark prince possess you?’

“In my defense, I was barely conscious when he took over. Being your conduit took one hell of a toll on me. I didn’t even know what was happening until I was already gone.”

‘Fair enough.’

“That’s your biggest fear, isn’t it?”

‘Huh?’

“Turning into a stereotype? A fear-inducing necro dragon.”

I went timid at the comment for a second and turned away from him. “I’m not a fan of labels.”

“Once you reach your final evolution stage, that’s what people are going to see. There is no escaping that.”

‘I know, and… it doesn’t bother me. As long as I do what I’m supposed to here, then I’m satisfied.’

“Yeah? What’s that?”

‘Saving the world.’

He laughed. That laugh reminded me of Nolan the first time I told him the same thing.

‘Hmph. Yuck it up all ya want. Just don’t blame me when I don’t invite you to my victory parade,’ I huffed, flaring my wings playfully.

“I’m not laughing at your goal, Freya. It’s just... refreshing. Most people I meet are just trying to survive the day. You’re thinking about saving an entire world.” He paused, and then I turned to him. “I believe you,” he said, his laughter fading into something more genuine. “I’ve seen what you can do. I’ve felt it. I can sense your determination, your heart. Your spirit is strong, and shit, your powers… if anyone could save Etheryn, it would be you.”

I hadn’t realized our bond had strengthened that much. The thought of him feeling about me in such a way suddenly made me self-conscious.

“You know, I thought about being a champion myself. But then I realized that I’d be leaving Typha alone out there. I’ll leave the heroism to the feisty baby dragon for now. If she needs me, all she has to do is say, Arise.”

‘The summoning call is awaken!’ I corrected. ‘And I’m not a baby dragon anymore!’

He chuckled and got up his feet as he said, “I don’t think you were ever a baby dragon.”

‘Hey, I’m curious—are you actually my thrall? I mean, you don’t operate like Dusty or Weedrill.’

“Well, of course not! I’m not a mindless monster.”

‘I know, but you seem pretty free-spirited, considering….’

He raised an eyebrow at me and slid his hands to his hips. “Are you trying to say you want to keep me inside your domain?”

‘No, of course not! I just want you to live your life, be normal.’

“Ah, I get it,” he said as he scratched his chin deep in thought. “You feel guilty that I am in fact your slave? Well, releasing me isn’t going to do you any good. Not that you know how to, anyway. And even if you did, I doubt you’d want to. I’m about the only entertaining friend you really have.”

Seriously?

“Not to mention, releasing me would break the conduit contract we currently hold, which would interfere with the whole not wanting to turn dark thing.”

I scrunched my face at him. ‘Do you even have an obedient bone in your body?’

His smirk widened. “Oh? So it’s like that?”

‘Kneel!’ I ordered him, my tiny finger pointing down on the ground where I expected him to be. But to my surprise, he just stood there, the man not taking me seriously one bit!

He snickered. “Adorable!” Then he reached over and patted my head. The nerve! “Maybe one day, I’d entertain that fantasy, eh?”

What kind of thrall was he, anyway?!

When I expected him to lift his hand from over my head, he lingered. I looked up confused, until he finally drew back softly and said, “You’ve gotten a lot stronger, haven’t you?”

I smiled. ‘Honestly, everything has been happening so fast that I haven’t given myself the time to pat myself on the back.’

“You’re almost at my level,” he said with a beaming smile on his face. “Heck, maybe I should feel intimidated.”

‘It’s complicated. I just hope it’ll all make sense soon.’ I turned to him, wanting to shift the topic on him instead. ‘So, um, you’re okay, right? Despite the damage from the flesh pits?’
