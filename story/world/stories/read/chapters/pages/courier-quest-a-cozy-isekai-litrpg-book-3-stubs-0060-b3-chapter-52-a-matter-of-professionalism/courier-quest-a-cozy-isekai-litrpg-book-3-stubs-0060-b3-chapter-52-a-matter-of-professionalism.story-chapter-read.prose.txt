The weather stayed the same until the late hours of the next morning, though neither Trevor nor Adabelle minded. A more relaxed shift for their honeymoon was welcome after all of the excitement back in Aletander. They had spent the evening enjoying each other’s company and that was enough.

Clay continued to work, but did so happily. They agreed to hop into the backpack when it was time to leave. As much as they were enjoying themselves, there was no reason for them to stay closeted away.

Lora had left a note at the front desk telling them to be at the observatory around nightfall. It seemed that she was taking a day to recuperate and wouldn’t join them. After whisking them all over the world, they could understand why.

Their hotel was an opulent one situated on the cliffs near Floatstone Junction. It seemed to cater more to travelers on business than anyone else, though the staff were very accommodating to the newlyweds. A statue of one of the Altabrae men was prominent in the lobby, though they couldn’t tell which one since it was unmarked.

The smell of the sea and rain was thick as Trevor and Adabelle took their first steps outside. It was both cold and wet, the kind of weather that could easily cause sickness, but neither were worried about that. Not only did they have their stats to keep them healthy, but also Croal’s blessing.

They looked at Rabinar spread out before them. It was the only settlement on the mass of floating islands, which were connected by bridges wherever the traffic called for it. Some were steep enough to be called stairs since the sections weren’t level with each other. There were also tunnels leading to the docks that had been carved out underneath the town.

Thanks to Rashie’s efforts to find the rainbow fish, Trevor knew that it wasn’t just the stone up above the water that was suspended, but everything deep below them, too. It was a natural wonder of the world, completely unexplained despite all attempts to do so. At least, that’s what the minotaurs claimed.

Once Adabelle was done taking in the view, Trevor guided her gaze to one of the four natural towers that surrounded Rabinar. They were made up of different pieces of rock, giving them a disjointed look. If it weren’t for the fact that they were made of Floatstone, they would have toppled ages ago.

“That one there is the observatory, though you can’t really see it from this angle,” he explained, pointing.

This was the one they were closest to, and it ascended high into the sky. They had to almost bend their backs to see the top. It almost seemed to touch the clouds, though it wasn’t nearly tall enough for that. A metal staircase went around it, bolted into the stone, and they knew it was going to be a long climb.

Adabelle nodded before turning to glance at the other towers. “And what of this King of the Sea?”

During Trevor's first trip here, she had given him a very simple quest in an effort to add to her then-boyfriend’s ascension, and that was to get a spicy curry dish from an apparently legendary crab shack. What she hadn’t known until he returned was that it was also on top of one of these natural towers. The eccentric elf who ran it had fallen in love with the view and decided to build his restaurant where very few people could reach.

“Do you remember which one, Clay?” Trevor asked instead, tilting his head to address his backpack.

The earth elemental stilled for a moment before popping their head out. They slowly clambered onto their master’s shoulder before looking around the island. It only took them a moment before they pointed towards the one that seemed more modified than the rest.

“Yeah, great job,” he said, patting his familiar. “I’m not sure if Adabelle is going to want to try the challenge, but it was a lot more fun than we thought it would be, wasn’t it?”

Clay shook their head.

Trevor chuckled. “Alright, it was exactly as much fun as you thought it would be, and more fun than I thought it would be,” he corrected.

This time, Clay nodded.

“I wouldn’t mind checking it out now,” Adabelle said. Though it was a passive statement, it was clear that she wanted to go.

“A little hungry?” Trevor asked as he reached for her.

She ducked under his arm so it was around her shoulders. “I will be soon, I think,” she said before giving him an accusatory pout. “I might not be if it weren’t for the fact that you woke me up so early even though it was still storming, all because you couldn’t bear to be without my company for a moment longer.”

“Hmm, I guess that’s true. I really needed your affection,” he agreed, stroking his chin. “It’s not as though you didn’t enjoy mine.”

“Maybe, but this isn’t about how much I liked spending my morning with you, it’s about how I’m going to be hungry soon,” she replied, sticking her nose in the air haughtily. “Like right now, I decided. And, of course, you must fix this.”

“I must?” he asked, arching an eyebrow.

“Yes. Must,” Adabelle repeated. “In fact, I’ll even go so far as to insist.”

“Oh, no. I’m really in a pickle this time,” Trevor sighed.

The demonkin shook her head. “Mm, no. I’m not in the mood for pickles. Try again.”

“I’m really in a jam?”

“Better, but I clearly want some curry for lunch, son of Ander.”

“Oh, curry!” he replied, tapping his forehead. “That’s easy. There’s this nice place right around the corner and then, like, a thousand steps up. We can go right now, actually. Man, I really thought I was in trouble for a second there, Clay.”

His familiar patted his shoulder to reassure him, and the Summoned laughed together. Adabelle was quick to kiss her husband on the cheek before pulling him towards the stairs. She deftly navigated the town, slowing her quest for curry only at the bridges to look down at the cavernous ravines below. It seemed as though the demonkin had mapped out how to get there from higher ground.

They arrived at the bottom of the natural tower. The sign was still here, listing the menu, prices, and the rules of the King of the Sea Tower Challenge that had allowed Trevor to win a lifetime of free meals thanks to Clay.

“You did good work, little buddy,” he said absently, petting the shoulder stoat as he looked upwards. They preened at the attention.

“There doesn’t seem to be anyone here,” Adabelle noted, looking around.

“I hope they’re open,” Trevor said, frowning. “I didn’t think about that; they might not stay for the whole off-season.”

Heading for the tower, he stopped in front of the horn that Aldern had used to send orders up to Alder, his father and the restaurant’s chef. It was connected to a pipe that traveled all the way up to the top. Taking a moment, he cleared his throat.

“Hello, excuse me. I was wondering if you were open, or would be later,” Trevor said.

He waited for a moment, listening for a response. There was only silence.

“Slightly disappointing, but I’m sure very few would want to ascend a tower like this after last night’s storm,” Adabelle stated. “If the shack is even still standing. From the way the wind howled, I could have sworn Rabinar was haunted by a banshee.”

“Run into many of those?” the courier asked.

“No, and I’m glad I haven’t,” she admitted with a thin smile. “It takes a special kind of fortitude to take on such foul creatures, and it's not something covered by our stats. You never know until it’s happened, so I’d rather not risk it.”

“Yeah, that sounds fair,” Trevor chuckled before looking back at the horn. “Well, we still have the ballabroody, but I think Lora would be sad if we ate it without her.”

“Very likely.”

“So how about we—”

“Now hold on just a moment, you’re not giving me time to get ready,” came a voice from the horn. Trevor smiled at the man’s accent, which still struck him as a Californian surfer who lived on the beach. It really wasn’t that far off, either. “We’re not open just yet, but you can come back in an hour or so.”

“Would you mind if we came up to enjoy the view?” the courier requested. “I took your challenge last year, and one of my favorite moments was seeing the Floating Isles from so high up. You might have remembered the little stone stoat and shark girl I came with.”

“Oh! Is that you, Trevor?” Alder asked.

He smiled. “Yeah, it is. I’m surprised you remembered my name.”

“That part’s easy. I remember everyone that goes up on my wall. That’s you, Rashie, and Clay. Are they with you?”

Trevor shook his head on impulse even though he couldn’t be seen. “Clay is here with me, but I’m actually with Adabelle this time. She was the one who gave me the quest to bring back some of your crab and curry.”

“I remember. That was your girl back home, right?” Alder asked.

“Yes, sir. Though, now she’s my wife. We’re on our honeymoon.”

“Honeymoon!” the elf exclaimed loudly, causing no small amount of feedback to come through the horn. Adabelle’s ears twitched, and even Trevor winced at the noise. “And you came to eat my food? Forget an hour, come on up. I’ll put something special on for you. Was she one of the spicy orders?”

“Yes, please,” the demonkin answered for herself. “You even remember his order?”

“Three mild, two spicy, with him eating one and Rashie eating the other,” Alder said proudly. “It’s a matter of professionalism for me to remember the orders of those talented enough to be put on the wall, whether it’s a name or order. But I’m just keeping you now. Come on up, and I’ll get you sorted. Careful of the stairs, though; they get slippery.”

“We’ll be careful, thanks Mr. Alder,” Trevor said. He turned to his wife when there was no response. “Looks like we’re in luck. No need to be disappointed after all.”

Adabelle smiled. “Come on, let’s not keep him waiting.”

The demonkin went up first with an undeniable bounce to each step. Her husband followed, happy that she was so enthusiastic. She had absolutely loved the food he had brought her from the King of the Sea, and he could admit that he was ready for more.

Walking up the stairs was also a much more easygoing prospect than the mad dash they had been asked to perform previously even if Adabelle set the pace. Regardless, Clay jumped off of their master’s shoulder and disappeared into the floating stone. They swam through it like a shark, even going so far as to make a fin like some of Rashie's outfits, and went ahead.

Trevor had expected to be winded when he neared the top, but found that it had merely been a pleasant workout despite climbing hundreds of feet worth of stairs. Adabelle waited for him at the last landing before she would reach the crab shack, smiling before offering her hand. He took it without hesitation, and they took the last few steps together.

The wind had been whipping at them freely, but now there was a distinct lack of air flow. It was even warmer than expected despite their high altitude, and not even the grass was wet. Trevor turned to look at the crab shack to see that it was no worse for wear from the storm. Alder was hard at work cooking, but he wasn't so busy that he couldn’t wave.

“Wow,” Adabelle said as she looked over the edge. “You jumped off of this?”

“Against my better judgment, yes,” Trevor admitted with a chuckle as he turned to take in the view. “But there’s an enchantment that makes it so you slow down before you hit the ground. I don’t think we’ll be using it this time.”

“Even though it does sound fun,” she replied.

“It was, but we have to test these out somewhere high up,” he said, gesturing towards the Feather of the Graceful pinned to his chest. “And this is as safe a place as ever to do it.”

“That is a very fair assessment,” Adabelle agreed, leaning her head against his shoulder as she wrapped her arms around him. “The way you described this scene made me very envious of your travels, by the way.”

“Oh?” Trevor prompted.

“Yes, and I can see I was completely justified,” she continued with a nod. “This is the kind of view I used to travel to see. A wonderful landscape composed of broken, floating rocks lies before us, upon which lies a town of hearty settlers. It might have been for capitalistic gain, as the merchant princes generally strive for, but there’s something… beautiful about the lengths people will go through to thrive. Add in a dash of the magical and I think it’s wonderful.”

“I didn’t think about that the last time I was here, but I can see where you’re coming from,” he replied, putting an arm over her shoulder.

They looked down on the town and the island for a few minutes, basking in the view, until even Clay came up to join them. The earth elemental shook off the dirt they had accumulated before clambering up Trevor’s side. They gazed out over the island before turning to the shack, waving at Alder, and then disappearing into their master’s backpack.

“All done for you!” the tanned elven chef announced, knocking the two out of their wonder.

“Already?” Trevor asked, surprised. “You finished that fast. I didn’t think we took so long getting up here.”

“The King of the Sea prides itself on great service, especially when cooking for a friend on a big trip,” Alder said with a grin. “Congrats, by the way!”

“Thank you,” Adabelle said as she took in the restaurant. It really wasn’t much more than a shack with a few stools at its bar. If she was disappointed, she didn’t show it. “I’m happy to be here, and to eat more of your wonderful food.”

“I’m just glad it was good enough for it to stick with you,” he replied, setting their plates on the bar. “But, between you and me, you don’t want to eat them here.”

“We don’t?” Trevor asked.

Alder shook his head. “Nah. You can keep it fresh, right? Fresh enough to take across the sea, at least.”

“I can.”

“Then if you can wait to eat, then you should head over there,” he said, pointing towards the second tallest tower. It was a little hard to see, but it looked like there was a glass sphere that encompassed the top.

“What is that?” Trevor asked, squinting to see better. “Was that there last time?”

“That’s the viewing platform and, no, it’s new,” Alder explained. “There’s a few seats inside that spin around the dome, giving you a complete view of the Floating Isles. It was set up by the Altabrae family to give tourists another reason to come here, but even I have to admit that it’s a nice place. If you don’t mind a gentle spin, that’s where you should eat. Plus, they’ve got one of those newfangled elevators, so the trip up is easy.”

“That does sound delightful,” Adabelle said before looking at Trevor. “What do you say?”

“You’re the one who insisted we eat,” he teased.

She gave him a look before placing a hand on her cheek and sighing. “I suppose there’s no helping it, then,” the noblewoman imperiously declared, her tail sharply swiping the air. Trevor stifled a chuckle at her tone. “You’d best make sure my food arrives there before I do, son of Ander, or we may have a problem.”

“I wouldn’t want to have any problems.”

“Then you better be fast, because—”

Adabelle didn’t finish her sentence. Instead, she took off like a shot. She jumped, spinning to wink and grin at her husband before disappearing from his sight.

“Oh no you don’t,” Trevor laughed as he sent the two plates to his inventory. “Thanks for the tip, Alder, and for the food. Take care!”

“You, too!” the elven chef said as the courier began running. “Don’t be a stranger!”

With a wave, Trevor also jumped off of the edge. He saw Adabelle below, but the moment she got eyes on him she activated her Feather of the Graceful on her belt. Her clothes rippled, and a white blanket came out of her arms and sides to form a wing suit. It caught the air, slowing her down, and she started gliding towards the other tower.

Trevor was right behind her, though he activated his much earlier. With a shout and a grin, he sailed through the air. The magic would only last for a few minutes, more than enough time to make it to their destination, but he would enjoy it while it lasted.
