"You really just put your hands on her stuff? You really are stupid, Lena," Ren said. The Sigil on her arm blazed. The air still shimmered with heat where her blast of fire had passed through.

Lena glared at Ren, while her two companions moved. One of them took a step backward. The other one, the rounder one, was trying very hard to appear taller than she was by standing on her tiptoes. "Stay out of this, Ren. This has nothing to do with you. Mind your own business for once," Lena snapped.

Ren raised an eyebrow and pointed at the satchel. "You tried to take her stuff without asking. I bet you tried to threaten her before that too, didn't you? Well, Lena, I'd say this is every bit my goddamned business. Don't fuck with my friends." Ren's mark blazed again for just a moment.

Friend? I had not given her that privilege. I almost pointed this out. Lena spoke first. "She's sitting at our table, Ren. Don't tell me you're blind on top of-"

"Your table?" Ren cut her off with a laugh. "Don't see your name anywhere here. You've got a big head for someone who barely even made Sigil. Claiming the furniture now are ya?"

That was likely the wrong thing to say. Even I knew that. It was obvious from the way Lena's face reddened, and the way her jaw set. Blue lightning crackled across her skin again, and her Sigil was growing too.

It was clear that Ren had done the opposite of defusing whatever situation this was. She had poured oil on twigs and then lit the match.

I could see it in the way Lena's jaw set and in the flare of blue across her Sigil. Lightning crackled along her forearms, dancing between her fingers. I had known many generals with the same talent. I felt the weight of Essence from this Lena.

"Fine." Ren's voice dropped. "Let's do a ranking match then. You and me. Tomorrow, in the yard. Actually, scratch that. Let's do it in the Arena." She cracked her knuckles. "What do you say? If I win, I get your slot. You win, I'll shut the hell up. Come on, what's the matter? If you're really such hot stuff, it should be easy, right?"

Lena went still. A ranking match. The girl had put weight on those two words. I did not yet know what it meant, but these girls seemed to think it important.

Lena's mark dimmed. "Watch your back, half-breed." The words were aimed at me, even though she was looking at Ren. Lena rose from the bench, and her two companions followed. They moved to the first table without so much as looking back.

The dining hall slowly resumed its noise, and this time, many people were looking at Ren instead of me. Ren dropped onto the bench next to me. She was still breathing a little fast. She looked at me sideways, as if waiting for something. Gratitude, perhaps. Or praise. "What business was that of yours?" I said instead.

Ren blinked. "What?"

"I did not ask for your interference. I was handling it."

"Handling it? All you were doing was eating!"

"Yes. I was handling it by eating."

Ren stared at me. Then she shrugged. "Whatever. Lena's always pissed me off. Walks around like the Divine's gift to Arianeth just because her daddy is the Marquis. I thought I told you not to sit here?" She paused, as if waiting for an answer. "Well, whatever. I guess you did good. It was funny to see her face do that at the end."

I did not tell her I had simply forgotten her warning about the table. There was no need to diminish whatever image she had constructed. The moment the girl had settled, my thoughts went elsewhere. To the place they had been circling for some time. I turned to Ren. "Lead me back to the measuring hall."

Ren tilted her head. "The measuring hall? Why the hell do you wanna go there? The broken Arbiter's being hauled away piece by piece -thanks for that, by the way. They made everyone leave. The whole place is-"

"Where is Ash then? Have you seen her?" I cut her off.

Ren's brow furrowed. Then her eyes widened and she nodded. "Ohhh. Hmm…" She seemed to be thinking. Though, that was an ability I was unsure she possessed. "Last I saw, all the instructors were crowding around her, asking her all kinds of questions. Who the hell knows where she ended up." She shrugged. "She'll be fine, yeah? The Instructors are always annoying when they see someone with potential. And she's a human so…" She trailed off.

I said nothing. Being accosted by these small men and women would explain her absence. It did not quite justify a retainer being late to attend to their Queen but it would save her a beheading. She would handle herself. I hoped she was alright.

Then I frowned. What, exactly was I to do now? The resonance chamber I had been to was likely useless to me now. Ash was somewhere I could not find, and somewhere I did not trust this girl to lead. Perhaps I should march over to the Archon and demand to see this chamber he mentioned? No…I would do that with Ash. It was a dilemma.

"Girl," I said.

Ren stared at me. "My name is Ren. You know my name, come on."

"Tell me where I might learn more."

Ren stared at me even harder. "More about…what?"

"About everything, of course," I said. "Places like this Academy are known to have facilities like this. I believe you call them 'libraries'." The one in my fortress had been called 'The Black Archive'. Humans did have rather simple names for everything.

Ren's head tilted the other way. "You mean like…the Athenaeum?"

"Athenaeum." I slowly tested the word. I had never heard it before, and the name alone was enough to make me much more interested. or all places worth going to had names that matched. This Athenaeum had the right amount of syllables in its name. "Yes. Lead me to this 'Athenaeum'," I said.

Ren raised an eyebrow and looked at the hall around us, then back to me. Strangely, the girl smiled. "Alright, can't say I get the appeal of that stuffy place but whatever. There are worse places to be. Let's go."

It took perhaps ten minutes for Ren to lead me to this 'Athenaeum." It was a large building somewhere in the Academy's northern quarter. The whole thing was carved from what must have been the same pale stone as the upper walls of Koralis. Compared to the rest of what I had seen at this Academy, this one structure could almost be considered impressive. Almost. I stared at it from the outside. Then stared at Ren. "What?" she asked, meeting my gaze.

"This was not part of your 'tour'. If I had not asked myself, then you would never take me here."

The girl shrugged, put her hands behind her head, and started walking. "Yeah, cos it's lame. It's just some books."

Ash. I will forgive you if you spare me the company of this girl. I pulled the satchel closer and followed behind Ren and stepped through the entrance. It was a pair of arched doors, flanked by large pillars that bore elaborate carvings. The carvings were clearly meant to depict dragons of some kind.

Even after centuries of living, I still did not understand the human and elven obsession with Dragons. Hydras had always been markedly better. On that, I and the Beastkin had always been in agreement.

The inside was massive. For a heartbeat my breath stopped entirely. The central space we stood in stretched high to a ceiling so far up that the light looked like it came from a different sky altogether. This Athenaeum was divided into several levels, each of them lined with balconies. I counted at least five, all of them filled with massive shelves that stretched far past where I could see. Everything stretched into a distance that should not have been possible in these walls. That surely was not possible.

Then, there were the books themselves. They flew. They flew through the air before landing into a waiting person's outstretched hand. Lanterns hung in the open air between all of the balconies, and they were not attached to anything at all.

Staircases connected each balcony to the one above it, and even they were moving. The one on my left moved to the right. Its lower steps remained where they were while the upper flight detached, and reconnected to a different balcony on the floor above.

A student was walking up it and did not so much as break stride. As if this was normal, and he trusted it completely. I could not help but suspect the entire reason the steps had moved at all was because of him.

I had never seen this Athenaeum's like before. A place like this would never have existed in my time. There, all knowledge was valuable, and so had been both hoarded and protected. The Dwarves had carved their secrets into vaults of stone that even I had struggled to breach. The Elves had woven theirs into living wood that burned when read by outsiders. The humans had put all of their libraries behind dozens of layers of wards. The Dragons had hidden theirs on great peaks where there was no air. Yes, a place like this would never have existed in my time.

Hunger stirred in me. A feeling that had nothing to do with either of my marks. The hunger for knowledge had always been my oldest and sharpest, and right now it ached. I reached for the word adequate, and found it wanting. Why had this fool girl not shown me this place first?! I stepped forward.

"Card first. I don't recognize you." A woman sat behind a desk near the entrance. She was thin, and wore large spectacles. The woman was currently frowning. She held out a hand towards me without even looking up from her ledger. Said arm had a Crest, one in the form of a book made out of black ink.

Ren looked at me. I looked at her. Then, at the woman. "I do not have this card you speak of."

"Then you do not enter."

"Then make one of these cards for me."

The woman looked up. Her gaze moved over me. She was perhaps the only person in this Academy whose gaze didn't go to my horns at all. Instead, she looked at my uniform, then looked at Ren.

"She's new?"

Ren nodded. "Yes, only been a few days."

The woman sighed, reached beneath her desk, produced paper, and slid it across the surface of her desk without commentary. She looked back down again. "Just fill this form out. Your card will be ready in three days."

I took the form from her and looked it over. Somehow, the questions were directly carved into the paper itself. I saw them all in neat lettering, and there were quite a few of these questions. I looked up from the paper and at Ren. "You just answer the questions then give it back to her. Might take a few minutes, but you only have to do this once," she said, as if she were explaining something to an idiot. She sighed and stepped closer. "Channel a bit of mana or Essence and think of the answer. It'll write itself out."

Everything I learned about this Athenaeum just made it sound stranger and stranger. I looked back at the woman. A mark in black ink. A Sovereign and something to do with…books? I looked back at the questions. Most of them were rather simple.

Full Name? I channeled the barest of Essence and thought of my answer. Lysanthia. Just as Ren had said, the words carved themselves on the empty space in front of the question.

Family Name? I channeled Essence again. None. Demons did not have family names. They had clan names. I wondered if that was true for half-demons.

Place of Residence? I paused as I read this. I had lived in one place for the majority of my reign. That place was gone now, and so I did not have an answer. I wrote The Dark Tower anyway. Another name came, one I banished.

I heard a sound from my right. I did not turn, but I was aware of Ren, standing three paces behind me, her head craned to read over my shoulder.

Emergency Contact? I did not understand this question. The words were clear enough, but the concept behind them was foreign. Ren leaned closer. "It means who they should tell if something happens to you," she whispered. "Like if you get hurt or something. They need to know who to tell, so they can come and find you. Get it?"

Ren's explanation did tell me what it was, but it confused me more all the same. In every emergency I had ever faced, I had simply resolved the matter myself. A part of me considered writing Ash's name, but why would I? If I were dying, I would not wish for someone to tell Ash. I would simply not die. I glared at the form. I thought the word: Unnecessary. Ren let out another sound, one that was louder than it had been last time.

Reason for Visit. This, at last, was a question worth answering. To acquire all the knowledge contained here.

I looked at Ren. This time she had let out a sound that could only be a groan. Her cheeks were pink, and she was pointedly shaking her head. I ignored her as I moved to slide the form back across the desk.

The librarian picked it up. She read it. Her left eyebrow rose a fraction of an inch and held there for a long time. She read it again, more slowly. "Three days. You'll get your card in three days. Come back here then," she said, and filed the form without further comment.

I made to move deeper inside the library. Ren scrambled to follow. "Three. Days." The woman did not raise her voice. "You can wait. This place isn't going anywhere, girl."

Girl?! I considered my options. They were limited. I did not know what I was supposed to do for three entire days. It did not matter that I hadn't known this place existed an hour ago.

"Hey!" Ren slid in beside me, producing a small wooden card from somewhere in her uniform. She held it up with a flourish. Or at least, I think it was meant to be a flourish. "She's with me, Madam Oris," Ren said. "So, you have nothing to worry about. I'll watch right over her the whole time."

The librarian regarded Ren the way one might regard a city where rebels kept appearing. Then she looked at me. "If she damages a single page, Ren-"

"She won't! Probably."

The woman sighed. She waved us through. Perhaps this Ren was not entirely useless after all.
