Prator closed the door behind him, wiping sweat from his brow. His face dissolved from the friendly, accommodating mask into pure exhaustion.

“That was close,” he said.

“She’s going to come back,” Elion said. “They’re going to track me here.”

“No,” Bortim disagreed. “After hearing about that mechanism they’re using to track you, I went out to see what I could learn about it. It’s quite an intricate piece of work they’ve got going, and I’m quite impressed with what they’ve managed to do. But they seem to have a lack of understanding of bond types, so I can interfere with it.”

“Bond types?” Elion asked. “What do you mean? That thing already lead them right to me once. I was lucky to escape.”

“They’re using it to spatially track symbolic bonds. It’s… a pretty ingenious bit of thinking.” Bortim said this with a grudging tone. “But the problem is that symbolic bonds don’t have to conform to our conceptualizations of space. They often do, but they don’t have to. Your bond to the blade will usually point straight to you.”

Bortim smiled, gesturing with his hands as he spoke. “But a skilled Binder, like myself, can push and pull on that bond, shifting it around through space. If they come near us with their machine, I can redirect them.”

Elion didn’t understand Bortim’s reasoning, but the man seemed confident that he could interfere with the warlock’s abilities to locate him.

“Satisfied?” Prator asked, clapping a hand on Elion’s shoulder. “Good. Now what were we talking about before we were interrupted? Oaths?”

Elion shifted his weight from foot to foot. Venya had been talking about Keyla, just before she left. Her words rattled around Elion’s brain, distracting him. ‘We’ll deal with her,’ had an ominous sound to it that distressed him.

“What did Venya mean, ‘Deal with her?’” Elion asked.

“You mean the girl she found?” Prator shrugged. “Probably some kind of aggressive warlock interrogation technique. Painful but effective. You know the girl well?”

Elion clenched his fists. “I can’t abandon her to Venya.”

“Was she involved in the assassination attempt?” Prator asked. “Or is that another of Venya’s lies?”

“Another lie,” Elion said. “She had nothing to do with it.”

“Then we’ll need to come up with a plan,” Prator said. “See how we can help her. But first, we had better get to the matter at hand. I wanted to ask you a few things.” He turned to address Bortim.

“Stay here and if the warlocks get too close, divert them.”

Prator led Elion back through the mansion.

“There’s a lot that you don’t know. You have not had the formal training of an Aurelian,” Prator said.

They emerged into a central courtyard. An Aurelian Altar stood in the middle of the courtyard. A tarp protected it from the sky, hoisted over the statue with ropes tied to the second floor banister.

“I extracted it and brought it here,” Prator said. Dirt still clung to the crevices of the sculpted woman, though efforts had been made to clean her off. “A risky move on my part,” he said. “But I wanted the protection the Altar can provide.

They passed through the courtyard and up a staircase, stopping on the second level of the mansion. As they passed out of the entrance hall, the elegant decor of the building changed. Signs of fire damage, weathering, and missing panels gave way to patches made of unusual materials.

“The grand hall and front facade of this building are likely pre-cataclysm,” Prator explained as they walked down the hall. “The rest of the building was destroyed at that time, but the beauty of the grand hall has been supplemented and maintained by various groups over the years. Before me, the original settlers of New Kairn Tol.”

They passed a portion of the wall that had been painted to imitate the decorative wood panels of the entrance hall. "As you can see,” Prator said, “We lack the tools, the supplies, and in some cases the art necessary to replace the work of the original builders. But we do our best to maintain this piece of history.”

They entered a small room toward the back of the structure. Pieces of machinery and equipment stood around the room. A soft hum filled the air.

At the back of the room, a window stood partially open, allowing a cool evening breeze to swirl through the machines. Many of the devices glowed with the familiar turquoise light of Artefin manufacture, but Elion could not place the make of other artifacts.

Prator crossed the room and sat beside a silvery machine, shimmering in a way that reminded Elion of the Skyskimmer. On a table nearby, someone had been working to repair one of the devices. Metal brackets, electronic components, and shimmering pearlescent disks were carefully arranged. A selection of rubber plugs and gaskets sat in a pile beside some screws and a pair of pliers.

Manipulating the controls of the machine, Prator pulled up a screen filled with lines of text. As he looked, Elion patted his pocket, feeling the green putty he’d concealed there. *Not yet. I need to wait for the right time.*

“Things are falling into place for me,” Prator said. “The strange events of the past few weeks take shape, and your presence is key to helping me decipher it all. I help you, you help me. Honesty is a core tenet of Aurelian ethos. I can forgive your past dissembling but I expect you to be honest with me now.”

He tapped on the screen. “Here’s a message log, discussing fighting taking place in Erod, the capital. It started about three weeks ago.”

Elion leaned forward, looking at the log as Praxis translated the characters for him. He made out a few messages, but they were filled with terms and references that he didn’t understand. Elion wondered if this might be the Aurelian Communications network that he and Gorman used to locate Zev.

“What is this machine?” Elion asked, touching the smooth, white curves. Reminding him of the Skyskimmer, it looked like something he’d expect to find in an Apple Store back on Earth.

“It’s a communicator,” Prator said. “But that’s besides the point. Warlocks are venturing out in larger numbers, roaming around, searching for something. Normally New Kairn Tol might have one or two rotating warlocks here, dropping in to keep an eye on our Thrandicite production at the Acid Pits. But Venya’s presence here… that was unusual. Then I realized who you were.

“I’ve been watching the messages, hearing rumors of armies forming up, of heavy fighting in Erod. I’ve heard of rebels rallying to the flag of a great leader there. It makes me wonder… but back to the issue at hand. Today my scouts confirmed that a force has come down along the Ater River from the direction of Upprifer. It now lies camped on the plain between New Kairn Tol and the Aterfel. I do not know what their goals are.

“Since you were recently in Aterfel, I hope you can enlighten me.”

"I don't know," Elion said, "They did mention Upprifer. They had called for reinforcements from neighboring towns to help in the attack to destroy the Shard. Gorman said that a force was coming from up river in three days, but the situation evolved and the fighting started before the reinforcements arrived. That’s all I know.”

"Is it?" Prator said, folding his arms. He leaned back in the chair, swiveling around to face Elion. Although he sat, he managed to loom over Elion.

Elion’s mind raced, trying to understand what Prator might be getting at. He struggled to remember what he had revealed already. He knew he had not mentioned Zev’s presence on Kylios, and had intentionally avoided mention of Liora’s existence. He didn’t see how the army from Upprifer camping out on the plains would have lead Prator to suspect those secrets.

"That's all," Elion said. "I don't have anything else to contribute."

Prator stroked his beard. "The timing is strange. Perhaps you are ignorant of it. I just can’t help but wonder if your sudden reappearance has anything to do with the unrest across Erod.”

The machine behind him pinged, a new message appearing on the screen. Prator turned to examine it. Elion saw Zev’s name translated, and his heart pounded.

“This is what I’m talking about,” Prator said, pointing to the message. “All kinds of speculation about who might be leading the resistance. One name floats to the top; Zev Starholder.”

Elion clutched his hands behind his back.

“I did not believe your story at first,” Prator continued. “I believed that you believed it, but I thought it might be some kind of trick, or ploy, or that there were important details that you didn’t understand. But I’m coming around to the idea that everything you told me was true. I’m starting to believe that you might just be the lost heir to the Throne.”

“It’s the truth,” Elion said.

“You told me Zev died.”

Prator examined Elion’s face. His pulse quickened, face flushing nervously. Elion hadn’t lied to Prator, but he had allowed him to believe that untruth.

“But you didn’t tell me that, did you?” Realization spread across Prator’s face. “You said they killed your family, and I assumed…” He laughed. “Well you know what they say about assuming! It makes a two tailed pemalion of us.”

The man chuckled at the joke, though Elion couldn’t help but feel like its meaning was lost in translation.

“Now, Elion, carefully consider your oaths before you answer this question. Is Zev alive? Is he on Kylios?”

Elion had promised to wield his sword in defense of truth. What did that mean in this situation? How was he expected to respond to this question? Did defending the truth mean telling the truth? Or did it mean obscuring the truth, to protect those he loved? He suddenly understood Prator’s earlier lecture about the difficulty of navigating the oaths.

But Elion had no real reason to lie to Prator, save a general wariness of the man. Elion met Prator’s gaze.

“As far as I know Zev is here on Kylios. He was not killed by Dorian when my mother was.”

Prator sprang from his chair. “What is he doing? Why is he in Erod, while you are in Aterfel? How did this happen? Tell me everything!”

Elion recalled telling his story to Gorman. That had turned out to be a poor decision, despite how trustworthy Gorman had seemed to begin with. Now Gorman was dead, and nobody in New Kairn Tol save Elion and Keyla knew the truth. He couldn’t bring himself to share everything with Prator. Something at the back of his mind warned him that if Prator knew about Liora, he’d find someway to exploit it.

“When Dorian came for me, we were separated,” Elion said. “I managed to use a device on Aterfel to contact him. He knows I was there. I assume he is doing everything in his power to get to me, to bring me back to Earth and out of Dorian’s hands.”

“So it’s true then? Zev leads the resistance in Erod?” Prator clutched his head in his hands, pacing back and forth. “It is so clear,” Prator said. “He’s not coming for you, boy, he’s going for Dorian. He’s fighting Dorian’s Black Wall. He’s going to overthrow him and place you upon the throne.” Prator grew more animated as he spoke. “This is what we have all been waiting for!”

“I don’t think that’s his plan,” Elion said. He recalled Zev’s words to him, before he left Earth to pursue Liora. “I think he intends to bring me back into hiding.”

“Nonsense,” Prator said, waving Elion’s words aside. “You were not safe there, not since Dorian discovered you. There is only one path forward. We will go to war. We will join Zev’s forces in Erod; we should leave tonight.”

“No!” Elion exclaimed, his assertion more forceful than he’d intended.

Prator stopped, turning curiously to Elion. “Why not?”

“Keyla,” Elion said. “I can’t abandon her.”

“And if I order you?” Prator asked, reaching for the Bondhold around his neck. “For the greater good of Erod, for the preservation of the Aurelian Ascendency?”

Discomfort welled in the back of Elion’s mind. He imagined Venya seizing Keyla, torturing her with amethyst warlock power. His eyes strayed to the open window at the side of the room. Wind shifted the maroon curtains softly. He thought he heard screams in the distance.

“I have sworn oaths, Prator,” Elion said, shaking his head. He could feel the man weighing his options. “If I have to abandon my bond to live true to them, I will.”

“Hah,” Prator said. “Loyal to your oaths when they are convenient to you. That is not the way of things.”

“It isn’t?” Elion spat. “You enslave people and work as one of Dorian’s minions, but you want to join the resistance as soon as you think they’ll win?”

“Do not speak to me of things about which you know nothing,” Prator spat. “For twenty years I have waited, watched as our Ascendency has been systematically eradicated, our people killed, and our Altars blasted from the face of the earth.”

Elion edged toward the table, getting further from his captor and closer to the window. Prator paced, gesturing emphatically as he spoke. When he turned his back, Elion reached into his pocket, retrieving the ball of putty.

“Face the problems in front of you, in New Kairn Tol,” Elion said. “My friend will be tortured by our enemies.” He kept his hands hidden beneath the table as he rolled the putty into two cylinders.

“None of that matters!” Prator shouted. “My small corner of the world, my little knife edge, balanced between usefulness to Dorian’s warlocks and violation of my own oaths, it’s all meaningless now. I hate them! I despise my existence. We will go to Erod. We will join Zev and reclaim the Golden Throne, tonight!”

Elion reached for his ears.

“Yes, tonight,” Prator said. “We have a small, loyal force who will go with me. We can bring the arachnatronics. We cannot delay, cannot allow Venya a chance to catch wind of us. Without transport, this is our best window…

Elion jammed the earplugs into his ears, pressing onto them hard to create a clean seal.

What are you doing?” Prator asked as Elion finished pushing the plugs in.

Prator’s mouth moved, but Elion could not hear him. Elion ran for the open window.
