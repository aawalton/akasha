Despite having difficulty waking up in the morning, Callie still managed to catch her brother before he left the house for his training session with Mister Blackthorn. She explained to him the situation with the egg and her guess that it was the merchant who had done the evil deed. In the stables where Mom and Dad couldn't overhear them.

"You think it's merchant Silas?" Callen arched an eyebrow.

"I don't know his name, but we currently do have only one merchant in town for the summer, right?" She asked. She had seen the man a few times in passing, but never actually spoken to him.

"Yes, but I highly doubt it is him."

"What? Why?"

"Because I don't see a reason why he would have to do that. He has plenty of things to sell, including lots of sugar and salt. I don't think he's lacking money. I also don't see a reason why he would go out of his way to approach dangerous mon—I mean, arcane beasts—if he can just trade within the safety of the village," Callen explained.

Callie had to admit that what her brother said was logical. But it wasn't proof of his innocence. "But he's the only outsider! I think we should in-investigate him!"

Her brother smiled and ruffled her hair roughly.

"Hey, what was that for?!" Callie tried to push his large hand away from her hair.

"I think it's cute you're trying to play the guard here."

"I am not playing! This is serious!" Callie puffed out her cheek to show her frustration.

Callen just poked her cheek, forcing her to let out the air. "The situation is quite serious, but how do you intend to approach this? You're going to walk up to him and ask to return the egg?"

She blinked a few times, realizing that she indeed had no clue how to approach this problem. Asking the thief outright whether he had the stolen egg would undoubtedly result in him running away, or hiding it, or even worse—breaking the egg.

"Brother, what do I do?" Callie asked.

"Just go about your work as usual. I'm going to talk to Silas some more, see whether I can get a better feel for his character," Callen said, smoothing out the hair he had messed up before. "Don't worry, I won't reveal anything about the egg, okay?"

"I… Okay…" Callie said dejectedly, feeling that she had been pushed to the sidelines.

"Good girl. Now hurry along to Granny Ethel's. I promise to bring you my findings in the evening," Callen said with a smile and left.

She couldn't help but feel conflicted. Could Callen really tell if the merchant was trustworthy by just talking to him a little? She felt that a thief would be smarter than that. But unfortunately, she didn't have the time to check the matter herself. She was still under punishment and with her apprenticeship and Mister Blackthorn's lessons, her day was filled to the brim.

Maybe I can check places while walking through the village. Just the most obvious ones.

With that idea in mind, Callie decided that the first place to check would be the neighbor's barn. An unlikely place to hide the egg, but it was warm, and certain corners were bound to be dark and pleasantly moist for the egg.

The adults should be gone for work, and even if Callie got caught, they wouldn't mind. She would occasionally visit the neighbor's barn to look at their cow and their goats. After all, Callie's family didn't have any.

However, as soon as she slipped inside the large building, she came face to face with Tristan, pouring feed into the cow's trough.

"What are you doing here?" Tristan narrowed his eyes.

She just gaped at him like a fish. Tristan should have gone to Granny Ethel's already, but it appeared that he was running late. Oh, she should have known. The boy was always running late.

"I… umm… came to look at your cow!" Callie pointed at the cow in question, who looked completely uninterested in what was going on, merely chewing a piece of hay.

"So early in the morning? I would have expected you to be at the healer's hut already."

"Why aren't you at the hut yet?"

"I slept in," Tristan spat and took a step closer to Callie. "Why are you in our barn? You've been acting strange lately. Even more strange than usual."

"I…I said I just wanted to look at your cow. I-I'm going to get going now!" Callie said and turned around to get away from the boy, but before she could, he had already run up to her and grabbed her hand.

"What are you doing here?" Tristan demanded.

"Let go!" Callie shrieked, trying to pry herself free from the bully.

"Stop squirming!" He said, pulling on her hand.

"Let go! That hurts!"

To her surprise, he actually did let go and mumbled something Callie couldn't quite hear.

"What?" She asked as she took a few steps away from Tristan, cradling her arm close.

"ARGH! I said I'm sorry!"

Callie blinked, staring at Tristan in bewilderment. Was that really Tristan? No, no, no. It must be some fever dream. There was no way Tristan would ever apologize to her. Callie pinched herself to wake up, but the pinch actually hurt. So it wasn't a dream.

"A-Are you perhaps ill?" Callie asked.

"I am not ill!" He snapped, then kicked his feet into the ground while staring at it. "I just… I…"

He muttered something Callie couldn't hear again.

"What?" Callie turned her ear towards him.

"I never meant to hurt you! Alright?!" He shouted so loudly that even the cow looked at him strangely.

Callie stared at the boy in disbelief, his cheeks turning the color of red apples. Was the world coming to an end?

"Is… Is this about that time you smashed my nose?"

"How was I supposed to know that you would jump in to protect a monster?" Tristan snapped.

"You were going to kill an innocent mireling!"

"But it's a monster! It can hurt you!"

"It's not a monster!" Callie protested and turned to leave.

"I don't get it. Why do you like monsters so much? They are dangerous. They kill people. You surely know that!"

Callie turned around, staring at the boy. "They are dangerous, but that doesn't mean that they only want to kill. The mireling you attacked had done nothing wrong!"

"Mirelings eat our crops! Their acid burns our skin," Tristan argued.

"No, it doesn't! They only burn people that they think are dangerous," she protested.

"How would you know that?" Tristan raised an eyebrow.

Callie just wanted to say that she had touched and held one in her arms, but obviously, she couldn't reveal Wiggles so carelessly to Tristan. "I watched one up close for hours, and it didn't do anything to me. Until you came and scared it away!"

Tristan flinched back at that. "B-But it could have hurt you!"

"But it didn't!"

Tristan gritted his teeth and scratched his head awkwardly. "ARGH! You're so annoying, you know that!"

With that, he dumped the rest of the feed into the trough and ran off, glancing back. "Don't be late!"

"What a weirdo…" Callie muttered, staring after the boy. She couldn't understand him at all. But he did apologize.

No, it must have been Granny Ethel who forced him to do that. No way he would actually mean it.

At least with Tristan gone, Callie could carefully examine the barn. It actually didn't seem as good a place for hiding an egg as she had thought. The pens were large, and while they were filled with hay, there weren't exactly any cozy corners. Barn animals could also trample the egg.

Not that she had actually suspected Tristan or his family. But now she knew that searching through other barns would likely be a waste of her time. Callie would have to look into some smaller places.

However, as she left the barn, Callie noticed something odd. The neighbor's family cat, usually as elegant as a cat could be, came running into the barn, her fur raised high. She latched onto one of the wooden beams and climbed up while hissing down at something, all the while.

Looking around, there was nobody around that could have startled the cat. However, there was… something. Callie didn't know what to call it—like something was tugging at her… magic?

Unfortunately, she never got to follow it as one of the adults came by and she was forced to go to Granny Ethel's hut.

Callen was worried for Callie. The little girl had decided to search for the glauxis egg, but this task was far too dangerous for her. Even Callen didn't feel comfortable snooping around and searching for the thing. How would they even go about finding something like that? It was like searching for a needle in a haystack while trying to remain unnoticed by the person who threw the needle inside of it.

The child had obviously jumped to blame the outsider first. It was obvious. He was a stranger, and Callie was inclined to believe the people she had grown up around, even if some of them were less than kind to her. But it must have been somebody from the village who took the glauxis egg.

Somebody who had travelled somewhere north sometime this year. This narrowed the search a little, but not by much. All of the hunters had traveled around, and many people had gone to the nearby town to sell some of their things.

Thinking about it, it would be easier to make a list of people who hadn't left the village, rather than a list of those who had.

Nonetheless, Callen didn't intend to lie to her sister, so he did his due diligence and went to talk with Silas before his training began. The merchant was setting up his stall, putting his many wares on display.

"Good morning, Silas!" Callen greeted him with a wave.

"Morning, Callen. What brings you here so early?" Silas extended a hand for a friendly handshake, and Callen took it.

"I was just on my way to training and decided to stop by. Setting up for the day?"

"That I am. Although I'm going to have to head to town soon to resupply. I've grown quite attached to this village. You have wonderful weather during the summer in these parts. None of the sweltering heat that plagues the seaside," Silas said with a chuckle.

This piqued Callen's interest. "How hot does it get there?"

"So hot that pulling off all your clothes and sitting in water hardly helps because even the sea is so warm it hardly provides any cold," Silas explained.

"You know, I've heard of the sea, but I've never actually seen it. How does it look like?"

"Oh, there are no words to describe it. It's vast and beautiful, with water as far as the eye can see. You have to see it to really understand it," Silas said, his eyes distant as he likely imagined the scene before him.

Callen's breath hitched. How he wished to see the sea with his own eyes. He had never gone beyond the neighboring town, although he craved to see what lay beyond the seemingly endless pine forests.

"Have you given any thought about becoming a merchant yourself?" Silas prompted.

"Oh… I'm not sure. I don't even know what being a merchant would be like," Callen said evasively. He feared what Dad would say should he even make the suggestion. Especially since he was still technically under punishment for letting Callie go to the Deep Forest. Good thing Dad had believed that lie.

"Why don't I show you? Come with me!" Silas suggested.

"What?" Callen blinked at him in surprise.

"Yeah! I told you that I need to resupply. I'll be gone for a week at most. Surely you could manage to convince your parents to let you go for a short while. You look like a strong young man."

Callen stared at him in disbelief, while his chest swelled with excitement. Travelling with a merchant. They wouldn't be going far, but even so, it had been so long since he left the village. Not since last autumn.

"I… Yeah! I would love to," Callen said, dumbfounded. "Although I'm not sure how my parents would react."

"Who were your parents again? I can talk to them," Silas suggested.

"The blacksmith and the head of the hunters," Callen clarified.

"Oh! They're tough customers, hard to haggle with, but I'll do my best. Your little sister is the ginger-haired girl obsessed with monsters, right?"

"Yeah, Callie. Why are you asking?"

"No reason really. I just saw her earlier this morning, sneaking around the house corners and peeking inside sheds and barns when she thought nobody was looking. The kid was quite funny!" Silas said with a chuckle. "Is this some game?"

Callen barely suppressed a groan. This was the exact reason why he was worried about this whole search for the egg. Callie's idea of subtlety wasn't subtle at all. "I guess you could say that. She believes something is hidden in the village."

"Not her alone, it seems," Silas said and pointed at one of the adults passing not too far from them and sneaking a peek inside one of the barns. "Since yesterday, it seems like many people are searching for something. But I haven't the faintest clue what. Did somebody lose something?"

Callen was surprised. Why were the adults searching for something? Were they after the egg, too? But how did they know?

"I don't know..." Callen replied evasively.

"Well, I suppose it doesn't matter. So, are you interested in coming along or not? Should I try talking to your parents?"
