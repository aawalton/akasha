The world of Potentia was one of fear, and hunger. For those who haven’t spent time there, it’s easy to believe in certain false versions of that terrible place. Insights gained only through stories, myths, and those poor few who made the mistake of staring back through a rift.

They might picture enormous demons fighting for supremacy, or the red skies and rusted mountains. They could see in their minds the endless cycle of death and rebirth that has created and destroyed billions in its time. But all of that was only the hunger of Potentia. The darkness, violence, and aggression.

Each day taught Valanor more of the other side of that world.

The fear.

His small force of thirty had been searching for nearly half a year since coming here from Nexum. In all that time, they’d only lost eight warriors, something that Valanor would consider a miracle, if he could ever stop blaming himself for those deaths.

Those feelings weren’t entirely misplaced. He was the commander and Vanguard of this mission, all those lives were in his hands. Still, he might have been able to forgive himself more easily if they’d actually found anything in all this time.

They knew what they were looking for, they even knew where it was supposed to be, but Potentia wasn’t cooperating. A Dimensional Bleed had forced the expedition to depart far to the east of their goal. Worse, the mountains were in different locations on Potentia–no group of Champions had ever created them on this world.

And so the group had been fighting in circles, barely making progress in the natural labyrinth of red peaks. It had meant months of fighting, and soaking in the corruption of this world, but hadn’t just resulted in the expected exhaustion and battle fatigue. Potentia had taught them about fear.

What most people likely did not picture when imagining this hellish world, was the profound emptiness that haunted most days.

At first glance, Potentia could appear barren. There were no birds flying lazily in the sky. No herds of animals moving in the distance. There weren’t even regular insects, it seemed. Most days were spent walking in almost total silence, only the wind whistling past their ears.

If they were truly alone it mightn’t have been so bad, but on Potentia, you were never alone. The group was surrounded by life. They could sense it. They could see it out of the corner of their eyes. They could hear it when a branch snapped or rock was displaced.

That was the fear that ruled this place, keeping the demons in check, spending most of their lives hiding. Even when the local terrors gathered in packs, they’d rarely engage a more powerful target or group. So Valanor’s force was watched, measured, and mostly avoided.

Until they weren’t.

The hunger could take over in an instant, and ambushes had been responsible for nearly every lost life. The moment that a pack of demons, or even a single large one decided it could defeat Valanor, the attack would come. Those empty skies would suddenly fill with murderous flying demons, or the ground beneath their feet would erupt with demonic insects the size of a carriage. Worst was near water…everything on Potentia learned to avoid deep water.

Each time Valanor would bellow a challenge, hearing an echo of the same from Timol, the other Twilight Vanguard. Then the two would face the rush of enemies together, creating a safe zone between them. From that space came the real destruction, as long-ranged Hunters unleashed spells at their attackers. The archer team did the same, Cara Fletcher having turned them into a deadly force.

They would rally, and fight with everything they had. With honor. And they would prevail, breaking the enemy until they were forced to retreat, or slaughtering them when they didn’t. Then the group would have time again. But their nerves grew ever more taught.

Having proven themselves, there would be a reprieve before another attack came. The creatures of Potentia quickly learned to recognize danger, and didn’t throw their lives away. Again, the fear contained them. Again Potentia would appear empty. But they all knew a terrible truth: the next attack would always be worse, and so eventually they’d reach a breaking point.

Valanor feared that today, day 193, might be that breaking point.

The group faced off against enormous, tusked lizards. These weren’t dragons, and showed no sign of obvious intelligence, but they were large, and aggressive. They kept trying to dodge around the Vanguards and go directly for the healers, and Valanor found himself continually pivoting and dragging the enemy back. Sometimes literally, as he needed to throw chains around the most determined ones.

Valanor’s people were holding up well so far, but the other unfortunate element of battle on Potentia was the real problem. Valanor could already sense the inevitable, as could many of his group, and the tempo of battle increased as they tried to bring the enemy down as quickly as possible.

They weren’t fast enough.

A swarm of flying insectoid creatures erupted from a nearby crevice. As always, Valanor’s group fought in red, rocky hills, surrounded by seemingly endless hiding places. These newcomers looked like ants, built for speed and only slightly smaller than a person. The moment they emerged from their place of safety they rushed to attack anything that moved.

“Shift the line!” Valanor called. He had to trust that his troops would react as they’d been taught, moving so that at least one of the Vanguards stood between them and the enemy.

The lizards died quickly, but not before a pained wail announced the fall of one of Valanor’s own. A glance in Timol’s direction showed that the man was overwhelmed, ill-suited to holding the attention of enemies that flew. The insects had gotten around him, and struck the archers. Cara was leaping from a tall pillar of stone, firing at the bugs as she fell, but one of her team was already down.

And it wasn’t over, Valanor knew that. Demon blood watered the stones, and that always led to the same thing: more demons. Howls echoed from all around them and Valanor’s heart began to break. Wolves nearly as large as Syph, his frost Familiar appeared. They stalked forward, bearing far too many teeth. Valanor could do nothing more than pound on his shield, trying to get their attention.

Toby, his enormous obsidian golem was already out, and stomping through the battle. Timol had a mound of rocks of his own, and a dozen more Dusk rank Familiars of all types were supporting where they could, but this was happening too fast.

Valanor’s people had long become used to the escalating nature of battles in this terrible world. So many groups were stuck at an impasse brought on by similar levels of strength. But when one of those groups was weakened by attack, the others pounced. The insects had taken their chance to kill the lizards, and the wolves now did the same to the flying demons. The only hope was that no other significant groups were nearby.

Valanor raged forward, shield crashing into the nearest wolf, as the heavy hammer in his other hand slammed down with Twilight force. Fire and lightning were hurled over him by the elementalists, and the putrid air of Potentia was suddenly made worse from burning hair.

His people were making a good showing of themselves, but he could see them weakening. They’d lost most of their archers now, and several of the furry beasts had managed to get past Timol–who was still assaulted by nearly a dozen of the quick-moving insects. They’d gone into this battle with twenty-two souls, and a quarter of that number had already been taken out.

If they had any hope of surviving this battle, they’d need to–

A titanic roar split the air.

Despite his years of training, the sound made Valanor freeze. He’d heard it before, and knew what to expect, though the truth was so painful he nearly couldn’t force himself to look. Through the thin eye-slits of his obsidian helm, he saw the dragon, and their doom.

It was parched on a nearby peak, wings spread and green flames raining down around it. Beautiful, deadly, and terrifying all at once. Hunger and fear.

“Shift!” Valanor called again. But as he turned to examine the line, he heard the terrible sound of metal crumbling. He looked just in time to see Timol crushed beneath the dragon’s weight. Enormous, clawed hands tore at the Vanguard with frenzy, and Timol’s bellowed defiance cut off after only moments.

“Timol!” someone cried from nearby, and one of his healers raced forward.

“Get back!” another called, but it was already too late. The dragon lazily swatted away the healer, and Valanor winced as he heard her bones snap from the blow.

“Rally!” he cried, hammering on his shield again. There were still more than ten wolves between himself and the now-isolated group Timol had been guarding. With no other options, Valanor played his trump card, and grew.

At Twilight, his final Vanguard ability allowed him to stand more than thirty feet tall, his armor expanding along with him. He charged forward, hammer swinging in front of him as if he were sweeping up his cottage. Demon direwolves went flying away before his rush, seeming tiny to him now.

Valanor managed to get himself between the black Twilight dragon and his own forces, but by now the battle had devolved to chaos. More of his people lay dead, and he glanced back just in time to see Cara hurled over the edge of a nearby cliff, four wolves diving after her.

His heart seemed to freeze in his chest. Why did I let her come? She’s not even a real part of the Church! She could have made Twilight someday, instead she dies here, for nothing…

Rage threatened to overwhelm him, but he knew he needed to lead. Through quick gestures and barked orders, he managed to corral his people behind him–only possible as there was little more than a handful remaining. Still, the bugs were dead, and the wolves were being brought down as much by the dragon as by Valanor’s group. There was a chance.

He kept backing up, crushing any wolf that came too close, and keeping his massive shield in front of those he needed to protect. They were slowly backing toward the cliff that just claimed Cara, but it was the only way to keep from being surrounded.

Valanor could make this work if–Toby!

The dragon had whirled unexpectedly, allowing several of the heavy wolves to scratch and bite at its scales. But the Twilight beast took the attacks willingly, as it gave it the moment it needed to heave itself on top of Valanor’s Familiar, the obsidian golem. Toby’s heavy body cracked against the stone ground, then shattered as the dragon brought its two front legs down on the golem’s back.

Valanor couldn’t believe the strength of that monster, and wasn’t prepared when his Bond nearly snapped. Toby had been defeated, and Valanor fell, one enormous knee catching his weight, while his shield dropped.

No!

That was the only opportunity the dragon needed. It breathed out a terrible blast of liquid green flame, covering the remaining wolves, who howled and ran, then it passed over Valanor in a wave. His armor protected him…but not those behind him. He heard their screams as the flames took them.

Valanor couldn’t even make a sound as he forced himself to his feet. Part of him had refused to believe this could happen, while another part had always been certain it would. He looked up at the dragon, the only other living thing on a battlefield that burned.

He raised his shield once more.

Valanor’s mana was nearly gone, and he couldn’t maintain this size for long, but he would give everything he had to take this creature down with him. He charged, shield crashing into the face of a dragon that hadn’t expected such a burst of speed. The hammer followed, and a spray of burning blood and sharp teeth flew through the air.

Valanor just kept swinging, not willing to lose the momentum, not even to summon another ability. The dragon stumbled back, confused by the fury and continuous blows to its face. Valanor could do this, he would do this.

He never even saw the tail coming as it crashed into his side, and his own immense weight brought him to the ground, shield sliding away. Valanor looked up, his breath and racing heart echoing in his ears. He watched the dragon move toward him, green fire spilling from numerous wounds. That was something at least.

He forced himself to one knee, prepared to meet his fate. Perhaps it would be better to die now, rather than continue, broken and alone, knowing how many he’d failed. But it wasn’t in his nature to give up. Never again.

He gripped his hammer in two hands, then staggered upward.

“Come then!” he roared in the dragon’s face. It roared back, the sound deafening. Then it stopped, the bellow turning into a series of choking sounds as the dragon’s head fell forward–limp.

Valanor looked from the head, to the dragon’s back, where yet another demon had arrived. He knew from a glance it was one of the most dangerous types.

The creature was somewhat humanoid, standing on two thick legs, and holding a single bone, nearly as large as the demon itself. The bone was essentially one long, sharpened blade, with the remnants of a joint at the end. Testament to the horrors of this place, he’d seen a smaller version on the forearm of something that attacked his force. This one was large enough to slice entirely through the dragon’s neck.

The demon dropped to the ground before Valanor. It had to be almost ten feet tall, and covered in boney white spikes. A tail danced behind it, and red eyes glared out beneath thick horns. It raised its makeshift weapon again, then tilted its head at Valanor.

“There’s always another demon,” Valanor muttered. They both charged, hammer raised and claws poised, in a field of the dead.

Hunger and fear.

This was Potentia.
