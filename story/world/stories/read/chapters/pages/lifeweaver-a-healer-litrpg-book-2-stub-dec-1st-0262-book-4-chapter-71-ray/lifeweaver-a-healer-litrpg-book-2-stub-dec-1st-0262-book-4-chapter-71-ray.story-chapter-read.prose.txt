"Okay, I'm going to say it," Ray groaned as he came to the top of the stairs in front of the bigass doors to the stupid church-thing. "I'm getting real tired of being jumped by tiny, vicious, little dudes!"

"Could be worse," Elijah said, despite his clothes being full of rips and tears with blood leaking out of him.

Ray sighed. "Could be better."

They'd fought through a sea of bastards. Open combat like that was not one of Ray's many specialities, but hiding wasn't much of an option either, not when he wasn't alone. At least Mr. Lightbringer wasn't a bad pair-up. The dude could fight even if he didn't have a brain. Or was it that he had half a brain? Luke mentioned something about that, but Ray hadn't listened. Who cares about the intricacies of healing? Just wave your hands in the air and do the thing. Stupid healers. Not that he would mind if one showed up right about then. Elijah wasn't the only one leaking blood, and the health potion cooldown was a bitch.

"Where the hell are the others?" Ray complained.

Elijah pointed down the long stairs going down to one of the side streets. "There."

"You sure?" Ray asked, leaning forward and squinting. "I don't see anything."

"Movement."

"If you say so."

"I'll go," Elijah said, and started down the stairs, taking bounding leaps that left blood splatters each time he touched the stone steps.

That guy had fallen hard for the devil-chick. He had been pushing way too hard since the moment they appeared in this weird-ass city. The emptiness of it was one thing, and the run-down buildings were creepy as hell, but it was the sky that twisted Ray's jimmies. Where the hell were the stars? And why three moons? Who the hell needs three of those things?

"Stupid moons," Ray grumbled before he jumped into the air at a new sound: that of screeching hinges.

The door opened on its own. Or did it? Ray entered stealth and jumped up, attaching himself to the side of the wall, right by the dark entrance. Peeking in, he didn't see much. It was dark. A couple of torches glowed in the far distance. Also, the smell puffing out from inside reminded him of yeast and old Thai food.

A sighing whisper seemed to echo from inside. "Raaaaaaaaaaaaay."

"Nope," Ray whispered to himself as he climbed away. "Nope, nope, nope."

A small laugh followed after he had landed back on the ground and stepped away from the door, and he pulled out his trusty dagger and a couple of potions he had been experimenting with, readying himself for combat. Hannah and Curtis stepped out from a corner. She pointed and laughed at him.

"Got you!"

Ray put away his tools of the trade. "No, you didn't!"

"Oh, I got you alright," she laughed, elbowing Curtis in the side. "Tell him I got him."

Curtis muttered something indecipherable.

"Did you come from inside?" Ray asked.

Hannah peered into the dark opening. "Nah. Did you open the door?"

"Are you fucking with me?" Ray asked.

"Maybe."

Elijah returned then, with the devil-woman, the healer-woman, and Priyah in tow. The women looked ragged as hell, but Ray didn't comment since he was a gentleman. Before anyone had a chance to speak, Penny's voice sounded from down below on the main staircase leading down to the wideass street cutting through the entire city.

"Get inside!"

She streaked through the air with lightning arcing around her, and she carried Mateo in her arms, who faced the street, spear in hand. They slammed into the metal of the massive door, like a fly against a pane of glass, thudded to the ground, and then dragged themselves into the building. Everyone stood there dumbstruck for a second before looking back the way they had come. A roiling darkness was making its way up the stairs, light streaking across its surface like veins down a nursing assistant's legs.

"We should probably follow them," Elijah said.

"Where is Martin?" Hannah asked as they made their way into the building.

No one had seen him, but he had definitively been alive when the light shut off back in that village. Ray briefly considered volunteering to go back out there to look for the lost lamb, but then remembered that risking his own life for someone he didn't even know was stupid, so stupid. The door shut behind them as that void mess on the stairs caught up. Rather than the expected banging and wheezing, all that good stuff, it was just silent. Ray couldn't hear a peep from the other side of the door. It was a thick door, sure, but not exactly soundproof.

"Martin is over there," Priyah said, her voice sounding small in the darkness.

Ray didn't see a thing except for the torches. The one in charge of lighting in that place needed to be fired. Two torches for a building the size of a football stadium? What the hell?

"I don't see him," Mateo said. He kept throwing worried glances at the door, but seemed unhurt.

"Priyah is right," Hannah said. "I can sense him."

"Martin!" Elijah shouted, his voice echoing. Either it was just great timing, or the loudness triggered something, because more lights came on, and not from where Ray would have expected. The huge windows far, far above, near the ceiling, weren't, in fact, windows. They were lamps.

Two long rows of them turned on at the same time, and light beamed into the chamber, criss-crossing through what Ray could now see was a mostly empty space. Small stone monuments dotted the otherwise empty floor, and on closer inspection, there was writing carved into the stone floor almost everywhere. Oh.

"It's a damn cemetery," Ray said.

Penny, who stood right next to him, ignored him and put up a hand to stop Mateo from rushing out across the room. "That's not Martin."

That made Ray pay attention to the figure who stood in the distance. It sure looked like Martin, but of course it wasn't. "This is the most boss-room-place I've ever seen in my life," he said, pointing over at Martin. "He's going to transform into some huge thing, isn't he?"

"Are we sure it isn't just Martin?" Priyah asked. "Looks like him to me."

"There's Source in him," Curtis said. The guy sounded hungry. Damn weirdo.

Vicky collapsed when she got inside the building and had stayed down since. When Ray glanced over, he saw her tracing a carved stone message on the floor. She whispered something about dates that he couldn't hear. When she looked up, there was something wild in her eyes. "It's dates. They're repeating. It's the same one over and over again!"

"What are you talking about?" Lemira asked.

"He's moving," Curtis noted, indicating Martin in the distance, who had started walking toward the group.

"The graves. Everyone here died on the same day," Vicky said.

"So?" Penny asked.

Vicky shrugged. "I don't know." She seemed small and fragile. Tears welled up in her eyes. "I don't know. Oh, Nick."

Right. Her boyfriend died like hours ago. No wonder she was a bit out of it. Whatever was outside took that as its moment to go berserk, slamming into the door again and again. Everyone jumped and stepped away from the wall. A crack formed along the wall, and stone dust drifted down.

"There's a lot of Source out there," Curtis mumbled, his hand reaching for the locking mechanism they had thrown the moment they made it inside.

"Curtis," Hannah said. "Don't."

"Martin is coming," Priyah cried, and she was right. The dude had sped up and was running full tilt toward the group, his smile growing as he got closer.

"So what?" Ray asked. "Do we fight him or not? Why am I hearing boss music in my head?"

"I hate this stupid dungeon," Penny said, before her words took on a different tone. Not the usual cheerful chant, but more like a darker variant in the form of a bad high school poem.

Wear my friend's face,

Mock his memory.

Make yourself ready,

I am the consequence.

Lightning crackled on all ten of her fingers, and she thrust her hands forward into a clap that sent a shockwave forward in a half-circle. It met Martin seconds before he would have made it all the way to the group, and the guy burst apart like someone had set off a bomb in his chest.

The only thing showing he had even been there before the attack was a wet spot on the ground. Vicky started crying. Looking at Curtis, Ray didn't see a single trace of sadness in there, nothing to indicate he cared about his brother's passing at all. Cold.

"Uh, are we sure that guy was... bad?" Ray asked.

Martin reformed in the same spot, looking as if the attack hadn't hurt him at all.

Ray sighed. "Forget I asked."

A bunch of animals, lions and wolves, popped into existence right in front of Lemira before pouncing on not-Martin, who had started laughing his ass off for some creepy reason. The demon-woman shouted something that made her pets glow as they closed the distance. Not-Martin ignored them and charged forward, sword thrust forward, aimed right for Lemira's heart.

Ray's kick took the bastard in the side and threw him to the ground, where he skidded and bounced a couple of times before he landed in a heap. Quite the save, by Ray's own estimation. Not-Martin's entire form shuddered and contracted, then rebuilt itself as Martin back up on his feet.

"I hate this place," Ray groaned.

"You need to attack its Source channels," Penny said, just as the damn wall cracked open.

Void tentacles slithered in as the group backed away from the wall. Lemira's pets bounded toward not-Martin again, and this time, the one wearing the face of their guild mate didn't ignore them. Metal tore through several lions, and their master cried out as if the weapon had struck her as well. Elijah grabbed her around the waist, and the group moved in toward the center of the room to get away from the creature pushing in through the cracks.

A voice echoed through the room, coming from nowhere and everywhere.

Can't you see where I have brought you, fresh Integrated? Even if you were to survive this day, you would just repeat history and serve those who gorge on your power to make it their own.

Not-Martin kept going, charging the group. Ray drew his dagger, and Curtis, who had been about to intercept the guy, flinched. In that interruption, Elijah took a sword through the belly, and Mateo narrowly blocked another using the haft of his spear.

Hannah's shadows wrapped themselves around not-Martin's legs and arms, and Curtis punched straight through the asshole's chest. That part of not-Martin turned into what Ray had begun recognizing as void-matter, or whatever you wanted to call it, and that warred with darkness for a second or two before Curtis won out. Not-Martin became, well, not-Martin, and imploded into a patch of void that disappeared a second later.

"There you go!" Ray said, waving his dagger in the air.

"You should put that thing away," Curtis grunted.

"Huh?" Ray asked, looking down. "Is my fly open?"

Vicky healed Elijah without too much of an issue, and they kept heading deeper into the building to get away from the mass of light-streaked void coming closer. A tentacle slammed into the ground and cut off from the main body, and just like that, Not-Martin returned.

"I'm getting sick and tired of all these mother-effin' Martins in my mother-effin' dungeon!" Ray shouted as he charged forward, dagger held high. No. He didn't do that, because of course he didn't. What kind of idiot would charge a monster-void-thing that could just resurrect itself over and over again?

Mateo. That's who.
