“First of all, what were you doing going through my basement?” Kyle asked, raising an eyebrow at the imp.

“If you’d have gotten home a little faster, I wouldn’t have had so much time to snoop,” Jarberry replied with a shrug.  “Now, back to the question.  Is that the object Suierrillax told me about?”

Kyle nodded.  “It is, got it from one of the hodags.  It’s got some really interesting properties, but I’m still working on figuring out how to really put it to use.”

“What are you using it for, fertilizer?  Looks to me like it’s more of a lawn ornament than anything else.”

Kyle gave Jarberry a flat look.  “I’m trying to incorporate the mana-resistant properties into some plant constructs.  It’s just a process that takes time.”

“Ah, yes, time.  That resource you seem to have oh-so-much of.  Kyle, I mean this respectfully, but when did you last put in real, intentional focus to work on a project like this?  One that has real application for your whole path?”

Kyle opened his mouth to respond, then closed it again.  The creation of his antifungal prosthesis was an interesting project, and undeniably helpful, but it wasn’t something that broadly lifted him up.  The improvements to the chaddlings, which led to the development of the legionnaires was also helpful, but even that had a relatively lower impact on him directly.  As he thought back, most of his big improvements came from his last jobs for the Pillar, where Kyle began improving his understanding of conceptual Death.  Other than that, his developments had been much more on-the-job.

After a moment, Jarberry crossed his arms.  “You see my point.  I’m not telling you not to keep doing the things you’ve been doing, particularly if you want your path to stall here for a while.  But don’t overlook the importance of momentum.  You’ll find that it’s easy to get caught up in somebody else’s plans for you, and spend years not making meaningful progress of your own.”

“And what about your plans for me?” Kyle asked, even though he knew the imp was right.

Jarberry shrugged as he floated in a lazy circle.  “My plans for you involve you actually getting strong enough to make a difference.  You’ve already provided a great return on investment, but I think you’ll really shine if you make it into mid-tier B Grade or higher.  And make no mistake, I’m confident you’ll be able to get that far.”

The casual way Jarberry talked about him making it to B Grade made Kyle take a moment to process.  Given Jarberry was still only at the peak of C Grade, it sounded audacious.

[IF I MAY INTERJECT, MR. BANDERSNATCH, WHAT EXACTLY ARE YOUR PLANS FOR DR. MAYHEW?]

“Hey now, don’t sell yourself short.  You’re included, too,” Jarberry said with a grin.

[THAT DID NOT ANSWER THE QUESTION.]

“No, it didn’t.  Suffice it to say that you’ve both got a unique set of abilities, and that I’ve got some long-standing projects of my own that will need your support.  I won’t force you, of course, but I’m also not going to show my whole hand before anything materializes.  Some things are best kept quiet.”

[THEN WE LOOK FORWARD TO HAVING THAT CONVERSATION AT A LATER DATE.]

“Back to the matter at hand, if you were in my shoes, how would you recommend balancing the trip to the Alabaster Court with the project downstairs?  I don’t want to delay them too long, but I think you made an important point.”

Jarberry rolled his eyes, then pointed to the spatial ring he’d just given Kyle.  “Use your head, Kyle.  Bring the project with you.  There’s a minor stasis field inside the storage, so it won’t make much progress while you’re away, but it certainly won’t hurt what you’ve done so far.  And plan on getting a lot of down time as you’re doing your visit.  The velgians like to operate on a different time table than the rest of us.”

Kyle felt foolish that he’d already almost forgotten the spatial item.  He’d grown so accustomed to only being able to store plants that he hadn’t even considered bringing the formation as an option.

“Oh, and that reminds me,” Jarberry said, holding out his palm.  A glowing sphere appeared in it, giving off gentle waves of heat.  “With your newfound spatial item, I’m done providing storage for this thing.  Though I’d ask, if you decide you want to sell it, come to me first.  I’ve got a few folks in my network that would pay handsomely for an item like this.”

Kyle took the sphere, and with a minor effort willed it to go into the spatial ring.  The air around the pyrocore sphere trembled for a moment, and then the item was simply gone.  Focusing his attention on the ring, Kyle felt the presence of the sphere nestled inside, and with another thought, willed it to come back out into his hand.

“Pretty nifty, isn’t it?”  Jarberry asked with a grin.

“No doubt about it,” Kyle agreed, putting the sphere away again.  It felt very different than the very natural space within the World Tree Seed, but there were echoes of the same concepts at play.  Of course, that would be a project for another time.

Glancing at his nav bracelet, Jarberry let out an exaggerated sigh, then floated toward the door.  “Well, you’ve got your jobs, your ring, and your glowing rock.  I’ve done what I needed to do.  So have fun, take care of yourself, and I’m sure I’ll see you when you get back.”

“Thank you, Jarberry,” Kyle said, a genuine smile growing across his face.

“You’re welcome, Kyle,” he said.  Then the imp was gone, and Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. were alone.

[HIS VISITS ARE ALWAYS INTERESTING.]

“That they are,” Kyle agreed.  He looked at the dull gray gem that served as the anchor for the storage.  “Any idea where we should put this?”

[I BELIEVE IT WOULD BE BEST TO LEAVE THE ANCHOR AT YOUR COURIER’S GUILD HOUSING, DR. MAYHEW.]

“Oh?” Kyle said, starting to trace the drone’s logic.

[WHILE THERE ARE LIMITED SECURITY RISKS HERE, THAT HOUSING IS MORE SECURE.  IF YOU ARE DOING HIGHER-PROFILE JOBS, IT ALSO CREATES A LOWER LIKELIHOOD OF BRINGING TROUBLE INTO THE KERTAN SYSTEM.]

Those were all excellent points, and Kyle nodded as he started packing things up.  “Then with that said, I think we should plan on spending some time back at the Hub once we get things cleaned up here.  We’ve got a few days until we take off for Alabaster Court territory, after all.  Is there anything you’d like to get done before we go, C.H.A.D.D.?”

[I THINK THAT WOULD BE WISE, DR. MAYHEW.  AS FAR AS PROJECTS GO, I AM MOST INTERESTED IN CONTINUING MY STUDIES WITH THE SPHERE, BUT THAT CAN ALSO WAIT UNTIL WE ARE IN THE COURTS.]

Kyle nodded, heading downstairs into his lab.  Very little progress was made since his last visit, but he remained undaunted as he pulled in the plants surrounding the egg-shaped item, then finally grabbed it to store within the ring.  For a moment, it resisted being pulled into the spatial item, its mana-rejecting properties pushing against the ring’s enchantments, but soon it was pulled in.

As it entered the space, Kyle felt the same lingering concept at play.  He activated Identify, then drew the item out again.  He watched the space around his hand bend as it arrived, as if a hole was opened up to reveal the item.  The same phenomenon happened in reverse as he put it back into the ring again.

With a frown, Kyle held out his prosthesis with its palm up next to his right hand.  Reaching into the World Tree Seed’s internal space, he pulled out an apple.  Rather than warping, this time the space just seemed to unfold as the fruit appeared in his hand, only to fold around the apple as he deposited it.  The difference was subtle, but it felt more like the apple was already there and just needed to be revealed, versus being forced into space from somewhere else.

Kyle repeated the process, watching the differences in space with Identify.  Slowly, gradually, he felt like was starting to understand something, until he was interrupted by a notification on his nav bracelet.  To his surprise, it was from Tillienne.

Kyle,

Made it back to the Guild lodgings, but the physicians back home want me under supervision while they help me with the next steps of recovery.  I’ve been asked to head back to our planet first thing tomorrow, any chance you’d be free for that dinner tonight?

I understand if not, there’s a lot going on, and we’ve only just returned.  Either way, thank you for all you did.  If not for you, I don’t think I’d be writing this message.

-Tillienne

Kyle looked to where the apple was resting in his palm, then at the other items to pack up his lab.  It was already evening, and by the time he took the teleporters back to the Hub and made it to the restaurant, it would be the middle of the night.  That would delay his packing and compress his already tight timeline, not to mention flying in the face of what Tillienne’s doctors were recommending.  So, he made the only responsible choice.

Pulling up his nav bracelet, he sent a quick message back.

I’d love to meet.  Your sister’s place again?

[DR. MAYHEW, ARE YOU SURE THIS IS WISE?  MS. TILLIENNE NEEDS TO RECOVER.]

The drone’s words echoed Kyle’s own thoughts, and he already had a response ready.

“She’s an adult who can make her decisions, and we know that she’s stable.  Besides, if we don’t meet now, who knows when we’ll be able to get together again.  Life has a way of keeping us all busy.”

[I DO NOT DISAGREE, DR. MAYHEW.  I SIMPLY WANTED TO POINT OUT THE OTHER CONSIDERATION.]

“I appreciate it, C.H.A.D.D.”

Kyle was in the shower cleaning up when the next message came through.

That sounds perfect.  I’ll get us a table, I look forward to seeing you there.

Kyle felt odd not wearing his full combat attire, instead wearing a slightly loose linen shirt and slacks.  The shirt buttoned up enough to cover all but the faintest edge of his injury, barely revealing the outline of a scar.  He still brought the C.H.A.D.D. pack, of course, though the sleek metal design was fashionable in its own way.  Finally, he wrapped his left prosthesis in bandages, covering the network of vines that made up his left arm.  With that done, Kyle took to the skies of Kertan Six for the second time that day.  He flew as fast as he could without activating Genesis Engine.  After all, he didn’t want to be late to his date.
