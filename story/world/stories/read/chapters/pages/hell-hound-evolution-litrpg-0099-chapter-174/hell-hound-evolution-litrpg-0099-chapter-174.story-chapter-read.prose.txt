After Grick explained how this latest hunting trip had gone and how’d they killed the bandits, he quickly ran short of things to say. He explained Lily tricking nasty humans by sounding like them and then told of Grick’s Ultimate Shadow Phantasma attack, which led to the room quietly murmuring and nodding to each other. They seemed impressed.

“That sounds like such a powerful attack!” said one.

“Yes, and such a creative name!”

“As expected of Hero Grick.”

He was being called a hero and getting so much attention, but he felt he needed to give them more. So out of other things to talk about he started telling about how he fishes.

“…when Grick is done making stick bendy Grick uses string and pointy hook for wormy. Fishy like wormy,” he explained, folding his arms and nodding.

“Incredible,” one of the women halfway down the table said. “And do you find all of these things just out in the wild?”

“Yup! Grick always looking for good stick for capturing fishies. Grick needs to get string from lovelys Grick trades for shineys. There’s no good string unless it comes from human things. Grick hasn’t traded shineys in a long time, though.”

“Shineys? What are those?” another man asked, leaning forward.

Grick felt a bubble of excitement. He supposed he shouldn’t have been so nervous after all. With all of the cheering and calling him Hero Grick, he was worried they may have thought he could do more than he was capable of. But of course, he knew he was a smart goblin who could fish and find shineys as well as help puppies on hunts! He doubted most humans could do those things.

But then the energy in the room changed as a large man with a bald head let out a grunt of irritation and pounded a fist on the table. “Ridiculous,” he murmured in a growl.

All of the chatter stopped as everyone turned to look at him. Rin’s red eyes focused on him from above her interlaced fingers. “Captain Yashida. Is there something you wish to say?”

Grick’s ears folded flat against his temples as the bald man turned a glare at him, then addressed the table.

“As a matter of fact, there is!” Yashida complained. “Don’t you think we are perhaps getting carried away here? Fishing and scavenging are useful skills, but that hardly makes this damn goblin a hero!”

Grick shrank in his chair.

Damn goblin.

He knew that tone, and knew those words. Those were the words humans would use when they did bad things to Grick. When they chased him away or called him stupid. Suddenly Grick was scared. He was scared all of them would realize Grick didn’t do anything special, and they would stop being nice to him. They would tell him he doesn’t belong here and make him leave, and chase him away with swords and scary shooty things.

Then, suddenly, one of the men stood up, breaking the silence as he slammed his hands on the table. “Yashida! How dare you speak of Hero Grick that way!”

“I’m telling you he ain’t no hero!” Yashida argued back.

“He helped take out an entire camp of armed bandits!” the man argued back.

“And devised that shadow attack himself,” said another.

“Not to mention coming up with such an impressive name for it!” added the woman who complimented his fishing earlier.

“Most people sitting at this table couldn’t do half of what Hero Grick has accomplished!”

“Not to mention the shinies,” another nodded in agreement.

“I don’t care!” Yashida continued to yell. “Calling him hero is a disgrace to the empire and a smear on what our glorious emperor has built! Calling the goblin a hero turns everything we stand for into a joke!” He threw an arm toward a royal banner behind him, portraying the crowned frog emperor staring stoically over the room.

“A joke?” Rin’s voice cut through the chatter, and immediately everyone turned to her.

“High Regent. I only meant to—”

“Our glorious Emperor did not build this nation by asking what someone was born as,” Rin said, her red eyes narrowing. “He built it by recognizing strength and loyalty wherever it can be found. This goblin has fought for citizens of the empire against murderers and cutthroats, and survived where many of our own people could not. And your objection is what? That he was born a goblin?”

“I…” Yashida gritted his teeth. “I only wished to preserve the dignity of—”

“And you’ve done just the opposite. If Hero Grick is willing to stand beside the Empire, and help us deal with all of those nasty nasty humans,” Rin continued, “then I would consider it far more insulting to His Majesty to tell Grick he has no place here. You, however, Yashida…” Her gaze sharpened. “I see I’ve misjudged you. You are the one who has no right to be sitting at this table.”

“But High Regent—”

Lily’s ears perked up as she sniffed the air. Grick looked around, seeing how everyone at the table seemed to be turning on this Yashida person.

She pointed her metal finger at the door. “Leave immediately, Yashida. You don’t belong here.”

Yashida shook his head. “But can’t you see that—”

“Now. Yashida! Or will I have to have you escorted out?”

Finally he bowed his head, gritted his teeth, and turned away.

After the door slid shut behind him, a murmuring swept over the room. One of Grick’s ears lifted as he tried to catch some of what they were saying. One of them whispered to another, “I can’t believe he would speak of Hero Grick that way.”

“Alright,” Rin finally spoke up once returning to her seat and everyone else quieted down. “Hero Grick, I apologize for that unfortunate interruption.”

Grick blinked, unsure of what to say. Usually when humans got mad at Grick other humans only joined them. Nobody ever stood up to defend poor Grick in the past other than the puppies. Yet here was a table full of humans nodding at him reassuringly.

Grick slowly straightened in his chair. “It okay,” Grick finally said. “Grick forgives bald human.”

A few smiles and approving nods appeared around the table.

“How gracious of our Hero,” one of the women said.

Below the table, Lily pressed her muzzle more firmly against his leg as her nose continued twitching as she sniffed toward the door Yashida had disappeared through.

Grick scratched behind her ear and said, “See? Grick told you these nice humans.”

“Well then,” Rin continued. “I believe that should be quite enough questions for Hero Grick. Surely he’s proven his competence by now.”

“Yes. Grick has many competences!” Grick agreed, though he still didn’t know what that word meant.

“So then, let’s get back to the main thing we came here to discuss,” Rin said as she nodded to Hakimo.

He opened his binder and took out a stack of papers. “Please pass these down the table, everyone.”

Grick’s ears instantly folded down as he watched the stack move, everyone taking one off of the top. When it was his turn he did the exact same thing but then frowned at the sheet of paper.

It had the human thing on it.

Writing.

Grick couldn’t read writing.

“So then, let me direct you all to page three,” Hakimo said. “Written there are the latest reports from the forward squad leaders, which should get us all up to speed on the situation in the northwestern province.”

Grick looked around in a mild panic as he saw every other human flip through the pages, and then Grick did the same, though he wasn’t sure he even opened to the correct page. Everyone just stared at their papers while Grick stared at his but was at a complete loss. Did they not know he couldn’t read? What would they think if they realized he couldn’t do the human thing?

He remembered how the merchants had him sign that thing they called a “contract” and then got angry at him when he didn’t trade with them correctly. They called him a stupid goblin that didn’t even understand his own agreement. Said he was lucky they didn’t drag him back in shackles for not abiding by the contract he had signed, as apparently doing that was very illegal. Luckily, those humans decided not to turn him in and traded for his shinies anyway.

Truthfully, Grick didn’t understand any of that. He just knew not being able to read the human things made humans treat him badly.

He swallowed, staring harder at the paper, thinking reading shouldn’t be that hard. Surely he could figure it out! But, his confidence quickly diminished as he couldn’t even begin to make sense of all the markings on the paper.

“So, any thoughts?” Hakimo asked the table once it seemed everyone was done.

One of the men cleared his throat. “It seems we should send some reinforcements.”

“But bring them from where? We don’t want to weaken our provinces in the process.”

“Not to mention the cost of moving them.”

“Well if you refer to page two, it seems…”

The humans went on to discuss things that Grick had no idea about. They kept referring to things on the papers, and Grick kept shuffling his papers around right along with them. But he got more and more lost as they said words he didn’t even know. Words like battalion, deployment, logistics, provisional requisition.

But Grick continued nodding right along, and shaking his head whenever the rest of the table seemed to agree or disagree with something somebody said.

Then, suddenly.

“What do you think, Hero Grick?” asked the woman who had complimented how good he was at making fishing poles. He liked her and thought she was very nice, but now he was worried he would look stupid in front of her.

“Ah, Grick. Grick thinks, we should, um…” He shuffled the papers around. “On page three there are strong humans to fight nasties!” He hoped that made sense.

But the way everyone looked around the table, he was sure he said something wrong.

“Well, in any case,” Rin spoke up. “Now that we are all up to speed, why don’t we take a day to consider things. We’ll meet again the same time tomorrow.”

Everyone nodded in agreement then began getting up from the table. Grick prepared to climb down from his chair as well until Rin said, “Hero Grick, if you might stay for a moment, I was hoping to have a few words in private.”

“Ah… Yes High Regent,” he said, trying to address her the way he’d seen others do. “Grick will stay.”

Soon the room was empty all but for Grick and Rin sitting at the far end of the table, her red eyes taking him in, assessing him. Grick gulped.

“Grick, is something the matter?” Rin asked sympathetically. “You were awfully quiet in the last part of the meeting.”

“Oh! Grick was just…” He looked down at Lily who whined. “Grick was just thinking hard. Grick thinks of good plans. Very good plans to get nasties.”

Rin frowned. “Yet, you didn’t seem to want to share them?”

“Grick was just…” His eyes lowered.

“Wait… I think I understand…” Rin stood up and walked around the table. She snatched one of the packets of papers up on her way and looked at it, and looked at Grick’s. “Grick… you don’t know how to read, do you?”

Grick stiffened immediately. “No! Grick reads! Grick reads very good! Grick best at reading human things!”

Rin frowned, pushed the paper away, then took the seat closest to Grick. “It’s alright, Grick. Honestly, it’s my fault for not realizing sooner. It doesn’t take away from how competent you are. Now come on, you can be honest with me.” She smiled at him and Grick remembered how Rin defended him from that other nasty bald human.

“Grick… Grick sorry for tricking High Regent,” he said, lowering his head shamefully.

“Grick, please, feel free to call me Rin when not in the middle of official business. That’s what all my friends call me.”

“Friends?” Grick asked.

Rin nodded. “Of course. But I am curious. Why did you try to hide that you can’t read?”

“Grick… Grick pretend because Grick doesn’t want human to think Grick stupid. Human mean to Grick if Grick stupid.”

“Yes… that is a very valid concern. You’re right to be scared of being seen as stupid,” Rin agreed.

Grick’s ears folded tighter against his head than they ever had before. He patted Lily to try to comfort himself and she licked his fingers, sensing how worried he was.

“Hmmm but what do we do about…” Rin leaned back in her chair, tapping a metal finger against her chin as her eyes drifted to the ceiling in thought.

Suddenly, she pounded one metal fist into the other hand with a clink. “I’ve got it!” she said, then waved her hand.

Grick turned his head, not sure who she was waving to. That is, until a new girl suddenly appeared from the wall behind them, right between two windows. It was like she had just detatched from the shadows there.

It was a girl Grick had seen before. She looked like a serving girl, dressed in similar clothes, but hers were black with pink flowers on them. Rin started making those strange gestures with her hands and silently mouthing words. The girl did the same, then bowed and left the room.

“Who that?” Grick asked, turning fully in his chair to watch her leave.

“That’s Xiu, she’s a very good friend of mine,” Rin explained. “I asked her to go get someone for us. Just please be patient for a moment.”

Grick waited patiently, just as Rin had asked, occasionally patting Lily’s head. He could tell Lily was tired of being here. “Don’t worry, we get back and eat tasties soon, Lily,” Grick assured her.

Finally, Xiu came back with someone else who Grick recognized.

“Mei! I mean… Sakura!” Grick said. Unlike last time, Sakura didn’t have shackles around her wrists and she was walking freely.

“S-Ser Grick?” Sakura asked nervously as she came in.

“It’s Hero Grick now,” Rin corrected her. “And please, have a seat Sakura.”

Sakura looked nervously back at Xiu, then came forward and took a seat on the opposite side of Grick.

Rin stood and walked across the room as she spoke. “So as it turns out, Sakura here does not know how to read either. So I had an excellent idea.” She pulled something off the wall and rolled it toward them. It was a flat black thing in a wooden frame. She set it up in front of them then grabbed a small white thing and began writing words on it. “I’m going to teach you both how to read.”

Sakura blinked. Grick’s ears lifted up. “You teach Grick to read human things? But… Grick doesn’t think Grick can…” he was slightly embarrassed to admit that, especially in front of Sakura. But if Sakura didn’t know how to read either, and if Rin was his friend, he supposed he shouldn’t be worried about it. Even Lily wouldn’t think he was stupid for not reading.

“Of course you can,” Rin said reassuringly. “Nobody is born just knowing how to read… Hmmm, well, other than…” she waved a thought away. “Anyway. Reading isn’t hard, you just have to be taught, and it’s much easier to learn when learning with someone else.”

“Um… H-High Regent.” Sakura bowed her head. “You are too kind, but I don’t want to be any trouble.”

“None at all,” Rin said. “In fact, you’ll be aiding Grick which is in turn doing a great service to me. So we’re killing a few birds with a single stone with this. Now, lets begin.” She pointed at the words she’d written on the board. “This one says Grick, and this one says Sakura. These are your names.”
