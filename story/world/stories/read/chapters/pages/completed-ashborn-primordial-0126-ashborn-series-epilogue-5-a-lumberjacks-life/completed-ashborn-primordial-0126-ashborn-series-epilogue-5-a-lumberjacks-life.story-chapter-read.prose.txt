Vir swung the axe with every ounce of his strength, sending it flying faster and faster until…

Thunk.

The iron blade came to a stop half an inch further into the tree, sending small bits of wood tumbling.

“I never actually got the chance to fell a tree,” Vir said, wiping away the sweat that had beaded on his brow. “I had no idea it was this tough.”

“A tree is a strong and long-lived thing,” Siya said, patting the trunk Vir had begun working into. “This one might not be as grand as its brethren, but it, too, is a proud and mighty organism.”

She was certainly right about that. Vir gazed up at the great trees of the Godshollow, marveling at their magnificence that hadn’t diminished even once since he’d roamed these woods as a boy.

“That it is,” Vir replied. “And with luck and a bit of effort, this great tree will become your new room.”

Siya said nothing, but the hungry smile on her face told him everything about what she felt about that prospect.

“Awooo!” came a howl, making both Vir and Siya turn to its source.

A spotted brown and white bandy came running up to them, a black, shadowy form on his heels.

“Awoo awoooo!” Neel cried, jumping onto Vir and tackling them both to the ground.

“Come on, you big oaf!” Vir said, laughing as the bandy licked his face relentlessly before turning to give the Ash Wolf a smug look.

“Yes, yes.” Vir patted the bandy as he sat back up. “Everyone knows you’re the fastest, most well-loved beast of the pack.”

Shan, meanwhile, huffed, approaching Siya before sitting on his haunches in a most dignified manner. As if he were sending a message to the bandy, who just looked back with wide, innocent eyes, and a lolling tongue.

“Not true,” Siya said, brushing the handsome beast’s jet-black coat. “I’m rather partial to Shan and his friends.”

Not one second later, she’d been all but swarmed by a half-dozen other wolves that Blinked into place. Each larger than Shan. Each vying for her attention before their leader howled, sending them scampering away.

“Silly pups!” Siya laughed. “There’s plenty of room for all of you now! No need to fight.”

Siya gave chase to the beasts, leading them into the meadow just next to the tree Vir was working on, while he leaned against Rudvik’s woodcutter’s ax and watched on with a proud smile.

“Do you see, Rudvik? I’ve a family of my own now, just like you always wanted. We’re not your average family, but well, we never were, were we?”

Had his father known when he’d accepted Vir from the four-armed giant that fateful night? Had he guessed the crying, gray-skinned baby would become the person he was now?

All his life, Vir had tried to do right by the values Rudvik had taught him. Tried to honor his memory.

It had not been an easy journey.

But there had been so much happiness there, too. So much accomplishment. So much adventure. So much life.

Looking back upon everything that had happened, Vir still found it hard to believe. From his time with Riyan to his forays in Daha. His time as an adventurer and his journey across the Ash. His discovery of the demon realm and his rebellion. His years as a Raja, the Ash Tears, Janak, the Fateweavers, and everything in between.

In just over a decade, he’d seen more than most did in a thousand lifetimes. Had endured more hardship than any being alive… had experienced more joys.

Despite the pain, despite the strife, he realized now that it had been a good life.

A wonderful life.

To think it had only just begun…

The distinct clacking of hooves brought him from his reverie, and these hooves were more distinct than most.

Though he’d long ago healed Bumpy’s bum leg, the Ash’va had spent long years living with its condition, and some habits died hard. To this day, Vir could identify Bumpy’s trot the moment he heard it.

“Siya? Come back now,” he called.

Obedient as ever, the girl came running back, laughing all the way as bandy and Ash Wolf alike played at chasing her down.

“It’s not fair!” she said in a voice that was part shriek part laugh. “They’re so much faster than me!”

“Sounds an awful lot like a sore loser to me,” Vir said, cocking an eyebrow. “Wouldn’t you say?”

“I’d have to agree,” Bumpy’s rider said, hopping lightly off the mount.

“Oh?” Vir asked, turning. “What might this be?”

“This? Oh, nothing. I just baked you a cake. Happy birthday, Vir!”

Before he knew it, Vir found himself in a great hug.

His vision filled with pine-scented locks of blazing red.
