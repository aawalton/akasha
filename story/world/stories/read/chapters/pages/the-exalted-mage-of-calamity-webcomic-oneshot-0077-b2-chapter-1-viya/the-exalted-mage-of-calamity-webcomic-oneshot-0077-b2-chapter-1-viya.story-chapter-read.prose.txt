Swift winds swept through the valley, carrying a cold, biting chill.

A temporary camp had been set up along the lower ridge. Members of the expedition moved between tents and supply crates, each with their own tasks.. Some carried food, others sorted documents; everyone else checked their tools. It was a standard operation; a simple coordinated hunt to study and harvest monster materials.

Above, dark shapes flickered across the distant sky. At a glance, they might have been birds. But as one drew close, that illusion fell apart immediately.

A sharp screech tore through the air as a wyvern plunged downward. Its wings sliced through the wind in a steep dive; the scales on its body glinted beneath the midday sun.

Below, a figure stepped forward. She carried no weapon and spoke no incantation. Instead, she cracked her knuckles and stared up at the descending beast. A smile plastered her face. She drew back her fist. A mysterious red sheen of energy coated her hand, warping the air around her. For a moment, everything seemed to compress. Wind curled toward her, the dense pressure of it all gathered at the edge of her knuckles.

Then she struck.

A violent burst of compressed air shot upward, slamming into the wyvern’s chest like a cannon blast. Its body jerked mid-dive, like it had run into a steel wall.

The creature shook its head, recovering from the sudden blow. It screeched again, wings flapping wildly as it steadied itself. It stared down at her, and prepared to swoop once more.

But before it could descend—

Another impact struck its body.

A shriek of pain echoed out, its body convulsing as the strike was even more powerful than the last. It growled, wings faltering as it struggled to regain control. But it wasn’t given time to rest.

The girl below was relentless. Another hit. And another. Each strike came faster than the last, invisible bursts hammering into its body. The air detonated with each strike, bruising its scales, and breaking its will to continue.

At last, its wings gave out. It fell straight down.

Dust and loose stones exploded upward as its body crashed against the mountainside. It tumbled down the jagged slope before coming to a crashing stop below.

“Sixteenth of the day,” a voice called from behind. “Though that one was still quite young.”

The girl lowered her arm and rolled her shoulder once, easing her tension. A grin spread across her face.

“Still counts,” she said. “It came at me first.”

A few paces back, a young woman with a small ledger in her hand watched her with unimpressed eyes. She quickly finished jotting something down before snapping it shut.

“Mm. If you say so,” she replied. “But its hide won’t fetch even half of what a proper adult’s would. So I’m not counting it.”

“Oh, come on, Viya. Don’t be such a hard-ass.”

Several members of the expedition hurried past them, carrying tools and satchels as they moved to dismantle the fallen wyvern.

Viya let out a quiet sigh. “Princess, please. Watch your language while we are around others.”

“If you’re going to call me Princess, at least use the full title—Princess Evelyn,” she shot back. “And besides, no one here is going to say anything. If they report it to my father, I’ll have their heads.”

“I must concur with Miss Viya’s assessment.”

The voice came from behind them.

An older man, nearing his sixties, stepped forward, dressed in an immaculate suit. His posture was straight, his hair neatly combed, and his beard was trimmed with the care of a gentleman. Despite his age, he carried a vigor in his stride.

“It would be wise to regulate your speech, even among your subjects.”

Evelyn clicked her tongue. “Butt out of it, Kael.”

Kael inclined his head. “It is my duty to remind you of such matters. I’m afraid I cannot ‘butt out’ of responsibilities entrusted to me by your father.”

“Duty, this. Duty, that.” She exhaled through her nose, unimpressed, and turned away.

“Princess—Evelyn,” Viya said slowly, “it is also your duty not to cause trouble during this expedition. That was His Majesty’s condition.”

“Saying ‘hard-ass’ won’t start a rebellion. Deal with it,” Evelyn replied, waving a hand dismissively. “Come on. There are more wyverns ahead. I want to hit thirty today.”

Viya sighed, clutching the ledger to her chest. “I don’t know how you do it, Kael.”

Kael stepped beside her, both of them watching the princess stride ahead.

“You grow used to it, Miss Viya,” he said with mild amusement. “Though you should have seen her when she was younger. It was far worse.”

Viya let out a small snort. “I somehow find that both hard and easy to believe.”

“Mm. Quite. I do hope you can help her become a bit more… regal. I’m not suited for such a task. She rarely listens to me.”

“You think she listens to me?”

He glanced down at her. “You are her only friend. Your words carry far more weight than most, aside from His Majesty.”

Viya shrugged. “Being her assistant is already enough work. Being her friend on top of that? That’s nearly impossible.”

“Mm. There is some conflict between the roles,” Kael admitted. “But as long as Her Highness sees you as someone reliable, it serves its purpose. She feels at ease around you.”

“That’s not exactly friendship,” Viya replied.

“It still works in your favor, does it not?”

Viya hummed, holding the ledger a bit tighter. Her eyes followed Evelyn as she approached the other expedition members, watching them begin their work.

“I suppose it does.”

Once the dismantlers finished processing the wyvern, the expedition moved on, pushing deeper into the mountains.

Along the way, Evelyn took down several more adult wyverns. She found it enjoyable—almost liberating. It was a simple way to relieve stress and escape the suffocating expectations of her title.

“Say, didn’t you mention the Mage Association was looking for wyvern claws?” Evelyn asked.

“Mm. Yes. They’ve sent requests across the major cities, offering payment for any they can get,” Viya replied.

“Wonder what for,” Evelyn said, driving another compressed strike into a grounded wyvern that hadn’t quite died yet. “Then again, mages are always chasing strange materials.”

A set of hurried footsteps approached.

They turned and spotted a man in a suit, carrying a stack of ledgers far thicker than Viya’s.

“Princess Evelyn. Miss Viya. This wyvern should be the last one we need for the expedition, once it’s dismantled.”

Evelyn turned. “What? We’re done already? We haven’t even seen any elder wyverns.”

“Princess—even an elder wyvern would be beyond your control,” Viya said.

Evelyn waved her off. “Won’t know until I try. Besides, the adults here are easy.”

“That’s because wyverns grow stronger with age,” Viya replied. “None of the ones you’ve killed would come close to a wyvern that’s lived over a hundred years.”

“Miss Viya is correct, Your Highness,” the man added. “Even Sir Kael would struggle against several at once while keeping everyone safe.”

Evelyn scowled. “Several? I’m not stupid enough to fight several. I just want one.” She crossed her arms. “Finish dismantling that wyvern. We’re not heading back until I beat an elder.”

The man’s expression faltered. He glanced at Viya, hoping for help.

Viya met his gaze and simply shrugged.

His shoulders slumped as he sighed. “Yes, Your Highness. I’ll relay the order.”

“Finally!” Evelyn shouted.

An elder wyvern descended toward her, its wingspan nearly twice that of the others. Its scales were darker, thicker—clearly aged. Wind curled around its body as it dove.

Evelyn didn’t step back. Instead, she stepped forward, grinning.

The air tightened and compressed around her as she clenched her fist.

“Come on!”

The wyvern roared, jaws open, claws extended.

She struck it hard. A violent burst of compressed wind slammed into its chest, halting its descent mid-air. The shockwave rippled outward, kicking up a powerful gust that lifted her hair.

The wyvern shrieked but held itself steady. It glared down, then dove again, unfazed.

Evelyn slipped to the side as its claws slammed into the ground, tearing up stone and dirt.

She grinned, pulling back her fist. “That’s more like it.”

Nearby, Kael fought his own battle while keeping an eye on her. His blade remained sheathed as he stepped forward and struck. Each motion sent slicing waves through the air, cleaving the wings of an adult wyvern that approached him.

Around them, the expedition members engaged the remaining wyverns, working together to bring them down.

Viya stood near Kael, her gaze fixed on Evelyn.

An adult wyvern spotted her from above and dove. Its claws reached for her—but passed straight through. The monster crashed straight into the ground, smashing its head and stunning itself.

From the side, Viya stepped forward, light shuddering around her as she reappeared. With a wave of her hand, flames erupted, engulfing the wyvern. It shrieked as Viya pressed again, summoning invisible spears of mana that pierced its body, pinning it to the ground until it fell still.

“I’m really not a fan of this,” Viya said, the four white wings behind her palm faltering.

Kael appeared beside her, his movements silent. His gaze shifted toward the princess.

“Hm. It is dangerous,” he admitted. “But it may be good for Her Highness to experience real combat.”

Viya crossed her arms. “It’s reckless. There are better ways to handle this. I hate that she constantly charges at things blindly.”

Kael chuckled. Turning, he struck again. His sheathed blade released a slicing force that split a wyvern in half mid-charge.

“Think of it as training,” he said. “It will benefit her. Besides, she rarely leaves the capital. Allow her this.”

Viya rolled her eyes. “That doesn’t mean she should throw herself into danger. She’d be safer staying in the castle all day.”

Ahead, Evelyn surged forward and drove her fist into the elder wyvern’s head as it landed. The impact sent it flying dozens of meters, skidding across the ground.

“Haha! Stupid wyvern. Take that!” she shouted.

Viya watched her for a moment… then sighed.
