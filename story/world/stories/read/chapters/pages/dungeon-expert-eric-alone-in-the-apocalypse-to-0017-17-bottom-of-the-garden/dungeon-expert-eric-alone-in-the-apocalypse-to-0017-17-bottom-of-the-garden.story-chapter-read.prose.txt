Eric walked on. Compared to the raw forest and dirt paths of the snakemen dungeon, this was a much nicer walk. He strode over mossy stone steps along a manicured path, with beautiful flowers and perfectly trimmed topiaries waiting around every corner. It felt more like he was taking a stroll through a fancy garden than delving a dungeon. Statues waited on the corners, and he rounded on each one with his bat ready to strike, but most were just lichen-coated statues of angels, animals, or bucolic farming children at 1/4 scale—the kind of cheap concrete statuary anyone could buy at their local gardening store.

He kept his eyes open for the houses or gnomes, but none appeared out of the beatific garden. The further he got without encountering any, the more nervous he got. His heart thundered in his chest, and he whipped around at any motion or any brightly colored object, ready to strike.

At last, Eric paused, leaning against a tree to take weight off his injured leg; it wasn’t like the burned skin bore weight, but the way it twisted and tensed when he put weight on it still hurt. Taking weight off it hurt, too, but after a moment, it settled into a duller ache. He’d already accepted the pain and moved it to the back of his mind, like a blister during a hike, but if he was taking a moment, he was going to do whatever he could to lessen the pain.

He wiped his brow and gazed ahead, at the wandering path that seemed to twist on forever. I can’t keep going like this. I’m going to wear myself out before I reach the end of this dungeon. The gnomes hadn’t spawned until he’d deliberately activated them. He was almost certainly safe as long as he wore the snakeskin gear and moved softly. He didn’t need to jump at every shadow and move on his tiptoes; he could walk normally and move with a normal amount of awareness. He was geared up to fight this dungeon in particular, so he might as well act like it.

Damn you, Cy, he thought, then chuckled. It wasn’t fair at all. The rabbit had activated the gnomes, but it was after he asked about it with the intention of discovering how the enemies worked. If he’d done his whole scouting run without activating anything—heck, if he’d gotten to the end of the dungeon without facing any resistance and came away empty handed, with nothing to show and no levels, he’d have been more disappointed. The dangers he faced today gave him the power to face the dangers he’d face tomorrow; the only wasted day was one where he didn’t grow and gained nothing.

It was a vast departure from his life up until now, the life he’d spent grinding away at a desk job, and at the same time, not that extreme a departure at all. He’d spent every day trying to grow there, too. To get promoted, and when he realized that was a fool’s errand and borderline impossible, to hone his craft and gain enough skill to get hired at the next place with a better salary. It had just taken longer to grow, and the endgame of “a better wage” was far more boring than doing actual magic, but he’d still tried his best. Thrown his life and all his hours into it, for all he had to show from that.

That’s over now. From here on out, I’m not a useless office drone. I’m a Player. Fighting for the sake of my world. Even thinking it felt embarrassing and cheesy. He giggled nervously under his breath, then sucked in air and pushed away from the tree. No more panic. Steady progress, even keel. When it was time to fight, then he could panic, but while he was just walking around, there was no point wasting his energy on unnecessary hyper-vigilance.

He kept on, keeping his eyes open but no longer exerting so much energy to pay attention to every single little thing. Up ahead, a stand of bamboo encased a wooden gate, the kind with posts and a crossbar but no actual door to block the way forward. A bamboo thicket spread beyond the gate. Narrow paths snaked through the tightly planted stalks, dancing with striped shade. A cool wind blew, and the bamboo shuddered, leaves keening as it swayed.

That’s not creepy. Eric swallowed nervously and adjusted his grip on the bat. If he was a betting man, he’d put money on the next threat awaiting him in that grove. With one last check over his shoulder to ensure he wasn’t being followed by anything, he passed through the gate.

The second his foot passed through the gateway, the wind stilled. Eric froze, startled into stillness himself. For a few seconds, nothing moved. Not Eric, not the bamboo, nothing. Everything remained still.

Eric chuckled to himself. Now I’m freaking out because it’s not windy. This is absurd. He strode forward, walking through the gate and into the glen proper.

One single small section of bamboo trembled. The trembling raced away from him, hurrying deeper into the glen and vanishing.

He turned to watch it go, then grimaced. Don’t like that.

Eric picked one of the paths at random and entered the glen. As he walked, more bamboo trembled, racing away from him. He whipped around, searching the bamboo for the source of the trembling, but before he could focus on the first spot, bamboo knocked together on his other side, and he spun rather than risk an ambush, only to find the same impenetrable knot of bamboo giving up no secrets.

Bamboo whispered behind him. Eric spun again and threw himself at the bamboo, trying to chase the shaking stalks, but there wasn’t even room in the glen for his feet. The bamboo nearly grew on top of itself, with no space in between. He had to twist his feet sideways and walk on the edges of them to progress, and even then, he slid and slipped on the thick base of the bamboo, too stiff and strong to bend under his weight. He only made it two steps before he backed out. He was moving far too slowly to catch whatever it was, and even if he caught it, he’d be at such a massive disadvantage that it wasn’t worth it to try.

Standing on the path once more, he pinched his chin. A well-placed firebolt might set this whole place on fire… but then, it was healthy green bamboo, so maybe not. I also don’t know that I want to spend my last mana on trying to set a glen on fire for no clear advantage. He had a mana potion, but that was it; just one. It would be a waste to use it here.

I did get two more rewards from finding these two dungeons, but now isn’t the time to open them. It took way too long to open the rewards for him to risk it right here, right now with everything going on around him.

He pressed on, doing his best to ignore the bamboo shuddering around him. The tall stalks crowded out the sky, their leaves as innumerable as the stars. They layered on top of one another, becoming a thicket of slender green tongues that lapped the sky.

Eric slowed again, peering closer at the bamboo leaves. They really were moving strangely, almost like tongues, wiggling of their own accord rather than according to the wind. Maybe it was just an illusion of his on-edge, exhausted mind, but they really seemed to be moving strangely.

An eye popped open on the stalk of bamboo he was examining. The pupil darted about madly, then locked onto him, gazing him right in the eye.

Oh shit. Eric turned around, meaning to flee, but the path had closed off behind him. Bamboo pressed close, leaving him only five or so feet to retreat before there was nothing but impenetrable thicket. The rustling started up again, but this time, it rushed toward him, little red hats pointed directly at him as a small army of gnomes hurtled through the stalks.

“Oh shit!” he shouted, backing up to the furthest point he could. Panicked, he cast around him, searching for something, anything, any way out of this death trap. A quick glance in his pocket confirmed that Cy was still asleep; no help forthcoming from that quarter. The Magic Wand still lacked batteries—the little general store he had access to right now didn’t stock batteries. Not that a single-target weapon like that would do much good here, anyways.

Wait. I have everything I need right here. Eric took a deep breath and slid his bat into his belt. If this situation was a puzzle, then he’d just figured it out. He didn’t like his solution, but it was a solution, and it was better than dying, trapped in this grove.

The first gnome reached the edge of the bamboo, its compact body and tiny feet perfect for shoving through the thickly planted stalks. Eyes glowing a maniacal red, it leaped off the ground, arms spread wide to latch on to Eric.

Eric bent and caught it out of the air. Its body cracked and began to glow, and the porcelain emanated an intense heat. Before his hands could burn, Eric turned and chucked it ahead, deeper into the bamboo. It flew down the short length of the path and hit the bamboo wall, then exploded. Bamboo shattered, blasting the deeper stalks with shards of pottery and hardened bamboo alike. The ground was still a mess of broken stalks, but now there was room for his body to fit through the grove.

He grinned. It does work.

Another two gnomes leaped at him. Eric sidestepped both, then chased one down and snatched it up from behind. Little legs and arms kicking and punching, it began to crack and glow. A third gnome leaped at him from the side, and he jumped back, barely dodging it, then threw his cooked grenade—ahem, gnome. It flew over the blasted patch and exploded, clearing his path out yet again.

Red light glimmered in the corner of his eye. Eric spun just in time to find a gnome halfway up a stalk at about head-height, skin already crackling, bursting with light. It locked eyes with him and grinned.

Eric widened his eyes. He dropped to the ground, curling up with his arms over his head.

The gnome exploded. The force of it threw him backward, sprawled against the bamboo behind him. Heat singed every part of him that wasn’t covered in snakeskin. Bits of bamboo and pottery struck him, pelting him with a dozen tiny wounds.

Sensing weakness, gnomes hurtled at him, flying at him from all angles. The clink of pottery grew louder behind him, and he felt small hands clutching at his clothes and hair. Eric threw his weight forward a second before a half-dozen explosions rang out, hammering his back. This time, he sprawled forward, barely catching himself against the stalks opposite him. More gnomes poured out of the dense green, eyes aglow and little hands grabbing, running directly at his face. He jumped up and backpedaled, stumbling over bamboo stumps. Another surge of explosions rippled through the bamboo before him.

For all that he’d almost died, the non-stop explosions had cleared out a good area in the bamboo. The footing was bad everywhere but where he stood, but he had more room to see gnomes coming and a bigger area he could snatch them from now. Gnomes rushed at him, but now he had the advantage. He grabbed one, dodged another three flying gnomes, then threw his grabbed gnome at the far end of the clearing, blasting his way forward. Snatching another gnome, he turned to find the area behind him a veritable minefield. Rather than throw it forward, he tossed it at the thick field of gnomes. It exploded, prematurely exploding the few gnomes who’d already began to crack, who in turn exploded the gnomes behind them. The chain reaction rapidly spread into the bamboo, blasting his field of vision yet wider.

Eric laughed aloud. “Come on! Let’s see what you’ve got!”

The ground shook. In the not so distance, the bamboo shook so hard it sounded like it was in a hurricane, then dropped flat to the ground as something truly immense stomped his way.

Eric’s smile drooped, and he swallowed, immediately regretting his bravado. Instinctively, he backed up, even though he had nowhere to go. I should’ve just kept my mouth shut.
