In one of the castle’s grand, ornate halls, King Julius leaned back in his throne atop the long staircase, massaging his temples as if trying to quell the pounding in his head.

Court had just concluded, and the news he’d received had only soured his mood further. Were it not for the need to maintain his image, King Julius might have erupted in fury during the proceedings.

“My king, we must decide soon—before we suffer further losses,” said a man in a clean, plain green robe, his tone respectful.

This was the Right Minister, Alexander Rufus. Though his manner was calm and composed, his eyes held a sharp, calculating glint—befitting someone who held a high position on the royal council.

“Alex... Where did I go wrong?” King Julius asked, his voice carrying a rare note of vulnerability.

“Your Majesty, we have always made the most prudent decisions based on the information available at the time. If anything, it’s our lack of vital intelligence that has led us to this point,” Alexander replied.

The king gave a hollow laugh, his double chin quivering with the motion. “I appreciate the sentiment, but you and I both know those are just excuses. From the moment we declared war on the Qin Empire, everything began unraveling.”

“I keep feeling this... pressure. As if I’m not the one making the decisions. Like an unseen hand is pulling my strings, forcing my moves—moves I would never make of my own accord.”

The Right Minister stiffened, the words clearly striking a chord.

“I thought it was just me...” Alexander murmured, his expression tightening. “It certainly feels like our options are being narrowed by something—or someone.”

The ominous suggestion caused the king’s eyes to flicker with sudden hope.

“You feel it too? Then what could it be?”

Alexander shook his head, extinguishing that hope as quickly as it had appeared. “We don’t have enough information. The Qin and Lhair armies are moving independently, so it’s unlikely they’re coordinating. Yet both continue to suppress our forces, making it nearly impossible to maneuver.”

“I suggest we withdraw from the Qin front and focus on fortifying our own territory to block the Lhair advance. We were fortunate to save Clayton City—if not, we might have been forced to retreat even earlier.”

The king sagged in his throne, letting out a long, weary sigh. His entire posture slumped, making him seem older than his years.

“If we retreat, that bastard will mock me for the rest of my life,” he muttered through clenched teeth.

“I’m sorry, my king. This useless servant has failed you,” Alexander said, kneeling at the foot of the steps.

“It’s not just you who failed, Alex,” the king said quietly. “A good king shoulders the failures of his subordinates as if they were his own. No excuses.”

His words echoed through the empty hall, a faint hum lingering in the air.

“Organize a full retreat of all our forces within the Qin Empire. Any fortresses we’ve captured are to be razed before withdrawal. Leave nothing behind for the Empire to salvage.” The king’s voice turned cold, his eyes glinting with malice.

“Understood, my king,” Alexander replied, rising to his feet.

Just as the king opened his mouth to dismiss him, Alexander spoke again.

“Your Majesty… would it not be prudent to send Prince Leo to oversee the retreat? He is your heir, after all.”

At the mention of his son, the king’s expression softened, if only for a moment.

“Leo… He is a capable boy. Very well—my heart will be at ease knowing he’s the one handling the operation.”

But Alexander wasn’t finished.

“If we send only the crown prince, there may be unrest in the inner palace, my king…” he said, his voice trailing off.

At the mention of the inner palace, King Julius visibly paled.

“What would you have me do? I don’t trust Lyon to accomplish anything. That boy’s been coddled by his mother all his life, but since the rest of my children are still too young, he is the only other heir…” His tone was laced with frustration and regret.

“There may be a way, my king. Perhaps we can use this opportunity to even the playing field—for the upcoming succession.” Alexander’s eyes gleamed with subtle intent.

Meanwhile, in Luke’s dorm room at the Royal Academy, Kayson, Sebastian, and Victoria were gathered. Weeks had passed since Victoria’s infiltration of the noble circle, and at last, the final batch of ceramic vials had arrived—just in time to store their newly crafted perfume.

Kayson and Sebastian were already fully briefed on the plan.

“Any word from Hilda?” Luke asked, carefully pouring liquid into an empty vial.

All eyes instinctively turned to Sebastian—unofficially conscripted as the liaison between Hilda and Luke.

Sebastian nodded. “She says Qin Hu’s been trying to get her hands on the perfume for the past few days. Apparently, she’s refused to attend any social gatherings until she acquires it.”

“That’s good,” Luke said without looking up. His hands remained steady as he filled another vial. “When will she make her move?”

“Not yet. Hilda says she won’t bring it up until Qin Hu speaks with her father. According to her, Ming Hu is far too cunning—he’d grow suspicious if she acted prematurely.”

Luke nodded in satisfaction.

It seems putting Hilda in charge was the right decision, he thought.

Still, it also meant a delay. He wouldn’t be able to launch his next set of products just yet. Originally, the plan was to use the Black Tiger Gang as an exclusive distributor, bypassing the established merchant networks in the city.

But now, Luke intended to deal a blow to the noblewomen of Ralis City. If they had to swallow their pride and purchase perfume and scented soap from Fairy Qin—the very woman they despised—it would rattle the city’s social foundation.

When the foundation is shaken, those hiding in the shadows will appear, he thought grimly.

Alone, this strategy might not stir much. But with the volatile second prince entering the equation, it would become volatile—explosive, even.

“Well, all we can do is wait for now,” Luke concluded, placing the stopper on the last ceramic vial.

Just as he leaned back to relax, a sudden knock at the door sent a jolt through the room. Instantly, everyone tensed.

His three companions—each empowered by the Sovereign System—shifted into alert mode. Individually, they were capable of defeating hundreds. Together, they were nigh unstoppable.

Luke gave Kayson a subtle nod before calling out, “Who is it?”

“Viscount Drakon, this is Tristan Gale. Do you have a moment to speak?” came the voice from beyond the door, calm and composed.

Tristan? The head librarian? Luke thought, surprised.

He glanced toward Kayson and silently signaled him to open the door. Rising to his feet, Luke straightened his robe, making himself presentable.

The door opened, revealing Tristan Gale—the middle-aged librarian Luke had met in the academy archives. The man wore a plain but spotless white robe. Standing beside him was a stranger.

“Head Librarian, forgive my lack of formality. I’d intended to visit you, but things have been hectic,” Luke said, cupping his fist in polite greeting and offering a shallow bow.

While his rank as a viscount didn’t require such gestures, Luke believed in showing respect to those who had earned it, regardless of their status.

“It’s no matter, Viscount Drakon,” Tristan replied, waving off the apology as he stepped inside. His eyes swept across the room and its occupants.

“This man is a royal messenger. Please hear his words,” he said, gesturing toward the man at his side.

The messenger was well-dressed but unremarkable in bearing. It was clear he was a vessel, not a voice. He drew a scroll from within his robe and unrolled it with deliberate care before clearing his throat.

“Kneel for the royal decree!” he announced, his voice unexpectedly high-pitched.

Luke’s jaw tensed. He certainly didn’t want to kneel—but royal decree was law. To defy it would be to invite charges of treason.

He cast a look toward the others, then dropped to one knee and bowed his head.

“Hear this, Viscount Drakon. By order of King Julius Marxx, you are hereby commanded to accompany Prince Lyon in organizing the retreat of our eastern forces from the Qin Empire. You shall act in the capacity of General of the Marxx Army. All fortresses and cities once held within Qin territory are to be vacated and razed, in accordance with this royal decree. Do you accept?”

Luke’s body trembled involuntarily at the weight of the royal decree. Had his face been visible, the others would have seen the shock in his wide, bulging eyes.

His mind spiraled, desperately trying to make sense of the situation.

Are they trying to get rid of me—alongside the second prince? he thought, panic creeping in.

But the logic didn’t line up. From what he’d gathered, the second prince’s supporters were mostly traitors and those discontent with the crown prince’s faction. It wouldn’t make sense to eliminate Prince Lyon, who was already losing in the succession race. If anything, his supporters would want to prop him up—to make him seem more capable and deserving of the throne.

Yet perhaps the greater shock was that the Marxx Kingdom was retreating from the Qin Empire altogether. Had they finally recognized Lhair’s invasion as a threat grave enough to warrant ending the war?

As thoughts raced through his mind, Luke suddenly realized he hadn’t responded. The messenger’s eyes had begun narrowing, suspicion flickering across his face.

Though every part of him resisted being dragged into whatever scheme this was, Luke had no choice.

“I have received and accept the royal decree,” he said firmly, his voice deep and unwavering.

The messenger seemed satisfied. He smiled, rolled up the scroll, and tucked it back into his robe.

“The meeting point is in two days—at the palace gates. You may bring one additional servant, should you choose.”

“Thank you, messenger,” Luke replied, rising slowly to his feet. His face had settled into its usual calm, but his heart beat with unease.

“I will now take my leave,” the messenger said with a small bow, then turned and exited.

The moment the door closed, Luke felt three pairs of eyes fixed on him. He didn’t need to ask what they were thinking.

“Viscount Drakon,” Tristan said gently, “I apologize for arriving without notice. The royal messenger was... insistent about visiting tonight.”

“Think nothing of it,” Luke replied, waving the apology aside. “Though I’m curious—why did he involve you? A quick word with the guards at the gate would’ve been enough to locate me.”

At this, Tristan smiled—but it didn’t reach his eyes.

“I am to accompany you, General.”

Luke blinked, stunned. It makes sense to send me since I’ve already proved myself, but sending a librarian?

Yet the name Gale carried weight. The Gale family had a storied history of military service—generals, commanders, and war heroes. But Tristan Gale had never appeared in those tales.

Sensing Luke’s confusion, Tristan chuckled softly. “I assure you, General, I won’t be a burden. I spent many years serving as quartermaster alongside my father. My experience lies in logistics and supply lines. I may not be a warrior, but I know how to keep an army running.”

Luke’s expression eased, though the tension in his chest remained. He cupped his fist respectfully. “Then I’ll be in your care, Tristan.”

“The pleasure is mine,” Tristan replied with a polite bow and a satisfied smile.
