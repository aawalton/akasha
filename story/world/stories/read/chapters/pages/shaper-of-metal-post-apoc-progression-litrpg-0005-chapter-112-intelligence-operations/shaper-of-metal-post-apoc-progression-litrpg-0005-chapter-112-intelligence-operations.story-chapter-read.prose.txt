Chapter 112: Intelligence Operations

Jack figured he had to at least mention Délight contacting him. It wasn’t as if that were some private thing, after all. Nor did it seem unreasonable to mention the apology. Primarily, he just wouldn’t go into detail on the deeper psychological things.

“Alright, so, strangely enough, I also have something to report,” Jack said into the gestalt. “Délight was waiting outside my door when I left. I thought exactly what you’re all thinking about that right now, but I’m pretty sure she’s aware of the futility of trying to manipulate me in her way. She actually apologized for her treatment of me before. Olive branch extended, clearing the air, that sort of thing. Seemed legit to me, and I don’t think I’m a terrible judge of character.” After a pause, he added, “Perhaps there’s tension because she’s changing a bit? Growing up a smidge, thanks to all the fieldwork she’s doing. I’m sure it does wonders to get out of this insular ivory tower setup.”

Bo immediately scoffed flagrantly and loudly. “Ssssffff! Right. Don’t think that shit wasn’t calculated. She always knows exactly what she’s doing.”

Highfive sighed grandly. “Man, is she good, or what? My bro gets sold down the river for her even after she gets caught with her hand in the cookie jar.”

“Blondy, that is a shit-hell of a mixed metaphor,” Jack replied teasingly. “Do better, would you? Anyway, what exactly do you lot think she hopes to gain with contrition?”

“Take your edge off, that’s what. Soften you up to her future advances.”

“What sort of advances?” Ira asked. “That word has several connotations, does it not?”

“Yeah. All sorts, basically. They don’t call her a Charmer for nothing. If you aren’t rejecting her, you’re accepting her. That’s stage one, already. From there, her alchemy has leverage, you know? Boom! She’s in. So, uh, it makes the rest easier. From that fascination shit, to warm, overpowering feelings, and even persuasion and seduction. Sh-”

“Wh-what?!” Ira exclaimed in alarm. “Seduction?!”

“Easy there, Ira,” Bo said soothingly. “Jack is not susceptible there at all. Are you, Jack?”

The tone was so blatantly leading that Jack closed his eyes, shook his head, and squeezed the bridge of his nose. “Not particularly, Bo,” Jack replied, fully allowing his annoyance to filter into his tone. “I’ve already pointed out how I’ve resisted those quote, unquote, ‘advances,’ after all.”

“She tried to seduce you?” Ira asked sharply. She immediately shifted in tone, however. “That is, I… I haven’t heard this story.”

“I only heard about it in the vaguest terms,” Bo said defensively.

“Right, bro, uh,” Highfive started awkwardly. “Think you only mentioned it to me, probs.”

“I knew,” Inkblot interjected. “Honestly, none of you should be surprised. All of you were definitely aware of the waves Jack made right off the bat. Délight’s interest is quite predictable, especially when no one knew anything much about his powerset.”

“Look,” Jack said in an exasperated tone, “It’s not that big of a deal.” At least, he damn sure didn’t want them to think it was, being his business. “As far as I’m concerned, it's water under the bridge in a troll-eat-troll kinda world. Not something to dwell on. She fragged up, she went overboard, she apologized. Time to move on.”

“Damn, man,” Highfive exclaimed. “You’re more forgiving than me. Your choice, though. I’ll hate her double for you, bro. No hesitation to smash her face into the pavement. Also, um, does that metaphor actually make sense? The troll thing.”

“Yes. Probably.”

“Gotcha. Just checking.”

“I also won’t hesitate,” Bo said. “I owe her a beating for Light’s sake.”

“I have no qualms taking her out,” Jack said, trying for thick assurance. “Once we hit the chamber, she’s just another target. If she thinks I’m an easy mark, she’s managed to hypnotize herself. Alright?”

“You got it, bro!” Highfive said. “Just keep a sense of rejection in mind like that going in, and keep on keepin’ hold of it. That’s the best tactic.”

“I am absolutely confident in you, Jack,” Ira said with a faint hint of passion. “You must not let her manipulate you, especially not via nefarious and unwanted seduction.”

“I definitely won’t, Ira,” Jack said, exasperated.

Inblot chimed in, tone contemplative. “We might pry more about this relationship, which I hereby dub ‘Double Dee.’ Most should know Délight’s tendencies, but emotions are what they are. Jealousy is unpredictable. Defiant could be thrown off if mocked or taunted. He’s not exactly physically imposing, for instance. See where I’m going with that?”

“Oh ho ho ho!” Highfive exclaimed. “Bro, I see exactly where you’re going. If anybody can push those buttons, it’s me! Sure we should go there, though?”

“All’s fair in love and war,” Bo said darkly. Mock darkly, she added, “Drive a wedge right between the Double Dee’s, Fiver.”

Jack facepalmed even as he laughed.

“Real nice,” Highfive said sarcastically.

“Wait, what?” Ira asked futilely.

“You’re a tank, Fiver,” Jack said. “Taunt away. No mercy.”

“I’m sorry,” Ira said, “but what is the issue? I don’t understand any of this.”

“Just the battlefield tactic of talkin’ shit about Délight,” Highfive replied. “Or about the comparative prowess of boyfriends, you know? If he’s really into her, he won’t like that. You get it.”

There was a longish pause. “R-right! Of course.”

“Something like using her powers against her,” Bo added. “But it does depend on his mentality.”

“So,” Jack said, changing the subject, “I guess Augur is still doing his thing. He can inform us later. We’ll get to team training after lunch, unless anyone has any objections?”

“Nope. Lunch is good for information-gathering anyway. We’ll stay split up. Report immediately if someone steps to you. Everyone, be ready to move if needed. No one is getting ganged on.”

After affirmatives went around, the convo abruptly died, and Jack went back to training. Wall Art and Augment.

They were very self-explanatory, grindy things to train, but pretty much all of it could be trained at once. Build a tough-ass wall, resist simulated damage from this or that combinations of forces, repair, rinse, repeat. Changing it up was mostly a superficial matter of different simulations of damage or making different structures. Reinforcing existing ones. The larger masses to work with made it a bit more of a workout than usual. There was more sustained, high-intensity output than standard offensive and finesse training.

Active reinforcement was high in particular. The best method was to take about a third of a given wall, bracing the inside portion. This way, the outer layer was allowed to do its work passively without any effort, and that material in front allowed him to sense what was about to fail, and where, without wasting effort and energy. A third was about as thin as he could go before losing efficiency and effective ‘grip.’

Lindsay had originally provided templates for bracing metal against impact in interlaced structures, but he’d been only marginally above ‘clueless’ until obtaining Augment. The closest thing to getting it was when he’d been training with the Jackette for active reinforcement, but that was hard to translate into anything ‘normal.’

Now, those lessons of Lindsay’s were making a lot more sense. It mostly involved intuitively linking collapsed memorite within a metal, into its own tight, inner matrix. It significantly improved his ability to block attacks, especially when ‘tightening’ in timing to an impact. He was essentially half again better at it than before. It used to be that any old force he blocked could knock a levitating shield, oftentimes right into him. Now that he had some technique to the matter, it legit needed to outclass the output he used in a frame, to move it. That or defeat his timing.

Not insurmountable, but it was a great improvement, and showed the potential for Augment at higher levels as a defensive boon. Too bad it was such a pain altering shit immediately adjacent to him. His carried shield would not lend him the same oomph. On the other hand, his continual improvements to metal made that shield a lot tougher than before. It could take a hit and keep on tickin’.

Suddenly remembering an idea he had, he took a break for a snack and hydration as he pondered it more and investigated. The ‘metal net gun.’ It was amusing in a way, being very ‘gadgety,’ but it simply seemed the best way to deal with someone inside his guard. Pull a trigger, boom! Metal in someone’s face. A massive surface area to work with, too. Those designed to snare people were about twice the width of a person. To avoid it, one either needed to abandon their advance or have superspeed.

The only problem was that they were typically designed with fabric nets in mind. This was a matter custom enough, and ‘optional’ enough, he had to deal with it entirely personally. Central wouldn’t do this for a humble junior agent. Hopefully that didn’t mean it was a dumb idea, after all? Unclear. However, Mini did help out by pointing him to an intranet request board for gadgets, inventions, and similar improvements.

Why not? He threw up a request for something adapted to what he wanted: a metal net for the payload, preferably a pistol-like shape — the default was apparently a dumb-looking giant flashlight. Maybe a little extra oomph than normal, and, of course, a quick-loading function for spares. He’d done literally nothing with the money he’d been making daily — other than buy overpriced food — and this adaptation might not even take much. He actually preferred giving money to these sorts of independent inventors, when he thought about it.

It was unlikely to matter for the upcoming match, but maybe something would come of it at some point.

Jack hopped back into his training — after exhausting his patience with walls and shields, he spent time on a little bit of everything, to keep his skills sharp. In a rare accomplishment, he reached more the limits of his focal fatigue than actual fatigue of any kind. He called it a bit early, before lunch, and went home to relax and let the gains settle enough for Mini to assess.

Augment: Absorption has improved to 0.5!

Augment: Tensile has improved to 0.2!

Control: Cloud has improved to 1.9!

Control: Collect has improved to 2.6!

Create: Lift has improved to 1.0!

Wall Art has improved to 0.7!

Parry (+Speed) has improved to 1.4(+1.0)!

Jack frowned. That’s it? Guess I got spoiled by the Stitcher-era gains. More than likely, I’m slowing down on a lot of shit. Ah well. I’ll get more after this match, I’m sure of that much. Hopefully, I level again, too.

For lunch, Jack got Augur duty. Rather than holing up, they went to the mess hall, since Highfive and Inkblot would be there, just a bit separated, as they finished up any information gathering tries. Bo was having lunch elsewhere, presumably with the ‘too cool for school’ type kids.

Despite expectations and precautions, nothing happened. No one approached them. Jack caught sight of Hiccup and Chime once, moving to sit with another couple he didn’t recognize. Cool gazes were exchanged; that was all. He never saw Defiant or Délight, nor did the others.

They must be doing the same shit we are as a whole — trying for more info on us. Namely, probably me and Ink. Kinda good, if they never think to dig about upgrades. They may not expect what Bo has. They’d have to crack our prior opponents to know that.

But maybe their absence communicated something, too. Maybe they didn’t want exposure on their new guy. Did that then mean he actually did have a weakness? Conjecture, obviously. There could be many reasons, not the least of which was reducing the chances of someone like Highfive or Bo getting into his head.

Well. If we do want that, we’ll have our chance at dinner time.

Augur didn’t talk about match or battle type stuff, showing uncommon discipline without a need for admonition on Jack’s part. Then again, he seemed to be bristling with the anticipatory energy of what he did want to say to all of them, and most certainly would, very soon. He had a special presentation planned, with ‘holographic highlights’ of his simulated mock battle. It was no joke, either; no irrelevant playing about with toys. That was literally the function of his unusual powerset.

Fun, fun, fun.

In that sim, Jack had a nagging feeling they’d lost.

The team was all gathered around in their customary couch area spots. Bo was lazing about on one couch that directly faced the giant vidscreen, in slight body contact with Ira, who was sitting much more properly next to her. Highfive was standing with a sports drink in his hand behind the couch, and Inkblot was sitting in a wooden chair in the back, nearer to the corner. Jack was on another couch that made a ‘square’ around the vidscreen. He could draw a diagonal line to Bo.

Augur was pacing in front of the vidscreen, lost in thought, muttering to himself. There was a holographic box on the central coffee table.

“Alright,” Jack said suddenly, shrugging with his hands. “What are we doing first, then?”

Augur paused and blinked, then waved his hand. “Get any further intel out of the way, please.”

“Sure,” Bo said, shifting to put her feet up on the coffee table with a clang — this was so close to the box that it made Augur twitch and almost lunge for it. Bo showed precious few signs of noticing this, but Jack was certain that it was deliberate, gremlin that she was. “So, first off, I met with Abundance and made him promise not to tattle on us, preferably sticking to Ten’s side for the remainder of time. He agreed to, well, basically everything I said. Yeah. Such a good boy.”

“Did you give him pets?” Highfive asked sarcastically. “A scratch behind the ears, at least?”

“I couldn’t find the other guy at all,” Bo said, ignoring Highfive. “By the way, he has the most idiotic name ever. Must’ve been brain-dead when he came up with it. Anyway, yeah, he’s very reclusive. Only rarely gets dragged out of his cave. Ten assured me she passed on the word to him, to curt agreement. Presumably, he’ll avoid them by his innate nature.”

“Wow,” Jack said, “really representing his totem, isn’t he?”

“What’s the name, Bo?” Highfive asked. “You can’t just tease that and not deliver.”

“I’m also rather curious,” Ira added.

Bo sighed as if it caused suffering. “Don Quillote.”

Jack burst out in laughter. “That is the dumbest pun! And he made it his name. Alright, I like this guy.”

Highfive made a face and laughed a bit, but it was obvious he didn’t get it. Thankfully, he had learned to search when necessary. He squinted and soon laughed again, louder. “The famous historical novel! Don Quixote, Don Quillote, ha!”

Ira was blinking and nodding slowly at the information. “I see.”

“Maybe more clever than it seems, at first glance,” Inkblot said. “In the novel, Don renames himself something ridiculous before setting out as a ‘chivalrous knight.’ Perhaps a commentary on what he thinks about our practice as Nons? Not that it matters.”

Bo was frowning over at him thoughtfully. She shrugged, turned back around, and said, “Moving on. It’s believed that Double Dee are both level six, and the others are level five. So they have a couple of levels on us. Not much information on what Délight got for her last level. Something nasty, I’m sure.”

A sober pause ensued.

“I heard mostly just rumors,” Highfive offered. “Délight got better at firing people up, for good or bad. Heard that twice, so maybe there’s something to it. Defiant also seems less invincible the further back in his history shit comes from. Like, he seemed weak as hell when he started. Definitely got smacked around.”

“Makes sense for a Conceptual powerset,” Bo said.

“Yeah. He toughened up over time. More recently, rumors that he just, quote, ‘gave up’ in fights of three or four on one, maybe because he knew he couldn’t offense through them, but wanted to preserve his mystique or even his secrets. Well, it happened for real at least once, because he got booted from a team for that shit. Partially, at least. Seems he’s also known as having an abrasive personality, I guess? Not a people person. They say he’s super blunt.”

Inkblot cleared his throat. “I acquired some intel on Defiant through some back channels. Unsurprisingly, he’s lost more directly at lower levels, in duels and unofficial team matches. To what? Overwhelming, apparently archetypal AoE, like explosions and energy bombs, in multiple fights. He was strong against single-target and focused attacks. Then, suddenly, even the AoE seemed to become less of a weakness, possibly at the level three mutation gain.

“He stopped doing one-on-one by any public measure, though. Team shit only. He was, at best, average speed, and accounted similar to a typical strong body metamorph, just as reported. Later levels, he just got tougher… and then hit his speed spurt recently. The speed has something to do with protecting others. Very, very bursty.”

“Damn,” Highfive exclaimed. “Good stuff, Ink. AoE, huh? Not good. We don’t have a focus on that.”

“It’s damned weird to begin with,” Bo said. “That’s literally something tanks, especially Guardians, are strong against, sometimes all but immune to.”

“Well, I got some AoE recently,” Jack said, “but it doesn’t seem likely to be our silver bullet against a tank, considering it's an irritant at best against them. Does anyone know if this guy goes in all cavalier? Maybe no helmet or something equally convenient?”

“No dice,” Inkblot replied. “He actually wears reinforced armor. A special allowance, too. Not everyone can get that shit cleared. Hardcore ratios between different body parts have to be maintained, unless you’re doing it with powers.”

“Why the hell would a Conceptual guy need armor?”

Inkblot shrugged helplessly. “AoE, presumably.”

“Right. But that isn’t really the totality of his weakness. Anyone else get the feeling we’re still missing something in this equation?”

“Yep,” Highfive replied.

“Ditto,” Bo said.

“We are!” Augur declared, holding up a pointed finger. That hand had a small controller in it. “But I think it’s time I conduct my demonstration!”
