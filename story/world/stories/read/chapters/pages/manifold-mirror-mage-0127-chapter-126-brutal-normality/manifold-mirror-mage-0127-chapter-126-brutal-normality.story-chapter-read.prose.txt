“You should say ‘Thank you’ now.”

Saint Baphelus’s urgings broke Gio out of his temporary stupor.

“Oh, Right! Thank you!” Gio clumsily said.

“And thank you for being a friend to my granddaughter.” The sage returned. “I hope that the skill will serve you well in your journey.”

Gio nodded.

“I suppose that we should be off.” The old woman said, “Baphelus, Eudoria, your hospitality is as strange and lovely as ever. We will be in touch.”

“Also, we should prepare ourselves.” The Queen announced. “As far as everyone present is concerned, this was a terrible meeting where no ground was ceded on either side, yes? Sister, should we wait to spread rumours, or would it make more sense to have them begin immediately?”

“I imagine that we might be able to let word of mouth spread naturally.” Madame Mata answered. “Chandrika’s absence from our court will likely set fire to the imaginations of idle busybodies, your highness.”

Gio noted the deference that Chandrika’s Aunt showed her sister, despite the overt familiarity that they seemed to share.

Royalty is weird. Chandrika’s country seems to show a lot of respect for the Sages, and especially the Queen. I’ll have to ask her about the nuances of that later. What would it be like if one of my younger siblings became the monarch of the Ringed City? Emma would probably be a fun and benevolent Queen… Sam would declare war for no reason and make naptime illegal.

Gio temporarily paused his daydreams about his twin siblings as Chandrika turned to hug her Grandmother.

“I’ll make sure to use the dreamcatcher when I can,” Chandrika promised. “With the end of the semester coming up, I’ll probably have more time to sleep than I’ve had recently.”

Her grandmother warmly returned the hug before looking at her with an amused expression.

“I am glad to hear that, my precious spider, but why are you saying your farewells already?”

“Because… you’re leaving? And I won’t see you for the… foreseeable future?” Chandrika guessed, with a confused tilt.

“My dear, that’s only going to be a concern if you manage to escape.” Her grandmother wryly smiled.

“But- I thought-”

“You have our blessing for the attempt, naturally, but did you think we were just going to let you go?” Madame Mata grinned. Several smiles crept across the faces of the Queen’s attendants, and the spear-holding automata seemed to thrum with excitement.

Chandrika’s eyes widened. “But you already know that I’m going to try it, so I’ve lost the element of surprise…”

Chandrika’s grandmother swiftly zipped herself into the form of a small doll, resting herself on Chandrika’s shoulder.

“To tip the scale, I’ll be coaching you on your egress. I’ve been meaning to show you some fun applications of the [Jungle Spider’s Climbing Orison] spell that you’ve been sorely neglecting.” The doll said. She summoned Chandrika’s spellbook to her tiny hands, flipping through the woven pages with a leisurely pace.

Madame Mata and the Queen’s guards lost a great deal of their confidence as they watched the Grand Weaver settle onto Chandrika’s shoulder.

“You can’t be serious. Headmaster?” Chandrika asked, turning to her mentor.

“It would be highly improper of me to intrude into your family affairs… but you are my pupil…” The headmaster began.

Chandrika turned to Eudoria, hopeful.

“... Which is why I shall watch and take notes so that we may review your performance after the fact.”

Chandrika buried her head in her hands. “I am going to make you all pay for this,” she swore.

As the eventful meeting tapered off, Gio wished Chandrika luck with her ‘escape’, leaving just him, his mentor, and Wink, who was napping in Saint Baphelus’s lap. The lich swirled a drop of honey into his cup with an ornate spoon.

“You’ve had quite the eventful few days.” The lich spoke.

Gio was tracing the outlines of the skill book in front of him.

“An eventful few months, really.” Gio reflected. “I want to say that I knew that this school was going to be an adventure, but so much has happened in so little time. I was hoping to have a bit of time to relax after mid-terms, but there’s just so much to do already, and now this mess with the Wicker Cradle-”

“Don’t worry about that.” Baphelus interrupted. “That’s not something that you need to burden yourself with.”

“What?” Gio retorted. “How could I not? If this thing- this sapling, or whatever, is coming to the city because of me, then I need to help stop it.”

Saint Baphelus looked deep into Gio’s eyes.

“My boy,” The lich said. “Though I won’t lie to you, and I cannot coddle you, I think perhaps you lack a scale of reference for precisely what a leak from a great dungeon entails. As you may know, several of the so-called ‘megadungeons’, Vitrium included, have been known to produce threats to the outside world.”

The lich tapped his foot on the stone below.

“The Ringed City is, and always has been, a powerful factory that churns out an endless number of machines. The Iron, Silver, and Gold Rings were once independent forges that would each churn out an impossibly large number of great metal soldiers that would patrol the countryside for miles, each machine capable of using malefic magic to scour away any intelligent life it happened across. Much of the city remains untamed even to this day, and the deepest forges below us still produce armies, eternally fought back by my allies, even as we speak.”

“I’ve heard parts of this before. You and the Saints fought to stop the machines and make the Ringed City habitable,” Gio noted, recalling his history lessons.

“Yes,” Saint Baphelus said. “The small fry are dangerous. As you unintentionally saw today, even the weakest of these creations is enough to pose a serious threat to even hardened combatants. The Gold and Silver ones used to be a real pain to deal with… but these aren’t the true threat that the dungeon poses, nor the real reason that we sought to subjugate it.”

Images flashed on the crystal walls of Baphelus’s chamber. A titanic centipede skittered over the obsidian walls, with waves of dark magic pouring out from its chitinous hide. In another, a flying mechanical colossus with two faces and several arms emitted beams of light that scarred the land. A dark shadow with too many heads and limbs erupted out from the top of the tallest spire of the Crystal Ring, nearly blotting out the sun with a forest of dark wings.

Gio’s mind itched, suddenly remembering.

“That one with the wings… that’s the thing from your vault,” Gio whispered.

“I suppose the term ‘Boss’ would be the correct terminology, accounting for ubiquity and breadth of use… but I prefer the older, stuffier descriptives: Elite Manifestations, Apex Creatures, and Spiritual focal points. Boss monsters. The construct that you faced in [Telchine’s Garden] would struggle to scrape the dead skin off a proper boss from the Crystal Ring’s depths.” The lich explained.

“And this Sapling from the wicker ring… is one of those bosses.” Gio guessed.

The lich leaned in with a smile.

“No.”

Gio staggered in his seat.

“T-then, what is it?”

“An offshoot of the Wicker Cradle itself. They are walking dungeons, and can contain anything, even several bosses on par with the corpse that I keep sealed. It’s happened before, though not for many aeons. Each time, they bring true disaster, and if my wife’s agents confirm that a sapling from the Wicker Cradle is indeed on the way to the Ringed City, it will provoke a multi-national threat response and intervention from the full might of the Saints.”

Gio deflated in his seat slightly. “I… suppose there’s not much help that I could offer, then. Even the construct that we faced in the dungeon was nearly deadly for us…”

“Giorgio, you’re a first-year student. You’re what, sixteen?”

Gio gasped in outrage. “I’m turning twenty in the fall.”

“Please excuse me for underestimating your venerable age by a whopping three to four years.” Baphelus quipped. “I shall have to congratulate you when the time is right for your graduation from the rank of teenager. Personally, I stopped counting how old I was when they invented a new calendar. And they have the gall to keep doing it every couple of years, and then they expect us to use it on all the documentation… I digress.”

Gio blinked. “Do you not use the-”

“Of course, I use the common era calendar. I just hate that they keep updating it and then calling it “common”. Do you have any idea how many ‘modern’ eras I’ve lived through? It’s maddening. It’s hard enough to keep track of history when everybody agrees on what the numbers should be called, never mind when everybody wants their pet empire’s founding to be the basis for a new benchmark of chronology.”

The lich took a long breath.

“Sidetracked again. If I could only keep my wife at my side for every moment of the day, I’d do that less. So you’re nineteen years old. I am approximately eleven thousand, nine hundred and forty-seven years older than you… I’ll let you do that math. You are a child. I have been responsible for the well-being of this school for thousands of years, and the weight of protecting it and this city falls to me, and those whom I count myself lucky to call my allies. One day, hopefully many years from now, I may invite you to join my ranks as an accomplished [Archmage] and ask you to join me in service to this burden, as either a teacher or a warrior… but for now, I want you to be a student.”

“After all that… All of the talk of mysterious strangers and dungeons, and you want me to just go to class?” Gio guffawed.

“Go to class… make friends… Hone your craft in whatever arts you may pursue.” Baphelus replied.

“How do you possibly expect me to focus on schoolwork-”

It struck Gio like lightning. Rio’s words came back to Gio from earlier this morning.

Saint Baphelus has not been a mortal… or a teenager… for a very, very long time.

“Look,” Gio said. “With all that you have seen, perhaps it might be hard for you to understand the immense strain that all of this has had on me. You and the other saints might be able to go back to ‘business-as-usual’ after having your whole world upended after millennia of practice… but this is my first time. I don’t want to be left out of your plans just because you feel like I am not ready for it.”

Saint Baphelus smiled at Gio.

“I don’t want to patronise you, Giorgio.”

“Then don’t!” Gio replied.

“As you are, my dear boy… You are useless to me.”

Gio had expected to be rebuffed, but that had hurt.

“I can see the strands that you can’t,” Gio argued, but the wind had already left his sails.

“And then what?” Baphelus asked, in an echo of their earlier conversation. “Will you follow those strands to their ultimate destiny, and use the powers of chaos that you can hardly control to bring a ruinous end to all those that stand before you? Will you use your catalogue of unmastered, slapdash spells to bring down the barriers before you? What exactly do you think you can even do?”

Gio scoffed.

“I’ve done plenty with my slapdash spells.”

“Yes, you have,” Baphelus said, sipping his tea. “More than almost any of my students at your age, and certainly more than any who have come from similar backgrounds.”

Gio’s shoulders went slack in his chair. Even he could sense that he was being foolish.

“I… I just…”

“Whatever the future may hold for us, I will not be facing it alone, and nor shall you. For the time being, I would like you to stay within my domain to avoid any repeat visits from our new ‘friend’, and yes, I would like you to try to return to a sense of ‘normalcy’ at school. Please trust that the issues that we have learned about today will be investigated by a full complement of competent individuals. I am already planning on getting my brothers and sisters involved… so you need not worry about anything.” Saint Baphelus gently stated.

Gio clenched and unclenched his hands in front of him.

“I don’t know how. I can’t just… forget about this. I have enough theories running through my head to form a whole plotted chart of conspiracies.” Gio muttered.

“I’m not asking you to forget. You’re still under my mentorship, and there will undoubtedly be times that we will ask a great deal of you in the future… but I do not need you to combat a megadungeon, or face an unknown rogue mage at this time. There will be plenty of situations that will distract you from your studies in the near future, so I can assure you that you will not be bored… visiting prophets and draconic diplomats included.”

Gio paled.

“I- uh, wow, I almost forgot about Lot, with everything going on. I have no idea when to expect him… or when the headmaster will want me to help her with the dragons.”

“Lamentable Lot is currently journeying through the old highways beneath the ocean. He won’t be with us for quite some time, but I shan’t try to predict him of all people. He’s likely a bit similar to you, in that manner. The gathering of dragons, however, shall take place at the end of the summer, just after your second year with us begins.” The lich answered.

“Oh… that’s good to know. So I’ll have a few months before then.” Gio hummed.

The lich thoughtfully drummed the table with his long fingers.

“I think that I might make you an appointment before the end of semester festivities, if for no other reason than to distract you.” The lich pondered.

“An… appointment, Saint Baphelus?”

“Yes. We are lucky enough to have a great support team here at the school. Among that number, we have a councillor that I think you might benefit from.”

“Professor Anaximenes is already making me see a therapist.” Gio shrugged.

“Not that kind of councillor, but good on him. No, she is a build councilor. A [System Theorist], to be specific.”

Gio’s eyes widened in recognition.

“Like… someone who studies the system? But… I’m a true mage. What good would that do for

me?” Gio asked.

“System theorists study the effects of system-granted skills and spells on the mortal soul, yes… but they also study things like magical warfare tactics, and the effects of training and stimulus on system dialogues. This one specialises in helping mages become more… rounded-out for combat or emergencies.” Baphelus noted.

Gio immediately wondered what she would have to say about him. He hoped that she would appreciate his defensive and evasive abilities… but he didn’t know for sure how he would stack up against a professional’s opinions.

“Consider me intrigued,” Gio said.

“Good.” Baphelus nodded. “I’ll make you an appointment for this week, that way you’ll have some time to work on her advice before the games.”

Gio squinted.

“Games.” Gio suspiciously said. “What kind of games?”

It was Saint Baphelus’s turn to tilt his head to the side. “The end-of-semester games?”

Gio blinked.

The lich exhaled a long-suffering sigh.

“I am glad that we are correcting your course.” The lich affirmed. “Because between the dogged pursuit of your domain and these strange happenings, you have been living under a rock. I will not have my mentee look a fool during capture-the-tome.”
