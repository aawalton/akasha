Lenna, in front of the portal…

I sneezed and shook my head, feeling the energy all around us untwist. The whispering voices spoke of danger and pain. This journey wasn’t going to be easy, but the voices left it up to me whether or not to continue. Taking this step would change my path, something that rarely happened. Something else whispered, though, almost unheard in the background.

I couldn’t make it out.

“That was unsettling,” I mumbled, taking a moment to ground myself. The very air here made me want to do something, anything. At first I’d liked it, but now I was worried about that push.

Danger. For me.

Alex glanced at the sky like I did, to the stars. “Just a little unusual,” she said.

Something happened to her, then. Her eyes froze just for a second, like she wasn’t there any longer, then she flashed back.

“Did you also get a warning?” I asked, still hesitating. That barley-there whisper kept at it, warning me.

“Yeah,” said Alex, stepping closer to the portal. She stared at it like it might bite. “But we already knew it’d be dangerous, with the Clan being there. What about your warning?”

Despite the direct question, Alex didn’t turn to look at me. Instead, she kept herself between the portal and me. Like they would protect me from some threat neither of us really understood. I doubted she even noticed it.

“The normal warnings, about how this choice might change everything,” I said with a soft chuckle, trying to not think about that other murmur.

Alex glanced at me with a nod. The power behind her eyes didn’t scare me. “Yeah, I think this is a big one for some reason. We can do it, though.”

“We can,” I said, as I stepped up next to her. “Let’s do this.”

Alex, on a new world…

Fresh air smacked me in the face as soon as we stepped through the portal.

My radar went a little wonky, but recovered as my feet touched the stone courtyard. A small clearing circled us, with a stone wall only knee high.

Lenna stepped into place beside me, studying everything around us as well. For a second she trembled, then steadied.

“You okay?” I whispered.

She nodded. “Mana pressure is high, but doable.”

I felt nothing. Or at least, nothing out of the ordinary.

The connection between Strange and I stretched out far in front of me. Much farther than I had anticipated.

“I think we arrived from a different portal than the one they traveled through,” I said, keeping my voice low. Something about the walls surrounding us drew my attention, and I changed to my second sight.

Mana formed a dome all around the portal, touching the tops of the walls and expanding above our heads. The dome fully enclosed the area.

Including the opening.

I stepped closer, studying the interaction, and comparing it to my shields. It was the same sort of thing. Critically, I wanted to know which way it faced.

I bent down, picked up a pebble and tossed it above the wall. It hit the shield and dropped back, hitting the ground on this side.

“Interesting…” I mumbled, as I stepped closer. I wasn’t worried about it keeping us contained, I was pretty sure I could disable it. And as far as I could tell, nothing appeared like it gave off a signal or anything.

I approached the opening, but Lenna's hand snagged my wrist. I froze.

She pointed at a mark near the ground.

I crouched down to look at it.

Something small sat on the edge of the barrier, like it’d fall in as soon as the barrier fell.

“Smart…” I said. Very smart. It was something most wouldn’t see.

Snagging another rock, I placed it on the other side of the razor thin barrier, to stop the perfectly round piece of metal from moving.

Lenna nodded.

I touched the inside of the barrier. It didn’t move. It was solid, but it wavered a little under pressure. Switching to my spear, I poked the shield and it shattered. My second sight showed the mana, not falling away, but regathering near the top of the wall, the shield slowly beginning to rebuild itself.

Both Lenna and I darted out of the circle. Within seconds, the barrier had reformed.

The round metal thing remained in place.

I blinked my second sight away. “Did that cause any visible signal?”

“No, nothing happened,” she said, though her eyes were already scanning our surroundings.

A short scrub surrounded us, along with rocks, and the distance appeared to feature mountains with massive valleys between them. The wind ripped along the ridge we stood on, and blew at my hair and clothing. The sun dipped down beneath the mountain range to our left, casting shadows across everything.

Far below us, the valleys teamed with life. My radar flickered with movement in the distance. A thin line crossed each of the mountains, and above that rested snow. Below that, everything remained green and lush.

Lenna shivered. “This is cold,” she said.

I snagged my ratty cloak off and tossed it at her.

She pulled it close and tied it around her neck. “Thanks. I should have grabbed something warmer.”

“Down we go, for the moment, at least,” I said with a tight grin. The connection with Strange led deeper through the mountains, but we had a good distance to travel. First, we needed to warm up.

This area hadn't been explored by Strange, lending strength to the idea that they came back to this place through a different portal.

A trail extended down into the valley, and I took the lead. As soon as we stepped into the shadows, I vanished. The cloak helped Lenna blend into the darkness on the trail, and we carefully picked our way down.

As soon as we crossed that line on the rocks, the temperature changed.

The air thickened, and heated. It was almost like we walked through soup. My skin turned sticky, which I disliked at first, but then ignored. Something made my skin feel itchy, though. After a few moments, it came to me. The mana wasn’t consistent, somehow.

It was like sometimes we’d walk through an empty patch of space, with no mana at all, then a normal one. It was weird.

Lenna tossed the cloak back at me, and I tucked it away.

The thick air made it feel like rain should be coming, but there weren’t any clouds nearby.

Buzzing echoed across the mountains, and I froze, sticking as close to the rocks as I could. The shadows hid me, and I focused on my surroundings as Lenna found a spot to hide.

In the distance, three creatures chased a fourth. The three were humanoid, but with wings coming out of their shoulder blades. The fourth wasn’t humanoid at all. It was some sort of massive bug, flying as fast as it could, directly up into the air.

The three were close to it, almost reaching it, then it crossed that line across the mountains.

All three of the humanoids backed off, then dove back down. They circled under bug-thing for several minutes, then flew away heading to the canopy of trees in the valley.

The bug creature flew to the west, keeping above that line for the most part, only dipping down every now and then until it vanished out of sight.

My eyes locked on a massive mountain in the distance. Once I saw it, I couldn’t look away.

Something deep inside pushed me toward it.

I closed my eyes and took a deep breath and thought my response at the feeling inside. I will find them. We must be smart. No one must follow.

The moment calmed down the frantic memories left behind by Apochas. It was almost like I carried an echo of the Great Devourer’s soul, even though I knew it was just their memories.

Lenna approached the area I hid in slowly, and I stepped back onto the trail, flashing her a smile. We continued on, going slow. The trail thinned out even more, becoming overgrown, before it vanished completely.

It ended at a flat boulder, still far from the valley floor.

Lenna paused on the rock. “They must land near here.”

I nodded and stared at the sky, before taking a deep breath. We had a few options.

“Can you jump us down?” asked Lenna, before I could reply.

I opened my mouth, then snapped it shut with a smile. My form shifted, and I gained my pitch black armor. Wings unfurled from my shoulders, changing my center of gravity.

“Got something better,” I said.

She just stared at my back, then nodded. Her bow vanished.

I leaped up and did a few circles in the air around her, before coming in and scooping her off her feet. She was heavier than I thought she’d be, but it was manageable. For a little while, at least.

My shoulders ached in only a few minutes, but I carefully flew us closer to the ground.

As we got close, Lenna nodded, and I let her go several feet from the ground. She landed gracefully, her bow appearing in her arms.

I landed next to her, letting the wings vanish. My stomach growled a little, and I tossed a bit of meat into my mouth. Flying took way more energy then I liked. Hopefully, that would get better over time.

My stomach growled again.

This time I paused and ate a couple of bites, waiting for the urge to eat to go away.

It didn’t immediately.

For some reason, it took longer than before. I stared at the hunk of dino meat, trying to puzzle out what’d happened. Last time I’d used my wings, gorging on meat had worked. This time, I’d barely flown at all, and the meat wasn’t working.

Was it the place, the meat, or the wings?

Or worse, was it the gaps in the mana?

I searched for a weird spot empty of mana and paused inside it. After a few seconds, my stomach growled worse, confirming my suspicions.

After stepping away, I ate a good amount of meat as Lenna patrolled the edges of the trees. Nothing big popped up on my radar.

Lenna appeared near me with a frown. She leaned close to me. “Is there anything here?”

I shrugged. So far, the only wildlife had been the bug, and the beings chasing it.

Nothing else.

We continued into the dark trees and searched for any other signs of life, as I followed the connection with Strange.

Strange, with his not friends…

I pounced up on a rock, and stared out over the mountain ridge. The cold wind made me shiver, as did the stillness.

Wrongness was in the taste of the air.

No food. No creatures anywhere, since we’d crossed the portal.

“You might need to go back in the pack,” said Vyrm, crouching down next to me.

The giant bug always talked to me. Though, he had a point. This mountain crossing was cold. Cold even to them, and they were from here.

I wasn’t sure exactly where we were going, besides to report in. Report what, I didn’t know. They hadn’t found Alpha, or even seen her.

Only me.

“We have more cold to get through before we can fly,” he said, motioning to the bag that Zephyr usually carried. It rested on the ground as we took a break.

Each of the bugs moved slowly. So slow in the cold. The wings were gone in the cold.

My map slowly filled in as we traveled across the big spaces. So much information. All waiting for Alpha.

I nodded at the pack, then jumped inside. He tossed a tasty piece of food in after me. I tore tiny pieces of the meat apart. Tasty.

Food is good.

Someone chuckled.

“We shouldn’t be making friends with him,” said Zephyr.

“Not like any of us can destroy that shield,” added Vyrm. “He has information on the devourer.”

I had a quest that protected me, but they’d figured that out days ago. Special skill from my new profession. Protection, to deliver even the most dangerous messages. I liked it.

My shield remained until I delivered them to Alpha.

The one that hid, that one had caused the issue. Alpha needed to see them.

Then the quest would be complete. The shield would drop, but Alpha would be here, so I would still be safe.

Alpha was coming. Soon.
