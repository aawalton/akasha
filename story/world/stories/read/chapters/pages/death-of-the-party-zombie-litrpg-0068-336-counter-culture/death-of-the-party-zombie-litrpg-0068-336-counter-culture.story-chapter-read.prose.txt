[I’m sorry.][I held them back for as long as I could.][We must now fight with everything we have.]

Sally ground her teeth together. She needed to tell Chuck to lock in, but it was difficult to read the System while her eyes were bleeding. Another rib cracked as the long bone-spear lifted her higher in the air. The tip of the natural weapon the odd monster had skewered her clean through the torso, an unexpected attack from the shadows.

“Healing negation, I should've expected that. Physical resists too.” She glanced down at the zombies trying to scratch through the plated creature. “Almost a total counter, and so soon?”

The tip of the weapon scraped the metal wall as it rose further, rupturing her lung. As Sally let out a pained gasp, she stared down the monster. Although she had killed several strange beasts, this centaur-crab hybrid had appeared out of nowhere. Normally she could sense that sort of thing. It seemed unable to communicate or reason. There was nothing really to say to it, even if she could. Her life energy seeped away.

Her limbs fell slack as the light left her eyes. With a flick of the long weapon, the monster tossed her to the floor before turning on the many zombies clambering around it.

For several seconds, Sally's body lay there, inert. Then Beyond the Grave kicked in, setting off a chain of prepared abilities.

Rites and Wrongs came next, imbuing all the aimless zombies still standing with magic damage to their attacks, as well as some damage reflection. Grave Consequences came next, triggering a blast of necromantic energy around any fallen zombie corpse.

The monster stumbled back as claws and teeth burned through its plated legs. Dark magic caused the exposed flesh to burn and decay. Even as the creature skewered two undead, it moaned in pain, barely keeping its footing.

Finally, Arise and Shine cast. Sally contorted as if she were a puppet as the spell brought her back up to her feet. Red light bloomed once again in her eyes. Regaining the use of her limbs, Meat Hook took her up to the flailing monster, and Eat Brains emptied its skull.

She rolled from the body, bouncing into a soft cushion of zombies, breaking her fall. Threads worked to reset her bones and piece her torso back together.

“What a pain”, she said, after swallowing down the gray matter. “My self-revive is quite a doozy to set up.” The battle had only just begun, and she'd used up one of her hidden aces.

Sally swept her messy blonde hair back and took stock of her situation. The hangar was all but cleared of monsters and bugs alike, but the doors were still sealed. She was now barely second on the leaderboard, but if the rest of the fleet had arrived, it was about to be a bloodbath down on the ground.

Bringing the dwarves over here might be a good idea, as they'd be able to get her out of this room and the other ship could fall, but perhaps giving up an asset was a poor choice at this stage.

Before she could decide, the entire ship shook. She stumbled back into her zombies before weightlessness took her. She bounced off one cage before catching her head on the corner of another.

“Fuck!” She clasped at the wound as stars flickered in her vision. A heavy pressure and sudden gravity almost made her pass out, and then there was warmth on her arm before the rush of cool air.

Sally pulled out a potion, but it slipped from her grasp. White thread repaired the gash on her head and the deep wound running down her arm. It must have severed some nerves. Injured again as the force had sucked her out of the hole in the hull.

She glanced up at the ship as she fell. The sky had darkened, even though it was clear of clouds. Partial eclipse? Part of her brain was screaming for an answer to her current predicament. To not dash against the earth below and have to revive in hell.

But maybe it was the undead side of her that wasn't bothered. She had long wrestled with her two ways of thinking, often resorting to mania to smother out the panic or sense of reason from her human brain. It hadn't been an issue lately, but something about these stakes was messing with her.

Oh well, she thought, as gravity whipped her through the air. This sort of thing always had a way of working itself out.

She closed her eyes and waited for the inevitable collision against the hard ground. But it never happened. Instead, she found herself in the grasp of something much softer, with a familiar smell. Her eyes opened again to see the vampire holding her.

“What are you doing here, goofball?”

He grinned and set her down on solid earth, where she wobbled slightly. “I sensed your demise and came to enact hot vengeance.”

Sally held her head as it continued to swim. “You’re right, that is hot.”

“I took care of my assignment, before you ask.” He turned his attention to the skies, where one large vessel fired off explosive rounds at another. “This is fast becoming a land war, and will only get worse with the recent appearance of their main detachment.”

“As soon as the world stops spinning, we’ll clear up here.” The zombie frowned, scowling at the ground as it twirled in her vision.

Theo put his hand on her shoulder. “Something left a persistent Daze effect on you. Return to the moon to cleanse it, and I’ll tidy up here.”

“Ugh, fine. I’ll see if someone has a better use of me. I’m not meant to fly.” She pulled him in for a quick peck on the cheek before vanishing.

A massive beam of light pulsed down from the Radoch ship as hundreds of Radochs teleported down to the ground. Although a sizable portion of the previous ship had been shelled to pieces by the commandeered vessel, there were still enough survivors to rally up with the fresh force.

Theo lifted a potion bottle full of black liquid to his lips. Several arrows and magical projectiles whizzed past him, or otherwise fell short of his position.

He dropped the empty bottle and rested his sledgehammer on his shoulder. Dark energy whipped around him, ruffling the edges of his suit. With one hand in his pocket, he gave the approaching army a dismissive sneer.

“Now, which of you will take responsibility for killing my wife?”

Sally rubbed her head. “Much better,” she mumbled to herself. As expected, she was the only one in the meeting room of their moon base. She waved her hand to bring up a holographic display of Sanctuary in the middle of the table.

Things weren’t as dire as she expected… yet.

[Outsiders: 5/5][Guardians: 5/5][Awakened: 3/3][Adventurers: 83%]

“Huh, there are two other Awakened-level people here. I wonder who they are.” After giving herself a brief shrug, she brought up her System windows to request deployment coordinates.

Instead, she was met with a full feed of updates from across the world.

Dent and Humphrey were holding their own against what was currently the biggest Radoch ground force. They seemed to be fighting through a rough blizzard, but had packed their mittens.

After failing to puncture the giant collection ship, Ren had run out of Aspect energy and left the form. Max had now joined her so that the pair could get through as many roaches in their area as possible.

Archie was now assisting the struggling defenders in the Forest area after having brought down an entire fleet single-handidly. While Wolf was holding his own, many of the Adventurers in that end of the world were low level - and it was probably the heavy assistance from the Sanctuary’s Bastion guild that had prevented them from wiping out fully.

Jackie and the team of wagons were making their way through one end of the Jungle to the other, pelting either ground troops or the scouting vessels with ranged weapons as they did. Out of all the areas, this one had been the most hostile to the invaders, and many bugs were falling to the monsters and Systemborn who naturally lived in the Jungle.

The other Guardians and high-level Adventurers were spread pretty thin, but were doing what they could. These casualty numbers were… before most of the Radochs—including their collected creatures and technology—had disembarked from their ships. Even with air superiority, they hadn’t been firing on the ground troops as much as they could.

Maybe they had better plans. Something to bring a world of immortals to their knees.

Hell knew what they might do once they found out Sanctuary had given up the golden goose already.

She furrowed her brow and glanced at the globe again. There were so many fronts to fight on, she didn’t know where to go. If she went one way, she could easily turn the tide, all for a different battle to fail.

A blue outline flickered into the space next to her, as a projection of Chuck struggled to form.

“Forgive the intrusion,” he began, his voice distant, “but to save you from decision paralysis, one of my sleeved cards is currently overrun with Radochs.”

“Say no more.” Sally brought out a teleportation scroll. “Off I go killing again.”

Edward paced around the balcony overlooking the proceedings. While he wasn’t a fan of putting himself in unnecessary danger… being stationed down in Hell unable to assist was really grinding his gears.

He was a Guardian, after all. One of the powerhouses of the world. Why not be on the surface, killing bugs and betraying allies?

With a sigh, he leaned on the edge and looked down at the wide courtyard below. A flash of light to the east revealed another five Adventurers who had died. They appeared confused at first, but once their resolve returned they walked over to join the other deceased milling around the dark brown stone flooring.

As Hell was the staging area for preparing everyone for their second—and final—lives, it made some degree of sense to have him down here to oversee things. It was supposed to be somewhere the Radochs couldn’t reach, but if they did, they’d have to face him and every Adventurer they’d killed so far.

Then again, there was also his ability, which made him one of the most important people on the planet. His eyes drifted back to his office and the large metal object within. Another thorn that he should be thankful for.

He sighed again and made for the stairs. It would be useful to arrange some sparring or combat training. Maybe it could make the difference.

Edward paused on the top stair and frowned. He turned his head up to the blank ceiling, glaring at it as if it might turn transparent for him and reveal the world above. It did not, but the hairs on the back of his head stood up.

Something wasn’t right.
