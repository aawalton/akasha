Mom…" Callie looked at Mom, her hands trembling.

Mom wore a hardened expression. It appeared she, too, had realized that only one person could do this. Two, in reality, but Callie was absolutely certain that her dear elder brother would never do something like this.

"I know, dear…" Mom whispered.

"Wh-What's going on?" Tristan asked, looking nervously between Callie and Mom.

"We need to get back to the village, children. Right now!" Mom said, grabbing Callie's and Tristan's hands.

Callie looked nervously around the forest, wondering whether Silas was watching them from somewhere. Had he seen what they had done here? Everything appeared to be silent, but realizing that the merchant had been at the heart of this event made Callie queasy.

Did that mean that he had also done all those other things? Or was it just this one incident?

As the three of them hurried home, Callie thought carefully about the unusual events that had happened in Greyfen lately. First, the trap that Snowglow had been caught in.

"Snowglow, are you still here?" Callie called. The glimmerhare had disappeared sometime during their cleaning of the drownhart's wounds.

I'm here!

The glimmerhare appeared not too far ahead of them, her horns shining a little. It was slowly getting darker.

I guide you home.

"Thank you," Callie said. "Snowglow said she'll guide us home."

Mom just nodded absentmindedly. Her gaze was darting around the forest, looking and searching for their enemy. It appeared she, too, was worried about being seen by him. Would Silas attack them if he knew that they knew the truth?

"Snowglow, do you know who placed that trap you got caught in?" Callie asked.

Not know. But likely the same person that trap the guardian.

"What did she say?" Mom asked.

"She thinks it's the same person who trapped the drownhart," Callie replied.

"Do you remember what kind of trap Snowglow was trapped in?"

Callie thought about it and described the trap. It was in essence the same kind of trap the drownhart had been caught in, except smaller. It had taken quite the effort on Callie's part to pry it open.

"It was likely the same person. Those traps sound quite similar, which is odd, knowing that they were a different size. Usually, traps meant for rabbits and traps for larger creatures like bears are different," Mom said.

Callie felt very cold. She thought about the other incidents. The glauxis egg, which had been stolen and buried underground. This trap had been buried and hidden under snow. And the incident with thunderfoxes, which had used identical snare wires to the ones that were wrapped around the drownhart.

Worst of all was that Silas was in a perfect position to execute all these crimes. He sold his merchandise in Greyfen, but traveled around a lot. He could have stolen the egg and brought it to Greyfen. Obviously, as an outsider, he wouldn't care much if something happened to the villagers.

When they were on their way to Drymon, he would be in a perfect position to set the trap for the thunderfox mother. Now, in winter, he had remained in Greyfen for some odd reason. Usually, merchants left during winter, because they would likely be stuck in the village until the snow melted.

But Silas had chosen to stay. Seemingly under the guise of helping the villagers, but in reality his goal was to hunt the drownhart. Worst of all, he had used Callen and Callie to get the mushrooms he needed to lure the drownhart into his trap.

Callie felt sick to her stomach. By bringing the mushrooms to this horrible person, Callie had inadvertently caused the drownhart to get caught in the trap.

"Whoa! What are you crying for?" Tristan suddenly called.

Only then did Callie notice hot tears streaming down her face.

"Callie, what's wrong?" Mom stopped, looking nervously over her. "Did you hurt something?"

"It's my fault that the drownhart got caught in the trap…" She sniffled out.

Tristan scrunched his nose at her. "Are you stupid?"

Mom looked at the boy incredulously. He let out a toothy grin.

"Sorry… What I meant to say is… Did you put up that trap?" Tristan said after clearing his throat.

"N-No?"

"Then how is it your fault?"

"But I found those mushrooms that lured the drownhart into the trap."

"I don't know anything about any mushrooms, but unless you're the one who laid the trap, I don't see how this is your fault."

Mom nodded. "Tristan is right. You never knew what they would be used for. Now, wipe those tears and let's hurry to the village. We need to speak to the chief."

Snowglow turned out to be invaluable help getting home. It got dark very quickly, and it was only thanks to her light that they managed to navigate through the forest.

Thank you, friend. For saving the guardian.

"Of course," Callie nodded and watched the glimmerhare hop away.

"Tristan, head home. If anyone asks, you were not with us today. You did see a drownhart, nor did you see any traps. Got it? This is for your own safety," Mom warned.

Tristan looked concerned as he didn't quite understand the seriousness of the situation. But he nodded anyway.

"I was just looking at snowmoths. That's all," Tristan said, and waved them goodbye.

Mom and Callie shared a look.

"Let's go talk to the chief!" Mom turned towards the village center.

But before they could go, they saw Callen waving at them with a big smile on his face.

"Mom, Callie, you won't believe what just happened?" He said, sounding quite excited.

Callie felt her heart clench. She knew that her brother had taken quite a liking to Silas. How will he react to this news? Not well, Callie suspected.

Callen couldn't believe his ears. No, he didn't want to believe it. Silas had been the one behind all of the happenings in Greyfen. The trap for the glimmerhare, the egg theft, and now the attempted capture of the drownhart.

Frankly, he couldn't even believe that the creature ruling over the Deep Forest was a drownhart, or that his little sister and Mom had gone to rescue it. But ever since Callie had gotten that strange mark from the dragon, all sorts of weird things have been happening.

It often felt like too much, but it was never more true than it was now. Silas had been the perpetrator everyone had been looking for. The very same man who had opened Callen's eyes to a large world. It had felt like a lie.

Silas had helped Callen so much. Told so many stories about the larger world, taught him about trading, and helped him earn his own money for the first time. Just yesterday, Silas had offered to take Callen with him during the spring. So he could see the large cities beyond Drymon. Experience the world.

It had felt like a dream. But now it came crashing down.

"Brother?" Callie looked up at him nervously.

Dad was sitting across from him, his lips pursed in a thin line as he tapped his finger on the table. "To think that it was him all along."

"I mean… I kind of thought so from the beginning," Callie said.

"What makes you say that? Did you see him do something?" Dad asked.

"No. It was nothing more than a gut feeling."

Gut feeling. Callen's gut feeling still couldn't believe that Silas had done something like that. Endangering the whole village. But then again… The glauxis's egg and the drownhart would have undoubtedly fetched Silas a high price from Dragonbloods. Maybe in the end, that was the very nature of a merchant. Gain profit without any care for the damage inflicted.

How could Callen walk this path now? It had felt like the right one. Like the one made for him! Would he have to become a hunter now?

No, that's even worse…

"Son, are you alright?" Dad asked.

"Y-Yeah… I just need a moment…" Callen said and stood to leave. He threw just a short glance back at Callie, who was peering at him nervously. She likely knew that it wasn't an easy truth for Callen to accept.

He looked away. He couldn't face Callie now. Especially when he had been the one to sell those glowing mushrooms to Silas. Callie undoubtedly blamed herself, but if there was somebody who was really at fault, it was him.

All because he wasn't yet adult enough to take care of himself. His inability to secure himself a coat for the winter had pushed his family to desperation, and ultimately, almost resulted in a tragedy. What would have happened if the drownhart was captured?

This thought alone made Callen sick to his stomach. So he fell into his bed and tried not to think about anything. But no matter how hard he tried, his thoughts never stopped. He kept thinking about Silas, about his own life, and his little sister. What would he do next?

At some point during his contemplation, Callen had fallen asleep. When he woke, it was early morning, as visible from the early rays of the sun peeking out beyond the treetops. It must have been quite late since the sun always rose late during winter.

Odd that nobody woke me up.

Callen got out of bed and headed for the kitchen. Mom and Dad were nowhere to be seen, and the embers had almost gone out. They must have left a while ago, so Callen added a piece of firewood to the hearth.

They were likely talking with the village chief. Mom had gone to explain the situation to him yesterday, but Chief Aemon likely needed both of their parents, no, likely more of the adults from the village, to solve this issue. How would they deal with Silas? Would he be captured? Sent to Drymon so the mayor could pass judgment on him?

That was something for the adults to decide. Callen decided that he would pick up the chores in his parents' stead.

"Callie, wake up!" He called and was about to head to the kitchen to prepare some breakfast.

But there was no reply.

"Callie!" He called again and went to knock on the door of her room.

There was still no reply. That was odd. Was she not feeling well after yesterday?

Callen carefully nudged the bedroom door open, only for his heart to stop at the sight in front of him. Callie's blanket and pillow were haphazardly thrown on the floor, while pages from her notebook had been scattered everywhere. The window was opened with cold winter air flowing in.

But the most shocking part was the clear signs of struggle. There were icicles sticking out in places where there shouldn't be icicles. Several were sticking upwards by the windowsill, and a few were growing out of the window.

"Callie!" Callen screamed and ran to the window, looking outside of it.

She was nowhere to be seen, but beneath the window, in the snow, Callen saw footprints. Those weren't Callie's footprints. Those were clearly the footprints of an adult man. Only one person came to Callen's mind.

"You bastard! I'm not going to let you hurt my sister!"

Callen rushed to put on his coat, as well as his pocket knife. But then he ran past the storage space. He stared at it for a long while, then finally reached in and grabbed Mom's sword.

Nobody would hurt Callie. Not while Callen could do anything about it.
