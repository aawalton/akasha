My stomach dropped. I stared up at Seth, my head reeling as I leaned further away, but he did not move. He just continued to look down at me, his expression unchanged. The cruel smirk still pulled at his lips, and it scared me to realise how comfortable it looked on his face.

“What?” I managed to get out.

There was a tremble to my voice, but that just made Seth’s lips twitch. He seemed terrifyingly amused by the fear that had edged into my heart, making my hands shake and my voice unsteady.

Finally, he moved away. He sat back on his heels, his eyes still fixed on my face, and I kept waiting for the coldness to be wiped from his eyes, for the gentleness I’d grown used to seeing to return. I wanted him to blink, to break and tell me he was joking, but he did not.

“That was easy,” he repeated, his words slow and deliberate. “I mean, I hoped it would be, but I thought it would take longer to convince you to trust me like that. God, you just completely fell for it, though. Didn’t you?”

My jaw felt slack as Seth laughed, the sound causing fear to crystallise in my chest. I could hear my heart thumping in my ears, feel my pulse surging within my body, but all I could do was stare at him.

Was it a game, I found myself wondering. It could have been some kind of mean trick. Perhaps Seth had been playing me for weeks. For months. Every time he’d been nice to me, that he’d seemed to actually care about me, may have been nothing more than an act so that I’d fall for him, but why? I couldn’t think of any reason why someone would do something like that to me.

There must be something else going on. Maybe it was a joke, I thought, but even I knew I was grasping at straws. I was just desperately trying to explain and excuse his behaviour because I couldn’t bring myself to actually believe what he’d said, that he’d really manipulated me. It didn’t feel right. Seth was a kind person. He was thoughtful, and he would never do something so cruel to anyone.

Or, at least, I didn’t think he would, I realised, my heart sinking. I hadn’t known Seth for that long. We’d only met at the Academy, and perhaps I didn’t truly know him. Maybe everything he’d done up until that point was just an act.

That would explain why he suddenly seemed so different. Something about him had shifted, and I couldn’t quite pinpoint what the difference was at first. When he spoke, his voice lacked the uncertainty it had held moments before, and his accent was different, but there was more than that. He looked different too. His posture was straighter, his shoulders rolled back, and he seemed more sure of himself. Even his gaze seemed more confident. He stared directly into my eyes without any sign of hesitation or anxiety.

Everything about him had changed ever so slightly. It seemed like he had transformed into a different person, but perhaps he’d just stopped pretending. He could have just given up on trying to hide who he was.

“What’s happening?” I asked, realising he was waiting for me to speak.

I couldn’t bring myself to respond to what he’d said. It had seemed like a rhetorical question, and even if he wanted me to answer it, I had nothing to say.

Seth laughed. The noise was a low, throaty chuckle, closer to a growl than an actual laugh, but the smile on his face seemed genuine. He seemed truly entertained by my question, but I didn’t understand. I didn’t get why he was enjoying it so much, but it terrified me.

That was his only answer, though. He didn’t even try to explain, and after a few seconds, I realised he didn’t intend to. He was content just to sit there, smiling at me, but I couldn’t do it. I needed to know.

“Seth?” I tried. “Are you okay?”

I don’t know why that was my question, but I was worried about him. It’s stupid, but I was genuinely concerned about how he was acting, and I needed him to say something. I needed some kind of explanation.

But my quiet, trembling question was enough to break through Seth’s silence. His expression contorted, and a snort escaped him. He threw his head back, laughter spilling from his lips.

My blood turned to ice, and the air was stolen from my lungs as loud, triumphant laughter rang out around me. It seemed to echo through the dusk, bouncing back towards me as if a thousand Seths were all mocking me, and I couldn’t help but notice I had never heard him sound so free.

The laugh bordered on maniacal, though. There was something terrifyingly unrestrained about it, and I scrambled backwards as quickly as I could. The warm roof tiles burned beneath my hands, and my eyes remained fixed on Seth, worried that he’d notice and throw himself at me, but he did not.

“Oh, Grace,” he said after a few seconds, still chortling slightly. He paused to wipe the tears from his eyes and shake his head, a grin still on his lips, before continuing. “You stupid, naive girl. That should really not be what you’re worried about right now.”

I could feel my heart hammering in my throat as I stared at him, continuing to back away.

“Oh, really? What should I be worried about?” I asked, my words spurred on by a sudden wave of confidence which had come out of nowhere.

But my question just made Seth’s lips twitch. He seemed to be struggling to hold back his laughter as he nodded, gesturing towards the edge of the roof with his chin.

“Why don’t you have a look?”

That time, I was the one who laughed. The brief, harsh sound hurt as it was torn from my throat.

“I’m not going anywhere near there,” I snapped.

Normally, I wouldn’t have hesitated. I’d stood at the edge of the roof with Seth before and peered over, but that was before. I didn’t trust the way he was acting, and I was terrified the moment I got close, he’d dive at me. I’d be falling towards the ground before I even had a chance to work out what was happening.

“I’ll stay right here,” Seth told me, holding his hands up in what I assume was meant to be a reassuring gesture.

It just scared me even more, though. For some reason, the fact that Seth understood my fear, that he’d anticipated why I didn’t want to do as he’d said, made me worry that he’d considered it. Perhaps he’d been fantasising about pushing me over the edge for weeks.

But I didn’t know what else to do. I didn’t trust him, didn’t want to risk dying, but my curiosity and fear were too strong, and it didn’t seem like Seth was going to just tell me what was going on. I was worried it was my only way to get any answers, but I didn’t know how it would help.

Perhaps I’d see my friends all standing down there, a small, stupid voice in the back of my head whispered. Maybe they’d be waiting in the middle of the induction wing courtyard, staring up at me and laughing. That would be horrible. I’d hate to know that they were all in on whatever trick Seth was playing on me, but I was almost hopeful. Part of me hoped that was what awaited me because at least then, Katie might still be there.

I had to do it, and as long as I was careful, it would be fine. I didn’t need to get too close to the edge to look down there, and I’d keep an eye on Seth, I decided. If he moved or anything, I’d be ready. I could put the self-defence classes I’d had at the Academy to the test, and everything would be fine. Even if it wasn’t, even if Seth managed to overpower me and throw me over, it wasn’t the end of the world. It would hurt. I had no doubt of that, but it wasn’t my reality. Death in that world wasn’t the end for me.

Still, my eyes remained on Seth, and my knees felt weak as I slowly climbed to my feet. For a moment, I wasn’t sure that they’d support my weight. I was worried they’d buckle, and I wouldn’t need Seth to push me. I’d go tumbling over the edge alone.

I did it, though. I felt unsteady, like I could fall at any moment, but my knees held. I did not look away from the boy who had become one of my best friends as I backed away until my shoulder hit the chimney that jutted out of the roof.

But Seth was true to his word. He remained seated, his eyes fixed on me as I reached out and wrapped my arms around the rough stone of the chimney. The walk had drained me, and I was so grateful to have something to cling to as I took a deep breath. I glanced at Seth once more, making sure not to turn my back on him, before peering over the edge of the roof at the induction wing below.

It was hard to see anything. The sun had pretty much set, and the courtyard was illuminated by a single solitary bulb, but at first, nothing seemed out of place. I couldn’t see anyone in the courtyard, and there were no obvious signs that alarmed me.

My eyes flicked towards Seth again, but he was still watching me, his gaze hungry, and that made me think I must have been missing something. He seemed to be waiting for me to notice something, and I couldn’t help but glance down again, trying to figure out what it was.

The weeks of observation training had paid off, though. My gaze quickly found the dark puddle spilling out over the cobblestones, and nausea churned in my stomach as my heart leapt into my throat.

“Is that blood?” I couldn’t help but ask.

I wasn’t sure how Seth would know. He couldn’t see over the edge of the roof from where he was seated, but I still found myself looking to him for an answer. He had known before, after all. Somehow, he was aware something had happened down there, so he must have had a way.

His lips slowly stretched into a smile that sent a chill down my spine.

“Yes.”

“Is it… is it Katie’s?” I whispered.

Seth just shrugged.

“Doubt it. She’s gone.”

His cold, uncaring words sent a wave of panic through me. Despite everything that had happened, how he’d been acting over the last few minutes, I was still shocked to hear how callous he seemed. He had been so upset about what happened to her before. He had cried, just as I had, and I didn’t understand how he was suddenly so unbothered by it.

“Who’s is it then?” I asked, unable to stop my eyes from drifting away from Seth again.

The need to take it all in, to catalogue everything I could see, rose within me, and I found my gaze combing the courtyard. The light had shifted, I noticed. I wasn’t sure if it was the wind or if something had knocked it, but the angle had changed, and something else had become visible. I could see something sticking out from under the parapet that surrounded the courtyard.

Feet.

My body turned cold as the blood drained out of me, leaving me lightheaded. I hugged the chimney even tighter, my ears ringing as I looked at Seth again.

“Why?”

That was the only question I could ask. I wanted to ask what was happening, who it was down there, and if they were dead, but I couldn’t. The words refused to come out.

“We don’t need them,” Seth replied with a shrug.

“What?” I asked, but the word came out too quietly. It was more of a gasp than anything else.

My head was spinning. I felt in shock, unable to grasp what he’d said, what I was seeing, and my body felt wrong. It felt as though if I let go of the chimney, I’d float away, and although I was terrified of witnessing more horror, all I could do was let my gaze return to the courtyard.

As I watched, a shape seemed to solidify from the darkness surrounding the courtyard. A person, dressed entirely in black and shrouded in shadows, stepped forward. They showed no fear as they walked directly into the light, their steps slow and deliberate, before looking up.

The air caught in my chest, and I jerked back, trying to hide behind the chimney to escape their gaze, but I knew there was no point. I couldn’t see their face through their mask. Their eyes were hidden, but somehow, I could tell they were gazing directly at me.

My breathing was shallow as I looked towards Seth again, but my gaze didn’t make it that far. It got caught on something else, and my eyes widened as I spotted a row of people appear from the shadows of the trees below.

They were dressed in the same all-black uniform as the person before, and I felt my stomach drop as I noticed how perfectly in time their steps were. Their movements were so terrifyingly smooth that if I didn’t know better, if I couldn’t see the moon glinting off their masks, I would have assumed they were nothing more than a dark cloud rolling across the grounds.

Hope flared in my heart, and I rose up on the balls of my feet to watch them draw closer to the Academy. Soon, they would be close enough for the security system to kick in. Alarms would cut through the silence of the night. Guns would burst out of the ground, mines would be triggered, and the people, whoever they were, would be stopped. The Academy would be protected, and then I could go find Rodgers again.

It felt a little childish to go running to him about Seth. The thought of speaking to him about boy troubles sent a faint wave of embarrassment through me, but I knew it was more than that. Whatever was happening, whatever Seth had done, was bigger than just me, but Rodgers would be able to help. I had to believe that.

But no alarm sounded. The world remained utterly silent, and the people continued advancing towards the Academy. I felt the hope fluttering frantically in my heart die as I realised something must have happened, that the security system must have been disabled, but I didn’t understand how.

If that was the case, though, I was right. Something big was happening, and Seth was a part of it.

“There it is!” he announced happily. “I was waiting for the despair to set in as you realised just how truly fucked you are, and there it is.”

Seth rose to his feet in one fluid movement, and I leapt away from the chimney. My feet carried me closer to the middle of the roof, and my knees softened. My hands clenched into fists as I prepared for him to jump at me.

“Who?” I asked.

Confusion flitted across Seth’s face, but it was quickly chased away by irritation.

“What?” he said, his annoyance clear.

He didn’t understand my question, didn’t get what I was asking, and I forced myself to swallow before clarifying.

“You said you didn’t need them,” I told him, gesturing towards the induction wing without taking my eyes off Seth. “Who do you need then?”

Seth’s gaze followed my gesture, and I took the chance to step towards the ladder leading down into the Academy. I wasn’t far from it, only a few paces. I just needed to distract Seth more. Then, I could make a break for it.

He was fast, though. That was the problem. He was a faster runner than I was, and I knew that, but as long as I got down there, it would be fine. I could shout, scream if I had to. I just needed to do anything to let the other trainees know about the people creeping towards the building. Then, even if I was caught, they’d be able to raise the alarm.

Maybe that was the problem, I told myself. The security system was obviously down, and perhaps that meant the GSOD had no clue about the intruders. As soon as they were aware, the Academy would spring into action and protect itself, and the trainees would help. They’d trained for this, and there were caches of emergency weapons all around the building, so it would be fine. The people probably wouldn’t make it into the building.

But the thought had only just entered my mind before being squashed. I didn’t have a chance to feel even the faintest flare of hope because I knew it was already too late. The people were already in the building. They’d made it into the induction wing, and I wasn’t sure whether it was just the person in the courtyard or if they’d already killed everyone in there, but they should have been stopped before that point.

“Oh, Grace,” Seth said, chuckling again. “Is it not obvious who we need?”

I stepped back again as his gaze returned to me, my heel banging painfully against the raised border surrounding the hatch.

“No.”

Seth cocked his head to the side. His expression transformed into something close to sympathy or pity, but it did nothing to mask the spark of cruelty still glowing in his eyes. He was enjoying himself too much, and that disgusted me.

“Really?” he asked, throwing his arms out. “None of this makes it clear?”

I looked around briefly, but I didn’t want my gaze to leave him for long. The thought of that scared me too much.

“No,” I said, carefully edging to the side.

The angle was wrong. From where I was standing, I’d have to pull the flap towards me before I could get to the ladder, and that would only take a second or so, but it was too long. It might give Seth enough time to reach me, and I was still clinging to the hope that I’d be able to raise the alarm and save the Academy.

A loud sigh escaped Seth’s lips, and he put his hands on his hips, shaking his head as he surveyed me.

“Man, I was right, huh?” he asked. “You really are stupid, aren’t you?”

I wasn’t sure whether Seth wanted me to respond to that question, but I didn’t. I wasn’t sure what to say because I truly didn’t know who they were looking for, but my mind was too focused on the hatch behind me.

After a couple of seconds, once it became obvious that I wasn’t going to say anything, Seth scoffed. His hands dropped from his hips, and he smiled again.

“Well, seeing as you clearly need someone to spell it out for you,” he started. “It’s you, Grace. You’re the one we need.”

I did not wait for him to say anything else or bother asking why. I just spun around and dove for the hatch. My slick hands slipped as I hurried down the ladder as quickly as I could, making it almost impossible to hold on, but I refused to slow.

My pulse was racing in my ears. I could barely hear the loud bangs of my shoes against the metal rungs, but still, Seth’s voice floated towards me clearly.

“We got a rabbit, folks!” he announced. “God, I always love it when they run. Bet you I get her first.”
