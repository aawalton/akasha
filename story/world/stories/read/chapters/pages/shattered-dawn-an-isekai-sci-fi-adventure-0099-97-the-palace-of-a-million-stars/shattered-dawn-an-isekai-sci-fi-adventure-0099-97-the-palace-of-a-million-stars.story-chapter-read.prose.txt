As Elion, Zev, and Yaret progressed through the streets of Erod, traffic overhead increased.

Explosions echoed down the streets, a low rumbling which rattled windows.

“That will be our main force,” Zev muttered to himself as they jogged on. “Right on time. They’ll be pulling the attention of the black wall away.”

The portion of the city they entered now showed more signs of life. The occasional movement in a window, or the closing of a door hinting at cautious inhabitants. Smaller, more homey buildings replaced the skyscrapers.

The ground sloped gently upwards, and the homes grew larger and more ornate.

“What happened to this city?” Elion asked. “It seemed like it was totally abandoned back where we landed, but this part is well maintained.”

“Much of Erod was destroyed or abandoned, after the cataclysm,” Zev said. “We worked for generations to rebuild and repopulate, but Dorian un-did much of that work when he shattered the celestial sphere.”

They pushed onward, until the sun hung directly overhead. They rounded a corner, stepping out of the shadow of a particularly tall and tower-like building. Elion gasped, his breath snatched away by the sight.

Slender, glimmering towers stretched upwards, impossibly slender. Hundreds of spires stretched up toward the sky, sparkling in the sunlight. The light split into a rainbow of color, giving the building an otherworldly aura.

Tall black pillars marked out colonnades, through which moved black robed figures on patrol.

“The Palace of a Million Stars,” Zev said, grinning as he took in Elion’s astonishment. “Your ancestral home.”

“Wow,” was all Elion could manage.

Zev slowed his pace, but they continued walking up the street.

“Shouldn’t we be a bit sneakier about this?” Elion said, feeling nervous as they approached.

“If I were in Dorian’s position,” Zev said, “I’d have patrols out on the streets, searching for people like us. Have you seen anything like that on our way here?”

"No,” Elion said. “But—”

“Dorian’s a fool. He is easily distracted by anything that looks like a big threat, and he’ll pull all his forces away to deal with it.”

“You’ve a lot of trust in your analysis of the situation,” Yaret said, gazing critically down the street, watching the patrolling warlocks.”

“He’s my brother,” Zev said. “I used to beat him at Ziff with the same strategies. Make a big, showy attack in one direction. Then slip in quietly from the side.”

“But this is real life,” Yaret protested. “Not a board game.”

“From Dorian’s perspective, he’s still just moving units around on a game board,” Zev said. “He’s obvious and predictable. In fact, I can tell you that he’s definitely in the palace right now. Otherwise he’d have those patrolling guards doing something else.”

Elion pursed his lips, staring at the ground as he bit back a retort. If Dorian was so obvious and predictable, why had he surprised Zev and murdered his parents?

He was nervous about Zev’s plan, but didn’t want to fight about it here, in the open, just down the street from Dorian’s palace.

“So what are we going to do, then,” Elion asked. “We have to get in there.”

“I grew up in this palace,” Zev said. “We’re not going to barge in through the main gate. There are other ways.”

Zev led them down a side street, and up an alley until they were nearly at the wall to the palace ground. They stopped in front of a manor, that had seen better days.

The walkway had been swept clean, and a small pile of weeds suggested someone was doing their best, but drooping eves and peeling paint hinted at larger problems.

“Don’t worry,” he said. “Isalyn knows about this place. She’s one of very few people who do.”

Zev walked the cobble stone path to the front door and rapped on it loudly three times.

Elion and Yaret stood a few feet behind Zev, wary of what might happen.

The door squealed as someone pulled it open a crack. An elderly man peered through the gap. “How can I help you?” he asked.

“Good day, Master Golin,” Zev said. “Do you mind if we join you for tea?” As he spoke, he gestured with one hand, and a golden star appeared in front of him, identical to the one on Liora’s locket.

Elion and Yaret glanced at each other, confused. Yaret shifted his back, rifle parts clattering.

The man opened the door and gestured for them to enter. They filed in.

Without speaking a single word, the man closed the door behind them. He latched it with several mechanical locks, then activated an artefin security system.

He shuffled along the wooden floor in slippered feet, heading deeper into his home, waving for them to follow.

“Master Golin is a former member of the Crystalline guard,” Zev whispered, as though that explained what they were doing.

They filed down a narrow hall, hung with dusty pictures, and then Master Golin pulled open an ancient wooden door.

A narrow set of wooden stairs disappeared into darkness below.

Zev walked confidently down the stairs.

Elion was about half way down the steps, and Yaret had just mounted them, when Master Golin slammed the door shut, plunging them into darkness.

“Hey!” Elion exclaimed.

“It’s fine,” Zev said from somewhere deeper in the room. “Keep walking.”

Yaret produced a small penlight. With its feeble illumination, Elion made his way down the creaky staircase, stepping onto a packed dirt floor.

A cool draft swirled around him, and the air smelled of rotten potatoes.

Zev muttered to himself from the other side of the room, brushing his hands along the wall in the darkness.

“Found it,” he said.

A switch clicked, and light illuminated a door set into a brick wall. The door glowed softly, etched with lines hinting at the artefin power that constructed it. A seven pointed star sigil marked the center of the door.

Zev again gestured with his hand, and the symbol of house starhold appeared in front of the door, lining up perfectly with the engraving.

A green light pulsed, then the door slid silently to the side. The trio passed through the door, and into a concrete corridor. The door shut behind them.

They emerged into a large room; like a cement box. It could have been a prison, save for the luxurious looking furnishings dotted around. Various cabinets, lockers, and pieces of what looked like kitchen equipment lined one wall.

Yaret whistled. He’d gone to stand in front of a rack of rifles. He picked one up and inspected it, examining the features and checking the energy pack.

“This looks like one of Davin Arden’s,” he said. “A good weapon.” He unpacked his ruined weapon placed it on the ground, then slung the new one over his shoulder.

“You need anything, Elion?” Zev asked.

Elion shook his head, still trying to take in the bunker.

“Then let’s go.”

A dark passage at the back end of the room cut through the ground, disappearing in darkness.

“This tunnel runs under the grounds of the palace,” Zev said. “It’s shielded and runs entirely isolated on it’s own system.”

“Dorian doesn’t know about it?” Yaret asked, still suspicious.

“He probably suspects,” Zev said. “But this was Aria’s escape. Each of us had a hidden passage out of our rooms, in case of an emergency. If he found it, he wouldn’t have let Master Golin live.”

They slipped further through the passage.

“You’re sure it’s not a trap?” Elion asked.

“If there’s a trap, it’ll be waiting for us in the palace,” Zev said. “Not here.”

They reached the end of the passage, and Zev peered through a small slot before declaring the coast clear and pushing open the door.

On the other side was a bedroom. The door clicked shut behind them, a portion of paneled wall blending so seamlessly that Elion couldn’t spot the hidden passage.

An elegant bed dominated the large room; four posters with ornate curtains.

A fireplace and mantle stood at the other end of the room.

Zev breathed deeply. “It’s good to be home,” he said. His face turned sad as he scanned the room. “A shame that your mother couldn’t be here with us. This place looks just like I remember. It reminds me of her.”

Elion took in the massive room with it’s vaulted ceilings. “This was my mom’s room?”

“Basically the way she left it,” Zev said. “But it does look like someone has been using it.” He nodded toward the rumpled bedding, then picked a single high heel up from the floor. “Didn’t I make you put the other one in Liora’s room?”

Elion nodded.

“If something happens to her…” Zev said, trailing off. “Remember, you’re the next in line to the throne. We will need you to step up.”

“Don’t talk like that,” Elion said. “She’s going to be fine.”

“No,” Zev said. “She might not be. You might need to make difficult decisions.”

“Glad you’re letting me decide now,” Elion said wryly, trying to change the topic. “Unlike back when we were on earth.”

Zev scoffed.

“By the way,” Elion said. “I hated talking to the police.”

“Did they give you a hard time?” Zev asked.

“Not really,” Elion said. “But still, lying to them sucked. Not to mention the fact that you just left me standing there in the garage by myself.”

“I’m sorry about that,” Zev said. “We all learn things in retrospect. I probably should have slowed down a bit, and figured out a way to take you with me. You make a great Aurelian Knight, by the way.”

Elion shook his head, face reddening. “I was mad about it for a while, but… I think I understand. you thought you could quickly rescue Liora and get back to normal. You were worried that she’d be killed like your sister was,” he said. “My mom.”

“Yeah,” Zev said, his voice thick. “Come on. We have to be careful, now that we’re in the palace. There will be guards around.”

Zev reached for the antechamber door.

It swung away from him.

A woman walked into the room.
