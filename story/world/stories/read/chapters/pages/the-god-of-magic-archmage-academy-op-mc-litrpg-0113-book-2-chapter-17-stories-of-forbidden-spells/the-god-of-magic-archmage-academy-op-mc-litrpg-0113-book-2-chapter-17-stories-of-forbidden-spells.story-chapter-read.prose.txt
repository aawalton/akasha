King Ragmár

During the Hundred Year Period Sen was Sleeping

The screams of his children filled the hallway. The wild noises of his sons and daughters chasing one another and playing in their room. A series of sharp thunks told him Caldur had convinced one of his other sons to spar. It brought the satisfying sound of wood against wood as they practiced swordsmanship with training swords. King Ragmár smiled. This was what it was all about. He’d fought to create a world where children could just be children. A world so unlike the one he and Sen had grown up in, where they’d needed to fight bandits, mana beasts, and demons just to survive. This was proof that it had all been worth it.

He pulled his cloak tighter as he walked. The castle halls were cold tonight. The snow outside was thick, and its chill had settled over the kingdom. But his court magicians had seen that no family would go cold tonight or any other until the snowstorm passed.

Ragmár leaned against the doorway to peer in on the children. Their room was in a well-defended portion of the castle and comprised of three separate spaces. The central space was a joint playroom where they could all spend time together. The room to the left was the sleeping quarters for the girls, and the one on the right for the boys. It was important to him that they spent as much time as possible together now. It was important that they enjoyed their youth before they grew up and had to deal with all the realities of being a prince or princess.

No, for now they didn’t need to concern themselves with politics or war. Tonight, they were simply children.

The playroom was filled with chests of toys. Fine blankets were draped over luxurious couches. A rug made from the pelt of a mana beast bear that Ragmár had taken down himself sat in the center of the room. In all, nine children scurried about the room. Ragmár had been blessed with nine children.

Mori jumped backward to avoid the small horde of children running past in a game that somewhat resembled capture the flag. Seressa, one of his youngest daughters, directed the others on her team. Ragmár grinned at that, crossing his arms as he watched. She was the spitting image of her mother. Whenever he told her, she beamed as if it were the best compliment in the world. She had beautiful, long brown hair that her handmaidens were obsessed with tending to. It fell to the back of her knees and was a constant source of compliments.

“Go right! Atraska cut them off! Wait, don’t let Mori grab you!”

Mori effortlessly plucked one of the boys by the collar of his shirt. The boy sighed and resigned himself to his fate.

Seressa frowned. “We were about to win!”

Mori smiled and sat the boy down. The others had turned to see why he’d interrupted. “Well, it’s time for bed, and you know using that gift of yours isn’t fair to the others.”

Seressa blushed at having been caught. “Can we at least finish our game?”

“Sure you can,” Mori said. “Tomorrow, after your studies are finished.”

A chorus of groans filled the room to Mori’s delight. It was all in good fun; they might not always appreciate his strictness, but the children were fond of Mori, extremely so.

Caldur looked up from sparring. “Don’t complain! Uncle Mori speaks with the authority of our father! If you’ve got a problem with that, then—” He snatched the wooden sword from his brother and held out its handle toward the others. “Grab a sword so I can defend uncle’s honor!” He was only ten years old, but already showed the promise of a boy who would grow tall and strong. The boy had short brown hair and the confidence of a prince. Sometimes to his detriment. There was a popular rumor that shortly after Caldur learned to speak, he declared he’d be a great swordsman like his father. From how hard he dedicated himself to his training, even Ragmár sometimes found it hard to deny such a story.

Seressa scoffed. “He’s father’s court magician. He doesn’t need your help defending anything.”

Caldur sneered. He tossed the wooden sword at her feet in challenge.

Ragmár decided it was best to intervene personally here. Mori could handle it, but this was a role for a father. As he uncrossed his arms and walked into the room, Seressa spoke.

She eyed her older brother with disdain and wisdom beyond her years. “You’re going to get scolded in three…two…one.”

“Caldur,” King Ragmár called out. He paused, as both he and Caldur looked at Seressa in unison. “How did you—”

“Reading the future is cheating!” Caldur accused.

Seressa shrugged. “Caldur’s being mean, daddy!”

Ragmár chuckled. He approached his oldest and knelt before him, placing a hand on the boy’s shoulder. “It’s kind of you to protect Mori, but you mustn’t turn your blade on family. You’re my oldest son. It’s your job to protect your siblings.”

Caldur groaned. “Even Seressa?” he asked.

“Even Seressa. One day you’ll be grateful for that gift of hers. She may save you from an arrow or a spell on the battlefield.” Ragmár smiled.

Caldur thought for a moment and then frowned. “She’d let it hit me.”

Seressa crossed her arms. “Oh, I’d definitely let it hit you.”

Caldur glared at her. Ragmár shot her an exasperated look.

Mori chuckled at the exchange. “Goodnight, children. Lessons will start bright and early tomorrow, so get some rest.”

“Goodnight, Uncle Mori!” the children called out nearly in unison.

Ragmár smiled. “Alright, children, let’s get you tucked in.”

“What? Aren’t you going to tell us a bedtime story?” Atraska asked.

“Yeah!”

“You always tell us stories!”

“Please, dad!”

He chuckled at the chorus of pleas. “Fine. What will it be tonight?” he asked.

Caldur stood and swung his wooden sword. “I want to hear about the time you fought Garlon’s general! It was like…” He made several movements with his sword, spun around and slashed the air with all his might. The whole time he made whooshing noises with his mouth.

Seressa rolled her eyes. “Caldur always gets to pick. I want to hear something new.”

Caldur froze. “New?”

“Yes, new! We’ve heard about that fight a hundred times already.” She ignored Caldur’s wooden sword flying to her feet to challenge her to yet another duel and turned back to face her father. “I want to hear about the ten forbidden spells. The ones that Sen vowed never to use.” Her eyes twinkled as she spoke.

If Caldur was following in Ragmár’s footsteps to become a swordsman, Seressa was by far the child most likely to follow the path of the mage. Most of her questions were about magic, and he knew he’d soon need to arrange private lessons for her with Mori. She was far more advanced in spell-casting than the others, not to mention her proficiency in catching glimpses into the future.

Ragmár sighed at her request. “I think that might be a little much for a bedtime story, Ser. What about—”

She frowned.

He wasn’t the only one who noticed. To his credit, Caldur actually came to her aid. “Actually, I’d like to hear that one too, father.”

Seressa looked as if she’d just seen a daunting future in response. It wasn’t like the two of them to agree on something.

Ragmár raised a brow. “The two of you conspiring against me? Should I be worried?”

“No!” Caldur shouted.

“Yes!” Seressa smiled like a gremlin.

He laughed. This really wasn’t bedtime material. But he would reward sibling camaraderie. If his children learned to actually work together, he had no doubt the world wouldn’t be ready for the good they would bring to it. He gestured for the others to join them, and they quickly formed a semicircle around him. Even his blade, the King of Swords, hummed in its sheath in anticipation.

Everyone could appreciate a good story.

“Well, it is said,” Ragmár started. “That there are tens spell so potent, so powerful and dangerous, that even the God of Magic believed they should never be used.”

“He wasn’t strong enough?” Atraska asked.

Caldur shushed her. “Don’t interrupt, Atra! Let father tell the story.”

“Sorry, Caldie.”

“Don’t call me—” Caldur sighed and waited for his father to continue.

Ragmár smiled. “To answer your question, no, strength wasn’t the problem at all. You see, the truth is, Sen—” He cleared his throat. Sen’s title was more appropriate for this particular story. “The God of Magic had a technique that very few people knew about. It’s a secret.” He held a hand over his finger to emphasize it.

All of his younger children and Caldur imitated him. Seressa chuckled at her older brother’s behavior.

“We decided there was only one way to bring peace and order to this world. We needed to hunt down every living god. Some we chased down to the ends of the world. Some came to us, hoping to strike a deal or cut us down before we could finish them off. One by one, they fell. Some were killed. Others Sen forced into submission, like the God of Demons, or sealed, like Serpia.” He smiled as the memories of their adventures rushed past. He might not have been the one to cut down the gods, but he was the one cutting down their subordinates. Sen was the God of Magic after all, but Ragmár was the swordsman who cleared the path. He’d fought against many powerful people in doing so.

“But some of those gods wielded magic unlike anything we’d ever seen. The God of Magic was a researcher of magic above all else. When he encountered magic that was especially powerful, he wanted it for himself. So he developed a technique capable of taking spells from others. He seized spells from the depths of our dying opponents’ souls. He only used this ability on the most exceptional of spells, and before long, he’d amassed ten of them. Ten powerful spells taken by god-level magicians in their final moments.

His children’s eyes were filled with wonder, most of all Seressa and Caldur.

“And each spell made him stronger, right?” Caldur asked.

Ragmár sighed. “It’s common knowledge that a magician doesn’t need to hide the name of his magic, or even his means of casting. Magic isn’t something that can usually be stolen or copied. As I said, the God of Magic’s technique cut away a portion of his opponent’s soul. The portion connected to the spell itself. The problem was, these were wicked people. A spell’s creator matters. Of course, there are common spells that everyone uses, fireball for instance. Spells like that are altered through Modifiers or Maximizers. But spells crafted by an individual are different. A person’s personality, their ambitions, their beliefs, even their affinities for different kinds of magics. All of these things affect the spell itself.”

“How so?” Atraska asked.

“Someone who cares a great deal for others might create spells that are naturally more inclined toward helping. Someone who only wants to hurt, well, their spells will be more devastating. The spells of evil gods were the spells of evil gods. Their stolen magic, even in the hands of a good man, is still designed to inflict maximum harm.”

Seressa swallowed. “So, what happened then, father? What happened that made him swear not to use them again?”

Ragmár’s eyes fell. As he’d said, this was not a good bedtime story.

“We learned the cost of stolen magic.”

Sen

Before the Uprising

Sen stood in the center of the abandoned shop where Drussan had left them. Mori stood to his right. Tutor was finalizing preparations for the spell.

Dozens of magic essence spheres of varying elements lined the room. Some rolled around as if pushed by invisible hands. Some sat perfectly still. Still others floated of their own volition, hovering around the room and bumping into walls and each other before flying away again.

He drew upon the power of the spheres and sacrificed their energy for a spell he hadn’t cast since his journey through time.

“Greater Creation Maxima!”

His last creations were still lost out there in the world without a trace. But this one at least would be short-lived, and once its purpose was done, he wouldn’t need to concern himself with it. This was the first time he’d used his new Maximizer, Maxima, with a creation spell. He could sense the difference immediately.

The formless mist appeared, ready for him to shape it into a creation of his own design. But he wasn’t done with the preparation. No, he still needed to embed the last ingredient. The Sixth Forbidden spell, the quintessential spell of the Plague God.

Plague Bearer.

[I’m ready.]

Sen looked to Mori for confirmation. He nodded.

For this purpose, he’d chosen a variant of the spell, a mixture of a spell stolen from another god-class mage’s soul and Sen’s modifications.

“Paragon’s Plague Maxima!”

A plague that targeted individuals or groups who met extremely specific criteria, rather than an unforgiving one that would cut people down with no regard for their differences.

With that done, he willed the creation to take form.

The mist coalesced into the shape of a man. The body was more like a silhouette than anything. It had grooves where its eyes should be, a small nose, and a line in place of a mouth. It was hairless, and its skin swirled with various shades of green. Some shades were as dark as leaves in a tropical forest; some were the colors of emeralds, and some were as light as grapes. It reminded Sen of a vat of dangerous chemicals, or a pond of algae-filled water.

Drops of liquid fell from the being’s hands and landed on the shop floor. The droplets slowly crawled back toward the body and were reabsorbed.

Both the creation and the plague spell had melded together seamlessly. After a moment, the living creation slowly stirred and looked around the room with newborn curiosity.

Mori stepped forward. “Excuse me,” he said, gaining the creation’s attention. “Are you aware of your purpose?”

The creation nodded. “Yes, I have understanding. Though, perhaps not the words with which to share it.”

“You were born to kill,” Mori informed it.

The creation tilted its head. “No…not quite.” It brought a finger to the spot where its lips should be. “I’m a…” It looked towards Sen. “A surgeon. I’m a surgeon. It is my job to cut away the corruption in society. There is impurity. Wretched, wretched imbalance. An oppression I have been born to carve away.”

Sen smiled. It seemed it had the words after all. “That is correct. You are not the sword, but the scalpel. Slavers plague these lands, and you have been created to eliminate them. I need you to do so aggressively. Cut them away, but cut no further than that.”

The being nodded. Sen’s words served two purposes. Instructions for his plague creation, but also the conditions for the Paragon’s Plague spell itself. It would only target dreadbeast slavers. There were many kinds of evil in this world, but the nature of stolen spells made anything broader than this too risky. If Drussan hadn’t provided him access to the substantial intelligence that the dreadbeasts had collected over the years, he wouldn’t even risk using the spell for this purpose. But as it was, Tutor believed the risks were within acceptable parameters.

Sen turned to Mori and nodded.

Mori produced the map of locations Drussan had given them. All the towns, encampments, and slave trading operations that were too far away for Sen and his allies to deal with or without Drussan’s agents present to set plans into motion. This plague creation would be responsible for ensuring no dreadbeasts slipped through the cracks of Garvhadda’s plan. It was the only way Sen could ensure the remaining slaves were freed by the end of the night.

Mori handed the map over. A green hand took the map and unfurled it. The creation nodded as it scanned each of the circled locations. “These are my destinations, then.” It held the map to its chest and pushed the paper through its liquid body. The map dissolved within it.

[Well, hope you have a backup copy because I certainly don’t.]

“How long will I have to complete my work? The plague may take time to spread.”

Sen smiled. “I will release you at night. By morning, the work must be finished.”

The creation was silent for a moment, before nodding. “You place much faith in me. A surgeon must perform when the patient is ready. Delay only leads to further risks. Yes, this will be a worthy existence.”

[Is it just me, or does he seem way too eager?]

It’s probably better if a plague creation doesn’t have moral dilemmas about its work in this case.

[Fair enough. It still creeps me out though.]

Sen held a hand out. “Memory Transfer Maxima.” He embedded several spells within the spirit to aid its work.

The plague spirit looked at its palms. Its entire body rippled as if someone had dropped a pebble into a still pond. In one hand, a green liquid flame appeared. On the opposite side of its body, a liquid wing formed on its back. Variants of Flesh Sculpt and Fireball, just two of the spells he’d given it deep knowledge of.

The line on the spirit’s face frowned. “Master…I don’t understand. Will my plague not be enough? Will the wicked endure even the ravages of diseases and infection? Surely not!”

It was time for the second condition, and arguably the most important one. “Listen closely. This is important. There is an outcome that must be avoided at all cost. Should you encounter a lord-level mage, you will use the techniques I’ve given you to kill them personally. Do not allow them to become infected by the plague. Furthermore, if you encounter a prince or princess-level mage or above, you are to remove the plague from that area immediately and get word to me. Not only should you not infect them. They should not be allowed to even come near it. Ensure this rule is followed.”

“I…understand. But what exactly, if I may ask, is the outcome you’re trying to avoid?”

Sen’s face hardened at the memory. “Mutation. This spell must not be allowed to mutate.” It was the reason for the conditions, as well as the reason this creation’s life would be short-lived. The creation, and the Plague Bearer spell, would fade from this world permanently once its work was complete. After breaking the vow not to use a forbidden spell, the spell would be lost forever. Knowing what the risks were, it was almost a relief.

“You have given me much, and although my life and purpose will be short, I find appeal in them. I wonder, might you grant me a name, master?”

“Do you have one in mind?” Sen asked.

The creation spirit nodded.

“The Green Man.”

Simple and straightforward. The creation was practical, finding a short and efficient path forward. That would bode well for the work it had to do, spreading from one victim to the next until none remained.

Sen smiled. “Very well. The Green Man it is,” Sen said, acknowledging the creation’s new name. “I will call on you soon.”

Sen

The Present

Moments after Drussan, Slari, and Micah went through the gateway, he unleashed the spell.

Sen held a hand toward the sky. “Rise. Massacre those who would take freedom from others, and free the Children of Garlon. Go forth and cut away corruption in the name of the God of Magic.”

The Green Man materialized, kneeling on the ground before him. “At once.” He looked up, and the shades of green swirled more vigorously. “The cleansing begins.” With a smile, he dissolved into a pool from which dozens of green birds made in his image emerged and shot into the sky. Each would travel to one of the many destinations the Green Man had been assigned.

For a moment, Sen heard a deep laughter coming from the pool. Then the last of the birds emerged, taking the remnants of liquid with it.

The Green Man embarked on the hunt that would last for a single night. In the morning, the dreadbeasts would be free, and the slavers dead.

[This reminds me of the old days, master. I don’t hate it.]

Sen felt inclined to agree. This was the kind of work that the God of Magic had done in his own time. Freeing the dreadbeasts, putting a stop to the plans of men like Lord Thessus and Malachi. He’d used his time at the academy to learn about this new world; now it was time to use his power to change it for the better.
