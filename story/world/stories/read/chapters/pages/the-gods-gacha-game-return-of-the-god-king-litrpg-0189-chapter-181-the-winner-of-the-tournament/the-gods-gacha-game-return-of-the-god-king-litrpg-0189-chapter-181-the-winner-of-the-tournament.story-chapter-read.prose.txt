Next up was the match between Boris and Erika.

In this match, it didn’t matter who won for me personally. Erika wasn’t a bad candidate for commander either. She might not be as naturally commanding as Boris, but she was calm-minded and capable of thinking several steps ahead. More importantly, she was someone who could listen and someone who wouldn’t dismiss better ideas simply because they came from others. And above all, she was someone I knew well and trusted.

“Missy, I won’t be holding back,” said Boris.

“Me neither,” Erika replied.

The moment the match began, there was no hesitation on either side. Boris, in particular, activated his signature skill immediately.

“Adamant Flesh: Diamond Body!”

The crystalline sheen spread across his body once more, hardening his form into something closer to a living diamond than flesh. Meanwhile, Erika didn’t retreat. She drew her twin blades in a single smooth motion as Sword Intent erupted outward—sharp and focused. Sword Aura followed, coiling tightly around her body as her presence sharpened to a lethal edge. The air between them seemed to tense as the two forces clashed.

Steel rang against diamond-hardened flesh as Erika struck with blinding precision. Boris absorbed the blows without yielding, countering with short, efficient movements that forced Erika to disengage again and again.

After several exchanges, it seemed as though the fight would drag on, with neither side gaining ground. Then, to everyone’s surprise, Erika stepped back and lowered her blades, as if she had already made her decision.

“I concede,” she said calmly. The words sent a ripple of confusion through the stands.

Boris paused, one brow lifting slightly. “You sure?”

Erika nodded. “I’m not fit to be the commander. To begin with, I joined this tournament to prove that I’ve grown stronger, but it seems… pointless.”

I caught a trace of bitterness in her voice, and I understood exactly why. It seemed that what had happened back in the tenth scenario still haunted her even now… She had four members of her party die before her, even though it had only been a temporary party. Ever since then, she had sharpened herself into a blade meant to cut through enemies, not someone meant to stand at the front and give orders while others followed.

Leadership meant bearing responsibility for lives beyond your own. Erika had already carried that weight once, and she wasn’t willing to shoulder it again. Perhaps that was why, in the previous nine scenarios, she had chosen to go solo.

“Unbelievable! The match has been decided by concession!” the commentator exclaimed, his voice echoing across the Grand Colosseum. “The winner of this match is Boris!”

A wave of mixed reactions swept through the stands, though it wasn’t nearly as bad as when Black Phantom—myself, in this case—failed to appear, or when Kaela had forfeited her match. Many people had bet their entire savings on the wrong candidates, which became the fuel of the outrage.

Still, I believed Erika possessed the ability to continue and push Boris to the brink, forcing him to take her seriously. In fact, I wasn’t entirely sure who would emerge victorious between the two. Even so, I doubted that Erika—or anyone else, for that matter—would be able to pierce through Boris’s defense now that it was made of diamond rather than steel.

The commentator waited for the noise to subside before continuing. “And with that, we have our finalist! Only one match remains to determine the commander of Istellia’s divine warrior army!”

Excitement surged through the stands as spectators leaned forward in anticipation.

“As usual, we will be taking a fifteen-minute intermission before the final match!” he announced. “Finalists, prepare yourselves! Spectators, don’t go anywhere—this last battle will decide everything!”

Michelle and the others decided to grab some snacks to eat while watching the final, so I joined them and bought a bag of caramel popcorn for fifty soul coins. It was outrageously expensive—daylight robbery, really. Still, considering how rich I was now, I didn’t bother haggling and paid without a second thought.

Boris didn’t return to the stands, apparently choosing to prepare himself for the final.

“Maxim, who do you think will win between Sir Boris and Ronan?” Michelle asked, glancing at me as we made our way back to our seats.

I paused for a moment before answering, “Boris has a much greater advantage. In raw durability, physical strength, and close-quarters dominance, Ronan simply can’t match him—especially now that Boris has Adamant Flesh: Diamond Body.”

Michelle nodded slowly, accepting my assessment.

“That said,” I continued, “Ronan isn’t weak. You fought him yourself, right? He’s extremely disciplined and experienced—and very well-rounded. Against most opponents, that kind of consistency wins fights. But I doubt it’ll amount to much against Boris. I mean, Boris is one hell of a man. Not to mention, the Great Warlock he fought in the previous round was even more dangerous than Ronan.”

“Ooh, really?” Tuilë asked, shoving a handful of popcorn she’d stolen from me into her mouth.

“You…” She’d obviously asked just to steal my popcorn. “Yeah, it’s basically Boris’s guaranteed victory…” I added, after a brief pause. Not that there’s an absolute guarantee in anything.

Before anyone could add more, the lights above the arena dimmed slightly, and the murmur of the crowd began to fade. At the same time, the arena had changed from the icy field into a dueling stony ring surrounded by deep ravines filled with iron spikes.

Moments later, the commentator’s voice rang out across the Grand Colosseum, brimming with excitement.

“Ladies and gentlemen, the wait is over!” he declared. “The final match of this tournament is about to begin!”

The massive holographic screen shifted, highlighting two names that now commanded everyone’s attention.

“On one side, the indomitable titan who has crushed every obstacle in his path—Boris, the Titan of the Colosseum!” the commentator announced.

However, rather than entering the usual way like he always did, Boris leapt down from the stands and landed squarely within the ring.

Cheers immediately erupted like thunder. At this point, almost everyone seemed convinced that Boris would win the tournament and become the commander of Istellia’s divine warriors. His presence—and larger-than-life demeanor—had even earned him popularity among middle-aged women and macho men alike. I’d even seen some young widows seemingly fainting in love as Boris’s gaze swept over them.

“And on the other side, the steadfast pillar of the Crimson Crest, a warrior of endurance and resolve—Ronan!”

Slightly quieter cheers erupted as Ronan emerged from the opposite entrance. Though not as thunderous as Boris’s reception, it was still far louder than what most contestants had received. It was clear that Ronan had garnered a substantial amount of support—or perhaps the Crimson Crest was larger and more influential than I had initially thought.

The two men stopped near the center of the arena, surrounded by the ravines of iron spikes as the bridges connecting the two entrances retracted.

Ronan broke the silence first, resting his sword against his shoulder. “Didn’t think my final opponent would be the rumored Boris.”

Boris chuckled. “Hah. Give me your all. Don’t hold back.”

“Naturally. If I weren’t giving it my all, I wouldn’t be here,” Ronan replied. His gaze sharpened as he looked Boris up and down. “You’re the strongest man in this tournament. I won’t deny that. If you become commander after this, I won’t complain.”

Boris raised a brow. “Oh?”

Ronan nodded. “I may be ambitious, but I know that strength like yours gives people confidence. And confidence wins wars.” He paused, then added, “But that’s only if you defeat me fair and square.”

A grin spread across Boris’s face. “Good. Just how I like it.”

The commentator’s voice thundered across the arena. “Finalists, take your positions!”

The barrier rose, sealing the dueling ring as the crowd leaned forward in anticipation. Both contestants shifted into their battle stances. Ronan exhaled slowly, blade lifting into a guarded stance. Boris clenched his fists, posture relaxed but filled with power.

“Begin!”

In the very next instant, both men charged toward each other without hesitation.

“Adamant Flesh: Diamond Body!” Boris shouted as a diamond-like sheen spread across his body.

At the same time, Ronan made his move. “Triple Sword Slash!” His blade flashed as he swung in rapid succession. Three arcs of compressed sword force tore free from the blade, flying forward like invisible crescents. The slashes screamed through the air, carving deep trenches into the arena floor as they raced toward Boris.

All three slashes slammed into Boris in quick succession, dispersing into fragments of fading light. Aside from pushing him back slightly, none of the attacks even managed to graze—let alone penetrate—his defense.

If Boris had used Adamant Flesh: Steel Body instead, the attack might have grazed him slightly. However, this upgraded form rendered his defense virtually impenetrable to physical attacks. Speaking of his signature skill, Boris could still freely switch to its inferior forms if he wished, but he wasn’t the type to hold back without reason. Boris was a man of honor, one who respected his opponents enough to meet them at full strength.

Undeterred, Ronan advanced immediately, using the momentary pushback to close the distance. His sword moved with disciplined precision as he chained into another sequence, aiming for joints and timing rather than brute force. Unfortunately, it was of no use, as Boris had no exploitable weakness.

In return, Boris met him head-on. His diamond-hardened hands clashed against steel, each impact echoing across the dueling ring. They collided again and again, sparks flying as steel met the hardest natural material on Earth.

“Heh. Not bad,” Boris said, grinning. “But you’re not going anywhere if this is the best you’ve got.”

“Hmph! Then let’s end this in a single attack!” Ronan gritted his teeth and took several steps back. Sword Aura surged as he poured everything into his blade, which became coated in molten flame.

“As you wish!” Boris laughed, his grin widening. “Diamond Form: Shield! Mountain-Crushing Charge!”

In the blink of an eye, Boris’s forearms fused together, expanding and reshaping into a massive diamond shield. The moment the transformation completed, he charged, causing the ground to detonate beneath his feet as he launched himself forward like a bullet train. The sheer mass and momentum of his advance compressed the air ahead of him.

Ronan planted his feet and roared, pouring everything he had into his blade. “Blazing Severance!”

Then he swung. A single, colossal arc of burning sword force tore through the arena, colliding head-on with Boris’s charge. The instant the two attacks met, a deafening boom rocked the arena. The shockwave blasted outward in all directions, forcing spectators to shield their eyes as dust and smoke swallowed the two finalists. The barrier flickered slightly as it strained to contain the force.

Moments later, the smoke began to thin.

At the center of the cratered ring stood Boris. His diamond shield was slightly scorched but intact, and his feet were planted firmly in the shattered stone. Across from him, Ronan lay on his back, his sword embedded half-buried in the ground beside him. His armor was fractured, his chest rising and falling heavily as he struggled to move.

The arena’s protective mechanism activated in a flash of light.

For a brief moment, the entire colosseum was silent, then the commentator’s voice exploded across the Grand Colosseum, barely able to contain his excitement.

“Th-The match is over! What an unbelievable clash of power and resolve!” The commentator took a breath before continuing with even greater fervor. “The winner of the tournament is… Boooriiis!!”

The stands erupted in deafening cheers as spectators leapt to their feet. Shouts, applause, and thunderous stomps blended into a single overwhelming roar. Even those who had supported Ronan couldn’t help but acknowledge the outcome.

Boris straightened at the center of the shattered arena, deactivating his skill as the diamond sheen faded from his body. He looked toward the stands, raised one fist high into the air, and the cheers grew even louder.

With that, the commander of Istellia’s divine warrior army had been decided.
