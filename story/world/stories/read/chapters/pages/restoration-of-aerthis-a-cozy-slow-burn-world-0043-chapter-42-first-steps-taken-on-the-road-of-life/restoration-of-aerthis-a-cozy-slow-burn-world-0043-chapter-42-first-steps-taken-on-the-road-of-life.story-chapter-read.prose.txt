Elynara woke the next morning to the faint hum of movement. For a heartbeat, her half-dreaming mind expected the usual stillness of the ruin – the quiet breathing of dust and wind.

But this sound was different. Rhythmic. And very much alive.

She blinked herself awake.

The guardian – no, her new companion – was standing. Unsteady, but upright.

The faint lines of blue that ran beneath her plating pulsed slow and even. She moved one leg forward, hesitated, then shifted her weight again with visible effort – and curiosity.

Elynara pushed herself up, rubbing her eyes.

“You’re moving already?”

The machine turned her head toward the voice. The motion was careful, deliberate.

“Attempting balance,” she said, the metallic undertone soft but clearer now. “Sensory calibration incomplete. Several servos… uncertain.”

“Translation: your knees are new,” Elynara said, grinning. “Here, let me.”

She crossed the clearing and steadied the girl’s arm. The plating – or what used to be plating – was warm under her hand, not the chill of metal she expected, but warm and faintly alive, thrumming with energy.

The weight shifted against her shoulder as the guardian adjusted again.

“Better,” Elynara said. “Try smaller steps. Don’t rush.”

The girl’s first attempt ended with a stiff half-stumble that nearly took them both down. Elynara caught her, laughing despite the impact.

“Alright, maybe smaller than that.”

Starpuff swooped in to observe, chirping what Elynara swore was unhelpful commentary.

“Yes, I know she’s taller than me,” she said. “ And you don’t have to gloat about your flying advantage.”

The puff trilled a triumphant melody and spun in circles, scattering light motes before zipping off toward the ruined walls.

By the time the sun reached its peak, they had made some progress. The guardian could walk several steps unaided, though she moved with the careful focus of someone relearning how to exist.

Every gesture – every shift of the head or flex of the hand – was deliberate, studied.

“You’re putting too much pressure in the shoulder joint,” Elynara said, crouching beside her as she recalibrated the limb. “That makes your attempts fizzle out. Try lifting slower.”

The machine obeyed, raising her arm with new precision.

“Better,” Elynara said. “See? You’re learning fast.”

The girl tilted her head slightly, her expression unreadable.

“Learning is… pleasant.”

That simple word caught Elynara off guard. She paused, looking up.

“Pleasant?”

“Yes.” The guardian flexed her fingers, the faint light along her frame rippling in rhythm. “Each successful adjustment brings a sense of stability. It is… satisfying.”

Elynara smiled.

“You’re not wrong. It feels good to get things working.”

The days that followed took on a new rhythm. Training in the morning, scavenging by midday, recovery and testing in the evening.

With her newfound success in control over Fire Magic, Elynara turned her sights towards things she felt were close to ‘leveling up’ too, and which would be the most help were push come to shove.

And that was Mana Manipulation and her Water Magic.

Plus, obviously, continuing to expand her Mana Capacity. While this took a backseat while she was pouring most of her mana into helping the spirit and the machine, her continuous cycles of mana breathing, meditation, and gathering made it far easier to do capacity training now.

Even in detours there were hidden benefits, as it turned out.

But at the same time the entire situation proved to Elynara just how incredibly important having more mana was. Could she have helped the spirit in days, rather than weeks, if only she had enough?

Could she have made greater strides helping people if she didn’t need to be quite as frugal with mana use?

She did not know, but she also knew the past could not be helped. And that each venture made her better prepared for the next.

Which is why Elynara left other ideas be, for now. She could have learned more types of magic, perhaps. Or tried to learn the Magitech script – as she called the weird writing in her head now – or maybe discerned what was so special about Starpuff’s curious shapes.

But when the world needed more mana for everything, and every enemy could be worse than the last, she could not help but focus on what could give her the most ‘power’ now.

And so she did, putting her own curiosity aside.

After all, there was training to do now, and she would eventually find the time to learn.

The machine-spirit’s presence gave everything new purpose – the quiet hum of her energy joined Elynara’s breathing and Starpuff’s restless chatter into a shared routine.

Conversations came slowly at first. The girl spoke in short, careful phrases, her voice carrying a faint metallic echo that softened with each passing day. Initially following the old pre-programmed phrases from her days as the machine, yet flowing smoother and less rigid fast.

Elynara smiled at the progress. The taller girl was perhaps a little intimidating at times, but to her she felt like a little chick just learning how to move.

Cute and precious, in a weird way. Weird she did not care for, and the cute made her mood better.

The girl also thanked Elynara for everything – for each repaired limb, each new adjustment, each small act of patience. It was never rote gratitude. Every word came weighted with intention.

One night, while Elynara tended the fire, the guardian sat beside her, knees drawn close in a gesture that looked almost human.

The light from the flames painted the smooth edges of her form in shifting color.

“I have been considering my function,” the girl said. “Purpose.”

Elynara looked up from the pot she was stirring – another interesting thing was that the girl actually ate now. No longer just feeding off mana, though apparently able to subsist off it entirely, but also happy to eat any meal she provided.

“That’s a heavy topic for dinner.”

“It defines my existence,” the girl replied simply. “In all recorded memory, I was built to protect. My archives confirm this. My systems resonate most strongly with shielding directives.”

Elynara leaned back, smirking.

“So you’re still a guardian at heart.”

The girl inclined her head.

“It appears so. My structure has changed, but the command remains. Protection is purpose. It feels… right.”

Elynara wiped her hands on her trousers.

“And who are you planning to protect this time?”

The glow in the girl’s eyes brightened faintly.

“You.”

The answer came without hesitation, simple and certain.

Elynara blinked, caught between surprise and amusement.

“That’s – well, that’s very formal of you.”

“It is logical,” the girl said. “You restored me. You gave energy when I had none. You guided recovery. My systems recognize you as priority.”

“Priority?” Elynara laughed softly. “That’s one way to put it.”

The guardian’s gaze held steady.

“It is what I am. What I was made to do.”

Elynara shook her head, smiling despite herself. Then she stepped close to the sitting girl, gently cupped her face between her hands, and spoke with all the gravitas she could muster.

“Listen carefully, and this is important. You do not owe me anything. I helped you – helped you two – because I wanted to. Because it felt right. Because I could. I did not do it because I wanted a helper, or a guardian, or even because I was curious. Well, maybe a little.”

She paused for a moment, the girl staring at her, unblinking and – was she leaning her cheeks into her hands? Elynara shook the errant thought away, then continued.

“You are your own person. You decide what to do. I will be happy to have you around until you find that something. But you are you, and free to make your own decisions.”

The girl stared at her. Then smiled.

“Understood. I have… updated the priority.”

“Good!”

“You are now deemed the primary directive – protect at all cost.”

“Wha–Whu–Why?!”

The girl smiled a cheeky grin, catching taking hands in her own, and squeezing them lightly.

“Free to decide. Decided now. Is… directive upset?”

“Don’t… don’t call me that. And no, I’m not upset! But! But! Um. Please promise me you will think such things carefully next time? Pretty please?”

“Understood. This has been a carefully deduced decision.”

Elynara groaned at that.

“You’re impossible, you know that? Fine then! But you’re not my guardian, you’re my friend now!”

Did the girl’s eyes sparkle just now? Was that amusement?!

“Yes. Friend and guardian. Will protect friend well.”

“Ugh.”

Elynara considered her reply for a second.

“...I will do my best to protect you too then.”

“Friend already did.”

Immediate reply! There was no winning with the machine!

…Speaking of. And to distract herself from the awkward situation.

“...Maybe I should start calling you Shield or something.”

The girl tilted her head again, her expression neutral but curious.

“If that is what you prefer.”

“I was joking,” Elynara said quickly. “Names should mean more than that. We’ll find you something better later.”

The girl nodded, accepting the verdict without question, and finally letting Elynara’s hands go.

Silence settled between them, gentle and companionable. If a little awkward – or was Elynara the only one feeling awkward?

But as the night air carried the scent of ash and faint flowers from the edges of the ruin and nothing more happened, she relaxed.

Starpuff had also already curled up inside her satchel, a small glowing bundle of contentment.

Elynara leaned back, stretching her legs toward the fire. The warmth reached her boots, the glow tinting her hair in faint gold.

She watched the guardian out of the corner of her eye – how the light caught on the shifting lines of her new form, how the blue of her eyes reflected the stars.

“Guess we’re both rebuilding,” she said quietly.

The guardian turned, the movement fluid now.

“Rebuilding?”

Elynara met her gaze.

“You’re finding out who you are. I’m figuring out where I’m going. Feels similar to me.”

The machine studied her for a moment, then said softly,

“Then we are alike.”

Elynara smiled.

“Yeah. Maybe we are.”

The fire crackled between them, sending a small spray of sparks into the night. The hum of the guardian’s core joined the rhythm of it – steady, patient, and very much alive.

Elynara glanced toward where she knew the faint edge of the Deadzone glimmering pale laid beyond the ruins, beyond the trees.

Having a companion now, a guardian made her feel things. But most importantly she didn’t feel alone standing at the world’s edge anymore.

Beside her, the guardian’s voice came again, quiet but sure.

“You may decide my name.”

Elynara laughed under her breath.

“Careful what you offer. I might give you something ridiculous.”

“I trust your judgment,” the girl said simply.

Elynara grinned into the firelight.

“Now that’s dangerous.”

The guardian tilted her head in faint confusion, and Elynara’s laughter rose again, clear and bright against the sleeping ruin.

The sound carried through the clearing – small, alive, and full of promise.

The days that followed were peaceful in a way Elynara hadn’t known for what felt like forever. And might as well have been, if her old holey memories were any indication.

The ruins, once hollow and brittle with decay, had quieted. The sharp, wrong feeling that clung to everything here was gone.

Life – still not much, but enough to notice – was returning.

Each night they built a fire near the courtyard wall, its glow reflecting on metal and stone. The guardian sat close enough for warmth, though she claimed she didn’t need it.

Starpuff hovered nearby, floating lazy circles above the flames, flickering brighter whenever Elynara laughed.

For once, they weren’t training or scavenging. They were simply talking.

Elynara found herself hungry for the sound of another voice. She asked questions – more than she had intended – half out of curiosity, half to keep the silence at bay.

“What’s the first thing you remember?”

The girl thought a moment before answering.

“The sun on glass. Voices. The sound of moving water.”

Elynara smiled.

“That’s a good start. Where was that?”

“In a place built for growth. Gardens and open halls. I was assigned there to protect the workers and their families. And to care for this space. The others here were caretakers, gardeners. They took care of plants, I took care of them.”

“Assigned,” Elynara repeated, poking the fire with a stick. “So you really were made for that.”

The girl nodded.

“Protection was purpose. Still is.”

“Do you remember any of them? The people?”

The pause stretched long enough for Elynara to regret asking. Then the girl shook her head.

“Only movement. Laughter. Faces without detail. Their absence is recorded, but not their names.”

Elynara exhaled, soft.

“That sounds lonely.”

The guardian tilted her head.

“It is data without context. I do not understand loneliness the same way you do.”

“That’s fair,” Elynara said. “Still feels lonely to me.”

They sat quietly for a time, listening to the fire crackle. The night was cool but gentle, the air clean. Beyond the ruins, faint streaks of light marked where new growth shimmered – tiny mosses glowing in the dark.

When Elynara finally spoke again, her tone was lighter.

“Alright then, what about your name? We talked about it, but you've got one already, right? Something you used before?”

The guardian hesitated, head bowing slightly.

“My designation: A-R1S-0410.”

Elynara blinked.

“Your what?”

The words came precise and measured.

“A-R1S-0410. Unit Protector Class. Registered under the Aerthis Regional Defense...”

“Stop, stop,” Elynara said quickly, holding up a hand. “That’s not a name, that’s a number.”

The machine’s voice faltered.

“It is what I was given.”

“Yeah, and it’s awful,” Elynara said, grinning despite the ache that crept into her chest. “Sounds like a warehouse address.”

The girl tilted her head.

“It identifies me accurately. Or it did.”

“Sure, but it’s not you.” Elynara leaned forward, elbows on her knees. “You’re not a unit. You’re you.”

The guardian blinked once, the faint blue in her eyes dimming and brightening as if processing the words.

“Then what should I be?”

Elynara thought about it, tapping the stick against her boot.

“Something better. Something that fits who you are now.”

The girl looked uncertain.

“Names are… chosen?”

“Usually,” Elynara said, smiling. “Sometimes given, but always chosen in the end.”

Starpuff chimed softly, spinning a slow loop in the air. Elynara watched the glow trace lazy patterns above them and found herself smiling wider.

“See? Even Starpuff has a name. And it was picky about it too!”

The puff trilled proudly and dove into her hair, tangling itself until she laughed and shooed it away.

“Was that name assigned?” the guardian asked.

“No, I made it up.”

The girl studied her quietly.

“Then you could make one for me.”

Elynara paused, the words catching her off guard.

“You really trust me with that?”

“You repaired me,” the girl said simply. “You gave me purpose again. I trust your choice.”

Elynara’s mouth opened, then closed again. There were a hundred names that came to mind – half remembered from old memories, half shaped by instinct – but none seemed to fit.

“Hmm,” she said at last. “You don’t rush these things. Names stick.”

The girl nodded.

“Then we wait.”

Elynara smiled.

“Good answer.”

They lapsed into easy silence again. The fire burned low, the faint hum of the guardian’s core blending with the night wind. Elynara stretched out on her side, chin resting on her arm, watching the flicker of light play over metal and shadow.

“You know,” she said after a long while, “you’re not what I expected to find out here.”

The guardian turned her head slightly.

“You were seeking the ruins.”

“I was seeking proof,” Elynara said. “Of what was lost. Of what’s still possible. And to learn as much as possible. And now I’m sitting beside a spirit machine that laughs at my jokes. So I would say I found more than that.”

The faint sound of an amused hum came from the girl’s chest.

Elynara chuckled softly.

“See? That right there. That’s better than a number.”

“I am learning humor,” the girl said, tone measured but proud.

“Dangerous skill,” Elynara said, smiling into the firelight. “Use it wisely.”

Starpuff made another approving chirp, landing on Elynara’s knee. The puff’s glow dimmed as it settled, small and warm against the fabric.

Beyond them, the ruin’s walls stood quiet under the rising moons. The corruption that once clung to the edges had faded entirely, leaving only stillness and the faint, growing hum of life returning.

Elynara closed her eyes for a moment, breathing in the scent of ash and clean air.

“Tomorrow,” she murmured, “we will start mapping the east wing. There’s more to uncover.”

The guardian inclined her head.

“Understood.”

“Good,” Elynara said, already half-asleep. “And we’ll keep working on that name. You deserve a proper one.”

The girl looked down at her, light catching along the curve of her new shield arm.

“I will wait,” she said.

The fire cracked softly, the warmth making her sleepy. Her new friend’s presence made her feel safe. And as Starpuff’s glow dimmed further – she realized also content.

It was a pleasant night.

Morning came soft and golden through the cracked walls, and for once, the ruin felt less ancient. Elynara stretched beside the faint glow of the rekindled fire, warmth curling at her feet.

Across from her, the girl sat upright, perfectly still, waiting for the day to begin.

“You don’t sleep, do you?” Elynara asked, rubbing her eyes.

“I enter standby,” the guardian said. “It is similar.”

“Sounds boring.”

“It is efficient.”

Elynara groaned, pulling her cloak tighter.

“We’re going to have to work on your definition of fun.”

The girl tilted her head, eyes flickering faintly in the half-light.

“Define fun.”

“Exactly my point.”

Starpuff trilled awake above them, flaring a small burst of blue-white light before darting in a lazy circle. The puff bumped into Elynara’s hair and bounced away, humming happily.

She let out some mana puffs and shapes after it, soon to be gleefully devoured.

“See?” Elynara said. “That’s fun.”

The guardian watched, expression neutral but curious.

“I do not understand the purpose of this activity.”

“Not everything needs a purpose.”

The girl paused as if processing the concept.

“Then… it is recreation.”

“Sure,” Elynara said with a grin. “And speaking of recreation – we still need to find you a name.”

That earned the faintest flicker of light across the girl’s chestplate.

“A name.”

Elynara nodded, sitting up straighter.

“Can’t keep calling you ‘you.’ Or ‘hey, metal miracle.’”

The girl’s voice stayed calm, but her tone carried something faintly hesitant.

“My designation was A-R1S-0410.”

“Right,” Elynara said quickly, waving her hands. “Too long, too formal, too… serial. You need something that’s yours.”

They started tossing ideas between them while sorting supplies, the conversation flowing in easy rhythm.

Elynara tried the obvious first. “Aris,” she said, rolling the sound around her tongue.

“Short, sweet, close enough to what you were called before.”

The guardian frowned, a surprisingly human expression that made her metal cheeks shift faintly. “Too close. It feels like the old designation. Not me.”

“Fair.” Elynara tapped her chin. “Then Arisu?”

The head shook.

“Also too close.”

Elynara sighed dramatically and crossed her arms. At the same time her eyes sparkled with happiness. Just the other day the girl was okay with being called anything. But being given time to think, she now had her own preferences.

That was good. But she had to play that game to the end!

“You’re difficult, you know that?”

The girl blinked once.

“I am aware.”

That earned a laugh – bright, and unplanned.

The sound startled Elynara at first, then pulled her grin wide. It was the first time the guardian’s laugh had carried so much warmth, the blend of synthetic clarity and something human beneath it becoming smoother with each passing day.

“Alright, fine,” Elynara said, still smiling. “Let’s see what fits, yeah?”

She began listing names at random – some half-serious, some completely ridiculous. Starpuff joined in with bursts of trills that Elynara pretended were suggestions.

“Lyra?”

“No.”

“Veya?”

“No.”

“Jun?”

The girl tilted her head.

“Functional, but brief.”

Elynara snorted.

“It’s a name, not a command.”

Starpuff beeped twice in what could only be laughter. Elynara tossed a pebble at it, missing on purpose.

They went on through the afternoon, the rhythm light and easy. The girl tested each suggestion in a soft, mechanical echo, weighing how it felt in her mouth.

Sometimes she would shake her head immediately; sometimes she would pause long enough that Elynara almost hoped it had landed.

Nothing did.

“You know,” Elynara said after the fifteenth try, “for someone who said she doesn’t feel things the way I do, you sure are picky.”

“I am learning discernment,” the girl replied.

“That’s just a fancy word for stubborn.”

The faintest hum came from her core – a sound that had become Elynara’s clue for amusement.

They worked while they talked, gathering more salvage from the southern wing. The air there was cooler, and the walls were carved with old symbols half-swallowed by moss. Elynara brushed her fingers along one, tracing the grooves.

“You must have known what these meant once.”

“I knew their placement,” the girl said. “Their meaning was for others.”

“That’s changing now,” Elynara said quietly. “You get to decide what things mean.”

The girl tilted her head, considering that.

“Including my name?”

“Especially that.”

They returned to camp as the light began to fade, the sky a deep wash of violet. Elynara sat cross-legged near the fire, chewing on a strip of dried root while Starpuff drifted nearby, tiny sparks flickering from its glow.

The girl sat across from her, hands folded neatly on her lap, eyes reflecting the flame. Elynara studied her for a moment – how the firelight caught on her new plating, how the faint veins of energy beneath the surface pulsed slow and steady, like breath.

She tried again. “Ari?”

“No.”

“Ria?”

The girl shook her head.

Elynara made an exaggerated groan.

“At this rate, I’m calling you Tinny.”

“Unacceptable,” the girl said, perfectly serious.

Elynara snorted.

“You don’t even know what it means.”

“I know tone,” she replied. “It was not affectionate.”

Starpuff squealed in delight, spinning in fast circles.

“Fine,” Elynara said, laughing. “We’ll keep trying.”

She fell silent for a while, poking at the fire, letting the names tumble in her mind. The guardian watched her, eyes faintly brighter now, waiting.

Then something in Elynara’s memory sparked – the way the old designation had sounded, its shape beneath all that sterile formality. She turned it over on her tongue, softer, smaller, reshaped into something meant for a friend.

“Aru,” she said aloud.

The girl blinked, head tilting slightly. A pause – longer than the rest.

Elynara leaned forward.

“That one?”

The glow in the girl’s chest flared faintly, a pulse that almost looked like a heartbeat.

“It feels… right.”

Elynara smiled, slow and certain.

“Aru, then.” She drew the name again, shaping it with warmth. “Would you accept such a name from the unworthy little me?”

The guardian’s head lifted, the faintest curve touching her lips.

“Accepted.”

Starpuff chirped so loud that Elynara winced.

“Alright, alright, we’re all excited.”

Aru watched them both, her voice quiet but firm.

“I will remember it.”

“I’m counting on it,” Elynara said.

The fire cracked between them, sending up a spark that drifted into the night.

For a long while, none of them spoke. There was only the rhythm of the flame, the low hum of Aru’s living core, and the soft sound of Starpuff settling on Elynara’s shoulder.

Names, Elynara thought, had power.

Even if their meaning wasn’t big. Even if they didn’t feel too great. But so long as they made one smile and feel warmth… who was to say they weren’t important?

She tested the name on her tongue again.

Aru.

It was a good name indeed.
