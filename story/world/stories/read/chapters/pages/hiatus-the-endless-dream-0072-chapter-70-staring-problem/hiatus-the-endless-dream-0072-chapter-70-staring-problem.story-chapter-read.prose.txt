Moonlight - Dorian

Molding the Dream felt more like making a sand castle than making anything solid, like sculpting or carving. Whenever I thought of extending a corner or building a wall, it was like every idea about how I went about that started overlaying itself simultaneously in that space. The best way I could describe it is as a superposition of every possible configuration in my mind, and whenever I'd focus on a location, it would lock into existence in the Dream proper. With a small object or even a concept, this was easy as the scope was fairly limited, but for something large like a wall or building… suffice to say, my work wasn't very coherent at first. The newly conjured walls held a plethora of mismatching stone types, textures, and sizes of stones all next to each other, making the wall look more like a serial killer's newspaper clipping confession than anything else.

I could've let it be, but something about the mismatching stones kind of pissed me off just looking at them, and while I loved being petty, there was nothing to be petty over in this case—it'd just be me being a dick. It took the better part of an hour before I managed to get a stone wall built out of the same type of stone and another hour until all the stones had the same size, coloration, and placement distance. The key turned out to be solidifying portions of the wall a little bit at a time in a wave. I'd start with one brick and let that influence my perception of the wall around that portion, setting those surrounding stones in place and building out until the whole wall was finished. From there, ironing out the imperfections was just a matter of looking for anything that looked off and getting rid of it.

Most notably, I did it without using Presence, which, infuriatingly, was an accomplishment for me. Ananke had told me before that Presence solidifies, and it was true. Whenever I embedded anything with Presence, it was like the gaps in my own knowledge or imagination were fulfilled anyway. It was good for getting this done, but I couldn't be throwing Presence away every time I wanted to alter something; there was just too much waste for that to be feasible.

The turnaround for the completed barrack/fort took four hours, and by the time I was done, Joey and Maerin had come to find me, since it should almost be day back in our world. Between entering the Dream, exploring, and then our negotiations, time had passed rapidly. I didn't like it. Entering the Dream before had been non-stop. Something was happening; that much was obvious. There was no other explanation for so many Visitors in the area looking for me, but it didn't seem that dire. They were weak, and they didn't even know it was apparently my Instance they were looking for, and I might as well join them, because I didn't even know I had an Instance. It put me on edge. There had to be something larger happening I wasn't seeing, so when Maerin and Joey said we should be heading back, I said no.

"It's better if I stay in the Dream, one of us needs to," I said.

"Dorian, your parents will literally kill me," Joey sighed. "They barely accepted you sleeping at my place."

"I can wake up for a bit and check in then, but something is off here. I want to take Carlson and some others into an Instance and see who put them up to this whole thing."

Joey hesitated and started pacing, to which I only gritted my teeth.

"I don't think you understand that our world is literally being eaten, Joey. We have to be realistic, even if my parents did kill you it'd be a noble sacrifice." I tried for comedy. Even I could tell it landed flat as the others met me with blank stares. "Look, I know this is confusing, but helping these people and finding the others. They're just helpful distractions. If we can do it, great, but figuring out how to keep the fucking world intact takes precedent over my parents comfort here."

Joey paled, and I regretted putting it so bluntly immediately. He was already having a shit day. Why did I have to keep reminding him that everything was shit? It made me a little frustrated with him, too. If he wanted to be blissfully ignorant, he shouldn't have agreed to be a Visitor. If he wanted to feel like the world wasn't falling apart, he could do that, just not where it stopped me from doing what I needed to do. I took the part of me that thought that and stomped it down as hard as I could.

He was my friend. He was there to support me. The frustration is worry. Be worried, not frustrated. I took a deep breath, re-centering myself. "Look, this is a good lead. I still need you and Maerin to look for the others in our group. Maerin can show you the ropes of an Instance. How much Presence do you have?" I asked.

"Ten," he replied.

I didn't let the hiss of worry touch my lips. That should've been the first thing I asked. If we had driven into an Instance by accident and he didn't meet the win condition, the Dream would've taken him. I let static take my vision and held my hand out. He hesitated for a moment before shaking. I transferred 100 over to him.

"What about Boons and Restrictions?" I continued.

"None."

I paused, trying not to let worry creep into my tone, "Items?"

"None."

"Was Ananke- The System told you about the tutorial?"

"No."

Shit. He had nothing. Like, actually nothing. Worse than what I had. I didn't have enough Presence to upgrade my title and copy a boon, which really only left one option. "All the more reason for me to stay today. I need to collect some Boons and Restrictions for you. They're real life savers."

He nodded slowly, "You'll wake up and call your parents?"

I gave him a thumbs up, "Ye."

Maerin stood to the side, arms crossed. As the conversation wound down, she finally said her piece. "I'll stay too. There's no reason for me to go back."

Joey hesitated. Maerin frowned and gestured for him to continue, "That's not true. Maerin has-"he closed his eyes and sighed, "My girlfriend's parents live in town. They're used to her being gone a few days, but you— she usually checks in with them in person."

Maerin said nothing, just shifted uncomfortably from side to side. "I… I'm not doing that today. I need time to mentally prepare for something like that. More the reason for me to stay in the Dream for now."

An awkward silence descended on our group, only broken when I sucked my teeth and let out a huff of air. "Well, on that note. I'll wake up for a few minutes. Should be able to put myself back to sleep easy enough. Maerin, see if you can find an Instance nearby. I'll be back soon."

"No." Joey forced out. His eye flicked from side to side and then between Maerin and I before Joey said in a lower, calmer voice. "I think it's important we decompress in the real world."

My heart dropped slightly as saliva thickened in my throat. I could pick up on a hint that was obvious, and from Maerin's expression, she could too.

"Okay," she agreed instantly. "I don't like it, but I think that's fair." Her voice sounded appeasing, but it was a thinly veiled thing.

I nodded, even if it was nothing, I already felt something was off. Better safe than sorry.

Forcing all of us awake was a lot more pleasant than dying and waking up. I just grabbed both of their shoulders and imagined us falling asleep. It didn't work, so I spent a point of Presence to fill in that gap, and we were back in the real world again. Joey rubbed sleep from his eyes, but I knew he didn't need to. Coming into or out of the Dream got rid of any lingering exhaustion I felt, at least physically. If it helped at all mentally, I certainly didn't feel it.

Maerin was the first to speak, "What was that about?" she asked, more curious and unnerved than anything else.

"Something's off with those people," Joey said.

I jumped at the opening, "How?"

"I dunno man. Just weird. They were fine whenever I talked to any of them or was just nearby, but as soon as I walked away they got this blank expression and became aimless. I noticed it first at the car and later when I separated from Maerin to explore."

I began pacing. It helped me think. Hivemind? Could it be Ananke controlling all of them? That was weird, sure, but what did it mean?

"That's not all. It wasn't just me. It's the same for Minerva and Barrow. Probably with Carlson too."

That made me freeze in my tracks, "What?" I asked.

"Yeah, it's like there's a bubble around us, and when anyone is close to one of us they're normal, but if they're out of that bubble. It's like— It's like they're not even there. Is this normal? If this is a false alarm, I'm sorry, but that Dream place is creepy if everyone's like that."

Maerin and I shared a glance.

"No," she said, "that certainly is not normal."
