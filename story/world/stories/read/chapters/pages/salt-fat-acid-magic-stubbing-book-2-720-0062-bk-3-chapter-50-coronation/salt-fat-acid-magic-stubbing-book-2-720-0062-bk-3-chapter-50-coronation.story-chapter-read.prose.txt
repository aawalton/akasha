There was no grand jacket celebration that year. Waldorf had occupied too much time during the feast to do it then, and his Gluttons had emptied the Academy’s pantries too much to throw another. Instead, Aubergine invited the students to come to the great hall for breakfast at their own pace and gave them their color-changing concoction then.

Archie slept on the lounge’s balcony, having laid out on its couch so that his crying wouldn’t keep his roommates up. He didn’t remember falling asleep. He must have passed out mid-sob. He woke up emotionless, as if his spirit had fallen asleep and never woken up. He would have been thankful for that, but gratitude required emotion. He noticed Blanche had slept on the couch just inside the lounge, but he thought nothing of it. He walked outside and up the slope to the great hall. Birds chirped and played in the early morning light like they always did, but he thought nothing of it. When he reached the top of the slope, he looked out at the forest. He knew his father’s body was out there, but he thought nothing of it.

He found a couple of eggs and cooked them. He burned the edges well beyond his liking, but he thought nothing of it. He ate out in the great hall and sat with his dirty plate for however long it took for the other students to wake up and for Aubergine to finish his morning jog.

Nori was the first one to approach him. “Hey.”

“Hey.”

She looked at him. He looked nowhere.

“You ate?”

“Yeah.”

“Did you get enough?”

“Yeah.”

“Alright. Let me clean this up and I’ll come back to sit with you.”

Archie didn’t respond. He didn’t think. Eventually, Nori returned and sat in silence with him.

“I’m going back to Sain tomorrow.”

“That’s good.” Nori sucked in her bottom lip. “Do you want me to come with you?”

Archie didn’t want anything, so he didn’t respond. She could come with him or she could stay. He would think nothing of it.

A few other students from their class filed in, but no one sat next to them. Rumblings and rumors of the night’s events spread like wildfire, and no one wanted to get close enough to see the ashes.

Julienne sat with them with the same shell-shocked expression as Archie.

“How’s Yarrow?” Nori asked.

Julienne shook his head once. His words came out sloppy, his newly regenerated tongue rolling around his lips. “Anise is still working on him.”

“Does your tongue hurt?”

He shook his head again. “It’s just weird.”

“Did you eat?”

“Not hungry.”

The three sat in silence. Julienne stared at the door. Nori stared at the table for the most part, looking up every so often at Archie, who stared at nothing.

And then Oliver walked up. Wooden splints ran across his bandaged left arm and left leg. He dumped his crutches against the table. “Can you guys believe I went twenty years of my life without breaking a bone, and then I managed to break twenty in one night?”

Nori frowned. “You broke twenty bones?”

“Well…I think I broke like six, but that’s not as poetic, is it?” Oliver grinned and plopped down on the bench. It took him several seconds to get his splinted leg over the bench and under the table. “Everyone doing okay?”

Nori shrugged. Julienne grunted. Archie didn’t do anything at all.

“Yeah. Me, too. I was supposed to go to Kuutsu Nuna next week. Looks like I’m waiting another year. Cress’ll be devastated.” Oliver rubbed his eyes and looked at the others. He nodded as if coming to an understanding that he might have gotten off the easiest, but that didn’t stop him from being Oliver. “I asked Cress if maybe I could ride one of the Kuutsu instead of walking. She said if Akando heard me say that, he’d break my other leg. I think I might load up on moondrop wine and let Cress take me along like a balloon.”

No one laughed. Oliver scoffed and leaned back to stare up at the ceiling.

Aubergine approached with four cups of green sludge. “Might I interest you bunch in becoming Green Jackets?”

He received an even colder reception than Oliver did.

Aubergine set the cups down. “Well, I’m glad to have the four of you here. About your…adventure.” He leaned in and spoke quietly. “You know that Waldorf isn’t stupid. He knows Tataki was there to kill him. And he knows you weren’t there to stop Tataki.”

Oliver drank his green sludge and groaned and coughed and made horrible faces and washed it down with water. As his jacket shimmered from yellow to green, Nori drank her cup with a similarly strong reaction. Julienne drank his, but instead of reacting with disgust, he looked at it with confusion. Archie drank his. It tasted awful, but he didn’t care to wash it down. He stared ahead, missing the once-in-a-lifetime greenification of his jacket.

Aubergine took a deep breath. “It took some convincing, but…he’s okay with the one story. A rogue agent trying to assassinate him—”

“He’s going to blame all of Uroko for it,” Nori interrupted.

Aubergine frowned. “That remains to be seen. One day at a time, Nori. One day at a time. He’s okay with the one story. But two stories? An assassination attempt and an attempted jailbreak? And he’d rather have his little private kitchen stay a secret. So, after some discussion, we’ve arrived at a compromise. You won’t be punished. The story is as it was initially reported. You were bringing food to his chambers and fought off an assassin. The truth of it is, you probably saved Waldorf’s life.”

Oliver groaned.

“So we’re running with that truth. But that means…appearances. Specifically, this afternoon. The coronation. You’ll all be part of the ceremony.”

Oliver groaned again. “Oh, no…”

Nori and Julienne joined him in complaint. Archie thought nothing of it.

Aubergine continued. “You’ll receive medals. It’ll be…political. The young, promising Chefs who support the reign of the new grand king. Who fought to protect him.”

Their groans filled the great hall, drawing attention. “Oh, you can’t be serious!” Oliver said.

Aubergine cut them off before they could protest further. His voice compressed into a hissing, authoritative voice that he rarely used. “You don’t want to know what Waldorf’s first idea was. You should be thankful.”

“I won’t do it,” Julienne stated.

Nori and Oliver nearly gasped. Archie didn’t think anything of it.

“Julienne,” Aubergine scolded.

“I refuse,” Julienne said. His voice had no emotion. No opinion. It was like the word of a god. Once it was spoken, it became the truth. “If my name is associated with his reign, let it be associated with protest.”

Aubergine laughed uneasily. “Well, Julienne, that goes against the story…”

“Leave my name out of it.” Julienne nodded to the others. “Let it be their story.”

“Julienne…” Aubergine had fully shed his loveable camp counselor persona. This Aubergine was an adult, and this adult was worried. “You don’t want to make an enemy of him…”

“He can’t do anything to me,” Julienne said, still calm and collected. “If a Julienne goes missing, the world will notice. And Labruscella is protected.”

“Perhaps you should talk to your uncle…”

“He controls Cafe Julienne, he doesn’t control me. And when I control Cafe Julienne, people like Waldorf won’t be served.”

Oliver whistled as Aubergine shook his head and walked away. “Well, I’m gonna go to the infirmary and see if I can’t get a more flexible splint.” He started the arduous process of getting his leg back over the bench.

Nori hopped up to help. “The whole point of a splint is to not be flexible.”

“It’s a magical world, Nori. Surely we can do better than this.”

“Need my help?”

Oliver blew a raspberry. “Well, I wouldn’t say no to it. These doors are heavier than a Glutton.”

Nori gave him his crutches and led him away. “Can’t make jokes like that anymore.”

And that just left Julienne, who stared at the table, and Archie, who stared at nothing. After a while, Julienne dug his fingers into the corner of his cup to eat more of the foul-tasting sludge. He worked his finger around the inside of his mouth and finally broke their silence with a mumbling confession. “I can’t taste anything.”

In an emotionless thought, Archie realized that Julienne was done. A Chef that couldn’t taste couldn’t cook. If he could, he would have felt bad for his friend. But he didn’t. Instead, he thought nothing more of it.

The trumpets and flowers gave the impression of a celebration. Children ran in the crowded streets. Chefs abandoned their restaurants in favor of pop-up food stands. Every street vendor in the city set to work pushing their products on the full crowds.

A line of guards separated the rowdy streets from the outer courtyard of the great stone cathedral where the most important members of society sat in rows of cushioned seats.

Archie, Nori, Oliver, and Yarrow followed the chancellor’s instructions as he ushered them to the front row. The old man seemed happier than anyone. Once a loyal servant of Flambé, the chancellor had adjusted quickly to Waldorf’s ways. He had put on drastic weight and couldn’t go a few minutes without grabbing a candy from his pocket.

“Did you hear?” the chancellor asked as he ushered their group to the front row. “I’m to be made Archbishop of the Culinary Arts. It’s a new position. On top of overseeing all five major academies, I’ll also be serving as spiritual advisor to our new grand king.”

“I thought the academies operated outside of the crown,” Nori said.

“Hm? Oh, yes.” The chancellor looked as if he had made a silly mistake. “The responsibilities are separate, of course. It is only the appointment that is the same.”

“So how are you meant to keep the two separate?” Oliver asked.

The chancellor muttered something under his breath and pushed them to their seats. Nori and Oliver grumbled back and forth about the imminent demise of society, but Archie thought nothing of it. He sat next to Yarrow, whose arms had been wrapped from fingertip to elbow and coated with a sweet-smelling ointment. Yarrow caught Archie’s gaze and shot him a dirty look, but Archie thought nothing of it.

“I’m surprised you came,” Oliver whispered to Yarrow. The crowd had taken on the atmosphere of a sermon, reducing everyone to hushed voices. “You’re in worse shape than me. Waldorf would understand.”

“Grand King Waldorf,” Yarrow corrected. “You will use his proper title. And I won’t miss my chance to advance myself.”

Oliver raised his eyebrows and turned back to Nori. “Not grand king yet, is he?”

Nori didn’t respond. Instead, she just watched Archie, who watched nothing.

A familiar voice came from behind, a woman who had not picked up on the general quiet that the seated crowd had agreed to. “I know, can you believe it? I’ll be teaching at the Academy next year.”

Archie turned to place the voice and discovered that Morita, the Glutton that had overseen their tributes, sat just two rows behind him talking to another Glutton.

“Oh, Morita! That’s fantastic. Have they told you what subject?”

“Well, you know, I prefer eating food to making food.” They both laughed. “So I’ll be putting my talents to good use and teaching them the most important thing for a Chef to know—how to satisfy a customer.”

Something stirred deep inside Archie.

“They’ve been a bit stiff,” Madame Morina complained. “The Head Chefs.” Her voice dripped with contempt. “They protested my appointment. Wouldn’t let me take on the title of Head Chef. So I’ll be Madame Morina.”

“Well I think that suits you! So…regal.”

“Yes. Well, I can’t be a Chef because I don’t have one of those silly jackets. But I don’t need a jacket to be a headmaster. Once that fool Aubergine is gone, I think Wally will appoint me.”

The Gluttons had already grown bolder, and they would clearly only get worse after the coronation. Archie thought someone should do something about it.

He’d have to be quick. Quicker than his slingshot. Something hidden. A noodle. Small, sharp, and hard. He pooled essence in his hand.

The trumpeters on the balcony of the cathedral burst into song, making Archie jump. The chancellor took to the podium. Beside him, a massive throne, larger than any that had ever existed, had been placed on top of a raised platform. An impractically ornate crown sat on its red velvet cushion.

“All rise,” the chancellor commanded.

Cellos joined the trumpets as the crowd rose to their feet. They turned to watch as eight men walked down the aisle carrying a royal palanquin the size of a small room. The wooden poles bit into their shoulders. The sight sickened Archie. All the withheld emotion of the day flooded through his body.

Looking back from the front, Archie got a good look at the other guests. Gluttonous royalty looked with adoration. The old council, Flambé’s men, half the size it had once been and soon to be obsolete, looked with worry. Some Chefs’ faces carried contemptuous looks. Others saw a payday.

The guards set the palanquin down and opened the double doors to let Waldorf out. As he stood up straight, the trumpets blared and the crowd applauded. Despite his size, he managed the stairs up to the throne and stood in front of it, waiting for his moment.

The chancellor turned to the crowd. The trumpets and crowd quieted. “Today we crown a new ruler. “Waldorf Puttick, first of his name, son of Grand King Flambé and Grand Queen Crosnee.”

Archie looked around for Grand Queen Crosnee. Apparently transporting her hadn’t been worth the trouble. Or maybe Prince Waldorf just didn’t want her condition to be made any more public. His wife was also missing from the crowd. Archie thought of her laying there in bed, half-dead, and conjured a sharp noodle that fit into the palm of his hand. It’d have to be strong. Strong enough to puncture. And to twist.

“First, a few words…” The chancellor adjusted his collar as he prepared to go into his rehearsed speech.

“No,” Waldorf said, just loud enough for Archie to hear. “On with it. He’s been dead for days now. Give me the crown already.”

The chancellor did his best to not look surprised and retrieved the crown with his tail tucked between his legs. He walked up to Waldorf and projected his voice for all to hear. “In the name of Ambrosia, I give you your new ruler. All hail! Grand King Waldorf!”

As he placed the crown on Grand King Waldorf’s head, the loyalists of the crowd echoed out the cry.

“All hail! Grand King Waldorf!”

The trumpets burst into a new song. Cheers spread like a wave through the crowd, echoed applause coming from half a mile away as the news spread. Tens of thousands of people celebrated the man that Archie considered the harbinger of doom. The music made it out to be a celebration, but Archie thought it sounded more like a funeral.

He wondered if these people knew the kind of man they applauded. If they would ever know, or if they would simply become more like him as his rot spread through society. Waldorf represented a turning point of what was acceptable. If a leader could be a Glutton, so could you. If a leader could be coarse and rude, so could you. If a leader decided that his own interests were above others, so could you. If a leader could be a monster, so could you.

“Thank you, thank you,” Waldorf said, grinning from ear to ear. He popped something into his mouth and swallowed. It amplified his regular speaking voice to the volume of a yell. “Oh, it is so wonderful to feel your love. I could devour it!”

He laughed. The trumpets stopped, but the people stayed standing.

“I would like to take this moment, my first moment as Grand King, to say a word about my father. I loved my father deeply. And he loved me deeply. We had a true bond. Anyone will tell you. Everything he knew, I knew. Everything he believed in, he instilled in me. He was a great man, and I’ll follow in his footsteps.”

His smile faded. His voice conveyed his disappointment.

“That being said, I believe that our great empire has faltered in recent years. Not for any fault of my father’s. It was the fault of the people he surrounded himself with. For that is the true ability of a king—the people that surround him.”

He offered a soft smile.

“Now, I never was a Chef, don’t hold it against me. But in my royal education, I learned about their practices. Farmer practices. Did you know that when you only grow one crop over a long period of time, you can deprive the soil of its nutrients? It’s necessary to change what you plant for the long-term health of the field. Crop rotation, it’s called.”

He paused. Adjusted his crown. Looked at the crowd. Then he spoke again with fervor, his voice swelling to a yell.

“Well, I see our empire as a field. And the same crops have had their roots in our great field for too long!”

The crowd split into groups of somber silence and rapturous applause. Grand King Waldorf rode the cheers, his impassioned voice ringing out for all to hear.

“But I need you! Let us pluck these crops that ruin us! Let us tear out these weeds that have spread their roots deep into our government! Let us plant new seeds and bring new life to our field! Let us sprout and grow!”

More and more of the crowd joined in the cheers.

“For our field to flourish, I must not become complacent! I must recognize the blessings that we have been given. Chefs. Chefs are our blessing. My father gave them autonomy. He let them use their abilities for their own gains. Their own profit. I say we put them to work in our shared field so that we may all eat!”

The crowd roared. Archie had seen how Waldorf would chip away at the Academy from within. Now he saw the plan on how to use public pressure to weasel his way into more power. Someone had to do something. He had to do something. Tataki had tried to do something, but Archie couldn’t think of him right now.

“Some Chefs are greedy. Selfish. They will oppose how I plan to bring prosperity to the common man. Just the other day, a Urokan made an attempt on my life.”

A Urokan. Not Tataki. Not a name. Grand King Waldorf couldn’t vilify an entire people if he used a name.

Gasps came from the crowd. Rumors of that night had spread quickly, but now it was confirmed. The Urokans in the crowd received dirty looks from their fellow citizens.

“But do not fear! For there are Chefs that have already seen my cause. They have protected me. They have joined me. Today, I will share my ceremony to honor them!”

The chancellor waved the students up to the stage as the crowd applauded. Archie remained seated for a moment. Just a moment. Just long enough to finalize the conjuration that he hid in a clenched fist.

The chancellor lined the students up on the steps below Waldorf. He held a display with four medals, whispering to remind Waldorf of the recipient’s names as they stepped up.

“Oliver Durtnell!” Waldorf announced as he lowered a medal around Oliver’s neck.

Oliver turned to the cheering crowd and offered an awkward nod. Archie sharpened the noodle in his hand until it cut into his first layer of skin.

“Yarrow Callahan!”

“Thank you, Your Royal Highness.”

Yarrow received his medal and turned, keeping his bandaged gloves out of sight. He bowed, soaking up the attention.

Archie tried to remember what Yarrow’s last name was—it certainly wasn’t Callahan. He chalked it up to yet another attempt by Yarrow to distance his name from his Khalyan roots. What a coward. Archie focused on two spots at once, sharpening the noodle in his hand while preparing to activate a sugar rush in his arm. He’d have to be fast. And strong.

“Nori Harper!”

The crowd cheered as Grand King Waldorf placed the medal around her neck. But then she turned around. Murmurs of “Urokan” replaced cheers as the applause died down to a pathetic smattering of claps. Archie grew his noodle to a dagger that took both hands to cover.

He stepped in front of Waldorf. He needed to pick his moment. Between the Glutton’s height and his position on a higher step, his face hovered three feet above Archie’s. Waldorf would have to bend down. That was the moment. His massive hands scooped up the final medal from the display. He opened its ribbon. He bent down.

Archie converted all of his essence into power.

Or at least, he tried.

As Waldorf’s hands descended to his shoulders, all of Archie’s conjured essence was sucked away. The more essence he poured forward, the more Waldorf took. The Glutton smirked. He knew what Archie wanted to do. He knew that Archie was powerless.

“Archie Kent!”

Archie locked his bewildered eyes with Waldorf’s proud ones. The Glutton’s massive hands turned Archie to the crowd. Whatever applause that had been withheld from Nori was added to Archie’s regular applause. Thousands and thousands of people cheered for him. Cheered for protecting their new king. The applause didn’t fill Archie with pride. It emptied him of it. It was nothing like his dreams.

Waldorf leaned down toward Archie while the people cheered, whispering in his ear. “I had the strangest sense of deja vu the other night. A Kent breaking out of my kitchen. It all felt so familiar. Would you know anything about that?”

Archie felt more than his essence leave his body. He felt his fight. His willpower. Everything that Archie could do, every aspiration, every possible future, was devoured by Waldorf.

The Glutton straightened up and spoke to the crowd.

“These four represent the future of Chefs! Loyal to their liege! Remember their names and let us watch their futures closely!”

Archie felt eyes on him. He peeked back over his shoulder.

Grand King Waldorf was staring at him.

“I know I will.”
