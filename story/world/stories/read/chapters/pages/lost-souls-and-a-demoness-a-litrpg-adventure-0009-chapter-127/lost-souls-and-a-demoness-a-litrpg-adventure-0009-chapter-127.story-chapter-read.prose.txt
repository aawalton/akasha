The city of Illysport was like no urban environment Jade had ever seen. Why someone had decided to build a major settlement on such a small island was beyond her. The obvious problem of a lack of space was readily apparent mere moments after entering the congested, maze-like streets. Buildings were quite literally stacked atop each other two or three structures high, and the ground level streets were covered by a multitude of crisscrossing bridges and walkways that formed elevated avenues hanging above the city and connecting the upper buildings. It was a chaotic mess that was so poorly laid out, it almost came all the way back around to making sense.

Almost.

"Are you sure this is the right way?" Jade asked, peering through the darkening gloom at a corner sign listing street names. "I'm not seeing it."

"Elmwood Boulevard… There!" Naomi pointed, indicating the lowest branch of the sign. The lettering was so faded, the Labyrinth's translation magic could barely interpret it.

"Ugh. Who designed this place." Jade complained, joining Luis and following Naomi. Boulevard was generous. The narrow street was little wider than an alleyway. "We've been looking for at least an hour."

"I don't think anyone did… And that's the problem."

Jade stepped to the side to allow a small group to pass them, having to practically flatten herself against the wooden wall of a building to make room for them. It was another guard patrol, the fifth they'd encountered in the past half hour. How many did they have in the city? They were almost more numerous than the actual citizens.

Is it because word of the demons is spreading?

Rain continued to fall, the air filled with the pitter patter of raindrops on tiled roofs and cobblestone streets. Jade was about to give voice to her musing, but Luis spoke first.

"Think we'll see Aylin? This is where she's from, yeah?"

"Yeah, and I hope so. I want to talk to her about the demons."

"Do you think she'd help if we asked?" Naomi said, inspecting the signs of each door as they started moving again.

"I don't think we could stop her from getting involved if we wanted to. You've seen how driven she is."

And how much she doesn't like demons…

The half-elven mage had helped their progression immensely on the second floor and even accompanied them into the stronghold threatening Lysara. Still, Jade couldn't forget how they'd nearly come into conflict when they first met.

"There it is! We're here!" Naomi said in relief, excitedly pointing ahead. A small sign hung from one of the taller buildings just ahead of them, reading "Sunken Treasure Inn". "Finally, we can get out of this rain."

Jade led the way inside, opening the door into a tavern that would've looked right at home in one of the fantasy RPGs she and Alan had played together. A fellian man stood behind a relatively empty bar, cleaning a line of mugs with a wet cloth. He glanced up as they entered, and Jade noticed the man's eyes flicked across their wayfarer marks before meeting hers.

"Welcome to the Sunken Treasure inn!" The fellian greeted them warmly, holding out in arms in a welcoming gesture. "I'm Ryl. Would you like a room for the night, or just a meal while you wait out the weather?"

The large, cat-like ears twitched atop his ahead, and Jade got the sense that their wayfarer marks were taken as a sign of wealth. No doubt the innkeeper was eager to host those he knew had money.

"We're looking for some friends. A few, actually." Jade said, leaning against the bar but not taking a seat. There were a few other patrons in the tavern, all humans and fellians, but none paid their group any heed as they clustered at the bar.

"Fenrin, Tara, and Siora." Naomi supplied. "An elf, half-elf, and-"

"Oh, are you taking the tek'kalla off my hands?" Ryl asked eagerly, fishing beneath the counter and producing a small key. "Here you go. Room twelve, up on the second floor. See that stairway? Yes, just up there!"

Jade accepted the key hesitantly, exchanging confused glances with Luis and Naomi.

"Ahh, shouldn't you check with them before giving this to us?" Luis asked.

"I'm sure it's fine. They're your friends, right?"

"What did she do?" Jade sighed, pocketing the key.

"No, no, let's just ask them." Naomi said, sliding a few coins across the bar to the very relieved looking fellian. "Sorry for the trouble."

"What makes you sure there was trouble?"

Jade and Naomi just looked at Luis, not saying anything.

"I… yeah, okay." He grinned, giving them an exaggerated shrug. "Let's go see what we missed!"

The door to room twelve opened just in time for a billowing cloud of smoke to pour into the hallway. Jade coughed, fanning the air in front of her and stepping through the threshold into the somewhat clearer air inside. A familiar figure stood before her, slender, with violet skin and stark white hair streaked with strands of rose. Wisps of fire curled around Tara's arm, sputtering out as she held her arm palm towards the door.

"Tara!" Jade coughed, blinking away tears from stinging eyes. "What was that for?"

"Oh! Jade! Umm… sorry about that." The mage stepped back, cradling her right arm against her chest. Her cheeks grew a little brighter. "I thought you were a demon assassin."

"What kind of assassin would knock before opening the door?"

"The clever kind?" Tara sounded like she was trying to convince herself as much as Jade.

"She's got a point." Luis said, stepping into the room behind Jade. "No one would expect assassins to be polite."

Tara's eyes brightened as the three of them stepped inside.

"I… I'm glad you’re back." She said, shuffling her feet awkwardly. "It's good to see you again."

The tek'kalla held out her hand, surprising Jade. Luis took it before she could, but instead of shaking it, the large man pulled her into a hug.

"Good to see you too, amiga! Glad you're okay. Where's Fenrin?"

"Oh! I… he…" Tara stammered, the scent of her mingled embarrassment and joy causing Jade to smile. "In the other room."

As if summoned by her words, the other door in the double bedroom opened, revealing Fenrin.

"Tara, are you terrorizing the maids ag-" He cut off, his face lighting up as his eyes fell on their group. "Jade! Naomi, Luis! You're back!"

Now it was Jade's turn to be swept into a hug. Caught off guard, Jade stiffened for a moment before relaxing, giving her friend a quick squeeze and a pat on the back before they let each other go. As more greetings were exchanged, Jade was surprised by the closeness shared by their group. Tara had been with them for less than two weeks, and yet the reunion felt far deeper than that. Bonds formed from shared triumph over dangerous adversity grew more quickly than she'd realized, and there was a comfort to being back with the people who had passed through that crucible with her.

"Wait, your arm!" Jade exclaimed, eyes wide with astonishment. "Fenrin!"

"Oh, yeah!" The half-elf held up his new, unblemished arm for them all to see. "I have Tara to thank for that. She got a healer from her clan to travel here to help me."

"I sent word to the Taskforce through the embassy here." Tara explained, clearly pleased with herself. "The Expeditionary Taskforce has medical units available for dispatch. I just told them I was the one who needed help."

"You didn't have to do that. That healer looked pretty unhappy when she left."

"Of course I did." Tara crossed her arms, blowing a strand of hair out of her face. "You're part of the team. We can't have you combat ineffective, especially not in a crisis."

"Will you get in trouble for that?" Naomi asked.

"Who cares. My clan isn't taking news of the demon invasion seriously enough. I sent everything we learned, all my notes, and our quest details about the fourth-floor rituals. All of that, and the only thing they're willing to do is send some scout fliers to sweep the local quadrants for demon activity. If they won't help, it's up to me to see this through."

"Did the people here respond better?" Jade asked, concerned.

"A little." Fenrin held up a hand, twisting it from side to side. "The duke has pledged to provide material support to Earth wayfarers clearing out the third and fourth floor cores, and local guilds are sending out scouts to identify threats. But they aren't sending the garrison out. If anything, they've pulled them back to fortify the city.

"There were a lot of guards at the gate." Jade recalled. They'd all been scanned by various magical devices upon entering town, and she was grateful her upgraded shapeshifter ability shielded her true form from detection. "So, basically, they're leaving the job of dealing with the demons to us while they fortify themselves?"

"Pretty much." Fenrin sighed. "I should've expected this. Even if they wanted to help more directly, Earth wayfarers are the only ones who can freely enter corespaces. If the rest of the demons are holed up inside them, supplying you is the best they can do."

"Couldn't they assign a couple of super high-level wayfarers to join our parties and clear the cores with us?" Luis asked.

"If only." Tara shook her head. "The Labyrinth responds poorly when anyone too over levelled enters a corespace. It'd make things harder, not easier."

"So, back to the original plan." Jade said, crossing her arms and sitting on the corner of one of the small beds. "We deal with this ourselves.""Not ourselves. There should be other Earth wayfarers here." Naomi reminded them. "We need to explain the situation to them as well. I'm sure some of them will help, taking down the cores is why they're here to begin with."

"I've heard a few Earth wayfarers are staying on the other side of town. Maybe we could head there tomorrow?"

They all nodded. It was as good a start as Jade could think of. As Fenrin spoke, she couldn't stop herself from glancing at the empty perch on his shoulder where Mirella had always stood. At first glance, the half-elf looked and sounded as calm and certain as always. But, looking more closely, Jade could see the loss in his eyes.

"Jade?" Naomi asked, sitting beside her. "You okay?"

"Huh?"

It took her a moment to realize that her hands were clenched into fists, her nails digging so deeply into her skin that a thin trickle of blood was dripping between her fingers onto the thin bedsheet.

"Oh, yeah. Sorry." She relaxed, noticing a set of alchemy tools on the small desk in the corner of the room. "Where's Siora?"

"She ran over to the market to grab a few supplies before they close." Tara said, organizing a pile of scrolls and spell books that covered half of one of the beds, trying to tidy up a little.

"She's out there now?" Naomi peered through a small, dark window that looked out into the street. "It's pouring out there!"

"You know how she gets about her alchemy." Fenrin laughed.

"I like that about her." Tara huffed. "Having passion for your vocation is important."

"I'd prefer if your passion didn't lead you to almost blowing me up when I knock on your door." Jade said with a smirk. The expression faded as she noted how gingerly the tek'kalla was moving her casting arm, and how she favored her off hand. "But really… how are you?"

"Oh, this?" Tara held up her arm for them to see. She was wearing something akin to a tight fit t-shirt with three quarter sleeves. She rolled them up, revealing light skin marred by a web of blackened and charred lines burned into her flesh. Jade hissed through her teeth, and Naomi took a startled step back.

"It looks worse than it is. Just… still stings a little when I push too much mana through it."

"Tara…"

"I'm fine, Jade." The tek'kalla's grin was genuine, if tired. "A fair price for wiping out a demon army. And you have to admit, that explosion was-"

Jade surprised them both by standing and pulling the other woman in for a hug.

"I don't think I ever thanked you properly. Either of you." She said, giving her a squeeze and looking at Fenrin. "We wouldn't have made it out of that fight without both of you. Or your sacrifices."

"…Thanks for trusting me to help." The tek'kalla replied softly. "No one else would give me the chance."

"We're a team now." Fenrin nodded. "An odd team, maybe, but a good one."

"Who are you calling odd, amigo. I'm perfectly normal."

"Uh huh. Exactly, Luis, you're the normal one." Naomi giggled.

"So… you're okay with me staying?"

"Who else could we trust to make our problems explode?"

"What Jade means is-" Naomi cut in, rolling her eyes. "We'd love to have you, Tara. You're definitely one of us."

"And so is Siora." Fenrin added, stepping into his room and pulling on a coat. "She's been working even harder ever since we told her what happened. Picked up some new spell books and plans on coming with us.""Really? Is she high enough level?" Jade asked.

"You can ask her yourself. It's about time I go make sure she hasn't gotten lost in the back of shop somewhere. Did you get a room?"

"Not yet. The owner seemed to think we'd be getting her out of his hair." Jade thumbed towards Tara, who rolled her eyes.

"All I did was spend one evening practicing in the yard behind the inn. How was I supposed to know the walls were flammable?"

Everyone paused to stare at her.

"Tara, this whole town is made out of wood." Luis said slowly. "It's known for that, at least where we come from."

"Here too." Fenrin stage whispered.

"I thought it was enchanted! Or at least treated! All the wood on the city ships I grew up on was. Who makes buildings that can catch fire so easily?"

"You burned down a building?!" Naomi asked, horrified.

"Of course not! I put it out." Tara sniffed. "I'm not reckless."

"Anyway…" Fenrin opened the door to the hallway with a shake of his head. "Let's get you some rooms, you three look like you could use the rest.""You don't know the half of it." Jade muttered. The journey across the lake had taken more out of her than most monster fights, and her stomach still wasn't quite recovered.

The innkeeper had them sorted away in short order, assigning them a multi-room suite across from the one Siora, Fenrin, and Tara were bunking in. Before long they were back upstairs getting settled in. Jade had just finished changing into something dry when the door flew open and an excited Siora flew into the room, wrapping her in an embrace and nearly sending them both toppling onto the floor.

"Good to see you too, Siora." Jade groaned, rubbing at where her shoulder had banged against the wall.

"Oh, sorry." The copper-skinned elf blushed, giving Jade some space. "I was just excited to see you all back and safe! Fenrin and Tara told all about what happened with the core." She finished more softly. "I wish I could have helped."

"You've done plenty for us. You made it here without any trouble?"

"That's right!" Siora perked up. "And I've made progress."Jade's heart skipped a beat, and she nearly couldn't bring herself to ask the next question.

"Really? With…"

"With a potion for your brother." Siora nodded. "I finalized an ingredient list, and I'm only missing two. And the best part is, I know where to find them."

"Is there a catch?"

"Ahh… that depends. How much money did you get in that corespace?"
