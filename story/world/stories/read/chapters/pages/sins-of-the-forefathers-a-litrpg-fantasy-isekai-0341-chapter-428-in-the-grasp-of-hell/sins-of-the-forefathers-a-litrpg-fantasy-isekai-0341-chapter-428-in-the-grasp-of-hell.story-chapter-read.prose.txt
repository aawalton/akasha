It was time for plan B.

Plan A had been to quietly make our way through Rhoscara while matters were still tense, and Anguis was setting matters up.

That was gone, now, in a literal fiery conflagration. The flames now engulfing Rhoscara were thick and hot, creating a dense smoke cloud in the air above the city. So much so that it almost formed a semblance of night, curling up to hang like a pall of darkness over Rhoscara. It descended on the world for the first time in months, only adding to the sheer chaos as the only light now available came from the fire.

Plan B was for us to fight our way to the expedition.

No more subtlety.

No more planning.

But plenty more rescues along the way.

I almost wanted to call Anguis arrogant in the extreme, in the way that he had never relieved me of all my weaponry and equipment while I was sitting in that cell.

But it was sure as Hell coming in useful now.

Terractus in my right hand, remaining dagger in my left, we fought our way through the burning streets of Rhoscara. There was an unspeakable cacophony all around us as two dozen members of the expedition fought our way forward. Screams from the panicked civilians of Rhoscara, the roar of the flames as they hungrily devoured the ancient city of ravens, the endless march of mailed feet through the cobblestones, and the laughter of Savoy soldiers as they carried out their grim, heartless harvest.

Not all of them were mind-controlled by he who held the reins of the collective Dwarven soul. Not every member of the Velancian armed forces did this because they were being quite literally compelled to do so. Those who were, it was easy to tell from their blank-faced look of non-comprehension as they robotically cut down those who had been their countrymen only months prior.

Those whom they had sworn to defend.

I did my best to dispatch those with efficiency. They were essentially trapped within their bodies, unable to stop themselves as they murdered the innocent under the orders of a maddwarf. They didn’t deserve my ire.

But they still had to die nonetheless.

Some, though, carried out their orders with glee.

Over the cries of the betrayed and dying, their laughter rang like the cries of hollering demons. Those, I showed absolutely no mercy towards. Let them taste the astonishing cruelty of the Capstone Skill I had gained only hours previous, on the eve of Anguis’s arrival.

For as much as I despised the flames that licked at the homes of Rhoscara, they were the perfect environment for it.

A sneer on my lips, I flipped Terractus into a back-handed grip, and thrust out my closed right fist in the direction of one Savoy soldier. This was one of the monsters, someone who seemed to derive far too much pleasure from the flames he was helping stoke higher and higher. He was busy fighting Walter, my Squire’s claymore having difficulty weaving through the defenses of the soldier’s quicker cutlass. For all of his effort and all of his improvements, Walter was still inexperienced.

I’d deal with this.

Grasp of Calithorne.

The malicious, bloody tendrils of the Capstone Skill for Thornblade Acolyte almost seemed to shiver in pleasure within my soul. They wanted this fool’s blood, and I would give it to them.

Behind the Dwarven sadist, a patch of the flames he seemed to love so dearly changed from their normal fiery orange…

To a deep, malevolent crimson.

From those flames sprang a length of familiar blood red thorns, the hallmark of my Class, only this time they glowed with a faint, hungry scarlet light, the same shade of the fire that had birthed it. The long, sinuous length of thorned vine speared forward toward the Savoy soldier, completely under my control in a way I had never experienced with neither Skill nor Spell. Every movement, every facet of the thorned vine, from the length of it to even the size of the thorns themselves, was under my influence.

The speed, the strength, the durability, the flexibility…

There was nothing I couldn’t influence about these creations.

And so, I directed it to wrap around the mailed neck of the Dwarven soldier and tighten. The Dwarf choked and tried to grasp at the vine around his neck, as the thorns speared through his armor and dug into his neck. He even dropped his blade in his sudden panic, as Walter froze in his surprise.

That…probably would have been enough, honestly. I could have killed this Dwarf, right then and there, simply by tightening the vine until his neck snapped.

But that wasn't enough.

No…

I had long since decided I needed to make examples out of people like this. Behavior like that of this sadistic bastard couldn’t be tolerated. Those who reveled in the suffering of others, who lorded their System borne might over the weak and helpless, needed to become tales. A story to spread, a story to fear, a cautionary story of outright punishment.

And so, I reach out my other hand holding my dagger, and called.

I had found I could create far more than just one length of thorn, after all.

Four more patches of flame turned crimson, and four more identical vines snaked their way out of the fires. They attached themselves to the other limbs of the fool, calves and forearms both. With a gesture, the gurgling, screaming Dwarf rose in the air, suspended by his neck to hang nearly a story off the ground…

And then all five thorned vines pulled tight.

This horrible, monstrous day may have been the first time I’d been capable of experimenting with Grasp of Calithorne…

But I was finding that they possessed a far greater strength than what I did at my strongest. Not even my full, transformed state, buoyed by Might of the Wyrdwood, had a grip mightier than this Skill.

Against that…

Joints were nothing.

The Dwarf’s screams were cut short when all five of his extremities were ripped from their place on his body. Arms, legs, and head tore from his armored torso in a downright obscene explosion of blood.

In the sudden ensuing silence that fell over the street we were fighting on, the sound of the very dead soldier’s limp falling down to the cobblestones was clearly audible.

Splat. Splat. Splat. Splat. Splat.

One after the other. Like particularly heavy raindrops, falling onto the surface.

Those Savoy soldiers who weren’t brainwashed by Anguis immediately broke and ran.

I didn’t blame them. I wanted them to spread the story of what happened to those who abused their power in this manner.

Less optimal were the Rhoscaran civilians who ran from the scene of the battle, screaming hysterically at the top of their lungs as they fled. It was a good thing that this group had already been told where they were to actually evacuate to, after we had stopped the Savoy from slaughtering them. Every Rhoscaran that we rescued was told to find one of the underground entrances to the Long Road, where Bleddyn had apparently been gathering his forces, and seek out the Unchained.

Was it reckless of us to direct Dwarves that could be mind-controlled at the drop of a hat by Anguis, to the base of our allies?

Yes. But what else were we supposed to do? I wasn’t willing to just let these people die.

Besides.

I got the feeling that Anguis’s control wasn’t as omnipotent as he wanted me to believe. Yes, he had absolute control over whoever he got his hooks into.

But the range…

There was no way he could exert an influence on an area of more than a few blocks at a time. Otherwise, why bother with having his soldiers go door to door to root out and slaughter the people of Rhoscara? He could have just instructed all of them to immediately kill themselves if he could just instantly grasp the mind of whoever he wanted, all over the city.

No.

There was still a chance to stop this. All we needed to do was kill Anguis.

But that was for later. For now…

I had to deal with the looks I was getting from my own men. Some ranged from outright horror, all the way to apathy. In particular…

Sylvia didn’t care. My Sculpted lover could, at times, be remarkably callous when it came to the gore of combat. Neither did Fade, honestly. He was a wolf, in the end, and the nature of a wolf was one of blood and death. If anything, he probably thought I was being a little bit dramatic and silly. But he wouldn’t condemn me. In the same way, Bella had been a Pirate Captain for a very long time now. She had been party to more than one slaughter in her time, and not always of the variety I approved of.

I remembered our old talks.

But Maria, Renauld, and strangely…Meia and Kierla?

I was getting more than one disapproving look, in one vein or the other. Some of those surprised me.

Maria, I could understand. The former spy didn’t look like she was happy, but she did look like she understood my tactics. Renauld was blatantly looking away, and that, I also understood. He was a Healer, first and foremost, and didn’t care to see suffering. Even if it was the suffering of an enemy.

But Kierla? Meia?

Odd.

Kierla just looked frightened, while Meia? The Orcs were a society of bloodletters. The look of near disgust on her face was shocking to me.

But one bothered me more than most.

Walter was staring at me as if he had never actually seen me before. It wasn’t quite horror, and it wasn’t quite condemnation. But it wasn’t approval either, on his now blood-soaked face.

He had been in the…splash zone, so to speak.

I…could deal with this later. I would deal with this later.

I rolled my shoulders, set my face into a grim frown, and jerked my head down the street. “Let’s go!” I barked, causing more than one of the expedition members to jump to attention. “We’re not done.”

As we got moving once more, I…couldn’t find it in me to be sorry for the brutality of my actions. Nor did I intend to stop doing it. If word of what happened to people who brutalized others, who took joy in the suffering of the innocent, got out and saved a single life, who might otherwise have been stolen…

It would all be worth it.

………………………………

I didn’t refrain from using my new Capstone Skill, as our squad advanced through the streets of Rhoscara, fighting for every inch as we did so.

I didn’t need to.

Somehow, someway…

It didn’t have a Mana cost.

That was shocking to me when I first realized it. As I had been growing in levels, I had found that the stronger an ability was, the more likely it was to need an input of power from me. Higher tiers of Mystical effects from the System seemed to need a tithe of Mana or Ki in order to function.

But not, it seemed, a Capstone Skill. Or at least not mine.

The result was that any flaming surface I encountered could grow an inhumanly strong, thorned vine at any moment and deal with my enemies.

That was the true ability of Grasp of Calithorne. It was a strange limitation, I had found, about the Skill named after the fourth planet of the Tarus system. The one that was the inspiration for, and the apparent location of, the Gyreite version of Hell.

I needed fire for it to work. I think…I think it might have something to do with my secondary Celestial affinity. Oftentimes, it manifested itself as a type of flame. Blue, almost heavenly tinted flames, yes. Very different from the standard fire that I could manifest the vines from. But fire, nonetheless.

It made me wonder just what the Skill would have been like if I had never received the affinity from Grey during my first Ascension Ritual. Would the thorns have grown from the soil beneath me? That would have been annoying and way less tactically flexible.

This was much stronger. After all, there was rarely a situation in which I wasn’t around some form of flame. A fireplace, an oven, a lamp, or even a match. I think I could manifest the vines from any kind of fire, from the feel of the Skill.

I should probably learn some Evocation, just for an easy-to-access fire Spell.

It was a monumentally powerful Skill. Not just in single combat. But in group battle as well. I could essentially control the flow of battle through as many vines as I needed, as long as fire existed. And as long as my physical stamina, and not my Mana, could hold out.

This would change my entire fighting style. Which, I suppose, a Capstone Skill was supposed to do in the first place.

Appropriate.

It…really only had one limitation I had found in my experimentation.

Eye contact.

I couldn’t manifest, or even control, a vine from a fire that didn’t exist within my line of sight. I didn’t need to maintain direct eye contact with the fire. But if I didn’t see it even out of the corner of my eye, I couldn’t create them.

Still.

I used it to deadly effect, as we fought towards the expedition, nestled deep in the heart of the Rhoscaran tanners district. Only...when we actually reached it?

We found something that caused my heart to drop.

The Ashen Bride…

Had been reduced to its namesake.
