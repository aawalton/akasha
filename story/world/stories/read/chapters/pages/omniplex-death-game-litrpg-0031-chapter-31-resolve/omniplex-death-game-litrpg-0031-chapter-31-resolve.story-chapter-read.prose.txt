Ciro looked at the creature. It showed no resistance. It allowed the ethereal chains he cast to coil around its deformed body, tightening every second. The beast hadn’t even stifled a groan under the pressure. As if all sense, all comprehension, all understanding had been seized. The creature had shown primal instinct previously, but all of it was replaced by dullness, a lack of will or hunger.

A predator no longer.

An array of thoughts burst through Ciro’s mind, a waterfall of theories, as he examined the weapon in his hand. He peered at the creatures, its lifeless eyes stared at him, tears still flowing down its face. Its gray skin and stature mixed with its almost humanoid head gave Ciro a sickening realization.

“A Xyphon…” Ciro whispered under his breath. What was the probability? Ciro could’ve been wrong, but he felt it in his heart.

This man, an alien—whatever you wanted to call him — was once a person. He had somehow turned into this creature. Ciro didn’t know what to make of it all.

“Yo Ciro, I’m loving those magic chains and all, but you gonna use that revolver or nah?” Birk’s voice called out. A look of unease written on his face, along with his brother.

Snapped out of his reverie, Ciro shifted his attention towards the twins. “Yeah, I’m just thinking.” He looked down to the ground to see Luka passed out form, drooling as he slept harmlessly with no worry about the world around him.

Ciro pointed to his body. “You two drag him away, further from the predator, else it might attack again.”

The twins nodded in response, going to each side and dragging Luka away from the captured creature and Ciro.

Now alone, Ciro took a deep breath, his grip on the gun tightening. It was a weapon not too dissimilar to something you would find on Earth. Nothing about it screamed otherworldly. He tried ignoring the sensation of the cold metal resting in his palm, the contours of the grip perfectly designed for Ciro’s grip.

He tapped the weapon against his thigh. He braced himself, taking a couple of paces towards the monster. He was a father, Ciro thought, as he edged closer to it. Only a few feet apart, Ciro crouched down slightly, facing its head that lolled down to the ground.

“Hello. Are you there?” Ciro asked softly.

No reply.

The creature seemed to be a husk. The only sign of life being the tears that dripped down its face. Ciro clicked his tongue. He grumbled before moving even closer. He took a minute to decide what to do. Ciro waved his hand past its face to no response, even shaking its shoulder did nothing.

This damn event turned him into this. Is anything even left of him?

Ciro didn’t know why he said it. The name slid through his mouth with ease. His voice but a whisper that should’ve never been heard.

“Nizin…”

Ciro had only whispered the sponsor’s name and, like a volcano, the creature came alive. The chains rattled as it shot up its head. A voice called out, nothing monstrous or animalistic in its tone. Only clarity.

“Nizin, my baby. Where is she? I miss my baby.” His voice sounded desperate, full of melancholy. That made Ciro’s pity grow. The man was still alive, his conscience was still there.

Screw this game. Ciro didn’t want to kill him, not when this man was forced to do the things he did. It didn’t seem fair at all.

He grabbed the creature’s shoulder. “Nizin is your daughter, right? You’re a Xyphon?” Ciro couldn’t help but feel uneasy talking to him.

“Yes—yes…my baby girl,” he wept. The man’s eyes met with Ciro’s. They no longer seemed like the primal irises that Ciro had grown to fear, becoming a silver color. “I tried to kill you…I remember.” The man’s actions came back to him in real time, recognizing Ciro as a victim of his transformation.

“What happened to you?” asked Ciro, his finger still hovering over the trigger.

His voice strained under the pressure of the chains. “Upon the event beginning, I gained a stigmata—that’s what turned me into this.” The man squinted his eyes, trying to remember the past. “I’m sorry.”

“Listen, it’s okay. We can maybe try and help you. If we keep you locked up till the event—”

Ciro couldn’t even finish his sentence when the man’s body convulsed. His body jerked under the chains, saliva frothing around his mouth. He let out a pained scream that left Ciro taking a few steps back.

“What’s going on?” Ciro’s eyes widened, each of his hands became slicked with sweat. The chain trembled in response, the faint purple glow emanating from it flickered like a candle.

The man’s eyes bulged, shifting back to the unerring yellow. His neck twisted at an unnatural angle, jaw clenched, biting at the air towards Ciro. It had returned to being a beast. At least that’s what Ciro thought.

“Do it. Shoot me.” He forced the words out amid the panting and growls, fighting back whatever was possessing him.

Ciro lifted the gun towards the man. He hesitated, this was different to killing those Krovalith or even the Xyphon he had inadvertently killed. This was an act of murder. He knew that. This wasn’t going to be an accident. He was going to kill a sentient being. One that had a child. A father to someone out there. Perhaps he even had a lover. And Ciro had to end his life.

He didn’t want to do it. Carina’s words flashed through his mind. Those words of accusation.

“You’re a murderer.”

Why did those words hurt him so much? It made him want to scream. He wasn’t a murderer. He didn’t kill people. To kill someone was going against humanity. Every life was precious. The man in front of him didn’t deserve to die. Not by his hands.

The alien’s teeth snapped towards Ciro, but the chains withheld him. Ciro held the pistol, his hand unsteady, as he aimed towards the father. Why does his daughter want me to kill him? His mind racked with an excuse not to shoot. If it was the other guy, he’d have shot him, without so much as a question. He wasn’t like him.

The chains reacted to his hesitation, feeling lighter in his hand. Ciro furrowed his brow, his eyes following the trail of chains. The purple glow had dimmed and small cracks appeared in each link. His breath caught in his throat as he saw each link scatter and evaporate, as if they were mere illusions.

The alien’s injuries hadn’t healed. Dents generated from Luka’s punches littered its body. Every limb had deep ridges in the shape of Ciro’s chains. Not phased, he rocked towards Ciro, his broken arms flailing like clawed whips. Its eyes gleamed with another chance at killing.

Bang!

The creature ceased all actions, eyes shaking with surprise. Looking downwards towards its chest, blood poured down his chest.

Ciro had fired.

The alien met Ciro’s eyes, once again reverting to the silvery irises, eyes that reminded him of the moon; his real eyes. The predator within him had all been expunged. Looking at him was just another man, alien maybe, but a father—something Ciro could respect.

“Thank…you,” he whispered, collapsing onto the ground. Never to see his family again.

A sense of relief washed over Ciro, knowing he had survived. Yet unease gnawed at him relentlessly. He had broken his own moral code. He moved over to the corpse of the Xyphon. Kneeling down beside his body, he clasped his hands in prayer.

“Please forgive me,” he begged. He sat there in silence, crouched beside the corpse. Desperate footsteps headed towards him. He looked over his shoulder to see the twins looking between Ciro and the corpse.

“We finally killed it,” said Birk quietly, his voice trembling in awe.

Ivo pushed past his brother, standing over Ciro and the dead body. He loomed over the creature that had disfigured his face, combing over every section of its body. A long moment passed as both men stood in silence. Breaking the stillness, Ivo leaned down and made a sucking sound with his mouth.

Gathering all the saliva in his mouth, he cocked back his head and spat.

The ball of saliva landed just below its eye, merging with the dirt and blood that caked its skin.

“That’s what you get. You ain’t so tough now, huh? Bitch.” His voice was calm, as his fingers trailed his face once again. He turned to Ciro. “Thanks for killing him. I’m too weak to do it myself.”

A pang of guilt washed over Ciro. He didn’t want to kill it. Luka and Ivo were both heavily injured by it. And he hesitated. He had no right to receive gratitude.

Birk came over towards the two of them. “Look at Ciro man. So silent and stoic. This is why you’re the second in command.”

Ivo frowned at his brother. “What? Ciro isn’t the second in command. I mean, yeah, he got the last hit, but the boss did all the work.”

“So then who’s second in command.?”

“I am,” Ivo lifted his head up high, puffing his chest. “My scars make me look like a badass.”

“You look ugly, lil bro.”

Ivo bit his tongue, pointing a finger at Birk. “Did you just call me lil bro?” He grabbed him by the shoulders, throwing him onto the ground, putting him into a chokehold. “I’m the older brother. Show some respect.”

“By…by a couple of minutes,” Birk wheezed out.

Ciro didn’t even register their rambling, too focused on the corpse of the alien he had killed. The twins ceased their idiocy. Ciro found himself surrounded by them both.

“What’s the matter, big bro Ciro?” Birk asked, leaning towards him.

“Oh, so you call him big bro, but not your actual big bro.” Ivo scowled, leaning into Ciro on his other side. “But seriously, we should go.” He pointed over his shoulder. “The boss probably needs some healing, too.”

Ciro let out a sigh. The twins glanced at each other before leaning in closer. Knowing they wouldn’t leave him alone, Ciro decided to say something. He lifted the pistol for the twins to see. “I didn’t want to shoot him.”

“So what?” The twins both replied at the same time.

Ivo raised an eyebrow. “You called it—a him?”

Ciro pointed at the corpse, taking a deep breath. “This might sound crazy, but that predator.” Ciro spat out his words.

“It was a person.”

Birk peered at the corpse more closely. “Like it was human?”

“No. A Xyphon.”

“What the hell is that?” They both asked.

“You know those gray aliens that Luka talked about. That’s their names. They're called Xyphons.”

Ivo let out a scoff, kicking the corpse’s head. “So what? It’s an alien. You killed it. We won. What’s the problem?”

Ciro stood up, clenching his fist. “He didn’t want to kill us!” he said, not realizing how loud he spoke. Both twins stared at him with astonishment. He looked at the corpse. “it was forced to kill us.”

The twins shrugged their shoulders, wiping the dust off their suits. Ivo grabbed Ciro by the shoulder. “If you didn’t kill him. He would have killed us. It doesn’t matter if it wanted to kill us or not.”

“Yeah,” Birk nodded in agreement. “If there’s one thing I’ve learned out here is we’re sitting ducks. We literally have no control. You killed an alien big deal. It’s not like it was human.”

Ciro bit back a retort. Bit back the point that this creature was a father. He had someone he wanted to see again. Unlike himself, who was drifting aimlessly, with no true memory of his past. I don’t even remember what my parents were like.

The twins walked away, leaving Ciro behind. “Hurry up, we need to get moving. Another predator could be on us soon,” they called out to him, swatting their air, motioning for him to follow them.

Ciro watched them move towards Luka. He couldn’t help but glance once again at the corpse. He bent down beside it once again, closing his eyes. They're right, I have no relation with this Xyphon. If he died to Luka, I wouldn’t have cared. But this isn’t fair. Ciro’s indecisiveness shattered, only replaced by a realization that was in front of him the whole time.

Palou. This was all his fault. The arbiter, the so-called ruler of these events, the manager of this planet. He caused this all to happen, turning that Xyphon into a monster. None of this was of Ciro’s doing.

He was at the mercy of an invisible hand.

Ciro stood up, a fierce determination welling inside of him. He looked around him, every corner and even the sky. Lifting both his middle fingers up, he flipped off every angle he could think of. I’ll survive this and then I’ll make sure to get revenge for you playing with people’s lives.

Picking up the knife he had used, he wiped it clean. Placing it back in his pocket, he staggered towards the three men waiting for him.

From this day forth, I will not be toyed with.
