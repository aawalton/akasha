Well, Z and Greg had been right, because there really were emergency stairs. Chloe stared down the concrete stairwell in front of them, blinking as she tried to take in just how ridiculously ordinary it looked compared to the rest of Divinity Inc.'s facility. The staircase stretched downward, each flight separated by small landings and enclosed by thick gray walls. Metal railings ran along both sides, while a series of glowing emergency signs pointed the way down, their green lettering standing out against the otherwise dull surroundings. There wasn't anything particularly impressive about it. Just stairs, lots and lots of stairs.

Chloe leaned over the railing and looked down, only to regret it when she realized the staircase seemed to disappear into darkness several floors below. "Wow... they really weren't kidding about the emergency stairs."

She straightened and looked toward Z. "But wait, why does Divinity Inc. even have emergency stairs? I mean, isn't this place basically full of angels?"

Z glanced at her. "Yes."

"And angels can fly, right?"

"Yes, that is obvious."

"So… Why do we need stairs again?"

"Chloe, have you ever asked why we used to have cars even though everybody in the divine realm can fly?"

"Well, um… because cars are cool! But stairs are lame, so I don't get it."

Z paused for a moment, as though considering how best to explain something that seemed perfectly obvious to her. "Anyway, flying inside Divinity Inc. facilities is technically prohibited under corporate protocol."

Chloe stared at her.  "That's... really dumb."

"Well, you will find that there are considerably more rules like that in almost any corporate environment."

"Even rules that don't make any sense?"

"Especially those."

Chloe considered that for a moment before slowly nodding. "Huh. I guess that's why Mom always looked so tired after work."

Z smiled. "Perhaps."

Greg apparently didn't see much point in standing around discussing the stairs any longer, because he shrugged and started toward the entrance. "Well, whatever. Let's just go."

Liz and Percy followed into the stairwell after him, and Chloe watched them for a moment before giving a small shrug of her own. If Greg wasn't worried, then there probably wasn't much reason for her to worry either, so she stepped onto the first flight of stairs and began making her way downward with Z, Raenof, and Sierra following behind.

At first, Chloe had expected the journey to be much more difficult. They had spent all that time talking about how dangerous the lower levels were, how many security forces might be waiting for them, and how there were apparently two whole Administrators somewhere between them and Mom, but so far, there hadn't been a single robot, soldier, trap, or even an angry employee telling them that they weren't allowed to use the emergency staircase.

But for the most part, it was just stairs. Chloe continued down another flight, resting one hand on the railing as she glanced over the side. The staircase seemed to wind endlessly into the depths of Tartarus, but despite how far they still had to go, the journey itself was turning out to be surprisingly uneventful.

"Wow," Chloe said, looking ahead as they continued downward. "This whole going down to the lowest floor of Tartarus thing is turning out a lot easier than I thought."

Raenof, who was a few steps behind her, immediately looked up. "Chloe."

"What?"

"Do not say things like that."

"Why? You think I'm going to jinx us?"

"Yes, and I should remind you that it has happened before."

"What? When?"

Raenof thought for a few seconds, but couldn't come up with anything. So instead, he shrugged. "Can't remember off the top of my head, but seriously, be careful."

She glanced around the perfectly ordinary stairwell, which looked exactly the same as it had a moment ago, with nothing more threatening than a flickering fluorescent light above them. "Really? I mean, what could possibly—"

Before Chloe could finish, the entire stairwell lurched violently beneath their feet. The sudden movement threw everyone off balance, and Chloe instinctively grabbed the railing as the concrete staircase shook hard enough to make the metal supports groan. Behind her, Sierra let out a startled cry while Z caught hold of the railing, and farther down the stairs Greg reached out to steady Liz as the whole structure continued to tremble around them.

Dust began drifting from the ceiling as the emergency lights flickered overhead, and Chloe could feel the vibration running through the railing and into her hands. The shaking became so intense that she had to press herself against the wall to keep from being thrown down the stairs.

"Okay! Okay! I take it back!" Chloe shouted.

After several long seconds, the shaking finally stopped, leaving everyone clinging to whatever they had managed to grab. Chloe slowly looked over her shoulder and found Raenof staring at her with a smug expression.  "I told you so."

Chloe lowered her head. "Sorry."

Liz, still gripping the railing, looked around the damaged stairwell with growing concern. "Can someone explain what's going on?"

Greg glanced upward and then toward the flights of stairs below them. "Dunno, but hold on!"

Chloe barely had time to wonder what he meant before something far stranger happened. A thin, jagged line suddenly appeared in the air several meters ahead of them, almost like a crack spreading across an invisible pane of glass. Chloe stared at it in confusion, but before she could even process what she was seeing, another fracture appeared beside it, followed by several more as the cracks rapidly spread across the stairwell.

The walls began to distort around them as though the entire structure had been reflected in a piece of glass that someone was slowly twisting, and the staircase itself seemed to bend at impossible angles while the fluorescent lights stretched into long, warped streaks across Chloe's vision. Through some of the growing fractures, she caught brief glimpses of something that wasn't part of the stairwell at all, a vast darkness filled with strange, indistinct shapes. Chloe might not get what was going on exactly, but this looked a lot like reality breaking apart somehow.

Chloe's fingers tightened around the railing as the fractures continued spreading across the walls, ceiling, stairs, and even the empty space between them, until it felt as though the entire world around them was being pulled apart.

The fractures had already made things hard enough to understand, but whatever was happening only grew more chaotic as crackling energy began spilling through the broken spaces. Bright arcs of electricity crawled along the edges of the fractured reality, snapping from one piece of the stairwell to another with enough force to make the air smell sharp and metallic, while sections of the staircase seemed to lose their connection to one another and drift apart as though the entire structure had been shattered and was now floating in some enormous invisible void. Chloe couldn't even tell which way was up anymore. The walls kept twisting around her, pieces of concrete hanging at strange angles while flashes of white-blue energy illuminated everything for brief moments before plunging it back into the distorted darkness. She tried to find Greg, then Z, then Raenof, but every time she thought she had figured out where someone was, another fracture would spread across her vision and make the entire stairwell seem to shift.

Then Chloe heard Sierra. Chloe turned toward the sound and immediately spotted her several sections away, standing on a chunk of staircase that had somehow remained intact despite everything happening around it. Sierra looked terrified, her ears pressed flat against her head as she gripped the railing with both hands and stared helplessly at the widening gaps around her.

"Sierra!"

Chloe pushed herself away from the railing and jumped across the widening gap toward the section of stairs where Sierra was standing, barely managing to clear the distance as a jagged arc of crackling energy snapped through the space she'd occupied only a moment earlier. The lightning struck the railing behind her with a violent flash, sending sparks scattering across the stairs, but Chloe landed hard on the other platform and immediately grabbed hold of the railing to steady herself.

She looked at Sierra, who was staring at her in shock. "Are you okay?"

Sierra swallowed nervously before giving a small nod, although the tremble in her voice made it obvious how frightened she really was. "I-I think so..."

"Okay, good."

Chloe breathed out in relief, then finally took a moment to look around and figure out where everyone else had ended up. Z and Raenof were together on another section of the staircase, with Raenof bracing himself against the wall while Z carefully watched the fractures spreading around them. A little farther away, Percy, Liz, and Greg were still together on the same platform, although even that section was beginning to separate from the rest of the stairwell.

Everyone was still there. At least, for the moment. The problem was that the stairwell no longer seemed interested in keeping them together. More and more cracks tore through the space between the different sections, and every new fracture sent another burst of crackling energy racing through the gaps. Pieces of the staircase slowly drifted farther apart, leaving widening stretches of darkness between them, while occasional arcs of lightning jumped from one floating section to another and forced everyone to keep moving or ducking to avoid being struck.

A few minutes earlier.

Ishgria had always dreamt of stepping down from her role to then live in peace with her daughter. She knew that Divinity Inc. was far from the most ethical company, but in her time, she always tried to make it a better place. As painful and slow as that path was, now though... she would have to face the employees of the company she once served in. A company that looked very different under its current leadership.

She stood alone in the center of a wide metallic platform, her shoes making a quiet sound against the smooth surface as she shifted her weight and surveyed the enormous chamber around her. The platform stretched several dozen meters in every direction, suspended above what appeared to be an endless void.

Ishgria paid little attention to any of it. Her attention was focused entirely on the two figures standing several meters ahead of her. She recognized them as Administrators immediately, although neither was anyone she remembered from her time at Divinity Inc.

The first was a tall man dressed entirely in black, from the heavy coat that concealed most of his body to the gloves covering his hands. His face was hidden behind an unsettling mask shaped like the head of a plague doctor, complete with a long, curved beak extending from the front and dark lenses that revealed nothing of the eyes behind them.

Beside him stood a woman dressed almost entirely in red. Her clothing was considerably more elaborate, with dark crimson material layered over what appeared to be some kind of armored suit, but it was her mask that immediately drew Ishgria's attention. It resembled the face of a demon, its exaggerated features twisted into what looked like a permanent scowl, with sharp contours framing the eyes and a pair of curved horns rising from the top.

Ishgria studied them carefully, resisting the temptation to let their appearances influence her judgment. Divinity Inc. had always possessed a particular fondness for presentation, and she had learned long ago that the appearance someone chose for themselves rarely told her anything useful about what they were actually capable of. Still, she couldn't help noticing how different these two looked from the people who had once worked beneath her. During her time as CEO, she had known most of the company's senior personnel by name. These two were strangers to her, and neither seemed like they were eager to pay their respects to a former CEO.

That meant they belonged to a Divinity Inc. she no longer recognized. She slowly exhaled and straightened her posture, allowing her expression to settle into one of confidence.

Whatever these two intended to do, she would not give them the satisfaction of seeing her intimidated. After all, she had spent years dealing with difficult people in difficult rooms. Two masked Administrators were hardly going to change that.

"So," Ishgria said, "which one of you is going to explain why the company I spent half my life building has apparently decided to send two strangers in ridiculous masks to kill me?"

The plague doctor mask tilted slightly toward her, the black-clad Administrator remaining almost unnaturally still as his voice carried across the metallic platform. "CEO Y's plans are already nearing completion. Tartarus has been successfully contained, and the repurposing process is proceeding according to schedule. It will not be long before this entire facility has been transformed into the asset our company requires."

Ishgria's eyes narrowed slightly. There it was again. That sterile, corporate language. Repurposed. As though they weren't talking about an entire world filled with living beings, but an obsolete office building that had been purchased and scheduled for renovation.

The red-clad Administrator finally spoke. "You should surrender, Ishgria. Whatever influence you once possessed within Divinity Inc. is meaningless now. CEO Y has already moved beyond your reach, and Tartarus will soon belong to us."

For several seconds, Ishgria simply stared at them. Then she snorted. The sound was almost dismissive. "You two really do sound utterly soulless."

. Ishgria's expression hardened as she slowly raised one hand, her  Purple light began gathering between her fingers. At first, it was little more than a faint glow, thin tendrils of energy curling around her hand like threads of violet smoke. Then the power rapidly intensified, brightening until the metallic platform beneath her was illuminated in brilliant purple light. Electricity crawled across her fingers,  crackling with increasing violence as the air itself seemed to vibrate.

The two Administrators barely had time to react. Ishgria thrust her hand forward. A torrent of purple lightning erupted from her fingertips with explosive force, so brilliant that the entire chamber was momentarily swallowed in violet light. The attack wasn't a single bolt so much as a massive, branching stream of electrical energy, dozens of jagged arcs twisting together as they surged across the platform toward the two Administrators.

The metallic floor beneath Ishgria's feet shuddered from the force of the discharge. The torrent struck both figures simultaneously.

Lightning wrapped around the plague doctor and the demon, exploding outward in a storm of sparks as the two Administrators were blasted backward. Their bodies were briefly obscured beneath the brilliant torrent, while the metallic platform beneath them glowed from the tremendous heat and energy coursing through it.

Ishgria held her hand steady. If they wanted to speak to her like an executive, then perhaps they should have remembered one very important thing. She had spent years running Divinity Inc. And she knew exactly how to deal with difficult employees.
