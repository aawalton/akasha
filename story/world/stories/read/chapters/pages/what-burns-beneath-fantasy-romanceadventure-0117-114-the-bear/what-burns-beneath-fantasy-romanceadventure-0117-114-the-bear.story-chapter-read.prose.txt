I smoothed my fingers through Rog’s hair, traced the slope of his bruised cheekbones and lifted my mantle to clean the foam and blood from his lips. Then I took the whole thing off, using the soft cloth to dab the wounds and warm his clammy skin.

Velidan’s gaze burned into me. Perhaps kneeling with the templar’s head in my lap was also unacceptable.

I ignored him, shivering in the frosty air and staring at the poor, battered face beneath my fingertips.

I’m so sorry. You don’t deserve this. I’ll make it a little bit better. I’ll help your friends.

I tried to convey it all with my eyes, but any message of hope was probably drowned by tears as they dripped from my chin and pattered to him.

The templar stared back; searching my face, nostrils flaring, breathing ragged around the gag. Probably wondering who in the realms I even was.

What if I left the mantle on him- and more importantly, the knife? Could he get free with it later? After some examination, any hope at the idea dwindled; the man’s shackles were iron bands with a slotted keyhole. A knife wouldn’t do much.

“-different than other witchwood weapons. These are carved from Thalessa herself, and they hurt.” Embriel was pacing in the arena’s center. He held a gilded silver sword levelly amidst the group of Fae.

The grizzled paladin stood before him, healed and stone-faced, with both hands clenched at his waist.

“Now, Fenwick,” Embriel purred as another Fae stepped closer, “my friend here has volunteered for a demonstration of your combat prowess.”

He licked his lips. “Sure. Unchain me, and the rest of you sorry sacks of salt can bugger off while he and I go at it.”

A chorus of laughter rang off the cold stones.

Embriel clapped him on the shoulder while handing over the sword. “Nice try. Go on, now; see if you can do some damage. Maybe it’ll slow him down later.”

I watched, frost on my skin and breath trapped in my throat, as the figures began circling each other. Then the Fae darted in, one hand out and teeth bared, moving so fast he seemed a blur amidst shadows and dim light.

Fenwick’s eyes glinted a familiar, glassy silver as he dropped one shoulder. He rammed it into the monster’s chest, lunged forwards when the impact knocked them apart, then swept his weapon up with a flicker of light and hissing air.

The Fae fell back, unharmed but clearly surprised, to a chorus of murmurs. I found myself echoing their shock.

Fenwick was breathing hard, but the rush of inhuman strength and blinding speed had been met without injury.

I watched the pair assess each other as the flap of wings sounded above. My gaze turned upwards and my breath hitched out in a gasp. Familiar sable feathers caught the hazy light, glinting as Horace circled along the archways.

He did say he’d be here, didn’t he? And he said something else, too… ‘Don’t be afraid to say-‘

“Hello.” I caught the wink of one golden eye as Horace glided lower. Then I looked down in panic, wondering if my call had drawn attention, but the combatants were upon each other again and no one was watching me.

Except for Velidan. There was a deep furrow between his brows. He followed my gaze upwards, frowning, then looked back towards me.

“Hello, morsel.” Horace settled on the ground nearby. He eyed the battered, wheezing figure in my lap ruefully. “I thought you might need a friendly face.”

I didn’t know whether to laugh or cry.

Horace turned towards the throng of Fae. “And how does today’s company suit you?”

“They’re monsters,” I whispered.

“Well, yes.”

“Show yourself.” A shadow fell across us alongside the hollow grind of Garruck’s rumble. He lurched over, towering over me with both wings flexed. Pit-like eyes scanned the stones. “Now.”

Icy fear swelled, then quickly gave way to shock. He… can’t see him? As if to confirm this, the monster’s nostrils flared. A quick glance beyond his massive frame showed that Velidan had disappeared.

No! My heart leapt into my throat. Where-

“Step away.” His silky voice came from just behind me. “Or I will fill one of these grooves early.”

“Will you?” He looked unimpressed. “I’ll fill one, too.” Garruck’s chest swelled as he searched the stones, then the air. “I smell something.”

“Probably your own stench.”

“Is there a problem, you two?” Embriel appeared at the monster’s side. Though tall, he barely came up to Garruck’s chest as he looked politely between the two Fae.

“Something’s here.”

Tense quiet, broken only by the grunts and thumps of the fight beyond, fell upon us. Embriel blinked, glancing around.

None of them can see Horace. Why? I cut my gaze to the owl.

He regarded me calmly. “Well, none of them need a friendly face.”

Leathery gray skin and corded muscle stooped, bringing the warped face far too close. The monster’s sheer presence felt like a weight closing in around me. I’d never felt so naked, so vulnerable, and it had nothing to do with how much of my prickling skin was bared.

Rancid, hot breath swept my hair back. “The woman spoke to something.”

I looked down, shoulders curling and shivering arms tightening around Rog. He was as rigid as iron.

“That’s no surprise,” Velidan scoffed. “She rarely stops speaking.”

“Come, my friend.” Embriel laid a hand where scarred skin transitioned to a thick, draping wing. “I trust your instincts, but-”

“Old.” Garruck’s claw crept out, dragging along the stones. “Not from here.” His massive black talons stopped just short of Horace’s small silver ones.

The owl didn’t flinch.

“Lamb,” Embriel said sweetly, “were you speaking to someone?”

I shook my head fiercely, eyes down and breath hitching. My teeth were chattering. “No, my lord. Just Rog.”

“She lies.” Jutting fangs inched closer.

A strained cry and a horrible, wet squelch pierced the ensuing silence. Chorused laughter and the sound of a body thumping to the rocks followed.

“Dawn, you’re not supposed to kill him!” Embriel snapped, stomping away. “Not yet! Vel, you mind?”

Icy stillness reigned behind me. “I won’t ask again.”

My heart slammed wildly in the ensuing pause. Then muscle and claws and teeth all shifted, rising like a gray mountain. Garruck bared his teeth at the figure standing over me before slouching away.

Velidan shifted before me, looking markedly unhappy as he searched my face. A question burned behind his eyes.

He wants to know if it’s safe. If he can step away from the- apparently- invisible monster he knows is here. At least, I think that’s what he’s asking. I nodded.

The clear frustration didn’t fade. He stooped, growling, “Do not speak again.”

“Mm. You’d better obey, little one.” Horace chuckled as the black-haired Fae stalked towards a fallen, wheezing Fenwick. “I’ll do the talking for both of us.”

I stared at him, eyes wide and lips parted. I didn’t make a sound, though, and quickly looked down when Garruck’s weighty attention slid back to me.

“Looks like he got a nice hit in,” Embriel’s voice trembled with laughter. “Good for him! Stings, doesn’t it?”

“Felt like it was trying to pull my dawned insides out,” a snarling voice replied. Fenwick’s opponent paced an erratic circle around him while pressing one hand to his own side, where crimson was quickly blooming. He hissed down at the wound. “It’s not closing.”

Embriel collected the witchwood blade. Blood gleamed along its edge. “Give it a bit. And you’re not wrong; this weapon’s hungry. Let this be a lesson for tonight, gentlemen; when it comes to these mortals, don’t play around. Just kill them quickly and move on. You can have your fun later.”

“Hubris,” Horace murmured, watching the group, “so often destroys those who would call themselves mighty. They all think only a sharpened claw can cut. But I assure you,” one talon scraped a teeth-grinding whine, “they’re wrong.”

“Any trick to killing them?” one of the Fae asked dryly.

“A snapped neck’ll do it. A blade in the heart, too.” Embriel tilted the sword this way and that, then snorted. “Or you could just rip them apart.”

“Shall I demonstrate?” Garruck closed one claw around the paladin’s throat, dragging him upright. The man remained stone-faced as his feet dangled several inches off the ground.

“Madness,” the little form beside me cooed as I watched in horror. “So much madness here. Do you hear it, mortal?” As if to accentuate the statement, distant screams and the clang of metal echoed off the cavernous walls. “This place brims with Fae who don’t remember themselves. Hundreds of them. What a terrible waste.”

“Not just yet,” Embriel tweaked Fenwick’s nose. “He’s going to put on a little show for us later. Him and his friends.”

“Then I assume you want that one intact?” Velidan was still looking towards me. Perhaps he’d never stopped.

Embriel’s brows rose, as if he’d forgotten about Rog entirely. “What, the templar? I suppose so. If it’s not too much.”

Velidan made a dismissive noise and strode back to me. Rog stiffened as he reached down.

“It’s not just the mad, though,” Horace trilled as the silver glow rippled across his feathers. “Not just the vicious, though your host has been determined to collect them. But there’s clever creatures here, too. Ones who aren’t ready to die.” He winked at me. “And a group of Thalessians caged right here on the ground floor.”

I drank the words in, trying to find a sliver of good in this chaos. Wracked my mind for what I could possibly do with the information.

The silver light dimmed and the figure in my arms grew still. My gratefulness turned to a horrified yelp as Velidan tore my mantle from the templar.

“This was for you.” He scowled down, probably assuming my wide-eyed panic was from something very different than a concealed witchwood blade nearly falling to the stones. “No one else.”

“Garruck, get those two locked back up.” Embriel was grinning at his audience once more. “Gentlemen, feel free to head on up. First fight’s in a bit, and your attendants have rosters. Get some drinks, place your bets, and enjoy the show.”

Frost coated my skin as Garruck dragged the Thalessians away. Warm fabric descended a moment later, but it did little to chase the chill.

Sable feathers spread. “You’ll have one chance to save a soul here, little one. Don’t hesitate. I’m betting on you.” As Horace swept up, his shadow fell upon the stones beneath me. Dark wings stretched barely wider than my shoulders, but as he climbed the blackness grew. Stretched. Deepened.

And when he reached the top, it seemed like almost everything was covered by his shadow.
