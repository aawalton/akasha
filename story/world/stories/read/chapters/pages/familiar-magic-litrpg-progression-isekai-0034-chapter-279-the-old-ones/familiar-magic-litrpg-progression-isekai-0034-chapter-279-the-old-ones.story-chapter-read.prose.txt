Selina struggled not to stare at the older Nator, as he carefully adjusted the translation rune below her left ear. The hand holding the enchanted inkwell was steady and strong, yet the man’s entire other arm was shrunken, and bent at odd angles. It wasn’t any fascination with the deformity that drew Selina’s attention, of course, but rather the intense–yet impotent–desire to heal the poor man.

He nodded with a blank expression when he was finished, before a younger Nator wheeled him out of the small stone building. This Nator had none of the twisted misshapen appearance of the first. Since Cara walked her through the city’s gate, she’d seen nearly a hundred of her lost people, and they appeared to fall into two separate categories.

The first was like the man who’d adjusted her translation rune to account for their native tongue. Those like him all suffered from some malformed part of their bodies, usually in more than one place, but were largely calm, with clear eyes–presumably clear minds as well.

The second type was like the young woman who’d escorted the man away. She had been even taller than Selina by at least a head, and had something like juvenile wings peaking out from her ragged clothes. Others had hands closer to claws, or extended jaws with vicious-looking teeth. Selina knew from experience that the comparison to dragons wasn’t coincidental.

She’d faced a Nator-turned dragon herself, once.

Unfortunately, balancing out the less debilitating transformations these Nator possessed was something affecting their minds. While this particular woman had been quiet, Selina had spotted many more who barely managed to keep their rage and agitation in check. Even on the short walk to the building where she now sat, Selina had spotted no less than four separate fights between these draconic-Nator.

Each time, their more peaceful counterparts had rushed in as quickly as they could, and spoke soothingly to end the bloodshed, but never fast enough to prevent injury. Again, Selina’s desire to heal had flared up, but she didn’t want to start casting unknown magic around these clearly tense people unless she needed to. Not while she couldn’t understand the language, at least.

Cara had nodded and smiled, leading the way through the city while Selina looked around nervously. This wasn’t the homecoming she’d envisioned when she imagined finding her people still alive, and dozens of eyes had watched her passing. After a while, Selina had begun to feel guilty about her relative normalcy.

The archer, who’d waited patiently while clearly trying to look encouraging, now gestured for Selina to follow her outside. Selina did so, unconsciously pulling her robes more tightly around her. Being dressed all in white felt ostentatious among the crumbling city and uniformly brown-clad refugees milling through the streets.

“Where are we going?” she asked, despite knowing Cara couldn’t answer. The archer pointed at the central keep, and Selina nodded, trying to keep her eyes forward. “I suppose it can’t be easy to keep the city clean and together,” she said, looking at a collapsed building, and a man with twisted legs sleeping in an alley. Cara just shrugged, and Selina found her normal lust for chatter conspicuously absent.

They walked through the streets in near-silence. The most noise came from the occasional scuffle between two or more of the draconic-Nator, and Selina watched them warily as they went. Twice they were “attacked” on the way to the keep, but Nator or not, these people were less than Dawn rank, and Cara and Selina were able to end the assaults before they really began.

Each time, Selina nearly came to tears after, as she saw the confusion and pain in the eyes of her attacker. They clearly had little understanding of what they’d done, and moped away in confusion and guilt mere moments after appearing ready to tear her apart.

Relief flooded through her as they finally entered the large stone keep. It really appeared to be more of a civil structure within than any kind of true building of war. Selina spotted desks and bookshelves aplenty, though much had rotted away with time.

“Bastion is not what it was,” a deep female voice spoke as Selina paused before some ancient leather-bound volumes. She and Cara turned to see a Nator woman slightly shorter than Selina herself walking toward them from an inner chamber.The woman gestured with a nod of the head back the way she’d come, and Selina hurried after her.

“It’s an honor to speak with you,” she said hurriedly, struggling to find the words.

The woman went on as if Selina had said nothing. “Bastion was ancient long before we came to use it as a refuge, and it has sheltered us for many centuries since.”

Selina barely registered the words, as she’d realized that this Nator wasn’t like the others. She was Dusk rank, like Selina herself, and appeared to be largely intact–from what was visible outside the ragged brown robes.  One horn was broken, and she had no hair, but otherwise could have passed for any Nator on Nexum.

“My name is Selina,” she said, “thank you for welcoming me into your city.”

“Vetta,” the woman replied without looking back. They passed through a large door on creaking hinges, and into a candlelit room far cleaner than the rest of the keep had been. A Nator man stood in a far corner, holding a book open. He was short, for Nator, but Dusk rank, and whole, like Vetta.

“Remarkable,” he said, looking directly at Selina. “Our people live.”

“As we were told,” Vetta said, moving to sit behind a desk carved from some kind of dark marble.

“I’m Selina,” she said, trying to find her usual smile.

“Oreth,” the man replied with a nod, and moved to stand next to the desk. “We were told you’d arrived, but little else. Why are you here? Why come back to our doomed world?”

Selina glanced at Cara, who shrugged. Could it be that she'd never fully managed to communicate with these people?

“We’re here to save this world, and its two reflections,” Selina said, then waited for the gravity of those words to settle on the others.

The two Nator remained expressionless. Finally, Vetta let out a sigh. “This one was found in the northern wastes,” she said, indicating Cara. “You know each other.” Not a question.

Selina put an arm around her friend’s shoulders. “She’s my greatest friend. We’ve faced the impossible together.”

Vetta let out a snort. “You face it again, if you truly mean to save Potentia. Your friend already died once in the attempt, I think.”

Selina whirled to look at the archer, eyes wide. Cara looked back defiantly, but gave a sharp nod. “How is that possible?” Selina whispered.

“Potentia does not so easily release those who walk its soil,” Oreth said. “Many who fall here are returned, though always twisted somehow. The more powerful you are, the more likely you are to return. This one was wandering the fields, lost and confused. She is still largely herself, however. Be grateful for that mercy.”

“Cara…I–”

“That can wait,” Vetta interrupted. “Potentia is many things, but not for millennia has it been a home to war. Now two armies march across our homeland, gathering the demons into hordes. We’ve never seen such madness. Is it your doing?”

Selina blinked, trying to think of where to start. “There are two armies, controlled by two separate parties. The one to the south is here to save Potentia, the other to finally end it, and birth new suffering.”

Vetta let out something that was nearly a cackle. “There is no new suffering, child. Potentia has shown us every kind of pain and torment that can be.”

“Sadly, you’re mistaken, elder,” Selina said with a sad shake of her head. “The Union is coming, and it will bring salvation, or fresh terror to our worlds. I stand with Ethan Bishop, the Chosen of Flagras, and a man who has faced down three Gods. If he can reach Dusk, he will face down the last, and bring about a new world, where Nator–and all races can thrive.”

The two locals had finally let their faces crack into expressions of shock, the word “Union” evidently being something they recognized. They shared a look, then Oreth spoke.

“The Union is a myth,” he said, although there might have been the faintest trace of hope in the words. Hope that he was wrong, Selina believed.

“It is not,” Selina said. “The Union has already begun. Nexum and Earth already feel the effects. You might have felt them here too, though perhaps they’ve gone unnoticed.” Dimensional Bleeds could be happening everywhere on this planet, Selina figured, but if one didn’t occur directly in this cavern, these Nator might never know.

“How does this end with an army at our doorstep?” Vetta demanded.

Selina sighed. She didn’t think it could hurt for these people to know. They didn’t seem strong enough to upset Ethan’s work. She went over the plan in broad strokes, focusing on why the taint needed to be gathered in such quantities, and how it was the key to The Throne. The Nator looked somewhat panicked at the mere mention of the mythological seat of Dusk, but otherwise they listened.

Their faces remained still throughout, and Vetta never seemed convinced, though Oreth stroked his smooth chin. Cara reacted most strongly, staring mouth agape at the audacity of Ethan’s goals, and the insanity of gathering as many demons as possible in one place. When the explanation was complete, silence echoed through the chamber for so long that Selina nearly started over. Thankfully, Vetta spoke first.

“This…plan,” she began, “I cannot believe it. Yet the armies are a fact, whether I like it or not. Oreth, I do not think we can judge this for ourselves.”

“I agree,” he said, shaking his horned head. “We must contact the benefactors.”

Vetta nodded, and Selina’s eyebrows lowered. “Who, or what, are the benefactors?”

Vetta stood. “Come see for yourself,” she said, then strode from the room.

Cara and Selina could only shrug and follow.
