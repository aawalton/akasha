Moonlight

The Survivors watched the Fiend and the Prophet leave. Each of them stared at the others in uncertain silence. Paltra, Maerin, and Krevith stood near the exit. Nico and Alder sat at an overturned table, shooting glances at Paltra's crossbow. Lastly, there was the woman in the three-piece suit and another woman in her mid-thirties in a simple set of work pants.

Yesemire was the woman in the suit, a lawyer. She had figured out pretty fast why she was here. She had been the other woman's, Illia's, defense lawyer. Her case never made it to Trial. It was a simple land dispute of someone muscling into the land her late husband left her, then it quickly spiraled into a defamation case where Illia was accused of besmirching the name of the man and his company. It was all espionage, but legally the company was in the clear, and would've been able to drag it on until Illia ran out of money.

It had been a week straight of living in the office, poring over precedent, legal, and spiritual texts until she found an argument. In a sleep-deprived delirium, she had submitted the evidence to the judge. From submission to Mourning Circle, Yesemire was placed in solitary confinement for 8 days, not allowed a visit from her husband, children, counsel, or anyone else. She didn't even know if they were aware of what was happening. That was easily the worst part, even worse than knowing they'd be happy that she was imprisoned.

There was no way to go back. In all her research, there wasn't a single person who came back from the Mourning Circle. The only options were to return to the River or leave for the many worlds beyond. For the first time in many years, she didn't know what she wanted. She could die, return to the River, or try her best to escape, to leave for the many worlds beyond.

It was a comment Illia made that had her pause. "It doesn't matter that I want to die. I won't."

The other woman wanted to live, and while Yesemire hadn't been able to help her legally before, maybe she could do something now. The hours ticked on. They were faced with awful challenge after awful challenge, never letting up for even a moment, and Yesemire was forced to confront the fact that the skill set this Trial demanded was one she just didn't have. She was no survivalist, she wasn't a fighter, or a detective. The one thing she could do was make an argument and persuade, but even that hadn't been much help. It had gotten all the Survivors in one place, then the Prophet and Fiend came in, and chaos followed.

Now she was left with the former Gladus with a gun and one of the now-dead high priests' scribes, and on the other side, there was the elusive Huntress, the reigning champion of the arena, and the woman who had murdered the high priest in their sleep. Both sides had murder in their eyes. It wasn't a powder keg waiting to explode. It was a prison block, and all of the cells had been unlocked after lights out.

No amount of flowery words would stop a blood bath once one of the groups made a move. It was inevitable, but she could use the one strategy that had never failed her. Stall. Then, when the first stall ran out, stall again. Until a better argument was found or time enough had passed for the situation to blow over.

The subtle shift of Krevith's posture let Yesemire know it was time to say something. She took great care with every word, "Exploring the ship would be good. It seems like we already have our groups." She gave the red-haired girl a nod, "Lady Maerin, if you want to take your group first, we'll wait a few minutes then Illia and I will go, she turned to the Gladus—Alder—then you and the Scribe can go next. Let's us cover more area."

It wasn't smooth. It didn't need to be. She wasn't tricking anyone, and she held no doubt that both parties could see through her intentions, but half of negotiations was always giving a group deniability. People wanted an out. Rarely did someone actually want to fight. It's why so many people took plea deals. Eventually, exhaustion beat out everything else.

Maerin hadn't looked away from Alder since the Prophet and Fiend left the room, and only managed to tear her eyes away to find the expression of her companions. "Agreeable." The girl spoke, but from her tone it was clear the situation was anything but. Why she still went along with it, Yesemire wasn't sure, but she was just glad she did.

Reluctantly, the three of them cleared out of the room. Yesemire breathed out a sigh of relief, leaning back in her chair and closing her eyes. Slowly, she could feel a small trickle of stress leaving her neck, and a nauseating headache replaced the receding tension. She pinched the bridge of her nose in a hopeless attempt for relief.

It was Illia gently pulling at Yesemire's sleeve that made her jump, and the words that followed that made her blood feel heavy. "We need to go." Illia whispered, "Nico's staring at us now."

She didn't need to open her eyes to know Illia was telling the truth. The hairs on her neck stood, and she felt the baseline desire to run. It felt like being in the woods at night, the feeling that something was right next to you, breathing down your neck.

Her eyes stayed closed as she stood and gently grabbed her client's hand, and strode confidently towards the exit. The other woman opened her mouth to say something, but just as quickly closed it with a forceful squeeze from Yesemire's hand. She had learned many things in her short life. Always trust your gut, walk confidently, and don't break momentum.

The two of them were out the doorway before the pair of men could properly process or react, but that didn't last long. Footsteps resounded not twenty paces behind the two women, and suddenly, Yesemire deeply regretted sending Lady Maerin away.

She grabbed onto Illia's hand tighter and began to jog. It was completely dark, except for the faintest red tint lighting the internals of the ship. Yesemire hadn't grabbed a light source before leaving the ballroom, too focused on making as much distance as possible. She got that distance, but it was being stripped away with each passing second.

The stairs came into view, one set leading up, the other leading down. The pair rushed down the stairs as quietly as possible, Yesemire pulling Illia behind a set of overturned barrels. She didn't dare open a door or move a crate for fear the noise would give away the direction she went.

The flicker of lantern light paused at the staircase, Yesemire seeing the shadows cast on the wall from her hiding spot.

"Up or down do you think?" The Scribe, Nico, asked.

Silence.

"Alder!" Nico snapped.

One of the shadows on the wall flinched, "Oh. Right. Sorry." The Shadows shifted as the lantern swung around. It moved closer, reaching the threshold between the two directions. Yesemire squeezed herself further against Illia and the ground itself, carefully covering both of their jewelry so the light wouldn't catch.

More silence.

No movement.

Not a word from Alder or Nico.

Yesemire's gut twisted, and she fought the urge to peek up and look at the shadows. Her ears strained and caught the barely perceptible sound of a wooden plank groaning.

Another groan and the tail end of a crackle from the flickering flame. She gripped onto Illia harder, her breathing growing less even as adrenaline washed over her.

"Upstairs. I don't see any signs that they went down there." Alder finally said, and the creaking of wood became more obvious as he made his way back to Nico, and then faded even further as the pair went up.

A few minutes passed before Yesemire felt like she could breathe again, and was only broken from her semi panicked state as Illia extricated her hand from the lawyer's grip, "You'll break my hand." The woman muttered.

The pair stood. The dark reds illuminating the inside of the ship were deeper at this level for some reason. It bathed everything in a bloody sheen. Yesemire shivered at the sight, uncertain if she even wanted to move from their hiding spot.

"Help me."

She froze. The words came again, choked, half-formed, and muffled, but unmistakable.

"Help me. Please."

It was Biancca. The seamstress's voice was unmistakable. They had found the woman, and she indeed needed help. It was the entire reason they had separated in the first place. However, there was no relief in that knowledge. Only some deep, primal fear.

She wanted to go back to the ballroom, to get the others and come back when they were all here. But she just didn't trust them. The look in Maerin's eyes. The fact that the two men had followed them right away. Even the Prophet was acting friendly with a Fiend of all things. No matter where she looked, she was surrounded by madness.

Torn between helping a potentially injured woman and fleeing, Yesemire froze.

"Let's find Krevith. He'll help us." Illia said, glancing towards the stairs.

"You trust him?" Yesemire spoke, genuine surprise coating her voice.

Illia nodded, "I went to the arena a lot. He… I saw how he came to this place. He's a good person."

Yesemire didn't need much more convincing, and so carefully made her way to the stairs. They began to ascend. They didn't make it more than halfway up before a creaking noise caught the pair's attention. They turned, staring into the red darkness of the ship. Whatever it was sounded heavy. "You're not going to help me?" The voice echoed down the halls behind them.

Both women didn't dare breathe, just took soft, continual steps up the stairs—ready to bolt at a moment's notice. So careful were they looking behind them that neither noticed the figure at the top of the stairs until Yesemire's back collided with it.

The lawyer whipped around, eyes wide, to find Biancca, staring at them both, eyes wide with fear. She flinched, her expression one of pleading. "Walk back down the stairs." The sound was jerky. Each syllable made a shearing, popping sound from the woman's jaw. Every time the mouth closed, her teeth made contact and ground against one another upon opening again.

"Biancca?" Illia questioned.

"Back. Down. The. Stairs." The eyes never stopped pleading, despite her commanding voice.

An echoing growl sounded from deep within the ship.

"N-no." Illia pushed out.

That was all Biancca needed. She threw herself recklessly from the top of the stairs, launching herself straight into Illia's chest.

Illia let out a scream, the sound cut off just as quickly as it started.

Yesemire watched as both women's feet left the stairs, and in a tangle of limbs, they fell halfway down before hitting the steps.

CRACK

The unmistakable sound of bones breaking. The lawyer could see one of the tangled arms bent at an off-angle as the two tumbled the rest of the way down the stairs. The growling grew louder as whatever it was drew closer. Yesemire stood at the top of the stairs—less than a handful of seconds had passed.

Her legs didn't move. She looked up the stairs— her path free of obstacles. She stared back at Illia. Biancca was straddling the woman, two unbroken arms pinning Illia to the floor and beginning to find purchase on her neck.

She watched as Biancca's hands finally wrapped around the woman's neck. Illia weakly punched with one arm, trying to push Biancca's face away, but the efforts grew weaker with each second.

Her client's words resounded in Yesemire's memories, her desire to live. It gave her a reason to move. She rushed down the stairs, using her momentum to collide with Biancca's back and sent the assailant sprawling to the ground. She snatched at Illia's shoulder and began dragging her up the stairs.

Again, they only made it halfway up the stairs.

The visage of a wolf, with an upside-down monkey head, revealed itself from the shadows. Walking past the collapsed Biancca and stepping onto the lowest rung of the stairs.

"Let. me. help. you." The Spirit of Judgment spoke. "Let me. [save] your. soul."

She didn't have the strength—all of it left her in an instant as she fell backwards onto her ass. Both of them sat sprawled on the staircase as the beast took another two steps forward. In tandem, Biancca stumbled her way to her feet.

"Don't be scared." Biancca said, facing away from the two women. "I'm helping. I'm— No! No! Spirit! Help! "

The body suddenly went limp and sprawled back to the ground. The wolf paused. Its head flicked between the two women and another direction entirely. It growled, spitting to the side in frustration before once again zeroing in on Yesemire and Illia.

It spoke again, and instead of a coherent voice, it sounded like all of humanity layered into one. "Let. me. [help]."

It leapt.

THUNK A bolt fired.

It hit the wolf in the side, the head jerked, and the jaws clamped around Yesemire's shoulder, inches away from her neck.

The Spirit didn't let go of its grip. It whinnied and slunk backwards, dragging Yesemire down the stairs.

A large figure barreled down the stairs, raised a makeshift club high above his head, and swung to the side, aiming for the beast's eye. The attack forced the Spirit to drop Yesemire. It growled, slinking further down the stairs.

Krevith hefted the bleeding Yesemire to her feet. She couldn't have resisted even if she wanted to, too disoriented by the sudden attack for her brain to catch up to the present. She watched blankly as he leaned her against the railing, raised the club, and stepped between her and the wolf.

She broke eye contact with the monster and looked up to the crying Illia. She nursed her arm as she crawled up the stairs. She didn't have to crawl long as Lady Maerin leaned low and helped her to her feet. Beyond the red-haired girl was the Huntress, reloading another bolt.

Waves of relief washed over her again, and she fought back tears and a growing headache at the sudden turn around in their fates.

Rend lingered at the foot of the stairs. It licked its lips, cleaning the blood off its stone fur and teeth. Slinking from side to side, the head rotated almost 100 degrees, seeming more correct than it usually did as it considered the group. The eyes of the beast flicked to the collapsed form of Biancca.

It took a step towards the woman. Krevith took two steps down the stairs.

The spirit hesitated, then the eyes flicked past the Gladiator, up past even Yesemire herself, Maerin, Illia, and the Huntress. It grinned.

Yesemire, followed the creatures gaze, and she barely had time to feel dread as she saw what the Spirit noticed.

Nico stood. Rifle shouldered. Aim fixed.

Yesemire shouted her warning.

Everyone except Krevith turned.

The wolf leapt.

The muzzle flashed, and the crack of the gunshot tore through everyone's unprotected ears.

Krevith staggered, still standing. He wavered, his body turning, and Yesemire saw the bullet hole in the side of his head.

He didn't get to crumple. The jaws of the Spirit closed around his side moments later and dragged him down, leaving a trail of red behind it.
