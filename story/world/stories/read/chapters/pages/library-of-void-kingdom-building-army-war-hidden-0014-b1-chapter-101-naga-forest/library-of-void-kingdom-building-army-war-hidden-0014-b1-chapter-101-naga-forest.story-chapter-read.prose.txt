Later that night…

Vector stood before the entrance of a cave they had chosen as their temporary shelter.

The night was already deep, and with Somira barely present in the sky—its pale glow thin and distant—he had decided to halt their advance. Traveling through the forest under such limited moonlight would have been reckless, especially with non-combatants accompanying them. Speed was important, but survival came first.

The cave itself was modest in size, partially concealed by dense foliage that spilled down its rocky edges like a natural curtain. It sat slightly elevated above the forest floor, granting both concealment from wandering eyes and a small but meaningful tactical advantage. From its mouth, one could observe approaching movement while remaining hidden within shadow.

As Vector stepped inside, a thought surfaced almost instantly.

Saltpeter.

The key ingredient for gunpowder.

If this cave served as a roost for bats, there was a chance—however slim—that it might also house deposits of guano, which could be refined into a critical resource for future firearm production.

His eyes moved quickly, sharp and expectant, scanning the cave walls, the uneven stone floor, the darker recesses where moisture gathered. But after a brief inspection, the truth became clear.

Nothing.

No guano. No mineral-rich residue. No faint crystalline deposits clinging to the rock.

His brief hope faded—but his resolve did not.

Not here, he thought, his gaze narrowing slightly. But once we reach Vantara, the surrounding terrain must be scouted thoroughly. If we can locate a cave rich in saltpeter…

The implications were enormous.

With that single resource, their ability to manufacture gunpowder—and by extension, firearms—would increase dramatically.

He made a mental note to assign a dedicated team the moment they reached Vantara.

Every advantage mattered.

Every edge could decide the outcome of the coming war.

Once the cave had been secured, the group moved with practiced efficiency. The interior and its immediate perimeter were checked thoroughly. No signs of beasts. No lingering scent of predators. No evidence that the cavern was currently inhabited.

Satisfied, they began to settle in.

Small fires were lit—carefully shielded so their glow would not leak beyond the cave mouth. Cooks set to work quietly, preparing the evening meal with minimal noise, while guards were assigned in overlapping patrols around the cave's exterior.

Outside, the Naga Forest stirred.

As darkness fully claimed the land, the forest came alive with distant rustling, faint calls, and unseen movement—an ever-present reminder that this wilderness was anything but empty.

Yet within the cave, a fragile calm took hold.

It was dinner time.

The group gathered, though several guards remained on duty, their meals delayed until the others had finished. The food was simple—flatbread, roasted meat, and warm soup prepared to provide essential nutrients rather than indulgence.

Still…

As Vector took a bite of the bread, he paused.

It was warm. Fragrant. Soft.

For reasons he couldn't immediately explain, it felt like the most satisfying bread he had eaten in weeks.

A glance around confirmed he wasn't alone in that thought. Faces that had been hardened by travel and tension softened, expressions briefly easing—as if they were tasting not food, but relief.

The meal passed quietly.

Once everyone—craftsmen, guards, and specialists alike—had eaten, Marcus organized the night rotations. Guards were assigned in shifts, ensuring constant vigilance while allowing others to rest.

Preparations for sleep followed soon after.

The Naga Forest remained still—for now.

But Vector knew better than to trust such calm.

Even with Sebastian present—the strongest individual likely within this region of the forest's outer reaches—complacency was a luxury they could not afford.

A single misstep deeper into the forest could mean annihilation.

Vector settled into a cross-legged position near the cave wall, his back straight, his breathing steady.

Rest was necessary.

Vector closed his eyes briefly and called out to the System.

The battle earlier had laid everything bare.

Even though he wielded strength comparable to a Second Stage Warrior, he had grasped his own shortcomings through today's engagements. His movements had been crude, driven by raw power rather than control. His force lacked refinement. Every strike, every step, had relied on brute strength—effective, yes, but inefficient.

Power without direction was waste.

And Martial Arts were precisely what gave strength direction.

They shaped force. Refined movement. Turned chaos into intent.

Among all disciplines available to him, there was no hesitation in his choice.

Archery.

The familiar blue light shimmered into existence before his eyes, forming the System interface.

Not a voice.

Not a command.

But a scene.

The image of a master archer appeared within his mind, every movement rendered with flawless clarity. The posture was calm, unhurried, yet absolute. Every muscle fiber, every tendon, every minute alignment of bone and breath unfolded before Vector as if etched directly into his awareness.

He watched.

And absorbed.

The tightening of the bowstring—controlled, deliberate. The precise nock of the arrow—silent, exact. The breath drawn in—not rushed, not delayed. It imprinted itself onto him.

Drawing. Aiming. Releasing.

Targets began to flicker in and out of existence. Some barely visible, others shielded behind illusory obstacles, some mimicking armored movement patterns, forcing prediction rather than reaction.
