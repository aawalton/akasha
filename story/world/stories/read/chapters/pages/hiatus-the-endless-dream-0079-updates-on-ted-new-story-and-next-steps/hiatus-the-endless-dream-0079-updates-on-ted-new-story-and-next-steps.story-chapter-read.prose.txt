- I am still working on The Endless Dream rewrite/continuation. I am about 40k words in, edited.

- There are major changes being made to TED. For one, it's no longer a litRPG; it's just straight-up fantasy horror. It will have the same mechanics of Boons, Restrictions, and Items. Ananke/The Being in The Static is also still present, but character sheets and screens, along with direct quantification of Presence, will not be there. Something that always bothered me about the original is that, while I did everything I could to make the System horrifying, the status screen always felt silly.

- The rewrite is a semi-continuation. All previous events are canon (except for the above, which has been removed), and Dorian and many of the other characters just don't remember them.

- All of the changes were made because I believe they allow me to write a more compelling horror story.

- Once I finish the first book/major arc, I will add an update to this page with a link to the updated page where the story will be.

- I am currently writing another series as well, called "The First and Last Emperor." "Why?" You might ask. To put it plainly, TED is mentally taxing to write. I have to constantly think about horrifying things and ways people can die, and it wears on me greatly. It's something I have to work on sporadically, which makes consistent updates difficult. The other, new, story is a litRPG apocalypse and is far more light-hearted than TED, without being a complete fairy tale. It's something easy for me to write and update.

- Lastly, I am going to show you the draft of the new prologue for TED.

"Heads or Tails?" I heard Maerin ask.

The wind cut through my winter coat and sent a chill tracing down my spine, drying out my skin even more. A curse slipped from my lips as I handed back the bar goer's ID and waved the next person up to repeat the process. Only a third of my shift had even passed, and the meager heat wafting out of the open door called to me.

"Well, heads or tails?" Maerin repeated.

"I'm working right now man." I shot her a tired glare.

"Gross, don't call me 'man'. That's just not right." She responded with the usual mock outrage that I found so endearing. Although I liked it more when her antics weren't directed towards me, especially while I was trying to work. A smile crept across my face, and I couldn't help myself from trying to push her off-center.

"If I were Axel I'd just tell you I like head and tail, preferably at the same time."

"You're right that is much worse, but I'm not talking to him, so heads or tails?"

I internally shrugged. It was worth a try. Outwardly, I continued my cursory inspections of the IDs. I was confident at least eight of them had been fakes, but my manager hadn't trained me on what to do in that case. He also didn't pay me enough to hold some annoying prick's card all night and risk being wrong. All the better in my eyes, too. If a twenty-year-old wanted to go to the bars, who cared?

Like my dad always said, 'if you're old enough to go to war or get hitched to someone four times your age, you're old enough to drink.' Not super eloquent, but there was wisdom I liked in there.

"It's a perfectly innocent request for you to pick one of two options," Maerin said again, and I was so lost in thought that I hadn't been listening to her at all. I had also autopiloted the last five or so people in.

"Tails." I gave in, knowing it would be a mistake in the long run. Maerin sometimes behaved like a food-motivated cat. If I gave an inch, she'd take it and then start poking at the boundaries. It grated me sometimes, but the rest of her made up for that.

"Are you ever going to pick heads? It's a literal weighted coin Doe. I literally have an ongoing bet with my roommates for how long until you pick heads."

"Is that why you've tracked me down?"

Maerin snorted. "Hardly, I'm waiting for said roommates. It's usually Axel here on Thursdays anyways."

"Yeah, he had something come—"

I was cut off as a drunk man shoulder checked me as he stumbled past onto the street. His body twisted, wrenching itself to the side. He turned to me, black hair disheveled, with a shirt soaked in sweat and beer and spittle running down the side of his lip. He glared at me, and I returned the favor. I quickly relented, breaking eye contact first. It wouldn't do for me to look like the aggressor.

The man wavered back and forth, and the lost look turned into one of sloppy anger. I caught faint mutterings under his breath. He then put his hands in his pockets and started fishing for something as he walked away. I looked back at him as he went, my eyes not leaving him except for the brief moments while I was checking more IDs.

"People drink too much sometimes." Maerin scowled.

"You like just saying stuff sometimes, don't you?" I responded dryly, and she glared at me.

A soft honk from a car lock caught my attention, and I looked up to see the drunk man from before, keys in hand, clicking the lock button over and over again, trying to find his car.

Confusion hit me first. There was no way someone that drunk thought they were in any position to drive; that'd be insane. Then worry that someone might be arrogant enough to have that thought. Followed by anger. I breathed deeply and pushed the feeling down. I thought of other reasons. He didn't come out with anyone; he probably had friends inside still and was just looking for extra cash or some shit.

Maerin wasn't saying anything, and she looked more than a little angry herself. "You don't gotta be a dick about everything."

I looked off to the side and raised my hands in defeat, "Just jokes. My bad, my bad. Nothing wrong with too many shots. An asshole is just gonna be an asshole. People like that come with the job, no need to get heated every time one shows up."

"In this weather it might do you some good."

As if her words brought it into existence, another cold trill ran through my clothes and around my back. "Damn, I was distracted. Just had to remind me."

Some time passed in amiable silence as Maerin checked her phone.

"Any luck on your roommates?" I asked.

"Ugh, they decided to go to Pipers and forgot to tell me. They've been there for ten damn minutes now."

"Well, tell them thanks for me. Between classes and work I barely get to see you—"

There was a shout, a scream, a honk, and then my ears rang like a gun had gone off. Except it wasn't the sound of a bullet. It was the sudden sound of hundreds of metal pieces groaning, shrinking, and then snapping, all overlapped and compressed all into two seconds of sound. The sound of glass shattering registered a second later, along with shouts of fright and a scream of pain.

I saw it then. A car crashed straight into a bike rack, bending and loosening it from the concrete. There, with a leg trapped underneath the front tire, was some poor college schmuck. The leg was bent and flattened, pinned against the curb and the tire. Red dyed his sweat pants, and something pushed against the cloth at an angle that looked almost perpendicular to the rest of the shin. Then the tire spun.

The opposite tire must have been off the ground. The car lurched, continuing forward towards the broken bike rack. Gaining a sudden burst of traction, the car caught and ripped the boy's leg down and back with such force that the already broken bone punctured through the boy's pants. The car couldn't make its way over the bike rack and began to roll backwards, pulping muscle and bone on the way back. The front bumper came down against the leg, trapping it to the curb yet again.

The car slid back, and I found my feet pounding against the sidewalk, my eyes filling with static. I grabbed at the car's handle and tried to yank it open, but the inhuman drunk bastard had locked it. He locked eyes with me, shocked and panicked. One eye was closed, and he had a hand raised to his face, a large shard of glass sticking out. Raising my elbow, I slammed it into the already cracked glass. Window shattering, I pulled back and sent another elbow into the side of the man's head. He reeled back, and I reached in and unlocked the car.

Wrenching the door open, I tore his leg away from the pedals, leaned in, and slammed my foot on the brake to put the car in park. The man looked at me, dazed and confused, as if he didn't know where he was. Rage filled me in that moment, and I wanted that man dead. The world was shit enough as it was, and 'people' like him made it worse, and they always seemed to fucking get away with it in the end. They got away with anything, while others suffered the consequences.

I stared at the glass shard in the man's eye. Not much time had passed since I opened the door. More than a second, less than two. I put the car in drive. Punched the bastard in the face, grabbed the back of his neck, and then switched my leg to hit the gas. There was a full-bellowed scream from the other side of the car, where the boy's leg had been pulped, but I shook the yell from my mind.

I moved forward with the car, and as we pressed against the bike rack, I twisted his head, aligning his eye with the steering wheel, and slammed it down. The man twitched, and I held my breath, hoping the glass would pierce his brain and kill him. I leaned over what I hoped was a dead body and put the car in park, waiting for the cops to arrive.

Unfortunately, it seemed history would repeat itself.

The bastard survived, although he no longer had an eye. He got some jail time, but only five years. Less with good behavior. He was too drunk to provide an adequate testimony against me, and the cameras didn't help or hurt my case any more than eyewitness testimony. My legal defense was that of a good Samaritan acting in Defense of Others, who tried to stop the bastard from being a public danger.

I was still in county jail for several weeks while all that went on, and I missed my exams. After all was said and done, the DA worked out a deal with my attorney for community service, with nothing going on my record. I also got fired from my job as a bouncer.

The boy was a freshman who had been trying to get into the bar with their friends with a new fake. He had to get his leg amputated.

Nothing changed, well, except one thing.

A pervasive, piercing static at the corner of my vision, calling to me.
