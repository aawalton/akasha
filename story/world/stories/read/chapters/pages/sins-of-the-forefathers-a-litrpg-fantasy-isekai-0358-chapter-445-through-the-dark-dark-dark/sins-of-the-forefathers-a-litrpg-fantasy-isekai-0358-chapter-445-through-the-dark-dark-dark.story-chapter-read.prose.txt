This place was just so big.

It was astonishing to consider, but I actually thought this entire underground cavern might just be around roughly the same size as the interior of Mt. Goryuen had been.

And that had been the single tallest mountain upon the face of Vereden.

At least, before I had finished it.

Sure, this cavern didn’t have the sheer upwards height the ancient Netherim stronghold had possessed, but it was still as wide as that place had been. The result was a seemingly unending plain of disturbing flat stone that stretched far off into the distance, beyond the limit of what a mortal eye could see. The darkness was oppressive, and yet…I was thankful that was the only thing that was oppressive about it. I well remembered the pressure within the base of Goryuen that had pressed down upon me.

This place...instead, it was just dead. In more ways than one, apparently.

It didn’t take long for us to get tired of cautiously searching through the darkness of the cave, trailing behind Fade as he followed the faint scent of death upon the Aether. There was absolutely no point in being so overtly tentative when this place was so strangely empty. The footing down here was close to ideal; there were no obstacles to be found, and there were no monsters.

If we wanted to solve this mystery on time, we need to pick up the pace.

At my urging, Fade stopped creeping along the trail of the scent and started sprinting. Sylvia and I had no trouble following along behind him, as the Spirit Wolf sped through the dark. Sylvia, because she was a Classer not far from the Third Breakpoint, and I…

Well, for as reduced as I was in my clone body, I was still Nathan Hart. Fade didn’t have the benefits that I did from my Status, no matter the strange abilities granted by the Eldrydd Path.

As we dashed through the darkness, our path began to change. Slowly, ever so slowly, Fade began to veer away from the center of the cavern we had been following for some time now. Our path gradually started to lead closer to the walls of the cavern, chasing something my mere Human nose could not perceive.

And then, abruptly, we stopped.

The last echoes of our party’s footfalls began to die around us as we came to a halt before…

Nothing.

As far as I could see, there was nothing special in front of us. We had been following the right-hand wall of the cavern for the last fifteen minutes, as far as I could tell. Fade had abruptly stopped in front of a stretch of it that didn’t look any different from the rest of it. It was just a blank, ever so slightly curved section of plain grey stone that stretched far off into the distance above us.

I frowned and stepped forward, eyes roving over the stone in front of me. I was vaguely aware of Sylivia joining me on my left, while Fade sat down, panting through his bandages on my right. After a moment, I lightly shook my head and looked down at him. “Is this it? I…don’t see anything out of the ordinary.”

Fade caught his breath and then nodded up at me. “I think so. This is the point where the Death Aether is strongest, I think, in the entire cave. It’s, uh, kind of hard to tell, though. Like I said, the Aether is really thin in the first place. It’s more like…” He stopped to think for a moment before continuing slowly. “It’s as if there was a powerful source of Death Aether right here…but it’s gone now.”

When I dutifully translated Fade’s words for Sylvia’s sake, considering she didn’t have a Familiar bond with him, she frowned and stepped forward. Now standing in front of the wall, she brushed a hand over it. “Nothing,” She murmured, shaking her head. “There’s nothing here. Except…could there be a hidden door?” She turned to look at me with a raised brow. “From what you told me, when your party came upon the entrance into the Netherim bunker, the door was hidden…”

“…until I placed my hand on it.” I finished for her, frowning. “That’s partly true. There wasn’t a door there, but there was something at the very least to indicate it was probably a door. Just like in Hollow Hill, I couldn’t tell what it was; it was just sealed. If there’s a door here…it’s a hidden one.” I stepped forward to join her, removing the glove on my right, flesh hand. I hesitated for a moment before I set it on the stone.

Both Sylvia and Fade noticed, though it was my lover who spoke. “What’s the matter?”

I looked over my shoulder at her. “I’m pretty sure that I also told you that the door into the bunker was trapped. One of the gods had laid a trap on it that drew all of us into a curse that nearly killed us. Not only that…but Aurelius encountered a trapped entrance as well. It killed his entire party.”

“The more I hear about these gods, the more they seem like huge assholes.”

I managed a small smile at Fade’s words, but it died quickly. “I’m not sure I should do this. What if there’s another trap? Something on the scale of the one that Aurelius found? That apparently killed everything in a radius of miles. To this day, the entire region is apparently still a no man's land.”

I could see doubt appear on Sylvia’s face as well, as my words got to her.

I…probably should have thought about this earlier. But frankly, I hadn’t really been expecting another sealed entrance. I had thought all of those had been marked, and this would be something different. I had to assumed that any actual Netherim repository was something which had been known to the gods, and thus wiped out during their rebellion.

This might just be too risky.

“Nah,” Fade said casually, letting out an almost amused lupine chuff in the real world. “It’s fine. Go for it.”

The tense atmosphere around us immediately popped.

I blinked, and I turned an incredibly confused look down at the wolf to my side. “What the Hell do you mean, it’s fine? How would you know?”

“Because I know,” Fade said simply. At my continuing look of incomprehension, he rolled his eyes at me. “Look, Nate. I get that you have terrible monkey-man senses, but I don’t. I can’t tell that there’s a door here, not for certain. But what I can tell you is that there’s no kind of malicious intent to the Aether in the area. A lot of my training with Taran was about sensing things like what you’re saying. You two-legs wield Aether like it’s some kind of club. No matter how good you get at shaping it, Spiritual existences can always tell when it’s been shifted around because you leave an impression in the energy. It’s something you can’t perceive, but I can. And there’s no hidden emotional impression. No desires. No intent. No curse. You’re good to go for it.”

“But…” I started weakly, raising an impotent finger. “You…said there’s a scent of death in the area?”

Fade rolled his eyes at me again. “Death isn’t an intent, Nate. It’s not malicious. It’s a state of being. Impersonable. Honestly, what was that old man teaching you?”

I opened and closed my mouth for a moment, feeling…mildly offended, honestly.

Monkey-man, am I? Just a clumsy ape swinging a stick to knock the banana down, eh?

Well, this monkey-man knows where you sleep, and just how much you hate the smell of garlic. I’ll get mine.

Eventually.

I sighed and turned back to look at Sylvia. “He says it’s fine,” I said tiredly.

She merely blinked at me before shrugging and gesturing for me to proceed.

I did so, turning back to look at the wall. As I did, I couldn’t help but mutter under my breath. “Ook ook, me big dumb hoomie, me no sense thing good.”

“Exactly,” Fade piped up smugly. “You get it. Good hoomie.”

I placed my bare palm against the stone of the cavern wall, directly over where Fade said the sense of Death Aether was coming from, and…

Nothing happened for a long moment. Enough that started to turn a suddenly smug look down at Fade…

Only for a grinding sound to grace the air, coming from the very stone of the wall my hand rested upon.

My eyes snapped back to it, just in time to watch as a large rectangular segment of the wall began to slide upwards. The motion wasn’t smooth, coming in fits and starts, as if the resistance of stone on stone was almost too much for the moving slab. But gradually, whatever enchantment was animating the apparent door won out, and the monolithic rock disappeared from sight.

Leaving only a gaping darkness before us.

“I suppose you told us…so…” I started, turning a resigned look down to Fade…only to stop.

The Spirit Wolf was suddenly much more alert, having stood up onto his paws. His emerald gaze was sharp and focused upon the darkened portal that had appeared before us. Furry ears twitched back and forth upon his head, while he was taking deep, long breaths from his nose, visibly scenting what I could only assume was the Aether coming from the doorway.

I tensed myself. “Is something wrong?” I asked warily, unslinging my staff from its place on my back. The amber capstone glowed faintly as I directed a small amount of Mana into it. Not much. Just enough to prime it.

My Familiar didn’t answer, too focused on the darkness.

Wordlessly, Sylvia copied me, unsheathing first her Lunar Basalt blade, and then the obsidian one she had inherited from Nyx. The Sculpted woman held them loosely at the ready.

“Fade?” I asked in a louder, more urgent tone.

That snapped him out of it. He blinked and turned his gaze up to look at me. “Ah…” I heard him start and stop tentatively. “I…don’t think anything is wrong? It’s just…you really didn’t feel that?” At the disbelief in his voice, I shook my head. “Wow, okay. Yeah. Um…this is definitely the place the Death Aether was coming from. That door must have been acting as a seal, because wow! When you opened it, it was like I got slapped in the face with more Death Aether. Whatever is in there, it’s packed with the stuff. I was just trying to see if there’s anything more to it.”

I frowned. “Are we going to be dealing with the undead?”

That was…typically what happened when large quantities of Death Aether gathered in one place, after all.

“Uh…no, I don’t think so,” Fade answered, in a somewhat uncertain voice. When I furrowed my brow at him, he hastened to explain. “Look, I’ve never encountered this before, okay? Taran took me around a bit to introduce me to the undead-”

(Excuse me? He what?)

“-And this isn’t like that,” Fade continued, unaware of my inner outrage. “There’s typically a sense of…of almost anger to the Death Aether wherever there are Undead. They’re mindless, yeah, but there’s a rage to them and the Aether that birthed them. This, though? It’s calm. Peaceful, almost. Like…”

“Whoever died in there…” I said slowly, turning my eyes back to the portal. “Was prepared to die. Accepted it, even.”

“Yeah…” Fade said quietly, trailing off.

We stood in silence for a moment before Sylvia broke it. She took a step forward to stand in front of us and looked over her shoulder. “I will be the vanguard,” She said simply. “You two, take up the rear.”

I firmed my expression and nodded at her. I followed immediately after Sylvia, while Fade followed after me. In my weakened state, I was the weakest person here, and as much as it rankled me, I needed more protection.

Besides.

Fade had the better senses. It made sense that he took up the rear.

For some reason, I almost expected the door to slide down behind us when all three had fully stepped inside the entrance. It didn’t, though. Our point of retreat remained, thankfully, free.

Thank goodness for small favors.

We ventured deeper into the unknown.

Together.
