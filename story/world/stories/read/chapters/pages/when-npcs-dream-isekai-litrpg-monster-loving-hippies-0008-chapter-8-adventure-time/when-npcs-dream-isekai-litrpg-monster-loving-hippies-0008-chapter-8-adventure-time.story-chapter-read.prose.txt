It was great to see the night sky again. Really.

For one, I hadn’t seen the sky in a week. For another, there was no light pollution here. I could see millions of stars. And what made it even more beautiful—it wasn’t Earth’s sky. I knew my constellations. These weren’t them.

This was an alien sky. And that was… kind of amazing.

Also, I was free. Finally. And my friends—yes, I have those now, plural—wanted to celebrate my glorious release at a specific tavern.

“Come on,” I said, dragging my feet. “Can’t we celebrate somewhere else?”

“There isn’t any other place,” Juliette replied. “And I’m sure Hildi has already forgotten about your little… incident.”

“If I get stabbed to death, the blood is on your hands.”

“It’s a deal,” Juliette grinned.

When we got to the tavern—The Eyeball & the Spoon—I realized how creepy the name actually was. We sat as far from the bar as physically possible. Ramon got us each a cup of ale.

“You’re probably underage,” he said, “but this is a special occasion.”

“I’m almost eighteen,” Juliette said. “Which is legal in France.”

“Well, they don’t have stupid drinking laws here,” said Ramon, raising his cup. “To Geshem’s freedom—and the many adventures we’ll have in this magical fantasy world!”

“Cheers!”

“Cheers!”

“Kampai!”

The ale was sweet and malty—less bitter than I expected. We got good and drunk. Juliette especially. We laughed, joked, and made ridiculous plans about forming an adventurer’s party with warrior, wizard and a healer.

I had a really great time.

Eventually, Juliette fell asleep on the table, and we decided to call it a night.

I figured I was supposed to sleep in Ramon’s room. He’d started working two days ago as a blacksmith’s apprentice. He’d already earned some silver and rented a room at the inn—15 sigots a day, which was half his income.

So… yeah. He had a room. And Juliette was a girl and half passed out. Obviously, she wasn’t going to share a room with either of us.

Still, was there any way I could make a case for sleeping in Juliette’s room without sounding like a complete creep?

“You’re sleeping in my room,” said Ramon with a grin, reading my mind.

We walked Juliette to the mansion, then headed to the inn. Ramon’s room wasn’t big, but it had a couch. We stayed up talking more about video games and old D&D campaigns until I drifted off.

The next morning, we went shopping.

The town had a basic equipment shop. With Juliette and Ramon’s combined silver, we got two bronze short swords, a long knife Juliette liked, some leather armor, and boots.

The swords were heavy. Or maybe I was just weak. Either way, I doubted I could swing one effectively. A knife was probably more practical for me, but didn’t feel very “heroic.” And there was no way I was letting Ramon be the only one with a real sword.

We discussed ways to get more money—maybe smuggle small stuff out of the mansion and sell it—but decided it wasn’t worth the risk.I also tried to sell some of my old Earth clothes—Nike sneakers, Iron Man t-shirts, colorful prints that should be exotic here. But the shopkeeper looked completely uninterested, which was fine. I wasn’t ready to part with my Iron Man shirt anyway.

So, it was time for Monster Hunting.Was it smart? Probably not. We could have spent another week or two in town. Learned more about the forest. Found out exactly what kind of monsters lived there. Got better gear. Maybe recruited another player.

It wasn’t just Ramon’s peer pressure.I wanted this too.

I was missing too many puzzle pieces. I desperately wanted data. I wanted to know how leveling worked. What loot looked like. What monsters were like.

And we’d be careful. We wouldn’t go deep into the forest. Just scout the edge. Only take on low-level monsters—the ones Ramon and Juliette had already spotted.

The thing was, the monsters weren’t from any Earth fantasy textbook. If they were, I could’ve had at least some basic knowledge about them—like slimes, goblins, or skeletons. But the monsters here were weird. And that gave me a feeling of… wrongness.

The three of us walked about two miles to the forest’s edge. It was thin here—trees spaced out enough that we could still see the town behind us.

There were insects here. I was trying to figure out if that weird beetle was alien or not—some insects look really strange on Earth too. But the more I looked at the plants and bugs around me, the more obvious it became: these were not from Earth.I stomped a weird ant-like thing. It died normally. No loot drop. No sparkling light. Just a dead bug.

“Here’s an Armadillo Care Bear,” Ramon said, pointing at a dog-sized, horned, bear-like creature with scales and claws.

“And a Scorpion Suricata,” he added, pointing at a smaller, cat-sized... fluffy scorpion? It had four legs, a tail with a stinger, no pincers, and sometimes stood on two legs like it was scouting.

“Hold on,” I said. “Who came up with these names?”

“Whoever finds them first gets to name them,” Juliette said. “You don’t like Armadillo Care Bear?”

It looked more like a pangolin-cat-deer to me, but since it was her name, I didn’t want to say anything mean. “It’s great,” I said diplomatically. “But all your names are two or three mashed-up animals, what about short versions? Like... Bearmadillo? Or Scoricata?”

“They translate badly,” Juliette said. “At least in French.”

“Same in Filipino,” said Ramon. “Better keep them simple. Let’s try that Suricata Scorpion near the tree.”

We approached carefully. As we got close, the thing jumped and sprinted about a hundred feet to another tree, then stood up and watched us, curious.

“Okay,” Ramon said, “we’ll need to surround it. I’ll circle around and hit it from behind. You two walk toward it slowly, keep it distracted.”

“If it runs again, we don’t follow,” I said. “We don’t go deeper into the forest.”

“Agreed,” said Ramon. “We stay within sight of the town.”

We moved. Ramon circled wide, and Juliette and I approached the creature slowly. It stared at us. Ten feet away now. Still watching. Ramon crept around the tree behind it. The thing didn’t hear him—its senses weren’t all that great.

Then—Ramon struck. The blade slashed through one of its legs and half its tail. Blood —Red blood sprayed. Bone. Flesh. And then—

The scream. A screech, high-pitched and horrifying. None of us spoke. Ramon stared. Juliette’s mouth was slightly open.“Please,” Juliette finally whispered. “Finish it. Please.”

Ramon blinked, snapped back, then drove his sword into its belly. The creature twitched, squealed a final quiet sound… and then stilled. A moment later, it dissolved into thin air.

No loot.

“I… I felt something,” Ramon said quietly.

“Like, stronger?” I asked.

“No, not stronger. Just... something tiny. Subtle. Like something changed inside me. I think—this is EXP. If I kill more, I’ll level up, eventually. I know it.”

“No more,” Juliette said, clearly shaken. “I really can’t. I feel sick.”

I felt sick too. We were city people. We ate meat, but we never saw animals die up close like that.

We were idiots. If this was a game, it was obviously extremely realistic—so really, what did we expect from killing living things?

“We should go back,” I said. “The creature’s scream might’ve attracted more.”

“I think you’re right,” Ramon said—and then he froze, pointing. “Look.”

Three more Armadillo Care Bears were emerging from the deeper forest. They were walking alongside a cow-sized beast with six legs. Were they together? That was… unsettling.

“I don’t like this,” I said. “Let’s move.”

We turned to leave—only 200 feet back to safety. Then—

Two more Care Bears emerged from the side. Less than 100 feet away.

“Guys…” I said.

And then one jumped out from behind the tree next to us—like it had been lying in ambush. It lunged at Ramon, claws outstretched, and ripped into his arm, hurling him to the ground.

“RAMON!” I shouted.

Ramon screamed, fumbling for his sword, but the creature was already on him.

I froze. I—was trying to find my sword, I think. But everything happened so fast.

The creature tore into Ramon’s neck with its claws. Blood sprayed. It was already over.

Ramon was dead. It happened so. freaking. fast.And then his body disappeared. Like the monster. Like… a game.

“RUN!” I screamed. I was already running.

Another Care Bear burst from behind a tree, blocking our path. I shouted, “This way!” and pointed toward a clearer route.

We ran. I tripped—some root or something—and slammed onto rocky ground. My head rang. Blood. Juliette grabbed my hand and pulled me up.

Then—another Scorpion Suricata leapt from a nearby branch, latched onto Juliette’s leg, and stabbed her with its tail.

She screamed.

I kicked it off her with all my strength, and it went flying.

We ran. Ran like hell.

We crossed the forest’s edge and didn’t stop until we reached the town.
