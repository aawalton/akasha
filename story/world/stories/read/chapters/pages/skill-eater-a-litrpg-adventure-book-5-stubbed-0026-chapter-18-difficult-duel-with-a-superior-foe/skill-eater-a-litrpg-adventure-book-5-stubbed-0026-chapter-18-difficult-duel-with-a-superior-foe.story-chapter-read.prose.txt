The aspirants assigned to the other gladiator moved into another room, along with the spectators who wanted to watch. His group stayed where they were, along with most of the audience. His pride was pleased by this turn of events, although they probably wanted to watch Opulence in action, rather than Edge himself.

As he squared off against Vivian and took his stance, he decided which skills he wanted to use. There wasn’t any point in tipping his hand during an evaluation match, especially anything that might reveal the existence of his Unique core. On the other hand, he needed to start making a name for himself if he wanted to qualify for any of the exhibition matches that offered juicy prizes, so he didn’t want to get his ass kicked either.

For this stage of the assessment, Krix decided to give everyone a taste of what it felt like to fight in an official match. He provided a theatrical introduction before signaling the start of the duel, modulating his voice like he was working the crowd.

“Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to the main event of today’s evaluation. While it is a humble beginning, you might be witnessing the first steps of a gladiator who will rise to the top of the ladder and whose name will be known across the galaxy… once the feed is restored.”

Krix’s joke evoked a few titters from the audience.

“I am pleased to introduce Edge Vasher, a gold-grade beast hunter who has survived the myriad perils of Ord to arrive at this moment. Despite the disaster that means death is forever, he has braved the frontier’s dangers and reached the depths of stage two. While he has never graced these hallowed halls, he is no stranger to putting his life on the line.

“Edge’s skillset is a question mark—his abilities a mystery—but today, here and now, we will get our first glimpse of what he can do. Armed with a masterwork glaive forged by our very own Helena, he is a contender for the polearm specialty ladder in addition to the main. Raise your voice and give him a warm welcome. A preview of the roaring crowds to come.”

The audience applauded, with a few whoops and hollers tossed into the mix.

“Evaluating our contender is an arena veteran. A stage-three warrior who has performed admirably this season and will climb higher still before the curtain falls. She is skilled with her swords and so much more. Put your hands together for Vivian Black!”

The spectators cheered as she flourished her weapons, taking the volume up another notch. It seemed Vivian had drawn some fans to today’s proceedings. Her stance was balanced, efficient, and effortless. The prospect of crossing blades with her was intimidating, even with magic protecting him from harm.

Krix’s showmanship hadn’t only roused the crowd’s passion. The man’s theatrics had Edge’s blood pumping too, putting him into the same state of mind he entered when engaging a deadly foe, which was probably the point.

This isn’t a pure evaluation of my combat prowess. He’s testing whether I will thrive under the pressure of all those eyes or collapse like a house of cards in a stiff breeze.

“Now that our introductions are complete, it’s time for some fun. Gladiators, activate your passive skills and auras, but wait for my mark before doing anything more.”

With that, Edge switched on Fear the Season’s passive component, All-Seeing Gaze’s combined mode, Aegis of Nullification, Mental Fortress, Second Wind, and Warlord’s Mantle, grinning as his magic turbocharged his attributes and enhanced his perception. Counter Disruption was shielding Rejuvenation and Shadow Play, neither of which he planned to deploy during this duel.

He considered adding Dragon’s Greed but decided to save it for his first true challenge in the ring, where the element of surprise could mean the difference between victory and defeat. He didn’t want to reveal the full extent of his passives either, but it was difficult to discern the details without witnessing their effects. Simply switching them on wouldn’t give anything away.

Edge was sizing Vivian up while completing his preparations. He could already tell she was significantly more skilled with her swords than he with his glaive, which only made him more excited. Thanks to the unique senses Skill-Eater provided, knew she was using several passive powers to bolster her attributes. There was an aura enhancing her blades, which would paralyze him if the weapons landed a direct hit.

All such thoughts were driven from his head when Krix raised his hand. “Ready. Steady. Begin!”

The instant those words left his lips, Vivian exploded into motion. She moved so quickly that her form blurred before Edge’s eyes. She would have taken him out then and there if he didn’t have so much experience fighting opponents above his stage. Thanks to Gaze, he could track her approach effortlessly and combined with the Speed bonus from Fear and Mantle, he raised his glaive in time.

He caught one sword on the polearm’s blade and the second on the shaft, backpaddling as the gladiator followed up with a side kick that would have knocked him across the room if he hadn’t seen it coming. He didn’t have a chance of winning with bladework alone. His best bet was to use his powers at an opportune moment, create an opening, and land a finishing blow before she adapted.

He lunged with Opulence at the same time he let loose with Roar, adding its Intimidating Presence for good measure. The concentrated blast of killing intent made Vivian’s eyes widen, slowing her reaction time as struggled to overcome the fear-inducing skill. She was forced on the defensive, using magic that made her boots slide back like she was staking on ice.

“Not bad,” the gladiator acknowledged, unleashing a lightning fast-combination slashes and thrusts with an occasional kick mixed in. Thanks to Gaze, he could track her attacks, but even with his boosted attributes, he couldn’t compensate for the chasm of experience and technique lying between them.

Vivian drove him back beneath her relentless assault, slapping down his defenses, stepping inside his guard, and going in for the kill. That would have been the end if he hadn’t used Repel Water, leaving the generational component off and shoving with the repellant field. Her natural Disruption deflected most of the force, but it was enough to make the woman take a step back, giving him time to retreat to the center of the room and resume his combat stance.

For the next desperate minute, Edge managed to hold her back with his polearm’s superior reach. He had the sense that she was probing him rather than trying to score a serious blow—that he had impressed her enough be treated as a genuine opponent. It was a valuable opportunity to learn how a gladiator fought when the gloves came off, and he could tell she had learned a great deal about his fighting style from that brief exchange.

When Vivian had taken his measure, she executed a beautiful series of strikes, each move seamlessly blending into the next. He deflected the first few, but he couldn’t stop them all. She reached down and trapped Opulence’s shaft with the hilt of one weapon, preventing him from pulling the blade toward her body as her second sword lashed out.

When Vivian’s blade struck Edge’s forearm, she expected the fight to be over. Her slash was sufficient to remove his hand at the wrist, and her magic would paralyze him if the sword so much as scratched him.

That left her off-guard when the ringing chime of steel against diamond resounded across the room. Instead of a fountain of red paint, the glint of gemstone beneath his lacerated armor was revealed. He still felt numb, but the glancing blow wasn’t sufficient transfer much magic.

He switched on Rejuvenation to purge the foreign mana from his system, which was hard to detect since it was internal. The warrior thought he was done for and was slow to notice a spray of paint hadn’t ensued. It was just a half-second opening, but that was enough for Edge to pan his water saw across her face, forcing her to block with the flat of one sword. Scarlet pigment dripped to the ground, simulating a light wound as the high-pressure spray drove her back before she spun out the way and he switched Repel off.

At that point, he decided to go all out. He didn’t use any skills he hadn’t revealed yet, other than Quadra Slash, which was bound to be common knowledge soon if it wasn’t already. He combined it with Harden’s defense, his water saw’s range, and shoves from his repellant field, almost catching her the first time he flared his quadrupled blades to extend their reach.

However, a gladiator of Vivian’s caliber was more than capable of dealing with the threat he posed. Once she internalized what his magic could do, she hit him with a magical flashbang and came gliding in low with that skill that let her skate on solid ground. He tried to back up, but she arrived before he had time to reposition.

She feinted with the sword in her right and unleashed an insanely-fast swipe of her offhand blade.

The strike was so fast that he couldn’t Harden in time, drawing a geyser of crimson pigment from the protective spell as it simulated his throat being slit. That kind of wound couldn’t kill him anymore. He could have healed it quickly enough that he would only have suffered a bit of blood loss without losing consciousness.

Edge knew the magic protecting fighters from fatal wounds was sophisticated enough that he wouldn’t have lost an official match in this situation, but the spell that transformed damage into paint wasn’t good enough to tell the difference. He could have let Krix know and resumed the match, but he decided not to. He didn’t want to reveal his high-speed regeneration and was satisfied with his showing.

Besides, she would have won if she wasn’t holding back, so this is a fair outcome. The crowd went wild as Krix signaled the end of the match. Apparently, Edge had done quite a bit better than they expected, which he took to be a promising sign.

She offered him a cheeky grin followed by a nod of respect, leaving him to get cleaned up while she moved on to next contender. The other matches were interesting, but none of the aspirants could hold Vivian back for more than a few seconds. She gave them a chance to show off some moves, then ended the fights with precision attacks to their vitals.

After Krix thanked the crowd for coming and the attendants finished cleaning all the paint, Edge followed the man up the stairs and into another room, along with everyone from the late stage-two group.

“I hope you enjoyed your first taste of gladiatorial combat. Our staff will review the footage from your matches and assign your starting rungs for the general and specialty ladders tomorrow morning. At that time, you will be eligible to participate in low-rung brawls until you climb high enough to qualify for the challenge system, along with any exhibition matches that are willing to accept you. I wish each and every one of you a long and illustrious career at the Grand Arena of World’s End.”

Edge thanked Krix and asked if he could attend the end of the mid stage-two evaluations, which were taking place two floors below. After receiving permission, he descended the stairs and took a seat behind the magitech barrier protecting the spectators from incidental fire.

As he’d hoped, he made it in time to watch Melenia’s final match. She used Swap and Force Cleave to press the gladiator hard before she was defeated. It was an impressive showing. The warrior was far above her stage, and she hadn’t shown her strongest skill, Titan’s Axe. Her experience as a guardswoman makes a big difference when dueling weapon wielders. He smirked. And she knows how to fight dirty.

He offered an enthusiastic round of applause, along with wolf whistle that made her blush. After her attendant brought the evaluation to a close, they decided to check out the list of exhibition matches taking place over the next few months and see which ones they were interested in qualifying for.

Edge walked with Mel to a level of the administration annex he hadn’t been in before, which was off-limits to non-gladiators.

The ground floor was devoted to registration. The second floor was for issuing official challenges to other rung holders, and scheduling matches when someone challenged you. They weren’t ranked high enough worry about either just yet, but half of the floor was devoted to exhibition matches. The top floors belonged to the people who ran the establishment and the stairs were barred by elite warriors he wouldn’t want to run into in a dark alley.

The duo came to a stop before a massive magitech screen containing information on every exhibition match for the rest of the season. Some were intended for low-stage gladiators, but the ones with good prizes had strict criteria for admittance and the most exclusive matches were invitation only.

“This is a lot to take in,” Edge said. "Let’s start with anything promising taking place within the next two weeks, then look at the next month or so. I don’t really care about climbing the ladder, but some of these prizes are amazing and might help us get a handle our pressing problems.”

“That sounds good to me. We can make a short list of everything with decent rewards, and see if we have a shot at qualifying for any of them in time.”

They picked up some scratch pads and pencils from a pile intended for that purpose and spent the next hour going through the listings. There was an attendant stationed to answer questions, and he took advantage of her expertise. With her help, Edge picked out two matches he was interested in entering. The Stage-Two Duos’ Clash was a little over a week away, and he had an idea that might let him come out on top.

The second exhibition was called the Carnage Cup. It wouldn’t take place until after his meeting with Coalition, but the prizes were topnotch. It was invitation only. However, one of the ways you could secure an invite was winning a major exhibition match, including the duos’ clash. If everything went according to plan, he might be able to win the clash and clear the conditions to enter the Carnage Cup at the same time.

Best of all, Edge could and Melenia could fight side by side if they met the requirements in time to register. They needed to be ranked within the top hundred warriors of their stage, and the cutoff was in four days, which meant they had to work fast.

Fortunately, the challenge system didn’t kick in until rung one thousand, which meant they could climb by participating in the low-rung brawls that took place daily, including one that was scheduled for tomorrow evening. They were looking forward to battling in front of a roaring crowd and planned to get in some practice a little later in the afternoon.

Melenia wanted to hit the showers and purchase training from an axe master, so Edge kissed her and left the arena complex—deeply satisfied by how his day had gone thus far. Today had been a wonderful change of pace from the endless ordeals he’d undergone since arriving on Ord. It was nice to spend some exciting hours in the company of someone special without having to worry about the big picture for a change.

He decided to step into his inner world for his daily Rue-training and avatar-bonding session. He watched the team participate in one of their mock-battles while petting the golden fox. When the bout was over, he played games with the diverse collection entities residing in his skill village. His avatars had grown intelligent enough to understand complex concepts, and he was eager to work on some advanced combos and strategies over the next few weeks.

When his time was up, he stepped into the Lakelands to steal three skills. He added their experience to Rejuvenation before skinning and butchering the unfortunate predator that had mistaken him for easy prey, wishing that all his problems could be resolved in such a direct manner.

Edge returned to World’s End and headed to the Lost, planning to grill up the fresh meat for dinner and get in some intensive training with Mel before calling it a night.
