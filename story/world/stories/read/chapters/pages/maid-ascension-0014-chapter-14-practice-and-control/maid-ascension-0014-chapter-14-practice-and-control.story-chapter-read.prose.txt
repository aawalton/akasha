By the next day, the library no longer felt like a ruin. Well, at least, the nearest space by the door didn't .

Deeper in, it was still a broken mess. Still, progress had been made, and no one could deny that.

Lira moved through the space, focusing on permanent tasks rather than the ever-encroaching dust.

The dumping ground near the western wall had grown into organized piles, stacked properly rather than thrown together. Broken wood was separated from the cloth, and metal scraps sat in their own corner.

Paths now crossed the room, and Lira no longer needed to think about where to step.

In the morning, she worked without pause: lift, sort, and stack.

By the time the light reached its highest point, Lira set down the plank she had been dragging and straightened.

Her arms trembled slightly. Dust clung to her skin, her clothes, and her hair.

She exhaled slowly.

Enough of that for today, she thought.

It was time for magic.

Her alcove felt smaller than she remembered.

Or perhaps the library no longer felt as claustrophobic, which made everything else seem more confined by comparison.

Lira stepped inside and closed the tapestry behind her, shutting out the lingering scent of dust. The air here was warmer, protected from the draft.

She reached for a tallow candle and whispered,

“Candira fila curad.”

The wick caught at once. She still felt giddy that she could do this.

She poured a small measure of water into a shallow basin and removed the scarf protecting her nose. Slowly, deliberately, she washed her hands. Dust bled into the water in thin grey streams, swirling before settling at the bottom.

Her fingers stung where splinters had grazed them, but she cleaned them anyway. She wiped her hands on the inside of her apron, brushed dust from her sleeves, and ran damp fingers through the tips of her hair.

She took her time, letting the small routine steady her before returning to the book.

When she was done, she sat for a bit, allowing her breathing to even out before reaching into the hidden gap in the wall.

She pulled out the fire primer.

The first pages passed quickly beneath her fingers, and she did not linger on them this time.

The same rambling tone greeted her, filled with half-mad instructions.

“Still yourself like unlit coal.Do not chase fire. Let it rise in you as breath.Fire does not obey hunger. It answers presence.”

Between the paragraphs, scattered like seeds, the same phrase appeared again and again.

“Candira fila curad.”

The candle spell.

Lira traced the words lightly with her finger.

“What are you…” she murmured.

It had to be a language.

The words were not random sounds or meaningless gibberish. she could tell they were structured somehow. Yet whenever she tried to examine them too directly, the meaning of the individual words slipped away from her.

She could tell in general that it had to do with fire, candlewick, and the concept of ignition, but she could not tell which word was which, nor any grammatical explanation.

The harder she focused, the less she grasped, like trying to hold water with a sifter.

Her gaze moved further down the page.

The next section shifted in tone. The writing felt less poetic and contemplative, as if the author had tried to explain something to himself.

“Flame moves.The author's narrative has been misappropriated; report any instances of this story on Amazon.From what is full to what is empty.From what is crowded to what is still.Fire is not made. It is allowed.”

The lines were tighter, more compressed, and even the hand writing was neater.

Lira frowned and read the passage again, this time more slowly.

Heat moves from hot to cold.

That much she understood.

But the text twisted around that simple idea, stretching it into something broader and harder to grasp.

“Heat is spread but never gone.

The cold is not and never is.

Movement comes from the mind, the fuel from the soul.

Control is a given, the will is iron.”

She tried to follow it, to force meaning out of the garbled words, but her thoughts tangled. The more she chased the idea, the further it slipped away.

Lira leaned back slightly and closed her eyes.

That was not how this worked.

The first time, she had not understood the text at all. She had stopped trying to understand and simply let the words settle in her mind.

Stillness was a requirement, not understanding.

So she did that again.

She let the tension leave her shoulders and allowed her thoughts to slow. The library faded from her awareness, followed by the cold and the dull ache in her hands. Her breathing steadied as she focused less on meaning and more on the rhythm of the words.

Time passed without her noticing, as she entered a deep trance one more time.

Then something shifted.

A small, quiet click echoed in her mind, just like before.

Lira opened her eyes and looked at the candle, watching the flame burn steadily on the wick. She raised her hand slightly, hesitating for only a moment before speaking.

“Pika fila frushum.”

The flame trembled, then shifted.

It was a small movement, barely noticeable, but it was there. The fire responded to her.

Lira leaned forward, her eyes sharpening with focus.

She spoke again.

“Pika fila frushum.”

This time, she focused on the flame itself. She imagined it rising, separating from the wick, lifting into the air.

The flame stretched upward.

The wick darkened as the fire detached, leaving it behind.

For a brief moment, the flame hovered in the air.

Then it vanished.

Lira leaned back slowly, a grin forming as understanding settled in. This spell controlled already existing flame. Unlike the first cantrip, this one required something already burning.

She glanced at the book, then back at the candle.

She tested it again.

“Candira fila curad.”

The wick reignited immediately.

“Pika fila frushum.”

The flame lifted once more, smoother this time, holding its shape for longer before fading.

Lira watched carefully, committing every detail to memory. If she wanted to rely on this, she needed to understand it beyond a simple demonstration.

She lit the candle again and reached for a scrap of paper from her pile. Holding it carefully, she brought it close to the flame until the edge caught. The fire spread quickly, thin and eager as it began to consume the surface.

“Pika fila frushum.”

The flame pulled away from the paper under her will, lifting just enough to break its spread. The burning stopped where she forced it to stop, leaving behind a jagged black edge that marked the boundary of her control.

She smiled. At least this time, she was not restricted to candle flame.

She reached for another piece of paper.

This time, she changed her approach. She let the paper catch fire again, but instead of stopping the flame entirely, she focused on shaping it. She imagined the fire narrowing, compressing into a thin line rather than spreading outward.

“Pika fila frushum.”

The flame resisted at first. It wavered and tried to crawl along the edge as before, threatening to spread beyond her control. Lira tightened her focus, holding the image steady in her mind.

“Pika fila frushum.” she repeated the incantation.

Slowly, the fire responded.

It thinned.

It drew inward.

A narrow, blackened line burned across the paper, cutting it cleanly in two before the flame flickered out.

Lira exhaled slowly as she looked at the result in her hands. The edges were uneven, but it worked.

Her thoughts moved quickly from there, turning over possibilities. Controlled burning was useful; she could imagine herself throwing torchfire into faceless attackers, or standing in a circle of safety amidst a burning forest.

This could be a utility spell, too. She could destroy small things, alter materials, or cut through obstacles without tools.

Her grip on the paper tightened slightly as another thought followed.

There had to be limits.

Size, distance, or duration. Something would restrict how far she could push this. The flame had already shown signs of resistance when she tried to shape it too precisely. That resistance would only grow with scale.

She needed more testing.

Her gaze drifted around the alcove and then, briefly, toward the tapestry blocking her view of the rest of the library. Testing those limits here would be reckless. Everything burned out there: dry wood, paper, and dust… especially the dust.

Testing within reason.

The idea of using the kitchen ovens crossed her mind. There would be space there, and fuel enough to experiment more freely. But the thought did not last long.

She would be too exposed: Too many people moved through those halls, too many chances for someone to notice something, and she did not know if magic could be sensed or tracked.

She did, however, know what would happen if someone realized what she was doing.

Some risks were not worth taking.

Lira let out a quiet breath and forced her thoughts to settle. Excitement would push her too far if she let it. She needed control, not just over fire, but over herself.

Her eyes returned to the book.

A third of it remained unread.

Her fingers hovered over the edge, feeling the faint warmth emanating from it. The temptation crept in again. She could keep going. She could see what the next section held and learn another spell before the day ended.

No.

Then she pulled her hand back.

She should learn magic the same way she learned languages and calligraphy, one slow step at a time. If she continued now, she would divide her attention. She would chase the new before she had fully understood the old. That path led to sloppy mistakes, and Lira Velora didn’t make sloppy mistakes.

She spent an extra hour practicing the new spell, trying different combinations and visualisation before she decided that she had learned enough for one day.

Lira closed the book carefully and slid it back into its hiding place within the wall. Her gaze lingered on the candle for a moment before she reached out and pinched the flame out between her fingers.

The alcove dimmed as the light vanished.

By the time she stepped back into the library, broom already in hand, there was no trace of what she had been doing.

She moved through the dust and debris as she always did, steady and unremarkable, just another maid working through an endless task.
