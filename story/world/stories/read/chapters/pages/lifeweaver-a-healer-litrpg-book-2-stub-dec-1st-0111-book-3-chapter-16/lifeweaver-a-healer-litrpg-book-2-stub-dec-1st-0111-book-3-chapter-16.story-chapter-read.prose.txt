They appeared in a deserted, broken-down village with a large keep on the outskirts. It was surrounded by a large wall and other fortifications. In the distance, tall mountains almost seemed to loom over wide expanses of grass and forest.

Luke stopped right inside the portal, eyes wide as he mouthed, "What the fuck?"

"What?" Ray asked, following after. He wrinkled his nose, sniffing the air. "Smells like shit."

Curtis joined them, and then Hannah, who drew in a sharp breath and held up her hand to her mouth. "Oh, no."

"What's up?" Nick asked, following close on their heels.

Ignoring them for the moment, Luke removed the mental block that held Weaver's Perception in place, allowing the skill to reach out to the entire group. Breathing a sigh of relief, he pulled it back in. "No radiation."

"Wait," Curtis said. "This is the same place?"

"Yes," Hannah said. She walked up to the nearest building and put her hand to the stone, shaking her head. "The barrier protected the keep and the village." Turning to Luke, he saw tears forming in her eyes. "What happened, Luke?"

"Someone mind filling us in?" Nick said, stepping up to the group.

"We beat this dungeon recently," Luke explained. "Everyone was fine when we left. Now, it's all reduced to rubble."

"Or it hasn't been built up yet," Ariel said, breaking in.

They all turned to her, except for Ray, who sauntered off, peering around buildings, heading closer to the keep.

"What?" Luke asked.

"Sounds like you talked to the locals?" Ariel asked. "Did you happen to get a year?"

Hannah shook her head. "As in what year it was in their calendar? No."

"Pity," Ariel said, looking around. "Anyway, the Portal Tester told us the dungeon is at level 33."

"That means a nice chunk of experience," Nick said, nodding in approval.

"Hold on, what was that about getting a year?" Luke asked.

Ariel gestured for Luke to calm down. "It's just another theory, but I think these places, the dungeons, are snapshots in time. The portals bring us to some big event in these worlds' history in an attempt to have us fix things, or at least reach a better outcome."

"Time travel?" Luke asked.

"Relative time travel, I guess. It's a new theory of mine. This place proves it! I need more data points, though, but I think I'm on to something. You should read my blog."

"A blog?" Dirk asked. "What year is it?"

Nick shook his head. "She'll be useless in a fight now."

"No, I won't," Ariel said.

"Guys! Over here!" Ray shouted from two streets closer to the keep.

Luke and the others hurried over to see what the fuss was about and found Ray pointing to the side of a wall, where someone had made a crude drawing. The white paint was coming off in places and looked worn, as if it had been subjected to the elements for a long time.

"What is it?" Hannah asked.

"Look at that," Ray said with a grin, pointing to the side. "Doesn't that look like someone we know?"

Luke leaned in. "Is that...?"

"It's you!" Ray shouted, face twisted in glee. "With that robe? Can't be anyone else! And look at that! People kneeling with their hands clasped. They're praying, dude!"

"Could be Hannah," Luke murmured.

She shook her head. "No. There are no runes on mine, not like those."

"Sick robes, by the way," Nick said, inspecting the painting with the rest of his crew.

"The future," Ariel breathed.

"Guess that dragon wasn't the only threat," Curtis said, nodding to what looked like a tall knight with red-gleaming eyes behind a closed helmet. The knight stood behind the humans, surrounded by corpses. It wasn't clear if the humans prayed to Luke or if they were begging for their lives.

New Quest: Take vengeance upon The Conqueror and free the lost souls.

Reward: 15,000 Credits or 5 attribute points.

"There you have it, Luke," Ray said. "Let's go kill the boss and get rich."

"We've never seen a quest give points before," Dirk said, his voice tinged with awe.

"You can thank Luke for that," Nick said. "He brought the dungeon's level up to this point. It'll be awesome."

George adjusted his glasses and cleared his throat. "Not if we die in the attempt."

Nick laughed out loud. "Always the optimist, George! Don't worry yourself. As long as we stick together, we'll get through it, just like every other dungeon we've seen."

Ray took a photo of the wall with his phone before turning to point right at the keep. "The boss will be in the throne-room. Let's go!"

"Hold on," Nick said, holding up an arm. "Rushing into things won't end well. First, let's talk strategy."

Hannah nodded in appreciation.

"Healers and casters in the back, while the rest of us kick ass and take names? All good? Let's go!"

"I'll need to stick close to the front to heal," Luke said. "Also, I should heal some of you before we go, and provide some... buffs."

"Fine. Just be careful, and we've got Clementine to help with healing when we need it, but I'll take whatever buffs you can provide."

"You don't have healing like this, Nick," Curtis said, giving Luke a look. "They're sick?"

All eyes turned to Luke, who thought it best to get this part over with. "George, you have an arterial blockage that'll give you a heart attack soon. Dirk has an old knee injury and Clementine... Well, Clementine has..." He cleared his throat. "...A VD."

With those minor things sorted, the nun keeping a blank expression throughout, and an extra deep check to make sure no trace of radiation hid somewhere in Hannah, they were all set to get started once Luke used Guardian Weave to add an extra layer of protection around each person's brain, heart, and meta-heart, including his own. With that many protective weaves, it took almost fifty percent off the top of his mana, meaning he'd never be able to reach higher than that until he dissolved the buff. Since the idea was that he would focus on healing, he figured it wouldn't matter much. As Luke grew in power, he'd strayed farther and farther from the pure healer role. Come to think of it, he'd never quite fit into those shoes. With all these fighters, though, it made sense to take a step back. A short step back, considering how short his reach still was.

Clementine added a buff of her own through a far-reaching aura that helped with mana regeneration, which was always a welcome effect.

"Let's go!" Nick shouted as he pointed with his now-equipped longsword toward the wide-open gate of the keep. As the large group walked, Luke inspected every member of Phoenix Phalanx, along with his friends, to see what they were working with.

Integrated Luke. Lifeweaver. Level 37.

Integrated Hannah. Vesper. Level 26.

Integrated Ray. Assassin. Level 17.

Integrated Curtis. Reaver. Level 20.

Integrated Nick. Knight. Level 24.

Integrated George. Psychebinder. Level 23.

Integrated Dirk. Mirror Rogue. Level 23.

Integrated Ariel. Poisoner. Level 22.

Integrated Clementine. Holy Whisperer. Level 21.

Nodding to himself, Luke read their classes and levels. Considering what Hannah had been through to get to her level, the guild must have been grinding a lot to reach those sorts of levels. In that regard, Ray was woefully behind. Working for the CIA hadn't provided much of that sort of experience, it seemed, though he was catching up. Ariel, being a Poisoner and not some sort of scholar, surprised him a little, but the nun's class made sense. Dirk told them about being a Mirror Rogue, whatever that meant, and George having a class that sounded like some sort of mental mage made sense, in a strange way, since the man reminded Luke of the energy vampire, Colin Robinson, in that show about incompetent vampires in New Zealand. Or was it Australia?

Nick was another surprise. His class, Knight, seemed a little, well, ordinary. Before he had a chance to ask Curtis's brother about the pick, Ray stepped in.

"So, Nick," Ray said, twirling a long dagger around his hand in a show of dexterity. "Why Knight?"

"It is the pinnacle of warrior classes, of course!" Nick said in a loud voice, slamming the flat of his sword against a large, round metal shield he carried. After that, he moved both to the side to show the assorted armor pieces of ring-mail and leather he wore. "The knight rallies all heroes when things are most dire! He is the shining example of valor and bravery, the strong, firm arm of justice without need for magic or flashy moves! Pure strength, agility, and grit! That’s all I need! For the most part, anyway."

Curtis grunted. "He roleplayed a Knight in D&D. He basically stepped into the character."

"Oh, brother," Nick said with a dramatic sweep of his arm. "There are no knights in Dungeons. I played a Fighter. A Champion to be precise!"

"We've got company," Hannah said, nodding toward the keep entrance, where torches had flickered to light, held aloft by a pair of someones in tattered clothes, holding spears. Perhaps 'someones' wasn't right. Monsters. Skeletons.

Ray patted Dirk's shoulder. "Why don't we deal with the guards? Show me what you've got!"

Dirk looked over his shoulder toward Nick, who nodded.

"Let's go," Dirk said, walking a little faster as Ray grinned and disappeared into thin air.
