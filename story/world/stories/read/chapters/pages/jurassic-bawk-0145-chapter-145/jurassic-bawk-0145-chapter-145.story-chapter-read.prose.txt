Charlemagne left the job of cleaning up and repairing the ship to the sailors and flew straight over to the top of the ramp.  Although it was difficult to spot, the rooster noticed the outline of an enormous door that would allow access to the interior.  The only problem was that there was no visible handle.  That never stopped Charlemagne before, however, and it wasn’t about to stop him now.

Clang!

The rooster’s lazy beak strike was completely stopped by the relatively soft-looking wood that formed the outer hull of the enormous vessel.  More interestingly, there didn’t seem to be any mana reinforcing the door at all.  It was as if the Ark itself was simply that tough.

Charlemagne put a bit more power into his next attack, but he didn’t even manage to leave a small indent.  Whatever the giant boat was made of, it seemed that no amount of force that the rooster was capable of generating would break down the door.  So, he decided to take a different approach.  He tried using his brain.

Well, maybe not his brain.  He had come to value the problem-solving skills of his group members, so he would allow them to contribute in their own way.  He was definitely not being intellectually lazy by refusing to think about the problem himself.  Definitely not.

“Bawk!” he yelled at the trio, who were in the process of hopping down to the island.  Technically, only Josephine really hopped down: Phasima floated to the island, while Phatagin redirected his acceleration due to gravity to propel himself forward.

“Sweet, sweet land!” the Attendant cried after landing with a decidedly unladylike grunt.

“Bawk!” the rooster called again, causing Phatagin and Phasima to hustle over and join him.  Charlemagne moved back to allow the pair to examine the Ark’s door.

“I’m going to stay right here,” Josephine announced, plopping down at the base of a small tree that provided almost no shade.  The rooster ignored her and turned his attention back to his Squire and the former Empress of the Forest, who were locked in a heated conversation.

“Why can’t you just grab the door with your Core and pull it towards you?” Phasima asked pointedly.  “That would be so easy.”

“It is not ‘easy’ to perform such a feat,” Phatagin shot back.  “Irregardless, there is no way that the problem would be able to be resolved so simply.  The door must be some sort of puzzle!”

“Well, we could stop all this bickering if you would just try,” the Vessarian argued.  “What’s the problem?”

“I…I already made the attempt,” the pangolin admitted.  “I was unable to budge the door at all.”

“Then try harder!”

“That is inadvisable!  If I were to increase my level of effort, it would harm my body.”

“Well, maybe I can help you then.  I can lower the air pressure on this side of the door, and then it would be easier for you to pull the door outward.”

“But what if the door opens inward?” the pangolin asked, confused.

The Vessarian shook her head.

“No, that’s not very likely.  The door should swing open outward.  That’s like, System 101.”

“Suppose the door was meant to slide horizontally in a recess?” Phatagin surmised in response.

“We can try that after, I guess.  But we’re never going to figure it out if we just keep talking about it and not actually trying anything,” Phasima groused.

“Well, I am amenable to attempting…” the pangolin began to respond, but Charlemagne interrupted.

“BAWK!”

“All right, all right, calm down,” Phasima said.  “Let’s try popping it open with negative pressure.”

The Vessarian instructed Charlemagne to create mana shields against the hull of the Ark in the shape of a box, ensuring that the entire area where they presumed the door to be was covered.  She then had him create a small opening in the top shield before filling the entire “box” with water, which displaced the trapped air.  Closing the top hole and opening one in the bottom caused the liquid to flow out, creating a vacuum above the water line.

“All right, try again,” the Vessarian said when the water had almost completely drained out of the box.

The pangolin strained his Momentum Core with visible effort, spending all of its stored energy to pull the door toward him.  Nothing happened.

In the aftermath, Phasima and Phatagin got into another angry exchange over what their next step should be.  Several nasty accusations were exchanged, and for a moment it looked as if the two would come to blows.  Charlemagne’s patience wore thin as they argued, but not, apparently, as thin as Josephine’s.

The Attendant suddenly pulled herself to her feet, brushed the sand off the back of her dress, and marched over to the gangway.

“I declare, the first moment of peace I’ve had where the entire world is not lurching like a drunken Earl searching for the lavatory,” she muttered angrily.  “Why am I the only one in this outfit that has any brains?”

But as soon as Josephine placed her foot on the sand just in front of the gangway, a deep grating sound emanated from somewhere deep within the Ark.  The door, for the outlined portion was indeed the entrance to the vessel, slowly began to slide into a recess.

“I was correct!” Phatagin panted, winded from both the exertion and the arguing.  “The door moves horizontally on a sort of track after all!”

“Yeah, yeah, there’s no need to get all smug,” Phasima said as she crossed her arms in front of her chest and sulked.

“Bawk!” Charlemagne declared as he slipped into the Ark.

“Coming,” Phasima answered before floating in after him.  Phatagin allowed her to go in first before following.

The inside of the Ark was well-lit and quite spacious.  There were rows of wooden benches on either side of an aisle that was so long it was difficult to tell just how many rows there were.  Wooden torches blazing with smokeless fire were set into the wooden wall at regular intervals.  But not everything was made of wood, or at least not entirely.  The wooden ceiling was covered in frescoes depicting scenes from Earth’s mythology.  Unfortunately, the symbolism was completely lost on the trio.

“I seem to recall that most humans wore more clothing than the ones depicted here,” Phatagin remarked idly as he examined the ceiling.  “Perhaps a high Durability attribute obviates the need for protective garments?”

“Bawk,” the rooster answered dismissively.  He wasn’t interested in what was above him.  His only concern was what lay ahead.  His Special Ability was going completely wild, indicating that something delicious lay just ahead.  He advanced at an even pace, testing everything with his mana sense in case there were hidden enemies or traps.  Thankfully, his caution proved unnecessary, and he arrived at the far side of the room without incident.

There, Charlemagne found a raised dais covered in rich blue carpet.  He ascended the steps, moving slowly as he checked each one carefully.  All he could see was more stairs in front of him, but despite the optical illusion of climbing an endless staircase, he could sense nothing nefarious.  And then he was at the top, staring at the most beautiful weapon in the world, which sang to him promises of the sweetest violence.

The Squiggles helpfully provided him with a description of this magnificent artifact.

Nimrod’s Unbreakable Cudgel

According to legend, Nimrod hunted when the world was young, before beasts feared mankind and before monsters learned to skulk in the dark.  His favored weapon was not a spear, sword, or bow, but a simple cudgel of dark wood that he tore from a tree at random during a battle where lesser weapons failed him.  Spears shattered.  Axes chipped. Bowstrings snapped. The cudgel alone endured.  When Nimrod finally breathed his last, old and full of years, he was granted his greatest wish: to hunt for all eternity.  He and his Unbreakable Cudgel were given the task to hunt nameless horrors in the far reaches of the cosmos.  While endless vigilance is required to safeguard the Earth from danger, it is a price he is more than willing to pay.

The Legendary Cudgel was situated on top of a small wooden pillar in the center of the dais, encased in glass and sitting on a pillow of the plushest blue velvet.  The rooster used mana manipulation to gently lift the glass enclosure up before setting it reverently aside.

Then he ate the Cudgel.

It wasn’t easy, especially because the legendary weapon was not a smooth cylinder, but rather sported a number of knots and bumps.  Nevertheless, Charlemagne was determined to get it down, and he eventually succeeded.

Phatagin and Phasima finally caught up just in time to see Nimrod’s Unbreakable Cudgel disappearing into the rooster’s mouth.

“I believe I win,” the pangolin laughed as he looked over at the Vessarian.

“Forget about that!  Charlemagne, what are you doing?  That’s the key to us finishing this Floor!”

“But that is incorrect,” Phatagin rebutted as he watched the rooster contort his neck in an attempt to get the entire weapon into his stomach, “it may be a key to finishing the Floor, but it is not the only key.  Charlemagne would not have eaten the weapon if that were not the case.”

“I disagree with that last bit, but you’ve got a point,” the former Empress of the Forest answered.

“Bwwwwk,” Charlemagne added before belching.

“See?” said Phasima.

Another round of bickering ensued, which the rooster tuned out.  Nimrod’s Unbreakable Cudgel had finally reached his stomach acid.  A huge burst of power erupted from the legendary weapon, and Charlemagne braced himself for the pain that always accompanied consuming something so formidable.  But something was wrong.  The surge shut off as quickly as it had arrived, leaving the rooster confused and tantalized.  There was so much power stored within the weapon, and it was supposed to be his.  Not only that, but the Cudgel was only one of twelve legendary weapons.  That meant he could eat a lot more of them.

As Charlemagne was dreaming of overwhelming power, the Squiggles arrived with messages.

Congratulations, you have consumed a sufficient amount of ERROR.

Error: Key Item DetectedWarning: Key Item Not ImmutableDigestion PossibleWarning: Digestion Will Destroy Key ItemDirective: Key Item May Not Be DestroyedSearching System PrecedentPrecedent FoundApplying HotfixNotice: Setting Key Item Immutability Flag to YesWarning: System Debt AccruedNotice: Applying Achievement

Congratulations, you have earned the Achievement “Digest the Indigestible”.  You have managed to consume a small portion of an item that was never meant to be consumed and that the System needs for other purposes.  Consider this a consolation prize since you were robbed of a very nice meal.  Attributes are twenty-five percent more effective.

Charlemagne didn’t know what to think.  The Squiggles had robbed him of his amazing meal!  But they had given him a nice Achievement as compensation.  But the power, the sheer power that was contained in Nimrod’s Unbreakable Cudgel was worth far more than just one Achievement, even if it did come with a nice bonus.  The rooster decided that, on the whole, he was mad.

“Bawk!” he angrily announced to the group before stomping off toward the exit.

Phatagin and Phasima rushed to catch up, their argument completely forgotten in the wake of Charlemagne’s explosive news.

“Wait, what does he mean we’re leaving the Floor?” the Vessarian cried.

“I do believe he has obtained insight as to where the Floor’s exit is,” the pangolin answered as the pair floated away from the dais.

“How do you figure that?  You think the club in his stomach is telling him where to go?” the former Empress of the Forest asked.  “That makes no sense!”

“I imagine that, from a particular point of view, none of this makes any sense,” the pangolin answered sensibly.  “I have not spoken to many other sapients, but the feats that Charlemagne performs on a regular basis seem to be rather mind-boggling.”

“Whatever, let’s just go.”
