Hey! Training finished early, so I just got home. Want to come over a bit earlier?

My heart thumped as I stared down at the message from Duncan and read it again. A frantic mix of emotions thrashed in my chest, and I didn’t know how to make sense of any of them. I was scared and worried yet excited, and that only made me more anxious.

It wasn’t three yet, though. Obviously, Duncan was aware of that. He’d invited me to go over early, but that wasn’t the plan. I was meant to have more time before I even had to think about leaving, and I hadn’t done my hair yet. It was still a frizzy but somehow limp mess, and I was going to straighten it. Or curl it, maybe? I hadn’t decided, but I was going to do something.

My thumbs twitched towards the keyboard on the screen, but they didn’t actually touch it. They froze millimetres away, and a frown appeared on my face as I tried to work out what to say to him.

I wasn’t ready. Literally or mentally. I still needed to do my hair and get dressed. Plus, I needed to pack a gym bag and find a way to stop panicking quite so much. That would take time, if it were even possible. I couldn’t just rush to his house immediately.

But I did want to see him. I was looking forward to it. Nervous too, obviously, but I was mostly excited. And relieved. Seeing his text come through quietened one of the fears I’d been experiencing all morning.

I was so worried that at any moment, I’d get a text from him saying that he’d changed his mind and didn’t actually want to see me, but that hadn’t happened. If anything, it was the opposite, and I didn’t know how to react. I wasn’t prepared for that.

Part of me was almost tempted to say no. It wanted me to lie and tell Duncan that I had other plans, but I’d see him at three, like we agreed. Perhaps it was just my stubbornness, but that idea was so appealing, and I had no real idea why. I didn’t like it when plans changed. Maybe that was one of the reasons why too?

There was no way I could leave so early, I told myself as I chewed my lip, and my gaze scanned Duncan’s message one more time. I was planning on telling Mom I was going to the gym, and she was way less likely to believe me if I said I was going to spend five or six hours there. I needed an excuse or a reason.

Slowly, an idea came to me. Classes. There were so many running over the summer. Someone had put a flyer through our mailbox about them, and Mom had commented on it. She’d told me I should sign up for some once I got back from Scotland, rather than spending the rest of my holiday sitting in my room and never seeing the sun or doing any exercise.

There had to be some on in the afternoon, I reasoned. I couldn’t recall what there was, but there were probably a few. I could tell Mom there were a couple of different classes I wanted to go to, but they were a little spread out, so I’d have to wait around in between. That would take most of the day, and Mom had been the one to suggest it in the first place, so she couldn’t get too annoyed at me for that.

Well… she probably still would, but it wouldn’t be that bad. Or at least, I hoped it wouldn’t be.

A smile pulled at my lips as I closed the messages app and found the class timetable for the gym, scanning it quickly. There was one in forty minutes that I could say I wanted to go to, and another later on, at five. That would work. Then, if I wanted to stay at Duncan’s house for a little longer, I could just say I wanted to go to the gym after the classes.

Mom would probably judge me for my choice, and I was fully aware of that. I could already imagine her laughter when I told her I was planning on attending a gong bath yoga and meditation session, even though I doubted she even knew what it was. I didn’t really know what it was either. I’d never been to a gong bath, and the description on the website didn’t help either.

The other class was self-defence, though. She’d be fine with me attending that. It had been years since I last went to one, and Mom was the one who insisted I attend in the first place, so she’d probably be happy I was going back. Or, she wouldn’t be too annoyed about it, at least.

I could go to Duncan’s early. The realisation made my smile grow, even as my anxiety began to swell and my pulse raced in my ears. I was going to do it. I was going to go to Duncan’s, and we were going to have our date or whatever it was.

My phone vibrated again, and I almost dropped it in surprise. I hadn’t been expecting that. I didn’t think Duncan would send me another message, but his name appeared at the top of my screen, and I clicked it with trembling fingers.

But don’t worry if you’re busy or have plans or anything, he’d written.

I let out a shaky breath that I hadn’t even realised I was holding and bit my lip as I tried to work out how to reply. I needed to phrase it carefully so I didn’t sound too eager or lame, and I didn’t want him to know I’d spent all morning panicking and trying to prepare to see him. That wouldn’t help. I wanted him to think I was cool, that I was unbothered by the thought of going on a date.

There was no way Duncan would ever think that, though. I was fully aware of that. He knew me too well to mistakenly believe that I was cool.

Oh, it finished early? Cool, I can come over now if you want! I wrote, staring at the words before deleting them all.

That sounded far too eager. I had to do better, to sound more relaxed and casual.

A huff slipped from my lips as I tried again.

Oh, how come it finished early? I typed. Yeah, I’m not really up to much, so I can come over a bit early.

My eyebrows drew together, and a grimace appeared on my face as I tried to work out why that still sounded bad to me. I couldn’t quite figure it out, though. It just wasn’t good enough, and I frowned as I deleted the message again.

Hesitation filled me as I started to type again, only managing to write one word before giving up. Maybe Phoebe could do better. She was much more skilled at talking to guys than I was, so if I sent her a screenshot, she’d be able to help. We could come up with a response together, and it would be perfect.

But that idea worried me. I doubted he was just staring at his phone and waiting for me to reply, but Duncan might have noticed the typing symbol appear on his screen. If I stopped typing and instead spent, like, ten minutes talking to Phoebe about what to say, that would seem weird. He might think I didn’t really want to see him.

I can do this, I told myself as I took a deep breath. It was just Duncan. I’d sent him so many texts before. We’d been friends forever, and it wasn’t a big deal. I could handle a simple message like that without having to run to Phoebe for help.

Oh yeah? Everything okay? I started. That sounds good! I’ll leave in a few.

That was good. It was relaxed and didn’t sound too eager or worried, I decided, my finger moving towards the send button before freezing. Or maybe the exclamation mark was a little too enthusiastic? But then, if I took it out, it sounded too cold. I didn’t sound excited enough. I needed to keep it in.

I hit send, fear simmering in the pit of my stomach. Part of me almost couldn’t believe I’d just done that. I was so scared and nervous, even though, realistically, I knew there was no reason to be. I was being ridiculous, but I couldn’t help it.

Still, when my phone vibrated again, I jumped so hard that I almost threw it across the room. Duncan had replied so quickly. Maybe he had been staring at his phone and waiting for my response.

Yeah, everything’s fine. I just tapped out of conditioning early, he’d written.

Worry pulled at me, and I chewed my bottom lip. The last time Duncan did that, his coach had gotten annoyed at him. He didn’t like it when people on the team skipped any part of training, and the guy scared me. Duncan loved him. He’d been coaching the team for ages, and Duncan said he was super nice, but I didn’t quite believe that.

Oh really? How come? I wrote, reading the message multiple times before hitting send.

I was probably just overthinking it. There was most likely another reason why he’d left early that had nothing to do with me. Duncan could have been tired, or maybe he’d gone too hard at the gym the day before and didn’t want to push himself too hard or something. That would have made sense, I told myself before the three dots appeared on the screen, and my thoughts ground to a halt.

Duncan seemed to be typing for a while, and that worried me. It took him more than a minute just to write his response, and I couldn’t do anything as I waited. I just stared at the bouncing dots, waiting for the text to come through.

I just wasn’t really feeling it today and I had a few things I had to get done, was all he said.

Confusion washed over me. How had it taken him so long to write that? And what did that even mean? Why wasn’t he feeling it? And what did he need to do? I had no idea, and that only made it worse.

Was it my fault? My confusion began to shift towards worry once more. Had he been planning to do stuff later, but then realised I was coming around, so he couldn’t and had to leave training early instead? I hoped not. Even though he said he wasn’t really feeling it today, it still made me feel bad.

Hesitantly, I began to reply to Duncan.

Oh really? I can always come around a bit later if you still have stuff to do? I wrote. Or we can always reschedule?

I didn’t want to. I was looking forward to seeing him, but the thought of ruining his day made me feel so guilty that I couldn’t stop myself from offering.

No, no, it’s okay. I’ve already finished everything I had to do, was his immediate response. Plus I’d much rather see you than do sprints.

The smile returned to my face as I read the messages from Duncan. I didn’t want to read into it too much. It didn’t necessarily mean anything. He’d mentioned before how much he hated running the sprint drills that his coach made them do, but it was still nice to see him say that he wanted to see me. That made warmth blossom in my stomach, marred only slightly by anxiety.

You sure? I checked.

Promise, Duncan wrote. So… wanna come over now?

My heart pounded. I did, but I was terrified, and I wasn’t sure if I could actually do it. My body felt weak from the nerves. I felt like my legs might not support my weight if I were to try to stand, and my hands were trembling. It would have been so much easier to say no and stick to the original plan, but that would mean I’d be just as nervous later when I finally went to Duncan’s.

Unless I told him something came up, and I couldn’t make it anymore.

The thought flashed into my head, but I pushed it away without much consideration. I didn’t want to do that. I wanted to see him. I was just scared, but I could deal with that. I’d been scared before. Fear was nothing new to me.

Sure! I just need to finish getting ready and then I’ll come over, I wrote.

Cool, want me to meet you at the end of your street? Duncan asked.

My lips twitched as I read that message. It was sweet of him to offer. He normally did, ever since his mom said something about how dangerous it was for a girl to be walking around by herself, even in a safe village like ours, but I still found it cute.

Nah, it’s okay. I’ll meet you at yours?

See you soon, he replied.

I stared at the screen for a few more seconds, even though I was pretty sure he wasn’t going to send another text. My brain wouldn’t do anything else. It was like it needed a few seconds to process and adjust to the change of plans, and I wanted to do something, to start getting ready again, but I felt like I was frozen.

After a few seconds, my thoughts leapt into action. The shift was so jarring, and I was suddenly so aware of how nervous I was. I already knew. It was impossible to miss, but it had somehow gotten even more obvious, and I could see my chest moving far too quickly out of the corner of my eye as I left Duncan’s message and clicked on Phoebe’s name.

Sooooo Duncan just texted me, I told her.

Oh shit! Saying??? she sent back.

He left training early.

Okay???? Phoebe replied.

And he said I could come over now if I want, I texted.

There was no pause before she began typing again.

Omg. You said yes, right?

I snorted softly.

Yeah. Going to finish doing my hair and go now, I wrote.

Shitttttt!! Okay, okay. You have to text me! Not whilst you’re with Duncan, obviously, Phoebe clarified. But if anythign happens, hide in the toilet and text me!!

I will, I promised. But I don’t think it will. Like I think we’ll just play video games or something.

We’ll see!! Phoebe wrote. But wait, don’t text me. You can do that on the walk. Go get ready!

I am, I am, I lied.

Good! Ohhhh I can’t wait to hear about everythinggggg!

I smiled at her message before locking my phone and staring at my reflection. I met my own gaze, seeing my wide eyes and obvious anxiety. I wished I could do something about it, that I could cover it up as easily as I did the bags under my eyes, but that was much harder. I had practice, though. That meant I was pretty good at hiding my emotions from others, but sometimes it was harder.

It would be fine, though, I told myself as I reached towards my hairbrush. Duncan probably wouldn’t notice, and if he did, he’d probably just think it was about something else. I doubted he’d assume I was anxious because of him.

Well, it wasn’t technically because of him, I reasoned as I pulled the brush through my hair before grabbing my straighteners. He didn’t make me nervous. It was more the situation. The date, if it was even that. I didn’t know what to expect. It felt like there was so much uncertainty, and I didn’t know how to cope with that, but I hated uncertainty.

My mind spun as I did my hair, trying to tame the flyaways that seemed intent on sticking out. It was hard. My hands were still shaking, and I almost burnt myself multiple times, but I managed to get most of them. A few stubborn pieces still refused to fall into place properly, but I wanted to hurry up and leave, and I was pretty sure it was the best I could do anyway.

Setting my straighteners down again and switching them off, I glanced at my reflection before sighing. I was tempted to leave my hair down. It looked alright down, and I almost always wore my hair up. I wanted to do something a little different, to make it look like I’d put slightly more effort into my appearance, but anxiety had made the back of my neck slick with sweat, and I could already feel hairs clinging to it.

I hated that sensation so much. It was hard to ignore, and it made me so uncomfortable for some reason. I was already uncomfortable and distracted, and it had only been seconds, but it was making me feel even worse.

Plus, I was meant to be going to the gym, I told myself. I couldn’t exactly have my hair down for that. It would get in the way and stick to my face, and the temptation to rip it all out would be too high. I knew I could always just tie it up once I got to the gym, not that I was actually going, but that seemed unnecessary. It made more sense to me to just keep it up the whole time.

Shifting onto my knees, I grabbed a hair tie and threw my hair into a high ponytail. Immediately, I felt better. My brain felt a little clearer, a little less jumbled by overwhelm, and I didn’t look too bad. It would do.

I stood up and looked around my room, trying to work out what I needed to do next. I was still in leggings and a top. I had to get changed into the dress Phoebe and I picked, and then I needed to put my clothes away again so Mom didn’t work out I’d been frantically trying on what felt like every item of clothing I owned.

And I needed to pack a bag of gym stuff, I told myself as I looked around for my backpack before spotting it on the floor next to my bed. It made sense to pack it first. All of my clothes were still out, so I could just shove some things in there and then tidy the rest away.

Grabbing the first pair of leggings and t-shirt I could see, I shoved them into the bag before hurrying across the room towards my chest of drawers in search of a sports bra. I could feel myself rushing. I was moving too fast and eager to leave as soon as possible, but there was nothing I could do about it. I was filled with adrenaline and nerves, and they made it impossible to slow down.

I threw the bra into my bag and ripped open another drawer, rooting around for a swimming costume. If I ended up staying at Duncan’s place for even longer than I expected, I could always just say I decided I wanted to go swimming after classes and the gym. The pool was open until ten, and I wasn’t sure whether Mom would believe I’d spent, like, eight or nine hours working out, but I could probably convince her of it if I had to.

Once I’d found my swimming costume and added it to the bag, I turned away from my chest of drawers and surveyed my bed again. A sigh tumbled from my lips as I took a step towards it, wishing I could just deal with it later.

It was a mess. There was so much on it, but somehow, it didn’t take me too long to put everything away. I even made the bed and folded most of the items of clothing. I didn’t always bother with that.

Sometimes, I’d just shove things into my drawers. I didn’t think it made that much of a difference, but Mom had told me off for it before. She said it made clothes creased and wrinkly, but I’d never really noticed it.

I hesitated before pulling one of my drawers open again as I realised I needed to get some underwear and a pair of tights out. And maybe I should change my bra. The one I was wearing was kind of plain. It was just a plain black one, but perhaps a different one would be better, just in case.

The thought made my hands freeze, and a flush started to creep across my chest as my breathing sped up again. Just in case of what, I asked myself. In case Duncan was to see it? We hadn’t even kissed yet, and I was already worrying about him seeing my bra? That felt ridiculous. I didn’t even know whether it was a real date!

Still, I couldn’t stop myself from grabbing a different bra, one that I’d bought with Phoebe. It was lacy and a little prettier than the one I was wearing, but I wasn’t changing in case Duncan saw it, I told myself. It was just because it was a better shape for the dress I was wearing.

I dropped the underwear on my bed and reached for the hem of my shirt, beginning to lift it before stopping as a random thought came to me. I wasn’t sure where it came from. It seemed like it was out of nowhere, but I couldn’t dismiss it.

Should I brush my teeth again?

It probably wasn’t necessary. I’d already brushed them once that morning, and I hadn’t eaten anything yet, so I doubted my breath would smell bad, but the worry had already begun to consume me.

Even if nothing happened between us, I still didn’t want to have bad breath. That would be horrible. No one wanted to talk to someone whose breath smelled, and if it was a date, then that would be even worse. I doubted it would happen. It felt so unlikely, but Duncan would never kiss me if I had bad breath.

But I couldn’t brush my teeth after I got changed, I realised as I let go of my shirt. I might get toothpaste on the dress, and that would be so annoying. I’d be furious at myself, so I had to do it first.

If Mom were to come into my room whilst I was in the bathroom, she’d see it, and that would be so bad. She’d demand to know why I was wearing a bra she’d previously told me she didn’t like, and then I’d never be able to convince her I was going to the gym.

Carefully, I lifted my pillow and tucked the underwear underneath before replacing it. I scrutinised my bed, making sure nothing was visible or looked weird, before smiling to myself. It looked completely normal. Mom would never suspect a thing.
