Okay, so I’ll see you soon hopefully? I typed, hesitating before adding, But if I don’t text you again for a little bit, it means Mom’s decided to come with, so I’ll come over after we’ve walked Rex?

I didn’t want to risk Duncan misunderstanding and something going wrong, but it seemed pretty straightforward. I wasn’t sure how he could misinterpret what I’d written, but I knew I didn’t always explain things as well as I could have.

Talking about Mom to other people always made me uncomfortable, though. It was hard to do. I hated saying anything to anyone that might give away how hard things were with her at times, and that made it even harder to plan things with Duncan.

My phone buzzed, pulling me out of my thoughts, and I blinked a few times before glancing down at it.

Okay! I’ll get ready to go if you manage to get out without your mom, read Duncan’s text.

I let out a breath before locking my phone. Duncan seemed to understand, and that was good. It meant if Mom did insist on joining me on the walk with Rex, it would be fine. Duncan knew it might happen, and I’d still get to see him after, so it would suck, but it wouldn’t be too bad.

Plus, I was really looking forward to going for a walk with him and Rex. I wasn’t sure why I was so excited for it, but I was. I’d been fantasising about it ever since we picked Rex up. Since before then, really. It just seemed like such a nice and normal thing to do with a boyfriend.

We’d been spending a lot of time together, and we’d made out a bunch, but were we dating? Was he my boyfriend? Were we even exclusive? For all I knew, he could have been meeting up with a bunch of other girls and doing the same with all of them. Or more. Perhaps things went further with him and the other girls.

I forced myself to take a deep breath, trying to get my racing thoughts under control again. I had to stop. I was overthinking things, and there was no point. It wouldn’t help anything. It would just make me even more anxious, and I had to be careful. If I wanted my plan to work, I couldn’t seem nervous or jumpy. That would just make Mom suspicious, and I couldn’t let that happen because then she’d definitely insist on coming with us.

It had been a little while since she’d done that last, so it felt like it was only a matter of time. Mom would assume I was sneaking about because I was either going to buy drugs or because I was meeting up with a boy, and for once, she’d be correct.

Obviously, I wasn’t planning on buying drugs. I didn’t even know anyone who sold them. Apart from that guy from school. I was pretty sure he was a drug dealer, but I didn’t have his number, and I had very little desire to even try drugs. There was a part of me that was tempted. An almost morbid curiosity burned at the back of my mind, but we’d been told too many horror stories in school. I wasn’t sure whether any of them were true, but I didn’t want to risk losing my mind.

I swiped across on the messages from Duncan to delete them before finding my messages from Phoebe and beginning to read through them. We texted each other so much that it took a few minutes to scroll through them all and get rid of any mention of Duncan and anything else Mom would get annoyed about, but I did it.

All I needed to do was go downstairs and get ready to go out quickly. As long as I had time to put my shoes on and get Rex’s harness and lead on before speaking to Mom, she probably wouldn’t come with me. If we were ready to go, she might feel like she had to rush to get ready too, and I doubted she’d be bothered to do that.

Plus, she didn’t seem too worried about Rex anymore. She had checked on us so often the day before, but I didn’t think she’d even walked past my room since I’d woken up that morning. It seemed like she’d decided Rex was not a danger to me anymore.

Although she did come into the kitchen when I went down to take him out and feed him earlier. It wasn’t until my alarm went off at nine thirty that he’d appeared to have even roused, and I was pleasantly surprised. Lily had warned us he might be a little unsettled for at least the first few nights, but he seemed fine.

“Do you want to go for a walk, Rexie?” I asked him.

Immediately, he was on his feet. His tail wagged even faster than before, and he let out a quiet yet excited bark before throwing himself off the bed. His legs scrambled as he landed, lunging towards my bedroom door and pressing his face against the crack. It seemed like he was trying to force it open through sheer determination, and that sight caused a giggle to slip out of my mouth.

Rex looked back at me, his expression indignant, but that just made me smile more.

“Sorry, boy,” I told him as I hurried towards him, grabbing my keys and slipping them into my pocket on my way. “There you go.”

He squeezed through the door as soon as I began to open it, bounding out into the corridor and running towards the stairs before turning to face me. He rushed back and circled me, seemingly just as eager as I was to get out of the house, and I couldn’t help but speed up.

I had to be quick. Especially once I got downstairs, I told myself as I reached the top of the stairs. But I couldn’t seem as though I was hurrying. I needed to make it seem like I was behaving completely normally, like nothing was going on, and I was just getting ready to take Rex for a walk. I could do that. It wouldn’t be the first time I’d lied to Mom about something like that.

I could hear the noise of the television as I walked past the lounge, but I didn’t look in. I didn’t even risk glancing through the doorway out of the corner of my eye. If Mom stopped me, it would have ruined everything.

I laughed as I straightened up, wiping my cheek with the back of my hand before bending again. I was more careful that time, though. I made sure to keep my face out of range of Rex.

I’d already run through it a handful of times whilst I was upstairs, though. I knew what I was going to say. I just wasn’t sure how Mom would react. She was unpredictable. It all depended on what kind of mood she was in.

I was so hopeful that it would go well, though. I wanted it to go exactly as I’d planned, and then I knew Duncan, Rex, and I would have a lovely time on our walk. We’d have to stay away from the busy roads, obviously. We wouldn’t go anywhere near the main road that wove through the village, just in case my mom decided to go out looking for me.

It seemed like too much of a coincidence, and that might make Mom want to check my phone or keep a closer eye on me until I went back to school.

Then, as long as I resisted the urge to make out with him unless I was certain we were alone and no one could see us, it felt unlikely that anyone in the village would tell her that they saw us together. I could do that.

Probably.

“I’m just going to take Rex out for a walk. Do you want to come?”
