With a bloody sword in hand, Wu Kangming stood there without any signs of the fatal injury that Song Wei had inflicted on him just a few moments ago. His spiritual pressure, which had been almost depleted in the battle, was once again strong and stable.

“How…how is this possible?” Shen Bolin cried out, backing away.

“What are you?” Song Wei groaned, pushing himself up slowly from the crater he'd made in the wall. He ignored his broken ribs and whatever internal damage the shockwave had caused to make his way over to where Shen Bolin had retreated, so that two Peak Lucid Lawbearers were positioned side by side, and the look on their faces was pure shock and fear.

I didn’t blame them because in the Dream World, death was supposed to be permanent, that was how the world worked, so seeing two people come back from certain death had broken something in their understanding of reality.

This was the best chance for us to strike, before their injuries healed enough for them to fight at full strength again, but before either me or Wu Kangming could make a move, Shen Bolin opened his mouth.

"We're no longer a team! This expedition is dissolved!” he shouted desperately at something we couldn’t see. “We'll face the rest of the trial by ourselves!"

I had no idea what he was doing. Did the old man actually think that announcing we weren’t teammates anymore would somehow stop Wu Kangming and me from killing them? Like we'd just accept that and let them walk away after everything they'd done? Not a chance.

Just as I opened my mouth to tell him that, the entire palace began to shake, and I had to spread my feet wider to maintain balance as the step bucked and heaved like something alive trying to throw us off.

The silver veins in the stone pulsed faster and brighter, while the spiritual pressure in the air became thicker and more oppressive, but what made my eyes narrow was seeing the space between us and the two Lucid Lawbearers stretching, like reality itself was trying to pull us apart.

A line of brilliant white light appeared in the middle of the step and expanded into a barrier that rose from the stone and climbed toward the void above to create a wall of pure spiritual energy that separated the step into two distinct sections within a fraction of a second.

That’s when I understood what was going on.

The trial had been watching us, and when Shen Bolin declared the expedition dissolved, the palace had taken him at his word and restructured itself accordingly. This also explained something that had been bothering me since we entered.

Where were all the other expedition teams? If the Celestial Dream Lotus was valuable enough to attract thousands of cultivators over fifty thousand years, why hadn't we encountered any other groups attempting the trial? Why was the stairway empty except for manifestations and our single party?

Now I knew the answer – the palace created individual instances of the trial for each group that entered, so that’s why we’d been in our own private version this entire time, isolated from anyone else who might be climbing their own set of steps toward their own Chamber of the Sleeper King.

When we'd worked together as one team, the palace had treated us as a single entity and adjusted the difficulty accordingly, but now that we'd split into opposing factions, it saw us as two different parties so decided to divide us.

“Do not think that I will forget this,” Shen Bolin’s roar could barely be heard through the barrier. "You will both pay for what you have done!"

The hypocrisy made me feel sick. He should be the one begging for mercy. They'd betrayed me, used me as a disposable resource without hesitation, and even killed me. But somehow, in his mind, I was the one who'd done something wrong.

"Hou." Li Na's voice cut through the chaos as she moved to the very edge of their side of the split. "I expected you to understand that we did what was necessary for the mission. I thought you were the kind of person who could see the greater good."

Something hot flared in my chest. This girl was trying to lecture me now? Now that they were separated and safe? Why didn’t she dare say anything when I had come back to life? She seemed content then to hide behind Shen Bolin.

I took a step toward the gap and started gathering energy for a technique that might penetrate the white barrier, so that I could shut up and show her, but before I could do anything, an ice arm wrapped around Li Na.

The ice arm was massive, easily three times the size of a normal limb, with fingers thick enough to encircle her body. With an explosion of spiritual intensity that I’d never felt from her before, Xie Ling materialized beside the ice construct.

"What Freezes Cannot Escape,” she whispered, revealing that she had been concealing her Lucid Lawbearer cultivation the entire time.

The white barrier between the two sections didn't stop her and her ice construct simply passed through like the wall didn't exist, allowing her to drag Li Na across the gap before the palace could finish separating the two areas.

As soon as Li Na's body crossed to our side, the barrier solidified completely, making it impossible for anyone to break through.

On the other side, Shen Bolin's mouth opened in what was a scream of rage, his one remaining arm gestured frantically, spamming technique after technique against the barrier in a desperate attempt to save his teammate.

But the wall didn’t budge. The palace had recognized the separation as final and wouldn't allow any further interaction between the two groups, we were now unable to see, hear, or sense each other anymore. We'd probably never encounter them again unless we both somehow reached the Chamber of the Sleeper King.

Xie Ling landed gracefully on the black stone, looked at me and Wu Kangming, then released the ice construct to throw Li Na to the ground.

The girl rolled several times before coming to a stop at my feet. With a small whimper, she looked up at me with wide, scared eyes that didn’t have a shred of the self-righteousness and moral superiority that it did only moments ago.

"You were saying?" I asked, towering over her.

Li Na’s head lowered and her shoulders hunched inward, as though she thought she could somehow become inconspicuous enough that I'd forget she was there. But it didn’t matter what stealth technique she had, or what she had to say. The girl was at my feet and my mercy now, and from the terrified look on her face, she knew it.
