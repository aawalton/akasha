As he found himself at the beginning of the integration for the fiftieth time, Ronan realised he hadn’t even considered which difficulty of the tutorial he wanted to attempt this time around. The choice wasn’t so clear-cut.

Actually, it’s quite simple. The only reason I dropped back down from the harder difficulty tutorial was because I didn’t have the strength or skills to defeat the goblin village, he realised. As I am now, that won’t be a struggle anymore.

Your difficulty has been set to [Normal]! If you believe this is an unsuitable assessment, you are free to voice your complaints. Stage 1 will begin in 5…

Ronan thought of his complaint the moment the notification appeared. He wanted to be put into the harder difficulty, but he tried to obfuscate that intention, not wanting the same heat from the assistant that he’d received the last time. Less attention from the authorities was always a good thing.

Your complaint has been received!

Please hold while it is being reviewed…

The delay was very brief, something Ronan appreciated. Bureaucracy wasn’t usually this smooth.

Administrative Assistant Shneevol has reviewed your complaint!

Your tutorial difficulty has been adjusted from [Normal] to [Harder]!

The lack of a note told Ronan he’d been successful in slipping under the radar, so to speak. He was glad for it.

With that annoyance out of the way, it was time to head into the goblin forest for the second time. This would hopefully be the last time, at least until he wanted to obtain more rare skills. His next goal after this was whatever difficulty lay beyond harder. Perhaps nightmare, at long last?

Your difficulty has been set to [Harder]! Stage 1 will begin in 5…

The countdown and ensuing transport weren’t that disorientating. Ronan was well used to them by now.

The moment he arrived in the forest he conjured a dagger and began moving in the direction he’d found the goblins last time he tried the harder difficulty tutorial. About four minutes later he heard the first sounds of them treading through the underbrush.

One snapped branch later, he was plastered against the trunk of a nearby tree, dagger clutched tightly in hand. His mana was cycling and he was already forming a mana bolt in his left hand.

The moment the first goblin rounded the tree, Ronan let the spell fly. He’d used sneak tactics last time, but this time he had no qualms about going in guns blazing.

His mana bolt screeched through the air, exploding in the face of the leftmost goblin. Before the other one even had time to react to his ambush, he was on the green bastard, stabbing it in the throat and heart in quick succession. Its body slumped to the ground as he released his grip on its throat.

The goblin didn’t get back up, and the successive chimes that rang in his ears told him he’d successfully passed the first stage. There were no breaks in this difficulty, but he was fine with that. He had no need for rest this time around.

The sheer quantity of classes Ronan had to choose from was mind-boggling. The last time he’d reached level 10 he’d had a myriad list, but that had expanded by almost double.

Dao Child was still the highest rarity class he had access to, and even breaking past the first threshold in all four paths of cultivation hadn’t been enough to unlock something greater. Perhaps he needed to go beyond the second threshold or even the third to get a mythical class from the get-go.

Or achieve something else equally absurd in the eyes of the system. Cultivating before level 10 was clearly unusual—even more so on a newly integrated planet. The more Ronan experienced of the system the more he understood how much it was geared in favour of those who were born in places that had been long-since integrated in the system. Growing up with it, having parents who had lived with it, and simply having the knowledge of what it offered; all these things would stack up to provide an overwhelming advantage to any with that privilege.

Ronan had a worse starting position than those sorts of people, but his own advantages were far more powerful. He would continually amass knowledge and power until he was an unstoppable force. One day he hoped to be on par with his ancestor, Azathere the Timeless, or perhaps even stronger.

As his goal was to defeat the goblin village, he needed as much power as he could acquire in this iteration. For that reason he chose dao child as his class once more. There was a time for experimenting with new classes and a time for taking the greatest advantage available to him. Now was the latter.

He would even receive two full levels’ worth of dao child’s stats from the moment the infusion finished, given that he had reached level 12 after defeating those two goblins in stage 1. The goblins in stage 2 wouldn’t stand a chance.

He clenched and unclenched his fist. He hadn’t immediately grown stronger, but something within him shifted each time he went through this particular infusion. He guessed it was to do with the second class skill that enabled him to comprehend daos.

He was about to get much stronger, though, given that he had just gained an extra 160 free stat points to spend. He considered his distribution, but felt that his previous strategy would work out just as well if he used it again.

That meant most of his points went into strength, agility, and dexterity, with the rest being spread among all of his other stats. They all benefited him in more ways than one, be they invisible buffs or obvious increases to his resources.

Once he was done he pulled up his status to have a look. Comparing this status to the one he’d had the first time he reached level 12 was hilarious. He’d felt strong, back then—and he had been in comparison to the Ronan who was first integrated into the system—but the Ronan at that moment could kill the original level 12 Ronan with a single slap.
