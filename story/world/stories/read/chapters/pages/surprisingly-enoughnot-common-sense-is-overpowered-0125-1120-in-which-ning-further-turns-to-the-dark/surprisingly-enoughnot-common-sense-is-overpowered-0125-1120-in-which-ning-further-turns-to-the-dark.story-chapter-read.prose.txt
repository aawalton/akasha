A competition between the Qi Condensation disciples of the six great sects.

Ning wasn't particularly surprised.

This was a cultivation world, after all. Sects had reputations to maintain, younger disciples needed experience, and putting the most talented members of the six great sects in one place to see who could beat whom was practically inevitable.

Ning could understand the appeal too. There would be disciples using techniques he'd never seen before, different cultivation methods, talented opponents, rewards, and plenty of opportunities to make a name for oneself.

For many disciples, being chosen to represent the Pure Qi Sect would probably be something worth celebrating.

Ning simply didn't want to go.

"Grandmaster," he called respectfully.

"Hm?" Fu Xuan looked over.

"May I ask something about the inter-sect competition?"

Fu Xuan nodded. "Go ahead."

Ning hesitated briefly before asking, "Would it be possible for me not to participate?"

Fu Xuan looked at him with some surprise. "You don't want to participate?"

Ning had expected that reaction. He had thought about this carefully, though.

"The alchemy competition is only about a month away," he explained. "After that, I still have the Spirit Tempering Pond reward waiting for me, and then there's the secret realm. I especially want to prepare properly for that."

Fu Xuan remained silent, waiting for him to continue.

"I also only recently reached the Seventh Stage, and I've barely begun cultivating the Sevenfold Pure Origin Art. I'm still learning to use my spiritual sense properly, my martial techniques have plenty of room to improve, and there are several other things I've been neglecting."

Ning paused for a moment.

"I understand that fighting disciples from the other sects would be valuable experience, Grandmaster. If the sect requires me to participate, naturally I will. But if I have a choice, I'd rather spend that time improving what I already have."

Fu Xuan gave him a brief look.

"You told me before that one person only has so much time and attention," Ning continued. "I've already learned quite a lot, so I think I should properly develop those things before adding even more."

He hesitated before adding, "There's also the secret realm to consider. If I participate in the inter-sect competition, I'll eventually have to reveal some of my techniques."

Fu Xuan's eyes narrowed slightly in understanding.

A competition between the six great sects was hardly the place to expect privacy. Even if Ning deliberately held back, there was only so much he could hide while fighting opponents strong enough to represent their respective sects. His techniques and even some of his usual habits in battle would inevitably become known.

And there was little reason to give that information away for free.

"I'd rather keep as much of my fighting style hidden as possible until the secret realm," Ning said. "I'll be competing with many of the same disciples for opportunities once we're inside. If they already know what techniques I use and how I fight, I'll be giving up an advantage before we even enter."

Fu Xuan considered that for a moment before nodding. "That's reasonable."

Ning cupped his hands. "So, at least for the next few months, I'd rather focus on preparing."

Every word of that was true. It just wasn't the main reason.

The Heavenly Origin Tower had taught him a valuable lesson about what happened when he spent too much time around Xiao Fan. Somehow, one thing always led to another until Ning found himself dangerously close to acquiring some sort of deuteragonist role, and he had absolutely no desire to see where that particular career path ended.

Admittedly, the Tower hadn't entirely been Xiao Fan's fault. Once everything went wrong, teaming up had been necessary for survival, and Ning himself had willingly competed during the trials.

In his defense, the numbers had been going up.

Every additional step on the Stairs of the Worthy had strengthened his foundation a little more, which made stopping increasingly difficult. He'd also been riding a strange sort of high after passing the illusion trial. Facing his fears, regrets, and everything else the Tower had thrown at him had left Ning feeling unusually cheerful afterward.

Perhaps a little too cheerful, in hindsight.

Then one coincidence had followed another, and somehow he'd ended up fighting alongside Xiao Fan during a disaster beneath the sect.

This time, Ning could already see where things were heading. A major competition between six great sects, hundreds of young cultivators gathered together, and Xiao Fan right in the middle of it?

That had a major arc written all over it.

Ning had learned his lesson.

Sometimes, the only way to win was not to play.

Fu Xuan considered his request for a while before asking, "You understand what you'd be giving up?"

"I believe so, Grandmaster." Ning cupped his hands. "But this is still what I want. Could you please help me?"

There it was.

The fabled reasonable conversation.

Ning had decided this was his strongest available technique.

Of course, genre tradition offered plenty of alternatives. He could suddenly fall ill when the departure date approached, disappear into seclusion, accidentally miss the group leaving, or somehow acquire a mysterious injury that made participation impossible.

Unfortunately, Ning knew exactly how those plans tended to end. A miraculous medicine would appear for his illness, the competition would suddenly become essential to his cultivation, or the prize would conveniently include a treasure capable of healing whatever injury he'd given himself.

Trying too hard to avoid the plot was practically an invitation for the plot to become creative.

So Ning had chosen a far more terrifying technique.

He would explain what he wanted to another human being and ask nicely.

"Please, Grandmaster."

Fu Xuan studied him for another moment before letting out a quiet sigh. "I had rather high hopes for you in particular. A good performance would also have been an excellent opportunity to spread your master's name."

Ning's expression froze.

His master's name.

That changed things slightly.

Ling Xin had taught him, fed him pills, spent an unreasonable amount of resources on him, and even taken on debts for his sake. Ning couldn't simply ignore an opportunity to make her look good.

He thought for several breaths before an alternative occurred to him.

"Grandmaster, I'll place in the top three of the upcoming alchemy competition."

Fu Xuan's eyebrows rose.

"That should spread Master's name too, shouldn't it?"

"It certainly would." Fu Xuan studied him more carefully this time. "But are you sure you want to promise that?"

Ning nodded.

"The competition will be considerably larger than usual," she warned him. "A strong performance will give participants priority when certain spiritual certifications are issued, so there will be no shortage of disciples competing. Several of them have practiced alchemy far longer than you have. Reaching the top three will not be easy."

"I know, Grandmaster." Ning rubbed his nose. "But if I'm asking you to let me skip the inter-sect competition, I can't exactly turn around and give a half-hearted effort here."

That was really what it came down to.

Ning couldn't guarantee that he would place in the top three. There were alchemists with far more experience than him, and pretending otherwise would just be arrogance.

Still, if part of the reason Fu Xuan wanted him at the inter-sect competition was to spread Ling Xin's name, then Ning could at least try to accomplish the same thing through alchemy.

His master had done more than enough for him.

Taking one competition seriously in return seemed fair. Besides, Ning had a feeling the exact result mattered less to Fu Xuan than the conviction behind his words. She wanted to see how seriously he meant them and how far he was willing to go to prove it.

As long as he could show her that, it should be enough.

Fu Xuan watched him quietly for a moment before shaking her head. "Very well. I understand what you're saying. For now, focus on the alchemy competition and your cultivation. We can leave the matter there until the final selection."

The tension immediately left Ning's shoulders.

"Thank you, Grandmaster."

"I admit, I thought you would be interested," Fu Xuan said. "Especially considering your current reputation."

Ning glanced toward her. "My reputation?"

"Word about what happened in the Heavenly Origin Tower has been spreading. Your performance during the trials was already enough to attract attention, and what happened afterward only made more people curious about you." Fu Xuan gave him a brief look. "You're one of the more well-known disciples in the sect at the moment."

Ning had mixed feelings about that, but at this point, things had already gone too far for him to worry about keeping a low profile.

He might as well adapt and make use of it where he could.

"That's one of the main reasons you were being considered for the inter-sect competition this early," Fu Xuan continued. "Still, the final group hasn't been decided yet."

She paused briefly before adding, "The yearly Courtyard Battle is also approaching. The arrangements will be somewhat different this year, and disciples who perform well will be considered for the inter-sect competition."

Ning raised an eyebrow.

Even the Courtyard Battle was changing this year?

A thought occurred to him. "Grandmaster, is that because the Heavenly Origin Tower is still being repaired?"

"Partly." Fu Xuan nodded. "The Tower won't be available as it normally would, so the sect has had to change some of the arrangements. The inter-sect competition also gives us a good opportunity to make the Courtyard Battle more useful this year."

"Because everyone will prepare more seriously?"

"Naturally. Once the disciples know that performing well could earn them a place among the sect's representatives, many of them will train harder. It also gives the elders another chance to judge their progress and decide who should be sent."

Ning understood. The Courtyard Battle had effectively become part of the selection process this year.

Of course, all of this happened right as Xiao Fan became an inner sect disciple.

Ning had only been in the sect for a few years, yet the Heavenly Origin Tower had suffered a disaster; the usual Courtyard Battle was being changed because of it, and now a major competition between the six great sects had arrived at exactly the right time to make this year's event more important.

As expected of protagonist magic.

Ning couldn't help but laugh quietly.

Fu Xuan glanced at him. "What's funny?"

"Nothing, Grandmaster. I was just thinking that this year seems unusually lively."

"That it does." Fu Xuan paused before adding, "Perhaps letting you stay behind wouldn't be such a bad thing after all. At least I wouldn't have to worry about you behaving like your master did at gatherings with the other sects."

Ning blinked. "Grandmaster, what did Master do?"

Fu Xuan's expression became strangely tired.

"Your master was exceptionally talented from a young age." She paused. "Unfortunately, she was also your master."

Ning stared at her.

That explained nothing and somehow explained everything.

"Whenever someone annoyed her during one of those competitions, she had a habit of feeding them whatever bizarre pill she'd recently created and then running away before anyone could catch her."

So his master had been a gremlin from a young age.

Some things truly were innate.

Ning couldn't help asking, "Grandmaster, were those pills dangerous?"

"Usually not."

Ning wasn't sure whether that answer was better or worse. Considering the possible stories hidden behind the word usually, he decided he didn't need to know any more today.

He cupped his hands toward Fu Xuan. "Then I'll focus on the alchemy competition for now, Grandmaster."

Fu Xuan nodded. "Do that. The final selection is still some time away."

"I understand."

"Besides," she added, "you've made quite a promise."

Ning smiled slightly. "Then I'd better make sure I don't embarrass Master."

He bowed respectfully. "This disciple will take his leave."

"Go."

Ning didn't immediately return home. Since he was already out, he stopped by the market to buy another batch of spiritual liquid for Pure Eyes.

His progress with the technique had slowed as it approached the next threshold, and the spiritual liquid wasn't cheap. At this point, however, Ning had accepted that spirit stones were fundamentally opposed to remaining in his possession for any meaningful length of time.

Still, he was in a good mood.

"Ji Ning."

A familiar voice called out just as he left the busiest part of the market.

Ning turned toward it and found Xiao Qing standing in a narrow passage between two buildings, far enough from the main street that passing disciples were unlikely to pay them much attention. Her long white hair made hiding rather difficult, but at least they were mostly out of sight.

She gestured for him to come over.

Ning glanced once at the empty passage before stepping inside. "You know, meeting like this makes it look as though we're conducting some kind of shady transaction."

Xiao Qing laughed softly. "Considering what I'm here for, perhaps we are."

Fair enough.

Ning reached into his storage pouch and pulled out a neatly bound manuscript before handing it to her.

My Sister Can't Be This Cute — Volume Two.

Xiao Qing had already received the first volume some time ago. Apparently, she'd liked it enough that one volume wasn't nearly enough, because she'd soon come back with several very specific requests for what she wanted to happen next.

Ning had naturally obliged.

She had paid far too much for him not to.

One should never underestimate the spending power of a gooner.

By this point, the cold and composed image Ning had once held of Xiao Qing had mostly disappeared anyway. It was difficult to maintain when she was standing in front of him with a dopey smile on her face, happily reading a romance novel she'd commissioned.

Eventually, Xiao Qing lowered the manuscript enough to peer over its edge.

"Brother Ning, do you have the Hands of God or something? This is almost exactly what I wanted."

"Haha, not really. It just happened to come together."

That was technically true. Ning simply wasn't going to explain that another civilization had apparently conducted extensive research into every possible variation of this exact premise.

Xiao Qing returned to the manuscript. After reading another section, her smile deepened.

Very quietly, she murmured, "There are so many tactics here I could use on Brother..."

Sometimes Ning wondered why cultivators kept forgetting that everyone around them possessed supernatural senses. Whispering under one's breath wasn't nearly as effective when the person standing a few steps away could still hear every word.

Ning maintained a pleasant expression and pretended he had heard absolutely nothing.

After reading for another minute, Xiao Qing finally closed the manuscript. "You can publish this volume too. I can't keep something this good entirely to myself, and perhaps if Brother Xiao eventually reads it, he'll..."

Her voice trailed off.

"...get the hint."

Ning silently mourned for the protagonist. He truly had a hard time.

"Of course. If you don't mind me publishing it, Elder Shen can handle the rest."

Ning doubted anyone would connect the characters directly to Xiao Fan and Xiao Qing. He had changed enough details that the story stood perfectly well on its own, and hardworking young cultivators with devoted junior sisters were hardly rare in cultivation novels.

Besides, Xiao Fan was hardly the type to sit around reading Ning's romance novels.

Surely the protagonist would not read novels, especially one like his.

"There is one more thing." Xiao Qing carefully stored the manuscript away before taking out a small pouch of spirit stones. "I want a third volume."

Ning looked at the pouch she held out.

"Already?" Ning was surprised.

"I have a few ideas for what should happen next," Xiao Qing added.

That somehow made the situation more concerning.

Still, Ning accepted the pouch and immediately noticed the weight. He checked the spirit stones inside and found much more than he would normally have charged for another volume.

A full three thousand spirit stones.

Whatever questions he might have had promptly disappeared.

More importantly, refusing perfectly legitimate spirit stones that a client had voluntarily offered went against several of Ning's deeply held principles.

He put the pouch away. "I'll write it."

Xiao Qing's smile brightened.

With their suspicious literary transaction concluded, Ning eventually parted ways with her and resumed his journey home, although his thoughts remained on the manuscript for a while.

Publishing the second volume made sense. He'd already written it, Xiao Qing had given permission, and his novels had become a surprisingly steady source of income. There was no reason to leave a finished manuscript sitting around earning nothing.

There was, however, a different problem.

Ning thought about his existing work.

Senior Sister, Please Behave Yourself!

And now there were two volumes of:

My Sister Can't Be This Cute.

With a third already commissioned.

His steps gradually slowed.

This was beginning to look bad.

He had started writing novels because people in this world apparently possessed an endless appetite for romance and questionable entertainment, and Ning had been perfectly happy to exploit that weakness for spirit stones. He had never expected his growing body of work to start suggesting something about his personal preferences.

Ning could tolerate many accusations.

Shameless author?

Fine.

Greedy?

Spirit stones weren't going to earn themselves.

Writing instead of cultivating?

Those people clearly didn't understand passive income.

But people questioning his fetishes was where Ning drew the line.

He really needed to branch out before the entire Pure Qi Sect decided Ji Ning had some kind of sister obsession.

Ning glanced down at the bottle of spiritual liquid he'd bought earlier and continued home.

Perhaps his next novel should involve absolutely no sisters whatsoever.

...

With the alchemy competition approaching, Ning had spent most of his free time in front of a furnace.

Recently, his alchemy practice had focused primarily on variety. Ning had no idea what the competition would ask him to refine, so there wasn't much point in making the same few familiar pills over and over again.

Healing pills, qi-restoring pills, detoxification pills, body-nourishing pills, and several more troublesome formulas had all gone through his furnace over the past few weeks. Whenever Ning became comfortable with one, he usually moved on to something different.

The ingredients had cost him a small fortune, but the results were showing. First-Tier middle-grade pills had become routine, and on a particularly good refinement, he could occasionally produce a high-grade pill.

Ning was quite satisfied with that progress.

He still had plenty of advantages helping him along, especially when it came to observing ingredients and controlling the refinement process.

Then there was his master.

She had accompanied him through most of his recent practice, watching from nearby and occasionally pointing something out when she noticed a problem.

Her explanations remained as troublesome as ever.

"This one is getting impatient."

"They're fighting."

"That part feels too heavy."

Somehow, those were perfectly reasonable descriptions in his master's head.

Ning had gotten better at understanding her over the year, though, and beneath the strange wording, her observations were usually extremely precise. Sometimes a single comment from her saved him several failed batches worth of experimentation.

It was easy to forget, considering everything else about her, that Ling Xin really was a genius alchemist.

With her nearby, Ning had also been able to try unfamiliar formulas more freely. Whenever something went wrong, there was a much more experienced alchemist sitting a few steps away who could tell him what he'd messed up or simply stop the furnace from exploding if necessary.

Though the last part did not always hold true.

Ning began cleaning the furnace after finishing another batch while Fenghuang hopped onto the table to investigate what remained of Ling Xin's pastries. Before the raven could get too ambitious, Ning caught him around the body and pulled him closer.

Fenghuang gave an indignant caw, though he quickly settled once Ning started scratching the feathers along the back of his neck.

Ling Xin watched them for a moment. "Yinran finally entered seclusion this morning."

Ning looked up. "Senior Sister did?"

"Mm. She was waiting for your explosions to stop first."

Ning froze for a moment before laughing sheepishly.

Fortunately, Ning had reinforced his furnace more than once over the years. Living with Ling Xin had taught him early on that preparing for an exploding cauldron was simply common sense.

Back then, he'd mostly been protecting himself from his master's experiments.

Now he was the one making things explode.

Ning sighed.

It seemed there was some truth to the saying that, given enough time, you eventually became the very thing you tried hardest to avoid.

Alas! How the tables had turned.

"Don't worry." Ling Xin smiled. "Yinran is already used to it."

Ning's smile faded slightly. "That doesn't make me feel better."

"Why?" Ling Xin looked genuinely puzzled. "Explosions just mean you're progressing."

Ning stared at his master.

Of course she would think that.

Back when he'd first arrived on Spiritual Flower Peak, Ning had been the one learning to distinguish an ordinary furnace explosion from one serious enough to warrant running outside. Now poor Ling Yinran had two alchemists making the same noises from different parts of the peak.

Ning resumed scratching Fenghuang's neck while silently mourning his serious senior sister's fate.

At least she'd finally escaped into seclusion.

"She's been ready for a while," Ling Xin continued. "Yinran worries too much. Her qi is well refined, and her Spiritual Sea feels very stable. The connection should form without much trouble."

Ning nodded.

He had spent quite a bit of time studying what came after the Ninth Stage since reaching the Seventh himself. By the Ninth, a cultivator had already completed the liquefaction of their qi and formed a stable Spiritual Sea.

The final three stages deepened the relationship between the Spiritual Sea and the spiritual root. The Tenth established the connection between them, the Eleventh spread the root's influence throughout the Spiritual Sea, and the Twelfth pushed that integration as far as Qi Condensation could support before Foundation Building. The further that integration progressed, the better the foundation a cultivator could potentially form afterward.

That was also where spiritual-root grade became much more important. Each stage placed greater demands on the root as its connection with the Spiritual Sea deepened.

"Senior Sister's root is Mid-grade, right?" Ning asked.

"Mm." Ling Xin finished her pastry and brushed the crumbs from her fingers. "The Tenth stage wouldn't be a problem."

Under ordinary circumstances, a Mid-grade spiritual root could reach the Tenth, a High-grade root the Eleventh, and a Top-grade root the Twelfth.

Ning had always thought of those limits in much the same way he thought about the tolerances of a machine. They represented what the spiritual root could reliably endure under normal conditions. Give it better support, reduce the strain, or reinforce the weak points, and there was room to push further.

Cultivators had naturally spent generations finding ways to do exactly that through pills, formations, and even specialized secret techniques.

Then there were special spiritual bodies.

Some constitutions complemented a cultivator's spiritual root well enough to change the usual limits considerably. Ling Xin herself happened to possess one.

Curious, Ning asked, "Master, what was it like when you reached the Twelfth Stage?"

"There wasn't much to it."

That tracked with everything else about his master. His master's cultivation experiences had never been especially helpful as a reference for what ordinary cultivators went through.

Ling Xin thought about it for a moment. "The Eleventh was easy. The Twelfth took a little longer because the balance kept feeling wrong."

"What was wrong with it?"

"The root and the sea weren't moving together properly."

"Moving?"

"Yes." Ling Xin raised both hands and vaguely brought them toward each other. "It was like this, but one side kept arriving a little too early."

Ning watched her hands for a moment.

He had been Ling Xin's disciple long enough to understand how she thought about cultivation. His master often understood exactly what was happening, even if putting that understanding into precise words wasn't her greatest strength.

"Do you mean your spiritual root's influence was spreading through the Spiritual Sea faster than the rest of your cultivation could adapt to the change?" Ning used his Ling Xin speak to interpret his master.

Ling Xin's eyes brightened. "Yes. That."

Ning sighed internally.

Talking cultivation with a genius sometimes felt like asking a bird to explain aerodynamics. The bird knew how to fly perfectly well. Asking it to explain why was another matter.

Fenghuang chose that moment to push his head harder into Ning's palm.

Ning looked down at him.

Perhaps that comparison had been unfair to birds.

He rubbed beneath Fenghuang's beak, earning a pleased little rumble from the raven.

"My spiritual root and spiritual body work well together," Ling Xin continued. "Once I fixed that imbalance, everything settled naturally. The Twelfth wasn't especially difficult after that."

That made sense. Ling Xin's High-grade spiritual root would normally have faced a meaningful barrier at the Twelfth Stage, but her spiritual body complemented her cultivation well enough to reduce that problem considerably.

Ling Yinran didn't have the same advantage.

"I'll prepare something for her before she attempts the Eleventh," Ling Xin said.

"A pill to help with the spiritual root?" That was the obvious choice.

"Something like that." Ling Xin frowned slightly. "I haven't decided which one yet. One formula has a stronger effect, but it's too forceful. Another is gentler, but it takes longer to settle."

"What don't you like about the stronger one?"

"It stimulates the root too much at once." Ling Xin tapped a finger against the table. "The qi flows through it more easily for a while, but the effect is too abrupt."

Ning considered that. "So it would make the breakthrough easier, but put more strain on the spiritual root in the process?"

"Mm."

"And Senior Sister is already trying to push beyond what comes naturally to a Mid-grade root. Adding more strain just to make the process faster would defeat the point."

"Yes." Ling Xin nodded. "It would probably still work, but I don't like it."

That sounded much more like his master. A pill couldn't simply improve the innate quality of Ling Yinran's spiritual root. If it could, spiritual-root grades wouldn't matter nearly as much as they did. What medicine could do was help the existing root endure the process and make its integration with the Spiritual Sea smoother.

Ling Xin wasn't interested in merely forcing the breakthrough if she could prepare Ling Yinran properly beforehand.

Ling Xin sighed softly. "If only Yinran could enter the secret realm with you."

Ning immediately knew what she was thinking about.

"Heavenly Jade Qi?"

"That's right."

Unfortunately, Ling Yinran had already passed the age limit for entering the secret realm.

With the help of pills, formations, and other resources, cultivators could sometimes push a stage beyond what their spiritual root would ordinarily support. For most, however, there was a limit to how much such methods could accomplish. At best, they provided another path toward forming a High-grade Foundation.

Top-grade was another matter entirely.

Once a cultivator had already reached the limits of a High-grade spiritual root, ordinary pills and formations offered increasingly little help. Swallowing more expensive medicine or piling on additional resources wouldn't suddenly bridge that final gap. If it were that simple, Top-grade Foundations wouldn't be nearly as rare as they were.

Heavenly Jade Qi was different.

It was one of the exceedingly rare treasures capable of pushing a cultivator beyond that usual ceiling, which was exactly why Ning wanted it before attempting Foundation Building himself.

Of course, that didn't mean every cultivator needed it. True geniuses could reach that level through their own talent.

Xiao Qing was a rather fitting example.

She had formed a Top-grade Foundation without relying on anything like Heavenly Jade Qi. Then again, Xiao Qing was something of a different beast altogether.

A Top-grade spiritual root was already enough to make her one of the most talented cultivators of her generation, yet she apparently possessed a special spiritual body as well.

And if the rumors going around the sect were true, she might even have some kind of supernatural talent on top of that.

Ning decided not to think about it too hard. A Top-grade spiritual root combined with even one of those advantages was unfair enough already.

A sharp crack interrupted his thoughts.

At some point, Fenghuang had hooked one foot around the pastry container and dragged it closer while Ning was distracted. The lid now had a crack running through it, and half a pastry was already sticking out of the raven's beak.

Ning slowly looked down at him. "I was petting you the entire time."

Fenghuang chewed.

Ning wasn't sure why he'd expected that to count as supervision.

Before he could take the container away, Ling Xin casually reached across Fenghuang and stole another pastry.

Ning stared at her. "Master, you're encouraging him."

"I'm not."

"You are literally eating the evidence."

Ling Xin looked at the pastry in her hand, finished it, and brushed the crumbs from her fingers.

"What evidence?"

Ning looked between his master and Fenghuang.

Then he sighed and resumed scratching the raven's head.

Sometimes Ning wondered if he was the only one in the room with any respect for rules.

Did Ning occasionally stretch those rules himself?

Nonsense.

He was a perfectly law-abiding disciple. Anyone saying otherwise was just lying.
