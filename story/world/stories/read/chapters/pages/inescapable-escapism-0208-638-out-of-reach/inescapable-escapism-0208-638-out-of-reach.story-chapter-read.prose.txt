I barely caught a glimpse of the creature before a thick waft of smoke blew in front of it, concealing it from view, but I heard the screams that followed. They reached through the smoke towards me, striking fear into my heart. I had no doubt they had come from the person, and that terrified me.

But I didn’t know what the creature was. I could barely even see their hazy outline through the thick green-tinged mist, and I knew it must have been some kind of animal, but I wasn’t sure if that was all it was. I hoped so. I prayed to whatever god would listen that it wasn’t a mutant, a horrific creature that had been ravaged by the radiation that spilt over some parts of the world after the globe.

They were rare, though. I’d never come across any. As far as I was aware, they died out fairly quickly in the years after the bombs. They couldn’t last long. Their mutated genes were too unstable, so their lives were short, filled with pain and rage.

I’d heard the stories, though. Even years after leaving camp, I remembered them all and the pained look on Garret’s scarred face as he recounted the tale of how he lost his eye, of the pup he thought was safe. I knew the signs, the symptoms to watch out for, but I couldn’t see anything through the fog.

Another scream came from somewhere else in the smoke, and my bow snapped towards it, but I still couldn’t see anything through the dense wall. Indecision and fear warred within me, and I hesitated before beginning to back away, keeping my still watering eyes on it. I refused to look away for even a second in case something appeared, and I wasn’t sure which would be worse, a person or that creature. At least if it were the creature, it would probably just kill me. I wasn’t sure the people would. If they still wanted me for some reason, I’d most likely be kept alive, but something told me I’d be wishing for death.

Gunshots echoed through the fog, making me jump, and I threw myself to the side. There was no way to tell if the shooter was aiming at me or if they were aiming at the creature, but I didn’t want to risk getting caught in the spray. I didn’t, though. I wasn’t sure where the bullets hit, but the street around me was strangely quiet and still, with the exception of the still billowing gas.

My breathing came in short, pained gasps as my eyes flicked back and forth, searching for any sign of movement, but the smoke was playing tricks on my mind. It made it seem like someone was constantly on the brink of appearing and throwing themselves towards me, but nothing appeared. There were only vague shadows that loomed for a moment before disappearing again.

I didn’t know what to do. Part of my brain was screaming at me to run, that it might be my only chance to get away. If the creature were a mutant, it would probably kill all of the people there, and if I got far enough away before it was done, I might survive. If not, if they had not sated its hunger, it would turn on me. I’d be hunted down, but I doubted that thing would be as easy to evade as the people were. At least if I were further away, I might be able to prepare. There was a chance that I could set a trap and kill it before it could do the same to me, just as Garret did.

But something was pulling at me. It was making me want to stay, and I wasn’t sure why. I knew it was stupid, though. I was fully aware of that, and there was a voice in my head screaming at me to turn and sprint as far away from the city as my scorched lungs would take me, but my feet refused to move.

A figure emerged from the smoke. Just like the person before, they moved in slow, deliberate steps, waving their gun back and forth. It was trained low, I noticed. Their gun was fixed on the ground, and I couldn’t see their face, but it appeared as if they weren’t even looking for me. They didn’t even seem to glance in my direction as they turned, and I had to assume they were searching for the creature instead, but based on the shouts still coming from the smoke, it was preoccupied with someone else.

They seemed just as blind as I was, I realised as I watched them from over the bonnet of the car I’d ducked behind, my bow still held at the ready. Their mask must have been protecting them from the harmful effects of the gas, but it didn’t seem to be helping them see through it. They had no idea where the creature was.

My breath caught in my chest as it appeared again, leaping out of the smoke towards the person. For a brief minute, I was tempted to shoot the thing as it flew through the air. I wasn’t sure whether a single, well-placed arrow would kill it, but I had a clear shot at its belly. It would wound it, at least, and I knew it might have been my best chance. Hell, it could have been my only chance.

But the arrow did not leave my bow. I couldn’t bring myself to kill a potentially innocent creature. Despite whatever it was doing to the others, it had not shown any aggression towards me, and I didn’t need it for its meat. It felt cruel and selfish to shoot it. Then, the people would definitely be able to kill it, and I hated the thought of that.

Plus, it was helping me. I watched as it crashed into the person, its claws tearing easily through their armour, as if the thick black fabric was nothing more than paper. They lifted their gun towards it, firing as quickly as they could, but the shots either missed or didn’t penetrate the creature’s thick black fur.

Before the person had time to fire again, the creature’s teeth found their throat. All I could do was watch in shock as it tore it away with a spray of blood. The person’s scream, muffled by their mask, turned to a faint gurgle that was drowned out by the hiss of the smoke grenade.

Wolf, a voice in the back of my head whispered as I stared at the creature in shock. The thing standing over the body, blood dripping from its muzzle, seemed far too big to be a dog. Surely, it had to be a wolf.

For a moment, all I could do was stare at the scene before me. Obviously, I’d seen people die before. I’d caused a few deaths myself, but it was never quite so horrific. I’d given them quick deaths, tried to make them painless, and there was never so much gore. I wasn’t a squeamish person. That wasn’t possible in the world I lived in, but the sight of the person’s gaping throat turned my stomach and made my knees feel weak.

Behind the creature, a shadow appeared. Their head immediately snapped towards the animal, and I saw them raise their gun. I lifted my bow as I opened my mouth to shout, either to distract the person or warn the creature, I wasn’t sure which, but it looked up before I even made a sound.

A jolt of recognition burned through me as I locked eyes with the dog, and I stumbled out from behind the car before I could stop myself, but the dog had already turned. It dove towards the person without hesitation, its teeth closing around their hand. Panic spiked within me, expecting to hear a gunshot and see the dog be thrown back from the impact, but no shot came. Instead, a loud gasp of pain sounded, and the dog tore the gun from them, his teeth leaving deep gouges in the person’s hand. My vision was still blurry from the gas, but even I could see the blood that immediately began pouring from their wounds.

I pulled my arrow back, trying to get a good shot at the person, but I was too scared of hitting the dog. He was standing directly in front of them, between the person and me, and growling softly. The low noise made the hairs on my arms stand on end, but I knew Rex posed no danger to me.

Rex pounced again. That time, he knocked the person to the ground, and they went down hard. My arrow remained trained on them, but I still couldn’t get a clear shot. I stepped closer, but the smoke began to sting my eyes and burn my throat again, making it even harder to aim. I couldn’t move away, though. I needed to be close so I could help Rex.

Another shadow solidified behind Rex, and a person appeared. Without hesitation, I let go of my arrow. Rex was distracted. His back was to them, and I was terrified that they’d be able to shoot him before he turned.

The gas made my shot worse that time, though. Tears were flowing from my eyes, and they made my vision swim, so the arrow didn’t find their throat. It thudded into their chest, but I knew it was not a lethal hit. It was closer to their shoulder than their heart, but the almost silent whistle of my arrow flying through the air seemed to be enough to alert Rex to their presence because he spun around before the person recovered.

I pulled another arrow from my quiver, slotting it into place, as Rex threw himself at the person. They raised their arms to shield themselves, their gun forgotten, but Rex simply caught one of their forearms in his mouth. With one vicious tug, he ripped the person’s arm clean off.

They did not react immediately. They were silent, motionless, as Rex dropped their arm on the ground with a disgusting thump, but then, a scream tore itself free from their throat. Their other hand reached towards their bleeding shoulder, as if searching for the arm that lay on the ground before them, and I fired.

Their body jolted as the arrow hit them, and Rex leapt towards them. His jaw closed around their head, and he jerked it to the side. A horrible snap echoed through the smoke, and they crumpled to the ground. Rex watched them for a moment longer, seemingly making sure they were dead, before turning towards me. His eyes found mine again, and he let out a single bark.

Confusion washed over me as I stared back at him. He didn’t run towards me or do anything other than look at me, and I wasn’t sure why. It seemed as though he was trying to communicate something to me, like he was trying to tell me something with that bark, but I had no idea what.

Rex’s head snapped around suddenly, and he dove into the smoke again without even a look back at me. My heart leapt into my throat, and fear danced through me as I scanned the opaque fog, trying to work out where he’d gone and if he was okay. More screams exploded from within it, each one sending a faint wave of relief through me.

If they were screaming, that meant he was attacking them, so he probably hadn’t been injured. That made me feel a little better, but it wasn’t enough. I had to know for sure, and I wanted to enter the fog too. I needed to protect him. We fought well together. Despite having no memory of it, I knew we did. With him at my side, I’d be able to take down every single person who was looking for me. I had no doubt of that, but I couldn’t help him from where I was.

My eyes fell on one of the bleeding corpses that Rex had left behind, and an idea started to form in my mind. They were wearing a mask, and I assumed it had not been damaged by Rex’s attack. He’d ripped their throat out, but he had not gone for their face, so if I could make it to them, I’d be able to take it for myself. Then, I’d be able to follow Rex into the gas without any issues. I just needed to make it that far.

The smoke was thicker near them. It was denser, and I knew it would be a struggle. I’d have to hold my breath and pray I made it that far, that the gas wouldn’t have time to do too much damage to my eyes or lungs before I could secure the mask into place. That was a risk, but it was my only option.

I took a deep breath, and immediately, the foul taste of the smoke flooded my mouth. My throat twitched, and fresh tears welled in my eyes as I spluttered, barely managing to hold back a cough. My body rebelled, fighting against me.

But I didn’t have time to recover fully. Another volley of gunshots came from the smoke, and frantic fear sparked in my heart. I forced myself to take another breath, slightly more shallow that time, and began walking forward.

Before I could get any closer, Rex reappeared. Even more blood coated his face and muzzle, and my gaze scanned him quickly, searching for wounds, but he appeared unharmed. Relieved yet determined, I took another step towards him, but Rex barked again.

I stopped, staring at him in confusion. For some reason, it seemed as though he didn’t want me to come any closer, like he was trying to tell me that he didn’t want my help, but I was torn.

Surely, I must have been misunderstanding. He appeared unaffected by the gas, and he didn’t seem to be having any trouble taking the people down. I had no doubt he’d be able to kill them all if he wanted, but I was still worried about him. I didn’t want him to get hurt, to die, and I couldn’t just leave him there to deal with them by himself. What if someone managed to shoot him?

An image flashed through my mind: Rex lying in the middle of the street, blood streaming from a wound on his stomach as an endless horde of black uniformed people holding guns crept closer. It felt so real that I could almost hear his weak, pained whimpers, and I couldn’t help but take another step forward.

The movement caused Rex to bark again, and his eyes remained fixed on me for a moment longer before his head snapped up. He stared at something over my shoulder intently, and I was torn between wanting to turn and look at whatever had caught his attention and watching the fog behind him.

I pulled another arrow from my almost empty quiver. Rex seemed distracted, fixated on whatever he’d seen, and I was scared someone could appear before he noticed them. If they did, I needed to take them down or at least buy him more time, but I was terrified too. My heart thumped loudly as I continued to scan the smoke, longing to glance back, but seconds later, Rex’s eyes returned to me, and he barked again.

That time, it felt clearer. He definitely wanted me to turn and run, and I hesitated before risking a glance over my shoulder, but I saw nothing in the street behind me. There were no people sneaking up behind me and no movement from any of the buildings. I had no idea what he’d seen, and that only made me more afraid.

I glanced back at him again, still debating what to do, when something caught my attention. There was a sound. It was barely audible above the dying hiss of the smoke canisters, but I recognised the soft rhythmic thump of a helicopter. They were rare in the world I was in.

Most had fallen into disrepair or become useless after running out of fuel, but I’d still seen a few in my time. I’d always been told to hide the moment I heard them, though. If the person flying it had managed to maintain it for so long after everything happened, they were dangerous, Yuki had warned me in hushed whispers as we huddled beneath a fallen tree. According to her, I should pray I never had the misfortune of meeting its pilot.

I couldn’t help but look back again, my eyes scanning the sky. At first, I saw nothing, but then my gaze landed on a tiny speck in the distance. Ice solidified in my veins, and I looked back at Rex.

That time, he took a step towards me, and the sound that came from him was somewhere between a bark and a whine. It sounded pleading, as if he were begging me to leave, and I couldn’t help the words that spilled from my mouth.

“Come with me,” I whispered, my singed throat making my voice harsh.

Rex seemed to hesitate, and I held my breath, but he just turned away. I watched as he disappeared back into the smoke before slowly doing the same. At first, my steps were slow, reluctant, but I soon broke into a run.

My already strained lungs burned from the effort, making my head spin, and I felt myself pulling back from the world as dizziness engulfed me. My last thought as I lingered between the world and my void was that of hope. I hoped that the version of me left behind would be okay, that I’d manage to get away from whoever it was that was hunting me down, but I knew that Rex would.

I was still worried about him, though. The anxiety was so strong that my chest hurt, even as I drifted further away from that reality, but I knew he’d be fine. He’d escape the people, and I was certain he would find me again, just as he had done so many times before. Somehow, he always managed to find me.

Confusion washed over me as my vision turned black, and my thoughts caught up with themselves. How, I found myself asking. How had he found me in that world, and how many other times had it happened? I didn’t know where he had come from or when it had started, but from what I could remember, which wasn’t much, he seemed to always find me when I was in danger.

That made me worry about my other world, my reality. Rex had appeared there too, but did that mean something was wrong, that there was some risk I was not aware of or had yet to see?

And how? How was he doing it? I could travel between the worlds, I knew that, but I’d never stopped to wonder if anyone else could do it. I just assumed that I was alone, but that was becoming more and more unlikely. I had seen others, other people in multiple worlds, like the people in the black uniform. It could have just been a coincidence. I tried to tell myself it was, but it was getting harder and harder to convince myself of that.

I clung to it, though. I didn’t want to believe that my fears were correct.

Anxiety lapped at me as I fell gently through the nothingness that surrounded me. More than anything, I just wanted to go home. I longed to leave all of the fear, confusion, and horrors that I’d faced behind and pretend none of it had ever happened. I wanted to check on Rex, to make sure that he was okay, and then just exist in my reality for a while, but it was still out of reach.

Frustration crashed into me so hard that I felt tears building behind my eyes. I tried desperately to reach out and grab it, unsure where it even was, but the harder I fought, the further away it seemed to retreat. Helplessness wrapped around me, dragging me downwards. It was useless, pointless to even try. For some reason, I couldn’t go back yet. Something was stopping me. Perhaps it wasn’t time yet, or maybe my world just didn’t want me.

My heart sank, and my body fell still as the fight drained out of me. I felt myself sag, my arms hanging loosely by my side, as I continued to fall deeper into the abyss within my mind.
