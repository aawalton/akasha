Knock knock knock!!

A loud thud interrupted my peaceful goblin dreams.

“It’s open!” I shouted.

Oh yeah, I was still sleeping in the straw pile at the hunting cabin. Probably past noon by now.

“Holy shit,” said Samar when she saw me. “You look… you look like…”

Come on, Samar, give it your best shot.

“El-Shamama!” she declared.

“Which is?”

“A smelly ugly monster who eats kids that don’t brush their teeth before bed.”

“And… did you remember to brush your teeth, Samar?” I made a scary face and raised my hand like a monster.

“If that painter brush we use counts,” she said, “then I did.”

“Then you have nothing to be afraid of.” I tried to… un-straw myself. Didn’t work.

“Listen,” Samar said, “we were like, looking all over for you. It’s Camila. She really wants to talk to you.”

“Camila?”

“The new lady player. She’s… sort of acting weird.”

“Oh,” I said. “You know, it’s super hard for her—with her child and all that.”

“Yeah, but she’s not acting that kind of weird,” Samar said. “Just go talk to her. She’s at the bakery.”

“Sure,” I said. “But shower first.”

“Good idea!”

As I walked, I tried to set my mind to something sensitive to say to the newcomer, but my thoughts kept drifting to the advancement I’d made in my CO magic. With the new skill, my ability had kind of jumped. I had real options now. I could finally start considering combat maneuvers—or at least dirty tricks to surprise and confuse the enemy.

But first things first.

Camila was staying in the bakery’s spare room—the one used for temporary accommodations.

It was the first time I’d seen her when she wasn’t a total mess.

She was actually beautiful. Latina, light brown skin, long black hair. She was older then me—maybe 23 to 25. That was probably around the upper limit for new human players in this world.

“Hi, Camila, right?” I began.

“You were a gamer, right?” she asked immediately. “Which games?”

“Uh… what?”

“Which games did you play?”

“Ah, let’s see…” I scrambled to remember, caught off guard by the sudden interrogation. “If you mean open-world RPGs, probably Skyrim, Witcher 3, Assassin’s Creed Odyssey, Elden Ring… Breath of the Wild, Horizon Zero Dawn. Uh… Red Dead Redemption 2 of course. I liked some racing games too—Forza Horizon 5, and more. Played a lot of older strategy games like Age of Empires II and StarCraft II. But probably most of my time just went to Fortnite and Minecraft.”

“So no The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion, Baldur’s Gate, Dark Souls, Dragon Age?”

“Hey, I always had school,” I said defensively. “My parents let me, like, three hours of screen time a day!”

Then I added, “You mean to say this world… resembles those games more than Skyrim?”

“You should’ve played Oblivion,” she said. “It’s older, but a much better game.”

“Yeah, I’ve seen some online debates.”

“Oblivion felt way more alive,” Camila continued, passionate now. “The NPCs actually had lives. They ate, they slept, sometimes they even got themselves killed doing dumb stuff. It made the world feel real. I remember walking into town and seeing people just arguing or stealing bread—and it wasn’t even part of a quest!”

“Yeah, but it was supposedly buggier,” I said, repeating what I’d seen in forum arguments. “Voice actors were repetitive, and Skyrim had better graphics. But listen—” I tried to steer the conversation back. “Even if this place does resemble a game more like Oblivion, this world is vastly different. Knowledge from games might help you wrap your head around concepts like levels, NPCs, and quests… but beyond that? It won’t help much. Did Juliette and Charlotte explain some of that to you?”

“Yeah, they did,” she said, sounding restless. “I even talked to a few NPCs myself. But this place… it’s too much like Oblivion to just ignore. You have to know about video games—you’re basically thrown into one! And they also told me how you do things around here. And honestly, it doesn’t make any sense.”

“What do you mean?”

“You’re all so low level after, what, two months? I could understand that if you had no idea how games work—but you do. So I really don’t get it.”

“Look…” I took a deep breath. “When we first got here, we thought like that. Grind weak monsters to level up. Then, not much later, our friend Ramón got butchered—because of a tribe of alien animals.”

“Juliette told me about Ramón,” Camila said, not fazed in the slightest. “So you rushed out without sufficient knowledge. She also told me about Farhad’s group and the dumb mistakes they made. But just because they made mistakes doesn’t mean we will. Is that a reason to just give up on leveling entirely?”

“Look, I don’t give a shit about leveling up!” For some reason, I was getting angry. It was hard to explain life here to an outsider. “Level 3 is a lot here—and I saw someone at that level die from a knife to the throat. I don’t know how to explain it to you, but this is real life. It’s vastly different from a video game.”

“Take Nayeem,” I continue, “came here from a poor village in Bangladesh. He never saw a computer in his life—but he knows the forest, survival skills. That stuff matters more here.”

“How can you not care about levels?” Camila asked. “They give you real power!”

“It’s not that I don’t care,” I said. “I do try to learn magic. This world does resemble a game, and there’s a lot you can do with that. But all that is secondary. Look—maybe I’m wrong, I’m not that smart. But you just got here. Take a week or two to observe. Learn how things work before making conclusions.”

Now I saw the tears in her eyes. Crap. I’d said the wrong thing.

“I don’t plan to be here for years, you know,” she said in a heavy voice. “Lucia needs me. I need to find out how to get back—and fast. Juliette said… you think it might be possible?”

Did I say that?

“Ah… when we got here,” I tried to be honest, “I said that if we were brought here by magic, there might be a way to get back. Maybe with some endgame-level quest or item. But that’s just a guess. I don’t actually know anything.”

“I see,” she said, considering this. “It may be so.”

“But there’s also the computer simulation possibility,” I added. “That we’re just copies—and our real selves stayed on Earth. If that’s true, Lucia might still have her mom. I… I don’t know if that helps.”

Camila’s eyes filled with tears again as she processed what I’d said.

“If… if I knew for sure that was true,” she whispered, “that I’m just a copy and Lucia still has her mama… it would be fine. I’d still miss her every day. But it would be okay. And if not—and we really were magically transported from Earth—then there has to be a way back.”

She sounded more resolved now.

“Yes,” I said. “And if there is a way, we’ll find it. But you need to listen to me.”

She looked at me, and I tried to be as serious and cold as I could. This was important.

“If you hurry… if you rush things,” I said, locking eyes with her, “you’ll die. Easily. And you’ll never see Lucia again. This world is extremely dangerous. And there’s no respawn. As much as you want to get back to her—you must not rush. Do you understand?”

“…Yes,” she said. “I understand.”

I really didn’t think she did.

Then we heard the trumpets.

“Tuuut-tuuu-tu-tuuut!”

“Tuuut-tuuu-tu-tuuut!”

“What is that?” Camila asked.

“I have no clue,” I said honestly, as we both stepped outside to check.

Seriously. Every day here, there’s new weirdness.

In the street, we saw three armored soldiers on horses. One held the kingdom flag, one blew the trumpet.

For a moment, I thought they were the snob players from the Order of the Phoenix—but they wouldn’t be allowed here. They were considered enemies of this kingdom.

“All gather, all gather!” the trumpet guy shouted. “A message from our great King Thalric! All gather, all gather!”

They were heading for the town square. We followed.

The NPCs gathered, along with Juliette, Duchess, Hyun-woo, and Charlotte. We joined them.

“What now?” Juliette muttered to no one in particular, clearly exhausted.

The herald opened a scroll and read:

“By command of His Majesty King Thalric of the realm!

The Lithuron enemy stirs beyond our borders. Their forces gather, and with grim certainty they will turn their march upon this very town.

Whether sooner or later, the threat is clear.

All citizens are to prepare for siege. Stock your cellars, bar your doors, and see your families made ready.

Any man of fighting age who can bear arms and wishes to serve the kingdom can report to the town garrison. You will be paid well.

That the king’s banner may stand strong against the coming storm.”

“The enemy he is talking about must be the snob players from the Lithuron Kingdom,” I said. “They mentioned something like this was going to happen.”

“So… it’s gonna be a Naked-Nula war?” asked Duchess.

“Not our war,” replied Juliette.

The herald opened a second scroll.

“The King calls upon the Band of the Stormcrows.

By name and by deed, you are summoned.”

“That’s us,” I said flatly.

We stepped forward.

“I’m Juliette,” she said solemnly. “Leader of the Stormcrows.”

“The crown entrusts you with a special task,” the soldier said, offering her the scroll.

Juliette opened it, and we all read together.

“Let it be known: The Band of the Stormcrows is henceforth taken into the service of crown and country.

No longer mere wanderers, you are named a company beneath the banner of the White Tiger Army.

Your charge: to ride with the border patrols, strike at raiders, and join the skirmishes of the coming war.

You shall serve under General Argothol.

Each man shall receive one gold coin weekly and greater rewards upon victory.

More than coin is offered—you shall bear the honor of serving king and country, your names etched into the rolls of the realm’s defenders.

So decrees the King!”

“Let your answer be known. We will carry it back to the capital.” said the messenger.

They wanted answer— it wasn’t an order. It was just a quest.

“Yeah,” Juliette said. “We’ll pass. But thanks for the offer.”

The NPC stared at her.

“We. Refuse,” Juliette said more clearly. “We. Don’t. Want.”

Without a word, the three soldiers got back on their horses and rode out of town.

“What? Just like that?” asked Camila.

“What do you mean?” I asked.

“I mean,” she said, “This was a main quest! A king quest! We could gain so much—loot, levels, magical items. Probably dozens of side quests. And you just passed on it like it was nothing. Don’t we, like, take a vote or something?”

“Nope,” Juliette said with a smile. “This is, in fact, a dictatorship.”

Camila stared at her.

“Look,” I said, “there are reasons why that quest is a bad idea. We also met the players who’ll be fighting for the other side. We don’t want them as enemies.”

“I get it!” Camila snapped. “You just like it here, don’t you? You all want to be cute little NPCs! Working at a bakery or… making panties!”

We didn’t answer.

Finally, Samar said, “We’re not dumb, you know. We do things this way for a reason.”

“I’m sure you do!” Camila shouted.

Maybe not every player fit into our group, I thought. Maybe someday we could point her toward the Order of the Phoenix. She might fit in with them. But it would take time until she could make the long trip.

“I’m starving,” said Samar. “Hyun-woo, do you have those custard tartlets ready? I can’t stop thinking about them!”

“Ooh, I know what you’re talking about,” Said Charlotte. “I love those.”

“Give custard tartlets!” Squeak Duchess.

We headed to the bakery for a late custard tartlet meal.

And it was really good.

Even Camila looked more relaxed.

“Bzzzzzzzz.”

A buzzing sound—like something vibrating.

“Oh, it’s just my phone,” Juliette said. “Excuse me.”

She pulled out a small glass tablet and set it on the table.

Oh I know! It was the receiver for the magical phone device—the one taken from Farhad’s group after the battle. Juliette had apparently been carrying it with her, and now decided to be funny.

She tapped at it a few times—clearly didn’t know how to answer—but it stopped buzzing anyway.

Then a loud female voice came out of the device.

“Hello? Farhad?”

“I’m sorry, you’ve reached Juliette. Probably the wrong number,” Juliette said cheerfully.

There was a pause.

Then, an angry voice: “I know who you are, Juliette! Where is Farhad? Where’s Arjun?!”

“Hey now,” Juliette smirked. “This is my first phone call in months. I was really looking forward to it, so don’t just yell at me! Let’s make it a nice phone call. Introduce yourself, will you?”

Murmurs on the other side. Then:

“This is Ananya. There are five of us left in the keep. Now please tell me—how did you get this device?”

“That’s better,” said Juliette. “You see, it’s pretty simple. They attacked us. It was all very dramatic. It’s just that, you know… they lost. And we won.”

“I don’t understand how that could be!” Ananya said, voice rising in frustration. “How could you actually win against them?”

“Because we’re badass,” said Samar, jumping in gleefully.

“Yes,” Juliette agreed. “We are badass. That’s why we won.”

“…Okay,” said Ananya. “Now please tell me. What happened to the group?”

“Seven dead,” Juliette said, her tone turning more serious. “The other ten escaped into the forest, some injured. They’re probably on their way back. You might want to send a group to meet them, in case they can’t make it on their own in that sorry state.”

I heard someone gasp faintly on the other side.

They didn’t consider that outcome. Not even a little.

“…Okay,” Ananya whispered.

“Anything else?” Juliette asked.

“…No.”

“Okay then,” Juliette smiled. “Call anytime. Bye!”

After a moment, the glass tablet gave a final soft vibration, then went completely still. I guessed it was off now.

“That was fun!” said Juliette. “I really do miss having a phone!”

“Oh! I almost forgot,” said Samar. “When I went to the forester cabin to look for Geshem, I met a goblin.”

“A goblin?” I asked.

“Yeah,” she said. “He invited us to their camp—for a grand celebration of the grand victory. They said ‘grand’ a lot.”

“When?”

“Tonight. I think.”

“OMG, Samar—you should’ve told us earlier!”

“Oh, yeah,” she added. “We’re supposed to bring the alcohol. Like… thirty bottles of ale.”

“Great,” I groaned. “It’s probably all just a scheme to swindle us into bringing free ale.”

But I was excited. A goblin party sounded like fun. I also wanted Juliette and Hyun-woo to see how cool their camp was. And… I wouldn’t mind seeing Vel’nari.

“Good,” said Juliette. “We could also talk to them about the plan to attack the keep. Start putting things in motion.”

Yes. There was that too. I could probably sell this idea to the goblin chief with no effort at all—building siege weapons, assaulting a human keep, smashing walls, causing chaos, and walking away with riches and gold. Easier than selling candy to a baby.

It was a complicated plan, but Farhad’s group wouldn’t reach the keep for a few days—injured, exhausted, and miserable.

If we managed to prepare in less than two weeks, we’d catch them still licking their wounds. That could be the final victory. After that, we wouldn’t need to worry about them anymore.

Of course, there was also the small matter of an entire enemy kingdom possibly attacking our village soon.

Honestly, I had no idea what to make of that.

But hey. In this world, there’s no need to trouble trouble until trouble troubles you.
