Emperor Gallow had forgotten what it felt like to experience new things. For more time than he could remember, he’d been bonded to the Devorare, and his place had been aboard the ship. They would travel from one star system to another, endlessly searching for the scourges that ruined their people. While the rest of the People slept, he would remain awake, guiding the great ship across the dark expanse.

Even upon reaching a new world, his role was typically ornamental. Before reaching Earth, there had been hundreds of generals, and even Champions among his forces–the rank he couldn’t achieve personally so long as he was connected to the Devorare. They knew their duties well, and went about them with efficiency, as Emperor Gallow silently watched.

Since the ship had been destroyed, Gallow had experienced one new horror after another. From the pain of the Devorare’s loss, to his own wound, still stubbornly refusing to heal. Masters possessed unfathomable power, and for one to put all of it into a single attack, Gallow knew he was lucky to be alive. That did nothing to dull the constant pain, nor restore him any faster.

That alone would have been difficult enough, but he also had this quest to undertake. Conquest may have been the means by which they were gathering the demons, but Gallow was the ultimate destination for the power. Each creature added to their army added another surge of Dusk’s power, and the Emperor could feel the effects.

The energy raged within him, volatile and alive, desperate to be used, spent. It also called out to its like, and Gallow could sense that same power all around him, suffusing this world. It was only dense enough to matter in the demons, of course. They devoured one another, concentrating that power countless times over, before Gallow eventually took it for himself, as was his right.

He sensed that power inside him all the time, and knew it was changing him in subtle ways. The latest and most noticeable was some kind of visualization of the power’s desire to be gathered together. Even with his eyes tightly shut, Gallow could always see a light in the distance. Following that light always led to more power, usually in the form of a Twilight demon.

Once it had led him to the sea. He wouldn’t make the mistake of following it that way again.

But for all the madness churning within him, for all the strangeness of this red, brutal world, he never expected what had plagued him for the last two nights.

Dread.

A storm had come, something of a relief after the dry, dusty climate, and a novelty for an Emperor who’d spent eons trapped inside. But the storm brought something else with it. Something dangerous.

The night had been illuminated only by the flashes of lightning, and his army was nearly invisible, covered as they were in the dark-borne. Gallow was inside the carried pavilion when the roars echoed through the nights. Demons, released from Conquest’s hold.

The other four of the Kin rushed about, trying to understand what was happening. Gallow emerged into the downpour of rain, squinting at the Twilight demon–some ape-like creature–as it devoured another of its kind, who remained perfectly still despite the attack.

“Restrain that thing!” Gallow commanded, and two of the kin rushed to obey. Bek stayed in the pavilion, eyes darting around, no-doubt expecting a trap.

Gallow was equally suspicious, but knew he had to get out there and take charge. His few remaining generals were likewise unused to such novel experiences, and he found that they floundered when left alone. Speaking of which…

Gallow spun in place. One of the kin was missing. Gurak. Two more were expected to be gone from the army, one searching for the human, and another bringing back another demon. But Gurak should be here.

“Where is Gurak?” he called out. The kin subduing the rogue demon paused to look around. Their snapping fingers indicated they were unsure.

Gallow cursed, then flew to the rear of the marching army, where Gurak should have been. He found nothing, but retracing the army’s steps revealed a stain of blood that Gallow knew belonged to the kin.

Bek had obviously sent the other two to join Gallow after they finished their work, as both landed silently behind him. He allowed them to follow him as he followed a conspicuous trail into the nearby woods. The grasping limbs retreated from his presence, and the trees went still as he walked by.

Soon, the low, almost imperceptible sounds of one of the kin in pain drew them forward, and Gallow raced deeper into the woods, his followers struggling to keep up.

He came to a halt when he found Gurak, his body pinned to a tree by more than twenty small blades.

“Such an insult,” one of the kin said from behind.

Gallow had to agree, and moved forward to begin plucking the knives out–Gurak should be able to heal through such wounds. Yet the moment Gallow touched one, all of them exploded.

Gallow immediately recognized the early Twilight power, and it only knocked him back a few steps, one forearm protectively across his face, the other folded across his wounded chest. But while he may have been unharmed, Gurak had been nearly annihilated.

Furious, Gallow spun on his heels. “Find the one who–” he stopped, realizing only one of the kin was still behind him. The other had been taken, just like Gurak. But the attacker had made a mistake–while it somehow managed to hide its own presence, it couldn’t hide one of the kin.

Gallow closed his eyes, and raised a hand, pointing unerringly in the direction of his own general. The blast of energy that erupted from his fingertips was purple and black, and spiraled out at unmatched speeds. It carved through trees, rocks, and anything else before it struck something.

Gallow lowered his hand. He sensed that the kin still lived, but was no longer being dragged away. It was the slightest of victories. They had lost this night, and Gallow would make sure it never happened again.

“It’s happening again?” Selina asked.

“We don’t know for sure,” Apollo said. He was hovering above the Fable Train, enjoying the sun, and Selina found it difficult to hear the man.

“But the scouting party did disappear?” she asked.

“Yes, but far to the north. There’s a chance they just ranged too great a distance from my control.”

“Can we risk that?”

Apollo squirmed at the question, then lowered himself into the train. “We can wait until Ethan gets back,” he said.

“Really? Apollo, if it’s another army of undead, we’ll need to know as soon as possible. I don’t want to lose our hard-earned demons.”

He sighed. “If something were to happen to you–”

“It would be tragic; I’m a delight,” Selina said. “But I didn’t come here to be safe. I’m part of this team, and part of this mission. Walter and I can go. Fable has been working on a way to keep me protected out there. Right?”

The metallic woman grew out of the floor in that weird way of hers, but was already nodding. “Yes, Runemistress. While I won’t be able to remove any significant mass and retain control–not without specific monster cores that are very limited–constructing a defensive installment will not be a problem.”

“Wait, what can’t you do?” Apollo asked.

“She can’t make secondary vehicles that are still part of her,” Selina said. She’d already discussed this at length with Fable. “So no intelligence, or reconfiguration, whatever she makes is effectively an independent object.”

“So she can build you a bunker, essentially.”

“That’s all I’ll need! We can strap it to Walter’s back, and it’ll be like Twilight armor.”

“Fine, fine, just be careful,” Apollo said.

Selina smiled. “I’ll be fine!”

Something was definitely wrong. Selina had been riding Walter through a dense forest for hours, trying to follow the trail that Apollo had given her. She was sure they were going the right direction, and yet every time they found signs of demons, it was like they were minutes too late.

Pepper, her purple bear Familiar lounged behind her in the small, box-like construct Fable had created. It wasn’t much more than a little shed atop the crab-like Walter, but Fable had been kind enough to give it air conditioning, in addition to the many exterior cameras. It had one more helpful function that Apollo had insisted upon, and Selina pulled a large brass lever to activate it.

There was the sound of something rolling off the top of Walter’s shell, and then landing below with a soft thump. She watched the communications tower unfold as they moved slowly on through the woods. In a few minutes it should connect back to Fable, and let her minder stay in touch.

While Selina waited to make contact with holier-than-Ethan, she leaned back in her chair, and glared at the wall of monitors before her. The gentle sway of Walter’s footsteps beneath her were hypnotic, and she felt her eyes beginning to close as she watched nothing but trees shifting.

Then her eyes shot open.

“What was that?” she demanded. Pepper groaned, rolling over in protest of the sound. Selina started adjusting the cameras, but couldn’t find the source of the movement she’d noticed.

“...there?” Apollo’s voice suddenly said. “Selina, can you hear me?”

“I’m here,” she said. “Having a bit of time out here.”

“Did you find the demons?”

“I don’t think so,” she said, struggling to describe what she’d been seeing. “We reached the site where you felt them disappear hours ago. There was a trail through the forest, so we followed it, and even found signs of a fight.”

“Okay, so maybe some bigger demons got them. No sign of undead?”

“Nothing yet, but something else is going on. The trail continued, so I followed. There’s been demon remains, and more signs of battle, but I haven’t found the cause yet.”

“Just come back, Selina. You’re way too far out, I can barely feel Walter at this point. We’ll send out more scouts to find whatever’s out there.”

“I suppose I can do that,” she said. It had begun to feel like something of a mystery. “I’ve just been on the trail so long, I really want to see where it leads.”

“Nowhere good, Selina. There’s never anything good on this stupid rock. Just come back where it’s safe, and we’ll get back to work.”

She sighed. “Understood.” Cutting off the communication, she turned to Pepper. The Familiar looked like an overweight panda at Dusk rank, and only ever seemed to want to sleep. “I just thought something would finally happen, you know?”

Pepper growled softly, then rolled over to lean against her leg. She pet the warm, furry belly, then turned back to her monitors. They’d given her some simple controls to steer Walter, which were actually hiding a somewhat convoluted method of navigation. Selina would push on a button that connected to a similar interface back at the train, which Apollo held. He’d see the button light up, then send a command to Walter to turn.

It was slow, but Walter wasn’t particularly fast anyway. He’d just begun his ponderous turn when something crashed into the side of the huge demon.

They were under attack.
