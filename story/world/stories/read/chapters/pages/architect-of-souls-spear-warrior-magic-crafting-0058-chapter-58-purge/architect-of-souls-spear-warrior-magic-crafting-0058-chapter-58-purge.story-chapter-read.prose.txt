The massive scarab rose up in front of Ferris. It stood between him and the Heartfire tower. One last obstacle before one of the few friendly areas left under the slimespace dome.

The scarabs controlled almost everywhere else.

The plates on its back flared out. Giant wings unfurled outward, causing a burst of wind. A gust pushed against his face. Useless posturing, Ferris hoped. Only the small ones have shown any real flight.

Bringing up his spear, Ferris stepped into a probing thrust at the thing’s leg. The scarab’s shell deflected the spear tip. He’d been able to pierce through the other scarabs, even the ones that were larger than the ants.

The leg drew back from the attack, then the scarab lowered its head.

Ferris jumped back, hoping to draw it deeper. Unfortunately, it didn’t take the bait. Instead, the thing swung into the line of ants. More of the small scarabs swarmed up around it. They surged into his line of ants, taking back ground.

He tried to continue his previous tactics, hunting for opportunities to shift the balance. Every time he moved, the massive scarab blocked him. It pushed him back, trampling ants underneath.

For a moment, Ferris considered retreating. He was hurt. The scarab was too big. He could try again somewhere else.

He didn’t know how much time he had. This thing was infesting Jade’s soul. It had followed his movements so far, could he even get away from it?

It needs to die.

Leveling his spear, Ferris charged back in. The massive scarab lowered its head again, ready. He thrust in at the thing’s eye, but it moved its head. The spear tip bounced harmlessly off its shell.

Retaliating, the scarab tried to swipe at him. It was big, but far too slow. Ferris pulled back, avoiding the blow. If it weren’t for the sea of reinforcements behind it, Ferris would’ve had a merry time dancing around it.

He stayed at the edge, testing the monster with his spear. Trying to hit weak points. Joints, places where the shell connected, anything. Nothing worked. Each time, the scarab moved back to block, then swipe back. Perhaps Ferris could’ve used his greater speed to dart it and score a hit… but not without exposing himself.

It would only take one hit for this thing to take Ferris out.

Yet he kept going in. His spear methodically searched for blood. Each time the massive scarab attacked back, Ferris dodged back. Little by little, he started to better understand it. The blows were powerful, but eventually… also predictable.

The key is to use the ants, he thought. This isn’t a duel. I need to force it out of position.

Ferris stopped trying to break the massive scarab’s hard shell. Instead, he started targeting the large scarabs around it. It had pushed the edge of the battle line into the ants’ area, but that gave an opportunity. If Ferris could remove enough of the thing’s allies, the ants could flank it.

It was dangerous. Each time he moved in, the massive scarab tried to punish his attack. Ferris was guessing where the next swipe would come from, then moving away before it even started. It would only take being wrong once.

Then, it happened. One of the ants got in under a back leg, without any scarabs near enough to pull it off. Ferris was on the other side and saw the massive scarab falter.

Without any hesitation, he charged in. His spear thrust forward, straight for the monster’s eye. This time, it sank deep.

Too deep. He tried to pull it back, but the spear held tight.

The creature let out a roar of pain, swinging its head wildly. Ferris was forced to let go of his weapon.

More ants came up behind him. Heedless of its mad struggle, they charged. A few were crushed, but enough made it up and on top. It was only a matter of time before they killed it.

Looking back towards the Heartfire tower, Ferris saw his chance. The scarabs had pushed forward with many of their nearby forces. There was still a line of scarabs holding up against the ants on the other side, but vastly fewer behind them.

Charging forward, Ferris ran for it. A few smaller, flying scarabs tried to get in his way. He pushed through.

At the final battle line, he aimed for one of the larger scarabs. Running directly up to it, he placed one foot on its back. Then leapt over.

He sailed over. One of the flying scarabs got in his way, only to be carried down to the other side with him. The landing wasn’t pretty, but he’d made it.

Nearby ants tore the scarab apart while Ferris lifted himself back up. Resolutely, he moved forward to climb the Heartfire tower.

At the top, he spared a brief moment to confirm Jade’s Heartfire was secure, then looked back out over the slimespace. The situation was worse than he’d feared.

The ants still seemed able to defend their pillars, but there were more scarabs than before. All along the outer wall, the scarabs were burrowing into the translucent green material. It was hard to see details from where he was, but it looked like they were eating the wall, and it had lost its glow wherever they were. The scarabs covered over twice as much of the wall’s area, compared to when he’d arrived in the slimespace just a few minutes ago.

A few of the spots in the wall had holes in them, and streams of acid trickled down. With no ants nearby, there was no sign of the damage being repaired. A few of the birds were flying above the hole, likely keeping the acid ocean at bay, but they could only do so much.

The area underneath each hole had a waterfall of acid falling down. The scarabs stayed clear of it.

They eat the wall, he thought. If they eat too much, the acid flows in, killing them.

He watched with a feral grin. I can use that.

He’d been worried about protecting his future constructions in the slimespace from acid. If Jade was hurt badly enough in the real world, he believed the ocean would flood into the area inside the dome in the slimespace.

This would usually be a bad thing. Today, however, a flood of acid covering everything above ground was exactly what Jade needed.

Ferris believed the ants had ways to survive the flood. The slimes at the ranch regularly healed from grievous injuries. The yellow pillars appeared resistant to the acid when Jade had overeaten and flooded the Consume Pool and Digest River. The scarabs, however, had no such safe haven.

If he waited too long, the scarabs might overrun the ants and get into their underground holes. At that point, the scarabs might be protected from an acid flood as well…

Then there isn’t any time to waste.

He pulled the core from his belt. Looking around the top of the Heartfire tower, Ferris considered where to leave it. The pedestal was down by the Consume Pool.

He just didn’t know how bad the acid flooding would get. From testing with Serena, he knew the holes in the dome’s wall didn’t appear directly overhead from typical cuts. But what would happen with more extreme damage?

After a few moments eyeing one of the nearby spires, Ferris headed for it. They were immune to the acid, and his best bet to protect the core.

He climbed up to the largest hole he could see, about three-quarters of the way up. When he got to the hole, there was an ant directly in the way, blocking him from entry.

After the last few minutes fighting in a life and death struggle alongside the creatures, Ferris was less wary of them. That didn’t mean he wanted to provoke an attack. He still didn’t understand them, after all.

Slowly, he moved forward to crawl by the ant. Like many of the roaming ants, it moved its antenna over him. Whatever it found, it must have liked, as it didn’t attack him.

Moving past, he brushed up against it. This was the first time he’d intentionally touched one of the grey creatures. They felt hard and unyielding. Unlike a typical warm creature, they were cold and dusty, like touching stone.

It was dark inside the hole. Only the light from outside let Ferris see anything. He was in a short tunnel that sloped downwards.

He placed the core on the ground of the tunnel, trying to wedge it tight. It wasn’t perfect, but these were desperate times. He just had to hope an ant wouldn’t carry it off, or a flood of acid wash it away. He’d protected it as much as he could.

Touching the core, Ferris closed his eyes to concentrate on leaving the slimespace. Once back in his soulspace, Ferris immediately left his soul. He woke up in his bed holding Jade.

With a bit of alarm, Ferris realized Jade was now leaking. Small puncture holes were visible in the slime’s outer layer, leaking acid onto him and the bed.

Setting Jade on the bed, Ferris grabbed his knife and looked down at the slime. Cutting them would likely make a mess, but Jade’s life was at risk. If he waited and the ants were overrun, he didn’t know what would happen to the slime.

With a grim scowl, Ferris began slicing long, shallow gashes through Jade’s outer shell. The slime’s inner liquid fell out onto the bed, absorbed into the sheets and mattress. Ferris was careful not to go anywhere near the cage protecting the core, but he kept up the cuts as Jade lost more and more volume.

Usually, this would have instantly healed. Instead, the wounds stayed open.

His knife was nothing special, just a mundane tool he had on hand. If he wasn’t careful, the slime’s acid would quickly dissolve it. Remembering how Serena’s dagger didn’t appear above the Consume Pool until a certain depth, Ferris opted to only use shallow, quick cuts. Just barely slice open the outer layer, then pull back and move to another cut.

He also remembered how Jade’s breakfast had taken progressively longer to digest. If the ants were unable to help empower the acid, how strong would it be?

Ferris wasn’t sure if it was the shallow cuts or Jade being sick, but his mundane knife didn’t show any signs of melting.

He tried several different ways of slicing, trying to cover as much area as possible. Ferris remembered the wide blades the ranchers used to cut the slimes in half. He couldn’t separate large chunks with the small knife, even if he was willing to cut deeper. Despite this, Jade was still visibly shrinking.

Soon, his familiar was much smaller; barely a thin layer of acid around the caged core. Ferris thought this was the second stage described by Serena, where a slime would still heal their outer body, but was smaller than before. It wasn’t yet the third stage, where they would just be a slimy core. Ferris shuddered at the sight; he could likely have poked the core with his finger without needing to risk his hand.

Seeing the change, he stopped cutting. Now he just had to wait to see how Jade would react.

At the ranch, Ferris hadn’t seen in detail what happened to the slimes after they had been cut down. They stopped moving after being bisected, then the rancher just dropped them back into the pit. After that, it was hard to see them among the rest. Apparently, they eventually ate and recovered.

Ferris wanted to check on Jade in the slimespace, but was afraid to try. If the area was flooded as he thought, then he might just appear in a river of acid. The entrance to the tunnel Ferris had left the core in was high up, but he didn’t know if it was high enough. Ferris had no idea how high the flood would be, although he dearly hoped the two towers were above it.

He knew slimes would survive the flood. The Heartfire may or may not be submerged, but it wasn’t like normal fire. It didn’t burn air, it burned lifeforce. A slime’s Heartfire being submerged in acid might not even bother it…

I don’t know, Ferris thought, because I was afraid to test it. No more half measures with testing.

He shook his head. There would be time for that later. For now, he needed to decide if the risk was worth it.

If Ferris’ mental body died, he would be knocked unconscious for several hours. Even worse, he wouldn’t be able to help Jade in the slimespace for at least two days. Rushing could be a fatal mistake… but could he afford to wait?

He sat there, next to the bed, watching Jade for a few minutes. At some point, Ferris would need to move on to his next plan: asking Elder Harper for help. He decided to wait a few hours; the normal amount of time Serena claimed a slime needed to recover. If Jade’s condition worsened, then he would act sooner, but everything Ferris had been told claimed the slime should be fine.

The wait was agonizing. Ferris paced in his small room—not easy with a crutch. A few times, Ferris thought he felt weak feelings over the bond, but nothing substantial.

Looking for a distraction, Ferris piled his ruined sheets on top of the bed. The slime fertilizer had soaked through the bedding and into the mattress. Thankfully, only a little dripped down onto the floor. He was able to soak it up with what remained of his sheets and contain the damage to just the mattress and bedding. The liquid was acidic, but not magically strong. Still, the fabric was discolored and stained. He was lucky it just ruined the bedding rather than anything truly dangerous.

After an eternity, he heard Jade’s voice over the bond.

“Ferris?” Jade said weakly.

“Jade!” Ferris said, excited. “How are you feeling?”

“Hard… to think,” Jade replied slowly.

The next couple of minutes showed rapid improvement as Jade was able to respond more quickly. The slime started to slowly roll around, although it was wobbly and awkward with so little size.

Jade was enthusiastic as ever when offered food, although Ferris was a bit hesitant. He fed the slime the remainder of breakfast, keeping a close watch for any new problems.

Lunch had come and gone, and Mother Lily had left two plates of food next to his door for him. Thankful, he took them and fed Jade all of it. The slime’s voracious hunger was back with a vengeance. Jade’s body started to grow larger with each piece of food dissolved. After both lunches were eaten by the slime, Ferris took Jade downstairs to the bundles of straw to continue feeding. He didn’t stop until they were slightly larger than usual.

At this point, Jade appeared to be back to normal. The slime could talk and move without any problem, although they didn’t understand what had happened or why.

Ferris decided it was time to risk investigating the slimespace, so he took Jade back to his room. The bed was ruined, so he had to lie on the ground to meditate, with Jade in his arms as usual. His thoughts were filled with concerns and questions about how this had happened. The meditation helped quiet his mind, but it didn’t really give any solutions. At least it put him in a better frame of mind to eventually solve the problems.

Waking up in his soulspace, Ferris quickly noticed a difference. The pedestal that usually held the core was completely melted. The green core, the ability seed for Assimilate Object, and the key to the legacy chest were sitting in a distorted puddle. He carefully removed them, then considered what had happened.

When Ferris had made the pedestal, he’d imagined it linked between his soulspace and the slimespace. When he first traveled to the slimespace, a matching pedestal already existed on the other side. If creating one on this side made a duplicate appear on the other side, then it stood to reason that the opposite was true: destroying it in one place would also destroy the matching pair.

Ferris smiled. Destroyed in a flood of acid, along with an army of yellow scarabs.

Well, he might as well go over and take a look.
