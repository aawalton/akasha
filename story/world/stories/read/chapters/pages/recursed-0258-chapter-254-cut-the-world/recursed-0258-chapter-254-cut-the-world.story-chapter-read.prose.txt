Nyxalraith clicked her tongue and spat. The acid immediately melted through the collar of the Scripture’s robe and he leaped away. She struck again for the Mad boy, but he was gone.

Time ticked by at a crawl as her heart pounded.

The one that had blocked her strike was holding his neck, but by the glowing runes shining between his fingers, her saliva wasn’t going to bother him for long. A few metres to his right stood a woman. Another Scripture. She was already removing her hands from Nyxalraith’s target, flinching away from the boy as if the touch burned.

Apparently they hadn’t liked losing one of their Mad experiments. Nyxalraith had been hoping she’d be able to cut down the more unpredictable combatants before she had to deal with these powerhouses.

It didn’t matter that she had been culling cultists by the thousand. It didn’t matter that she’d faced cult leaders and come out on top. Tenth evo cultists were still tenth evolution. They would not fall easily.

Nyxalraith felt her darkness shift. Her shadows momentarily pulled against her, as if being commanded by something else. Fury engulfed her mind. Who dared think they could usurp what was hers? Before the Scripture could even seize the tiniest of hand-holds, Nyxalraith locked down on all nearby shadows, barricading them from those whose fingers didn’t belong.

One of the Scriptures staggered with a gasp. Her whole left side exploded in black wisps that seemed to peel away layers of flesh as if they were held together with nothing but steam. The ritual carved into her skin backfired spectacularly.

Another woman beside her had only lost an instant in her sideways glance, and it almost cost her.

Nyxalraith’s chainsaw roared to life as she swung her body, tail coming around with enough strength to topple buildings. The Scripture raised her arm to block. A ritual burning from wrist to shoulder summoned a gleaming silver shell just in time to take the blow. For a moment, the strike was stopped dead. While the chainsaw ground away without leaving a scratch, it was as if all momentum had vanished from Nyxalraith’s motion.

Then, the shell screamed. An ear-tendril-quivering squeal that warbled as if submerged in tar. The Scripture’s defences cracked, and so too did her arm.

Where a human’s flesh was limited by an upper limit — only surpassed externally through names — Nyxalraith’s mutations were not. At least not so far as she’d discovered. While this was the summon of a ninth evolution and she was only seventh, Nyxalraith had spent months of slaughter to fuel her mutations.

She now hit hard. Very hard.

If only her bones weren’t such a bottleneck. As she was now, only the enhanced flexibility and strength provided by her names, and her rapid regeneration allowed her to get away with using so much power. And even then, it wasn’t as much as she knew she could achieve.

Nyxalraith couldn’t wait to change her bones. The prerequisite altering the deepest and most fundamental building blocks of her body had taken over a month — the longest of any mutation by far — and she was desperate to take on some of the changes she’d seen in her name, just waiting for her.

But she wasn’t going to have the benefit of that mutation for this fight. Not that she needed it.

To the woman’s credit, she did little more than grunt and shrug aside the chainsaw now making mince of her arm. The absence of momentum conservation whatever that summoned armour offered her before it died certainly helped. She hadn’t gone flying after Nyxalraith’s strike. It left her close enough, and with a good enough idea of where her opponent was in the blinding darkness to land a hit of her own.

The weapon she summoned appeared less like a blade and more like a massive shard of glass taken directly from Coral’s upper dome. In the instant the ritual was performed — without so much as a word of hymn — a shimmering crystalline structure appeared along her good arm. It was larger then she was, its sharp edge curving from a metre behind her shoulder to twice her body’s length ahead of her. The thing had to be ten times heavier than her body, yet she swung it around easily.

Nyxalraith was quick. And so when the oversized blade swung for her, her flexibility arced her back harmlessly over the glass that shimmered with her shadows.

But then it was there again, right where it had been a millisecond before, swinging for her again.

She beat her wings, and somehow, Nyxalraith managed to twist her body around the razor sharp crystal a second time. The third time, though? She didn’t have the time. The best she was able to do was angle her body just enough that her bark-coated shoulder took the brunt of the blow. The crystalline blade sliced through the rubber flesh of her upper arms and, if she was seeing this right, it was trying to siphon something into her body. Only… her swirling storm of darkness put an end to that before it could ever settle.

The blade hadn’t even had the time to get stuck in her arm before it was gone again. Not for long. The weapon had backed up a mere few centimetres and struck her again with all the force of the initial blow.

Despite the pride Nyxalraith had in her mutated body, the jackhammer of inescapable blows sliced through her arm in moments.

That was a terrifying name. The Scripture could ultimately reset her attacks to whatever point in the swing she wanted, conserving the full strength from that moment in time. If she could get close, there weren’t many people that would survive such a rapid assault. Unfortunately for her, Nyxalraith was one such person.

As the glass blade hammered past the arm and into ribs, Pushy and Curious swung rapiers with all their improved strength. Shy and Cuddly were a moment behind their siblings, unleashing intense streams of fire that lit the woman before Nyxalraith’s shadows could swallow the light. The arm wielding the huge glass blade flew to the side, severed, and the woman was starting to teeter, having lost the balance her leg had provided.

Then, she was suddenly back to pristine condition. The crystalline edge slammed into Nyxalraith’s side again.

Ok. Now she was annoyed.

Nyxalraith lunged. Her tail shoved against the ground and her wings slammed against the strangely thick air in this Null Scar. When Nyxalraith snapped her mouth closed, her dual sets of razor sharp teeth clacking, she was still half a metre from the woman’s head. Didn’t matter. O̅s̫s̋thͤ̂o̲t͛h stretched those deadly edges beyond her reach, and they clamped down on the Scripture’s head.

Another summon appeared trying to protect her skull, but it did little against teeth that bypassed all defences entirely. Nyxalraith felt the soft squish of the juicy organ beneath her teeth, but the only outward indication that the Scripture’s brain had been sliced was the sudden cloud of blood in one eye.

For good measure, her tentacles stabbed their blades in the woman’s chest. They twisted viciously so that any further recalling would do little to save her.

Nyxalraith stepped forward, her chest opening, and bit off the woman’s upper half before she could even hit the ground. Her momentum didn’t stop. The high creed harbinger’s guts dribbled at her side as she flew through the air and into the first woman who’d thought she could influence her darkness.

She was almost fully recovered now, but that meant nothing when Nyxalraith had finally gotten started.

Her claws — fully regenerated — slammed into the Scripture. Her defences seemed to alter her body from flesh to some pitch black rocky substance. Like obsidian, but still flexible. Clearly not something natural, given the way darkness seemed to spill from it like some sort of inverse, soupy light. A questionable option, given the darkness fed Nyxalraith the moment it left the altered flesh.

While her pincers slammed down hard on the harbinger, they didn’t pierce nearly as deeply as she would have liked. No matter. All Nyxalraith needed was to hold her still.

Each of her tentacles rested their rapiers at Nyxalraith’s side, ready, patiently waiting as she gathered herself for what was one of the most difficult combinations of abilities she’d ever attempted. O̅s̫s̋thͤ̂o̲t͛h and V̠ͅa͍i̝k͍na̓ displaying the heights of their power.

Nyxalraith breathed, and slowly, far too slowly for the pace of the battle so far, swung all four blades.

The scream was instant. First, it was the whistle of air rushing into the tips of each blade. Then, it was the woman’s screams. Those didn’t last long. And finally, as the four rapiers carved out the back of the Scripture’s altered body, the world itself screamed.

It was a horrifying wail, though somehow lesser than the one other time she’d tried this attack.

From the edges of her rapiers, unreal blades surged forth. Four lines sliced through the air as a pair of crosses, shrieking ever louder as more space was swallowed by the slow-but-relentless incorporeal blade.

If the woman wasn’t dead from the initial strike, then the way she was sucked into the lines and reduced to a shower of blood made sure of it.

The shriek continued. Nyxalraith didn’t know if she’d heard a worse noise. At least, she didn’t think she had until the sluggish lines expanded outward enough to consume the grass. As soon as the knife sliced the hill, the howl grew a thousand times worse. Her tendrils flinched back, but that did little to muffle the sound. It was the cry of reality being shredded.

As much as she wanted to gawk at the growing rift, she was still in a battle. Another rushed her. She slipped out of the way, but it was concerning how they were all finding her through her shadow storm.

A moment to glance around revealed why. One of the Scriptures had sat down to cast a dizzying array of rituals. Given the way they connected him to the others, he was likely feeding them information. What was just as concerning, was the massive mountain of summons the other Scripture was in the middle of manifesting.

Nyxalraith made to cut down the man who tried to rush her again, but something in the corner of her eye made her put her everything into dodging. Something she’d kept one eye on at all times.

She phased through space and landed roughly almost a dozen metres away. Thankfully, unharmed. The man who’d been attacking her though? He was not so fortunate. The Mad boy’s invisible art trick had removed him from existence. Nyxalraith’s eyes couldn’t so much as see a sliver of his soul remaining.

The battlefield fell into a lull as the Scriptures took in their reduced numbers. From seven to four in a matter of seconds. As high evolution harbingers, they probably never expected their defences to fail so quickly.

There was motion by the Null Scar. She watched an eighth evolution cultist step through, appearing clearly rushed. From his soul, she could tell he was no harbinger. A messenger, then.

Nyxalraith let out a chuckle. Her echoes made it incredibly eerie even to her own ears. She watched the moment these cultists turned their wary gaze from the Mad boy who’d killed one of their own, to the messenger no doubt bringing terrible news from above, then to her. Her storm swirled closer to those remaining, and she relished their hesitant steps back.

With the horrifying world-tearing sound of the gradually expanding air-fissure, Nyxalraith assessed her remaining opponents. The Mad kids had terrifying attacks, but so far they hadn’t shown an interest in defending themselves like the man-boy. There was the one who had first blocked her claws, and the other three, clearly preparing much bigger rituals than she could immediately unravel.

Ta’Malis wanted her to test the new weapon, right? Wasn’t this the perfect opportunity?

“They say not to play with your food,” Nyxalraith said, her thousand voices making sure everyone could hear over the shrieking tears slowly working their way across the landscape. “But where’s the fun in that?”

Nyxalraith stepped into Pink, and donned the war machine.
