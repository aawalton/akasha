M-17 no longer blocked their way, and that alone felt like a small miracle.

Gilbert gave Sakura a short nod.

“Let’s go.”

They slipped into the passage that had once been blocked by the heavy glowing mass. The dying gatherer had been right. The path really was there: a narrow corridor, two short descents, a rusty grate with a plaque that read LOWER DISCHARGE. Farther ahead, Sakura spotted a staircase and rough red smears on the walls, made from clay and something tar-like.

“Lower discharge,” she muttered as she squeezed through the grate.

“A drainage route, most likely,” Gilbert said. “The lab had to send dirty water, spent mixtures, and everything left over from the experiments somewhere.”

The way out of the lab turned out to be less awful than the way in. There was less slime around them, the smell was better, and there was less trash and filth underfoot. The red marks led Gilbert and Sakura down, then up, through service corridors, half-flooded landings, and badly ruined staircases that sometimes felt like a suicide attempt just to walk on.

At one of the turns, Sakura noticed that Gilbert’s hair had gone light again and his features had returned to normal.

“The shapeshifter flowers wore off,” she said quietly.

Gilbert did not even look back.

“I noticed.”

“And that doesn’t bother you at all?”

“Not anymore.”

Sakura frowned.

“Because they already know who we are?”

“Yes. After the Drunken Lady, the disguise stopped mattering.”

That sounded far too calm for such terrible news. But no matter how much that calm annoyed her, Gilbert was right.

They climbed another level by a narrow staircase, following the red marks. There was almost no trash or slime left on the way. As they walked, Sakura kept wanting to talk about something else.

Her talent.

The System had finally named it, and the name had been sitting somewhere under her ribs ever since, refusing to leave her alone.

Bond Weaver.

She had repeated the name to herself several times already, and every time she felt the same strange mix of anxiety and pride. Like she had finally gotten an answer, only the answer had come with even more questions.

Sakura glanced at Gilbert.

He walked ahead, lighting the way with the white-blue flame in his palm, and looked focused. Sakura found herself thinking that his talent was probably just like him: dangerous, cold, and insanely useful. Something like “Storm Lord,” “Contour Breaker,” or “Kills Everything Alive with a Single Glance.”

She had almost opened her mouth to ask when she suddenly noticed a thick strip of slime on the wall, fat and impossible to miss. It glistened on the stone and filled the cracks.

Sakura slowed.

“Listen...” she said. “Should we take some of this slime?”

Gilbert looked at her over his shoulder. Sakura kept going.

“Not the mother sample, obviously. This stuff left on the walls. The notes said they used the slime as a painkiller at first.”

Gilbert slowed down.

“Go on.”

That was already a good sign. It meant he did not think the idea was complete nonsense.

“Well,” Sakura said, “if it really has painkilling or healing properties, maybe alchemy could turn it into something useful. Not a drug. A real medicine. Or at least a base for a potion.”

Gilbert looked at the glistening trail of slime more carefully.

“In theory, yes,” he said at last.

“So it’s not stupid?”

“It’s still risky,” he corrected dryly. “But it’s not stupid.”

Gilbert stepped closer to the wall, but he did not touch the slime with his hands. He only studied it, head tilted slightly.

“To put it simply, a substance like this could be used as an alchemical base,” he said. “Not a finished potion. A base is something you purify, bind with other reagents, and stabilize afterward. If the slime really keeps its painkilling property, we could try purifying it, stripping out the psychoactive part, and leaving only the useful effect.”

Sakura frowned.

“That sounds complicated.”

“It is. But it’s possible. You’d need a neutralizing reagent, a good filter, weak stabilizing magic or a rune, and at least some knowledge of alchemy.”

He crouched, pulled a small glass vessel with a narrow neck from his Inventory, and took out a thin metal spatula. Sakura watched with unwilling interest.

“Do you always carry tools for collecting suspicious slime?”

“You have no idea what else I carry,” Gilbert said, and Sakura had no idea how to react to that.

Then he said nothing more. Carefully, he slid the spatula under the edge of the glistening mass, taking only the top layer, the part that had not mixed with dirt and stone grit. The slime stretched into a thin thread and slowly slipped into the vessel.

Sakura grimaced.

“That looks disgusting.”

She could not help thinking that at the city gate they had only pretended to be drug dealers, and now they were actually carrying something that could alter consciousness and cause euphoria. Though it was unlikely to help them with Faerd. Unless he suddenly turned out to be a fan of questionable slime-based entertainment.

Gilbert gathered a little more, only a small amount, then sealed the vessel with a stopper and held it in his hand for a second before the glass disappeared.

“If this doesn’t explode in my Inventory, that’ll already be a good start,” Gilbert muttered.

“I really hope it doesn’t.”

After that, they kept going, and soon realized the lab was far behind them. The corridors grew wider and rougher, more like old drainage tunnels. Wide gutters of murky water ran along the sides. The slime was gone. Now it smelled like silt, rotten fish, and foul water. No slime toxins anymore, but that mix was no better.

Sakura grimaced after her first breath.

“Oh gods...”

“Drainage tunnels,” Gilbert said, looking around. “And judging by the smell, we’re already on the other side of Ogrom. Somewhere beyond the market quarters.”

“You figured that out from the smell?”

“From the masonry. And the smell too.”

They walked a little farther, and Gilbert pointed ahead with the flame. Sakura focused and saw the old guts of the city: a broad vaulted ceiling, narrow stone ledges along the walls, and rusted side grates.

“In old cities like Ogrom, waste is often carried outside the wall,” Gilbert said. “Through lower arches, drainage openings, or old runoff channels. The water and all the filth flows into a moat or a ravine beyond the city. If we’re lucky, we’ll go out the same way.”

“Lucky?” Sakura pressed her mask tighter against her nose. “I’m starting to think you and I have very different definitions of luck.”

The farther they went, the worse it smelled. Sakura stopped trying to preserve any dignity and kept the mask pressed to the lower half of her face almost constantly. Gilbert, as usual, looked completely calm. As if stench, filth, blood, and insane government mages were not reasons to panic, just parts of a very bad day.

“I still hope Faerd doesn’t catch up to us,” Sakura said, trying to breathe through her mouth.

“He will,” Gilbert answered calmly.

She glanced at him.

“You could at least lie comfortingly right now.”

“I don’t want to waste energy on unconvincing fairy tales. We’ll meet him again. That isn’t the question. The question is what we’ll be when we do.”

Sakura stayed quiet, and Gilbert gave her a quick look.

“You need to level up and develop your skills. The sooner, the better.”

This time she nodded without joking.

“I know.”

Soon they heard a stronger rush of water ahead. The tunnel began sloping downward, and the air turned even colder. A few dozen steps later, they reached the discharge outlet itself.

Sakura stopped.

Ahead of them yawned a wide black arch where foul, murky water poured outside, beyond the wall of Ogrom. Somewhere ahead she could already make out the predawn sky and the weak gray light beyond. The sight made her realize just how exhausted she really was, how badly she wanted to sleep, how much her legs ached, how badly her head throbbed. But right before the exit, a heavy iron grate blocked the arch, buried under a century of buildup.

Sakura went even paler when she understood the second part of the problem.

To get out, they would have to jump right into that stream of water.

She stared at it, and her expression changed. Everything inside her clenched with such pure, almost physical disgust that for a moment she even forgot about Faerd. The murky water carried all of Ogrom’s filth outside, and now they were expected to willingly become part of that current. Nausea hit her at once, sharp and immediate, even though her stomach had long since gone empty.

Sakura slowly turned her head toward Gilbert. He was looking at the grate with not the slightest trace of disgust, exactly as expected.

“No,” Sakura said quietly. “No. Don’t say it. I already understand.”

Gilbert pulled the familiar case of scrolls from his Inventory.

“Good,” he said, holding it out to her. “Now understand one more thing. How do we get outside?”

Sakura took the case. The mage was testing her again. Probably not out of cruelty or spite, though Gilbert certainly knew how to be spiteful sometimes. Right now he simply wanted her to work with magic more, learn faster how to read a situation and think like a mage.

Sakura crouched by the wall, opened the case, and began quickly flipping through the scrolls, reading the information written on them. The paper rustled dry and familiar in her hands. Titles flashed past her eyes, and she had to force away the first impulse to grab whatever seemed usable.

She knew explosive and cutting scrolls would make too much noise, leave an obvious trace, and draw the wrong kind of attention. They did not need to destroy the whole thing or alert the guards. They needed to remove metal quietly.

Her fingers stopped on one of the scrolls.

“This one,” Sakura said. “A crafting scroll. For smiths or foundry work. Something like localized melting.”

Gilbert gave a small smirk.

“Good.”

“We melt the iron instead of breaking it.”

“Exactly.”

Sakura looked up at him.

“You’re a real treasure vault of scrolls, Gilbert. Where did you get a collection like this?”

Gilbert answered in a completely serious tone.

“I collected them from dead mages.”

Sakura just stared at him for a few seconds.

“Oh... Sorry. Sometimes I ask things before I think.”

“Don’t apologize. It was a reasonable question. It’s good that you’re not afraid to ask. It would be worse if you stopped trying to be curious at all.”

Sakura looked back at the case. After those words, the scrolls inside it no longer felt like a miracle. They felt like something much grimmer. Each of them, apparently, had once belonged to someone. Some mage who had also studied, improved, and tried to survive.

But failed.

Sakura found herself wondering where exactly Gilbert had collected them. After battles? In burned-out hideouts? In the southern colonies he had mentioned? Or in places even worse, places she still knew far too little about?

She wanted to ask, but she did not. Here and now, it was definitely the wrong time.

“All right,” she said more quietly, looking down at the scroll again. “Then let’s try not to waste this one.”

Gilbert held her gaze for a second, maybe understanding what she had chosen not to ask. Then he gave a short nod.

She took a breath, unrolled the scroll, and focused on the grate. This time, making contact with the magic came more easily than it had during her first training or when she cast the net on the obsidian beasts. Sakura reached toward the scroll’s energy, trying not to clamp down on it, but to guide it outward the way Gilbert had taught her.

The magic answered.

[LOCALIZED MELTING]

A steady dim orange glow spread over the bars of the grate. The metal darkened at first, then began to sag, as if it were softening right before her eyes. Drops of molten iron dripped down and hissed in the dirty water. For one moment, the smell of hot metal overwhelmed the rest of the stench, and that felt like a blessing.

The grate slumped.

Gilbert shoved it with his foot, and the softened edge slid aside almost silently, opening a gap wide enough for them to squeeze through.

He looked down at the flow of water below the discharge, then at Sakura.

“We jump.”

And without giving her time for an extended private tragedy, he jumped first.

Straight into that disgusting, murky, reeking water.

Sakura squeezed her eyes shut in revulsion from the sight alone.

“I hate Veyr,” she whispered.

Then she opened them again, saw Gilbert already standing below almost waist-deep in water and looking up at her, and understood that once again she had no choice at all.

Fighting back almost animal disgust, she jumped.

The water hit her with cold, stench, and the feeling of being dumped into a bucket of waste. Sakura curled in on herself, but Gilbert caught her at once, keeping her from slamming into the rocks or vanishing under the foul current.

For one very short moment, she ended up in the mage’s strong arms. In another situation, she might even have lingered on that.

But right now they both smelled like they had been personally cursed by the entire sewer system of Ogrom. Romance had died somewhere much higher up, along with the last scraps of her dignity.

“Let’s not waste time,” Gilbert said. “We need to go.”

They climbed out of the stinking water and immediately ran farther, away from the wall of Ogrom. The ground underfoot was slick, their clothes clung to their bodies, and they smelled so foul that Sakura would have preferred not to possess a sense of smell at all.

Once they had put enough distance between themselves and the wall, she finally looked back.

Smoke was rising over Ogrom, and not the ordinary kind that belonged to any city. It was dark, thick, and restless, hinting that something large and bad was burning somewhere inside. An explosion? A fire? Something worse?

Gilbert stopped too and looked back at it.

For one moment, they simply stood there side by side, breathing hard after the escape, staring at the dark walls of Ogrom with the smoke rising slowly behind them.

The city was behind them.

But for some reason, that did not make anything feel safer.
