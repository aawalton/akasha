Beep…

Beep..

Beep.

My head throbbed, the piercing sting of an ache from front to back like nothing I’d ever felt. It was as if someone had taken a fucking ski pole and shoved it through my brain, then—just for fun—pulled it out, then back in, over and over again, until my gray matter was more like a gray slushy.

No. Not a ski pole… An icicle.

I really hated that bitch. And Jericho.

I was probably dead for real now, right? I had to be dead.

I opened my eyes to a mostly dark room. The obnoxious glow of several monitors beside the bed I lay in illuminated my surroundings enough to see. A machine beeped in time with my heart.

Someone sat in a chair by a window, asleep. Heavy snoring vibrated his entire silhouette.

Derek? My… my brother.

I tried to sit up, but my body was sore, my abdomen a wreck. Barely audible groans came out when I called to him, my throat hoarse and shredding with pain.

Avery rested in a chair near my feet—Avery! Avery?!

I felt a sudden sensation of déjà vu. Had I seen this before?

The memory came flooding back to me. I thought it'd been a dream, but now it felt so real. I had been here before. I'd glimpsed it after Zeus zapped me.

It was real. My heart thundered joyously in my chest.

Avery lay with her upper body on my legs, her long brown hair draped over her shoulder and splayed out across the end of the bed. Her head was turned toward me, eyes closed, her back rising and falling with long, sleepy breaths. An adorable line of drool hung from her mouth, leaving a small wet spot on the blanket over me.

I’d never been so happy to see dripping saliva in my life.

But if I was here, then maybe I had it backwards. Maybe that meant everything else had been a dream. How could it possibly have been real? Just a really awful, weird, realistic—yet unfathomable—dream.

I mean, come on… Sins manifesting as monsters? Lunatics prancing around with literal scythes? Magic?

No. I was alive.

There was no Umbral Plane. No Jericho. No Reapers. No crazy bitch with an icicle lobotomy skill.

No Nuzzle. (Though, that thought made me surprisingly sad.)

But it didn’t matter. He was a figment of my imagination. Never existed.

I allowed myself to sink into the less than comfortable pillow behind me. The plasticy mattress crinkled beneath as I shifted my weight slightly.

I didn't even care that I was in a hospital. I was here.

Exhaustion overwhelmed my body, even though I’d just woken up. Several bags of liquid hung from a pole near my head, draining down through tubing and into my arm where I felt the sting of an intravenous needle. Probably some sort of sedative.

What the hell had happened to me? Really happened?

I wanted to wake Derek and Avery. Ask them everything.

My mind wouldn’t let me, though. My eyes drooped closed. There would be plenty of time to talk to them in the morning.

I’ll just…

A yawn escaped my lips.

I’ll just have to wait until then.

When I woke there was no bed. No beeping machines. No Derek. No Avery.

My body spun rapidly in the air, feeling like a rag doll, then slowed, and my feet were somehow on the ground. I stumbled, my vestibular system failing to catch my balance, and fell to a knee in the grass.

It was night, but even in the dark I could tell the grass was far too green.

I shot to my feet, still dizzy.

No…

Fuck no.

“Whyyyyy?” I screamed into the sky.

It was supposed to be a dream.

The landscape around me was depressingly familiar, though bathed in the white light of a nonexistent moon this time. The same violet pansies nearby. The trees in the distance with gold leaves and red trunks. The burned swath of grass from my battle with the gator-lady still giving off the scent of charred yard waste. The hill behind me that Jericho and the wagon had first appeared over. Even the small hole in the ground before me where my scythe had been staked.

All there. All real.

My Amber cloak billowed in the cool nightly breeze. I clenched my fists, one with normal human skin, the other armored in dark Amber-hued carapace.

I was back in Penumbra.

I closed my eyes and allowed an exasperated sigh to leave my body.

The angel-god whatever being had said something about it being the last time I ever slept when he spoke to me. I understood now.

There was no apparent injury or even residual pain from having my face murdered. And while the hole in my shirt remained from where Zeus had struck me with lightning, the melted, blackened skin beneath was completely healed. The conversation before I'd been stabbed in the eye with an icicle made complete sense now. They’d known this was what would happen.

The red bar of health had replenished, but the blue one had dropped even further leaving me with only about a third left.

I made my way to the top of the hill. Of course, I had no idea how to get back to the tavern. If I’d known I was going to be dropped back here alone again, I might have paid more attention during our travels.

Though… Did I even want to go back to the tavern? I might be better off figuring this shit out myself, rather than relying on Jericho’s incompetence and his psychotic apprentice’s torture.

From the hill, the landscape looked much the same. Several souls wandered around the plains, shadows lurking in the night. They seemed more menacing now for some reason, the cool air giving me goosebumps not helping ease the vibe of unease.

I had no path, no goal. Maybe if I started walking aimlessly, I’d eventually come up with something or arrive somewhere that might help me understand this place better.

I chose a direction at random, avoiding the same way I’d originally left with Jericho, and started walking. Two Sins meandered nearby, but Jericho had said they’d remain passive if unprovoked.

Naturally, I’d only made it three steps before something stopped me in my tracks.

A menacing roar ripped through the air, vibrating the hairs on the back of my neck, reminiscent of the first horror I'd experienced at my bachelor party.

And it was close.

A monster emerged from the valley between two hills before me. The head of a rhinoceros, horn and all, rested upon broad, armored shoulders. Eyes literally burned Amber flames, trailing tendrils as it moved its head. It stood on two legs, taller by several feet than any other Sin I’d seen so far. It had the same lava-like veins as the first Sin I'd defeated, and an Amber aura burned around it, casting a glow on its surroundings.

Rank: Amber

Affinity: Vigor]

An orange skull floated above its head. It's marked for death, but… The skull image was cracked in half. No idea what that means, but it can't be anything good.

The rhino hybrid approached the closest passive Sin—a thin, frog-like humanoid with awkwardly long arms—and lifted a thick hand with five metallic horns instead of fingers. With one swipe, it sent the frog launching through the air and out of sight.

The rhino locked eyes on me. And it… grinned?

“Well, fuck,” I cursed.

Stumbling backwards, I called my scythe. It slid impossibly slow from my carapace and formed in my hand like some strange shapeless liquid.

The rhino crouched to all fours and charged. Dirt and grass flew in its wake. The ground rumbled.

I couldn’t outrun that thing. Not in a million years.

So I stood my ground. Maybe I’d get lucky. Maybe this was one of those cases where the bark was worse than the bite.

It barreled through the other Sin in its path, skewering it with a horn as it passed, and sending it tumbling to the side in a bloody pile.

Nope. Its bite was still pretty bad.

I ran in a sideways arc, hoping the monster’s straight-line momentum would keep it from cutting me off. It worked—sort of. The beast slid, dragging its horn-claws in the ground to slow itself like some devil-spawned mutant snowboarder. I’d gained a little distance, but the rhino quickly made up that ground. I changed direction again and found my movement mirrored by the rampaging Sin. It seemed hell-bent on targeting me for some unholy reason.

My tactic wasn’t working. I could already feel my legs aching and I had a suspicion I wouldn’t be the one with more stamina in this race.

Fighting was the only option.

Slowing my run, I allowed the rhino to get closer until I could practically smell the putrid hot air from its nostrils on my back. I could hear the heavy heaving of its breath, the pounding of its feet. The metal horn tickling at my back.

Then I Aero Shifted to the side.

The rhino stormed past me. I swung my scythe, screaming incomprehensibly, aiming for its neck.

Instead, my blade clattered against its legs and flung from my hands, sore from the impact.

The beast skidded to a stop and turned on me.

I stood there, weaponless. Out of options.
