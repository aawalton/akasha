Mother Lily led Ferris to a staircase in the hallway. He awkwardly hopped down the steps while Jade rolled behind. The storeroom was neatly organized with jars and boxes stacked on shelves along the walls. Mother Lily showed Ferris a corner with ten large green bricks in two tall stacks.

After helping him get one down, Mother Lily and Ferris examined it. Each brick of wet straw was pressed together and wrapped with some kind of plant fiber string. Grabbing Jade, Ferris set the slime down on top and sent a confirmation over the bond to eat the brick.

At first, Jade was unsure how to get the straw inside their body. They couldn’t just melt down into a flat surface, but with a little effort, they could consume the brick's corner. It was slower than usual, but Jade used the corner to methodically melt down chunks of the straw like bites into an apple. Each ‘bite’ created new edges that Jade could use to consume more of the brick, until there was nothing left—not even the string.

Ferris and Mother Lily watched in fascination as Jade ate something about five times their size. Typically, an animal cannot eat more total volume than their stomach. Jade’s Consume Pool ability, however, was doing something magical. Ferris wasn’t sure yet if the food actually moved into the slimespace, or if the copy that appeared there was technically different.

However it worked, and the food appeared to simply disappear, melting away to nothing inside the slime’s body.

Ferris kneeled to examine Jade closer. While before, the slime had always been smaller than Ferris’ head, now they were about the same size.

Bigger, but nowhere near as much as they should’ve been after eating something five times their size.

Ferris grabbed another brick and set it down for Jade. The slime took to it with the same enthusiasm as the first.

Jade was in heaven. The slime didn’t rate the wet straw very high—at a two out of ten—but Jade couldn’t stop rippling as they finished the second brick, followed by a third. Then a fourth, fifth, sixth, and finally seventh brick.

With more and more food, Jade steadily grew until Ferris decided to stop at about twice their normal size. This was about the size Ferris had seen the slime ranchers allow their slimes to grow to before harvesting fertilizer, which made it the largest ‘safe’ size he knew of. He was afraid of over-feeding, as Foreman Carter had said a slime would eventually throw up slime fertilizer.

That would be a good test, but the current goal was feeding the Digest River to produce qi. Also, Mother Lily definitely wouldn’t appreciate slime fertilizer all over her basement floor.

Jade had eaten six and a half bricks. Ferris had purchased ten: twice what he expected the slime to eat, which showed his estimate was low. If he converted a single merit point to currency to purchase animal feed, he would get less than a week—just six days—of food.

While paying a merit point each week would hurt, at least it would be temporary. Once he started delving, Ferris could replace the straw with corpses of monsters in the rifts. Until then, the qi he expected to get from the Consume Pool ability each day would give Ferris a big advantage getting ready to delve and working towards healing his leg.

Leaving the storeroom, Ferris now had to deal with a much larger Jade. The slime had doubled in size, and Ferris had trouble carrying them while using his crutch. Thankfully, they weren’t outside, so Jade could roll around beside him.

Ferris still didn’t want the slime to roll around in public, fearful that someone might mistake Jade for a wild monster. It wasn’t common, but monsters do break into the bastion from the Wild, and specifically slimes were often regarded as vermin.

Ferris thanked Mother Lily and led Jade back to their room to cultivate.

Lying down on the bed, Ferris tried to meditate, thinking about his day. Brix had given Ferris a lot to consider. She’d complained that the clan had made the test harder for her than Cyrus, and Karl had agreed. But, instead of thinking it was unfair, the man had just explained why they held her to a higher standard. Ferris couldn’t imagine the clan would let him off any easier than Brix, with a familiar bond to a conventionally weak slime monster.

The idea of ‘easing into delving gently’ went against all of the stories Ferris had heard growing up. The tales praised young cultivators who delved more than anyone else, saving the day when things went wrong. They would rescue their companions, and then eventually advancing tiers while their lazier friends languished behind.

On the other hand, Ferris wondered how long Karl had been delving. It was rude to ask an Adept’s tier directly, but the man had never spoken—or boasted—about fighting tier two monsters. He had talked about some of the more dangerous tier one monsters he had fought over the years. Years of delving, even before becoming a retainer of Hollowfield.

Ferris didn’t care for Karl’s judgmental attitude of Jade, but that didn’t make him wrong. Clan Hollowfield wouldn’t have their strength and standing if they were weak, and they slowly introduced cultivators to delving. At the same time, Ferris wanted to advance. Did it make sense to listen to the oldest tier one he knew? Or was that just an example of where ‘easing in’ got you: nowhere fast.

In the end, he wasn’t sure. It was common to take between five to ten years to reach the second tier, so Karl could still be within that range. Ferris just didn’t know, so he tried to focus on meditation. Let the thoughts come and go.

Awakening together in the soulspace, Ferris noticed that Jade’s mental body was just as large as their real one. Ferris quickly took Jade to see how the Consume Pool and Digest River were doing.

The acid had swelled far higher than he had ever seen it, raising up the banks all along the Consume Pool and the Digest River. There was some flooding around the river, and ants danced around frantically where the acid touched some of the yellow spires.

Interestingly, whatever sandstone-like material the pillars were made of was able to resist the acid. Ferris had been afraid to collect any of it, cautious of provoking the ants to attack. If the ants turned permanently hostile, then Ferris wouldn’t be able to set foot in the slimespace, acid-resistant rock or not.

Thankfully, the flooding didn’t come anywhere near the three towers, or where Ferris had planted the two ability seeds.

The color of the green acid was different. Usually, it was a clear green. Now it looked cloudy and muddy. Ferris assumed this was the dissolved food in the liquid, as the only other time he had seen this was after Jade had eaten a large number of demons.

The level had risen across both the pool and river evenly, and Ferris took a closer look at where the river fed back out into the ocean. Somehow, the green acid was draining through a hole in the dome back outside. That hole appeared to be the limiting factor for how fast the level went back down. The green liquid was much clearer here—although still a bit cloudy—presumably filtered by the clams as it came down the river.

Ferris remembered when he dug into the hard, green, transparent dome to plant the Grow ability. Whatever material the dome was made of, it was also resisting the acid along the edges. In retrospect, this was obvious as it kept the acid ocean outside.

Ferris wondered, if he dug out some material from the dome’s wall for crafting, would it regrow? This was potentially as dangerous as using the stone ant’s yellow sandstone; a hole in the dome could flood the slimespace everywhere, instead of just around the pool and river.

Seeing the river flood, Ferris realized he had a new problem. There were qi chunks along the riverbed, but they were now much further from the edge of the river than before. His fishing chain would need to be much longer to reach from the new shoreline.

Not many of the clams had produced a qi chunk, which Ferris suspected was due to not having enough time since Jade had eaten. The qi that was there was likely from breakfast or lunch.

This left Ferris with a conundrum. He had planned to collect qi, experiment with some crafting, and then begin work on Assimilate Object. Now, he would need to wait for the acid to drain first, and he wasn’t sure what he could actually do without qi.

Ferris and Jade left the slimespace and went to harvest from the Air Passage. The Qi Pool was almost empty from creating the crossbow yesterday. Thankfully, Ferris was able to make a lightstone while leaving a bare puddle of qi left.

After gathering every chunk of qi from the Air Passage, Ferris and Jade set out to check on the chain fort. There were groups of demons around again, but nothing they hadn’t seen before. Jade had to adapt to being larger, but it didn’t hurt their tactics at all. Once again, Ferris had to reteach Jade how to ‘practice’ consuming the demons while leaving the chains behind. The slime’s score had reverted, having lost the memory of how to separate the pieces while eating. Jade was happy to practice, and quickly regained the former skill—and score.

The chain fort was, thankfully, unchanged. The oath statue’s white chains had grown downwards toward the ground again, but Ferris had Jade cut them back.

Looking around at the fort’s construction, Ferris considered how he could replicate it in the slimespace. Something this large would take him months, but it would be worth it. The time and effort required to properly develop an ability like Assimilate Object enough to delve with was daunting.

It was also why he didn’t try to just develop both Grow and Assimilate Object. They would be even better together, after all, but he wanted one solidly built rather than two unreliable abilities.

Ferris would also need to spend whatever resources were needed to get the ability to heal his leg as well. Worst case, Ferris would get stuck and have plenty of time to develop Grow after Assimilate Object was stable, but he hoped to be delving long before that.

The chain fort was constructed with large stone blocks, too heavy for Ferris to move. They were held together by some kind of mortar. Ferris had no idea how to make it, although he thought qi intent forming could help. The library would also have some information on spiritual construction, either in Mental Materials or another manual.

The fort was covered in heavy chains, like the ones that wrapped around the chain demons. Ferris wanted to collect them all for crafting: either fishing chains or other projects.

Unfortunately, it appeared that what Ferris needed more than anything else was a wheelbarrow. He needed to start transporting the bodies and chains back to his Heartfire.

For the fort itself, Ferris wasn’t sure what to do with it. There were always demons around it. If he could keep them away, there might be a use for it. It was big and strong, after all. Was it worth the effort to break it down for resources, or was there an easier way to get large quantities of stone? Would there be any benefit to keeping it out here, undefended and far away from his Heartfire?

Ferris had heard that as he advanced, such projects would become easier. The giant metal walls inside the refinement device were likely made by some advanced technique. Unfortunately, his uncle hadn’t given specific details, claiming that it required resources found from delving to begin. This was likely the frustration that Brix was feeling. She had to delve to get what she needed… to delve. Ferris would have to build his first ability the hard way.

Grabbing as many of the chains as he could comfortably carry, the two of them returned to the Heartfire. Ferris intended to use them to make his fishing chains for harvesting from the Digest River, once it had drained low enough.

Jade had shrunk a bit, but not nearly enough. With some extra time, Ferris decided to hunt demons. He wanted to find groups as close as possible to his Heartfire, and then collect the bodies for crafting.

He had more difficulty finding demons to hunt than expected. He’d grown used to the constant presence of groups near the fort, and usually actively tried to avoid them. He found and killed one pack of red imp demons nearby, but afterwards he had to look further afield.

He had the best luck searching in the direction of the chain fort, finding two more groups. Ferris could only carry one chain demon corpse from each—Jade eating the rest—but soon he had a pile of corpses ready at the Heartfire.

Combined with the corpses from earlier, he had: two and a half fat chain demons, one lanky tall chain demon with black chain, about three dozen small red lizards from the spirit gem refinement, three blue frog demons, and finally four red imps. Thankfully, they didn’t appear to decay like in the real world.

Examining the ruined pot filled with hard stone, Ferris wondered if he could create a building material using the same process. He was wary of ruining his new pot, but such was the nature of experimentation.

First, he took his shovel and dug a shallow trench in the ground. Filling the good pot with parts from the chain demons, Ferris heated it over the Heartfire. He did not add any liquid qi—not that he had much to add—as he was hoping to find a method that didn’t need it. After the pot had melted to a liquid, Ferris quickly poured it out into the trench.

After the liquid cooled, Ferris had a trench filled with hardened stone. Grabbing one end, he lifted it up out of the dirt. The stone beam was messy and uneven, with clumps of dirt stuck to the irregular surface. He brushed the dirt off, finding many small tendrils where the liquid had flowed down into holes in the dirt before solidifying.

The stone itself was dense and unyielding. It lacked flexibility, like it would break before bending, and it was easy to crumble the smaller pieces around the edge.

It was also heavy. Unlike qi forming, which created things smaller and larger based on his mental image, it appeared he got out the same volume he put in.

Ferris propped the stone beam up between two piles of dirt, then tentatively stepped on it. It held his weight until he started jumping up and down, at which point the stone cracked in half, dumping Ferris on the ground.

Overall, Ferris was happy with the stone. It was strong enough to build with. Best of all, it didn’t require any liquid qi. The mold he’d dug into the dirt wouldn’t work, but he could use some qi to make a cleaner cast to pour into. Once he figured out a mortar, he could easily stack bricks into any shape he wanted. Ferris was planning on building large walls for the Assimilate Object ability, so a source of hard blocks that didn’t require qi would help a lot.

Hunting the demons and experimenting had taken several hours, and Jade had almost returned to normal size. Ferris took the slime to the slimespace to watch the Digest River drain until it was low enough to harvest.

Sitting near the edge of the dome, Ferris watched the green acid slowly flow back out into the ocean. He was wary of the giant creature he had seen out in the darkness before. Thankfully, nothing appeared.

The color of the green acid had completely returned to normal, but the only clams with a qi chunk were the same as before. This wasn’t completely unexpected, as Ferris guessed it took a while for the clam to produce qi. Jade had only eaten just after dinner. The problem was, if the darker color of the acid represented the dissolved food, had it all drained away before the clams had time to process it? Did Ferris make a mistake by overfeeding Jade all at once, when he should have slowly fed the slime throughout the day? Or had the clams eaten their fill, and just needed time to process it?

Ferris sighed. It was yet another variable he would need to test. He would just have to wait until tomorrow night and see how many clams produced qi. The priority was to find any solution that gave maximum qi, and then worry about optimization afterwards.

After the river—and Jade—had returned to normal, Ferris and Jade collected the qi chunks that were ready. They had to reforge the acid-resistant chains once, but they had plenty of materials for it. Finally, they used most of the collected qi to water both the Core Tree and the two abilities before exiting the soulspace to sleep.

It had been another long night, but at least Ferris wouldn’t have to work the next day.
