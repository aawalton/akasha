Archie was currently mid-leap, his boots pressing off the rough bark of one branch before landing on another, the rhythm of his movements sharp, precise, and relentless. He kept his eyes trained on the Bi-Beak Dodo below him.

For a creature that looked so ridiculous at first glance, a plump bird with awkwardly long legs and that bizarre second beak, it was absurdly nimble. It was to the point where Archie found himself gritting his teeth, forced to keep Adrenaline Rush active at a steady 5% just to match its pace. The extra burst of Agility received from it gave him the edge he needed to avoid falling behind, though it irritated him that a bird was pushing him to use it at all.

He’d been tailing it for close to forty-five minutes now, letting it draw him deeper into the forest and towards its nest, where he could then plan and attack them all at once in order to maximize his profits, given the limited time he had.

Glancing at the secondary beak dangling limply from its neck, Archie first assumed the extra beak jutting beneath its normal one was some kind of weapon – perhaps a brutal finishing strike or a predatory tool specialized for crushing weaker creatures, or maybe it was a secondary body of sorts that it used to escape when in danger.

The thought made sense; after all, what else would be the use for having a dangling secondary beak hanging off your neck for. But his theory crumbled when he finally saw it in action against a creature close to its own level, an Okapi

The Okapi in front of them both was the size of two school buses, its antlers were twice the size of his own forearm, and its body rippled with corded muscle; even visible through its fur.

The fight was almost absurd – Archie had to blink several times just to accept what he was watching. The dodo didn’t fly at it like some oversized bird might or anything like he assumed a bird to fight. Instead, it dashed, weaving with startling grace through the Okapi’s stomping hooves and sweeping horns. Its speed practically doubled, and while he was still able to perceive it, unless he pushed Adrenaline Rush to 25% there was no way he could match it in a contest of speed.

Minutes ticked by, five in total, as the battle drew on. The forest shook from the sheer force of the Okapi’s thrashing, splintered trees and churned earth marking every miss, but the dodo never faltered. Its stamina seemed endless. Eventually, the larger beast collapsed, buckling onto its knees, lungs heaving as exhaustion broke it.

And then came the part that made Archie’s brow furrow so deep it nearly met the bridge of his nose.

The second beak didn’t strike, didn’t snap or crush, or do anything of that sort; instead, it enlarged.

The lower mandible stretched grotesquely wide, distorting far past what bone and flesh should’ve allowed, gaping open into a fleshy void. Archie tensed, thinking it would devour the Okapi whole in some horrific display of predation.

But instead, it regurgitated.

From the warped beak, three creatures tumbled out, slick and dazed but alive: a one meter-tall Level 101 Mothcoon, its wings shuddering weakly against its fur and cocoon, a one meter-tall Level 97 Jackalope, with one of its antlers digging against the dirt as it attempted to escape its fleshy cocoon and scramble onto its feet, and lastly, a two-meter-tall Level 102 Howler Monkey that was completely knocked out and almost entirely wrapped in the fleshy cocoon it was in; with only its head being exposed.

Archie’s eyes widened, his leap from one branch to the next faltering just enough for his landing to shake beneath his boots. What in the hell…?

That wasn’t a camouflaged one-shotting weapon, nor was it the true part of its body that it could use to escape... That second beak attached to its neck acted like some sort of spatial storage while keeping them alive.

Somehow, impossibly in his mind, the dodo wasn’t just consuming prey and using it for fuel for what he assumed to be for its hibernation period; it was keeping them alive and storing them in its body.

Archie crouched low on the branch, as his head began filling with thoughts.

Is it collecting strong creatures for its family? Or does it need to for hibernation, and the okapi just has a better nutritional worth than the three of them, and its spatial storage beak only has a certain capacity size? How does that even work? Is this a normal ability for Bi-Beak Dodos?

As the second beak finished disgorging its haul onto the grass beside it, with each one stacked atop the other, Archie’s eyes narrowed at the sight before him. The three creatures, the Mothcoon, the Jackalope, and the barely visible Howler Monkey, were still breathing. Bruised, dazed, and slick with some kind of semi-transparent placenta-like cocoon, but alive. Very much alive, judging by the faint noises their hearts were making.

Holy fuck, Archie’s grip tightened against the bark of the branch.

At first, he thought the second beak was simply some weird predatory evolutionary thing, but this… no. This was on another level.

Archie’s eyes flicked over each of them, Vital Sight confirming his hearing as he could see their vital energy outline still solid and their Sparks of Vitality still sparkling bright.

“…It’s storing them alive,” he muttered under his breath, a low disbelief coloring his tone. I know I’ve practically said this at least ten times now, but… holy fuck.

Even his own spatial storages couldn’t do that, not even the one he’d gotten from the System Bazaar.

He’d even tried to stuff in a live lizard into it once to test what would happen, but unlike what his imagination thought would happen, the creature simply couldn’t enter his Spatial Storages. Leaving him with the discovery that unless the creature was already dead, the Spatial Rifts, Pockets, and Storages simply wouldn’t accept living beings.

But this Dodo had just shattered that law in front of him because when it regurgitated the creatures it held, it retained its same size, indicating to him that it had some sort of pocket storage stomach and or beak of sorts.

Archie’s brow furrowed, his mind churning with equal parts intrigue and greed. If that ability is tied to its core…

His lips pressed thin, his next thought sharp and decisive. Then hunting down Bi-Beak Dodos has now become my top priority.

Previously, his plan was simple: track them, kill them, sell the carcasses whole to a butcher, and rake in the profits. Not to mention considering his low D-Grade level; it would help him quickly reach Level 105 in his racial level and allow him to enter the dungeon.

But now he wanted to dig into their cores and glean into whatever it was that gave them the ability to store creatures alive without changing their size.

Hopefully, every Bi-Beak Dodo in this forest is able to do what this one can, Archie thought to himself.

And as the dodo stretched its grotesque second beak wide once more, now the size of a wheelbarrow, pushing itself to swallow the bleeding and unconscious okapi whole, Archie’s eyes shifted back to the three placenta-wrapped creatures it had regurgitated earlier.

While the drops of blood that seemed to be leaking out through various holes in their placenta wraps should have grabbed his attention, they did not. Considering how he watched the dodo fight against the okapi and how sharp its claws are, it was a given that they would bleed.

But the thing that drew his attention from them was how their skin looked off; as though it were thinner than it should’ve been, almost parchment-like in spots, with a faint, shriveled texture clinging to their limbs, visible to his eyes even from three hundred meters away.

They’re dehydrated, Archie realized before thinking of a reason why that could have been the case. Does the dodo’s spatial storage need moisture to function? If so, why moisture? Could heat be a viable substitute?

Nevertheless, it looks like its spatial storage is not without drawbacks, Archie noted, disappointment pinching faintly at the corner of his mouth. Still… it’s better than nothing, if I can be able to transport living things in a spatial storage, such as a critically injured person, and quickly get them to a hospital or healer without worrying about injuring them on the way there, then a resource cost shouldn’t be much of an issue.

Once the dodo finished swallowing down the okapi, its enlarged secondary beak shrank back to normal before snapping back to its usual limp form as though it hadn’t just unhinged wide enough to consume something the size of a house.

Then, without looking around at its surroundings or back at the three other creatures it regurgitated, it sprinted off. As such, with its absurd speed, it forced Archie to react immediately.

Gripping his Steel Dagger in one hand, Archie dropped from the branch with barely a sound and crossed the 300-meter distance between himself and the three unconscious creatures writhing weakly on the ground.

With swift movements, he drove his dagger through each of their skulls, killing them and tucking their bodies into his Spatial Storage Cube.

One dodo’s trash is another’s treasure, he muttered under his breath.

*[Mothcoon Lv 101] has been slain – Partial XP has been given.*

*[Jackalope Lv 97] has been slain – Partial XP has been given.*

*[Howler Monkey Lv 102] has been slain – Partial XP has been given.*

Then he was off again, sprinting through the branches above as the forest thinned further with every meter.

Following it through the forest, when the dodo suddenly leaped into a large valley with a 600-meter drop, Archie matched it, keeping pace as he landed silently atop a tree just two hundred meters behind and above it.

As Archie’s boots dug into the bark of a broad tree limb as he came to a stop, he crouched low and pushed aside many of the leaves covering his sight, but in doing so, his eyes widened in surprise at what it revealed.

For a few seconds, he kept crouching where he was and stared at the massive structure that he hadn’t seen prior to jumping into the valley.

Ignoring the sheer number of Bi-Beak Dodos running about, outstretched in front of him was a massive, ruined stronghold made of cobblestone.

From high above the trees, he was able to peer inside the sixty-meter-high walls of the stronghold to see hundreds of dome-shaped buildings in one section of the stronghold and many other different-shaped buildings scattered about on the other side.

Then, catching sight of the massive building in the far middle of the stronghold, Archie finally muttered. “Is this the…?”

“But I thought every dungeon in the Tutorial was…” Archie mumbled to himself as his eyes then became drawn to the forty, maybe fifty dodos, clustering about in loose groups across the shattered courtyard and through the hollowed-out and gouged-out halls.

Some of the dodos hacked up cocoon-wrapped creatures from their second beaks, spitting them onto the stone. Not long after, smaller, younger-looking dodos crawled out from beneath the wings of the fatter resting ones and began pecking at the bundles.

“I thought the dungeons were created solely for Tutorial Participants to go through,” Archie said, continuing his train of thought. “But if what I’m looking at is correct, then this must be the Dungeon of the Lost Valley.”

As if to echo his words, a chorus of monstrous roars rolled out from the valley, making the dodos below jolt upright. They hastily gulped down their placenta-slick meals before scattering, some darting into the gouged-out walls of the stronghold, others fleeing into the forest.

Just as Archie was about to turn his head to look for what creatures let out their roars, he suddenly stilled himself as he heard the rustle of leaves from just beside him.

His body stilled as his eyes quickly snapped to the right to see a massive slitted eye staring right at him.
