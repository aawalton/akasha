I moved in. The stairway was slightly shorter than the third-floor one, maybe enough for two people and a kid to walk. Torches, like the other gates, were fixed to the wall, casting a warm glow on the stairway.

Then again, who in their right mind would bring a child into a dungeon?

I hurried down, skipping steps to save time, my hand brushing against the rough stone of the right wall for balance as I rushed toward her.

Then, suddenly, I heard Mabel’s humming again. She was skipping a stair each time, swaying left and right as if she were the most careless adventurer.

I walked down, ready to scold her, but thinking of her credits, the words caught in my throat. Why would she listen? She’s better than me, after all.

As the end of the stairway approached, Mabel stopped humming; her steps became smaller; she had stopped skipping.

“Arnos…” she whispered, glancing back.

I walked to her side. She was just a step away, “yes?” I murmured, now standing beside her.

“Do not take any monster lightly. Stay by my side for today… don’t get lured by any monster or plants, and our battle plan is killing a small goblin tribe and using them as bait to catch bunnies.” She described the plan, her voice as low as a whisper.

“The red-eye ones!?“ I asked, excited to finally see them.

“Shush!” Mabel pressed her finger tightly along her lips.

Taking the hint, I said nothing else, simply nodded. Mabel blinked, and then turned back to the gates opening, and so did I.

Where is that light coming from? I wondered, noticing the faint glow along the gateway’s border. The lower dungeon was supposed to be without any light, was it not? I asked myself.

We resumed walking; the steps ended. And in front of me, just outside the rocky gate, was a dark wall. But considering this floor was a labyrinth one… it made sense.

“Should we go in? Any doubt you want to change your career?” Mabel asked, blinking.

How could she even think I, Arnos, would ever think about changing careers? It’s not like I’d ever consider driving a cart or becoming a farmer or something.

I shook my head and stepped behind Mabel. I mean, she said it herself, right? Stay with her.

“I am ready!”

Fuck, did I yell again? She’d chew me out.

Contrary to my belief, she did not chew me out and instead walked outside of the gate first, and turned to the right.

I swallowed. It was an unnerving experience. Maybe I was being a scared cat; maybe I feared the unexplored. But whatever it was, I really did not want to go in.

Calming my nerves, I walked in behind Mabel, my eyes fixed on the floor.

I moved through the gate… a muted pop hit my ear, just like the strange shift one feels when moving from water to air.

My foot pressed onto solid ground instead of the muddy floor from the earlier level… Wait, what?

My eyes widened; my mouth fell open right after. The stone floor had moss-like growth… it glowed. I turned my gaze up, following where Mabel had turned.

The dungeon, I always thought to be dark and lifeless, was instead glowing… the mosses were along all the walls, even the ceiling. Their colours vivid and vibrant, the one in front of me glowed blue-green and yellow.

“Hey?” A sharp voice entered my ears.

I turned my eyes to Mabel.

She stopped walking, her head tilted, arms crossed. “I said don’t get lured, didn’t I?”

I shook my head. “You didn’t say mosses.”

“Don’t get lured by mosses, happy now?” Mabel asked, then in a matter-of-fact voice she continued. “By the way, they aren’t mosses.”

I began walking, following right beside her. My eyes still darting around—I was amazed.

“Then, what are they!?” I asked.

“Mosses require sunlight to grow, don’t they? They are green and uh… they have fake roots, right?”

“Well, yeah, I think, but why is this information anything you’d—oh…” She wants to be praised, huh?

“That’s cool, Mabel. Didn’t know you were… uhh, this knowledgeable. Like my mind is completely blown away,” I said, trying my best not to let my voice sound sarcastic.

Mabel smiled, maybe even grinned? She looked happy from the compliment.

“Ahem,” she cleared her throat.

Then glanced at me.

“These are not mosses,” she began. “Any creeping plant inside the dungeon… is dungeon grass. They don’t get specific names, well, unless they are useful like Ereceus.”

“So,” I looked to my front, “they derive energy from the dungeon walls?”

“Yeah, with how mana-rich the dungeon is, it’s only logical, right?”

I nodded, then stopped. In front of me, there were two ways to go. And considering we were on the labyrinth floor, I concluded both of them were traps. I looked up. Maybe we were supposed to dig a hole through the ceiling?

“What?” Mabel asked, looking at me curiously.

“Don’t we have to check for traps?” I asked.

Mabel blinked. “No,” she muttered, turning her gaze away.

I followed her. We turned to the right, the corridor widened, and just like before both the floor and the wall glowed here as well. There weren’t any monsters though… not one.

“Is it always barre—”

“Water bullet,” Mabel muttered. And with her words, two small balls of water emerged from her palm, both moving diagonally in opposite directions. Then a loud [Kreee!] followed.

And I finally saw what they were. They were monsters—bunnies? The monster's body glowed. Technically, it didn’t start glowing but returned to its normal colour, bright white.

I didn’t even notice they were here. They blended into the darkness perfectly.

“Why are they here?” Mabel wondered out loud.

“They are red-eyed bunnies, right?” I asked just to confirm.

“Yeah… carnivorous bunnies. Their small bodies allow them to hide pretty easily. Keep an eye out for them. Noticing any flicker in the darkness is a good way to catch them.”

“Are they not supposed to be here?” I asked.

Mabel began walking toward the corpse of one of the bunny. “They are not usually found here, is all,” she murmured.

I walked behind her, following up with another question. “How many credits do each of them give?”

“Same as goblins for some reason. They are not as hard to kill, though.”

My eyes turned to the bunny. It wasn’t very big in size, maybe the same as a large ordinary rabbit. Its mouth was slightly agape, the teeth sharp and long—sharp enough, I was sure, to bite someone’s hand off with ease.

It also had a small black horn on its forehead, one that allowed it to mask its presence, and blend into the surroundings.

After finishing looking at the corpse of the bunny, and getting some useles— ‘useful’ knowledge about it from Mabel, we started walking again.

We crossed one corridor after another before encountering another monster. This time though, I was the first one to react.

Turning just around the corner, I saw a tiny humanoid creature. Not wanting it to notice us, I stumbled back, tugging Mabel behind the wall and pressing myself against it.

“Did you see something?” she asked.

“A goblin, I think. Do we kill it or use it to take us to its nest?”

“You sure it didn’t see us?”

“Pretty sure. Goblins have glowing green eyes, don’t they? You said it yourself.” I replied.

“Hmm,” Mabel nodded. “Well then, go for its hand and injure it,” she added.

I nodded, then leaned to the side, raising my arm to strik—? The goblin was gone.

“Ahhh!” Mabel scoffed, moving back out into the corridor. “Should’ve attacked it first!”

“You were the one who said I should ask first; don’t pin it on me. And why are you shouting? Weren’t we not supposed to do that?”

“Right…”

She nodded.

“I guess it’s your first day here… despite the dungeon being available to us for the past two months. Oh… I should’ve been more patient with you.” Mabel turned her eyes away from me. “Sorry for being so rough… I’ll try better next time~,”

“Die…” I murmured. Idiot Mabel, I added in my mind.

Mabel smiled and then started walking again.

I followed her, observing the corridor we were in this time. It was definitely narrower than the one where the bunnies ambushed us, maybe enough for four or five people to stand side by side, with their hands raised.

There was also less dungeon grass, only two-three dense networks of green dungeon grass spread on the wall.

The light was dimmer, the darkness more prominent.

A rancid metallic smell hit me. Rotten flesh? I wondered, but maybe it was just mud. I glanced at Mabel. She didn’t show any reaction; maybe it was just my imagination.

“Do you smell something weird?” I asked her.

She shook her head, her eyes fixed at the approaching turn. “My nose is stuffy today, sniff sniff.” She sniffed for emphasis.

“I smell something weird,” I said, deciding to tell her about my observation. “Like flesh… the smell is weak though. It could totally be fecal material as well.”

Mabel looked at me. “If the smell is weak, I wouldn’t really worry about it too much.” She paused. “It’s one of the upper floors. The monsters’ flesh isn’t worth much, so a lot of adventurers leave them lying around.”

She turned her gaze back to the turn that was barely a few seconds away.

“Just keep your eyes open, and do not get lured by dungeon grass.”

I nodded.

We turned to the right. It’s not like we had a choice—this turn didn’t give us one. We both stopped. I squinted my eyes to get a better view. There was no flickering, meaning there weren’t any bunnies, nor were there any shiny eyes goblins.

There were no monsters at all.

Mabel sighed tiredly. “I suppose we should start talking loudly!” she yelled. “The dungeon is too scarce today… we might find some more that way!”
