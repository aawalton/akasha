“Next, we have ten small bottles of Fasting Pills. Each bottle contains ten pills. The price is three hundred spirit stones for all of them, or thirty per bottle. Moreover, with the addition of sweet lily extract, the flavor is guaranteed,” a masked man announced.

The surroundings were filled with similarly hooded figures, all seated in an orderly yet suspiciously secretive manner.

Hearing the announcement, most people remained quiet and indifferent. Fasting Pills were the most basic type of pill used to suppress hunger, and while the added flavor was a nice touch, it was hardly enough to make them stand out in such a saturated market.

“I’ll take five bottles.”

A hooded figure stepped forward without hesitation and completed the purchase.

After that, two more people made their moves, and before long, all the bottles were sold out.

“Finally…” Ning leaned back in his chair with a sigh of relief. Selling Fasting Pills in bulk was far more troublesome than he had expected.

The market for Fasting Pills was heavily saturated. Not only were there many people selling them cheaply, but some even added other ingredients to improve their flavor and effects. Ning could only slowly sell off his stock by keeping his prices low and adding minor flavor improvements of his own.

Fortunately, his ingredient costs were minimal since he grew most of them himself, so at least he managed to break even.

He turned to the man beside him. “Thank you, Brother Sui Feng. I managed to sell everything in one go.”

“It’s nothing,” Sui Feng replied with a smile. Then he glanced around and lowered his voice slightly. “Though I still haven’t gotten used to this kind of gathering. I don’t understand why the organizer insists on dressing everyone like this every time.”

Gatherings like this were quite common in the inner sect. Some were meant for socializing, others for exchanging treasures, and some required special identities to attend. This one was simply… eccentric.

There was nothing illegal about it. The organizer just had a very particular aesthetic, as one could see from the dress requirement.

From the outside, anyone unfamiliar with the situation would probably think this was some kind of cult meeting.

“It’s fine,” Ning said with a small smile.

As a transmigrator, he had seen far stranger things. Dressing like a cult barely made it into his top five.

“Next, I have Image Recording Stones for sale. Two hundred spirit stones each,” another hooded figure announced.

Ning straightened upon hearing that.

“Finally,” he muttered as he stood up.

This was one of the main reasons he had come to the gathering.

Image Recording Stones could record a scene and replay it when infused with qi. They weren’t particularly rare, but each stone could only hold a single recording at a time and was easily disrupted by external spiritual qi.

Because of those limitations, there wasn’t much demand for them. Most cultivators who actually needed such a function preferred the more advanced versions, which had formations engraved into them for better storage and stability.

For Ning, however, a basic Image Recording Stone was more than enough.

This was essentially a video recorder, and that made it quite useful.

Being able to watch himself from another perspective would make it much easier to find mistakes in his training, especially the small habits he might never notice while actually fighting.

Without hesitation, Ning stepped forward to make the purchase.

“As expected… professionals really are on a different level. I’m just a casual chef compared to this,” Ning said as he took another bite of the spiritually enhanced ice cream, made with year-old ice. The cold sweetness and rich chocolate flavor instantly overwhelmed his taste buds.

Fatty Li scratched his head and grinned. “We couldn’t have done it without your foundational recipe, Brother Ning. Next, we’re planning to improve those ‘cake’ ideas you gave us.”

“That would be great. You’ve really put in a lot of hard work,” Ning replied with a nod. With this ice cream, I can butter up Master even more. Nice.

Fatty Li sighed, his expression turning a little downcast. “Alas, that’s about all I can do. I don’t have Brother Xiao Fan’s strength or your level of comprehension, Brother Ning. I’m thinking of trying to become a battle spiritual chef.”

Battle spiritual chefs were a special branch of spiritual chefs who used their dishes in combat. The meals they prepared could manifest phantoms based on their ingredients, such as tigers, dragons, or other beasts, which would then fight on the chef’s behalf. Their strength varied greatly depending on both the quality of the cuisine and the chef’s technique.

Ning had to admit that when he first heard of it, he found the concept pretty impressive. These guys were essentially summoning stands to battle it out.

“That’s the path I see for myself,” Fatty Li continued. “I’m not usually this negative, but I know my limits. As an outer sect disciple, my talent is pretty average compared to others. That’s why I didn’t even participate in the outer sect competition. I’m… aware of my situation.”

Ning blinked once as he listened.

“Fatty Li,” he said. “Let’s fix that misunderstanding of yours.”

Fatty Li still looked confused as he stood in the middle of the training field, scratching his head while staring at Ning.

“Brother Ning,” he said helplessly, “I still don’t understand. One moment we were talking about cooking, and the next moment you dragged me here to fight.”

Ning stretched his arms and rotated his shoulders as he prepared for combat. “I told you,” he replied calmly, “it’s just a spar. We won’t use weapons or anything, just physique versus physique.”

Fatty Li blinked. “But I’ve never trained in body refinement.”

“That’s exactly why I want to try,” Ning said, finally stopping and looking at him with interest. “You have unusual strength. I noticed it before.”

Fatty Li laughed awkwardly. “Unusual? I just eat more than others…”

Ning only smiled. “We’ll see.”

Before Fatty Li could respond further, Ning stepped forward, closing the distance in the blink of an eye as his fist drove toward him. Fatty Li instinctively raised his arm to block, and a dull thud echoed across the training field as he stumbled backward.

“Oof!”

“That hurt?” Ning asked casually, already stepping in again.

Before Fatty Li could answer, another strike came, followed by another. Ning’s attacks were built on precision rather than brute force, and he kept up the pressure, forcing Fatty Li to react again and again until the latter found himself retreating without even realizing it.

“Wait, Brother Ning, slow down!”

“Focus,” Ning replied calmly.

His fist struck Fatty Li’s shoulder and forced him to stagger once more. From the outside, it looked like complete suppression, which was hardly surprising considering Fatty Li had barely reached the fourth stage while Ning was already at the sixth stage of Qi Condensation. Even with neither of them using qi and the spar focused entirely on their physiques, the difference in experience and training was obvious.

Ning gave him no time to recover. He stepped forward again and drove a controlled punch into Fatty Li’s ribs, knocking the breath out of him, then followed with a palm strike to the chest and a shoulder check that sent him stumbling back once more.

“Is this all?” Ning asked calmly.

Fatty Li froze. “I…” he started, but no words came out.

Ning pressed him again, but Fatty Li was barely fighting back anymore and seemed content to endure until Ning finally stopped a few steps away.

“Why aren’t you fighting back?”

Fatty Li slowly lowered his arms. “I can’t win,” he said honestly. “You’re too strong.”

Ning stared at him for a moment, then shook his head slightly. “That’s not what I asked.”

Before Fatty Li could respond, Ning moved again. This time, his strike was heavier, slamming into Fatty Li’s shoulder and forcing him back so hard that his foot dug into the ground.

“Then fight,” Ning said.

Fatty Li looked up, stunned.

“Winning or losing doesn’t matter,” Ning continued. “If you don’t even try, then why are you standing here?”

Fatty Li clenched his fists. “I’m not like you,” he muttered. “I don’t have talent. I don’t have techniques. I just—”

“Stop.”

Ning’s voice cut him off sharply.

“You think you don’t have talent?” Ning stepped forward. “Then what do you call this?”

He struck again, and this time Fatty Li reacted instinctively and blocked. The impact echoed loudly across the field, but although the blow still hurt, Fatty Li held his ground.

“You blocked it,” Ning said. “Even though I’m not using qi to strengthen my body, I’ve already reached the marrow refining stage. My base strength alone is enough to punch through small trees.”

Another strike came, and Fatty Li raised his arm more firmly to meet it.

“You endured it,” Ning continued. “These strikes, even without any training.”

He looked directly at Fatty Li. “When you grabbed me during the outer sect competition, I couldn’t shake you off easily even with my attainment in physical training.”

Fatty Li froze.

“You think that’s normal?” Ning asked.

Fatty Li looked down at his hands, seemingly unsure what to say.

“You’re not weak,” Ning said. “You’re just holding back. I can feel that there is extraordinary talent within you.”

Ning stepped forward and struck once more, but this time Fatty Li did not retreat. His hands came up and caught Ning’s punch. The movement was neither clean nor skillful, but he had stopped it.

“…I…” Fatty Li muttered.

Ning’s eyes sharpened. “Good. Then show me.”

Fatty Li’s breathing grew heavier as he tried to understand what Ning was asking of him. “I don’t know how.”

“Then stop thinking,” Ning replied calmly. “Just fight. In most battles, instinct matters more than anything. And if what I suspect is right, you won’t have trouble with that.”

Fatty Li took a deep breath, and his expression gradually turned serious. As a cultivator, how could he not yearn for strength, especially after watching how far his friends had progressed? Ning, someone Fatty Li respected for both his combat ability and his skill in spiritual cooking, was now telling him that the strength he had always dismissed might actually be something worth cultivating.

“Then fine!” he shouted.

This time, he stepped forward instead of back and threw a punch. It was still crude and unrefined, but there was far more strength behind it than before.

Ning met it head-on.

A deep boom echoed across the field as their fists collided, and for the first time since the spar began, Ning slid back half a step.

Fatty Li’s eyes widened in disbelief. “I… I did that?”

Ning straightened and gave a faint smile. “Yes.”

That seemed to be all Fatty Li needed. His heart began pounding faster as a strange surge of power rushed through his body, causing heat to rise from his skin while his muscles tightened and his entire frame seemed to grow heavier and denser.

“Brother Ning… something’s happening to me,” he said, his voice trembling as he felt an unfamiliar strength filling his body.

“I know,” Ning replied. “Don’t stop. Keep fighting.”

Fatty Li grinned. “Then let’s go!”

He charged forward again, his steps striking the ground heavily as he threw another punch. There was still no real technique or finesse behind his movements, but the raw strength alone was enough for Ning to take him seriously.

Ning adjusted his stance and met him head-on, only to immediately notice that Fatty Li’s strength was still increasing. Each clash grew heavier than the last, as though the pressure of the fight was drawing out reserves of strength that had remained dormant inside his body until now.

Ning relied on his experience and precision to deal with the attacks, but by this point the conclusion was obvious. Fatty Li possessed some kind of special physique or supernatural ability.

It made sense. As the classic “fatty friend” of the protagonist, there was no way he would remain ordinary forever.

Another punch came, and this time Ning truly felt the impact travel through his arm.

“As expected, Fatty Li… you really are something,” Ning said, striking back.

Ning had already reached the marrow refining stage, and although he had been controlling his strength throughout the spar, Fatty Li was quickly reaching the point where that was no longer necessary. That suited Ning perfectly, since testing Fatty Li had only been one reason for starting this fight; he also wanted a chance to properly exercise his own body refinement.

Fatty Li laughed. “I’m starting to get the hang of this!”

“Then do your best,” Ning replied.

He stepped forward again, lowering his stance as Fatty Li charged toward him. This time, Ning stopped meeting his attacks directly and began using his superior technique instead, redirecting Fatty Li’s strength, striking at openings, and disrupting his rhythm before he could build momentum.

The difference in skill quickly became obvious. Fatty Li had strength, perhaps even more than Ning had expected, but he had almost no idea how to use it in a fight, while Ning had spent years practicing martial arts and fighting cultivators stronger than himself.

Still, the spar continued for quite some time, and Fatty Li seemed to improve with every exchange. His attacks remained crude, but he was becoming more comfortable with his own strength and less hesitant about using it.

Eventually, however, his lack of endurance began to show. This was his first time drawing out so much of whatever power had been hiding inside his body, and after maintaining it for so long, his movements gradually became slower.

Ning noticed the change and ended the exchange cleanly.

Fatty Li stood there breathing heavily as the steam rising from his body gradually faded, leaving behind a mixture of exhaustion and excitement.

Ning lowered his hand. “You see now? You have talent. There’s no need to doubt yourself.”

Fatty Li nodded. “I do. I never thought I had this kind of potential.”

Fatty Li had discovered that he possessed far more potential than he had believed, while Ning had gained someone with unusual physical strength who could potentially become a useful sparring partner in the future.

Soul Ferrying Scripture.

The Soul Ferrying Scripture was a special technique Ning had been trying to complete. After three months, he had finally made some progress.

It was a rather unusual technique, possessing only a single realm rather than being divided into stages such as Starting, Minor Accomplishment, or Great Accomplishment.

This was something Ning had only realized after studying it more deeply. As his understanding changed, his panel, which was ultimately shaped by his own comprehension, changed along with it.

[Soul Ferrying Scripture: (10/10000)]

The technique allowed one to ferry the souls of the dead, particularly those trapped by resentment, toward the afterlife. It was a profound Buddhist art originating from the Western Desert, where such teachings flourished.

At first glance, one might expect Ning to shift his focus toward other techniques, especially now that he had acquired several that could directly help his cultivation.

However, the Soul Ferrying Scripture had another benefit that made Ning reluctant to abandon it.

Merit.

Cultivators who accumulated merit were said to experience fewer calamities and encounter more fortuitous opportunities, while also gaining some protection against curses, karmic backlash, and techniques that targeted the soul.

Some ancient records even spoke of great Buddhist cultivators using accumulated merit to weaken tribulations, cleanse negative karma, or assist themselves during critical breakthroughs. Of course, reaching that level required an absurd amount of merit, usually accumulated through saving countless lives and souls, but Ning had to start somewhere.

The problem was that he couldn't actually use the technique.

“But… I’m stuck,” Ning muttered.

After three months, he had not successfully activated it even once. No matter how carefully he followed the method or adjusted his qi circulation, nothing happened.

Eventually, Ning sought out his master and explained that, despite applying all his usual methods, he had made almost no progress.

Ling Xin gave him a surprisingly simple answer.

“You think too logically. You keep trying to break everything into a structure and make it behave. That works for most things, but not for every technique.” She waved her hands vaguely. “Sometimes you just have to follow your instincts and go more bam and whoosh. Not everything runs on logic. You need to align your state of mind.”

That left Ning thinking.

What exactly was comprehension?

Until now, he had mostly treated comprehension as understanding. He would break a technique into smaller parts, figure out what each part accomplished, practice them individually, and eventually put everything back together.

That approach had served him extremely well.

But perhaps it was incomplete.

The Soul Ferrying Scripture was a good example. Ning understood how its qi was supposed to circulate, knew the chants, remembered the seals, and could perform every individual step correctly.

Yet the technique still didn't work.

That meant he was missing something that couldn't be solved simply by performing the steps more accurately.

Ning returned to the scripture.

It spoke of emptiness, compassion, a clear heart, and understanding the weight of life and death. These weren't additional instructions hidden somewhere in the manual. They were the state of mind expected from the person using it.

That was where Ning had gone wrong.

A cultivation technique was created by someone. The movements, qi circulation, chants, and seals were all based on that person's understanding of what they were trying to accomplish.

Following the instructions allowed Ning to reproduce the method, but understanding the intent behind those instructions was another matter entirely.

That was probably why cultivation novels were always filled with strange phrases like clear heart, unfettered mind, and zen mind.

Ning had once considered most of them unnecessarily vague.

Now he was beginning to understand.

They described ways of looking at something.

His own strength had always been his ability to analyze, define, and understand things logically, and there was nothing wrong with that. It had carried him this far.

He simply couldn't expect every technique in the cultivation world to fit neatly into that approach.

If he wanted to learn the Soul Ferrying Scripture, he would have to understand the perspective behind it rather than simply reproduce its mechanics.

And if he could learn to do that here, perhaps he could apply the same idea elsewhere.

Ning slowly exhaled.

“If I can learn this kind of comprehension, I can go even further.”

Instead of being discouraged by the realization, Ning found himself even more interested.
