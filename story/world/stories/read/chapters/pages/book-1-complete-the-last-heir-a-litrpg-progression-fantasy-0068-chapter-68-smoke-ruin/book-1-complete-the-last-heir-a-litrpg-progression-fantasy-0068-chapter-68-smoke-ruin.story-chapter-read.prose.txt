Rowan didn’t hesitate, he drew upon his mana and cast [Blazing Chains].

A searing length of flames burst into existence, followed by two more in quick succession.

His heart beat like a wardrum, a single-minded focus to eradicate the monster standing in front of him welling up from somewhere deep within.

The Demon's eyes widened a fraction at his display, but its confident smirk never faltered.

“Oh, you are going to be a treat,” it licked its lips, disappearing in a puff of smoke. But not before he managed to scan it.

Level: 68

Race: Greater Demon

Sin: Envy

Rowan growled, lashing his chains against the bar, pulverizing it. He didn’t stop there. He’d spent hundreds of hours poring over tomes about their wretched race, the Demon’s little disappearing trick wouldn’t work on him.

Demons of Envy were shapeshifters, illusionists, and power-stealers. Not teleporters.

His spells were equal parts power and grace as they seared furrows into the stone floor, seeking out his opponent.

It didn’t take him long.

A dome of Crystal appeared over a seemingly empty space, a low hiss following soon after.

The Demon's form shimmered, reappearing with a glare on its hideous face.

“Stronger than you appear, huh?” it said slowly, clenching its clawed hand. “I suppose—”

Rowan didn’t let it finish. This was a creature that absorbed a tier-three affinity. He wasn’t going to hold back.

With barely a thought, he cast [Fire Shield], though with the amount of mana he was pushing into it and the sheer control he had over it, the spell mimicked the Chant [Dome of Flame].

Then, a heartbeat later, two of his chains dissipated.

Rowan used that mana to start forming a [Fireball], the orb of heat flaring to life in his palm.

Him and Kai were protected by his shield, but the rest of the tavern wasn’t so fortunate.

It had been barely seconds since the start of the fight, with the Silver-ranks just starting to move towards him. Rowan didn’t plan on letting them get closer.

The Demon’s eyes widened in panic at the sight, its Crystal defenses growing thicker in preparation for the calamity Rowan was about to unleash.

This was an infestation, and he planned on rooting it out.

The [Fireball] erupted from his hand, impacting the Demon’s spell just moments later. It exploded with the sound of a mountain splitting, demolishing the back wall and everything else around it. Bodies flew back, hitting the stone with sickening crunches, bones breaking and the forms the Lesser Demons took finally dissipating.

It seemed this tavern was a honeypot for weary travelers, and the Demon of Envy was using it to build up its forces. Rowan didn’t know what for, but whatever it was, he wasn’t going to let the beast have its way.

Rowan’s mana barely dropped as the explosion from the [Fireball] pressed against his shield, the dome not shimmering in the slightest.

An instant later, another orb formed.

Rowan threw it at the Crystal barrier he assumed was still there, following it up with another thrown behind him just for good measure.

Five seconds after the fight started, the entire tavern was demolished.

Rowan left the [Fire Shield] up for a few seconds longer, allowing the flames to dissipate before dropping his spell.

The storm outside howled through the skeletal remains of The Resting Stag. What had once been a solid, if eerie, roadside tavern was now nothing more than a shattered husk. Smoke billowed from the scorched remains, rising into the night like a pyre. The stone floor, once polished and worn smooth by years of foot traffic, was now cracked and blackened, still glowing faintly with dying embers.

Rows of wooden tables had been reduced to splinters, chairs strewn about like broken bones. The bar—where the Demon had first stood, smug and overconfident—was nowhere to be seen, with the scent of burning wood mixed with something more acrid hanging in the air.

Bodies lay scattered amidst the wreckage. Some still wore human faces, their disguises flickering like candlelight before finally fading, revealing twisted, grotesque forms underneath.

The Lesser Demons had been burned beyond recognition, their corpses now little more than charred husks. Blackened blood sizzled against the stone, forming oily pools that hissed and bubbled where the heat still lingered.

The Imperial Road—untouched by Rowan’s magic—stretched out beyond the destruction. The cobbled stones remained pristine, the enchantments woven into them keeping the fire from spreading beyond the tavern’s ruins.

The stark contrast was almost surreal—a road built to withstand the test of time, and a tavern that had fallen in mere seconds.

Kai flapped his powerful wings once, clearing the haze of smoke circling around them. He cawed, tilting his head at Rowan, concerned.

“I’m fine,” he lied, the rage that had boiled up within him simmering just below the surface, transforming from a hot tempest into cold, seething fury.

Seeing a Demon had stirred something inside him—pulled at a string he’d thought under control. But considering the only thought currently on his mind was eradicating the beast, that was obviously a lie

Rowan flared [Iron Will], yet it did little besides focus his anger.

As the smoke cleared, the Crystal dome beneath which the Demon stood cracked. Its structure fractured as its mana dissipated, leaving behind nothing but a scent of wrongness in the air.

Rowan walked towards it, casting an empowered [Gust] to clear away the ground.

What that revealed was a Crystal plate covering what used to be the tavern floor, leading to what Rowan assumed had to be the monster's lair. Demons were a lot of things, and predictable seemed to be one of them.

His mind managed to focus despite the single-minded need to burst through immediately.

It’s the lair of a Demon of Envy. There are going to be traps.

Rowan knew he had to be careful. He glanced around, shaking his head at the sight.

It has a tier-three affinity and who knows what else. I can’t let this new strength go to my head—I need to calm down.

He’d made that mistake more than once already, and Rowan felt like he managed to learn his lesson.

His first fight against a shaman had ended with his leg shattered and his Core depleted. Then the Wyrmlings happened, which left him with an injured Soul and more than a few questions. Surprisingly, his duel against the apprentice went mostly to plan, but Rowan threw that away by Feather Falling into the middle of a goblin horde.

Those were all times where he could have died, and making sure that didn’t happen needed to be a priority.

Rowan took a steadying breath, focusing on [Iron Will].

His rage slowly receded. Not extinguished, but set aside for the moment.

Seeing a Demon for the first time since Eiselyth managed to unbalance Rowan. There was a visceral hate that welled up inside him, and he spent a moment analyzing it.

Rowan tried to recall what the Demon looked like as it unveiled its form, yet the image was vague, almost fuzzy. That was strange considering how good his memory usually was, but he knew it wasn’t because of a skill or spell. He’d seen red as he realized what was in front of him, not a being, but a disease.

Kai nuzzled against his cheek, letting out a questioning trill.

“Give me a minute,” he said, petting his neck. “And please keep an eye out. I think I got them all, but you never know.”

His familiar nodded, standing tall on his favourite perch.

He took comfort from his presence, staring at the Crystal plate covering the ground.

I think it has something to do with my guilt and feelings of helplessness, Rowan thought, the desire to enter still there, the same as his anger. Like that Demon is a representation of that day, and killing it is my way to atone.

Whoever had orchestrated the fall of his House was still out there, and he had every plan on taking his pound of flesh in turn, but it had been a Demon that did the deed.

A burning city appeared in his mind, the sky a strange orange, a deep wrongness in the air. Yet that tragedy was only the backdrop to the central focus of Rowan’s memory, with two indistinct shapes throwing cataclysms at a horned being ten times higher than Eiselyth’s walls taking center stage.

The pain wasn’t as cutting as it used to be, and Rowan let it settle.

Demons aren’t a unified race, he reminded himself. And that was an Archdemon of Pride. The difference between that and the Demon of Envy hiding below is the same as between a house-fly and a dragon.

Rowan’s heartbeat started to slow, his thoughts calming. A question popped up.

“There’s no way it made its lair on the Imperial Road for no reason,” he frowned. “And with how many Lesser Demons there were, it occupied it for a while.”

The fact that no one from the Storm Crown noticed was extremely strange. But there was another possibility. This one darker.

Or they let it happen.

Rowan didn’t know what the situation was in the capital of the Stormspire Heights. He’d expected it to be mostly stable, but that could easily not be the case.

As a regional Capital, the amount of support the Storm Crown got was vastly greater than Litwick, yet that didn’t mean it was safe. The threats in the Wilds were ever-present, and they only grew larger the more prosperous the region.

Rowan shook his head. Thinking about that could wait until he had more information.

Besides, he had a feeling that he’d find out if there was a Demon infestation going on soon enough.

There was no way he was walking away from this.

A Demon lay somewhere below him, waiting for him to try and kill it, planning on how it’d do the same in turn. And it definitely had plans. Ones that Rowan had no desire to see come to fruition, even if he didn’t know them.

The notification after the battle against the tribe said that his deeds resonate with his Path, and Rowan was going to make sure this action resonated with it as well.

He was going to be killing Demons—hopefully a lot of them—and he wasn’t going to stop until that Archdemon of Pride lay begging at his feet.

“Kai, I need you up here,” he said after a moment. “I don’t know what’s going to be waiting for me, but I can’t risk you getting hurt.”

His familiar cawed, equal parts in disappointment and understanding, bumping his head.

“As soon as we reach the city, we’re pushing you to Silver,” Rowan promised. “And after that, we can finally do the Bonding ritual.”

Kai perked up, excitedly flapping his wings.

A smile tugged at Rowan’s lips, but the landscape around him kept his mind focused on the task at hand.

The ruined tavern still smoldered, the smell of charred corpses and fresh rain mingling in the air.

“It’s a shame, though,” he said, glancing at the cover. “The strongest monster we’ve fought yet, and you won’t get a meal out of it.”

Rowan’s fists clenched, a familiar thrill running through his veins.

Kai cawed loudly in support, pecking his ear before flying up with a flap of his powerful wings.

Rowan cast [Blazing Chains], three of them appearing in seconds. Then, with the grace of a rampaging leviathan and a storm's fury, he set about destroying the Crystal barrier separating him from his opponent.
