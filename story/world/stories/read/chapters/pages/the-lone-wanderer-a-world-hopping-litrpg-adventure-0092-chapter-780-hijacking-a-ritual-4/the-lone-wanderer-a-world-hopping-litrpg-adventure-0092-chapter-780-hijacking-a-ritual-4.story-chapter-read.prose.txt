Hearts were ultimately just muscles, so mending one was technically easier than repairing nerve damage or regrowing severed limbs.

Percy knew that his grandfather should be able to piece the organ together in an instant. The issue was that it was going to keep ripping itself apart until he identified and corrected whatever was causing it to beat erratically and unnaturally fast.

‘Maybe the pulses aren’t entirely random,’ Percy speculated. ‘What if it’s just two overlapping rhythms that are out of sync?’

He decided to lean further into that theory – mostly because he didn’t have time to come up with a better one. Paying closer attention to his irregular heartbeats, he realized that he might be onto something. If he ignored every second thrum, the intervals seemed mostly constant. The same was true if he focused on the odd-numbered beats, though the faster rhythm overtook the slower one every few intervals, making it harder for Percy to keep track.

Unfortunately, understanding the problem wasn’t the same as fixing it.

Percy was tired – more so mentally rather than physically, since his body was currently brimming with unprecedented power and vitality. But the past forty minutes had been some of the most intense he had ever experienced, and they were starting to take their toll.

He shook those thoughts away. There would be time to rest after he was done – he couldn’t afford to give up so close to the finish line.

Tampering with the electrical impulses that his brain sent to his heart, he clamped down on the two rhythms, forcibly synchronizing them. His body protested the intervention with a jolt of pain, but the destruction slowed down, indicating that this was better than letting his heart rampage freely.

‘Progress.’

Using his willpower, he pushed the pieces closer together, nodding at his grandfather to mend them. The Clear mana had already dried up, since it had no boosting art or mana core to replenish it. Archibald wasn’t doing much better. There was still plenty of mana inside his channels, but it was getting dangerously close to the minimum threshold that the Cradle required to keep it compressed.

The last thing Percy wanted was for the patriarch’s grade to drop while healing him. Demotions could be safe when attempted slowly and cautiously, but rushing one under pressure could be deadly.

“You can slow down now,” Percy hurriedly said. “Just focus on keeping me stable and replenishing your reserves. I’ll take it from here.”

Despite his assurances, Percy knew that he was in a tough spot. He would have to reinforce his heart with his domain until he solved the issue. That wasn’t very different from what he’d been doing up to that point, though his window to act was shrinking with every drop of Heartblood that his body consumed.

Left with no other choice, Percy carefully but swiftly adjusted his heartrate, searching for a rhythm that the organ could handle, yet which would also satisfy his body’s steep requirements.

Exhausting all the simple options didn’t take long, so it soon became clear that imposing a single, fixed interval between pulses wouldn’t cut it. There had to be several complex processes occurring within his body, demanding a variable flow of blood. Percy didn’t know the exact reason for that – it probably had something to do with the interaction between the Remiorian and Thess’kalan physiques – but he knew what he had to do.

It was similar to the problem of fine-tuning the pulses of pressure and gravity mana to compress his potions.

Increasing the number of variables to two, Percy alternated a pair of different intervals between heartbeats. Most of the time, he kept one fixed while varying the other, though he had to modify the first and start over whenever he failed to find an acceptable configuration.

In theory, there was no upper limit to the number of distinct intervals that Percy’s body might need, but every variable he added would compound his experimentation time. The ritual would end long before he finished testing a third interval, so he could only pray to Phoebe that two would be enough.

The putrid sludge inside the vat had grown nearly inert, and even the Blue potions in Percy’s seal had almost run out, as he’d flooded his body with hundreds of doses over the past few minutes.

He didn’t really mind the loss, as he no longer needed the potions. The ingredients had stopped affecting him at some point, and he was pretty sure that even the Violet ones would be useless moving forward, whether in ritual baths or during combat. He’d already internalized, combined, and even amplified their effects.

‘Good riddance,’ he thought.

Once he raised his cores to Green again, nothing short of Clear potions would stand a chance of affecting him, and their effect was bound to be negligible even if they did. He wouldn’t bother brewing any – especially because he would outgrow those as well after advancing to Blue or Violet. Other people could use them, but it was too much of a hassle to waste his time preparing consumables for everyone, so he was more than happy to close this chapter of his life if he survived today.

His most pressing issue was that the Heartblood was also nearly gone.

It was normally supposed to last for two days and nights, but Percy had somehow managed to extract every morsel of power from the exotic reagent in a mere fraction of the time.

And he needed the Heartblood a lot more than the other ingredients.

His body, which had felt as pliable as soft clay throughout the bath, was starting to grow rigid. Percy’s internal sight hadn’t degraded in the slightest, and he was confident that he would still be able to manually adjust his heartrate after the ritual ended, but his body wouldn’t be able to adapt to the changes.

Gritting his teeth, he sped up his search, trying to find the right configuration before he doomed himself to an early grave – or worse, a life of perpetually keeping his domain wrapped around his heart to support it.

Something clicked.

Percy had to pause and backtrack slightly, as he’d already moved past the rhythm that had elicited the reaction.

He forced his heart to beat to the intervals that had stood out, noting that they felt right. With every breath he took and every pulse of blood that got pumped through his veins, his muscles rose and fell in relief, his rattling bones calmed down into a lull, and tension slipped away from his joints like a forgotten nightmare.

He carefully released his domain’s grip on the organ, right as the last trace of Heartblood evaporated from his veins.

Percy finally relaxed, inadvertently flushing one final wave of impurities from his system. His skin was no longer covered in wounds, but an unsettling quantity of black sludge still managed to spill out of his pores, crusting almost instantly and baking his frame into Remior’s smelliest statue. He wasn’t even sure where all the filth had come from, as he’d already purged every flaw that he had spotted.

Scanning his body – he could still do that, thankfully – he realized that his blood was different. It had the same deep red colour as always, though it was much thinner, like water. Despite its deceptively soft texture, Percy could tell that it was richer in oxygen and nutrients than any blood he’d seen. It flowed through his veins and arteries swiftly yet quietly, like the ultimate fuel.

His body was toned and brimming with strength, but notably leaner and more flexible than before. All the excess fat had been burned off, leaving only muscle and bone behind. His eyes, hair, and skin had retained their usual colours, though the latter was entirely smooth and unblemished, like a newborn baby’s.

The arrangement and proportions of his internal organs had been altered slightly, but the differences were so small that even a healer of Archibald’s calibre might struggle to identify them.

Percy’s bones still sported their previous ruby-like quality, yet they’d been reforged even stronger, his marrow had been cleansed to perfection, and his muscles had been knit anew with a far more intricate and efficient pattern.

That said, Percy’s external appearance hadn’t changed that much.

He somehow felt both human and not – a superior variant of his species, broken down and practically rebuilt from scratch, to optimize all his bodily functions while clearly paying homage to the original.

The most distinctly alien feature wasn’t the faint crisscrossing pattern within his muscles, nor his crystalline bones. It was his heartbeat. Percy had done his best to contain the mutations, but some concessions had been unavoidable, and this had seemed like the mildest one.

‘I hope it doesn’t bother Nesha too much the next time she rests her head on my chest,’ he thought with a sigh. Not that he minded it, personally.

Every time his heart thrummed, it did so twice in quick succession. The beats were followed by a pause about as long as a regular human’s, followed by another pair of beats.

While searching for an acceptable configuration earlier, he’d noticed better results as he’d kept one interval normal and continued to shorten the other. Eventually, he’d ended up whittling the gap between them down to nothing.

His blood was circulating much faster, yet his muscles remained firm like steel plates, not bulging by even a millimetre unless he intentionally tensed them. His lungs kept rising and falling at their usual, leisurely pace, and his skin was unperturbed, refusing to allow the rivers of blood quietly simmering beneath it to redden it.

Percy felt strong. So strong, in fact, that he nearly forgot for a second that he’d demoted his cores to Yellow before the start of the ritual and would have to restore them soon.

He grinned, flexing his muscles and breaking himself out of the brittle prison of solidified impurities right as his Status spoke.
