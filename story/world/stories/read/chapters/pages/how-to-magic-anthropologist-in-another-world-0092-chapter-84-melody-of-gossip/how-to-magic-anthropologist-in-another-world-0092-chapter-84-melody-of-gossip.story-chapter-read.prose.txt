I opened my eyes inside my dream sanctuary, finding myself sitting on the couch inside my college flat.

A second later, the front door burst open.

"There you are!" Nina called cheerfully as she hurried inside. "Come on, let's go!"

She grabbed me by the hand and led me into her own sanctuary. Nina was always energetic and enthusiastic, but today she somehow seemed even more excited than usual.

I supposed that was only natural. Tonight we were visiting the Great Dream again.

We skipped over the floating islands and headed directly to the Great Dream Gate, the ornate doorway that connected Nina's sanctuary to the endless web of shared dreams.

The wooden door stood exactly where I remembered it. Hundreds of tiny symbols had been carved into its surface, weaving together into a pattern that looked alive whenever I stared at them for too long. Brass locks covered the frame from top to bottom.

Nina placed one hand against the wood, and I did the same. The locks clicked one after another.

I could smell a faint tang of ozone before the doorway opened, and light poured through the entrance, surrounding us until everything dissolved into a sea of soft pastel colors.

Pink drifted into blue while magenta melted into green. Shapes formed and vanished before I could recognize them, and around us, countless dreams flickered in and out of existence. Some lasted only a heartbeat before dissolving, while others lingered long enough for me to glimpse fragments of lives that weren't my own.

I saw a little girl proudly showing her mother a drawing, a fisherman standing knee-deep in a dark river beneath two moons, then someone flying over an endless forest while laughing. The images had no sound, but I could almost hear the joy on his face.

A blink later, the scene shifted into a nightmare. Endless running through dark corridors, looking behind your back for something that was never there.

Before I could focus on it, Nina grabbed my hand. She squeezed tightly, and the constantly changing scenery immediately stabilized into a quiet park.

Stone paths crossed between carefully trimmed hedges while flowering trees swayed gently in a breeze that smelled faintly of spring rain, and cheerful songbirds filled the air with gentle melodies.

"I am not letting you wander off like last time," Nina declared, still holding my hand. "We actually have somewhere to be today."

"Yes, you're right." I smiled at her.

We walked forward together.

After only a few steps, the scenery folded around us. It wasn't the same sensation of teleportation I normally experienced with Star mana. Instead, I didn't feel like we were the ones moving. It was the world that shifted out of our way while we continued walking normally.

On the horizon, towering buildings rose around us, each one stranger than the last. I wouldn't call it a city per se. We were still walking through the park. It was just that landmarks were appearing around us, almost like advertisements.

Some resembled ordinary stone and brick buildings but stretched impossibly high into the sky. One looked like a castle balanced on the tip of a needle, another appeared to have been carved entirely from crystal, and my favorite was still the giant building shaped like a poop.

"People are people." I couldn't help but laugh.

Nina just rolled her eyes.

A little farther ahead stood our destination. The building resembled an enormous harp.

Its curved frame formed the outer walls while shimmering strings stretched hundreds of meters overhead, vibrating softly whenever the invisible dream breeze passed through them. Every surface had been painted in bright rainbow colors that somehow didn't clash despite the sheer number of hues.

"Welcome," Nina announced dramatically, spreading both arms. "To the Melody of Gossip."

As we stepped inside, I blinked at what I saw. It was not what I had expected at all. The space looked like an empty tavern preparing to open for the evening. Rows of wooden chairs faced a small stage, and a few empty tables stood scattered throughout the room.

Nina smiled, clearly expecting my reaction.

"Come sit." She pointed toward one of the tables. "This is only the preset room."

We sat down.

Nina deliberately blinked, like a certain hero trying to stop time. When she opened her eyes again, reality had shifted. The empty tavern instantly filled with life as every chair around us became occupied by dreamers. Some looked completely human, while others didn't. A woman with fox ears quietly sipped tea while chatting with an elderly dwarf, while a translucent figure that looked almost made of mist applauded politely.

On stage, a shirtless man with magnificent golden wings gently played an elegant harp.

The music filled the room without overpowering the conversations. It sounded more like ambient music than a performance.

I leaned toward Nina.

"So we can switch between rooms and watch performances?" I said in a whisper, not wanting to disturb the performance too much.

She laughed.

"You can speak normally."

"What do you mean?"

"No one here can actually hear us."

She tapped the wooden table.

"You see, I connected a small piece of my sanctuary to the Melody of Gossip. Technically, we're still sitting inside my own sanctuary right now."

I looked around the crowded room again.

"So none of these people are actually here?"

"They are," she said. "But we aren't."

She smiled mischievously.

"We're watching another room through my sanctuary."

"So we can't interact with them?"

"Sadly, no."

She nodded toward the winged musician.

"Not without permission from the host."

Nina blinked again.

The performance changed instantly.

Now a man sat cross-legged on stage beating a hand drum while a woman danced gracefully beside him, her colorful skirts swirling with every movement.

Another blink.

The stage became a marble courtyard.

A woman wearing a flowing white toga played an ornate silver harp while dozens of listeners lounged comfortably in cushioned chairs.

"There are several kinds of rooms," Nina explained. "Some are completely public. Anyone can enter them."

She blinked, and we were inside a crowded tavern where people were almost talking over one another. Some of the people looked directly at us when we appeared. A man smiled when he saw me, and a red rose appeared in his hand. He started walking toward me when Nina blinked again.

The scene shifted, revealing a single performer singing a cappella. The room was empty except for us and him.

"Some are only open for remote viewing."

Another blink, and we returned to the empty preset room.

"Some are completely private. You need permission just to observe them, let alone visit them."

I thought about the endless possibilities.

"How are we supposed to find your friend?" I asked.

"I have access to her private room," Nina replied.

She blinked, but nothing happened.

"It appears she isn't asleep yet." She sighed. "She's always been a night owl."

"I guess we have a couple more hours to kill."

She turned toward me.

"So, what do you want to do?"

"I've heard this is a good place to gather information."

"It is," Nina nodded, "but remember, unlike The Crafter's Paradise, I have very little authority here."

"So you can't just take me to wherever the hottest gossip is."

"I didn't say that." She smiled. "It's just going to be more chaotic than a properly curated room where only experts are allowed to enter."

She blinked.

The empty preset room dissolved around us.

A heartbeat later, we appeared inside what looked like an enormous guild dining hall.

Long wooden tables stretched from one end of the room to the other. Each could comfortably seat twenty people, and nearly every seat was occupied. Hundreds of conversations blended together into a constant background murmur.

Above the entrance floated glowing letters.

Hano - Room 16: Food 3

I looked up.

"Sixteen?"

"There are lots of Hano rooms," Nina explained. "Rooms one through five are mostly Freelancer gossip. This one is dedicated to restaurants, taverns, markets, and food."

"There are three food rooms?"

"More."

"Hano is a hyper-consumer city."

"Right, It really is," I said, remembering the countless villages I had seen on the road dedicated to satisfying the big city's hunger.

Nobody paid us any attention as we slowly walked between the tables.

"The Golden Crown made their stew less spicy," one bearded man complained loudly. "I'm telling you, there's a fire root shortage."

"I know," grumbled his companion, "but I'm also telling you it's temporary."

"The goddess Damada personally blessed her enclave," a third man said with a confident nod. "Next harvest there'll be more fire root than anyone knows what to do with."

Another nearby conversation drifted into my ears.

"The tea shipment from the Sky Islands is late again."

"When isn't it?" sighed someone beside him. "Honestly, somebody should just grow it here."

"Don't be daft." An older Naga woman snorted. "The flavor comes from growing it on floating clouds. How are you expecting that to happen?"

"Magic."

"That's not how magic works." She rolled her eyes. "You'd need half a dozen mages with the right affinities just to imitate the environment. It would cost a lot of gold."

We wandered farther through the room. Every table was its own little discussion forum.

Some debated recipes, others argued over bakeries, and one entire table seemed devoted to cheese. The last one was getting a little heated, especially when someone started arguing the merits of megafauna cheese, the cheap mass-produced product that only cost a few dozen coppers per kilogram.

I honestly hadn't expected foodie culture to be this prevalent in a fantasy world. But that was my bias speaking. Whenever there was communication, there would be organization. Sadly, less than a sixth of the people in Hano had access to the Great Dream. Otherwise, the world might have been far more advanced than it was.

"I'm telling you, Tan Je'e is planning something big."

I slowed down as I heard a name I didn't expect to hear.

Four men sat around a table. Two of them had Valkyren wings like Nina, a third had fox ears, while the fourth looked like an ordinary human if not better dressed than most.

"I'm sorry, Vargo," the fox-eared man laughed. "You're talking out of your ass. Tan Je'e is a jeweler. Why should food critics care what she does?"

"No, I'm serious," insisted Vargo. He had black raven wings. "She's entering the food business."

The other three looked at him.

"What?" asked the other Valkyren. His wings were ash gray, not rock-pigeon colored like Nina's, but completely gray.

"She hired Silnar," insisted Vargo. "The boy who makes the best Konta in Marketside."

"Second best," scoffed the gray-winged man. "My cousin makes better Konta."

"Shut up, Wabi," said the fox-eared man. "Your cousin makes bricks. Silnar's Konta is light and creamy."

"Hey!" protested the gray-winged man, Wabi.

"I'm serious, Yura," said Vargo to the fox man.

"So she really hired him?" Yura now looked completely interested as he leaned forward.

"I swear on my mother's cooking," said Vargo. "I spoke to Silnar myself."

"What about the little shop near the Holy Temple?" asked Wabi.

"The old lady will keep it running until she retires," answered Vargo.

"So what exactly are they making?" asked Yura.

"He wouldn't tell me." Vargo shrugged.

"But..." He lowered his voice dramatically. "I did some digging. I found out they've been hiring apprentices all over the city."

"What apprentices?" asked Wabi, his gray wings flaring.

"Mostly bakers, I hear, but some cooks too."

"I bet it's a cake shop," said Yura.

"That would make sense," said Vargo.

"I don't know," said Wabi. "Cake doesn't require that many apprentices."

"Unless they're planning something huge," insisted Vargo.

Well then, what do we have here? People were already talking about my pizza franchise before it even opened.

I smiled to myself. This was too good to pass up.

I pulled out a chair and sat beside them.

"So you've already heard about it."

All four men looked at me.

"Of course we have," Wabi said proudly, puffing his wings. "We're among the best food critics in Hano."

"If somewhere serves good food," Vargo added, "we usually know before it even opens."

"Our recommendations can make or break a business," said Yura, his fox ear shifting.

Dear God, they were fantasy Yelp reviewers.

How wonderful…

Honestly... Louis needed to impress these kinds of people if she ever hoped for her business to succeed.

Without newspapers, advertisements, or billboards, word of mouth was everything.

Or perhaps... it was word of dream.

"You sound like you know something," Vargo observed.

"I do." I smiled back at him.

"I'm one of the investors."

That immediately caught everyone's attention.

The fourth man, who had remained completely silent until now, finally spoke.

"Nina... is she telling the truth?" He looked past me toward my friend.

"She is," Nina nodded.

"This is Alice," the Dreamer inventor declared. "She's friends with Tan Je'e."

"In fact," Nina continued proudly, "Alice introduced me to Lady Je'e, and both of them helped finance my spyglass workshop."

The quiet man slowly nodded.

Apparently, he knew Nina well enough to trust her word.

"So..." Vargo leaned across the table. "Are you opening a cake shop?"

"No." I grinned. "It's something completely different."

"What is it?" asked Wabi.

"You see, there is a Holy girl named Louis," I said in a gossipy tone. "She's engaged to Silnar. Well, she invented a new dish called pizza."

"When I tried it, I knew it was going to be the next big thing," I gushed. "It. Was. Increee'Dii'Ble."

"So of course I went running to the only merchant I knew, Lady Je'e, and together we agreed to fund the business."

"I also invested quite a bit of gold myself," I added.

"You paid for it?" Yura blinked.

"So this Louis girl is going to be the head chef? Not Silnar?" asked Yura.

"Yes and yes."

"So why hire all those apprentices?" Wabi asked.

"Don't know." I shrugged. "Je'e is in charge of management."

The quiet man looked toward Nina again.

"Have you tasted this pizza?"

"I have, once." She smiled. "It was the best meal I've had this year."

The entire table became noticeably more interested and questions immediately started flying from every direction.

"What kind of food is it?"

"Is it expensive?"

"Will they serve alcohol?"

"Can you eat it while walking?"

"Is it spicy?"

"Will there be meat?"

I answered what I could while carefully avoiding too many details.

"There will be several different varieties."

"They'll all look similar but taste completely different."

"There will also be a completely new dessert."

"And a new drink."

"So it's not just a restaurant."

"No."

"It's more like..." I searched for the right word without saying fast food.

"It's an entirely new way of eating," I ended up saying.

"When does it open?" asked the quiet man.

"Hopefully next week."

"And where?"

"In the Guild District. Near the bank."

The quiet man slowly stood.

"I'll be there."

Without another word, he turned and walked away.

I watched him disappear into the crowd.

"Who was that?"

"That's my landlord," Nina said. "Lord Mick Ira."

"The man owns nearly half the buildings on Market Street," Vargo added. "But his real passion is food."

"I didn't know you were renting," I said as I looked back toward Nina.

"Want me to buy you your house?"

The entire table fell silent.

Nina slowly closed her eyes.

"For the love of the Sleepless Father..." she groaned. "Stop throwing your money at me."

Oops. I put my foot in my mouth again.

"Right." I rubbed the back of my neck awkwardly. "Sorry, I guess that was a bit much."

"You can buy me a house," Wabi said, raising his hand. "I'm renting too."

"I'll do that," I nodded seriously. "Once you craft me a soulbound spear like Nina did."

"I am a bard," Wabi protested. "I can make you a song."

"I think I'll pass."

The table burst into laughter. Even Nina looked considerably less annoyed.

I decided to steer the conversation back toward the mysterious noble.

"So..." I prompted. "What's the deal with Lord Mick Ira? He seemed like a pretty big deal."

"He is," Nina answered. "Lord Mick is a scion of Clan Ira, the premier Light affinity bloodline. That makes him influential by default."

"His family has owned land around Hano for generations," Vargo nodded.

"He also writes a weekly column in Hano's broadsheet," Yura added. "It's mostly restaurant suggestions, recipes, and food trends."

"Wait." I blinked. "There's a newspaper?"

"There is," Nina nodded.

"How come I've never seen it?"

"It's only for nobles," Nina shrugged. "Each copy has to be handwritten by professional scribes."

"They are also really expensive," said Yura.

"How expensive?"

"A dozen silver for a yearly subscription."

I whistled.

"No wonder I never saw them."

"The common people have us bards instead," Vargo laughed. "They don't need to pay silver for a stack of paper."

"We spread the news the old-fashioned way," he added.

"By gossip?" I asked.

"By gossip," he grinned shamelessly.

I rubbed my chin.

"I have the San title. Would that allow me access to the broadsheet?"

"It should," Yura nodded. "San is mostly an honorary title, but it's still recognized by the nobility."

I really needed to get my hands on a copy, preferably the older copies as well. A newspaper is a snapshot of society. Reading the city's newspapers would tell me what the people in power wanted everyone else to think.

Even if it was mostly propaganda, understanding what people chose to print was almost as important as understanding what they refused to print. The lies revealed just as much as the truth. It would tell me whether the nobility currently feared the Freelancer Guild, whether the military was gaining influence, or whether a manufactured crisis had been put up front to hide something even more sinister behind it.

As I was lost in thought, another question occurred to me.

"Speaking of nobles," I said, looking back toward Nina. "How is Lord Mick here in the Great Dream? Isn't he an Elemental bloodline noble? I thought only Dreamers could enter it."

"He has one," said Yura. "A Dreamer servant, I mean."

"He employs a Dreamer whose full-time job is bringing him into the Great Dream every night."

"That's it?" I asked.

"That's it." Wabi shrugged. "It's a cushy job. It even pays better than singing in taverns."

"Honestly." I couldn't help smiling. "That's kind of genius."

"It is," Nina admitted. "Dreamers are rare in Hano, but they are not so rare that the truly wealthy can't simply hire one."

"Money buys most things," Vargo shrugged. "I've even heard that the Soulscribes over in Sky Daisy are getting close to creating a Soulbook that grants Dreamer abilities."

"Bah." Yura waved dismissively. "They've been 'close' for three hundred years. They'll probably still be close another three hundred years from now."

"Dreaming doesn't seem like the sort of thing you can force with soul magic," Wabi said with a shrug.

"I don't know," I said. "Soulscribing may have a few breakthroughs in the upcoming years."

Nina suddenly clapped her hands together.

"Well! I think that's enough food gossip for one evening."

She stood up from the table and stretched her wings.

"There are dozens of rooms worth visiting." She looked down at me with a mischievous grin. "Come on, Alice. Wanna hear some Freelancer gossip."

I stood as well.

"Lead the way."

The food critics waved goodbye as I held out my hand. Nina took it before she blinked once more.

The bustling dining hall dissolved around us, replaced once again by the shifting dreamscape of endless possibilities
