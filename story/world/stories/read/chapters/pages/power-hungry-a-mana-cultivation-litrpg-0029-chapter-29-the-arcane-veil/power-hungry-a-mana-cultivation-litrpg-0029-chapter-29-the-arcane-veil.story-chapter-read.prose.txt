Cale caught up to the group shortly after they stopped for their midday break. The bitter wind had momentarily died down, leaving an eerie stillness across the blasted landscape. Red particles hung suspended in the air, catching sunlight like microscopic rubies. It was almost beautiful, in a strange, alien way.

The recruits had set up a makeshift rest area in the shelter of a massive, crystalline formation that jutted from the ground at an unnatural angle. Its black surface was laced with pulsing red veins, but it provided decent cover from the elements—and potential threats.

Cassidy was handing out bowls of spagon mush and brown slop. The smell wasn't exactly pleasant, but after hours of cultivation and fighting murderbots, even inedible garbage was starting to smell good.

Cale idly wondered why the Sullied wasn't made to serve the food. DR-12 was sitting down against a red and black crystalline outcropping, eating the spagon with his fingers, focused on the food. Cale figured food was one of the few comforts the Sullied had. Had Cassidy served DR-12 the food? Surely not?

Who cares? I have a headache and bigger concerns.

Namely food and getting Sintra off his back.

Ragnak noticed him first. He raised his bowl in the air. "Hero returns!"

That drew a few dry chuckles. Even Galvon, who normally looked at Cale with thinly veiled contempt, managed a smirk.

"Thought the Citadel ate you," Risto drawled, not looking up from his food.

Cale slid the hoverboard to a stop and carefully lowered Sintra onto the ground. She stirred weakly but said nothing. Her color was better than before—the healing potion had done its work—but she was still far from recovered. He made sure she was stable before stretching out his arms and rolling his shoulders. His muscles protested with dull aches.

"How is she?" Artan asked, his voice neutral but his eyes sharp.

"She'll live," Cale answered, rotating his stiff neck. "Just needs rest."

Madia shot him a glance but said nothing. No more scathing remarks, no accusations. Just a nod. Maybe that was as much credit as she was willing to give.

"What'd I miss?" Cale asked, looking around at the makeshift camp.

"Nothing special," Risto said, leaning his back against Artan's. They both nodded at Cale as he got closer. "Just trudging through hell."

"We are making good time," Artan added. "Should reach the spire before nightfall."

"If we don't get eaten by something," Galvon muttered into his bowl.

Cassidy approached with a bowl and a familiar lopsided smile. "Eat up, hero."

Cale took it without hesitation, but the back of his hand stung as her clawed ring nicked his skin. It felt like a mosquito bite. Was it on purpose?

Another power play? Or just watching for a reaction?

Cale was too hungry to care. He shuffled a few paces to a patch of flat land and sank onto his hoverboard, letting it hover four inches off the ground. The relief of sitting after standing and moving for hours was almost blissful.

"That's the stuff..." He exhaled, closing his eyes for just a moment.

A thin shaft of sunlight pierced through the clouds, warming his face. For a brief moment, the wasteland almost felt peaceful. The constant edge of danger, the exhaustion of survival—it all receded for a precious second. Cale let himself feel it, knew it wouldn't last.

Without a word, Fiara came up to him and scooted down next to him, pushing him to the edge of the hoverboard. It was a tight fit. Close enough that Cale could feel her warmth. She smelled faintly of vanilla.

“Your butt’s too big,” she muttered, still focused on her meal.

Cale cracked an eye open. “Mine? What about yours?”

“My butt is perfectly shaped,” Fiara said, shooting him a look.

Cale knew better than to answer that. He let it go and forced himself to chew the spagon. It was even worse than this morning—bitter, dense, dry. The brown slop was clumpy and tasted slightly  burned.

He took a look at the makeshift camp. Everyone was sitting with a bowl in their hand. Even Ragnak was quietly leaning against his hand, slumping over his bowl. Cassidy was still standing by the payload. Probably keeping watch.

Cale turned his focus back to Fiara. “Want to share?”

“Your butt’s too big,” she said lazily.

Cale rolled his eyes and let himself lean back slightly, slumping over his stomach in earnest. Fiara didn’t move away. His movement sucked out the remaining space between them

She was noticeably close.

They sat like that for a moment, the conversation dying down around them as the recruits ate in silence. The wind howled through the jagged structures around them, whipping dust through the air in slow, lazy curls.

For the first time in hours, Cale felt still. His whole body was getting gradually calmer. His heartbeat slower. He yawned.

Fiara’s voice cut through the quiet. “Did you eat a pill?”

She didn’t lift her gaze from her food. Her tone was low.

Cale took a quick glance around. Everyone was sitting. No one was paying attention. Cassidy was looking the other way. She would tell them if something attacked…

He lowered his voice. “I don’t need to. Aura...”

Fiara made a small sound in her throat. “I’d be pissed at you if you weren’t so nice.”

Cale smirked. “Pays to be nice, I guess.” He forced down another bite.

Fiara chewed slowly, then spoke again. “I made a note of you staying back to take care of Sintra. That’ll rack up a few merit credits. But Darius is going to scold you.”

Cale snorted. “I can handle that.”

They sat in a comfortable silence. Fiara’s cheek slumped on his shoulder.

“Why is it racking me points?” Cale asked after swallowing down a piece of spagon.

Fiara shrugged, a slow, lazy motion. Her voice was quieter now, softer. “It’s the line.”

“Line?” Cale frowned.

“Between bravery and stupidity. Between genius and foolish.” She chuckled to herself. “What matters is the results.”

She swayed slightly. Her shoulder pressed against his.

She’s slurring.

Wait.

Fiara giggled again. “It was a tactically dumb move but you succeeded… You are… good at succeeding…”

Cale’s mind went sluggish for a second. Fiara was leaning against him. It was nice.

She felt warm.

Cale leaned back, exhaled slowly. His heart was beating in slow heavy thuds.

A nap… yeah. Just for a second…

…

“Cale!” Aura’s voice blasted through the fog of his mind like a gunshot. “Stay awake! You are poisoned!”

His eyes snapped open.

His breath hitched. His heartbeat spiked. His limbs—numb.

He tried to snap up off the hoverboard but—

His legs didn’t work.

His arms didn’t work.

He crashed to the ground, the bowl of spagon spilling beside him. The red dirt felt cold against his cheek. Cale let out a half-grunt-half-exhale.

Cassidy turned her head slightly, watching with idle amusement.

Oh… So that’s how it is.

She smiled.

She took a few steps towards Cale. He struggled to get up, but couldn’t move. His muscles and mana were both locked.

“Look at you,” she said and crouched down and traced a hand along Cale’s chin. “I was a little worried about you, since you have seem to have the ability to pull rabbits out of a hat. But it seems the Dreadfang venom was effective.”

Cale growled and tried to thrash around as if willpower alone could break through. But it seemed he had lost all ability to move. His body was numb. “Auf— Auffh—”

“My my, you are a persistent one,” Cassidy said and smiled. “Don’t worry, the poison is not lethal. Not killing you is just as effective for my needs. Besides, I’m not stupid enough to want to deal with the heat.”

She walked away and started checking on other recruits, making sure they were paralyzed. Cale managed to spit out a word. “Au… Ra…”

“Oh!” Aura said immediately. “I can simply counteract the poison with my healing sigils. Why didn’t you say anything earlier!”

Cale managed to get out a grunt as an answer and then he relaxed. His mind was a foggy swamp, slow and unclear. But as Aura worked, healing him on a cellular level, doing whatever advanced magic technology that Cale didn’t begin to understand, he was starting to feel gradually better. The numbness was receding. His breathing got easier. His heartbeat increased gradually, and suddenly he felt a great flush overcome him. A heatwave that made his skin prickle and his brow soaking wet almost in an instant.

[A.U.R.A skill advanced.]

[Molecular Tissue Regeneration: 5]

“I can’t work miracles. You’re going to get a high fever from this. I assume you are not going to be a little crybaby and call it in sick?”

Cale flexed his fingers, as feeling returned to them. Then the other muscles. He tightened every muscle on his body. Then relaxed them instantly, letting the mana expand. A tiny outline of green enveloped his skin as it turned translucent.

Slowly he got up.

“I wouldn’t dream of it.”
