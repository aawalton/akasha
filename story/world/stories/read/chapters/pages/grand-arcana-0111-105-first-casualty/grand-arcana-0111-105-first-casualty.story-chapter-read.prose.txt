I watched as the goth with the mascara reluctantly took out a stone from the bag, sliding it over his forehead, then, in turn, the rest of the group. Every second, I wanted to rush up to him and punch him in the face.

It was obvious that Eleonor had resorted to her divine state, probably the reason why the rest of them had managed to survive. There was no telling what Pup had gone through, but he seemed drained as well.

Weren’t there four of them? Serp asked.

Yes, I mentally replied. There were.

One week in, and we already had our first casualty. When it came to trials, this was rather good; some might even call it exceptional. The sixth trial during my run had been an absolute massacre, as had the dozen afterwards. There hadn’t been any pact holders back then, though, not to mention that the number of cards in everyone’s possession averaged five.

“Happy?” the goth asked, his arrogance streaking through despite the humiliating defeat.

There was a long moment of silence. I could see the crossbowmen tense up, ready to drill him full of holes should the order be given.

“Lower crossbows!” Lord Steen shouted. “Close the gates!”

Before anyone had a chance to react, I darted up to Eleonor.

“It’s fine.” I grabbed hold of her, combining my charm special with the Lover’s card. “Sleep, sleep…” I started humming, but there was no need.

The girl fell asleep in my arms before the Bard skill could take effect.

I waved for the guards to approach.

“Take her to a temple,” I said as I handed her to them. “And don’t wake her up.”

“A temple?” one of them asked, surprised. “The healer—”

“A temple,” I said in a firm tone. There wasn’t a healer in the city that could do a fraction of what her deity would provide. “Pup.” I turned to him, completely ignoring the goth leader. “How is it?”

“Err… I’m fine.” He looked up at me.

The scars on his face were healing as we spoke, though not fast enough to be efficient in battle. I glanced at his status box. He had the Fourteen of Chalice, though that wouldn’t explain the current speed of his regeneration. None of his specials or items provided any clear reason for it, either.

“Just a little tired.” He attempted to smile.

I bet you’re more than tired, I thought.

Same as in the past run, he was trying to overcompensate for his weakness by pretending he could take on a higher load than his body would allow. The only difference was that instead of Avon, he had this jerk to impress.

“What happened out there?” I asked.

Pup looked at his leader, waiting for an indication of how to answer.

“Nothing you need to know,” the other goth hissed, shoving his way between us. “We didn’t kill it. That’s what counts.”

He probably messed up really bad. Serp laughed.

In this case, I agreed.

“Go to the temple,” I hissed.

Even now, I had too much baggage from my last run to think clearly. I was already pissed at Lord Steen; any additional agitation could well force me to start something I couldn’t take back. Thankfully, Serp didn’t offer to kill the goth off. If he had, there was a chance I might have accepted.

“Was headed there anyway,” he uttered, then walked off, deliberately bumping into me along the way.

“Wise move,” I whispered. “Can you walk?” I turned to Pup.

“Yeah, I can walk fine…” It was a lie, but at least he seemed to be able to stay on his feet.

I put his arm across my shoulders, taking part of the weight. “Let’s go.”

The crowd broke up, forming a path for us. Lord Steen seemed to be occupied with the gate, which was for the best. I caught sight of the pair carrying Eleonor and hurried after them.

“So, what happened?” I asked.

“It was an ambush,” Pup replied. “Eleonor tracked down the creature. It seemed so easy. We saw the infected animals a mile away. Even the worms hiding in the trees. When we got close to the mountain, I thought we were almost there.”

“Don’t rush.” I interrupted. That was one of his bad habits—always wanting to say more than his lungs allowed.

“Sorry…” He looked down.

I slowed my pace. As much as I wanted to get to Eleonor, I also didn’t want to tire him out.

“So, you reached the mountain,” I invited him to continue. “What then?”

“There it was… standing on the cliffs.” He paused. “The boss rushed towards it.”

Of course, he would. Someone that arrogant had to be the one to claim the prize. There was more than that, though. Eleonor not stepping in meant that she was second in the pecking order. If nothing else, the goths followed a very strict hierarchy. Those with authority issued orders for the others to obey. It was the same during the initial trials. Back then, Eleonor had been the one giving the orders. I remember how people had sacrificed themselves for her to continue and gain an advantage in the process. Whoever this new guy was, he had to be a pretty big shot.

“Then they appeared.” Pup’s entire body shivered as if he were reliving the scene. “Dozens of them. They were all infected… and had better cards than us. Lars charged in to protect the boss. I tried to as well, but I was too slow.”

In the past run, Pup had mentioned that he had once been part of a cult. At the time, I had taken that to be something related to our old world. It could never have occurred to me that he was talking about an organization within the Grand Arcana itself. There was no denying it. That’s precisely what the goth faction was. They didn’t have any concept of self whatsoever. Their only goal was to protect the higher-ranking members at any cost while exploiting the lower-ranking ones as best they could.

Was that Hypnos’ idea? Had it come to be purely due to the delusions of grandeur of his followers? Knowing the major deities, there was a good chance that there was a bit of both.

“Past participants?” I asked.

Pup nodded.

“You met them?” he asked in semi-awe.

“Something like that.” After the experience with Sparta, I had come across a few more of them. They were in groups of two or three and always willing to fight to the end. From their point of view, dying was probably the best that could happen; at least then, they wouldn’t be a walking nest of parasites.

The nearest temple ended up not being as near as I had hoped. It took us several minutes to reach it, and one more to get inside. Apparently, lots of the women had gone there to pray that their loved ones be spared from the worms, and refused to move even as the soldiers fought to bring Eleonor in.

I was tempted to use the Emperor card to get them to leave, but chose to sing a calming song instead. With the immediate fear gone, the people quickly left, leaving the place virtually empty.

“Put her by the altar!” I ordered the guards. “Then leave.”

One of them turned around to argue. One look at my expression was enough to block the words in his throat. Lord Steen’s orders aside, he didn’t want to mess with someone like me, and he knew it.

Gently, they placed Eleonor on the floor, by the central altar, then quickly left.

I went to the nearest statue and let Pup lean against it.

“Stay there.” I stepped away. “And don’t try to walk!” I pointed at him in a threatening manner.

Where are the mountain cliffs? I thought, seeking information from Breath of Moonlight.

Messages appeared before my eyes, indicating the exact spot. As vague as Pup’s description was, the moon saw all, even during the day. As long as the fight took place above the canopy of trees, the deity would know, and now, so did I.

“How could you be so stupid?” I said under my breath as I stood over the unconscious oracle.

If only she had used a fraction of her power before the start of her trip instead of its end, all this would have been avoided.

A faint glow was emanating from the girl. No scraps or open wounds were getting healed, suggesting that there was a lot worse unseen damage.

“What did I miss?” a female voice asked beside me.

“Nice to see you, too.” It was almost too convenient that she would show up once Eleonor got to the city. “Where were you all this time?”

“There was something I had to do,” Sparta replied unapologetically. “What happened?”

Something she had to do... That was the downside of having relations with a follower of Mercury. One moment, you could be the love of their life; the next, they’d be gone without a note of explanation. For some reason that didn’t seem to bother me as much during my past run.

“They tried to kill the monster and got ambushed,” I replied. “More participants.”

“Interesting.” Sparta stepped closer to Eleonor. “My group came across some of them as well. How many do you think there are?”

“Enough. Our good friend, the former participant, has been sending groups to kill the creature for a while, I think.”

“You can’t blame him. It’s his city now. Besides, that’s the goal of the trial.”

No, I thought. It’s one goal of the trial.

If I had to guess, this was the weakest of the three creatures we had to kill. Unless things were drastically different, there was a hydra nested beyond the forest. Time-wise, this trial took place considerably earlier than the one I had done, so there still weren’t that many hydra spawn. Normally, killing a hydra would be a lot more difficult than dealing with the beast plaguing the city, though that was without taking the possessed participants in mind.

“Keep an eye on her,” I said, then looked over my shoulder. “And him.”

“Quick hint: asking your girlfriend to watch over your ex is never a good idea.”

“She’s not my ex,” I said firmly. I should have added that we weren’t lovers yet, although with every passing trial, the distinction was getting less and less obvious. “I don’t trust Lord Steen. There are too many participants for them to have gotten randomly infected.”

“Got you.” She didn’t argue.

By now, even Jean, the new goth leader, had probably come to the same conclusion. For this many participants to have gotten infected, there had to be something else in play. I doubted that Steen was doing it himself, though I couldn’t reject the idea outright.

“Pup.” I turned to him. “Stay.”

“But I—”

“Stay,” I repeated.

The boy struggled to respond, but ultimately nodded. Some things apparently didn’t change. It also helped that he had far less confidence at this point. As the saying went, there were two types of participants: those that had a Grand Arcana card and those that were shit.

Taking one last look at Eleonor and Sparta, I left the temple. Some would have called that stupid, considering how many divine requests I could have gotten. For the moment, that was irrelevant. In order to win against the higher-level participants, I needed to boost my Observer card, and that didn’t come cheap. Up until now, I had fed it three booster spheres, and it had refused to reveal its second skill. In order to speed up the process, I needed to fulfill the request I had taken before stepping into the trial-world: I needed to capture a skyfox.

Where’s the dream field? I asked as I walked along the streets.

People quickly moved aside as I approached, feeling the anger emanating from me.

A message appeared, displaying an exact image of the area in question. It was a small patch of land located on top of a large hill. The only issue was that it was within the Crystal Fields.

Come on, old man! Serp protested. Don’t tell me we’re going there!

I didn’t reply. The moment of calm with Sparta had almost made me forget that I was still in the Grand Arcana. The attack on the goths had been a wake-up call—not so much for them as for me.

Gain every advantage, I thought as I walked on. Make use of every opportunity. Everything else could wait for the preparation hall.
