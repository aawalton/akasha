“So do you want to trade it or not?” the punk kid from two blocks down the cul de sac cawed at me. He wanted me to trade my secret rare PCP Charizard V for a lousy VMAX Darkness Ablaze. I looked through his gapped bucked teeth one more time before my eyes trailed upward to that sleazy expression on his face. He must have pegged me for a noob. No one in their right mind would entertain that trade. I deadpanned him and stood my ground, giving him my third and final rejection.

“No, Elliot. I am not interested. So quit squawking and shoo.”

“Whatever. Your loss.” He spat on the ground and stomped away, grumbling something I couldn’t hear under his breath.

I watched him slink off, knowing he would come back around. They always did when they were trying to pull something over on me. Those boys were out of luck if they thought my pigtails and pink Hello Kitty skirt meant I was going to be an easy swindle. I’d been collecting Pokémon cards since I was five, and at eight, my binder was the envy of the neighborhood.

That was probably the third time in two days that Elliot had tried to run me for my Charizard. I admired his persistence, if nothing else.

It was still sunny outside for a Sunday winter morning. I wanted to stay by the porch a little longer to organize my trading card binder, and had even more of a reason to when I saw a black Rolls Royce Phantom pull up by our curb. I was only nine years old, but I knew that car by brand now. This wasn’t the first time I’d seen it, and it certainly wouldn’t be my last.

The car rolled to a smooth stop, and I could see the silhouette of a man in the driver’s seat, straight-backed and still, like a soldier waiting for orders. With tinted windows, you could hardly see the passenger in the back, but I already knew who it was. On cue, the chauffeur exited the car and walked over to the passenger side. With his white gloved hand, he opened the door, and out stepped the tall and stout Henry B. Boson, looking spiffier than ever. He wore one of those long wool coats over a white suit—perfectly imposing. He paused, scanning the street with quick eyes behind those fancy sunglasses, like a hawk in tailored slacks. I braced myself as he turned his gaze toward me, and I was foolish enough to pretend that I didn’t see him when I buried my eyes in my binder.

Dad didn’t like it when he visited the house. So when Henry B. Brazen, the bald uninvited guest, proceeded to casually approach me as I sat on the porch, I tried to keep my head down and my business to myself.

“Well, if it isn’t my little friend,” he said in a deep raspy voice as he walked up the steps. He gave me that toothy grin of his, one of those phony looking ones, too.

“What’re you doing here?” I asked bitterly.

“Just thought I’d drop by on this fine morning.” He peered over his glasses, pretending to be surprised by my cards. “And what’s this? You’re still playing with those things?”

“Collecting,” I mumbled.

“Ah. A collector. One of many good signs of a promising young entrepreneur in the making!” He laughed, a little too loud for how quiet it was outside. I didn’t laugh with him. “You know, you take after your father. He was into collecting antiques when he first started his empire. Now look at him. A big fish in a very grand pond.” He shifted his weight from one foot to the other, like he was planning the next move in some invisible game of chess.

“He doesn’t do that kind of stuff anymore,” I said as I stuffed my cards back into their laminate slots, feeling offended at his remark. Boson was referring to the dark trade business I accidentally found out about a year ago. It was my Dad’s side gig, and I didn’t know what it was exactly, but whatever it was, he’d closed the doors on the operation and never looked back. So whenever someone like Boson, a reminder of his dark past, kept coming over here and bringing it up, it pissed me off.

“Oh?” he said almost condescendingly. “And what does he do now?”

“Just works from home.”

“Doing what exactly?”

“Writing,” I said, standing up. I watched his face for any sign of reaction, but he only adjusted his glasses with those pudgy fingers of his and kept on grinning.

“Interesting,” he said. “I suppose it’s never too late for a man to change course.” The way he spoke, it was hard to tell if he genuinely believed it or if there was some other meaning tucked beneath those elegantly enunciated words. “Is he home?”

“No, he’s—”

“Boson!” My dad called out from across the street. His stride sped up when he locked eyes to his ex-business partner, the expression on his face hardening with every step. “You stay away from my daughter!” he shouted.

“Where’s the hospitality?” Boson said, throwing his arms wide open like he was expecting a hug. “Is that how you treat an old friend?”

“We talked about this.” Dad was on the porch now, his face dangerously close to Boson’s. He reminded me a lot of Marvel’s Dr. Strange, even had the gray hairs on the sides of his head, too, and was just as tall. “I told you, we are done!”

Boson didn’t miss a beat. He looked amused, as if Dad’s irritation pleased him. “I figured you’d warm up to me eventually, you know. After all we’ve been through.”

Dad’s eyes cut sideways at me. “Go inside, Freya.”

I hesitated, but he didn’t look away. Reluctantly, I packed up my binder and ran toward the door. The last thing I saw before closing it was Boson pulling something small out of his coat pocket. It was hard to tell what it was, even harder to hear their conversation from the other side of the closed front door, until—

“I’m not going to tell you twice, little lady,” Mom tweeted lightheartedly from behind me as she dusted the family room’s ceiling fan from a mini ladder. “The laundry isn’t going to unload itself!” But then that sweet smile on her face went south when she read the expression on my face. “What’s wrong?”

“Boson’s here,” I said blankly.

For a moment there was silence, my mom holding her breath in at the news, looking visibly uncomfortable about it. She went down the short flight of steps and settled her duster down on the coffee table before brushing her hands down her denim jeans. “Get that laundry done for me right now, okay, sweetie?”

I nodded, settling my trading book down on the couch before heading to the laundry room. My Mom, however, went toward the route of chaos. Whenever she went head to head with Boson, a storm followed. Mom hated Boson more than Dad did, and I didn’t blame her. He was like a weed that kept on sprouting no matter how hard you tried pulling it out of the ground. I overheard that Boson was responsible for a lot of trouble back in the day, that he and Dad were two peas in a pod, until they weren’t. I didn’t know exactly how things fell apart, but knowing Boson, I had an idea.

I opened the washer door and pulled out the damp clothes for the dryer. Not a moment after, I heard the door open again, my mom’s voice joining the fray. “You’ve got some nerve, Henry!” she exclaimed. She was usually the diplomatic type but not with Boson. Adjusting my ear against the wall of the laundry room, I hunkered down by the laundry basket and listened in, being nosy, as usual.

“Ah, Marilynn. Looking as gorgeous as ever,” Boson said, his tone almost playful.

“You need to leave now!” Mom demanded. She was good at setting her foot down when she needed to. He should be gone in two seconds.

“Let’s not be too hasty, now,” Boson replied. “Rest assured, I came here in good faith.”

“Can’t you take a hint?!” she hissed. “Grab Lurch over there, get in your car, and get the hell out of my house!”

“Mary, I understand you’re upset, but—”

“You damn near ran my family to the ground!” she growled. “The balls you must have to show your disgusting face here again!”

“And that’s why I’m here, to make amends,” Boson said. “I’m offering an opportunity to help, nothing more.”

“We don’t need any help,” Dad spoke. The tightness in his voice made my stomach churn.

“Is that so?” Boson’s voice was lower now, like they’d all stepped closer to each other. “You know, Cal, rumors are interesting things. They travel far and wide, find ears you never expect them to…” His words drifted off with a pause. “Word has it that your housekeeping efforts have broken your once fruitful accounts. Your holdings, frozen. Your assets, liquified. Bleeding out faster than a cow in a slaughterhouse. A pity, really.”

“Did you really come all the way here to run your mouth and shame me?” Dad scowled.

“Not at all. Like I said, I came here to make amends. I’d like to help. After all, Cal, you have mouths to feed.”

“We’d rather starve,” Mom retorted. “Get rid of him now or I’m calling the cops on charges of harassment!” She gave her final warning and went back inside the house, the doors slamming hard.

I stuffed the damp load of clothes in the dryer and then peeked out into the hallway in time to see Mom dash up the stairs and into her bedroom. The rest of the clothes could wait. I snuck back to the living room’s big windows overlooking the front porch and held my breath close to the glass. The curtains hid most of my face as I continued to eavesdrop. Dad was still out there with Boson, and they were arguing, but this time there was less yelling.

“Consider this a token of my appreciation,” Boson said. “The list I gave you is minor, something to help you get back in the game. Nothing you can’t finish overnight, I am sure.” He grinned. “As for the anathema, this will serve useful,” he said, offering him a rusty looking copper ring. “You know what to do with it.”

Boson dropped the ring in his hand. Dad paused, as if considering something, before glaring up at him. “If you know what’s good for you, you’ll keep off my property. The next time, I won’t be so forgiving….”

Boson chuckled. “It’s been good seeing you again, Cal. I suspect we will be in touch.”

He tucked his hands into his coat pockets and turned away, a bounce in his step as he walked toward the car and shut the door behind him. The chauffeur drove off, the Rolls Royce disappearing down the street, leaving Dad on the porch looking smaller than I’d ever seen him before. Before he could turn to the door, I dashed off and raced up the stairs for my bedroom. The sound of the steps creaking followed, Dad no doubt heading to his office when he made that right turn in the hallway.

His office was an off limits zone where the door was always locked. But this time, as I crept out of my bedroom, I noticed it wasn’t.

I couldn’t count the number of times my curiosity got the best of me with my fingers. I was daring to a fault, and always ready to see how far I could go without finding myself knee deep in some hot bubbling trouble. But this was a rare opportunity to peek inside the hub of my father’s mysterious world. He had always shut me out, but right now, I was itching to know what that ring was all about.

I stepped out of my room, tiptoeing down the hallway like a lone ninja on a recon mission. Dad’s office was right at the end of it, and when I finally reached the slightly open door, my eyes ballooned up to what I was seeing—my dad over his desk with his hand out, holding the ring as he infused some type of purple magic into it!

At least that was what it looked like. Purple glowy, swirly magic!

My jaw dropped. I held my breath as his fingers waved over the band. I had always known my dad was a man of many talents, but this was some next-level stuff! My hand went to scratch my eyes—I had to have been dreaming. But the energy pulsating from the room felt so real.

“Enphius, zenthos, almass….” he chanted under his breath, the words sounding like another language. I squinted more as he continued with his spell, the ring now levitating an inch above his palm. “With this curse, I bestow upon you a small fraction of my power,” he whispered. And then—

“Freya!” Mom called out from her room. My heart jumped as I jerked back, and then she called my name out again more harshly. “FREYA! WAKE UP!”
