No matter how much Oswald pestered Selene for more information. She never did tell him what these so-called "enemies" were in Volstarn exactly.

Every time he pressed her, she deflected with that same cryptic response: "You'll know in due time." Always offering nothing more than the barest hint of amusement at his frustration.

Eventually, Oswald gave up, at least for now. No point wasting energy on a brick wall.

The days that followed passed without incident. The wagon rolled on, carrying them toward Volstarn at a steady pace, and Oswald filled the time with whatever he could, stretching his legs whenever the wagon stopped. The monotony was dull, but at least it was peaceful.

Right now, he was asleep in the wagon. He'd been enjoying this nap for a good few hours or so. But then, he felt pressure on his shoulder.

Oswald stirred awake slowly. "Huh… are we there yet?"

"We are entering Volstarn as we speak." Selene said.

Oswald exhaled through his nose, rubbing the sleep from his eyes with the back of his hand. "Took long enough."

Out of curiosity, Oswald reached for the canvas flap and pulled it aside. A dull, gray light filtered through as he leaned forward to take a look.

This was the first city he’d laid eyes on outside of Loria. And unfortunately, Volstarn looked like it was rotting from the inside out.

Even the air smelled weird, it wasn’t the stomach-churning stench of piss and unwashed bodies that he had grown up with in the slums, but it wasn’t pleasant either. There was a sharpness to it, something stale and sour, like damp wood left to decay under a leaky roof.

Most buildings leaned with weary slants, their warped wooden beams and soot-streaked stone walls buckling under years of damp and decay. Thatched roofs sagged at the edges, dark with rot, as if the moisture in the air refused to let anything truly dry.

Farmers in patched tunics guided thin, sluggish livestock through the streets. Near a crumbling well, a woman hunched over, pressing a damp cloth to her mouth as she was wracked with violent coughs. He also saw a child whose cheeks were hollowed by hunger. She sat at her feet, picking at a crust of bread with feeble fingers.

This place is just… sad. Even the slumfolk in Loria had some life in ‘em. Oswald thought to himself.

Oswald let the canvas flap fall back into place, the fabric swaying slightly before settling. He leaned against the wagon’s wooden frame, arms crossed as his gaze swept over the town sprawled before them. “Well, this place looks real invitin’.”

Selene, as always, remained unreadable. If the crumbling town or the reek of sickness unsettled her, she gave no sign of it. “There are worse places to be.”

“I guess? But was Volstarn always this rough?”

“It has always had a certain somber charm, but this is the worst I have seen it in quite some time.”

Oswald frowned, the unease in his chest settling in a little deeper. If even she’s saying that, then this place has to be in real bad shape. “Is it safe for us to even be here? I mean, with all the sickness and everything. Doesn’t exactly look like the kind of place you’d wanna stick around.”

“We have nothing to fear. As bearers of the Animus, neither of us are susceptible to illness, unless, of course, it is of a particularly potent magical nature.” Selene explained.

“You sure? Like, sure sure?”

“Oswald, trust me. There is no need to worry.”

The boy wasn't entirely convinced, but he knew that Selene wouldn't lie about something like this. Good to know I can't get sick.

A small rustle at Oswald's side drew his attention. Vivi stirred from her spot atop a bundled cloak. She stretched, sleek black fur rippling as she arched her back, then gave a slow blink.

Oswald scratched behind her ear. “C’mon, up you go. We’re gonna be walking around soon.”

Vivi flicked her tail, then leapt onto his shoulder. She settled against the curve of his neck, her body draping across him like a living scarf. The warmth of her fur was a small comfort against the town’s dreary air.

Soon, the wagon rattled to a stop with a jarring lurch. Volstarn awaited.

“We can get off here,” Selene said as she stepped out of the wagon.

Oswald followed, landing on uneven ground that gave slightly beneath his boots. He pulled his cloak tighter, shaking off the lingering chill from the ride, and looked up just in time to see Selene passing a few coins to the driver.

The old man dipped his head. “Much obliged, Lady Whiteheart.”

Selene returned the gesture with a slight nod before turning on her heel. “Follow me, Oswald.”

Oswald pulled his cloak tighter as he trailed after Selene. The streets ahead were a labyrinth of winding paths flanked by sagging buildings.

They moved through the quieter back roads, avoiding the busier thoroughfares. The further they walked, the fewer lanterns remained.

The path opened into a small clearing where a lone inn stood, set apart from the crumbling structures surrounding it. Its wooden beams appeared old, but still sturdy. The door had been reinforced with iron, likely a precaution rather than an aesthetic choice. Above the entrance, a sign swayed gently in the breeze: The Hollow Oak.

Selene stopped just before the entrance, turning to face him. "I shall introduce you to the innkeeper shortly, but after that, we must part ways."

Oswald frowned. “Where’re you going?”

“Getting some refreshments. Meeting a few people. Then leaving Volstarn.”

“You’re leaving already?"

“For a short while, maybe a week at most. Once I’m done, I’ll meet you again here.”

Oswald flicked his gaze between the inn and Selene, already knowing he wasn’t gonna get much more out of her. She had a way of sidestepping details, giving just enough to keep him from asking too many questions, but not enough to be satisfied. No point pushin’ when she’s like this.

“Do you have enough money?” Selene asked.

“I'll be fine, and since you're leaving, shouldn’t I be asking you that?”

“Oh my, was that an attempt at sarcasm I just heard? You’re learning, Oswald.”

“I try my best,” Oswald said, flashing a grin.

“Try not to get yourself killed while I’m gone, Oswald.”

“No promises.”

She gave him one last glance before stepping into the inn. Oswald followed her inside, the heavy door swinging shut behind them with a dull thud. His eyes swept the room. Huh. Not bad.

The lobby had a low ceiling, supported by thick beams which pressed the space into a snug, if somewhat dim, enclosure. A handful of patrons sat scattered at the few tables, most nursing drinks quietly.

From behind the main counter, a man perked up, his green eyes locking onto them the moment they entered. His brown hair, streaked with faint traces of gray, was tied loosely at the nape of his neck.

For a moment, he just looked at Selene, then a wide grin split his face. "By the gods, you ain't changed a bit. All these years, and you still look like ya just walked outta some fancy portrait."

"How is your family, Gilbert?" Selene asked, walking up to the counter.

Oswald trailed behind her, watching as Gilbert wiped his hands on a rag, shaking his head with a short chuckle. “Martha's healthy as ever, thank the gods. Always fussin’ over me, says I don’t rest enough, but what’s new?”

Oswald folded his arms, listening as Gilbert leaned against the counter, rubbing at his beard. “As for the young’uns, they ain't here right now. Sent ‘em off to Loria to stay with my brother ‘til all this sickness clears. Figured they’d be safer there. Ain't easy bein’ apart, but better that than watchin’ ‘em fall ill. Town’s no place for ‘em right now.”

Selene tapped her fingers against the countertop. “Judging by the state of the city… business must be slow these days.”

Gilbert let out a long breath, rubbing a hand over his face. “Dryin’ up fast. Folk barely got enough coin for food, let alone a warm bed. Ain’t right, watchin’ good people waste away like this. Some of ‘em ain’t gonna last the season.”

Selene didn’t respond right away. Instead, her fingers dipped into a small pouch at her waist. She slid a handful of gold coins across the counter. The weight of them hit the wood with a solid clink, the sound cutting through the low murmur of the inn. A few heads turned, looking at the glint of coin before looking away just as quickly.

Gilbert blinked a few times. “Now, now, you can’t just be throwin’ money ‘round like that,”

Selene rested a hand on the counter, barely touching the coins. “It’s payment for my disciple here. His name's Oswald, and I’d like you to take good care of him.”

The boy raised a brow, glancing between them. That’s a whole lot of coin for a place like this. Guess Selene trusts this Gilbert guy a lot.

Gilbert’s gaze flicked to Oswald, running over him with a quick glance before a soft chuckle rumbled from his chest. “You’re tellin’ me this boy is your student? Thought you worked alone.”

“I usually do, but I've since changed my mind.” Selene said.

Gilbert hummed, then turned to Oswald. "Well, boy, if you're under Selene’s wing, you must be somethin’ special. I'd’ve put ya up for free, you know."

Selene shook her head, tapping the gold with two fingers. "Take it. If nothing else, use it to stock up on what you need. I know you’ll put it to good use."

With a small, resigned chuckle, Gilbert pulled the gold into his palm. "Thanks, you ain't gotta keep helpin’ folks like me, but the world’s better off 'cause ya do."

"I just do what I can."

Gilbert reached under the counter, brushing over a row of hanging keys until he found the right one. The dull brass caught the flickering candlelight, glinting for a brief moment before he plucked it free.

Stepping out from behind the counter, he gave the key a quick twirl around his finger. “Alright, boy, let’s get you settled."

Oswald followed Gilbert. However, Selene took a step back as she addressed the innkeeper. “Sadly, this is where we must part ways."

“Ya know, Selene, ya could always stay the night. I’d even throw in breakfast on the house.”

“Tempting, but I’ll have to decline.”

"Yeah, figured as much. Ain’t like you to sit still for long. Always movin’, always got somethin’ to do.”

She dipped her head slightly. “Regardless, thank you for the hospitality, Gilbert.”

“Anytime, you watch yourself out there.”

After giving a curt nod, Selene walked to the doors. Guess that’s that, then.

Gilbert turned and jerked his thumb towards the stairs. “C’mon. Room ain’t far.”

Oswald fell into step behind him as they climbed the wooden staircase, lit by small wall-mounted lanterns. Reaching the end of the hall, Gilbert stopped in front of a door and pushed it open with a nudge of his shoulder.

The hinges groaned softly as the room was revealed. It had a simple wooden bed, a small desk pressed against the far wall, and a single chair tucked beneath it. The mattress looked worn but clean, and a thin woolen blanket had been folded neatly at the foot of the bed. A lone window, half-covered by heavy curtains, let in a sliver of the dreary Volstarn sky.

"Ain’t much, but it’s a roof over your head," Gilbert said, stepping aside to let Oswald through.

Oswald took a quick look around, then shrugged. "It’s good enough."

Gilbert gave a satisfied nod, but his gaze drifted to Oswald’s shoulder, where Vivi still perched. The innkeeper didn't seem displeased at the cat, but neither did he look happy about seeing it.

"One thing, though. Your cat’s gotta stay down in the lobby. Don’t want her scratchin’ up the furniture."

"Got it, then could you also look after her while I'm staging here? I need to leave for a bit soon."

"Sure, that's no problem."

Oswald looked at Vivi, tilting his head slightly. "You good with that?"

Vivi gave him a slow blink before hopping down from his shoulder, landing gracefully in his arms. She nestled against his chest for a moment before giving a small nod.

Oswald huffed, then turned toward Gilbert. "Guess that’s settled."

He handed Vivi over, carefully adjusting his grip so she wouldn’t slip. "Her name’s Vivi, by the way."

Gilbert accepted the cat, his rough hands surprisingly gentle as he cradled her. "Well, Vivi, it’s nice to make your acquaintance," he said with a grin, giving her a slow stroke along her back.

Vivi let out a quiet purr, settling in his arms like she belonged there. Oswald watched the exchange, then smirked. Guess she don’t mind him much, that's good.

"Anyway, think I’d like some time to myself." Oswald said.

“Fair enough. I’ll be down in the lobby if ya need anything.” Gilbert’s voice carried its usual warmth, but there was an edge to it—a weight, maybe, that hadn’t been there before. He didn’t wait for a reply, just gave a nod and stepped out, the door clicking shut behind him.

Oswald sat down on the bed, the mattress dipping slightly beneath him. He let out a breath, leaning forward with his elbows on his knees as his gaze wandered to the window.

The glass was smudged, streaked from old rain, and dust gathered in the corners of the wooden frame. Beyond it, Volstarn stretched out beneath a heavy, overcast sky. The whole town looked lifeless. The town's worse off than I thought…

Sickness clung to this place, sinking into the bones of the people like a weight they couldn’t shake. Not a single person I’ve seen looks like a threat. So what was Selene talking about when she said there’d be enemies here?

His brows furrowed as he leaned back, running a hand through his hair. She probably knows something don’t. Wouldn’t be the first time. But then, why not just tell him straight? Instead, she left him with half an answer.

Not my problem anyway. I should just go find that doctor tomorrow morning and start finishing my quest.
