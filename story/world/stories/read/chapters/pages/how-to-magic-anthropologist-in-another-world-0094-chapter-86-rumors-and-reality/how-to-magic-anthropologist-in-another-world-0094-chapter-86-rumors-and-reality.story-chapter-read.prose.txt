"The myth is growing because every genuine act of kindness performed by Miss Alice is now being absorbed into an already established narrative."

Silence settled over the amphitheater as everyone tried to process what those words actually meant.

My own heart felt ready to burst.

Was I understanding this correctly?

Was he actually saying the rumor itself was being powered by Belief?

I had often wondered whether the Mythic Realm's magic was powered by Faith or by Belief.

They sounded similar, but from how I understood them, they were completely different concepts.

Faith required a relationship. Someone devoted themselves to a higher being, offered prayers, rituals, or conviction, and in return received miracles.

Vena's magic fit that model almost perfectly. She worshipped the Holy, an abstract ideal rather than a person, and in exchange received healing magic.

Belief, on the other hand, simply accumulated. It created monsters in forgotten forests because enough people feared that something lurked beneath the trees. It created river nymphs because every village had stories about beautiful women appearing beside the water. And it created mermaids because lonely sailors were horny.

Perhaps it had even created the gods themselves when people needed a frame of reference to understand the world around them.

Reality adapted to meet those expectations.

Now... it was trying to create a Saint simply because it helped explain a weird girl with mysterious knowledge.

A story had formed around me, and now reality itself was slowly beginning to reinforce it.

The white-winged speaker folded his hands behind his back.

"There are essentially two responsible courses of action we, as bards, can take." He slowly looked around the audience, "The first is to actively correct the misinformation wherever possible. Explain the misunderstanding. Clarify the facts. And allow the myth to collapse naturally as fewer people believe it."

He paused.

"The second option is simply to observe."

Confused murmurs spread throughout the audience.

"Yes." He nodded. "Not every myth survives to completion. Some grow rapidly before disappearing within months. Others evolve into something completely different."

He smiled faintly.

"Quite often, reality eventually catches up with the story." He shrugged. "But sometimes... it doesn't."

"If a genuine Saint truly does rise in Hano one day," he continued, "that would hardly be a tragedy."

Several people nodded enthusiastically.

"It would be a blessing not only for our city, but perhaps for all Seven Realms."

I wanted to climb onto the stage and strangle him.

"There is, however, one course of action I strongly advise against." His expression became serious. "Do not intentionally manipulate the myth."

The entire amphitheater quieted.

"If you value your continued existence, do not attempt to steer the narrative toward a desired outcome."

Several paddles immediately rose, but the moderator motioned for patience.

"The Holy Lady takes a particularly dim view of those who deliberately manufacture sacred myths for personal gain."

Uneasy whispers spread throughout the audience.

"She has intervened before." He allowed the words to settle. "And she will intervene again."

That honestly frightened me. I had experienced Goddess Damada's presence. Simply standing near her had felt like trying to stare directly into the sun.

If the Holy Lady possessed even half that power... no. Somehow, I suspected she was even stronger.

"The Lady is fair," he continued. "She is both Paladin and Inquisitor."

He smiled.

"And just like all Inquisitors, she always knows the truth."

Several people instinctively straightened their backs.

"The people most at risk are those who knowingly exploit Belief for political influence, wealth, authority, or the pursuit of divine power."

He slowly surveyed the room.

"So regardless of your intentions, do not insert yourself into the myth."

He pointed toward the audience.

"You may repeat stories. You may investigate them. You may even embellish them in a song for dramatic purposes."

His voice hardened.

"But never attempt to make yourself their hero."

My blood turned cold. What if I was already inside the story through no fault of my own? Do I just do nothing and allow strangers to turn my life into a prophecy?

The thought made me feel sick.

I grabbed Nina's hand.

"We need to leave."

She took one look at my face and immediately understood.

Without saying a word, she blinked.

The theater dissolved into drifting fragments of dream before reforming into the quiet preset tavern.

Only the two of us remained.

I immediately stood and began pacing between the empty tables.

"This is bad."

Nina leaned against one of the chairs.

"It is."

"It's worse than bad." I ran both hands through my hair. "It's horrifying."

She waited.

"What if I'm trapped in a self-reinforcing belief loop?"

She frowned.

"What do you mean?"

"The more people believe the story..." I pointed toward where the theater had been. "The more reality subtly nudges me toward becoming the person they're expecting me to be."

She remained silent.

"When I started randomly teleporting to save people, was that entirely my idea?"

"What if I've started making slightly different decisions? What if every choice I make from now on is a little more aligned with the narrative?"

"Until one day... I'm no longer Alice. I'm just... the Saint."

"I doubt this rumor can interfere with free will," Nina said, trying to sound reassuring.

"Maybe not." I swallowed. "But it could be something subtler than that."

"We could have stayed in the Dead Zone for another week, but we decided to go home early, just in time to see the Twenty Man Army slaughter the mercenaries."

"That was exactly the emotional push the narrative needed to make me behave more like a Saint.” I felt my breathing quicken. “Was that just coincidence, or fate?"

"Alice."

"What if I'm already changing?"

"Alice."

"What if I become nothing more than a husk acting out someone else's prophecy?"

"Alice!"

I barely heard her.

I kept pacing. As I took my next step, the world shifted, and I lost my footing as everything around me disappeared.

One heartbeat I was standing in the tavern.

The next, I was surrounded by water.

I was at the bottom of a lake, sunlight breaking through the water in twisted golden rays. I gasped in shock, only to notice that I could breathe.

Right... I was inside a dream.

I turned around only to notice people floating weightlessly through an enormous underwater amphitheater while schools of brightly colored fish drifted lazily overhead.

At the center, two mermaids performed upon a sand stage.

One beat a broad steel drum with open palms while the other struck polished bars of coral like a xylophone. The music they produced was sharp and resonant, notes that echoed like whale songs across the water.

Their long hair floated around them like drifting seaweed. Their tails swayed gently in time with the music. And neither of them wore anything above the waist.

For nearly a full minute, I completely forgot why I had been panicking.

I simply enjoyed listening to music that was unlike anything I had ever heard, something beautiful precisely because it was completely alien.

Nina held my hand once more, then she blinked again, and the underwater concert vanished.

The quiet tavern returned.

I expected to have wet clothes, only to remember that dream logic was still in effect.

Nina looked at me with a small smile.

"Feeling better?"

"Yes." I let out a long breath. "I think I was spiraling."

"You were." She grinned. "But apparently nothing a pair of mermaid titties can't fix."

I couldn't help laughing.

"I was honestly more distracted by the fact that there's an entire underwater musical tradition."

"But the titties were nice too, I suppose." I let out a small chuckle.

"So, now that you're calmer, what do you plan on doing regarding the whole Saint situation?"

"I don't know..." I said as I sat down and looked at the ceiling. "A lot of people inside the Great Dream know the rumor is probably false. Maybe it will fizzle out on its own."

"The bards in that discussion room are only a minority of the dreamers in Hano," she said, shaking her head. "And most people spreading the rumor aren't Dreamers."

"Dreamers are a tiny minority in Hano, Mythic communities are always more numerous than us, except in the Dreaming Realm," she continued. "They have a much higher birth rate as a built-in feature of most of their faiths, while we Dreamers get carried away with our obsessions so often that we sometimes forget to breed."

"And at this point, the rumor is already spreading outside the Great Dream," I sighed. "I met a kid in a remote village who had already heard about the new Saint."

"Yeah." Nina shrugged. "Bards, pilgrims, traveling merchants, Holy Messengers, and families writing letters are just a few of the ways a rumor can spread outside the Great Dream."

I sank even farther into my chair.

"So how exactly am I supposed to prove I'm not a Saint?" I asked. "I'm not exactly willing to start kicking puppies."

"I'm sure that would backfire somehow," Nina laughed. "People would probably invent a reason why you did it."

"They'd say the puppy was secretly possessed," she suggested. "Or maybe kicking it somehow saved its life."

I slowly buried my face in my hands.

"The more famous the myth becomes," Nina continued, "the harder it becomes to disprove because people don't like abandoning stories they're already invested in."

I let out a long groan.

"So what am I supposed to do?"

"You could redirect it toward Vena. She'd actually make a believable Saint."

"Doesn't pushing the myth onto someone else sound exactly like the sort of manipulation that white-winged guy warned everyone about? I don't want to end up on a goddess's shit list."

"I honestly don't know." She hesitated. "After all, you didn't create the rumor, nor are you trying to gain power from it."

"My guess," she said, fluttering her wings, "is that you'd probably be fine."

"Probably?"

"I don't make a habit of guessing the actions of demigoddesses."

"That is fair, I guess." I sighed once more. "I really hate Jaime right now."

"The poor guy didn't mean for all this to happen," Nina laughed.

"I am still going to murder him," I grumbled before standing up again. "But for now, let's head back."

"Head back?"

"Yes, let's head back and do some damage control." I nodded seriously. "I'm going to need you to raise your paddle."

"Me?"

"Yes."

"But wouldn't that make me a target of the Lady?" she asked.

"No, because you're only going to tell the truth."

We returned to the amphitheater in the middle of the discussion. The white-winged man had already returned to his seat while another volunteer enthusiastically recounted yet another sighting of me.

"...and then she simply appeared beside him," the man explained, acting the entire scene out with exaggerated gestures. "The poor fellow had a piece of meat lodged in his throat. Before anyone could react, she hugged him from behind, forced the obstruction free, then disappeared before the man even managed to thank her."

Several people quietly wrote notes.

The moderator nodded.

"Do we have anyone who can verify this account?"

Three paddles rose.

"Firsthand witnesses?" the moderator asked.

The three Dreamers lowered their paddles and shook their heads.

"Secondhand direct testimony?"

One paddle rose again.

The moderator nodded and wrote something in his notebook.

The man sat down, immediately replaced by another speaker.

This one described an increasingly ridiculous version of the story where I had rescued a young couple who had been skinny-dipping in the river. The mossbear had somehow become a pack of terror birds, and apparently I had teleported them all to safety after killing the beasts, which was funny because doing something like that would have been nearly impossible for me outside the Sunless Reach.

I slowly rubbed my forehead.

"That's not what happened."

"I figured as much," Nina smiled.

Using other testimony, the moderator managed to separate the obvious embellishments by restoring the mossbear to the story, but he still overestimated my teleportation ability.

Another person somehow connected me to Goddess Damada's manifestation.

That one confused me more than anything.

"I was there," the speaker insisted confidently. "The Goddess only appeared because of Alice."

"Technically true," I muttered. But the fact that he had arrived at that conclusion, while Vena had been the one standing front and center during that entire incident, was baffling to me.

The Holy girl had walked directly past a Goddess without even bowing after arguing with her High Priest, then calmly started healing the sick woman herself.

Yet somehow, the man still managed to make the story about me.

"I saw her comforting the child who summoned the Goddess," he explained. "She must have instructed him how to do it. She was the only one present there who was born in the Mythic Realm, after all."

The audience accepted that explanation with thoughtful nods.

Between each testimony, Nina quietly raised her numbered paddle, patiently waiting for her turn to be called.

Finally, after a young kitsune alchemist finished recounting how he had sold me a fever remedy shortly before hearing rumors that I had miraculously cured a dying woman with that very same potion later that night, the moderator finally pointed at Nina.

"Number Four Hundred Twenty-two."

Nina stood.

"I am Nina Rockfeather," she said. "I'm an inventor from Hano."

Several heads immediately turned toward her.

"I also know Alice Hecate personally." She hesitated only briefly. "I designed and created her soulbound spear."

A ripple spread across the amphitheater.

The moderator blinked.

"Would you be willing to join me on stage?"

Nina visibly swallowed before nodding.

"Yes."

With a single beat of her wings, she floated gently down onto the stage.

She looked noticeably uncomfortable standing before several hundred people. Even so, she was willing to do this for me. She really was a true friend.

The moderator looked across the audience.

"Does anyone present recognize Miss Nina Rockfeather?"

Nearly a dozen paddles rose.

He pointed toward one.

"Number Eighty-three."

A middle-aged Naga stood.

"I know her by reputation."

Another paddle rose.

"Number Thirty-one."

"So do I."

More followed.

"She's a respected artificer."

One after another, Dreamers vouched for her.

"She doesn't usually participate in public discussions," someone laughed. "She spends too much time hiding in her workshop."

Finally, a middle-aged man spoke.

"The fact that Miss Rockfeather came here voluntarily tells me she genuinely cares about Miss Alice."

"Thank you, Joji," Nina said with a small smile.

The moderator smiled.

"Then perhaps you could summarize your experiences with Miss Alice."

Nina took a slow breath.

"I first met Alice shortly after she arrived in Hano."

"At the time, I was completing a commission for the Temple." She smiled apologetically. "Unfortunately, the details remain confidential under the terms of my contract."

"She was referred to me through that same project." Nina looked down for a second before continuing. "From the moment we started talking..." She smiled to herself. "I knew she was going to become my Muse."

Several Dreamers leaned forward, as Muses were an important aspect of Dreamer culture. It usually referred to someone or something that generated a great deal of Inspiration.

"She kept asking questions nobody else had ever asked," Nina explained with a soft smile. "She approached problems from angles I had never considered."

"She commissioned a bident specifically designed for Lightning magic." Nina's wings fluttered slightly. "I invited her to my Dream Sanctuary to design it."

"While building it..." She smiled. "I entered a Creation State."

That caused a considerably larger reaction than anything else she had said. Dozens of Dreamers immediately started whispering. That too was a celebrated achievement in Dreamer culture, as the Creation State was known for producing incredible artifacts that couldn't be easily replicated.

The moderator raised an eyebrow.

"Was that your first time entering the Creation State?"

"No." Nina shook her head. "My first was when I crafted Songstress Riri's mandolin."

Several appreciative murmurs spread throughout the audience. Apparently, that instrument was famous enough to need no introduction.

"Since then," Nina continued, "we've entered a business venture together."

"With the help of Lady Tan Je'e, of course." She nodded toward another part of the audience. "We're currently producing the spyglasses that have recently entered the market."

Several paddles immediately rose again.

The moderator looked surprised.

"The new optical devices?"

"The very same." She smiled proudly. "It's a tool that reproduces the effects of the Eagle Eyes Soulbook without magic."

"So," the moderator summarized, "Miss Alice contributed new medical knowledge to the Temple." He paused. "And optical knowledge to you."

"Do you happen to know where she acquired all this information?" he asked.

I held my breath. This was why I had asked Nina to raise her paddle in the first place.

"Both pieces of knowledge are related," she explained. "I can't go into further detail because of my contract with the Temple, but the medical knowledge is optically related."

The moderator blinked, but Nina continued.

"Alice is obsessed with the Ancient Telepaths. They're basically her favorite historical civilization, and she spends an unreasonable amount of time studying them."

"I've seen it myself." Nina shrugged. "She even teleported me to see one of their ancient devices."

"The construction techniques resembled the same ideas we used while designing the spyglass."

The moderator looked thoughtful.

"So your opinion is that her mysterious knowledge originated from archaeological discoveries?"

"We all know that the Ancient Telepaths were more technologically advanced than the rest of the Seven Realms." She smiled awkwardly.

That was a perfect delivery. With it, no one would assume that my mysterious knowledge was something unattainable. I was just a girl who had gotten lucky once while sifting through the rubble of an ancient civilization. There was no need to vivisect me to uncover my secrets.

The moderator nodded.

"Very well."

He turned toward the audience.

"If anyone has questions for Miss Rockfeather, please raise your paddles."

Nearly twenty immediately rose.

The moderator pointed toward the familiar blue-scaled scholar.

"Number Twenty-five."

The man stood.

"Do you know where Miss Alice lived before arriving in Hano?"

Nina nodded.

"According to her, she was in a small village when she accidentally teleported for the first time and arrived here in Hano."

Technically true. By Earth's standards, Sidi Bou Said was only a small village of six thousand people.

The blue-scaled man slowly nodded.

"Which would explain why nobody had previous records of a teleporter in the Mythic Realm."

He quietly wrote something down before sitting.

"Number Two."

The woman adjusted her glasses.

"Would you describe Miss Alice as fully Mythic, or is she part Kindred?"

Nina thought about it.

"No."

A surprised murmur spread through the audience.

"She's definitely mixed."

"I don't know whether she has Kindred ancestry, but she certainly has enough Dreamer blood to possess a tiny Dream Sanctuary."

She smiled.

"Not enough to access the Great Dream, though."

That explanation sounded perfectly reasonable. By confirming a mixed Dreamer ancestry, it further cemented my existence within the Seven Realms, making it less likely for people to guess I was an outsider.

"Number Six."

The broad-shouldered Guild gossip stood once more.

"Is Miss Alice romantically involved with Sergeant Raik Agame, Takur Agame..." He looked down at his notes. "Or maybe Sergeant Nakera?"

"No."

Nina answered without hesitation.

The man looked disappointed for a second before narrowing his eyes at Nina.

"Then are you romantically involved with Miss Alice?"

The entire theater suddenly became very quiet.

Nina froze, and her cheeks immediately turned bright red.

"No..." The poor girl looked absolutely mortified.

"I believe..." She cleared her throat. "I've answered enough questions."

Without waiting for permission, she flew back into the audience and dropped into the seat beside me.

She stared determinedly at the stage without looking in my direction, probably to avoid drawing attention to me.

The discussion continued for nearly another hour.

People reported sightings of me from Hano, Verraden, and the lake town, the fishing settlement ruled by one of Nada's relatives from the Lore clan.

Some accounts were accurate enough that I remembered them clearly.

Others described places I had never visited, people I had never met, and heroic deeds I had certainly never performed.

With every retelling, Alice Hecate became a little less like me and a little more like the woman everyone expected her to be.

Finally, after another improbable story involving Flight Magic I didn't possess and children I had never saved, the moderator raised a hand.

"I believe," he said, "we have discussed Miss Alice quite thoroughly."

The conversation gradually shifted away from me and toward the next member of the team, San Ja'a.

Compared to the circus that had surrounded my own presentation, her report felt almost relaxing.

The moderator summarized her background as an experienced merchant from the Bloodline Realm, noted her Soulseer abilities, and listed several members of her unusually influential family.

Most of the discussion centered around Lady Tan Je'e, whose history as a jeweler and recent investments in Hano had already made her a recognizable figure throughout the Great Dream.

He mentioned Ja'a's father, one of the wealthiest merchants in the Bloodline Realm.

"He is also famous," another Dreamer added with a laugh, "for failing to produce a single son despite having seventeen daughters."

A ripple of amused laughter spread through the audience.

Ja'a did, in fact, have sixteen sisters. She called all of them sisters, but technically most were only half-sisters, as her father practiced polygamy like most Souldealers.

The discussion briefly wandered through the family's accomplishments.

One sister, who was half Dreamer, had become a well-known bard.

Another managed a merchant caravan that regularly crossed four different realms, transporting goods between the Bloodline Realm and the Mythic Realm.

A third had married into a prestigious Soulscribe family in Sky Daisy.

Compared to the rest of her family, Ja'a almost sounded ordinary.

She was Raik Agame's merchant friend, a Soulseer, and a contract negotiator.

As a Freelancer with very little actual combat experience, her contribution to the team was her senses, logistics, and ability to find opportunities everyone else overlooked.

After that came Katar.

His presentation was even shorter.

He was described as Raik's bodyguard, a swordsman, and an indentured servant who had entered the Agame household while both he and Raik were still children.

Nobody seemed interested in digging any deeper.

Calr and Shingo received even less attention.

Both were described simply as former members of Yon's Misfits who had likely joined Raik's challenge as placeholders, since the nature of the challenge prevented Raik from bringing elite Freelancers with him.

Shingo was described as reliable, dependable, and strong enough to be a frontline fighter, but nothing particularly remarkable for someone with Giant blood.

Calr fared even worse.

They described him as a mediocre marksman.

They had absolutely no idea about his connection to the Ancient Telepaths, nor about his eidetic memory.

Nobody realized he was probably one of the smartest people in the entire city.

I supposed this explained why Obora had underestimated him so badly. As a Dreamer, she had probably gathered her information from discussions exactly like this one.

Eventually, the moderator closed his notebook.

"I believe that concludes tonight's discussion."

People immediately began standing, forming dozens of smaller circles throughout the amphitheater as they continued debating everything they had heard.

I noticed the white-winged man who had spoken about the Saint rumors and the blue-scaled Mythic expert slowly turning in our direction.

They probably wanted to talk to Nina some more.

Nina didn't wait to find out.

She blinked once more, making the amphitheater disappear, and we found ourselves back inside the empty preset tavern.

For a few moments, neither of us spoke.

"Well..." Nina finally sighed. "We were only supposed to waste a few hours until Riri fell asleep so we could invite her into your dream."

She laughed weakly.

"Instead, we accidentally attended your public investigation."

"I've definitely had quieter evenings."

She smiled.

"Do you think they bought the story about your obsession with the Ancient Telepaths?"

"They will once they hear I'm claiming one of their ruins as my home."

I leaned back in my chair.

"If everyone starts looking for knowledge in lost ruins instead of looking at me, I'll consider that a victory."

"I think it was a clever seed to plant."

"It has a lot of room to grow, especially when Ko'i Outspring and Sara decipher the Telepath language."

"Okay then." Nina stretched her wings. "Before we meet Riri, let's go relax."

"Oh?"

"There's a famous Naga dancer who performs at this hour." She wiggled her eyebrows. "Watching her perform puts people into an almost blissful trance."

I stood.

"That actually sounds nice." I stood up.

"It is." Nina smiled. "Come on."

She offered me her hand, and as I took it, she blinked us toward our next destination.
