How long does it take to Advance?

Assuming you are ready, that there is nothing to delay you further, it will usually take a matter of minutes, maybe a couple hours at most. There is no clear way to accelerate the process, nor to delay it once begun.

Passing the Wall is another matter. A rapid advancement will leave you missing half a day. Most will be done in twenty-four to thirty-six hours. The slowest are still typically done within two days.

If any Seer or Healer knows why it takes so much longer, why you remain completely unaware and vulnerable for so long, they have never said, only suggested the massive changes between level 9 and 10 require it.

Fay saw a message notification pop up on her feed. She had just enough time to notice the sender before Sandra bounced through the door, followed almost immediately by Sam. She'd have to look at it later, talk to Bri about it, if only to talk about her past.

"Fay!"

"Have a seat," Fay said. "Both of you."

"Auntie said you'd be able to explain things," Sam said as he took a seat. "Why the word that actually resonated best was Ef-"

"No," Fay said quickly, cutting him off. "You do not need to share your Affinity. You should not share it, except with people you trust completely."

"And with a Seer if they are advising you on Path Selections," she continued before they could ask what Affinity meant. "But that to ensure you don't overlook a choice, and because the Selection process will display it regardless."

"Affinity?" Sandra asked.

"The actual term for what you were trying to identify," Fay said. "Or rather that you were picking. You can advance with an Affinity that isn't your best choice, or that is actively harmful to your long term progress."

"What is it?" Sam asked.

"An influence on your Energy, on your Techniques, on everything that makes you Awakened."

"So when things froze around Mother…"

"Yes. It was tied to her Affinity."

"And made me more aware of them in general."

"We don't have the same advantages Combat Pathers do. Not exactly," Fay said. "Awakened are taught to work on suppressing the effect of their Affinity and Advancement around those who are weaker, especially those who haven't passed the Wall. We get less chance to feel other Affinities, to feel how they shape and influence Energy."

"Depthsborn don't suppress?" Sam guessed.

"They don't. Prior to level 10 not all of them will have one, and some will be completely useless for them. A slime with an Affinity for Organization will have minimal advantages. A Goblin in a group will be able to coordinate them much better."

"They still have to recognize what is happening, and then make a connection to themselves," Sandra said. "If they can't see how it relates to them they get stuck, they get…"

"Yes," Fay said softly. "And if they don't like what it means or implies it can be challenging. In some cases a less well matched Affinity might have been easier to handle."

"How did Auntie know I had the wrong choice?" Sam asked.

Fay turned to look at him. She couldn't tell what his Affinity was, at least not until she saw his Selections, but she'd heard Sandra talk about him enough to make a few guesses based on what he had almost said.

"I don't know. Not precisely. When you did figure it out, did you have any doubts? Did you hesitate to advance?"

"No..? I knew it fit, I knew I could use it. I didn't speak to her, I went ahead."

"And the other times, you asked her because you weren't so certain?"

"I felt a resonance, she told me I'd recognize when I found the right term. But thinking back, it wasn't nearly the same."

"You weren't sure. That was probably her reasoning."

"Did you know I would figure it out so quickly?" Sandra asked.

"No," Fay said. "I expected you to think about it for a bit then come join us. If I'd thought you were that close I would have had you wait until you were in a proper chamber, somewhere you wouldn't risk bouncing your head off the bed frame."

"I missed the entire visit."

"Is that all you care about?" Sam said. "That you got to take a vacation and then missed the entire thing?"

"It was not a vacation," Sandra answered. "It was a reunion with my sister and her Partner."

"We still had work to do, you know Yshe expects things done by a specific date whether or not you're helping."

"Helping?" Sandra said, pushing out of her chair. "Helping? I actually get things done, I actually squeeze through the crawlspaces that you have less trouble fitting into. I came back to next to nothing having changed."

"Not my fault you-"

"Enough," Fay said with a sigh. "I agreed to talk to both of you at the same time so I didn't have to go over this twice in a row. Which was apparently a mistake, especially since you can't both be making your Selections at the same time unless you want the other to see your choices."

"No!"

"Why would I want that?" Sam asked.

"Sandra? Would you go find Bri and ask her for some coffee? Maybe some lunch? I have something to talk to her about after you two are done."

"Can't Sam?"

"He'll be going over his choices, you will when you get back."

Fay stared at the unread message in her feed. She knew she should read it, she knew she was going to read it, but Bri should be arriving soon. She could wait.

"You don't normally ask me to join you for lunch."

"Bri," Fay said.

She stood and crossed her office, pulling her fiancee into a hug while taking one of the coffees.

"What's wrong?"

"Deirdre sent a message."

"Who?" Briana asked.

"The new Seer from Scattered Creek."

"The one you recognized right as we were leaving?"

"Yes."

"What does it say?"

"I don't know," Fay admitted. "I haven't read it yet."

She walked back over to her desk, pulling one of the chairs around so they could sit side by side.

"Why not?"

Fay paused, coffee halfway to her lips. Why hadn't she simply read the message? Why was she so… so… so tense at having the name pop up. She hadn't thought of Deirdre in years, hadn't seen her in even longer. They'd spoken a handful of times, mostly while working on overlapping research.

"We were… friends? Maybe?" she began. "She's about the same age as me but not nearly as driven, I wouldn't be surprised if she only just hit level 25."

"That's still fast," Bri said, each word drawn out a bit more than the last.

"Some of the work I did for Valene, some of the work he sent me from other OverSeers had us looking in to the same things at the same time. She asked my opinion a few times, for help finding some details that just wouldn't show up."

"That makes sense."

"Once or twice we just talked. Not about work, not about anything in particular. She's an excellent researcher, and from what I saw of her record she's a skilled teacher too."

Fay wasn't sure what she expected. She wasn't even sure why she was explaining any of this. Bri's laughter, soft and full of warmth, nearly set her to panicking before she recognized the tone and felt herself start to blush.

"Fay," Bri said. "You're saying you had a friend you talked to about things that weren't work related. That you enjoyed spending time with. That you put the effort into finding out extra details about that were probably none of your business?"

"Yes..?"

"Did you have a crush on her?"

"I don't think so."

Had she? Thinking about her disastrous dates, about how things had not worked out time and time again, about how different Bri was, even how different Anya was, she realized she had her answer.

"No. At least not at the time. I started spending more time researching things that needed special access and we stopped crossing paths. If we hadn't? Maybe?"

"I love you," Bri said. "You're allowed to have friends. You're even allowed to have had crushes or relationships before we met."

Fay felt herself start to smile as she considered the words. Of course she was allowed. She'd turned nothing into something for no reason. She knew Bri had exes, they'd talked about it once or twice when something sparked a memory. So why had she-

"I love you too," she said. "I got so surprised seeing her name pop up that I…"

"I get it," Bri said. "Memories and surprises can be weird. So should we see what the message says?"

Fay nodded and pulled the message up.

Seer Phelann,

I thought I recognized your name in the records for Holding Syl. Your name is still, to my knowledge, unique among the Seers of the Council, although I do suppose you aren't one any longer.

Holding Syl was not the next group I intended to research more deeply but seeing you leaving the grounds made me realize they might be an easier task than I had anticipated.

To clarify. I am reviewing information on all the combat teams to customize their curricula. The prior Seer had a clear agenda in mind that would have penalized certain groups. Knowing that you are their Seer removes many of my concerns, if they don't know something I'm certain you'll be able to help explain things to them.

Their new team members, did you know that such a change has no recorded occurrence at Scattered Creek and that I could only find two others across all the Halls, I went looking. Are you able to share any insights as to what they might already know? Their background does limit my ability to find information, let alone reach out to anyone for answers. If you know, or can vouch for their future learning, it would be ever so helpful.

Seer Deirdre

P.S. Did I actually see you hugging them? And smiling?

P.P.S. Who were you holding hands with when you left, she's cute.

"No…" Fay groaned.

"Oh…" Bri said, gasping for air as she laughed. "That… she…"

Fay reread the last few words of the message for the third time. They didn't change. Why didn't they change.
