The Demon hadn’t been idle while Rowan dismantled its ally. Its form had shifted, the muscles in its legs now coiled tighter while its torso was leaner. All around, its features were sharper, more streamlined.

It’s going for speed, Rowan thought, whirling his whips to the front while the Wind blew at his back.

A Changeling was capable of shifting its stats through a body transformation skill, making the creatures deadly opponents when given time to adapt. Something this one definitely had.

The Demon crouched low, throwing itself to the side just as Rowan’s spells lashed through the air. Instead of five long claws, a single narrow blade grew along both of its forearms, the edges gleaming under the light of his flames.

Rowan flared [Iron Will] in preparation, knowing he’d need his thoughts focused for the battle ahead.

It’s a mage killer. He narrowed his eyes. Don’t let it get close, and watch for movement techniques.

A muscle twitched, and the Demon blurred forward.

Rowan kept moving, guiding the Wind to send him sideways, lashing all three of his whips at the rapidly approaching creature.

The Demon ducked under the first, whirled around the second, and deflected the third with a swipe of its bladed arm. Through its defense, his opponent's momentum never faltered—like a dancer following a choreography. But even still, Rowan managed to keep his distance.

His whips flared, growing stronger, faster, more durable. It was almost instinctual at this point. There was no need to tweak the circuit or modify his Intent, all Rowan needed was to think it, and his mana answered.

But his opponent wasn’t weak.

From what he’d seen so far it was close to evolving to a Great Demon—the same as the one currently hiding somewhere in this Rift. Unlike the hound or the spider, it showed an above-average intelligence. Not only had it taken its time to adapt, but it had obviously led the former trio.

Each whip was either dodged or deflected, with the former becoming rarer as their fight progressed.

Rowan didn’t let down his guard, but he felt increasingly confident that this fight wouldn’t be a challenge to overcome. In fact, he was thinking about using it to train.

It wasn’t often he got an opportunity like this, and while the responsible part of his brain told him to just end it, he didn’t really want to.

A wide smile stretched across Rowan’s face as he directed [Tailwind] to push him to the left, all the while responding to the Demon’s rapid movements and adjusting his whips.

It took a sharp mind and a focused hand to keep it all flowing, something Rowan had in abundance.

Mana moved through his channels at a rapid pace, keeping his spells manifested. His body moved in kind, knees bending and arms shifting as he blitzed across the stone floor.

On his own, Rowan wasn’t faster than the Demon, but each step he took moved him three. An updraft blew against his back, his feet lifting as the creature lunged.

He answered its attack with one of his own.

On and on it went, with both of them jockeying for position and attempting to overwhelm the other. Rowan slowly built his advantage, his spells forcing the Demon to peel away.

The creature snarled, a fang peeking from behind its thin lips.

Suddenly, its body pulsed, angry red veins appearing all along its legs.

Rowan’s eyes widened.

With a wet, sickening tear, two elongated bones punched through the Demon’s palms, slick with putrid blood.

Definitely don’t want that touching me.

The air hissed as the blood coating the crude weapons began to eat away at them, releasing a caustic haze that shimmered in the dim light. The scent of burning rot clawed at Rowan’s nostrils—whatever taint ran through the Demon’s veins, it was designed to kill slowly, and painfully.

He pulled his shield forward just as the creature lunged, all three of his whips moving to intercept it.

But now, his opponent had four blades to deflect with, and it showed.

With an almost frightening agility, the Demon blurred toward him, the ground beneath it cracking from the force of its strides.

Rowan tried to slash a whip across its side, but it easily ducked under, a bladed forearm shielding it from damage.

The Demon shifted its grip, holding the two bones like you would a sword just in time to deflect a second whip coming from above.

Rowan narrowed his focus, a bead of sweat dripping from his brow.

His third whip lashed at the creature like a coiled serpent, growing both longer and brighter as he poured more mana into it.

The Demon’s eyes widened, its knees bending half a heartbeat later. With a leap, it soared straight over his attack.

Rowan hesitated as he saw his opponent descend, the bone blades pointed straight at his head. He grimaced, the responsible part of his brain finally pulling through.

Wind mana burst from his Core, rapidly tracing a path through his channels.

Just as the Demon pulled back its arm to thrust at Rowan's throat, a [Gust] appeared in his palm, tightly bound and eager to be unleashed. He let the spell go, watching as the vortex of Wind impacted his opponent in the chest.

A pained grunt escaped the Demon’s gaunt lips as Rowan’s spell connected. It launched it into the air, its body impacting the stone ceiling with a dull thud, loose stones and dust falling from above.

He held it there for a moment before letting his spell drop, repositioning to create some space, effectively resetting their fight.

The Demon whirled as it fell, landing on its feet, eyeing him with a wary, uncertain expression. It knew Rowan could have ended their battle right then and there, and it knew he chose not to. It took a hesitant step back, an ugly snarl splitting its face.

Rowan grinned.

“Come on, don’t shy away now,” he taunted. “We were just starting to have fun.”

His heart beat rapidly in his chest, not from fear, or exhaustion, but from the thrill still coursing through his veins. This was a monster that a group of Silver-ranks would have trouble dealing with, and here he was, toying with it.

That thought sent a shiver down his spine.

Rowan knew he shouldn’t have been surprised. He’d already seen—and felt—the changes [Storm Touched] wrought on his capabilities. But even still, there was something visceral about seeing a Demon cower before him.

Now that’s what I call progress, Rowan thought, letting [Tailwind] drop.

This wasn’t the only Demon he would need to deal with in this Rift. In fact, Rowan had a feeling that these three were the first of many. So it made sense to learn all he could while he still had the chance.

To compensate for his drop in speed, Rowan added two more whips, bringing his total to five. That number was far below what he was capable of, but practicing control was always a worthwhile effort.

Rowan’s spells moved like living serpents, following the Demon’s movements as it circled. His [Gust] had left it disoriented, wary, but its confidence quickly returned.

The veins on the Demon’s legs pulsed again, launching the creature forward.

Rowan set his stance.

They continued their dance, with his whips lashing out only to be deflected by boned blades. But with two more of them to deal with, the Demon started taking hits for every mistake it made.

A scorching gash appeared on its arm, flesh burning as Rowan’s spell tore into the limb. The creature hissed in pain, dodging back only to try a different angle.

Rowan’s focus seemed to sharpen as their fight progressed, his body and mana moving in unison to overwhelm his opponent. He kept his distance, slashing and thrusting with all five whips, changing their length to come at the Demon from behind—like a leviathan of living flames.

The creature tried to retreat, but Rowan didn’t let it. He kept the Demon encircled with his flames, forcing it to come at him head-on if it wanted to avoid losing its head.

Their fight went on for another few minutes, with Rowan focusing on maneuvering all five of his spells while his opponent grew increasingly frantic.

It’s flagging, he thought as another strike connected, burning an ugly gash across the Demon’s torso.

But that wasn’t what Rowan wanted. This was his chance to get acquainted with the enemies inhabiting this Rift. So instead, he let one of his whips drop.

Immediately, the Demon took advantage, rushing forward with a manic frenzy.

With only four spells, Rowan was forced to up his speed, in turn working on his control. That went on until he once again had an advantage, dropping another whip and repeating the process all over again.

Sweat dripped from his brow, the ache in his muscles growing as Rowan pushed himself to the limit. It may have been an artificial one, but no one ever got better by overpowering their opponents with sheer might.

At two whips, he felt his focus strain.

The Demon moved like a blur, its ragged form barely slowing despite the wounds covering its body. Each pulse of its veins sent it hurtling forward, trying to close the gap.

Rowan’s whips lashed out, carving through the air in sweeping arcs, but with only two, his ability to pressure his opponent had lessened.

The Demon adjusted, its movements growing sharper, more aggressive, more demonic. Boned blades met blazing flames, deflecting one strike, then another, before Rowan was forced to throw it back with another [Gust].

But it wasn’t enough to deter it. The Demon realized what was happening, that Rowan was using it to hone his skills, and that only made it angrier.

The Demon came again, weaving past one whip, slipping under the other, pushing Rowan’s reflexes as far as they could go. He had to stay a step ahead, reading its body language, tracking the telltale shifts in its stance. Every twitch of muscle, every shift in weight—it all exposed its next move.

Rowan’s world narrowed. There was no Rift, no distant threats—only the battle and the consequence of failure.

He adjusted again, shifting his weight, forcing his whips to move more fluidly. No wasted movements. No excess mana. Just pure precision.

The Demon darted left, and Rowan sent a whip curving around to intercept. It feinted, then bolted right, but the second whip was already there, barring its approach.

A loud, rage-filled snarl echoed off the walls.

Rowan wasn’t just reacting. He was dictating the fight, forcing his opponent to move where he wanted, to play by his rules.

Its movements turned frantic, each failed attempt to close the distance costing it another wound. A fresh gash across its leg. A burning lash over its limbs.

The acrid stench of charred flesh reached his nose, but still, the Demon persisted.

Rowan could feel his body start to strain, the burn of exertion settling in his legs. But he didn’t let up. This was what he wanted. To push himself, to refine his control, to make sure the next time he fought a creature like this, it wouldn’t even be a challenge.

The Demon tried again—one final, desperate surge forward.

Rowan met it with a downward lash, slamming a whip into its shoulder and driving into the stone. Before it could rise, his second whip cut clean across its throat.

The creature froze, a wet, gurgling sound escaping its lips as black blood spilled freely from the deep gash. Its burning eyes locked with Rowan’s, flickering with fury and fear.

A trembling hand rose to its ruined neck, claws slick with its own blood, as if trying to hold the life in. But it was already slipping away.

Rowan didn’t move. He held its gaze until the light faded from its eyes and the body crumpled to the ground with a heavy, final thud.

He exhaled, rolling his shoulders as the tension drained from his limbs. His whips flickered out, the fire dispersing as he withdrew his mana.

Rowan glanced at the smoking corpse of the Lesser Demon, a satisfied smile tugging at his lips.

He’d done this. He’d reduced this monster that, if given half a chance, would have wreaked havoc on the helpless and the innocent alike. But now, because of his actions, that was a fate that would never come to fruition.

He clenched his fist.

I’m not calling down cataclysms yet, but I’ll get there.

Rowan glanced towards the gate leading further into the Rift, taking a deep breath before walking towards it. His mana was down a quarter, and he’d take a few minutes for it to replenish before proceeding.

But first, he needed to take a look at the writing on the wall. Literally.
