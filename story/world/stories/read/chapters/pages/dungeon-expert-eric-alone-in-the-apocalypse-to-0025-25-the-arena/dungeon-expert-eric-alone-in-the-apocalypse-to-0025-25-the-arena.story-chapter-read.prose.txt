The blue boxes vanished. The Trinotaur’s collar opened, dropping to the ground, and the creature instantly charged at Eric. They were on the opposite sides of the arena from one another, and Eric had expected to have a little time to cast, but the Trinotaur’s massive hooves ate up the distance between them. It was almost like watching something teleport, or seeing a racecar zoom by, except it wasn’t zooming by, it was charging right at him. As it closed in, it lowered its head, pointing its razor-sharp horns right at Eric.

Eric threw himself to the side, bodily leaping out of its way. The Trinotaur trampled past just behind him, hurtling off toward the arena wall. As it charged away, Eric pointed the sword at it. “Firebolt!”

The spell left his arm, but instead of immediately shooting at the Trinotaur, it rushed along the length of the sword first, still in its mana form. The sword glimmered with red-hot light, and the spell intensified, growing hotter and stronger than his body could handle. In return, it took longer to cast than he was used to, and cost a little more mana. He pointed the blade at the Trinotaur, continuing to track it as the spell flew down the blade, and a hotter, faster Firebolt than he’d ever shot before hurtled toward the monster. The bolt smacked it in the back, and it roared and arced its back in pain.

“Damn!” Eric said, impressed. He gave the sword a complimentary once-over, turning it in his hand. As much as this blade leaned into the cringey snake aesthetic a little too hard, it was an awesome piece of equipment. He shouldn’t have been sleeping on it.

“You had a focus this whole time and you weren’t using it?” Cy asked, taken aback.

“I didn’t know what it did and I didn’t have enough mana to really use spells in dungeons when I got it, and then I… kind of forgot about it.”

Cy muttered something under his breath. Eric couldn’t make it out, but it didn’t sound particularly complimentary. “You certainly didn’t come from a world with magic, that’s for sure.”

Across the arena, the Trinotaur roared again. It seemed to like doing that, Eric noted, and not only that, but the crowd ate it up, jeering and cheering alike. It rounded, turning on Eric. Steam poured from its broad nostrils, and the third eye in the center of its forehead rolled wildly, seemingly at random. It lifted its slab of a hand and pointed at him, tail lashing behind it.

“I know, big boy. Come on.” Eric lifted his hand to taunt it back, sword dangling at his side. At the same time, he prepared an Explosion. He pushed it into the sword, waiting almost the full time for the spell to travel the sword, then snapped it up and aimed at the Trinotaur.

The Explosion blasted out of the sword, a compressed ball of heat and pressure. The spell was fast, but the Trinotaur was faster. It saw the distortion hurtling at it and leaped to the side, avoiding it more nimbly than its enormous frame would imply possible. The Explosion blasted the wall behind it, throwing it slightly forward but doing no more damage.

If it’s still, it can devote all its time to dodging. It’s faster and stronger than me. I can’t hit it while it’s trying not to be hit. Which meant he only had one option left. He lifted his chin, doing his best to look down on a monster much, much more massive than him, while loading another Explosion into his sword. “Is that all you have? Some fancy footwork? You don’t scare me, little guy.”

The Trinotaur roared. It leaned forward, dropping into a sprinter’s stance. Eric lifted onto his toes, eyes wide, adrenaline pumping. This one was going to be faster. The first one had already been too fast for him to follow, and this one would be faster. He had to get out of the way. Had to.

It moved. In the blink of an eye, it went from stillness in that sprinter’s stance to hurtling at top speed. His mind didn’t properly record it. One second, it was crouched there; the next, it loomed over him, blocking out the sun, its massive body a wall of death and pain closing in on him. He released the Explosion directly in its face, then threw himself to the side, desperately twisting out of the way of its horns. One horn clipped him, then its shoulder slammed him, too. He heard bone crunch and the whole world spun as he was lifted bodily up and thrown through the air. The sky chased the earth. For a few seconds, he flew; then he crashed down, smashing onto hard rock and rolling through the dust.

He laid there, stunned. His mind was a mess. Everything hurt. Get up. Get up! He felt like he’d been hit by a linebacker, not that he’d ever played football. He felt like one of the mice his childhood cat would pick up and toss around. He was nothing, fluff on the wind, a chew toy, and this monster was going to take its time and really enjoy destroying him.

Get up. Eric rolled over laboriously. He pushed himself to his knees, then placed a single foot on the ground. His side was a red-hot mess, and every motion triggered a spike of sharp pain through his abdomen. But he had to keep moving. He had to. If he stopped, he died.

Good thing I didn’t pick melee. I’d be dead right now. I’d have no way to harm this thing. He couldn’t get close without getting flattened. If he used all his strength, he still wouldn’t be able to cut through its tough hide and thick muscle. Spells were his only hope to get past this encounter.

On the other side of the arena, the Trinotaur paced, waiting. Its face bled, the third eye red and irritated and crying big red tears, and its centermost horn was chipped, but it was still standing. Compared to Eric, it had only taken a scratch. It pointed its finger at him in mockery and tossed its bull head back in a simulacrum of laughter. Eric continued to climb to his feet, but slower, wracking his brains as he lollygagged through standing fully upright. It was waiting for him. Transparently, obviously waiting for him to get up. The second he stood, it would knock him down again, and again, and again, until he got up no longer. It was a cat, and he was the mouse. It didn’t just want to kill him; it wanted to enjoy killing him.

“Cy,” Eric whispered, his voice dry. He used Regeneration, but only fed a scrap of his mana into it, using the spell to fix anything that might immediately kill him but not trying to pull his whole body back together again.

“Yes?” The rabbit clung tightly to his shoulder, his whole body flattened against Eric’s. A bubble of aura surrounded him, holding him to Eric’s body and protecting him both.

“How many Firebolts and Explosions would it take to kill this thing?”

“More than you can survive hits from it, if that’s what you’re asking,” Cy replied.

Eric took a deep breath. He closed his eyes. “What if I combine them?”

“Combine… Firebolt and Explosion?”

He nodded.

“It… will take most of your mana. Two spells that synergize combine to make more than the sum of their parts, and cost more than the sum of their parts to boot. But if you pull it off… it might be enough.”

“Might?”

“Two would certainly be enough, but I don’t know that you have the mana for that. And pulling it off once is miracle enough. You could explode if you do this wrong, or tear your mana circuits apart. Still, if you can mange it, it might be your best chance at survival.”

“Okay. Then let’s do it.” Eric shook his head, then snapped his mana circuits into place. He shaped Firebolt in his left hand and Explosion in his right, then pushed himself upright, taking the final step to return to his feet.

Opposite him, the Trinotaur stopped pacing. It lowered its head and spread its feet, preparing a charge. It snorted, the sound carrying across the arena, and pawed the ground. In the background, the crowd had gone silent. Everyone, every last one of the goblins held its breath, waiting for the final strike.

“Godspeed,” Cy murmured.

Eric held the sword out in one hand, but didn’t push the spell into it yet. Lifting his other hand, he clasped the grip tight. He pointed it at the Trinotaur, levelling the tip at the space between them. This was simple geometry—no, not even that; linear algebra. Two points, intersecting in a line. It would have to clear the space between them in order to intersect with him.

The Trinotaur kicked off the ground, moving so fast it all-but-vanished.

Eric shoved the spells forward, pushing them both into the blade at the same time. The two spells combined, the circuits changing and shifting as they intersected. The complexity of the spells doubled, then tripled. Eric watched as closely as he could, chasing the shape of the new spell. Mana poured out of him, gushing out of his body and into the sword. The sword glowed twice over, beaming with both the bright red of Firebolt and the yellow-white of Explosion, and the new spell took form.

The Trinotaur appeared before him, its massive arms out wide and low to sweep him up, this time, carry him off his feet and slam him into the wall.

“Go.” Eric pushed, and the spell blasted out of the sword, tearing all his mana with it. He had no energy left to dodge, nor time to do it, but that had never been his plan. A ball of heat, fire, and energy burst to life between the two of them, and for the second time, Eric went flying.

“Your aura! Harden your aura!” Cy shouted. Eric did his best, pushing as hard at he could at whatever he had, but he could still barely feel his aura, let alone harden it. Cy grunted, and he felt something like a hand wrap around the back of his head, and then he hit the ground. Eric bounced, then skidded straight backward, sliding on his jacket over the dusty stone. The aura wrapped around his head kept it off the ground and prevented him from grinding his brains across the stone.

He felt dry. Empty. Not just exhausted, but voided. Like he’d been hollowed out. As if he was a cantaloupe, and someone had taken a spoon and scooped out all his goopy innards. Before, when he’d run out of mana, it had just felt like being tired. This time? It felt different.

Why? What’s changed? Did I completely wipe myself out? Or is it just because I’m getting used to having mana or have more of it now or something?

“Get up! Get up!” Cy insisted, smacking his forepaw repeatedly on Eric’s shoulder.

Eric sat up. It took effort. Real labor. The front of his body ached, all of it, and his face felt crispy. His jacket looked crispy, the snakeskin dried up and withered from the force of the blast. He lifted his eyes, taking in the Trinotaur.

The monster laid on its back, too. It hadn’t flown as far as Eric had, and as a result, had absorbed more of the force of the blow. Its skin was bright, raw red, and blown open in parts; he could see its sternum and jawbone. Its slavering jaw dripped frothy spittle, and its third eye was still and white, completely burned through from Eric’s spell. But it wasn’t dead. It shifted, arms and legs twitching on the ground.

A tomato splattered down next to Eric. A can clattered by on his other side. He turned, his neck aching with every inch. The goblins shouted, though their voices were muted, distant, and rained both abuse and trash down on him and the Trinotaur. They mostly missed him, but they hit the larger target.

“Eric, it’s getting up,” Cy hissed. “Eric!”

“It’s okay. I have a plan.” Eric turned to face the monster and opened his inventory. He couldn’t usually use it in battle, since it took too long to open, but with both him and the monster on their respective asses, he had the time he needed to utilize it. He drew out two bottles, one full of red liquid, and one full of yellow, and downed the health and mana potions one after another. His wounds closed steadily, and that empty feeling inside of him rapidly refilled, like a tap filling up a sink. Once more, he pushed his mana circuits into shape. They resisted, as exhausted as the rest of him was, but he ignored the pain and went on anyways. He lifted the sword. The blade trembled, and he looked down. His hand shook, from weakness, from exhaustion, from fear and adrenaline, he didn’t know. He clasped it with his other hand, meaning to steady it, and they trembled together.

Okay. He couldn’t change his reality, but he could deal with it. He braced the butt of the sword on the ground and pointed the tip at the Trinotaur.

The Trinotaur sat up slowly. It shook its massive head, sending spittle flying, then roared. The crowd roared with it, cheering, goading it back to his feet.

Eric ignored all that. He focused on the sword and nothing else, carefully straightening it to point directly at the monster.

“What are you doing?” Cy asked, concerned.

“Two will finish it, right?”

“Eric, you’re using a spell well above your skill level… well above your level level. You’re lucky you survived the first attempt. A second… who knows the damage you could do to yourself? Your mana circuits? Your core? You’ve barely solidified any of it, and you’re going to push yourself beyond your limits in more than one way. You—forget being in good shape afterward, you might just ignite your core and explode where you stand.”

The Trinotaur leaned forward, getting its legs under it. It rose to a knee, then faltered. Its hands hit the ground, barely catching it, and the crowd booed; then it pushed up, getting back on one foot and one knee, and the goblins went wild.

“Sit,” Eric corrected him. “I’m not standing. I’m sitting.” He felt a little goofy. A little silly, like none of this was real, like he was floating in his head. He was detached from reality, drifting from it, not quite in his body but over it, watching himself do these insane things. At the same time, he was utterly present, locked into the moment on adrenaline and pure raw fear. If he didn’t kill the Trinotaur, the Trinotaur would kill him. A chance at surviving was better than no chance. Risking self-detonation was better than sitting here and letting those dinner plate hoofs crush his skull to paste.

The sword shook. He lifted it and slammed it down, over and over, beating a hole in the dust for the butt of the sword, then braced it in the hole. It would not shake. It could not shake. He was taking this thing out whether it liked it or not.

The Trinotaur reached its feet. It turned to the goblins, lifting its massive arms in a victory pose. They screamed and stomped their feet, going nuts for it. In their eyes, it had already won. In their eyes, Eric, still slumped on the ground, barely sitting upright, was already dead.

Shows what they know. He adjusted the sword, pointing it at the Trinotaur’s back. This would be a coward’s blow, but it was what it had to be. If he waited for it to charge again, he wouldn’t survive it; either the charge or taking his own spell to his face. It had to be now. Now, while it was distracted and showboating for the crowd.

His mana had refilled enough. The sword was stable, aimed at the monster’s spine. He pushed, shoving the mana through his circuits, and once more shaped that secondary spell, the one above his level, the one that might detonate him.

“Fireball,” he whispered with scorched lips, and the bolt burst from the sword tip.
