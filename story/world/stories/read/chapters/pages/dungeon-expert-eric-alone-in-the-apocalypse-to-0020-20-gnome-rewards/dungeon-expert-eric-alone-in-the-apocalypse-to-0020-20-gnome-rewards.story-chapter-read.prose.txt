The chest opened. A golden glow emanated from it. He peered in to find a pile of red shop tokens and a ragged, blackened book that looked like it’d been through a campfire and somehow survived.

Eric’s eyes widened. Spellbook? He auto-collected the red tokens, then snatched up the book. The cover was almost too blasted for him to read, but under all the ash and soot, he managed to make out: Explosion.

“Hell yeah,” he muttered to himself.

He glanced around to make sure he was alone, then opened the book. Information started flowing into his brain. As usual, his forehead heated up and his eyes ached, but it hurt less than usual. Am I getting used to reading spellbooks? Don’t mind that.

Explosion slotted into his spell list, and Eric quickly opened his status to take a look at what two levels had done for him.

“Damn,” he said aloud. A pretty decent boost to all his stats and a new skill? This was an awesome level. Set of levels, since he’d leveled up twice again. He hadn’t been given a choice between skills at this level the way he had previously, but to be fair? If he’d been given a choice, he’d have chosen Mana Pool every time. That, and he suspected that he got to pick his skill on some, but not all levels. He’d definitely played games where the skill tree sometimes collapsed to a single option in order to progress to the next skill option, so it wasn’t a revolutionary situation to be given no option on his skill pick every now and again. He turned his senses inward, feeling his internal mana. There was more room in his core than he remembered, though it wasn’t full yet; it seemed it had arrived empty, and would need time to fill.

Time… or a mana potion. He held back, since he’d just finished the dungeon and had no real need to mash spells, but it was something to remember: if he had a mana potion on hand, any upgrade to his pool could immediately materialize into real mana.

Honestly? This was a watershed level. He could already feel that his mana pool was big enough to support actually casting spells for most of a dungeon now. He’d still have to play it carefully, but he wouldn’t have to play the dungeon like a melee fighter for the entire dungeon and save his spells for the boss anymore. He wasn’t quite a real mage, but he was close enough that he felt deserving of the title now. On top of that, his increased MAG score meant each spell would do more damage. It was pretty awesome all around.

The only downside was that he still had no answer to the lava dungeon, but that was a future problem for future Eric. This area—the zone behind the gate—was large, and had several dungeons in it. He still had plenty of options before he got shoehorned into the lava dungeon. Plenty of time to build the strength to take on the lava dungeon… or, if nothing else, build up shop tokens and roll rare prizes until he got an Armorer Shop Token again. The point was, there was still time to figure things out. Worst case, he could always walk away. It wasn’t like he was obligated to wipe out all the dungeons behind the gate.

He was on a timer, so he didn’t open the rewards yet. Two levels and a new spell were enough for now. He hobbled off toward the exit, picking his way through the bamboo grove.

A few more gnomes charged him on the way back, but without quantity on their side, they weren’t much of a threat. He cooked ‘em and tossed ‘em.

For the most part, he followed the tracks the giant gnome had carved through the bamboo, but when that ran out, he took it slow and squeezed his way through the bamboo, half-walking on the stalks, half-holding himself up with the poles. It was like a really clumsy, slow version of acrobatics. His socked feet helped him there, since he could grip the bamboo with his feet as well as his hands, rather than just bracing the stiff bottoms of shoes against the stalks. It was a little awkward to deal with gnomes in that pose, but he managed—and the gnomes blasted him a little bit of clearance whenever he caught one.

Out of the bamboo forest, no more gnomes awaited him, and he made it back to the dungeon’s entrance without facing any further resistance. At the garden gate that marked its start, he paused. It was foggy outside again. There was no fog inside the dungeon, but the path and forest outside was thick with fog, so thick he could barely see a foot from where he stood.

He twisted his lips. Don’t like that. Is that misshapen monster out there again, too? He was stronger now, but that thing still spooked him. Then again… who knew? He hadn’t exactly fought it. Maybe it was a spooky pushover, and he was the idiot for running from it like this was a horror game without even testing its strength. I could try lobbing a firebolt or explosion at it if it shows up again. Get a feel for how strong it is.

Eric looked to the sky. It was still bright, but the shadows were longer. If he had to guess, it had been morning before he’d set out, and after clearing the dungeon, time had passed, and it was now afternoon. Does the fog appear at noon every day? I don’t think I can clear a dungeon before noon.

He especially didn’t want to explore the zone any further after the fog appeared, especially not if that monster appeared again. I bet that’s its function, he realized, lifting his hand to the garden gate and stepping out into the fog. It’s meant to prevent you from clearing more than one dungeon a day. You get pre-noon to select your dungeon and explore the zone, but post-noon, you need to scurry back to the safe zone. Tomorrow, I’ll spend all the pre-noon time I can exploring the zone, then select my dungeon at the end of it. I’m wasting the pre-noon time otherwise, and after all, as long as I enter a dungeon before noon, it doesn’t matter when I exit it; I’ll still have to walk back in the fog, unless I miraculously pull off some sort of crazy speedrun.

He’d barely made it a dozen steps from the gate before his footsteps doubled. He deliberately swung his next step longer than usual, keeping his foot off the ground for a heartbeat longer, and heard it for sure: a footstep when his foot wasn’t on the ground. Eric glanced over his shoulder, but the fog ate up his Mana Sight, and his ordinary eyes couldn’t pierce any further than they could last night. Once again, he sped up without changing the pace of his steps. This time, though, the footsteps didn’t fall any further behind; they matched pace with him, still just at the edge of his hearing.

Eric pressed on, retracing his steps back to the safe zone. The steps grew steadily closer, but at such a slow pace he couldn’t be sure they were growing louder at all until the thing behind him kicked a rock and the stone went bouncing out in front of him. He glanced back and made out its shape again, looming closer than he would’ve liked.

Ah, shit. He prepared a firebolt, gathering the mana to his hand and snapping the spell circuits into place, but didn’t shoot it yet. Ideally, he wasn’t planning to fire anything at it until the safe zone was right there for him to duck into. If it attacked, then all bets were off, but if he could, he’d rather let it follow him to the stairs, then experiment. The last thing he wanted was to find out it had some kind of turbo danger death mode that only activated when it was hit while he was still miles from the safe zone. Better to find that out when he was a hop, skip, and a jump from safety… or better yet, already within the safe zone’s boundaries.

The safe zone loomed. Eric whipped around, only to find the misshapen monster looming just out of reach in the fog. He blasted it with his firebolt and leaped onto the porch with the same motion.

“Welcome to the safe zone,” a neutral vaguely female voice intoned.

The fire blasted away the fog, and for a split second, he made out a twisted, fleshy, horrible shape beneath it; then the fireball struck the creature. It screamed and darted forward, lifting a massive clawed hand high as it closed in.

Eric scrambled back, his stomach sucking to his spine even though he knew he was in the safe zone. The monster’s claw bonked off an invisible wall, and it staggered back. For a moment, between the firebolt and the safe zone blocking the fog, he could make out its details. It was man-sized, with raw, almost painful-looking skin, and its midsection twisted, the skin falling against the organs so he could see each one clearly outlined, all the way to the spine pushing through at the bottom. One doleful eye stared at him, not quite aligned with the other eye socket, and bare teeth gritted his direction, the cheeks burned away to scraps on its skull-like face.

“Yuck,” he muttered, taking another step back.

The monster stared at him, then retreated, leaping back as suddenly as it had appeared to vanish into the fog. He watched it go, then shook his whole body. That was gross. And terrifying. That thing looked like a punishment monster in an exploration game, or a killer in a asymmetrical one-versus-many killer-survivors game. Whatever it was, it’d shrugged off a firebolt even more casually than the giant gnome had. He didn’t want to encounter that out in the fog.

So… one dungeon clear a day, enforced by spooky mc spookums. Got it. Why? Why was this zone so dead set on that? It was suspicious. Was there something at the end of it, after he cleared all the dungeons?

Eric smirked to himself, excited. Now I’ve got to find out.

“Hey kid, you back?”

“Yeah, Joe, what’s up?” Eric called, stepping into the house-cum-shop proper. His socked feet left tiny puddles behind as he dripped his way over the floor.

A mountain of a woman stomped over, gesturing him closer. She braced herself against her knees, then sighed and slowly lowered herself to a seat. The seat creaked under her, barely holding itself up. “Phew. This one’s a dozy. Does she ever love food, though. I’ve never felt this good from just eating.”

Eric just nodded, not sure what to say.

After a beat to catch his breath, Joe looked up. “So. Second dungeon in the books! Not a bad showing, all around. The livestream is up, but I haven’t edited down the video yet, so it’s just a longform—”

“A VOD?” Eric suggested. That was what all the streamers called it when they posted their unedited longform video content on other platforms.

The woman waved her hand. “Sure. A VOD. Your culture has the damnedest things. You don’t have—” Joe said something, but the word simply slipped through Eric’s mind, a sound that he couldn’t record, “—but you have memes and VODs. Crazy.”

“We don’t have what?”

Joe frowned. He tried again, then waved his slablike hand. “Mana. Magic and shit.”

“Oh, yeah.”

“Anyways. Livestream’s doing… okay. It hasn’t caught on yet, but that’s just because people don’t know that you exist to go looking. I’ll run a few ads once the edited video’s up, and we can take a stab at gaining traction. Still, even before the feedback… got a few notes for you.”

“Lay them on me.” Eric sat down at the bar, putting his back to the cooking area. Water puddled in his butt as he sat, and he made a mental note of which stool he’d sat on so he wouldn’t sit on the soon-to-be soaked cushion later.

Joe nodded. He made a vague gesture that Eric couldn’t interpret, then began. “Well, first one’s obvious. Do the silent killer thing or the commentary thing, but doing both like you did today isn’t going to work. The silent killer viewers want you to shut up, and the commentary viewers are bored to tears from the lack of chatting. You can lean into either audience, but right now, you’re cutting the middle and as a result, leaning out of both.”

Eric nodded. That was a fair criticism. He truly had meant to commentate more, but it was hard to chat and fight at the same time, especially right now while he was still figuring out his fighting style and having to fight melee and conserve his mana. That had just changed, probably, but even then, he’d have to figure out fighting as a mage. There was probably a one, two dungeon lag before he could realistically keep up a patter, but he could work on that. He really didn’t want to be the silent killer type. He could imagine the kind of people who were into silent killer types, and he was pretty sure he didn’t want that kind of attention. I could be wrong. I did like my fair share of silent killer movies back in the day, and I’m… I think I’m normal. Relatively normal. Still, between silent streamers who do longplays and stuff like that and commentator streamers, commentators are wildly more popular, so I don’t think this is the wrong decision.

Joe glanced at him, then moved on. “I saw you doing commentary at the end there, that’s good stuff. I can cut that in with some scenes of you doing the actual battle on top of your demonstration, so if you want to keep doing that and go the guide route—”

“I do.”

“—keep it up. You can even go longer if you want, gives me more to work with. I’ll do two videos, the guide and a highlights reel; shouldn’t be too much extra effort. We’ll focus the guide at your homeworld and the highlights at everyone else, and let them discover the guide if they like.” Joe sat back and fixed him with a stare. “Now, understand I might be singing a different tune tomorrow if they totally flop.”

“No, I understand. Please tell me.” He did want to be able to react to failure; otherwise, he could never grow.

“Course, right now, “success” looks like getting ten, maybe a hundred views—well, scale’s different, but the equivalent of that on your world’s smalltime servers—so unless you pop off or totally flop, I’ll probably not have much to tell you for a while. It’ll take a little while to gain traction, but you’ve got all the pieces in hand. You just have to play them right.”

“Got it,” Eric said, nodding.

Joe pushed himself laboriously to his feet, arms and thighs flapping. “I’ll leave you to it. Go practice your spells or do pushups or whatever it is you do in your free time. I’ll be back tomorrow with some metrics, unless they’re totally boring.” He stomped off toward the back exit to the store.

“Oh, Joe—”

He paused, looking back. “What?”

“This area, with the fog and the gates and everything… are all the dungeons in here linked? And what’s that monster thing?”

Joe waved his hand. “That’s for you to figure out, bucko.” He took another step forward and vanished. In his wake, the floor creaked as its burden suddenly disappeared.

Eric stared after him for one more second, then sighed. He wandered off to the front of the safe zone. First, the last two rewards; then, practicing Explosion until he had a handle on his newest spell. Pushing the creaky front door open, he stepped out into the porch.
