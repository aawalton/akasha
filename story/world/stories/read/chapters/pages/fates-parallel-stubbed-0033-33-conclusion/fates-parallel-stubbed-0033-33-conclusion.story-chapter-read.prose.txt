Lee Jia glared balefully at Jianmo, but her attempt at a retort was cut off by her own choking and gagging on the vile corruption caught in her throat. Jianmo laughed at her as she coughed up the disgusting sludge.

“Hahaha, oh that stuff is terrible. I am so glad I never had to go through that. Humans sure have a hard time of it.”

Lee Jia wiped at her mouth in a vain attempt to get rid of the sticky black substance, but she knew from experience it was hopeless. That taste was going to stay with her for days. She glanced up at the annoyingly cheerful demon and scowled.

“I thought you left.”

“Oh, I planned to, but imagine my surprise when you and the little demon girl started dual cultivating right then and there! Scandalous! I don’t hate shameless girls like you.”

Lee Jia blushed more fiercely than expected at his lewd allegations, vividly recalling the first thing she had remembered when they began meditating. Eui finished hacking up her own lungful of sludge and croaked out her own question.

“Why do you keep calling me a demon?”

Jia could feel Eui’s anxiety as Jianmo grinned at her and tapped the glowing red core sticking out of his chest.

“Because it’s what you are, dear—same as me. Fiends and elementals use cores to make up for the parts they are missing. Mind and body, respectively. Spirits—well, they’re special so let’s forget them for a moment. When a human forms a core, that makes them a demon. Well, I was never human to begin with so let’s just say I’m exceptional.”

Lee Jia frowned.

“Don’t you mean you’re an exception?”

“I meant what I said, darling.”

Eui sighed in frustration, trying to comb the goop out of her hair with her fingers before giving up and turning back to Jianmo again.

“If you’re not human then what are you? And what does being a demon even mean?”

Jianmo held up a finger and Lee Jia felt that foreign influence once again intruding on her domain—realizing with a start that it hadn’t turned back into a regular aura. This time, there was a will behind the influence as Jianmo’s domain completely oppressed her own. The corruption flowed away from Lee Jia and Eui, gathering into a ball above his finger until they were both completely clean.

Eui narrowed her eyes at the demon.

“Couldn’t you have done that sooner?”

“Yes, I could. To answer your other question—well, that’s a bit more complicated. It really depends on who you ask, but the main advantage of a demonic core is the ability to rob another cultivator of their essence.”

Jianmo shrugged and flicked his finger, sending the ball of black goo away. Lee Jia felt nauseous as it landed with a sickening splat right on Yan’s headless body. She shook her head and tried to ignore it.

“Essence?”

“Mana, qi, ki, spiritual energy, whatever. There’s too many words for it, so I’ve taken to simply calling it ‘essence’. You’ve already discovered one way of sharing essence—you little scamps—but it’s not the only way. A demonic core allows for a more one-sided and less—shall we say, consensual exchange.”

Lee Jia’s expression darkened at Jianmo’s phrasing and she felt Eui radiating an aura of concern as she glanced over. Before Jia could say anything, Eui quickly spoke up.

“Is that it? Being a demon lets you steal essence?”

Jianmo shook his head and laughed.

“Haha, no of course not. You can also consume mortal souls, and forcefully bind spirits. And you’ve already discovered that it can absorb unstable essence. I imagine that’s probably how you ended up forming the core in the first place.”

Lee Jia paled at that. Consuming souls!? She was distracted by a feeling of suspicion coming from Eui—wait, was she feeling Eui’s emotions? It had been happening for a while now. Eui narrowed her eyes at Jianmo.

“You never answered about what you are, if not human.”

Jianmo’s perpetual carefree grin seemed to become slightly more sly as he regarded Eui.

“So I didn’t. Well, obviously I’m a demon! Though I suppose that answer won’t satisfy you.”

The sword that had been stuck in the altar—the same one he used to kill Yan Zhihao—appeared in his hand, rising from the surface of his skin as if it were water.

“What the brainless corpse over there thought was my seal was, in fact, what was being sealed. This is my true body—or perhaps it’s more accurate to say my original body, before I gained this form. I am, in short, a sword.”

Jia and Eui’s minds went blank as they blinked at Jianmo in confusion.

“Yes, yes, I know. It’s strange, but the full explanation is a long story which I have neither the time nor the inclination to tell. In fact, I only wanted to come back to congratulate the pair of you for your breakthrough into the second stage. I had hoped one of you would unify your cultivation and use your domain to scatter the divine essence, but I didn’t think you’d try to absorb it! You girls are crazy!”

Lee Jia flushed with embarrassment. She hadn’t realized that was an option, but in hindsight they should have been able to simply let go of the essence that had been flooding their aura. Jianmo ignored her and went on.

“Little demon, my offer stands. Come find me if you wish to learn more. Don’t worry about how to find me—if you seek me out you’ll reach me eventually. I hope that you do, I’ve never had a disciple before—it should be fun. You can even bring your little cat with you, and we can make it a threesome.”

Lee Jia bristled at being called a cat, and was downright furious at Jianmo’s lewd implications. Jianmo just pointed and laughed at her as she suppressed the urge to hiss at him—she hadn’t done that since she was a baby!

“Hahaha! That’s the spirit! Loving the anger and outrage. I don’t hate prudish little kitties like you. Until we meet again, girls!”

With a flourish, Jianmo disappeared in a flash of purple flames, leaving them alone in the dark cave. Jia grumbled with dissatisfaction at his sudden departure.

“Showoff...”

With Jianmo gone, Jia and Eui could finally check on their friends and take stock of their situation. Their friends were alive, but in bad shape. They had all collapsed unconscious at some point and had suffered hemorrhages from Jianmo’s divine essence. Yan Zhihao was obviously beyond help, and Eui judged from the state of his corpse that they had been meditating for days.

It all served as a grim reminder that as affable as he had been, Jianmo was every bit the demon he claimed to be. He had spared Yan Zhihao’s life as a reward, then taken offense and killed him seconds later without hesitation. Then, in his ‘gratitude’ he had left them with a trial that would have certainly killed them if they had failed. He had even been fully prepared to leave them entirely to their fates if he hadn’t been curious about Jia and Eui’s cultivation method.

“I hope we never meet him again.”

Eui sighed, but nodded her agreement.

“He seemed knowledgeable, but I think you’re right. He’s too danger—”

Eui cut herself off when she noticed Jia’s shocked expression.

“What?”

“Eui! I didn’t say that out loud, but you heard it! It was the same after our breakthrough, when we first woke up! Do it again!”

Eui’s brows furrowed as Jia grabbed her hands excitedly.

“Do what, exactly?”

“Think something! Anything! Just say the words in your head.”

“Like this?”

“Yes! Exactly!”

Eui’s eyes widened as she realized what was happening.

“Jia, how are we doing that?”

“I don’t know! It’s like when our minds are connected but...easier? It’s not as confusing, that’s for sure.”

Eui put a hand up to her chin, her eyes darting back and forth in deep contemplation. Jia could feel Eui’s confusion, anxiety, and mounting excitement.

“Jia, do you think this is because we broke through together? While we were...someone else?”

Jia deflated slightly as she recalled the strange experience. There had been a clear line while they were meditating—an exact moment when they stopped being two people and became a singular entity. When they stopped meditating, that line...wasn’t there. There wasn’t a moment when she stopped being someone else and went back to being Jia—even now, she felt in some small way like she still was that other person.

“Maybe. I think it’s something we’ll just have to figure out together. After all, we’ve broken through now, right? Didn’t Ienaga say that’s the point of no return?”

Jia was surprised by the sudden feeling of anxious dread that she felt from Eui, but it was quickly replaced by excitement.

“You’re right! We’re stuck with each other, now. Whatever this connection is, we’ll just have to figure it out. Another advantage for us!”

Jia and Eui spent the rest of the day healing their friends and recovering from Jianmo’s trial. While Eui’s ability to heal others was still lacking, she made up for it with the improved quality and quantity of her qi. Within the first few hours, they’d gotten Xin Wei back on his feet and, with his help, dramatically improved the recovery rate of the others.

Once everybody was conscious again, they agreed to spend the remainder of the night resting and recovering while Jia and Eui did their best to explain what had happened, without going into too many personal details. Their friends expressed shock at their sudden breakthrough, but Jia and Eui were able to explain it away as being a result of the trial.

Before returning to the academy, they agreed to burn Yan Zhihao’s corpse. It was in a rather sorry state, and none of the others had left bodies behind anyway. Lee Jia had mixed feelings as she watched the pyre they had built for him burn down.

On one hand, Yan Zhihao had been consistently rude and obnoxious—he’d even tried to kill them, and definitely brought about his own demise. But on the other hand, did she really think that he deserved death? Eui certainly seemed to think he did, and most of the others were politely silent on the matter—but their thoughts were clear.

Was that really what the world of cultivation was like? When the time came, could Lee Jia be that ruthless—or would she sow the seeds of disaster with her own hesitation? One thing she was certain of, was that she would never let herself become like Jianmo—gleefully murdering someone and desecrating their corpse over some minor offense.

The return trip was unusually quiet and uneventful. Even if they didn’t know them well, the deaths of a dozen fellow disciples weighed heavily on the group. In the end, they made it all the way back to the academy without meeting even a single magical beast. They were met at the gates by their instructors.

“Ah! You’re back, and just in time. Some of us were starting to get worried, hoho.”

Elder Qin scoffed at Magus Hwang and regarded the returning group coolly.

“Speak for yourself, Magus. Tell me, children, have you heard any word of disciple Yan Zhihao?”

The suddenly grim expressions of the group said enough for Elder Qin and his expression darkened.

“I see. Very well, we can discuss the details later. An unfortunate conclusion.”

That was it? ‘An unfortunate conclusion’? Lee Jia was baffled as Qin Zhao simply left without saying another word. Magus Hwang glanced back at him and chuckled nervously.

“Hoho, yes, unfortunate indeed. It’s good that the rest of you are well. Now that all the teams are eh, ahem—accounted for, we can begin the award ceremony. Come now, the other team is already waiting at the central courtyard.”

As Magus Hwang turned to depart, Master Ienaga addressed the group with a soft, comforting expression.

“I’m so sorry about what happened. We can talk about it later, when you’re ready. I’m just glad you’re all safe.”

The award ceremony was a relatively small affair. It was mostly just the two teams and the instructors, plus a few curious onlookers who saw that something was happening in the courtyard and came to investigate.

First, they counted out the mana cores of the beasts that they had culled during their expedition. By silent agreement, Lee Jia’s team elected not to include the cores that they had collected from Yan’s remains—which still left them with several dozen cores of varying grades and elements. The prize of their collection was still the perfect quality third grade magma core from the Obsidian Wolf alpha, since Jianmo had destroyed the fourth grade elemental’s core.

When it was Hayakawa’s team’s turn to present their kills, each member of her team dropped a sack filled to the brim with cores. Hundreds of them—mostly second grade or lower, with a few third grade mixed in—were piled up together in an impressive display. Lee Jia suddenly understood why they hadn’t encountered any on the way back—Hayakawa really did scour the mountain.

Hayakawa herself presented only their most valuable finds, three perfect quality third grade cores and a single low quality fourth grade core—apparently from a beast that had only just barely broken through. There were gasps of shock and awe all around as she presented the proof that they had slain a xiantian tier beast—though Lee Jia thought she caught a look of mild disapproval on Ienaga.

Magus Hwang adjusted his glasses and gestured for the group to be silent.

“Well, I dare say we have a clear winner of the first objective. Good work, all of you. In addition to keeping the cores you have collected, your reward is exclusive access to the mountain’s peak. Due to the leyline nexus, the mana density at the peak is extremely strong, and it’s very dangerous. You’ve proven yourselves capable however, and as such you are hereby granted permission to use it for your training.”

Magus Hwang continued to describe the conditions and stipulations of their reward. They were allowed to bring one person each as a training partner, but could not bring anyone who wasn’t at least second stage in one discipline without an instructor present. Lee Jia felt envious of their prize—with her newfound domain ability, her ability to absorb mana had improved dramatically, and she could scarcely imagine how a training location like that might improve her cultivation speed.

“Next, we’ve confirmed that the mana disturbance on the mountain has been cleared. Presumably one of you knows why, but it may have simply been a natural phenomenon that has passed on its own. Each team may send a representative to argue their case for why they believe their team has discovered and dealt with the cause.”

Lee Jia stepped forward, sweating nervously under the gaze of everyone present. In particular she felt Hayakawa’s cold gaze boring into her—her team had not sent a representative. She quietly described to Magus Hwang their journey, their meeting with Yan, the encounter with the elemental, and then Jianmo’s appearance.

Some of the details were slightly altered—she attributed Yan’s death to the elemental, and made no mention whatsoever of either his betrayal or Jianmo’s trial—but both instructors seemed satisfied with her description of the events as they happened.

“That is impressive, but troubling news. It’s unfortunate that you were trapped by such a powerful elemental, and a mixed blessing that you were saved by that ancient demon—it’s best if you avoid speaking of that to the other students. I have no doubt in my mind that you found the source of the disturbance, and I believe your actions were correct given the circumstances.

“As your reward, you and each member of your team will be given unrestricted access to the academy library. It contains the sum of the knowledge that all three nations have contributed to the founding of this academy. Originally, it was agreed upon that only the instructors would have access, but we have agreed that minds as keen as yours shouldn’t be restricted only to the paths set before them.”

As with Hayakawa’s reward, there were restrictions. They could share the knowledge they found freely, but were not allowed to remove anything from the library for any reason. Unlike Hayakawa’s group, they were not allowed to bring anyone with them unless that person already had access themselves.

Lee Jia had mixed feelings about the reward. It didn’t have the immediately obvious benefit to her cultivation like the mountain training did, but it would mean unfettered access to techniques and the ability to do her own research on things like joint cultivation, demons, and her newfound abilities without having to ask her instructors so many awkward questions.

Once they were dismissed, Jia and Eui were more than happy to finally return home to relax. Jia collapsed on one of the couches in their humble little living area and heaved out a long sigh.

“Haaaaah, that was exhausting!”

Eui nodded as she took up a lounging position on her own couch, both girls taking full advantage of their short stature.

“Yeah it was, but it was also kind of exhilarating. Sure, I almost died, became a demon, almost died again, released an immortal evil on the world, and then almost died again, but it was an adventure! I’ve never even imagined doing something like that in my lifetime.”

“Y-yeah...”

Jia agreed nervously, recalling just how closely they’d courted death over the last week. Eui was right, though, it had been exciting.

“I’m not in a hurry to go on another adventure like that but...I’m still glad we did. Though maybe without so many people dying next time.”

“No promises!”

Jia couldn’t help but giggle a bit at Eui’s dark humor. As Jia started to settle in for a well earned nap by the fire, Eui slowly turned to look at her.

“Say...”

Jia felt a sinister feeling of mischief and broke into a cold sweat as Eui shot her a familiar, evil grin.

“If we can sense each other’s thoughts and emotions, what about...”

Eui pinched herself hard in the forearm and Jia felt a sharp pain. She tried to school her expression, but she couldn’t hide her mounting dread as Eui’s grin grew wider.

“You know, Jia, I’m pretty ticklish, but of course, you knew that already. It’s always bugged me that you weren’t but I wonder...”

“Wait, Eui, we can talk about this! I’m sorry about before, ok? That was my mistake!”

Eui smiled softly at her, and Jia knew that she had Eui’s forgiveness.

...

But not her mercy.

“Nooo!”

Jia learned two dark lessons that day, as her desperate pleas for mercy fell on deaf ears. The first was that Eui was completely capable of tickling herself. The second was that she could really hold a grudge.
