Solomon

He woke up lying flat on his back on a Vitree rooftop. His opponent stood over him, his white cloak shining so brightly that Solomon was forced to cover his face with a hand. He squinted and peered through his fingers.

What he saw jolted him awake.

The white-haired man stared down at him with three eyes.

His eye color had changed once again. They’d gone from a divine gold to a demonic red. But Solomon wasn’t concerned with those eyes. No, his real focus was the third eye that now floated in front of the man’s forehead.

An Asteroid Eye.

“I told you, Solomon. I told you I was going to take something from you.”

Sen

A Few Moments Earlier

When we walk through fire, we find our real strength.

When Ceres broke through his defenses, he acted on instinct.

He abandoned the eclipse spell. There wasn’t any time. He created a protective cocoon from the excess mana his Mana Flood spell had poured into the air around him. Then, he cast the only thing he could to protect the people trapped in their homes below.

“Temporal Gateway Maxima!”

An explosion of silver blinked into existence as a gateway large enough to swallow Ceres formed. It was the largest and most powerful attack Sen had ever been on the receiving end of, and he was bringing it with him into what could very well be a one-way trip.

One moment he was floating above the streets of Vitree in a shadow-filled domain created by his authority. The next, he floated among the Temporal World’s endless confines, surrounded by mimics of stars and planets alike.

In the invasion of Harpis Castle, Sen had demonstrated what happened to those who entered this plane without protective runes to his flesh golem opponent. His mana clone copy had pressed a hand beyond the barrier.

That hand turned to bone, and that bone to dust.

But rune circles were effective because they carved a small sliver of order and protection into an otherwise lifeless plane. The sheer momentum of Ceres alone would crush any such protection in an instant. Even if there was a way to adjust for that, there hadn’t been time.

The situation had been simple. Take Ceres through a gateway, or let thousands of people die.

For him, the latter was never an option. That just wasn’t the kind of person he was. The God of Magic did not make concessions or false promises.

It was something he intended to teach Solomon shortly.

Temporalis was not a living being; it was simply a place. However, the way it ate away at Ceres felt like a predator devouring prey. As large as the asteroid was, it was nothing compared to the power of a plane beyond space and time itself. Despite that, Ceres still beat down upon him.

Sen wasn’t spared from the effects of this plane either. Without his protective runes, there wasn’t anything keeping the destructive force of Temporalis from turning against him as well. His only protection was the vast amount of mana he’d covered himself with in a protective bubble. Temporalis consumed that mana greedily.

Neither Sen nor Ceres had much time.

He pressed against the deadly spell with both his hands, empowered by the boost of his Hybrid Stat Burn. But the spell only lasted for sixty seconds, and time was running out. When his hybrid stats hit zero, he’d have neither the strength nor constitution to protect him from the crushing force Ceres presented.

[Master, we need to return now. The mana protecting us won’t last much longer.]

“How much…time?” Sen asked, straining against the meter.

[Twelve seconds…]

Great.

His mind was still enhanced from his spell, and the remnants of Time Crawl were present as well. He closed his eyes for two seconds; that was all he could allow himself.

Two seconds to form a plan.

Ten to execute.

His eyes shot open, and he got to work. He gritted his teeth, wrenching one of his hands away from the asteroid and turning it toward the open confines of the void.

His connection with Xochi was strong, but the Mana Axolotl was beyond communication, and not in position. Not to mention Xochi didn’t have enough energy to serve as an anchor for him again anyway. No, Sen was forced to focus on the only mana signature that would work in this situation.

The one he was currently pressing a hand against.

Solomon’s Asteroid Eye tapped into the powers of space itself. Surely the connection between this devastating asteroid and Solomon’s third eye would be enough to anchor Sen’s gateway out of here.

He still didn’t fully understand the spell, but understanding was a luxury he didn’t have right now. Before fighting Solomon, before this walk through fire, he’d have thought using the spell like this was impossible. But defying the impossible was the entire purpose of magic itself. Magic was the counter to reality, and he hadn’t earned his title by letting the world bend him to its will.

He’d earned it by bending the world to his own.

He forced mana through his palm and roared at the top of his lungs.

“Temporal Gateway Maxima!”

Power surged from him. His cloak roared to life, and the golden mana channels pulsed with mana. The burst of power was too much for his disguise. His hairband snapped. The restrained black hair returned to a free-flowing white, and his eyes glowed the color of his mana channels. Malleore returned to the form of a necklace and flew to its place around his neck, clasping of its own volition.

In the distance, a massive silver gateway clawed open to prepare for their departure. He grinned, but there was still work to do.

“Eclipse Magic Maxima! Silent Sphere Maxima!”

The first spell was one he’d attempted to cast earlier. It would target the connection that Solomon’s third eye had to the asteroid he used to control his other spells. The one that floated in space above Vitree. The second spell wrapped around Ceres itself, masking its approach further and minimizing how much attention Sen was about to draw.

He used the last remnants of the black wolf pack leader’s power to send out a mass of shadows to encompass the world around Solomon. Then Sen pressed off against Ceres and shot out of its way just as his hybrid stats spiraled to zero.

Ceres was much smaller than it had been when Sen had dragged it into Temporalis. However, most of the mana within was inside its core, and its momentum alone made it a devastating projectile. It was large enough to do what Sen needed it to. It was certainly powerful enough to take down Solomon.

Solomon realized it far too late. The eclipse spell had worked as intended. But to Sen’s surprise, the man’s eyes were filled with something unexpected.

Wonder.

Solomon took in the view of the temporal world through the gateway. A pseudo-space so like the one Solomon apparently loved. His eyes flicked to Sen, and then to what was left of Ceres as it broke through the gateway and slammed into him.

Ceres erupted into a silent explosion that the citizens of Vitree would never know about. Silent Sphere obscured the sound, and the shadow domain concealed it from the eyes of others.

Solomon was just one of many pieces of debris that fell from the sky in the resulting explosion. Fragments that descended upon the town.

Sen lifted a land.

“Butterfly Fire Swarm Fireking.” Flaming butterflies appeared and shot towards each of the fragments and shards of Ceres. Not a single piece reached the ground or buildings below.

“Where is Solomon landing?”

Tutor calculated the man’s trajectory in an instant. [There.]

Sen scanned it with his mana senses and found the building devoid of life. He didn’t interfere with Solomon’s fall.

The man crashed through the roof.

Sen followed.

He slowly descended until his feet landed in front of the crater Solomon’s body created. The Asteroid Eye still floated on the man’s forehead.

[Are you sure about this, master? In the past…]

Sen nodded. He was aware of the consequences. He jumped into the crater and knelt beside Solomon. His hand wrapped around the floating Asteroid Eye that was so crucial to the techniques Solomon commanded.

With his other hand, he controlled his mana like a surgeon, rooting around in Solomon’s soul. He searched for the thread of magic that connected the two. The thread that allowed a magician and his magic to work together seamlessly.

Sen closed his eyes and searched for the connection in his own web of magic. He just needed a connection. He just needed one connection to make the technique his own. Like surgically connecting one spiderweb to another.

Solomon’s threads were…frayed. It appeared as though many of the threads had once led elsewhere, but those paths had been burnt away. Something had altered the threads of Solomon’s magic at the deepest level. But the threads connecting his asteroid and meteor spells were strong. At their center was the one he was searching for. The core technique at the center of Solomon’s magic.

The Asteroid Eye.

A young woman laughed in his ear.

Sen’s frowned. “What was that?”

[Master, we should stop here. The spell is too deeply embedded.]

Sen shook his head. “No.” He’d made a promise. This was the prize he would take. It was the prize he wanted.

Memories flashed across his mind as he cut the spell away from Solomon’s web.

Memories of braiding his daughter’s hair. Giving her the figure he’d carved for her on her birthday. Holding her hand as they walked to the church. Teaching her magic for the first time.

Tutor’s voice sounded far away when he spoke.

[Master!]

Sen finished isolating the Asteroid Eye, careful not to take the memories as well. The power was enough; he wouldn’t deprive Solomon of these.

Not after feeling the grief deeply entwined with the man’s soul.

Solomon had lost her. His daughter…his whole family had been taken from him.

A single thing drove the man forward. The pursuit of those responsible. A worthy hunt.

More memories attempted to flash across his mind, a few from the battle they’d fought only moments ago. He severed the connection.

[Are you okay? It seems there was a clean break. But that was risky.]

There was a reason Sen rarely stole spells. There was a reason he never used any of the others he’d stolen this way in the past.

There was always a price to pay.

The question was, who would pay it?

Sen rose. A mana clone copy appeared beside him. “Bring him with us. We need to talk.”

There was still much he didn’t understand about Solomon, and his curiosity had been thoroughly piqued..

The mana clone nodded and lifted Solomon in its arms.

They landed on a rooftop well away from the wreckage of a building. Sen would rather not be around if the town guard found themselves brave enough to investigate.

He looked up at the asteroid in the sky, releasing the eclipse spell that hampered it.

“Asteroid Eye Maxim.”

The asteroid flared brighter under the power of his maximizer. Sen understood now, how Solomon had become such a force to be reckoned with. This technique was far beyond the level of a lord.

So Sen’s estimation of the man had been correct.

Solomon had the potential to reach the rank of a god-class mage one day.

Using the power of the Asteroid Eye wasn’t unlike using a spell to peer through Xochi’s eyes. Except it was like he could look through the eyes of a thousand people at once. The Khans were still fighting the members of Meteor Haven. Drussan and Bartholomew were grappling in the city streets. A sniveling dreadbeasts sat in fetal position in an alleyway and Sen realized who had led Solomon and the others to the East Gate.

The dreadbeast that Solomon had mentioned, Mi, was Micah, the slave trader’s son. The idiot must have escaped. Sen would deal with him later. For now, he turned his attention to Solomon, who was just waking up.

Solomon held a hand up to cover his face at first. But when he noticed the Asteroid Eye floating in front of Sen’s forehead, his own eyes widened.

“I told you, Solomon. I told you I was going to take something from you,” Sen said. “Now tell me what happened to your magic.”

Solomon’s memories had revealed something to Sen. This man could see the threads of magic as well.
