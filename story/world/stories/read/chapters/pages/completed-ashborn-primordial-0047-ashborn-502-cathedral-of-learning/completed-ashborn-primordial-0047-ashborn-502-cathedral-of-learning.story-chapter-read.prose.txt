Maiya couldn’t believe the fervor that surrounded something as mundane as a school’s opening ceremony. A magical school, granted, and the foremost Academy in the realm. Maiya had once dreamed of this moment. Of conquering their arduous entrance exam. Of sitting in these very seats, gazing down at the Headmaster giving his fiery speech.

She’d dreamed of the elation. The excitement. The pride… and yet all Maiya felt now was boredom. Boredom and frustration that she was made to sit through something so banal while Vir was off searching for useful information.

Perspective was a funny thing, Maiya reflected. What had once been a distant, glorious dream was now little more than a joke. An annoyance that filled Maiya with a certain sense of emptiness.

How she had ever respected such mejai—worshipped them—she did not know. Such was the fate of dreams, she’d found. Once achieved, they never seemed to mean as much. And while she might currently be without the bulk of her power, the work she was doing with Vir in puzzling out the secrets of prana manipulation brought her infinitely more joy than being ‘accepted’ by an institution she once upheld as gods. The reality was that Maiya was stronger than all the mejai assembled here, and it would not even be a contest.

How would they react once she obtained her true power, she wondered? These Balar 50 hopefuls and smug Balar 100 brats, thinking themselves lords in this little pond? How would they react if she unleashed the full might of her S-Rank magic? How would they react when that magic—already enough to level cities—was made manifold more powerful with the aid of Imperium Artifacts obtained in another universe?

Still, she’d be lying if she said she wasn’t looking forward to learning what the Altani had learned of the new rules of magic. Perhaps there was something there she’d learn. Something that would speed up her and Vir’s progress.

Maiya chuckled, earning herself narrowed eyes and whispers of disgust at the ‘weird’ one. Yes, even her meager display had earned her a name, despite being so weak she’d honestly been embarrassed. Charging even a single orb had proven surprisingly difficult.

She’d been all but sure she’d have failed, but apparently even that had been impressive enough to get her admitted with flying colors. The standards had simply changed.

Or rather, she thought darkly, the Fateweavers had crippled the ability of this world to fight back.

Unless that changed, this universe truly had no hope.

As for Vir, she could only hope he made the breakthrough he was looking for.

Their very survival might depend on it.

— —

While Maiya attended the entrance ceremony that Vir was sure to prove a waste of time, he instead delved down the Academy’s halls. The Academy had already assigned everyone their student badges, giving Vir the excuse he needed to explore.

Vir had never delved into the Academy in his own universe, having scarcely visited Alt Ashani a handful of times, all of which were political trips, often meeting with the city’s leaders. He’d briefly visited the institution once to meet with its headmaster, so this exploration was quite the novel experience, and what a place it was.

The Academy had been sizable even in his universe, but here, at the last bastion of civilization in a dying universe? The compound was incomparable.

So much so that its halls formed a labyrinth, with new additions sprouting at random places, as though shoved into wherever they would fit, stretching on and on. And when they ran out of space, they burrowed down into the earth.

This wasn’t a single structure, nor did it exist on only one level, as he’d initially guessed. The Altani magical academy, in addition to holding its upper level classes on the floating island above, dominated a sprawling complex in the lower city. The complex delved deep underground, and after descending several flights of empty stairs, Vir began to wonder just how far the place went.

The magical lift he’d ignored earlier would have brought him down much faster, of course, but it required the charging and discharging of Lighten Load orbs. Even if he’d had his powers, that would have been off-limits.

It was an issue that had plagued him even back in Brij where he’d grown up. Only their abject poverty had minimized the issue’s importance back then.

Vir minded his footsteps and stuck to the shadows wherever possible. Even if he was technically allowed to roam down here, he didn’t want people asking too many questions if he could avoid it.

Most rooms were empty spaces that looked purpose-built to host bouts of magical combat.

He’d initially wondered why the Academy needed such a massive facility given the number of students, even if they did number in the thousands. This answered it. There were combat arenas and experimentation spaces. Even Thaumaturgical facilities, though Vir was sure Saunak would have scoffed at them all.

But what lay on the lower floors? More such rooms? Or something else?

Vir was about to head back when he heard movement in the distance. Instead of lingering as he normally would, Vir beat a track back to the surface. He’d have plenty more opportunities to explore the depths of those halls. Besides, if there were secrets to be uncovered down there, he doubted they’d be accessible to any mere student.

No, his top priority right now was to regain the full extent of his powers. Many more options would present themselves once he did, if only on account of Dance of the Shadow Demon.

Which was why his next stop was the Academy’s library, conveniently located nearby. Though there was a separate library on the floating island above the city, that he’d have to check out later.

What he wanted now was the basics. The information the Altani had uncovered on the new mechanisms of magic, and specifically, how best to manipulate it.

He’d scraped through by the skin of his teeth during the entrance exam, and it had only been favorable luck that had gotten him through. That wouldn’t happen again. Between the upcoming battle exams, regular magical coursework, and the constant displays of magic that students seemed to love showing off to one another, the use of magic was omnipresent in Altani society, and doubly so among the student population.

And so it was with excitement that he walked through the massive double doors to the oval-shaped Cathedral of Learning, its white-stone walls speaking to a level of wealth not even Kin’jal could match.

It was a library unlike any he’d ever seen. Built like a cylinder, with the vast majority of its interior space wasted, the number of books on display still blew Vir’s mind.

For all along its perimeter, soaring from the second story all the way to its ten-story height, were lined bookshelves upon bookshelves, with balconies that ringed the space, allowing access.

Craning his neck, Vir could scarcely make out the top of the building.

Not even bothering to try and search himself, he enlisted the help of a librarian—a middle-aged man—who thankfully didn’t ask him why he wasn’t currently attending the ceremony, though by the glint in his eyes, Vir assumed the man had guessed he was simply an overeager student.

Before long, he was settled at a desk on the first floor with two piles of books. The much larger pile pertaining to the topic of prana manipulation, while the other, smaller pile was on prana affinity conversion.

Two hours later, Vir had learned that while several of the books on prana manipulation mentioned the problem—how could they not—one actually talked about the solutions.

Which, in hindsight, he supposed made sense. The Academy had the keys to a secret so desirable it had royalty from other nations salivating.

This was information that would only be taught. Or was perhaps only recorded in strictly confidential tomes, to be gifted to their worthiest students.

Needless to say, he did not plan on waiting that long. He had to find these books, and he had to find them fast.

Vir then turned his attention to the books on affinity conversion. To his pleasant surprise, he found several leads there, with all the books recognizing the existence of Ash prana, though they called it ‘pure’ prana, which, he supposed, worked equally well.

They’d even done some research into its nature, which, now that Vir thought about it, made perfect sense. If they had in fact managed to enter and control the Vimana, such knowledge would be mandatory.

This gave him hope. Considering how not a single human wielded Ash Prana, it likely meant the Altani of this universe had figured out some way to convert Affinity-aligned prana to Ash, and if that was possible, so too was the inverse.

The books, while they didn’t mention such a thing, boiled it down to two fundamental issues. The first was potency. Ash prana was simply stronger than that of any affinity.

On the surface, it seemed like a simple problem. Dilute it. Reduce the flow.

Unfortunately, the books also revealed that the issue proved far more complex. Prana wasn’t a continuous stream of energy, but rather broken into discrete granules that the books called motes. A mote was the smallest grain that could be manipulated, and it was these that were bound to blood—something Vir had found a way to decouple long ago.

The problem was that Ash prana’s motes were several times more potent than the motes of any other affinity. That meant there was a hard limit to how weak he could make his own magic. One that the authors of the books hadn’t found a way to bypass. Vir doubted it was even possible.

It was ironic, really. There seemed to be no such limit to how much prana he could surge through his body, limited only by his surroundings and his ability to store prana within him.

Weakening it, on the other hand, ran up against the very rules of this rewritten reality itself.

And yet, potency was only half the trouble.

The actual conversion process seemed equally daunting, though on this front, he at least had a few theories.

Mejai and demons alike manipulated ambient prana of a particular affinity to cause effects in the world. Orbs were rigid about this. A Wind Orb could only operate on Wind magic, for example. Demonic arts on the other hand, being a higher-order Imperium magic, could function with several different affinities. It was why some arts, like Aspect of the Eternal Storm, could use either Lightning or Wind as its base, as well as Earth, Life, or Water affinities to further modify its effects.

This was true of most unaffiliated demonic arts. It was why demons from different clans could all use them. That meant inscriptions, at the very least, had some ability to take in multiple forms of prana. While that wasn’t quite what Vir needed, there was surely something there. Some thread he could follow.

But before he could open another book, a voice from behind startled him.

Vir whipped around, cursing himself for losing himself in his work.

“Well well,” a skinny, wrinkled woman with long braided gray hair said, her piercing blue eyes scanning over Vir’s table full of open books. “Prana manipulation? And… affinity conversion?”

The light in her eyes seemed to sharpen. “What curious topics indeed. A new student, yes? Tell me, do you know who I am?”

Vir’s eyes narrowed, only now noticing the blue and white robes. Professors’ robes.

“I’m afraid I don’t.”

“Oh?” the woman said with a smirk. “I teach prana manipulation. For the higher classes, but it seems you’re an odd one. Come. Let us see if your proficiency matches your curiosity.”

Vir cursed under his breath. Why did he have to run into someone like her now, of all times?

Reluctantly, he rose from his chair, praying to all the gods that this secret remained intact.
