I was back in the hallway.

I could make out the grand foyer on one end, lush shades of blue and pearl shimmering in flickering candlelight. The walls around me were luminous with cascades of moonlight through looming, crystal-clear panes of glass. The moon was full and round.

I wonder if the sky ever changes? They called it the ‘Night Court’. Does that mean it’s never daytime? Is there never sunlight? How awful.

It seemed a silly thing to focus on, standing in this cold prison. So instead I turned my attention towards something more important.

The bolt.

I clutched it between my gown and one white-knuckled hand. Rubbed my thumb along glassy wood.

It worked. And now… now I have a way to defend myself.

My nightdress had no pockets, so concealing the weapon was awkward. But not impossible. I tucked gray cloth over the silver, then bunched the fabric together with one hand.

There. I’ll look a bit odd, clutching at my gown like this, but it’s better than nothing.

I chewed my lower lip, looking around. The hallway was empty. Besides wavering shadows, I saw no movement. The house was shrouded in silence. Closed doors lined one side of the wall, each one bathed in ghostly silver. Now what? Should I just wait? Wander?

My eyes slid to one of the tall windows, and to the immaculate sky beyond it. I spent a long moment staring, nearly breathless at the sight.

The foyer. That’s the smart thing to do. Familiar territory, or at least as familiar as it can be. Then if I need to run, I’ll… well. I’ll certainly still be caught, but if I’m to be tortured or killed then at least it will be outdoors and not trapped in all this awful marble.

I squared my shoulders and crept along, flinching at every flickering light, until I was back in front of the grand staircase. It was still dark at the top. The shadows of the manor’s upper level seemed unwilling to yield to starlight.

“Children at play.” The voice was feminine, and very faint.

I jumped, clenching my fingers around the bolt.

“Insolence.” The reply was deep. Him.

I trembled.

It was coming from the very wide hallway underneath the staircase. It stretched- presumably- towards the manor’s far side. I inched closer, holding my breath.

The feminine voice grew louder. “Surely the antics of a few-”

“Shall I use a smaller word for you?” Every word dripped menace. “Treason, perhaps?”

Silence snaked through the foyer. I was now just past the staircase landing, beside another wrought iron sconce, and could see that the space in front of me was less of a transitional area and more like some type of gallery. There were paintings framed in gold and pearl and vivid blue, crystal pedestals set with elegantly curved goblets and sparkling decanters, otherworldly statues carved of ivory and alabaster and stones blacker than sin, and… more doors. One of which was closed, but through which I could make out the muffled voices.

“My lord,” the woman sounded contrite. “I’m on your side. You know that.”

“That’s why she’s in one piece.”

A long pause, then, “This will not happen again.”

“On your word alone?” I could practically picture him sneering.

The woman did not answer. And in the ensuing silence, I heard something else. A sharp, quick inhalation of breath. Coming from just there…

A scream bubbled in my throat. The little Fae from the mist stood beside a statue, peering up at me with her enormous white eyes. Red lips peeled back, baring those thin, curving teeth, and she gave a little hiss.

“I smelled you before.” All traces of sinister playfulness had vanished from the Fae’s piping voice.

I ran. Instinct sent me straight for the grand double doors. I was nearly past the staircase when I slammed to a halt. The little figure was in front of me, blocking the exit, squinting up and sniffling at the air.

“You tried to hurt Forthys!”

I stepped back, fist clenched tightly over the bolt. Every muscle tightened.

Boots tapping against marble echoed from behind. “And now your whelp is planning to make a mess on my carpet, Saerith. I just cleaned it.”

I wasn’t sure whom I was more afraid of, so instead of fully turning to face the danger behind me I sank to one side, heart pounding.

The child stepped back, lowering her eyes as the footsteps grew louder.

“Behave, Vessa,” the woman growled.

I turned and caught my breath.

Just as he was beautiful beyond words, the woman before me was positively mesmerizing. She stood willow-thin, with skin the color of freshly fallen snow. Long silver hair glittered like a cascade of diamonds. Ice-blue veins traced patterns beneath her flesh, and I almost thought I could see the motion of fluid within them. Her eyes were pearl-colored and very large, framed by long lashes that seemed to catch and reflect the flickering candlelight.

I tightened my fist, flinching back at her approach.

But the Fae brushed past, paying me no mind. She swept to her daughter, stooping down.

Vessa mumbled something, flicking her gaze towards mine.

“Yes, but we don’t take food unoffered,” the woman crooned. One elegant, bejeweled hand stroked Vessa’s white hair and her hypnotic eyes settled on me. She looked down her nose, then turned with polite incredulity towards the figure doubtlessly looming behind me. “Have you begun keeping humans, my lord? I’d heard the rumor, but I thought it… unlikely.”

Keeping humans. Like we’re pets. Property. My stomach roiled.

He was silent for a moment. Then, evenly, “Rumor?”

She peered at him through her exquisite lashes. “Ah… apparently lord Embriel caught wind of mortal blood the other night. Out in your lovely garden.”

I shivered, wondering if she was referring to when I had pricked my wrist. Had those few drops of blood been enough for these monsters to smell me?

Her lips curled slyly. “He was quite… intrigued… by it.”

“Mortal blood? What a shame.” He chuckled. “No, Saerith, I’m not amusing myself with livestock. Perhaps someone on the grounds is saving her for later.”

Blood drained from my face at the sheer, casual flippancy of his tone. As if monsters here regularly kept humans around to ‘amuse themselves’ with. I looked over in poorly-contained horror, but he seemed entirely disinterested in my presence.

The Fae woman dipped her head, hair glinting silver. “Of course. I thought it seemed… ah, a bit unlikely, given recent disputes.” Her mouth curled into something between a smile and a sneer. Like the child’s, Saerith’s teeth were too many, too thin, too curved and much too sharp.

“I did not invite you here for gossip.”

She straightened, laying one elegant hand on Vessa’s shoulder. “Of… of course. This,” her fingers tightened, sharp white nails digging into the cloth and flesh beneath it, inciting a whimper, “will not happen again.”

“Good. I’ll trust you to keep your word. And,” he stalked past me, glowering at the smaller figure, “trust me that the next incident ends very differently."

Vessa’s milky eyes fell to the floor. She said nothing, pale hands curling into fists.

Saerith cleared her throat. “Thank you, my lord.”

He inclined his head. “You are dismissed.”

The little Fae’s simmering gaze settled on me again. Her lip curled and those awful, snake-like teeth bared into a snarl. Saerith drew her away, murmuring things in a harsh tone.

I held my breath, heart pounding, until stone scraped against marble. When the door clicked shut, I was left in silence.

But not alone.

He turned, acknowledging me for the first time. “You were later than I expected, mortal. I was beginning to think you wouldn’t sleep.”

I swallowed, trying to calm my racing heart. “If only.”

He chuckled and strolled back through the foyer, gesturing for me to follow. “I have something for you.”

A cage with my name on it. I bit down on the thought and, curious despite myself, followed warily.

He led us further into the gallery and through a door. It opened into a small parlor decorated in lush reds and golds and blacks, with iron bookshelves lining the far wall. He ushered me towards an ornate, circular little table wrought from night-black metal and studded with rubies. On top of it was a small iron chest and a clear crystal decanter filled with garnet-hued liquid. There were two chairs set opposite each other, each lined with plush red cushions. He sat in the far one, then idly picked up and sipped at a crystal goblet. Its contents looked like fresh blood and smelled like currants.

I waited with baited breath, one fist trembling over the concealed bolt.

I am not defenseless. I may be trapped, at least until Teela wakes me up, but I… if he tries to hurt me then I can hurt him, too.

The thought was immediately followed by a cold swell of fear at the idea of what this monster would do if he saw me holding a weapon specifically designed to kill him. My pulse fluttered. I quickly sank into the chair, so the table obstructed his view of my lower half.

He said nothing for a while, just sipped his wine and regarded me, seemingly lost in thought. Eventually the silence was unbearable.

“What you said to that… that woman… is that to be my fate?” My voice quavered. “You’ll just feed me to something after you get what you want?”

He scoffed. “‘Feed you to something…’ How barbaric.”

“You said-”

“I’m sure your flesh and memories and hopes would all make a fine meal for some other Fae, mortal. But I’ve absolutely no interest in watching such a scene.”

Ice crawled down my spine. “M-memories? What…?”

One hand waved absently. “Memories, fears. Secrets, laughter… I could give you quite a list, but you’re starting to look ill.”

I stared in horror, and was indeed beginning to feel viscerally ill. I think I’m going to throw up. It’s not enough for these monsters to kill people, first they… what, take pieces of them?! The nauseating thought was followed by another, and I somehow managed to voice it aloud. “And what… w-what do you… eat?”

The Fae regarded me, eyes glinting like freshly drawn steel. Then he tilted his head to one side, lips curving upwards, and murmured, “Dreams.”

I was on my feet before I even realized, backing towards the hallway on shaking legs. Dreams. He eats dreams. Is he… is that what this is? Saints, what does that even mean?!

Soft laughter snaked through the room. “Don’t run, mortal. I’d rather not chase you.”

One shaking hand clutched the door. The other remained clenched around the sharpened witchwood. “How can I not?” I choked out, staring at the silent, twisting statues and sparkling crystals that loomed ahead. “This ends with me… dying, or devoured, or-”

“You’re such a dour creature.” There was a faint rustle of cloth, as if he’d moved. “This ends with you returning what you stole.”

“Stole?” I whirled around.

The Fae stood near a bookcases, idly thumbing one hand along dark, leather-bound spines and sipping his wine. He glanced my way, then resumed examining his collection. “Stole.”

“By you!” I cried. “You took it from Fellbrook!”

“‘Fellbrook?’”

“My home!”

“Ah. What a… charming… title.”

I stepped forwards, practically quivering with anger.

He glanced over, mouth twitching upwards. One pale hand pulled a heavy-looking tome off the shelf and held it towards me.

I stared. “What am I supposed to do with that?”

“It’s a book. Traditionally, they’re read.” The Fae took another drink, then placed his glass on the table. After a moment’s thought his brows creased in thought. “Ah. You’re well-spoken, so I had assumed… Are you literate?”

“Yes! I meant, why are you giving me a book?”

He leaned forwards, lowering his voice. “For you to read.” Red lips curved.

I swallowed, trying to calm my racing heart. What kind of trick is this? What is he playing at? “Why do you want me to read a book?”

“Because you’re terrified of me. You reek of it.” He chuckled. “Not that you shouldn’t be. But at the moment, you should be looking at me as a friend.”

“Looking at… what? You attacked my home! You nearly killed Royce! Why would I ever believe anything you say?!”

“Many complicated reasons, and the book will shed some light on them. I’m not your enemy, mortal. Truly. I’d like to help you, in fact.”

“Well… I don’t believe you!”

The Fae rolled his eyes skyward, as if searching the ceiling for patience. “Humans are exhausting,” he muttered. “You’re the first one I’ve ever had a conversation with. If your incessant shouting and accusations and tears can all be considered conversation. Is this behavior normal for your kind?”

“It is when we’re being threatened by monsters!”

“You’re shouting again.”

“Of course I’m-!”

“Enough.” He crossed the room in a heartbeat, scowling down from a breath away. “Calm your-”

My arm snapped up. Muscles tight, fingers wound like iron around the bolt now pointed directly at his side. Razor-tipped wood met silk. I felt the resistance of flesh, then the horrible give as my weapon sank in. I heard a hiss of pain, felt a hot exhalation of breath, and then-

I ran.
