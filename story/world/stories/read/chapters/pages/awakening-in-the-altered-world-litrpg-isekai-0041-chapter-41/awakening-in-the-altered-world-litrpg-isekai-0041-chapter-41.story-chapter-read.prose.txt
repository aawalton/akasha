As it turned out, the final boss did have a prescribed area of activity.

Alex felt it as the two of them approached, a wall of shimmering Pure mana—the default choice of mana for rift administrative tasks, it seemed; Alex recalled the chests were also secured by a certain kind of Pure mana.

And the two felt like very different applications of Pure.

Alex only briefly observed the wall, but he couldn’t even tell what precisely it did, besides presumably detection. Which was something he hadn’t even been aware Pure could do.

Pure was pretty much his default mana type, and by its nature it was supposed to be the most flexible kind of mana. It was a nice reminder he’d barely scratched the surface with it—which was also what he was thinking about Force mana already.

And there’s ice, and fire, and space, and time, that I could touch on, he reflected. Even steel and water.

He was quite interested in tinkering with a few of his other affinities a little more seriously once he had a little time.

But first he regarded the nine-ish-foot-tall giant knight frozen in the glacier.

It made quite the striking sight, glimmering in the sparse rays of sunlight filtering through the clouds; the ice wasn’t perfectly clear, blurring its form slightly, so it almost seemed like he was observing a snapshot of blurred motion, captured in film, frozen in the ice.

It felt nice, and somewhat relieving, to turn his attention to this.

The two of them stared in awed silence.

Its sword, which started deep-blue at one end, ran lighter in streaks, until it was very pale, almost white at the edges; very much a glacier of a sword.

Alex identified it.

Frigid Knight

Around it were five somewhat blurry shapes in the icebergs. Smaller armored figures, all Level 5. Clearly its minions. Two of them were slighter in build, and were frozen with twin longswords. Two seemed more like classic knights, with sword and shield. A final minion wielded a staff with a light-blue orb on it shot through with white.

Five Level 5’s and a Level 7.

A decent amount stronger than anything else we’ve fought, and that’s not accounting for the fact that the Boss is likely a higher quality rift creature.

“Before we think about giving this a try,” said Beckett, meeting Alex’s eyes again. “If we think about it, Alex—if you’re not mentally 100%, tell me.”

“I will,” Alex promised. And then—“I feel pretty clear-eyed about this, actually.”

At least, he hoped he was—he felt like he was. He was aware he was feeling certain emotions, but he was also aware it wasn’t in a way that’d affect his discernment, or his ability to fight.

Certain people might’ve fought better animated, or a little emotional; Alex suspected Beckett might be that way. But personally, as long as Alex was certain his mind was clear—that he wasn’t affected, even if he was feeling certain emotions—he’d be fine.

Beckett turned away and said, “Alright;” whatever he’d seen in Alex’s eyes seemed to reassure him. He seemed a little more relaxed, as was Alex.

“The giant sword,” said Beckett after a few seconds. “It feels weird. Are you getting this?”

Alex focused on it, curious; it wasn’t easy to feel past the mana in the iceberg. But once he did, he realized it felt kind of shiny, a little reflective to his soul senses.

Alex saw what Beckett was seeing. “That complicates things,” he said. And then, glancing at Beckett—“We won’t be able tell all of what it’s capable of, obviously,” he said. He wanted to be as objective as possible. “Let’s keep the crystals on hand. If it’s just absolutely insane, we’ll bail.”

He half-expected Beckett would protest a bit more, but all Beckett said was, “Okay.”

The two of them seemed on a similar page right now, in terms of the seriousness with which they were taking this.

Up next, a little strategizing. In this time, Alex realized the ice mana in the icebergs wasn’t perfectly distributed. Certain ice-chunks circling the boss’s legs, rising up from the ground, were denser. Alex wasn’t sure if it was significant, but he did wonder.

In what remained of their time, the two focused on shoring up their final weapons.

This was a continuation of what they were working on, but it also involved a bit of light sparring. A few strands of Force cycled up the structures of Alex’s arms, and back, and legs, creating a kind of reinforced chain; Beckett thrust, and Alex parried, and the jarring sensations felt noticeably gentler than usual.

Beckett, with his gear, still had a slight physical edge, which Alex expected. His experiments with enhancing his body with Force were still quite early on, and it was a slight structural reinforcement so far.

There was also a balance to be struck between acquiring new abilities and going deep, and really exploring the potential of his abilities—ideally he wanted to be as familiar with every one of his abilities as he was with his Mana Shield.

Alex decided he’d apply his abilities intentionally mid-fight and get a little extra skills practice, but that’d be it.

With a few minutes left, Alex and Beckett inspected the frozen boss.

“God, that thing’s sick,” whispered Beckett. He shivered a little.

A few fake Mana Spears were floating behind Alex, while Beckett had a few shells of his own mid-air.

The two of them exchanged a glance before stepping into the clearing.

Alex felt a very slight sensation as he passed through the invisible wall of mana.

For a few seconds, stillness.

CRACK!

Crshhh—CRACK-CRACK!

Here it comes. He felt at once clear-headed and brimming with anticipation. 100% of Alex’s attention was on the icebergs as they brightened, becoming almost blinding. The sunlight in the clearing seemed to increase.

The two of them watched as what they’d expected unfolded, violently.

Seams of brilliant mana carved up the icebergs.

And then, a vast shattering.

Giant chunks of ice-mana tumbling down, shattering into pieces; a mist of ice-mana rose, along with a series of CRSSH-CRSHHH!

The lightest of the minions, the ones dual-wielding thin blades, were free.

Their armors and weapons seemed made of darkened ice, and their bodies were pale and semi-translucent.

Thin sheets of residual ice mana still clung to their bodies in places, but in seconds, it was clear this ice was visibly melting.

Alex’s focus was on the final boss.

It rose very slowly, but its legs were still frozen in blocks of denser ice mana. But like the residual mana on its minions, these dense blocks were also melting.

The Knight didn’t seem in any hurry to free itself; instead it grasped for its giant sword and wrenched it free.

It then regarded the two of them with pale, unseeing eyes.

It leveled its giant sword at the two of them.

Several things happened in quick succession.

The Elementalist thunk’d its staff into the ground. The crystal atop the weapon shone with a peculiar icy energy, before splitting into five beams of light and striking the rest of the minions.

Alex watched, fascinated, as this peculiar mana flowed within each one of the minions, including itself; it seemed to assimilate seamlessly into the creatures’ icy bodies. Some kind of buff?

“As soon as we get a little space—” Alex warned, glancing at Beckett.

He didn’t get a chance to finish. The two dual-wielding Duelists were already springing forward. The two Guardians charged right after; it seemed like Alex and Beckett would be getting one each.

Beckett’s lances were already filled with intertwined fire and lightning, surging forward, as were Alex’s Swordforce-tipped Spears.

Five projectiles, and all five went straight for the Elementalist.

The initial idea was pretty simple; first kill the minions, and especially the caster, while fending off the final boss.

The idea seemed especially sound when the boss didn’t seem interested in joining just yet. Maybe those ice-blocks had to clear first?

Alex’s spears actually arrived first. The Elementalist tapped its staff against the ground, and icy rectangle-shields sprung up, intercepting them.

But Alex’s three spears cleared the way for Beckett’s first fire-lightning lance, which struck the Elementalist square-on. As clean an opening strike as Alex could’ve hoped for, but the peculiar mana within the Elementalist flared at the instant of impact, and Alex saw the Elementalist’s ice-like skin brighten, resisting and blunting the effect of Beckett’s mana.

The instant Alex saw it, he was almost transfixed. What was that?

It wasn’t the effect that caught his attention, but the way the mana worked in that split-second—he was certain he wasn’t just seeing it. I swear there’s something there.

He couldn’t articulate why; it was sheer intuition, like if he paid closer attention—

And then he had to tear his eyes away because, as Beckett’s second lance arrived, the final boss, the Frigid Knight, moved.

It raised its blade leisurely, and as it did, the whites along the edges exploded with mana; within the blade, not simply over it, channeled brilliantly.

The Frigid Knight slashed. A arc of jagged ice mana, fifteen feet tall, whipped through the air.

A portion of it touched the lance, which exploded very peculiarly.

The arc of ice touched the outer shell, which didn’t explode but instead froze, hardening; Beckett’s mana slammed against it, largely dissipating. The little that was able to emerge touched the icy mana, and Alex watched as this stifled explosion was frozen mid-air; the ice-mana hardened over the shape of the explosion, confining it, so that even after its energies died, a cold shell outlining its shape floated for a second mid-air, before falling and shattering into a thousand pieces.

But this was only the middle portion of the arc; the rest of the arc raced against the ground, leaving a long strip of hardened ice, before it slammed against a tree, icing it up.

“That’s fucked up,” said Beckett, watching the explosion-case shatter; he looked somewhere between awed and disgusted.

Alex knew it wasn’t the fact that the Frigid Knight was apparently a freaking spellsword that shocked him; they’d guessed that after seeing its sword, and comparing how it felt to Alex’s bracers.

Alex very much agreed, but he wasn’t looking so much at the frozen explosion; instead the image of the giant arc of ice, flying through the air, was very clear in his mind.

If his intuition told him, I swear there’s something there with the Elementalist’s buff…

Alex stared down at his own sword incredulously.

And then there wasn’t time to think more because the swords-wielding minions were upon them.
