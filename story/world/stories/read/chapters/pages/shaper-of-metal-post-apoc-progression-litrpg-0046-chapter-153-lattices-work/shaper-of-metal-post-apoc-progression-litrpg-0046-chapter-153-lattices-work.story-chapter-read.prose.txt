Jack and Lattice chose to have their meditation session in one of the more isolated parks that dotted the lower surface of the Commons, underneath the very high ceiling where most ANPs had their domiciles.

Under a tree in the pleasant relative wilderness — albeit more obviously indoors than most with the structures so visible above — they sat down cross-legged facing one another. Lattice had her shades up on her forehead, just in front of her extensive braid that left the sides just a brown fuzz.

Eyes of blue crystal regarded him placidly as she said, “Here’s the biggest secret, Ferro: don’t close your eyes. That wins the lion’s share of this shit if you can manage it. Throw away all the aides before you ignore this suggestion. Yeah, it’s creepy to anyone around, but to the gutters with ‘em; they can just deal with it. Closing your eyes is a pointless affectation.”

Jack blinked a few times at her as he nodded thoughtfully. So much for my eye pillows. Probably, anyway. “Did you learn this because of something with how your eyes are?”

She frowned slightly. “Are you jumping to that conclusion just because of this obvious abnormality of mine being at the forefront of your perceptions?”

Jack took a moment to ponder that. “I’m not sure. I was wondering if you possibly found closing your eyes less natural, effective, or comfortable than others.”

Lattice stared at him for a moment before shaking her head. “I don’t get it. You were a flytaxi, and stumbled into all this randomly — skip-to-my-lou on a highwire — yet time and again, you show all this obsessive observation and deduction digging, even dog-ass tired like this. Like, who fraggin’ cares? Ferro, that’s who. He’s gotta know, know, know. You’d memorize the license plate of the car that knocked your head off. Why didn’t you get into military intelligence or something like that? Why piloting?”

Jack chuckled at her colorful characterization and shrugged. “Caught the fever for the airways, I guess. I did consider other paths. Just walked a different one.”

She snorted. “You should’ve never been where you were. You should’ve been another ‘Agent Vacation’ dryly providing us mission specs.”

“I dunno if I could handle being that dry. I’m often parched as it is.” At Lattice snorting again, Jack smiled and asked, “What about you? Are you where you’re supposed to be, or did you have other plans way back when? Miss a calling?”

Lattice sighed, shook her head, and looked away, muttering, “Of course you want the life story routine. Fine. That’s only fair with all the nitty-gritty we got about you in the reports. First of all, I was born blind. This was an exceptionally rare and unfortunate occurrence in our society, because with all our tech, they don’t tend to go modding around on babies. Despite painstaking preventative measures, shit still happens, doesn’t it?

“I got into a program at age five to get bio-implant replacements, but the tech was such that they didn’t grow with me, so they had to be replaced almost yearly. At least I got to see. Such a drastic change in my perceptions made me especially fascinated by the visual arts. That’s what I was into and what I wanted to do: be an artist. I even had an option class-wise that would’ve been beautiful in representing that fascination, but I didn’t end up picking it.”

She shrugged. “My father put his ass on the line out there almost exclusively so I could be in that program. He died in the line of duty when I was ten. At first, I thought I’d honor him by pursuing what his sacrifice allowed me to in the first place. Everyone told me he’d want me to keep pursuing my dreams.

“But after my implants excruciatingly popped out of my sockets at my Awakening, replaced by these things, these orbs searing my brain with a light I can’t describe… and soon ready to transform one of three different ways, my soul with them… I ended up choosing differently. Mostly because I was different; new. I came from trauma, and through greater trauma, was changed. Not healed… changed.”

She shifted in her seat on the grass, pausing in possible thoughtfulness, though it was hard to tell with her cool, controlled expression. “I can’t say if my father would approve of me racing out there after him, but I’ve done enough to exceed all expectations of a birth like mine. I didn’t do it out of revenge. I did it because of who I am — how I choose to honor my place in the world and all who brought me forth into it.” She gestured at Jack with a hand. “Does that satisfy you, Ferro?”

Jack nodded soberly. “You can call me Jack, you know.”

“I won’t.”

“I’m sorry about your father.”

“Thanks. I’m sorry about yours, too. Living or not, that’s nothing to envy.”

“Yeah. Thanks. And by the way, your powers are still beautiful.”

She took a long moment to decide how to take that, her demeanor rather guarded. “I guess. They’re rather simple and inflexible for the most part, and generally see-through on top of it.”

“I mean that they save lives. There is nothing more beautiful than that.”

Lattice stared at him incredulously for a moment, and then developed a little lopsided grin. It felt very much like making Bo smile. Amused and perhaps fighting off laughter, she shook her head and said, “Alright, Mr. Sentimental, enough of your shit. Start practicing and stop talking.” She pulled out a little wireless speaker and wiggled it. “I’d suggest your music be external. Helps with the sense of attuning to your environment.”

As she set it down, she hid a yawn behind her hand, which made Jack grimace as he fought off doing it himself.

Under waterfall sounds, a relative quiet descended. Before he forgot, he summoned memorite and created a tiny Anchor of the minimum power needed, forming it into a teardrop in the grass. Otherwise, his power usage remained minimal and indefinitely sustainable.

Jack turned his body sideways to face the nature scene, since looking at Lattice was not going to make doing this with his eyes open any easier. Quickly, it was feeling awkward and unnatural to him. He’d always closed his eyes.

“Don’t try too hard at first,” Lattice said softly. “You won’t get into Calibration immediately. Get used to this, first. How? Well, you know you best. If focusing on breathing works well, do that.”

He went with that, and it wasn’t long before the fatigue wore at him and he began fighting nod-offs.

Behind a yawn, Jack said, “This is great for putting me to sleep. Even keeping my eyes open is difficult. Same if I focus on the sounds… that’s just too damn relaxing.”

“Hmm, well, let’s change that up,” Lattice said, and switched the music to some weird harmonic stuff.

“What is that?”

“Don’t worry about it. Just shut up and try it. Listen and absorb.”

Jack did so. Listening to it, it was somehow both relaxing and epic, and vaguely reminded him of some ‘experimental rock’ type stuff. Heavy on the rhythmic vibes.

After a while, Jack said, “This is good shit. Gonna have to give me the details later.”

Lattice ‘tsk'd’ in annoyance. “You just can’t shut up, can you? Turn your damn brain off already.”

Jack blinked. “Oh. Turn my brain off! Why didn’t you say so? I can do that easily. Alright…”

He thought about absolutely nothing. Even the music was perfect for it, disappearing like a part of the environment.

…

Perhaps partially from being in a bit of a hysterical state, he didn’t fall asleep. Only after a while did a rogue thought crop up like a reminder of why he was doing this in the first place.

I should segue this into Calibration.

Gentle, easily, like slipping past a veil, he did so.

There was Star Memoria floating in space, both hands slapping her cheeks in overdramatized surprise. “Oh my, Jack! You’ve done it with ease!”

Jack grinned smugly. “So it seems, so it seems.”

Memoria returned the grin and gave a thumbs-up. “It could be more challenging in sleep deprivation scenarios, but it's a good start. Well, you’re here. I take it you want to try to sleep as the next part of this little experiment?”

Jack looked around the relative spatial void, nodding uncertainly. “Not sure how feasible that is. The environment isn’t exactly conducive.”

Memoria laughed. “This is entirely virtual! An easy fix. After all, you chose the void.” She snapped her fingers, and suddenly everything became more solid on a dime, and they were inside a contained room. A bedroom, and Jack had on striped blue pajamas.

Memoria appeared in a very motherly-looking dress of red-and-white checkers, like a housewife from the 1950s. She smiled and leaned toward him. She was still oversized. “Shall I tuck you in, little Jack?”

Jack grimaced as he backed away and waved his hands. “Uhh, this won’t work. It’s way too creepy. Sorry. You’re overdoing it. Mother.” He cleared his throat.

The uncanny housewife made a faux-sad face with her hands on her hips. “Awww. Is little Jack feeling embarrassed? Let’s get you more comfortable, then.” She snapped her fingers.

A replica of his old room in Everywhere Hall appeared around him, mostly dark, though any light was plenty for him. Memoria, now looking like her default self in the suit, poked her head in from the door. She smiled and winked at him. “Hollar if you need anything, dear!” With that, she closed the door.

Wow, she even went through the right door.

Jack shook his head as he looked at himself. He was wearing simple sweatpants and a plain t-shirt. Taking off the shirt and tossing it aside, he hopped on the bed and lay down.

He didn’t really feel tired here — at least, not in the same way. He was aware of fatigue, but it felt distant… possessed by another person somewhere. It was actually disconcerting having a more physical-feeling body in this place, because he had the vague sense of his real body elsewhere, though he lacked proper information. He suddenly understood why the more ‘spiritual’ body form was better for Calibration.

Nonetheless, he got under the covers and closed his eyes.

It wasn’t long before he entered another unique and strange unconscious state. It wasn’t quite sleep, and there were no dreams. Multiple times, he flickered back into consciousness, and a new, vague sense of time was there on a dime. He was never sure how much time had truly passed, but each occurrence of awareness, he resisted asking and slipped back ‘away.’

Finally, he could endure the oddity no longer and sat up, running a hand through his hair, feeling little more than restlessness. “Memoria?”

She appeared before him in a flash, a faint smile on her lips. “Sleep well, son?”

“I don’t think so,” Jack said with a sigh. “How much time passed?”

“Two hours.”

Jack nodded thoughtfully. “It can certainly kill some time. Um… how is my body?”

“Well, it’s a bit stiff after sitting this long, but remains pretty much as you left it. The Anchor remains stable. I’m not exactly sure how you’ll respond to your body when you’re back. You’ll just have to return and see. On goes the experiment, hmm?”

“Yeah. What are the odds this works like sleep?”

“Zero. But I think it’s better than trying to hold your eyes open. High probability that it’s useful.”

“Really?”

“Mm-hmm. Not get out of here!” She clapped her hands loudly. “Chop chop! Prepare for a return to meat space!”
