Suffice it to say, Dragonspire's final plans underwent significant revisions. Starting with the time they'd attack.

The plans were revised to extend the attack date by three weeks.

They all knew it was a significant trade-off—at this stage in Malzareth's recovery, every week made a difference.

It also meant that Zane would have almost a full four weeks to work with. It was Dragonspire's final tactic.

If it wasn't Reina requesting this—and if it weren't for Zane specifically—it would've been impossible to push through the Council.

And it was very transparent that this was a measure made just for him. It was a simple bet that the improvements he made in those three extra weeks would exceed what Malzareth managed to recover in that same time.

By now Zane's System Store was an open secret, and with how the War had turned out so far—including the decimation of Malzareth's army and the downing of a Prime—he and Reina had some political capital to work with.

It also helped that—under soul contracts—she briefed the Grand Council on the specifics of his Store's Section V and gave key elites the chance to spar with him in the coming weeks.

After that it wasn't at all difficult for her colleagues on the Council to understand her decision.

In this time Zane made his final improvements.

First he purchased his Unlimiters, and an invisible line which constrained his shard counts fell away.

When he'd first maxed out his Destruction shard count it'd felt like he'd hit some hard wall in his soul. It'd felt like it simply couldn't carry any more capacity.

He purchased his Unlimiter of the 1st Edict and his soul seemed identical afterward, but the difference was instantly noticeable when he tried to take in a new shard.

This resistance he'd always felt in it had vanished. His soul could simply handle significantly more shard count, which he took advantage of right away.

The days went by, and shards flowed in.

Shard of Destruction Integrated!

Shard of Destruction Integrated!

...

Shard of Destruction Integrated!

His Reality Distortion Field looked essentially identical after it took in the 'Universal Field' passive—the passive left almost no trace of its presence. He assumed the passive simply allowed his Field to handle the extra shards, to act as a shard sink, somewhat akin to how Empyrean Universes worked. Every new shard he integrated was stored directly in his Distortion Field. It was still his soul, just an extension of it.

The Unlimiter of the 3rd Edict had a similar effect on his body. He'd felt this increasing resistance to every new Creation shard—much less than with his Destruction, but it was there. Then that body limit fell away, and the rate at which he could take in Creation shards saw a noticeable uptick as well.

This four weeks' time was carefully calculated. It should be just enough time to finish up all his goals.

And he didn't purchase his physical Unlimiter directly, since it was redundant—he simply took in the Aeons Titanbone.

The Bone, like his last bone, carried a notification:

Note: Any bone which survives 100 Million Years has survived the rise and fall of a Chaos Cycle. It is known as an Immortal Bone and cannot be destroyed by conventional means.

It'd appeared before him on a bed of System light. It was the size and shape of a thick shield, curved irregularly all the way through, in places most shields wouldn't curve. It was also blemishless and looked like a piece of pearly steel from top to bottom.

It had a simple, understated grandeur to it—kind of like his 100-Million-Year Bones, or like some other ancient Origin-grade items he'd seen. It didn't need a showy aura; he touched it, and inspected it, and that was enough to ascertain its quality immediately. It seemed to him a Bone that let what it was speak for itself.

In the Astral Plane it was visually a bit more remarkable. Tiny time-Laws smoked off it in the Astral Plane, and there were other silver Laws endowed within the material of the bone—quite profound Laws he couldn't begin to parse. He was pretty certain it was a fragment of some minimum—T8 Law, but not Creation or Destruction... something akin to the complete versions of Time or Fate, maybe, which were also theorized to be T8 Laws—or at least, T8 Concepts, if such things existed.

The description carried yet another note:

This is a Cornerstone Bone.

A limited number of such bones exist across any Godbeast species. This is one of six that exist in the noble line of the Titan Rhinoceros. It is the newest of its cohort, designated with distinction 89 B.S.E.

And with it came the promised Cornerstone Skill.

Skill learned!

Last Will of the Titan [Unique]

This is a Cornerstone Skill.

If the mind has not broken, why must the body?

Substitutes the soul for the body while on the brink. Damage will strike the soul first before the body. Note that if the soul takes critical damage, this spell will end.

Damage taken in this state bypasses all soul defenses and therefore should only be accessed as a last resort.

Let the Will of the Titan reign supreme.

This was the Skill that, as far as Zane was could tell, justified about half that 6,000 Credit price tag.

He wasn't sure how useful it'd be for others, but for him specifically, this could be quite useful—he could definitely see the price. Even with that caveat that the damage would bypass his Pagoda.

He didn't plan for the effect to be necessary, but it was undoubtedly a nice bit of insurance.

Notably, it wasn't saying that so long as his will stayed unbroken, he couldn't be killed; it specifically referred to his soul and soul damage. Reina had made that point to him when they'd analyzed the Skill; otherwise...

Frankly—his will had never been broken before. And she was certain Malzareth didn't have what it took to manage that feat. If it really were based on only his will, he would've gladly paid a lot more.

***

One week flowed seamlessly into the next. It didn't feel to Zane like a true training camp; it felt more like a start-to-end sprint.

Shards taken in, one after another. Soul, and then body, and then soul—after he finished the Bone and purchased the edicts, they were his first priority.

After a week, he sat at over 700 shards of Destruction, and over 400 shards of Creation.

After two weeks he'd maxed out Destruction and focused all his attention on Creation.

In this time—

Skill evolved!

Asura Titan's Body, Fifth Form (Mythic -> Mythic+)

Skill up!

Skill up!

...

Skill up!

Asura Titan's Body, Fifth Form IX -> X

If he'd thought his pace of improvement was fast before, what he underwent in those four weeks defied imagination.

He allowed himself almost no rest. He almost felt like some athlete with the finish line in his sights—he simply couldn't bring himself to rest.

And by the end of those four weeks, his stats looked something like:

Vitality: 113.655B

Regeneration: 202.938B

Strength: 122.486B

Dexterity: 94.329B

Speed: 100.137B

Suffice it to say—his body had reached figures the likes of which Dragonspire had never seen.

And still, somehow, that wasn't all he'd done.

Because there was still time for a single Concept at the end—one Concept only, since it'd simply take far too much time, and they'd decided that the tradeoff wasn't worth it, given what he already had.

And it was a Concept he wouldn't have been able to manage if he didn't purchase that 2,400 Credit Universal Prism.

He decided on Stellar Collapse—it was the most bang-for-buck of the Supernova Concepts, due to its dual effects on Supernova's 'loading phase' and also the spatial aspects it added to his gravity.

The Universal Prism captured an instance of a Supernova far outside this Galaxy—and, the way a prism can filter out light, it allowed him to filter out Law, and essence, and all the other cluttering noise which might distract from an insight.

It was stunning to feel the first time. Learning a Concept was always an abstract and difficult endeavor; the Prism was so precise in its filtering, and the fidelity with which it captured its Law, that it almost felt like it was delivering insights directly into his mind—it was such a precise tool that it was pretty much impossible to avoid understanding after seeing. It almost felt like a much improved version of those crystals he'd seen in the Pure Yang Starfields.

He was helped quite a bit in this time by conversations with Striker and Noughtfire as to what precisely to look for; they helped him design a mini-curriculum of the Concept. And they were absolutely world experts on the subject.

After he was finished, he couldn't imagine how it would've been possible to learn that Law faster.

The rest of his Concepts would have to come at a later stage.

The Superdungeon stayed relatively quiet during this time but its Corruption Levels had swelled—nowhere near its previous levels, but the Scryer's Guild believed that Malzareth had at least stabilized its wounds; the precise degree of its recovery was unknown. The Guild expected a slight comeback in the size and strength of its army as well.

And then all was done.

And it was time to conduct the final exercise of the war.

It was time to delve the Superdungeon.
