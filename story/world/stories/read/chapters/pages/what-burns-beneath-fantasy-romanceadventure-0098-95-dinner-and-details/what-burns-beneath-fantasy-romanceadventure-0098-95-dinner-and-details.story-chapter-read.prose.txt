I opened my eyes to dizzying streams of moonlight and polished white marble. Spots danced along the corners of my vision and I sat down hard. I expected the slam of hard stone against my backside, but was instead met by plush fabric.

A breathless glance around showed that I was at the manor’s back end. The familiar main hallway stretched away into darkness not fully pierced by moonlight or scattered, pale candles. Statues and paintings gleamed from the sides and closed doors lined the walls. And ahead… the huge window loomed crystal-clear, illuminating a sea of swaying flowers and a cascade of stars that took my breath away.

Velidan reclined in a burgundy, satin-lined chaise nearby. He had his long legs crossed at the ankles and a thin book propped open in his lap. His blood-smeared garments had been traded out for a creamy silk shirt and dark pants that draped down to even darker boots.

A graceful crystal pedestal between us was topped with a silver platter of half-eaten food. The smells hit me as intensely as the vibrant colors; sunset-hued cheese scented like a campfire, strips of cured, smoky-smelling meat, and a thick, bronze wedge of bread that made my mouth water. And, of course, a thin-stemmed glass of ruby-red wine.

The Fae watched silently as I rubbed my temples, blinking the spots away.

Then, still swelling with all my brazen hopes and the recent triumphs of my new runes- regardless of how confusing they were- I turned to him, took a deep breath, and announced with perhaps more pride than I’d ever felt in my life, “I made a bee.”

He regarded me for a very long while. Finally, after closing his book and taking a thoughtful sip of wine, “I don’t know what that means.”

I started laughing. I couldn’t help it. My shoulders shook and I tangled both hands in my hair, looking around helplessly. “I made a bee!”

“Thank you for clarifying.”

His dry tone, and the look on his face, only made me laugh harder. Giddy, baffled, and slightly hysterical giggles peeled out as I collapsed back onto the cushions. The sounds echoed around us, ringing off of marble. “I used a rune! Zellen. And a bee appeared!”

“Ah.” He snorted. “You didn’t make a bee, Brin. That would be ridiculous. You summoned a bee.”

I grinned, eyes tracing the play of shadows and moonlight along the glittering chandelier. It shone pure and bright in the light, looking for all the world like a waterfall of stars. “I thought so. Like how I summoned you?”

There was a short silence. Then, darkly, “Yes.”

“Was it from your garden?”

“What?”

“The bee. Did I take it away from here?” I sat up, teeth sinking into my lower lip. “I don’t know how to send it back, and I hate the thought that it’s just out there lost now. I hope it’s not frightened.”

He blinked at me over the goblet’s rim. “It’s an insect, Brin.”

“Yes, and I took it from its home! I pulled it away from its family! And it definitely had one. I saw them when I looked through the… the...” My breath caught as I pictured the swarm of insects flying straight towards me from a sea of sunshine-tipped emerald leaves.

My gaze trailed to the glass window and the constellations outside. The endless silver and the impossibly luminous moon.

And during the day…

“It wasn’t from here!” I cried. “It… it couldn’t have been! There’s no sun here, and I saw sunlight when it came through!”

Velidan’s gaze settled on me, glittering with what almost looked like mirth. His mouth curved up at one corner and he offered, in an impossibly casual tone, “Then it was probably from the Day Court.”

I gaped at him, shaking my head in sheer incredulity. Then I lunged upright and began pacing. My boots clicked along the marble and his obvious amusement grew with every frantic step.

“The Day Court. Sure. Sure. Sure. Why not? If I can accidentally rip a High Fae out of here,” I gestured wildly towards both him and the window, “then why shouldn’t I be able to yank a salted bee out of somewhere else?” Wild, breathless laughter bubbled from my lips. “Stars above, what do I know about how magic actually works?! Why shouldn’t I be able to pull bees from a Fae realm?!”

He chuckled. “Fae realms are where all magic comes from, Brin. Not just yours.”

I stopped midstep, turning to squint at him. “Magic comes from the gods. I know that much.”

“Do you?” He set the glass down and leaned back, hands laced behind his head, watching me with a sly smile. “Perhaps some education is in order. That history lesson I spoke of, though it might take a while.”

“We have time.” I spread my hands, offering a tentative little grin. “I’m literally a captive audience.”

He snorted at that. Then he paused, eyes narrowed and head tilted to the side, regarding me with an unsettling weight. “You’re… different tonight.”

I waited for more of an explanation, but he didn’t give one. After several long breaths I cleared my throat and sank back into the empty chaise. “I… I had a nice evening.” Expectant silence crept along the marble and I bit down on a smile. “I did some new magic and it barely hurt at all, it just made me a little dizzy at the end, and Ren- er, I h-had a nice night.” I ducked my head, trying to chase away the sudden rise of a blush. “The best one in a while, actually.”

“Mm.”

A dozen more thoughts chased that deluge, rising on the tide of Joselia’s story about my mother and Renner’s hands on my waist and the wild thrill of magic. I’m not afraid of you. Not right now. My mother put all this into motion, she wanted all this to happen, and right now… right now I’m brave.

I’m beloved.

But those thoughts were too precious to share with him, so I just smiled. “Even supper was nice. More for the company than the conversation or the food, though. It was actually a lot like yours,” I nodded towards his platter as one pale hand lifted the hunk of bread, “though probably not nearly as good. The bread was a bit stale, and the cheese tasted like it had been sitting out all day.”

Velidan paused with his mouth half-full. He chewed slowly, swallowed, and cast a cold glance in my direction. “You ate old food for dinner?”

One shoulder rose in a shrug. “Like I said, it was the company that made it nice.”

Full, red lips curled in obvious disdain. “So your definition of a ‘nice evening’ is one in which you were barely injured and ate stale food?”

I sighed. Right. Just like the bed. Stars forbid he ever not be wrapped in silk and served only the finest of wines. He’d probably murder someone.

Then, with a sudden resurgence of the evening’s fierce courage as his glare fell from me and towards his own dinner, I took a deep breath and burst out, “Do you eat hearts?”

He blinked, clearly not having expected the question. “If they’re properly prepared.”

“Right. Um… human hearts, too?”

One black eyebrow inched its way upwards. “I… haven’t indulged. Though I’m told they’re quite the delicacy.”

A little growl rose in my throat at that. My nails dug into my palms. “Well, are you… planning to?”

He chuckled. “Are you offering?”

“No!”

“That was a joke, Brin.”

“Well, it wasn’t funny!” I snapped. “And if you’re planning to eat me, then the least you could do is… is tell me, or reconsider, or… s-saints, I don’t even-”

Confusion rose alongside his obvious amusement. “Mortal, have I given you the impression that I’m going to eat you?”

My face grew hot. “No, but… alright, are you planning to use my heart in some kind of nefarious, wicked ritual?”

“I’m not planning to.” He cocked his head to one side, dark hair hissing against creamy silk, and added thoughtfully, “Though I suppose if an opportunity presents itself...”

I glared at him, arms folded and spine ramrod straight.

“That was another joke.”

My teeth ground together as Horace’s amused, ominous claim echoed through the back of my mind. “So you’re not… not going to eat me?”

Those endless, star-lit eyes glittered wickedly. “Not unless…” he smirked, then inclined his head as his face smoothed into a neutral expression. “Mm. No.”

I kept glaring for a while, but the Fae’s only answer was an inscrutable stare. Finally I sank back into the cushions, breath gusting out. Both hands lifted to tangle through my hair.

It’s not as though he’d admit it, though, would he? Although… Horace isn’t exactly transparent with all his ominous warnings and predictions and…

And perhaps I should focus on something a bit more helpful, at the moment. While he’s being… talkative. Saints, he’s almost being pleasant.

The thought gave me pause. I peered at Velidan’s carved features through my lashes. He was being surprisingly pleasant, especially when our last meeting had ended with… well, with me trapped between him and a table as he warned me about being insolent.

But now…

I eyed him, torn between cautious suspicion- my certainty that this veneer of good humor would soon shatter- and the fierce, giddy courage still licking at my heart.

I sucked in a deep, steadying breath, willing my limbs to relax, and leaned forwards. Crimson cushions shifted beneath me and moonlight rippled along the fabric. “This… history lesson. Will you tell me about runes? About magic?”

Something like approval flickered across his face. “Gladly, but not until later.”

“Why-”

“You owe me an answer.”

My pulse thrummed at the sudden drop in pitch. Not a menacing lowness, but… one that definitely veered from pleasant.

Because if I say no, his threat still stands. We’ll sit here and hopefully stay cordial until I wake up, but then tomorrow night he’ll appear and drag me away from a bloodbath and my dying friends no matter how much I protest.

Because, no matter how pleasant he’s being, I’m still a prisoner.

I swallowed, closing my eyes and curling my fingers into my hem. When I managed to find my voice, it was hoarse. “This… event. What can you tell me about it?”

He thought for a moment. Then, every word measured, “I don’t know what to expect from the specific occasion. Embriel’s been tight-lipped. But I know we’re going to a place called The Endless Pit.”

“Sounds cheery.”

“It’s a spectacle arena, a feeding ground, and a slave auction all in one.”

My mouth went dry. “For?”

He held my gaze, voice edged in steel. “For any creature a High Fae deems suitable.”

“Oh. R-right. Saints.” I shivered.

“And given your intense concern for a bee earlier,” he scoffed, “I’ll remind you that you’ll need to behave. Charging off to try and save a sprite’s screaming infant or something equally foolish will end very poorly for you.”

I searched those fathomless eyes, feeling the blood drain from my face. “A-and you… you go to this place?”

He blinked. “What?”

“You go there? To watch other creatures get hurt? F-fed on? Sold?”

Velidan opened his mouth, looking as though he was about to snarl a very angry answer, then snapped it shut with an audible click. When he finally spoke, his voice held a bite of menace. “Mortal, what I do in my spare time is none of your concern. And if you agree to this, we’ll need to have a stern discussion on etiquette.”

“It was just a question,” I whispered, curling back against the cushions. I gnawed my lower lip. “And… in this place, I’ll be safe? Even though there will be monsters everywhere?”

He sneered at that. “You’re my property, Brin. No one’s going to touch you any more than they’d try to steal my boots.”

A chill snaked its way through my belly, squeezing out echoes of that horrifying calm claim. I looked at him for a long while, feeling like I could collapse into tears and seize him by the throat and scream all at the same time. But my lips felt numb, and all I managed was, “Oh.”

He leaned back, watching me through hooded eyes. Ruby wine sloshed within the crystal glass as he took a slow sip.

I rubbed both hands along my upper arms, trying to chase away the chill. “But it… you really think it might help stop this other Fae? Help protect the city, save lives?”

“That’s my hope. But I can make no promises because, as I’ve said, I don’t know what to expect.”

My fingers delved into a pocket, closing around dusty, folded parchment. “I may have a clue.”

“Oh?”

I handed him my charcoal-smudged letter. “We found this carved into a barrow outside the city. Some of the paladins said they’d seen something like it before, in a cave, and I… I don’t know, exactly, but I think it looks like a rune. It feels like a rune. And you said you recognized some of mine, so I thought maybe you’d know what it means.”

Velidan leaned forwards, knees braced wide apart. One pale hand lifted the paper while the other swirled his goblet absently. He examined my crude sketch, lips pursed in thought.

After a few breaths I paced towards the window. Every plant outside rippled in a gentle breeze. The tips and edges of each vibrant petal was tinged silver beneath the moonlight, giving the garden an ethereal glow. I pressed my fingers to the cool glass and leaned against it, unable to stifle a longing little sigh before such beauty.

After some time the silence behind me turned to icy stillness, as though a frost had settled through the room. I turned, dreading what awful realization he might have come to… and went rigid. My heart nearly stopped.

Velidan had turned the parchment over. The side facing me was the charcoal drawing, and the side he now examined with a deep crease between both brows was-

My letter to Durst.
