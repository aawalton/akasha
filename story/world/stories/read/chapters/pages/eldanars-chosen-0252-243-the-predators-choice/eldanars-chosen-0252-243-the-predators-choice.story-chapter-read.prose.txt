The corrupted northern forest was no longer a silent, brooding wasteland. It was a deafening, chaotic nightmare of snapping timber, howling wind, and the relentless, terrifying pursuit of the dead.

Perberos sprinted through the freezing undergrowth, his boots completely silent against the frost-covered moss. He wove through the dense, dead pine trees with liquid, effortless grace. His elven physiology, naturally attuned to the deep woods, allowed him to navigate the treacherous terrain purely by instinct. He did not trip over hidden roots, and he did not snag his leather armour on the grasping, thorny briars.

But the two men running ahead of him did not possess those same woodland instincts.

Ronald and Garth were veteran hunters, but they were exhausted, battered, and running entirely on the fumes of their adrenaline. The feline beastkin was leading the way, his muted green cloak practically invisible in the gloom, but his breathing was ragged and harsh. Garth, the massive human tracker, was faring even worse. The grizzled man was stumbling heavily, his boots slipping on the slick, black mud, the heavy weight of his massive crossbow dragging him down.

The sound of the pursuit was apocalyptic.

Thousands of lesser zombies, corrupted forest beasts, and the terrifying, silent phalanx of the Wight elite guard were crashing through the forest just a few hundred yards behind them. The dark magic of the Bone General was actively pushing the undead forward, granting them unnatural, terrifying speed. The horde did not care about avoiding trees or navigating the terrain. They simply smashed blindly through the underbrush, snapping thick branches and tearing the forest apart in their singular, mindless drive to run the assassins down.

Perberos risked a glance over his shoulder.

Through the suffocating, swirling necrotic fog, he could see the glowing green eyes of the skeletal hounds leading the pack. They were closing the distance.

The elven scout smoothly unslung his curved longbow without breaking his stride. He did not bother reaching for his heavy, armour-piercing bodkins. He drew three standard iron broadheads simultaneously, nocking them between his long fingers. He pivoted his torso backward, aiming blindly into the darkest, most violently moving section of the brush, and released the string.

Three sharp, hollow shrieks echoed over the cracking timber as the arrows found their marks. The sound of tumbling bodies and breaking bone briefly disrupted the front line of the pursuit, but the delay lasted only a fraction of a second before the hounds scrambled over their fallen packmates.

It was entirely useless. They were trying to empty a flooding river with a teacup.

"Through here!" Ronald roared, his raspy voice cracking violently.

The beastkin ducked low, sliding beneath the massive, rotting trunk of a fallen oak tree that spanned a shallow, freezing creek bed. Garth practically threw himself under the timber, splashing heavily into the freezing, stagnant water. Perberos vaulted smoothly over the top of the trunk, landing silently on the opposite bank to cover their rear.

Ronald did not keep running. The beastkin scrambled up the muddy bank and came to a dead, sudden halt in the middle of a small, dead clearing.

Garth dragged himself out of the freezing water, collapsing onto his hands and knees in the mud. The tracker was gasping for air, his chest heaving violently. A thick stream of dark blood trickled from his nose, a clear sign of severe physical overexertion.

Perberos dropped into a low crouch beside them, his bow drawn, his silver eyes scanning the treeline they had just crossed. The grinding, crashing roar of the horde was growing deafeningly loud.

"Why are we stopping?" Perberos demanded, his voice a harsh, urgent whisper. "They are less than a minute behind us. We need to keep moving."

Ronald slowly stood up, turning to face the elven scout.

The beastkin looked incredibly old in the pale, filtered morning light. The usual cocky, predatory arrogance that defined him was completely gone. His tawny fur was matted with freezing mud and sweat, and his jade-green eyes were filled with a heavy, profound sorrow.

"I know what you are doing, Perberos," Ronald stated quietly, his voice perfectly steady despite his ragged breathing.

Perberos frowned, maintaining his guard. "I am covering our rear flank. We need to move."

"You are tethering yourself," Ronald corrected, shaking his head slowly. "I have watched you train in the Guildhall courtyard. I have seen you spar. I know you possess a high-tier Shadow Jump skill. You can physically step through the veil of darkness and cover massive distances in a fraction of a second."

Perberos tightened his grip on his bow, a cold knot forming in his stomach. He knew exactly where this conversation was heading. "The mana cost is severe. I cannot carry passengers through the shadow veil."

"I know you cannot," Ronald smiled, a sad, knowing expression. "And that is why you have not used it. You are deliberately holding back. You are running at a human pace because you refuse to leave us behind in the dark."

The crashing of timber grew louder. A massive, dead pine tree violently snapped in half just beyond the creek, announcing the imminent arrival of the vanguard.

"We do not leave our squad behind," Perberos argued fiercely, the stoic mask completely fracturing to reveal the desperate loyalty underneath. He had already lost Silas. He absolutely refused to lose anyone else. "We all walk out of this forest together."

Garth pushed himself up from the mud, leaning heavily against the trunk of a dead tree. The grizzled tracker spat a thick wad of dark blood and chewing tobacco onto the frost.

"The boss is right, lad," Garth grunted, his voice a low, painful wheeze. He unhooked his heavy crossbow, setting it carefully against the roots of the tree. He pulled a massive, jagged hunting knife from his belt. "We are slowing you down. My lungs are bleeding, and Ronald’s legs are completely shot. We are not going to outrun skeletal hounds."

"I can provide covering fire," Perberos insisted, taking a step towards the older men. "I can blind the Wights. We just need to reach the eastern hills."

"Perberos, listen to me," Ronald commanded, his voice suddenly ringing with the sharp, uncompromising authority of a senior Guild instructor.

The beastkin stepped forward, placing his heavy, clawed hands firmly onto the elf's leather-clad shoulders. He looked directly into Perberos's luminous silver eyes.

"We signed up for a suicide mission," Ronald stated plainly, stripping away all illusions. "We marched into a camp of ten thousand monsters to kill a god. We knew the mathematics before we ever left the city gates. We accepted the cost."

Ronald tightened his grip, giving the scout a small, firm shake.

"But your job is not finished," the beastkin continued, his green eyes burning with fierce conviction. "The General is still alive. The city is still in extreme danger. Your sister is standing on those walls, and she needs you. Josh and Brett need you. You are one of the only fighters in this entire province capable of turning the tide of that siege."

"I cannot just abandon you to the dead," Perberos whispered, his voice trembling violently with the sheer, crushing weight of the choice.

"You are not abandoning us," Garth chuckled warmly, a harsh, grating sound. The tracker slapped the flat of his hunting knife against his leather thigh. "You are deploying tactically to save the city. That is the order. Now, get back to Ashenfall, and make sure that massive human friend of yours keeps his shield high."

Ronald released the scout's shoulders, taking a deliberate step backward to stand beside the grizzled tracker. The beastkin drew his twin curved hunting daggers, the dark steel gleaming in the pale light.

"Get out of here, kid," Ronald ordered, his voice dropping to a soft, affectionate rumble. "Tell Caistina we gave them hell."

Perberos looked at the two veteran hunters. He saw the unyielding resolve in their faces. They were not afraid. They had accepted their fate, and they were choosing to give him the gift of survival.

The elven scout slowly stood up straight. He did not argue any further. He knew that lingering would simply dishonour the sacrifice they were making. He placed his right fist firmly over his heart, offering them the deepest, most respectful bow of the ancient elven tradition.

"May the Light guide your blades," Perberos whispered, his voice thick with profound gratitude and sorrow. "I will not waste the time you buy me."

Perberos turned his back on the two men. He looked towards the dense, shadowed treeline leading south towards the city.

He closed his eyes, tapping deeply into his mana core. He called upon the dark, swirling energy of the shadow veil, bypassing his physical limitations entirely. The world around him seemed to lose its solid edges, shifting into a blurred, monochromatic spectrum of greys and blacks.

He triggered Shadow Jump.

The elven scout literally vanished from the clearing. He did not run. He stepped seamlessly into the deep shadow cast by a massive oak tree, and instantly materialised twenty yards away, stepping out of the shadow of a large boulder.

The sensation was disorienting, a freezing, rushing vacuum that stole the breath from his lungs, but he did not stop. He burned through his mana reserves with reckless, desperate speed. He jumped again, and again, flashing through the corrupted forest like a strobe light.

He bypassed the treacherous, frozen mud. He bypassed the tangled, thorny undergrowth. He moved entirely outside the physical realm, a ghost skipping across the landscape.

Within a matter of minutes, the deafening, crashing roar of the pursuing horde began to fade into a distant, muffled rumble. He had successfully broken out of the immediate danger zone, charting a wide, sweeping circular path that would take him safely around the eastern flank of the valley and back towards the southern gates of Ashenfall.

As he jumped to the crest of a high, overlooking ridge, Perberos paused for a single fraction of a second. He looked out over the sprawling valley, seeing the towering timber walls of his city bathed in the pale morning light, shadowed heavily by the dark smoke of the siege.

He set his jaw, drew a deep breath, and plunged back into the shadows, racing towards his family.

Back in the dead clearing near the freezing creek bed, the silence left by the elven scout's departure lasted for only a fleeting moment.

Ronald watched the shadows completely swallow Perberos. The beastkin let out a long, slow exhale, the freezing air pluming from his lips. A massive, heavy weight lifted from his chest. The kid had gotten away. Their mission had failed, but they had ensured the city’s best asset had survived the fallout.

"He is a good lad," Garth murmured, leaning against the dead tree. The tracker tested the edge of his heavy hunting knife with his thumb. "He moves like a bloody phantom. Never seen anything like it."

"He will be a legend one day, if he survives this war," Ronald agreed, his ears swivelling to track the approaching noise.

The cacophony of the undead horde was practically on top of them. The ground beneath their boots was vibrating violently. They could hear the wet, guttural snarling of the ghouls and the heavy, metallic clanking of the Wight knights pushing through the brush just beyond the fallen oak log.

Ronald turned his back on the southern escape route. He faced the creek bed, taking a deep, slow breath of the foul-smelling, necrotic air.

His legs felt like columns of solid lead. Every single muscle in his lithe, athletic body was screaming in pure, burning agony. He had spent his entire life relying on his impossible speed, his fluid agility, and his predatory instincts to survive. He had run from monsters, he had danced around giant spiders, and he had evaded traps in the deepest dungeons.

But suddenly, the thought of taking another step backward felt incredibly distasteful.

He was a proud beastkin. He was a senior instructor at the Ashenfall Guild. He had trained dozens of the city's finest adventurers, teaching them how to hold their ground and fight with honour. Fleeing through the mud like a terrified rabbit, waiting for a skeletal hound to drag him down from behind or a stray crossbow bolt to pierce his back, felt like a complete betrayal of everything he stood for.

Ronald let out a low, rough chuckle. He spun his twin curved daggers, settling the leather-wrapped hilts comfortably into his palms.

"I am too old for this shit, Garth," Ronald announced, a genuine, relaxed smile touching his face.

Garth pushed himself away from the dead tree, stepping up to stand shoulder to shoulder with the beastkin. The massive tracker looked completely exhausted, his chainmail covered in freezing black sludge, but his scarred face was set into a mask of grim, stubborn defiance.

"My knees were complaining miles ago, my friend," Garth grinned, coughing another splatter of dark blood onto the frost. "I reckon this is as good a place as any to stop running. Better to look them in the eye when the lights go out."

"My thoughts exactly," Ronald nodded, his long tail lashing steadily behind him.

The beastkin rolled his aching shoulders, feeling a strange, profound sense of peace settle over his mind. The terrifying anxiety of the chase was gone. The burden of leadership, the desperate need to protect his squad, had vanished the moment Perberos stepped into the shadows.

There was absolutely nothing left to worry about. There was only the simple, primal purity of the fight. He was going to go out exactly on his own terms, facing the apocalypse with a blade in his hand and a snarl on his lips.

The dense brush on the opposite side of the freezing creek bed exploded outward.

A pack of massive, corrupted skeletal hounds burst into the clearing. Their fleshless jaws were snapping wildly, their bones glowing with a sickly, furious green light. Behind them, lumbering through the shattered timber, poured dozens of bruised, purple-skinned Crypt Ghouls, shrieking in mindless hunger as they finally spotted their prey.

And rising silently from the freezing fog behind the lesser beasts came the elite guard.

The Wights marched in perfect, terrifying formation. Their pristine, blackened plate armour absorbed the pale morning light, projecting an aura of severe, spectral frostbite. The icy blue soul-fire burning within their dark visors locked entirely onto the two exhausted men standing in the clearing.

They had brought an entire army to kill two hunters.

Ronald did not feel fear. He looked at the massive, overwhelming wave of dark steel and rotting meat preparing to crash down upon them.

The beastkin raised his twin curved daggers high into the freezing air. He let out a deafening, predatory roar that echoed across the dead clearing, completely drowning out the shrieks of the ghouls. It was a sound of absolute, unyielding defiance.

"Come and get it, you rotting bastards!" Garth bellowed, raising his heavy hunting knife and stepping forward.

Ronald did not wait for the hounds to cross the creek bed.

He planted his boots into the freezing mud and charged forward. He threw himself directly into the teeth of the approaching nightmare, his daggers flashing in the pale light, eager to carve a bloody, legendary end to his story in the dark.
