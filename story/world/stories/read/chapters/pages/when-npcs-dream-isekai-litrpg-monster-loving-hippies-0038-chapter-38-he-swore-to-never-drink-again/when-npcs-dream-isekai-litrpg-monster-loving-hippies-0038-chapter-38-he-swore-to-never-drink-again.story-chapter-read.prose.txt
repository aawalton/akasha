It wasn’t very characteristic of me, but I can say it with certainty—I was going to a party!

I even got invited to it.I wouldn’t say it out loud, but this was probably my first real party. So I was… excited.

The other Stormcrows looked psyched too.Well—Camila wasn’t coming. She wasn’t in the mood for a party.And Charlotte and Duchess didn’t exactly enjoy the forest trip. Charlotte wasn’t a forest critter like the rest of us, and Duchess was… well, she was Duchess.

But Santiago? He shined.He took every opportunity he could find to look cool and manly in front of his girlfriend—He helped when she stumbled, held her hand during the more difficult parts, and explained things about snakes and poisonous plants in a very “expert tone.”

A real expert he was. I had explained these same things to him not even a week earlier.

I really wished I had a girl in distress to impress.Juliette and Samar were already forest pros, so I was short on candidates.

“Help! Help!” a loud squawk. “Cumtowel-snortblister! My fur’s stuck in the bush again!”

Thank you… God. You heard my prayers and answered, I thought, sulking as I went to help the poor bird.

“Also sap is matting my tail fur,” Duchess continued. “Chuu! It’s sticky and gross!”

“Hey Duchess,” said Juliette, “I wanted to ask you something. When we first found you, you talked about separating from your fellow bird-people. You said there were boats coming to this continent and you got separated. Don’t you ever want to look for them?”

“No! I don’t like them,” she said proudly. “I will stay with Naked-Nula.”

“You… you will?” Juliette’s disappointment was clear.

“Sure,” squawked Duchess. “You are ugly and stupid, but at least you are not Lickbucket-assnoodle.”

If each of her people was as insufferable as she was, it’s no wonder she preferred us.

But honestly, I doubted there were many bird-people like Duchess.I just didn’t see how civilization could actually function if it was full of Duchesses.

We reached the camp.I expected them to be impressed by it, but Juliette was only mildly impressed… and for some reason, Hyun-woo was extremely impressed and excited.

I thought maybe he just really liked parties.But he didn’t even look like he wanted to get inside the camp.He just stood near the palisade, inspecting it like it was the most interesting thing in the world.

“These goblins are amazing!” he claimed. “All the NPCs in this world are cheaters—they get raw materials with magic, they have all their machinery and buildings ready. But these goblins… they started from nothing! They actually did all this by themselves, even before we gave them some basic tools. They were using their weapons to work wood! Incredible!”

Charlotte, on the other hand, wanted to be anywhere but here.“Will they threaten to skewer us with spears?” she muttered, nervously glancing around.

“Don’t worry,” said the chivalrous hero. “I’ll always protect you.”

“Does anyone actually want to get inside?” I asked.

Eventually, we herded Hyun-woo and Charlotte in. Immediately, some goblins came to surround us—but not with spears this time. There was respect in their eyes. Some even cheered when they saw us.

Some shouted: “Humans!”

The goblins, it seems, really liked us now.

When I thought about it, it made sense. We had improved their lives through all the trades, and we’d also led them to their first victory. Against their hated enemies, no less. To goblins, that wasn’t just victory — it was honor, pride, the stuff they lived for. Plus, they’d gotten a ton of loot out of it.

“The guests of honor!” we heard a heavy voice shout. Rash’gul, the chief. “Welcome!”

He came to me, and I suddenly wasn’t sure how to greet him. No hug—that was for sure—but I imagined something like a manly arm-embrace between two warriors. That didn’t feel right either.So I just raised my hand casually and said, “Hi.”

“All my goblins are in great spirits!” he said. “And the party preparations are almost finished! We will have a grand celebration!”

It did look like the party prep was nearly finished.We saw a large cooking operation underway. Stew was on the menu, along with more meat dishes and, of course, all the ale we brought.

But before we all got drunk and danced around the fire, we pitched our plan to the chief—the assault on the keep.

As expected, he was thrilled by the idea.But this time, I made sure to clarify some important details so we didn’t create unrealistic expectations.

“No slaughtering all humans,” I said firmly. “The idea is to break the walls, scare them, let them escape, take the treasures.”

“Relax,” Rash’gul gave a toothy smile. “We know how to have fun even without slaughtering humans. Breaking human keep with large rocks is also great fun.”

…I secretly agreed with him.It did sound like a lot of fun.

But there was the matter of actually building the catapults.

“Have you done it before?” I asked Rash’gul. “Built siege engines, like catapults?”

“We goblins can build anything,” said Rash’gul with pride. “We have a great goblin engineer. The best of them. But—we haven’t done it before. We don’t have schematic. Drawings. You need to get that. Get us some human catapult book. Then we build.”

I had thought of that possibility.The problem was, Kamatouu didn’t have a proper library. And there wasn’t any real chance of just finding a catapult schematic handbook lying around.

For that, we’d have to go to Kuriolis.

But I didn’t want to waste time. Even if we planned to travel to Kuriolis anyway, I wanted the catapults to be built while we handled that, not after. We needed the two things to happen in parallel to save as much time as possible.

No. We’d have to design the catapult ourselves.

I wanted Hyun-woo to join the conversation. I looked around—where had he wandered off to?

I spotted him near some food storage, inspecting a piece of rope like it was the most precious thing in the world.This Hyun-woo could be weird sometimes.

“Hyun-woo!” I shouted. “Can you get over here for a sec?”

He came over and showed us the piece of rope.

“This rope wasn’t from our supply run,” he said enthusiastically to the chief. “You made it yourself! Which plant did you use?”

“There is a type of succulent here,” said the chief. “Has good strong fiber. Resemble Sisal we had back at home. Took my goblins forever to find a plant suited  for rope making.”

“So interesting,” said Hyun-woo. “And what technique do they use to spin the fiber?”

“I don’t know,” said the chief. “I’ll tell the one in charge of the textile work to speak with you. She’ll love to tell you all about it.”

“Wow…” murmured Hyun-woo. “Such an advanced culture!”

“You see?” said Rash’gul. “We goblins are not only about slaughtering and breaking stuff! We are also amazing builders. Your friend here knows how to appreciate us!”

“Yes, amazing rope!” I said, trying not to sound too impatient. “Hyun-woo, can we talk about catapults for a moment here?”

“Sure,” he said. “Do we have the schematics?”

“We’ll make the schematic,” I said.

He looked at me, eyebrow raised. I continued.

“I studied old mangonel diagrams once for a D&D campaign. I remember the general design. But we’ll need real calculations — beam dimensions, rope tension, payload. You know… math and physics stuff. The goal is to launch a fifty-kilo stone about 150 meters.”

“That… actually sounds fun,” Hyun-woo smiled. “But you do remember I’m a computer science student, right? Not a civil or mechanical engineer. It’s not the same. Unfortunately, my coding skills don’t help much in this analog world.”

“Yeah, but you’re also, like, super smart,” I said. That was my winning argument.

“I’ll need the formulas for beam bending, moment of inertia, stress, and deflection,” he said thoughtfully. “I probably remember enough for a basic derivation. But I don’t have the material constant values for wood—like Young’s modulus or the shear modulus. Even that would change depending on the specific species of wood and whether the wood is dry or wet.

“Awesome! I knew you could do it.”

“But I just said I lack the parameters!” Hyun-woo sighed. “I could probably guess a little and get the dimensions close. It won’t be a one-shot design. We’ll need to do a lot of testing.”

“That also sounds fun,” I said.

“Yes,” agreed Rash’gul—who probably didn’t understand a word of our conversation, but said, “My goblins like building things that break other things. Those are the best things!”

Then a small female goblin with messy hair approached. Rash’gul introduced her as the head of their textile work. She and Hyun-woo immediately spun into a passionate conversation.

Actually, now that I think about it, I’d never seen Hyun-woo so excited.Who knew he had such a thing for textiles?He must be so jealous of me and my work at the clothier.Maybe I could give him a lesson or two in sewing panties.

Speaking of panties—Vel’nari joined me and the chief.

“Hi chief,” she said, giving me a side-glance. “Is my human behaving properly?”

“I like this human,” said the chief. “Now he wants to build catapults, destroy human castle with large rocks, and pillage them. He thinking like a real goblin warlord.”

“Yes,” agreed Vel’nari, flashing a toothy grin. “I like him too.”

It was actually to break a wall, not destroy the whole castle—but since Vel’nari liked it, I didn’t correct him.

“All the preparations for the party are ready, Chief,” said Vel’nari. “We should stop all this talking and start having some fun.”

“That’s right!” agreed Rash’gul. “Come, see how we goblins party!”

I really did want to see this.I expected a kind of tribal party, with a large fire and drum music—like the Ewok forest celebration in Star Wars Episode VI.

And to my great pleasure… I wasn’t far off.

They had set a massive fire in the center of the camp, and all the goblins began gathering around it.

Indeed, there were leather drums, pounding out a tribal beat that added a real wild energy to the atmosphere. But it wasn’t just drums—three goblins were playing curved wooden flutes, producing surprisingly decent melodies. Not the kind of skill I expected from goblins, but hey, that just teaches you not to have goblins prejudiced.

Then came the food. Giant pots of stew were passed around — full of meaty, unidentifiable things that I very intentionally decided to never identify.

And of course—the ale we brought. Poured into big wooden mugs.

But before the feast could begin, Rash’gul had to give a loud and happy speech. Humans and goblins together! Destroy enemy! Slaughter enemy! Spill enemy blood! Crush enemy bones! Blah blah!

Then finally, he raised his mug—and we could all dig in and drink.

I looked around at my friends. Everyone seemed to be having a pretty decent time.

Santiago was deep in conversation with two ugly goblins, probably about bows. Juliette was arguing with the chief about something. She looked serious—but then suddenly laughed. She was in a good mood.

Even Charlotte, to my surprise, seemed to enjoy her stew and was showing a female goblin how to braid hair.

Duchess had impressed a group of young goblins who were very much enjoying petting and rubbing her fur, for some reason.

And me?I was having fun too—eating, drinking, and talking with Vel’nari.

“I guess everything worked out,” I said to her. “You’re building a great place here.”

“Yes…” she said hesitantly, “I should probably say that… it wouldn’t have been so, if it wasn’t…”

Was she trying to say thank you?

“It was just really good of me… to find you,” she said.

“Finding me?”

“And trusting you.” She smiled, putting her hand on my arm. “You are good human.”

“Thanks.” That was probably as far as Vel’nari compliments could stretch. It was fine.

We drank more ale. I could already feel a little tipsy. The music, the fire, the food—all of it put me in a great mood.

“What are they doing?” I asked as I saw a bunch of goblins carrying a huge wooden log and placing it in front of the chief.

“This is a common goblin party game,” explained Vel’nari. “It’s pretty simple and fun. You’ll see.”

Then one tall, scary-looking goblin picked up a long wooden stick from a pile and stepped to one end of the log. He stood there, roaring to the loud cheers of the crowd.

A moment later, another mean-looking goblin rose to the challenge, grabbing a stick and stepping to the other side. More loud cheering.

Oh, I get it.They’d try to unbalance each other with the sticks. Last one standing on the log wins.

Then Rash’gul gave the signal.

And they began.

Except… they didn’t try to unbalance each other.No. They basically tried to injure each other as much as possible before someone fell.

The tall goblin was a tough one—he deflected a powerful swing with his stick, actually caught his enemy’s weapon with one hand, and then thrusted his own stick into the rival’s face, giving him a bloody bruise on the cheek. One more vicious thrust to the head, and the other goblin toppled off the log.

The crowd roared.

“That’s Resh’rir,” Vel’nari explained. “One of our strongest. He’ll be tough to beat.”

Then another huge goblin stepped up, took a stick, and roared like a beast.

“Oh boy,” Vel’nari muttered. “Genh’ga here is dumb as the log he stepped on, but also big and mean. He can give Resh’rir a problem— if he doesn’t fall over by himself.”

“Oh cool,” I said. I liked the commentary. “So who are we cheering for?”

“Genh’ga,” she said. “Resh’rir is an asshole. I hope he gets hurt.”

Vel’nari cheered. I joined her. I was also pretty drunk by this point.

But to our disappointment, Genh’ga’s size and stupidity didn’t do him any favors.After a quick exchange, Resh’rir smacked him straight in the nose. Probably broke it. Genh’ga looked dizzy and fell off almost immediately.

Again, the crowd roared.

Damn, I thought. Someone needs to take down that Resh’rir.And also look very manly in the process.

Actually… it didn’t look that hard.

Goblins were smaller than humans. Even the big ones. They weighed, what? Maybe 120 lbs at most? I could probably manage it.

“What? Where are you going?” Vel’nari asked as I suddenly stood up. “Let’s keep watching the fights!”

“I’ll be back in a minute,” I promised.

I just needed to…

I found myself standing on the log. Stick in hand.Facing a very confused Resh’rir.

A moment later, he smiled—finally realizing that a puny human had actually challenged him.

Let me just be clear here:From my drunken point of view, this seemed like a really okay-ish idea.

A loud cheer from the crowd.

I heard Rash’gul’s voice shouting, “That is a tough human! I told you!”

Then he declared the battle would begin.

More loud cheering.A human against a goblin—apparently, it was a sensation.

I even heard shouts of “Geshem! Geshem!” from both my human friends and Vel’nari.

Cool.Now I just needed to win.

Resh’rir approached—smiling, moving slowly, cautiously.Guess he figured that a weak-looking human like me must resort to dirty tricks if I wanted a chance.

He was spot on.

When he got close enough—just barely in range of his stick—I cast my gas magic circle.

I didn’t will it to do anything—just created a large, bright, vertical magic circle exactly between me and Resh’rir.

Because of the darkness, the bright circle completely blocked his view of me.He probably got confused—thought I was going to cast a fireball or something.

Then I tossed my stick lazily into the air—just a distraction.

While it was still midair, I lunged forward—jumped through the glowing magic circle, and kicked.Right into his chest.

The goblin toppled off the log, fast.

The kick almost dragged me down with him. I caught myself on all fours, my body teetering at the edge—but I didn’t fall.

I probably looked stupid.But I won.

The crowd went quiet.

Then—

Explosion of cheering.

Guess it was a good idea after all.

I jumped off the log.

Resh’rir came over to me, and somehow he looked a lot bigger than before.He gripped both my upper arms, just below the shoulders, and pulled me half a step closer.

He looked… nasty.

So.I guess it was a bad idea after all.

Then—he laughed loudly and shook me.In what was probably a goblin-friendly way.

I laughed too.Because I was drunk.And scared.And also feeling really good.

The party continued.More goblins rose to fight.More noses got broken.But it was all in good sport.

Then came the dancing.

Apparently, goblins did dance. A tribal, energetic style of dance.I was more than happy to join in the fun.

Goblins came to laugh with me, congratulate me on the fight, and give me more drinks—which I may or may not have consumed. At that point, things started to get a little blurry.

Then someone was pulling at my hand. Hard.

I looked. It was Vel’nari.

I let myself be taken.

We arrived at some tool shed nearby.Vel’nari then threw me—violently—into a straw pile on the floor.I blinked up at her. Then the goblin was upon me.

It started with kisses. Deep and messy, the ale making everything feel even warmer, even softer. Her touch, her weight, her laugh. It was one of the best things I’d ever felt.

But this time… the kiss was only the beginning.
