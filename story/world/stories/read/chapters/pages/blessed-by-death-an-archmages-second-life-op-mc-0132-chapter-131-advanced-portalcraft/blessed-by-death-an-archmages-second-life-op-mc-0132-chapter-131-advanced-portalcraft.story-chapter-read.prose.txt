The Institute of Ascension had received some major upgrades to its security since the last time Levi was there. There were over a half dozen new wards layered over the front entrance alone, not to mention two additional high-tier adventurers standing guard outside. It seemed that the Institute hadn’t taken Selene’s escape very well.

Of course, just because they were major didn’t mean that they were effective. Levi breezed past the defenses like they were nothing. He strode between the two guards, numerous presence concealment spells wrapped tightly around him, and didn’t miss a step as he swiped his hand dismissively to the side, as though he were pulling back a curtain. The wards rippled and allowed him through without triggering a single alarm.

He crossed the entrance hall, reached the staircase, and began ascending. In just a few minutes, he was before the sealed door to the Restricted Sanctum once more.

On his first day in this new world, Levi had stood in front of this very door and deliberated over whether he should blast his way through. Back then, he’d decided against it, wanting to try living his new life as an adventure.

Now…

Levi reached out and placed his hand against the smooth adamantine surface. The door was protected by countless defensive enchantments, some old and some very new. The magic was so dense that it emitted a faint silvery glow. For several seconds, Levi didn’t move, listening intently to the flow of the magic.

Any time now…

Ding!

[Essence Decryption!]

Magic welled up from deep within him and poured forth from his hand, sinking into the door and interfacing with the internal locking mechanisms. The skill emitted a distinct whirring sound, like a thousand revolving gears.

Thirty seconds later, another ding! sounded.

[System Notification: Decryption successful.]

Levi exhaled. This really wasn’t fair. It would’ve taken him hours if not entire days to manually hack the locking mechanism.

The door disassembled into motes of light. Levi made sure he was wearing the identification bracelet Heimler had given him and stepped into the light. He braced himself just in case the bracelet’s permissions had been revoked, but he passed through the light harmlessly.

The Restricted Sanctum’s interior looked the exact same, all dark wood and gleaming crystal. There were no windows, and the only light came from the flickering chandeliers above. This late at night, it was completely empty.

Levi activated his True Sight, and for once, the unholy light of dark magic didn’t greet him. Even in its absence, though, he still felt a muffled sense of wrongness in the air. Or was it in his mind?

He let out a deep breath. Perhaps he should’ve asked the others for backup after all. But… No.

For his sake, Levi had to handle this alone.

He went deeper into the Restricted Sanctum, ascending the spiral staircase and navigating the halls. Before long, he found himself standing before a familiar door.

Professor Heimler’s lab.

For a while, he stood outside the door, feeling his magic steadily build up within him. Truthfully, he didn’t know exactly what to expect. Perhaps he was just overreacting. Perhaps things would be okay.

But…

His expression set. He dispelled his presence concealment, raised his hand, and knocked.

Any hopes that the lab would be empty were dashed when the door dematerialized into motes of light. The invitation was clear.

Levi stepped through the light and emerged into Heimler’s laboratory. Immediately, he halted.

The room was an utter mess. Pieces of paper and magical devices littered the ground, some of which were broken, seemingly in fits of frustration. The blackboards that lined the walls were covered in dense handwriting, but so were the walls and floors themselves. Endless rows of formulae and theorems had been scribbled across every available surface. The handwriting started out neat, but slowly devolved into a frantic near-illegible scrawl over the length of the lab, the calculations written over one another as though the writer didn’t have the time to erase.

The bed in the corner was expertly made, except unlike last time, it was now covered in a visible coating of dust. If Levi were a betting man, he’d say that it hadn’t been used since the last time he was there. Same with the wall of meal rations; it was still the same height as last time, untouched. The pleasant floral scent was gone, replaced by the fragrance of ink and sea salt and something metallic.

All in all, the lab was a far cry from how Levi had remembered it. He almost wouldn’t have believed that it belonged to someone as elegant and dignified as Heimler, were it not for the fact that the man was lying on the desk at the end of the lab.

At first, Levi thought that Heimler was unconscious. The professor was lying horizontally across the desk, eyes closed and hands clasped over his stomach. His legs half-dangled haphazardly off the edge. But then Heimler’s eyes snapped open and he sat upright, glancing over at the entrance.

“Ah, you’re here,” he said, his tone conversational. “Good evening, Levi Ironwood.”

“Good evening,” Levi said, much more warily. He glanced around. “How, ah… How are you doing?”

“Better now that my assistant is finally here,” Heimler said. “We need to have a talk about your tendency to disappear with no forewarning or explanation. I’m afraid if this trend continues, I’ll have to dock your pay.”

Although the professor’s words were reproachful, his tone was light. Pleasant, even.

“I apologize…”

Levi trailed off as Heimler hopped off the desk and landed without a sound, turning to fully face him.

The professor looked… terrible. His short blonde hair, normally immaculately slicked back, was messy and disheveled. His facial hair was scraggy and ungroomed and he had dark circles under his eyes. He was far thinner than before, downright gaunt, his sharp aristocratic features now so sharp that they looked painful. Yet despite his ragged appearance, Heimler looked more pleased than Levi had ever seen him. Radiant, even, although it was more akin to the glow of a toxic flame.

“Apology accepted,” Heimler said. “If only because your assistance is no longer needed. I owe you quite a lot, Levi Ironwood. You provided the key I needed to make my breakthrough.”

With some disbelief, Levi realized that some of the theorems scrawled across the walls hadn’t existed a few weeks prior. Had… Had Heimler invented a whole new field of magical theory using the bare fundamentals Levi had given him?

“Breakthrough?” Levi asked.

Heimler smiled. There was an unnerving light in his eyes. “Indeed.” He didn’t elaborate.

“Professor… Are you okay?”

“Of course. Why wouldn’t I be?”

Levi stared at him. “Because I don’t think you’ve eaten in the past few weeks. Or gotten enough sleep. Or left this room, for that matter.”

Heimler blinked. “Oh. Haven’t I?” He summoned a mirror to his hand, looking at his reflection. His appearance seemed to surprise him. “Dear me. Perhaps you’re right. Forgive me for my unsightly appearance, Levi Ironwood. I have been… busy.”

“With what?” Levi asked. As he spoke, his eyes roved around the room, trying to decipher what Heimler had been working on. Ward matrices, schema links, thaumaturgical analysis… It didn’t paint a pretty picture. “What sort of breakthrough did you have? Did you create the intercontinental portal like we’d discussed last time?”

“Intercontinental?’” Heimler chuckled. “Oh, we’re a little past that by now. What do you know of alternate dimensions, Levi Ironwood?”

Levi stilled. “Not much…”

“Countless worlds and realms exist other than our own,” Heimler said, his voice taking on a lecturing quality. “We are but one dimension of many, perhaps even infinite, separated by what can only be described as an unforgiving void. A perpetual expanse of nothingness that not even Time nor Space can penetrate. Infinite, yet finite all the same. Indeterminable.”

Levi examined the professor’s expression, trying to figure out what his angle was. Had Heimler discovered Levi’s origins? Where was this coming from?

“Throughout history, we have tried to conquer this void between worlds,” Heimler continued. “Tried, and failed. The closest we’ve gotten is forming a contract with the Hollow, which possesses the ability to summon archdaemons from the infernal dimension that they call a home – and really that’s no method at all. Although we’ve certainly received intruders from other dimensions, never before have we managed to intentionally pull one through.” He paused. “Until now, that is.”

Levi kept his expression measured even as his mind raced. Was Heimler referring to Selene? “How?”

“Portals, naturally,” Heimler said. “Portals allow for the instantaneous transportation between two points without traversing the physical distance in between. Incredible phenomena, they are. Yet for centuries, they were discounted and regarded as largely useless due to being limited by the Subaru Paradox. Which, because of you, is no longer an issue.”

“Right… What does that have to do with alternate dimensions?”

“Everything. You see, if we have portals, then we don’t need to penetrate the void between worlds. The void becomes irrelevant to the equation altogether. With portals, it is theoretically possible to connect not only two points within a world, but two worlds altogether.”

“But for a portal to work, you need warp anchors,” Levi pointed out. “If you can’t cross the void, then how do you get the warp anchor to the other world in the first place?”

Heimler chuckled. “This is why I recruited you to be my assistant. You are right. A portal requires warp anchors at either point to work. But what if a warp anchor already exists in the other world? And all I have to do is create the corresponding warp anchor on our side to connect it?”

“That… would work,” Levi said slowly. “But how would a warp anchor already exist? Why would someone create only one half of a warp anchor in another dimension? And how would you even know about it?”

“Research,” Heimler said. “Research into history and legends and myths and all that came before. I explored the ancient ruins of Alass, I infiltrated the catacombs of the Grand Cathedral, I scoured the Hill of Lost Dreams in the land of the elves, I plundered the graves of Dragons, all so I could gain the necessary knowledge. And as for why someone would create only one half of a warp anchor… It’s because they wish to be freed. Tell me, Levi Ironwood. Do you know of the Old Ones?”

Levi’s blood turned to ice. For the first time in a long time, genuine fear coursed through him. “Those who dwell in the Outer Realms?”

Heimler blinked before laughing. “Yes! That’s exactly, precisely right! It should be impossible for you to know of them, yet for some reason I find myself not the slightest bit surprised. The Old Ones are those who were exiled to the outer planes of the void. Beings of such incomprehensible breadth and magnitude that they make archdaemons look like playful kittens. They created numerous unlinked warp anchors in their world in the hopes that someone across Time and Space would complete the loop.”

“And that’s you?” Levi asked in growing horror. “You’re trying to summon an Old One to this world?”

This was… Holy fucking shit. This was extreme. The Old Ones were old lore, for lack of a better term. Most people didn’t know of them, and those who did certainly would never dare summon them. Levi hadn’t expected this from Heimler at all. This was the sort of stuff that Death used to send Levi on missions for.

“Trying?” Heimler repeated, a strange note to his voice. He smiled. “I already did.”

Reality shifted. The faint scent of iron and salt from before intensified a hundredfold. Something emerged. From where, Levi couldn’t describe. The feeling of wrongness grew to torturous levels.

Before Levi’s wide eyes, an Old One oozed into existence right before him.

It was vaguely humanoid, standing upright, except instead of limbs, it had dozens of tentacles that moved independently of one another. Except the movements were profoundly unnatural, tracing unknown patterns that had a primal part of Levi’s brain screaming in agony. From the patterns fell flakes of blackened ash, fluttering through the air. Levi forced himself to look at the creature’s head, from which five tentacles sprouted like a twisted flower. Instead of a face, it had a bottomless maw of pure darkness that sank into itself.

Yet, what made Levi so awed wasn’t the eldritch creature’s inhumane power or its impossibility, but rather…

“It’s docile,” Levi breathed out. “You managed to control it?”

“A summoning contract,” Heimler said. “Even the Old Ones follow the primordial laws of equivalent exchange. In exchange for obeying my commands, after it finishes carrying out my will, I will forfeit my soul in entirety and perpetuity.”

“Your soul? Your… will?”

“I have many plans,” Heimler said. “You came right on time, Levi Ironwood. I only finished the ritual and summoned the Old One earlier today.” He clapped his hands together softly. “First things first, though… I need to rectify a mistake I made a while past. You see, a few months ago, I happened to run into a rat outside the Restricted Sanctum. Against all odds, it managed to scurry away. Careless of me, I know, but in my defense, it accomplished something I thought impossible.” He tilted his head. “Say, wasn’t solving the Subaru Paradox also supposed to be impossible?”

“... I don’t like what you’re implying here,” Levi said. He injected as much innocence into voice as possible. “You think the rat was me?”

“It wasn’t?”

“Nope, no. Definitely not.”

Heimler gazed at him for a long time. “I wish I could believe you. In all honesty, I want to believe you. However… Once is happenstance. Twice is a coincidence. I don’t intend to let it reach three times. Fortunately for me, the rat also made a mistake.”

He held up his hand, and between his index finger and thumb, a thin black thread materialized.

No. Not a thread.

Hair, Levi realized. During their fight, Heimler had cut off some strands of Levi’s hair. But Levi thought he’d burned it all. Had he missed one?

If he had, then this…

Ah, shit.

“The hair of a mage is always valuable,” Heimler said. “Especially to something like an Old One. I don’t know what senses they use to navigate this world, but it’s certainly not the conventional ones. It is said that if they ever get a piece of you, even if it’s only a single singed strand of hair, they will never let go. They’ll hunt you down to the very ends of the earth.”

Heimler handed the strand of hair over to the Old One, who accepted it with a surprisingly dexterous tentacle. The Old One seemed to examine it for a second before raising it to its maw and swallowing it whole.

Then, ever so slowly, the Old One turned to face Levi Ironwood.

“So it was you,” Heimler murmured, sounding rather disappointed. “I thought… Well. It is what it is.”

“Let’s not be hasty here,” Levi said. “We can talk about this–”

The Old One roared.

“… for the record, this is total fucking bullshit.”
