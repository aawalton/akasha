Ekon should’ve left the camp outside by now, Viktor thought.

He could practically see how the scene unfolded at the entrance. Guards and employees of the Guild gaped in disbelief when they saw the bald man stumble out after burying himself underground for over half a month. Most of them had already written him off as yet another corpse in the dungeon, no doubt.

Once they recovered from their shock, they would rush over to help him, checking his physical condition, examining his injuries. Realizing he was stable enough, they began to bombard him with questions. And Ekon would present the cover story that had been prepared beforehand. It was riddled with holes though, so people would naturally grow suspicious of the whole thing. But it was fine. That was part of the plan, anyway.

Regardless of whether they believed the story or not, they couldn’t accuse Ekon of lying without proof, nor could they detain him simply because they were skeptical of his claims. At most, they could only offer to have guards escort him back to town under the pretense of concern for his condition. And the bald man would politely decline the proposal before leaving the encampment by himself.

Of course, all of this existed solely within Viktor’s imagination. After all, he couldn’t personally witness any of it, not from where he was sitting. While he could have sent Galatea to verify it, he had chosen not to. Because he needed her elsewhere, doing a more important job.

And, at that moment—

[Master, Galatea reports that the entrance to the Folded Chamber has opened, and one masked man has emerged from inside.]

Viktor chuckled.

Yes, that was precisely what he had been expecting.

[Master, you think this man was sent because of Ekon?]

Viktor shrugged. “It’s still possible the timing is merely coincidental, but we should have our answer soon enough. Galatea is following him at the moment, right?”

[Yes.]

Only one man, huh? It appeared the enemy had decided against mobilizing an immediate large-scale response. Instead of attempting to capture the bald man right away, they had chosen to send someone to follow him from a distance, waiting to understand the situation before committing to anything more drastic.

[I wonder how they knew Ekon had come out.]

“I believe there’s a spy among the guards and Guild employees in the camp,” Viktor said. Yes, planting a spy there was exactly what he would have done if he were in their position.

[But how did the spy pass information to the masked men? And how does the man sent after Ekon know where to find him?]

“There are many ways to accomplish that. For example, the spy could’ve placed a magical marking on Ekon’s body when they were checking his condition. All it would take is a moment of contact. So when the men inside the Folded Chamber saw that a mark had been activated and was now in motion, they immediately understood what had happened.”

[I see.]

Viktor stood up. He had constructed multiple scenarios of what could happen, and this was one of them. Now, there was only one thing left to do: carrying out what had already been planned.

He glanced at the items laid out before him.

First, the wind gems and fire gems. Of course. These were his bread and butter. Not the most devastating assets in his arsenal by any measure, but damn if there existed a more consistently useful pair of options.

Second, the ringed crosses he had received from Khenemhotep. The priest had made them from gold, because he made everything from gold, and that meant despite their small size they were quite heavy. Dense little things, each one shaped like a cross except that the top curved and closed into a droplet-shaped loop. Khenemhotep had spent considerable time imbuing his magic into them, and he had been clear about the constraints: even if unused, they would begin losing their power after a single day, and completely run out within three. But it was fine. Viktor had no intention of letting them sit idle; he was going to put them to work right tonight.

Third, a buckler. He had asked Celeste to transfer the power housed within Sebekton’s breastplate into this smaller, more portable vessel, just for tonight. Yes, he was going to borrow this, the ability to completely negate any physical attack coming from one direction. After all, one could never have too many layers of protection.

And finally, a wooden case, the contents of which he had ordered Kazyk to produce to his exact specifications. Technically, this one was not necessary for tonight’s assault. But he had always found a particular satisfaction in the efficiency of killing two birds with one stone, so there was no good reason not to bring it along.

As for the rapier, he had decided to leave it at home, since he still wanted to keep the weapon a secret, to hold it in reserve for the final confrontation with Xander.

“Teleport me back,” Viktor said, after he had gathered everything up.

[Understood.]

The transition came and went in a blink. One instant he stood by the Dungeon Core, the next he was back in his room.

Without wasting even a glance at the familiar surroundings, he walked toward the window. Easing it open, he slipped out into the evening air. The wind gems stirred to life at his command, invisible currents curling under his boots and lifting him upward.

He climbed in a steady spiral until the rooftops below shrank to the size of scattered playing cards. The last remnants of sunlight had already vanished beyond the horizon, which meant even if someone down there happened to glance up, they would see nothing more than a passing shadow against the darkening clouds. He hovered for a moment, turning his gaze westward.

There lay the haphazard jumble of the slums, chaotic and uneven and perpetually on the verge of collapse, yet stubbornly enduring all the same.

Claire was there, and he had been with her for most of the day, right up until Alycia arrived to take over the shift. Then, he hurried home. He grabbed his dinner on the way instead of wasting time cooking. And, once everything was done, he asked Celeste to teleport him to the dungeon.

[Master.]

“What is the situation?”

[That masked man is indeed following Ekon, exactly as you anticipated. He maintained surveillance from a distance, keeping Ekon within sight while avoiding the risk of exposing himself. Ekon, for his part, has reached the designated waiting spot by the river. He is pretending to rest there, just as you instructed.]

Viktor allowed himself a smile upon hearing the report. Then, he turned east and began gliding across the sky, the wind whistling softly past his ears as he picked up speed.

The Voskryn emerged from the night like a long ribbon of tarnished silver. Farmland spread in neat patches along one bank, broken here and there by lonely farmhouses with candlelight glowing through shuttered windows, while on the opposite side rose the woods, the endless forest that had swallowed the heart of the empire he once ruled.

He continued flying along the river until the chosen meeting spot finally came into view, a flat stretch of muddy ground directly across from the charred ruins of a certain outpost that had been tragically reduced to ashes by some dark spirit not too long ago.

Viktor activated his “Dark Veil.”

Shadows gathered in response, weaving themselves around his body like layers of black silk. Had anyone been watching from below, they would have seen him vanish completely, becoming indistinguishable from the night itself.

Now. Where was the masked man?

Viktor closed his eyes, seeing through Celeste, who was seeing through Galatea. There he was, a lone figure wrapped in a black robe and a hooded cloak, standing motionless on the thick branch of a tall tree overlooking the riverbank, his head tilted slightly downward as he studied Ekon from his elevated vantage point.

Having located the target, Viktor adjusted his course and drifted toward the tree in question, suppressing every unnecessary movement as the wind slowly, silently carried him into position right above the unsuspecting watcher. The masked man never looked up. His attention remained fixed on Ekon, utterly unaware that death was already hanging over his head.

Viktor dropped from the sky and, in the same breath, triggered another Thauma. Water materialized above his palm, forming a globe of clear, cold liquid that hovered for only a fraction of a second before he sent it forward, slamming into the masked man’s face.

The water did not splash. Instead, it moved like a coherent body with intent, flowing over the man’s face and sealing around his neck with a wet, smacking sound. In an instant, his entire head had been encased in a prison of liquid.

The masked man let out a scream that never left his throat, the sound swallowed by the water and reduced to a muffled, bubbling gurgle that barely reached Viktor’s ears. He stumbled backward on the branch, boots scraping frantically against bark as he tried to regain his footing.

The man’s right hand shot upward and caught a higher branch, fingers locking around it with desperate strength just enough to prevent an immediate fall, while his left clawed at the sphere of water wrapped around his head. But there was nothing to grip, nothing to tear away. Only cold, unyielding liquid that parted around his fingers and flowed back together the moment they passed through, endlessly reforming whenever it was disturbed.

Viktor hovered a few paces away, watching. All he had to do was maintain the shape of the water, and wait.

This was a simple trick, yet it was brutally effective against inexperienced opponents, especially when he used it as a surprise attack. After all, most people, upon finding their heads suddenly submerged in water, tended to panic and act on raw impulse, incapable of mounting any reasonable or effective response.

What this man should have done was hold his breath, then launch a counterattack. Since Viktor needed to stay close to maintain the globe of liquid, if the man could force him to retreat or break concentration, the water would lose cohesion and dissolve on its own. But that required clarity of thought, a luxury the man simply did not have right now.

The masked man’s movements grew more and more erratic. As instinct took over entirely, his right fist abandoned its grip on the branch above to join the other in the hopeless assault on the water enclosing his head. Naturally, he fell.

The body hit the ground with a heavy thud. It lay there motionless, limbs sprawled out across the dirt. In the end, he didn’t even live long enough to drown.

Well, it mattered not. The result was the same anyway.

Viktor descended in a controlled glide. Looking down at the corpse of the masked man, he reached into his pocket.

Then, he pulled out a golden, ringed cross.
