The Master stood, his golden eyes sweeping across the landscape of the Sunken Fane.

"Actually," the Master murmured, "I find this environment rather... distracting for the depth of conversation I require. If we were to talk, let’s do it in a more fitting environment.”

With a casual, almost lazy snap of his fingers, the air behind him tore open. A jagged rift of violet-black energy manifested, swirling with a gravitational pull that made the loose pebbles on the ground dance toward it.

"Shall we?" the Master asked, gesturing toward the portal with a graceful hand. "My private chamber is far more conducive to civil discourse."

Virelle, still pinned to the rock by the crushing weight of his gravity spell, let out a sharp, pained hiss. "And why should we follow a scumbag like you into the dark?" she spat, her voice trembling with the effort of speaking. "You speak of civility while you grind my bones into the dirt!"

The Master looked down at her, his expression devoid of malice, yet chillingly detached. "I have already promised no harm, Starweave. If my intent were to kill you, or your Master, I would have done so ages ago when you were still hiding in that little thicket.”

"Why would I believe a word from a leech who hides behind portals?" Virelle countered, her prismatic orb flickering weakly on the ground. "This is obviously a trap!”

The Master sighed, the sound of a weary teacher dealing with a stubborn student. He looked over his shoulder. "Sylphaine. Stand up. Enter the portal."

Sylphaine, who had remained kneeling in absolute silence, stood up instantly. She didn't hesitate, throwing a brief, smug glance toward Virelle before stepping into the swirling violet void and vanishing.

"I would not send one of my key subordinates to their doom," the Master said, turning back to the group. He raised his arm slightly, and Aiven heard a sickening crunch as the gravity force on Virelle intensified.

Virelle let out a muffled groan of agony, her forehead touching the stone.

"Virelle!" Aiven shouted, his heart hammering against his ribs. He stepped forward, his matte obsidian arm whirring as he looked at the golden-eyed man. "Stop it! Let her go! I’ll go with you! I’ll talk!"

The Master immediately dropped his arm. The heavy pressure vanished instantly, leaving Virelle gasping for air as her mana began to stabilize.

"Excellent," the Master purred, his gilded gaze returning to Aiven. "Practicality is a trait I admire." He glanced back at the gasping mage. "The Starweave—or rather, Virelle, as you call her—may also follow if she desires."

"Of course I am following!" Virelle snarled, pushing herself off the ground and floating back into her defensive position beside Aiven. Her hands were still shaking, but her pride was returning in a frantic rush. "I would never leave my Master alone with a creature as unrefined as you."

They stepped through the portal. Not knowing what to do, Pelka and Vane also stepped inside.

The sensation was like being plunged into a vacuum. The scent of salt and the sound of the wind vanished, replaced by a suffocating, absolute silence. Aiven blinked as his vision adjusted.

They were standing in a long, wide hallway that seemed to stretch into infinity. The walls were made of a dark, seamless stone that absorbed the violet light pulsing from sigils on the floor.

"We usually call this place the Fold," the Master explained, walking ahead of them with his hands tucked into his pockets. "Just so the space has a name.”

As they walked, they passed through a cavernous throne room. Aiven’s eyes widened as he saw the massive obsidian pillars and the bone-white throne atop the high platform.

"This is where we hold our regular meetings," the Master noted casually, not even looking at the throne as he passed.

They eventually arrived in front of a lavishly decorated door made of a dark, polished wood with gold-leaf trim. The Master stopped and turned to Aiven.

"Now," the Master said, his voice dropping into a softer, more intimate tone. "I would like to speak with Aiven Roan alone."

Virelle bristled instantly, her hair flaring. "Alone? Absolutely not! I have already seen enough of your 'hospitality.' I am not leaving my Master’s side for a heartbeat."

"Virelle, it’s okay," Aiven said, placing his human hand on her shoulder. He looked at the lavish door, then at the Master’s calm face. "Judging by everything that's happened... he probably really just wants to talk. If he wanted to do anything else, he wouldn't have brought us here."

Sylphaine, who was leaning against a nearby pillar, let out a melodic, mocking giggle. "The Master is not some underhanded brute, little doll. In fact, you should be on your knees worshiping him. He is currently accomplishing a grand duty of saving this rotting, broken world."

Virelle hissed at the vampire, her magenta eyes glowing. "Saving the world? He is probably just some delusional guy playing the hero while he ruins every life he touches.”

As the two girls began to bicker—Sylphaine’s teasing drawl clashing with Virelle’s theatrical vitriol—the Master offered Aiven a small, genuine-looking smile.

"They can be quite exhausting, can't they?" the Master remarked, reaching out to open the door. He gestured for Aiven to enter. "Come, Aiven. We have much to discuss... and very little time before the world notices you are gone."

The chamber beyond the lavish door was not the den of shadows Aiven had expected. Instead, it was an environment so ordinary it was jarring. The room was a cozy, well-appointed library, smelling of old parchment and cedarwood. Warm, golden light emanated from elegant lamps, and the walls were lined with leather-bound volumes.

In the center of the room sat two high-backed velvet sofas facing each other across a low mahogany table.

"Please, sit," the Master said, gesturing to the sofa with a hand that looked entirely too human.

Aiven hesitated, his obsidian arm whirring as he lowered himself onto the plush cushions. The Master sat opposite him, leaning back with a relaxed, professorial ease.

"I must apologize for not introducing myself sooner," the man began, his gilded eyes fixed on Aiven with a patient warmth. "My name is Lucien. I imagine you are quite confused about who I am, what my objectives are. You likely have enough questions to fill every book on these shelves."

Aiven gripped the edge of the sofa. "That would be an understatement."

Lucien smiled. "There is no need for worry, Aiven. I intend to explain most things to you. It is only fair, given that you are a central pillar of the coming alignment."

He paused, leaning forward to pour two cups of tea from a porcelain pot that had been waiting on the table. "Tell me, Aiven... what do you know of the history of Aerilis? I don’t mean the conquests of the Sovereign or the industrial breakthroughs of the last century. I mean the origin. How these floating islands came to be."

Aiven blinked, his mind reaching back to the dusty classrooms of his youth. "I... I haven't given it much thought since I was a child. We were all taught the same thing. The God of the Skies saw a world of chaos and lifted the righteous pieces of the earth into the heavens to protect them. The islands stay aloft because of his eternal grace."

Lucien let out a soft, melodic laugh. It wasn't a cruel sound, but it carried a weight of profound pity.

"The God of the Skies," Lucien repeated, the name sounding like a bittersweet memory. "A convenient story. And what do they teach you about what lies beneath the islands? Beneath the mist and the clouds?"

"Once the lands were destroyed due to chaos, the God of the Skies eradicated everything, so there is only bottomless sky," Aiven answered automatically. "It’s common knowledge that the pressure becomes so overwhelming that no airship could survive the descent. To fall is to be crushed into nothingness."

Lucien shook his head slowly, taking a sip of his tea. "You are all like dumb sheep being led by a shepherd. You believe everything the authorities tell you because the truth is far more terrifying than a void. The government maintains the 'bottomless sky' narrative to ensure no one ever looks down. They fear what people would do if they realized their 'paradise' is actually a cage."

Aiven felt a cold sweat prickle his neck. "And you? Do you know the truth? Have you actually been to the bottom?"

"I don't need to venture there to know," Lucien said. He set his cup down, his golden eyes suddenly flaring with a brilliance that made the lamps in the room dim by comparison.

"I know what is down there," Lucien whispered, his voice resonating with an authority that felt older than the islands themselves. "Because I am the descendant of the God of the Skies. And I am the only one who can undo the mistake my ancestor made when he hid the world from itself."
