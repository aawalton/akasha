Five days after Aodhán’s Kidnap (real-world time)

A portal hummed to life on the island of Sementra—only a few miles away from the 5th sector of Sunstone—and out of it, nearly three dozen awakened individuals walked out, dressed in the respective colors of their different kingdoms.

Ragnarok in silver and black. Unoros in white and ice blue. Calodan in burnished copper and opal. And lastly, Sunstone in pure gold.

To the Sunstonians within the alliance, the air smelled like home and sunshine—rich with salt and brine from the surrounding sea. But to the rest, it reeked of betrayal and pompous exuberance—a display of wealth so extreme it was no doubt intentional.

Despite being a small mining island, only partially attached to Sunstone, the island of Sementra glittered with all manner of precious stones—each one gleaming with an aurora of colors so bright it was nearly blinding.

Even here, on the fringes of the island, hidden away from the view of common citizens, the soil beneath their feet was threaded with glitter. Crystalline shards gleamed in the dirt like embers caught in twilight, catching the sun and scattering fragments of color across their armor and faces.

It was wealth laid bare, and it was all Artemis could do not to give in to his greed in that moment and begin pulling them out.

Sunstone was a blessed land—of that, there was no dispute.

Despite their constant wars with Ragnarok, the kingdom was still one of the most beautiful kingdoms in Lutia, second only to Unoros, whose icy beauty was almost ethereal. They were, however, the wealthiest, and they made no effort to hide it.

Their streets were paved with gold, and their buildings—even a mundane mining shack—were adorned with gemstones. The opulence was disgusting, and the delicate beauty sickening to many.

The Calodans, for instance, were irritated by the unabashed display of wealth, their deep frowns passing across their distaste clearly, and from their ranks, someone muttered, "I guess this is the kind of wealth that continent-wide betrayal buys you."

The Unorians, on the other hand, looked down on the beauty around them as a shabby comparison to their home, their noses turned up in disdain and snobbish superiority.

The Ragnarians—save for Artemis—had the least reaction to the wealth around them. Their kingdom might not be as opulent as this small mining town, militaristic and minimalistic as they were in terms of architecture, but to them, those minimalistic buildings held a practical beauty that the garish opulence of Sunstone simply lacked.

It was beautiful but utterly impractical and therefore a waste of resources that could have been put to better use.

While they all took in the beauty of their current location, General Li Fang stepped in front of the group to address them.

"Welcome to the island of Sementra," he announced as if he were stating a change in the weather. "We are only a few miles away from the 5th sector and will be led to the anchor site very soon."

"So, in the meantime," Ambassador Morozov drawled sardonically, "you expect us to feast our eyes on this garish display of wealth?" His lips curled in disgust. "Is this supposed to impress us?"

General Fang blinked, his neutral mask unchanging as he slowly spoke, enunciating as if he were speaking to a child. "If I planned to impress you, Ambassador Morozov, I wouldn't have brought you to a small mining island at the border of the 5th sector. I would have taken you to the holy city in sector seven or the city of white-gold in sector two."

"Oh goody," Ambassador Hakim scowled. "More wealth on display. How long do we have to bear this?"

General Fang regarded both ambassadors silently for a moment before sighing. "Look, I know you don't want to work with us. I don't want to work with you either. But circumstances have forced us together. The least you can do is not nip and bite at everything I do like children. If you have grievances to express, then ask your kings to declare war. We will gladly accept."

"Alright, that's enough." General Lucas stepped in before things could escalate further. "In case you've all forgotten, our Supremes all signed a peace treaty we cannot afford to violate."

"I cannot begin to imagine what they were thinking," Ambassador Hakim spat.

"That is because you do not think of or value the lives of your citizens," General Fang replied coldly, triggering a burning rage among the ranks of the other kingdoms. Even General Lucas turned to glare at the man, but he ignored them all and continued. "I do not agree with the decision of my Supreme either, but at least I have the decency not to show it."

"Then I must be seeing double," Ambassador Morozov sneered. "Because it's as clear to me as day."

"I doubt anything is clear to you, Morozov, with your sight half-hidden by that garish helmet."

That seemed to be the limit both ambassadors could stomach, and they began raining insults on each other while General Lucas futilely tried to play mediator.

While they argued, their entourage watched in fascination and agreement, many even making comments in support of their ambassadors. And though no one from the Ragnarian or Unorian entourage spoke, it was obvious they were thoroughly enjoying the scene.

Some even hoped within their hearts that it escalated into something more violent.

One such person was Tyrus Hadjen. Aldric may have permanently destroyed his fiery rage against the Sunstonians, but that did nothing for his hatred of them.

He stood close to Aldric's silent form now, a glare etched into his features as he watched the tension between the Calodans and the Sunstonians begin to thicken. Beside him, Aldric sighed in resignation, most likely feeling the rising tension on a much deeper level than the rest of them.

He glared at the Calodans and then sighed. "This is a waste of time."

"That's not true," Tyrus disagreed. "If anything, this is the most productive use of my time." His gaze hardened, and, in a whisper, he added, "Sunstonians deserve every insult that comes their way."

"And my brothers deserve to be rescued as soon as possible by people willing to put their differences aside and work together."

Tyrus gritted his teeth but said nothing, unable to deny the truth in Aldric's words. However, in the presence of Sunstonians, such logic was easily forgotten.

Fortunately—or unfortunately—a swirling portal chose that moment to appear, putting a temporary lid on the chaos. Tyrus wasn't sure whether to be happy or annoyed. His expression, however, still morphed into a grimace when a dozen Sunstonian soldiers stepped out of the swirling portal, dressed head to toe in liquid gold.

Their soul flames blazed brightly to his senses, and Tyrus placed them roughly between the seventy-fifth and ninety-fifth tiers.

They were strong—elites at least—and when their gaze landed on General Li, they all saluted, their fists striking against their breastplates in unison. "May light guide you, General."

"At ease, soldiers," General Fang responded, his expression morphing back into a neutral mask. "Light guide our steps."

The soldiers eased into a relaxed stance, and their commander stepped forward. "We're ready for you, sir. We've secured the perimeter of the anchor site and screened everyone present to make sure there are no cultists among them."

"And the anchor itself?" General Fang questioned. "What can you tell us about it?"

The commander shifted awkwardly. "No one has entered it, sir. In fact, the researchers only just found it a few hours ago."

"It's a building?" Ambassador Hakim frowned. "I'm not sure what I was expecting the anchor to be, but a building?"

The soldier glanced at the ambassador and reluctantly answered. "It's not exactly a building, sir. It's very strange."

"Strange how?" General Lucas asked.

The commander returned his gaze to General Fang but answered anyway. "Strange in ways I cannot explain, sir. I think it's better if you see it for yourself."

The ambassadors exchanged glances, and General Lucas sighed. "Then there's no need to dally."

He gestured toward the portal, but the soldiers waited until General Fang did the same before moving. General Fang stepped into the portal first, followed by the Sunstonian entourage. The Unorian entourage went next, and the Calodans followed. General Lucas and the Ragnarian entourage went in last, with Ali'Hassani and Artemis bringing up the rear.

The portal snapped shut behind them, and an instant later, they all arrived on a ridge of weathered sandstone that overlooked a sight none of them were prepared for, including General Fang and the Sunstonian champions.

Below them, surrounded by a sprawling camp of soldiers and white-robed researchers, stretched a circular basin of rust-colored sand carved into Sunstone's otherwise verdant landscape. The desert pocket was about a hundred yards across, its edges marked by the abrupt transition from golden grasslands to wind-carved dunes.

It was as if someone had carved a piece of Calodan and transplanted it here, complete with bone-dry heat that immediately began drawing moisture from their skin.

But it wasn't the unexpected desert itself that caught their attention.

It was the inverted ziggurat that plunged into the earth like a wound in reality—its rust-colored tiers carved in perfect symmetry. Its crown shimmered with a dense layer of runic inscriptions that shone golden in the sunlight, pulsing in a slow, hypnotic rhythm that looked almost organic, like veins of lava flowing through living rock.

The alliance stared at it in shock, and while most were simply enraptured by its beauty, the Calodans were not.

For the desert kingdom, ziggurats were a common sight. Most of their temples were ziggurats, each one a testament to their devotion to Tihamun, the desert god.

The ziggurats were symbolic of a ladder of faith—of advancement toward the divine, each tier a step closer to the heavens. But an inverted ziggurat? That was no climb to the gods. That was a descent—an intentional profanation. A blasphemy that sought not to honor the divine but to drag it downward. To hell.

"An affront!" Ambassador Hakim snarled at the repulsive structure. "A descent to evil. These cultists have truly gone mad."

But while the Calodans expressed their anger, General Fang and the rest of the Sunstonians battled their shock. As a direct assistant to the Supreme, Li Fang was well-versed in his kingdom's history.

He knew just about every secret there was to know about Lutia and Sunstone. And yet, he knew nothing about an inverted ziggurat or how a structure this massive could possibly be here with no known record of it.

His mind raced with a thousand thoughts, confusion apparent in his features, but before he could think of anything logical to say, the dreaded question came.

"What is the meaning of this?" General Lucas spoke, his tone laced with unveiled suspicion. "I thought you said you knew nothing about the anchor. A structure this big? And you knew nothing?"

General Fang grimaced. He was not oblivious to Sunstone's reputation, nor was he blind to how suspicious the whole situation seemed. First, they find an anchor in Sunstone of all places, and now they discover that it's a structure that couldn't possibly have stayed hidden for long.

Sunstone couldn't deny knowing of it. In fact, saying they had absolutely no record of this structure would only make them seem more suspicious.

And yet, it was the truth.

Grimace tightening, he turned his gaze to Nayra al-Sahr, the resonance awakened whom he'd been told had found the anchors in the first place, and asked, "Are you sure this is the right place?"

Nayra's scowl bordered on disgust. "It is the right place, General. I can sense the anchor within that unholy structure."

"Of course you can sense it, Nayra," Ambassador Hakim snarled. "It makes perfect sense. Sunstonians in cahoots with cultists—"

"We are not in cahoots with the Children," General Fang cut in with a sharp glare. "Did you forget that our citizens were also kidnapped?"

"Oh, we remember it alright," Ambassador Hakim snapped back. "And we remember that while every other kingdom lost dozens of citizens, you only lost three. Three! If those aren't convenient numbers, then I do not know what else is!"

By now the suspicion had grown so thick that even the Sunstonians themselves began doubting General Fang. It was all too convenient. And a structure this large—how could the General say he had no idea it existed?

They didn't need to speak before their doubts began to show. And before long, even General Fang began to doubt the kingdom itself. However, one thing held him steady.

And that was the fact that if Sunstone was in any way connected with the cultists, they wouldn't have left a structure this large as evidence. They weren't stupid.

Which meant this was orchestrated. Planned.

His gaze narrowed as he glared down at the rows of research tents littering the basin. When his gaze landed on the largest tent, he spoke. "I'll only say this once, and I care not if you believe me. Sunstone is not in cahoots with the Children. I know nothing of this structure—"

Ambassador Hakim scoffed, but before he could speak, General Fang continued. "You don't have to take my word for it, Hakim. I'm just as confused as you are. But perhaps the lead researcher on this project can shed some much-needed light on the matter."

He glanced at Warp, and the reality awakened instantly vanished. She reappeared a moment later, a raven-haired woman held tightly in her grasp.

The woman's eyes widened when she saw them all, and she immediately bowed. "Greetings, generals and ambassadors. Greetings, General Li Fang. I wasn't expecting you so soon, sir."

"Tell us what you know of this structure," General Fang asked without preamble, and the woman immediately nodded. Warp placed her gently on the ground, and she bowed again.

"My name is Pythia Fibonacci, lead researcher appointed to this project by Supreme General Jia Ling herself." She glanced at General Lucas and the other ambassadors before continuing. "I and my team received the missive from Ragnarok concerning the anchors two days ago, and while General Fang worked on getting a temporary truce going, the Supreme sent me and my people here to investigate the site."

"However,” she gestured to the basin. “When we got here, this entire area was a grassland of golden plumes. The structure was nonexistent. In fact, this entire basin was nonexistent.”

Everyone frowned, and Pythia nodded, validating their confusion.

“I understand how that can be confusing, but I swear on my core that I am telling the truth.”

Essence gathered around her form, oath chains forming to validate her words, and after a moment of silence, General Fang spoke. “What then happened? If the structure was nonexistent, how does it stand before us now?”

“Through a fusion of magic and what I and my team have been able to identify as ancient technology. For the first twenty-four hours, I and my team searched the area tirelessly for a cave, an artifact—anything at all that might serve to anchor a foreign reality to ours. But after the first day, we began thinking that perhaps we'd been given the wrong location.”

Pythia paused, building the suspense. “That was until a member of my team found this."

She pulled out a black spike from her spatial storage and presented it to General Fang, but General Lucas grabbed it before Fang could, eyes narrowing as he scanned the awakened artifact covered in an intricate layer of iridescent silver runes.

Confused, he presented the spike to Geneva, and everyone turned to glance at her as she cradled the spike with an expression of awe and fascination.

And then she frowned. “This is strange…”

“What is it?” General Lucas probed.

“I… I don’t know exactly,” Geneva stuttered, her expression deepening. “But the spike is covered in not one, but two layers of runes—the first on the surface and the other within?” She glanced up, as if searching for anyone who could validate her hypothesis. She found none, and her expression deepened even further. “I can sense elder reality runes deep within the spike, and yet, it’s somehow connected to the perception runes on the surface, both working in concert to create a fusion of two elder runes…” She glanced at Pythia in shock. “How is this possible?”

Everyone glanced at her in shock. The Witch of Selia—the prodigy of runes—had no idea what function a circuit of runes served. Just what was this black spike?!

Pythia shook her head. “We do not know for sure. We had been hoping you would be able to tell us. What I can tell you, however, is what the spike does.”

“What does it do?” Ambassador Morozov asked impatiently.

"It bends reality and perception to a degree I’ve never seen or heard of before," Pythia replied somberly. "We found thirteen of them scattered at irregular points around the area. And the moment we uprooted them all, which was just a few hours ago, the ziggurat and the basin suddenly revealed themselves. As of yesterday, I could have sworn that we were on plain land."

General Lucas sighed, trying to make sense of the convoluted explanation. "So, in one word, the ziggurat was hidden from the senses of mythics and calamities by thirteen of these spikes?"

"Yes," Pythia replied confidently. "We marked the points we uprooted each spike from. If we reinsert the spikes, this basin will disappear again. If you want to test it—"

“We believe you,” Ambassador Hakim cut her off with a shake of his head. “We just cannot imagine how such a thing is possible. The senses of Calamities are no small thing. They are practically gods. How can the cultists possess items that can fool gods?”

“Perhaps, they do not possess it,” Pythia spoke, her voice tentative, showing that this was an idea she was only just processing. “The cultists we know are not advanced enough for technology such as this. They are a thorn in our flesh, true, but they are not a world-ending threat. The fact that they possess technology such as this can only mean one of two things.”

She glanced at General Fang, eyes widening as her mind began connecting dots. "We haven't been able to do much testing, but we've been able to measure the age of these spikes, and they are old. Ancient even—predating the Attilan incursion.”

"What!" Ripples of shock echoed through everyone present, and General Fang grabbed the spike from Geneva, peering at a technology that was supposedly a millennium old. Even the champions gathered to stare at the spike, their hatred for each other temporarily forgotten in the face of this relic.

"How—" Ambassador Morozov began to ask, but General Fang cut him off with a growl.

"Where are the other spikes? You said there were thirteen of them."

"Yes," Pythia replied, lifting a hand to point at a research tent. She'd barely indicated which exact tent when Artemis and Warp teleported, space and reality twisting in response to their abrupt displacement.

They reappeared an instant later, both with six spikes each. And the tension between them suddenly stretched taut. The generals and ambassadors glared at each other for a moment before General Fang finally spoke. "I shall take four of the spikes since we agreed to share whatever we find equally."

"That is acceptable," Ambassador Hakim muttered, grabbing three pins from Warp before she could so much as blink. A moment later, each kingdom representative held four of the reality-bending pins in their hands, holding onto them as if they were realm-altering artifacts.

They might as well be. Any item that could fool the senses of calamities and mythics was a god-tier item.

"How is this possible?" Ambassador Morozov finally asked his question.

Pythia spoke confidently. "There are two possible explanations. The first is that these items were created by the first cultists, actual followers of Sárán Beithir."

“And this set of cultists have only just inherited it.” General Fang concluded. “What’s the second?”

He regretted the question almost immediately.

"The second possible explanation is that the items and structure were created by Sunstone before the Attilan war."

"And they hid it?" Ambassador Morozov asked, suspicion leaking into his voice once again. "Why?"

This time it was General Lucas who replied, his expression hard. "We won't know until we enter it. Whoever created this structure is of little importance. We know that it is an anchor—a potential gateway into the realm of the cultists. We will deal with the intricacies later. First, we need to enter it."

He glanced at Pythia. "Have your people entered the ziggurat yet?"

"No, sir. We wanted to wait for your arrival."

"Well, we are here now." General Lucas glared at the ziggurat. "Take us to the entrance. We have wasted enough time talking already."

Pythia nodded, and a few minutes later, the alliance stood before the entrance of the ziggurat, so close now that they could see the individual runic veins on its surface. General Fang frowned, deeply concerned.

Pythia's statement about the structure being created by Sunstone was too dangerous, and if it turned out to be true, what could he say in their defense?

Anything about the Attilan war was bad for Sunstone. Cultists or not, it didn't look good that a kingdom with a reputation for betrayal was the only one housing an anchor in the whole of Lutia. To make things worse, they might have been the ones to create this structure that the cultists were now using as a gateway into the real world.

It didn't look good at all, but the truth could wait for now. Right now, catching the cultists was more important.

With a small nod, General Fang stepped through the entrance, a whispered prayer echoing on his lips. "Light guide me."

Do not kill—capture. If we can bring Sebastian home before the alliance finds him, the better. It must never be known that a rune was captured by cultists."
