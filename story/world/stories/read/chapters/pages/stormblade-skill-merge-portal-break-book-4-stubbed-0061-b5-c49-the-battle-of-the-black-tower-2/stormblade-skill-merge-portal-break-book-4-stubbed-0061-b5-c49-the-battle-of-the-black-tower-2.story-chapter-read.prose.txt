We didn’t have him. The God of Thunder couldn’t be had. Not by two S-Rankers, and certainly not by a gimmick like the Traynor-Overholz Cannons. Not at his level of power.

He swept his spear across the space between us. Thunder crashed out, and the Storm’s Blade caught the blow. Shadow rippled across him—Ellen’s Voidwalker-enhanced Abyssal Storm tearing into both him and me—and rain poured down around us. The storm overhead slowly ramped up into something more. The Eye of the Storm was starting to open.

That was how we’d get him. I lashed out. Laminar Blade and Stormfall enhanced the thrust, forming another wave of thunder as the blade severed the very air. The blade sank through the God of Thunder’s armor and exploded, bending Stormsteel and maelstrom alike. But under it, the Paragon’s lightning body was intact.

“Nice one, Kade. Let me show you how it’s done.” Before I could react, the spearhead crashed into my pauldron, a fraction of an inch over my shoulder. Metal warped. The maelstrom flickered out. He’d shattered the shoulder-guard in one blow, and he’d done it without seeming to enhance his weapon. The blow had been fast. Too fast to see.

I’d have to do something about that.

I growled. Pulled my aura back until it was nothing. Until it only enhanced me. The Stormshade Anvil—that intense, storm-centered focus—filled me, and my reactions accelerated. The battle trance. But without fury. Without rage. Nothing but tightened muscles, eyes that saw the God of Thunder twitch.

His spear struck like a rattlesnake biting.

The Storm’s Blade moved a few inches.

And the spear passed over my head, missing by a hair.

More blows rained down. The Stormshade Anvil tightened its grip. Miniscule adjustments in the God of Thunder’s posture led to attacks from predictable angles. Even his electric arc-and-orb body obeyed the laws of reality, and those laws gave me all the tells I needed. A shift of weight to the left foot—the blow that followed it might as well have been called out loud. The Storm’s Blade needed only the barest hint of adjustment to deflect it, and I had time to think about the angle to parry at so that, this time, it didn’t shred my joints.

But it wasn’t just reaction time and reflexes. As I leaped a sweeping blow that shattered brick as it passed inches from the Lightning Tower’s wall, I saw the real potential of the Stormshade Anvil—and its weakness.

It let me see. It let me react.

And it also let me understand what was happening. The God of Thunder was still holding back. He was still…

Oh.

He was still teaching. Just like my push to C-Rank, Eugene was still teaching with every blow. I had openings in my defenses, even with the Stormshade Anvil, and he wasn’t taking them. I tested it. Launched myself into an attack that would force the God of Thunder into a hit-or-be-hit situation. If I was right, his spear would punch through my breastplate. It was the perfect opening.

The God of Thunder didn’t take it. His spear reversed, and its butt slammed against my stomach, driving the air from my lungs. Shadow gripped at him, but he spun, breaking dozens of black, incorporeal wrists. “Take the gift, Kade. I can end this whenever I want. I just don’t want it over yet.”

“I do, though. I want you dead.”

“Oh? That’s interesting. Most of pupils—“

“I don’t care.” Fury coursed through my veins. Fury and rage. The Stormshade Anvil fell apart around me.

And the Stormshade Avatar began forming.

Far below Nathan Anders, the city’s 303 Wall exploded.

The moment the Traynor-Overholz Cannons fired, though, he threw himself straight up.

Smoke rose over Phoenix, and the 303 Wall’s defenses activated, sealing the city in a bubble of energy that held the gunsmoke in an uncanny reversal of the Siege of Phoenix. He wasn’t fooled as it passed.

The Phoenix Reborn reached the top of the shielded dome. His flaming wings spread to slow him down. And behind him, the shells rained down.

His S-Rank senses showed him everything. A street intersection downtown was instantly obliterated, the skyscrapers’ facades blowing out and raining brick and glass down on a handful of B-Rankers. A single A-Ranker fighting against overwhelming odds found himself at the center of an explosion that wiped out most of the cultists he was up against. Two halves of a squad found themselves cut off from each other when a building collapsed.

Explosions across the city—both from the roughly third of the guns loaded with explosives and from anything that could ignite doing so—rippled in a machine-gun chain reaction. Now, it all fell on the Phoenix Reborn.

Nathan Anders circled two more times as the guns fell into an unsteady rhythm, each team loading and firing as quickly as they could. The air stabilized, and he swooped down toward the city.

And a new threat rose to meet him.

Anterox, Storm’s Sentinel, Paragon of the Cold Front: S-Rank

A winged serpent. But more massive than the one Kade had bound to himself. Where his was lithe, with clouded wings, this one’s body seemed entirely cloud-formed. A pale blue body stuck from between the cold, wispy cumulus clouds, its eyes almost too small and its skin almost too smooth to be such a powerful monster. But all around the Phoenix Reborn, the air grew colder, and ice formed on his battle armor.

Anterox rose to meet him. The winged serpent’s aura of freezing vapor whirled around them, blotting out Nathan’s vision of the city—and the battle raging below. He clenched his teeth as flames guttered out and rekindled across his body.

Beams of flame ripped out from Nathan’s outstretched hands, slammed into walls of ice, and melted through them. Pulses of heat tore into the screaming, frigid winter wind. Neither could focus on where those missiles were heading. Neither cared about the booming guns, the fighting delvers, or the cultists that continued to pour into the city’s streets.

The war raged.

But for them, there was only the battle in front of them.
