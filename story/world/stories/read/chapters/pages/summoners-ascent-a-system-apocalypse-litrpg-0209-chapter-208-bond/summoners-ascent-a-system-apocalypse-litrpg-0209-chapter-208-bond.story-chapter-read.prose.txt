"How unfair," Oka let out a long sigh, shaking their head back and forth. "You're ridiculously strong, and now your technique is even improving?"

Ashton turned around toward them from atop the corpse of the giant beast that had attacked them. It was the size of a house, though it was now collapsed on top of this desert dune.

"Well, it took me long enough," Ashton sighed, jumping down from the massive sand-fish. He turned toward it, seeing the relatively intact corpse. Until not too long ago, this thing would look like a complete and utter mess, since all Ashton could do was randomly slam his weapon into it.

It was messy and took an unnecessarily long time to take monsters like this down, but now that he had Oka's teachings? The carcass was kept so well-intact that Ashton would be able to sell some of the materials he would have otherwise lost out on.

"Hm, I think you've made pretty good progress for just a few years of training with me." Oka shrugged. "You are completely hopeless at moving your body, but you have great strength, flexibility, and stamina, and I only need to show you the movements once."

Ashton sighed and grabbed the dagger sheathed at his hip. He started digging it into the sand-fish's thick hide and cut into it so that it would bleed out. Since it was already on a slope, they would just have to wait for a while before being able to grab something to eat from this.

"Sure, but that doesn't mean that I can do it right away. The only benefit I have is that I can correct myself easily enough since I know exactly what it should look like," Ashton explained with a sigh, as he spread out some of his internal energy and bloodlust to scare away some of the scavengers that would usually come after this carcass.

"But it's paying off," Oka said with a smile, placing their arms around Ashton from behind. "You finally have the basics of staff combat down almost perfectly. So the rest should come to you faster, now."

"Hm, we'll see. I doubt it," Ashton sighed. By now, after seeing just how hard it had been for him to just pick up the basics, he had no hope of being a 'master'. "But well, I have other strengths. I've learned a lot of things here and there."

He reached out to one of the sand-fish and tore off one of the plates of sandstone-like scales. It was as large as his palm. Oka peered down over Ashton's head curiously.

"What is that?"

"Something pretty neat," Ashton replied, running his thumb over the scale's edge, rubbing away part of the stone. "When these sand-fish go out to hunt, they harden the sand around them as another protective layer. But otherwise, they just swim through the sand, and as the tiny particles rub against the scales, they turn rather beautiful."

Finally, the layer of sandstone cracked off. Oka was momentarily blinded by the sunlight that bounced off the surface underneath. Ashton smiled and continued removing the stone, until all that was left was a silver, iridescent scale that glittered beautifully under the desert sun.

Ashton reached the scale up toward Oka. "This is something like what you were looking for, isn't it?"

Oka's eyes widened as they snatched the scale out of Ashton's hand. "Yes! This is perfect! The one I'm used to was a feather, but the colour and shine is almost identical! I can definitely work with this, thank you!"

Hugging Ashton tightly, Oka pulled him closer. "Are all the scales like this?"

"Mm-hm. But you need to be careful, they're very sharp as well. If you want to use them to make something, you might need to blunt the edges a little first," Ashton warned, but Okay instantly shook their head.

"No, even better! The feathers I mentioned come from the steel rok! They're incredibly sharp and are known for injuring people as they fall from the sky." With a shockingly bright smile while explaining that, Oka let go of Ashton and approached the sand-fish. "I'll grab as many as I can!"

"Sure. After that, let's have some fresh fish for lunch," Ashton suggested, but he had a bad gut-feeling right now. "Wait... stop, I think I might be getting–"

"Again? It's been more often recently, right?" Oka turned to him, abandoning the scale that they were trying to pull out. "You'll come back... right?"

Ashton nodded. "Yes, I'll come back. They're getting impatient, but I'm still showing results, so it should be okay."

As he spoke, Ashton started feeling something wrap around him. It wasn't a physical sensation; it was more like a tight chain was wrapping around his soul, about to drag him into the ground. "It doesn't feel like a one-way summon, either. Those are a lot more violent."

Oka nodded with a pale face. "I'll wait here..."

Ashton smiled, trying to calm them down. "It's okay. If I'm not back by nightfall, go back to that cave we walked past earlier. I'll find you there."

"Alright. My heart is yours."

"My heart is yours," Ashton replied. He had felt a bit awkward about that phrase at first, but it was important to Oka, so he got used to it.

Almost as soon as he finished speaking, the world around him shifted. It was like the sand fell away into eternal darkness, before being replaced by the snow-covered ground of a frozen tundra. The moment he was called, Ashton dropped to his knees and lowered his head until it touched the snow.

"I greet the great Summoner," Ashton said reverently. It was an act, of course, but one that he had to put on whenever he was called back.

"Human. Tell me what you have found," the Summoner commanded.

Their voice sounded... weird. Weirder than usual. It was always a mess off cobbled-together voices and sounds that came together to form words, but usually, it sounded chaotic, yet harmonious. But now, it was disjointed, like the sounds weren't synchronised anymore.

Ashton had heard this twice before. Five years after he was summoned, and then another time, thirteen years after being summoned. And each time afterward, the Summoner entered a long, deep slumber. The first time they took almost two years to wake up, the second time just five months. But from what he gathered from the summons that have been around longer than him, the shorter the last slumber was, and the longer it had been since the last one, the longer the next one would be. It had been fifteen years since the last slumber, which was only a few months long.

This would be a long one. Years, most likely. Ashton was almost giddy at the idea, to the point where he couldn't stop himself from smiling while giving his report. Something that the Summoner took notice of. Ashton felt sharp pains in his back, as the Summoner pushed three of their claws through his clothes and into his skin.

"From what source comes your joy, human?" they asked, but Ashton just froze up. He tried to reply, but the Summoner's claw had pierced his lungs. With a groan that sounded like a mountain crumbling under its own weight, the creature that controlled Ashton dragged its claws up toward his neck. They slowly released from his flesh before the large hand-like appendages of the Summoner wrapped themselves around Ashton's neck and throat.

They pulled him up and held him forward to look into his eyes. "My slumber will be long. You better complete your journey by the time I am awake. If you disappoint me, and you have not found the object of my desire, and have not found the truth that I seek, then you will see what the word 'wrath' truly means."

The summoner threw Ashton to the ground. "Replenish the potions, and the inks, and repair the enchantments on the broken items. When you are done, I will send you back so you may continue your journey."

Ashton could feel his body heal. It was painful, like his flesh was stretching out toward the other side of the wound, but it healed. "Yes, Master."

He turned, getting ready to rush toward the tent. It would take days to finish everything. He had to hurry. He had to get back to Oka.

"Oh, and before I forget..." the Summoner said, leaning over Ashton. A number of their tentacle-like arms wrapped around Ashton's body, as their fake, crumbling face leaned over him and looked down. The Summoner's neck twisted so that they and Ashton were face-to-face. "Do not think that I do not know about your little 'friend'. If you fail to do your duty, then do not kid yourself that you will go unpunished."

Ashton's heart stopped for a moment. He could feel it. The fear from the very idea made his run blood cold, and his entire body stopped functioning for just a moment.

"Please, Master... I swear, I promise, I vow that I will do as you ask. Just please leave them be. They are naught but a companion. They have taught me to fight, so I may be more useful to you. They have taught me to sew, so I can be more pleasing to your eye. They have given me knowledge that has been useful on my journey. They are a tool that I am using so that you may thrive as you deserve to."

This was the longest unprompted thing that Ashton had ever said to the Summoner. Before, the only times he would say so much was when he was giving reports. So this was clearly unnatural, and they both could tell.

"I will consider allowing you to keep this 'tool' of yours as a reward if you succeed," the Summoner said, clutching Ashton's body tightly. It still took a while until they finally let go of him, and Ashton could get started on his work.

He worked without a single break, without mistake, without a moment of inefficiency. It was to the point where the other summons were concerned for him. Those that hadn't seen him in a while wanted to drag him away and get him to rest, but he couldn't afford that.

He simply apologised to them all whenever they came to him. And when he was done, he ran to the summoner, who finally sent him back without another word.

It didn't seem like they were able to 'speak' anymore. They would fall asleep in just another two or three days.

Ashton found himself back in the desert. The sand-fish's carcass had been rotting away, and had been picked half-clean by scavengers. But he didn't even look at that for a second, and instead immediately turned and ran toward the cave.

The frost of this cold desert was clinging to his skin, but Ashton didn't care. He ran into the cave, and soon, sitting in the light of a fire meant to keep them warm through the night, he saw Oka.

The moment they spotted each other, Oka jumped up.

"Ashton! Finally you're back! I was starting to get worried that you–" Oka spoke, almost tearing up out of relief. But they stopped when Ashton dropped to his knees in front of them.

"I'm sorry... Oka, I'm so, so sorry..." Ashton said, apologising profusely. "They know about you... My summoner knows you exist, and I–I think that if I don't manage to complete my task, they will hurt you... I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I didn't want to drag you into this, I just–"

Oka dropped down and wrapped their arms around him, pulling him close. "It's okay. I figured this would happen sooner or later. But it's alright. I will help you complete your task, and it will be okay."

"No, I can't drag you into this, I just can't..."

Oka pulled back slightly and held Ashton's face in both of their hands. "Please. You already have my heart, so I will never stray from your side."

They stood up and walked back to the campfire, picking up something from beside where they were seated before.

It was something like a crown made from the iridescent scales of the sand-fish. "I wanted to show this to you when it was done, but I figured this may work as well. In my tribe... when one wishes to bond with another, they present them with one of these. Usually made from feathers fallen from the plumage of the steel rok, but I had to improvise."

"Hm?" Ashton stared at Oka confused. He knew what the word 'bond' in their language implied, but he still wasn't following.

"We have shared our bodies, and we hold each other's hearts," Oka started, holding the crown toward Ashton. "And I hope now, you will share your soul with me. In your language, that would be, uhm... marr–"

"Stop. Stop, just... just hold on," Ashton stood up, rushing over to his bag. He had left it behind since he was summoned so abruptly. In there was a chain with quite big links that were useful to set traps for stronger beasts.

Ashton infused his internal energy into his hands and the chain, snapped off one of the links, and then bent it into shape, before going back to Oka.

"Uhm... I know that your world doesn't really have the concept of 'gender' the same way we do, and I know you always struggled to understand it when I tried to explain... but in my world, traditionally, the one who asks this is the man. Well, not always, and especially more recently it's been changing a lot, and it's actually kind of–" Ashton could see Oka's confused expression as he rambled on. He let out a deep breath. "More importantly, I always thought myself as being the one to ask this when the time came."

He held forward the ring that he had improvised just now. "In my culture, we present our partner with a ring. It should be a lot fancier than this, but, well... I'll get you a better one later. Oka. Will you marry me?"

Oka smiled down as Ashton knelt in front of them, and slowly nodded. "Yes, of course. And will you be my bond?"

"Of course I will," Ashton replied. And as he pushed the roughly-made ring onto Oka's finger, they placed the unfinished crown onto his head.
