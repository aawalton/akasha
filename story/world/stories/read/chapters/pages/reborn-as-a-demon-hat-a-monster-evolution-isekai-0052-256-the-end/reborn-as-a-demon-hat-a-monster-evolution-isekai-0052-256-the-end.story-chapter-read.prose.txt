Eastmarch

Camoran, Palace of Unification

Far to the East, Cardinal Langley was sheparding the rebuilding process of Kaedmon’s Cathedral.

The rain that had dominated Eastmarch for the past few months had finally abated. And the people, once scared, hungry, and divided, were finally starting to come together.

He looked out from the top spire of the grand building – renamed the Palace of Unification by his own decree – and couldn’t help but smile at the progress that had been made since the Archon’s coming.

He’d opened the gates to the Hybrids who had revolted in the Hinterlands, offering them a real say in the politics of the new nation that was growing in Ethan’s name. To the people of the city, he had proclaimed a new dawn – a new foundation of cooperation that would be built between all peoples of Argwyll. Voices of dissent had – of course – roared at first. But when the people of the city heard the war was over, when mothers and fathers could be placated by the knowledge that their corrupted children could be cured by the Fifth Column Druids, they had started to come around.

The process was slow. And it would be arduous. But already Langley and his own council had started to make headway. He was due today to receive a delegation from the newly established Hybrid-run farmsteads in the Hinterlands, sheparded by none other than the Master Druid Malak of Westerweald.

Though he expected a ferocious debate on both sides, he consoled himself with the belief that, for the first time in his life, he was sheparding his people towards a cause that would actually benefit them. All of them.

“Maybe I can finally take a break,” he whispered as he looked towards the rising sun on the horizon. “Maybe, finally, the promise of peace might actually be re-“

“CARDINAL!”

His reverie was interrupted by a newly anointed Hybrid initiate – a Minxit named Varys. The young cat-man barreled through his doorway, fur matted with sweat, and began screaming before Langley could even ask how he managed to get through his securely locked door.

“Cardinal..Langley!” he shouted. “C-Cardinal!”

Langley spun, speeding towards the young lad as he dropped to his knees, panting with sudden exertion.

“Varys…what ails you, son?” he asked. “Come, sit. Sit by the fire and –“

His voice died in his throat as he looked into the eyes of the Minxit.

What he saw in those eyes was not evidence of a mortal consciousness.

Where once the Minxit’s baby-blue irises had peeked at him in agitation, now his eyes were awash with an impure, sickly green.

“Varys…”

The cat-man suddenly threw him off, knocking the young priest aside. Langley got to his feet in an instant, panic overcoming all his other senses.

The Minxit tore off his drab robe and fixed Langley with cold, dead eyes. The eerie glow within them, coupled with his twitchy, almost puppet-like movement, made him seem more like a kind of zombified corpse than a living, breathing being.

“What is the meaning of this!?” Langley shouted – knowing as he did so how useless the question was.

Because all Varys did was smile at him and whisper – in a voice that was definitely not his own:

“It…is…time…”

Langley stepped back, quickly readying a [Blinding Bolt] spell that could paralyze the lad so that he could at least work out what was wrong with him.

But as his hands worked in the somatic pattern that would summon the spell, he noted just how empty the gesture felt.

And with a sudden, horrific realization he knew: there was no latent power within him any more at all.

Vary’s mouth twisted in an unnatural grin, the green eyes becoming more and more lambent with each passing moment.

“There shall be…no escape.”

From behind there then came a roar – something that reached Langley’s ears all too late. Then a blast of force threw him against the far side of the room, hard. He wheezed, feeling the world begin to drip away into darkness.

And then, he saw it.

Far beyond the city, rising from the oceans as though to challenge the heavens themselves, a column of green energy the exact same hue as that which lay within Varys’s eyes was billowing, twisting the clouds and causing them to swirl around it. The sheer power of the thing was palpable, and no [Appraisal] Langley attempted could identify it.

His ears rung as the sound of the concussive blast still echoed through his being. But even still, he could hear the dull, dead sound of laughter that echoed from Varys’s throat:

“There shall be…no…escape.”

Consciousness Shutdown Initiated.

Spirit Core Siphon in 3…2…1…

Westerweald

Arcona, College of Sorcery

Mara had been teaching some of the Hybrid and human children in the Arcona College of Sorcery a new variation of the basic [Firebolt] spell.

But something had happened during her lesson.

The children – human and Hybrid both – had started acting strangely. After the first bout of target practice, a few of them had complained of a migraine. Mara had taken it as just a sign of magical fatigue. Even Agna had been telling her how much she’d been putting these kids through their paces recently.

But Mara had also felt something when the first complaints had begun. A kind of knocking inside her skull had started up. She started to look differently at the white walls of the college training room around her and the children. She’d started to see them come…undone. They’d began to wobble as though an earthquake was occurring right under their feet. But she could detect now seismic blast.

She looked to Agna, who had been beside her during the lesson, and saw the lines of worry that were creasing the old Praise-Singer’s face.

Mara eyed her suspiciously.

“Agna..?”

“Shh, child,” the old woman said, whispering like there was someone who had suddenly entered the training hall and was listening into their session. “Do you hear it?”

Mara shrugged, but the old woman grabbed her arm as she began to turn away to attend to the increasingly disoriented students.

“A voice on the wind,” Agna muttered from beside her – in a voice that barely seemed like it belonged to her.

“…a voice that carries death.”

When the Hopla child looked up, she saw that Agna’s body was shaking.

“Go,” she told her. “Go, child. Run.”

Mara shook her head.

“What’s hap-“

“GO!” the Praise-Singer roared before she doubled over in pain. Mara flew to her side instantly, but was then struck by the sudden inexplicable sense of silence that now dominated the room.

Looking up, she saw something glowing in the eyes of the students around her.

A sickly green light, lambent and shining.

A light that spoke of death.

She felt Agna push her away as he coughed, hands flying to grab her head where every vein was starting to bulge.

“RUN!” she pleaded. It was not a request.

Mara stepped back, trying to reach out to each of them through telepathic communication. She tried entering their minds, knowing how dangerous it was, but trying anyway. Whatever spell was taking hold of them, she could fight it. She could –

“AH!”

She grasped her head, feeling something claw at her as she attempted to at least tell her friends they were ok. Instead of sending them an image of peace, she had seen something else. A set of burning green eyes against a desolate, burnt wasteland…

It had always been watching.

“It’s alright…now…Mara,” he murmured – voice completely devoid of emotion. “You’re…going…somewhere…safe.”

She saw the evil light burning in his eyes. She saw the intent behind them.

And with an eruption of raw magic energy, she threw him off her and bolted from the training room.

She ran, directionless, for what seemed like hours. Everywhere she went she saw more children bumbling through the hallways of the college, knocking against each other, laughing like they were drunk.

And every one of them met her eyes and pointed shaking fingers towards her.

“Mara!” they chanted as one. “Mara – come…with us…”

Mr Borlor…she thought. Or Mr Malik – they’ll know. They’ll…

The hand of someone familiar snapped her out of her thoughts.

She turned to see Elise smiling at her, green energy swirling around her eyes.
