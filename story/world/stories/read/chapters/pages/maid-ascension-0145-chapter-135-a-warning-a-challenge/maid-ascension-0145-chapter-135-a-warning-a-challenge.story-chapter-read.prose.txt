Lira didn’t manage to stick a paper on Kavard yet, mainly because she never saw him. The man did not leave the main hall of the palace, and her magic still did not work in that section for some reason. She had more or less confirmed that fact after noticing the link to Celene's sheet failing to connect on a few occasions. When she asked if Celene was out, she was told that she was talking with the royals in the main hall.

Luckily for Lira, there did not seem to be any alarm that activated whenever she cast a spell toward that part of the palace. The effect seemed closer to an absolute negation of magic that blocked anyone who was not authorized to cast spells, or at least that was what Lira believed, since nobody came looking in her direction on the few occasions she tried and failed to activate the link while Celene was there.

That left two options: either going near Kavard and planting the paper on him herself, or waiting for him to leave so she could use her paper manipulation spell to sneak it onto him the same way she had done with Deren, Dalwin, and Beren.

She considered putting the paper on Estophon instead, since she had seen him far more often than Kavard, but she decided against it. The man was the one most likely to notice the thread of disturbance, and he was not the one she wanted to spy on anyway.

In the end, the opportunity arrived on its own. It was the morning before the princes' departure, and Lira was sitting in her alcove, switching through her various paper links, activating a link, listening for a short while, then deactivating it. Normally, she would have done this in the garden, but after using the spell for two weeks, she had grown more comfortable casting it in the safety of her alcove, the alcove was full under mana obfuscation field. Besides, it seemed that she had more or less overestimated the magical perception of the guards and knights, and through spying, she had learned that only a few of them were magically trained. Disturbance was a natural thing in the palace, with nobles casting random cantrips and carrying magical items, so no one was actively using Mage Sight to investigate every little thread of disturbance.

Plus, with a linked paper near the door of the library, she felt more secure, since she could hear anyone approaching with advance warning. Case in point, she heard footsteps coming through the twin of that sheet. She cast the Remote Eye divination spell on it and saw three figures from above, one walking in the middle with a guard on either side of him. Lira recognized him from his posture immediately, but she shifted the perspective for a bit to focus on his face. Prince Kavard.

She let the view fade, broke the link, and sat up at once, her mind racing.

Why was the prince here? Nothing good, she assumed...

Still, this was an opportunity. She slipped a folded sheet into her sleeve, one with the Hano rune linking it to its twin as well as a funnel rune drawn in garlic ink. She also added a blot of regular ink in case she needed to cast more runic spells later. With that preparation done, she left her alcove, smoothed her apron, and went to greet him.

"Your Highness," she said with a curtsy. "Can I help you find a book?"

"No, I did not come for a book." Kavard stopped a few paces from her desk, the two guards walking behind him, close enough to hear everything. He frowned and turned back to them. "Excuse me, can I have some privacy?"

The two guards hesitated but returned to standing next to the door, watching Kavard even if they could no longer hear him.

"I have been wondering how it was done, you know," said Kavard, stepping closer to Lira, his voice low so that it would not carry. "Luxia's debut was supposed to ruin a very pleasant evening for my brother, and instead it went exactly as it should have, as though someone had read my plan beforehand. It took me a while to find out who that someone was."

"I am sorry, Your Highness, but I do not know what you mean."

"Of course you do." He smiled slowly, as if he had caught her in a lie. "You were there, weren't you, acting like a dutiful little servant?"

Lira remained silent.

"My brother is going on an expedition to the Holtarry Mountains. I thought long and hard about how to hurt him while he was gone, and I decided that hurting you would probably do it. Could you imagine him returning home only to find his mistress dead?"

Lira glanced at the royal guards, who were staring at them, and the prince followed her gaze and sighed.

"Yes, you are lucky they are here. Though this state of affairs will not last long, I assure you. Once I am free of my shackles, I will come and find you. And with Aric away, no one will protect you from me."

Lira couldn't help but frown. Did he plan to escape Estophon? Would his watch be loosened once they left the capital? He seemed confident that his confinement would end while Aric was away.

"I am sorry," she finally said, "but why are you telling me this?"

"So that you would spend the rest of your days dreading my arrival." Kavard gave her his most evil smirk.

"I don't get it..." At that moment, Lira didn’t feel fear, only disdain, and it seeped through her voice. "You know I could go to Aric, repeat every word you just told me, and ask him to take me on the expedition for my own protection."

Kavard blinked, shocked at the shift in attitude, or maybe at the response, as if the idea of a maid not laying down and taking it had never crossed his mind.

"Wow, you are that dumb," Lira said before she could stop herself.

"What did you call me?"

Oh well, now she had done it. She decided to double down.

"Do not worry, Your Highness." She gave him a polite smile. "You just proved yourself so unbelievably dumb that I promise I will not go to Aric for protection."

"Watch it, maid."

"Nah, I don't fear you. You can come at me all you want; you will fail the same way you failed with Maren."

Kavard lunged at her, trying to slap her, but Lira stepped back. Both guards left their positions by the door and rushed the prince, restraining him from both sides and dragging him away from Lira.

"I will kill you, you bitch!" he screamed, his mouth almost frothing. "I swear I will kill you!"

Lira widened her eyes and pretended to be shocked. She did not know what had taken hold of her. She was not supposed to call him dumb, but she could not help herself.

Who announced that he was going to kill someone before doing it? Had Estophon not warned him against this exact same thing?

Still, she could use this. After all, Kavard would definitely be coming for her now. There was no going back.

Luckily for her, the guards dragging him away gave her the perfect distraction to cast.

"Pika. Asatan. Frushum."

The paper slid out of her palm and drifted low along the floor, small and flat enough to pass for a scrap swept in from the corridor. Once it reached the prince, it lifted abruptly, rolled up into a thin strip, and slipped inside the coin pouch at his belt. She made it narrow enough to fit through without disturbing the string.

Later that evening, Estophon showed up to ask about Kavard, and Lira truthfully explained that the first prince seemed to hate her because he falsely believed she was Aric's mistress. The Captain of the Royal Guard sighed, apologized for the prince's behavior, and asked her to keep that day's interaction a secret.

Lira nodded and promised him she would keep it to herself, and that not even Aric would hear of it.

She didn’t tell him that Kavard was planning to escape him. Why would she? Being away from Estophon was the perfect opportunity for her to deal with the prince.
