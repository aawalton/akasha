Charlie

Merlin looked over his shoulder as the knights ran past the house they were hiding within, still giving chase. The storm was raging now, rain poured heavily, and thunder crackled in the sky. The knights stopped what they were doing and talked for a minute. A moment later, they ran back in the direction they’d come. Hopefully, they’d realized they needed to get back to their post.

Orb floated openly beside Merlin. Most of the civilians would be heading to the shelter now, if they weren’t there already, and they were far from prying eyes. “Since Charlie uses, like…earth magic in a way, do you think he’s grounded? Like, will we be safe from lightning or…should I float a little closer to the ground?” he asked.

“We need to get to the tower, Arkady and Vetica will be waiting!” Merlin called out over the rain.

Charlie nodded. This was it. It was time to save Mary.

Merlin ran through the rain, his boots slapping against the quickly muddying ground, and he wrapped his cloak around himself to cover Charlie as well. Even Orb took cover inside. The rain was so hard it was actually pushing him down in the air, so he needed the support. It didn’t take long for them to reach the tower. They passed hex knights guiding people to safety. A few of them called out for them to join or get home quickly, but Merlin ignored them and kept running. It seemed not all the knights knew they were here. That was what Charlie had guessed. The ones after them were acting on their own. Charlie had a suspicious feeling he already knew who they were working for. That hex knight they’d crossed paths with at the gate. Sir Calda. It was the only thing that made sense.

When they arrived, Vetica was waiting. She’d already gotten the door to the tower open, and waiting just inside were two unconscious hex knights. She beckoned them inside and out of the rain. “I got started without you. These guys tried to get into the tower. I overheard them saying something about getting to Gregory. I figured I’d just deal with them now.” The base of the tower was a wide circular room. A large spiral staircase started on their right, and it went upwards until it reached the next floor of the tower. The tower was even all the way up until the end where a few slightly larger bulges appeared that were visible only from the outside.

“Where’s Arkady?” Vetica asked.

Merlin shook his head. “We’d hoped he was with you.”

Charlie bit his lip. The plan was to go together, but…they needed to move as fast as possible. They couldn’t afford to wait. And if Arkady wasn’t here, he had a good reason. They had to trust that he knew what he was doing. He would catch up when he could. “We have to go,” Charlie said.

Merlin looked surprised. But Vetica nodded. Charlie sent a thought to Bleedy within his dungeon. “We’re going in. If I need you, I’ll summon you, okay?” he asked. Bleedy would stick out too much around the fort, so even though he’d come in with them at first, the others had quickly realized he would need to sit this espionage mission out. But now the fighting was starting, and Charlie might need the racooneever’s mobility if it came down to it.

Merlin and Vetica hurried up the staircase. Charlie bounced with every step Merlin took from the baby carrier. “We’re coming, Mary, just hold on.”

Sir Gregory

“Commander! It’s starting!” A knight burst into the room. Sir Gregory sat around the table with most of his captains. Thespin, his second in command, sat on his left. Obie, the third strongest knight under his supervision, sat to his right. The only captain missing was Sir Calda. He’d sent him on that hunting mission days ago, but regardless of how it’d gone, he should’ve been back by now. The fort’s protection was a priority.

“We’ll need to reinforce Calda’s section of the wall. We need every knight we can muster on the walls keeping the beasts out. The bad weather's only just started, but the beasts won’t be far behind.”

They were only lucky the more damaging magical weather wouldn’t affect the fort. If natural magic lightning struck the ground, or thunder rocked the night, it would be devastating. Destruction and shattered eardrums. They’d chosen the spot well.

“Well, shall we get into position then? I’m sure we all know our strategy by now?” Sir Thespin asked.

Gregory nodded. “Yes, we—”

An alarm blared, and everyone turned in unison to stare at it.

Obie perked up. “Oh! Intruders! That’s fun! I wonder if they like games?” he asked.

Thespin looked toward Gregory in shock. “Could it be a false alarm? Who would invade at a time like this?” he asked.

Gregory’s face hardened. “No. No, this isn’t a false alarm. I had a feeling they’d make their move soon; I didn’t expect it to be now.” This changed things. It would take some of his heavy hitters out of the fight, but they’d still defend the fort. They’d prepared for every possibility.

Thespin arched a brow. “You were aware intruders were coming?” he asked.

Gregory nodded. “I was. You brought them through the front gates yourself.”

A series of surprised noises filled the room.

Obie looked over suddenly in the midst of leaning back in his chair. He fell back at the revelation, and his landing was softened only by the beard curled around his body. “What?!”

Thespin stood and shook his head. “You cannot mean that…Oh no. Sir Calda was right?” he asked.

Gregory nodded. “This couldn’t be worse timing. Everyone, to your positions. I’m activating the tower defense mechanism. From now on, this is a two-front operation. Follow the chain of command, fight hard, and hold the line. Leave the rest to us.”

Charlie

They ran up the stairs until they reached the next floor. It was devoid of knights, though there was plenty of room for people. It looked as if some of them gathered to eat here. There were still fresh plates of food and dining tables and chairs littered the room. It was clear many of the people had left in a hurry as soon as the storm started. He wanted to know what was going on outside. The cracks of thunder weren’t the only explosions he’d heard so far, and he had a feeling the fighting had began. Luckily, there was a small balcony to one side of the room, just under part of the stairwell that led further up. Merlin hurried over at Charlie’s direction and they scanned the world outside. There were hundreds of beasts leaving the treeline and slowly approaching the fort. It was as if they were each waiting on a different monstrosity to charge the fortress first. He spotted small lizard beasts, horned beasts, flying beasts and beasts that looked like normal animals on steroids. The hex knights stood atop the top wall of the fort and their collective attacks were like the force of a mighty hammer. They rained down magic hexes, hitting the outskirts of the monster horde, and deterring them from moving forward. It seemed as if their efforts might hold the creatures back, and that made Charlie feel better. The creatures were still filing out of the forests, he saw even more of them further within. Shifting, hideous shapes he couldn’t quite make out in the dark forest covered by a cloudy stone-gray sky. There were enough beasts to outnumber his scaler horde easily, and that was concerning. He’d never seen this many creatures at once.

But the hex knights fought undeterred. If they were worried about the condition of the battlefield, they didn’t show it. Several hex knights worked together to conjure a singular, massive hex with their combined power. It lit up the sky like a fireball and then went sailing towards a large group of creatures. Opposite it, a large turtle approached. The creature dwarfed even King Scalea in size by two or three times. Charlie hadn’t even realized it was a beast until it moved and its head poked out of its shell. It had almost appeared like part of the landscape. As the fireball approached, a large purple hex appeared in front of turtle.

Vetica’s eyes shot open. “A hex beast! A strong one.”

The fireball met with the hex, and for a moment it appeared it might push through. But then, it suddenly disappeared, sucked into the hex. If the knights had been unconcerned before, he wondered how they’d felt now. The purple hex did not disappear, so its purpose had not been satisfied. A perfect purple replica of the fireball manifested, it oozed even more power than the original attack, and swelled to be twice as large. In an instant, it was hurled back to the fort. Several knights joined together to form a unified diamond defensive hex. The massive protection spell sprang to life, but it did not hold. The purple fireball broke through and for a moment, Charlie thought that entire section of the fort would be taken out.

But from above in the tower, more knights descended. Leaping from the top of the tower to join the fight. At their front, a knight with long purple hair shot forward and into the attack directly. It enveloped him, and then disappeared. The man fell back, landing on his feet atop the outer fortress wall. He stood, and now purple lines ran along his armor as if he too had absorbed the power. Charlie smiled pumping a fist.

“The turtle can reflect magic. They’ll focus their efforts elsewhere now. A hex like that likely lacks its own offensive power. As long as they avoid it, it won’t be a threat,” Vetica said. Her meaning was obvious. They needed to keep moving.

The turtle roared, and the beasts shot forward to engage the knights fully now.

They hurried up the stairs, and Charlie hoped the knights would win. It was an odd thing, rooting for people who wanted him dead. But they were protecting the people of this town right now, and that was a worthy cause.

They passed through several floors with nothing of importance on them. Furniture scattered in a hurry to respond to their assignments, spare weapons, sleeping quarters, storage—the more they went up, the more Charlie worried they might not actually find Mary at the top. But finally they reached a room with someone inside.

Charlie’s heart sank when he saw who it was.

The old-looking man smiled and gave a big wave alongside his grin. “Hey Charlie!” he said excitedly!

Obie. Obie stood before them. The room Obie waited in was, of course, largely made of stone. Its walls were lined with wooden chests filled with toys and other assorted treasures. There was a table with all kinds of sweet snacks, and a dresser where dozens of identical combs like the ones Obie carried around all the time sat in front of a mirror. A hammock hung unreasonably high from the ceiling, in a way that nobody could possibly reach it. To their immediate right, in the center of the wall, was a doorway. The staircase leading higher had to be behind it.

“Obie, what are you doing here?” Charlie asked.

Obie threw his hands up. “Well, I thought I’d get to play with all those monsters outside, but Gregory activated the tower's defense, so now I’m on guard duty.”

“I don’t like where this is going,” Merlin said, swallowing.

“Can we pass?” Charlie asked. “There’s someone up there I need to see,” he said.

Obie looked surprised at that. “Really? Well, you can, but you have to follow the rules. That door won’t open unless you accept the challenge,” he said.

Charlie looked down and saw a paragraph embedded in the ground. He couldn’t read it, but Vetica leaned down to scan it. “You can’t be serious.”

“It is,” Obie said. “The most serious thing ever. Like super serious. I’m talking crazy serious.”

Before she could read it out loud to them, a voice appeared, and it sounded eerily similar to an interface.

[The Trial of the Tower has been activated.]

Vetica turned her attention to the sealed door. She activated her void hex and shot toward it, smashing into it with all her power. The door didn’t budge. Vetica did. She shot back and slammed into the opposite wall.

Charlie blinked in surprise. “Are you okay?” he asked.

But Vetica was clearly fine, just surprised.

“Gregory designed it himself. It’s called the Trial of the Tower. It’s a popular training technique the knights use,” Obie explained.

[Three challengers must defeat three opponents to move higher through the tower. Once a challenger agrees to fight the champion before you, the sealed door will open, and the others will wait inside. If your challenger loses the battle, you will remain sealed within until the champions decide to free you. If the challenger wins, the next door will open, and they may proceed to the next floor.]

“Basically, if you want to go higher, one of you has to agree to fight me. If you win, your friends keep climbing, and if you lose, they’re trapped! It’s not fun to be trapped. It’s dark in there. I hate it.”

Merlin shook his head. “You can’t mean that…Gregory would do all this to keep us from Mary? Even while that storm rages outside?” Merlin asked.

Vetica stepped forward. “And what would stop you from just sealing us inside afterwards even if we win?”

Obie laughed. “Gregory believes in honor. The artifact powering the tower has full control of the trial. It’s not up to us! It controls itself. If you take on the trial, you’ll see. So…who's going to fight me?” he asked.

Vetica sighed. “I’ll handle this. You two wait in the room. When I’m done, I’ll follow you and take care of the next two as well.”

Obie shook his head. “Each person only gets to fight once.”

Merlin

Merlin sank. “Oh, no…” he said. The math was obvious. Charlie and Vetica could fight. But Merlin wasn’t a fighter at all. He looked ready to cry.

Vetica shot a look at Charlie, and he nodded. She walked up and grabbed him from the baby carrier on Merlin’s chest. A moment later, the two of them headed toward the metal door.

Merlin looked called after them in surprise. “Where are you going?” he asked.

“We accept the challenge!” Charlie said with a smile on his face. “Merlin, Obie, don’t hurt each other!” he said.

To Merlin alone he added another message. “You’ve got this. Don’t worry about it.”

Vetica and Charlie stepped into the sealed room, and the door slid downward, covering them in darkness.

“Wait…guys?” he called out just before the door slammed shut. What were they thinking?
