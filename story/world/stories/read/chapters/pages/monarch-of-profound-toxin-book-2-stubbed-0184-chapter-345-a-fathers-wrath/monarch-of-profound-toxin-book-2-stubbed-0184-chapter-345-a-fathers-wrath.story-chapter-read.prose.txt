“Goo, what are you looking at?” Bin asked her little brother after he had suddenly dropped down into a crouch to look at something on the ground. She leaned in to look down over his shoulder.

He offered a brief glance up at her before returning his full attention to the ground. “Worm.”

“Ah, what?” she moaned with disappointment. “That’s boring. Let’s go and jump down from that ledge over there instead!”

“Nuh… uh,” came the answer, muttered into the collar of his shirt as he poked the worm with a finger.

“Ugh!” Bin groaned but gave up on trying to convince her brother. She had lived with him long enough to know better than to try to push him to do something he wasn’t interested in. He was stubborn as a roadside rock when it came to stuff like that.

Well, it’s not like she couldn’t jump off the ledge on her own. She left him to stare at the worm.

She had just about gotten the jump to a point where it was ready to be demonstrated in front of the adults and harvested for compliments, but before she could get to that, her dad’s crystal clear voice rang through the plaza as he called them back.

“Bin! Goo! Food’s ready! Come back to the table and eat!”

Showing off could wait!

She leaped from the ledge one final time and raced over to pull Goo away from the worm on the ground, but the boy had already taken off toward the table, running much faster than her.

One day soon, she would Awaken as well, and overtake that little punk in strength.

She watched her brother weave between tables like a whirlwind and make to pass closely by a table of five people dressed in armor with weapons at their waists, when one of the ladies stood up right as he went by, too quickly for him to correct his course.

The lady gasped as he barreled into the back of her thigh, losing her balance and stumbling forward while Goo bounced off her and onto his ass on the pavement. Two of the men rose from their seats as well and came around the table.

Goo quickly got to his feet and was about to dash off again, but one of the men caught him by his shirt sleeve and forced his little hand up.

A large strip of fabric was clutched in his little fist and the woman looked down at herself. “My skirt!” she gasped. “That cost me a fortune! It was enchanted!”

As if it would solve the issue, Goo simply opened his fist and let the fabric fall to the ground. He looked slightly offended when the man didn’t let go of his wrist as he tried to wiggle free. Dad had told him not to transform in public unless it was absolutely necessary, and the boy was doing as told.

“I’m really sorry for my brother! He’s just a little bit wild, you see,” Bin breathed as she came to a skidding stop in front of them and bowed respectfully. “Our dad is right over there and he can pay for your clothes, miss.”

She glanced over at dad, but he had his back turned while talking to the lady at the restaurant. But that was okay! She could handle a little problem like this on her own no problem!

“I don’t give a shit about the money, you fucking whelp!” the man snarled and stabbed a finger into her forehead. “It’s about disrespect!”

“Oh, uuh… I’m-I’m sorry!” she said, but her voice trembled and stumbled even though she had tried to keep it calm.

“Hey, Gurm,” one of the other men, who was much calmer, said, grabbing the angry man by the arm “How about we for—”

In a second, Goo was between them, growling menacingly as he came flying in and buried his teeth in the man’s finger.

“Ah, you little fucker!” he howled and used his other hand to slam a fist into little Goo’s stomach, displaying the frightening strength of a veteran Awakened. The boy bounced off the pavement with nothing but a thud. He didn’t move.

Was he dead? Bin felt her stomach drop and her head go light at the sight. “Goo!” she screamed and dove to her brother’s side. “Dad!”

“Yeah, why don’t you just call your daddy? I’ll take care of him too,” Gurm said, shaking his bloody finger. “Fuck, that hur—”

Suddenly, as if he had appeared out of thin air, a man was on his knees in front of the boy, a surging, green substance flowing from his hands over and into the unconscious boy. His brown hair whipped forward in reaction to his sudden cessation of movement.

An arm rippling with dense muscle gently guided the little crying girl around to stand behind him. Gurm couldn’t see the man’s face from this angle but the air seemed to shimmer around him. A skill?

Was he somewhere around C-rank? He had appeared rather suddenly. A little too quickly to notice, but then again, Gurm had been preoccupied at the time, so it made sense that he had missed it. As a B-ranker, he could wipe the floor with this dumb bastard.

“Daddy…” the girl sobbed. “That man hit—”

Her father’s voice was warm and full of love as he spoke to his daughter, reassuring her. “Shh shh, my little one. It’s all going to be all right, I promise. Goo is going to be just fine. He’s just sleeping for a little bit right now. I’m here, I’m here.”

It was difficult to hear properly, but Gurm thought he heard something else in the father’s tone. Ice.

“It’s not going to be all right for you, fucker!” he said, planting a boot on the man’s back as the two other adults who had been with them arrived. They watched but didn’t get closer. Cowards. “I’ll let you live, but we can’t just let people get away with anything. It would ruin our reputations, y’see,” he said with a smirk.

“So that’s why you did that to my son, then? Reputation?” Yep. There was definitely ice in that voice. A shiver ran down Gurm’s spine. He must have gotten soft if his subconscious was reacting like that.

“You understand, don’t you?” he asked, exposing a missing front tooth in a wide grin.

The father’s voice was very quiet when he spoke. Almost too quiet to hear. “So people like you are real… You’re like the perfect caricature of the shittiest stereotype in a fantasy world.”

“That’s just how the world works. The strong step on the weak.”

The father gestured for the two other adults to come and take the kids, and they quickly complied. “Eik…” the smaller of the two muttered.

Standing up slowly, the father patted down his pant legs for dust. “Indeed,” he whispered, a slight tremble running through his body. “Yes, you’re right, aren’t you? The strong can step on the weak…”

Gurm watched his back as he turned his head slightly to look at him. The one eye he could see was solid blue, crackling violently with energy. What did the other guy say again? Eik…?

A lump formed in Gurm’s throat as the first trickles of a realization formed in his head. Judging by the throat-constricting gasp behind him, one of the others had arrived at the dreadful conclusion faster than him.

But he got there.

“H-Hey now… We can t-talk about this, can’t we? I— You know, I couldn’t ha-have known that you were…” Gurm’s voice was stuttering and shivering. Shit, shit! How the hell did this even make sense? Why would a god damned rich as all hell X-ranker be eating here at some random ass restaurant?

Eik Magnasen, the Reaper of the Moon—a moniker that had sprung up after his prominent role in the vanquishment of the greater forces of the cult of Moon Shall Swallow, whose mission it had been to bring back the Lord of the Moon—looked ready to commit murder in broad daylight.

And he could get away with it too. Gurm knew that.

“If you wanted to talk, you should have talked to my kids,” came the chilling reply. “You stepped on the weak, so I will grind you to dust.”

With a movement quicker than teleportation, Eik crushed the B-rankers feet and ankles. Well, perhaps vaporized or obliterated might have been better descriptors for what happened. Even though he held back so as to not destroy the entire area, a table-sized crater was still opened in the ground.

Eik caught Gurm by the neck before he could fall in. The man screamed in agony, but Eik simply regarded him with eyes devoid of sympathy. “You should not…” the Worldbreaker muttered as liquid Profound Toxin slithered up what remained of Gurm’s shins and thighs.

“have touched…” The liquid had reached his groin when it suddenly constricted around his knees, crushing them completely.

“my children.” Gurm’s entire body with the exception of his eyes was enveloped completely.

Heavy tears flowed as he felt his hands and elbows be pulverized. His screams were muffled and barely audible through the tight Profound Toxin but it was pretty obvious what he was trying to convey.

“A little too late for begging, don’t you think?” Eik wondered, expressionless.

“Uuh, dad?” a tiny voice said. Eik looked down to see Goo pulling at his pant leg. The boy didn’t look affected by the violent scene playing out in front of him in the slightest, more than used to gore from his many trips to different battlefields with his father.

The same thing couldn’t be said for the rest of the public. Other restaurant goers had long since retreated—mostly completely, but a good number of guests had stayed behind, hiding behind buildings as they watched things unfold.

At this point, given his popularity and recognizable face, the majority of the spectators didn’t look that terrified, but there was still good reason to stay out of the way of an X-ranker dishing out pain.

“What’s up buddy?” Eik asked and knelt down for his son while a Living Manifestation took the encased man from him.

“When are we going to eat? I’m hungry.”

Failing to hide his surprise, Eik’s eyes widened. “It’s just… I’m in the middle of…” he trailed off. The boy didn’t even look at the frightened Gurm. “How does that man make you feel?” A gesture at the bandit.

Goo just shrugged.

“You’re not afraid of him?” Eik asked.

“No.”

“Why not?”

Goo looked up at Eik with his big eyes. “You’re always here.”

Fuckin’ hell. So X-rankers could cry. Eik almost heaved a big sob right then and there. He didn’t deserve this kid. His eyes sought Bin. “And you, honey?” he asked her. “How are you feeling?”

Tears still streamed down her cheeks, and it sent white-hot flames through his veins. “I-I’m… I’m okay,” she managed to push out before rushing into his arms. He picked her up and walked back to their table and sat down with her on his lap. His son hopped onto the other leg.
