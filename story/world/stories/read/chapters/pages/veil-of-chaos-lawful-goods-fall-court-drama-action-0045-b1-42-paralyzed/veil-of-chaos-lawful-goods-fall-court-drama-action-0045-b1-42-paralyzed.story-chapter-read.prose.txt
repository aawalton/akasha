Aria’s mind raced as Queen Colemour gestured to the seats in the corner, a thousand lies colliding in her head, and she landed on the only truth that might save her.

“I’m…here because of your grandson. He’s entered into an indentured servitude contract with me,” she carefully articulated, forcing herself to meet those sharp, light-brown eyes that were so unlike her mother’s violet. “Everything I’m doing is connected to him.”

Not a lie. Completely true. Just missing literally all the context.

The queen settled into her seat, leaning back in her chair as Aria stiffly sat opposite her. The woman’s fingers steepled as she studied her with the intensity of someone who’d spent decades reading people like books.

Aria couldn’t help but fidget a little. Out of the corner of her eye, she still saw the magic mirror beside them, showing that frozen image of Aria in the carriage, and she had to resist the urge to blurt out more.

“My grandson,” the queen repeated slowly, tasting the words. “A boy whom my daughter hid the truth about, carrying dark elf blood—the child whose very existence has thrown our kingdom into chaos. Now you make this a diplomatic issue by tying him to your people?”

Son of a—what is she even asking? Is she—how do I respond to that?

“Damon isn’t an issue. He’s only trying to live. Why is that a crime?” Aria demanded, surprising herself with the defensive heat in her voice. “Your Majesty, I am not speaking for sun elves. I only speak for myself.”

“Oh? You speak his name with familiarity and without disdain for dark elves.” The queen’s eyebrow arched. “Yet here you are, a sun elf of apparent noble bearing, as you claimed as much to our Archmagus. And now you are bound to him by divine contract, or so I hear. That suggests either remarkable misfortune on his part or…remarkable planning. Which is it?”

Aria’s saliva glands shriveled like a prune, vision defocusing at the accusation.

Oh, she’s good. Everyone hates dark elves, yet I was way too fast to defend him, and to the queen… She’s crazy good. She’s essentially saying, I’m a naive child, totally exposed, or a dangerous schemer.

Colemour wouldn’t give her brain a break to process, though.

“May I see this contract that has our court in such upheaval, Lady Aria?”

Aria’s stomach dropped, but what choice did she have with a queen’s gaze on her?

With trembling fingers, she pulled up her status menu and materialized the contract for her to see, swallowing and making it available for the older woman to inspect. Her practiced eyes slid over it with ease, her irises moving across the ‘divine scripture’ with the methodical focus of someone who’d reviewed thousands of legal documents.

The silence stretched like taffy, each second feeling like an hour as Aria watched those dark eyes scan line after line. The queen made small sounds occasionally—a thoughtful hum here, a soft “How strange” there—that told Aria absolutely nothing while simultaneously making her want to scream.

She’s going to see through it, Damon! What do I do when she finds out it’s a fake? It’s totally illegal to use the Holy Emperor’s name. She’s going to—why is Damon’s EXP going up so fast? Huh?!

Her mind immediately swapped gears as she saw her big brother’s status with the menus open—all but the contract private.

How the crap… He’s almost Level 3! And when did we get a new bond? That shark girl he mentioned…

Aria’s vision narrowed as her focus darted between the various menus, scanning Copper, Damon, and Cassandra’s current states. She couldn’t help but be distracted by what she was reading.

They’re sharing physiology Feats? Damon can actually do that? And what’s with his stats? They’re hyper buffed… He’s syphoning stats with his lineage abilities. He’s gone full vampire mode. Damon, what are you doing?

Everywhere she looked, things had changed dramatically; each new revelation was a slap to her attention.

And Copper is now part demon? Cassandra is flipping Level 5! And immortality—oh, no, she’s got the [Gluttony] Feat. That’s totally going to be OP! Those Mako-Shark Feats are insane, too. And it looks like she’s got a chakram weapon since the mana drain corresponds to those Feats… What didn’t you tell me?

“Is something wrong?”

Aira’s vision snapped to the calm queen across from her, hands resting in her lap.

“I—no, I just—” Aria scrambled for words. “The contract bond—Damon is just doing some confusing things.”

The queen watched her for another long moment before returning to the contract. More silence. More agonizing waiting, but it didn’t take long for Aria to realize her mistake.

I said the bond… Contracts don’t do that. It isn’t in the contract that I can know what Damon’s doing… I just admitted I’m Unity Bound to Damon. Son of a monkey squirrel! Why did I do that?! And…why didn’t she call me out?

In that breaking silence, Aria waited, focus drifting from her brother’s status to the battle in front of her, the queen’s emotionless gaze sliding down every line meticulously. And, finally, she looked up from it with a sigh that could have meant anything.

“There are many oddities here, Lady Aria,” she said slowly. “The wording is…unusual for the Holy Emperor. Non-standard to His typical framing. And there is the issue of me knowing about my grandson possessing [System Trickery]…”

I’m screwed! Aria internally screamed. How does she know so much? She wasn’t at the trial. How long has she been watching with that mirror? How did she not figure Mom out before?

The queen tapped one section in the air with a perfectly manicured nail. “However,” Colemour continued, “if there is foul play hidden in this divine scripture, it’s beyond my ability to easily detect. The wording is off, yet the laws invoked are all legal, if he agreed…”

Her mouth pulled into a tight line as she leaned forward. “Which either means you’re extraordinarily clever, young lady, or extraordinarily lucky that it is I investigating this.”

She smiled, and something in that expression made Aria’s blood run cold. “Without a doubt, I am convinced you are extraordinarily connected to my family in ways that transcend normal contracts… You’ve admitted to as much already.”

“I don’t—”

“No need for more bluster, Lady Aria.” The queen rose gracefully, smoothing her elaborate gown with a twinkle in her brown eyes. “After all, my dear, whatever the truth beneath your mystery, as a sun elf, you’ve provided me the perfect excuse to do something I typically would not be afforded, given the current political climate.”

“Your Majesty?”

“We will visit my daughter, as you requested. Only, you will allow me to accompany you for…supervision.” The queen moved toward the door. “Come. We have much to discuss, or at least, my daughter does. And I suspect she’ll be very interested to meet her son’s…contractor.”

“Wait, why couldn’t you meet with your own daughter?” Aria asked.

She had to scramble to keep up with the queen’s suddenly brisk pace as they swept out. Colemour didn’t even have to make a gesture as the company of Paladins outside immediately fell into line, keeping a respectable formation.

The woman paused, looking back with an expression that mixed amusement with something deeper. “You truly must be quite new to our kingdom, My Lady, if you are asking that question… But I’d expect you to be able to puzzle out why that is. Though I suppose I can offer you an explanation.”

The queen resumed walking, the Paladins listening intently to understand what they were doing. It said a lot that they weren’t questioning the ruler, but the looks the servants and nobles made when they saw their brisk-paced group said it all—people were shocked to see her out.

Aria had to practically jog to keep pace, despite her longer elf legs, Colemour’s voice soft and slightly bitter.

“My husband, the king, is as loyal as they come to the Holy Emperor. Every decision filtered through divine law, every judgment measured against rigid, good doctrine. The law is not just his guide—it’s his gospel.”

They turned down a corridor Aria hadn’t seen before as the woman took them through the maze of the giant palace. This hallway was less grand but somehow more oppressive—fewer decorations, more guards.

“I, however, am not a follower of the Holy Emperor.”

Aria nearly tripped. “You’re not? B-But you’re the queen! I thought—no, your brown eyes mean you’re not a part of the royal line. Right?” she absently reasoned as it clicked in her head.

Colemour glanced back with a small, praiseworthy smile.

“Good. You are intelligent, merely ignorant, and this can be corrected. I am a queen, which means I came from another country. Not something I’d expect a sun elf to understand culturally, but it is politics, my dear—a marriage of treaties.”

She turned her head forward, maintaining their speed. “I follow Terranak, the World Serpent. As I understand it, sun elves all worship the Holy Emperor, like my husband, so this must be shocking to you that I stand firmly in the Neutral-Good camp rather than Lawful-Good. It’s caused no small amount of tension over the years in parenting.”

They passed another pair of Paladins who bowed deeply to the queen but watched Aria with expressions she couldn’t quite read. Suspicion? Awe? Fear? Maybe all three.

“My preference would be to take a sledgehammer to half these laws,” Colemour continued conversationally, as if discussing the weather rather than what Aria feared was treason. “So much of this web we’re stuck in is asinine procedure, running counter to everything I believe to be good. But my husband is bound by law, and I’m bound by my duty, which I swore when entering into this kingdom’s family tree… In addition to my love for him. So we…navigate.”

Aria couldn’t believe it, but the more the queen talked, the more she thought, Yeah! This woman’s awesome! She’s a badass! Screw the rules! But why is she telling me all of this? Is my sun elf privilege…an access card for her to be able to vent?

They descended a narrow staircase, and Aria noticed the decorations becoming even sparser. This wasn’t a prison, exactly, but it wasn’t far from one.

“The law demanded Princess Catelyn be confined until sentencing,” the queen went on, apparently reading Aria’s expression. “My husband ensured the confinement was at least comfortable. Small victories, but there are many who would use this scandal against our family line…and my other children, attempting to turn them against us.”

Just as they reached a heavy wooden door flanked by several more Paladins, Aria’s status menu flashed urgently.

[Damon requests access to: Spell Echelon and Lich’s Touch]

The world tilted, Aria’s brain fixating on two of the most powerful and broken spells in her arsenal.

No. No no no no NO. That combination—you’re trying to bypass [Lich’s Touch]’s 20,000 mana cost. I didn’t even think about that yet… Why do you need that—no, if he asked for something this powerful…

The whole world blurred out as she stared at the request, gut twisting into knots.

Those are emergency spells! That combo is an extinction-level attack—total victory one-shot, last-resort nuclear option… What are you fighting, you idiot?! No, I can’t hesitate if he’s asking for those!

“Lady Aria?” Colemour’s voice seemed to come from very far away. “You’ve gone quite pale for a sun elf. What is wrong?”

The Paladins were immediately on their guard, over a dozen men’s hands going to their weapons as they spread out, scanning every shadow with murderous intent, as if an assassin prowled.

“I—” Aria’s fingernails were pressed into her palm as she stared at the authorization, and mentally, she accepted it with faith. “I’m worried about Damon… Really worried now,” she admitted, the words tumbling out.

Her adopted grandmother’s expression sharpened. “Hmm. Save it for when we are alone. Come… Sir Jacob?”

But before Aria could answer, one of the Paladins cleared his throat nervously. “Your Majesty, the princess is confined by royal decree. You ordered us not to allow you—”

“And clearly, the situation has changed if I am with Lady Aria, has it not, Paladin? No law will be broken. Lady Aria requires an audience with my—with the prisoner. Open the door.”

They exchanged glances but obeyed, the heavy locks clicking open with sounds that echoed in the narrow hallway.

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

The room beyond was…nice. Nicer than the place they had their secret meeting earlier that morning, when her mother gifted her the dress she now wore. It wasn’t a cell but clearly a containment area. Surprisingly big. Books, letters, a desk, a comfortable bed, and even a window.

Princess Catelyn—her adopted mother—looked up from a letter she was writing, and her expression cycled through about twelve different emotions in two seconds.

“M-Mother? And…” Her voice cracked slightly in a way Aria had never heard. In the next instant, her focus snapped to Aria. She almost sounded a little scared. “My Queen, what—why are you—”

“I’ve brought your son’s contractor,” Colemour stated, stepping inside with Aria following, feeling very much like she was walking into a trap. “Lady Aria was quite insistent about speaking with you. Sir Jacob, you can leave us.”

“My Queen…”

“Yes, I understand your hesitation,” she sighed, giving him an almost impatient smile. “You have the window watched, and it isn’t as if I am the first visitor the former princess has had this evening. Do your duty…and I will do mine.”

Every Paladin’s gaze drew far away, unable to meet the mother’s gaze, as they gave up the war, and Sir Jacob whispered, “Holy Emperor, when the broken fall alive, let the light take me too… When the waters turn to fire, Heaven, please let me through… Let the light take me in.”

With a bow, every Paladin mirrored him with a sign made against their chests before the doors were closed, with sorrowful faces and one last parting mantra Aria didn’t understand. “God, take the earth, forever blind, and now the sun will fade, and all we are is all we made.”

The door closed behind them with a final-sounding thud, the Paladins staring after them in paralyzed silence.

Mother and daughter stared at each other for a long moment, some complex communication happening in looks and tiny gestures that Aria couldn’t decode. Then Catelyn’s gaze shifted to Aria, and something in her expression broke.

She laughed. Not happily, but with a kind of exhausted hysteria that Aria recognized from her own stress breakdowns.

“Mother… The only reason you would challenge your own decision to lock yourself away…would be if you know. You know…” Catelyn said, pain and exposure, Aira had never witnessed showing in her powerful mother’s violet eyes.

In that silence, Colemour mirrored the Paladin’s previous prayer, only without invoking the Holy Emperor’s name and adding a few last lines.

“…Hope start the heart, the last beat and lie…the cold walk the earth as love fades to white. I hear them call the tide. Take me in. I see the last divide. Hopelessly, I leave this soul behind. And I am paralyzed… Sorrow has left me here. All we are is all we made…

“My daughter, you repeat these words in your eyes to me. Yet, the dagger you have placed in my heart for not trusting me to carry your heavy tide places a burden unbearable on my soul.”

Catelyn turned her head downward, a tear sliding down her cheek. “I have left you desolate, Mother… It was a choice I must live with. Yes, in my heart, I have adopted another child that is as if my own bone…which is why she appears in your mirror now that you are aware of her. Aria is my child… Damon’s little sister from his previous life, whom he brought.”

“Mom…” Aria mumbled in disbelief at her mother’s quiet admission. “You just…said it?”

“I already deduced as much, Aria,” her grandmother whispered, taking several steps forward to cup her daughter’s cheek and raising her gaze to meet hers. “I cannot describe the pain I feel from every person I love…

“Though I know the reasons, the blood still spills from these thorns you have chosen to wear. Far away…so far away you have pulled from me, my daughter. My sun is fading, and all I cry is for you to stay with me as you allow your heart to take you far away…so far away from me. I become the lost within the sorrow of your fall. Heaven, please let me through.”

Aria couldn’t breathe as her mother’s tears fell freely. “That is one prayer I cannot grant, My Queen… This sorrow has been chosen since I was thirteen years old. For my children, I would even see your heart bleed, Mother, and bear the pain, as unbearable this bitter cup is.

“For I have faith that these wounds will heal…and we will rise higher from these waters that have turned to fire. I know you cry, let the light take me, too… But I must bear this alone, and know the claws I rake against your heart…because I am a mother, too.”

Catelyn drew closer to wrap her arms around her mother’s as the queen’s legs gave out, and they both fell to the carpet, tears falling freely.

“So, please, Mother…rest within this wreckage…this flood of sorrow I have so selfishly brought upon our family… Sacrifice for me as my mother until my son brings the light to break this storm. Have faith…in my faith in my children. This tide is not the end, but the beginning.”

Aria’s legs grew shaky, her eyes widening before finally giving out, tears welling as pressure compressed her aching heart, and one message broke all thoughts.

[Damon has died]

[Misty Escape has activated]

[Damon has suffered extreme soul damage]

[Soul partially recovered]

Damon died… Why did Damon die? My brother died. What do I do? He can recover—he’s immortal! No, but…soul damage. [Lich’s Touch]... I gave it to him. He’s not a Lich. He can’t use it without… Did I kill my brother?

What do I do… I don’t know. I don’t know…

[Unchained Heart: Deactivated]

[Lich’s Heart: Activated]

The panic. The heat. The tears. Everything faded, leaving only cold, rational thought.

Damon got himself in this mess; he can get himself out of it…

Watching her mother cry in her grandmother’s arms, Aria knew the thought was wrong. Utterly wrong. Yet, she couldn’t find herself to care. In fact, the very reason she was here felt rather absurd. Then, more rational, rather uncomfortable thoughts began to filter through, mouth turning into a deep frown.

Wait… If Damon truly dies and doesn’t just regenerate in his coffin due to soul damage…then I will revert back to a piece of jewelry. Hmm… I need to chain him up. But no, that won’t work. Unity Bonds account for mind control… I need to find a way to get the Feat without him…

[Unchained Heart: Activated]

[Lich’s Heart: Neutralized]

Abruptly, feeling welled up in her chest, cold sinking in, and the tears welled again.

I’m…the worst little sister ever.

“M-Mom…”

Crawling forward, she entered the hug of her recovering mother and grandmother as they accepted her in. They soothed her with her world collapsing around her, unable to even articulate the horrific situation that had just happened.

“Useless… I’m so useless!” she sobbed. “What am I even doing here?”
