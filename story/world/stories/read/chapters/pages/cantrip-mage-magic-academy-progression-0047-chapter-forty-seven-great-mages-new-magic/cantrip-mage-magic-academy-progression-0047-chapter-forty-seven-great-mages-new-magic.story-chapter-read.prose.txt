47 - Great Mages, New Magic

Eola Lemiene wasn’t very good at magic.

She was working on it, though. She’d put in the effort in every class she’d taken—especially her apprenticeship. Alright, perhaps she could have worked harder in Mana Studies, but she was never going to succeed in any class run by Madame Reyana. The red gnome had it out for her, and even worse, Catrine was her favorite. No, Mana Studies hadn’t been worth the effort; she’d learned what she needed, and that would have to be enough.

But her other classes…yes, she’d put in the effort there—and in her own studies, too.

As Patrice handed her not one, but two letters, though, her heart pounded in her chest. “You’ve got mail, Eola. Second time this year? I’m going to run these letters to Roth and Colin. Want to come with?” Patrice flashed a stack of envelopes at Eola and waited, tapping a foot on the wooden floor.

“Yes. But I need to know what these say. Can you…”

“Sure. I’ll be here. Hurry up, though. I’m not opening mine until I get back. No point in ruining a good morning, right?”

“Sure,” Eola said. Then she broke the seal on the first letter and prepared to have her morning ruined.

Miss Eola Lemiene,

Your marks for your first-term second-semester courses are as follows:

Ideograms: 5Mana Studies: 2Introduction to Monsters: 3Magical Dueling: 3

Apprenticeship: 5

Combat Track Exam:

Varin’s evaluates its students on a five-point scale. As end-of-term scores, these marks will be used as reflections of your true weaknesses and strengths, and will be used by your masters to determine course loads for your next year at Varin’s Academy.

She’d moved up in Mana Studies and Introduction to Monsters. The first was a minor miracle, and she whispered a quick thanks to Y’aer for their help. A 2 was enough. It wasn’t a solid pass, but it was enough.

More importantly, her Apprenticeship and Ideograms scores were well above expectations, and those were the subjects she was most interested in. Not swordplay. The Taron-Li was fun, and with practice, she’d be good at it, but the combat track was out of…

She looked again. Her score was blank. Not a fail.

Blank.

“What does that mean?” she asked.

“Don’t show me. I’m not interested in your scores or mine. Just hurry up and read the other one so we can go!”

Eola rolled her eyes. The blank score taunted her, and she looked at it one more time. Then she slid her finger through the Varin’s Academy seal on the second letter.

It was a single half-sheet of parchment. Nothing more.

Miss Eola Lemiene,

Your presence is requested in my office as soon as possible regarding your continued enrollment at Varin’s Academy and the nature of your education.

Headmaster Tomlish

That was it.

Eola’s heart dropped. She took a deep breath and reread the note, hoping for something that might give a hint as to what the headmaster might want. The phrasing Headmaster Tomlish had used sent a clear enough signal. It just wasn’t one she wanted to see.

“Okay, I’ve got to know what that one said,” Patrice said after a moment. “Are you okay, Eola?”

“No. I need to go meet with Headmaster Tomlish. It’s not likely to be good.”

“I’m sorry. I’ll let the boys know you wanted to come but couldn’t. They’ll understand.” Patrice hesitated for a moment. Then she gave Eola an awkward, one-armed hug. “Good luck in there.”

“Thanks, Patrice,” Eola said, and she started the long journey to Headmaster Tomlish’s office.

Headmaster Tomlish’s office was at the end of a corridor in the same building as Instructor Tarik’s, but it was two floors up from Eola’s master’s. Unlike the Ideogram professor’s dark, typically-messy room, Tomlish’s desk was lit by two massive glass windows that let natural light fall across the beech-wood surface and deep brown stained floor. A gigantic globe hung from the ceiling, the entirety of Alemia painted on one half and the other two continents on the other, and a long, orange snake’s head stared at her from the rafters as she sat in a shockingly comfortable, pink chair across from Varin’s Academy’s headmaster.

“Instructor Tarik informed me about your troubles during the Library Exam,” the headmaster said as he stood behind his seat. His bald head glinted in the morning light. “He also told me, in detail, about your efforts to recreate Ordered Magic without the elements that make it ordered. The mark you claim to have discovered is of great interest to me in particular. Would you please demonstrate it?”

Eola took a deep breath. This wasn’t how she’d envisioned this meeting going. The blank space on the letter in her robe pocket haunted her, though. Until it was filled in, she hadn’t officially failed the Library Exam, and she had to do whatever she could to pass it. “Can you cast Frost Bolt, sir? It’s easiest to demonstrate on spells I’ve already seen a few times, is all.”

“Yes.” The headmaster reached for his staff, and Eola drew her wand. He sketched the symbol quickly and efficiently, but even so, Eola reacted fast enough to Cut the Silenced mark away. The rune faltered, and both Eola and the headmaster let it go.

After a moment, he nodded and sat, resting his staff against his desk. “Very interesting, Miss Lemiene.” He didn’t say anything else, and Eola didn’t press until he changed the subject.

“After some intense debate between myself and a few other professors, I have decided to pass the three of you on to the combat track for next year. Mr. Tremory, Lady Clerk, and you all possess unique talents suited to the three sub-tracks—him as a siege mage, you as a spellsword, and Lady Clerk as a battle mage, provided we can figure out her attunement before the end of next term and get her caught up on casting.”

“Thank you, sir,” Eola said. Her stomach, which had been churning since she’d opened the letter, settled, and her heart rose. Then she realized what he’d said. “What about Roth?”

“Mr. Gerr’s talents are not suited to the combat track. Don’t worry, though. We have no interest in separating him from you. His education would be better served in a more specialized set of classes suited to his strengths. It’s very similar to illusion work, and that is my specialty, as well.”

“Oh. That’s fine, then.” It was. Roth would probably be angry, but the headmaster was right. His skills were more suited to support magic than to fighting. His attention-warding songs had saved their lives at least twice—against the Srilla and again during the study room siege.

“Quite.” Headmaster Tomlish went quiet. “Now, as to your longer-term future…”

“Yes? What about it, sir?”

“Instructor Tarik has informed me about your attunement, and I’ve seen you cast spells. The sheer amount of Mana required is far beyond what other students need to use, and your soul’s growth will only slow with time. Some spells will remain out of reach for you, mot likely forever.” Eola nodded slowly, and Headmaster Tomlish continued. “You are aware that you’ll likely never reach the rank of Truemage, correct?”

“I hadn’t thought about that.” Eola tried to keep her face neutral, but felt herself failing. In truth, the sentence had hit her like a physical blow. Both of her parents had been Truemages, and they’d gotten trapped in Lord Card’s dungeon even so. They hadn’t been strong enough to escape from his trap. If she couldn’t at least match their power, how could she possibly free them? It wasn’t just a matter of loving magic and being told, once again, that she couldn’t do it. She needed that strength. To hear she couldn’t have it was painful.

“I see. You may grow enough to pass the Journeymage trials, but beyond that…well, most souls never grow as large as yours has, and very few make it to Truemage. I’d estimate, based on what Instructor Tarik has told me, that you’d need as much Mana as I have to attempt the Truemage trials, and that is unrealistic to hope for.

“So, put Truemage out of your mind—at least in the traditional sense. I have a feeling, though, that the work you’re doing will leave a lasting mark on Ordered Magic. Keep up with your coursework, but keep experimenting, too. And don’t give up on Mana Studies.”

Eola nodded slowly. It wasn’t the reassurance she desperately needed, but it was something—even if she never intended to set foot in a class taught by the red gnomish woman again.

“Oh, and speaking of Madame Reyanna…a word of warning for next term. She is petty, spiteful, and small-minded, but she’s discreet. She didn’t tell a soul about you after that ill-advised duel of yours. Some of the other staff members are much less discerning about who they share information with. Now that your unique talents are more well-known within Varin’s Academy, expect them to become known outside of it as well.”

“I understand,” Eola said, trying to wrap her head around it all. “Instructor Tarik warned me about that, too, sir.”

Headmaster Tomlish stood and walked to the door, pulled it open, and bowed his head slightly. “Good. You’re dismissed, Miss Lemiene. Enjoy the rest of your day.”

As she left, she looked back. The headmaster was already back at his desk, writing on a piece of parchment. Then the door shut, and she half-jogged down the hall. If she hurried, she could still catch Patrice, Colin, and Roth at the hospital.

No fewer than a dozen different rituals were running full-bore over Colin and Roth’s beds; Eola recognized a few as Instructor Clearance’s work, and there was something that looked like a highly advanced version of the ‘purification’ process turned into a rune. She had no idea what the others did. The air reeked of magic—both the purified kind and a sickly, dark scent that Eola had never smelled before, but that nevertheless smelled like Mana.

Roth had his pair of letters propped up on his chest. He’d read them both, clearly, and he had a self-satisfied look on his face. “I passed everything except the combat exam.”

Eola picked up his letter. There, in clear marks, was the word ‘Failed.’ She stared at it for a moment. “You do know that—“

“That Colin passed his somehow? Yes. The other letter helped, though.”

As she skimmed the other letter, Eola nodded. “Headmaster Tomlish made me demonstrate the new mark before he’d pass me, but all three of us are going on to the combat track next term.”

“Hey! I told you not to ruin my morning!” Patrice said.

Eola found herself by Colin’s bed, her hand on his chest. After a moment, she realized he was shirtless, with only a thin blanket between their skin, and tore her hand away, blushing. “Sorry.”

Colin went red as Roth coughed to clear his throat and glared. “Don’t be sorry. He’s been pining for you since I met you two. That touch made his whole cursed day.”

Y’aer dammit. Eola’s blush only deepened, and when she glanced at Colin, he wouldn’t meet her eye. How long had she been missing his hints? What would the term have been like if she’d realized? Did she even have time to do the courting and dating thing, between her magical studies and her need to rescue her parents?

“Don’t tell me you didn’t realize,” Patrice said. “He was so obviously interested in courting—“

Before she could respond, Colin interrupted. “So, what are you all doing for term break?”

“I was planning on staying here,” Eola said quickly. “I’ve got more studying to do, and the library should be quiet. I’ll avoid the third floor for now, though. There’s plenty to learn elsewhere. What about you?”

“My parents are visiting Highstone, so I’ll have to spend the break with Latrine Catrine. Four days down, thirteen in her perfume-infested castle, and four days back.” Patrice shuddered. “It’s going to be awful.”

Colin perked up for a moment. “You’ll be in Highstone, too? The Mercantile Guild’s got a caravan coming up, and I’ll be meeting Dad there. We’ll have to meet for dinner—a real one, not whatever passes for it at Catrine’s place.”

“We’ll see. House Clerk’s got designs on power, and being seen with a non-noble boy in Highstone could send the wrong message, but if it’s as part of a meeting between Craghold Mercantile and House Clerk, it might be fine,” Patrice muttered. Then she shook her head. “Who am I kidding? Y’aer take all that—I’ll sneak out of the castle and swim the moat if I have to. Just tell me the night, and I’ll be wherever dinner is.”

“I’m staying here, too.” Roth coughed loudly. It was a wet, nasty sound, and Eola flinched; it sounded a lot like Eddarn’s. “No way I’ll be healthy to travel north, and the road from Foglight to my village is pretty awful. It’s not worth the time—not with the kind of welcome I’d get.”

“I understand that. Greenarbor’s not that far, and the rivers should be clear enough of ice to travel, but…there’s nothing for me there.” Not yet, there wasn’t, but Eola was working on changing that. She might never reach Truemage, but if there was one thing she’d learned this year, above all the rest, it was that Ordered Magic had serious holes in it. She was going to keep learning about those holes, and the Cutting mark was probably only the most basic way to exploit them.

Before she could say anything else, though, Instructor Clearance emerged from the potted yew in the corner. “Miss Lemiene, Lady Clerk, these two need rest. I’ve given you almost an hour. That’s all you get.”

“Fine, fine,” Patrice said. “I’ll see you two later.”

Eola started leaving, then gathered all her courage and planted a quick, pecked kiss on Colin’s forehead. His blush redoubled, as did hers, and Patrice howled with laughter. “That’s not how you do it, Eola!”

“Out!” the hamadryad snapped, and Eola and Patrice fled the room.

Patrice didn’t stop laughing the whole time they walked back to their suite. It was only when they stepped inside that she finally pulled herself together and looked Eola in the eye. “You really had no idea, huh?”

“About Colin?” Eola asked. Y’aer save her, she did not want to keep dealing with this. She collapsed onto the couch and sighed loudly. “I’ve been way too busy to pay any attention to that kind of thing.”

“Ah.”

Silence.

Eventually, Eola broke it. “It’s not going to be a problem for us, is it? Me and him?”

“Why would it be?” Patrice asked. “If you’re happy, and Colin’s happy, then I’m happy. Besides, I’ve got a betrothal waiting for me. No matter how little I care about House Clerk’s reputation, it’d be bad form to actually date a commoner in the middle of a betrothal. That’s the kind of thing that starts petty little wars. I’m happily—and hopefully permanently—a bachelorette.”

Eola’s shoulders sagged. She relaxed into the couch as Patrice sat next to her, bouncing Eola’s cushion a little. Her hand touched Eola’s, and she smiled. “I’m happy for you, Eola. You need someone, and Colin’s a good boy.”

“Yeah. I hope to Y’aer I know what I’m getting into, though.”

“Oh, you have no idea.” Patrice laughed. “No one does. My brother and Latrine Catrine are the same way, just a lot more bound by nobility’s rules.”

They sat for a while, talking and relaxing as the last bits of tension left them. The Library Exam was over. They’d passed—even if Patrice still hadn’t opened her letters—and they’d be going to the combat track next year. The third floor’s knowledge was Eola’s to take if she was smart about it. And—

A loud, insistent meow interrupted their talk, and Eola sighed. “Atta’s probably about to cough up another hairball. I should go check on her.”

“I’m going back to my bed and napping for the rest of the day. Enjoy cleaning up after your familiar,” Patrice said.

“Thanks,” Eola said dryly.

She stepped into her room and locked the door, then warded it. Atta landed on the floor a moment later with a thump. “Eola, I take back what I’ve said about you. You’re not stupid.”

“What about ‘silly?’” Eola shot back as she searched for a cookie—or for anything else worth eating. The cat only stared at her as she came up empty, and after a moment, she shrugged and dropped into the chair by her desk. “Atta, I’ve been thinking…”

“Don’t make me take back what I’ve just taken back.”

“Rude. I’ve been thinking about what happened to Eddarn’s familiar and what Instructor Tarik said about the backlash when one half of a bonded familiar and master dies. That backlash hurt Eddarn, didn’t it? He could have kept fighting otherwise, but he left.” She held up a hand. “I don’t need an answer. That was rhetorical. What I need to know is if the same thing can happen in reverse. Could a familiar survive their master’s death?”

“Familiars vanish when their masters die. Everyone knows that,” Atta said.

“You didn’t answer my question, Atta.”

“I can’t answer that question, Eola. Sorry.”

For a moment, master and familiar stared at each other. Neither broke eye contact, not even when Atta licked her paw. Their eyes stayed locked for almost a minute before Eola tore herself away from the blue cat and crossed her arms over her chest. “Y’aer’s curse, you’re impossible.”

“Yes, I am.”

“What are you?”

“I can’t answer that either. An ally, and potentially a friend. That’s all you need to know.”

“Ugh! Can you at least tell me what you’re after?”

“No.” The cat curled up in Eola’s lap and butted her head against her hand.

The room went quiet, and Eola scratched Atta absent-mindedly. She was missing something. What had she forgotten?

“What I need,” she said after a minute, “is to understand…oh…oh, I remember! I messed up.”

Atta cocked her head. “How so?”

“The book monster…Eddarn’s familiar. It had the rest of Varin’s journal. I was supposed to be putting it back together. Now I’ll never get the chance, and his notes might have some of the answers. By the time I get back to the third floor, they’ll be scattered to Y’aer knows where.”

“I wouldn’t be so sure about that,” Atta said. She hopped up onto the desk, arched her back, and started to hack and cough. The coughing fit continued for almost a minute as Eola watched, horrified. Then the first balled-up scrap of yellowed paper dropped from the cat’s mouth.

“That’s it? All that for one page?” Eola unfolded it carefully; the handwriting matched the two pages Eola had. It was a minor miracle that Atta had even gotten this one.

Atta shot her a long-suffering look and kept on hacking, and a second one followed. Then a third, and a fourth. Each was, if possible, more soaked and covered in…whatever Atta had in her stomach…than the one before it.

“Oh. Oh no. Y’aer save me!” Eola whimpered as an impossible number of balled-up, soaking wet, disgusting papers slowly rolled off her desk and onto her lap. A cat couldn’t fit that many pages inside of it. It wasn’t possible—and yet, it was happening. The eruption went on and on as she fled to the safety of her bed, and by the time the cat was finished, slimy, crumpled parchment covered her floor and desk.

“Ugh. That tasted horrible! I figured you’d forget in the moment, so I cleaned up your mess back in the library.” Atta smirked, coughed one more time, and hacked up a final page. It bounced on the floor and stopped against the door. “You’re going to have plenty of reading material to go over before next term, though!”

Eola stared at the mess, moaned softly in disgust, and peeled a half-unfurled page off her boot. It tore in half as it came free, and she carefully set both pieces on her desk.

It was going to be a long break before the second term started, but as Eola kicked the papers into a rough pile in the corner to dry, she couldn’t help but smile. She hadn’t been very good at magic before, but now? Now, she was well on her way to rewriting its rules—and to learning from the mage who’d invented them.
