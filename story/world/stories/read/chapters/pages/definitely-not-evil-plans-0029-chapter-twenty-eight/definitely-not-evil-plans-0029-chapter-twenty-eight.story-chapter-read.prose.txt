Thursday November, 10th

The interview was a disaster from the moment I walked in. Damien Designs occupied a sleek glass building downtown. It very much had the new ‘in’ architecture. Minimalism, sharp angles, exposed concrete and ceilings. It was the kind of place that screamed we’re hip and fresh and edgy while having about as much warmth as well, my mother.

I’d worn my nicest blazer, it was actually very cute, red with black piping. I’d even put on real pants instead of the yoga pants that I’d practically lived in for a month. I did up my hair and make up. Something inside of me said, Kate will forgive you if you do this, which I have no idea why she would. But sometimes things aren’t logical.

The receptionist, a young man with professionally sculpted eyebrows and the fakest customer service smile I’d ever seen, led me to a conference room where three people were already waiting. Two women and a man all sat expectantly at a glass table, they all glanced up and watched me come in. I felt like some kind of cell being observed under glass.

“Samantha,” the older woman said without offering her hand. “I’m Kathy. This is Alan and Pip. Thank you for coming in.”

“Thank you for having me.” I settled into the chair across from them. I had brought with me a small physical portfolio as well as a tablet on which I could show a digital one. I laid them both on the table in front of me beneath my clasped hands. “I’m excited to learn more about Damien Designs.”

“Yes, well,” Kathy exchanged a glance with Pip and Alan. “We are very interested in hearing about your experience. Eight years at Harrison & Sons is impressive.”

“Thank you. I learned a lot there.”

“I’m sure you did.” Alan leaned forward. He tapped his pen on his notepad nervously. “I’ve heard a little about the fallout of the situation at H&S, I mean it’s hard not to, right?”

Tap. Tap Tap.

He let out a very fake and unnerving laugh before going back to pen tapping.. To which I smiled politely.

Tap. Tap.

“Terrible what happened there,” Pip added solemnly.

An unfortunate feeling began in the pit of my stomach but I attempted to tamp it down. “It has been… challenging,” I said carefully.

“That CEO, Greggory Harrison Jr.,” Kathy shook her head. “I just can’t believe it. Despicable."

“Did you work closely with him?” Asked Pip.

“I reported to the creative director, Theo. Greg was between offices a lot, I saw him mostly at quarterly meetings or holiday gatherings.”

“But surely, you must’ve noticed something,” Pip asked, eyes gleaming. “Working there for eight years? The culture couldn’t have been that toxic that such a scandal goes unnoticed, right?”

And there it was. The nervous queasy feeling in my stomach had been right afterall. They weren’t going to hire me, they wanted gossip they couldn’t get from a news article. I was a walking tabloid, maybe even someone who would let slip valuable insider information.

“The culture in my department was actually very supportive.” I said keeping my voice as level as possible. “Coming in straight from college I worked with colleagues and mentors who were excellent and very supportive. I have a list of them in my references as well. Once I became a senior designer I took on my own team of mentees and worked hard to foster open and honest feedback.”

“But the harassment,” Alan pressed while continuing to tap that damned pen. “The cover-ups, you had to have seen something. Or do you think Greg’s attempt at retraction is legitimate?”

Tap. Tap.

“I don’t know anything about Greg’s interactions with others as I said. I really only ever saw him at a distance and spoke to him maybe a handful of times.”

“So you think his retraction could be legit?” Pip asked.

“I didn’t say that. What I said was, I saw people working hard to produce good design work. The whole of a company is much larger than one individual. I often would be early to work and would stay after helping my mentees and never leave before my work was done. Over my tenure there I secured eighteen high-profile clients on my own, some even became repeat clients on retainer.”

The three of them exchanged dubious and disappointed looks.

“So, tell us about your mentee and the fallout with Allmart.” Kathy prompted.

“Daniel?” I asked feigning ignorance.

“There are rumors going around that someone let in a hacker cabal to destroy reputations of various employees who were working on an Allmart rebranding.” Pip offered.

“I heard it was their competitor, Bullseye, who did the real hacking. They are trying to sabotage a rebrand.” Alan added taping his pen more quickly.

Tap. Tap. Tap. Tap.

“Listen, I don't know about that. I’m actually unable to speak about specific clients due to NDA, I’m sure you understand.” I said, hopefully.

“Did you know Daniel,” Alan looked down at his notes, “Park?”

I thought about the NDA, and the severance I would have to pay back if I leaked anything. I wrinkled my nose, “I really can’t get into specifics about other employees. If you’d like I can show you some examples of my non NDA works?

Alan went back to tapping before shrugging, “Yeah sure.”

Tap. Tap.

Pip and Kathy exchanged a look of disappointment.

I opened up my portfolio. “Would you prefer to start with digital or print?” I asked hopefully.

Kathy yawned, “Oh, it doesn’t matter.”

I pulled out the tablet and navigated to my slide show.

“Have you had the opportunity to work with others on client projects?” Pip asked.

“Oh yeah. This one right here, I worked on with my former mentor, James.” I slid to a shoe branding campaign we’d worked up.

“Did James ever make you uncomfortable?" Alan asked.

Tap. Tap. Tap.

I concentrated on the pen, the tiny spring mechanism inside and the screwed on clicker holding the whole thing together. With a small push, I added way more ink than the pen could contain, and made the interlocking spiral smooth. The clicking end shot off and pressurized ink burst free from its container splattering and raining down on my three interviewers.

I gasped and quickly grabbed my portfolio back.

All three interviews pushed themselves backwards, and then the yelling started.

“Damn it Alan!” Pip yelled. “Not only did you get it all over my new blouse but you got the carpet and chair too. How am I supposed to explain this?”

I began to back out of the room.

Kathy looked sternly at Alan and Pip. “Get back to your desks and send Steven in to get this mess cleaned up.”

My back hit the handle on the door. “You know guys,” I said, “I really don’t think I’m going to fit in here. You guys are a hot mess.”

Pip looked up, confused before remembering why I was there in the first place.”

Alan turned to face me. The entire underside of his face was covered in iridescent blue black ink. It had splatted up the right side of his face and had clearly gotten into his mouth because his teeth and tongue were blue when he spoke with bewilderment, “Oh.”

Kathy cleared her throat, the right side of her face was speckled with blue ink on her otherwise flawless makeup. “You know this is not typically the impression that Damien Designs wants to leave. Why don’t you stay and just you and I can chat about your portfolio. This was a first interview for both of these two and–”

I held up my hand, “Thank you, but, no. Have the day you deserve. I’ll send a follow up email to the head of HR thanking them for the opportunity.”

All three began protesting, but I didn’t hear them as I pushed the conference room door open before closing it behind me. I didn’t slow down until I got back to my car.
