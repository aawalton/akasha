The adrenaline of the fight gave way to the grim business of the aftermath. There was no celebration, only the quiet, efficient work of professionals. "Alright, let's strip 'em clean," Gunnar ordered, his voice still rough from the battle. "Fen, get your light up. Elara, keep watch. No more surprises."

Fen, his hands still trembling slightly, muttered a short incantation. A globe of soft, white light bloomed above them, casting long, dancing shadows across the clearing. Under its glow, the scene was even more grotesque. The ground was slick with the dark ichor of the Scrabbler Fiends and the thick, red blood of the Grubtusk.

Arthur watched the others for a beat, learning. Gunnar and Torvin went straight for the Grubtusk, their knives flashing as they began expertly carving out the valuable tusks and harvesting the thick, armored hide. Elara, perched silently in her tree, scanned the darkness, an arrow nocked but not drawn. It was a well-oiled machine.

Following a pattern he'd seen other adventurers use, Arthur moved to the smaller Scrabbler corpses. He knelt, his own looted dagger in hand, and began the work. He’d seen this done before, but doing it himself was different. He focused on the creatures’ heads, cutting through the chitinous armor to extract the small, dimly glowing mana cores. The work was methodical, bloody, and strangely calming. It grounded him in his new identity. Each core he dropped into his pouch was another layer of the disguise settling into place. He was Zane, the flanker, harvesting his due.

The journey back to Copperpine was tense. The surprise attack had put everyone on edge. By the time the colossal silhouette of the city wall appeared against the bruised dusk sky, a collective sigh of relief rippled through the party. As they approached the gate, Arthur felt a familiar knot of anxiety tighten in his stomach. It was the same cold dread he’d felt before every deep-cover infiltration, a razor's edge of fear and focus. He forced his body to remain loose, his posture relaxed, mimicking the casual slouch of the adventurer whose life he had stolen.

Gunnar strode forward with a practiced swagger. "Hail, protectors of the gate!" he boomed.

The guardians on duty didn't move, their expressions holding the flat, mercenary gleam of men who knew their position was unassailable. One of them grunted, "Gunnar. Back so soon? The Dire Weasel population must be taking a beating."

"They breed faster than weeds after a rain," Gunnar chuckled, unclipping a heavy pouch from his belt. He opened it, letting the guardian peer inside. The guardian nodded, a slow, greedy gesture, and pointed to a large, iron-banded crate. With a theatrical sigh, Gunnar tipped a full half of their hard-won loot into it. The sound of the cores clinking against the wood was obscene.

"Fifty percent," Gunnar muttered as he walked back, his jovial mask gone. "Fifty percent! For what? Standing on a wall and looking pretty? May their ale turn to piss and their bedrolls be infested with rock mites." He spat on the ground. "Thieves. The lot of 'em."

Arthur filed the raw resentment away. It was a crack in the foundation of any system, a fissure of dissent between the adventurers who did the bleeding and the guardians who reaped the rewards. A crack he might be able to widen later.

He led the way into the city proper, and Arthur fell into step, a silent shadow; the moment he passed through the gate, the city assaulted his senses with a thick soup of smoke, unwashed bodies, and the chaotic din of a thousand desperate lives, most of whom were gaunt, collared workers who could only be slaves. Internally, his training took over as his heart hammered against his ribs, his mind a cold, calculating machine mapping the streets and clocking faces, knowing that a single mistake could mean a blade in the back.

Externally, he was Zane. His shoulders remained slumped in weary indifference. His gaze was downcast, his expression blank. He was just another adventurer, tired from the hunt, thinking only of the ale and coin that awaited him. The performance had to be perfect.

"Here we are," Gunnar's voice cut through Arthur's thoughts, pulling him back to the present. They had stopped before a squat, windowless building with a heavy oak door. A crudely painted sign hung above it, depicting a monster's claw holding a glittering gem. "The Beast's Bounty. Let's see what kind of pittance old Silas will offer us today."

Gunnar pushed the heavy door open, and a bell chimed somewhere in the gloom. The air inside was thick with the cloying smell of chemical preservatives, dried blood, and something musky and reptilian. The shop was a chaotic hoard of monster parts. Hides hung from large iron hooks on the walls, strings of teeth hung like grotesque garlands, and shelves were crammed with jars containing pickled eyes and shimmering glands.

Behind a counter cluttered with scales, knives, and ledgers, a wiry old man with spectacles perched on his nose looked up. This was Silas. His eyes, magnified by the thick lenses, were sharp and missed nothing.

"Gunnar," he rasped, his voice dry as old parchment. "Still throwing yourself at the local fauna, I see. What treasures have you brought me today? More chipped Scrabbler claws?"

"Only the finest, you old vulture," Gunnar retorted, upending their sack onto the counter. A cascade of Grubtusk tusks, Scrabbler cores, and bundled hide spilled out.

Silas poked at the pile with a bony finger, separating the small Scrabbler cores from the rest. "Scrabbler cores. Barely worth the mana to light my pipe. Five coppers each." He then turned his attention to the larger items. "The Grubtusk hide is decent, I'll give you that. Twenty silver. The tusks… this one's cracked. Ten for the pair."

"Five coppers? You old thief!" Gunnar shot back, leaning on the counter. "And twenty for a perfect hide? We fought a whole swarm for these. Don't lowball me, Silas."

Arthur stood back, silent, letting the familiar dance of commerce play out. The haggling began, a rapid-fire exchange of insults and inflated values. Arthur listened intently, committing every figure to memory. A common Scrabbler core was nearly worthless, but a solid hide from a larger beast was worth a significant amount. This confirmed the hierarchy he had suspected. A single, high-quality core from a creature like a Grubtusk would likely be worth a small fortune, enough to sustain a party for a month.

Eventually, they settled on a price. Silas counted out a stack of tarnished silver and copper coins, which Gunnar then divided into five equal piles on the counter.

"Your cut, Zane," Gunnar said, pushing one of the piles toward him.

Arthur scooped the coins into his pouch. The weight was real, tangible. It was the first currency he had earned in this world, and he had done it without spilling a drop of his own blood.

"Alright, lads," Gunnar declared, his mood instantly improved by the clink of coin. "To The Rusty Flagon! First round of ale is on me. We earned it."

The others cheered. Arthur simply nodded, his mind already moving past the shop, past the coins, and onto the next phase of his infiltration. The tavern would be loud, full of loose tongues and valuable information. His hunt was far from over.
