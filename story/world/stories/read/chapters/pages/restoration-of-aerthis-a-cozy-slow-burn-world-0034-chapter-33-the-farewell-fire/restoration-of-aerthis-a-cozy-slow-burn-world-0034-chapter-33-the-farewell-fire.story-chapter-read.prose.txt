Her satchel bulged so full it looked ready to burst, but she still knelt on the church steps, fingers checking every strap one more time.

Chalk, twine, dismantling tools. Rations wrapped in wax paper, extra socks that Mina had all but shoved in herself. A waterskin newly strapped, her well-thumbed notebook tucked on top with a sprig of dried mint between its pages to fight the musty smell.

Everything laid out, everything squared away, but weirdly enough, her hands refused to stop fussing.

Mina hovered just behind, Pufflet leashed at her feet and tail wagging slow. Arms crossed. Brows drawn.

“You’re packing like you’re never coming back.”

Elynara blinked up at her.

“I just don’t want to forget anything.”

Mina’s face softened. She crouched without a word and tugged one strap tighter.

“Don’t overdo it. You’re allowed to have fun, you know.”

Fun. Elynara rolled the word in her mouth. Indeed, the journey would be fun. It made her anxious, yes. But it also promised her new things to see, even more to learn, and the magitech ruins waited at the end. The possibilities were many.

She closed the satchel carefully and stood, shifting it across her shoulders.

“I’ll be careful.”

Mina tilted her head.

“You always say that.”

“And I always mean it.” Elynara smiled faintly, brushing a thumb along the edge of her staff. “Promise.”

But the children caught her before she could slip away.

“Miss Elynara! Show us again!”

“Just one more before you go!”

“Fire Queen! Fire Queen!”

Her cheeks burned hotter than any flame she could conjure. She was halfway to protesting when Mina gave her a little push from behind, smirking like a conspirator.

Traitor.

Elynara turned back to the expectant faces, sighing theatrically.

“Alright. One last time. But you all stay back, you hear? No grabbing or touching. And no running under the fire. Understood?”

A dozen nods followed, far too eager.

But who was she to judge?

Elynara loved her magic just as much, if not more. And if nothing else, it would be another chance to practice!

She raised her staff and let the mana rise slowly, more ribbon than river. This time she didn’t try juggling sparks, or making silly loops. Instead, she shaped it with care, coaxing it into a trail of flame-butterflies, wings flickering with warm orange-gold ambience.

They drifted upward, light brushing soft against courtyard stone, casting gentle shadows that danced with the movement. The morning sun not yet competing with the display at this angle.

Gasps rose. Small hands reached skyward. One bold boy leapt to catch a butterfly, only to fall flat on his back in the dirt, cackling.

“Careful! I did say no grabbing,” Elynara called after them, trying to sound stern. Her smile ruined the effect though.

Next she conjured a bird. A fire-lark, wings wide and bright, gliding in a long arc before dissolving into a scatter of sparks. Pufflet barked at it, tail up and ears forward, ready to give chase.

Starpuff, not to be outdone, launched straight through the cloud of embers, carving out a shimmering spiral in its wake.

“Show-off,” Elynara muttered, but the children erupted with cheers, convinced it was all part of the performance.

The last shape she picked was a simple one – a slow spiral of flickering petals that fell like drifting leaves, fading as they touched the ground. No great trick, she was just letting the mana disperse near the ground. But it made the youngest gasp in wonder all the same.

And maybe it was.

She let the flame go and lowered her staff, brushing a loose braid behind her ear.

“That’s all for today,” she said, voice pitched above the rising clamor. “You’ll have to wait until I’m back for more.”

Groans and pouting followed, of course. A few tried the wide-eyed stare. One clung to her leg, dramatically pretending to weep.

“Off, you menace,” she grumbled, lifting the boy by the shoulders and handing him back to his giggling sister.

The crowd didn’t follow her as she stepped toward the church steps, but their voices rang on. Little feet stomped in place, trying to mimic her steps. A girl waved her arms wildly, yelling something about fire feathers. Starpuff trilled back at them once before perching smugly atop her satchel.

“You’re enjoying this too much,” she muttered, enjoying the appreciation herself.

Mina was waiting at the top of the steps, arms crossed and smiling without trying to hide it.

“You sure you’re not going to miss them?”

Elynara looked back one last time.

“I’ll miss the noise.”

“That’s not a no.”

She sniffed, adjusting her pack.

“You’re the one who pushed me into that mess.”

“I was helping. You need to learn to accept admiration with grace.”

“That wasn’t admiration. That was sugar-fueled chaos.”

“Same thing!”

Elynara snorted. Mina leaned in and pulled a stubborn strap tighter on her pack.

“Everything packed?”

“Twice over.” She reached to tap the side. “Maps, pencils, tools, food, socks. Happy?”

Mina didn’t answer right away. Then hugged her with a softer,

“Be careful out there.”

Elynara hesitated. The weight of trust and distance sat heavy between them.

“I will,” she said, returning the hug. “I promise.”

Mina stepped back and lifted a hand.

“Mhm. Then go. I will do my best training too!”

So Elynara turned, with a smile on her face. Staff in hand, satchel on her shoulder, Starpuff in her hood pulsed a dim green. Hopefully in approval and not planning more pranks!

Oh, who was she kidding. Of course it was planning more pranks!

Not that she minded.

Much.

Elynara passed the courtyard again, the children waving now, shouting farewell in uneven chorus. She waved back. And then was on her way.

At the guild, Alicia was already waiting, the ledger open, papers lined in perfect rows. Her pen moved quickly, but her eyes lifted the moment Elynara stepped inside.

“Everything ready?” Alicia asked.

“Yes,” Elynara said a tad too quickly. “I checked everything twice. Maybe three times.”

“Good.” Alicia’s tone stayed crisp, but the corners of her eyes held something softer. “I don’t have any updates for you this time. So just stay sharp. Don’t take unnecessary risks. And write things down.”

Elynara nodded.

“Of course.”

Marcus appeared then, leaning an elbow on the counter, clearly listening in the whole time.

“And don’t explode half the forest this time!”

Elynara spun on him with a glare.

“That only happened once, and it was hardly even a clearing!”

He grinned wider.

“Ha! Once is all it takes to make a story, you know?”

She felt the blush rise and turned on her heel before he could see it.

“I’ll be fine.”

The two of them shared a glance she didn’t catch. Starpuff, perched smugly in her hair now, trilled in amusement.

Marcus’s voice drifted from the guild doorway.

“Bring back something interesting this time!”

Elynara didn’t turn.

“I always do!”

Outside, the square felt brighter than she remembered. People milled around, forest folk ears twitching as merchants called to one another. The rhythm of the town moving steady and alive.

She took a breath, letting the ambience fill her, before following the road towards her destination.

The western gate didn’t feel particularly grand, or important, or even ceremonial. It was really rather unremarkable, if a tad quieter than the others. Only a few trade carts lined it. But mostly it was just the stretch of road leading out and the stone wall tapering into forest shadow in the distance.

Elynara adjusted her satchel as she approached, her fingers twitching at the straps. New road to traverse. A new direction she didn’t explore yet. Familiar joy at the possibilities, and old nerves stretching their legs too.

They really didn’t like being forgotten!

The staff in her hand felt more like an anchor than tool this morning, its grip worn familiar from long hours of training. Elynara hadn’t expected to feel this tight in her chest. She got so used to having people around that a journey alone felt difficult again.

Well, at least she had Starpuff with her! The little prankster would not let her get all moody for too long. Not that she planned to. There was just so much to learn and explore that it felt like nothing could keep her feeling down for long!

Of course, that was when she spotted Josh leaning against the gatepost.

Spear in hand, half-lidded eyes already sharp with amusement.

“Well, well,” he said, pushing off the post as she drew closer. “If it isn’t our local flame thrower. Off to set the horizon on fire?”

She kept walking ahead.

“Not unless provoked.”

Josh glanced pointedly at her staff, then bent slightly at the waist to address it with mock solemnity.

“You hear that, stick? She promises she’ll behave. Do you believe her?”

Elynara stopped in her tracks.

“Stop – stop talking to my staff!”

He tapped it once, utterly serious.

“It’s probably the only thing that listens.”

Her jaw dropped.

“Josh!”

The smirk returned, wider than before.

“See? It worked. You’re already less nervous.”

She spluttered, torn between a retort and a swat. Laughter won instead, bubbling through as she half-shoved him with the end of her staff. He didn’t budge, just leaned on his spear again and watched her with that annoying calm.

“You’re impossible,” she muttered.

“And yet, here I am.” He tilted his head, eyeing her pack. “Going far?”

Elynara straightened.

“Out to the edge of the Deadzone. On official Guild business!”

His expression flickered, just for a moment, and then returned to neutral.

“Alicia sent you?”

“I volunteered.”

“You always do.” His voice lowered, not teasing now. “Watch the land, and don’t go into the Deadzone, under any circumstances. If the stone feels wrong under your boots, it probably is.”

Elynara blinked, surprised by the seriousness.

“I will.”

Josh nodded once, then hooked his thumb toward the road.

“Then go, Fire Queen. Before you talk yourself into staying.”

She hesitated.

“You’re not going to make another joke?”

“Oh, I have a dozen. But you’ll miss your daylight.” He grinned again. “And I’m not walking you to your destination.”

“Good.” She stepped past him, the northern gate now open in front of her. “You would slow me down!”

He gave a theatrical gasp, hand to chest.

“You wound me!”

She was already walking, but her smile pulled wide despite herself.

“Stay safe,” he called after her. “And if you meet any cursed artifacts, maybe don’t touch the first one.”

“I make no promises!”

The gate passed behind her, stone giving way to a dirt path, the line of Oltenar shrinking behind every step. Trees rose to either side in slow procession, the wind carrying the scent of moss and damp bark. Birds chattered somewhere above, and Starpuff peeked from her shoulder with a low, amused warble.

“Don’t encourage him,” she muttered.

The puff spun lazily in place, then vanished into the folds of her cloak again. Completely unapologetic.

Elynara’s fingers loosened on the staff without meaning to as her steps took her further and further away.

The first stretch of road felt almost like flying.

Boots struck the packed earth in a steady rhythm. Her breath matched it. So did her heartbeat. The weight of the satchel didn’t bother her. The staff in her hand moved easily, more companion than burden.

The world ahead stretched open. With no looming tasks, early curfews, nor towers of parchment stacked on Alicia’s desk. Just the road, the wind, and the scent of grass and flowers lining the sides of the road.

Starpuff looped around her in delighted arcs, trilling a series of chirps too smug for their own good. Then it dove into her hair without warning, setting the ends puffing out in every direction.

She hissed and tried to swat it down.

“Menace,” she muttered, trying to smooth the worst of it. “You’re lucky I like you.”

It dove again. Elynara gave up on neatness and kept walking instead.

Surprisingly, a few others shared the road.

A pair of merchants coaxing tired donkeys forward, a group of travelers with dust-coated cloaks and sharp eyes – adventurers? And a family of four waving as they passed with a cart of grain and foodstuffs.

The road, even when occasionally half-full, felt wide enough to breathe in. It gave her time to stretch, to think, to plan. She liked that.

And once Oltenar finally dipped out of sight behind a low hill, her shoulders settled further. No more people pressing in around her.

Only going forward was left!

Elynara passed a shrine not long after – it was a single worn stone nestled beneath a pair of crooked trees. Weeds had overtaken it, the old carvings dulled from rain and time. No spirit greeted her there either.

She marked it on her map as ‘Empty’ anyways.

The act calmed her more than she expected. A small moment of purpose, solid and simple. Her steps came quicker after that.

But excitement, she learned again, wasn’t endless. It flickered into calm. Calm gave way to quiet. And eventually, the quiet invited thought and random musings.

Elynara thought back to the village requests she were to work on. To the map.

And the real reason she set out in the first place.

Magitech ruins.

Her fingers twitched at the thought of them. What might they still hold? Forgotten machines? Maybe records of things no one else remembered. Things that might answer questions she didn’t even know to ask.

Elynara really didn’t know, and that excited her all the more!

Though the unease remained.

Just walking close to the Deadzone could wear a person down, so the stories went. Alicia had been careful when she mentioned it.

And very insistent.

Elynara rolled her shoulders, shifting the satchel’s weight.

The puff poked its head out from her hood, blinking sleepily. Then it trilled and nuzzled her neck in a gesture far too proud.

She laughed quietly.

“That’s not a vote of confidence. You just want snacks.”

Still, she formed a ball of mana for it to nibble on and carried on.

Midway through the afternoon, she stopped to take in what looked to have once been a stream. Barely a trickle now, the edges crusted with mineral silt.

The land here was thinner.

Another little sign of decline. The stream could just as easily have been nothing, but put together, it looked worrying if you knew to look.

The shadows stretched longer across the road as the sun tilted west.

Her pace remained steady, but the sameness was starting to gnaw at Elynara. She distracted herself with shaping practice, tying together faint fire threads and tracing lines in the dust with them.

A flicker here. A wisp there.

Starpuff watched, flying out with obvious interest.

Then dared to critique her work with impatient warbles! The gall!

It blew through one of her hoops, ‘correcting’ it with a happy trill.

“Fine,” she said, exasperated. “You’re hired.”

It flipped in a happy roll and vanished upward into the treetops.

Elynara laughed, and continued on her way. Heart a little lighter somehow.

A lot of free time walking meant a lot of time to think though. And once Elynara went through the immediate worries… her thoughts turned to others.

Mina came first.

Mina, squinting at a flicker of light that only now began to register as magic. Mina, who held her breath when reaching for threads too thin to grasp, whose fingers trembled with effort just to feel them. And yet she kept going.

The image stuck with Elynara more than she expected.

Pride swelled behind her ribs. It shouldn’t have. Not really. It wasn’t her accomplishment. But pride wasn’t always logical.

Mina had learned to sense mana. And that changed everything.

It meant the Plan could work! And that perhaps she could be the caretaker it needed. One day, maybe she would teach others too.

Elynara adjusted her grip on the staff, boots brushing through patches of dry grass. The road here was thin, narrower than the well-traveled merchant lines nearer Oltenar. Just enough room for one cart, if it came through at all.

She liked it better this way. Less dust, more sky.

But her smile faded with the rhythm of her steps. Because it was still only the beginning. And there was a lot more to do.

Mina wasn’t ready yet.

She needed time. Room to explore the sense without pressure. Without looming expectations, or constant corrections. Just quiet trial and error.

So Elynara gave Mina the space she needed.

Which meant for now, the Plan could only grow at the edges. Slowly. Carefully. A sprout without sun, waiting for stronger roots.

She snorted at herself.

The Plan.

It sounded absurd every time she called it that in her head, capital letters and all, like it was a mission drafted by gods.

But it really wasn’t. It was just a simple plan she made quietly in a corner of the chapel while Starpuff snored upside down in a bowl of dried berries.

Still, it was the best approach Elynara knew.

Help Mina. Teach others. Build a circle. Grow the network of spirit caretakers until the world breathed a little easier again.

A loop of life. Giving and feeding and restoring!

Except.

Her hands curled around the staff a little tighter.

Spirits needed mana to do their thing, and grow, and spread.

And the world didn’t have any to spare.

She kicked a stone down the road, hard. It bounced twice, then vanished into the weeds.

Mana was the lifeblood of spirits. Everything pointed to that so far.

But how could they pour water into a well that was already dry? You couldn’t pour nothing into something and expect a miracle.

Elynara sighed. Took a sharp breath. Then another. She walked faster, though it changed nothing.

It all felt so backwards. Spirits kept the land alive, and the land kept the mana flowing, and the mana fed the spirits in turn. Beautiful. Elegant. And precious beyond belief.

Until it all broke.

And now that it was broken, the whole system demanded mana to fix itself. Mana that was thinning faster than anyone wanted to admit.

“That’s a scam,” she muttered aloud.

Starpuff trilled from her shoulder, clearly completely unbothered by the collapsing state of things. Its glow remained faint and cheerful.

Elynara grumbled, brushing a lock of wind-tangled hair out of her face.

“The world’s rigged and you don’t even care.”

The puff bobbed once in mock sympathy, then did a lazy flip just for the heck of it.

She rolled her eyes, but it did ease the tightness a little.

It wasn’t fair, but what else was new?

Celestia might not have forced the job onto her, but now that she got to live here, and met all those lovely people… Elynara would not just sit still and mope when the math got ugly. She wasn’t going to quit just because the loop looked impossible.

So, fine.

If the only way to jumpstart the cycle was to spend her own mana, then she would spend it. She would train harder, level faster, carry more of the burden until others could share it. Until Mina was ready. Until they had five more like Mina. Or ten. Or twenty.

That was the work.

But Elynara would be lying if she said it didn’t weigh on her a little.

Though the longer she walked, the clearer and lighter the weight felt. Moving around really helped! If taking a step after another took her places, why wouldn’t big plans work if she only worked on them one part at a time?

The logic was flawless, she knew!

She thought of Oltenar again and of how full of life it had looked at first. The bustle of trade. The chatter at the guild. The sound of bells through narrow streets.

But it had been a mask.

The mana there had been extremely low. Nearly drained dead. With little hope for more.

The edge of it was still with her now.

How easy it was to pretend things were fine.

A town with good stew, warm blankets, and laughing children. And hidden just behind it were empty shrines. Dim spirits. Soil growing weaker season by season.

It frightened her how normal it all felt.

Elynara paused long enough to take a sip from her waterskin. The liquid was cool, clean, with a hint of sweetness from the mint she had tucked inside earlier. It steadied her. Not by much, but the cheer returned.

The road kept going.

She didn’t know how long until the next marker, or the next spirit, or the next ruin. But she knew her feet were moving.

And so long as she kept moving, every destination could be possible.

She just knew it!

The sky opened above her in quiet defiance of her thoughts of doom and gloom.

With no smoke, ruined peaks, or thinning mana lines that only she could sense.

Simply wide blue, clouds drifting lazy arcs with all the patience of the old gods. The breeze curled under her hood and tugged at the ends of her scarf, cool enough to raise goosebumps but not cold enough to bite.

Elynara tipped her head back and watched the clouds pass, boots slowing just long enough for her to feel it.

The world might be fading. But it hadn’t stopped being beautiful.

Not yet!

The road creaked faintly beneath her steps. Earth and gravel. Dandelions at the edge. A bird called once and flitted overhead.

Everything felt suspended between stillness and purpose.

Her thoughts softened with it, the sharp edge of earlier worries easing into something more bearable.

Maybe nothing was simple. Maybe the land really was losing its breath. But she was still here. Walking and working.

Still trying.

Elynara adjusted the strap of her satchel and breathed in deeply, letting the weight on her shoulders settle. A kind of quiet resolved around her.

Celestia’s presence flickered faintly at the edge of her thoughts. The memory of warmth, of that gentle encouragement laced through every gift the goddess had left behind. That reassurance that even if the world cracked, someone would keep walking forward anyway.

She would. Step by stubborn step!

Ruins still waited. Spirits still lingered, quiet and needy. And maybe – just maybe – the answers weren’t yet gone. Just hidden.

She could live with hidden. Hidden could be found and uncovered.

The wind picked up again, teasing a strand of hair across her cheek.

Then–

Poke.

Her eye twitched. She didn’t look.

“Don’t.”

Starpuff hovered just beside her head, pulsing an unconvincing shade of innocence. Its glow wobbled faint green, which she had come to interpret as the color of I am absolutely about to misbehave.

“Not now,” she warned.

The puff rotated in a slow, showy circle.

Bonk.

Straight to the forehead.

She stumbled mid-step and caught herself on her staff, teeth gritted.

“Stars above, I will turn you into a lantern.”

Starpuff trilled, smug and proud. Its glow sparkled with mirth, bouncing in tight loops of victory above her head.

“You little–!”

She swung her staff in a lazy arc. Starpuff zipped away in a wild zigzag, looping once, twice, before circling right back like it hadn’t just tried to give her a concussion.

Elynara sighed, rubbing the spot between her brows.

“One day you’ll run into a tree, and I won’t help you.”

The puff pulsed again – definitely smug – and settled against her scarf, nestling with the contentment of a cat who knew she wouldn’t follow through.

She kept walking, staff tapping steady rhythm into the road.

It would’ve been easy to stay tangled in thoughts. In worries. The land was thinning. The Plan needed help. Mina needed more time. And even with all her training, even with growing skills and numbers that crept upward, she still didn’t have enough answers.

But she had movement.

She had a path beneath her feet.

And, unfortunately, she had Starpuff.

Her smile tugged through despite everything.

Sometimes the world didn’t need fixing in one day. Sometimes it just needed walking. Even if the path led through thinned woods, forgotten villages, and the frayed edges of magic, she could still walk it.

She could still bring light with her. One breath. One cycle. One laugh at a time.

The road dipped ahead, winding gently toward a shallow valley. Tall grass danced in patches between old stones, and wildflowers tangled through the weeds without asking anyone’s permission.

Elynara stopped at the crest and looked forward.

Everything still needed doing. Training. Mapping. Shrine work. Protecting the next village down the line. The ruins were coming closer too. Those old bones of magic were out there, curled deep in the trees, waiting for someone curious enough to find them.

She was curious enough.

And more than that, she was ready.

“Let’s go, you little menace,” she said, adjusting her satchel again.

Starpuff perked up, chirping a delighted warble, and launched itself ahead, darting a lazy figure-eight across the road. Its glow glinted against the sunlight, casting playful shadows in the dust.

Elynara followed, her smile steady now. The morning had been heavy, and the world still whispered its worry under her feet, but the day was hers again.

The journey ahead waited, wild and open.

And she would walk it.

All of it.
