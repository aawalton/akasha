After Rosslyn gave her impromptu speech, she didn’t immediately run away like Adon would have wanted to in her situation.

That probably would have undermined the gravity of everything she’d said. So it was a good thing she wasn’t Adon.

Instead, she stuck around with the peasants, administered healing magic, asked about where they were from, how they had been holding up, and what their plans were. As she had explained, almost apologetically, during her monologue, she didn’t have an abundance of resources to distribute to her adoring public. She avoided making any extravagant promises of material support or even housing.

But she generally acted like someone who understood the concept of noblesse oblige and was determined to practice it as best she could, despite her limited means.

“Your Majesty?”

Rosslyn turned around. Behind her, there was another peasant woman, but Adon could tell this one was a local from the hamlet.

“Um, yes?” Rosslyn asked.

“Would you like us to make room for your guests somewhere in the village?”

Adon understood instantly, telepathically, that the woman was talking about the new arrivals. They weren’t nobility, but the Queen had spent so much time on these people that to the locals, they registered as her “guests.”

Rosslyn looked back and forth between the peasant group and the local woman a couple of times, deep in thought.

More of these people are going to gather, Rosslyn, Goldie sent.

You probably won’t be able to send them away if that’s the case, Adon added.

I was never going to send them away, Rosslyn thought in response. But that does give me pause.

“Please do,” she finally said. “But we will also need to prepare for more refugees to gather here, if that can be done. I know that your village is small. But there is a forest all around. Do you have woodcutters nearby here? And where is the nearest farmland?”

The peasant woman answered as best she could, then brought others to discuss the matter with the Queen.

Soon Rosslyn had given instructions for both the temporary accommodation of the displaced peasants and the construction of some rude shelters from the wood lying closest to the village. The woodcutters were ready to donate their labor, and the peasants who had come were prepared to share what food they had brought from their ruined farms.

Adon could hear the nervousness in Rosslyn’s mind, completely different from the way he would be thinking about the same situation. She wasn’t bothered at all by the process of giving commands to other people, but she couldn’t help but doubt her own decision-making.

If we get this wrong, people go hungry or unsheltered, the Queen thought. If we don’t secure supplies in time to feed them, the people here will die.

At the same time, Rosslyn was also, undeniably, excited. She had lost her father, her city, half her territory, but not her people. With the people still willing to follow her, she still had agency. She still had a lot of control over how the next events would play out; she wasn’t completely at the mercy of the Parmonians and the Empire as the two powers prepared to fight each other and determine the Kingdom’s fate.

As she finished delegating tasks and healing and finally returned to the cottage, Adon sensed a pleasant energy buzzing beneath the surface of the Queen’s mind. Under the circumstances, he thought she was as close as she could be to happy.

As they opened the door, Adon recognized that King Janus had departed the building, returning to his army for the moment. It was just the people closest to Rosslyn, plus the Sergeant, remaining now.

Good, he thought to himself. She could use a little relief from this pressure.

At least, it would have been severe pressure for Adon. The Queen seemed better adapted to the strain of dealing with other people than Adon was, of course. But he could only imagine that it did still register as an unpleasant feeling.

“Your Majesty, did everything go all right with the peasants who came to see you?” Sergeant Angus asked as she reentered the dwelling.

“Yes and no,” Rosslyn replied after a moment of thought.

“Why both?” asked Frederick, who had moved from the bed but was still taking it fairly easy, stretched out on the floor, doing light leg exercises.

Goldie dropped from Rosslyn’s shoulder and went over to him as the young lord was speaking.

“It worked out all right in the sense that I think we have a plan for how to feed and house this one group of peasants for the next few days at least,” Rosslyn said. “They did not actually want anything in particular, but their circumstances are difficult. But the longer term will be murkier. I think there is a plan for that, too, but, um, Sergeant—” Her mouth twisted in a complicated frown as she looked at him—“I think that over the next few weeks…”

“Go ahead and say it, please,” Sergeant Angus said, his lips forming a thin line.

“I think this peaceful village where you have made your home might be forever transformed by the circumstances we find ourselves in.”

“Well, if that is our fate, we will adapt,” the Sergeant replied after only a few seconds. “You do not suffer invasion and loss of hearth and home every year, or even every century. I doubt we would have ever felt any insecurity in this land if not for the Demon Empire present, always sniffing around. No one will hold it against you if some changes must happen to make and keep us secure.”

Rosslyn nodded, a stoic expression concealing her feelings, but Adon caught them.

‘Some changes’ is quite the understatement, she thought. I hope you will still feel the same in a few months.

A week passed.

The butterfly and the spider had been out hunting in the woods, in what had become a daily ritual for the two mystic beasts. They returned with game animals—the Claustrian Fallow Deer and a dozen Black Grouses tied together by their feet—slung over their shoulders, in their human forms. It took a moment for the sentries posted outside to recognize them and let the mystic beasts back in.

During that brief window, Goldie’s eyes widened as she stared up at the walls that had risen around it.

“What do you think of how this place changed?” the spider asked.

“I was surprised that it happened so quickly,” Adon replied. “Rosslyn seemed to think it would take months, and she was dreading how the locals would react when she made that prediction.”

The two massive wooden doors swung open to admit the two mystic beasts, and they stepped inside of the fortified settlement.

On either side of them rose newly constructed buildings, watchtowers and soldiers’ barracks.

There was a cobblestone path running through the middle of the fortified town, large enough for carriages or a row of knights on horseback to ride through. It was significantly broader than the path through the center of the hamlet had been. With superpowered royalty and nobility present, as well as magical knights, it had turned out that moving small buildings a short distance was not an especially challenging task. So the people with cottages kept their cottages, just several feet further apart now, and interspersed with a lot of newer construction.

Everywhere, people were at work, whether building new buildings, laundering clothing, maintaining armor, making equipment, cooking, or performing one of the other thousand tasks involved in the upkeep of an army.

A large number of new recruits were in the back of the fortification, receiving training from Sergeant Angus.

Rosslyn had been correct that the hamlet would be changed irrevocably. She had underestimated the timeline and how large the changes would be.

The more displaced peasants appeared, the more new soldier recruits the young Queen found herself dealing with.

There were also more hungry people, which presented its own logistical challenges, but many of the nearby farmers were taking the opportunity to trade with the army that was now camped close by to them. It was far more convenient to trade with this new military town than to trek to the nearest formal city after the capture of the capital, and no one wanted to sell to the Empire. The Dessian Duke had sent regular aid, perhaps partly motivated by the fact that his son was here.

The Parmonian Army had brought money with it, and they were ready to spend on what was needed. The rank and file also had trades and skills they could use to barter for anything additional that the ordinary soldier wanted.

So far, things were holding together somehow, though the fortified town and the tent city that had sprung up around it felt like they should be inherently unstable.

But the food was flowing in, enough that they were all surviving. There was energy about the place, and the feelings were mostly positive. The Parmonian knights had yet to wear out their welcome or abuse the local peasantry in any way that anyone had noticed.

The most important factor, probably, was that the Demon Empire had not yet poked its head out of Wayn and tried to conquer the rest of Claustria. Once their force and the Parmonians clashed, the real merit of this place would be tested.

Adon frankly doubted that they were ready for that trial by fire.

A trio of soldiers approached the mystic beasts as they stepped inside, and Adon refocused on his immediate surroundings.

“Thank you for bringing back meat for the camp, Prince Adon,” said the nearest man as the butterfly’s eyes fell on him.

Adon tried not to recoil at being called that.

Rosslyn had announced their engagement a few days prior, and people were attaching all sorts of titles to Adon that neither he nor the Queen had labeled him with formally.

There are worse things, Goldie sent telepathically, than people thinking you’re someone special.

That’s right, he affirmed.

It still felt weird, but it was his job as a consort in waiting to try and be a complement to the Queen—or at least not actively weigh her down as she led her people.

“It was my pleasure,” Adon said. “Er, our pleasure!” He tilted his head to acknowledge Goldie. She had contributed as much as him.

The two mystic beasts handed over the game to the soldiers.

“Every bit of meat really counts when you have thousands of mouths to feed,” said the second soldier. The men seemed to be at relative ease with the mystic beasts as they spoke, as if the butterfly and the spider were a sort of happy medium between the soldiers’ stations and the untouchable nobility who would expect them to bow and scrape and salute in every interaction.

That made the situation less uncomfortable for Adon, at least.

“You have a strategy meeting,” the third soldier said. “Um, when you arrived, I was to tell the two of you that there is a strategy meeting you are meant to attend with the Queen, the King, and the High Command.”

Of course there is, Adon thought.

“They will be wanting Prince Adon,” Goldie replied, barely hiding her amusement at the situation. “Not me, I think.”

The butterfly gave her a sidelong look. That was true, but she was too pleased about it. She seemed far too happy to hear about or witness Adon being thrown into these social situations with little preparation or warning.

Rosslyn tried not to pull him in when he was actually unneeded, but the last week had seen Adon experience more social interaction than at any previous time in this incarnation.

It was exhausting.

“I believe they wanted you as well, Lady Spider,” said the soldier in response.

Adon smiled as Goldie frowned.

“Well, it seems there is nothing to be done about it,” Adon said, barely restraining a laugh. “They want to see both of us. I guess further training will have to wait until later.”

Goldie let out a little grunt of disbelief, but she followed Adon. The two invertebrates in human form advanced down the central path of the town, drawing stares but ignoring them, as they moved toward the tower that had been erected at the very back of the fort town.
