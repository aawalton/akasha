Valanor had never experienced anything like the mad rush of energy that coursed through his body and spirit. He wasn’t sure that anyone had ever been in a situation quite like the one he’d found himself in.

The power of Dusk, the power of a god crashed into him, while a madman–and one of Valanor’s closest friends–sat a few places away on a throne that was only meant to exist in legend. Ethan Bishop was trying to save them all, and he was taking his gods-be-damned time with it.

Valanor could barely see the other four men assembled with him, though Prince Calevaro stood to his left, close enough to reach out and touch. The power was everything, and it seemed to be burning away everything else. Valanor’s mind was no exception, nor was his soul.

But he would hold, and he knew the other men would as well. The Bishop brothers had proven themselves, and after getting to know Ethan, Valanor wouldn’t doubt the willpower of any who held that name. As for the prince…Valanor would have bet the young Twilight was smiling, enjoying this novel experience, despite the pain and the stakes.

There was no telling how long this had gone on for, but Valanor recognized that his frail form was reaching its breaking point. No one was meant for this, none but a god, and Valanor knew better than most how far from a god he truly was.

“Come on, boy, finish this!” he murmured through gritted teeth.

Then, as if in answer, something changed. A shift. A realignment of some kind. The power that had been surging through Ethan’s spirit and into the five men suddenly blasted out in every direction, rolling over the red landscape of Potentia like a golden wave.

Valanor’s eyes widened at the shock of it. The sudden abrupt absence of a power he’d never been meant to hold, yet he found he still longed for, even knowing it had been killing him.

That power was already gone, and the world seemed clear once more. There was Ethan, eyes squeezed shut in concentration, sitting on The Throne, fingers bleeding as they dug into the stone that made it.

Valanor felt his eyes drawn upward, and his jaw dropped as he beheld a maddening sight. Ethan’s spirit had acted as a buffer between Dusk’s power and the foolish mortals forced to wield it, somehow cleansing it of the terrible taint that had so ruined Potentia. Yet it seemed that it had not remained unchanged for the experience.

The spirit loomed in a way that defied imagination. It would have towered over the Champions in the same way the Champions had towered over the gathered humans. While it was kneeling, spectral hands wrapped around them and The Throne protectively, its head was still high enough to be lost in the clouds above.

“Goddess…” Valanor heard himself whisper.

But again his eyes were drawn away, as something flashed past him, and through the immense rift, which now appeared small next to Ethan’s gargantuan spirit.

Dusk, Valanor realized. The god had awakened, and finally moved on. That could only happen if–

The world disappeared into blue-white light.

***

Valanor awoke to the sound of birds singing, and a warm breeze rustling his curly hair–it had grown long during his time in Potentia. Opening his eyes, he found himself face-down on lush green grass, and felt the warmth of a perfect golden sun on his skin.

He prepared himself for the flood of pain that returned awareness would bring–he hadn’t escaped the battle with the Emperor unscathed, and his shield arm had been broken in several places.

Yet, when he pushed himself up, Valanor found that he felt better than he could ever remember feeling. His armor was gone, and his clothing beneath was still filthy, but his body was revived and refreshed.

He got to his feet, looking around in surprise and relief. He was in an open clearing, dense woods ending a dozen paces away, and the trees were the furs and elms he knew from back home. This was Viridus! This was Nexum! He was–

No… Something was different. Valanor turned around, and found that he was near the edge of a steep cliff. Cautiously, he moved forward, and found himself looking out over an enormous, lush valley, ringed with mountains.

It was so much like his home, yet also not. The rivers were in the wrong places, the forests not quite right, even the mountains were shaped differently. And to his right–was that a city?!

It was impossibly large, like Sanctustella itself, and covered in buildings of unfamiliar shape. Valanor blinked at it in confusion. This was like nothing he’d seen in his homeland, and it lacked a wall! Surely, monsters could just–

He saw some. A pack, in the distance, though thankfully moving away from the city, not toward it. But what type were those, Valanor had extensive knowledge of monsters from numerous areas across Viridus, but these were unfamiliar.

“Demons…” he said aloud. But he knew that somehow the word didn’t fit. Or no longer fit.

These were like the simiavus he’d fought so many times, yet their bodies were more sleek, and they moved with something bordering on grace. They raced from one copse of trees to another, and showed none of the aggression they were known for.

And they were blue. Demons were almost always some shade of red, or at least a darker color, with red highlights. Valanor had never seen anything like this.

But only a moment later, he spotted lutumstriders moving across a distant lake. The tall, long-necked lizards were unmistakable, and most definitely native to Nexum.

“He did it,” Valanor said at last, somehow certain it was true. “The boy really did it.”

“Without us!” an irritated voice said from behind him.

Valanor spun to find Selina marching out of the forest, Cara beside her, shaking her head slightly. Valanor narrowed his eyes at the tall Nator woman. She seemed…different.

Not so much as the simiavus, but Valanor would have sworn to the subtle changes. Her features were softer, somehow, less angular, and her skin-tone was more of a deep shade of red than it had been before. And…was she glittering slightly, in the sunlight?

As she came closer, his eyes widened at noticing smooth, tiny scales running across her exposed flesh. It was them that sparkled, reflecting the afternoon sun.

“Yes, I have scales now,” Selina said, “and no, it is not polite to stare, First Shield.”

Valanor shook his head. “Forgive me, Runemistress. The change suits you,” he said hastily.

“Ethan’s work, I expect,” Selina said, tapping her lip thoughtfully. “The changes are too close to some that I studied in those of my people who’d been trapped on Potentia, to be a coincidence. I suspect this is the beginning of what I might someday become.”

“He took some license here,” Cara said, running a hand through her hair.

Valanor didn’t understand, but Selina quickly clarified.

“I miss the horns,” she said sadly, but her smile quickly returned as she moved to join Valanor by the cliff’s edge. “He… he really did do it.”

“So it would appear,” Valanor said, looking out at the strange mix of green, contrasted against the alien city.

“Without me,” Selina added. “To think what I could have learned!”

Cara sighed. “She’s been talking about it since we woke up,” she said. “She barely paused to check on the Bishops before storming off.”

“My own people are missing,” Selina added. “I have to trust that Ethan squirreled them away somewhere safe. He certainly took good care of his extended family–and good that he did.”

“They’re well, then?” Valanor asked.

“More than ‘well’,” Cara said. “They were convinced that a few of their most-recent casualties had actually returned to life.”

“But not all,” Selina said in a quiet voice.

“We must take what wins we can on a day like today,” a voice called out.

They all turned as Prince Calevaro strode out from the trees. He wore a dark suit of a style Valanor didn’t recognize, but he looked well. His trademark half-smirk brought a smile to Valanor’s own face, despite the pain of the day.

The prince would always be his charge, and knowing he’d survived the mad ordeal of Dusk’s power lifted a weight from Valanor that he’d been ignoring.

Selina ran over and hugged Calevaro, unsurprisingly, and Cara placed a gentle hand on his arm as he came to join them.

“So, this is our new world,” the prince said. “I wonder if it has a name.”

“Oh, let’s name it quickly before anyone else does!” Selina said. “How about ‘Potearthum’?”

“Terrible,” Cara said, and Selina pouted for nearly a full heartbeat before her smile reasserted itself.

“How about ‘Earthenpotux’?” someone said, and they turned just in time to see Dean slap his brother Richard in disapproval. The other one–Michael–followed short behind, shaking his shaved head slightly.

“I like it!” Selina declared, then ran over and hugged Richard. He spun the taller woman around in a circle, and the two laughed before joining the group.

“Your people are well,” Michael said when Selina’s feet were back on the ground. “I found them in another clearing in the opposite direction. They seem shocked by their own transformations, and I felt it was appropriate to give them space. I mentioned I’d direct you to them if I found you.”

Selina looked relieved. “I’ll go to them soon. I just want to…” She looked meaningfully at the line of trees, and everyone else followed suit.

“He’s coming, isn’t he?” Cara asked, and Valanor found himself surprised by the undisguised emotion of it. She’d confided in him once that Ethan had risked his life restoring her ability to walk, and it was easy to forget how meaningful an impact Ethan had had on the taciturn woman.

No one answered, no one spoke at all. They all just watched the treeline, hoping. After an eternity, each slowly looked away.

“He’s alive,” Richard said, voice almost pleading. “He’s too stubborn to let something like this stop him.”

“Something like taking the power of a god, and rebuilding the world?” Michael asked.

Dean turned a glare on his brother. But Michael shrugged.

“No, I agree,” he said, and Dean smiled before putting an arm on the shorter man’s shoulder.

And so they kept watching, letting the minutes slowly tick by. When at last the sound of snapping twigs echoed through the trees, and a figure stepped forward, Valanor found himself unsurprised.

It didn’t last.

Tomo stepped into the clearing, and he stepped in alone.

The demon–no–he couldn’t be called that anymore. He was transformed, and looked more like a Nator than ever before. Valanor had only seen the Twilight Familiar briefly back on Potentia, but even he could spot the changes immediately.

The man–monster? Valanor didn’t know what to call him, but he stood nearly nine feet tall, towering over all of them, and he was framed by a set of leathery wings that seemed not quite large enough for flight. Two arms were clasped behind the figure’s back, and two more pushed tree limbs out of his way as he emerged, revealing a face somewhere between that of a Nator, and a dragon.

No one seemed to know what to say as he came closer, and their eyes darted between Tomo and the forest, the hope obvious on each of their faces.

Tomo smiled, revealing somewhat pointed teeth.

“Ethan Bishop lives,” he said, and the relief was palpable.

After a moment of sighs and a few celebratory hugs, Richard asked the question burning in all of them.

“Well, where the hell is that drama queen?”

Tomo’s angular face twisted slightly.

“I…do not know. Our Bond remains, and yet it feels…different. Changed. I awoke on my own, an experience I had thought I had left behind. Revan and Deevee were nowhere to be found, and Ethan Bishop...”

Two sets of shoulders slumped. “He is out there. That is all I can say.”

Selina moved to him and took one of his hands, smiling up at the taller Nator, but no one else seemed to know what to say.

Finally, Calevaro stepped forward. “Our friend is missing,” he said simply. “We will find him.” His smile returned, then broadened. “And perhaps, we’ll even find some adventure along the way. There is a whole new world to explore.”

Valanor actually managed to bark a laugh. He found that he believed the prince. He was still pained from losing Savilar, but grateful that his brother had chosen a final, selfless act, and Ethan…Ethan needed him.

“Very well,” Michael said. “The Union is complete, and the Followers defeated, but there is work to be done.”

Selina nodded. “Billions of people have been displaced, and this world is no-doubt filled with surprises and challenges. Not to mention new gods possibly being born as we speak.”

“Then what are we standing around for?” Cara asked, drawing her bow.

Valanor looked at the group as they all gathered together around Tomo, and he felt their conviction and strength. Their utter faith in the man who'd brought them together. Finally, he stepped forward to join them, the familiar weight of his shield settling on his arm.

“Well then,” he said, “we’d better get started. This new world has need of a doctor.”
