Day 359, 12:45 AM

[We would be honored if you considered an in-person meeting with us to parlay and discuss the terms of our cooperation.]

The golden screen flickers before my eyes, but even before I dismiss it, the words dissolve on their own, replaced by new ones.

[We can offer you general guidance and help with the problem which you will face in one hundred and sixty years, since it obviously does not put us at odds with each other. Also, should you live a long enough life, which we believe you will, we offer a portion of the divine energy unleashed with this world’s destruction. A godling as young as yourself needs energy to grow and evolve their powers before they can accumulate faith.]

I am interested. I think at the Guide. But we will discuss this later. Call off the bugs for now.

The screen disappears, and with the distraction gone, I look at Edna, one train of thought focused on me, the other on her. I’m a wreck. My heart races and my hands shake. I’m an addict and my drug is right there before me.

As for Edna, there’s not a bit of resemblance, save for that gleam in her eyes and that smile. I dare not hope. But the words were the same, as was her determination. Could she really be my goddess?

But what would that mean? That I suspected her, that I was willing to sacrifice her, to experiment on her and turn her into a monster. My Manny…

“What’s wrong Aang?” she says, and my throat clenches.

“Griff! Griff!” Edna shakes me, and I realized my mind had fabricated the previous words. I changed the name from the one she used to the one I wished to hear.

Fuck you, universe! I choke my tears and look at her doing my best to beam her a winning smile, but no doubt send something wretched her way.

“I just did the same as last time.” She justifies herself, probably thinking I’m a mess because she killed me with fire.

I nod, not wanting to worry her. “Don’t mention it, you did well.”

What if Manny really is somewhere in there? Can I bring her out?

No. It would be torture of the worst kind for loops upon loops for the both of us. My wife is gone. And even if her soul is right here, in Edna, her memory is gone, leaving behind only her sacred essence. Essence I have no intention of defiling again.

“I love you,” I almost say, but clench my teeth before Blunt betrays me.

What I need to focus on right now are the outer gods working to devour this world. I need to fabricate a story they will buy. Obviously, being tempted with the energy and knowledge they offer is a given, and accepting their terms is a foregone conclusion from their point of view.

Not so much from mine. Especially if my wife lives in this world.

Focus!

“I know how we can beat them. I’ll explain the plan. The best part is the outer gods can even hear it, because there’s nothing they can do to stop me. Anyway, I have devised a spell, but it will require me to die…”

I will come to negotiate with you. We need to fake your disappearance and my death. That way those hunting me won’t be able to find me in the future. Do you know of a way to conceal me from the eldritch?

[There are ways to avoid distant tracking, and we are willing to share the method, but it is impossible to escape direct scrutiny.]

[We have ordered the drones out of your path. As for faking our disappearance, it would slow our plan, but it might be acceptable should we fail to find a better solution.]

They bought it. I am long-lived, but not immortal. How much will this change delay you? Because if it means I won’t live long enough to see the end and receive the energy to advance, I’m not interested in anything else you have to offer.

[That is not a problem. You will live long enough with a physique as high as yours.]

The swiftness of the response means they believe me. Or they are bluffing better than I am. I don’t doubt there’s a double-cross somewhere along the line, but that’s fine. They will probably think they bought themselves years or decades of peace, but I have to act quickly. They can amass a whole dungeon’s worth of elder abominations every two weeks, plus call over the monsters roaming the wilderness, drowning us in bodies.

I can’t even have a week of peace. Otherwise, they might overwhelm me and all my effort would be in vain.

I will lead them towards the dungeon, then you will need to create some disturbance in the tunnels, to force them to stay back and report my death. Can you collapse the dungeon? Or just the first floor.

[The first few floors are a negligible loss, but you would need to create a new way outside. Dungeon’s spawn cannot change it.]

All right, another interesting piece of information, but if things go according to plan, this is my last fortnight in Everrain. A track of my parallel thoughts engages with the outer gods, I asked their names. The garbled characters I got in return were unpronounceable, but thinkable, and I now know there are two of them.

As for the second track of my parallel thoughts, it’s focused on Gila a smidgeon, and the rest is checking out Edna, looking for similarities, but mostly inventing them. While I probably won’t have any need of mana, I still sink my free attribute points into physique. Being paranoid never hurts.

The talk I have with the two women is a preparation for my ultimate death. I guide Gila through the speech she’s going to make, and we all agree it would be best if Edna disappeared as a hero who fought against the invaders. The destruction of Holy City would certainly spark a thirst for revenge amongst the population, and while Edna could defend herself, there was no need to make her life more difficult.

I explain what I have found out about the archmage class, the wormlord class, and other interesting details of Hadriuse’s abode, including the hidden basement and the archmage’s original grimoires.

That should get them going in the right direction. I try to draw out as much information as I can from the outer gods, but they won’t share anything useful until we sign a pact, so I ask them how they came across Everrain.

[Someone opened the path ages before we found it. Parting the wail which limits mortal knowledge also parts the fences mortal worlds have against us. The only gods which are allowed to walk a world are those like you - gods and demigods born of the flesh of the world which they inhabit. The rest of us sail the void, searching for cracks we can exploit. Around half the worlds have high enough mana concentration, manifested in one way or another…]

Walls of text fill my mind, including all the harmless information the eldritch entities are willing to share. They have the locations of eight more archmage mansions, all the dungeons connected to the hub, and much more.

Do you mind if I tell them about the mansions? I can lie that I found them in my future travels.

The Guide gives me permission, and I explain the mansion locations to the girls.

What happened to wormlords?

[They and their opponents fed the rift with their essence. The magic which created the dungeons did not entomb the wormlords, it stripped them and the archmages of their magic and channeled it into the rift, solidifying it. The one exception was Hadriuse, we needed a tool, and he was the most suitable for our needs]

Well, that was slightly unexpected. I had thought it was possible something like that had happened, but getting a confirmation makes me more uneasy. Are they bringing me down to the in-person meeting so that they can do the same to me, reinforce the rift, turn me into a mortal, and kill me?

It’s a decent plan - kills two abominations with one stone, but it’s kind of obvious. Maybe they actually intend to keep their word? It’s a funny thing, but in all stories I know of otherworldly creatures, they tend to make pacts which bind the mortals and then they fulfill their wishes in the way the mortals stated them instead of the way the mortals intended them.

Days pass as I learn more about mortal magic, but little about anything which could help me with my other lives. The eldritch horrors have confirmed that classes are world-related, and can be influenced even by the culture and the times in which they appear.

That kills the idea of compiling a universal leveling guide with which I can advance in each new life. Fortunately, the classes are defined by the roles they are expected to take. Slaves are expected to work while suffering poor conditions, bodyguards are expected to protect certain people or organizations, soldiers to fight wars, and so on.

Meaning, even without an exact class progression blueprint I will be able to advance in the general direction which I choose.

Days pass, and we’re just outside the dungeon when a swarm of abominations attack.
