Viktor stared at the weathered door he had seen three times a day for the last week.

Usually, it was a simple routine. He knocked on the door, he handed over the food to whoever was inside, he asked a few questions, and then he left. The entire sequence never lasted more than a few minutes.

Yet today, standing outside this very same door, he found himself oddly rooted to the spot. And, for one fleeting moment, the thought of simply turning around and walking away had crossed his mind.

Coward, huh? His jaw tightened. Damn, he really hated that shiny woman.

He drew in a slow breath, forcing air into lungs that suddenly felt too tight, and straightened himself.

Then, he lifted his hand and knocked.

The sound echoed through the corridor, and then came silence. Seconds dragged past with unbearable slowness, stretching so long they felt more like entire hours than mere moments, until at last the door creaked open, and a familiar face appeared in the narrow gap.

He had met her here several times over the past week, though never stayed long enough to properly look at her. Now he did, and he saw she had changed. Her red hair was disheveled as though she had run her hands through it one too many times. Her once-porcelain skin looked worn and tired, her freckles nearly lost in its darker, dulled tone.

“Ah, Quinn. You brought her breakfast?”

Viktor opened his mouth, only to realize he hadn’t thought of what to say. After a pause, he asked, “Can I come in?”

Jeanne froze, staring at him with widened eyes as though she was unable to comprehend what she had just heard.

“Come in?” she repeated. “You want to come in? Of course you can. What kind of stupid question is that?”

She quickly stepped aside and pulled the door fully open, allowing him enough space to enter. So he did. He crossed the threshold.

The room beyond was the same as ever. Bare walls, bare floor, barely enough furniture.

And there she was.

The woman lying on the bed looked frighteningly fragile, skin pale as if carved from candle wax, stretching thin over sharp bones. Her eyes were closed, lashes dark against hollow cheeks. Viktor could barely recognize her.

An ache twisted in his chest.

It had only been one week. How could someone become this thin in only one week?

“Let’s get her to eat her breakfast,” he said. “How do you usually make her eat?”

Jeanne awkwardly scratched the back of her head. “Well, actually...” she said with a quiet voice. “She can’t eat any of the food you’ve been bringing. She can’t swallow by herself, you know?”

“What?” Viktor frowned. “Why didn’t you tell me?”

“Because... we were afraid that if we told you she didn’t need the food you made, you might stop coming here altogether.” She lowered her gaze to the floorboards. “I’m... sorry.”

Slowly, Viktor shook his head. “No, it’s my fault for not even noticing something so obvious.” He let out a sigh. “What does she eat, then?”

“Thin broths, goat’s milk, honey water, sometimes ale,” Jeanne said. “Tobias uses a tube made from pig gut to deliver them through her mouth. He always does it himself, every time. He said if he let someone untrained handle it, she could choke, or be injured, or get an infection. Rhea is learning how to do it, but you know, with her job at the Guild, she can only be here in the evenings or on weekends.”

“Is it enough? Can her body survive on just that?”

“It isn’t. So Tobias had to frequently cast a sustenance spell on her in addition to feeding her like that. Of course, that puts a strain on him, and he has other patients too. But don’t worry. There are also Lloyd and other Emerald Mages who occasionally visit. And Calyssa told me just yesterday that the caravan station would send two of their affiliated mages. They’ll remain stationed here, just for Claire, until she wakes up.”

Viktor let out another sigh, heavier this time. No wonder she looks like that.

How much longer could her body endure? If things continued like this, she might starve to death one day, or grow weak enough to succumb to whatever she was fighting against.

Galatea was right. Claire’s condition could deteriorate at any moment. If he hadn’t come today, he would never have realized what was really going on until it was far too late.

Celeste, he called his Dungeon Core.

[Yes, Master?]

Ask Galatea if the Light also provides sustenance. If it does, I want her here to cast her spell on Claire.

[Understood.]

That wouldn’t solve everything, but it could at least help stabilize her and ease some of the pressure being placed on Tobias.

Only then did he remember the wooden case he was still holding in his hands, so he offered it to Jeanne.

“Well, if she can’t eat this...”

“That’s already what we’ve been doing with the food you brought before.” She let out a short, tired chuckle as she took the case. “Sorry about that.”

“Don’t be. After everything you’ve done for her, this is the least I can do.”

“Have you eaten yet?”

“Yes, before I came here,” Viktor said. He hadn’t, actually, but he wasn’t in the mood now. Turning his gaze back to the bed, he asked, “When is Tobias coming to help her eat?”

“Oh, he already came early this morning. Served her some diluted soup. And he’s cast the sustenance spell as well. He won’t be back until noon.”

Viktor nodded. “Can you... leave me here alone with her for a while?”

Galatea might be coming, and while she could stay invisible under normal circumstances, he doubted that would remain the case when she was using her magic.

“Sure,” Jeanne said. “If you need anything, just call me.”

After she left the room, Viktor dragged a chair up to the bed and sat down beside Claire. For a long while, he simply looked at the woman lying motionless under the thin blanket, the woman who was his—

Even now, he still didn’t know what he was supposed to call her. Calling her his sister still felt wrong somehow, while saying she was not didn’t feel right either.

He leaned back in the chair, rubbing at his eyes.

Damn, he was tired.

Yesterday had been a long, exhausting day. The duel with Sebekton had taken a physical toll on him, and the conversation with that shiny woman had taken a mental one. And, after that, he had spent the whole night staring at the ceiling, wondering what he should do next.

Now, after finally forcing himself to come here and confront what he had been avoiding, all the accumulated fatigue had finally caught up with him.

Heavy.

His eyelids began to droop.

He sat down on the floor, folded his arms on the edge of the bed, and lowered his head onto them. He closed his eyes.

Just for a little...

Galatea would wake him up when she arrived...

...

The next thing he felt was a hand resting lightly on his shoulder.

Is it Jeanne? He stirred, his mind disoriented as he blinked himself awake. He lifted his head, and he saw... Rhea?

“So you finally came, huh?” the girl said, smiling.

“Shouldn’t you be at the Guild?”

“I was,” she said with a shrug. “I just got back.”

“What?”

“It’s evening now.”

“What!?”

Only now did he realize the dryness in his throat and the sharp emptiness twisting in his stomach. Well, yes. He had not eaten a single thing the entire day.

His gaze shifted toward Jeanne, who was standing by the door.

“Why didn’t you wake me up?” he asked, frowning.

“I did try when it was lunchtime. But you were sleeping too soundly, and I couldn’t exactly make too much noise with Claire here. Then, I thought maybe you were just really tired, so I decided to let you rest.”

“At the very least you could’ve carried me to another bed instead of leaving me to sleep in this posture the whole day.”

Jeanne grinned. “I’m not so heartless as to separate you from your beloved sister, not when it’s been so long since you were together.”

Ugh.

Rhea, on the other hand, now looked at him with a serious expression.

“Quinn, have you been getting proper sleep this past week?”

“I have,” Viktor said, waving a dismissive hand. “I slept every night without any problems whatsoever.”

Though he had to admit none of those nights had felt remotely like the sleep he had just experienced. He genuinely remembered nothing. No dreams, no thoughts, no sense of time passing. One moment he had closed his eyes beside Claire’s bed, and the next the entire day was gone.

Wait.

“Has Tobias come here to help Claire with her meal?”

“He has. Twice.” Jeanne chuckled. “And you slept through them both.”

What the...!?

“Anyway,” Rhea said. “Dinner is ready. Let’s eat together. You must be very hungry now, right?”

After a brief pause, Viktor said, “Yes, but could you two leave me here for a couple of minutes? I’ll come out shortly.”

Rhea and Jeanne exchanged a glance, then they nodded. They left the room together, the door closing behind them with a soft click.

“Celeste.”

[Master, I am sorry. I too found you sleeping so soundly, so I didn’t want to interrupt.]

“It’s fine,” Viktor said. “How about Galatea? What was her answer? Did she come here?”

[She said her healing spells... do not include a sustenance aspect. As she has told you before, the Light is primarily focused on repairing specific forms of damage in a localized manner, rather than restoring a weakened body as a whole. However, she added that if Claire’s condition ever deteriorates to the point where she is... on the verge of death, the Light could be used to temporarily prevent her from dying, prolonging her life until a more complete solution is found.]

Viktor sighed. That certainly didn’t sound pleasant, but if things reached their worst possible outcome, then at least what Galatea offered could be relied upon when all other options had failed.

[I am sorry, Master. For not being of use.]

“Don’t be. It’s not your fault.”

Now that Galatea’s spells had been ruled out, he needed to explore other options. If the shiny woman couldn’t help, then who could? How about... Mandragora?

Yes.

The Druidess might have some concoctions that could help Claire. And since they were liquid, getting her to drink them shouldn’t be an issue.

Besides—

“Celeste,” he said. “I think I’m in my best shape now.”

To be fair, at the moment, he was very hungry and very thirsty. But such discomfort felt trivial compared to the strange clarity that had settled over his thoughts.

It was strange, indeed.

Nothing about Claire’s condition had objectively improved. In fact, everything he had just learned suggested her situation might be far more perilous than he had believed before.

Yet, despite all that, he felt better than he had at any point in the past week.

[What do you mean, Master?]

“It means I’m ready to deal with that hidden lair of those masked men. We can now begin preparations for the attack.”

[But...]

“Don’t worry. I am not once again focusing on that task just to look away from what is happening to Claire. No, there’s no reason to neglect one for the other. I’ll find a way to help her, and I’ll avenge her. I am the Emperor; I do not choose. I am going to do both at the same time.”

[Understood. Still, how will you proceed? Are you going to enter that Folded Chamber alone?]

“No, not alone,” Viktor said with a chuckle. Yes, his mind was now indeed clearer than it had ever been. “I’ve just come up with a way to bring in more bodies.”
