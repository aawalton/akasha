The chamber was quiet. Whatever pressure Ash's Essence had left in the air was gone now. The crystal walls still held their light in shifting patterns, though the patterns moved slower now. Ash sat against the near wall. Her breathing came slow and long, and some of the color had left her face. Perhaps I should not have asked her to flare her Essence so soon after what she had done to get it.

"We should leave," Ash said. "I have my core. We can come back tomorrow, when you're-"

"No."

Ash looked at me. "Are you sure?"

"I have not formed mine. I will not leave until I have," I said.

Ash held my gaze for a moment. Then she nodded and leaned back against the crystal wall. "Okay. I guess then we'll-"

"You may leave," I said. "This may take some time."

"I won't leave without you, Lys," Ash said, and her voice was fierce. It was a fierceness that told me arguing against it would be pointless.

I closed my eyes and drew in more mana. The chamber's supply of it was still reasonably thick despite what we had both taken from it. More than full enough to form the two Essences I would require. I tried again. And as always, Requiem's carving tore through the Cradle's, even as the Cradle's tore through the Requiem's. The pain made me hiss.

On my next attempt, I tried different ratios of the two powers. Far more of the Requiem, with barely a thin thread of the Cradle. It held. It held for five seconds before everything came apart again inside me. This time I coughed, even as something warm touched my lips. I wiped it away without looking. It could not have been blood. A Queen did not bleed. I had done this test before too, but I simply did not know what else to try.

"Stop doing that to yourself."

I opened my eyes. Ash was watching me. She had not moved from her place against the wall. "What?" I said.

"You're hurting yourself. What you're doing is painful, isn't it? Stop doing that, Lys."

Her objection made no sense to me. "Then how else am I supposed to experiment?"

Ash stared at me for a long moment. The look on her face was one I could not parse, but it was not a hard look. "Just do it outside," she said.

"Outside?"

"Don't test the powers inside your body. You should just fire them at each other out here, where they can't tear you apart. At least, that's how I'd do it," Ash said. "It makes a lot more sense than what you're doing."

I stared at her for some time. Her suggestion was an obvious one. It was so obvious I should have thought of it myself. That was almost enough reason to dismiss it on principle. A Queen's methods should not be improved by her retainer's common sense. And yet perhaps... "That is perhaps not a terrible idea," I said.

I rose and raised both my hands. I let Essence build in both marks. A minute later, I fired black and white flames into each other. They met in the space between my palms, and the world warped around them. The crystal walls threw back reflections that split my silhouette into black and white copies across the stone. They coiled, devoured, and were devoured in turn until there was nothing left for them save for the faintest of heat against my skin.

How interesting. I did it again. I used more Ruin and less Bloom. The black consumed the white in an instant, then guttered out. On my next attempt, I used more Bloom and less Ruin. The white swallowed the black. Equal measures of both, naturally, lasted the longest. However long it lasted, it was still utterly pointless.

Ash stepped in close to me. I had not heard her approach. She watched the point where the two flames met with an intensity that surprised me. The light played across her face, carving one half of it white and the other black. "They look beautiful," she murmured.

"Of course they do. They are my power."

We watched in silence for a time. Eventually, Ash spoke again. "Have you given it any thought?" Ash asked. Her voice was low. "To what the man said. About using only one mark, I mean."

"That is out of the question."

"Why?"

I looked at her. Slowly, I asked, "Are you really asking me that?"

Ash smiled. It was faint and brief. "Suppose not."

There was silence then, one filled only by my two powers burning away at each other. I did not know why I spoke, but I did. "I need power," I said. I fired Ruin and Bloom at each other again. I watched them die. "You have seen this new world with me. Seen all the strange things within it. Only a fool would not reach for power when it is so readily available to them." I paused. "And perhaps reaching for power is simply who I am." I gathered more Essence. "I will not halve myself, as that small man suggested. I will not tie one of my own arms behind my back."

There was more to say. Something about the Cradle, and wanting to be- The words were stuck in my throat. I fired more of Ruin and Bloom into each other. "Power," I said again. "I need all of it. I will have all of it." Ash nodded slowly. Her gaze was strange. As if she had heard the words I had never said. My gaze moved to the far wall of the chamber, where I did not see it.

An hour passed, and nothing changed at all. Or perhaps I only thought an hour had passed. It was hard to tell in this room. My experimentation had led to only destruction, one that left nothing behind at all. Nothing but the faintest of heat. What else had I expected, from me?

Ash was fading. She had been fighting her exhaustion for some time, and she had been losing for some time. She sat straighter when her posture slipped, widened her eyes when they drooped. I had been watching her do this instead of working. She was distracting me. "You may leave," I said.

"No." Even that singular word came out slow.

Why were Heroes like this? "Then you may rest here."

Ash looked at me and her jaw set. "I'm fine."

She was not fine. She was pretending otherwise because she was too stubborn to do the sensible thing. I returned to my work. More time passed. Perhaps half an hour. Perhaps it only seemed that way.

When I turned back, Ash was asleep. Her head had tilted against the crystal wall and her breathing was even. I looked at her. The chamber was quiet save for the faint sound of her breathing. She was truly a stupid Hero. The Inker had spoken at length about how painful this process was. There was no way she wasn't exhausted. She was a stupid, stubborn Hero.

I moved to her and adjusted her position carefully against the wall, so she would not wake with a sore neck. I moved her until she was lying more than sitting, one arm tucked beneath her.

My hand lingered near her. Near her throat. The skin there was unblemished and faultless. The bruises were all gone. I had healed them myself. But I remembered the silver hand. I remembered Ash's feet leaving the ground. I remembered the sound she had made. My fingers almost touched her throat. Almost. "I did not want to see you like that again," I whispered. I pulled back.

I fired Ruin and Bloom at each other one more time. I was watching them carefully now. Peering at the point where the two met and destroyed each other.

There. I saw a flicker, one so brief I had missed it every time, because the destruction that followed was so striking that the eye went to it instead. But there, in the fraction of a heartbeat before the two flames consumed each other, there was something else. A third thing. One that was neither beginning nor ending.

I fired the two again even as I watched harder. It really was there. This was no trick of the light. Where the two powers met they became something else first. Just for an instant. Just at the boundary where they first met. I fired a third time, and this time I did not watch the flames at all.

I watched the point where Ruin met Bloom. What I was looking for lasted for no longer than the time it took to blink. There was a third thing, one that belonged to neither mark. It was real. It truly was no trick of the light.

I sighed and looked towards the egg in the satchel that rested beside Ash. The egg was gold and black. It was not gold despite black. It was not black despite gold. It was both.

Perhaps there was an answer here. An insane answer, built on what was no more than a guess, or perhaps a hope.  I had to force these two Essences to meet inside my core. I had to let them destroy each other, over and over again. If I did that for long enough, then the third thing might emerge and carve its own structure. I hesitated.

I hesitated because there were far easier paths. I could have cut my core in two -with the Requiem itself, or asked Ash to use Sever- and healed the halves into separate cores with the Cradle. That was certainly dangerous, but it was possible. Then, there was the safe path. I could have chosen one mark and built a conventional core. Either of these would have been wise.

And all I would have to do is admit that the Cradle and the Requiem could not exist together. I could not. Something inside of me would not. I took a breath and steadied myself. I would make both of these marks work. This was a mad gambit, but I had attempted those before.

I refilled my mana core, and then I drew in both Cradle and Requiem Essence. They met inside my core, as they always did. Each destroyed what the other built. Every time before, I had stopped here. This time I did not stop. This time I held the two powers together with all the willpower I could muster. The annihilation came. Then again. Then again.

My vision blurred and the entire chamber swayed. One of the walls cracked and hissed, and still I did not stop.

Eventually, something about that sharp stabbing pain changed. It became a…grinding thing inside my mana core. It felt like something alive crawling through me. No. A thousand somethings, each one tearing down what the last one built.

The chamber went dark. It flashed white. It went dark. I almost stopped. My hands were shaking. Every instinct told me this was destroying me from the inside. I did not stop.

I could barely see the chamber. Instead, I saw faces. I saw a child with nubs for horns, pressing himself against a wall as I passed. A little girl older than Sara, buried in a shallow grave. A grave that would not have been dug at all, had I not demanded it. They were all my kind. My blood, or what was left of it. I had clawed across a thousand years to break my own chains. I had thought only of myself, and they had paid for it. They were still paying.

Finally, I saw Ash, and the silver hand around her throat that had effortlessly lifted her into the air. The sound she had made. I had only been a few paces from her. It might as well have been miles. The collisions happening inside my core became less controlled. I was losing the rhythm. What little rhythm I had. Then I felt it. Something as familiar as it was ancient.

A gaze settled over me. It filled this entire space. It was broken and faded, no more than a mere fraction of a fraction of what it once was. But I knew it the way I knew my own name. It was the System, or what remained of it. It was watching.

"S-so you've come to see me…do the impossible again," I whispered.

There was blood on my chin. "You bastard," I hissed. "How dare you come and watch." The System did not answer. It never had, and it never would. The pain was almost blinding. I ignored it. I ignored it and forced the two powers to meet again, one last time. Ending tore through beginning. Beginning tore through ending, and in the instant before there was only destruction-

The third thing began to carve. Compared to the grinding pain, this carving was a small thing, but I felt it all the same. And this carving was happening slowly, so slowly. What this would make of my core was something that neither I, nor anyone else, would have a name for.

My face hit the ground, and I did not have the strength to rise. Ash was breathing. I could hear her, distantly. The egg was surely pulsing still in its satchel. Both were very far away. My eyes closed, and I forced them open. I remembered the Inker's words well, even now. If I passed out before this was done, then that would be it. There would be no second attempt.

Eventually, even the pain of the carving was gone. I was left only with the hum. A second heartbeat, almost. I smirked at that ancient gaze that was still watching. Then there was nothing.
