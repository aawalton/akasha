With that charming conversation out of the way, Henry turned his attention to more pleasant pursuits, and it was a mere coincidence that one of them happened to be knocking a particularly aggressive and persistent baboon-like monster out of the sky. It had been hounding the squad heading toward the orb located in the Reach, throwing up fireballs that were certainly about to create a wildfire as it somehow managed to keep pace with Henry’s Avatars. It had refused to back off when Henry told it to, opting to propel itself up from the tree-line toward Henry’s Avatars.

So Henry summoned three Lifeblood Avatars through that squad and had the former continue their trip while the three faced the baboon.

[Fury of The Scorched Wilds (A) - Lvl 21]

One moment, the simian was exploding upwards like a rocket—which it was managing by creating some sort of controlled explosion below its feet—and the next, it was crashing back down to the ground as one of the Lifeblood Avatars slapped it down with all of its might.

Henry had to admit, that felt quite good. Especially when he had superimposed the Depth Lord’s face on the baboon’s snarling mug. The creature was not very large, when compared to everything he’d seen. But it was still nearly three meters tall. Its eyes and red fur looked as if they were made out of angry flames, and just by standing on a branch, everything in its vicinity would ignite. As if the detonating fireballs it kept lobbing at his Avatars weren’t enough of a fire hazard.

Taking a second to assess the situation, Henry put forward an Octomind to handle this fight while he observed. “Use one Avatar at a time, and use only Telekinesis or Lightning Discharge,” he ordered it. Those were the two skills he had to upgrade as soon as possible, so he’d better start working on it. Throughout the Reach, his spreading network of Avatars filtered through the encounters, putting aside the peaceful and fearful A-ranks and underlining the aggressive kind for further… training. Depending on their nature or attitude, he might let them go, unless they were a danger to anyone they encountered, just like this baboon, and in that case, well, Henry still needed cores. After having spent time in space, fighting the local monsters didn’t feel as rewarding as the bugs. But cores were cores, and he needed to be at his best for the eventual return to outer-space.

“Oh… and make sure to get a bite of this one. We couldn’t ask for a better monster to pick up a fire skill,” he added after a beat before Henry pulled his attention back to Velistraine and Maurice.

While Henry had been talking with the Merfolk, the two had somehow managed to start a small campfire on which Velistraine was grilling some poor lizard’s tail. Henry had been floating along with them, so he could recall the circumstance well enough. It had been a B-rank monitor lizard which had dropped its tail and run for it when Velistraine had invaded its warehouse-sized burrow.

Seeing the fire and turning his gaze toward the baboon pelting his clones with fireballs, it was a wonder the Reach’s forests were still standing. Then again, it did seem like the fire was having some trouble catching on the massive, magic-imbued trees of the area. Not to mention, a mundane wildfire could be a danger for weak creatures, but Henry would bet many of the B-ranks and A-ranks out here wouldn’t put up with a wildfire. With skills, it couldn’t be too hard to suppress it fast.

“The fire’s hot, so you gotta turn every so often so it doesn’t burn,” Velistraine leaned over, mouth practically watering at the skewer of juicy, still bloodied meat, which she had cut up with a small hunting knife. “You could also move it away from the fire, but then the cooking will be a bit more thorough. And it’d take longer. I like my steak a little bloody, so this distance right here is fine.”

Maurice nodded along standing next to her over the fire. There had been no easy way to start a fire back in space. In space itself, it was, well, impossible. And on the mystical road, it was too visible and would have given them away. As for the SS Henry, that would have just been unpleasant, not to mention, a bad idea. Henry had no way of filtering smoke out, so it was a nonstarter. But out here, there was wood. There was fresh meat to roast. And the two were making the most of it.

Henry had to admit, it was a bit of an amusing scene. Funnily enough, the crab had kept his diminutive size, though his smoothed, polished shell still reached Velistraine’s hip. Noticing that Henry was fully back with them, Maurice looked up. “Henry! You got any salt? Spices? I ran out of mine.”

Henry… did have some spices, which he’d packed back when they were on the Emerald Isles. But he knew for a fact that Maurice had packed as much with him. And they hadn’t been cooking much since then.

… had the crab been eating the spices raw?

For a moment, Henry considered asking, but then he thought better of it, and just summoned the bundle of paper-wrapped jars, carefully labeled with their content, and as he watched the two pull the meat back from the fire and slather the stuff on generously, burning their hands and wincing as they did, Henry wondered whether to tell them now that his flying Avatar had reconnected Thalis, or wait for them to gulp down their meal. On one hand, these two were very excited to eat their half charred, half bloody piece of lizard. On the other hand, Thalis was bound to drag them into yet another celebration once news of Velistraine’s survival reached them. And Henry was working toward that. The Avatars he had left in Thalis were connected and already moving, which, unsurprisingly, had been already detected.

“Just a sec,” Henry trailed off as he left the chaotic duo and turned his attention back to the Avatar that broke the water surface. This one had been hidden in a hollowed section of one of the many trees that supported Zerathstra’s domain, dormant, and now, as an A-rank himself, the protector of the area had taken note.

Like a flitting shadow, Zerathstra fell on him so fast Henry had barely managed to raise his arms, and with Henry’s cognition, in that fraction of a second, he noticed many things. First, it was relief. It seemed that for some reason, the ape had been worried about them. Second, and more importantly, it was the blindingly fast club that was about to smack into Henry. This wasn’t the first time the kraken had seen this particularly violent greeting, but as soon as he recognized it, Henry put all his back into defending.

Velistraine had been sent down like a tossed rock the last time she’d been on the receiving end of this move.

Good thing this Avatar’s a Lifeblood one, Henry thought, bracing as Trickster’s Fortitude roared into life, fed by a thimble of Unfettered Echoes and a nice dollop of Lifeweaving. But tanking the hit wasn’t enough, so Henry channeled a tiny bit of Unfettered Echoes to the Flight skill keeping him up, and suddenly, it was as if all the air around him had tightened its embrace around him, not restraining him in any way whatsoever, but strangely giving him a strong feeling of being grounded.

Then Zerathstra’s club hit Henry’s two folded, guarding arms.

It was, simply put, like a giant had picked one of the massive trees around them and had, after a slow, ponderous windup, batted him away with enough force to shatter the damn log. Henry’s arms were immediately shoved into his face and eyes, and it was painful as all hell. But Henry wasn’t launched back into the water. He was sent recoiling, enough that one of his arms hit water, but Trickster’s Fortitude had blunted the impact a little with the cushioning magic and by reinforcing his flesh, while Flight allowed him to keep some control over his trajectory. The skill-nexus groaned in his soul from the impact, but it didn’t break or dispel. It was merely overloaded, which Henry was more than capable of handling.

Looking up, Henry found the ape grinning, his club resting on his shoulder, and the kraken chuckled in turn.

“Look who’s back. It’s been what, a month or two? No little message to your elder, eh, little Henry? No visits?”

“Well, good morning to you too, Zerathstra,” Henry shot back, lifting himself up in the air while he activated Life Surge to heal his numb, but somehow still painful arms. He was pretty sure a decent amount of their muscles had been utterly pulped. “How’s old age treating you?”

Zerathstra shrugged. “Oh you know. Tutoring junior, watching the territories. It’s a good, comfortable routine. How about you, any news on your end?”

The ape was pleasant, as always, but Henry caught a whiff of… hope? Worry? It felt a bit like a mix of both, which, for some reason, made Henry feel a little nervous. It was Henry who’d given them hope, the last time, and right now, he had much better to show for it than a few theories. For a beat, Henry considered how best to approach the situation and the reveal, but then, why should he?

It took him half a second to get into a position from the other side of the sea, then he activated his skill. It was surprisingly cheap, all things considered, and one moment, his Avatar had been standing in front of the curious ape, and the next, his real body was here, with a frozen Velistraine, eyes wide, mid-bite, and a still munching Maurice. The silence was only punctuated by the crab’s chewing for a second before the ape blurred and caught the captain in a big hug.
