Crimson-gold energy sank deeper into the ritual’s heart as Cade pressed his palms against the ancient stone. The magic fought him like a living thing, emerald power flaring against his intrusion with venomous intensity. Each altered line sent shockwaves of resistance up his arms, but he pushed onward, teeth gritted against the pain.

“Work, you stupid. little. thing.” Cade punctuated each word with a flare of his newfound magic.

Yes, he was more powerful. Yes, he felt like a brand new person. It was almost as if the balance between flesh and magic in his body had finally shifted in favor of the latter, and he practically thrummed with energy.

Did that help him graffiti an ancient ritual designed to drain a goddess?

No. No, it did not.

As was the case when he ranked up to silver, his senses had taken another huge leap forward in their power and nuance. Everything was louder, clearer, and more granular. If not for the life-or-death stakes of his present situation, the cacophony all around would’ve easily induced the worst migraine of his life.

Granted, it was causing the worst migraine of his life, but he just didn’t have time to deal with that via a warm bath in a dark room.

He had work to do.

His enhanced eyes narrowed on the intricate scrollwork aglow with vermillion energy that did not like his tampering. The ritual’s structure was impossibly complex—layers upon layers of magical geometry flowing into each other like a three-dimensional puzzle. His gold-rank perception revealed pathways previously invisible, connections and vulnerabilities hidden within the ancient craftsmanship.

But with those revelations came the sinking realization that if his eyes could see these, then whatever or whoever wrote these runes had even greater vision. Because just beneath what he perceived as vulnerabilities, he could barely sense some sort of failsafe.

If Evil Bernard wrote all of these, then he was good.

Sure, he’d identified the weak points in the perimeter and worked on them accordingly, but the seventh node—the master control—remained untouched. Crawling on hands and knees, he approached the final anchor point, avoiding the debris raining from the crumbling cathedral ceiling.

When he reached it, he froze. Unlike the others, this node was a spiral of interlocking runes that seemed to shift and change even as he watched—a nightmare of emerald sigils pulsating with ancient malice.

“Well, shit,” he whispered, studying the complex geometry.

This wasn’t like picking a simple lock. It was like trying to crack a vault with an ever-changing combination while the building burned around him.

His fingers traced the outer edge of the node, testing its resistance. Emerald lightning arced from the sigil, slamming into his chest and sending him tumbling backward. Pain lanced through his body, temporarily paralyzing his muscles and magical pathways at once.

“Noted. Don’t piss off the angry letters,” Cade groaned.

Across the cathedral, his crew fought desperately against Bernard’s corrupted minions. Nora’s origami soldiers clashed with skeletal horrors while Elena’s whip-dagger sang through the air. Bunny darted between Bernard’s legs, providing a crucial distraction with his nimble movements and sharp teeth.

In the center of it all, Chubbs’ small form arched in silent agony, golden essence flowing from her into the widening fissure where something massive stirred in the darkness.

Time was running out.

Cade crawled back to the node, blood dripping from his nose where the magical backlash had struck him. Direct manipulation had failed, but perhaps there was another way. He placed his left hand at the edge of the emerald sigil and began tracing a new pattern with his right—not attempting to overwrite, but to supplement.

The sigil resisted, emerald energy flaring against the intrusion, but Cade persisted. Pain bloomed across his chest as the ritual pulled at his own magical reserves, the drain intensifying as he worked closer to its heart.

“Almost...” he gasped, adding the final stroke.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened. Then the emerald sigil pulsed once, twice—and accepted the new pattern, crimson threads merging with emerald lines in a reluctant harmony. The entire ritual circle shuddered as his changes propagated outward from the central node.

“Wait,” Cade said futilely.

That wasn’t right.

His eyes retraced the work he’d done. It was perfect. But the effect should’ve been immediate. What had he missed?

An origami soldier appeared above him, sword raised. It’s crimson eyes gleamed with chaotic magic that he could now sense as an echo of his own. It slashed down. Cade lifted his hands above his head, but he knew it’d be a wasted movement. He’d seen the animated paper slice cleanly through stone.

A rotting limb shot out from beneath the young thief, an unholy cry screeching right next to his ear. The sword separated the clambering arm from its socket, and the creature below Cade bellowed in agony.

The soldier stood above him, and he could’ve sworn it gave him a tiny nod before it rushed to paper-cut another undead monster.

Fantastic.

Cade scrambled to his feet but did his best to remain in a low crouch. He followed his work back, finding no errors.

Cursing, he went back to the edge of the central seal. This was taking too long.

From the fissure, shadowy tendrils began to spill out of the rip in reality, and a figure emerged. Just glancing at the humanoid silhouette filled Cade with an unnatural dread. It raised a hand toward Nora, who had her fear magic tethering this unwelcome guest to her location.

Dark lances shot toward the paladin and the two of them were consumed in a sphere of impenetrable night. The battle flowed around the new barrier. Not even the eldritch horrors pouring out of the ground wanted to breach that nightmarefest.

“Get out of there, Nor. You can do it,” Cade breathed.

His foot collided with a jagged piece of debris, and he cursed again. The stone shifted slightly.

“Oh, you’ve got to be kidding me!” Cade hissed and clenched his fists.

While this piece of column wasn’t the culprit, another, larger, piece of stone covered a section of the seal. Crawling over to it, he saw what he’d missed. With as much finesse as he could muster, he started to blow wind beneath the uneven rock while he shoved with his shoulder. The boulder moved easier than he’d anticipated, but whether it was due to his body’s increased resilience or his magic, he couldn’t say.

There, in the dust, was the final connector. Bernard’s chanting had reached a fever pitch, but it halted sharply as Cade crouched by the green runes. The Son of Ruin looked up slowly, silently praying that the ritualist was distracted by some other big rock or imposing thief.

Six eyes circumferencing the crown swiveled toward him.

Cade slid behind a fallen column just as Bernard turned, and his voice reverberated through the cathedral walls.

“I sense you, thief,” Bernard called, emerald light spilling from his eyes and mouth. “Scurrying in shadows like the vermin you are.”

Cade pressed his back against the cold stone, mind racing. The alteration was ninety percent complete—but that final connection had to be made before the ritual reached its crescendo. He needed a distraction.

“Hey, Bunny!” he stage-whispered, catching the dragonling’s attention. “Eat the mustache!”

Bunny’s eyes glimmered with mischief. The white dragonling launched himself at Bernard’s face with renewed vigor, claws extended and tail whipping wildly. Bernard roared in frustration, swatting at the nimble creature as it darted around his head, pulling at the tendrils connected to the crown.

In the momentary confusion, Cade lunged from cover, hands extended toward the unfinished node. Crimson-gold energy erupted from his palms, searing the final connection into the ancient stone. The ritual circle flared with a blinding light as his changes locked into place. Emerald light flickered as the flow struggled to reverse itself.

Cade dove behind another column, chest pounding as he waited for the alteration to take effect.

Nothing happened.

Bernard’s voice rose in triumph as the ritual neared completion, the fissure widening as massive roots began to emerge from the darkness.

“At long last, my brothers and sisters, the time of reckoning has come!” he proclaimed, arms raised in exultation. “Creation will fuel its glory, and the gods will kneel before our power!”

Bunny landed beside Cade, scales slightly singed but he was otherwise unharmed.

Across the cathedral, Bernard suddenly froze, the crown’s eyes swiveling in unison. “Something is wrong,” he hissed, scanning the ritual circle with newfound suspicion. “What have you done?”

Cade grinned despite the danger. The creature sensed something amiss, but couldn’t pinpoint the exact nature of his tampering. He could use that uncertainty while he searched for what was taking his alterations so long.

“What’s wrong, Bernie?” he called, stepping into view with deliberate casualness. “Ritual not going according to plan? Maybe you should double-check your calculations.”

Bernard’s corrupted face twisted in fury. “You think to distract me with your foolish bravado? I’ve conducted rituals that razed capitals to the ground while your ancestors were still learning to make fire.”

“And yet here we are,” Cade replied, slowly circling to position himself closer to the unfinished rune. “Me messing with your ritual, you throwing a cosmic tantrum. It doesn’t really inspire confidence in your abilities… lich?” Cade hedged.

The crown’s tendrils pulsed with emerald light as Bernard stalked toward him, rage temporarily overriding caution. “I see you understand nothing if you think me as low as one of Death’s progeny. And what you’ve done to my seal is meaningless. This ritual was designed to withstand the interference of gods. What chance does a mortal thief have?”

“Good question,” Cade admitted, stepping backward as if retreating. “Though I’ve heard that overconfidence is a pretty common downfall in your line of work.”

Bernard laughed—a hollow, ancient sound that was devoid of any humor. “Overconfidence? No. Experience. I have torn worlds asunder, thief. I have watched civilizations rise and fall. I have survived the betrayal of my own creators.” His voice dropped to a venomous whisper. “What have you done but steal trinkets and hide in shadows? What could you possibly hope to do to me?”

“Fair points,” Cade conceded. “But you know what they say about old dogs and new tricks.”

He took another backward step bringing him within arm’s reach of an unfinished rune. He let out an internal sigh of relief at spotting his error. Now, he just had to fix it before he got skewered by whatever foul magic Evil Bernard possessed.

Before Bernard could respond, Cade let his heel catch on a piece of debris. He dropped to one knee as if stumbling, his hand brushing against the stone floor. Crimson-gold energy flashed briefly as he completed the missing rune, then disappeared as he rolled to his feet.

“Or in your case,” he continued smoothly, “old gods and new thieves.”

Bernard’s eyes narrowed, suspicion returning as he studied Cade’s casual posture. “You’re playing for time,” he decided, emerald energy gathering around his hands. “A futile effort. The ritual cannot be stopped.”

The floor beneath them trembled as the fissure widened further, massive roots emerging like serpents from the darkness. Chubbs whimpered, her small frame shuddering as more golden essence flowed from her despite Cade’s partial alterations.

“Maybe not stopped,” Cade admitted, eyes flicking to the completed rune. “But I’m not trying to stop it.”

The cathedral shuddered violently as his changes finally took effect. The ritual circle flared with intersecting lines of red and green. The emerald light flickered, dimmed, and then exploded outward in a wave of transformed energy.

Cade gasped as he felt his core begin to drain into the ground, red and gold tendrils wisping off of his skin and into the ritual.

He’d done it. He’d actually done it.

“What have you done?!” the creature in the crown roared.

“Can’t you feel it?” Cade asked with a smug look despite the exhaustion sweeping over his body.

Gods, I need a nap.

But the Son of Ruin already knew the answer to his own question. He could see long trails of green energy sweeping out of Bernard and into the ground.

The flow was reversed. He’d made it so that the power for the seal no longer drew from Chubbs, but instead pulled from the cathedral’s perimeter inward.

Bernard staggered, momentarily disoriented as his own power became the ritual’s fuel. He looked down at his hands, watching as the emerald light fed the very circle he’d created.

For a moment, confusion reigned on his corrupted features. Then his gaze tracked the new flow of energy, following it to the protective bubble now surrounding Chubbs. Understanding dawned, followed by cold fury.

“Clever,” he acknowledged, voice dangerously soft. “You’ve reversed the flow. You even managed to create a barrier around the girl.”

That last part had been tricky, but it turned out to be the most important step he’d taken.

Cade shrugged, affecting nonchalance despite the exhaustion creeping through his limbs. “Just a teensy alteration. I didn’t think you’d mind.”

Bernard’s laugh held no mirth. “Mind? Why would I mind?” His eyes gleamed with predatory amusement. “The ritual continues, thief. Slower, yes. Less efficient, certainly. But Creation’s essence still feeds the seed of the Throne.”

Cade’s triumph faltered as he realized Bernard was right. Despite his modifications, golden essence still seeped from Chubbs—reduced to a trickle, but not halted entirely. And now everyone in the cathedral, including his crew, was feeling the effects of being the ritual’s new power source.

“I see you understand,” Bernard continued, circling Cade with measured steps. “Your ‘solution’ merely changes who suffers. A fitting moral quandary for one who fancies himself a hero.”

“I’m no hero,” Cade replied, gathering what remained of his strength. “I’m just a thief who really doesn’t take kindly to you murdering my surrogate sister, is all.”

“And what about my family?” Bernard’s voice dropped to a dangerous whisper. “What I’m doing is setting the cosmic scales back into balance, you shortsighted dullard. This is about justice. Vengeance. Through this Throne, I will restore what was ripped from us.”

“Us?” Cade prompted, buying time as he assessed the situation. “You and the crown having a lot of heart-to-hearts these days? It seems a little clingy, but who am I to judge relationship dynamics? My last romance nearly started a religious war over a particularly robust goose.”

Bernard’s eyes flashed with emerald fire, but spoke with a facsimile of the crime lord’s old buttery voice. “Mock if you wish. But we are more now than either of us could’ve been apart. He has given me such insights into the mind, Cade. He predates humanity and other mortalkinds by centuries. He’s not just a Progenitor. He’s the firstborn child of Life.”

Cade reeled from that little revelation.

The Clover Clan has to know what was inside that crown, right? What could they possibly want with one of the first Progenitors, assuming that’s even true? The young thief had to ask himself.

“And let me guess,” Cade said, edging closer to Chubbs while keeping his movements as casual as one could in a collapsing building. He had to buy time to ensure the next part of his plan would work. “Family reunion with pops didn’t go so well?”

“They murdered us,” Bernard hissed, genuine pain bleeding through the ancient rage. “Their own children. They made us, shaped us, then destroyed us the first moment they discovered more obedient toys to play with.”

“Sounds like some serious family issues,” Cade acknowledged, his flippant tone masking the true empathy he felt. But understanding his reasoning did not justify what this Progenitor was attempting to do… what it would cost him and everyone else in their world.

“But let me get this straight. You’re upset at the gods for killing off your brothers and sisters, and so thought, ‘hey, I’m going to terrorize and kill a toddler. That’ll show them’?” Steel laced Cade’s words. He met the Progenitor’s multifaceted gaze. “I think you’re just an opportunistic hypocrite. You’re a coward.”

“That ‘toddler’ is Creation reborn,” Bernard spat while he slowly rolled out his shoulders. “She is not an innocent like your baseless actions insinuate. She was the architect of our downfall. She stood with our parents when they turned against us. Watched as my siblings were torn apart. Hells, she destroyed her own offspring when they proved too unruly for her vise grip to control.”

“And you think revenge will fix that?” Cade asked, inching closer to the ritual’s protective bubble. “Seems like a lot of effort for something that happened millennia ago.”

Bernard’s laugh sounded like broken glass. “Says the mayfly to the forest. You cannot comprehend the depth of such betrayal, nor the patience required to avenge it.” He gestured toward the emerging throne. “This Oakthorn Throne shall rise, regardless of your interference. The only question is how many you’re willing to let suffer in the process.”

The corrupted crimelord smiled then. It was a cruel, bottomless, pit of malice.

“You weren’t the only one stalling, thief.”

He snapped his fingers. Runes burned in the air.

The ritual’s pull intensified and Cade felt the drain on his reserves. It started as a persistent weakness spreading through his limbs, but now it was like a torrent of energy escaped from his new core. He couldn’t take this for much longer.

Across the cathedral, his crew fought with visibly diminishing strength, each of them now unwilling power sources for the altered ritual. The sphere of abyssal energy burst all at once, right as Nora punched the dark humanoid in the jaw. The impact sent a resounding crash through the space. The creature reeled back and whatever had been anchoring him here was now yanking back toward the fissure.

Bernard advanced with predatory patience, studying Cade with cold calculation. “I will admit, your magic intrigues me, thief. That is Chaos energy, if I’m not mistaken.” His head tilted, the crown’s eyes focusing intently. “That dominion died with its god. Yet here you stand, wielding its power like a child with his father’s sword.”

“I’m full of surprises,” Cade replied, gathering his remaining strength.

He would have to be quick.

“Indeed. Though it changes nothing.” Bernard raised a hand, emerald energy coalescing into a spear. “Your alterations merely delay the inevitable. The Throne still rises. Creation still pays for her betrayal.”

“That’s not Creation! She’s CHUBBS!” Cade snapped, his patience finally breaking. “Creation was an arrogant monster. Her—” Cade pointed at the young girl, “she likes bunny crackers. She tries to hug every animal she sees—even the ones that could eat her! Whatever Creation did, she isn’t that person anymore.”

Something flickered in Bernard’s expression—a momentary hesitation. The crown’s eyes swiveled in agitation, its tendrils pulsing as it fought to maintain control.

Then Bernard’s face hardened once more. “Sentiment,” he spat. “Another human weakness.”

Without warning, he attacked—not with magical energy, but with physical speed that belied his corrupted form. His fist connected with Cade’s jaw with bone-crushing force, sending the thief flying across the cathedral floor.

Cade crashed into a broken pew, his newly achieved gold rank the only thing preventing his spine from shattering on impact. He rolled to his feet, spitting blood as Bernard advanced with inexorable purpose. Each menacing step he took toward the Son of Ruin sent flares of green wisps from the seal. That, combined with the strange afterimage he created while in motion, made him look like a nightmare come to life.

“You will die here, thief,” Bernard stated, emerald energy gathering around his calloused hands. “Your friends will follow. And Creation will fuel the Throne’s rebirth, as was always intended.”

Instead of quipping back, Cade launched himself forward, crimson-gold energy exploding from his hands in tight bursts. The percussive blasts shot him back to his feet and into the air. With a roar, he punched down at the Progenitor. He curled his magic up to his forearms, his bracers aglow with amplifying runes.

Bernard parried each strike but was forced back step by step, his confident smirk fading as Cade’s relentless assault continued.

“Will. you. just. die?!” Cade shouted with each punch. He lunged down and punched at the monster’s abdomen, but he was too slow. Bernard sidestepped and slammed a knee into Cade’s nose. He barely felt it. With a snarl, he kicked from the side, his chaos-laced shin colliding with the Progenitor’s ribs. The force was enough to send the crowned man rocketing into an overturned column over a dozen feet away.

He pressed his advantage, unwilling to let the man recover. Two more of his strikes met their marks, and Bernard howled in pain as Cade’s chaos and destruction tore into his reinforced flesh.

For a breathless moment, Cade believed he might actually prevail. His twin magics were working in perfect harmony as he drove Bernard toward the ritual’s center, where the drain would be most severe. He could see the thick vines of energy spill out of his crown and chest and into the ritual’s hungry maw with each passing second.

Then Bernard smiled again, and the world turned upside down.

Instead of continuing to defend, he dropped his guard and took a blast directly to the chest. The impact sent him reeling backward, but he used the momentum to position himself perfectly beside the bound form of Chubbs.

“Enough,” he snarled, emerald energy condensing around his extended fingers.

Horror froze Cade in place.

The Progenitor’s fingers sharpened into vermillion claws conjured by whatever dark magic he possessed. They hovered over Chubbs’ restrained formfrom. He started to dig them into the barrier the ritual had conjured around her thanks to Cade’s machinations.

His fingers sizzled as the ward burned through his conjured talons, but his true digits sunk deeper and deeper into the small dome.

“Don’t,” Cade warned, desperation cracking his voice.

Bernard’s smile was glacial. “Tell me, thief. Do you think if someone forgets a crime they committed, that they shouldn’t be held accountable?”

His hand hovered inches from Chubbs’ terrified face.

“Answer me!”

“Whatever happened,” Cade said carefully, taking a slow step forward, “it’s between you and the gods. Not her. She’s just a kid.”

“A vessel,” Bernard corrected. “As am I.” His gaze softened momentarily as he studied Chubbs’ face. “Though I admit, this face she wears is a convincing one. The fear in those eyes seems quite genuine.”

The crown’s eyes swiveled in agitation, its tendrils pulsing as Bernard continued to hesitate. For a moment, something like clarity flickered across his corrupted features—recognition, confusion, horror at what he was doing.

“She...” Bernard’s voice was suddenly his own again, confusion replacing rage. “She’s just a child. What am I—”

The crown flared with emerald fury, silencing Bernard’s brief resurgence. When he spoke again, the ancient resonance had returned, deeper and more malevolent than ever.

“Irrelevant,” the Progenitor roared. “The vessel’s innocence changes nothing. My aunt shall pay for her cruelty, even if I cannot savor her despair and regret when I do.”

His hand plunged through the protective barrier, emerald energy cutting through Cade’s modifications as he reached for Chubbs’ throat.

“NO!” Cade screamed and he drew on as much of his new magic as he could in that instant. It overwhelmed his magical pathways, lacerating their way through every inch of him.

But he didn’t care.

He shoved every ounce of it into his legs and pushed.

When he moved, air parted around him in such a rush that it took a moment before a thunderous clap echoed behind him. The sudden shift in space reverberated through his bones, and he could feel several of them fracture.

But that didn’t matter either.

He threw himself between Bernard and Chubbs, crimson-gold energy forming a desperate shield as emerald power crashed into him with the force of a collapsing mountain.

His shoulder collided with the Progenitor’s chest, and he was sent backward so fast even Cade’s vision couldn’t track the movement. A brazier filled with green flames was rent apart in his passage, sending up plumes of smoke and ashes into the sky.

The young thief stood there, legs wobbling and body screaming at him. But he remained on his feet between Chubbs and this foul creature that would do her harm.

“It’s okay, Chubbs. I won’t let…” He stumbled to the side. His vision swam. “I won’t… I won’t let him… hurt you.”

“Careful what you promise,” Bernard growled from the billowing smoke.

Pain exploded through Cade’s body as corrupted energy overwhelmed his defenses, punching right through his hastily erected shield like paper. An emerald lance formed from Bernard’s extended fingers and it impaled Cade’s chest in a spray of crimson and golden energy.

“CADE!” Chubbs screamed, her voice somehow deeper, older.

The world tilted sickeningly as Cade collapsed, blood pooling beneath him on ancient stone. Through a haze of agony, he forced himself to look at Chubbs.

The child staring back at him was no longer a toddler—she appeared to be almost ten, her features sharpening with each passing moment as the ritual continued its work despite his modifications. And in her eyes, he saw recognition. Not the innocent trust of Chubbs the toddler, but something deeper, more ancient. More aware.

“Run,” he managed to gasp.

Bernard loomed over him, studying Cade’s fallen form with a clinical detachment. “Impressive sacrifice,” he observed. “Futile, but impressive nonetheless.”

He raised his hand, emerald energy gathering for a killing blow.

“I’ve existed for millennia,” Bernard continued, voice oddly conversational. “I’ve witnessed countless acts of heroism, each as pointless as the last. Yet they never cease to fascinate me.” His head tilted, the crown’s six eyes studying Cade with an almost academic interest. “What did you hope to accomplish? Truly?”

Cade tried to summon his magic, but the emerald lance had done its work well. His connection to both chaos and destruction felt muted. Distant. Every single magical pathway in his body felt like it was laced with fire. The ritual’s drain, combined with his fresh injuries, left him barely able to maintain consciousness, let alone fight.

“I just… wanted...” he whispered, blood bubbling at his lips, “to be enough.”

Something like recognition flickered in Bernard’s corrupted gaze. “Ah. The eternal human lament.” The emerald energy in his hand intensified, forming a sphere of corrupted power large enough to destroy his entire torso with no issue. “Rest assured, thief—you were never meant to be.”

The sphere descended toward Cade’s chest. He tried again to summon his magic, to move, to do anything—but his broken body refused to respond. Darkness encroached at the edges of his vision as blood continued to pool beneath him.

I’m sorry, Chubbs. I’m sorry, everyone.
