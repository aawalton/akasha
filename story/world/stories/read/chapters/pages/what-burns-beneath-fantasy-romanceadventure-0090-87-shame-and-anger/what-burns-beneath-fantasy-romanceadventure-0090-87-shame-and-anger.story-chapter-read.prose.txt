I hurried through the main room, practically crashed out the back door, and whirled. Renner nudged the door shut with one boot, then leaned beside it with his arms folded.

“That man is… is… vile!” My hands shook. Bile oozed its way back up my throat.

Jareth cleared his throat from near the fence, where he was affixing his armor. “My lady, I can assure you you’re not the only one who thinks that. But he means well.”

I rounded on the paladin. “He means well?! He said I’d… saints, he implied… ” the words tangled in a sputtering knot of frustration. Helpless tears welled in my eyes.

‘Not beyond your own shame.’

I felt like vomiting. Like screaming. He’d said it so casually, so dismissively, when the dread and shame of that very thought had kept me awake in the dark, had breathed down the back of my neck and left me weeping in my bed just a few nights ago.

My own shame. Shame! How dare he? Blood and bones and all the cursed… how dare he?! What right has he to say such a thing?! To me, to anyone who’s been threatened with… with torture, with rape, with all the things Velidan made me think might happen if I tried to refuse?!

My ears rang so loudly that the next thought was nearly swallowed. But I didn’t so much hear it or think it as… feel it. Quite like a hammer to my gut.

What right have I?

My breath gusted out. Something warm dripped down one cheek, but I barely felt it. Someone seemed to be speaking, but their voice was a distant, fuzzy hum.

Shame. Saints. Isn’t that exactly what I felt just a few nights ago? Sitting in Snowmelt with Renner and Royce and Teela, and they asked… what the pact was. What Velidan offered. And I stood there and cried and barely managed to say a word because I was disgusted and ashamed and… and… of course they wouldn’t have blamed me if something had happened. They wouldn’t have been disgusted or ashamed. Not of me.

Cool evening air filled my lungs. I blinked in the growing twilight, staring up at rustling green fir branches and the sky beyond. Lavender clouds floated in a sea of deep blue, and to the north a great plume of shimmering white smoke rose from the treeline. I took it all in, breathing deeply, feeling as if a knot inside my chest was being slowly unwound.

I didn’t do anything wrong. Even if something had... had happened. If he'd hurt me. It wouldn't have been my fault.

My eyes found Renner. He was watching me silently just a pace away, asking a thousand questions with his gaze but voicing none. I sniffled, wiped my tears away, and gave him a nod.

“-very stern man, and quite strict,” Jareth was saying. He knelt with a grunt, gauntleted hands working at the leather straps and buckles around his legs. “Perhaps the harshest taskmaster I’ve met when it comes to training. And he can certainly be unpleasant with anyone suspected of consorting with Fae. But… well, consider his duty! Pacted come in all sizes, and all guises. Sir Brisimere’s methods root out lies.” He glanced up with an easy smile, though it faltered when he saw my tears and doubtlessly blotchy cheeks. “I… ah, I only… meant to say that perhaps discomfort is a fair price for discerning innocence. Are you… alright?”

“I am, but that wasn’t just discomfort. He told me… sir Vhaik said if the Fae had… ashes, forced himself on me, that I wouldn’t be punished, but that I should be ashamed of myself. Especially if I became preg-!” I snapped my jaw shut, one hand lifting to cover my mouth, but it was too late.

Teela, sitting atop the fence with her legs dangling down, went very still as every eye turned to her. Then she coughed once and bared crooked teeth in a half-smile.

“I’m sorry,” I choked out. “Salt. Teela, I’m sorry, I didn’t-”

“His opinion,” she said calmly. Her red eyes glinted like twin drops of blood in the evening light. “Not yours.”

Jareth looked incredibly uncomfortable in the ensuing silence. His handsome face turned ashy and he opened his mouth several times, looking between me and Teela, but he seemed unable to find any response.

“He couldn’t have… maybe you misheard… no one could think something like…” Royce looked at me somewhat helplessly.

“I didn’t mishear him, Royce! That’s what he said!”

He flinched back as though I’d hit him. The helpless expression grew somewhat panicky as he searched the arena and the surrounding trees. “But… he’s… good,” the word came out so meekly, and in such an uncharacteristically tiny voice, that all my anger cracked like glass. One hand rose to further muss his manic hair. “I mean… right?”

I thought of the blood-spattered glove, of the hand so casually placed on my leg, and gave my head a solemn shake.

“I told you,” Renner’s voice was measured, and the slowness of it further grounded me, “most Thalessians are decent people.” His eyes, gleaming burnt bronze in the shadows beneath the building, flicked to Jareth and Teela before resettling on me. “I didn’t say all. Vhaik’s always been a walking sack of-”

The door cracked open, revealing the man in question. He wore a thin leather surcoat beneath the emblazoned white cloak. A swordhilt made of familiar, glassy silver shrouded in supple black leaves glinted at his belt. He looked between the five of us, then announced in a clipped voice that made every hair on the back of my neck stand up, “Sir Jareth. You’re with me.”

The younger paladin tried, and failed, to hide a wince. “Ah… with respect, I think I-”

“Now.” Vhaik swept back into the barracks.

Jareth’s throat bobbed. He looked at me, then again at Teela, and once more seemed to be trying to think of something to say.

“His opinion,” Teela said again. Her voice carried an unusual weight; an edge of wary challenge alongside something like pride. She leveled a stern look at the paladin. “Not yours. Right?”

Pools of dark red blossomed in his cheeks. “Yes. Of course, yes.”

“Then,” she hopped off the fence, dusting her hands and tone lilting to its usual, easy chirp, “that’s all there is to it. Right?”

His flush grew, and he looked uncertainly at me. Metal rattled as he took a step forward. “I… my lady, he shouldn’t have said-”

“Well, he did.” Renner gave the other man a chilling look. “Almost like not everyone in the Order’s as noble as they like to pretend.”

I glanced between the two men. There was a palpable edge behind that statement, and I could well imagine what Renner meant by it.

Does Jareth know about consecration? He probably doesn’t; Renner said it’s something only the paladins participate in, that they’re the only ones who see it happen. Jareth was only blessed a short while before he traveled here.

Unless… unless Renner told him. Before he left the Order. They were friends, from the sound of things, and surely Renner would have told people what he saw. So…

Jareth cleared his throat. “There’s no pretending. Sir Vhaik obviously spoke in error, but…” brittle silence threatened and, with an uncomfortable cough, he squared his shoulders. “But his opinion on such a delicate matter is worth little. It certainly does not,” he snapped, glaring at Renner pointedly, “Reflect any other Thalessian’s. Nor tarnish their nobility.”

Renner didn’t bat an eyelash. “Keep telling yourself that.”

The larger man grimaced. “Look, brother, I’m not having this argument again. Not right now. If you want to fight again later, fine. We can go as long as you want. But-”

“But you’ve got some orders to follow.” Every word was a blade. Cold and razor-sharp, words I would’ve bet anything he’d said before.

Jareth’s reaction confirmed it. He froze and, for the first time, genuine anger flashed across his handsome face. I didn’t know what this argument was about, exactly, but it was clearly a tense topic between the pair.

The paladin’s expression smoothed quickly and he answered, with deliberate slowness, “I’m going to go help Jo… Dame Joselia figure out what’s going on. Help her defend this place and save lives. I hope to see you all later tonight. Truly.” He nodded to me, Teela, and Royce. Then he glanced at Renner and, jaw tightening, gave a mock-salute. “Killer.”
