Entering the Battle of Wills space with the lion-like beast, I find the torrent of resistance far weaker than I was expecting – even more so than it was when I faced Nessie. I frown as I try to work out why. Sure, we defeated the Tier threes in battle, but that was true of Nessie as well. Perhaps it’s because we’ve immobilised it so thoroughly.

Whatever the reason, the evidence speaks for itself, and it's not long before I've pushed into communication distance with the lion-like Tier three, almost unbothered by the waves of anger and defiance that attempt to batter me – at their current strength, they feel little stronger than caresses.

Stay away, the lion roars as forcefully as it can, given its inability to move. If you dare to chain me, you will regret it.

I step closer.

Stay away! he insists again, yet I can feel fear threading through his voice and the emotions around me as he realises he can’t stop me.

I step closer still, coming to a stop an arm's length away. By now, fear has overtaken the anger – fear and a desperate rejection of what he must know is all but inevitable.

Don't chain me. I'm meant to be free, the lion insists, but the roiling fear in his emotions undercuts the threat he attempts to press into it and makes it more of a plaintive plea.

“You have led a group of beasts to attack me; to attack those under my protection,” I point out. “Sparing you at all would be offering you mercy that I doubt you would offer someone threatening the same. Why should I allow you to walk away without consequence?”

Confusion ripples through his emotions, briefly replacing the fear.

I did not invade your territory, he argues. You invaded mine.

I frown.

“I have gone nowhere. This territory does not belong to you. Another won it fairly, and I know for a fact that you have not defeated her.”

The lion's emotions ripple in agitation.

That cannot be, he argues, though he can surely feel my sincerity. I have ruled my territory for many seasons, and I do not remember leaving it to challenge another. Why would I? I have everything I need.

I study him, confusion giving way to realisation as pieces that previously didn’t seem to fit now come together.

“You say you do not remember leaving your territory. Does that mean you don't have any memory of leading an attack alongside another of your rank, bringing many weaker beasts to a hill with a cavern within it?”

The confusion that radiates from him answers me before he says a word.

Lead an attack with another of my rank? I would only do that with a mate, and I have none.

I nod slowly, observing the honesty in his emotions. Either he is a better actor than any I have ever encountered, capable of presenting a perfect facade with his emotions, or there’s another factor here. And I have a strong suspicion I know what it is.

There are just too many coincidences for my liking. First, my people are infected with a parasite that makes them feral and aggressive and controls them to the extent that they have no awareness of their actions when I speak to them in their soulspace. And now I’m encountering a beast who follows the exact same patterns – the only difference is that the feral, aggressive behaviour is slightly less outside the norm for them. Though I can hardly say the same about the way they work together and their lack of self-preservation.

When considered like that, the conclusion seems obvious.

“I strongly suspect that you have been overcome by a parasite,” I tell the Tier three seriously. “One that has been controlling your actions.”

Uncertainty radiates from him, as if he's weighing whether I'm telling the truth. Then, presumably feeling the sincerity and gravity in my emotions, horror replaces the fear and anger.

I have not been in control of my own body? he asks, almost to himself. For how long? What has it done with me?

I can't answer those questions precisely, but I can tell him about what’s just happened.

“I strongly suspect you no longer have a territory unless you are sure you would maintain your claim even in your absence. As for more recent events, had we not chosen to take you alive, you would now be dead.” I pause a moment to let that sink in. “The good news is that I have had practice finding and removing this parasite. In return, however, I require a Bond – both to accomplish the task and as recompense for my time.”

After all, unlike the others I have healed today, this beast is not one of my subjects. Controlled or not, he has threatened my people, and I can't simply let that pass.

And if I don't accept? the lion asks, though from the uncomfortable rolling of his emotions, he already knows the answer.

If you are unwilling to pay for your healing with service, I will simply not intervene, I tell him flatly. That will mean you remain a threat to our settlement, and we will deal with you accordingly.

I will not handicap my forces by urging them not to kill the beasts that are trying to kill them, just because they’re not in control of their own actions – as much as I wish I could find a solution that didn’t require slaughtering victims of the parasites’ infection.

The lion is intelligent enough to understand exactly what I mean. A fresh wave of fear goes through him, though it’s quickly followed by contemplation. Though not capable of reading his mind exactly, I’ve had enough practice interpreting emotions to be able to gain a good idea of the direction of his contemplations. My interpretation is tested, however, as an unusual combination surfaces – a sense of cunning, patient and quiet. After a moment of consideration, however, I believe I’ve interpreted it correctly: he's willing to accept the Bond for now, but intends to wait for a better moment to break free.

That is a genuine concern. While it’s easier to hold a Bond than establish it in the first place, I well remember what it was like when Windy attempted to free herself when she first Evolved to Tier three. I only won that fight because I managed to trap her entirely in rock. Sure, I’ve advanced since then – in both number of Willpower stat points, and practice, but I’m also spread far more thinly. Nessie’s Bond isn’t precisely settled, and I’m currently holding more Bonds with non-Classers than I ever have.

At least the situation should improve – once I’m certain that there are no more complications to fear with the parasite, I’ll release all of those – that will free up some of my Willpower. In the meantime, I'll have to be careful not to put him in any position where he could do significant damage if he slips free while I'm distracted or not able to immediately act. In the short term, though, I suspect that the first commands I’ll have for him will be ones he’ll happily obey – assuming that my guesses are correct, that is.

I will Bond with you, he finally decides, his emotions reducing in intensity, fear still at the forefront, but muted.

I don't let on that I've felt his intentions and simply nod.

“Then I will place the Bond now. I will check your body briefly to confirm my theory, but I won't attempt to heal you until I've dealt with your Tier three co-general.”

Hopefully that will discourage him from trying to break free the moment we leave this space – assuming he's capable of that kind of thinking while the parasite still has influence over him. Given how the parasite seems to force its host into unconsciousness, I suspect that he won't be able to try, but better safe than sorry. I’d worry that the parasite would try to break the Bond on his behalf except for the fact that I’ve never seen any indication it affects anything in the soul.

The lion agrees, reluctantly. I trigger the Bond and it falls into place.

As the normal world reforms around me and colour fully returns, I use my wings to propel myself up onto the ice pile to reach the lion's head – mostly clear of the ice vines – and place my hand on it.

"Markus?" I hear from Layton.

I spare a glance toward the lords who have gathered together even as others have started stripping the dead of meat – that was a good call: we need the food. Though, if my guess is right, we’ll need to make sure it’s safe to eat first.

"I need to test a theory," I tell him shortly.

I hear murmuring, but I don't focus on it. Instead, I close my eyes and press my mind into the lion-like Tier three's body.

I'm entirely unsurprised to find the parasite's network spread throughout him, though far more advanced and extensive than anything I've seen before. This infection is not recent, I decide early on. The lion's brain, when I reach it, confirms it: the parasite settled there is massive.

Usually they're marble-sized, hidden in the rear of the brain. This one is roughly the size of my two fists held together, seeping into not only the back of the lion's brain but spreading into the front parts as well.

With my suspicions confirmed so thoroughly, I'm far better placed to approach the okapi-like Tier three. He is considerably less antagonistic than the lion was, and far more frightened. Though a Tier three, I suspect this one has managed to Evolve through surviving long enough on Energy-imbued plants to build his internal matrix rather than using the quicker method of combat. He's certainly no strong combatant and he agrees quickly to my deal.

I pull out of the Battle of Wills with a second Bond in place, and am soon able to confirm the parasite's presence and influence in the okapi as well.

Unsettled, I descend from where I've been touching the second Tier three's head and approach the lords. Enforcing Bonds with Two Tier threes who are not entirely willing is harder than I was expecting – just maintaining the Bonds is sapping my focus in a way that holding a hundred Bonds with my people simply doesn't. It’s not enough to poorly affect me right now, but I can feel that I’m reaching a limit.

But I’m distracted by something else too – because if these beasts are infected by the parasites, what if some of my companions are too? I haven’t been checking them since so far only humans have been showing symptoms. But now…

I resolve to check them as soon as I can.

Elarion, Layton, Valence, and Josiah are talking quietly as I approach but break off as I come within earshot.

"What did you find?" Elarion asks, his eyes sharp.

"Would you like the good news or the bad news?" I ask, my lips twitching in a humourless smile.

Josiah and Layton exchange a glance.

"Whichever, Markus," Layton replies, with a touch of impatience. “Just tell us.”

"Good news first, then. I've determined the source of the issues assaulting our settlement."

The other lords exchange hesitant glances.

"Do you speak of the sickness? Or the beast waves? Because I thought we had already identified the source of the sickness," Valence replies uncertainly. "A parasite, no?"

"Yes. However, I’ve confirmed that when we speak of the cause of one, we are speaking of the cause of the other too." I gesture to the two Tier threes. "Both of them are infected by parasites. And the infection is far more advanced in them than in anyone else I've observed."

"How is that good news?" Josiah demands.

I raise an eyebrow.

"Is it not better to know that the problems besetting our settlement are connected rather than separate? If we solve one, we can solve the other."

"You have a point," Layton acknowledges thoughtfully. "Then what is the bad news?"

I fix him with a steady gaze.

"The bad news is that anything capable of affecting a Tier three to this extent is going to be powerful.” I pause a moment to let that sink in.

“You think it’s a single beast?” Josiah questions, then glances at Elarion. “Couldn’t it be a plant? Elarion’s been talking about some sort of network that’s causing issues – maybe it could be linked to that.”

“If it were the plant network, do you not think we would see more plant-based attacks?” Elarion asks with exasperation. “I spoke of the network causing trouble with our farming efforts – not the current situation.”

Josiah grunts in acknowledgement and turns his attention back to the Tier threes, evidently conceding the point.

“I agree that it’s unlikely to be a plant-based mastermind,” I agree after a moment. “The parasites are clearly flesh-based. I wouldn’t be able to affect them so easily otherwise. No, I think it’s far more likely that this is a beast capable of controlling others."

"A powerful Tier three territory lord?" Valence asks hopefully.

Elarion turns his scathing look on Valence next.

"Don't be foolish," he admonishes the younger lord, then turns his eyes to me. "You think this could be a Tier four, don’t you?"

“I hope so," I agree grimly. Then, seeing Valence’s confused look, I expand. “Because I’m really hoping it’s not anything more powerful than that.”

Valence’s face shows his dawning comprehension, then his expression firms.

“I’ll send my scouts out.”
