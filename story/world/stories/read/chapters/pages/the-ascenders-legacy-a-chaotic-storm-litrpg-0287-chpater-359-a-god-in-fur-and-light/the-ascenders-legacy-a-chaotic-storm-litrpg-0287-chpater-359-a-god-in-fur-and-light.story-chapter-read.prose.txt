While Dravion basked in the true meaning and glory of his path, another breakthrough was unfolding only a stone’s throw from his home.

Sophran’s breakthrough.

Where Dravion had secluded himself to mend and refine his path, Sophran had done the same—but his purpose was different. For three days, he had remained within his cultivation chamber, a pendant resting against his chest as he meditated on a single idea.

A familiar.

He already knew what he wanted. Had known since the day he awakened. He knew its nature, its temperament, and had planned his entire path around it.

And now, after three days of unwavering focus, that patience had finally paid off.

Congratulations! You have created a familiar never before seen in ÆFLYM. [Click to view details]

Sophran sat in the center of his cultivation chamber, a rare, unguarded smile spreading across his face as he looked at the creature before him.

It was a Vaelryn—a mythical creature from his home world known as the spirit of peace and regeneration. It resembled a fox—sleek, elegant, wise—but that was where any resemblance to the mundane ended.

Standing nearly five feet tall at the shoulder, the Vaelryn’s body was not flesh. It was translucent, like liquid glass given form, its fur composed of hair-thin strands of light and energy, each one crackling with barely contained vitality. Within its body, soft currents of pale light drifted—slow, steady, endless. Like vitality given form.

It was beautiful. Exceedingly so.

Luminous blue eyes met his, and Sophran let out a shuddering breath, awed that he’d actually done it. That he’d brought an object of folklore from his home world to life here in ÆFLYM.

Slowly, he placed a palm on the Vaelryn’s snout, his smile widening as he watched lightning crackle gently between his fingers. The Vaelryn pressed back against his hand, a playful glint in its eyes, and Sophran shuddered as vitality surged through him—not a spark, not a trickle, but an entire river that crashed through him, setting his whole being ablaze with life.

His skin smoothed instantly. Wrinkles vanished. His senses sharpened as if everything within him had become brand new. Even his spirit felt revitalized, restored to a state he hadn’t experienced in decades.

Sophran’s grin threatened to split his face in two.

This was the power of the Vaelryn. The reason he’d chosen it above all others.

The Vaelryn wasn’t a creature of battle. It couldn’t defeat any serious opponent in direct combat.

But it would outlast them.

Its regeneration was far beyond what Ancestral Continuance offered him. This wasn’t healing. This wasn’t even revitalization.

This was restoration. Reversal. A complete reset to a state of absolute perfection.

“We have just become very hard to kill, partner,” Sophran said quietly, his voice thick with satisfaction.

The Vaelryn cooed—a soft, crystalline sound that resonated through their bond.

He didn’t understand its words yet, but he understood its intent. Joy. Agreement. Partnership.

He smiled. “I shall call you Halo.”

Halo licked his face, and Sophran laughed—a genuine, open sound that rose from somewhere he hadn’t visited in a very long time. After years of waiting, he had finally bonded his familiar.

And it couldn’t have been more perfect.

“Come,” he said, rising to his feet with an ease that still surprised him. “Let’s go introduce you to the others.”

Halo yipped, excitement thrumming through their bond like electricity.

Sophran laughed again, scratching behind her ears as they headed for the door. “You’re about to meet your family, Halo. Brace yourself—they’re a lot.”

Halo yipped again.

Sophran smiled. “Good girl.”

***

They arrived at Aodhán’s mansion a few minutes later, and just as Sophran had said, it got very loud very quickly.

Crixis was the one who opened the door, and his eyes widened the instant he saw Sophran. He began to bow, then his gaze landed on Halo, and the movement came to an abrupt, stunned halt.

His eyes widened further. His jaw dropped.

And in a stunned whisper, he asked, “By the storm… is that your familiar?”

Halo yipped playfully, and Sophran smiled widely. “Yes, Crixis. I gained her less than an hour ago.”

“She’s beautiful.”

Halo pressed into the approaching hand, and Sophran watched proudly as Crixis got his first taste of her restoration powers. He jerked his arms back as though he’d touched something scalding, but the surge of vitality had already taken effect.

His skin smoothed. His eyes, dulled by mourning, brightened, and even his spiritual presence shifted, its weight pressing more firmly against the world.

“How do you feel?” Sophran asked with a wide smile.

But instead of responding, Crixis spun and rushed back into the house, shouting, “Everyone come out! Sophran’s gained his familiar!”

Chuckling, Sophran followed him inside with Halo at his side, arriving in the living room just as the underlings came thundering down the stairs.

The reaction was immediate and gratifying.

“Is that a fox?” Su Tianan blurted, eyes wide.

Halo yipped, and Sophran laughed. “No. She’s a Vaelryn. And she has healing powers.”

That was all it took.

They descended on Halo in a wave, stroking her fur and exclaiming every time she sent a pulse of vitality through them.

“It’s like being doused in ice water,” Kairn said, grinning despite himself, “but in a good way.”

Sophran laughed, fielding their questions with the patience of a man who had waited a very long time for exactly this moment. They asked about her abilities, her limits, the perks of the bond—and he answered everything slowly and proudly, like a parent bragging about their favorite child.

Time passed in that manner, and then Rathis Cloudrend asked the question that changed the room.

“Can Halo cure a coma?” A beat passed. “Can she heal Darien?”

Rathis’s question hit Sophran like a fist to the chest, and he realized that in the warmth of the last hour, he had forgotten—genuinely forgotten about Darien.

For a moment, the joy in the room faltered.

He looked at Rathis. Then at Halo.

Then a small, quiet smile found its way onto his face.

“We’ll never know until we try.” He pushed himself to his feet. “What do you say we find out right now?”

They agreed in an instant and moved toward the door—only to pull up short at the sight of Aodhán and Nymera standing on the path outside, hand in hand.

Embarrassment flashed across their faces immediately, and though Nymera tried to yank her arm free, Aodhán refused to let go. His grip tightened instead, a small smile tugging at his lips as he opened his mouth to speak—

Before he could, Halo yipped from behind them, drawing everyone’s attention to herself.

Everyone turned, the underling parted and Aodhán’s breath caught at the sight of the familiar standing beside Sophran. He let go of Nymera’s hand instinctively, eyes widening with excitement.

He glanced at Sophran. “You did it.”

“I did,” Sophran said proudly. “She’s a Vaelryn, a myth from my home world. Her name is Halo.”

“She’s not a real animal?” Nymera asked, already moving closer to stroke her, a small smile tugging at her lips.

Sophran shook his head, smiling down at Halo. “Not quite. If anything, she’s more spirit than animal.”

Aodhán nodded slowly. He could see it—even now, the Vaelryn’s form was almost transparent, ghostly pale, soft blue luminescence running through every strand of fur like light through water.

It reminded him of the Skalk after bonding with Aldric, and something warm stirred in his chest at the thought.

With a smile, he reached out as well, his hand finding Halo’s fur at the same moment Nymera’s did.

Halo leaned into their touch, a playful glint dancing in her gaze.

And an instant later, Aodhán jerked his arms back, eyes wide as electric vitality surged through him.

His reaction caused a burst of laughter from Sophran, Crixis, and the other underlings, all of whom had expected exactly that reaction.

“What was that?” Aodhán asked, a smile blooming across his face as he stared at his friends. But the words had barely left his lips before an overwhelming wave of restoration washed over him.

There was barely any physical change in his body as Lightning Body—gold kept him in near-perfect condition already—but his spirit was a different matter entirely.

Restoration poured into him like a Ruinous Baptism, only ten times better than anything Aodhán was used to. The energy filled him to the brim, setting his spirit ablaze and sharpening his senses to an almost frightening degree.

“By the storm,” Aodhán breathed, voice hushed with awe. Never in his life had he felt so light, so… perfect.

He glanced up at Halo, awe gleaming in his gaze, and that was when he realized that unlike him, Nymera hadn’t pulled her hand away from the Vaelryn. She hadn’t moved at all, her body absorbing far more of that radiant vitality than he had.

Light gathered around her, glowing brighter with every passing second until she seemed to shine from within, her entire body blazing with the same pale white light flowing through the Vaelryn.

She turned toward him then, frost-white hair shimmering like spun starlight and eyes like distant constellations.

For a moment, Aodhán forgot how to breathe.

Nymera had always been beautiful. Otherworldly, even. But in that moment, she was almost unrecognizable.

Her skin looked flawless, her presence elevated, refined, as though reality itself had softened around her.

Aodhán swallowed, momentarily robbed of words.

But he wasn’t the only one. All around him, Sophran and the underlings watched in stunned silence, caught somewhere between awe and disbelief.

For one brief, blinding moment, the glow intensified.

Then slowly—it dimmed.

But it didn’t vanish.

Just like the Vaelryn, Nymera retained a faint glow, her body luminous in that same ethereal blue way spirits often were.

For a moment, no one spoke. Then Aodhán did.

“What just happened?” he asked, glancing at Sophran for answers, but it was Nymera who responded, her voice soft with wonder.

“Halo blessed me.” She shook her head slightly, still smiling as she looked down at the Vaelryn. “She gave me a temporary perk called Undying. For the next three hours… I can’t die.”

“What?”

Shock rippled through the group. Even Sophran looked taken aback, his gaze snapping toward Halo.

“You can do that?”

Halo yipped once—bright and affirmative—and Aodhán let out a slow, shuddering breath as the weight of that revelation settled over him.

Undying?

He glanced at the Vaelryn again, brows knitting in thought. Just how powerful was Halo, to grant something like that?

And beyond even that—

How could she grant a blessing at all?

She was a familiar.

…Right?

***

It took them a while to figure out why Halo was different from other familiars, and when they did, Aodhán let out a deep sigh.

“So,” he said slowly, “what you’re telling me is that Halo can hand out blessings because she doesn’t have perks of her own?”

Nymera shook her head, amused. “Not quite. It’s not that she lacks perks—it’s just that she doesn’t share them with Sophran like normal familiars do, and that’s because she’s not a normal animal. She’s more like a spirit.”

She turned slightly, glancing at Sophran. “Vaelryn… they’re worshipped in your world, aren’t they?”

Sophran hesitated, then nodded. “Yes. They’re… revered by some.”

“See?” Nymera smiled. “That explains it. Halo isn’t an animal at all. She’s not even a spirit. She’s a deity. And unless someone else from your world has also created a Vaelryn as a familiar, then Halo is the first of her kind in ÆFLYM. Her abilities were entirely dependent on your belief at the point of creation.”

“Really?” Sophran’s frown deepened. “So, in other words… I gave Halo this ability?”

“In a way,” Aodhán said, glancing at Nymera to make sure he was understanding correctly. “The Origin Plane created her according to what you believed her to be. You gave it the blueprint. It filled in the gaps.”

“Exactly,” Nymera nodded. “And because that blueprint wasn’t animal… or even strictly spirit…” She looked down at Halo, eyes glinting faintly. “It gave you something higher.”

Sophran exhaled, long and slow. “Great. So, I accidentally created a god as a familiar.”

Halo yipped happily, and Aodhán chuckled. “I doubt the system would ever let anyone create a god, but this is a pretty good imitation.”

Sophran shot him a playful glare. “An imitation that bestows blessings.”

“And curses,” Rathis Cloudrend said, cutting into their conversation without seeming to realize it. When he noticed the sudden shift in atmosphere, he paused, eyes flicking between them. “Deities lay curses on people too, don’t they?”

Silence followed. Not the easy kind.

Then slowly—almost reluctantly—everyone turned to look at Halo, who was still happily licking Nymera’s frozen palms.

A second passed. Then two.

Then—

“No.” Sophran shook his head firmly. “She’s a good deity. She would never lay a curse on anyone.”

Aodhán glanced at him. “Halo, or the Vaelryn itself—which are you referring to?”

“Both,” Sophran whispered. “They are good deities. She just blessed Nymera. She’s a good deity.”

“Unless the blessing comes with a drawback,” Rathis added, drawing a sharp look from Nymera.

“What?” he asked, genuinely confused. “Deities play games with mortal lives all the time.”

“Rathis, stop talking,” Sophran snapped, irritation bleeding through his voice. Then he glanced at Nymera, something more serious settling into his expression.

“There’s only one way to find out. Nymera… cut yourself.”

Anyone would have hesitated. Nymera didn’t.

Right there and then, she created a knife construct and drove it into her left arm. A quiet hiss slipped past her lips—but when she pulled the blade free, only a mystery remained.

Her skin was flawless.

“Did it cut?” Crixis asked, voicing what they were all thinking.

Nymera shot him a glare. “Yes. It cut. There’s blood on the blade.”

Aodhán glanced at the knife and frowned. There was indeed blood on it.

“Try dragging it this time,” Kairn suggested, and though Nymera looked moments away from ripping his head off, she spared him with only a glare before doing as he said.

She drew the blade across her arm.

But once again, her skin remained pristine.

There was more blood on the knife this time, and that was when they realized the blade was cutting her. She was simply healing so fast that the wounds barely had time to form before they vanished.

And there were no drawbacks.

“It’s amazing,” Sophran exclaimed a few tests later, his smile impossibly wide. “I created a god.”

Halo yipped, and they all laughed.

Sophran was ecstatic. And rightfully so.

A blessing that rendered a person practically immortal—even if only for a few hours—was no small thing. And if applied to them all, it could change the outcome of every battle going forward.

With a blessing like this, they could fight as recklessly as they wanted. They would never have to care about their safety, or about defending themselves from attacks.

They could focus entirely on offense. Like berserkers.

Only they wouldn’t be taking injuries.

They would be immortal.

Despite himself, Aodhán smiled as he watched Sophran hugging Halo close, laughing about how he’d created a good god.

Aodhán wanted to launch into a rant about how useful Halo’s abilities would be in the next battle, but he restrained himself out of respect for Kairn. They were still mourning Seamus, after all.

And the thought that this blessing might have saved him… sat unspoken between them.

That didn’t mean he wasn’t thinking it, though.

They all were.

But there was a more pressing matter now.

And after a few more minutes spent congratulating Sophran—or in Aodhán’s case, admiring Nymera’s luminous skin—they all made their way to the clinic to see Darien.

It was time to wake their friend up.

And if it worked, Aodhán wondered if it would be called a miracle.

After all, a god would be the one restoring him.
