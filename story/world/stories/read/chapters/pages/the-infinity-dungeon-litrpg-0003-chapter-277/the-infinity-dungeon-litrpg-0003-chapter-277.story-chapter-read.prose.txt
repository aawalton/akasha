Chapter 277

Michael led the way, followed by President Terris and Travis, with Johanne lagging a few paces behind. They crossed the threshold of the shield, and a strange landscape opened up before them. From inside, the shield was no longer black but only slightly tinted, with its hexagons glowing brightly, letting the sun shine on the slightly curved barren ground. Molten stone now solidified, it was the aftermath of the explosion of Faith that had destroyed everything.

“Is this all that’s left?” the President asked. “There is nothing.”

“Yes,” Travis said gravely. “What you see is all that remains of Huntington, West Virginia.”

“Were there any other survivors?”

“None,” Travis replied. “The world lost some good men in there.”

The President scoffed, leaning against the glowing pane of force that had been put there to make him feel safe. Even though the slope of the ground was gentle, they were already several feet up in the air, walking on a solid shield made opaque and given the texture of concrete.

“Some good men,” the man whispered, echoing Travis’s words. “Please, do me a favor and do not treat me like an ailing old man stricken by dementia. You took the trouble to save me and then make sure I was not a bumbling idiot anymore for a reason, there is no need for subterfuge. There is also no need to tell me that all you have given me can be taken away, I am more than smart enough to understand that myself. The stick is plenty big, you can dangle the carrot now.”

His words elicited different reactions in the various people present. Johanne snorted, muttering something about how refreshing it was to hear those words from a politician, while Travis frowned. Michael was amused, mostly at Travis’s expense. He was sure the man had prepared a whole speech, but the President at his prime was more than a match for him.

Too bad the situation was nothing short of inescapable for the man. At the same time, he was sure that what they were going to offer him would be tantalizing enough to distract him from his predicament. Not enough to forget it, but at least it would be a good distraction.

“Very well,” Travis said. “I will let Michael explain the situation. You and I will hammer out the details later.”

“As you can clearly see,” Michael said as he took over. “A certain number of things that were normally considered outside the realm of conventional science turned out to be quite real.” He looked at the wasteland behind him. “And quite relevant.”

With a snap of his fingers, he summoned a flame that hovered in the air around him.

“Magic, it turns out, has existed since the dawn of time. We do not know in what measure, nor do we know why what we call Ancestral magic slowly faded away from the world, but there is tangible proof that once upon a time, it was much more powerful and abundant than it is now. The modern world, with all its wonders and hard science, stands on the shoulders of wizards, shamans, ritualists and devils. This strange past left behind artifacts, anomalies and places of significance where the latent power of the world still gathers.”

The small flame grew into a column of fire.

“The artifacts are dangerous. It was one such artifact that allowed Don Casellaro to contact the realm of devils and make a deal with one, obtaining a tool capable of converting the faith of believers into magic capable of almost anything. Imagine, the faith of a billion believers erupting into pure unadulterated destruction all in one place at the same time, the punishment of God, nothing less!”

The flame, which had reached the center of the wasteland within the shield, detonated in a fiery blast of superheated air and white-hot fire. A mushroom cloud grew above it, reaching the top of the dome and washing over it, taking the shape of the half-sphere and expanding until it engulfed everything.

“And yet, even though the Faith erased everything, even the devil, we were able to come out unscathed.”

A snap of Michael’s finger, and the flames vanished. Much closer to them, a small platform made of the same shield as the dome appeared. On it rested a large wooden chest, dusty and rusted over after many years of neglect.

“Artifacts are a subset of what we call anomalies. An artifact is an object with a well-defined power. A shard capable of piercing the veil between worlds, a bottle that spills infinite water, a thing that does something. An anomaly, on the other hand, is a complete unknown.”

He took a step backward, watching as the President’s eyes widened the moment he passed straight through the shield and began to walk on solid air. He approached the chest, and as soon as he was within a few paces of it, the thing suddenly sprung to life. Its lid opened, revealing row upon row of ugly, sharp teeth and a tongue that shot toward Michael.

The President shuddered in reflexive, primal fear when the tongue reached Michael’s face, his body tight as a spring but immobilized by panic and by the unexplainable yet tangible reality of the situation. The tongue impacted an invisible shield, which then boxed the chest inside a transparent cage.

“That is a mimic,” Michael said as the chest thrashed against the walls of its prison suspended in the air. “One of the first anomalies we ever encountered. Later, Travis will tell you all about how we came to know about it, and about the agency you probably don’t even know existed. An agency that was dismantled following the chain of events that began with this very mimic right here.”

Returning to the platform with the others, he snapped his fingers. Suddenly, it was as if they were somewhere else, except for the faint outline of the shield visible in the distance around them.

“Don’t worry,” Michael said. “It’s only a hologram but, do you recognize this place?”

Grassy plains surrounded them. Great monoliths of stone were arranged in a circle, and the setting sun could be seen through the gap between two of them.

“This Stonehenge,” the President said. “A place of significance?”

Michael nodded. “All this is old magic. Powerful, but a known quantity in the world. There are people who are in the know, keepers of secrets, and thus the world has been made to forget. Had this been all the magic that there was, you wouldn’t be here. I wouldn't be here.”

“Had this been all the magic that there was,” Travis interjected. “I would still be the CEO of PetroLink Global. She,” he nodded toward Johanne. “Would not even be on this planet. The Don would have probably taken his time, or perhaps he would have never learned how to use his artifact, and the world would be a different place.”

“But it is not,” Michael said with sudden gravitas. “And it can all be traced back to one phenomenon. A global outbreak of these things.”

The hologram changed.

“Caves?” the President asked.

“Dungeons,” Michael said. “Appearing all over the world, they are openings into an interconnected quasi-space that spans the whole multiverse, and that we know close to nothing about. They are bringers of a new, powerful kind of magic capable of disrupting the old balances. They are governed by ancient rules written by their enigmatic creators, rules that allow suicidal people brave or foolish enough to venture in their depths to come out forever changed, wielding powers never seen before. Not only that, but dungeons interact with the old magic of the world, revitalizing it, breathing new life into it much like we did with your failing body. Wherever a dungeon appears, anomalies are sure to follow.”

“These dungeons, how many are there?” the President asked.

“Johanne,” Michael said with a nod.

She stepped forward. “Yes, Michael. Mr President, there are currently two dungeon openings that we know of. They are both currently under our control, and the spread of magic around them is being contained by a shield such as the one that surrounds us at this moment. Our data suggests that there might be as many as five other entrances, and more are forming as we speak, however we are not currently able to pinpoint their exact location. Furthermore, most of them are in places that would not be easy to reach and secure while remaining inconspicuous. At least one of them is located inside a densely populated city.”

The President nodded. “And these anomalies could pop up anywhere?”

She nodded. “Yes, with greater probability, density and danger the higher the concentration of magic in the air.”

“Plus,” Michael added. “There’s the possibility of delvers.”

“Your powers come from delving, don’t they?” the man asked.

“They have evolved well beyond simple dungeon-given powers but yes, they originally came from delving.”

The President crossed his arms, brows furrowing. “What would happen if we removed all the dungeons and the magic from the world? Would you retain your magic?”

“Yes,” Michael said.

“Would others also be able to do that?”

“It depends. Generally, no.”

“Then we must do exactly that, and pray that you are the only–”

“This is where I will stop you, Mr President.” Travis said, holding up a finger. “And remind you of your position.”

“I understand, Mr Tyrell, but from what you have said, the dungeons are dangerous.”

“Please,” Travis said condescendingly. “You asked us to treat you with respect, and we ask the same of you. You are only advocating for the destruction of the dungeons which, mind you, is not something we are currently able to do, because it is us who can exploit them, and not you. What if I told you we can give you similar power to what they offer, with none of the danger involved?”

“What danger?”

“Well,” Michael took over. “Let me show you.”

He snapped his fingers, and they were somewhere else. Ten minutes later, they all emerged from the dungeon, all of them looking the same as when they had entered save for the President. The man was shaking, about to break down into a screaming fit or something, when Travis suddenly grabbed his shoulder with fingers of steel, to the point it hurt.

“You understand now? The power the dungeons promise us? The danger?”

“I certainly understand one thing,” the President said. “The world will never be the same, even should the dungeons disappear at this very moment.”

“But they won’t,” Michael said. “Mr President, we cannot fight against them. They are here to stay whether we like it or not. That’s why we have been studying them and their magic. To make sure we are the ones exploiting them, and not the other way around. Because know this, Mr President. The dungeons are not neutral entities. They are not just… things. They have a will of their own, they took entire civilizations and reduced them to nothing more than pawns, slaves forced to do their bidding and provide challenge to new delvers.”

His voice grew as he began to talk with more and more strength. “We do not know their motives, but we are going to do everything in our power to make sure that we do not end up as slaves to the dungeons, that magic will not ruin our world, instead bending it to our will with science and human genius, advancing our civilization until we will soar the stars and live forever, gods of our universe and of other universes alike, new masters of the dungeons and you, Mr President, are going to help us with that whether you like it or not, for this nation and all the nations in the world, for civilization, progress, power and the will eternal of humanity and its allied races!”

The speech left Michael looking slightly winded, and he took a deep breath to steady himself.

The President levelled his gaze at him. “And if I help you, you will give me power like yours without having to go through the dungeon like you do?”

“Of course, Mr President,” Travis said. “All in due time. Before that, we should talk about what your next moves need to be. First of all, you will need to declare a state of national emergency, then…”

Before he could continue, Michael’s face lit up. “I just got a better idea. Mr President, you must be tired after all of this. There’s a helicopter waiting for you, it will bring you back to the White House. Rest for the day. As for the magic,” he said, noticing the President’s expression of quiet acceptance transform into one of greed. His thirst for power was palpable. “We will not ever let you into the dungeon, not unsupervised.”

“And I never want to go in there! You said there is another way!”

“There is. It comes at a price, but you are in no position to negotiate,” Michael said coldly. “If you truly want it, I will give you the Origin System.”

“What is it?”

“It’s the pinnacle of magical technology, Mr President. A gamified system to give everyone access to power beyond belief, leveraging their unique talents and situations. A much more elegant solution, a democratic one, superior in every way to the selective brutality of the dungeons. It is still in early testing. In fact, you would be the first outside of Unity Corporation to get it. Do you want in?”

“You’re damn right I want in.”

“Very well,” Michael said. “Travis, there is no need to instruct the President on anything. Talk to Icarus instead. The Origin System will take care of everything. I’ve upgraded it quite a lot since last time, you will like it.
