I sprinted toward the portal the God of Thunder had disappeared into.

Stormrunner threw me forward as fast as it could.

I didn’t think. My mind couldn’t grab onto anything. The battle trance pressed down on it—that, and my failure to remember one of Dad’s lessons.

The trap I’d been setting was a good one. If it had been completed, it would have been as effective against the God of Thunder as it had been against the Placid King. I’d only forgotten one simple thing: that the God of Thunder could watch me at any time. He’d been paying attention.

Worse, he’d probably set his own trap.

Magda.

She was a traitor of sorts. No, not a traitor. An agent. Magda, the Sliding Surge, had offered exactly the weapon I’d thought I needed. Something deeply personal, that couldn’t possibly be anything but a truth about the God of Thunder, and that I could wield against him.

Then she’d used it to set us up.

And we’d fallen for the trap, just like I’d fallen for my dad’s ‘hidden bishop’ traps and pawn walls dozens of times as a kid.

My mind couldn’t grasp anything but that failure. My body only wanted to fight. And my core…my core and my soul just wanted to get my ally back.

If it hadn’t been for Ellen, I’d have thrown myself through that portal and into the God of Thunder’s world, trap or not. I was inches from making it through when my core shuddered and my shadow ripped itself free of my body to form a second, dark Kade.

His sword hovered in front of my eyes, crackling with yellow-white lightning, and he dropped into Torrentborne Stance. I wasted no time. Laminar Blade. Stormfall. I attacked, and the shadowy Kade used Mist Trance to ride out the attack. The Storm’s Blade clashed against the shadow Kade’s sword. Thunder ripped across the Void Garden.

And a hand pressed against the small of my back.

“Kade. Breathe. Please.”

Ellen’s voice sounded like it was coming from a hundred miles away. It was sharp, drawn with pain, and weak at the same time.

I forced a breath. The shadow Kade didn’t move. Neither did I. Then, gradually, his sword faded to nothing, and his body slid to the ground to reform under my feet.

“I’m not enough. This wasn’t enough. He saw through it.” The words came out painfully slowly. “I need more…more than what I have. More than what I am.”

“You don’t. You can beat the God of Thunder as you are.”

“I’m not sure I can.” The portal stayed open for another few seconds, then blinked out. “We weren’t enough to save Tanat. She’s still alive, but we can’t save her. If I could claim SS-Rank right now…”

“Kade Allen Noelstra, you’re being stupid. If you could claim SS-Rank right now, that wouldn’t be enough, and it’d condemn you to the same fate Eugene’s facing.”

“Don’t say that name.” I’d been thinking it not twenty minutes earlier, but hearing it only reminded me of what, exactly, that monster had been.

“I’ll say it if I want, Kade. You’re being stupid,” Ellen said again. “Eugene is making mistakes right now. Look around you. He could have tried to destroy the Lightning Tower, or flattened the Void Garden’s maze. He didn’t. All he cared about was attacking what he thinks your weakness is. And the worst part is that you were seconds from proving him right about it.”

“What do you mean?”

“I mean that he’s attacking you in your allies. He…well, frankly, if I wasn’t S-Rank, he’d have killed me.” Ellen lifted her robe, showing the bruises and cuts running across her body. My throat tightened, and my eyes flicked away from her and to where the portal had just been. She dropped her robe again and poked me in the side. “No. You’re doing it again. Look at me, Kade. He didn’t kill me, and he didn’t kill Tanat. He’s trying to get you to lose your mind and go after him alone, or push to SS-Rank. If you do that, we won’t be able to trust you not to betray us. None of us will. So you’ll be alone, just like Eugene wants you to be.”

I finally pulled my eyes away from Ellen’s injuries and the now-vanished golden portal and looked around. Ellen was right. The portal world was…shockingly intact. The Lightning Tower had borne the brunt of the fighting, and it teetered slightly as, overhead, the Eye of the Storm began to fall apart. But the earthwork ramparts and lightning rods were all intact save the single path the God of Thunder had carved through them, and he’d retreated in the same direction.

For the first time in forever, air entered my lungs slowly, and I forced myself to breathe. Ellen let me do it, and she didn’t interrupt as I dropped to crossed legs and engaged in one of Jessie’s meditation rituals until I’d slowly calmed. She simply stood there, eyes closed, and began repairing the world around us.

And when I was ready, and I opened my eyes, she was there. “So, Stormblade Noelstra, what now?”

Ten minutes later, the seven of us—Jessie, Jeff, the Portal Tyrant, the Spark of Life, Councilwoman Harriet Myers, and both Ellen and myself—sat around a conference table as Ellen debriefed them on the God of Thunder’s first move.

She kept saying ‘Eugene,’ and worse, emphasizing it. And while I was calmed down, I wasn’t calm. Not really. The storm still raged inside of me, and I wanted nothing more than to unleash it.

“So, that’s the current situation. Kade has some thoughts on how we can turn this around,” Ellen said.

I squeezed my eyes closed and nodded as she sat next to me, fingers running from my shoulder to elbow as she did. Then I nodded. “This is a simple plan.”

“That’s ideal,” the Portal Tyrant said. “Anything complex will just fall apart.”

“Exactly.” I turned to Jeff. He wore his armor’s gauntlets and held the frigid null core in them. “What happened with the Crone?”

“She offered to trade the information for more cores,” Jessie said. “But I reminded her that we were killing an SS-Plus-Ranker for her, and we’d already killed a different one. As far as we were concerned, she owed us.”

“Right. So, the Crone…that’s one scary woman. She said that the key was how I approach using the core. I’m not after power. I’m after the reset to nothing. I can still take advantage of the power spike, though. When I use it, I’ll gain a similar level of power to Ellen when she used the core you brought her. Something like Tides of Darkness, but less offensive, probably. Then, as my core comes apart, I’ll gradually lose power.” Jeff shuddered. “It’ll also be exquisitely painful.”

“Tell them the rest.”

“Getting there, Jessie. To force the process to reset my core instead of just breaking it, I’ll need to withdraw from the fighting the moment I hit the equivalent of C-Rank power. At that point, I’ll need to meditate on my core and what I’d like it to become.”

“So, we have you for how long?” I asked.

He shrugged. “Fifteen minutes? Twenty? Less? The Crone wouldn’t say.”

“Perfect.”

“It is?”

I nodded. “That’s plenty of time for us to use a turbo-big tank. Here’s the plan. The God of Thunder is trying to bait me into his portal world. We’re going to give him exactly what he wants.”

“Really, Kade?” Jessie said. She stood from her wheelchair, fist balled. I flinched, but this time, not from her punch. It was from her look. “You’re gonna throw yourself at the God of Thunder, just like he wants?”

“Yep. But I’m not going alone.” I held up the keystone to my solo portal. “You’re all coming with me. We’re going to attack the God of Thunder right where he’s at his most vulnerable.”

For the next five minutes, I sketched out a map of the God of Thunder’s portal world. We’d almost certainly come out on the flat platform between the dozens of floating towers and pinnacles. I’d open the portal to the Lightning Tower, and from there, my job would be to distract the God of Thunder as long as I could. Any injuries were acceptable. Every second would matter.

The others—Ellen, Sarah, and Terrel, along with a temporarily-strengthened Jeff—would step out into the portal world and start attacking the Mana spires. The more of them we destroyed, the better.

And, when the God of Thunder turned on them, Jeff would tank him for as long as he could while I vanished and searched for Tanat. Or for her body.

Then, we’d retreat. Jeff would leave as soon as he had to, heading through the portal to the Lightning Tower, then immediately out into Phoenix. The others would leave right behind him. Whatever damage we did in there, it’d all help to set the God of Thunder back, and to enrage him. Enraged people—as Ellen had reminded me—made mistakes, and we needed the God of Thunder to make a lot of them if we wanted to win.

We were in check, after all, but if this gambit worked, we could not only avoid a mate, but turn the tables on him.

“It’s a dangerous plan,” Jessie said after I’d finished. She looked like she wanted to say more. Or like she wanted to punch me again. Instead, she sat down heavily. “But I think you’ve thought it through, at least.”

“Since when are you the voice of reason here?” I asked, grinning.

“Don’t give me that, Kade. You’re fully focused on what you’re trying to do. On winning. You’ve always been like that—willing to sacrifice everything to win. And that’s great, but right now, you’re risking so much. Just…”

“I’m going to make sure I keep all my promises, Jessie. I promise.”

Councilwoman Myers had been shockingly quiet. She’d arrived during our fight with the God of Thunder, and she’d mostly been listening as the debrief rolled on and we discussed our plan. But now, the hawklike woman cleared her throat. “I’m more concerned with what happens after you conduct this raid.”

“Well…” I trailed off.

Ellen picked it up. “It depends. We can’t do a great job of predicting the God of Thunder’s reaction. There’s a strong likelihood that he falls into our trap and enters the Lightning Tower again. If he does, he’ll run head-first into the tower—and this time, we’ll have the Overholz Cannons ready—and the Eye of the Storm raging and ready. We think that’s most likely to be his move. However, there’s a chance that he comes through over Phoenix. If that’s the case…”

“If that’s the case, how long do we have?”

I waited. Ellen glanced at me, and I nodded slowly. “We’ll have two minutes to force him to leave before he starts doing irreparable damage to Earth.”

“And can you do that?” The Councilwoman’s eyes narrowed intensely.

I matched them, staring back like a fellow predator. “I believe I’ll be able to. Not by myself, though. Your job is to get the city ready to fight back as hard as it can. And it won’t be a clean victory. No matter what, there’s going to be damage.”

“What’s your gimmick?” Terrel asked.

Instead of answering, I just smiled. “Ellen and I are about to go work on that. Be ready for this in one hour. We’ll be back.”

I stood, and so did Ellen. She followed me out the door, then pulled up next to me. “Do you think they bought it?” she asked the moment the door shut behind us.

“Yes. They’re all-in on the plan.”

“Even though they saw all the places it can go wrong?”

“The places it will go wrong?” I nodded slowly. “Yes. They saw them, but they were supposed to. No plan survives contact with a portal, and the monster we’re up against is one with plans of his own. Speaking of which…”

“Yeah, speaking of which, let’s go pay Magda one more visit.” Ellen’s eyes narrowed, and for a moment, her aura pulsed out a wave of fear.

I grinned. It wasn’t a friendly one—but then again, this wouldn’t be a friendly visit.
