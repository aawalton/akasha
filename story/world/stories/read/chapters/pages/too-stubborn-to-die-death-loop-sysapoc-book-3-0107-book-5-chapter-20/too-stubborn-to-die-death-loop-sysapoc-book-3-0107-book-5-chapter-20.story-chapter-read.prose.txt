The goblin brothers were more preoccupied with the recently cleansed undead armor, and were going over their readings and checking countless things back and forth. When Aaron finished up with the lawyer, Gork came, wandering back from a quick experiment, wiping gunk from his hands onto his clothing. “You’s gave us lots to do. You need to envision drawing your mana out at your anchors, but without expelling it. Then, we store it, but you’ll need something to store it in,” Ernest explained.

As he spoke, the cowboy replicated the practice, slowly drawing mana out so that Aaron could sense what he was doing, and storing his mana into a bullet.

“See. Pick something you’re familiar with. It’ll help, probably.”

Ernest went on to explain that anything could be imbued with mana.

Aaron decided to use a steak from his storage. Food was near and dear to his heart, and it had been cooked by his hands and, in a sense, already imbued with his mana. He didn’t know if that would help, but it couldn’t hurt, right?

As Aaron focused, he sat down and meditated, listening and sensing Ernest’s instructions, using [ Gorgon’s Soul Vortex ] to better read the fluctuations of mana pouring out from Ernest.

“I see. Fascinating,” Aaron said as he tried to mimic the practice, gradually drawing mana out from his core, circulating it, and holding it in place as he tried to imbue his steak with the mana.

It was no easy task, though. Perhaps because it didn’t come as naturally to him. He was a melee combatant, after all, and a hand-to-hand combatant at that.

Imbuing seemed steered toward ranged types, essentially making it the complete opposite of what Aaron was naturally drawn to, and so despite all of his talent, he meditated for hours out front of the gunsmith as Ernest instructed and provided examples.

“You’re getting close,” Ernest said. “Just like that. Keep going. Push yourself. Remain focused. Don’t let it crack.”

Aaron’s brow creased as he tried to harden a mana shell around the steak with external mana to try and stop it from escaping the meat, and focused desperately on keeping it from dissipating and failing his attempt. As he did this, he poured more and more mana into it, as if tempering the mana casing that would keep the mana within the steak, but like water seeping through the cracks of one’s fingers, it was no easy task.

His style was incredibly wasteful, and soon Aaron was forced to eat and mend his soul as he increased the power of his core. Mana radiated from him, and he started to convert other energy sources and draw in ambient aether to further fuel his clumsy attempts at creating the mana-infused steak.

“You can have a break, you know?” Ernest said, voice wavering slightly. “Take your time. This clearly isn’t natural for you.”

But Aaron didn’t listen, even as a crowd of nervous onlookers began to fill the streets around him.

Instead, he just pushed his core harder, and soon arcs of mana were lashing out around him, streaming across the ground as his eyes turned white, and absurd amounts of mana were wasted, expelled into the atmosphere as he threw one layer after another onto the stake in a desperate attempt to stop the mana from leaking.

Everything about what he was doing felt wrong. This mana anchor was almost completely alien to his style. But he didn’t give up. And started to pull insights from his [ Bombardier Fist ] Skill, using the mana makeup of the Skill to guide him in storing and keeping the mana within the steak.

This helped. But he was still a cat trying to swim, and it was all kinds of wrong. Even a melee combatant that relied on weapons would likely be naturally more inclined to use imbued anchors, and so it took a goliathan effort to push himself toward his goal.

But if Aaron was one thing, it was stubborn. And he refused to give up, pressing forth even as his soul cracked under the pressure.
