Nyoka, Ferris, and the two familiars moved from the dinner table to chairs by the fireplace.

Nyofu curled around Nyoka’s chair, while Jade sat in Ferris’ lap. Mother Lily left them with a fresh pot of tea, which Ferris poured for both of them. He offered to pour a cup for Nyofu, but the snake shook her head with a somehow gentle hiss.

“Thank you for visiting, Adept Nyoka,” Ferris said, “I would appreciate any advice you could give. My clan has no knowledge of familiar bonds, and you are the first cultivator I have met that has one.”

“Adept Ferris… Bluereef. I have not heard of Clan Bluereef before.” Nyoka said, after taking a sip of her tea.

“We live in a smaller bastion nearby. Scalefort is the closest bastion large enough to trade supplies. My clan has a single martial tradition, and it was unlikely to help me with my leg. Hollowfield has been kind enough to provide me an alternate path, but much of it is confusing to me.”

“I can answer some questions,” Nyoka fixed him with a pointed stare, “but understand that I do this for Mother Lily. Her husband was a comrade of mine, and she asked this of me.”

“Why?” It wasn’t one of the many questions Ferris had planned to ask, but it slipped out. “Why did she do this for me?”

“That,” Nyoka smiled, a bit softer this time, “would be her story. We are here to discuss yours. All that I ask is, if she calls on you for similar advice for a youngster, you accommodate her.”

As Ferris organized his thoughts, he considered the dinner discussion with Brix. He hoped it had helped her, but she had shared many particulars of her cultivation. He was reminded of the records of Janice Landsworn, and the other familiar bond observation journals. He had sometimes wondered how they had such detailed information, but if the cultivators discussed their plans for advancement so plainly… he could understand it. If Brix had stayed completely silent, she would have missed an opportunity to receive advice. Was it a constant balance between asking for help versus revealing too much?

He wanted to ask Nyoka a lot of questions, but he needed to be careful not to overshare his own secrets. The problem was, he didn’t even know what details were important to hide.

Information had value. His entire life, he’d been told the importance of keeping clan secrets. Not that I even met anyone from outside the clan until leaving home, he thought. He was in a contract to share certain details with Hollowfield, not Adept Nyoka.

Additionally, his uncle had directly ordered him to hide as much of his combat ability as possible. Some things were impossible to keep secret, like that he’d bonded a slime. That didn’t diminish the value of a trump card, or even just obscuring the limits of what his obvious abilities could do.

Nyoka appeared friendly, but...

I just met her today, he thought. For all I know, she sells details of other retainers on the side. It’s not nice to suspect her without even knowing her, but that’s the point. I don’t know her.

Years from now, he may run into someone unfriendly in the rifts. Fighting an unknown opponent was dramatically harder. Many stories warned against careless bragging ending up being a fatal mistake.

At the same time, if her advice helped him advance, Ferris would be stronger. It may be worth the risk.

Ferris decided to start with something he strongly suspected was common knowledge. Adept Theodore White, when teaching Ferris how to refine spirit gems, had expected him to already know how to remove the pollution.

“Does a familiar’s core need to be refined, like a spirit gem?”

“Yes,” Nyoka replied evenly, “but beyond that, it needs more. Tell me, Adept Ferris, do you know the steps involved in forging and enchanting a weapon suitable for bonding?”

“No,” Ferris shook his head, “just the general idea.” He knew it was a core taken from a monster, embedded in a forged weapon, and then enchanted.

“Then, I would recommend you learn,” Nyota said. “A bonded weapon gives the cultivator advantages a familiar lack, but these are not permanently out of reach. Think of it as a spiritual forging you must do yourself, instead of receiving someone else’s work.”

Ferris hadn’t considered that viewpoint, but it made sense. He was still worried about hurting Jade, but experimentation always carried risk. He would just have to chip away some of the black rock and see what happens.

“Bonded weapons have advantages, but the reverse must be true as well,” Ferris said, “what do familiars have that I can expect?”

“You will have to learn to notice them yourself if you want to grow,” Nyoka said with a shake of her head. “Let us play a game. I will tell you something helpful for each advantage you can name.”

Ferris tried to keep a straight face, but this ‘game’ all but confirmed his suspicions.

He had heard the ‘figure it out for yourself’ line often in his life. Sometimes, it was said with good intentions. Other times…

Nyoka was doing Mother Lily a favor, but she was also likely taking the opportunity to pump him for information. She gave the first answer for free, and then hid her motive behind real advice.

At the same time, this was an opportunity. Could Ferris learn enough to be worth the risk of exposing something? Could he get more than he gave? It was… exciting.

Ferris considered what he could possibly know that Nyoka would want. He was inexperienced and new, but he had a bond with a different type of monster than her. Bonds were for life; even a large group like Hollowfield had to search for people willing to try new ones. People like Ferris.

Nyoka likely wanted information on Jade’s slimespace, it was the only thing he knew that she didn’t. She said her family had a tradition of familiar bonds. Whether they focused on snakes or familiar bonds in general, any opportunity to learn about other types could help them.

With a guess of what Nyoka wanted, Ferris decided to start as far away from it as possible.

“I have been investigating the clan’s available cultivation advancement methods,” Ferris said, “it appears that many of them could benefit from two minds. An Adept with a bonded weapon won’t have anyone helping them.”

“Yes,” Nyoka nodded, “while a bonded weapon starts with spiritual constructions already completed by the crafter, a familiar bond can advance faster over time. Twice the potential speed, more or less depending on the method, but also twice the potential for setbacks and bottlenecks. Expect to need more initial resources as well, although they are almost always worth it. Look for ways to become more than the sum of your parts, together. If you can’t be a team player, your life will be harder, although that applies to almost anyone.”

So far, Ferris had been mostly doing his tasks while Jade followed along. The slime helped wherever there was an obvious way, like fighting demons and refining spirit gems. They were able to harvest the qi from the Digest River together, where individually they would both have failed. However, he hadn’t even tried to give Jade their own tasks to work on. Ferris wasn’t even sure how he could, maybe it would be easier as Jade grew more intelligent?

“As for my side of the game,” Nyoka raised a single finger to her mouth, dragging things out, “have you learned how aspected abilities work?”

“No,” Ferris shook his head, “just a warning, that they require touching the core.”

“Yes, but consider this: while I may have trouble reaching Nyofu’s core,” the woman placed a hand on her snake familiar, “she is always touching it.”

“So,” Ferris said, glancing at Jade, “it is better to focus on aspected abilities performed by the familiar?”

“Indeed,” she said, “it is like you said, a mind is an advantage. Bonded weapons can’t use their own abilities, but a familiar is much more capable.”

He thought back to some of the abilities he had read about. He couldn’t use an ability like Acid Bolt until he had Acid Resistance and could touch Jade’s core without losing his hand. Instead, he thought, we develop Acid Bolt in the slimespace, and Jade uses it?

It wouldn’t work for abilities that depended on a human body, like the one that turned blood acidic. Still, it was a valid workaround to the problem.

“One more thing,” she said conspiratorially, leaning in closer and lowering her voice, “I cannot say I know for sure, but it is rumored that all core bonds, familiar or not, will eventually gain intelligence. It may take years and higher advancement. At some point, the weapon they are swinging around will wake up and start thinking for itself. If anyone gives you a hard time over bonding a sentient creature, just smile and think about their surprise.” Nyoka winked at him and leaned back.

Ferris had no idea what to think about that. If bonded core weapons gained intelligence, then did that make them monsters? Were all bonds familiar bonds, in the end? Or was it something about what happened when a spiritspace became a soulspace? How did that work for the bonded weapon? The questions had interesting implications, but they didn’t really help him in the short term.

The information about aspects was useful, but he wasn’t sure if it was valuable. Not that he didn’t appreciate her time and help, but he wanted to at least know if she was pumping him for information without giving any back. It was vague, and based on concepts he could learn from the library. If Nyoka’s words were obvious to anyone who had created an aspected ability, then had she given him anything of real value? He now knew not to avoid having Jade learn aspected abilities, and that was something.

Perhaps she’d seen him avoid giving her anything and decided to respond in kind. If he led her closer to what she wanted, would she give him something he couldn’t learn on his own?

“I have been considering the ability seeds from the Core Tree. If I had bonded an axe, I would expect to receive an ability to cut. With a familiar, I expect abilities related to their body. This could be useful, as I could develop those abilities in my soul as well.”

Ferris had no idea what would happen if he took something like the Grow or the Jump ability seed and planted it in his soul. He knew there were ways to learn abilities from a familiar; that was the entire goal of his research project to heal his leg. But how did it all work?

He had seen that certain abilities had restrictions at the library, but did that matter after he had the Core Tree’s seed? He was hoping Nyoka would tell him.

Nyoka didn’t respond for a minute, staring at Ferris with a quiet intensity. He began fidgeting in place, uncomfortable with the sudden silence. When she did speak, it came in a low, careful tone.

“I would be careful with that,” Nyoka blinked, and suddenly her eyes were completely red, with vertical black pupils, like a snake. “It is a tricky thing to accomplish. If you succeed, you may end up more monster than you expect.” She blinked, and her eyes were back to normal, yet a chill persisted down Ferris’s spine.

Ferris took a calming breath, then collected himself. He tried to focus on his next question. She was likely trying to put him off balance, so he’d let something slip.

“Personal demons,” he said, “I have been told they are a rewarding challenge to overcome. Twice the challenge would give twice the reward.”

Nyoka snorted, which caught Ferris by surprise. It was different than the serious demeanor she’d held so far.

“Few would call that an advantage,” she said, “but yes. The soul of a familiar will spawn demons like a cultivator. The damned things will eventually travel between souls, so be ready for that.”

Ferris didn’t like the sound of that. He had enough trouble from the demons in his own soulspace, he didn’t need them in the slimespace. At least they would have as much trouble with the acid as he did.

“The soul of a familiar may not be forged and enchanted like a bonded weapon, but mine started much fuller of life than expected.” Ferris felt the slimespace was a bit too full of life with the ants, clams, birds, and fish.

“Yes,” Nyoka said, “the spirit animals are vital to some of the more unique abilities familiars can support. Cultivators who bond a weapon must work to create an ecosystem to support natural life, where we don’t need one. Here is a hint: they may start as mere copies of the familiar, but with time and study, many variations will appear. Nyofu’s soul now contains thousands of different types of snakes.”

Ferris was… confused. He took a sip of tea to give him time to think.

The term ‘spirit animal’ had been mentioned once in the book on familiar bonds. It was praised as an advantage, but no details were given. At this point, he was pretty sure the strange grey ants, birds, and clams were spirit animals.

But Nyoka claimed the spirit animals would be copies of Jade.

He had so many questions.

Could there be a colony of slimes living in the slimespace, hidden away? If so, what were all the other creatures?

Had all of Jade’s spirit slimes been killed? Was there a spiritual technique required to get more? Were they ever hostile, like the fish that attacked Jade? Had the spirit animals attacked the ants, and been killed by them?

Unfortunately, asking the wrong question could reveal more than he wanted. Ferris didn’t know what was normal, so he might accidentally ask something obvious. It would be smarter to wait, and either ask his uncle or purchase information at the library.

“Qi cultivation,” Ferris said instead, changing the subject, “a familiar can help gather qi, while a bonded weapon would just sit idle.”

“Yes,” Nyoka nodded, “this is one of the primary benefits of a familiar. It is regrettable that maintaining the population of spirit animals requires so much of a familiar’s qi. There is a delicate balance between using the qi to advance faster or support a larger population. My advice would be: leave the qi your familiar produces alone at first, but slowly find ways to take some for yourself after you have experience delving.”

Once again, Ferris was confused. He had been taking all the qi. Nothing in the slimespace had touched the qi chunks. Was Ferris accidentally killing off the ants and other creatures by taking their qi?

This was exactly the kind of question Ferris didn’t want to ask, as it might reveal more than he wanted. At least Ferris was now forewarned about the potential consequences of over-harvesting qi from the slimespace. It would be a disaster if something happened to the clams in the river.

The conversation’s balance had shifted, putting Ferris at a disadvantage. Nyoka and Nyofu staring at him didn’t help. There was more that he wanted to learn, but he had lost any semblance of control. He would rather research and discover for himself than reveal something unintentionally. Nyoka was helping him, and he was grateful… but she knew the value of her advice, and Ferris did not.

“That is all that comes to mind,” Ferris said, deciding to stop. He had learned some areas to investigate, and that was enough. It was hard to tell what Nyoka had learned from him. Hopefully, it wasn’t more than he wanted to give.

“Very well,” Nyoka said, finishing her tea. After putting down the cup, she held his gaze for a second, as if deciding something. “You have chosen a hard path, without an established tradition to guide you. If… when you stumble, you may reach out to me for advice.”

“Thank you, Adept Nyoka,” Ferris said, bowing his head as she stood to leave. It was a kind offer, although he feared she’d only made it based on wanting to learn what he was hiding. That would mean she knew he was hiding something, although hopefully not what. She might know it was related to spirit animals based on his reaction.

Nyoka and Nyofu left after saying goodbye to Mother Lily, and Ferris slowly finished his tea in the common room. He wouldn’t be able to cultivate that night, so he relaxed. There was a lot to consider.

He had never understood the strange creatures in the slimespace, but now he would need to prioritize studying them.

At least he knew what to research next: spirit animals.
