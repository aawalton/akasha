Noah gaped at the newcomer. “You’re… The Devourer?”

“I am. More or less.”

“My ability?”

“Aspect.” He made a disgusted face. “Abilities are a much weaker, much more transient thing.”

“And you’re not the manifestation of my Shadow Heart?”

“No. He’s in there too, but he doesn’t need to come out for this. His job is to keep you from getting lost.” The Devourer grinned. “My job is to knock some sense into you, but first we need to move. Though I must say, you collect the strangest of pets. First that worm you call Tony, and now this…thing."

"Tony is not a worm,” Noah protested.

"I am not a pet," Dylan squeaked in the same moment.

"He technically is,” the Devourer said with a smile. “Are you bringing the pet, or no?”

Dylan scowled, but didn’t resist when Noah knelt to pick him up.

The Devourer nodded, turned around, and began walking through the strange environment.

Dylan bounced up Noah’s arm to sit on his shoulder and stared after the departing Devourer. “So that’s what we were supposed to be keeping in check. I wonder what would happen if we just let him go.”

“He is part of me,” Noah said, slightly uncertainly.

“Best part of you,” the Devourer called back. “Though you haven’t done too badly yourself.”

Noah had no idea what to say to that. He started walking.

All he could see all around was just this endless sort of slightly grey-black. Footsteps made no sound, and that silence was enough for him to feel for the second heartbeat overlaid on his own.

Noah was dead, his own heart unmoving, but that only meant his shadow's heartbeat was all the more prominent. Rather than layered over his own, it beat with a steady tug off somewhere to his right, the direction he arbitrarily thought of as southwest.

Without thinking, he started to turn that direction.

"Hey! You're going the wrong way!" The Devourer pointed firmly off to the left, the direction Noah had decided to think of as east. "We need to go that way. You're heading for the exit, but our business here isn't done."

“I like the sound of an exit,” Dylan said hastily. “Ignore the creepy copy.”

“I’m not a copy,” the Devourer said casually. “I’m the original. Though speaking of ignoring influences, you should be more careful with your associates, Noah Brown. There's no way that you should have been able to reach this place. The number of people working against you is unprecedented. This was not chance."

"Obviously not. It's what I chose to do."

"Or it's what instructions you were given."

"Given by whom? Septah? I asked a question and she gave me an answer."

"Really? You call this an answer? You asked a question and she set you on this path. She sent you after something that could destroy you, and you followed without question."

"I chose this commitment for myself. I'm the one who volunteered to join this war."

The Devourer laughed. "You have no idea who you're even fighting, do you?"

"The cults who regularly harm, enslave, and destroy the lives of innocents. Primordial deities who give power indiscriminately to people who abuse it. Everyone who creates a world where pain and fear and exploiting one another is commonplace."

"And you think you can eliminate such things from the world? You are more naive than I imagined." The Devourer's smile turned pitying. "But I can see how desperately you want to believe the world can be changed. I also see how vulnerable that makes you to your so-called friends and allies. All they need to do is promise you the impossible and you'll dance to their whims without question."

"So?"

The Devourer paused, taken aback by Noah's nonchalance.

"Why would I care who's in charge?" Noah pressed on. "If we're all working toward a better future for everyone, it doesn't matter who's making the plan."

"You wouldn't feel betrayed if you learned your allies were manipulating you?"

"Erandir is a beautiful world of wondrous things. If I can be a part of making it safer and more wondrous, why would I not want to give everything I could to seeing it through?"

"Even if it costs you everything?"

Noah shrugged. "Hey, don't make me sound too much like a self-sacrificing saint. I still want to live, obviously. If I were really the kind to throw away my life for a cause I wouldn't have lasted a day on Erandir."

"But you will give it away day by day."

"It's my life to live. Who are you to say what I should or shouldn't spend my time on?"

"Who is Septah to say what you should do? Who is Zax?"

"People trying to make a difference."

"Trying."

Noah exhaled and shook his head. "First they're exerting too much control over me, now they're ineffectual. Pick a lane, dude. Unless you're just messing with me, in which case, stop wasting all our time."

"Oh, you have a great deal of other things to be doing with this time, do you?"

"I could be getting to know Dylan better rather than being interrogated by my own powers."

"I am not your power."

"Sure seems it."

"You are my vessel. You can borrow my power for now, but that does not make it yours."

"Says the amnesiac dead god."

"I am no more dead than you are."

Noah laughed and gestured around them. "Did you not notice this is the Underworld? We're both dead."

"And yet neither of us is."

Noah stopped walking.

The Devourer went a little way further before noticing and turning back questioningly.

"Why are you here? Really?" Noah asked. "Because it feels like you're trying to make me doubt myself and you should know that's not going to work. Self doubt isn't my kind of thing."

"Good. You'll need that confidence if you're going to survive what's coming."

"What does that mean? What do you know about what's coming?"

"There's an enemy here that has been searching for you for a long time, Noah Brown, and as soon as I leave you alone and unguarded, he'll come for you."

"You expect me to believe you're protecting me? First the Void, now you..."

The Devourer turned half away. "You are my vessel. The Void wants you for itself. The Reaper will seek to destroy you. The Watcher won't be far behind. It is in everyone's best interest for us to be in alignment."

Noah didn't respond. They resumed walking.

"I will say," Noah finally said, "of all the things I expected to find in the Underworld, my own power manifesting as yet another clone wasn't on the list."

"When you are dead, all that you are is separated. When it is something not entirely yourself, all the more so. There is no Astral Script here, no external additions. Only what you are."

"And what you are, apparently."

The Devourer smiled and nodded. "We are the same and not the same. I do not remember how this came to be."

"I'm just glad you're reasonable. The way people were talking, I sort of expected you to be an uncontrollable monster."

"Is that why you try so hard to hold me in? To prove you can control the uncontrollable?"

"I don't want to hurt anyone who doesn't deserve it."

"There is no such thing as deserving. I am hunger, they are prey."

"You're wrong."

"And this is why we are both here. Until we align, we cannot unify." The Devourer put his ghostly hands in his ghostly pockets and continued walking. "Be prepared.  This next region is not friendly to intruders. Even though it all looks the same, if you sense it carefully enough, there's a lot of difference within the Underworld."

Before Noah could ask what he meant, the featureless flat emptiness peaked. To his surprise, Noah found they'd been walking up a steep hill, though without the orientation of weight he'd not noticed the slope.

They crested the ridge and finally an actual landscape spread out below them. A river valley, grey roads and grey buildings amid lifeless grey ground, but even with the eerie lack of shadows even this much variation was a relief after the unvarying emptiness up until now.

“One of the three rivers that form the Underworld,” the Devourer said. “If you have any sense, appreciate it. The rivers of Death's Mana are what carry all the dead souls towards the Reaper's domain, because obviously the Reaper won't go around collecting each soul one by one."

The river itself was mesmerizing, dark enough to form dramatic contrast with the ground on either side even without the glowing blue-white wisps running through the water. But Noah's attention was drawn back to the buildings.

Dark walls of varying shapes and sizes and heights, all clearly forming ruins. They all had cracks that spread across them, and they all looked so old that Noah wondered just when they'd been made, perhaps before the foundation of civilization itself. He wondered why those walls had been built, why they were formed in this specific shape, why they were now broken, what could have broken them.

"Who built these?" Noah asked breathlessly. "What is this place?"

"Oh, once upon a time, long, long ago," the Devourer said theatrically, "there were civilizations that were not only strong enough to navigate to the Underworld when they were alive, but they also fiercely worshipped Death. The Reaper was young then and foolish, eager for any company at all. So when these people came here willingly, they were welcomed with open arms. But even creatures as strong as them would be too corrupted when in close proximity to someone as powerful as the Reaper.”

“Corrupted how?”

“They went mad, sooner or later. All of them. Most of them, the Reaper killed personally. A few slowly withered." The Devourer walked up to the closest wall and ran his hand on top of it, almost gently. "A few of them I consumed. But is the distinction really important?"

"You are horrifying," Noah said.

"Noah." The Devourer blinked at him. "I am also you. So I must advocate for more positive self-talk. We aren't horrifying. We are this powerful."

"So," Dylan finally chose that moment to speak, and he sounded almost irritated, "the Devourer is displaying an unsurprisingly biased perspective here. The civilization that chose to come down to the Underworld chose to do so because this was before the Divine Hierarchy was formed, and the Watcher's influence on the upper ground was immense. If they had stayed there, they would have gone crazier even sooner, and in a much less pleasant way than they did down here with the Reaper. These people came here and the Reaper welcomed them not because they were both sentimental fools, but because the alternative was having their souls consumed by the Watcher. In my opinion, it would be better to have your soul consumed by your friend here than that."

Noah and the Devourer both turned to stare at the tiny void creature.

“What? I'm technically dead, so I no longer have any contracts binding me. I can freely explain ancient history without getting penalized for it.” Then Dylan paused, and his expression scrunched up even further as he realized what he’d just said. "I can't believe I'm handing over information for free now,” he grumbled. “Normally, I would have fleeced someone out of so much before I gave them even this much information. Hanging around with you is clearly corrupting me."

The Devourer looked at him with the same grin, but something in its expression made Noah shiver. "I tried to drink one of the rivers once. It was very tasty—almost like chocolate, if your memories of Earth are anything to go by—but I didn’t manage to empty it before the Reaper caught me and that was the end of my visit. A fascinating taste nonetheless."

"Death's Mana tastes like chocolate?"

"Not precisely but close enough. Eat enough things and you’ll find many of them share similar flavors. There are very few things that are unpalatable, if you're willing to eat. Souls, in particular, are a delicacy. The taste of a soul’s memory, of emotions, every mark that existence has left on them and everything that they were able to gain. Though I will say, the real delicacy is..." And then it trailed off, its grin fading a bit. "Well, you'll understand eventually. No point spoiling it for you."

"You know, I prefer when you just tell me things."

"Now where would the fun be in that?" the Devourer asked. "I've got to do something to amuse myself in this space where there’s so little to eat. There are few enough surprises in an immortal lifetime, watching your reactions when you finally try it for yourself will be fun."

Noah walked down to the ruins, taking in the sight in front of him that felt somber and yet so very grand. The place was sullen in a beautiful sort of way, and yet the entire atmosphere felt eerie. When he touched one of the walls, reaching out with his left hand, he felt his heart skip a beat. Which was odd, considering he didn’t have a heartbeat at the moment.

You do realize that walking straight up to ancient ruins made by an ancient civilization that you're not aware of is a quite risky endeavor, yes? A voice echoed within his head, so familiar and so close to his own inner voice that hearing it say something he didn't expect felt deeply disorienting.

“Shadow?”

Who else? You thought I was done for and fully gone just because we merged? That's very sweet of you, but that's not how it works.

"So the Protector returns," the Devourer muttered under its breath. "I did wonder if and when you would surface. Or if you would at all."

Of course I would. Don't think for a second that you can just get away with what you're doing right now just because the Underworld stripped away enough of the Astral Script's framework that Noah’s soul can split into parts once again. The second I get the chance, I'm going to pull you back in.

"You say that as if you can," the Devourer retorted. “Leave him to me, and I promise not to consume you. We can part ways amiably. Does it not sound appealing, having one of the Outer Gods’ blessings?"

No. No, it does not. I have no interest in surrendering.

The Devourer shrugged and walked into the ruins.

They might have laid traps. Shadow warned. The Devourer might intentionally be messing with you and watching with glee as you mess up and step in one of these traps.

Noah paused. “Is that likely?”

Shadow considered this for a long moment. I don’t know. As arrogant as he may be, he does still need you. Being trapped in the Underworld with only the Reaper for company would probably not be a good situation. From what little we know about the place. But who knows what lengths he’ll go to in his attempts to reshape you in his image. He wants to get rid of me, at least.

Noah laughed and followed the Devourer down toward the city. “I’ll take my chances. I’m very good at staying myself.”
