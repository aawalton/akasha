Floorboards creaked as I returned to the kitchen. “Will he be alright?”

Brother Pel was cutting away Gil’s ragged shirt with a small pair of shears.

Sister Embra sat nearby, a potted bowl wedged between her thighs. She held a wooden spoon in one plump fist and was mixing together a pale brown paste that smelled like honey and earth. “Our Master Gil’s a tough old boot, Miss Brin. He’ll be right as rain. But you,” she pointed the spoon at me, “Are supposed to be wrapped up in a blanket.”

“Thank you for your concern, but I’ll be alright. I really need to be going.”

“Leaving,” the stablemaster rasped. His watery, weary eyes fixed on me with an intensity that was unnerving. Pleading.

“Soon. There’s something I…” I chewed my lower lip, watching the two clerics closely. Renner’s warning rang like a gong through the back of my mind. They seemed so friendly, so genuine, but… how could I be sure?

And the possibility that they were somehow involved in all this wasn’t my only concern.

Would it put them in danger if I told them what happened? If there’s other pacted in town and the clerics start asking questions…

The thought made me nauseous. I chose my words carefully, watching Gil’s ashen face for any kind of reaction. “Do people ever go missing in Snowmelt?”

Embra’s brows rose. “Missing, dear? What a thing to ask. Of course not.” She hesitated, then asked gently, “Did something happen on the road, Miss Brin?”

I didn’t answer, just held Gil’s gaze as he shook his head back and forth. Begging me to be silent.

Her tone became thoughtful, “We’ve heard there’s been some danger to the east. Monsters prowling at night, and folks dealing with them. Horrible.” She set the bowl down, then dipped both hands into the mixture. “I’m sorry for whatever trouble you might’ve seen, dear, but our town is very safe. You’re safe.”

I sighed, managing a weak half-smile. “This does seem like a very nice place.” I’m only half-lying. The town itself seems very charming. The monster and the nefarious going-ons in the night, less so.

She bobbed her head and began spreading the brown paste over Gil’s exposed flesh, murmuring soothing little sounds whenever he winced. “It is, dear. May I ask where you came from?”

“Fellbrook. My friends and I stopped here last night. Friend,” I amended quickly, blood rushing to my face.

‘It ends with you tied up and gagged.’

My pulse quickened. “J-just one friend. But I haven’t been able to find her this morning. Her name’s Teela. She’s about my age, very thin, with short red hair.” I watched their faces closely, hoping for a glimpse of recognition and praying I wouldn’t see anything damning.

Pel shook his head, carefully measuring crushed mint from a jar.

Embra shrugged. “You’re the first new face I’ve seen in months. But I’m sure your friend is just fine, wherever she may have gone.”

I swallowed, turning towards the door. “Right. Thank you, but I should be-”

“Hold.” Brother Pel’s quiet command stopped me in my tracks. He stood, walking towards me with his lips thinned and forehead wrinkled… and one liver-spotted hand undid the clasp of his faded gray cloak. He swept the garment over my shoulders, grunted, and tottered back to his herbs.

His wife winked at me. “Always the gentleman. Don’t worry about returning it, dear. You just stay warm. Alright?”

I blinked back the sudden prick of tears. “A-alright. Thank you.”

As I headed back out into the bitter air, I could just feel Gil’s eyes following me. I stood for a long moment, breath puffing out as silver mist, and considered my next course of action.

I could go to the Watch. Renner seemed convinced that would end badly, but… perhaps if I’m very careful with what I say. Perhaps if I don’t mention Fae or pacts or anything so grim, but just say that my friend is missing. And then ask very casually if other people have ever gone missing, and if there’s been any trails or clues.

But Embra said Snowmelt was a very safe place. Unless she was lying. Or perhaps her memories have been muddled, like Master Lewen. Unless he was lying, too.

I groaned into my palm.

This is impossible! I don’t know who to trust! And if I don’t know who’s lying and who’s earnest…

No. Calm down. Focus. How do I find this thing?

Gil said he left me. Left me, implying that he was originally going to take me somewhere. Does he bring people to the monster? That’s certainly what it sounded like. But… then he left. He couldn’t go through with it, or he was worried about Renner interfering. So instead of us going to the Fae… the Fae came to us.

To the window.

I sprinted towards the inn. By the time I reached it the sun was higher, though still dim behind a haze of clouds. The air had warmed up, and there were quite a few people bustling by and chatting happily on the streets.

I hurried around the back of the building, glancing behind me with wide, furtive eyes. I had gotten several strange looks while running, but no one seemed to be taking untoward interest in my behavior.

Where was our room? The birch tree is over there, at the base of that hill, and it was right outside our window, so… there.

My gaze latched onto the glass, then traced the half-frozen ground for footprints or tracks. Nothing.

Salt. I suppose a trail was too much to hope for. But, let’s see… the hill goes that way, and it looks like the town wall crests at the top. If there’s a monster nearby, it would have had to find shelter from the sun. Unless it went back to the Fae realm. But… if that’s what happened, then there’s no hope of me following it. Not awake, at least. I couldn’t possibly fall asleep right now, and I don’t think bludgeoning myself into unconsciousness is the wisest strategy.

So. I’ll assume it stayed here, in the mortal realm. And unless it’s hiding out in someone’s cellar… then it’s outside the town. Maybe in that direction. Maybe.

I stared at the top of the hill. It was a lot of speculation on my part, and didn’t feel like much to go on. But I didn’t have much else.

A whiff of festering meat and dead plants hit my nose. I covered my mouth, glaring up at the window.

So much for the rotbutter. It didn’t do a salted thing.

The yellow smear resembled old, congealed fat. I frowned. The rotbutter was far above my head, but the pungent odor was coming from somewhere lower. Closer.

My eyes trailed down and around, then grew round. It was the tree. It looked normal enough in most places; scaly gray bark, swaying branches, knots and divots patterned all around.

But… I stepped closer, following the smell. There. Just above my eyeline there was a patch of wood, a bit wider than my head, that looked sunken and almost wet. Dark liquid glistened within the cracks of rotten bark. For a moment I froze, afraid that if I looked any harder I would see bloody crimson.

Breathe. Breathe. Teela needs me.

I stood on my tiptoes, squinting. The smell hit me full in the face. Rotting plants. Dead meat. Decay. It almost looked like viscous, black oil had seeped into the warped wood.

Well, it definitely isn’t blood.

Unless it’s the tree’s blood.

The thought was so sudden, so bizarre, that I huffed a laugh. It was a ridiculous idea. The kind of thing someone would make up in a frightening children’s tale. Trees had sap and resin. Trees didn’t bleed.

And yet… I couldn’t tear my eyes away from the glistening dark, and couldn’t quite chase the thought away.

It smells familiar. Like the rotbutter, and like… like… yesterday.

Like the lifeless birds, blackened leaves, and fetid patch of grass we’d stumbled upon.

So… the same creature that was wandering around a giant bird-killing trap is creeping into town and stealing people?

My face was so close that I was gagging on the stench. But I could also make it out more clearly. A chill crawled up my spine.

There was a clear figure to the form. One end of the warped, damp wood tapered to a curve. In the middle it became broader, rounder. The other end narrowed, stretching out into five spindles that curled around the trunk.

It was a handprint.
