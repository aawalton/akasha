Kyle thanked the attendant, then gathered his few belongings into the pack.

[ARE YOU READY TO GO, DR. MAYHEW?]

“As ready as I’m going to be.  No matter what happens at the Tournament, I expect things to remain tense here for a while.  We won’t be able to stay for long once it’s wrapped up, so hopefully Mangalica either makes their move, or waits long enough for Duroc to keep making progress in mid-tier C Grade.”

[THAT WOULD CERTAINLY MAKE A MEANINGFUL DIFFERENCE.]

After his time with the orc back on Earth, and then seeing other D Grades in action in the Collective, Kyle truly believed that Duroc was an elite among elites.  His friend’s intuition for combat and battlefield control was only rivaled by Arhades among the D Grades Kyle met, and he expected that talent to keep carrying Duroc a long, long way.

The biggest advantages Mangalica had against Duroc right now were momentum and levels.  The other War Chieftains were still on edge, and unwilling to make any moves against the new upstart.  However, the Tournament of Ascension would go a long way toward quelling those fears.  Kyle expected that seeing Duroc compete against them and fight around their level would help to provide some much-needed stability.  And the longer Mangalica waited to make a move, the more Duroc would be able to make up the level difference.

From Kyle’s perspective, Mangalica was likely about halfway through mid-tier C Grade at the most.  Powerful, certainly, but not insurmountably so.  Given the oddities with his path, Kyle also expected the orc to hit some pretty challenging bottlenecks trying to advance to high-tier C Grade.

Given the legacy and secret training ground available to Duroc, he expected his friend would not share that struggle.  The longer Mangalica waited, the more powerful his competitor would become.  That meant the most likely time for him to make a move would be at or shortly after the Tournament of Ascension.  If his intuition was correct, Kyle would be more than happy to help his friend in the ensuing fight, and hopefully fuel his own breakthrough to mid-tier C Grade.

Kyle could feel Duroc’s aura as he walked into the meeting room.  All of the War Chieftain's advisors hustled about, shuffling documents and parcels as they did.

“Kyle, good to see you again,” Duroc said with a smile.  The orc’s presence felt stronger, more stable after his breakthrough.  He wore his upgraded armor and shield, and the wickedly sharp spear gleamed in its sling on his back.  Moreover, Kyle got several powerful impressions from the thick leather pouches attached to Duroc’s belt.  Probably the projects he was working on, Kyle mused.

“Good to see you too, Duroc.  I take it we’re almost ready to go?”

“Just putting the final touches on our greeting gifts,” the orc confirmed with a nod.

Kyle wanted to ask more about Duroc’s training, though he thought better of it with all of the different people bustling in and out.  Given Duroc’s caution about his whereabouts, it was clear to Kyle that there was valid fear of an informant.  The last thing Kyle wanted to do was reveal anything to a potential spy and give Mangalica a chance to develop countermeasures.

So, with little else to do, Kyle looked at the map to see the path toward the Tournament.  Fortunately, they would be able to use more established roads to get there, not needing to follow the twisting ancestral paths.  It would still take the better part of a day to get there, though Kyle expected they would move a bit slower to watch out for any potential ambushes.

Soon, they were on their way.  To Kyle's surprise, a large group of attendants came as well, with a caravan of goods trailing behind them.

“I thought I had to be there as a witness.  Seems a little redundant if you have two dozen witnesses from your village, doesn’t it?” Kyle asked.

“You’ll still be my witness for the tournament proper,” Duroc reassured him.  “There’s still diplomacy to be done here, and the advisors will be handling that.  We don’t all get together very often, you know.”

Kyle nodded, the explanation sufficient for him.  With that settled, he kept his eyes on the horizon while C.H.A.D.D. kept its scanners on maximum range.  Fortunately, the journey was uneventful, with the biggest excitement simply being the ever-growing crowds of travelers approaching the tournament grounds.  Initially, the sight struck him as odd, but the more he considered the social implications of the event, the more it made sense.  After all, the Tournament of Ascension was something that typically occurred every few decades, at most.  Why wouldn’t it be well-attended?

That also made Kyle second-guess his theory about Mangalica attacking at the tournament itself.  The collateral damage would be massive, and all but guarantee that the remaining orcs and War Chieftains would unite against him.  For all his power, Kyle doubted that Mangalica could stand up to the combined forces of all the other C Grades.  Which means an ambush is all the more likely, Kyle realized.  He sent the thought to C.H.A.D.D., who replied with agreement.  The more he thought about it, the more sense it made. If Mangalica could get more information about the others, he could also use that to plan counters of his own.

Resolving not to drop his guard too much as a result, Kyle turned his attention to the massive stadium that was rising in the distance.  It looked to be carved from the very stone of the ground itself, with massive spires jutting above the walls in regular intervals.  As they walked in, Kyle followed Duroc and the rest of his procession as other groups split off and went toward different spires.

“This is where we’ll be staying,” Duroc explained, gesturing to the symbols present on the walls.  “Each village has their own quarters maintained by a small delegation.  At least, under normal circumstances.”

“Let me guess, Mangalica withdrew his the moment he took over?” Kyle guessed as they ascended a spiral staircase.

“Exactly,” Duroc confirmed.

Once they reached the top of the spires, Kyle looked out to see the coliseum proper.  It could easily seat thousand in its spaced-out rows.  The arena in the middle contained a wide variety of environmental conditions; craggy rock and pools of water mixed in with otherwise flat terrain.  It makes sense that they’d want to play a bit to everybody’s strengths, Kyle thought, considering how he might approach a fight inside the arena.  As he did, a nagging thought came to him.

“Duroc, how can the average orc actually see what’s going on?  The arena is pretty far away, and the speed of a C Grade fight will be difficult for most to follow.”

Duroc gave a confused look, then chuckled.  “We broadcast it, of course.  A recording crystal will be watching the matches, and that’ll be projected up for everybody in the stands to watch.”

“And you’re all fine with these recordings just… floating around out there?”  Kyle pressed.

Duroc shrugged.  “Not really my place to have an opinion one way or another.  But it should be fine.”

As they settled into their quarters, Kyle learned more about the structure of the Tournament of Ascension.  There would be seven matches per day, going until either every War Chieftain had a chance to fight Duroc, or until any War Chieftains decided to bow out.  They would then be assessed a ranking based on total number of victories, as well as total number of matches completed.  Teams of Mages and Healers were on site to help repair the arena and triage any injuries, though Kyle was growing increasingly worried.

“What happens if you’re unable to keep fighting before you match with all the others?” Kyle asked.

“My village loses prestige, and I look weak.  But it won’t come to that,” Duroc said in a reassuring tone.  “Now, stop stressing about the tournament.  My people have been doing this for a long, long time.  We’ll be fine.”

“Okay, I trust you,” Kyle finally said with a shrug.  The orc left no room for argument, so Kyle took his leave to look out the window and watch the approaching crowds.  Despite the obvious tension in the atmosphere, there was also a nearly electric energy among the groups of orcs arriving.  It was clear that they were excited to see their War Chieftains in action, and only now did another piece of the puzzle fall into place.

Most of the War Chieftains had powerful variant hodags lurking nearby on the ancestral paths.  Kyle originally thought they were placed there to hinder his deliveries, but there was a second layer to it he hadn’t understood.  With C Grades capable of destroying a village looming over them, there was no way the War Chieftains would have left their home unguarded for the days it would take to reach the tournament grounds.  Mangalica not only set things up to prevent the ceremonial deliveries, he also established a means to undermine the tradition in its entirety.

At least, until Kyle made some detours to eke out a handful more levels.  His push toward mid-tier C Grade inadvertently allowed the rest of the War Chieftains to make their journey to the Tournament of Ascension, further thwarting Mangalica’s plans.  And when Kyle considered the resources that would have gone into raising those hodags, he finally started to appreciate the spot the orc rebel was in.

Mangalica’s ambition was always tenuous, only feasible at all by exploiting the individualistic nature of orcish society.  He’d created a prisoner’s dilemma that preyed on limited trust and interaction, and encouraged each of the already isolated groups to isolate further.  Now, that plan was turned on its head.  Suspicions were growing, powerful pieces were taken off the board, and the War Chieftains were all set to meet, albeit in the arena as opposed to a conference room.

Now all Duroc has to do is perform well at the tournament, and not die in an ambush on the way home, Kyle thought.

He ate dinner with the orcs, and stood guard through the evening, watching a stream of excited spectators make their way into the coliseum to watch the matches set to begin soon.  Finally, as the first rays of sun began to emerge over the horizon, an announcement rang out across the complex.

The first match of the Tournament of Ascension will begin in one hour.  War Chieftain Svynar will  test his blade against the hopeful ascender Duroc.  May he prove himself worthy.

The whole room shook with raucous cries as cheers erupted from the nearest spire.  Chants of both Svynar and Duroc’s name soon found a rhythm as Duroc emerged from his chambers, clad in his armor and prepared for battle.

“Come on, Kyle.  We’ll make our way to the arena, you’ll be spectating from the floor.”

“Understood,” Kyle said, falling in step behind Duroc as the orc set a brisk pace toward the arena’s entrance.

The cheers only got louder as Duroc entered the arena and made his way to the far side, and exploded when Svynar appeared on the other.  Kyle could make out the wicked obsidian axe as the other orc hefted it into a stance.

“Other than watching, anything specific you need me to do?” Kyle asked, still a little fuzzy on his exact role.

“Just bear witness,” Duroc replied with a grin, raising his spear and shield in response.

War Chieftains, the match will continue for fifteen minutes, or until one of you chooses to surrender.  Fight well, and bring honor to your clans.

The moment the announcement ended, Svynar and Duroc exploded into action, a tsunami of water crashing behind Svynar as the air around Duroc began to shake.

The Tournament of Ascension had begun.
