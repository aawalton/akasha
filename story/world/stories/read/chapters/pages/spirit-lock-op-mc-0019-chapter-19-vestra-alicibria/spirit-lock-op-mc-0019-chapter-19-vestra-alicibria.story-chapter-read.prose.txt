Carian creased her brow, wondering whether Hayden was being serious or deadpanning a joke. The fact that he didn’t crack a smile after was an ominous sign. “Interesting…” she muttered, leaning back in her chair. “And you think that’s just… gonna work?”

“It’s the only thing that does work,” he replied.

“Until their parents retaliate."

“And then I’ll find someone else’s parents to retaliate back.”

“You make it sound so simple.”

“Ms…” He read her name tag. “Raina. Nothing's simple. It’s all just a matter of skill and will. And whether it’s easy, difficult, complicated, agreeable, or unpopular—I will make it happen. Do you have any other questions?”

Carian chuckled darkly and pressed the pass button on his report. “Enjoy the written exam.”

When Hayden left, Carian reread Hayden’s report, specifically the details of Galma’s Redemption Festival. She put down her pad and grinned. “Okay then…” It seemed like her year at Junolyx wasn’t going to be completely boring, after all. She turned to the door. “Next!”

As Carian conducted her interviews, Hayden prepared for the written exam. He sat at a desk, patiently listening to the proctor as she finished the instructions.

“You have two hours to answer the questions to the best of your ability,” she said. “You may begin.”

Hayden raised his hand.

She turned to him. “What is it?”

“I cannot type or write fast enough. Can I connect thought to text to this…” He lifted the pad. “Device?”

Nearby testers covered their mouths to suppress laughter.

The proctor thought he was joking, but Hayden's eyes weren’t laughing. “Of course you can.”

“I figured, but… I don’t know how to do it.”

“How do you not know…” Her voice trailed off.

He responded with a disarming smile. “I’m better with my fists than my technology.”

The proctor cracked an involuntary smile when a nearby student whispered, “Moron.” She then shushed the others and knelt to help Hayden.

Unlike his looks, Hayden didn’t mind if people found him stupid. He even preferred it, as underestimation was a powerful advantage in times of war and personal conflict. So he blocked out the noise, followed the proctor’s instructions, and then read the first question.

“Explain Why Spirits Require Different Subgates Gates.”

The moment the cursor entered the box, the proctor watched in stupefaction as line after line appeared on the screen. It was so rapid and crisp that she almost forgot to leave.

The laughing students interpreted her expression as saying, “Are you really this illiterate?” so they laid their chins against their forearms and took deep breaths to suppress further laughter. Then, the room fell silent as students entered the zone and worked to finish the test.

Once it concluded, one of the men said, “You’re better with your fists, huh?” while looking him up and down. “Well… you’re probably screwed then.” He and a woman then strode off laughing.

Hayden barely noticed them. He was too busy focusing on the profound ignorance of some of the questions. Even the questions showcased their ignorance, like someone asking, “In what century did blue become a fruit?” The most exasperating one had a drawing of Lina's core. It was shaped as a hollow bulb with a tiny sphere inside. The question read: “What disease causes this core shape?”

Hayden scoffed internally. Disease… so that’s why she was rejected.

He thought about Lina’s rejection for the rest of his walk to the physical exam, exasperated beyond words. Between his knowledge and the universe’s sheer ignorance, causality had a massive handicap. That's what he concluded as he entered Junolyx University’s grand arena.

Loricks watched the exam on a widescreen in the president’s office, dreading the moment Hayden would get on stage. To his chagrin, the test was a bout between students—with spirits permissible.

Mayle Haya, the cofounder of Junolyx, studied him amusedly. “You look concerned, Loricks.”

He laughed darkly. “Oh, I am.”

“About Hayden?”

“Yes.”

“Well you shouldn’t. The scholarship students are matched up against scholarship students. We haven’t had a major incident in quite some time.”

“Major.” Loricks laughed, and his smile faded. “Well, you’re about to have one.”

Mayle’s eyes flashed with interest. “Hoh? That sounded like a hard fact.”

Loricks grinned. “Did I hook you?”

Mayle frowned. “Loricks, I can’t send you resources every time I want to know something.”

“Well… considering the number of information requests Junolyx took out on Hayden, I think it’s worth it this time.”

“Do you have information I can’t get out of him?”

“I… have a year-long relationship with Hayden working in my office. Does that count?”

She narrowed her eyes. “How much?”

“Two scholarships.”

“No.”

“Then I’m walking.”

“I’ll give you one.”

“It’s non-negotiable.”

“Non-negotiable? You’re seriously going to turn down a scholarship for something so petty?”

Loricks’ lips curved into a grin. “Turning down scholarships? Of course not. I’m planning to raise it to three by tomorrow.”

Mayle scoffed. “By tomorrow?”

“Yes, tomorrow. I'm certain you’ll contact me within twenty four hours of the exam results.”

Mayle matched his grin. “That sounds like a bet.”

“Only if it’s honest.”

“And if I lose?” Mayle mused. “You just want three scholarships?”

“No. That's the price. If it's a bet, I want you to act as that thing’s parent when shit hits the fan.”

“That thing?”

“Care to lose the bet? I’ll~tell~you.”

She wagged her finger playfully. “You haven’t changed… What if I win?”

“What do you want?”

“I want you to return to Junolyx. I’ll make you provost tomorrow, and give you full control over the academic curriculum. At the very least, I want you on the Board of Regents.”

"I'll accept the board. But only if you act in good faith. The only reason I’m agreeing is because you’re going to lose—spectacularly.”

“Interesting…” A smile crept onto Mayle’s lips. “Is there anything I should know? In good faith.”

“Yeah. I’d keep him away from anyone important. Remember that kid I was talking about? The sabatour?”

“Yeah.”

“He didn’t speak for four days after the festival, and he’s still walking around with ear plugs. It’s no joke.”

“Once again, we keep our scholarship students…” Her eyes widened when she saw Vestra Alicibria on the stage. “What the hell’s going on?”

Loricks’ heart pounded. “What’s wrong?”

She didn’t answer. Instead, she made a call. “Yes, this is Mayle Haya. Why the hell is Vestra on the stage? I gave firm instructions to keep him out of the matches! What? I don't care who he threatened. Get him off that stage!”

“Oh my God…” Loricks muttered.

Mayle looked up and saw Hayden walking to the stage.

“What do you mean his link's off?” she screamed, even louder. “Fuck! Run there and do it yourself. And I swear to whichever god you believe in, Mosley, I'll price match Vestra’s threats and execute if you're late. Now move!”

As Mayle was learning that her coerced referee had turned off his link, Hayden was facing off against Vestra Alicibria, a young man in his early twenties—clearly stunted in his education. He looked annoyed as he exchanged heated words with the referee. In the end, Vestra walked onto the stage and looked Hayden up and down.

He got into a fighting position—and then abruptly stopped, stood, and made a dramatic T angle with his palms.

“Hey, mate,” he said. “Before we start, I gotta give some fair warning. I was tryin’ to face off against this other kid, who was supposed to be like, the scholarship student of the year, but this idiot said he doesn't know who I'm talking about.” He pointed at the referee. “So I got you instead.”

Hayden stared at him blankly. “And?”

“And, that sucks. ‘Cause I went through a lot of effort to get here. See… okay. You see that girl over there?” He pointed to a pretty blonde standing beside the stage. “I was trying to impress her, so I said a bunch of stuff about how strong I was and… well. I still gotta prove it.” Vestra paused for dramatic effect. “Sorry.”

Hayden didn’t blink. “What are you sorry for? Are you sorry it’ll be so effortless, or that… you’re going to brutally maim me to impress a woman?”

Vestra faked a wince and sucked air through his teeth. “That one. Sorry, mate.”

The blonde giggled, and Vestra's nearby friends grinned. One said, “This’s going to be brutal.”

“I see,” Hayden said. “Well, since you were courteous enough to give a warning, I feel I should return the favor. It takes a third-evolution healing spirit two hours to heal a shattered jaw, and the blueprint is never the same. So unless you want to experience life altering damage—I’d forfeit.”
