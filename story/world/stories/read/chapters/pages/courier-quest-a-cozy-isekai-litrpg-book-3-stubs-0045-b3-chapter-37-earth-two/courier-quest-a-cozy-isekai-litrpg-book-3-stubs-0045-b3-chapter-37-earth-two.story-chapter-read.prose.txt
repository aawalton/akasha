Trevor waited patiently on the steps of the Sephie Dahs Concert Hall. The building was made of dark green stone with columns lining the front and sides. It was surrounded by a large, grassy park that was regaled by soothing music played from the speakers along the roads.

The robot gallery surrounded the area, but there weren’t any booths here. Most of the art installations were abstract, such as the one that almost looked like an upside-down, four-tiered snowman, a series of three-dimensional geometric shapes that made a lion-like creature if you looked at it from the right angle, and a series of underground tunnels covered with kaleidoscopic colors.

That last one wasn’t really abstract, but it was fun. Not only was traversing the tunnels allowed, it was actively encouraged. Trevor had been impressed, but not as much as Adabelle. It was there that they learned demons could see a few more colors than humans, and apparently that was passed on to the demonkin as well despite having a different race according to her status screen.

Trevor watched the road as he waited. He wore a dapper black suit courtesy of Corrin. The outfit had come with gloves, but he was wearing them reluctantly. His right one was fingerless, which allowed him to use his Power. He wasn’t sure if that would be acceptable in a setting described as fancy fancy, but he had been assured that it was fine.

Unfortunately, familiars and other animals weren’t allowed inside. There was no stoat on his shoulder. This didn’t bring Clay down at all, though, and they even vibrated slightly from excitement.

Set upon his tie was a brand new golden clip with a small black marble on it. The little guy seemed stoked to be an accessory again, and was keeping an eye out on the other guests as they headed up the stairs. They especially liked it when no one could tell they were actually a creature.

“Sorry we took a little longer than expected,” Lora said as she teleported both her and Adabelle to a safe spot between the columns.

“No worries, there’s still plenty of time,” he replied with a smile as he turned to see them.

Adabelle wore a slim black and white dress. Straps were tied into ribbons above her shoulders, leaving her arms and neck bare. The skirt was white and started with a split on one side. From there, it flowed around her naturally, no longer hugging her body as it flared out. With a smile, she moved to Trevor’s side and took his hand.

Lora also wore a two-tone dress, but hers had a sweetheart neckline. The upper half was black aside from a band of pink that traveled across her chest before meeting in a ribbon in the front. Her skirt matched the color, going down to her knees with a slight poof to it. Black gloves adorned her arms while two more ribbons were pinned to her hair on either side. For the first time, she was without her green one.

“We had a discussion while we were changing,” Adabelle informed him. “If you’re okay with it, we could invite Lora to share our balcony with us.”

“Yeah, I’ve got no problem with that,” Trevor said with a shrug.

The demonkin turned towards the teleporter. “See? It really is that easy.”

“I know, I know. You three really are the coolest,” Lora said, looking down and kicking at the stone beneath her. “I was just told by the boss to give you some room, right? And I want to do right by him. So, yeah.”

“Trust me, if you were in our hair then we would tell you,” Trevor replied, but he paused after. “Well, Adabelle would. I’d probably beat around the bush for a bit because I didn’t want to hurt your feelings.”

“As if I wouldn’t be nice about it,” the demonkin retorted, giving him a look.

“Of course you would, lady. You’re much better with words than I am,” he said as he wrapped an arm around her shoulder. “What I’m worried about is that you already have a ticket. Right, Lora? Would you be allowed to come up to the balcony?”

“Yes, actually,” she answered with a nod. “Mine is assigned seating, and I did pay for it myself, but yours can allow up to eight to enter the balcony with you.”

“And it’s just us?” the courier asked. “That sounds like a lot of room for what was supposed to be two people.”

“It is, but, that’s what the boss had me set up for you,” Lora replied with a shrug and a smile. “How about you two head inside and I’ll see what I can do about this spare ticket I have. The extra ticket that I no longer need. One additional ticket that might go to a good home! I’ll meet up with you.”

Trevor chuckled as the teleporter raised her voice in an unsubtle announcement that someone could purchase a ticket from her. He shifted and glanced down the steps, trying not to make it obvious, and there was already some interest.

“We’ll see you inside, then,” Adabelle said as she gently guided her husband away.

Her posture was the very picture of nobility. Graceful, confident, and poised. Someone immediately went out of their way to open the door for the newlyweds, and she offered them a small nod in thanks.

Trevor’s posture was loose in comparison, but Adabelle was quick to correct it. A swift tap to his lower back with her tail followed by raising his arm at the elbow. He followed her nonverbal instructions, straightening up and pushing out his chest.

“There’s my husband,” Adabelle said just barely loud enough for him to hear. Her tail snaked between them and tapped his chest gently. “You are handsome, but you become so much more when you’re not slouching.”

“You’re just enamored by my chest,” he jokingly accused, whispering much lower than her because he knew she’d hear it even over the crowd.

“As if that doesn’t go both ways,” she challenged, arching an eyebrow as if daring him to deny it.

“Wow. You’re a little saucy today,” Trevor said with a low laugh.

Adabelle giggled. “I’m being escorted to a popular concert by a handsome man who happens to be head over heels for me; of course I’m in a good mood. However, I could pretend otherwise if that’s what you prefer.”

“No, no, you’re perfect,” he quickly replied with a shake of his head. “If you want to be saucy, then be saucy, my love. I just can’t be held accountable for what happens later if you keep teasing me.”

“Good. I'm looking forward to it,” she said, giving him a brilliant smile and leaving it at that.

It almost took more willpower than Trevor had to take his eyes off of his lovely wife, but he managed so he could check out the lobby. Most of the people were already beyond the ushers, leaving the large room feeling empty. There were stairs to either side leading to a second and third floor, which he imagined they’d probably be taking since they had a balcony to themselves.

The floor was the same green stone as outside, but polished so much that he could almost see his face in it. Several pieces of painted artwork hung from the walls of various performers, some solo and some in groups, reminding Trevor of movie posters. One was lit up with a sign saying “tonight only.”

Trevor tapped his wife’s arm to get her attention, and they headed that way. The performer tonight had been a surprise since Lora didn’t want to tell them too much, but they both immediately noticed that she was a Summoned.

The black-haired woman with olive skin wore a modest green dress that matched her slitted eyes. She was holding up her left hand, which clearly had her summoning tattoo wrapped around it. Two cat ears popped out of the top of her head and a black tail wrapped around her waist. Since there were no horns to be seen, that left one conclusion.

“Catgirl,” Trevor said as he looked at the painting.

“A girl who is a cat,” Adabelle surmised. “I suppose that is a fitting name for her.”

He grabbed two of the small pamphlets underneath the art and handed one to his wife. “Rachel Levy, with the Power of Song, singing the greatest hits of Earth Two,” he read aloud. “Prepare yourself for music that is truly out of this world.”

“Earth Two?” his wife asked as she looked at her own program.

“It was so good they had to make a sequel,” Trevor replied absently. He was about to open the pamphlet when he realized he didn’t want to, and he sent it to his inventory instead. “Is it bad that I kind of want to be surprised by what she’s playing?”

“No. I would be, too, if she had come from my world,” Adabelle said with a smile. She continued flipping through the program before shaking her head. “Nothing in here about an Earth Two.”

He shrugged at that. “Wonder if there’s going to be a meet and greet afterwards, then.”

“No, Rachel Levy is pretty reclusive,” Lora stated as she returned with a pair of copper coins in her hand. She held them up for Trevor. “Don’t want to spend the juice to go back to the hotel just for these. Could you keep them safe for me?”

“Sure thing,” he answered easily as he reached out and sent them to his inventory. “A whole two copper, huh?”

“The guy seemed really excited and was prepared to try to listen to the whole concert from the doorway, so I asked how much he had on him and made a dream come true,” she replied, shrugging. “Are you ready to head up to the balcony?”

“We are. Would you like a program?” Adabelle asked.

Lora shook her head. “I got one when I bought the tickets, so I already know what’s being played. The opener is—”

“I want to be surprised,” Trevor quickly interrupted. “I mean, it’s Earth music, or the sequel to it, so I want to go in blind, you know?”

“No problemo,” the teleporter said with a smile. She gestured for the newlyweds to follow, and she spoke on the way. “Earth Two is just what she calls it to drum up interest. Most people know there are several worlds named Earth out there on account of Summoned being from the same place but different, so it’s just a branding slash marketing decision.”

“It’s working, then,” Adabelle replied. “That was the first thing I asked when I heard it.”

“Me, too!” Lora said with a laugh. “Like, a lot of the Summoned I know are from Earths but they just call it Earth. That’s what we called it, too.”

“You’re also from one?” Trevor asked, leaning forward to look past his wife.

The teleporter flashed him a grin before pointing towards the usher that was approaching them. “Tickets, please?”

“Ah, right. Got it here,” Trevor said as he reached into his pocket and pulled the rather nice black card from his inventory. No need to get fancy here.

The usher took it and looked it over before smiling. “The Harl Key Balcony is up these stairs to the left, all the way to the top floor, and will be the last door on your right. It’s almost time to start, so I hope you enjoy the concert.”

“Thanks, I’m sure we will,” he replied before taking the ticket back and slipping it into his pocket. They began their ascent.

Lora waited until they were all the way on the third floor before speaking again. “Now’s not the time to get into it, but I’m halfway from an Earth.”

Trevor frowned thoughtfully. “What does that mean?”

“Were you a part of a dual universe?” Adabelle asked at almost the same time. Her husband gave her a glance as they walked down the long hallway, and she explained. “A dual universe has at least two worlds that are connected through a portal or gate of some kind, or even through spaces that exist in both. Imagine if this world and your Earth could be traversed through, say, a door.”

“That is correct. Kudos to youdos, Lady Adabelle,” Lora confirmed with a smile. “I was not from Earth, but I often visited there with my sister who I mentioned before. While I could tell you where I’m actually from, I think there’s a good chance that Trevor might figure out what I was and there won’t be any fun in that.”

The courier nodded. “So it’s something common to Earth, but—”

“Hey, hey, hey! Listen, you. No trying to figure it out,” the teleporter pouted. “I said I’d tell you, now’s just not the time. Besides, the usher said the show is almost starting.”

“Alright, I’m done. For now,” Trevor chuckled as he gestured for Adabelle to enter their balcony. It was the only door that was still opened, and he closed it behind them.

Two chairs had been set out for the newlyweds, but the courier summoned a third one for Lora. She smiled in thanks before moving to sit on the other side of the balcony. Trevor and Adabelle sat next to each other, and her tail slipped between him and the back of his chair.

The concert hall was absolutely packed. When he scanned the room, it looked like there were only two empty seats in the whole house. That changed when an excited man came through the doors, practically skipping to fill the one near the front.

“That’s the guy I sold my ticket to,” Lora whispered. “This view is much better anyway.”

Before either of them could respond, the lights dimmed except around the stage. The red curtains stayed illuminated, as did small stones set into the aisles below, and they started opening to reveal an orchestra set up around the stage they had been obscuring. The musicians were already waiting.

A voice cut through the darkness. “Now introducing the idol from Earth Two, Rachel Levy!”

The audience applauded as a woman with cat ears walked onto the stage. Her dress sparkled in the light, and her tail swished behind her with a dazzling ribbon and bell tied to the end. She waved at the crowd, all smiles as she took her place in the center.

“Good evening, Grafam!” Rachel Levy called out, her voice easily heard throughout the space. The crowd applauded even harder. She basked in this for a few moments before holding up her hands to quiet everyone down. “So, we have a special treat tonight. It has been brought to my attention that we have not one, but two Earth Summoned in the crowd tonight.”

Trevor and Adabelle both looked at Lora, who joined them by looking in the same direction. “Not from Earth, huh?” the courier asked.

“I confirmed I was Earth adjacent,” the teleporter answered with a mischievous grin.

“Well, first off,” Rachel continued. “None of the songs I sing tonight are those that I own. They belong to their original owners, so on the off chance that you’re a copyright lawyer or something, please don’t come after me. I wouldn’t put it past some of these corporations or bands to literally hop worlds if they could get access to, say, some platinum coins.”

Trevor snorted a laugh. He could see it.

“Second, it’s a special treat for me because I always like to open up with a song that I don’t sing very often, so it’s not in the program. For the rest of you, it’s a little something extra,” the singer said with a wink and a smile for the crowd. “Let's get right to it!”

Leaning forward in his chair, Trevor kept one hand on Adabelle’s leg as the room fell deathly quiet. She patted him gently, keen to hear the music Rachel Levy would be singing.

The drums came first with an upbeat rhythm before the guitar started an unmistakable tune. Lora devolved into quiet giggles when she heard it, while Trevor grunted and put a hand over his heart.

“Are you okay?” Adabelle whispered as Rachel began singing.

“Yeah. Yeah, I’m fine,” he replied before shaking his head. “I just thought I had escaped.”

The noblewoman was momentarily taken aback. “Escaped from what?”

Trevor turned to her and let out a low laugh. “The rickroll, love. I thought I had escaped from the rickroll.”
