Guardian Arena, The First Floor, The Dungeon

-0-0-0-0-0-

First Crusader Haliet hacked off the final leg, this one even larger than him, and the mass of carapace and white crab flesh landed on the ground with a thud. Immediately, men were there pulling it away, dragging it out of the dungeon. His task done, he turned to the front of the enormous crab corpse to see Artic shoulder-deep in the eye cavity of the dead monster. As he watched, she pulled her hand out, revealing the monster core. Compared to the grape-sized one, this one was as large as a closed fist, displaying an investment of mana that had Haliet shaking his head in disbelief.

"Fascinating..." Artic said as Haliet approached, examining the dim sphere. "It's almost entirely drained of mana. It certainly didn't use enough to totally drain a core this size, so what happened to the rest of it..."

"Perhaps it's related to the Guardian's fabled immortality?" Gregg suggested, while swinging around the crab's sword-like claw. "Something about the guardians remembered and adjusted tactics against individual parties. Unless they could overcome it with superior speed, strength or power, they were forced to adapt their own tactics. Those that survived would no doubt benefit tremendously from such an opponent."

"That makes sense," Haliet agreed, sheathing his now-clean sword. "It's mana is somehow siphoned away as it dies, and perhaps imprinted upon another monster, which takes its place. There's plenty of evidence that memories imprint on mana. Whatever the dungeon has set up to facilitate this will likely fail soon, as its mana reserves start draining. We should expect the Guardian to return once, maybe twice." His piece said, Haliet turned to examine the rest of the room.

Though the Guardian's corpse dominated the arena, the clear area around it was growing as the hundreds of smaller crab monsters were hacked apart and taken out of the dungeon. The constant assault had ended when the Guardian died, a decision that Haliet assumed was the dungeon trying to conserve its mana. As they waited, the rest of the Templar and Heroes spearheading the push into the dungeon emerged from the triangular tunnel.

When they'd all gathered, Haliet cleared his throat, drawing their attention. "Well done so far, everyone. Between this strike and removing the dungeon's source of mana, we should expect its reserves of those crab monsters to be extremely low, if any remain at all. However, given its previous behaviour, it may have another monster or two at the Guardian's level in reserve, so we will need to establish a rearguard. Pan, until we get deeper, your skills are more valuable in keeping our forces alive and healthy than anything else, and I am confident you'll be able to handle any ambushes. Pick two of the summoned heroes to stay with you."

Though she frowned, Pan nodded and walked towards the shuffling group of heroes. She pulled her apprentice and the dark-skinned fire mage with her as she passed them, getting wide-eyed looks as they couldn't resist her strength. As Pan dragged the two away, Haliet cleared his throat to regain the crowd's attention.

"The rest of us will push into the Second. To review, this floor is reportedly a half-flooded, shifting maze filled with traps.

"We have enchanted helmets for you all to wear, so you don't need to worry about breathing or loss of vision underwater. Stay aware at all times. Every hallway, corner, or staircase could be trapped." His piece said, Haliet turned and led the way past the Guardian's corpse and into the staircase chamber. In the centre of the circular room was a pedestal and basin, as expected. The basin, however, was empty, and the enchantment on the stalactite above it was deactivated.

Without further delay, he led the way down the stairs, noting it twisted clockwise, which would give anyone fighting from below the advantage and more room to swing a sword. He descended slowly and carefully with this knowledge, waiting for defenders to suddenly appear. They didn't. He eventually stopped for an entirely different reason: The staircase was flooded, filled with totally still water. This... was not in the reports. By everything they'd learned, the staircase led to a room in the centre of the maze, and they had to navigate a constantly changing layout that changed every hour. At least, they'd only have to deal with it until the enchantments ran out of mana. But this...

"What's the hold up?" Gregg called from behind him, stopping and looking over his shoulder. "Ah... that's not good."

"Indeed," Haliet said, a little sardonically. "The first floor wasn't much different from what I expected, but I suppose it's easier to add more water than to shift layouts entirely. Everyone!" Haliet called, louder so everyone on the stairwell could hear him. "The dungeon has entirely flooded the second floor! Many of the traps likely won't work underwater, but watch out for those designed to split parties! Remember your underwater training!"

Haliet pulled his enchanted helmet from his bag and donned it, activating the enchantment as he did. He also activated the enchantment on his weapon; the dungeon-core-powered sword began rapidly heating and vibrating at an incredible rate as he entered the water.

The Creator, Occupied Atlantis, The Kalenic Sea

-0-0-0-0-0-

As soon as the lead crusader fully entered the water, the fish patiently waiting around the turn of the stairwell attacked. A school's worth of Sharp-Scaled Bluefins shed their scales, using their water magic to send the cloud up at the man. Arrowfish and Jetfish by the dozens ascended in v-formations, making attack runs against the man's armour straps, gaps in the armour, and exposed skin.

At first, the attack worked perfectly. The cloud of bluish-silver scales engulfed the man and the entire section of the staircase as well. The arrow-shaped monsters shot into the cloud, cavitation bubble trails left in their wake. Then a pulse of yellow light burst from the cloud, and the monsters were flung away, burned and flailing in the water. The man swung his bubbling sword with incredible speed as the cloud rapidly burned away, almost unhindered by the resistance the water should provide. With every step, he proved exactly that; he was unhindered. He moved, and the water was forced aside.

Almost casually, he continued down the staircase, deflecting or killing the regrouped Arrowfish and Jetfish with contemptuous swings of his longsword. Then the second man entered the water, his eyes brimming with energy. Even as part of me coordinated and guided the aquatic assault on the crusaders, another was further into the Second, adjusting the traps there.

As much as I hated to admit it, that templar was right. The Second being flooded invalidated some of the traps; pitfalls being the most obvious example. The party-splitting traps would still work, though, as would the Decay, Rust and Wither traps I'd added. Now, two corridors would either rust or age all their non-organic items into uselessness, decay their food and organic items, like leather, into nothing. And one corridor, the final corridor between the maze and the boss room, would age anyone passing through it. Not by a lot, though. If they passed through quickly, they might lose a year or two. If they dawdled, though, a decade or more was possible.

As previously explained, these time acceleration spells began rapidly increasing in cost as the area of effect and acceleration grew. This was the best I could do at the moment.

It took another ten minutes for the crusaders to reach the Second proper; their leader pushing his way in after slaughtering hundreds of arrow and jetfish. As soon as he stepped out of the staircase, he was bullrushed by hundreds of other fish. Bloodfish, their evolution, and yet more Jetfish and Arrowfish kept him under constant assault. The yellow pulse that'd burned my fish had turned out to be an energy shield, a water-permeable shield, but one that activated to defend him. One that now burned constantly, giving him a halo-like effect. I watched the effect carefully, tracing the mana in it, and if I could have, I would have scowled. In fact, my Avatar did just that. It was his enchanted armour. Just like the one in his sword, it had a dungeon core. The gem was embedded in the breastplate, one that had enough hoarded mana to keep that shield up for a long time.

The only way I'd drain it was by keeping it up as long as possible, and by inflicting blows, it'd have to expend more energy on the counter.

The second man entered the room, and energy beams leapt from his eyes and every single one of his fingers. Each beam found a unique target, burst through them, then carried on through another five monsters before petering out. Within minutes, the room was clear, even a certain distance into the hallways. Their ineffectiveness was clear. I pulled the rest of the fish back, holding them in reserve for after the strongest crusaders had passed. They'd proven my fish monsters, even the evolved forms, were more effective against the normals and guilders than these powerhouses. It was a lesson I would take to heart. Either way, though, one thing was clear.

The Crusaders had made their foothold on the Second Floor.

The Docks, Atlantis 2: Electric Boogaloo, The Eleventh Floor

-0-0-0-0-0-

Sophie Ravenfield was... antsy.

The sun was warm on her skin, to which the cold, wet sand under her toes was a lovely contrast. Her swimsuit was skimpier than she used to wear, but she was proud that she could shred cheese on her abs now and wanted to show it off. All along the beach, Guilders enjoyed the almost tropically warm waters. That was one of the only differences she could discern from the true Atlantis, which was who knew how many miles above them. The guilders were certainly taking advantage of the warmer waters for their rest day.

Still, Sophie stood in the surf, looking out on the horizon. From what the guildmistress had described when she explained their situation, there was an entire island chain out there that not even her aunt's party had managed to reach yet. They'd only managed the floor above this one. Not for the first time, Sophie tried to wrap her mind around how a place like this could even exist this far underground. A planet's crust was thinnest under the ocean, even considering the thickness the volcano had no doubt added. This floor had a working water cycle. They'd only had one rainstorm since coming down here, but it was enough to prove the theory.

"You alright there, Soph?" A voice called from behind. Sophie turned her head, looking over her shoulder to see Bruce walking up, a leather ball in hand. "You've been staring at the ocean for like, half an hour now. If you were in full armour, I'd call it aura farming, but with your swimsuit, it doesn't quite work."

"Oh, shut up," Sophie smirked. "I think the frills are cute."

"But pink?" Bruce asked, like he couldn't comprehend the decision. "I thought for sure you'd wear, like, a black one-piece or something."

"So everything I wear has to be black?" Sophie asked, raising an eyebrow. She could see the moment Bruce realised he'd put his foot in it, and swallowed his response.

"Of course not," he answered, cautiously. "You can wear whatever you want."

"And today, I wanted to wear something pink with white frills to feel cute," Sophie answered, before she turned away to look back at the ocean. After a moment, Bruce stepped up beside her, letting the surf rush over and around their feet.

"You're not the only one that feels... cooped up," Bruce commented, guessing her reason perfectly. She sighed; her friends knew her too well. It left her with a pleasant feeling in her belly. "I've heard it from the other Guilders too."

"...Am I that easy to read?" Sophie asked. Bruce snorted.

"Kinda."

"Damn."

"So you want to play volleyball with us?" Bruce asked after another moment of silence. "The guilders are picking it up scarily fast. They're practically setting up their own tournament. We've had to set up a new net, since they're monopolising our old one."

"Sure," Sophie sighed and turned, starting the walk over to their friends. Bruce was quick to follow and fall into step with her. "You think The Creator will actually let us join in the defence?"

"It's a toss-up," Bruce answered with a thoughtful frown. "On one hand, we know Haliet will be at the forefront, right? He's so far beyond us it's scary; we'd lose instantly. But if it came down to fighting the other summoned heroes? Maybe. They probably wouldn't want to fight us, either. Might be able to talk-no-jutsu them on to our side. They've got to know the church is evil by now, right?"

"I don't think they're evil," Sophie answered, frankly. "I think they're people, with their own beliefs and reasons for doing what they're doing. It was the gods that called the crusade, the same gods that brought us here, in response to something The Creator did. He didn't come here of his own accord either, and is just trying to survive and protect his creations. Are the crusaders in the wrong for trying to commit genocide on the Children and kill The Creator? Yes, but they justify it to themselves by pointing at all the times a necromancy or a dungeon has nearly caused the end of the world."

"Everybody is the hero of their own story," Bruce nodded. "Yeah, I get all that." The rest of their walk passed in companionable silence. They went on to prove their dominance on the volleyball court against Elize and Akio. The Guilders ended up popping their ball and needed a replacement. Thankfully, they were pretty easy to make, and they turned it into a competition. Whoever popped the ball was the loser, and had to make the new one. The afternoon turned to evening, and the Tiki torches were lit.

The beach day turned into a beach party, and Sophie found herself lying on the sand, once again looking up at the sky, tracing familiar constellations she'd once thought she'd never see again.

Would she ever see them again, for real?
