“Is this all these orcs have? This wasn’t even a warm-up.”

Rusty heard his current boss shouting behind him as she pulled one of her axes from an orc’s head. The unsettling sound of breaking bones echoed for a moment before being replaced by the jolly laughter of both the adventurers and mercenaries.

“Oh, this is going to be easy. Did we really need to bring so many people?”

One of the adventurers commented, and several others nodded. The more people there were, the less everyone would earn when they returned, as the pool of money had been decided beforehand. Unless the mission was completed, they would get nothing.

“Stop squawking like baby chicks and clear the bodies. Check their gear and take anything worth a coin. You can keep what you find!”

She pointed toward the dead orcs, and both the adventurers and mercenaries cheered. They were free to plunder the place clean. To Rusty, who had already gone through most of the camp, there wasn’t much that caught his eye.

‘This is about the same as that camp I raided, and I was only E-rank back then.’

He was reminded of how, after killing several orcs, he had been discovered by higher evolved variants and chased out. He had then ended up in the barbarian-filled mountains, where he was mistaken for an evil spirit. If things continued like last time, it was possible that stronger opponents would arrive, ones that could be C-rank.

‘I want to fight that one...’

There was one orc stuck in his metallic head, the one that had driven him out and whom he hadn’t even been able to scratch. Now, with this body that was at least durable enough to take hits from monsters like that, he was curious how well he would fare. There were also many humanoids here, so even if he wasn’t up to par, he felt confident that their group would be able to defeat a C-rank if they did it together.

“What is it, Second?”

Galiena approached him and gave him another smack on the shoulder. The armor didn’t move an inch this time, as Rusty had reinforced his stance beforehand. His boss instead winced and looked at her hand, which was now aching.

“I’m just waiting for Gleam and analyzing our surroundings. This camp was merely a forward outpost. The lack of heavy equipment or proper fortifications suggests they could have been sent here to monitor the road.”

There was a nearby road that hadn’t been used much in recent years. No merchants traveled through these orc-infested forests, instead taking the long way around them. It was probably possible to clear out these forests eventually, but the nobles didn’t seem to be in any hurry to do so.

“Heh, aren’t you being too paranoid, Second? These are still monsters. They aren’t smart enough for that.”

Galiena shook her head before walking away to look at a weapon lying on the ground. Rusty, who was a monster himself, was a little annoyed by the assumption that all monsters lacked intelligence. These orcs seemed smart enough to form tribes, ones that rallied under some kind of leader while continuing to attack the city. Last time, they had also changed their strategy and nearly broken through the western gate.

“There you are, Gleam. How was it? Any hidden enemies anywhere?”

“Didn’t see anything. We should be safe for now, then.”

After his last run-in with the Orcs, he wasn’t taking any chances. If a sentry spotted them attacking a camp like this, or even heard the battle taking place, they could alert more enemies. Normally, he would have been happy to fight them, but with these adventurers and mercenaries, it wasn’t that simple. He needed to play his role and babysit them for now, at least until they arrived at that mine, which he was also interested in.

“Humanoids really love money, don’t they?”

Rusty commented as he moved over to one of the trees and leaned against it. Every single adventurer and mercenary was scavenging the place. The Orc bodies were being ripped apart, their teeth, ears, and claws taken as trophies. He even saw some cutting out the Orc hearts and other organs, including those between their legs.

“Mortal greed knows no bounds, my dear Rusty! They are truly such lesser races... and this was what the hero gave his life away for.”

Aburdon mocked Alexander as they watched the mercenaries and adventurers harvest the bloody remains like starving vultures.

“They are just trying to survive and feed their families.”

Alexander argued, though his voice lacked conviction.

“It is a hard world for those without immense talent or status.”

“Survival?”

Aburdon sneered as he pointed out something happening nearby.

“That one over there is already arguing over a rusted iron ring. Pure, unadulterated greed.”

Rusty ignored their internal bickering and focused on the horizon. The sun had begun its slow descent, indicating that they were slowly running out of time.

“All right, pack it up!”

Galiena shouted and kicked one of the mercenaries who was trying to pick up an old copper coin from the ground.

“We have more ground to cover before it gets dark. If you fall behind, you know what’s waiting for you!”

She grinned while making a chopping motion with one of her axes toward an outstretched finger. Both the mercenaries and adventurers recoiled in fear and quickly moved forward.

Rusty and Gleam were within the Vanguard, and as they proceeded, Gleam was used as their main scout. One camp after another was cleared with meticulous efficiency. Thanks to Gleam’s aerial reconnaissance, the combined group ambushed two more small patrols, cutting down the surprised greenskins before a single one could sound an alarm horn.

By the time the sky turned to twilight, they had reached the mine, and their true mission had finally begun.

“Hold up.”

Rusty called out to the others. Boros and the other adventurers instantly stopped, now changing their tune and listening to his orders without any backtalk. The mercenaries did the same, and it was all thanks to the smooth trip here.

“The mine should be just up ahead. If we go any further, we might alert the Orcs, and... there seem to be traps here.”

He pointed down at a rope tied to a smaller tree. It was around ankle height and hidden behind the long grass. Jakub snorted, though he took a cautious step back from the hidden tripwire. While most of the group seemed to be on Rusty’s side, this man and a few others still remained disrespectful.

“Traps? Since when do greenskins set wires in the grass? Are you sure this even activates something?”

“Why don’t we have a trap expert check it, then?”

Rusty asked while looking toward the adventurers. The mercenary band was mostly composed of direct combatants, but the adventurers had a few hunters and thieves in their group who could easily disarm the mechanism.

A young scout in leather armor, armed with a pair of long daggers, stepped forward. His eyes darted along the string as he knelt in the damp soil, carefully brushing away fallen leaves to reveal a crude wooden stake holding a coiled spring and a heavy log studded with iron spikes.

“A standard log trap. Nothing special. Give me a moment.”

The man was quick with his hands and soon disarmed the mechanism. They also discovered that, besides the logs, the string was attached to something that would produce a lot of noise. Several bells were tied to the same mechanism and would create a loud sound whenever the trap was triggered.

“Good. Scout out the area and see if there are any other traps. The rest of you stay here.”

Even though Galiena wasn’t the sharpest tool in the shed, she wasn’t a bad leader. She knew who she could rely on and waited for Rusty to give her a nod whenever a decision needed to be made. Sometimes, a good leader simply needed to know who was trustworthy. However, Rusty was still a monster in disguise, so perhaps she needed more time to gain experience.

They slowly moved forward, disarming traps along the way. Some were quite old and decrepit, while a few seemed new, indicating that whoever had set them up had to be nearby. Yet, as they continued toward the mine, a problem arose.

“So... where are the Orcs?”

What they found was an abandoned encampment, one that looked as if it had been deserted not just for days, but for months. There were torn tents, overturned tables, and empty fire pits that seemed not to have been used in quite some time.

“Something is off here...”

Alexander mentioned it as Rusty looked around.

“You think?”

Aburdon replied in his usual mocking tone. Some things didn’t add up. Faint signs of battle could be seen in a few places, but the recently placed traps made no sense if the surface camp had been abandoned for months. A settlement would not leave its perimeter guarded while allowing its inner grounds to fall into ruin unless the occupants had moved elsewhere or had been eradicated.

“Second, what do you make of this?”

Galiena walked up beside him, resting one of her broad axes against her shoulder as she asked the question.

Rusty continued to listen to his guides as he took in the information and tried to make sense of it. He had come a long way. His diction was now indistinguishable from that of a proper humanoid, and his disguise seemed perfect.

“I’m not sure. The Orc tribes might seem like they’re working together, but the peace between them is paper-thin. The reports said that they only answer to one leader, who gathers them periodically, but these small encampments are independent. It’s possible they could be involved in some infighting...”

He said a lot of words, and Galiena nodded along, but he wasn’t sure if she actually understood what he was getting at.

“There are some signs of battle, but the traps have been recently replaced, so there are a few possibilities.”

“Oh? Like what?”

“Perhaps there are no orcs here anymore, and the traps were replaced by one of the smaller tribes that use the mine to catch animals, or…”

“Or?”

“Or they could be waiting inside the shaft and only come out when they need to.”

That was the assessment of Aburdon and Alexander. The orcs might have either moved out or were staying inside the mine as their new home, seeing no reason to venture outside.

“I see…”

Galiena thought for a moment before looking toward the main entrance of the mine. It looked dark and imposing, a gaping black maw carved into the ragged rock face of the slope. Broken wooden beams leaned precariously against the stone archway, draped in thick cobwebs filled with spiders.

“There is also another possibility… It could be a trap.”

He said this as Gleam landed on his shoulder. Once she settled there, her antennae began to glow brightly, providing some light in the silent night.

“How would the orcs predict that we would be coming here and set up an ambush?”

Jakub finally decided to chime in, and it was clear that some of the other mercenaries thought Rusty was being a little too cautious.

“There is probably nothing here. Can’t we just go into the mine and get this over with?”

“But what if orcs are waiting for us inside?”

“Then we just kill them like the rest. Easy money!”

The adventurers and mercenaries began talking among themselves. After making it this far without any casualties, their morale was high. None of them wanted to spend the night here either. They had expected to be fighting orcs by now, so if they could clear out the mine, perhaps they could cut the mission short by an entire day.

“Alright, quiet down, all of you!”

Their current leader shouted again as she slammed her axe against a piece of wood to get their attention. It cracked loudly, silencing everyone instantly.

“You heard Second. Something is fishy here, but we can’t just sit here all night, so we’ll do this…”

She seemed to take his advice to heart, but that did not mean they would simply remain outside.

“Some of you will stay outside. We can’t have orcs blocking off the only exit if this really is a trap. The rest of you will come with me inside. Got it?”

It wasn’t a bad idea. Keeping some forces outside in case of an ambush was sensible. If the mine collapsed, they could also dig them out or at least try to call for them if something went wrong.

“Not a bad choice. If she refused, she could have lost the support of her men.”

Alexander commented as he pointed out that both the adventurers and mercenaries were still motivated enough to hunt more monsters. Sometimes, it was best to take advantage of such momentum and keep moving forward. In Rusty’s case, he was also curious about what was lurking inside the mine.

“Very well, then. I’ll be the vanguard.”

He took his shield down from his back and replaced his halberd with a regular sword. The tunnels inside such mine systems were narrow, so swinging a long weapon around could easily get him killed.

“You heard Second! Get ready. We’ll be moving in five.”

The remaining warriors quickly fell into line, checking their armor straps and sharpening their blades one last time. While a third of the combined force formed a defensive perimeter around the ruined surface camp, Rusty stepped toward the cavernous entrance. Gleam perched on his shoulder, her glowing antennae casting long shadows across the damp stone within.

‘It looks similar to the sunken mine dungeon. Perhaps this place is actually connected to it…’

He thought to himself as he slowly moved forward. There had been no orcs in that dungeon, so the probability was low. Still, it was possible that the greenskin monsters had never fully explored the area. If they had ventured inside, they had probably remained in the first or second largest chamber and established their camp there.

"Keep your eyes open and your weapons ready. If anything comes out of that hole that isn't us, chop it down."

Galiena warned the rear guard before turning toward the shaft and following Rusty's lead. The air inside was chilly, carrying several faint smells that the humanoids clearly found unpleasant. Rusty moved slowly, keeping the light produced by Gleam faint so they would not attract unnecessary attention.

“Hm… these scratches on the walls. They don’t look naturally formed…”

As he ventured deeper inside, he noticed several grooves carved into the wall. He compared them to his own hand, which was larger than that of a regular human and roughly the size of an orc's. Yet the marks before him were far larger and considerably deeper…
