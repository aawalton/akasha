Green lightning ripped across the sky in thick, living veins. Entire slabs of the realm sheared free and dropped into the black below, dragging bone-shore and the wrecks of corpse-ships with them.

"She's finally lost it." Baldr said, looking around. "Let's go, mortal. This place is doomed."

Baldr seized Barry under one arm and launched them both into the chaos, pale light spilling from his back in the shape of wings that strained against the collapsing sky.

Barry wasn't looking at the destruction. His eyes stayed on the scattered remains of his own projections, dozens of them, crushed, burned, or simply abandoned across the fracturing platforms.

Each still clutched a different experimental version of the portal gun. Some were flattened under falling stone. Others smoked where their power cores had cooked themselves. One sparked weakly in the ash.

"Lower," Barry said.

Baldr's voice came tight. "We don't have time."

"Three seconds. That's all I'm asking. If you don't drop for three seconds, we both die when this place finishes coming apart. That's not a threat, Baldr. That's just simple maths."

Baldr met his eyes, calm, steady, even in this situation, so he didn't argue and just right down.

Barry jumped before they touched the platform. He hit hard, rolled, and came up already tearing the first gun apart with both hands. Jaw still broken. He stripped the power-crystals from one, the containment dragon shell from another, the targeting matrix from a third.

He worked under falling stone and green lightning, muttering through bloody teeth, "If they are illusions of me, they better be smart illusions."

"Fast, mortal," Baldr said, looking around, his wings beating hard. He saw a boulder falling from the side, ready to crush Barry where he sat.

"Shit! No more time!" Baldr plummeted in and hauled him upward before the platform was smashed by the giant boulder. Barry kept his grip on the unfinished hybrid the entire way.

They found a relatively intact stretch of black rock. Barry slammed the final connections home and the gun hummed, unstable but still working.

It worked for half a second. A tiny blue tear appeared, no larger than a fist, then collapsed with a sound like glass under pressure. Barry slammed the activation crystal to turn it on again, but it needed a power source capable of forcing open another portal, a human-sized portal.

Barry looked at Baldr. "Baldr…"

Baldr kept his eyes ahead, being to busy not dying. "What now?"

"I need your power…"

Baldr understood immediately. "No."

"How much divine energy can you release before it kills you?"

"I said no. I will take us to safety." The words came out cold.

Barry looked around. He wanted to believe him, he truly did, but no. The devastation happening all around looked like an apocalypse. All because she threw a tantrum. "We will die at this rate."

Barry held his gun for a second. He looked at Baldr and then drew the sampling needle anyway.

Baldr's eyes went wide. "Human, I am warning you."

Baldr would have let him go, he actually should have, let him fall and die, but for some reason, he just couldn't let him go.

"Sorry…" Barry didn't make it sound apologetic. It came out the way he said most things: utterly flat and already past arguing.

He punched the needle in. Divinity erupted through the improvised circuitry like liquid fire. The wire carried it into the gun. The gun started to shake. So did Baldr.

The gun heated up. Barry's hands burned as the energy overloaded every junction.

"Shit shit…" Even though his fingers burned, he forced the coordinates into the least broken section of sky he could see. The portal began assembling, then the gun started cooling as most of the energy bled off as waste heat.

He had seconds. Most people would have given up or tried to repair it. He kept going.

"I… told you… not to do it." Baldr's body went limp in his grip. They began to fall. Barry held him and worked the controls one-handed. He did not waste the fall on regret. He used it.

The portal still would not stabilise.

[System notification]  [Mission: Free the system and you will be saved]

"Ha!" He grunted and read the so-called mission. He didn't like it.

He still tried to fix the wires, tried to clear the over-clogged containment field. Nothing held. His vision tunnelled. The machine was seconds from burning through the last of Baldr's divinity.

[System notification]  [Mission: Free the system and you will be saved]

"No," he replied. Then he remembered Thor.

A stupid lesson. An embarrassingly primitive Asgardian technique for forcing energy through unstable structures. Barry had dismissed it on the spot, too crude for anyone who understood real engineering. Falling now, with Baldr dying in his arms and lava rising from the cracks below, he understood for the first time why it worked.

Technology required precision. Magic only required force and intent.

Barry laughed once. He sucked at magic, his human body couldn't handle true Aesir magic, but this, this he could do. He forced the spell through his damaged hands.

The portal gun detonated with light. A massive tear ripped open, not pure blue but a swirling mixture of blue, green, and black. For a fraction of a second something on the other side appeared to be looking back at him through it.

"That's probably bad," Barry said, almost to himself.

The lava reached them. They fell through the portal and it snapped shut right behind them.

Silence.

Somewhere else, the same portal opened vertically. Both of them fell out of it, bodies rolling across dark tile-like ground.

Then the gun overheated once more. Before it could explode in his hands, Barry flung the wreckage away as it detonated.

Barry stayed on the ground, breathing hard, heart still hammering. He looked around: smooth black stone.

There was no earthquake here. No lava. No lightning. He lay still for a moment and let the silence be, feeling the simple, stupid relief of still being alive.

Baldr was breathing beside him. Shallow, but breathing. His skin had gone grey, the warmth of divinity mostly spent. Barry looked at him for a moment without touching him or saying anything, because there wasn't anything to say yet.

Then Baldr seized his arm. "Down."

A wind tore across the ridge with enough force to fling them both into the void. Barry drove his fingers into the stone hard enough to split the remaining skin on his knuckles. Baldr used what was left of his strength to anchor them. In time the wind passed.

"You dumbass, you know where this is?" Baldr said, finger pointing up.

Barry looked up to find its source.

What he had taken for a wall was a column so enormous his mind couldn't process the scale. It rose and disappeared into the darkness above. Another gust came, and this time he tracked it properly: not coming from any side, but from above.

He looked higher. And higher.

A vulture. Not merely large. Colossal. Its body wrapped around the upper reaches of the column like a living mountain. Each slow movement of its wings created a storm that reached the farthest edges of Helheim.

"That's the vulture. My energy source."

"Oh my god…" Baldr's voice came as a bare whisper. He said its name.

"How big," Barry said, "is it?"

The creature opened one eye and looked directly at them. That answered the question well enough.

Across Helheim, the Bifrost deposited Loki onto a massive black bridge.

She looked up. A palace rose before her, not merely tall but delusionally tall, its towers disappearing into clouds, its architecture built by something that remembered castles but had never actually stood inside one. The heaviness of it sat in her chest.

"I hate doing this…" she said to herself. Her body glowed green and slowly grew. And grew. And grew.

She grew to fifty feet and approached the gates. They opened without being touched. She stopped in the doorway, feeling the presence beyond. The darkness beyond was completely silent, too silent.

"You better be right, Mother." She stepped inside anyway. One foot. Then another.

Something scraped across the floor. Loki froze.

Two eyes opened in the darkness. Yellow. Glowing. A shape gradually came forward. She had time to think wolf  before it kept coming, and coming, its head rising above hers, its body larger, black fur, claws grinding against the palace floor like stone on stone.

But the size wasn't what stopped her breath.

It looked at her, closely.

The creature lowered its enormous head until its eyes were level with hers, and spoke one word.

"Mother."

Loki stood very still. The word moved through her differently than she expected. "Hello… son."
