“I’m honored you would want to teach me,” Samantha said, gently pulling her hands away and giving the old druid a small smile. “But my friends and I are sort of in the middle of something. Not to mention we really don’t live around here, so any sort of teaching arrangement wouldn’t exactly work.”

“Yes, yes, hunting monsters, I understand,” Sedrick said, waving her concerns away as if he didn’t even hear them. Seeing as the man looked to be in his mid-eighties, Asher wouldn’t have been surprised if he hadn’t heard them. He’d known people much younger who had already started losing their hearing back on Earth. “But this is far more important than that! You are a daughter of the forest, it is my duty to teach you what she knows!”

“Look, as interesting as stumbling upon some old master of the trees or whatever you are is, we don’t have time for this crap,” Moxy said bluntly as she pulled her head out from the door leading into the man’s house. As annoying as it was at times, for once, Asher was thankful that they had a companion who was always willing to be the one to cut to the chase. “I didn’t see any sort of secret basement in there, which means you’re not what we’re looking for. Enjoy your life of huffing leaves and munching on bark, or whatever it is you do here. We’re leaving.”

Sedrick didn’t even bother looking back over his shoulder at Moxy, but he definitely heard her. Without warning, the earth split open beneath Moxy’s feet, and the warrior’s eyes widened as she started to fall into the sudden crevice. Of course, being Moxy, she canceled the fall almost instantly with a reflexive blast of air from Air Nova, landing in a crouch just beside the hole as she threw a dangerous grin toward the druid’s back and raised her fists.

“Oh, old man wants to fight, does he?”

“Moxy, wait,” Asher said, quickly stepping between her and the druid. “Sedrick, Moxy is a bit rough around the edges, but she’s not wrong. Samantha already told you herself, we’re sort of busy right now. I’m sure you’ve got plenty to teach, but we don’t have the time.”

“Nothing is more important than hearing what the deity of the forest has to say,” Sedrick countered, turning to frown at Asher. It was only then Asher realized the sheer weight behind the man’s aura, and he blinked as he momentarily felt like he was looking up at a towering, ancient oak tree bringing its full weight to bear. He didn’t know what sort of skills this man had, but they were strong.

That said, Asher had faced off against stronger.

“Respectfully, I don’t care if you live in the middle of the woods, kidnapping is still kidnapping,” Asher said, laying a hand on the hilt of his void dagger as he prepared himself for anything. “Samantha said she’s not going to be your pupil, and that’s that.”

“Kidnapping?” Sedrick repeated, recoiling slightly as though he’d been slapped. “Who said anything about kidnapping?! And she did not say she would not be my pupil, she merely said you all were busy! I was just trying to explain that my teachings are no doubt more important than whatever you are here to collect shards for.”

“I believe it is safe to tell him what we are truly here for,” Kroult said as he stepped out of the druid’s cabin. With all the tension going on, Asher hadn’t even noticed he’d gone in. The lich was so naturally quiet that he always seemed to fade into the background without even doing anything. “I can confirm Moxy’s findings. I do not believe he is associated with our target, and he may be able to help. Druids know their forests better than anyone.”

“That’s a good point,” Rulfar muttered, slowly returning his axe to its sheath now that it didn’t look like they would be coming to blows. “Even if he doesn’t know exactly where Jormon is, he can probably help us find him more easily.”

“Jormon? What’s a Jormon?” Sedrick asked, frowning at Kroult. “And don’t you know it’s rude to enter someone's home uninvited? You don’t see me trying to peek under that illusion you have over you, now do you?”

For the first time, Kroult actually looked taken aback, staring at the druid in shock before tugging his hood more fully over his face.

“You’re a wizard?” Asher asked, gripping his void dagger a bit more tightly.

“No, I’m not some fancy-pants wizard,” Sedrick snorted. “But I’ve been around for a while, and I can tell when someone is using tritessence. His whole body is glowing, like he’s trying to hide something. Getting a feel for that sort of thing is just one of the many things I wish to teach you,” Sedrick said, turning back toward Samantha. “You have so much to learn!”

“Again, I’m honored you would think so,” Samantha said, taking a deep breath before giving him a far more stern look. “But like I said, it wouldn’t work. While it is a lovely forest you live in, I have no desire to live here as well. As soon as we finish what we came here to do, we will be going home. Which is very, very far from here.”

The simplest solution of course was for Asher to toss down an Eternal Mark that they could return to as needed, but Asher understood her hesitation. He had a limited number of marks he could have active at any one time, and it felt as if they’d been brushing against that limit more and more recently. Samantha was clearly trying to be considerate of him, and before Asher could take her aside and tell her they would make it work if studying under Sedrick was something she was genuinely interested in, the druid spoke up.

“Distance is no concern, and one forest can be a teaching ground as much as the next,” Sedrick said, waving her words away. “All I ask is you answer me these two questions, child, with complete honesty. Do you want to learn more about the forest deity your father spoke of? Do you wish to embrace your origin element like never before?”

Samantha hesitated, throwing a worried look over Sedrick’s head toward Asher. Asher was just as confused as she was, but above all else, there was a thought tickling in the back of his mind that kept shouting at him. He didn’t know if it was because the man was old, or due to some other trait about him. But there was only one person that Sedrick seemed to remind him of.

He felt like Gran.

Asher was all but certain the old druid was in the Stage of Power, and that meant what he had to teach Samantha would almost certainly be worth it. He didn’t quite understand what the man meant about distance not being a concern, but if that was true, then there was really only one answer to give him.

Shooting her a small smile, Asher shrugged, as if to say your call. Samantha shot him back a small frown, shaking her head slightly at his unhelpful advice. After a moment, she sighed.

“…Yes, I would be interested in learning more. But like I said, the timing—”

“Yes, yes, you young people and your insistence on thinking that everything needs to be done right here and now,” Sedrick grumbled, stomping a foot onto the ground and surprising them all as a small sapling rapidly grew out of the dirt. Despite not having come from any sort of seed, the sapling grew quickly. As it did, Sedrick motioned for her to hold out her hand. Samantha hesitated for only a moment before doing so, watching in awe as the sapling grew and gently twisted around her wrist. Just as quickly as the sapling had started growing, it stopped, slinking back into the ground and vanishing from sight.

While leaving a small, wooden bracelet secured around her wrist.

“That will let me find you,” Sedrick explained, giving her a warm smile. “Do not fret. It is not an artifact, merely a facet of one of my skills. You and you alone can break it quite easily if you so desire, but should you decide you wish to learn from me, simply hold the bracelet against a decent-sized tree and I will take care of the rest. Take as much time as you need, but not too long! I’m an old man after all, there’s no telling when I’ll finally kick the bucket!”

“Thank you,” Samantha said, rubbing at the bracelet as Sedrick laughed to himself. Rulfar made a come on motion with his hands, and she jumped slightly as if only just then remembering what they’d originally come to the forest to do. “Sedrick, like we said, we’re not really here to hunt monsters. We’re trying to find a lich that has been living in this forest for some time. We think he specializes in diseases, plagues, that sort of thing. Would you happen to know where we could find him?”

“A lich you say?” Sedrick muttered, glancing at Kroult and causing the poor hedge wizard to pull his hood even farther over his face before the druid looked away. “Can’t say I’ve seen any liches anytime soon. But if he is here, that means there’s only one place he could be. The heart of the forest.”

“The heart of the forest?” Rulfar repeated, pulling out a small notebook and preparing to write. “What is that? I didn’t read about that in any of my research.”

“Of course not, it’s not something you’d find on a map. It’s a portion of the forest about a hundred and forty miles in that direction that nobody dares enter. Not even me,” Sedrick admitted, looking like doing so physically pained him. “The monsters there are far deadlier than anywhere else, and the very land is tainted. I spent many years of my youth trying to force my way into the heart and figure out a way to purify it, but to no avail. No matter what I did, I got horribly sick whenever I tried to enter. Nearly died a few times, I’m not ashamed to admit. If it weren’t for my evolved resistance to disease and ability to draw vitality straight out of the forest, I probably would have.”

“That’s got to be where the lich is holed up,” Asher said, growing excited at the news. “Sedrick, would you be willing to come with us? We’re planning on wiping out the lich responsible for that tainted land, which should return the portion of the forest back to normal. We could really use your help.”

“As much as I do wish I could, I’m afraid my days of danger are beyond me,” Sedrick chuckled. “Maybe if I were a hundred years younger…”

“What?” Moxy asked, furrowing her brow as she looked him up and down. “How old are you?”

“Old enough,” Sedrick said with a wink. “Sadly, you will have to do this one on your own. Though now I understand why you all have those weird bubbles on your heads. I thought it was just some stupid fashion statement, but I wasn’t going to say anything. Maybe they will allow you to get farther than I ever could.”

“When we finish what we’re doing… I’ll find the time to contact you,” Samantha promised. “I can’t say I’m agreeing to be your pupil or whatever it is you’re looking for, but I do want to know more about the deity of the forest. It was something very important to my father, and I want to know why.”

“That’s all I can ask for,” Sedrick said, giving Samantha another warm smile. “Ah, the youth. How brightly you all burn with energy… It’s almost blinding!” Shaking his head, he trudged back to his cabin, the hole in the ground sealing shut without him seeming to do anything just in time for him to cross over it. “Anyway, it was lovely to meet you all. Best of luck with your journey into the heart of the forest. Whatever you do, watch out for thistles! Damn things nearly killed me on more than one occasion.”

With those last words of wisdom, Sedrick slammed his door shut, leaving them all standing outside. The five of them all looked at one another for a moment, Samantha still fidgeting with her new bracelet. After a few moments of silence, Moxy spoke up.

“…I’m thinking two hundred. Two hundred and fifty, maybe. Anyone else?”
