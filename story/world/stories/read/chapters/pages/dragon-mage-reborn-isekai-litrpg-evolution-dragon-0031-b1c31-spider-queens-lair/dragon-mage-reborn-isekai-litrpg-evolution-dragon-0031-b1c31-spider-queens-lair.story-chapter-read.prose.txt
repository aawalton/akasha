A mega insectoid showing up right after a greater insectoid—why did I feel like they were connected? I looked down at the pile and noticed an opening in the middle of it, as if something had escaped. Rocks pushed aside, large pieces of carapace scattered along the ground—could those two bugs have somehow fused into this massive behemoth?

He made a high-pitched screech, those long segmented antennae twitching wildly like he was ready to go on a frenzy.

The mega insectoid’s mandibles clicked together rapidly, his compound eyes focusing on us as he took slow steps toward us. Without warning, he dropped from the ceiling, landing with a heavy weight that sent tremors through the tunnel floor. I made a fair observation from the way he was moving—he wasn’t as fast as his two predecessors. While he was technically stronger, he was also slower, which worked out better for us, I hoped!

“Fusion, original,” Nolan said as he armed his shotgun. “New body, pretty top-half heavy, too. I bet you can’t move as fast as before, can you?”

‘Hey, wait, there’s a small black gem on his head,’ I said, or should I have called it a helmet? It looked like something Loki would wear, the horns of his headpiece curled backwards just the same. In the middle of his forehead was something shiny that matched the shape of the concave socket in the wall we couldn’t pass.

It was hard to see with the lack of light in this place, but I was able to spot it, and soon, Nolan was too. “Oh yeah, there’s our key.” He smirked. “As always, you gotta work hard for it.”

With a battle cry, the monster leaped, but Nolan’s finger was already on the trigger. He pulled, and those pellets pulsed. In the air he fired magic like I’d never seen before. The tats in his arms glowed, and out of those pellets came gold glowing circular sigils in the air.

Within those inlays were slow-spinning glyphs that intensified in heat. Before I could bat an eye, electricity jolted out of them and into the beetle on steroids.

The humanoid bug screeched, his carapace making popping noises in places as bolts of energy coursed through his armored body. The electric assault stunned him momentarily, but it wasn’t enough to put him down.

‘Nolan, it’s not doing anything,’ I insisted.

“Different composition,” Nolan replied. “The fusion probably reinforced the exoskeleton. We need to hit it harder or find a weak point.”

I channeled my mana into a concentrated [Astral Blast], hoping to dent his armor. With my skill at a higher level, I expected to do some real damage this time, and it did, slightly. The real problem was the armor he had throughout his entire body. Even the expected squishy underbelly was armored up—the monster was damn near impenetrable from head to toe.

There was something we just weren’t seeing….

As soon as Nolan’s magic died down, I leaped, but so did the monster. He rolled up into a ball and with the force of unseen momentum, he sprung right at us! We scattered like bowling pins with legs, dodging his attack. Without a moment to breathe, he charged again, and this time, he had his eyes on me.

Picking a baby dragon over a human with a powerful shotgun?!

….

Okay, yeah, that made sense.

The plan was to get him out of his ball form, the only form where he had speed. When the battle beetle was slower, I could maneuver around him more safely. I ignored Nolan as he shouted at me to keep away, insisting that we tackle this fight together, but I was tired of little results. Nolan didn’t want us getting too close, however, with my foot speed and my size, I was at an advantage.

I summoned [Cyclone Shield] and relied on the AOE to push him away. It did its job, slamming him into the wall, but the monster was still balled up. Didn’t matter—I had a plan b. I then used [Ice Breath], the increased level bumping its range and strength. The icy chill glued him down, and he had no other choice but to break free from his ball form to get out.

Now it was time to strike.

My tiny feet fired like pistons as I closed in. As soon as I was within arm’s length, he barfed. With an angry grunt, he chased me with a pool of acidic ooze at my tail as I circled his legs. My plan wasn’t to engage with him, but to find a weak spot. The joints, I analyzed. They weren’t covered in armor, which made sense. He had to move freely, and padded hard shells around those bendy areas would limit mobility.

The moment I made that discovery, he caught on to what I was doing. He instantly stopped trying to melt me with acid spit, and resorted to trying to stomp me instead. With one heavy stomp, I nearly sank. His step created a gaping hole in the ground, followed by another, and another.

“Hey, short fry!” Nolan cried. “Get out of there!”

‘Yeah, in just a minute!’ I was busy running figure 8’s around this guy’s feet, until his last step broke through the ground, causing his leg to sink through.

When he went down a few notches, I was able to catch a lift on his arm, where I assumed the title of pest. Quickly, I dug my claws into his exoskeleton. Naturally, he tried to shake me off, those elongated mandibles working overtime to snap at me. Fortunately, he couldn’t keep up as I continued scurrying from one limb to another like a tiny terror, biting and scratching. I was a full blown menace.

‘Strike the neck, Nolan!’ I advised, giving Nolan an opportunity for him to hit his target. With the monster's head swiveling back and forth, he was bound to get the perfect shot. However, not with Scarlet. He tried his luck with something else, tossing his throwing blade right into the beetle’s throat.

The blade sank deep, wedging itself between the segments of the monster's layered neck. A shriek erupted from the creature, high-pitched and painful enough that I had to cover my ears as I jumped down his shoulder. Pus-colored ooze spurted from the wound, the smell so rancid that it had me parkouring along the wall as I ran back to Nolan so I wouldn’t accidentally step on it.

[HP: 0%]

‘Kill confirmed!’ I cheered.

“Nice work, tiny terror. I’m proud of you.”

‘You did great too, Nolan!’

“Yeah, but I’m a veteran. You, however, are still fresh out the gate, or should I say, egg?” He smirked. “You’ve got a lot of guts.”

I puffed my chest out, the ego boost putting a little strut in my step. ‘I’m learning from the best, aren’t I?’ I giggled. ‘But yeah, I’ve always been a bit daring. Gets me in trouble sometimes. I just… really hate being seen as weak or incapable. I guess you can say I got that from my father.’

“Well, you don’t have to worry about proving anything to me, hot shot.” Nolan retrieved his blade, wiping the viscous goo on the monster’s own exoskeleton. “That being said, I still have a lot to teach you about dungeon crawling.” He examined the corpse, and then leaned in closer for a better look at the gem infused in its skull. Without hesitation, Nolan began carving out the gem with precise, erm, no, wait, reckless motions. It wasn’t an easy pry, the gunslinger’s impatience making me sweat. But eventually, the stone popped out, gleaming with a bluish-violet hue.

It caught the dungeon’s dim light in mesmerizing patterns. Unfortunately it wasn’t something we could keep. I had a feeling it’d fetch a pretty penny if we were to sell it. As soon as Nolan got up, we went back to the wall and inserted the gem. Instantly, these thick branches of web began expanding from the gem and outward, until the entire wall was covered in it. A bright white light swallowed the wall whole, and then finally, we were granted entry into the spider queen’s chamber.

The moment we stepped through the threshold, my nostrils were bombarded. I gagged almost instantly. The smell hit me hard, a noxious blend of rotting bodies, digested flesh, and something sickeningly sweet that clung to the back of my throat. The black and gray chamber stretched before us, impossibly vast, with a ceiling that disappeared into darkness. Everywhere I looked, webs, and not the delicate, gossamer kind you'd brush away from a corner, but industrial-strength cables as thick as my body, glistening with an iridescent violet sheen that served as the only source of light in the lair.

My eyes continued to roam, my curiosity catching corpses left and right. Our first dungeon wasn’t sunshine and daisies either, but in terms of gore and guts, this queen’s body count was scary. Hanging from the dark walls were husks of countless victims—humans, monsters, and creatures I couldn’t even identify, all suspended in various states of webbed mummification. Some were fresh, their features still recognizable beneath the silk wrappings, while others were ancient, withered to little more than outlines.

My little body stood there frozen with my jaw dropped as I took everything in. It was then and there where I felt like I was way over my head with this. A tier three dungeon? I was still a freakin’ baby! While this adventure cured my fear of bugs, I wasn’t ready to face the mother of all spiders in her own throne room.

“Don’t touch the webs,” Nolan whispered, his voice barely audible. “They’re alarm systems. Pressure-sensitive.”

‘Okay. Anything else I should know before we march into the lion’s den?’

‘Spiders have excellent vibration detection,’ he replied to me internally, scanning the ceiling. He wanted to keep this conversation private, seeing as he rarely talked to me telepathically. ‘She’s probably already aware we’re here, but let’s not make it easier for her.’ Nolan pulled out a shell that had a skull and crossbones on it etched in some glowy silver metallic liquid. ‘This guy is loaded with a potent magical neurotoxin that should slow her down.’ He pressed down on the protruding top of it, and when it caved in, the etched sign on the casing began to swirl. ‘I activated the internal mixture. It’ll take time before I can use it.’ He looked at me seriously. ‘Be careful with this one—she’s not going to make it easy on us. You need to stick close to me, Freya.’

I nodded. ‘Okay.’

‘I mean it,’ he said more seriously. ‘No storming in without a backup plan. The queen will be a strategic fighter—she isn’t going to rely on brute strength or magic alone. She’ll try to separate us, use the environment against us, and any other means to get her kill.’

I nodded again, more solemnly this time. Admittedly, the severity in Nolan’s tone made me nervous. This fight won’t be like our previous ones—I could feel it. It was do or die here, and I had to give our toughest challenge my all.

“Ahh! Visitors!” a sweet and sultry voice sang from above, my stubby legs instinctively taking a step back. She’d startled me, the queen descending on threads of webs toward the middle of her lair. “Or should I refer to you succulent morsels as my meals for this evening?”
