Back in Lutia, the mysterious plot of the crimson spire and its cybernetic assassin was thickening.

Having lodged themselves into a sketchy-looking inn near the heart of Ironfel, Geneva Rhyntharion stood by a darkened window, gazing down at Aldric as he spoke with a group of city guards gathered outside, doing his best to convince them that they hadn't found Alan's assassin after all.

His words were too faint to make out, but the roaring bursts of laughter rising from the guards told her everything she needed to know. Whatever Aldric was saying, they were buying it.

She grimaced.

Beside her, Daruk Brystion wore the same expression.

He glanced at the bound assassin behind them for a moment before slowly turning to look at her. A small smile tugged at his lips, softening his cold expression just a smidgen.

"You find my brother's abilities disturbing, do you not?"

Geneva kept her eyes on the laughing guards for another moment before answering.

"I find them extremely useful." She glanced back at the bio-engineered assassin and scowled. "That one is what has me disturbed."

Daruk followed her gaze, his brows furrowing slightly. "Her modifications or her presence?"

"Both." Geneva let out a small breath. "Anything that has to do with Attilan cannot be taken with levity. And if there's one assassin, there's most likely others."

She stared at the assassin for a moment longer before returning her gaze to the darkened window. "I can't think of any reason why an Attilan assassin is in Lutia, save one. And if I'm right…" she shook her head. "We are not ready."

Daruk stared at the assassin for a moment longer before slowly turning away.

He didn't know much about the enemy continent. Everything he knew of them came from Aodhán, and even that felt woefully insufficient. He knew nothing of their people, their motives, their strengths…

All he knew was that they had invaded Lutia nearly a millennium ago, just after Saran Beithir's death, and their leader—the Red Witch—had been the one to curse Lutia with the Limit. He had also learned from Aldric that the ziggurat—the Way the cultists had been hiding in—led to Attilan, but had been shut months prior. Every other Way found alongside the ziggurat had also been shut to prevent exactly this.

But it seemed they hadn't quite shut them all.

Daruk grimaced.

He might not know much about Attilan, but the little he knew—the little he had seen and heard—was enough to convince him that they were a dangerous enemy. The assassin alone—an unawakened able to evade Geneva Rhyntharion even for a few seconds—was proof of that.

The fact that one of them was here spelled immense danger for the continent. And the viscount's suspiciously convenient absence, despite news of the murder in his city spreading, wasn't helping matters at all.

Something terrible was going on, and they had to find out what it was as quickly as possible.

A terse silence settled over the room as the seconds passed, the only sound the occasional chink of iron chains as the assassin shifted her weight behind them.

Neither he nor Geneva reacted, their gazes fixed on Aldric as he worked his magic on the guards. They ate it up like starving hounds, laughing at whatever he was currently saying—not bothering to consider the impossibility that a target had escaped the Witch of Selia, despite seeing her runes blanket the heavens only hours ago.

Daruk couldn't blame them though. Aldric's power was practically irresistible. And a moment later, when the guards finally lost interest in hunting the assassin as though one of their own hadn't just been killed, Geneva broke the silence, her grimace deepening.

"You're right," she whispered. "It is pretty disturbing."

Daruk smirked. "I want to say you're alone in your sentiments, but that would be a lie. And he's my brother."

Geneva cracked a smile before shaking her head. "You Brystion brothers really are a strange bunch."

Daruk's smile waned a little, the coldness returning. He glanced at the assassin once more before sighing, "Some more than others."

Geneva looked at him, studying his expression for a moment before speaking. "I took you on as an apprentice, Daruk, because I wanted to help you."

"And because Aldric was very insistent." Daruk muttered quietly, glancing at her. "Academy dropouts have very few prospects, after all."

Geneva nodded. "Those are all true. But I did it mostly to help you after…" she cleared her throat. "After the tournament."

Daruk's expression hardened further, but he said nothing. His face showed nothing.

Geneva let a second pass before continuing. "Whatever is going on in this city, Daruk, I have no doubt it'll grow very dangerous very quickly." Her gaze softened. "I did not recruit you to put you in such danger. If you want to leave, I suggest you do so before we dig any deeper."

Daruk licked his lips, truly considering the idea before eventually discarding it with a shake of his head.

"As dangerous as all of this is… this is the first time in months that I don't feel so in danger of a limit." He looked at her, a faint smile touching his lips. "It's as though whatever hand had been looming over me is suddenly lifted."

Geneva smiled. "I told you it was all in your head."

Daruk wasn't entirely convinced. He didn't argue though. He simply shrugged. "Perhaps you're right. But I'd rather stay. There's nothing left for me to do if I go back."

"Are you sure of that?" Geneva pressed, her gaze growing serious. "Unlike your brothers, Daruk, you haven't been to war before. If you stay, human bloodshed is not a distant possibility."

Daruk shuddered slightly, but stood his ground. "I'm sure."

Geneva studied him for a moment longer, before nodding. "Good, because you and your brother are about to do something very important for me, and I wouldn't want you to feel squeamish."

Daruk glanced at the assassin and hardened his jaw. "Is it torture?"

"It's information extraction," Geneva replied flatly. "With the Viscount missing, she's our only source of information, and I must extract whatever I need from her. By any means necessary."

The assassin's breath hitched audibly, and Daruk's jaw tightened.

He had never tortured anyone before. At least, never intentionally. And despite the severity of the situation, he wasn't quite sure he had what it took to start now.

His mind, however, begged to disagree.

Ideas tumbled into his mind—freezing, ice conversion, cryostasis, memory manipulation… They came in a never-ending stream of the best ways to torture a person with his collection of skills. Cold, effective, clinical ways—far cleaner, less bloody, yet far more painful.

He grimaced. "I'm not sure I can torture her."

Geneva's expression softened. "It's not my first choice, Daruk. If she cooperates, then physical torture will not be necessary. I might not even need you."

What if she doesn't cooperate?

Those words sat unsaid between them, and Daruk felt the first sign of squeamishness curl in his gut. He nodded regardless.

"I understand."

Before Geneva could say anything else, laughter erupted from the city guards downstairs as Aldric separated himself from them, cheerfully waving goodbye.

Geneva sighed. "Look alive, Daruk. The hour is here."

Daruk inhaled deeply, straightened his shoulders, and turned his attention to the door of the chamber.

Wood creaked a moment later as Aldric pushed it open, his expression somber. He met their gaze and nodded once. "It should take a day at least before the thrall wears off."

"That gives us the whole night," Geneva whispered, her gaze hardening as she glanced at the assassin. "That should be more than enough time to get all the information we need."

Aldric nodded, then glanced at Daruk. "Are you alright being here?"

"I'm fine." Daruk waved off his brother's concern, his gaze fixed on the bound assassin. "I'll see what I can do if your method doesn't work."

His words caused Aldric to stiffen ever so slightly. He shot Geneva a glare, which she ignored, before slowly walking towards the assassin. When he reached her bound figure, he pulled a metal stool from his spatial storage and set it gently before her.

The assassin didn't look up. She didn't move.

To anyone else watching, it may have looked like defiance. But the scent of her fear was too intense for Aldric to mistake it for something else. It filled the entire chamber like a cloud of smoke, pressing against Geneva's determination, and Daruk's cold discomfort.

She wasn't merely afraid. She was terrified. And Aldric was forced to confront the fact that beneath all the wires and bio-engineered protrusions, she was just a girl barely older than Daruk.

Perhaps he wouldn't require any of his skills after all.

Drawing a quiet breath, he sat down and steepled his fingers. Geneva and Daruk came up behind him, silent as they took positions by his shoulders.

Aldric cleared his throat quietly, his voice soft but firm. "Look up."

The assassin did, her dark-red hair spilling down her shoulders as she met his gaze with her crimson ones. There was not a spark of fear in her eyes. Not a smidgen of dread.

Yet the terror roiling off her had intensified, a stifling cloud of emotion that coiled and undulated around him like a living thing. Even her heartbeat was audible.

Grimacing, Aldric leaned forward and asked his first question.

"What is your name?"

Confusion rose—an undercurrent to the sharpness of her terror. Then a hint of disbelief, as though she couldn't believe that was what he'd chosen for his first question.

None of it showed on her face, though. She said nothing either.

Aldric held her gaze, seamlessly parsing through her emotions and drawing conclusions despite her silence. She was scared, not only of what was to come, but of what would happen should she begin speaking. There was no resolve that would hint at a magical oath or soul binding that would bar her from speaking.

If there was anything she didn't eventually say, it would be because she didn't know.

His eyes narrowed slightly as her silence continued, and after a moment, he spoke. "I don't want to make this hard for you. But if you do not cooperate, you'll force me to compel you."

Her terror spiked. Not just any terror, but a terror that stemmed from understanding. From knowing exactly what a person was capable of and dreading it.

Aldric frowned slightly, drawing another conclusion.

Her terror had not spiked from experience, so he could assume she had never been compelled before. The source of her terror, however, suggested she knew exactly who he was and what he was capable of. She knew what they were all capable of.

And if that was true, then it meant her actions at the bar—with Alan—hadn't been accidental, but premeditated.

She had been briefed.

Any sympathy Aldric had held toward her vanished in that moment, and a chill suddenly settled over him. The air around him stilled, the temperature dropped slightly, and a subtle sense of despair bloomed around him.

If the assassin had been terrified before, now she was utterly filled with panic. Her mask of defiance broke as her breath hitched—the first sign of terror. But Aldric didn't let that stop him.

He, however, did let it soften his actions.

He could have filled her with despair and taken away her desire for life. He could have forced her emotions into a pit of wretchedness that would have had her bawling in seconds. He could have wrecked her entire emotional pool, twisting everything into pain, sorrow, and agony.

Instead, he took away her desire to hide the truth from him. And the moment that desire vanished, she cracked—her defiant expression crumbling entirely to reveal the terror beneath.

Aldric exhaled, sensing the discomfort in his companions. Geneva's fear of how powerful he had become; how easy emotions had become for him to manipulate.

Daruk's concern at how easy he was finding this.

Aldric ignored it all, and leaned forward.

"Now," he began, his voice soft. "You will tell me who you are, all that you know about your mission here, how many of you there are, and any other relevant detail you may think of. You will leave out no details."

The assassin looked at Geneva, then back at him, before slowly nodding.

"My name is Khaezar Vaherkesh, daughter to Rahim al'Qadir Vaherkesh." She drew in a deep breath. "And I am not an assassin. I am a prisoner."

Far beyond the walls of Ironfel, in an outpost on the far edge of the sector, Viscount Alistair Veyron paced frantically within a darkened room, his formerly ash-grey robes now browned from dragging across the dirty floor.

In one corner of the room, his assistant, Edmund, stood alongside two of his most trusted guards, each one armed to the teeth with weapons distinctly not Lutian.

But they were not alone.

Two more individuals stood in the room with them, the symbol of a crimson spire gleaming on their dark robes.

Just like Khaezar, these individuals had also been cybernetically enhanced, the body of one almost completely replaced by metal. He was the bigger of the two and vaguely male in appearance. The other was slighter, sharper, yet even after several weeks, Alistair still couldn't tell if they were male, female, or something else in-between.

Both figures hid their faces beneath dark cowls, but their gleaming red irises were bright enough to produce a dim glow in the darkened room.

All six of them had raced out of Ironfel the moment the Witch of Selia began asking questions, and had been hiding in this outpost ever since, waiting to hear news from the one they had sent to distract the champion and eliminate Alan before he could spill anything of worth.

But nearly six hours had passed since their disgraceful exit. And yet, they had received no news about what was going on.

The silence in the room was oppressive. And with every second that passed, it grew heavier still, until, finally, the viscount couldn't take it anymore.

He turned to the Attilans with a scowl. "Where is Khaezar? She should be here by now. At the very least, she should have sent word!"

The Attilans exchanged glances but kept silent.

When neither responded, the viscount snapped. "Answer me, rust-bloods! Your masters sent you here to be useful. Be useful."

"Point us in a direction and we shall go," the slighter Attilan said, their voice an unnatural hiss that grated on his nerves. "But we have done our best to contact Khaezar, Viscount. Wherever she is, our messages aren't reaching her. Perhaps she's still hiding from the rune-witch you invited, knowing how precarious our situation is."

"I wouldn't have had to invite her at all had your masters not sent a horde into my domain to test the Way." Alistair shot back. "Had Alan not been there to create a portal, they would have wrecked my home to pieces!"

"Mind your tone, Lutian." The bulkier Attilan sneered, crimson eyes flashing. "The masters act with purpose beyond your understanding. I'm sure they had a brilliant agenda. You just failed to see it."

"Of course," Alistair scowled. "Perhaps they ought to begin sharing those agendas with me so I can better protect my city and family. I am the one providing the waypoint, after all. How are we to prepare Ironfel for their coming if they destroy it first by not following instructions?"

The Attilans hissed angrily, but Alistair just turned away, muttering angrily to himself as he resumed his pacing.

His arrangement with the Attilans had begun months earlier, when he'd found a naturally occurring Way deep within his city. He had sent two of his soldiers in to find out what was on the other side.

Neither had come back out.

Instead, an envoy of Attilans had appeared—and after much, desperate begging, they had agreed to spare his life should he dedicate Ironfel to them. They had shown him their weapons and power, how every individual was awakened, either naturally or cybernetically.

Mere children had crushed his guards, and Alistair had seen then, just how little hope Lutia had of defeating the one who had cursed them in the first place. Rather than suffer with the rest of his continent, he had chosen to dedicate his city as an arrival point in exchange for protection and the chance to hold onto his power when the Attilans eventually arrived for what they called the "reaping."

They would have come anyway—breaking through the continental shield should they have to. His Way simply provided them an easier method of entry. And after much haggling, the Attilans had finally agreed to his terms.

However, with the recent disasters—the horde and now Alan's death—that plan was in danger of crumbling. Should the Witch of Selia discover the origin of the horde, the reason for his sudden absence, or, even worse, one of the Attilans, then his plans to keep Ironfel from the reaping would be all for naught.

He had to put a stop to it. He had to find a way to spin all of this into an elaborate misunderstanding. Before he could come up with a well-spun tale, however, the bulkier Attilan spoke, his tone cold.

"If the rune-witch has found Khaezar, then it's over. We must return to Attilan and report to the masters."

"No." Alistair blurted out, his eyes widening. "Not yet. It's just as likely that she's hiding from Geneva, as you say. We must give her at least a day to show up before concluding that anything bad has happened."

The Attilans hesitated. "How will we know that we haven't been discovered before then?"

"Because if we have, my house and my family would have been contacted," Alistair said calmly. "Until then, we wait. We will make no report until we confirm that Khaezar is either dead or has been taken captive by the champion."

The Attilans exchanged uneasy glances, but acceded to his wishes in the end.

Alistair grimaced, his mind racing as he thought of ways to keep things under control. If Khaezar had succeeded in killing Alan, then Ironfel would be in a state of disarray at the moment, and their absence was most likely not helping matters.

Drawing a deep breath, he looked toward his assistant and commanded, "Return to Ironfel, Edmund. Tell them I was delayed at a meeting and will not be back till tomorrow. I need you to be my eyes and ears."

Edmund hesitated fearfully, glancing first at the Attilans before slowly nodding. "I will relay all the information back to you as best as I can, Viscount."

"Good," Alistair nodded. "I will expect news from you in an hour."

Edmund nodded, and after he left, Alistair glanced toward the Attilans, frustration rising within him.

"Find your friend!" he snapped. "Whether dead or alive, we must find her before anyone else does. If not, our mission will be compromised before it even has a chance to begin. And I'll be damned if I let that happen."

Thunder boomed beyond the inn with the promise of distant rain. Winds howled through the streets, and a fine drizzle spattered against the darkened windows.

A storm was coming.

But while the winds raged outside, a heavy silence reigned within the room as Khaezar spilled every detail she knew of Attilan to her captors, the words pouring from her lips like water through a broken basket.

She told them about her bioengineering. About the others like her who had fled with the viscount after he and Geneva had begun asking questions. She told them about their mission in Lutia, and the reason she had killed Alan.

She withheld nothing, whether important or trivial.

For nearly three hours, Geneva, Aldric, and Daruk simply listened, piecing together fragments of truth, confirming old suspicions, and discarding false assumptions, while the danger Attilan represented grew with every revelation.

By the time Khaezar finally fell silent, there was no longer any doubt about the true might of their enemy.

Attilan wasn't merely a continent.

It was an empire of rigid hierarchy and unquestioning worship, ruled by a single bloodline—the descendants of the Red Witch, each one seemingly a reincarnation of their progenitor, inheriting the same insatiable hunger for conquest.

For millennia, they had invaded continent after continent, cursing them with the Limit and carrying off their people as slaves. Many continents fought back, and some had regained their independence, but many—just like Lutia—had become subsidiary states instead.

And according to Khaezar, herself a slave from another subsidiary continent, that was all subsidiary states really were: tribute-payers.

Lutia had enjoyed a millennium of relative peace, and now it was time to "reap" their harvest.

Daruk could scarcely believe it.

All this while, he had wondered why the leaders of Lutia hadn't destroyed the curse yet. Why so much of their history was hidden. Why every answer seemed wrapped in half-truths and convenient lies.

And it turned out it was simply because the leaders of Lutia were too ashamed to admit they weren't quite as independent as they pretended to be. Weren't quite as sovereign.

It was sad and disappointing.

But more than that, it put the power of Attilan in perspective, seeing how easily they held other continents beneath them and constantly farmed them for resources—treasures, soul seeds, items, artifacts… the list was endless.

For several long seconds, no one spoke, their minds racing as they struggled to process the sheer magnitude of the threat Lutia was facing.

A millennium ago, when Attilan had first come to Lutia, they had been weaker than they were now. Fewer in number—yet they defeated them with ease. How much more terrifying were they now that their numbers and power had both grown? Now that they possessed bioengineered weapons capable of standing toe to toe with normal awakeneds?

Daruk shook his head worriedly.

Lutia wasn't ready. Not in the slightest. And from Geneva's expression, it was obvious she too thought the same.

"What shall we do?" Aldric asked, his expression heavy as he turned toward them. "Can we trust our superiors? If the viscount has jumped ship, can we trust they haven't?"

"We can trust some superiors," Geneva said, massaging her temples. "The Supreme, for instance. We can trust him."

"Then we have to take this directly to him," Daruk muttered. "No one else. And we have to find something to do about that Way Khaezar spoke about. Whatever Viscount Alistair is planning, we cannot let it happen."

"No," Geneva shook her head hurriedly, throwing both hands out to stop everyone for a moment. "Let us take a moment. We cannot be rash about this." She looked at Daruk, then shook her head again. "Confronting the Viscount or doing anything at all to the Way is a bad idea. The Attilans planned to do this in secret. Revealing that we already know will only cause them to hasten their timeline."

She looked at Khaezar. "How much time do we have?"

Khaezar hesitated, and it wasn't until Aldric prompted her that she responded, her expression going blank once more.

"Three months. The masters have been preparing for it for a year."

"Then the Way is their second option," Aldric deduced, glancing at Geneva and Daruk. "If the viscount only found the Way about six months ago, then they must plan to invade through the continental barrier."

"A bold move." Geneva frowned. "The barriers are notably hard to traverse. They allow Calamities to cross fairly easily, but breaking through them with anything else is nearly impossible."

"Unless they have devised a way to do so," Daruk muttered, glaring at the assassin. "If my main profession is conquest, then that would be my first hurdle."

"And the first thing you solve," Aldric finished, his gaze slowly returning to Khaezar. Drawing a deep breath, he asked, "Do they have a way to breach the continental barriers?"

Khaezar nodded once. But said nothing else. She knew nothing save the fact that they did.

Aldric looked at Geneva. "Okay, that's a big problem."

"This whole thing is a big problem," Geneva hissed, before forcing herself to take a deep breath. "What we have to do now is not blow the fuse. We leave Ironfel as though we suspect nothing, and report this to the Supreme."

"What about her?" Aldric asked, gesturing to Khaezar. "Her companions will be searching for her by now. If we kill her—"

"They'll know they've been compromised," Geneva finished. "The same goes if we capture her."

A long beat of silence passed. Then Geneva glanced at Daruk.

"Can you distort her memory of this meeting? If we're going to release her, then she can't remember anything that happened here today. She needs to believe she escaped our grasp."

Daruk looked at Khaezar for a long moment before slowly exhaling.

"I can do you one better." He met Geneva's gaze. "I can wipe her entire memory of this meeting clean and replace it with ice."
