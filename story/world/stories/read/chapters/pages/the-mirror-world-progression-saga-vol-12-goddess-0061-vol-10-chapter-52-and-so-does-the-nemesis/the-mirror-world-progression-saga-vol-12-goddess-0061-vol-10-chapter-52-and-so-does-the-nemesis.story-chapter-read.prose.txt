As soon as the mental attacks began rolling in, the Brain Trust, Jeeves, and the whole order knew what was happening and triggered contingencies. Samantha obeyed what was needed even as the spells were ripping through her wound negations: she fell back under the cover of her illusions, and others immediately reacted to cast very high-level invisibility and generate even more illusion layers along the way, making it seem the goddess went in twenty different directions.

This wouldn’t do anything for resisting the attacks, but it would prevent gang-piling her with dispels from the enemy army’s scattered groups.

“You’re on your own for a while,” Samantha said grimly into the gestalt, knowing the elite core needed her now more than ever. But she’d forged this temple of pragmatism out of her order — allowed it to become her anchor and her check, knowing that as powerful as she’d become, even she needed to bow to something greater.

Ideals and principles collectively enforced to protect them all. To preserve life and preserve their ultimate goal: to reach the end together.

They all checked each other, ensuring the right play at the right time, with endless layers of support, and no soul allowed to be an island.

Samantha flew at top speed back behind her lines, distancing herself from Matrazere’s towers in the process. Maybe that lack of proximity would help. Even one EL could mean the difference.

To that end, her pass was timed to trigger some final saved buffs from the stronger aggregate spellcasters, to narrow the gap. A lot of [Shaman] stuff. Spirits wrapped around her. Great Toad’s Untouchable Imperiousness, penalizing supernatural spell or ability effects affecting her by -3 ELs for 30 seconds. Discipline of the Great Hunt, adding a unique +4 (Supreme Discipline) modifier to any form of mental or spiritual resistance. An elven sage burned a sacred treasure and granted her Memory of Slain Glories, adding a unique +10 modifier to a single contest of choice.

Samantha herself began immediately engaging in the provision for Trilogy Fusion Soul, to refresh its wound negations, which could be done once every five minutes. It took five seconds, however, so she had to make other ‘arrangements’ in the meantime.

The next attempted ‘mind flaying’ still had to have an auto-success utilized from [Alter Fate], as she could sense the lethality of it.

Zooming over the heads of her soldiers, Samantha crossed her fingers that she could generate enough of those to get through their strongest strikes. That their collective effort would endure the might of a vast and extensive empire, in its penultimate organizational act, all that they were arrayed as one to destroy one little ‘parasitic’ nuisance.

I’ll make them earn it at the least, damn it.

To that end, Samantha immediately branched through the waiting mirror portals, targeting things constantly tracked and ready to be pointed out by Resemblants: her own lesser Servitors to target negatively in the battle. A matter they’d been practicing and had prepared in diligence for this sort of anticipated occasion — and so she would be cursing her own people to recharge her precious [Alter Fate] ability per its inherent rules.

She felt guilty — small as the relevance might be, it had to be relevant. She knew the sheer wrongness was the cost more than anything. A symbolic sacrifice of obeisance on the altar of Fate, and a little more of her ego, soul, and conscience picked away to do it. But she did it all the same: an Angel was cursed and failed in his attack on random zombie soldiers. Samantha’s ability recharged.

Another overwhelming attack came, and the same scenario played out once more. She forced her own success, and she recharged it by cursing a fighting Servitor. She continued her casting to restore the spell’s wound negations. Two more seconds, the rest of her focus and action economy purely dedicated to movement.

Come on, come on, come on…

Again. Auto-success, and the last recharge. Another attack… too strong…

After the forced auto-success for the fourth time, the fifth attack was just another strike from another angle, overwhelming like the rest… wait. Was it slightly less strong? She felt a thin hope at that. But how many would there be of these, too? It was still too much to allow.

Samantha almost expended the last [Alter Fate] function to force failure. But she hesitated. What if they were saving some of their strongest for later in their sequence?

She had to risk it — just in case. She used Memory of Slain Glories, to claim the ridiculous +10 bonus for one resistance.

Briefly, she saw a vision:

A nameless elven god fighting an overwhelming force of mind mages, hitting him with everything they had. He kept charging, even as his mind was being ripped apart.

“I’ll take all of you with me, you bastards! May the land I’ve loved grant me the strength!” He cut one mage to ribbons and staggered against the attacks, but turned to the next and pointed his blade. Grinned. “I don’t slay easily, scum.”

With a feral cry, enduring tremendous pain, he charged.

The elven god’s strength briefly became hers as her mind fought off the damage and held firm. Nonetheless, she felt pain and a sense of worry, particularly for the interruption of her lengthy spellcasting.

A Minor Wound, but then even that was reduced, thanks to [Timeless Coalescent]. It became Superficial.

Thank the heavens.

Her recharging of Trilogy Fusion Soul was completed, giving her two more precious — nearly unheard of — mental wound negations to burn through.

Samantha made it to the healer’s camp, which was now a very impressive, physically walled-off, heavily protected structure with tents raised inside, so healers could engage in the more intimate acts of healing, and even perform physical surgery if useful.

She wasn’t physically noticed by the people shuffling and flying around. Not only were they busy, but she was invisible, and her lingering antimagic shield was not innately noticeable when inactive. She simply parked herself inside the walls and touched her boots to the dirt, to finally claim the full bonus of Tashome’s altered artifact.

No additional spells had been hitting her for a long while, and nothing assailed her except for the constant assault on her mind.

In the free time available as the wound negations protected her, Samantha cast her best anti-detection and scrying spell — one that required you not to attack, which was basically perfect for her purposes. It might not do anything to the ritualists, but… if the enemy had some lesser ritualists on later strike teams in the sequence, they might fail to find her, or get additional penalties… who knew. Aside from that, it ensured problems for everyone without Sympathy to find her and target her. Her totals were already preposterous on that front, outside the range of even the best specialists.

In her mind, she quickly went through all the things she could leverage and the final emergency acts she might need to execute. Mental attacks had a much shorter list of feasible strategies. The ‘tree transformation’ trick Redberry had pulled in the Merrington battle wasn’t going to save the day, this time. She was nearly indomitable in the physical realm, but she’d failed rather historically against the other realms, after all.

The Dominion had done a fabulous job wiping out the mental specializations of other factions. Healers of that specialty practically didn’t exist outside the secrets of their order. Rogue psychics were almost universally telekinetics, telepaths, and such, without healing specialties. It was some lost special class they’d annihilated. Much the same as someone like Samantha would have to start a new culture of Magineers, such specialties didn’t always thrive.

She did find a little extra trick that would stack with everything. [Spirit Sight] could improve resistance if the eyes were closed, and the Third Eye, specifically, was utilized. It wasn’t an exotic bonus, but instead improved the base multiclass resistance. Her base was off of [Magineer] at Level 14, with [Goddess] at 11 providing a multiclassing bonus of +4. With [Spirit Sight], the virtual [Pneumamancer] class at the max of Level 20 provided +7 instead.

To squeeze the extra +3 for resistance, Samantha closed her eyes and saw everything only in the spirit world reflection. With mostly temporary things around, it was very vague and ghostly.

No matter. I’m unlikely to need to see for this bullshit. Hello Darkness, my old frieeeeend… hehe.

The wound negations were soon blown through, and another scouring mental attack came. Weaker, this time. The math was processed in her head about what she’d need. Could she cobble together enough without blowing another +10 biggun through My Future Now?

She was closer with the Third Eye shenanigans, meaning she needed +7, possibly even just +6, because this was weaker than what was beaten before. She had [Alter Fate: Hoping For Daylight] that could give +4… and two different (Luck) type boosts. One was [Rogue’s Luck], which was +1, or +2 if it would result in a tie. The other was a one-use spell buff that had been on her all day, which gave +3.

Good enough. I can move to other options after this. Thank the Counter Crew for keeping all my damn buffs on me, or I’d be totally screwed right now. We learned from Merrington.

She went for it with [Hoping For Daylight] and the temp buff, for a total of +7.

Clean. A Superficial Wound dealt — nothing more than a sting in her head. That probably meant she’d tied them.

Cool beans.

But the attacks didn’t stop. Another came…

Memories and sensations of trauma came back to her — mutilation, fighting against Dreixia Hillcrusher… the monstrous ‘spirit-claw’ of the lich goring into her soul’s substance, pain enough to make her black out… the arrow of the Archer piercing her heart. Pain enough to make crawling into a hole to die seem like a great alternative to continuing.

She tuned them all out like the buzz of mere noise as she steeled herself. If that was what it took, she’d bear it all again. Ten times over, if need be.

She recalled the rampaging elven god and knew she was thinking much the same as he did. If nothing else, she would last long enough to see that tower destroyed.

I swear I will. No matter what. I promised them all: the whole army. They’re dying for me, for this, in droves. I can’t let them down.
