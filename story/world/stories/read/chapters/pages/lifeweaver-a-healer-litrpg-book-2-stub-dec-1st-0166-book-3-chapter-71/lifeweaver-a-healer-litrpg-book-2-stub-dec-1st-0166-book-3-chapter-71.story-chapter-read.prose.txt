One of the human nurses, a middle-aged guy a few pounds overweight and with the red-flushed face of someone who indulged a little too often, grabbed Luke's forearm and shoved it near the injured Singer's face.

Luke pulled back, trying to get a handle on the situation. "No! I don't heal Singers!"

"We don't turn anyone away! This is the ICU!" the other nurse shouted, a wild look in his eyes.

Still not grasping the situation, Luke did at least register that the two men before him weren't what they seemed. In fact, they weren't even human. It was more of a struggle than he would have thought, but in the end, monster cores meant monsters, not humans. With both their cores tucked into his inventory and their corpses on the ground, Luke turned back to the Singer. This one wasn't a monster. It was an actual Singer, and an injured one at that.

Claw marks across the chest, a piercing wound straight through the head, a huge chunk of thigh muscle missing, and a piece of its jaw gone were just some of its injuries. Whatever had happened to the Singer, it couldn't have been pleasant. Luke stood there over the dying Singer, ignoring the bustle outside the door to the triage room. He read the quest description again, pondering its meaning.

"Eliminate or unite," he said, wondering how literal it was. Make a friend of the enemy or kill it? Movement from Dot somewhere above made him snap out of it and come to a decision, one he thought it likely he'd come to regret before he was through the dungeon. Luke healed the Singer. In doing so, he came upon organs wrapped in mana in a way that was a little too familiar. Inspecting the patient confirmed his suspicions. There was a reason this female Singer still lived despite her mangled state.

Integrated Luom. Lifeweaver. Level 23.

"Lifeweaver," Luke breathed, his eyes going to his patient's face. It was ashen, but the life hadn't fled, not yet, not with Luke having healed her. He'd used her mana to do it, of course, which left little for her to work with. Unless he returned some to her, it would take quite a while for her to wake up.

"Dammit," he cursed, pulling a little mana from himself to funnel it into her Meta-Heart.

That made her eyes flutter open, and she frowned when he saw him, then turned her head to survey the room. "Hmm?"

"Who are you?" Luke asked.

Loum reached out with a thread of mana, pushing into the mana channels of Luke's hand. From there, it continued all the way up to his shoulder, then on to his heart. In the blink of an eye, a Needle of Life appeared on the end of the thread. The Singer was quick, but not all that skilled, and her will was weak. Luke wasn't sure how he knew that, or how exactly he used it against her, but he did. Pushing back dissolved the thread before it could do any damage whatsoever, and Luom looked like she'd just been slapped.

"Stop that," he said, extending his own threads of mana, positioning several near vital organs and one by her throat, as well as her Meta-Heart.

Luom's eyes widened, and she pressed herself against the chair, as if trying to escape his threads. Looking at him warily, she then narrowed her eyes and wet her lips. "You speak my language, Lifeweaver."

"You're speaking mine," Luke said, still unsure if he should let her live. "Who are you and why are you here?"

"A trick of this space, then. I'm sure you can see my name, Luke, and my reason for being here is the same as yours, I would imagine. The destiny gateway. A human attacked me the moment I stepped through." She eyed the corpses on the floor. "Looks like you have met them, but fared better."

"They are monsters disguised as humans. You should have been able to tell that."

"There wasn't much time," she said, eyeing the threads hovering right in front of her. "Do you mind?"

"Your people are attacking Earth, and your first reaction when you saw me was to attack."

"A natural reaction in my situation, wouldn't you say?"

"You're not very good at it," Luke said. “Attacking, I mean.”

Luom shrugged, a very human-looking response. "I'm a healer, not a fighter. Also, you have reached almost twice as far on the path toward ascension."

"The path toward what?"

"We must ever raise our voices toward the stars," she intoned, as if reciting some religious text.

Not a great answer, but the aftertaste of zeal in her words didn't agree with him, so Luke jumped back to a more important subject. "Why are you attacking Earth?"

She gave him a searching look, then leaned forward a little as her expression turned to one of confusion. "You agreed to come under our rule in exchange for protection and safe passage out of your system for a few select individuals. I am just a healer, but this information was made known to every Singer. Surely you must know? Your name was on the list."

Luke blinked. "What?"

"It is well that I found you here, and you have my gratitude for saving my life. Now let me return it and ensure your safe return, Lifeweaver Luke Quinn."

"What other names were on this list?" Luke asked, getting a terrible feeling about this whole thing. What the hell was she going on about? Was it a ruse of some sort? A way to throw him off balance? If so, it was working. Working a little too well.

"Lifeweaver Dorothy Marlene, Lifeweaver Timothy Hastings, Lifeweaver Christos Stergios, Lifeweaver Luke Quinn, Fallen Shepherd Relian, last name unknown."

There it was. The end goal of Relian's scheming. As if the failed deal with the void wasn't enough, he'd come to an agreement with the Singers, handing over Earth in exchange for safe passage, no matter which Lifeweaver he ended up possessing. Diabolical.

"Well, shit," Luke said, taking a step back and shaking his head. "This won't stand. That man had no authority to deal with you. Earth won't roll over and do whatever you want."

"Let me assure you, resistance is futile. Those on the list will be offered safe passage, but other Integrated will learn to live as our subjects. Like I told you, I'm just a healer, a good one, but still a healer, so I have no insight into who brokered the terms or why."

"I have a pretty good idea," Luke said. He drew in a few deep breaths, forcing himself to calm down. "Like I said, the deal is off."

"I'm afraid that is both out of the question and out of my hands."

"Then take me to someone who I can broker a new deal with."

"Bringing you to our system is one of the objectives of our coming to Earth. Going back on the agreement will not be an option, but I'm sure you can have your voice heard if you so wish."

Luke considered that for a moment before nodding. It was a way forward that would result in fewer deaths if the Singers agreed to tear up the agreement. A long shot, to be sure, but an avenue he didn't want to ignore.

"Let's go then," Luke said, gesturing for her to stand. "We'll get through this dungeon, and then I'll make your people see reason."

Luom's expression told him everything he needed to know about the chances of this new plan succeeding, but he owed it to the rest of the world to try.

"I'm assuming you have portals opening in every place with one of the countdown objects?" he asked as he cracked the door open to check outside. The corridor just outside was full of blood and injured people, with hospital staff rushing back and forth trying to help. It was as if they would be stepping out into a hospital receiving patients from some nearby massacre.

"I'm just a healer," Luom reiterated. Either she didn't know or refused to answer his question.

Luke glared back at her. "Then tell me this much: why have every Singer I've met tried to kill me, if you're supposed to offer me safe passage, or whatever?"

Her expression darkened at that, and she set her jaw. "Honor is a complicated topic. A need to prove oneself, or vengeance for a slight, imagined or not, should not trump a royal decree, but we Singers don't always work as a cohesive unit. I can offer apologies for the actions of any Singers who have made attempts on your life."

"Eh," Luke grunted. He was over it. "Let's just go. Before anything else, we need to get my people and get out of here."

Luke was about to open the door and step out when he thought of another question, one he was sure Curtis would want answered once he got back. "How did your forerunners even get here? And how did you open portals to Earth?"

"All I know is that we were provided with an item from Earth through a shared dungeon. It allowed us to communicate with a local guild from your system, and this item also acted as an anchor point for a few select Singers to travel between systems. How the travel itself was achieved, I do not know. The name of the guild was shared with me once, but I cannot recall."

Luke was piecing the whole thing together now, he thought, and had a pretty good guess. "Luminous Larks?"

But she shook her head. "No, that's not it. Something about different ways of achieving a goal."

Luke got a sinking feeling. "Integrated Solutions Group?"

Luom's face shone, and she nodded with a smile. "That's it!"
