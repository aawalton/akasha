Later that night, Victoria returned to the room, the floral scent of jasmine and lotus trailing behind her like a delicate veil. A serene smile graced her lips, as if simply smelling that way brought her a deep sense of contentment.

Her eyes lit up the moment she saw Luke, and in a flash, she was by his side.

“This soap is amazing! I’ve never felt so clean and fresh in my life,” she exclaimed, grabbing his hands in excitement.

Luke offered a subtle smile. If Victoria was this enthusiastic over soap, he could only imagine the stir it would cause among the noblewomen of Ralis City. More than that, he found himself surprised—he had never seen this side of her before.

Victoria was usually one of two things: either feminine and playful, or wildly dangerous and drenched in blood. Seeing her this thrilled over something so mundane created a jarring contrast, one that unsettled his expectations of this so-called femme fatale.

“This is only the beginning,” he blurted out before he could stop himself. “I plan on making an entire beauty line for women—moisturizing cream, rouge, nail gloss, even special clothing to make the monthly cycle more convenient.”

He winced inwardly. Why had he revealed so much? There were still countless hurdles ahead. He didn’t even know how to make most of these items yet, nor did he understand what ingredients were available or viable in this world.

I’m still lacking a lot of the alchemical knowledge here, he thought. I’ll need to absorb those books first before I can start theorycrafting the ingredients.

While Luke had successfully created gunpowder, soap, and perfume, he’d had at least a basic understanding of those things from Earth. His Scholarly Pursuit skill helped him deduce the necessary components, but the process itself still required plenty of trial and error.

Victoria’s grip on his hands tightened, her brown eyes glowing with excitement in the flickering candlelight. “I don’t know what most of those are, but if they’re anything like the soap and perfume, I can’t wait to see them.”

Luke found himself lost in her gaze, her eyes warm and vibrant, brimming with genuine interest. It took a moment before he snapped out of it and gently pulled his hands away.

Clearing his throat, he shifted the topic. “They’re still a work in progress. Some of the ingredients might be expensive to acquire, so we’ll need to be strategic. I suggest we start by distributing free samples to the noblewomen’s circles in Ralis City before talking to Hilda. Once we secure a steady income, we can focus on expanding the product line.”

He looked away, trying to calm the rhythm of his heartbeat.

“How will you hand out the samples?” he asked, more composed now.

“There’s a gathering at the end of the week,” she replied confidently. “I’ll attend and distribute them then.”

If she hadn’t already proven herself, Luke might have worried about the risk. But this was the same woman who’d pulled off high-stakes maneuvers in both Clayton City and Valand City.

“Alright,” Luke nodded, letting out a slow stretch. “I’ll need to prepare a few more batches with different scents. These noblewomen will want something unique—something that sets them apart.”

After saying this, Luke bid Victoria goodnight and slipped into bed. He had a lot to do in the coming weeks, but for now, he was confined to the Royal Academy until he could confirm whether the Black Tiger Gang was still after his head.

With the gunpowder recipe already handed over to the royal alchemist, Luke hoped the assassination contract had been rescinded. After all, unless the gang bore a deep personal grudge, there was no longer a reason for him to be targeted.

Or at least that’s how he justified it to himself.

The following week passed peacefully. Luke settled into a new routine, structured and productive. Each morning, he rose early and made his way to the library, where he absorbed nearly a thousand books before attending his lectures.

Afternoons were spent refining his swordplay or experimenting with new batches of perfume. Sometimes, he would scribble down ideas for additional products—refinements for his budding beauty line.

Evenings were a welcome reprieve. He, Kayson, and Sebastian would gather for dinner at their lodgings, sharing idle conversation and laughter. Compared to the chaos of a siege, life at the Royal Academy felt almost serene.

But Luke knew better. That peace was only surface-deep.

Beneath the pleasant exterior, he sensed a current of ambition and deceit. Everyone was clawing for power, and vipers like Ming Hu were simply waiting for the right moment to strike.

Without power of his own, Luke had to rely on strategy and subtlety. His line of perfumes and feminine products was the first step—his gateway to wealth, influence, and survival. But anonymity was key. If the noble families discovered his involvement, they might see him as a threat to be eliminated.

At some point, Victoria had disappeared without a word. Still, he wasn’t worried. The noble gathering was tonight, and she had already laid the groundwork. All that remained was for her to execute the plan.

Luke had no doubt that she would.

In an undisclosed location within Ralis City, the sound of live music echoed through a grand, candlelit hall. The elegant plucking of a pipa carried through the air, adding an exotic liveliness to the luxurious setting.

Despite the lively tune, the guests weren’t mingling as one might expect. Instead, nearly twenty noblewomen clustered together near the edge of the room, their voices hushed, their eyes alight with anticipation.

“Hurry, where is this perfume you spoke of, Violet? I’ve been waiting all week,” one of them said, barely able to contain her excitement.

The woman they called Violet turned, a brilliant smile gracing her features. Her olive-toned skin and tightly pinned dark hair exuded elegance, and her earthy green robe accentuated her natural beauty. With a graceful motion, she produced a medium-sized wooden box from within her sleeve.

The box was beautifully crafted, its polished surface catching the lamplight. The word Parfum was carved into the top in elegant script.

A collective breath was held. The noblewomen leaned in.

“Now, now, ladies,” Violet said with a playful smirk. “I only have ten vials, so some of you will have to share. These are just free samples. If you wish to purchase your own, you’ll need to wait until my friend is ready to distribute them properly.”

“Can’t we just buy directly from you, Violet?” one woman asked with a pout, pressing her fingers together. “That way, I’ll know for certain the product is authentic.”

Showing incredible patience, Violet smiled softly and shook her head, “I am far too busy to resell my friend’s products, I’m merely doing him a favor. But before we talk about the details, you should try out the perfume.”

The women seemed to realize they were getting ahead of themselves, their excited chatter tapering off.

Violet opened the wooden box with practiced poise. A luxurious lining of deep red velvet greeted their eyes, adding a sense of opulence to the moment. Nestled inside, in individual compartments, sat pristine white ceramic vials, each adorned with elegant calligraphy.

“Jade Mist? Silken Petals?” one woman read aloud, brow raised in curiosity.

“That’s the name of the scents,” Violet explained, gesturing toward the vials. “Remember, you’ll need to share with a partner until we’re ready to distribute them for sale.”

The box emptied quickly, each noblewoman claiming a vial with eager hands. A quiet anticipation lingered in the air as they examined the fine craftsmanship and delicate lettering.

“Open the vial and cover the hole with your finger,” Violet instructed, kneeling to place the now-empty box on the ground before retrieving her own vial from within her robe. “Tip it like this, then dab the fragrance onto your neck and wrists.”

With measured grace, she demonstrated. One by one, the others mimicked her movements.

“Even though I used so little, the scent is so powerful…”

“This is like heaven—I never knew I could smell like a blooming jasmine flower.”

As the perfume settled onto their skin, the expressions on their faces shifted—wonder, delight, envy. Violet watched it all unfold, her smile serene.

But only those with a keen eye would have noticed the derision flickering behind that smile.

“Announcing the arrival of Fairy Qin!”

The announcement echoed through the chamber.

Instantly, the women clustered around Violet tensed. As if responding to a signal, they raised their heads in near unison, a shared expression passing among them.

The entire atmosphere of the room transformed.

On one side, the noblewomen’s eyes sharpened, turning cold and venomous as they stared toward the entrance. On the other, the men gazed in silent awe and longing, craning to catch a glimpse of the famed beauty.

“Let’s flaunt our new perfume in front of this bitch,” one of the women sneered, her voice laced with spite.

“Yeah,” another hissed, “now’s our chance to show her who's really superior.”

The group sauntered toward Qin Hu, their intentions thinly veiled. For years, they had endured her fame, her elegance, and the admiration she commanded. Now they saw their moment to finally knock the Fairy of Ralis City from her pedestal.

Meanwhile, Violet quietly slipped away, her exit unnoticed. With attention fixed on Qin Hu, no one spared a glance her way.

She had done her job perfectly.

Pausing at the door, Violet glanced over her shoulder for one final look at Qin Hu. A subtle smile tugged at her lips before she vanished into the corridor.

The woman masquerading as Violet was none other than Victoria. A master of disguise and infiltration, she had one peculiar habit—every alias she used began with the letter V.

Outside the grand hall, a towering figure stood by the door. Broad-shouldered and imposing, the figure’s sheer size might’ve led one to mistake her for a man—if not for the long blond ponytail cascading down her back.

“Babysitting kids now, are you, Hilda?” Victoria teased, appearing beside her like a wraith.

Hilda frowned and turned. Recognition flickered across her features, but with a subtle gesture from Victoria, she stifled her reaction.

“What are you doing here? Shouldn’t you be at the Academy with Sebby and Luke?” she whispered, her tone low and guarded.

“Luke knows I’m here. It was actually his idea…” Victoria said as she stepped in closer.

She began to outline the plan, keeping it deliberately vague—there wasn’t much time. Qin Hu was likely already being humiliated inside the hall, and there was no telling how long the woman would endure before leaving.

“Sebastian told us you would help facilitate the deal—using Qin Hu as a distributor for a new line of feminine products,” Victoria explained. Her tone sharpened slightly as she met Hilda’s gaze. “All you need to do is bring it up if she mentions it. That’s it. Just plant the seed.”

Victoria’s instincts were rarely wrong. As someone skilled in espionage and manipulation, she understood what made people tick—and she knew that invoking Sebastian’s name would likely compel Hilda to agree without much resistance.

But to her surprise, Hilda’s face twisted into a pained expression.

“Little Qin is such a sweet girl…” she murmured, her brow furrowed. “I’m not sure if I can use her like this.”

Victoria forced herself to remain calm. She softened her voice and extended her wrist. “We’re not taking advantage of her,” she said gently. “These products are revolutionary. Smell for yourself.”

Though still skeptical, Hilda leaned down—her towering frame casting a long shadow in the corridor—and sniffed the offered wrist. A moment passed in silence.

Then her eyes widened.

“This scent… is it jasmine?” she asked, awe creeping into her voice.

Victoria gave a nod. “It’s called perfume. One of Luke’s new products. Our goal is to sell this and other feminine items to the Black Tiger Gang using Qin Hu as the intermediary. It benefits everyone involved, and no one gets hurt.”

She watched as her words settled in. Hilda’s fingers rose to her chin, her thick brows knitting together in thought.

“I see… the Black Tiger Gang gains a monopoly on something entirely new,” she murmured. “And Luke keeps his identity hidden. Clever.”

Victoria stepped closer, her voice steady. “So, I ask again. Can we trust you with this?”

Hilda looked at her with solemn eyes. “As long as no harm befalls Qin Hu,” she said, “I will fulfill my duties… to my husband.”
