It turned out Luke was far from immune to pain. He'd known that, of course, but thought himself to have become somewhat of an expert in handling it. Perhaps that was still true, but it didn't help. Didn't help at all. Screaming himself hoarse from the sheer pain of having infernal mana passing through his system at an unprecedented rate, Luke struggled to stay conscious long enough to deal with the massive demon before it caught Penny and crushed her like an insect. Speaking of insects, he'd attached himself to the back of the demon's thigh, like a tick hoping not to be discovered before it got what it came for. Luke wasn't looking to drain the demon's blood because he was pretty sure that would burn him to death, but rather its infernal mana. To be fair, that burned about as much as the sludge did, but the mana Luke could use.

The use he'd chosen this time was to sever the demon's head using Needle of Life in the shape of a long knife. With one such knife, the work would take ages, if he ever made it through the demon's thick skin, but he used more than one. Six, to be precise. Not that long ago, that would have been more than he could handle. Now, it was nothing. Adding the three he used to cause small explosions of force in the demon's eyes and infernal core, and the three to keep him fixed to the demon, now that was starting to become somewhat of a challenge. Sweat poured down his face from equal parts white-knuckling the pain, since even numbing the nerves didn't work - the pain was metaphysical in nature, after all - and equal parts concentration to keep up the attacks. The focus required was so much that he didn't even notice the demon hand coming to slap him. It was like being struck by a bus. Even with the healing mana stored in Luke's Meta-Heart, he blacked out for a second. When he came to, he had just fallen. Reaching upward and forward with a thread, he weavestepped back toward the demon's exposed skin, then latched himself to the leg again. This time, he was just below the back of the knee.

"Won't get rid of me that easily," he grumbled, feeling his broken body putting itself back together from the various broken bones and crushed organs. He wasn't sure he could take another one of those slaps. With that in mind, he redoubled his efforts. The other demon of this size they'd met, Curtis and Hannah killed by decapitation. If they could, Luke should be able to as well, damn it! Ignoring the infernal core and other attacks, he settled in to focus on the damn neck.

Penny: "How are you doing down there in the muck, loser?"

Luke: "I told you already, I got here as fast as I could!"

Penny: "Pfft. You owe me! There are Singers in Chicago, too, apparently!"

Luke: "We'll get them all. Now let me focus, girl!"

Penny: "Oh, you didn't just call me 'girl', you little bitch!"

Luke chuckled to himself, knowing she'd blow up over that.

Lightning, I’m done asking! Hit!

Strike here! Hard, fast, now!

Keep the party going!

Lightning surged from straight above the demon and slammed into the top of its head with incredible force. Rather than dissipating, it stuck around like a long string connecting the huge monster to the sky above. With their opponent now occupied and seemingly in a whole lot of pain, judging by the shrieks, Luke got the window he needed. He formed Needle of Life into an assortment of medical tools used for slicing a person open, pushing ribs out of the way, and even performing amputations. With all that focus, he saw some results. From his position, there was no way to see what was happening up above, but he didn't need to see the wound to sense it using Weaver's Perception. It was small at first, but grew at an impressive rate. Given enough time, he could do this. Luke could end the fight.

The demon died before he got the chance. Sustained lightning to the brain was enough to get it to the point that it pushed the skull apart with enough force to hurl pieces of it several streets over. Orange sludge rained down, and the rain hissed when coming into contact with it. Luke held on for dear life as the demon fell, crashing into a building and bringing it down. Once it hit the ground, it was like an earthquake had hit the city. Not the worst experience after the death of an enemy, but far from the best. Solid two out of five.

Penny: "I didn't kill you, did I?"

Luke: "I'm alive. Go check on Mateo."

Penny: "Shit! Mateo!"

Luke used Weaver's Perception to find Mateo buried under a mountain of dead lesser demons. Many live ones still roamed the street, but Penny blanketed the ground with more bolts of lightning, either killing or scaring them off. Ray was down there with Mateo and, funnily enough, the heap of dead monsters had protected them from being overrun. Not that they saw the humor in it. Mateo, at least, grinned at Luke and threw his arms around him with a whoop of joy.

"Luke! ¡Mi hermano! Together again at last!"

Ray tried and failed to wipe dead demon gunk off his shirt and then shook his head. "This is so stupid. Demons are a bitch to fight against."

"Hey, Mateo," Luke laughed as Penny descended, holding another demon crystal.

When she set down on the ground, her knees almost buckled. She regained control but looked exhausted.

"Can't you do something about the rain?" Ray complained.

Penny looked up and closed her eyes, as if savoring the downpour. "Nope, I can't. The storm kind of got away from me. Never done something this big before, and I rode it all the way from Chicago. Do you know how hard it is to get clouds moving with wind? Really hard. Better to let this keep going until I know I won't be needing my bolts anymore."

"What about mana?" Luke asked, waving at the sky. "Doing all this must have taken a lot, but you're still about half-full."

"You peeking at me?" Penny asked with a playful glare.

Luke shrugged. "I am the all-seeing eye."

Ray frowned. "Sauron?"

"Odin?" Mateo suggested.

"Horus?" Luke said, shrugging again.

"Well," Penny began, holding up a circular object that looked like a glass bead about the size of her fist. "I've been hitting the orbs pretty hard."

"Orbs?" Luke asked.

She smirked and tossed it to him. "Helps having a lot of good friends. There's no telling what might become useful."

Catching it, Luke extended his senses into the object, but found nothing. "What is it?"

"Push your mana into it."

He focused for a moment, unsure what she meant, then moved a thread of mana into the orb. It pulled mana out of him with so much force that it scared him a little, so he cut it off. When he looked again, a faint, light blue glow had appeared inside.

Penny nodded. "Now pull it back."

Luke did so, marveling at the simplicity of it.

"This is great," he said.

Penny produced two more out of her inventory and threw them to him, forcing Luke into an impromptu juggling act until he brought two out of three into his own inventory.

"Remember your old pal, Simon?" Penny asked. "Like I said, it helps to have great people skills."

Luke looked up from the orb, which he was now in the process of filling. "This is great. The mana just stays in there?"

She nodded. "No cooldown either, so they're way better than potions."

"Potions rule," Ray scoffed.

"How much do they hold?" Luke asked, thinking of Simon the Mana Refiner. He'd hoped for something along the lines of these at some point, but he had kind of forgotten about it.

"A bunch. There's an art to filling them, you'll see. Once I got the hang of it, I could almost get half my mana into one, but I'm still improving. Simon asked me to give you some, and now you have three, and I have three. Fair?"

"Fair," Luke said, staring into the orb. The mana inside looked a lot like a thread, except it took the form of a glowing cloud. "I'll have to thank Simon."

"About that..."

Luke looked up. "What?"

Her face fell. "He died."

"What?" he barked. "How?"

"You remember how he worked, right? Going inside dungeons to do his thing, but stayed out of trouble? Yeah, well, it turns out some monsters want to leave and head over to Earth through the portal. One surprised Simon. He was dead when the rest of the Integrated had completed the dungeon and were leaving."

"Shit," Luke said.

Penny nodded. "Shit."

Checking his inventory, he saw that the orbs were called Mana Orbs. Simple enough. He held an orb up. "To Simon."

"To Simon," the others said.

They stood in silence for a while, listening to the rain pattering on the metal roof of an abandoned food cart.

"Hey!" Lemira shouted, exiting the building where Luke had found her on the roof. "Well done, everyone!"

"I like this one," Penny said, nodding toward the approaching half-demon.

"Don't know about that," Ray said.

Glaring at Ray until he shied back, Penny shook her head. "Lemira is right. The demons are a pain. We should deal with them before the Singers. As much as it irks my ass to allow them free rein of Chicago."

"Our fellow Integrated in our home city will do an admirable job in making sure everyone is safe," Mateo said, but it was clear he wasn't so sure.

"What about your family?" Luke asked.

"I drove them to my beloved's parents a few hours outside of the city. They'll be safe there. Just in case."

Penny looked from Mateo to Luke. "If you're wondering about your own parents, you don't have to. The DIA sent some sort of envoy to the Singers, and that guy you're so chummy with, Johanson, let us know the Singers won't attack anyone as long as they don't resist."

"What a shit-show," Ray said.

"I'm afraid our beloved country has had a rough go of it," Mateo said.

"Country?" Penny asked. "The Singers are all over the world, Mateo."

Lemira caught the tail end of the conversation when she reached the group, and it was clear what she thought about their worries over the Singer invasion.

Luke took one glance at her, then held up his hands in surrender, not letting go of the Mana Orb he was filling with mana. "Yes, yes. Demons first. Don't worry. We'll have enough crystals gathered in no time."

Penny held up the one from the demon they'd just exterminated, then handed it to Lemira.

"That makes six," Lemira said. "Three more."

"Let's split up," Ray said.

Luke shook his head. "I don't think that's a good idea. The demons are a bitch to kill, and Lemira is the only one who knows where they are. If we go together, we can wreck them."

"To wrecking!" Mateo cried.

Everyone looked at Ray, who sighed. "Fine. To wrecking!"

"To wrecking!" they all shouted.

It was time to end the system event and exterminate every demon who dared set foot on Earth.
