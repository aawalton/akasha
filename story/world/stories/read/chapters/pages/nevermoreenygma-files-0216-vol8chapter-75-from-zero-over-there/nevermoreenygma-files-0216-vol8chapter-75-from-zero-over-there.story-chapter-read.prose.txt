Chapter 75

From Zero… Over There

The young-looking man with pointed ears and long hair awoke facedown, spitting blood as he groaned. Every movement sent a sharp jolt of pain through him.

It felt as though his body had been pressed against a bed of long nails, and now his bones were piercing through all his organs. He tried to move, only to collapse again with a groan, breathing rapidly.

The sound of bones cracking came from inside his body, along with other noises like flesh being torn apart, only for everything to shift back into place. His muscles and bones twisted beneath his skin and exposed wounds. Knees. Arms. Feet and hands. The blood that seemed to have poured from his body at some point was evaporating and disappearing.

He didn't know how, but he was broken.

And at the same time, he knew that his bones and muscles were each finding their proper place, little by little. It hurt like hell, but whenever something seemed to settle back into place, the pain subsided considerably, almost disappearing.

For several minutes, he remained like that, groaning and breathing heavily while listening to his heart pound at a frantic pace. Until, finally, the pain stopped, and he breathed.

When he opened his eyes, the night wind was blowing fiercely through a place in ruins.

Through his blurred vision, he thought he saw something white.

His vision slowly came into focus.

Was that a mouse? What was it doing there?

It was right in front of him. Small as a snowball, standing out against the dark ground.

The animal squeaked and ran away.

The young man tried to look around. It was dark, but he could see through the gloom thanks to the moonlight, which bathed everything in a pale clarity that somehow felt dark at the same time.

Fragments of stone, broken columns, and unrecognizable figures lay scattered everywhere. It was some kind of plaza, or something resembling one, filled with sculptures.

Was that collapsed thing a destroyed elephant?

Yes. It was a shattered elephant.

An elephant? What was that thing on its back? An obelisk?

After several minutes that felt like an eternity, he finally felt strong enough to try sitting up. Everything still hurt, but the worst had already passed, and with every second, it was as though the pain were drifting farther and farther away.

He shifted, trying to get his bearings. He looked around.

At the center of it all was him, lying in a dark crater with a wide radius.

In the middle of that crater, there he was.

Him.

More air suddenly rushed into his lungs, as though he had just remembered how to breathe. His body tensed, his muscles reacting before his mind could. It took him several seconds to move.

Longer to understand that he was awake and not dreaming.

Him.

Who am I? he wondered.

[What am I?] a second voice asked, almost as if questioning the first.

Who was he?

He remembered nothing.

Not a name. Not a face. Not a place.

He slowly pushed himself up, bracing one hand against the cracked ground. His skin was bare, covered only in dark dust. The warmth beneath his fingers made him lower his gaze.

The ground was scorched. Broken. He was at the center.

A thought crossed his mind, quick and shapeless, and he looked up at the starry sky, clouds moving rapidly overhead.

I fell. I fell from somewhere. From where?

[Why did you fall?]

He didn't know from where. Nor why.

But he knew that was the truth. He had fallen there.

The wind began to blow harder, brushing against his body as he listened to the sound of grass and plants growing among the ruins.

He stood with difficulty, staggering slightly before regaining his balance. His gaze swept across the area.

Ruins.

Structures that seemed to have once been important... long ago.

Grass, plants, and low trees had reclaimed the place. The place must have been abandoned for a very long time.

He recognized them. He didn't know how, but he did.

Fallen columns. Broken statues. Shapes that his mind identified without giving him any context. It was like reading a language he couldn't remember ever learning.

And then he saw it when he turned around.

The pyramid.

He had thought it was a mountain behind him. But no. He didn't know how, but what stood before him was something his mind recognized.

It towered before him, enormous and dark, absorbing what little light the night offered. It reflected almost nothing. It did not shine.

It was simply there, as though it had always been there. He didn't understand why, but it felt familiar.

What was it? Why couldn't he remember?

Who am I, and what is this place?

[Just try to get your bearings first.]

He looked toward the side opposite the pyramid again.

He was in a very elevated place, and from there he could see a landscape barely illuminated by the moon. He could make out plains and a distant forest. Farther on, heavy clouds loomed over the horizon, with lightning striking somewhere in the distance.

The place where he stood could be considered a plaza built upon a plateau, reached by a long staircase of white stone. The staircase was interrupted at several points, forming a series of stepped terraces, almost like landings along the ascent. At the bottom was another, smaller plaza filled with sculptures. But because of the darkness and the distance of several meters, he couldn't make out their shapes, except for a few fallen elephants with long, slender legs and obelisks on their backs.

Marble? he wondered, looking at the staircase.

[Where does it lead?]

Then he looked down at the ground beneath him. Large white slabs formed a pattern he couldn't make sense of, but the area where he had crashed was stone that had been blackened by the impact.

The plaza had once been enclosed by walls, but now everything was in ruins, and plants had reclaimed the place. He could even suspect that his fall had contributed to making the place look even worse.

On the other hand, the pyramid was so enormous that, from a distance and in the darkness, it could easily be mistaken for either of the two mountains standing on either side of it.

The young man walked around the area for a while, but all he could hear was the sound of the wind.

He knew it was cold. But he didn't feel cold. He knew he should. But he didn't.

He couldn't know why he was there, but something moved through the cracks of his mind like a crouching ghost.

Am I dead?

The thought ran through every fiber of his being. He couldn't know for certain, but somewhere in his mind, he knew he was supposed to be somewhere else.

Another place. Another time. And this place was completely alien to wherever he had been before.

Almost as if he had vanished from somewhere and now found himself inside some kind of dream that felt far too real.

The sounds. The sensations. The whisper of the plants. All of it contributed to a feeling he couldn't explain.

He had been somewhere else. He was sure of it.

But his mind couldn't summon any images of that place. Only the weird sensation that he had left somewhere important behind, only to appear here.

Am I dead? Or am I really dreaming? he thought again.

But it felt real.

It's cold. I know it's cold.

[But you're not cold.]

What was that other voice?

He was confused. Every time he thought something, it was as if he thought it twice. One voice would ask or answer something, only for another to question it or form another idea.

It was a little annoying.

Then a sound broke the silence.

Footsteps. Irregular. Light, but hurried. Bare feet were approaching in haste.

He looked toward the source of the sound. It was coming from the long staircase below the part of the plaza where he stood.

He turned his head just as a figure burst into the plaza from the stairs, leaping between the ruins.

It was a young woman.

Breathless, frightened, and utterly terrified.

Long black hair clung to her body. She was naked like him but she was trembling. Her feet struck the stone without rhythm, as if every step of her were a desperate decision.

She stopped dead when she saw him.

Her green eyes widened, reflecting what little light was available, filled with fear.

For an instant, neither of them moved.

The young man took an instinctive step forward.

"Wait..."

His voice came out rough, unused. It sounded strange even to him.

The young woman immediately stepped back, her body tensing like that of a cornered animal.

She didn't understand. She didn't trust him. She couldn't.

He slowly raised his hands, trying to show that he wasn't a threat. But even he wasn't sure of that.

"Wait! I'm not going to hurt you."

The words felt right. But empty. Because he didn't know whether they were true.

Who was he?

Who was she?

The man looked at her more carefully. Even in the darkness, he could clearly see her green eyes, caught somewhere between surprise and terror.

His heart tightened in his chest.

He didn't know who she was. But something inside him was screaming that he knew her.

Who are you?

[Who are you?]

The two voices in his head repeated the same words in unison.

He knew her.

From where? How? When?

The girl continued backing away but circling him, putting distance between the stairs and him, her eyes darting around as she searched for an escape that didn't exist amid the chaos. Her breathing was ragged.

She was on the verge of breaking.

Then she pointed toward the staircase.

And that was when something else entered the plaza.

The man hadn't heard any footsteps this time.

He simply knew. Whatever she had been running from was now there, behind him.

He turned around.

A low, viscous sound filled the air, like something dragging itself along without ever truly touching the ground.

From the shadows between the ruins, the shapes emerged.

They were tall and deformed. Unstable in every sense of the word.

Shadows was perhaps the best way to describe them, because they seemed liquid, solid, and gaseous, without ever being any of those things at the same time. Their bodies seemed unable to decide what shape they should take, stretching and contracting as if they were made of something that did not belong to the world.

They had no discernible faces.

Only hollows where something seemed to be watching from a time impossible to pinpoint.

The girl took a step backward, stumbled, and fell to the ground.

The man didn't think twice. His body moved before any thought could form, placing itself between her and the creatures.

Protect her!

[Protect her!]

That was the only thing that appeared in his mind.

He didn't know why. But he knew it was right. He had to do it. He needed to do it.

Why? Who was she?

How could he know that he had to protect her when he couldn't even say what kind of person he was?

[Don't think. Just do it.]

That was all his soul told him. All it tried to convey to every fiber of his body.

Just do it.

And then it happened.

First, he felt an itch deep inside his body. Starting from the lower part of his back, something seemed to break free, spreading through his entire body.

It wasn't flesh, nor bone. It was black particles. They had sprouted through his skin and hung around him like dark smoke.

At first, they looked like black soot floating in the air. They swirled around him, covering his skin in layers that hadn't existed a second before, enveloping him as though they were a second skin.

It was as if he were wearing… Some kind of suit molded tightly to his body?

No. It wasn't that. He didn't know how, but he knew.

It was an armor.

The black armor, fitted tightly against his body, formed without a sound, yet moved with perfect freedom and fluidity along with his movements.

He wasn't surprised. He didn't know why, but he felt that it was part of him.

[The form. The form. The form is the most important thing. Yours. This is yours. You know how to use it. You can use it.]

I can use it?

There was no time to process everything that was exploding inside his mind. Concepts and ideas appeared in his head, while at the same time another voice questioned them and spoke alongside him, telling him to remain calm.

He was aware of it. His mind was questioning or commanding its own thoughts.

[Move. Fight. Protect. Just like you've always done.]

That was what emerged from his very being, even without knowing who he was.

And the creatures advanced.

So did he.

His first movement was clean and direct, like a spear cutting through the air.

His hand swept diagonally and pierced through one of the forms as if it offered no resistance. But the moment he made contact, he passed straight through it from one side to the other, and the creature collapsed. Its body dissolved into fragments that were neither matter nor light, scattering through the air like ash.

Almost as if they were made from the same substance that now covered him.

But it felt different. It was different.

Another attacked from a different angle, launching itself at him like a blade thrown by an invisible hand.

He spun and kicked. Too fast. Even he was surprised by the flip that sent him through the air before he landed gracefully, almost like a cat.

His body knew exactly what to do.

Strike. Deflect. Advance. It felt good to move his body that way.

Every action flowed without hesitation or pause. As if he had done it thousands of times before. His body felt light, moving with an instinctive naturalness rather than conscious thought.

Was he used to fighting?

The creatures did not scream when he pierced them or stopped their attacks. But the air vibrated whenever they were destroyed.

One after another.

Two. Three. Four. Five.

One final creature appeared alone behind him, but the man turned and leapt forward, pushing off with one leg. His hand pierced straight through it.

The shape vibrated. Then it dissolved like the others.

Until none remained.

Silence returned, broken only by the sound of the wind, the grass, and the branches, while the clouds continued drifting lightly across the sky and the moon illuminated the place.

The man breathed heavily.

How had he done that? Had he really taken them all down?

The girl was still on the ground, staring at him with wide, terrified eyes.

The armor slowly disintegrated, the black particles separating from his body and vanishing into the air as if they had never been there, leaving him naked once again.

Deep inside his bones, he felt a strange sensation return, as if whatever had detached itself from him had found its way back into place.

He didn't know why, but he could feel those dark particles inside him again. They hadn't dissolved into the night air. They had simply returned to where they belonged.

The cold returned all at once, but his body still felt warm.

His breathing had already steadied. Too steady for someone who had just fought for his life.

And then something clicked in his brain.

It wasn't a complete memory. Not even a story. Just one word appeared in his mind.

A name.

He stood still, staring at the landscape before him and the distant storm.

"...Shin. My name is Shin."

He said it quietly, in Russian, but he knew it belonged to him.

He didn't know why. But for the first time since opening his eyes, he had something concrete.

Then he thought about it.

Russian.

Russian?

He didn't know why, but when he thought of the words, he knew it was a language.

Ever since he had awakened, words and concepts had been appearing one after another, but his confusion hadn't allowed him to think about them properly.

Mouse. Elephant. Crater. Statues. Pyramid. Nudity. Armor. Protect. Language. Fight.

The things he had been thinking about linked together inside his brain. He knew what they were, but he couldn't understand how he knew.

Russian was a language.

Where did it come from? Why did he know that? Where was he from? Was Russian spoken there?

Why was he naked?

Or was that normal, and he simply couldn't remember?

Shin didn't move immediately after quietly saying his name despite all the process just took few seconds after the battle. The word Shin still floated through his mind, as though it hadn't quite settled into place.

Behind him, the girl remained on the ground, curled in on herself, trembling despite her efforts to hide it. Her arms awkwardly tried to cover her body, more out of instinct than effectiveness.

The wind wasn't helping.

Shin looked at her and took a step toward her, this time more slowly, measuring the distance so he wouldn't frighten her again.

His gaze wasn't aggressive, but neither was it completely calm.

"Hey… are you ok? Can you stand-up?" he tried, his voice low and more controlled than before. "Can you... understand me?"

There was no answer.

Only the sound of the wind passing through the ruins.

He frowned slightly, as if something inside him were searching for an alternative.

And it found one.

This girl had features that told his mind something.

He didn't know why, but alongside the feeling that he had to protect her, something else was connecting inside his brain.

He searched through his mind, though he was doing it more by instinct than anything else.

He knew. She spoke another language.

[Japanese?]

What was Japanese?

Where did they come from?

[Earth.]

Almost the moment he thought it, several of the words and concepts that had appeared earlier transformed into other words, different from those belonging to what he called Russian.

Tones and pronunciations shifted, transforming into symbols.

It was another language.

And he could use it.

When he thought about that language called Japanese, more words appeared in his mind, accompanied by a sharp pain on the right side of his head.

But they kept coming.

English.

Some of the words from that language called Japanese belonged to another language.

The ideograms came from a different source. Two more languages popped into his head: something called Chinese with its different variations, Mandarin, Cantonese and Pinyin.

From where this is coming from?

Another one.

What is English?

[English.]

[Anglo-Saxon.]

The words in his mind continued jumping around without rhyme or reason.

Something called French. Italian. Spanish. Portuguese. German. Latin. HIndi.

He tried another language. Then another. He didn't want to, but his brain wouldn't stop anymore.

Where did all those words with the same meanings, but different pronunciations and writing, come from?

Make it stop, he begged. Make it stop once and for all.

He tried to focus.

The words came without effort, shifting from one language to another with a naturalness he didn't question at the time. It was as if his mouth remembered things that his mind still couldn't reach.

His eyes shifted slightly, and finally he spoke.

"...Japanese...?"

Shin held out a hand towards her.

The girl finally looked at him in the eyes.

For several seconds, she stared at him intently.

He didn't know why, but he felt as though she herself was having the same thoughts he had experienced only moments ago.

Her voice was weak, barely more than a broken whisper.

Shin stopped for a moment.

He didn't know how, but that word, she understood it.

"Yes..." she replied, almost hesitantly. "...Japanese is fine."

She took his hand and stood up, trying to cover herself. ‘What tiny hands,’ thought Shin.

The tension didn't disappear, but it changed a bit. It was no longer pure fear. There was something else.

The smallest connection that hadn't existed before.

The girl silently watched him, assessing him as if trying to decide whether to trust him or run again. Her body was still trembling, her shoulders tense, but she no longer backed away.

She looked at him, confused.

Shin asked. "Do you know me?"

She shook her head.

"Where are we?"

"I don't know. I don't remember... anything," she finally said with difficulty. "I don't know who I am."

Shin lowered his gaze slightly, as if those words resonated far too closely. "Me neither. You don't remember anything? Your name?"

She shook her head. "What is this place? Are we dead?"

The feeling that had made him shudder wasn't unique to him. She had it too.

"I don't know. Why do you think we're dead?"

"I-I don't know... I just feel like I was somewhere else and suddenly appeared here. I woke up choking in water."

"Where did you wake up?"

The girl simply pointed toward a distant spot and shuddered.

The wind blew again, colder and more insistent.

They both felt it.

Shin looked around for a moment, assessing the surroundings. The open ruins offered no real shelter, and staying there was not an option. The girl was trembling, trying to cover her nakedness.

If they were dead, this felt far too real.

If they were alive, then that was another matter. They needed to figure out where they were and protect themselves.

Shin looked toward the pyramid again.

It had an enormous double door, but it seemed to him that one of the doors was slightly open.

If the place was abandoned, at the very least, it could protect them from the cold.

She’s cold, he thought. Heat. Fire. We need fire.

"We need to move," he said, more firmly this time, pointing toward the door. "That place... at least it'll block the wind."

The girl looked over and hesitated, but finally nodded slightly. With some hesitation, she followed him while trying to cover her breasts.

She stepped with difficulty, her legs still unsteady, and moved close enough that she wouldn't lose sight of him, but kept some distance between them.

It wasn't trust, just was a necessity of the moment. And so they walked together.

“Those things… what are they?” Shin asked .

“I don’t know. They came following me from down there. They came out from behind some trees.”

“Are you hurt?”

“No. They never caught up with me… did… did they hurt you?”

“No… I’m fine.”

Shin glanced at her from the corner of his eye.

Was the girl really that short? Or was he too tall?

She glanced at him in the same way, but both of them tried to focus on their surroundings.

The plaza seemed even larger while moving through it. As they advanced, the scale of the place became more absurd.

Rows of decorative columns rose on both sides of the pyramid, extending far beyond the plaza and stretching as far as the eye could see, as if marking the passage of one or several civilizations that no longer existed there.

There weren't just a few. There were hundreds, perhaps thousands.

The place must have stretched for more than a kilometer. And in height, it wasn't much different.

Shin raised his gaze.

At the tip of the pyramid, he could see what appeared to be some kind of sculpture, but the angle from where they stood made it impossible to see clearly.

As they continued forward, Shin suddenly stopped. Something on the ground near them had caught his attention.

Among fragments of stone and the remains of structures, there was a huge stone elephant statue that was relatively intact, except that it had been split open and its stomach was hollow.

But it wasn't empty.

There was something inside.

Several sculptures stood within it, though they had also been destroyed.

A man and a woman made of bronze, dressed in richly sculpted clothing.

He leaned down slightly, brushing away dust with his hand.

The sculptures of the woman and the man had their heads separated from their bodies.

Some kind of metal, Shin thought.

At that thought, an entire chain of words discharged into his mind, each associated with different types of metals and rocks.

There was an inscription on the base of the woman's sculpture.

Latin.

He didn't know how he knew.He simply did. He read it aloud: "Take the head and leave the body."

What is that supposed to mean?

"What is that?"

Shin flinched when the girl appeared behind him.

She looked at him, clearly not understanding. "What does it say?" she asked.

Shin hesitated for a second. "I don't know..." he replied, although that wasn't entirely true. "Tell me... can you read this?"

He showed her the section with the words.

The girl looked at the inscription under the moonlight and then her eyes widened. "Take the head, reject the body?"

"Tell me. The language you're thinking in now... did you already know it, or did it just appear?"

"...It just appeared," the girl said after thinking about it for a moment.

Shin nodded.

If what she said was true, and he had no reason to assume she was lying, then the two of them really were in the same situation.

They continued forward, and a few meters later, another inscription appeared, this time carved into a fallen column.

This one was more chaotic. Different styles and strokes.

Languages were combined into a single sentence, surrounded by other letters strung together without any apparent meaning, along with hieroglyphs.

Greek. Arabic. Italian. Latin.

Arabic. Greek. Another one for the collection, Shin thought as more symbols appeared in his mind.

As if several voices had tried to leave the same message. Shin narrowed his eyes, concentrating, and translated it.

"Do not harm... the mice."

"Mice...?" she repeated, confused.

At that moment, as if the word had been a signal, something small moved among the rubble several meters away.

A white mouse.

Was it the same one he had seen before?

It crossed in front of them quickly, showing no fear. It ran several meters before slipping through the opening of the double door and disappearing into the darkness of the pyramid.

They both followed it with their eyes but neither said anything.

The pyramid's double door was indeed partially open. And the damn thing was gigantic, too.

Now that they were still some distance from the entrance, the doors looked like the entrance to a giant's house.

Giant.

Another associated concept appeared in both their minds almost simultaneously, and they looked at each other.

The door was thirty meters high, perhaps more.

More than twenty meters wide, divided between the two leaves.

The proportions didn't seem designed for people like them.

And they couldn't say with any certainty that the decoration was normal either.

There was something about the shapes, the reliefs, the way the figures were arranged, that didn't quite fit anything their minds could classify easily.

Geometric patterns intertwined with sculpted figures. They weren't sure, but the associations came anyway. Eight-legged creatures. Spiders. Creatures with diamond-shaped skin and somewhat flattened heads. Snakes.

But when they raised their eyes, the carvings seemed more geometric than anything else, until their vision adjusted to the more organic parts.

In the moonlight, they could see fairly well, but there were details that still escaped them.

Flying beings were also depicted, with starfish-like heads and barrel-shaped bodies, soaring through space and the sea.

Strange forms with countless eyes and a single foot.

Tentacled creatures and a kind of dragons being ridden by humanoid beings.

There were conical beings with several appendages fighting against the many-eyed, one-footed creatures.

Mermaids and mermen fought against the barrel-bodied beings and other creatures from the abyssal depths.

Minotaurs and centaurs moved across the surface, while unicorns stood almost as if they had been petrified in the act of trying to escape through the doorway.

Humanoid beings and animals emerged from trees, from the earth, from water, from the sea, and from the air.

The warlike imagery might have seemed like the most abundant theme, but there was beauty represented there as well in contrast with the geometry. In the graceful movements of the mermaids, or in the horses that almost seemed imbued with genuine life. Even the unknown creatures, set among geometric patterns of triangles within triangles, had been depicted with a certain grace and aesthetic quality that was admirable.

In the middle, when the doors closed, they seemed designed to complete a drawing of interconnected spheres, almost like a tree.

Shin initially counted twelve, but there was a thirteenth at the base, roughly at their own height.

Some kind of strange tree that didn't seem to belong there, yet at the same time had to be important, since it had been placed at the center of the doorway's entire sculptural composition.

Shin felt a headache coming on from trying to find some meaning in it.

But that tree of spheres made the back of his neck itch for some reason.

That sensation couldn't belong only to him.

The girl was looking at it too, occasionally turning around to look behind them as if she felt that something was watching them.

The wind swaying the plants and producing a faint whistle seemed to be the only thing moving there besides them.

The enormous spiral columns on either side of the doorway were also covered at intervals with creatures of all kinds.

Imps. Chimeras. Satyrs.

A kind of dog-like creature that seemed to emerge from strange angles. Air spirits and winged humanoids. There were letters and hieroglyphs interwoven throughout, which he couldn't interpret.

Series of latin letters strung together in some kind of Nomina Barbara that concealed their meaning from the eyes of the uninitiated.

Everything coexisted in a strange, unsettling harmony, and the opening almost seemed to invite them inside.

Even so, if the place was empty it would be enough.

Shin looked away, thinking.

Then he turned his head toward the right, toward one side of the structure about seventy meters away, where part of a long exterior wall had collapsed. There were trees. Not many, but enough.

"Wait here," he said, pointing toward the area. "Don't go anywhere. We need something before enter."

She didn't answer, but she didn't move either.

On his way, he came across a winged sculpture with a starfish-like head and a barrel-shaped body, the same kind depicted on the door.

He walked around it, trying to associate it with something, but the hieroglyphs and words carved into it were impossible to translate.

There was something about that shape that unsettled him. He didn't know why, but he felt as though he knew it from somewhere.

The girl watched him curiously, and Shin decided it was better not to keep her waiting.

He headed toward the trees.

They were pine trees with low-hanging branches. The ground changed there, becoming softer, covered with earth and vegetation.

He crouched down and snapped off a few branches with straightforward force, testing the resistance of the wood. Then he gathered some fallen pinecones, quickly inspecting them before tucking them under his arm. He ran his hand along the trunk of one of the trees.

Resin. A little sticky, but hardened.

He collected it without hesitation.

There was a tree with thin leaves that caught his attention. Looking at it more closely, he realized it was an olive tree.

He plucked a couple of olives and put them in his mouth. The taste told him they were ripe. He spat out the pits and looked down toward the slope once more before heading back.

Then he saw it.

On the sunken side, there was a statue of a gigantic colossus, shattered into several pieces.

Its hands and arms lay nearby, but the rest had fallen deeper into a ravine several dozen meters below, now illuminated by the moonlight.

It was a giant. But what caught his attention was that, under the moonlight, the inside of the statue seemed to contain organs.

Was it real? Why would a statue have organs?

Thinking about the elephant… Why would the elephant have statues inside it too?

He thought he could see shattered lungs and a digestive system.

The heart seemed to lie at the very bottom, beside the statue's head, which appeared to be wearing some kind of hat. Maybe it was his imagination, but the heart seemed to glow. As if it were made of some other material.

Whatever the case, he couldn't climb down from there, and he had no intention of doing so at that moment.

Perhaps when daylight came, if he and the girl were safe.

Back in the plaza, his eyes settled on the elephant statue. He approached it and, after a brief inspection, took the bronze head of the man.

It was hollow. That would work.

He put the pinecones and resin inside, then tucked the branches under his arm.

The wind was still blowing hard.

Starting a fire outside would be useless.

Shin lowered his gaze toward his free hand and remembered.

Not as a clear image. More as a sensation.

The fight.

[The particles.]

Without thinking too much about it, he tried.

The black particles appeared again, emerging from his skin as if they had always been there.

They didn't form the armor this time. They simply floated around his hand and Shin watched them in silence.

He didn't know how he knew, but he did. He curled his fingers slightly and snapped them. The black particles reacted, colliding with one another and producing blue sparks.

Small flashes appeared as they struck each other, brief and unstable.

It wasn't fire. But it was enough to light one.

He returned to where the girl was waiting. "Do you want to come?"

She looked at him for a few seconds, then looked toward the entrance. She thought about it for a moment before looking back at him and nodding.

And they entered the pyramid.

The opening was nearly two meters wide, but no light reached inside. Everything in that section was completely dark.

Using the pinecones to make torches really would be a good idea.

The interior cut off the wind almost completely. The silence was different in there, much denser, as if the air itself were heavier and the sound bounced endlessly off the walls.

Shin searched for some dry grass outside and carried it inside. Then he gathered a few pieces of wood and the dry grass near the entrance. Using the sparks against the stone and wood, he began trying to light them.

It wasn't immediate, but with patience, the wood finally caught.

The girl sighed and moved closer. Her embarrassment had given way to relief now that she could warm herself.

"Warm yourself up. I want to do something. Just in case."

Ever since Shin had seen the pyramid's door, he had thought that the place would make a good shelter from the cold. The problem was what he had just confirmed.

The place was completely dark.

The fire illuminated only about six meters around them, and nothing beyond that.

They were in a series of corridors that branched off in different directions, and nothing could be seen beyond them.

Using some pieces of the stone sculptures, he arranged them around the fire to keep the wind from putting it out. He placed a few more stones in a smaller circle, leaving enough space for the flames to breathe without being smothered.

From a shattered sculpture of a monstrous nymph near the entrance, he took a thin seashell and placed it over the stones. Then he put the hollow head on top of the shell and added the resin inside, letting the heat liquefy it until it slowly began to boil.

The girl, now a little warmer, watched him move around while Shin occasionally glanced at her from the corner of his eye.

"You still don't remember anything?"

She shook her head.

The strange smell of the resin filled the space.

What a stench.

[ I know what this is. ]

He recognized the smell, which meant that the idea he had come up with—and the knowledge behind it—must have come from some previous experience where he had done something similar.

He took one of the pinecones and used it as a base, soaking it in the resin before inserting it into the branches. He had split the branches open at their thickest points, though he wasn't sure whether that had been a good idea.

He looked outside, walked over to the elephant, and once again used the particles to cut the bronze man's hands off at the wrists. He brought them back inside.

He used the hollow parts to insert the branches and, with a little force, bent the fingers so he could wedge the pinecones between them.

It worked perfectly.

The hands could hold the pinecones, and he would be able to remove them once they burned out.

He couldn't say what time it was, but if the night had only just begun, they would need light in case something threatened them.

Seeing how much resin was still available, he didn't stop. He placed several pinecones inside the statue's hollow head, coating them thoroughly.

Seven pinecones in total, and two torches. One for each of them. Enough for now.

They only needed to light one.

With nothing else to do, he fed the fire.

The light began to stabilize, casting long shadows across the inner walls.

He was thirsty. He had only just realized it.

He explored a little farther near the entrance until he found a small pool of water between cracks in the floor. It was clear, but he didn't drink it.

"Are you thirsty?"

The girl looked at him and nodded slightly. "A little."

"Wait here."

The girl followed him with her eyes as he went outside and retrieved the other head from inside the elephant sculpture—the woman's—and brought it to the fire to examine it.

He didn't know what he was looking for, but he knew he had to do it.

The color was the same. That told him something, but he had no idea what. Without remembering what it was supposed to mean, he gave up and filled it with water anyway. He placed it over the improvised embers. The water slowly began to heat, and the woman watched him, discreetly licking her lips.

She must have been thirsty too. Her body already seemed dry, except for her long hair.

Why was it so long?

It must be a nuisance to maintain, Shin thought, but he focused his attention on the head and the water so it wouldn't tip over and spill.

When the water boiled, he left the head away from the fire for a few moments to let it cool.

For half an hour, he tried to talk to her, but her modesty had returned somewhat. He didn't know what to say to her. She only tried to cover herself, and he couldn't blame her.

It was difficult not to look at her.

He thought she was pretty, but it was better not to make her uncomfortable. He felt there was something familiar about her, yet at the same time, they seemed like strangers in that moment.

Had they lost their memories, but known each other before?

Shin simply didn't know.

After half an hour, once it had cooled enough, he put the head back over the fire and drank some of the water.

It tasted like the best thing he had ever drunk in his life. He only drank a little before handing it to the girl, who also drank eagerly. Once it was empty, he filled it with water again and boiled it.

It is better to have something to do, he thought.

He had recovered his name after getting himself moving, and perhaps doing the same thing would bring back more memories.

What were those shadows?

The fire crackled beneath the stone head, and for a moment, it seemed as though the world had been reduced to that small circle of light.

But that didn't last.

From outside, beyond the open entrance, something broke the silence.

A distant scream carried on the wind. Then another. It wasn't recognizable at all. But it wasn't completely animal, either.

It was a ragged sound, as though something were trying to imitate a voice without understanding how.

It dragged across the plaza, bounced off the columns, and reached them with an unsettling clarity.

It wasn't far away.

The girl tensed her fingers clutching her own arms.

"They're still there..." she murmured, her voice breaking. "They found us..."

Shin turned his head toward the entrance, his expression hardening.

The fire illuminated his profile, but his eyes remained fixed on the darkness outside.

She was now staring at him, as though she had just noticed something about his face and body.

"What is it?" Shin asked.

She didn't answer immediately.

Shin looked down at his own body.

He hadn't noticed.

He was covered in scars. He looked at his hands. His arms. Under the firelight, he could see them clearly.

As he stared at his hands, he realized something else. There was something abnormal.

He brought his hands to his face.

How had he not noticed?

He could only see through his left eye.

His right eye was there, but he couldn't see with it.

Great.

He was covered in scars, and he could only see with one eye. He couldn't imagine it was a very pleasant sight.

No wonder the girl was so frightened.

"We should... go farther inside," she said. She looked away, embarrassed and hesitant, as though she feared the answer as much as she feared the idea itself. "Deeper... into the pyramid."

Shin didn't like the idea. Obviously, neither did she. But he nodded.

It made sense. Outside, they already knew what was there. Inside, there was uncertainty, but at least everything was silent.

They hadn't heard anything since entering. Not even the mouse that had come in before them. There was no strange smell, either.

It just seemed abandoned.

He picked up the torch, making sure the flame remained steady. Then he looked at the girl, making sure she could move.

She nodded, although her body was still trembling.

They placed the unused torch inside the hollow head of the bronze man. She carried it while he carried one torch and the woman's head filled with water in the other hand.

As a precaution, they wouldn't leave anything behind in case they had to go farther inside, especially if those things decided to enter.

They put out the fire so it wouldn't attract attention, though they couldn't be sure whether those creatures could see in the darkness and discover the remains of the fire anyway.

The interior of the pyramid greeted them with a silence deeper than that of the night outside.

The torchlight barely revealed fragments of walls, incomplete reliefs, strange curving hieroglyphs, geometric shapes made of repetitive patterns, and surfaces that seemed to absorb the light rather than reflect it.

Every step echoed as if the place were listening.

They stopped in front of an opening with three paths, corridors that descended at a slight incline into the darkness.

There were no doors, no clear signs, only a clean break in the structure, as though it had always been there.

Shin raised the torch, trying to see farther.

He couldn't see anything.

The corridor seemed to swallow the light, and they both hesitated.

But neither of them turned back.

Then, without warning, the girl brought a hand to her head and dropped the bronze head she had been carrying.

Her body suddenly tensed.

"Ah..."

A low sound escaped her throat. And then she collapsed.

Shin reacted instantly.

He dropped the head containing the water and released the torch to the side without thinking, catching her before she could hit the floor hard.

He held her with both arms, pulling her close.

"Hey! What's wrong?"

There was no clear response.

Her body trembled but not from the cold this time. It was different and much more violent.

She was convulsing.

Her fingers clutched at her chest as if she were trying to contain something inside herself. In the flickering torchlight, Shin could see her digging her nails into herself as though she were in pain, while her other hand trembled.

"It hurts..." she whispered, squeezing her eyes and teeth shut. "My head..."

Shin held her more firmly, not really knowing what to do, but refusing to let go.

He watched her for a moment. Even like this… She was beautiful. And he couldn't shake the feeling that he knew her.

Not in some distant or unreachable way.

There was something closer. Something more... human?

Fragile. Real.

His eyes moved toward her ears, noticing something. They were slightly pointed like his.

No.

She wasn't entirely human.

What was human?

The girl's eyes suddenly opened.

She stared directly at him for several seconds and something changed.

"I..." Her voice trembled. "I remember..."

Shin said nothing and waited.

"My name…" A brief silence. "Rei... Mai... Suzu..."

"Reimaizusu?"

What kind of name is that?

"No... Mai. Just Mai. Rei and Suzu... I know those were also my names, but people called me Mai. At least... that's what I remember."

"Anything else?"

Tears began falling before she had even finished processing it. "It's... the only thing..." Her voice broke. "I don't remember anything else... there are no faces. Just words."

And then she cried. Not loudly. No screaming. She simply let bitter tears run down her cheeks. Shin didn't try to stop her with words. He simply pulled her closer and gently embraced her.

Their naked bodies touched without shame.

The shared warmth was real, immediate, and in a situation where he didn't know what to say to her, that gesture was the only thing he could think of to comfort her.

He was surprised when he felt her sink against his chest, crying. She embraced him too.

Woah...

She was surprisingly soft.

The girl named Mai had soft muscles despite her fragile appearance. Her hands were soft as well, though the tips of her fingers were slightly harder.

She didn't seem like a girl accustomed to heavy labor, but the calluses on her fingertips told him she must have engaged in some kind of activity. Her feet and legs revealed an athletic build. She showed no signs of malnutrition or anything similar. She was slender, yes, but her hips were wide and her ankles quite strong. From what he could tell, she must have engaged in considerable physical activity. Some sport maybe?

"It's all right..." he murmured, with a calmness he didn't know where he had found. "That's enough."

It wasn't a solution. But at least it was something. They remained like that for a few more seconds before Shin pulled away just enough to look at her.

“Are you ok, now?”

Her face was covered in tears.

"We have to move."

She nodded, wiping her tears with the back of her hand. "Y-Yes. I am sorry. Let's go."

"Can you stand?"

"Yes... thank you."

Carefully, he helped her to her feet. Her legs were still weak, but this time she didn't fall.

Shin picked up the torch from the floor, checking that it was still burning correctly.

The water in the stone head was only half full now, but they didn't want to go back outside to refill it.

"Shin," he said.

"What?"

"My name is Shin."

Mai looked at him. "You remembered your name?"

"After fighting those things that were following you."

"Y-You didn't tell me."

"You didn't ask. And I didn't want to scare you. It was better to tell you once you could remember something. That way, we'd be on equal terms."

Mai continued looking at him.

"I'm not hiding anything. I only know that my name is Shin, and that I know several languages I don't even know where I learned."

Mai lowered her gaze slightly. Then she spoke. "I'm in the same situation… I know I know things, but no from where the came from."

Another scream came from the direction of the entrance.

It was better not to make any more noise that might attract whatever was out there.

The sound of the bronze head hitting the ground must have carried all the way outside, and it was better not to say anything else until they had gone farther inside. Without another word, Mai picked up the hollow head holding the pinecone torch.

They advanced deeper into the passage.

The air grew heavier as they walked.

The walls seemed to slowly close in around them, even though he knew that wasn't actually happening.

It was only a feeling.

They had to be forty or fifty meters from the entrance by now. Maybe that was enough.

Yes. They could stay there and wait. Nothing more.

Then a dull, deep sound reached their ears.

Like stone moving.

They both stopped at the same time and turned around.

The torchlight reached just far enough to show it.

The entrance.

The enormous opening they had come through it was no longer open.

A wall covered in carvings occupied its place. As if there had never been a door at all.

Mai took a step back. "No..." she whispered.

Shin moved quickly toward the place where he remembered the exit being.

He ran his hand over the cold, solid surface. He struck it once. He searched for some mechanism, some irregularity, anything that might indicate it could be opened.

Nothing.

There was no sign that the wall had ever contained a door.

Silence answered him.

Slowly, he lowered his hand and looked at Mai. And for the first time since he had awakened, the situation settled clearly in his mind.

They were safe from the dangers outside.

But they were trapped inside the pyramid.
