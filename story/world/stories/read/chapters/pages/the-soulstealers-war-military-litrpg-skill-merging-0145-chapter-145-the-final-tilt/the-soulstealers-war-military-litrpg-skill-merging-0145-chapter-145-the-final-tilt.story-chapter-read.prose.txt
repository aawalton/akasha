Milsige navigated through the sheltered booths until he reached Hinlaw and Hinfic, then stopped, kneeling down beside the two, arms crossed. They looked out onto the tournament grounds, watching as Lord Ceonwin trotted back and forth, giving some banal speech that no one seemed all that interested in. They were here to see lances shatter, after all.

Once Milsige was close enough, he whispered, “We have four tipped lances ready. The siphons are hidden inside the tip, and if Sir Hinfic pushes a burst of maegen through the lance, the siphon will activate.”

“No skin contact required?” Hinfic whispered.

“None,” Milsige replied. “It is why we were only about to obtain four with our sparse travelling funds. They are the best we could find.” Sparse was relative. It still took nearly a hundred and fifty gold sceats to acquire the siphons.

“Save them for Lord Ceonwin,” Hinlaw said. “My son can defeat this Ricdine ruffian without the lances’ help.”

“Father?” Hinfic whispered.

“You can. No doubt, son. You are a lion. Earn your place. He is only a Titanium.”

“And what if I need the extra help?”

“I will be disappointed. Deeply disappointed. But if it is critical…yes, I would consider it.”

~ ~ ~

As if designed to make my anticipation worse, my joust against Hinfic wasn’t the first joust of the day. Lord Ceonwin rode against another thegn who’d made it to the top four contestants.

I didn’t know the man, but I watched from the tournament stables, holding Burr’s lead and holding my breath at the same time.

At the moment, I was alone in the stable. Hinfic waited somewhere else, but waiting behind the gates was probably below him. But it let me watch the joust in peace, rather than having him taunt me or try to see through my disguise.

Lord Ceonwin’s joust went about as normally as it possibly could. I was expecting something. Maybe they’d try to interrupt the event with orcs or cheat.

Lord Ceonwin broke three lances, one on his opponent’s helmet. His opponent landed three shots, except only on Ceonwin’s armour, and though the lance broke every time, it wasn’t enough to score him the points he needed.

No interruptions. No obvious cheating.

After the match, the Warlord’s messenger declared Ceonwin the winner, and that he would advance to the final round. But first, Sir Ricdine had to ride against Sir Hinfic.

As I climbed up onto Burr’s back, a pair of servants pushed open the stable gate. I pulled on my helmet, then rode out onto the lists.

The crowd roared. I couldn’t tell if it was for me or for Hinfic, but I rode a circle around the tournament grounds anyway, passing by the crowd. When I rode past the outer fence, where a crowd of civilians gathered, they cheered a little louder. A young boy on his father’s shoulders raised his fist to the sky.

I swallowed, then pulled the mouth protector of chainmail up, hiding my expression. When I circled past the sheltered booths, there were a few reserved cheers, but nothing audible. Finally, I passed Hinfic in the opposite direction. He stopped and said, “So, Ricdine? Still a Titanium?”

“Something like that, sir,” I replied. He didn’t have any way of checking.

“It doesn’t matter,” he said. “You won’t win.”

“Why are you so sure about that, hm?”

“Just get to your starting position, Ricdine.”

I snapped Burr’s reins and trotted across the churned dirt, then hoisted up a lance from the rack. I gave it a quick toss, adjusting for the weight. Although I’d practiced jousting early in the morning, in my ‘squire’ training with Lord Ceonwin, I hadn’t had nearly as much time to get used to jousting with my newly-enhanced body as I would’ve hoped.

But I’d make do.

I tightened my grip, keeping the messenger centered in the corner of my vision, watching the starting flag. The crowd fell silent, save for a few murmurs from the audience.

The starting flag fell. I charged forward, and so did Hinfic. His lance lowered, aiming right for my chest. He wasn’t taking any chances.

For the opening round, to keep it easy, I aimed for his chest as well. We both tucked our shields in, mirroring each other, and both our lances bobbed slightly as our horses approached the middle.

With a thud, his lance struck my chest. The scale mail clinked, the chainmail threatened to cave inward, but my body resisted the impact. My newly enhanced sternum spread the impact out across my chest, and my skin was iron. My muscles quelled the vibrations.

I swayed, but not as much as before. Wooden shards pelted against my helmet and armour, and the same went for Hinfic.

My lance broke only a millisecond later, shattering on his shoulder and exploding at its tip. We both rode past each other, holding tight to our horses.

I tossed aside the stub of my lance, then trotted in a circle. The crowd was cheering again, fists raised to the sky, and I glowed as I rode back to the starting position. But in a matter of seconds, my stomach fell. Lord Ceonwin was staring at me from the audience stands, still in his armour.

Was something wrong outside? Or was he simply skeptical?

Or was he worried?

If I wanted to succeed in my challenge, I’d need to unhorse Hinfic. It wasn’t enough to just win by accumulating more points than him. I had to win by such a massive margin that I was obviously better suited to rule his lands than he was.

I picked up a new lance. Technically, if you wanted to unhorse a foe, it was best to go for headshots. The higher you hit, the less they could absorb the blow. But you also needed to get a direct hit, otherwise the head would just snap back and your lance wouldn’t break—let alone knock your opponent off his horse.

But I had to try. If I didn’t succeed at unhorsing him, then I was done for. I wouldn’t get any of the land I needed, and all of this would be for nothing.

Maybe that was for the best. Ricdine would ride off empty-handed, never to be seen again, and I could return to being a soldier. Maybe eventually, I’d find enough gold to actually buy some land.

And by then, it would be too late.

No, I did this right here, right now. I wasn’t going to abandon this project, not after so many late nights and so much pain to enhance my body.

I readied my lance, and when the second flag dropped, I charged. This time, I kept my lance low at first, as if I was going for a body strike again. Going for the body on the second round after tying the previous round might have been considered rude: you expected your opponent to fail once or twice, so you were playing it safe.

But Hinfic aimed the same way. I was tempted to trigger a gravity Art overhead, something to help boost my foresight and ability to piece together what he was going to do, but I didn’t have time, and I couldn’t spare the Presence. I had to concentrate on the strike, and when the time came, I had to attack. I waited until the last minute, holding my breath, then…

With a tilt of my wrist, I raised my lance slightly, bringing the tip up and aiming at Hinfic’s head.

Behind his eye-slit, Hinfic’s eyes widened, and my lance struck his helmet.

It bounced off. My lance didn’t break.

His lance, on the other hand, smashed into my chest, shattering just like the previous. I swayed, nearly falling back, but I held on as tight as I could. Burr whinied, and she nearly reared up.

Patting her neck, I calmed her, then leaned forward, then turned around. The crowd was quieter than before, with only a few scattered cheers. Sir Hinfic wasn’t as popular as Sir Ricdine, huh?

Trying to stay composed, I trotted back to the starting position. That trick would only work once. Now, if I aimed for Hinfic’s body, he’d expect me to raise my lance again at the last minute.

I needed a miracle to pull this off. He was up one point on me. Even if I unhorsed him, unless he withdrew—which he wouldn’t—I needed to make sure he didn’t score too many more points. It wasn’t a guarantee yet.

I had to make this count. Sure, there were two more courses. But if I could unhorse him on the third, I had to. I shut my eyes, then triggered a gravity Art high overhead, far above the jousting grounds. It only cost three points, but it boosted my foresight, and it helped me concentrate.

I could only go off what I knew about Hinfic. But I estimated he would aim for my body again. I could use my shield, and leverage my memory of his preferred target, to deflect the lance with my shield, stopping it from breaking. As long as I aimed directly for his helmet…

The flag fell, and I charged. This time, I didn’t make any pretense of aiming low. I just pointed my lance at his helmet, where I was hoping to hit, and made it as perfect as I could. He would loosen his neck, but I would catch his helmet right between the eyes, and it wouldn’t matter.

I clenched my teeth, having set everything up, put all the pieces into motion. Now, it just had to play out properly.

I closed my eyes at the last moment. Something scraped on my shield. Wood trembled, but his lance didn’t break. Mine jolted, though, and gave a satisfying pop, like cracking a knuckle that hadn’t cracked in days.

Hinfic flew backward, launched off his horse. His feet ripped out of their stirrups, and he fell hard on his back, staring up at the sky above.

I trotted back around after Burr recovered, then leaned over. “You can withdraw,” I tried.

“Never,” he snarled. He stood up brushed his armour off, then glared at me. “We have one more course to ride. Be ready.”
