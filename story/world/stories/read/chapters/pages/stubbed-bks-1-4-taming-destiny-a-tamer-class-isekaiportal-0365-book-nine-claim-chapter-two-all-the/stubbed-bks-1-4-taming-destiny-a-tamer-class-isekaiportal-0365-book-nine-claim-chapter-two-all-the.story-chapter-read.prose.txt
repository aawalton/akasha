In the mess hall, I find more of my companions. Rory is there, though he blushes when he sees I’m watching him speak to – or rather, flirt with – one of the cooks. As it turns out, he was able to convince his love interest to come to the Lost Continent with him. Or perhaps she was interested in the adventure to begin with. Either way, I’m glad for him.

Rory himself has been spending most of his time either helping Master Marin in the leather workshop with very basic tasks to free up Athos for more skilled work, or training to fight – Maxen has made it clear that Rory has a decent hand with a club. I can imagine where that comes from, but I’m not going to ignore the skill if I can turn it to more legal and useful ends. At least he seems to have settled down – whether it’s due to trusting me more, his sweetheart, or that he’s on a ship in the middle of the ocean, I haven’t caught him planning any escape attempts recently.

Catch and Iandee are present too, sitting with Maxen and a few of the fighters. Clearly the morning’s training is done. It must be later than I thought – the fighters tend to train all morning, and over the midday period. That didn’t make sense to me at first – training in full sun while wearing armour seemed like a good way of over-taxing the fighters. But, again, I’d overlooked that we now live in a world where magic is an integral part.

Not only does training for hours at a time increase Endurance, but fighting against one’s own body in order to keep going trains Constitution too – just as fighting with live blades does. Maxen admitted that he might push them a bit less if I hadn’t made it clear that he had access to healing potions as and when needed. But with those made readily available to him, there was no need to hold back. And if the blood staining the clothes of the men I see at the table is any indication, this morning’s session was just as difficult and bloody as any of them.

While the raptorcats go over to demand meat from the cooks serving at the counter that leads through to the galley, I head over to speak to Maxen, using Seal to ensure that his report is kept to our ears alone.

“At this point, are there any you would recommend for receiving fighter Class stones of one sort or another in the near future?” I ask after hearing his routine report of the morning’s activities. Thanks to Tiria, I have a number of Common stones now – a nest egg for those who earn them.

“There are several who have indicated that they have the discipline and dedication necessary,” Maxen confirms. “However, I would suggest that you wait for them to distinguish themselves in battle too.”

“Have they not done so already?” I ask with a raised eyebrow. Almost all of the fighters present were also in Zlona – there were a few additions at the last moment who had volunteered while we were absent, but not many.

“Some, yes, to an extent. However, that is only in one type of battle. I would like to see them continue showing their mettle in at least three before considering giving out any Class stones.”

“Is that how it worked in the military?” I ask curiously. Maxen immediately shakes his head.

“No one would ever receive a Class stone who has not served for at least a year, and that only with exemplary service during a time of war. In times of peace, the minimum time increases to three years, though there is no requirement of having been part of an engagement.”

“I see,” I muse. “Then we’ll wait for now – but not too long,” I warn. “Those we choose will need to have time to get used to their Classes and Skills before we arrive.”

“Of course, sir,” my commander agrees immediately.

“On another note, how is Noir doing?” The not-so-little alcaoris was apparently not happy with how I came to grief while mounted on his back. I tried to point out that he’d kept me safe from the steam dragon – the reason he was there – but he was convinced that if he’d been faster or stronger, he’d have been able to avoid the shockwave that had knocked us both against a tree. Since being squashed between a tree and his weight was a large part of why I was so badly injured, I couldn’t deny his argument.

“Noir is doing well,” Maxen assures me. “He’s indicated that his physical attributes are steadily increasing, and it seems that the training you and the samuran Pathwalkers are doing with him is having results on his mental and soul attributes too.”

I nod, glad to hear it, though the last is no surprise – it seems that me giving him an order for him to fight against until his will gives out is a good way of training his Willpower.

It doesn’t seem to work on everyone – for some it had little effect while others suffered strain to their Core spaces. I’m eager to try to increase my own Willpower by undergoing another Challenge in my soul space – something I just haven’t had enough uninterrupted time for – but I’m aware that it leaves me unreachable for long periods of time. The journey has been relatively quiet so far, but I can’t help feeling a hint of trepidation at just how quiet it’s been considering how dangerous the ocean was said to be. It gives me a distinct feeling that the shoe is about to drop.

After finishing the perfectly good fare cooked by the ship’s chefs, I decide that I could probably do with a bit of time on the training ‘field’ myself. I therefore forgo continuing my reading and instead head up to the decks above, accompanied this time by Catch and Iandee. The raptorcats are going to return to my cabin – since they’re my Bonded, the enchantments will allow them entry; just another bit of security that Nicholas and Sarran both insisted on.

Lathani, meanwhile, is still eating, and then will get going on the exercises she’s been assigned by her teacher – that, I’ve learned, is the way most mages are trained: little outright explanation, but lots of exercises designed to lead the mage to a useful conclusion. Though not always the same one – Tiria ruefully related a story yesterday where her Master had intended her to learn how to create a shield, and instead she’d ended up learning how to create a bubble instead. But since that bubble later became a shield she could cast on objects or people other than herself, it seems that it was useful nonetheless.

Climbing the last of the stairs, I blink briefly in the bright light of day until my eyes adjust. Curled around the mast, I see Noir and Ivor dozing in the sun. From what Maxen was saying earlier, Ivor has also been taking part in the training, even if he is less committed than Noir is. I suppose he doesn’t see such obvious effects of it, reducing his motivation.

There is plenty of movement on the deck, though it’s far emptier right now than it is at shift change, during training sessions, or at dawn and dusk when the fishermen come out to try to supplement our food supplies.

Seeing a figure leaning motionlessly against the rail to the right and looking behind us, I head in his direction.

My eyes go beyond him, brushing past Josiah’s and Valence’s ships, to gaze at the grey-blue water and the horizon at the edge of its expanse. Land finally dipped out of view even from Sirocco’s viewpoint yesterday – even with the restrictions on how high she can safely fly, she can still get up double the height of the crow’s nest. That, at least, is how the word translates to me even though I’m sure there are no actual crows in this world.

It’s been almost two tendays since we set off. Whalehost sits in a large bay-like shape that makes up the whole of the coastline on the east side of Moriax, so the first tenday was spent just sailing across and out of the bay. The Lost Continent is to the North East of Iseria, so we needed to sail around the promontory of Damayar that sits to the far north of Moriax.

Our journey could have been shorter if we had cut between the continent and the archipelago of islands that sit at its furthest North-East corner, but Sor’az was very firmly against that idea. He emphasised that those islands are notorious for harbouring powerful Beast Lords, with the largest ruled over by a Beast King. Sailing too close to them would have invited trouble. And, honestly, considering the journey ahead of us, the less trouble we have at the onset, the better.

So, we took the longer, but safer route, giving those islands a wide berth. Now, it’s just ocean all around. At least magic means none of us have to suffer seasickness – River and her teacher, Sera Kerst, have made sure that everyone who expressed any nausea received a potion that cleared it. But that doesn’t take away from the sheer intimidation that comes from knowing that we are now out of reach of land.

“It’s something, isn’t it?” I ask Loran as I move to lean next to him on the rail of the ship. He glances at me briefly, then returns his gaze out to the rippling waves of the sea.

“It is,” Loran agrees quietly, then falls silent. He’s obviously been practicing his wood carving – the knife the Master carpenter gave him at the beginning of his apprenticeship and his current project are on the box next to him.

I study it for a moment, trying to work out what it is. Maybe Bastet. Or Lathani. Definitely a feline of some sort to judge by the head. Losing interest, I return my gaze out to the sea. I was considering offering to check out his Core space, to see if he’s started developing anything after the Cores he’s been absorbing, but the moment doesn’t feel right.

There’s something about the ocean that takes away the hustle and bustle of life on land – the gentle laps of water against the side of the ship are calming; the way the ocean and sky stretch out to the vast horizon makes me feel small in a way that’s almost reassuring in a strange way. A reminder that however impactful I think my decisions and actions are, in the whole scheme of things, they matter very little.

So, instead of speaking, I listen to the sounds of the ship – it’s never truly silent. Even during the night when the fighters and fishermen have gone to bed, there’s the occasional watchmen calling out the hour and the weather status. And at all times, there’s the sound of the creaking ropes, sails, and boards. The ship is constantly in motion, and like all moving things, it announces its presence.

Not too much, though – I’ve flown away from the ship a few times and I’m surprised at how quickly the sound dies away. One of the enchantments, I have to guess, as Josiah’s ship certainly isn’t nearly as quiet. The additional magic to it was rather last-minute and limited, and I can understand not considering silencing magic as urgent as reinforcement of its planks or ensuring it could keep up with the other ships.

“I didn’t realise it would be so vast,” Loran comments quietly, and my attention returns to him. “That is, I know we talked about it taking months, but I guess it didn’t…sink in.” He makes a nervous-sounding chuckle, glancing to the stern of the ship which was the last place from which land could be seen on deck, a faint smudge on the horizon that finally disappeared entirely. Then his gaze returns to what lies before us. I can almost hear his thoughts – and now there is nothing but water.

“Water, water, everywhere, and all the world did sink,” I murmur quietly. Loran gives me a curious look. “It’s from a poem back where I came from – the Rime of the Ancient Mariner,” I explain quickly. Then I give a wry smile. “Hopefully our luck is rather better than his was, though.”

I should know better than to say such things. Because, of course, a moment later, the lookout calls from the crow’s-nest above.

“Sails to stern! Gaining!”

I curse and exchange a grim look with Loran.
