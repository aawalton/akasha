The grand library inside the citadel was quiet. Rows upon rows of towering shelves stretched beyond sight, laden with books and tomes older than thousands of years. Victor moved methodically through the aisles, his fingers brushing the spines of every single book related to curses, allowing the Shadowlink Mark to record their contents and turn them into e-books. In this way, he could later perform a quick search for any mention of the Soulrot Curse without wasting a single moment.

As he touched the 2,643rd book, Victor finally came across a book mentioning the Soulrot Curse. Rather than reading it through the Shadowlink Mark, he pulled the ancient tome from the shelf and carried it to the nearest desk. Dust scattered as he set it down, and the parchment gave a dry rustle as he opened its pages. Before long, he found the section detailing the exact curse he sought.

[Entry 64: Soulrot Curse]

Origin: Unknown. Classified as a Gimmel-tier or above malediction. Documented only once in recorded history.

Nature: The Soulrot Curse targets the very foundation of being — the soul and core simultaneously. It consumes vitality, mana, and spiritual essence, converting the victim’s strength into sustenance for an unseen source. The curse evolves in response to interference, growing stronger when resisted.

Notes: Believed to have originated from contact with an extra-dimensional entity worshiped by the Doomsday Cult, known in ancient texts as the “Devourer Beyond Stars.”

Victor’s brows furrowed. “An eldritch god…”

Wait, this book is thousands of years old… For it to mention the Doomsday Cult… just how ancient is that organization? He questioned himself in his heart.

Turning the next page, he noticed the ink fading, the text growing distorted and nearly illegible. Only fragments could still be read:

No known counter-curse exists.

Attempts to erase or disrupt the malediction result in exponential growth.

Only a being of equivalent or higher existence than the curse’s source may nullify it.

Alternatively, power drawn from beyond the material plane may succeed in neutralizing it.

Victor sighed slowly, the weight of the words sinking in. “So even the ancients couldn’t cure it…” He closed the tome with a grim expression. But wait, power drawn from beyond the material plane? What’s that supposed to mean?

Suddenly, a word surfaced in his mind. “Dreamforce…”

Could it be? The notion felt far-fetched, almost laughable, yet it fit the description. Dreamforce was one of the few known energies that didn’t belong entirely to the physical realm. Of course, this was merely a hypothesis, an astray thought born from desperation. Even so, Victor wasn’t one to dismiss a possible lead, and thus a hope rose inside his heart in an otherwise dark situation.

“Looks like I’ll be running some very risky experiments soon,” he muttered.

He had vowed never to return to the Dreamscape until he became far stronger, but who would have thought that he might have to visit that perilous realm again soon? Worse still, this was while he was at his weakest, which would make this all the more dangerous.

As he made his way toward the exit, the massive doors of the grand library opened automatically. Waiting on the other side were two familiar figures — the twin overseers of the citadel: Garry and Gabrielle.

“Did you find what you seek, Master Victor?” asked Garry, the male overseer, his voice as composed and formal as ever.

“I did,” Victor replied with a weary sigh. “But it’s not looking good.”

Gabrielle tilted her head. “Not good? How ominous. Mind telling us what has befallen you, Master?”

“Well, I’m cursed,” Victor admitted flatly. “Something called the Soulrot Curse. It devours both my core and soul simultaneously. I managed to seal it temporarily, but it’s only a matter of time before it consumes me completely.” He went on to summarize what he had found in the ancient tome.

Both overseers exchanged a glance, their expressions becoming tense. Garry folded his arms. “A curse capable of afflicting a Nexus Temporal Magus proficient in both Cosmo and Chrono elements… that’s troubling indeed. Only something far beyond mortal craft could accomplish such a feat.”

Gabrielle nodded. “If it truly originates from the Devourer Beyond Stars, then we’re dealing with an entity outside conventional existence. Its power isn’t bound by material laws.”

“Unfortunately,” Garry added, “the citadel’s restoration isn’t complete yet, and we cannot provide direct assistance. However, most of the low- and mid-rank combat golems have been fully reactivated. There are currently over one thousand mechanical golems at Elemental Adept-level combat capacity, and nearly one hundred operating at the early stage of Nexus Temporal rank. They can be deployed at your command.”

Victor’s eyes widened in surprise. Though he had continued sending all sorts of materials, including aetherite and voidstones, to the citadel, he hadn’t expected such progress. To learn that there were so many powerful mechanical golems available filled him with genuine relief. With this, the Sanctum finally had enough high-rank combat power to stand its ground. And more importantly… there would be reinforcements ready when he returned to the Dreamscape to proceed with the experiment.

“Hopefully, dreamforce can counteract the curse. Since it isn’t tied to the material realm like mana or elemental energy, it might not provoke the same reaction,” he said thoughtfully.

“Hmm. An intriguing hypothesis,” Garry mused. “However, manipulating dreamforce directly is extremely unstable. Even Nexus Conjurer Magi have perished attempting it.”

Gabrielle placed a hand on her hip with a slightly somber tone as she said, “Master, if you intend to pursue this path, there’s something within the citadel that can be useful. We possess a Dream Reactor — a construct designed to stabilize dreamforce and power massive vessels or structures such as airships and citadels. However, it’s… incomplete.”

Victor arched a brow. “Dream Reactor?”

“It was originally part of the citadel’s alternative energy system,” Garry explained. “It channels dreamforce directly from the Dreamscape. The Steelrose Order once used it for prototype magi-tech experiments, but it was decommissioned long ago after a… nightmarish incident.”

“Of course it was,” Victor muttered under his breath, rubbing his temple. “Nothing’s ever easy.”

The Dream Reactor’s potential was undeniable, but if it was incomplete and unstable, it might be more of a liability than a solution. Fortunately, he had a far more reliable option. He had Lillie, who was the darling of the Dreamscape — someone born with a natural connection to that realm. As long as there was her, Victor had nothing to worry about not being able to harness dreamforce.

With that thought, Victor decided against using the Dream Reactor for now. The twin overseers assured him that the device would remain available should he wish to inspect it in the future, and he accepted the offer, keeping the option open in case the need ever arose. With his business at the citadel concluded, Victor turned on his heel and left the citadel after voicing his thanks for their assistance.

While on his way back to the academy, Victor opened the Headmaster’s Journal to review the new main and side missions that had been issued.

—— [Headmaster’s Journal] ——

Mission: Buy Your Academy Time

In the short term, an envoy from the Arcane Radiance College will arrive at the Sanctum, demanding submission. As the headmaster, you must meet the envoy and buy as much time as possible for the Sanctum to prepare for the inevitable confrontation. Whether through negotiation, deception, or manipulation, every day you gain will determine the academy’s survival.

Type: Main mission

Time Bought: 0 days / 30 days

Reward: 25,000 academy points

————————

Mission: Cure the Curse

Having been afflicted with the deadly Soulrot Curse, you must find a way to undo its effects before your body and soul succumb completely. Otherwise, only death awaits at the end of this path.

Type: Side mission

Reward: 10,000 academy points

…

These were the missions that had appeared while Victor was busy searching for information about the curse back in the citadel.

“Buy time, huh… It seems even the System acknowledges how precarious the situation has become.”

Victor wasn’t the least bit intimidated by the surrounding Magus factions. None of them possessed even a single Sigil Conversion Magus, and under normal circumstances, he could have crushed them with ease — even now, while afflicted by the Soulrot Curse. However, the Arcane Radiance College was another matter entirely. It was one of the oldest — and the most powerful — Magus organizations in the kingdom, and facing them head-on would be sheer madness.

Still, stalling them for thirty days shouldn’t prove too difficult. Victor had more than enough resources and hidden cards at his disposal. He could try to curry favor or even trick the envoy. If he could secure even that much time, it would be enough to reinforce the academy’s defenses, increase the total number of players, complete the activation of more mechanical golem units, and possibly find a way to lift the curse before it devoured him completely.

As for the Cure the Curse side mission, he already had a few leads, though none of them were simple. Dreamforce... Lillie... or perhaps the Dream Reactor, if it comes to that. The more he thought about it, the clearer it became that this curse was a problem that could not be solved through sheer brute force or magical might alone.

When he exited the pocket dimension, the Cosmic Rubik suit stirred in response to his mana fluctuations, faint lines of starlight tracing across its compact form. “At least I have you now. As long as there’s energy to convert, I can still wield a fraction of a Nexus Temporal Magus’s power.”

With that thought, he activated the Teleportation Waypoint to return. Space folded around him in a flash of violet light, and in the next instant, he reappeared within the academy’s square right in front of the Headmaster’s Building.

Because he hadn’t concealed his presence quickly enough, the nearby players immediately noticed him.

“Whoa, who’s that?” one of them exclaimed, pointing in his direction.

“A player? No way! How did he get such a cool sci-fi full-body armor set? I want one too!” another shouted, eyes gleaming with envy.

“Hey, handsome. Want to party up with me?” Even a nearby female player called out teasingly, clearly impressed by his imposing appearance. She must have thought that he was a pro player who could carry her all the way.

“Go away. He’s wearing a helmet. How can you tell that he’s handsome?”

“…” Victor was utterly speechless.

His dark-purple, futuristic-looking armor made him stand out like a sore thumb amidst the crowd. And with his face fully hidden behind the sleek helmet, none of the players realized that the “mysterious elite” before them was actually their own headmaster.

Without wasting a moment, Victor erased his presence with the suit’s ability and made his way toward Lillie’s room. His mind was focused on how to undo the Soulrot Curse. If anyone could help him find a way through dreamforce, it was her — the child of the Dreamscape itself. It was time to put his theory to the test.
