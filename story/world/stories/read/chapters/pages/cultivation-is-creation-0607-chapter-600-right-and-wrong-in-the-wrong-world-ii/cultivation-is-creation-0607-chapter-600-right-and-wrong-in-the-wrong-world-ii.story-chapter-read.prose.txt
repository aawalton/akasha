My main concern was Li Na using her ability on Xie Ling because while Wu Kangming and I could come back from death as many times as necessary, the ice cultivator would be gone permanently if something went wrong.

"It's called Nine Deaths Transfer," Li Na said, her voice still carrying traces of the fear that had been overwhelming her moments ago. "It's a bloodline ability that's been passed down through my family for generations, but not everyone inherits it and even fewer can use it to a level where it is useful."

Seeing my nod, she continued.

"The technique lets me transfer my injuries instantaneously to another person within my spiritual sense range, and it works on any type of injury, physical or spiritual."

That matched what I'd already observed when she'd used it on me, the sudden appearance of all those broken bones and ruptured organs without any warning or transition period, but I was more interested in its limitations. After all, no matter how overpowered a technique might seem, it would have some cost or something to hold it back.

"I can only use it nine times in my entire life," Li Na continued, and the way she said it made it seem like this limitation was more significant than just a simple usage cap. "Every time I transfer my injuries, it consumes a portion of my bloodline essence permanently. After the ninth use, the ability burns itself out and I can never use it again. I've already used it twice, once several years ago when Shen Bolin recruited me and once just now against the Three Calamities, which means I have seven uses left."

Seven? That was enough to get us from step ninety-one to step ninety-eight, leaving just one final step before we reached the Chamber of the Sleeper King. That was actually better than I'd expected.

“How did Shen Bolin recruit you?” I asked.

“He saw me use the technique to save my family from some rogue Oneiric Sovereigns thirteen years ago," Li Na sighed, "three cultivators had broken into our compound to rob us. They…they’d already killed my parents and my older brother. I…I was hiding with my younger siblings when they found us."

"Watching my family die must have awakened my ability," she continued, wrapping her arms around herself like the memory was physically painful to recall. "So, when one of the rogue cultivators tried to kill my sister, I jumped into the way, then used the Nine Deaths Transfer without really understanding what it would do, and suddenly he was dying on the floor screaming in pain. Before his friends could get revenge, Shen Bolin arrived. He said he had been tracking the rogue cultivators for days because they'd caused problems in multiple cities."

Clearly, the dynamic between student and teacher had developed over the years in a very unhealthy direction.

"After that, he took me in, arranged proper funerals for my family, and gave me a place in his sect where I could train my abilities..."

"And in exchange, you agreed to use your bloodline technique whenever he needed it?"

"He's been good to me," she said, a defensive look appeared on her face like that was a totally normal thing for him to ask of her. “He trained me, protected me from those who wanted to exploit my bloodline, gave me resources and techniques that I never would be able to have without him, and he never forced me to use the Nine Deaths Transfer. It’s the least I could do.”

That last part seemed true but was actually misleading, because Shen Bolin hadn't needed to force her when he could just cultivate a sense of obligation and loyalty that made her volunteer for dangerous missions. This girl had been groomed over years to be a sacrificial pawn who believed she was making meaningful choices.

"You know that's not actually how healthy relationships work, right," I said, "being useful to someone doesn't mean you have to accept being treated as disposable, there's a difference between mutual benefit and exploitation."

Li Na looked at me like she thought I was naive or idealistic of how the cultivation world worked, and maybe she was right and it was wrong of me to apply standards from my previous world to a reality that operated on different principles.

"For me it's worth it," she said quietly, "I get to live, I get to cultivate, I get to have purpose and meaning in a world that took everything else away from me, and if the price of that is occasionally putting myself at risk for Master Shen's benefit, then I'm willing to pay it."

There was no point trying to convince her more than that, I had no idea how to explain to the girl that her morals had been distorted by years of manipulation and trauma bonding because that required psychological training and expertise that I didn’t have.

"What's the relationship between Shen Bolin and Song Wei," I asked instead, "they act like they hate each other, but it seems fake to me."

"I don't know," Li Na admitted, her expression turning complicated, "I've been with Master for years and have seen him interact with Song Wei a handful of times, the dynamic between them has always been strange."

She shifted her weight slightly, clearly uncomfortable discussing this topic but recognizing that she didn't have much choice given her current position. "They act like bitter rivals in public, arguing about strategy and criticizing each other's techniques, but then they meet up in private."

"Do you know why they pretend to hate each other," I asked, "what benefit do they get from making everyone think they're enemies when they're clearly working together."

"I don't know," she shook her head, sounding genuinely lost. "All I know is that they both recruit parties to attempt the Sunken Dream Palace every few decades, and somehow they're always the only ones who survive and escape afterward."

From my knowledge of cultivation novel tropes, I could make a few guesses about what was going on, and none of them were reassuring. The first being that they were harvesting fresh sacrifices for one reason or another. Maybe they’d made some deal with whatever entity controlled this palace and had to sell other cultivators' lives so they could continue living? Or maybe they were working together to revive the Dreamgod…

I didn’t know the reason, but what I did know was that we couldn’t trust anything Shen Bolin or Song Wei had told us during the expedition.

"I need you to take an oath," I said, looking directly at her to make it clear this wasn't a request or suggestion but an absolute requirement for our continued cooperation, "swear by the heavens that you will only use your Nine Deaths Transfer technique on me, and that you will never target Xie Ling with it under any circumstances."

Li Na's eyes widened slightly because heavenly oaths weren't something cultivators took lightly since they carried binding force that would punish oath-breakers severely, and could even kill cripple or kill them.

But seeing that was the only way I would let her continue with us, she took a deep breath and began.

"I, Li Na, swear that...”

"She can use it on me too,” Wu Kangming cut in.

I turned to look at him, grateful that he had offered to share the burden. I'd been prepared to take all of Li Na's transferred injuries myself if necessary, but dying over and over again wasn't exactly a pleasant experience, and having someone else who could alternate with me would make it more bearable.

The physical pain of death wasn't the worst part, sure it wasn't fun having your organs rupture, your bones shatter, or your spiritual foundation collapse, but the terrifying part was the moment right before resurrection when your consciousness faded and you weren't quite sure if you were actually going to come back this time. Would the tournament's safety mechanisms fail, would I die for real, questions like this all created an existential dread that couldn’t be rationalised with.

"Alright," I said, turning back to Li Na, "swear by the heavens that you will only use your Nine Deaths Transfer technique on me and Tian, never on Xie Ling or any other person we might encounter, and that you will use the technique when we request it during combat situations to eliminate enemies that threaten our group."

I knew cultivators could be very sneaky so I watched her face carefully as she began speaking to make sure she wasn't trying to find loopholes or clever wording that would let her escape the binding.

"I, Li Na, swear by the heavens above and the earth below that I will only use my Nine Deaths Transfer bloodline technique on Hou and Tian," she said, "I will never use this technique on Xie Ling or any other person regardless of circumstances, and I will activate the technique when told by Hou or Tian during combat situations to eliminate threats to our group, I swear this oath freely and accept the consequences of breaking it."

As soon as the oath was sworn, it came into existence as a golden light that surrounded Li Na’s body, writing the oath directly into her spiritual foundation. When the light faded, nothing seemed to have changed, except there was that sense that something fundamental had changed around her.

Honestly, a part of me had been worried that heavenly oaths might not work properly in the Dream World given that this was an inner world and didn’t have a Heaven like the Cultivation World did, but apparently the metaphysical laws governing such things applied to all realms.

Now that the Li Na situation was sorted, I looked up at step ninety-one, grateful that no enemy had appeared yet despite us standing here talking for several minutes, but that also suggested something had changed about how the trial operated now that our group had split.

Before we could move forward with this new arrangement and start using Li Na's bloodline ability to clear the remaining stairs, there was one more thing that needed to be addressed, and that was the situation between Wu Kangming and me. We both knew exactly who the other person really was now, we needed to talk about that before it became a problem that could get one or both of us killed and ejected from this realm.
