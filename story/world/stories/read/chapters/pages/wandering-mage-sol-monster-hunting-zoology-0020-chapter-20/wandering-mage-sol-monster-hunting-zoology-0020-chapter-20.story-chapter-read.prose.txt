An hour after wandering around the market street with vendors calling them over, it seemed word had spread of the elf who exposed a vendor’s lie. Some merchants seemed hesitant to approach them now, but still, others appeared eager to have them check out their wares. It seemed that either there was some conception that all High Elves were wealthy, or it could be that word had also spread about a group of odd hunters who had recently completed a bounty and were now spending their reward.

It seems gold truly is the great equalizer, Tressa thought with a sigh after the third or fourth merchant called to them, whereas most people would typically try to avoid them under most circumstances.

Tressa had given in and bought a new bag, some notebooks, and a few quills. She had been upset over the price of the bag, as it had cost her two silver, and the books, ink, and several quills had cost another silver and seventy copper. Not to mention she'd have to spend more on food and alcohol later as well. Her share of the prize was already dwindling. She had never much cared for money before, but that mentality she realized changed quite quickly now that she was living a life where she actually needed it.

But the new bag would be useful for collecting things like plants and herbs that she didn’t want stuffing in with her notebooks and potentially damaging them.

Lisha had gotten some cleaner and a sharpening stone for her halberd. And Briadon had been sucked into buying a new holder for his club that was easier for him to unbuckle, so he could grab his weapon two seconds faster, as he had put it. Tressa and Lisha had just shared looks, as they had no clue how he had come to that after only messing around with the strap.

After wandering around and ending up lost among the countless vendors, they finally had to stop and ask for directions and found the smither wasn't even in the market district, but on the other side of town near the stables and woodworkers, in the industrial area.

They made their way there, and by late day the three stood outside a large building, staring at the old wooden and stone structure. The smell of fire and ore drifted from the barn-like doors, and hammering could be heard from within, along with a deep grunt.

Going through the open door, they found a heavily built man with old burns and scars on his arms and face. He stood pounding a massive hammer against glowing red-hot metal as it rested on an anvil. Sparks flew as he hammered over and over, making the shape he wanted with the glowing metal. Finally, he dipped it in a bucket of water, and it fizzled out as steam rose.

He pulled it out and hammered again, and finally dipped it one more time and set down the hammer. After a moment, he pulled off his gloves and sighed.

“You just going to stand there or are you going to say something?”

“Ahem,” Lisha coughed in surprise.

“That all you got to say?” he said. “If so, get out. I'm busy.”

Briadon huffed and began to step forward, but Lisha stopped him.

“Sorry, she was just impressed with you work!” Tressa cut in.

The huge man turned and laughed. “Ha! Thanks, kid.” His eyes flitted to Briadon, who stood a good head or two taller than him, then back to Lisha, and finally he focused on Tressa. His eyes widened for just a moment before turning away.

“I don't do business with elves. Get out.”

“Hang on a second!” Lisha said, her hand shooting out and raised.

“I said I don't work with elves. Now go,” he said, picking his hammer back up.

Briadon folded his arms as well and laughed.

“What's so funny, minotaur?”

“She isn't so bad, trust me. Plus whatever you need hunted she is skilled at tracking. She comes in handy.”

He glared at them, then looked at Tressa, who stood a bit back. She was small, but looked confident. He could tell by her thick accent that she didn't quite know the common tongue.

“How old are ya, elf?”

“195, sir.”

“Young for an elf. Blood elf or wood elf?”

She instantly realized he knew little of elves, if he could mistake her silver-blonde braids for belonging to a wood elf.

“High elf, sir,” she explained, pulling her hood down, letting her ears free for him to see.

He grunted and turned his back. “Leave. I won't do business with you.”

The group looked at each other.

“As I said, sir, she ain't so bad. For an elf,” Briadon said.

He looked over his shoulder. “Don't care. Get out.”

“Wait! We are hunters," Lisha said quickly, trying to get his attention before he started hammering again. "Master Merlle sent us your way. Said you needed a job done."

He groaned and muttered, “Damn that fat bastard,” then let out a deep sigh as his shoulders slumped.

“So Merlle sent you my way, huh?”

“Yes, sir.”

His eyes once again landed on Tressa. “Means I have no choice. He thinks highly of ya and wouldn't send you here if you weren't capable. What's ya name, elf?”

Tressa bowed her head. “Tressa Windshade, sir.”

“At least ya got some manners. All right, I do have a job that needs doing. Come on in,” he said, leading them to the back of the forge.

Pulling open a drawer, he moved some parchment aside and finally found what he was looking for.

“Since y'all hunters, I need ya to hunt down a Sawtooth Boar and a Belwer Bear.”

Tressa looked at the two rough drawings. “What's a bealmer brear?”

The man looked at her oddly, raising an eyebrow.

“It's pronounced Belwer Bear,” Lisha said, sounding it out to help her.

Tressa played with the words on her tongue, and after a second, shook her head. “I can't say it.”

The smither cracked a smile as he watched her struggle. “You're very odd for an elf.”

Tressa looked up, confused. “What?”

“Nothing. But those are the bounties I need hunted.”

Briadon handed the paper to Lisha, who looked it over. “So you need both intact, correct?”

“Aye. Bring them back in as good condition as ye can. They make good armor and weapons. I'll also give you a map of the areas they tend to be located in, as I have had my eyes on them for some time, and I feel if Merlle sent ya my way, you are plenty capable.”

Lisha looked over the papers, seeing the reward or best offer, and then looked to the time limit for the quest, which was two months. “Hmmm,” she muttered, reading on as it asked for them to be in as good condition as possible, and that parts would be traded for value.

“Two months?” Lisha asked as she looked up.

“Aye. They are over here, more to the west,” he said, tapping the massive map mounted on the wall behind his desk.

“Sounds good. It doesn't say anything in here about us having the monsters inspected. May we have them inspected once we get back to have them listed for the proper price? After all, Belwer Bears are asking a lot. They are dangerous.”

He sighed. “Was hopin' you wouldn't catch that detail. But yes, that's fine.”

Lisha nodded. “All right, we have a deal. Oh, I'll get this to the guild and have it approved, get a copy, bring one back to you, keep one, and then the guild keeps one.”

“Aye, that is what I thought. Now let us conclude this,” he said, taking back the papers. He signed them, melted some wax over the line, and stamped his seal into it. Any person who did business was required to have a personal seal of work. Merchants, smithers, woodworkers, and the like all had seals based on their type of work. His was no exception, with a large anvil and hammer and small letters under the anvil showing his initials. Once the wax dried, he passed it to Lisha, and she placed it into the bag at her side.

“Oh!” Tressa said and nudged Lisha.

“What?” Lisha asked, leaning down, and Tressa came up slightly on her toes and whispered something into her ear. A smile spread over Lisha's scaly lips. “Good idea.”

The smither looked at them and raised an eyebrow.

“Sir, is it possible to sell you a few horned rabbit horns?”

“Ye have some?”

“Yes, sir.”

Lisha pulled out three of the horns. “We ran into a few on the way here and thought we could find someone to sell them to.”

“Let's have a look,” he said as he picked up the horns she placed on the table.

“No damage,” he said after a few seconds of looking. He rolled them around in his hand and nodded. “They're in good shape. Eight silver for the three.”

Lisha's jaw dropped. “Isn't that overpaying, sir? Normally, we get fifty copper per horn.”

He looked at her. “You've been underpaid. These horns have a lot more value than just turning them into weapons, and I have a connection with an alchemist.”

“Alchemist again, huh?” Tressa muttered in Elvish as she slipped out her notebook.

The smith’s eyes shot to Tressa as he heard the scribbling of a quill. An eyebrow rose as he looked at her hand writing quickly across a book.

“Sir, what else can horns be used to make? Is it only making weapons?” An insane sparkle of excitement lit up her eyes as she inched closer.

“W-w-what?”

“Are they used to make potions? Or enchant weapons? Turned into a powder for beauty products?”

“Uh. Yeah, some take the top layer of the horn off. The hard outer shell can be used to enhance weapons, or ground into a powder and used for a fire potion. Sometimes it can be used to clean weapons and…” He trailed off as Tressa quickly wrote down everything he stated.

“Mind explaining what in the hells you're doing there, elf?”

“Oh, of course!" She handed the book to him, and he saw the most detailed drawing of a horned rabbit and the sketch of what the horn looked like after removal. His eyes shot to the drawing of the belwer bear he possessed, instantly recognizing the difference in quality and skill.

Her detailed drawings would put most scholars to shame, and his mind instantly went to how he could get this off her and add it to his own collection.

“Tressa's pretty amazing, isn't she?” Lisha said. “She has books full of monsters and what their body parts can be used for. Even what is edible and not edible.”

“And poisons!” Tressa added excitedly. “I test to see what’s poisonous and like to find antidotes. Or change so it is safe to eat.” She nodded.

His eyes shot to Tressa. “You experiment with poisons?”

She chuckled nervously and scratched at an ear. “Yes sir. Um, sometimes.”

“We ended up camping out for a few days cause this dipshit decided to mess around with a Terosnake that made her so sick we thought she was going to die.” Briadon huffed in annoyance at the memory.

His jaw fell open. “Are you a fool, elf!?”

“No!” she said defensively. Then she looked down and twiddled her thumbs sheepishly. “Just curious.”

“A Terosnake? You ate a Terosnake!?”

“No. I was messing with its fangs, and one punctured my hand.” Suddenly, her face lit up. “Can you eat them?”

“No! Gods, are you mad?!”

“I like to think I'm a genius. So no, not mad. If I can see what is safe and understand monsters, then I can keep hunters and travelers safe. Maybe. So I like to learn all I can!”

He stared at her, open-mouthed, then burst out laughing. “Ye ain't bad after all, elf. Hmph. What would you say to making a copy of this for me? And make it in the common tongue? I'll pay well for it.”

Tressa looked at Lisha and Briadon, then back to the man. Lisha gave a toothy smile.

“I… I will do my best, sir. I'm not well taught in writing, but with their help, I can.”

He nodded and grunted in gratitude.

He handed her back her book, and she carefully placed it back into her bag. “Even the alchemist would pay handsomely for a copy of that. Assuming your notes are accurate, of course.”

“They are!” Tressa said almost defensively. “If I do not know or it is just… teary?”

“Theory,” Lisha corrected.

“If it’s theory, I write it.”

"If I may ask," Lisha cut in. "Just how much would you pay for something like this?"

"Me? Well, I'd have to look more thoroughly to see precisely what you got. But the right content to the right buyer, I wouldn't be surprised if you could sell one for ten or more gold."

"Ten gold!? Without having to fight?" Briadon gaped. "Tressa, you better get on that!"

Tressa grinned sheepishly, thinking of the few times she's had to copy even a few damaged pages. "It will take a very long time."

The blacksmith nodded. “What you have there is potentially a gold mine, elf. I suggest you keep them safe. Many would kill for that kind of information.”

Lisha and Briadon shared a look, but then Briadon cleared his throat as he picked up their contract, looking it over and bringing the conversation back to their current business. “Where are the locations of the hunts?”

“The Belwer Bear will be to the west. And you know Sawtooth Boars can be found almost anywhere. I'd like them intact as much as you can. I'll make sure to pay you in the meat and maybe even some coin, as stated in the contract. But I need the body parts.”

“Yes sir.”

“Why must they be intact as much as possible?” Tressa asked.

“Their parts are used in weapons. I can strengthen armor and give it attributes and resistance to certain attacks.”

“Amazing,” Tressa said with excitement.

“Aye, it's quite handy. It keeps soldiers and hunters safe. Especially with the growth of the monster population around these here parts. If ye took out those horned rabbits, that's quite a feat. They've been up and causing trouble. The farmers are having trouble as they come in and eat the vegetables, and the harvest has been poor. Ye can ask the farmers after my bounty for a job. They might have something. The local hunters aren't all cracked up and worth the dangerous jobs. They say it doesn't pay enough and refuse to take it.”

“That's good to know,” Briadon said. “That gives us more work and a bit more to put aside.”

“Aye lad.”

Lisha looked over the papers again. “So two months?”

“Aye. Think ye can handle that?”

“Of course,” Briadon answered.

“Then we are set,” he said and shook hands with Briadon. “Good luck to you three.”

The group thanked him and left the forge, heading back through the winding streets and back to the guild to turn in the paperwork and see if they could get approval for the job.

As they went back onto the street, Briadon muttered, “So two months. We best leave right away. Belwer Bears live in caves or massive holes dug out by other monsters.”

“What else do you know of Belwer Bear?” Tressa asked as she pulled out her notebook.

“It's a massive bear. Think of a grizzly, but bigger, and the fact it has horns and uses earth magic. Its claws are like short swords and can slice through most armor. Teeth sharp and deadly. What else?” Briadon thought, pulling at his braid. “That's about as much as I know.”

“They have a fear effect that can temporarily paralyze the body,” Lisha added.

“Ah yes, that. And well, they can use earth magic and lightning. And I guess terror?”

Tressa quickly jotted that down. The fear effect interested her the most. Surely it involved some kind of mana, but what kind? Magic that affected the mind would be completely new ground for her to research, and maybe that’s the direction she’d have to go in to create something suitable to pass the dragon’s trial with.

“Get a move on, damn it!” yelled a man nearby as he smacked a small boy upside the head, cutting off Tressa's conversation with the others.

“Yes, sir!” he said as he picked up the box he had dropped, and the man sighed. His eyes caught Tressa's, and fear flashed across his face as he saw the ears. She realized she’d forgotten to put her hood up, and quickly corrected that, pulling it over her head.

“What you looking at, boy?” he spat.

“Nothing, sir!” he squeaked and quickly stood.

Tressa looked at Lisha for an explanation.

“Well, he is either a slave, an apprentice, an orphan, or that's his father.”

“Slave?” Tressa said as her face darkened a bit.

“Yes,” Lisha said, catching the look.

“What's the problem, Tressa? No slaves among elves?”

“No. We don't. After the mad king died and the new king took the throne, the slaves were freed and returned home with great payment as apology. King Faldin hoped it would fix some of the hatred towards us. Or that's what I'm told,” she said as she turned away.

“I see,” Lisha said, her tongue flicking.

“Anyway, where to?” Tressa asked, trying not to think about the boy or unpleasant memories.

“Right. We will need supplies, so let's go back to the market street and see what they have to offer.”

After a few minutes, they made it back to the loud and clambering street filled with stalls, merchants, buyers, and traders.

As they traveled through the market street, Tressa was pushed and she tripped. She landed on the ground with a heavy thud and let out a yelp.

“Hey!” Lisha shouted and instantly ran after someone. Tressa blinked as she looked up from the ground, seeing Lisha running after someone Tressa at first mistook for a child, but it was a really small man.

“A dwarf!” Tressa said excitedly, until she saw what he was carrying and she looked down at herself. “My new bag!”

Thankfully, Lisha was faster. Before he made it around the next corner, she grabbed him, her clawed hand gripping his shoulder and swinging him around. It was a dwarf.

“I suggest you give that back before I bite your neck,” she said, baring her fangs at him.

“Stitiki!?”

“Yes. And now that I have your scent, I can track you anywhere. So I suggest giving—”

“My bag!” Tressa screamed. She started cursing in elven, and many people stopped as she looked around frantically.

“Calm down, Tressa. The thief was caught,” Briadon said as he helped her up and they walked over. Lisha had a firm grip on the drawf, not letting him go. He still held Tressa’s belongings, and Briadon towered over him with folded arms.

“Give it back,” he said, and the dwarf dropped Tressa's bag. She scooped it up, going through it to make sure nothing was missing. Her face turned angry. It had only lasted a total of several seconds, with him slamming into her, cutting the strap, and then running off.

“Give me back my quill and book.”

Lisha grabbed him harder. “Now.”

He reached into his coat and dropped the book and the metal quill.

“You!” a man yelled, running up. “You're the damn thief!”

“Over here!” another yelled as town guards came running up and grabbed the dwarf.

“Get your hands off me!” the dwarf yelled, struggling against their hold.

“Like hell I will. You were warned that if we had any more trouble with you, you'd be arrested,” the human said as he shackled the dwarf's hands. The whole time, the dwarf cursed at the human guard.

“Thank you, you three. That man has been causing problems all over the city. Who knows how much he has stolen and hidden.”

“You're welcome,” Lisha said. “He attacked our companion out of the blue.”

Tressa meanwhile was examining the cut strap of her bag and clutching her book and quill to her chest with her other hand, her face turning hot with the injustice of losing something she'd only just bought with coin she'd worked so hard for.

“Tressa, you alright?” Briadon poked her cheek with his massive finger.

She swatted his hand away. “Not! That stupid man break my bag! Tried steal my quill and book!” She sniffed as she had trouble speaking the common tongue when her emotions were too high.

“An elf!?” said a human as he stepped back a bit.

“A high elf…” said another with a growl.

But a few others looked at her with sympathy as she held the broken strap of her bag and looked so clearly upset.

She stepped up to the guard. “He owe two silver for bag!” she demanded.

“Wh-what?”

“She paid two silver for the bag, and she wants the dwarf to pay her back,” Briadon explained.

The guard looked at the dwarf, who paled.

“I don't have that!” he cried.

Tressa held out her hand. “Silver. You ruined my bag.”

“I said I don't have that!”

Tressa glared at him. “Then work and pay me back.”

The guard laughed, and Tressa looked at him.

“Sorry, I've just never seen someone so dead set on getting paid back.”

“I work for that money. He ruined my bag. I want him pay it back!”

Another guard patted down the dwarf, found a small money pouch, and opened it.

“Hey! Get your hands off that!” he yelled.

“You do owe her for cutting her bag. If you can't deal with the punishment, don't do the crime,” he said and opened the coin pouch.

“He doesn’t have more than eight copper.”

Tressa glared at the dwarf. “Pay me back one day, or I ask Lisha to give you a beating.”

Lisha laughed. “I'd be happy to.” She cracked her knuckles. “I have your scent, so I'd be able to find you easily,” she said as her tongue flicked out of her mouth to emphasize the fact.

The guard sighed. “Unfortunately, there is not much we can do to get you paid back, hunter, but this man will be arrested for his crime.”

As his backup finally arrived, he and the other guard held onto each of the dwarf's arms and they walked off, ending the scene. Everyone on the street got back to their business, but Tressa still muttered angrily in Elvish as she looked at her bag. “Damn it. What am I supposed to do about this?”
