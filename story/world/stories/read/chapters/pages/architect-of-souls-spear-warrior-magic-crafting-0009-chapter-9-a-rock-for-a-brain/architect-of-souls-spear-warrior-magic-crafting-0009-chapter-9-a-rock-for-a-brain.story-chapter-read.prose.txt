Ferris made an appointment with Elder Harper, and it didn’t take long for her to meet him. She had an office on the ground floor of a building near the library.

When he told her his decision, the smile she gave him was pure predatory. “Excellent,” she said. “Are you aware of the most common use for acid type cores on delver teams?” She asked him.

Ferris knew little outside of his clan’s teaching and what he’d learned that week. He’d paid a lot of attention to the journal on Janice and Gumly, but it didn’t describe any weapons using acid cores. He could, however, hazard a guess.

“I have not learned about them, but perhaps melting enemies?” Ferris suggested.

“Yes, but they are specifically good at melting enemies with armor.” She replied. “Scalefort Bastion is so named for the prevalence of scaled monsters. Acid is particularly effective at removing armor, it even makes enemies more vulnerable to team member’s attacks.”

“Let me guess,” said Ferris. “Clan Hollowfield has delvers with acid core weapons.”

“Indeed. Several martial traditions within the clan are dedicated to them,” she confirmed. “That is good news for you, as the clan knows many offensive acid abilities. This is also good for me. Our research project can focus on discovering the requirements of Acid Reconstruction, and then attempt to share it with another Adept. If successful, it will help many of our delvers.”

“What if I had picked something else?” Ferris asked. He wasn’t opposed to Hollowfield profiting off him—it was a fair trade after all—but he was curious.

“If you had chosen the plant core, we would have focused on offensive abilities to empower Hollowfield’s growers. The clan’s knowledge in that area is lacking.” She waved her hand in the air. “If you had chosen the prosthetic leg, we could have documented an entirely new bond item. For the fire core…” she shrugged, “then I would have likely investigated underwater fire abilities. Perhaps a new opportunity for research would have presented itself, perhaps not.”

Ferris briefly wondered if she had filtered the information she gave him. Somehow, the only options he had seriously considered were the ones that benefited her the most. She had promised the choices would be his, yet she still got her way. Although… maybe he was being harsh, and the Elder was simply good at finding advantage out of any situation. There is nothing I can do about it either way, he decided. Besides, I am happy with my path.

Ferris handed her the reports he had written on the six potential monster types for familiar bonds. Elder Harper barely looked at them before placing them on her desk, but immediately gave him a piece of feedback on their contents. Somehow, she had read all six pages in a single moment.

“One note, for the sake of clarity. You place high expectations on the Adaptability ability described by Janice Landsworn. I should warn you: while it will likely give you an uncommon advantage finding targeted abilities in the first tier, understand that the second tier has similar techniques available to all. The general advice is to focus on a bonded weapon’s primary strength in the first tier, and gain versatility in the second.”

“Thank you,” Ferris said, “I understand.” The ‘general advice’ didn’t help him regrow his leg, and the Adaptability ability still felt right to him. If it had an advantage in the first tier, he imagined it would grow to give even greater flexibility in the second.

“Now, you are in luck,” she continued. “Normally a bonded weapon is expensive, but a slime familiar will be substantially cheaper.”

“I heard that slimes in the rifts can have special abilities. Could I find one with a special ability, or even an additional aspect like water?” It was something Ferris had wondered, but the journals had not answered.

Elder Harper shook her head. “The bond requires the strength of your soul be stronger than the monster’s spirit, or at least similar. You could wait until your cultivation progresses, but there are other advantages to guiding the familiar’s development yourself.”

That made sense. Ferris had never heard of young cultivators bonding ancient dragons, after all.

“The bond also requires cooperation,” she said. “Consent would be the wrong word, as monsters inherently don’t think like that, but it will look the same from the outside. Most monsters act instinctually. Convincing it to accept the bond is usually an arduous process, sometimes requiring imprinting a newborn. Luckily, slimes are extremely food motivated.”

Ferris nodded. All the journals had a section on befriending, although they were all guesses by the authors. The journal on Janice and Gumly had simply suggested choosing a docile slime and feeding it.

“Additionally,” she continued, “I have completed my audit into Hollowfield’s known tier one healing abilities. Your reports will be appended for reference. It will be added to the library, and we will both receive merit. Your share will be fifteen points.”

That was quite generous of her. Ferris did not believe for a second that his reports deserved the reward. Hemon had explained the merit rewards for the basic work retainers were assigned—such as guard duty or assistants—and fifteen points would take about six weeks to earn.

Elder Harper had probably created something valuable to the clan, but Ferris’ observations wouldn’t add much. The only thing he could think of was it being a reward for choosing a bond she could research. It would help him progress, which would further her project. What would my ‘share’ be if I had chosen something of less interest? Would I have got anything at all? He wondered.

“Thank you, Elder Harper. That is very generous,” was all he said.

Her piercing eyes made him wonder if she could read minds.

“There is no point in delaying, normally we would have to place a request to the mission board for a team of delvers to capture a live monster to bond. Fortunately, the sixth branch keeps slimes as livestock.” She wrote a short letter of introduction, and then gave him directions.

Ferris took the note and examined it. Imagine, he thought, using paper just to pass a message. It was addressed to one Foreman Carter, and simply requested help acquiring a slime.

“How much will this Foreman Carter charge for a slime?” Ferris asked.

Elder Harper chuckled. “I doubt that will be a problem. You will see,” was all she said, and dismissed him.

A thrill infected Ferris as he headed for the sixth district. Using a crutch always involved a bit of hopping, but he could not help adding an extra spring to his steps. This is really happening, he thought, I am really going to do this.

It was nice outside. Some days the light from the Wild would shift colors or darken, but today it was as bright as could be. He hummed a tune as he made his way into the fifth branch’s section.

The fifth district… stank. There was a haze in the air that almost spoiled his mood. Ferris didn’t have much experience with alchemy, but he recognized its sour smell. It seemed like every other building had colorful plumes of smoke billowing out. While the fourth branch focused on research, it was obvious the fifth branch specialized in brewing potions and creating pills. Ferris moved quickly to pass through and breathed a sigh of relief to finally leave the smell behind.

The sixth district, on the other hand, was delightful. Flowers lined the streets, and blossoming trees popped up everywhere. The sweet fragrance was a nice change as Ferris slowed down to enjoy the walk. The clan was laid out so that most important buildings were along the main road, but Elder Harper’s directions took him off to the edge of the wall.

As Ferris approached his destination, the fragrant smell of flowers turned sickly sweet, giving way to the stink of sulfur. He began to understand why his destination was so far out: it reeked of decomposition. A sign with the words “Slime Ranch” hung over a fenced off area. This must be the place, he thought. I wonder what they need slimes for. Is it really safe to keep monsters penned up like this?

Someone at the entry directed him to Foreman Carter. The foreman was a large man, blessed with a full beard but cursed with a bald head. Ferris could not help but compare the top of his head to the drawing of Gumly from the journal on Janice Landsworn.

“Excuse me, Foreman Carter. Elder Harper from the fourth branch sent me.” Ferris introduced himself and handed over the letter. The man read it briefly, and then eyed him.

“What’s this about? Another alchemy experiment?” The man seemed dubious of Ferris, but friendly enough.

“No, I intend to bond it.” Ferris said, giving the man a big smile.

Foreman Carter stared at him for a second, mouth hanging slightly open. “Bond a slime? Why in all the hells would you do that?”

Ferris deflated a little, not sure how to answer. “The slime bond will provide a healing ability.” Gesturing to his missing leg, he figured the rest was obvious.

“Blast it kid,” Foreman Carter said, “a slime is no good familiar companion. Anything would be better than binding yourself to the awful things.”

Ferris was a bit taken aback. He knew slimes were considered a weaker monster, but Elder Harper hadn’t seen it as a bad choice. The slime journal had mentioned Janice fiercely advocated the merits of slimes—perhaps a bit too fiercely in retrospect. Ferris was not sure what to make of the man’s ferocity.

“Come, I’ll get you a tour of the place. That will set you straight.” Foreman Carter led Ferris up to a large pit in the ground. As they approached, he could tell this was the source of the smell.

“The first thing you need to know about slimes, is they have rocks for brains,” the man said with practiced ease. He had probably given this lecture before. “They roll around until they find food, eat until they can’t eat any more, expel it all out, and then start all over again. You can train them by rewarding behavior with food, but they forget it all in less than a day.”

“They don’t stop eating when they are full?” Ferris asked. Monsters needed food just like humans, but it took time to digest.

“Nope,” Foreman Carter shrugged. “A slime can live for a couple weeks with nothing to eat, but if there is food it will keep eating all day long. They only stop to sleep, and then right back at it.”

“If you can train them, they must be smart enough to learn, right?”

“Train… is a generous word.” The man scowled. “You can get them to do some stuff, but the damn things don’t respond to commands. Worst of all, a day later the slime will forget everything. I used to work with dogs, now there is a beast you can train. You should find yourself a nice dog familiar, or maybe a wolf.”

They had reached the hole, and Ferris looked down. It was a huge pit, with hundreds of slimes flopping around inside.

It was Ferris’ first time seeing a slime for real, and he was struck with a sense of wonder. They were small green blobs about the size of his head. The slimes were climbing all over each other, covering the floor like a flowing carpet.

A fence rimmed the entire length of the edge of the slime pit, while a ramp rose up in the middle. The narrow ramp led up to a cage suspended in the air. A stream of slimes slowly climbed up the ramp, while two ranchers with long sticks knocked some off.

“Now look here,” Foreman Carter explained. “We feed the slimes down in the pit.” He crumpled up the letter from Elder Harper and tossed it in. Ferris cringed at the wasted paper. “They would eat anything, but we only use organic material. All of the sewers in the clan route to right here. Other than that, the growers and the butchers send us any remains they don’t need. The slimes would eat metals if we let them, but that hurts our product.”

Ferris looked around. There were several people standing around the pit with long poles, but none of them were cultivators. Taking another look at Foreman Carter, the man was also a mundane.

“Is it safe?” Ferris asked. “There must be hundreds of slimes in there. They are monsters after all.”

“No, it’s definitely not safe. We have very strict procedures for controlling the slimes.” The foreman got serious. “Handled right, a slime is no more dangerous than a fire or a sharp knife. There are accidents from time to time, but no one has died since I took over.”

“What are they doing?” Ferris asked, pointing at the ramp leading up to the cage. It had a constant line of fat slimes rolling up it, but a closed metal gate prevented the slimes from getting inside the metal cage.

Every so often, a ranch worker with a pole would knock a slime back down into the pit.

“We only allow a full slime to climb the ramp,” Foreman Carter said. “This trains them to fill up before climbing the ramp, but every day they forget, so we have to knock them back down.”

Ferris watched, and the ones being pushed off the ramp were indeed smaller than the others. “Why do the slimes climb the ramp?”

“Because that is where we keep the juiciest food.” Foreman Carter smiled, gesturing to the metal cage at the top of the ramp. Ferris could see something in the cage, but it was hard to see details. “They’ll eat anything, but they love magical food most of all. We have a steady supply of special berries the farmers grow for us. They barely have a smidge of magical residue, but the slimes still go crazy for them.”

“Step one, let a couple fat slimes into the cage.” As Foreman Carter spoke, a rancher pulled a lever that opened the gate to the cage. The slimes at the top of the ramp rolled in, eager for the prize inside.

“Step two, lock them in.” With the same lever the rancher closed the gate, trapping several of the slimes inside.

“This is where it gets tricky. A slime can fit through any hole big enough for the core.” He held up his fingers in the shape of a circle, a little smaller than an egg. “It’s the only solid part of their body. The rest is just gelatin.”

A couple ranchers started pulling on ropes, and the cage lifted away from the ramp.

“The metal grate of that cage is too small for the core,” the man said as they watched. The trapped slimes were lifted with the cage, gelatinous bodies hanging down beneath, unable to fall through. One enthusiastic slime hung on to the cage from the outside, but a rancher with a pole knocked it back down to the pit.

“Step three, take the slimes to the collection pit.” Foreman Carter gestured to another hole—containing no slimes—next to the slime pit. It had a pool of green liquid at the bottom.

The ranchers grabbed another set of ropes and swung the cage over to the second pit.

“Step four, collection.” A rancher hefted a pole with a wide blade on the end and began cutting away the bottom of the slimes. The monsters could heal most cutting wounds instantly, but the broad width of the blade kept them from reforming before portions were completely severed. The separated pieces of slime fell into the green pool.

“What is it for?” Ferris eyed the pool of… slime. Unlike the bouncing, rolling madness of the slime pit, this pool was still—barely rippling as green chunks fell into it.

“Fertilizer.” Foreman Carter seemed a bit surprised Ferris didn’t know. “The waste we feed the slimes would eventually decompose, but the natural process would take months. A slime can give better quality fertilizer in a day than a compost heap in a year.”

Ferris looked at the large, green pool. It seemed to be a lot of fertilizer.

“The farms need an endless supply of the stuff.” Foreman Carter stroked his mustache as he explained. “They don’t have much space with the Wild pressing in on us and all, but they can grow a harvest almost instantly with their magic.”

Ferris knew some of this from the plant aspected core primer, but apparently there was more to it. He wasn’t surprised it was complex to grow enough food for an entire bastion, plus trading the excess to places like Bluereef.

“Farm in the same ground too much, and your crops gets weaker and weaker until they won’t grow at all. Frankly, we can’t keep up with the farm’s output. They take every drop we can make to rejuvenate the land.”

The ranchers finished chopping off the bottom of the slimes, then swung the cage back over the slime pit and dumped the caged slimes.

“Does it hurt the slimes, losing so much of their bodies?” Ferris had read that slimes would survive as long as their core wasn’t hurt, but it didn’t look pleasant.

“Far as we can tell, they’re fine. They take a while to recover, but as long as we don’t damage the core they always return to normal after a few hours. Motivates them to eat more than anything.” The man shrugged.

Ferris watched the slimes that had fallen out of the cage. They were a lot smaller, and they quickly got lost in the swarming mass.

“I heard there are a lot of variations of slimes, in the rifts,” Ferris said, considering the Adaptation ability. “Do the slimes here develop ways to get out?”

“Once a month, maybe,” Foreman Carter said, with a shudder, “but that’s when they get you. We cull any at the first sign. Mostly, these slimes stay the same.”

That confused Ferris, as even monsters without some special ability to adapt would grow stronger just sitting around eating all day. The ranchers chopped the slimes up, but that shouldn’t matter. It was the monster’s spirit that got stronger over time, after all.

“So,” the man said, breaking Ferris out of his thoughts. “You can see it now, right? Slimes aren’t worth it. You can find a much better bond.”

Ferris looked down into the pit of slimes, but all he could think about was his leg. Looking at the man, Ferris could only shake his head and smile. “Nothing has changed my mind.”

“Well, it’s your life.” The man deflated a bit, but quickly recovered. “Now I can’t just let you take off with a monster, even a bonded familiar. Especially one as slippery as a slime.”

Foreman Carter placed a hand on his shoulder.

“First, you must prove to me that you can handle it.”
