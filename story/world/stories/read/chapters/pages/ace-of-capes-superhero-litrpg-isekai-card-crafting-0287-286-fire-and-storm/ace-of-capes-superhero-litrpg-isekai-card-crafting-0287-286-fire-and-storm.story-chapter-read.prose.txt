After Lexie gave Pvilycht his instructions on infiltrating the Emperor's lab, she crafted a card he could use to disguise himself for the task at hand. He would be invisible for the most part, but occasionally, he would need the appearance of a human to interact with other humans. Lexie also offered to give him lessons on how to pass as a human, but he shook his head.

"I know how humans talk now," he said. "I've been around them enough."

Lexie raised an eyebrow. She wanted to point out that he hadn't spent much time interacting with humans other than Lexie and the esteemed Dr. Bossetti, but she held back. She didn't have time to teach him anyway, and the Emperor's soul-testing lab techs were probably weird enough people that Pvilycht would fit right in.

Once they were done, she activated the creature card, sending his essence back to Earth.

Following that, it was time to craft. Lexie had a pretty complicated deck ahead of her. Finding cards that combatted the Lightlarks while still using Eldritch mana would be difficult. She almost started making the mana and physical boosting cards, as they'd discussed, but then she paused.

She looked at the problem from the top down.

The Lightlarks' main power source was light, both light that they generated and light in the atmosphere that they utilized.

The first thing she needed to do was to cut off their access to external light sources. Which meant removing every trace of sunlight or even so much as a fluorescent bulb from the environment before the fights.

She couldn't use VOID because that was vulnerable to Lightlark magic, and even a thick veil of shadow could be pierced by sunlight.

So, instead of Eldritch darkness, she focused on a more natural phenomenon, one that wouldn't easily fall to the Lightlark's charm.

A shadow veil or even a VOID dome placed over them could eventually be broken through to access their light again.

But if there was simply no light to be had anyway, if a storm broke out with clouds heavy enough and a mist thick enough to obscure and capture stray ligthrays…then there would be nothing they could do about it.

Except make the light stronger.

No, a storm wouldn't hold them down for that long. They would break through eventually. Lexie needed to think bigger, better.

It came to her in bits, although the sheer magnitude of the idea brought some apprehension.

There was only one way to take out natural light completely. It was to take away the sun.

In essence, Lexie had to trigger a full solar eclipse.

Figuring out how to do that was the first gargantuan task of the afternoon. She and Pvilycht (the real one that remained) talked about it, though he would often get distracted by whatever his copy was doing on Earth. The impression of Pvilycht that was left in the dungeon was faint, and sometimes she would feel him draw from her when he used his magic there. It was a good thing she didn’t need him to actually use his magic in the dungeon, because it would be hard for him to split focus.

They moved on to discuss the effects of a short-term eclipse on Earth. Lexie didn’t have access to the system here, but she had Earth Pvilycht stop by a library and instructed him on how to search through astronomy books to find out.

They found that the long-term effects were evident: tides shifting, crop deaths, and a possible famine. Lexie didn’t want any of that to happen. The rest of the world shouldn't suffer just because she was battling a few idiots.

But it should be fine to trigger the eclipse for a few minutes. There would be no long-term effects then, and that should give her plenty of time to capture and eliminate the Lightlark.

No, not eliminate. Merely subdue, for Xena's sake. And so Stella and the rest of the association stopped seeing her as a monster.

God, it was irritating having to consider the well-being of creatures that were trying to kill her.

Lexie assumed the next time Leo Lightlark faced her, he would have backup. So her eclipse card would be able to weaken them all at once, giving her temporary control of the battlefield.

Then, to get rid of their light within, she could activate a VOID vortex to draw in the Lightlark's magic and remove all the remaining light in the area.

This would be another fairly complex card. Essentially, it would create a whirling maelstrom that attracted its direct negative, both physically and metaphorically. The VOID couldn't actually eliminate the light, but that wasn't what she was trying to do. She was trying to trap light with its magnetic pull, to keep it busy, stuck battling the endless void, and leaving the Lightarks down only to their physical abilities.

Then, she would pick them off one by one, overwhelming them with her speed and strength.

It sounded like a good plan. In theory.

As they mapped it out, the first question was, did they have material strong enough to hold that level of magic?  It was far beyond the power level of anything Lexie had ever tried before. If her previous cards were ranked on a scale of 1 - 20, then this card was somewhere around level 100. It would have been overwhelming, but she broke it down into three stages.

First, finding the appropriate dungeon. Second, finding out how exactly eclipses worked. Third, finding out how many cards she would need for the eclipse [probably an entire lattice of it] and what arrangement they would need to be in. Fourth, crafting the card.

Fifth, practice? Maybe.

Hopefully, that would only take a few days.

For the appropriate essence, Pvilycht suggested they approach a Level 10 dungeon belonging to the Lord of the Broken Stars, Vaerynth.

"His power deals with cosmic shifts and spatial anomalies, so you should be able to use his essence for both the eclipse and the void card with ease," Pvilycht explained. "Like Yasycht, he exists between space and time. His dungeons are mind-bending, and they do not often land on Earth. So they are notoriously difficult to find."

Fortunately, Lexie didn't have to go out of her way to find it.

She simply had to ask Yasycht for access, and to her surprise, Yasycht was all too happy to agree.

In fact, he was almost suspiciously compliant. He didn't even ask her for a favor in return.

He simply held up the image of the dungeon heart, and when Lexie said, "Do you want me to give you an access point here also?" he responded with "No need."

Which, of course, only raised her concern.

Yasycht transported them to a dungeon heart which looked like a veritable shifting galaxy. There was no ground below them, no walls around them.

Only shattered planets.

They simply hung in space, and it was fairly disconcerting at first, but Lexie told herself to get used to it as she pulled out her crafting equipment.

“Pvilycht, remind me again how a solar eclipse works.” She had an idea of how it worked in Earth 2, but she wanted to make sure Earth 9’s science was similar.

He paused to recover the information in the book he was reading. Solar eclipses occur when the moons shifts between Earth and the suns. In this way, a shadow is cast that completely blocks out light in some areas (known as the umbra) and partially blocks it out in other areas (known as the penumbra)."

Yeah, that was what she thought. “Is there a way to predict or control the Umbra?”

"Probably, although advanced astronomy calculations will be involved."

"How fast can you become a astronomy genius, Pvilycht?”

“Relatively fast.”

“Alright. Get to it then."

While he did that, she decided to kill two birds with one stone and get started on the void card.

She closed her eyes and reached out to the core of the dungeon. It scorched her, imprinting itself on her soul even as she connected with it, hot and blazing like a star.

Oddly enough, she didn't have to do much to calm it down. It didn't talk to her, nor did it struggle when she began to extract its essence. As a matter of fact, it was silent, almost compliant. That was weird. Weren't Level 10 dungeons supposed to be harder to control, more aware, and stronger?

"You said this Lord Vaerynth is missing?"

"No, not missing," Pvilycht replied. "He appears whenever he wants."

"Oh." So he wasn't like Yasycht then. He wasn't restricted from entering this plane, but he stayed away. Why?

As Lexie's card took form, she wondered if the Vaerynth would appear now that he knew she was here.

Or maybe Neqal warned him away. Or maybe he was busy.

She hoped whatever kept him busy would continue.

After drawing out the essence with suspicious ease, she got started on the Void Vortex card. That one was relatively simpler to figure out, which wasn’t saying much because it was still probably going to take her at least five cards to get what she wanted.

First, she crafted the typical pathway for VOID, allowing for different variations, including metaphysical emptiness, sadness, etc. Then she added a card that had a magnetic component, drawing the opposite end of the VOID towards it.

That part was the trickiest. She placed a lot of nodes and sensors in place, because if left unrestricted, the card could lead to dimensional folding, which could cause…well, she wasn’t sure, but probably a lot of bad things.

Left uncontrolled, reality might fold into itself until nothing was left. It was a terrifying power to have, and she had to carefully limit it; otherwise, she might just bite off more than she could chew.

At this level of card crafting, every path had to be meticulous, carefully tested to make sure that it would do what it was set to do.

So Lexie was very careful, adding stipulations to corral the power of the card even if it took her longer to do so.

Time passed, but it was impossible to know while hanging in the cosmos. Lexie got in the zone, calculating and crafting with occasional murmured conversations with Pvilycht. Pvilcyht jerked at a point, and she glanced over at him, but his eyes were trained forward. He didn’t speak for a long time, but then he finally relaxed.

“Everything okay?” Lexie asked.

“Yes. It was just the librarian making sure that I had everything I needed,” he cleared his throat. “She’s very kind.”

He seemed surprised, and Lexie bit her tongue to keep from telling him that most people were kinder to humans than they were to Eldritch. The librarian probably wouldn't be as nice if he hadn't been in his human disguise.

It kind of pissed her off that this was the first bit of kindness he'd probably received from a stray human in his entire Eldritch existence.

“That’s good,” she said instead and went back to work.

She finally finished crafting the two initial cards, linking them. Then she needed a separate card that would continuously grow the vortex until she cut it off.

As she worked on it, she felt a pickle at her spine.

She glanced around, but nothing was approaching her. Not that she expected anything to. This was meant to be the purgatory, but it was a level 10 dungeon, which meant that strange things could happen. Level 10’s were known for acting atypically sometimes. Delvers didn’t even raid Level 10’s anymore because of how dangerous they were. The likelihood that a delver wouldn't come back was practically a hundred percent, so the Fae fitted it such that those dungeons rarely appeared on Earth at all.

In any case, Lexie had to be careful. She was pretty sure she was rested enough to take whatever was lurking in the beyond, but she couldn’t let herself get too confident.

As she got done with the void vortex card, Pvilycht finally spoke up again, “I think I understand the eclipse mechanism now.”

“You do?”

He nodded. “I’m currently running calculations to be able to decisively predict the umbra and the penumbra before activation. It's not as complicated as I thought it would be; however, the sheer scale...I doubt the current card materials will do. We might need something stronger to contain it.”

“What’s stronger than Fae crystals?”

“Dragon crystals,” he said.  “Those are indestructible. We must get them.”

“Dragons?" she gaped. "Where the heck are we supposed to find dragons?"

“They're in a lot of places. One of them is in a dragon cave I visited once.” Pvilycht transferred the image to her mind.

"That’s Elfheim, a Kingdom on the planet of the Elves. The dragons reside on one of their most remote mountains. Mostly no one goes there, because while the dragon sleeps for centuries at a time, it's very angry when it's awoken."

"Is it that deadly?"

"Yes. They are also immortal, so it is almost impossible to kill them. The saving grace of the Elves was the dragon's long sleep cycle and the fact that dragons tend not to leave their cave unless they're bothered."

"What do they eat?"

He shrugged. "I don't think they do. Do you want me to find out?"

“No need,” Lexie said. “You focus on your mission and find out as much as you can about this emperor’s shenanigans. Oh, also, can you stop by and let my dad know that I’m okay? He’s probably worried at this point.”

His lips tightened. “Fine, but dragons are ancient and immortal menaces. If you wake one up, it can be very dangerous for you.”

Lexie shrugged noncommittally. "I could use the chaos anyway." She had her void vortex card, which she was pretty sure she could use to trap and swallow fire, too.

She disappeared and appeared at her destination, the jagged entrance of a yawning cave at a frosty mountain peak.

Lexie entered the echoey space, thinking about exactly where these crystals were and how to extract them without the dragon looking at her.

One problem, though.

The dragon was already awake.

She couldn't see its full body as it was coiled around the walls.

But she did see two big yellow eyes beaming in the dark, looking right at her.

Oh. Shit.

“Hello,” Lexie said. “I’m Lexie Sparrowfoot.”

The creature snorted. A final warning.

"I want to leave, trust me, but...I don’t suppose you’ll let me have your lovely crystals, without a fight, will you?”

In response, it opened its mouth and breathed fire.
