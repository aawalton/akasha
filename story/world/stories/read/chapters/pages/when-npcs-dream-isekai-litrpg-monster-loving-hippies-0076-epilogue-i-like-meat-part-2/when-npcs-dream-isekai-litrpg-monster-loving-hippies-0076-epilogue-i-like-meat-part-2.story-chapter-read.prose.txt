We scrambled into a thicket of ferns, pressing our bodies flat against the damp earth. Through the gaps in the leaves, we watched them.

There were three of them. They were tall, covered in deep red scales, with thick tails swaying behind them like pendulums. They wore clothes and they were talking.

These were the Red Lizard players.

I didn’t know what their deal was, but I knew it was big—bigger than the monster. But the most important thing right now was to stay hidden. These players could be dangerous, or enemies, or... both.

They stopped near the spot where I had killed the creepy-tongue dear creature.

“Maybe it was some of the monsters,” said one. “Some of them make weird shouting noises.”

“But I’m sure I heard words, too,” another said. “Are you sure none of the monsters can talk?”

“Not in this batch,” the first said, checking a tablet-like magic device. “Tomorrow a sentient monster should arrive, but not today. Well, let’s head back and just put it in the report.”

They looked around a bit more, then turned and left.

I finally remembered to breathe. That was very, very weird.

“Let’s hurry back,” I whispered. “That’s really something the grownups should know about.”

They all nodded in agreement, and we hurried home.

When we got back to Stormcrow Village, it was late afternoon. The village was bustling with activity, same as always. We hadn’t been gone that long, but it felt like a lifetime.

I spotted Father sitting on our porch with Yichen again. They were hunched over a low table, scribbling furiously on parchments.

He glanced up from the parchment at us. “Hi kids,” he said. “Had fun at... ah... not across the river?”

“Father, I’m sorry,” I said seriously. “We actually went to look for monsters.”

“I see,” he said, his eyes going back to the parchment. “That’s nice. Did you find any?”

“Yes,” I said. “New players, too.”

“That’s nice, Miriam,” he murmured. “We will do the reading lesson later today.”

Then Yichen looked at us—I mean, really looked at us, and, snatched the parchment from Father’s hands. “Hey! Look at them!”

Father finally looked up properly.

“God! What happened to you?” He leaped off the porch, rushing over to kneel before me. “Are you all okay?”

“Yes, Father,” I said. “We are fine.”

Father’s eyes darted over my clothes. “Miriam… is that blood? Is this…”

“It’s the monster’s blood,” I explained. “I killed one, but it vanished, so I couldn’t bring the meat.”

“The monsters almost killed us!” Rollito added, waving his arms. “But Miriam saved us!”

Father brushed my hair back and looked me in the eyes. “Are you sure you didn't get hurt?”

“I fell when the monster threw me,” I admitted, lifting my shirt to show the bruising on my ribs. “But it’s just a scratch. It doesn’t really hurt.”

“I see,” Father said. He crouched down to my level, kissed my forehead, and gave me a tight hug. “I’m so glad you’re okay. I’m proud of you.”

When we came to tell him, I was afraid he wouldn’t believe me because I lied all the time. But Father believed me. He was proud. I felt my chest swell with pride, too.

I really loved my Father.

“She said she saw players too,” Yichen reminded him, looking at me. “How did they look?”

“Red Lizard Players,” I said.

“I see,” said Yichen. “We need to rally the others. Call the Order and the other bands.”

In a short time, the whole village had stopped working. People dropped their tools and gathered around. Even people from the other nearby villages came, Felix included. My friends' parents came too, but nobody looked angry, not even Juliette’s mother.

They all stood there looking at me like they were waiting for something.

Then Father leaned down to me. “Could you show us the way?”

Oh... they were all waiting for me. Only I could show them where the monsters were. That was... very weird.

“After me!” I called out, and started heading toward the thick forest clearing. I was tired, but I understood that this was important. All the others wanted meat, too. With this large group of strong players, we could easily locate the main herd, capture them, and have Mother kill them.

If that happened, we could all have a huge feast of monster meat this evening!

And... maybe they wanted to check what the deal with the new players was, too.

I walked through the forest and the grownups followed. I didn’t need to slow down for them; Father, Mother, and the adults were capable, moving through the undergrowth with ease.

When we were close to the small clearing, someone from the Order stopped us.

He held a strange device—a bowl-shaped dish attached to a handle that he held to his ear. He pointed it in one direction.

“There is a large clearing not far from here,” he said, pointing in a different direction than where I was heading. “I can hear them. Something big is going on over there.”

He led the way, and we all followed.

The magical device gave him super hearing. That was very useful, and I have to get one for myself somehow.

We walked some more, and when I stepped out of the tree line with Father and Mother, I saw something very, very weird.

There were tons of people here. Red Lizard Players, but also other weird centipede-like people, people that looked like a pile of rocks, I even saw some fluff-ball agents and a few bird-folk.

And they were all very busy building stuff. Building... houses?

There were three huge magic portal in the air. In one of them, players went in and out, carrying objects and construction materials. Wood beams, plates, tables, roof parts... there was no end to them.

In another portal, a few lizard people with large sticks were herding monsters out. It was the same ugly deer monsters that attacked me; they chased them right into the forest.

I knew a lot of things. I was smart, I think. But right now, I had no idea what was going on. I tugged at Father’s shirt and looked up at him, silently asking for an explanation.

He put a hand on my shoulder. “These are System Workers,” he explained. “They are building a new NPC town. I think they are readying this continent for new players. The agents here are part of the workforce, not our enemies.”

Oh... so it will have everything we had in the old continent. Monsters, NPCs, towns, and players. This was really exciting. And fun to watch, too.

Then the workers started to notice us, pointing and talking among themselves.

Then, one specific red lizard man—wearing a stiff uniform with many golden buttons and a pointy hat—adjusted his clothes and walked our way.

“Greetings,” the lizard man said to us in a calm voice. “My name is Zaak. I am Regional Management and in charge of this Site Development. You are from the Athersu continent, correct?”

“That’s right,” Felix answered.

“You are not supposed to be here,” said Zaak, plain and simple.

“Yes, we are not supposed to be here.” Felix’s voice was cold now. “We were forced to retreat from our home because it was invaded by strong players from a different continent. Flying fluff-ball players and bamboo players. These players started long before us and had an unfair advantage. Your safeguard for separated players—the desert—proved utterly useless, and as a result, many of us died. Your System failed us. Our only option was to migrate... You should take responsibility, remove the invasive players from Athersu, and supply us a means of return.”

The lizard guy looked at Felix for a long moment. Then he answered in a flat voice. “I acknowledge your concerns. Allow me to consult with my manager regarding this irregularity.”

He took out a phone tablet, moved a few steps back, and traced a magic circle that was similar to the air pressure circle my father uses. Suddenly, we couldn’t hear what he said.

Oh... it was like a quiet bubble. Cool.

He spoke for like five minutes, then finished, and stop the quiet bubble magic.

We were all quiet. If I understood correctly what Felix said, he just asked the system guy to remove our enemies and take us back home. Could it be that easy? Could that silly lizard just agree, and all our troubles would be over?

“I have informed my manager about... your unique situation,” he started, “and about your grievances. Unfortunately, it is outside the scope of our operational guidelines to assist with the removal of invasive player species from a continent. However, we have been authorized to allow you to reside on this continent, on the condition that you do not interfere with the designated players chosen to populate this region.”

I didn’t understand a lot of the big words, but I got that he said "no," and that he was a huge jerk.

“Save your pity for the starving and dying,” Mother answered angrily. “We have enough strength to outlast the winter, and the spirit to guide our blades to take back what’s ours.”

Zaak the Lizard looked at Mother, and suddenly, for the first time, he looked surprised.

He pulled a ring from his pocket, pointed it at Mother, and looked at it closely as it emitted a weird sound and colors.

Then he sighed. “You have a Type-4 NPC among you. That might be another administrative issue.”

I felt Father’s hand clench on my shoulder, tense. He stepped forward until he was right in Zaak's face, only a few centimeters away.

Father spoke in a cold, scary voice I rarely heard him use. “If you do something to Lori—anything at all—I will burn your System to the ground. Somehow. From here, or from Hell.”

Father was brave. I admired him at that moment.

But Zaak didn’t phase, he only sighed again. “I need to consult with my manager again. Apologies.”

He walked a few steps back, put up the quiet bubble, and talked with his boss again.

When he was done, he returned to us and annoyingly explained things. “A Type-4 NPC... without getting into technical details... is a powerful and highly regulated entity. We acknowledge that this is a result of a system anomaly. But as this continent is not the native environment for the Type-4 NPC...”

“Her name is Lori!” Father cut him off, angry.

“Apologies,” he continued. “As I was saying, this is not Lori’s native continent, so it is a matter of a grey area within our guidelines. We can let her stay here, but she will be under special System Monitoring and needs to obey strict limitations. in the most basic sense, she is forbidden to harm local players. Lethal force is prohibited. If she violates this, it will result in an Audit.”

He stopped talking and sighed again, lowering his voice. “On a personal note, a System Audit is a catastrophic event you wish to avoid at any cost. So take it to heart.”

He paused for a moment, brought his tail to his hand, and slightly pulled on it a few times. That was weird.

“Oh, and one last matter,” Zaak said. “Do not talk with... the workforce over here.” He gestured behind him. “They are not allowed to speak to you.”

“You mean the OSAD workers?” Hyun-woo added.

Zaak’s lips twitched slightly; it might have been a small lizard smile. “My guidelines are very limiting regarding providing additional information.”

“You are a jerk,” said Samar. “You know that, right?”

Zaak looked at Samar and smiled. “I am a Construct,” he said. “Just like you. And I am only trying to do my job and minimize individual suffering while doing it.”

“How much time,” asked Naked Nula Juliette, “until you finish the work?”

“About two months until we finish all the towns,” said Zaak. “But it could be years until the system stabilizes and the players start appearing. However, some Beta Players might appear much earlier. Please be kind to them, as well as the new NPCs.”

“We are always kind,” said Juliette.

“Do you have any more questions?” asked Zaak. “As I said, I am limited in the information I can disclose, but I will endeavor to assist where protocol allows.”

“What if the Bamboo players aren't satisfied with only Athersu?” asked Father. “What if they use controlled players in Athersu to build more airships and try to take control of more continents? What if they come here? Will you still not intervene?”

“This depends on executive decisions from higher-ups,” Zaak said. “But mostly, we do intervene in such matters. I do not have information regarding this specific circumstance as it is not my administrative jurisdiction.”

“That’s not an answer,” Father muttered under his breath.

Then a girl from the Order I didn’t recognize asked, “Will we ever be able to go back to Earth? Our old world?”

“Your old world does not exist,” Zaak said. “I’m sorry.”

No one had any questions for a moment, so I asked, “Will you bring tasty monsters here?”

Zaak looked at me for the first time and seemed baffled for a moment. Then he smiled again. “The tastiest,” he said.

With that, it seemed no one had any more questions.

“Well,” Zaak said, straightening his hat. “The new kingdom here will be called Evaim, and this small town is called Elelelmahm. You can come and watch the construction from here as you please. After we finish and the workforce leaves, you may visit it at your convenience and enjoyment.” Zaak nodded and pulled his tail again. “I do hope you fare well.”

Then he went back to his workers.

“Well,” said Samar, “he was exactly like I imagined system guys to be. Boring jerks.”

“This is...” Hyun-woo murmured, “fascinating.”

“The Bird-folk players are proof,” said Felix quietly. “They are dead players. Workers from Hell.”

“Are they slaves?” asked Naked Nula Juliette. “Is that what happens in the OSAD?”

“I’m sure that technically they are not,” said Hyun-woo, “as they are supposedly protected by welfare laws. But that is 'supposedly.' In essence, they may still be slaves.”

“Should we talk to the workers anyway?” asked one of the Order’s guys.

“No,” said Felix. “For now, we listen to Zaak.”

He nodded.

I didn’t get why they called the working people slaves. They were building houses and stuff all day—that was exactly what our bands did all day, too!

No one said anything else, so we just stood there for long hours to watch.

It was mesmerizing. I thought we built fast, but this was something else. They had all the building parts ready from the portal and only needed to put them in place.

And the monster portal... I couldn’t avert my eyes from it. More and more of the creepy tongue-deer came out—hundreds of them. Then they stopped coming, and after some time, another type of monster came out. This time it was small scaly balls. They didn’t even look alive, but when left alone, they opened up slowly and turned out to be long snake-like creatures with hundreds of legs.

These looked tastier than the creepy tongue-deer.

“Let’s go, it’s getting late,” Father said to me. I didn’t notice, but most of us had already left. “We can come back some other day and watch the progress.”

“But they might let out more types of monsters,” I said. “I will miss them.”

“You will see them,” Father said, patting my head. “When you go hunting in the forest, it might be more fun for you not to know what to expect. Since you are a hunter.”

That was true. It would be more interesting.

“Can we come tomorrow too?” I asked.

“It’s not like you’ll listen if I say no,” Father said and smiled. “But if you wait for me, I would be glad to come and join you.”

We went home and I couldn’t stop thinking about all the things we saw.

And it wasn’t just me; every person I saw was excited about the New Player’s Kingdom. I heard a lot of conversations going on: thinking about expeditions to locate all the nearby towns, debating if the magic shops would offer the same devices and diagram books, and wondering which jobs we should take in the town to earn local money.

What I wanted to know, of course, was if they would sell tasty meat at the local restaurants and taverns.

We went back the next day and saw new buildings erected that weren’t there yesterday, and roads, too.

We went there every day to watch. Each day we saw more and more buildings, and the clearing quickly turned into a real town. It was similar to Kamatoo in size, but all the buildings looked funny. There were thin, tall towers with round, bowl-shaped roofs.

On the ninth day, the town looked complete. The construction material portal and the monster portal were closed, and all the workers and their equipment started to exit through the third portal.

On the 10th day, we could finally visit.

Every single one of us came—from Stormcrow, the Order of the Phoenix, and all the smaller bands. We all arrived at the clearing to see the empty town. No portals, no workers. It had been so noisy before, but now it was eerie. Absolute silence. No machinery, no shouts. Just the wind.

“Can I go, Father?” I asked, as the grownups were slow and taking their time.

Father nodded, and I immediately ran down to the new town. I saw Juliette and Rollito running after me, and even Advik, struggling to match our speed.

We got to the town and looked around with wide eyes. It was so pretty, and weird.

And... it wasn’t like our village at all. This was like our town back home, Kamatoo.

The buildings here looked different—taller, with weird roofs, yes—but they were real buildings. They were made of cut stone blocks and smooth wood, with glass in the windows and cobblestones on the street, not the straw, mud, and stick houses of our village.

We hadn't actually seen a real town with real buildings in over two years. It filled me with longing.

“Like old home...” murmured Rollito. “I remember.”

“Pretty,” said Juliette, his voice stunned and eyes wide. “Too pretty! Are these really houses?”

Only me and Rollito remembered our old home. Advik was too small and too much Naked Nula to remember, and Juliette hatched on this continent and never saw anything else.

So to them, seeing buildings with wood carvings and slate roofs... was something straight out of a dream.

Then—CREAAAK!

A loud, thunderous crack split the air.

I jumped, scared, my hand already clutching my knife.

But as I looked, I saw a portal appearing not far from me, in what was probably the town square.

“What now?” I asked.

Father and Mother arrived at my side. Father held my hand. “Now we will finally see them,” he said. “The new NPCs.”

They came rolling out of the portal like colorful balls. Upon closer inspection, I saw the colors were actually clothes. As they slowed down, the balls uncurled and stood up.

They were hairy, with hard shells on their backs and large, fluffy ears.

“Fox-monkey-armadillo people...” murmured Father. “We’ll probably just call them Armadillo-folks.”

“They’re cute,” I said. “I like them.”

“Even if they look different,” explained Father, “they will probably act similar to the NPCs we had back home.”

That was nice, too. They wouldn't replace Grandpa, of course, but I like NPCs. It was another thing that would make me feel a lot more at home.

As each NPC stood, they started walking with purpose, as if they knew exactly where they were going. Some went into shops, others into houses.

“Greetings, adventurer,” called one of them that happened to go our way. “You’ll find the inn just up the road if you’re looking for a warm bed.”

“I appreciate the word,” replied Mother, “but the wind hasn't tired me yet.”

The NPC then continued on its way.

More and more came, dozens of them. Each looked slightly different and wore different colorful fabrics.

Suddenly Mother jumped, focusing. She started walking decisively toward one of the NPCs that had just opened up. We followed.

“Greetings, fellow hunter,” Mother said. “I’m Lori, leader of the Band of the Storm Crow. I hope your quiver is full and your spirit is patient.”

“Greetings, leader of the Storm Crows,” the hunter answered. “The hunt has been steady; the wind was at my back this morning. If your band finds their spirits weary or their wounds stinging, follow the path to my cabin just over the rise. I’ve got herbs, roots, and poultices for sale.”

“I saw you come through that rift.” Lori pointed at the portal. “It’s not every day you see a hunter step out of thin air. Where did you come from?”

“My spirit has never wandered far from these woods,” he answered. “I was born in this town, and I’ve lived within its walls every day of my life.”

The hunter then continued on his way, but Mother looked suddenly taken aback. She looked at her hands, confused and worried.

I got it. She was like that NPC. If she had memories of growing up in Kamatoo, they were fake memories.

Father caught and held Mother’s hands. “All the memories you made for the last seven or eight years are very, very real,” he said, and kissed her on the cheek. “All the memories with us too.”

I hugged her, too. Her memories with me were definitely not fake!

“Us Players may also have fake memories,” Father continued. “I don’t really care about it. This is our life now, and this is what matters.”

Mother looked like she calmed down.

“Construct,” she said after a while. This was the word the system manager guy used.

“Yes,” Father said. “Whatever it means, we all are.”

“Where should we go now?” I asked.

“I have an idea,” Father said.

He walked and we followed until we got to a weapon shop.

Father pulled out a knife—good quality, if a bit old—and sold it for a pouch full of weird orange  rectangles coins, local money.

Then we walked to what I was sure was a restaurant.Father gave all the money he got to the Armadillo NPC chef. He told him—and I would never forget it—to give us as much meat as possible.

I love my Father so much.

We sat and waited for the food.

My mouth drooled so much it started dripping onto the table. I waited patiently.

Real meat. I was going to get real meat, finally! I truly almost couldn’t believe it.

Now we will have all the things we had back home. Wait. Does that mean...

“Can we build airships now?” I asked. “Can we return back home?”

“We can,” said Father. “But if we did so, the Bamboo and Agents would just beat us again.”

“Oh... right,” I said. “So what will we do?”

“We build stronger-than-magic machines,” Mother normal-talked. “Then we go back.”

“What are stronger-than-magic machines?” I asked.

“High explosives. Machine guns,” she answered.

Father nodded.

Ah, I see. We build those things, we go back, and we win. That sounded like a good plan.

The chef returned. He placed a huge plate on the table—a mountain of juicy, steaming steaks, dripping with fat and gravy. The smell hit my nostrils like a physical blow. I almost fainted from the sheer glory of it.

But I didn’t faint. That would be a waste of eating time.

I dug in, tearing through the meat pile savagely, like the warrior goblin I was. It was heavenly. It was blissful. Tears of happiness streamed down my face, but I didn’t stop chewing.

I was eating meat. And I was extremely happy.

End of Book 1
