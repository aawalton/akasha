As per usual, the true indication of Terra being busy was the fact that there wasn’t any sign of Theodore waiting around at the town’s entrance with one of his ridiculous stunts when Vin and Alka returned the following morning. With only two days left before the town’s first real holiday, everyone was running around at two-hundred-percent, trying to perfect that final skill or unlock that new ability to show off to everyone else. The air felt electric, and Vin could only smile while he walked through town, barely even getting any eyes tossed his way as everyone focused on their own thing.

Alka had already slipped away upon leaving the forest dungeon, claiming she had a lot she needed to check up on regarding her Slayers. She refused to go into detail about precisely what they were going to be doing, but she’d dropped hints that the Slayers as a whole had some sort of surprise planned for the festival where they were planning to show off like everyone else. Seeing as it was Alka working on this surprise of all people, Vin was admittedly just a tad worried she might be planning to drag an elite monster into town and have her Slayers dispatch it in front of everyone. But when he tried to get her to spill her plans, she refused to elaborate, just promising him everything would be taken care of.

“Vin!” a voice called out. Vin turned to find Ronald rushing over to him, the large, portly Magical Baker smiling as he held out a tray containing what looked like simple sugar cookies. “Just the man I wanted to see! Can you try one of these? Tell me what you think!”

“Of course,” Vin replied. Picking up one of the cookies, he paused when he sensed faint traces of magic radiating from the dessert. “Uh, Ronald. What exactly does this one do?”

“Try it and see for yourself!” Ronald beamed. “I was trying to crack a new recipe before the festival, and I think I managed to pull it off! Integrating magic into my baking isn’t easy, but it’s nothing a little bit of hard work can’t fix!”

With a shrug, Vin took a bite out of the cookie, his eyes going wide when the once-firm treat seemed to dissolve in his mouth like it was made of cotton candy. A sweet, pleasant sensation rolled over him starting from his tongue, and he shuddered as he stared at the treat in his hands.

“…What did that do?”

“You’ll see,” Ronald grinned, now pulling out what looked like a piece of lettuce of all things from a bag on his pack and handing it over. “Here, try this.”

Vin was thoroughly intrigued at this point, and he bit into the plain lettuce without hesitation. Yet rather than get a mouthful of dry, bland vegetable, his eyes shot open when his tongue told him he’d bitten into that delicious sugar cookie a second time. He quickly took a second, and then a third bite from the magic-free lettuce leaf, unable to connect the delicious taste with what he was eating.

“Well?” Ronald asked, standing on the balls of his feet while he waited for his reaction. “What do you think?”

“Did you create a magical cookie that makes anything a person eats taste delicious?” Vin asked, looking between the lettuce he held in one hand and the cookie in the other.

“Yep!” Ronald said, his grin widening as he let out a deep belly laugh. “So what do you think? There’s a couple of contests going around for the festival, and one of them is for the most impressive culinary creation. I’m hoping to take home the gold!”

“I think you’ve got a real shot,” Vin admitted. Popping the last of the lettuce leaf into his mouth, he let out a sigh as he finished off the delicious vegetable.

“That’s all I needed to hear. Thank you!” Ronald said, spinning around and rushing back toward the crafter’s guild. “You can finish that off!”

Shaking his head, Vin did just that, eating the rest of the magical cookie as he continued through town. He was tempted to try and get whatever spell Ronald had picked up to create such a magical treat, but he had quite a few things on his plate at the moment, and it wasn’t as though Ronald was going anywhere.

Contests, huh? While he walked, Vin took a closer look at all the craziness going on around him. A couple of thousand people hard at work with their chosen classes was no small sight, and he grew curious as to just what these supposed contests would look like. If there was any form of contest, that meant there was going to have to be judges. And if there would be judges…

Vin walked into town hall, waving at Linda when the Scribe smiled at him. There was a new person sitting close to her as well, currently neck deep in paperwork as the two of them worked hard to make their way through all the eager townsfolk trying to get permits approved and ideas signed off on so close to the festival. Despite being so busy, Linda managed to slip away for a moment to walk up to him.

“Glad you managed to make it back in time,” she said, nodding toward her newest assistant. “That’s Gabe, a Scribe who has been helping me recently. We had him power leveling by copying a few of the tomes your people brought over from your world, but Spur decided now was the perfect time for him to make the switch to town hall.”

“Oh yeah, I remember you mentioning him a while back." Watching the poor Scribe start to flounder as he tried to tackle the entire crowd on his own, Vin shook his head. “…I think he might need your help.”

“He does,” Linda chuckled. “I just wanted to let you know that we’ve got a room in the basement already reserved for you. Spur didn’t know what you had in mind for your banner, so you’ve got pretty much whatever materials we had available for you to pick between. He’s a little busy with everything going on, but he wanted me to relay a message.” Linda paused, clearing her throat and trying to make her voice sound deeper and more serious. “…Make sure it doesn’t suck.”

“Yep, that sounds like Spur,” Vin drawled. He shared a quick laugh with Linda before she went behind the counter once more and began helping her new partner.

Vin was quite curious about just what his work set up looked like, but he had something much more important than an art project he needed to check in on. Last night, he’d checked in with Lumel with a quick Dream Walk, only to find the pulmon sitting in a dream-approximation of the Underside. She’d informed him that she’d spoken with her people’s Airwalker and would be back in Terra today, and then asked him to give her some time to organize her thoughts before they talked about it further. Agreeing and canceling his Dream Walk had been one of the hardest things he’d ever done after seeing the pained expression on her face, but he'd honored her wishes.

With that in mind, Vin left the town hall behind him, slipping through the energetic crowd as he made his way over to their apartment. The moment the door shut behind him and the noise of the town was silenced, he let out a sigh of relief.

“Vin?” Lumel called from the common room. Before he could even reply, the Dimensional Mage appeared before him in a flash of purple, giving him a huge smile as she threw her arms around him. “I missed you so much!”

“We saw each other almost every night,” Vin pointed out, smiling as he returned the hug. After sharing a long, tender kiss, he laughed at her pouting face.

“Dream visits don’t count,” she muttered. With a sigh, she slouched down and placed her head on his shoulder. “…I don’t want to make decisions any more. I’m tired.”

“I’m going to go out on a limb and assume this is about your Airwalker meeting?” he asked. When Lumel weakly nodded, he surprised her by scooping her up, chuckling at her startled yelp. Careful not to smack her head into anything, he brought her over to one of the couches and sat down with her in his lap. Once they were situated, he started rubbing her back. “Want to talk about it?”

“Yes… no… I don’t know what I want anymore,” Lumel admitted with another sigh, staring up at the ceiling as if she’d be able to find all the answers up there. “My greatest dream has come true… but for all the wrong reasons. My people want me back. My banishment has been lifted.”

“Wait, are you serious?” Vin asked, his mouth falling open as he stared at the uncertain pulmon in shock. “That’s fantastic news! Lumel, that’s amazing!”

“I know, it should be, right?” Rather than celebrate, the uncertain pulmon bit her lip and kept staring upwards. “The thing is… it’s not for the reasons I wanted. I was banished because the queen hates me. To her, I was just a permanent reminder of her husband’s infidelity. Getting rid of me was something she’d wanted to do ever since I was a little kid, and she leapt at her chance when she discovered me sneaking out of our fragment via the dungeons. Ever since being banished, I’ve dreamed of my father somehow managing to get the queen to see reason. To blame him for his mistakes instead of myself. But from what I gather, that’s not the case at all.”

“What did the Airwalker say?” Vin asked, pulling her closer.

“Essentially, my people have finally come to realize the true extent of what Edregon is,” Lumel explained. “Obviously, seeing as my people are all but trapped down in their city under the water, they didn’t really know about all the fragments and different worlds being brought together. It took a while for the Airwalkers to get up and running again after the Great Reset, and by the time they were even attempting to explore again, I’d already been long since banished. However, before I’d left, I’d gone into extensive detail to my mother about the few fragments I’d managed to venture through up until that point, such as the floating library and other such marvels. When the Airwalkers returned and revealed the truth about Edregon, my mother spoke up, informing everyone of what I’d already accomplished during my travels. I’ve told you before that as much as she hates me, our queen is honestly a phenomenal leader for our people. She realized immediately the benefits of having access to my knowledge, and agreed to revoke my banishment. All this time, the Airwalkers have had standing orders to inform me of this should they happen to run into me.”

“So despite revoking your banishment… you think the queen still hates you,” Vin surmised, his heart going out to the poor pulmon.

“I know the queen still hates me,” Lumel frowned. “You have no idea the petty stuff I had to put up with growing up. I mean seriously, who bullies a young child for being born? It wasn’t my fault my dad couldn’t keep it in his pants!”

“I have a better idea than you think,” Vin muttered, kissing her forehead as his mind cast back to some of the dreams he’d gone through while working his way through Lumel’s dream journey to rescue her. There was the librarian in the palace informing her that the queen had checked out the books she wanted to read, or the dream about everyone being evacuated from the palace other than Lumel, who had been ‘forgotten’ in the panic. While she was technically a princess, Lumel had been forced to put up with a lot of crap as a child. It was a wonder she’d turned out to be the amazing person she was. “I suppose the real question then is what are you planning to do?”

“I don’t know,” Lumel groaned. Scrunching her eyes closed, she pressed her face into his chest and curled up into a ball in his arms. “I’ve been away from my people for months. I want to see my mom and my dad and tell them all about my insane adventures. All about you. I mean, I not only managed to survive my banishment, I’ve thrived. I’m nearing my third prestige, I have my very own lifemate who cares about me, I’ve got friends I can rely on, and I’m more confident in myself than ever before. Yet even with all that, I’m… I’m scared,” she slowly admitted, seeming to deflate. “What if going back there brings back the old me? The Lumel who chose to run away when you guys were attacked in the floating library? The Lumel who shies away from people picking on her? I’ve come so far, and I’m scared going back home will take that away from me.”

“I don’t think you have to worry about that,” Vin said, smiling down at her. “I’ve gotten to witness firsthand just how amazing you’ve become, and I know for a fact you’re only going to keep growing even more impressive as time goes on. Besides, I’ll make sure what you’re scared of doesn’t happen. If you do decide to go home and visit your parents, I’ll be right there beside you when you do.”

“Thank you, Vin." Uncurling herself, Lumel laid a soft hand against his cheek and gave him a passionate kiss. Once she finally pulled back, her cheeks tinted purple and her eyes twinkling, she giggled as she squeezed him tight. “That means the world to me. Whatever I decide to do, I’m glad I’ll have you there next to me to help.”

“Of course,” Vin said, deciding that was as good a segue as any. “Speaking of helping one another… any chance I might be able to get your assistance with something in the meantime? I’ve got a bit of a problem, and I think your Divine Decipherer Capstone you got for hitting level 50 might just be the solution.”
