The following weeks flew by in a blur of activity. Mother would drop me at Piraka’s house every morning. She was really happy about me finding a place in the village. It was a high honor to be trained by the elder.

“The other mothers are so jealous,” she once said with a mischievous grin before dropping me off.

Piraka provided me with an endless supply of liquid mana bags and potions he wanted me to infuse. I was more than happy with this training. This was a new way to apply myself, and most importantly it was structured practice. Instead of just playing around with mana, which was valuable in its own right, infusing potions had a clear goal. I was using mana purposefully and that generated fast levels.

Piraka was very pleased with the murky green tonics I was generating. After a few days of infusing, he sent for Konako to acquire more refined mana.

Of course, I didn’t need the mana bags, but I used half of their contents for infusions. The other half I just grabbed from the air. I wasn’t clear on the economics of the villagers. Most exchanges happened through barter or the village's quota system.

It was some kind of an economical system, where any surplus you generated was basically marked down and could be used to get stuff you needed. The elder and Konako did some calculations and apparently my potions boosted the Fisher and Jungler output a lot. The infused potions were scarce, and a large portion of them went to my father, which benefitted the whole village, because he was one of the best Fishers. Well, he was the best obviously, and I’d hex anyone who said differently— as soon as I learned to hex people that was…

Father had been so over the moon about getting free daily energy potions, that he had thrown me into the air and played and talked with me all night, even though I could tell he was really tired. Of course he was also happy about me getting the apprenticeship. He did his best to hide being bothered about me not becoming a Fisher.

That was enough. I didn’t care about the money or quotas for now. I was two years old for crying out loud. Which naturally meant I was a petty little scamp. Pissing off Marek was almost as delightful as making father happy.

But that didn’t mean I wasn’t enjoying the personal benefits I was getting from my improved status. The word had spread about the talented little boy, who had not only awakened his System at merely two years old, but had some strange magic Skill which had impressed Elder Piraka enough to teach him. I was everyone’s favorite little baby whenever mother and I stopped to talk with the villagers. Konako had also started to treat me with more respect, and I could see the glint of greed in his eyes when he and Piraka talked about the value I was generating. All this was good.

But the most important part was the training. As days passed, I got better and better at infusing the potions, and this paid dividends in my favorite currency — learning and power.

I also started my lessons in magic. FINALLY! This was a pivotal moment and I had been working towards this for so long.

“Will you teach me now?” I asked after finishing the last potion I had been assigned.

Nirama gave me an ugly look. The elder noticed this and patted her head.

“You two are walking a different path,” he said.

“No! Teach me too, master!”

“Did you reach total level fifty to unlock a Skill Slot?

“N-no…”

“Then it will have to wait,” Piraka said and turned to me. “Do you have an open Skill Slot?

“I do, master,” I said. “I wouldn’t have come here without one.”

I had been saving that bad boy for a while. I would soon reach total level fifty myself and acquire another free Slot, so I felt safe to spend one. I was interested in Heigthened Senses, which was a staple for all Junglers and Fishers. My mother even had an evolved form of the Skill called Sixth Sense. It was an annoying Skill, because it seemed to make mother uncanny at detecting all kinds of magical mischief I amused myself with.

While that Skill was interesting, and would increase my Perception, I wanted a spellcasting Skill!

“Good answer. Very well. Let me show you something.”

Elder Piraka picked up his staff, which immediately piqued my interest. My whole journey into magic had begun by witnessing a miracle in my birthbed. I watched the glowing amber of the gnarled staff with mesmerized concentration.

“Most magic requires a tool,” Piraka said.

Entirely untrue, but carry on.

“This amber in my staff is a magic stone. It can provide mana to cast spells. Observe.”

Piraka focused, and started muttering some words and undulating the gnarled staff in a slow circle. I immediately turned on my Mana Sight to see what was happening.

The staff’s amber was gathering the surrounding mana! It was really slow, and certainly couldn’t be used for quick effects. Then again, most of the magic I had seen Piraka perform were healing spells, blessings and a nifty repair spell he used for cleaning stains and fixing frayed rope and stuff. But being able to use ambient mana was very interesting. So there were tools for magic to do that. Would I benefit from one?

“Now the staff is charged,” Piraka said.

“What Skill are you using?”

“I evolved my Mana Manipulation skill to Staffcasting,” he said, then added. “You clearly have the Mana Manipulation Skill as well.”

I flinched, which made him smile.

“Come now, Luka. I was not born yesterday. It would be a good idea if you gave me your full Status, so I could better plan your future.”

“Maybe later,” I said.

The old man sighed, but carried on his explanation. “The next step in casting a spell is using an image. Magic requires a strong imagination and mental clarity. I will have to keep an image of a spell formula in my mind and channel my will into the staff.”

I watched as the mana from the amber flared into a ring of particles, the width of my small fist. This ring formed fast, almost instantly. That must have been part of the amber gemstone’s magic like the elder had said earlier. Within the hovering ring of light, symbols made of mana particles started to form, as the staff’s light dimmed. This was much slower, and apparently a result of Piraka’s Skills. I watched with rapt attention at the small shapes forming within the circle.

Then he pointed the staff at me, and a familiar green and golden faint mist emerged and enveloped me.

[Minor Blessing of the Tribe] acquired.

Description: 5% increase in Skill Experience for all Skills

Duration: 720 days

Elder Piraka smiled at me. “I believe your previous blessing expired. Did you get a System message?”

“I did,” I said and smiled. “Thank you for the blessing! So that was magic?”

“Yes,” Piraka said. “Of course, you couldn’t really see anything about what was happening, but I will show you the images you will need to imagine.”

Of course I saw everything that was happening, but I was eager to learn the details.

Piraka grabbed a booklet from a shelf and to my surprise took me outside to the stairs. He looked around and when he saw nobody around, he sat down with me.

“You didn’t bring the staff,” I said.

“That is true,” Piraka said.

“What is the staff used for? Specifically.”

“Without the staff, I would have to use refined mana,” Piraka said and then gave me a look. “Are you going to ask me to give you refined mana to cast spells? I should hope not, for that would be wasteful.”

Piraka smiled kindly and expectantly at me. I blushed but didn’t say anything.

“We are not stupid, Luka,” Piraka said. “Konako and I have done some calculations. It is clear you are somehow using the ambient mana to infuse the potions.”

“I— is that bad?”

“Luka, my dear boy, I have never heard of anyone being able to do that.”

“Ever?”

“Not anyone in the archipelago at least. The world is large and there are many Skills. Maybe there are powerful sorcerers out there who can do what you can. Did you get an Achievement?”

“Yes,” I said. “For unlocking the System early.”

That wasn’t too far from the truth. Piraka nodded, seemingly accepting my answer.

“That makes sense. I know you are a good boy and your parents are good people. It is well to hide your Skills. Usually people share their builds only with their close friends.”

“I have only told my parents.”

“That is good,” Piraka said. “But remember that people are always watching. Especially if you are exceptional.”

“Is it dangerous to talk about your Skills?”

“Usually not,” the elder said. “People can’t take your Skills from you. But they might want to learn how you got them, and sometimes be coercive about it. But this never happens in our village.”

“I will keep that in mind, elder. Thank you for the guidance.”

“You’re a good boy, Luka,” the elder said. “Now then, let us have a look at this booklet of mine.”

A booklet? Did this old man mean a spellbook?

I couldn’t help myself. I let out an excited squeal. It wasn’t dignified, but I couldn’t care less. Elder Piraka picked me up in his lap and opened the booklet.

In the middle of the page was a circular symbol. It had lines trailing towards a middle, and strange symbols that looked like letters. Everything seemed to be communicating something.

I gaped at the picture. So this was magic! It required structure. It was like a language.

Elder Piraka traced a finger around the outline of the picture. “This is a magical formula. These were given to us by the Ancestors, who were masters of magic. It is said that they learned the language of magic from mana itself. This is what I imagined in my mind and asked the staff to draw.”

“You asked it?”

No wonder Piraka’s magic worked so slowly. You couldn’t get anywhere with mana by asking.

“Mana is not a living thing, but it acts almost as if it has a will of its own,” Piraka said.

That much was true. But I had tried asking and goading with mana in my early days, and the results had been shoddy at best. Maybe it worked differently with Staffcasting, but I was just going to keep doing what had worked for me.

“What spell is this?” I asked.

“This is called Basic Water,” Piraka said. “It is not very useful, because we have abundant water, but sometimes it is convenient.”

Piraka went to pick up his staff and showed me the spell. I kept cross-referencing the spell formula his staff was creating and the picture in the booklet. Piraka’s work wasn’t bad, but it was far from perfect accuracy. That was interesting. So it seemed like you could draw a picture of a shitty dog, and the mana would understand, for lack of a better reason that you wanted a dog. If you drew an accurate dog, would you get a better spell?

It took a minute, but eventually Piraka had materialized a floating orb of water the size of my toddler head. It was clear and glinted beautifully in the sun.

I laughed and clapped. “Good spell! I want to learn this.”

“Fire is easier to cast for me, but I don’t know why. It was the first spell I learned, and I was never interested in water. But if this spell calls to you, by all means learn it. You have more than earned a little autonomy.”

That was interesting. Was there such a thing as affinities?

“I will leave the booklet to you, Luka,” the elder said. “Just take good care of it.”

And so I began my studies of the arcane. I studied the booklet intensely. There were about ten different spells in the booklet, and they all followed a similar, yet distinct design. Every spell was contained in a circle. As I studied the esoteric shapes, I also began to idly learn to arrange the mana particles in a circle shape above my open palm.

All of the different spells used a magic circle and a formula. It seemed that the symbols required a different kind of literacy altogether. But the basis was the circle of mana within which the symbols were cast. So that was what I set out to learn first. I wanted the creation of the magic circle to become second nature to me. Fortunately Mana Weaving was the perfect Perk for me, and I picked up on creating shapes with the mana quickly. It seemed to me Piraka didn’t have this Perk, and I wondered how much my Sage Perk and Mana Sight had affected acquiring Mana Weaving.

I was certain I could learn to cast spells without a staff. I had already been able to cast elementary proto-magic without one. So whenever I wasn’t infusing potions, or studying the spell circles, I was practicing creating a perfect circle of mana symbols above my palm.

I practiced all day, only stopping to eat and take a small nap. These days I came home later than even my father. Thankfully, mother always brought food during lunch and we all ate on the floor. Even Nirama was starting to warm up to me, although she was still giving me unfriendly looks when Piraka praised me. Sometimes Danek looked at the spell booklet with me, but I could tell he wasn’t interested. I liked him a lot, but I wasn’t convinced he was right in the head. Who isn’t interested in magic!?

I also learned about other stuff with Danek, although I was mostly studying to spend time with him at this point. It was nice having someone as devoted to learning as I was. Since his father involved him in his business of managing the village’s economy, he learned about potions too, and was getting increasingly interested in my infusing the potions. With him I also learned about other potions, such as the Strength Surge Tonic, and several antidotes.

I infused a few Strength Surge Tonics. It was a pink little potion in a finger sized vial, that turned blood red after an infusion. There weren’t many needed, because it was mostly used by Junglers to escape or fight threats. It gave you a large boost in physical power for a short duration, but after the effect waned, it left you weak and hurting.

I nicked one for myself. You know, just in case.

With Piraka’s permission I also got to sip on the energy tonics that I made, which made my long days of practice easier.

I trained hard and was starting to slowly get the hang of creating a perfect magic circle. I alternated the size, but usually I pushed to create as thin a ring as possible.
