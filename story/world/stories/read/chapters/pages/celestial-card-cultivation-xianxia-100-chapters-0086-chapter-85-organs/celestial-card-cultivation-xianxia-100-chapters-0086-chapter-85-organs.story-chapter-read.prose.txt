One more thing to do.

Jianyi was almost giddy in excitement.

From his perspective, the three and a half weeks in the wilderness had been an incredible success.

He had unlocked his Celestial Core Card again, including an improved ability to cultivate. He had started on the path of body cultivation, improved his abilities with the spear, and learned a completely new set of skills in surviving—and maybe even thriving—in the wilderness.

Now, it was time to take the second step in the Body Tempering Realm.

In his right hand, he held the Wood-infused Soul-Forged-Body Elixir for the Body Tempering Realm that Minghao had sold him a while ago for a very fair price.

He had taken position close to the small stream that they had used the past few weeks as their water source, had disrobed, and set out the formation flags around him—it would be unpleasant to be surprised in the middle of his advancement by a spirit beast.

The second stage of the Body Tempering Realm—as it would be in any of the subsequent realms, too—was about strengthening one’s organs, especially the heart and lungs, but if all went well, his senses would also improve.

Before beginning the procedure, he reflected on the last one. He had deviated from the prescribed method by using his ability to split his focus.

In a way, it was paradoxical; due to his strong soul-forged will, it should be easier for him than for others to push the energy that came with the elixir into his body holistically—and he probably could do it—but instead, he had found a way to make it even easier.

The reason why he was a little proud was that this way used an ability he had learned when building his Veil of the Immutable Will and not just a consequence of his large soul, for which he had done nothing.

He decided that he would try his own method again.

There was no way to be sure, but he thought that his approach had saved him significant time, and the less time he needed, the more energy ended up in his body instead of dissipating into the environment.

With a final nod to himself and a few deep breaths, he began by drinking the vibrantly green potion.

The elixir ran down his throat like a dew drop on a leaf as the sun rose.

With each realm, the elixirs, natural treasures, or spaces one would have to expose oneself to changed. If one were to reach the final stage, Jade Body, one would need exposure to Thousand-Year-Ginseng, an enormously rare and valuable treasure.

That was decades, centuries in the future for Jianyi. The elixir he had just consumed was simple in that it used Wild Herbs—and that’s how it tasted. Slightly minty, fresh, and energizing.

The moment it arrived in his stomach, the powerful energy within spread out like a herb taking root, easily pushing through the stomach lining to enter his bloodstream.

Once it was there, Jianyi took over.

Just like last time, he started with circulating qi through his spirit. Then, he followed the guidance of the path’s manual and pushed two-thirds of the affinity-laden qi towards the five primary organs: heart, lungs, liver, spleen, and kidneys. The rest was distributed to the secondary organs, such as the intestines, as well as to his eyes and ears.

It took a moment to get there, but eventually, he had coated all organs that were part of this stage with the green qi.

He hesitated for a moment. If he were to follow the manual to the letter, he would now push the qi into all of them at the same time.

If he followed his personal approach, there would be a few moments in which the organs would be in different stages. Would that be a problem?

Something he should have thought of before.

He decided to go through with it anyway; he just had to be fast.

There was a cautious part in him that wanted to wait with the heart to the very end—he did the opposite.

It wasn’t just a knee-jerk decision to be bold; no, his thinking was that if he could stabilize his heart first, any other organ going out of sync with it for a moment would be survivable.

He got to work and, carefully, pushed the green qi into the constantly moving muscle that was his heart. The walls, the chambers, bit by bit.

The feeling almost tore him out of his trance-like state: Almost for the first time in his life as a cultivator, there was progress without pain.

Instead, the heart eagerly consumed the energy, and he could see as its fibers strengthened and it grew more compact, every beat sending energy through his body, as if sunlight had touched the leaves of a herb.

Even that small change felt vitalizing, energizing, and he could see minor tears in his muscles elsewhere, result of his intense training, accelerate their healing process.

Relief flooded his body—he was willing and ready to take the pain of another transformation like with his bones, but not having to go through it lifted a weight off his shoulders.

The lungs were next and went equally smoothly. Every breath felt like the lungs themselves were producing oxygen.

Step by step, he went through his body and transformed it—until he was just done.

For so long, he had wished for something to be simple—just once—and here it was. At the end, the Celestial Core Card hummed briefly, flushing through the transformed organs.

After the smooth transformation, he had expected there to be fewer disgusting impurities, but the opposite was the case. Before he could even make it to the stream, he had to vomit from the intense stench emanating from the thick layer of gunk on his skin.

As he jumped into the cold water and started to clean himself, he thought that the liver and kidneys likely had a lot of impurities stored in them as a consequence of processing mortal food for the first sixteen years of his life.

Slowly, his thoughts focused on the changes in his body.

He didn’t think that he had grown again. Unfortunate, but not unexpected.

In general, he didn’t think there were too many external changes in his body at all. Maybe his skin was a bit softer, but that was hard to tell.

But he felt different. Every heartbeat pulsed with energy, every breath brought strength through his body.

The cold water of the stream seemed to matter even less, even without cycling qi through his limbs.

He had the feeling that his endurance had grown quite a bit, and with a chuckle, he thought back to the laps he had to run around the training yard in the outer sect and how much he had struggled in the aftermath of his failed gate opening attempt. He was sure that now he would run so fast that nobody in group Lapis would be able to keep up with him.

It would require some testing, but he somehow felt that he would also need less sleep than before—more time to train, especially with his higher endurance. What a wonderful improvement.

He had done it. If Minghao agreed that he had progressed sufficiently on woodcraft and with the spear, he had achieved the goals set by Elder Sun.

For a moment, he just floated in the stream, grinning towards the Heavens.

***

“What do you think he will say?”

Minghao looked at him, smiling. “I think he will be quite impressed with what you achieved.”

Jianyi felt warmth settle in him, and it was not just from the campfire that they were sharing.

His mother had always been proud of him and praised him for his success in school—but it hadn’t always felt earned. He could have worked half as hard, and she still would have been proud.

This… felt different.

Yes, he had gotten help—without the elixirs from Minghao, he wouldn’t have been able to achieve what he did—but he had still earned them. Had defeated spirit beasts that most of his sect brothers and sisters would not have been able to beat on their own.

“But…” Minghao had raised his finger. “You must not forget that the others are not lazing about. Many started with an advantage, and they won’t give it up easily or without a fight.

“And of course, there is no such thing as a weak demon—well, there is, but you should not bank on that,” he said with a chuckle.

His teacher poured a bit of cold water over his celebratory mood, but it didn’t change the fact that Jianyi was happy and that his happiness would not stop him from keeping on pushing as hard as he could.

Once they were back at the sect—and they would start the journey back the next day—he would focus his preparations on the mid-year spear assessment.

It would be nice if his friends were available to train with him. They probably were still doing the weekly meeting.

Though without Jun and with him gone…

Maybe they also wouldn’t forgive him for just leaving for a whole month… More than Minghao’s words, this dampened his mood.

He had written Shuhua a letter, but was that sufficient?

He didn’t know.

He sighed and focused on Minghao. “When you and Elder Sun ‘suggested’,” that word did a lot of work in the sentence, “that I come out here, I didn’t know what to expect. To be honest, I was a bit skeptical. But I really enjoyed this last month. Thank you!”

Minghao laughed. “I am glad you enjoyed it. One never knows whether the skills you learned will become important, and it is better to have them and not need them than the other way around.”

***

The next morning, they quickly packed their things, which primarily comprised their tents, and started the journey back to the sect.

The most dangerous enemy they faced for the first part of their journey was a mortal fox who really didn’t like them coming close to its burrow. They quickly moved on and left it to its own devices.

Jianyi managed to collect another bit of spirit grass, which he planned to sell in the sect for contribution points, and a few other herbs and mushrooms for their dinner that night.

Eventually, they returned to the surprisingly busy road.

Multiple traders, most with ox-pulled carts, moved in both directions, and Jianyi was curious to see how Minghao chatted with all of them. After the third such conversation, he couldn’t contain himself anymore. “Are you interested in something specific?” he asked incredulously. How could spending time talking with strangers be helpful?

The man chuckled. “If you get older, it gets harder and harder to stay in touch with how life feels for mortals; a little chat like that helps. Also, you never know what you might learn. One might tell you about higher rice prices in a village. The next might tell you that it was caused by a spirit beast, and after the next, you might be on your way to a rare beast that you would have never heard about otherwise.”

Jianyi struggled to see how one could lose touch with the life of mortals—it was still most of what he knew, after all—but he also couldn’t imagine being a hundred years old, or even more! The argument that one could learn things that one would otherwise miss was more accessible to him. Maybe it could also be a way to suss out rumors about demonic cultists. That could be quite valuable indeed.

The trip back was another confirmation that Jianyi had improved. They could travel significantly faster than when they had traveled in the other direction.

Time flew by, and without any trouble, the blue-tiled pagodas of the sect reflected the light of the winter sun as they started the ascent of the mountain.

Jianyi was curious whether anything had changed during his absence.
