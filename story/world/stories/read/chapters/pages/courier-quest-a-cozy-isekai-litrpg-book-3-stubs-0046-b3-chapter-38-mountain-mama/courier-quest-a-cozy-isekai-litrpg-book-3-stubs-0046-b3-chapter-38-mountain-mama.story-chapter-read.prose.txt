Even though the first song had been a meme meant to troll people from Earth, Trevor still enjoyed hearing Never Gonna Give You Up by Rick Astley. He had never minded the song even after it became an internet staple just about everywhere, and his reaction had been a playful one.

It also helped that Rachel Levy was a magnificent performer. There hadn’t been any doubt in his mind from the moment he found out she was a Summoned with the Power of Song, but it was still wonderful listening to her. Music that he had heard hundreds of times in the past touched his heart and soul in ways they hadn’t before.

Rachel’s range was unreal, at one point going from soprano to bass with a simple clearing of her throat. Not only that, but she played many of the instruments that were on stage as well, moving from a piano to a viola to a harp. There was nothing but confidence in the Summoned’s movement.

The Summoned and her ensemble performed orchestrated versions of Fly Me To The Moon, You Raise Me Up, Respect, and Smile Bomb. Trevor found himself singing along to more than a few of the songs. Adabelle had leaned against him when he started and he had quieted, but she urged him to continue. He had no choice but to oblige her, then.

Everybody Wants To Rule The World, In The End, Simple And Clean, and several more made their appearance after the intermission. She made the leap between the wake me up trifecta, from the song of the same name by Avicii, to ‘before you go go,’ and ending with ‘when September ends.’

Her big finisher was Country Roads. The nostalgia had hit him hard since he recognized the majority of the music, all the way from the beginning. A lot of the songs had been those his family listened to while he grew up, bringing back some fond memories.

The courier was one of the first to rise to his feet for a standing ovation as Rachel took her bow, a big smile on his face as he watched the rest of the audience do the same. Lora put her fingers in her mouth and whistled loudly. Adabelle didn’t like that much, but allowed the woman her cheers. Even Clay was vibrating with joy on his tie clip.

Rachel looked up at their balcony as the curtain started to close, then struck a pose with a peace sign before blowing a kiss just as she disappeared out of sight. Apparently, she had already known where her fellow Earthlings were. Or, at least, where one of them was since Lora was a new addition.

As people started shuffling out of their seats and down the aisles, Trevor sat back down. “Wow. That was… wow. She was incredible.”

“She was,” Adabelle agreed as she joined him. “I knew the music on Earth was vibrant and varied, but I don’t think I’ve heard that many different styles back to back. And that was just a small, curated selection.”

“Most of the music I’ve heard that came from here from my world is a little off,” the courier admitted. “Like, there are small differences that make me think that they’re from an Earth that isn’t mine or they aren’t musicians and they’re trying to inspire people who are. Rachel had very few variations, and almost everything was perfectly how I remembered them.”

Adabelle nodded. “She was very passionate. I could easily see her being a musician before she was summoned.”

Trevor turned to his wife. “Which one was your favorite?”

The noblewoman hummed in response as looked down at her program, which had been kept open almost the entire time. “I would have to say that I really enjoyed both Shania Twain’s I Feel Like A Woman and John Denver’s Country Roads,” she answered after she had scanned the list of songs and who their original performers were.

“Those were good,” he agreed with a nod.

“Shania’s song just felt empowering, and I really understood it. John’s had a lot of references to places that I’ve rarely heard of before, but it made me think of home, and of you.”

“Of me?”

“Yes,” she answered with a smile as she leaned against him. “Life on the road, reminiscing about coming home. I became very attached to Tosa when I was out adventuring. It was the first place I really got to know, and it felt right.”

“I know what you mean,” Trevor replied with a chuckle as he stroked her hair.

“And that’s why it made me think of you. Because I know that, no matter how far you go, you’ll be yearning for home the same way I did. To come back, tell everyone the tales of your grand adventures, or not so grand adventures.” Adabelle paused with a pensive look on her face. She glanced up at her husband. “Based on how the song went, would that make Tosa your West Virginia and me your mountain mama?”

The courier laughed a little louder than he had meant to at the innocent question. She gave him a curious look. “West Virginia is a mountainous region, and I’m pretty sure that he was calling the state his mountain mama,” Trevor explained. “But don’t worry, love, you’ll always be my little dragon slayer.”

Adabelle buried her head in his chest. “No, don’t call me that.”

“Are you sure?” he teased. “I remember you saying that just calling you lady was lazy.”

“Forget that conversation immediately,” she demanded, putting on a haughty tone even though she wouldn’t look up at him. “Lady is fine. It’s steeped in years of friendship. Definitely better than the alternative.”

“I won’t forget it, but I’ll put it away for a while,” he said, patting her head gently.

“If that’s all you can promise, then it will suffice for now,” Adabelle replied before pulling away. She glanced over to where Lora had been sitting to see that she had left them alone, though they knew she wouldn’t have gone far. “How about you, Trevor?”

“Me?” he asked in surprise. “I’m great.”

She turned back to look at him. “You just seemed a little more emotional than usual for some of those songs, so I wanted to make sure you were all right. Nostalgia is a powerful thing to be struck by, even if you know it’s coming.”

“Yeah, that’s true,” Trevor replied, giving her a smile. He took a moment to really consider her words. “There were some parts that really hit me, yeah, but not in the kind of way that makes me wish I could go back, you know? It was more like… like I’m glad I had a chance to hear something so pure from my past. As if I were visiting the place I grew up again.”

“But not home.”

“But not home,” he confirmed warmly. “Tosa is my mountain mama now, and there’s nothing else like it.”

“Good. I don’t want you pining for the days before you met me, son of Ander,” Adabelle said as she patted his chest before standing up and stretching.

“I could never,” he replied, getting to his feet. Her tail wrapped around his wrist as she led him back into the hallway.

Lora was leaning against the wall about halfway down, one foot raised as she waited patiently for the newlyweds. Her eyes lit up as they appeared and she pushed away. “Ready to return to the hotel to change?”

“Yup,” Trevor replied, taking Clay off of his tie and sending what little husk there was into his inventory.

“Cool. You’ve got about a half an hour before we’ll teleport again, and I’ll be out of your hair for that,” she said with a smile before offering her hand. Once they both touched it, they found themselves out of the dimly lit hallway and back in their room. “I’ll be knocking on your door soon. Bye!”

Lora disappeared, leaving them alone in their suite’s living room. Trevor was quick to bring out Clay’s stoat husk, allowing his familiar to get their legs back. They began running around furniture as if to look for a spot that was as great as the Red Dream’s circular chair.

Adabelle led Trevor into the bedroom while the earth elemental went scouting. Once she let go of him, his body turned dark as he swapped out his outfit for his pajamas via Quick Change.

“Lucky,” the noblewoman jealously stated. He just smiled; it hadn’t been the first time, and it certainly wouldn’t be the last.

Instead of taking off her dress, she moved to the side of their strangely-shaped bed and let herself fall backwards onto it. With the raised head and feet, she was somewhat sandwiched in the middle. A frown appeared on her face as she reached for something behind her shoulder blade.

“What’s up?” Trevor asked. He had been ready to fall beside her, but placed his hands on her legs instead.

Instead of answering, she retrieved a small black remote from behind her. There were buttons on it, and she accidentally pressed one of them. Both ends of the bed lifted up further, making the canyon in the middle greater.

“Oh, it’s one of those moving beds,” he said, delighted. They couldn’t see the hydraulics before because of the bed skirt. “That’s cool. Always wanted to try one of these.”

“That explains the odd shape,” Adabelle agreed as she looked at the buttons. She pressed the one that depicted a horizontal line and both ends started to slowly fall. Another caused the bed to vibrate, but stopped it from moving, so she had to press them again. The noblewoman grabbed a pillow as it became flat. “Could you get this off of me, honey?”

“Don’t have to ask me twice,” he replied as he laid down next to her. His hand rested gently on her stomach, feeling the soft fabric of the dress, before drawing it into his inventory. It disappeared in a flash, leaving her in the slip she had worn underneath.

A wry smile crossed her lips as she stretched languidly across the bed. She pulled Trevor’s head to her chest, and he wrapped his arm around her. “You’d be an absolute nightmare, you know.”

He chuckled. “This again?”

“This again, and I’m serious,” Adabelle replied. “If I wasn’t a willing participant in the matter, I’d say being able to disarm your opponents as easily as you do would be cheating.”

“But you are a willing participant,” Trevor pointed out as he looked up at her. “Quite often, actually.”

“That’s because you’re convenient. It’s one of the biggest reasons I married you.”

“I’ll accept that because it’s true; I really am quite convenient to have around.”

Adabelle kissed the top of his head before setting the bed to vibrate again. “Really, though. If you had your Power in Halvsar, even without the stats, you’d have wrecked the tournament scene.”

“I would have needed some fighting sense for that to happen,” he chuckled. “Plus, wouldn’t my Power make me excluded?”

She shook her head. “No. You know how there was magic in my world, right?”

“Of course. Reddigar and Ospheline were both mages in your adventuring party,” Trevor replied, shifting his head to her shoulder so he could watch her more carefully.

“They were. Thank you for remembering them,” Adabelle said with a smile. “Yes, they had magic. Ospheline was a priest, so he received spells from an outside source, but some had it come from within. If you somehow had the Power of Inventory, likely as some sort of bloodline magic that noble houses liked to collect, then you would be able to use it in a tournament.”

“That’s fascinating. I think this is the first time I heard of noble houses collecting bloodlines.”

“It was usually something like spells that were practiced so much they became innate, increased affinity for certain elements, and the like. They could manifest in different ways, and they were the reason for most of the nobility to rise to their station.”

“Did House Katine have something like that?” he asked, interested in the turn in discussion.

“No. We rose to our position through skill and service, which was a point of pride for the family,” Adabelle replied as she took on a thoughtful expression. “It left us in a strange position. We were elevated due to our own hard work, but aside from a Katine being so close to the king’s lineage for so long we were also looked down upon by other houses who thought themselves superior.”

“I imagine someone who could sling fireballs would really shake up a sword fighting tournament,” Trevor mused.

“You’re absolutely right about that,” she replied with a light laugh. “Certain abilities that were overly destructive weren’t allowed, but things like that were. Your Power most certainly would, though I think there would be a lot of complaints.”

“Because of how easy it would be to disarm people,” he said, already knowing the answer.

“Indeed.” Adabelle hesitated for a moment before turning to face her husband. She left her arm underneath his head so he would have a pillow. “I could see you being a shield user.”

“Technically, I think I kind of am,” Trevor chuckled, thinking of the bulwarks he had sitting in his inventory.

“A little bit, but I mean I could see you with a bunch of really cheap shields.”

He arched an eyebrow. “Really cheap ones, you say?”

Adabelle nodded seriously. “The kind that a sword could cut through easily, but get stuck about halfway through,” she explained, making a gentle slashing motion with her tail. “While your opponent is trying to pry either their blade free or your shield away, you’d have a somewhat safe avenue to touch their weapon and make it disappear.”

“And if I couldn’t?”

“Summon another shield or use a different part of the mostly broken one,” she answered easily. “Disarming was a good way to win matches, and it was something my family excelled at given our style. Your method would probably be frowned upon, but no one would actually call you out on it. No one that mattered, anyway.”

“I bet you would have done well in those tournaments, if you had been allowed,” Trevor said, reaching forward and cupping her cheek in his hand.

“Oh, don’t you worry. I competed plenty after I had left home on my adventure,” Adabelle replied, reaching up to take his hand in hers. “It wasn’t the same as fighting back home, and you’re right that I wasn’t allowed to, but I was a right nightmare when abroad. We didn’t have a lot of time since we were chasing after Medivene, but they were lucrative opportunities when we were running low on funds.”

“There’s always money in popular competitions,” the courier said. His finger trailed down from her chest to stomach, resting against her navel. “You know, we still have about fifteen minutes.”

She pouted. “And you’re going to stop there?”

Trevor arched an eyebrow at his wife’s expression, and she doubled down. He shifted so he could lean his face close to hers. “I warned you that I couldn’t be held accountable for what happens later.”

“You did,” Adabelle confirmed, her glowing eyes flicking to his lips.

“And we have some time before we’re needed again,” he said slowly.

“We do,” she agreed, leaning towards him slightly. Her fingernails scratched his chest.

“And…” he breathed, her eyes glowing brighter as his hand traced her hip.

“And?”

“And… we’ll pick this up later when we have more time,” Trevor said as he leaned up and kissed her forehead.

Adabelle’s tail whipped against the bed as she pouted even more heavily than before. “You’re going to make me wait?” she whined.

“It’ll be worth it, promise,” he answered with a grin. “You don’t want to be interrupted, do you?”

“I suppose not,” she relented, but not without a huff.

Trevor planted another kiss on her forehead before laying beside her, pulling his wife into his embrace. She clung to him, always a comfort.

“Alright,” he started slowly. “Tell me about your favorite tournament. Win, lose, draw, whatever. You have ten minutes before I’m making you get up and get dressed.”

She thought about it for a moment. “Fifteen minutes. I have a really good story.”

“Sure,” he agreed. “But then you have to rush so we can go drive cars.”

“Deal,” Adabelle agreed with a smile before diving into a story about a tournament whose arena rose above the sea.
