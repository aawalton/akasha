Gio sailed through an ocean of blurred colors. Little sparks of light accompanied him on his journey, spiraling around him like a constellation of miniature stars. He reached out a hand to the side, brushing his hand against a passing cloud of green spirals that had the same texture and feel as a field of plush grass on a pleasant summer day. He leaned his head back and closed his eyes, drinking in the sensation. Before too long, though, he felt something.

A black storm cloud loomed ahead.

“Ugh. Again?” Gio sighed.

Knowing from experience that the cloud would find him no matter where he tried to go, Gio bravely dove straight into the bulk of it, doing a really cool flip in mid-air. As his bare feet touched down on a smooth surface. He looked around, momentarily confused at the angle at which he was seeing the room before the whole world seemed to finally decide which way gravity was supposed to pull, and he painlessly flopped down onto a checkerboard-tiled floor.

“Gotta work on that landing.” Gio hummed to himself.

The environment looked like a collage of several scenes that he had seen in Hart’s Casino, albeit fuzzy and indistinct. There were hanging planters, brass banisters, broken slot machines, and, strangely enough, several broken coffee-making stations pulled straight from the sealed cafe that he had spent the night inside. Dilapidated walls breathed in and out, mirroring the rising and falling of his chest.

Giovanni Tongue faded into view under a single beam of light in the middle of the space. The criminal wore a filthy smile plastered across his metal-studded face. He pressed a hooked blade into the cheek of a young woman, extracting a drop of blood that glistened in the low light, its reflection trailing off into the air in a fractal bloom.

Recognition.

Fear.

A bloodcurdling scream resounded outward, shaking the room like an earthquake.

“Ugh. Stop screaming, will you?” Giovanni sneered.

“Hi, it’s me… again,” Gio said, announcing himself to the room.

“Ah, nice of you to join us. You’re just in time for the show.” Giovanni replied, not even bothering to look at him.

“And what a privilege…” Gio deadpanned. He braced himself for what he knew was coming next.

The necromancer dragged the bloody hook across a prepared effigy. It was a simple trinket in the shape of a pristine white doll, devoid of any identifiable markings. As the victim’s blood seeped in, the doll seemed to grow more detailed, in a parody of her features.

“Please… don’t-” the woman begged.

Giovanni ripped the doll in half. The noise rang through Gio like the peal of a clock tower bell.

Pain.

The woman’s humanity pulled apart at the seams. Her face caved inward as her body slumped to the floor, and white wisps of fog leaked out of her.

Anger.

A glittering ghost emerged from the wreckage of what used to be a person, flying through Gio’s chest and weaving through his body like a sewing needle through gauze. Gio felt the ghastly appendages worming their way through his spiritual form.

Light.

The ghost emerged from his chest and contorted back around, still anchored inside him. It craned down upon him, gripping his face with razor-tipped claws. Gio stared into bejeweled eyes, entranced by the cursed light within them.

Confusion.

She screamed. It was a reverberating wail, shrill and metallic. Giovanni’s laughter echoed in the farthest corner of Gio’s mind.

Despair.

Gio looked deeply into the sockets of the spectre, seeing many copies of himself in those facets.

“WHY CAN’T YOU HEAR ME?” She screamed, her voice garbled.

“I can hear you, Sarah,” Gio replied. “That is your name, isn’t it? Sarah… please, you need to let me go.”

The ghost screamed, taking fistfuls of Gio’s flesh and ripping it apart. Long claws tore into him, cutting away ribbons.

“Sarah… I’m not the one who hurt you. Look at me.” Gio demanded.

Gio’s hands crystallized as he became the Aspect of Chaos once more. His shattered flesh dug into the ghostly spirit as he held her.

“I’m sorry about what happened to you… But haunting me isn’t going to make your pain go away. You need to let me go.”

The ghost froze momentarily before lashing out with a renewed burst of vitriol and gnashing teeth.

“Argh!” Gio grunted as her spectral claws finally found purchase on a part of him, drawing a very real spike of pain.

“How droll. Let’s change the subject, shall we?” drawled an unfamiliar voice.

“What? Who said that?” Gio asked, looking around. He saw nothing.

The tortured ghost melted away like ice cream under a hot summer sun as candy-colored clouds filled the room. The rest of Gio’s dream became pleasant nonsense.

“That’s better, isn’t it?”

Gio awoke from a death-like sleep, his short silver hair jutting out at odd angles and his face plastered to his pillow with drool. A pinprick of blood ran from his nose, staining his pillow.

“Morning.” Rio signed, his hands still alight with the barest hint of phantasmal color. Chromatic corruption, as their shared mentor had called it.

Gio’s chaotic reflection sat at their research table in the mirror dimension, evidently already reviewing some notes this morning.

Sitting up, Gio yawned, blinking away the last remnants of sleep. He wiped his nose, frowning at the blood.

“What time is it?” Gio asked.

“Chandrika will be expecting you in about 45 minutes. I was going to give you another five before I woke you up.” Rio said. The Aspect of Chaos looked at Gio’s blood-stained face. “Are you having nightmares again?”

“Almost every night, now that I’m not hyperfocused on the domain. I guess the stress was proving to be an adequate distraction, or something. Saint Baphelus said that I should expect them to get worse before they get better.” Gio answered.

“Trio says that you need to get it checked out,” Rio said.

Gio paused, momentarily beset by the humour of his present situation.

“My second reflection, who has for some reason taken on the mantle of ‘the responsible one’, is talking to me via a gaping hole that we ripped in the mirror dimension. This information has been relayed to me by my main reflection, who just so happens to be the bearer of a piece of primordial chaos-”

“Yeah, yeah, we get it. Improbable situation. You should probably get used to those. Anyways, just… make sure you talk to someone if the dreams get too bad. She’s clearly doing real harm to you.” Rio said.

“Just because I’m not seemingly invincible and pain resistant like you are doesn’t mean that a nosebleed is going to take me down.” Gio retorted.

“I’m not invincible. I happen to have a huge weakness in that my very human originator likes to pretend that he’s much tougher than he actually is.” Rio replied.

Gio scoffed. “Saint-”

Rio interjected before Gio could finish his sentence.

“Saint Baphelus is an incredible mage… but he hasn’t been a student or a mortal for a very, very long time. He might not necessarily know the type of stress that you’re able to tolerate. Just promise that you’ll go see the Medical department if necessary." Rio said.

That is… fair.

“Okay, I’ll keep that in mind.” Gio agreed.

“Good. Now get dressed. You have a Queen to meet.”

“So your mom-”

“Is not going to kill you.”

“Yeah, but-”

“Or hunt you for sport. Or declare you an enemy of Ujjbala Sūrya, or whatever horrors that you’re imagining. If she’s rude to you, she will have to deal with me.” Chandrika said.

Gio grimaced. He did not feel ready to meet royalty.

I only barely survived Jean’s mom…

Last night, Gio and Chandrika had not managed to convince Jean that they shouldn’t go to his family’s party.

“If you’re concerned about safety, then you should absolutely come.” Jean had said, “Basically, half of the leadership of the Ring Guard is going to be in attendance… my dad’s the Captain, after all. That club is probably going to be the safest place in the city.”

The milestone celebration before had been pleasant, if not somewhat difficult to enjoy, having just survived an attempted murder at the hands of a mystery man who was currently prompting a massive manhunt.

Isadora Castallane, Jean’s mother, had been a spectacle. The noblewoman effortlessly managed to coordinate all of the catering for the evening, greet every guest, relentlessly check to make sure that her son and his guests were having a good time every few minutes, and somehow also manage to report the strange man at the train station to the authorities and also arrange for an escort of Ring Guards to ferry all of the students who had come to the party back to the Crystal Ring as the party ended.

She had done all of this while politely mining Gio for every possible piece of information about his family and upbringing, and ferrying the trio of friends in front of various nobles so that they could tell and re-tell the tale of how they defeated an undead dragon and saved the city.

Everyone was mostly polite to me, but I still felt massively out of place. I felt like… a novelty. Jean’s poor friend. The kid in the right place at the right time to leech onto the upcoming legend of Jean-Claude Castallane and his foreign princess friend.

After returning to the Crystal ring full of hors d'oeuvres and bitterly expensive champagne, the three students were informed that they were ordered to be present in the morning for a surprise visit from Chandrika’s mother, the Queen of Ujjbala Sūrya.

Which led to this morning, as Gio stared down the immaculately over-dressed form of his best friend at the breakfast table.

The young Sage was wearing her ceremonial Saree, a golden silk garment that wrapped around her body, catching the light of the Pearl Cafeteria elegantly. She had done her makeup in a way that reminded Gio of what she looked like while her domain was active, a small detail that had terrified him.

She never does her makeup like that. She’s acting formal, which means she’s nervous, which means that I should be nervous.

Out of nowhere, an inquisitor flashed into existence.

“AAAH!” Gio yelped. Chandrika pursed her lips, half laughing at Gio, and half expectantly looking at the blank metallic mask of the Inquisitor.

“I apologize for the interruption. Miss Chakraverti, Headmaster Vespertine requests your presence in her office.” The inquisitor explained. Their metallic voice drew stares from a few surrounding students catching breakfast at this early hour.

“I had thought that we all planned to meet in Saint Baphelus’s chamber. Gio was supposed to come with me.” Chandrika frowned.

“Mister deGloria should still proceed to his mentor’s side. The Headmaster requires you to confirm some things… it appears that your family has brought an entourage of sorts… and they are already on their way from the teleportation hub. It’s drawn quite a bit of attention, and we do not have much time. The Castallanes are stalling them as we speak.”

Chandrika cursed. “Mom brought her war party. Sorry, Gio… we’ll meet you there.”

Gio mouthed the words “War party?”

“It’s… complicated. I’ll explain later.” Chandrika said. She took an offered hand from the inquisitor, vanishing alongside the masked figure.

Gio quietly cleaned the rest of his breakfast off his plate, trying to avoid the curious stares of the cafeteria.

Well… I guess I’ll go wait with Saint Baphelus.

The Manse opened up in front of Gio, and he stepped inside the yawning portal.

The ‘astral pathways’ of the Manse, as Saint Baphelus had called them, often looked like hallways of darkness, barely lit by countless stars. Gio had often wondered about the mysterious spaces between his mentor’s portals. Did the Manse borrow space from some other dimension, the astral plane, perhaps? The obvious parallel that Gio had drawn was from his own experiences in the Between space of the Mirror dimension, but something about the formless grey interstitial of the Between had felt much larger than the Manse. Gio often felt that, were he not careful, he could get lost in the Between… a thought that shuddered him to his core.

A crystalline door shook Gio out of his musings.

That’s odd.

The door was closed. Usually, when Gio arrived for their routine Tea-times, the door was already open… as Saint Baphelus was always aware of what happened in his domain. Certainly, his mentor knew that he was coming, right?

“Uhh… should I knock?” Gio asked, mostly to himself.

A system message flashed before his eyes.

<BAPHELUS: With company. Sorry for the wait, I’ll have them gone in a few minutes. Feel free to wait in your room, I’ll grab you when I’m ready for you.>

Gio raised his eyebrows.

How… unusual.

A familiar doorway appeared to his side, already opened to the familiar sight of his dormitory. Gio made a few strides towards it before a small shape melted out of the inky void. Two purple eyes opened, looking up at Gio.

“Miao,” Said the cat.

“Oh!” Gio preened, kneeling to greet his newfound friend. “Hi, Wink!”

Headmaster Vespertine’s familiar brushed against Gio’s leg, purring softly. Gio took this as implicit permission, brushing his cloud-soft black fur.

“Who’s a handsome being of unfathomable power? You are!” Gio cooed.

“Miao.” Wink agreed.

The only things that differentiated Wink from a fluffy black cat were two: his purple, shimmering irises and his massive, feather-duster-like tail. Wink’s black tail was almost twice as big as his body, and it lazily swished through the air. This was only the second time that Gio had met with the mysterious cat-like entity… and although he had been warned that Wink was powerful, he also apparently liked to be treated like a cat, which Gio would be more than happy to indulge.

Wink’s diminutive silhouette stood in the light at Gio’s doorway, blocking his retreat into his room.

“What’s up, buddy?” Gio asked. “Do you want to hang out with me? I don’t know if Headmaster Vespertine is cool with it… But you’re more than welcome to chill out in my room.”

Wink yawned. Tiny white teeth and a pink mouth emerged from the void.

Gio started to move past the cat, but Wink stood on his haunches, reaching out a paw with tiny pink pads and batting at the air.

Gio’s eyes sparkled in adoration. “Is there something you want? I don’t know that I have anything you’d like… do you need me to sneak treats from Marie? I’d do it. I would do crime for you.”

“Mrow.” Wink denied.

“Okay, then… I’m a little confused. I’d love to help you, bud… but I don’t know what you want from me besides scratches.” Gio frowned.

And then, Wink folded in on himself. He became little more than a ball of black fur that started rapidly expanding.

“Uh…”

Black strands erupted outward as a pair of gargantuan purple eyes looked down on Gio. Any faint trace of remaining starlight became blotted out, and a tail as big as a furry tree gently coiled around Gio. Wink became so large that Gio couldn’t tell where the void of the Manse ended and the beast began.

“UHHH… Mister Wink?” Gio nervously prodded.

“Mao.” replied a deep, bassy purr.

The Gone-in-a-Wink gently picked Gio up with his tail, depositing him in a tuft of fur on top of his head. Then, the world went fuzzy, as Wink pounced through Baphelus’s door in a puff of black mana.
