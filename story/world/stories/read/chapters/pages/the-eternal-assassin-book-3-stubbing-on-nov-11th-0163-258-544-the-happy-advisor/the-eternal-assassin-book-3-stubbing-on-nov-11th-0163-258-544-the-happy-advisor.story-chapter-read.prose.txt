“We have a problem.”

Asher’s sudden appearance and statement startled both Samantha and Moxy, both of whom had been in the middle of shooting down a group of zombies running their way. Each of them snapped toward him in an instant, and Asher blinked as he found himself looking at a glowing arrowhead and a fist capable of punching deadly air blasts, both trained at his face.

“Asher?” Samantha asked, shaking her head and immediately going back to firing at the rapidly approaching zombies. “Be careful! I nearly killed you!”

“Not that that would have put him down for long,” Moxy snorted, leaving the zombies to Samantha as she looked at him. “So what’s the problem?”

“Before we get into that, let’s take down the horde of monsters that want to rip our faces off,” Asher suggested, watching as the first of the zombies reached Rulfar and received little more than a battle axe to the face for its troubles.

Working together, the four of them made quick work of the large group. This close to the edge of the Deadlands, there weren’t any weird, mutated zombies running around, and certainly nothing even remotely close to Smiles to worry about. After the last of the zombies went down, Asher let out a sigh of relief.

“The gradual increase in this place’s Death Aura is hard to feel going one mile at a time, but I can definitely feel it now,” he admitted, able to breathe a bit easier now that his body wasn’t constantly being decayed and regenerated.

“Is that the problem? Did the aura end up being more than you could handle?” Rulfar asked with a frown. “I thought your resistance skill and regeneration were stronger than that.”

“No, that’s not the problem. Though admittedly, I only made it about a dozen miles in, I’ve still got a long way to go to get to the center. This is the problem.”

Holding out his hand, Asher pulled Smiles’ severed head out of his Personal Rift, holding the monster by the hair as he angled him toward his teammates.

“Oh, I’m outside again!” Smiles said, laughing as his bloodshot eyes darted between the others. “Hello there! Do all of you bleed bright red as well?”

“Asher, what in the nine hells is that?” Samantha asked, her eyes wide and locked on Smiles’ head.

“This is an example of what’s waiting for me deeper into the Deadlands,” Asher explained, ignoring Smiles as the undead began asking Rulfar how many bodies he’d eaten to get so big. “There’s more than just regular zombies roaming around in here. They don’t just grow stronger or faster, some are mutated, and some, like Smiles here, are even more special.”

“I’m a Servant!” Smiles grinned, trying and failing to nod along with Asher’s explanation. “We’re made special by the King of Death himself. We’re all pretty different, but we roam the Deadlands and deal with anomalies like Rulfar. Anyone who can shrug off the Death aura gets a visit from us long before they can reach the King!”

“Anomalies like me?” Rulfar blinked, pointing at himself. “I can’t shrug off the Death Aura.”

“Oh, sorry, I told him my name was yours,” Asher admitted, grinning sheepishly. “It felt like a good idea at the time.”

“Wait, there are super zombies further into the Deadlands?” Moxy asked, ignoring Rulfar as he opened his mouth to argue. “Are you serious?! I want in on this!”

“They’re much deeper into the Deadlands, deep enough that not even my Complete Resilience is enough to shrug off the aura. Not to mention these Servants aren’t just stronger zombies. They are dangerous. Really dangerous.”

“Aw, thank you,” Smiles said. “The other Servants always tell me I’m not threatening enough, so I appreciate that! Oh, going to the bathroom! Can you share the sensation with me? I’ve always been curious about that! Sometimes I scare people enough that they wet themselves, and it looks kind of fun.”

Asher rolled his eyes as he placed a hand on Smiles’ forehead and shoved a handful of memories into his brain. He didn’t actually have any memories of wetting himself, so regular bathroom trips would have to do. Seeing his team staring at him, he snapped. “Alright he’s a bit weird, but still, super dangerous! Stop looking at me like that, come here!”

Rather than waste time trying to explain, Asher took advantage of Smiles being distracted sifting through the barrage of new memories in order to share his brief encounter with the Servant with his teammates via Incorporate. Once they’d witnessed what the undead had been capable of for themselves, their tones changed.

“That… is one fast opponent,” Moxy admitted, frowning as she stared at Smiles. “I’m not actually sure how I’d fight something like that myself.”

“The only reason you’re still alive is because it wanted to play with its prey,” Rulfar said, looking at the head with unease. “Still, excellent job defeating it with Incorporate. That was very clever.”

“You said there are more of this thing? More Servants?” Samantha asked, shuddering at the memory of getting stabbed in the stomach and placing a hand over her own naval as if she could still feel the wound bleeding freely. “Forget the lich, if you encounter another one as fast as Smiles who’s not quite so playful, you’ll die instantly.”

“That’s the one silver lining to all this,” Asher admitted. “From what Smiles told me, each of the Servants are unique. He was the only one created with such insane speeds.”

“So what makes the others so dangerous?” Moxy asked, starting to look excited again. “Immense strength? Durability? The usual?”

“Unfortunately, Smiles isn’t exactly the best at describing the other Servants,” Asher sighed, shaking Smiles out of his memory-induced stupor. “Smiles, can you tell them what you told me about the Servants?”

“Sure! Like I said, there are six of us. There’s Grunt, who never wants to play with me and won’t let me touch her hammer. Marrow, who’s got a super cool bone collection that he’s always adding to. Chill, who’s the King’s right-hand Servant and is always sucking up to him. Scream, who likes playing with people who come into the Deadlands even more than I do. And Fade, who doesn’t really talk and mostly sticks to himself. And me, of course. Smiles!”

“Thank you, Smiles,” Asher said, pushing the memory of a squirrel into the undead’s mind to keep him occupied before turning back to his team. “I tried asking him for more specific details, but that’s about the best he’s got. There’s a lot of ground to cover across the whole Deadlands, so they usually stick pretty spread out from one another. Because of that, he’s never actually seen any of the others fight. Though he did confirm he’s the fastest of the Servants.”

“If the Deadlands are so vast, and there are only five other Servants, do you think you could just try to avoid them and make your way straight to the King of Death?” Samantha ventured.

“Wait, why are you avoiding them at all?” Moxy asked, giving him a confused look. “Don’t you want to fight them? I mean, I want to fight them, and I know we’re two birds of a feather in that sense.”

“You saw what that soulsteel blade was capable of in my memories. I’m a little worried about what other tricks these Servants might have up their sleeves, if I’m being honest. I don’t mind danger or putting my life on the line, but my soul? I’m not afraid to admit that wigs me out a bit.”

“I’ve never heard of soulsteel, but I’m aware of certain monsters that can harm the soul directly,” Rulfar said quietly, his voice low enough that they almost didn’t hear him at all. “Soul damage is a terrible thing. It changes who you are as a person, and can turn even the gentlest of people into a raging maniac. It’s not something to take lightly or play around with.”

“Asher, you got stabbed with that sword twice,” Samantha said, looking worriedly at him as she took his hand. “Are you feeling okay?”

“Honestly, I feel fine,” he shrugged. “If I had to guess, I think Eternal Regeneration probably heals soul damage as well. That or the thin sword doesn’t actually do any lasting damage. Like an acupuncture needle.”

“What kind of needle doesn’t do lasting damage?” Moxy asked, crossing her arms. “Sounds like a sad excuse for a weapon.”

“Not a weapon, a form of weird massage I never understood,” Asher said, shaking his head. “But I agree with Rulfar. Probably best to avoid these Servants as much as possible. Hey, Smiles.” Asher shook the severed head, getting Smiles’ attention back on them. “How did you know where to find me, anyway?”

“Oh, that’s easy! We Servants can feel when the undead within the Deadlands are snuffed out. We tend to ignore the ones around the outskirts of the Deadlands because we don’t like being so far away from the King of Death’s aura for too long, but you were deep enough into the Deadlands that I didn’t mind the lighter aura. Speaking of, can we go back now? It’s exhausting being this close to the edge.”

“So all you need to do is avoid killing the zombies, and then you can keep going without them detecting you,” Samantha said, looking relieved. “Seems simple enough!”

“You haven’t seen how some of these zombies end up mutating,” Asher snorted. “One of them spat acid. Nearly melted my face off. But yeah, I guess I can start just chopping legs off. That way they can’t follow me, and the Servants won’t be any wiser.”

“I still kind of want to try fighting one of these Servants for myself… But I suppose this is the ‘smart’ way of going about things,” Moxy sighed. “Damn, I wish I’d picked up an evolved death resistance. Hey, mind if I chat with Smiles for a moment?”

Asher shrugged and tossed Smiles over to Moxy, shaking his head at the joyful laughter coming from the severed head as Smiles soared through the air. While Moxy turned and began whispering to the head, Rulfar stepped up to him.

“Be careful, Asher,” Rulfar warned, his face still hallowed. His eyes were dark and heavy, as though something haunted him unseen. “If the King of Death is capable of creating these Servants who can mess with souls, then he must be able to do the same. This isn’t an opponent to mess around with. You need to take him out as quickly as possible.”

“Don’t worry, that’s definitely the plan,” Asher reassured him. “Though I still need to get to him first. I’ve got another two dozen miles to cover, and if the Death Aura continues to grow thicker as I go, I’m going to need to start Recalling out for breaks pretty soon.”

Satisfied with that, Rulfar moved over to join Moxy and Smiles, presumably to ensure the warrior wasn’t trying to get the location of the other Servants out of him before running off on her own in some sort of suicide attack. Glancing at the other two, Samantha moved in closer, lowering her voice.

“Asher, please tell me you’re not planning to keep Smiles around. I mean, adopting a stray cat is one thing, but a severed head? I don’t think I could get used to that.”

“Don’t worry, Smiles is definitely a temporary thing,” Asher laughed, pulling her in for a quick hug. “I’ve already promised him I’d crush his head once we’re done with all this, and from what I understand, he can’t survive out of the King of Death’s aura anyway. And seeing as killing the King of Death means no more aura, Smiles isn’t sticking around.”

“Thank the realms,” Samantha muttered into his shoulder, frowning up at him as he continued laughing. “Hey, you’ve done weirder stuff before!”

“Then I apologize for all that as well,” he grinned. “A cat, huh? I didn’t know you wanted a pet. You guys didn’t have one back at your cabin.”

“Raising Brian on my own was hard enough, I didn’t want to add anything else to the mix,” Samantha said, blushing slightly. “But I’ve always wanted a pet. Obviously now isn’t a great time, but maybe one day once things are a bit more stable.”

“Hey, I like cats,” Asher smiled. The two of them shared a quick kiss, before Smiles interrupted them with a shout.

“Oh, that looks fun! I want to know what that feels like next!”

“Yeah, no,” Asher said, reaching out and grabbing Smiles back from Moxy with a tunnel of compressed space. “Those memories stick with me.”
