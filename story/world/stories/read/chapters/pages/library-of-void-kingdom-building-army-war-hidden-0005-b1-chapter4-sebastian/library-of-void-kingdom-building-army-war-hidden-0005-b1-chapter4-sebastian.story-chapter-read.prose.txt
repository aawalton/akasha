Just before the assassination attempt on Vector…

Dawn had barely announced itself.

In a dimly lit room, the first yellow threads of morning had made its presence through the window, brushing faintly against walls.

At the center of the room, Sebastian sat cross-legged on a mat. Back straight. Eyes closed.

He was practicing.

Blood-colored energies coiled around him.

It moved like a living thing—slow and fast, appearing to move without direction, but they followed a uniform pattern—circulating around his body in measured currents. Releasing heavy pressure around it, but in a small domain. Restrained.

Then, the energy suddenly stopped all movements. The energies rushed inward, collapsing toward a single, large point, struggling as if eager to return to its source. To Sebastian.

His body shuddered as it poured back into him, disappearing beneath his skin.

He opened his eyes.

They were calm. Sharp.

Extended his aura briefly—silent, invisible—probing the adjacent room.

Vector's presence was there.Steady. Seated. Unharmed.After observing for a moment. Not finding anything suspicious. He withdrew his aura and exhaled slowly.

His gaze shifted to the side.

Three small phials rested neatly beside him, their glass catching the faint dawn light. Dark-colored liquid sloshed within them, thick and heavy.

Blood Elixirs.

They were not truly made from blood—only refined herbs and rare essences from the elixirs, though he didn't know about their content—but because they were essential to cultivating Blood Energy, the name had taken its inspiration from that.

His phials were known as Blood Elixirs III.

Sebastian knew their value too well.

They were the resources required to advance to Expert mastery in Blood Energy—which was compulsory to step into the realm of the Fourth Stage Warrior. A resource so scarce that even empires hoarded it desperately.

Basic mastery in Blood Energy was required to advance to the First Stage Warrior. After awakening Blood Energy.Intermediate mastery for the Second.Advance mastery for the Third.

After reaching First Stage Warrior Blood Elixirs I needed to advanced to the Intermediate mastery, and then Second Stage needed Blood Elixir II to advance to the Advance mastery. All in Blood Energy.

But Blood Elixirs III, the ones needed to reach the peak strength of Ethereum?

The herbs and elixirs needed to make Blood III were almost impossibly scarce.

No one could find them in the forests or Ancient Ruins. Forests, Ancient Ruins—years could pass without a single discovery. Though if one was lucky, perhaps one in a million times, they could find it. But only in Ancient Ruins.

Each empire possessed only three to five Fourth Stage Warriors at most. Even the invading Lionheart Empire and resisting Novastra Empire had five each. The smaller number of Fourth Stage Warrior existed solely because of its non-availability. Otherwise, who didn’t want to had more force? Or to become stronger and live for hundreds of years?

And yet, three of them lay beside Sebastian.

Enough to drive the Lionheart Empire mad with greed.

He shook his head faintly.

The Astrums…

The Astrum Family, the unseen force behind the Astral Alliance, one of the Astrum Forces—to all Third-stage Warriors under their command had been supplied with these resources as though they were trivial. Even now, Sebastian found it difficult to comprehend.

Even he didn’t know the number of Fourth Stage Warrior under the Astrum Forces existed. He had seen, or his father had spoken of, at least fifteen.

His Father? Also, a Fourth Stage. An Elder of Astral Alliance.And he himself would soon become one. After crossing to the Fourth Stage Warrior, the threshold for being Elder. He had reached the peak Third-Stage Warrior a year ago. This stage took two or three years at most. Each stage was subdivided into four tiers—early, mid, late, and peak—a realm he already stood at. The first three tiers had taken him five to six years, while reaching the peak had required another three years.

Pushing the thought aside, Sebastian reached for one of the phials.

Once every three months he took one for cultivating. "Provided" didn't mean it was supplied with abundance. But enough to reach a Fourth Stage Warrior if one had talent.

He uncorked it and swallowed the content of Blood Elixir III in a single motion.Heat bloomed in his chest.Forcing him to circulate Blood Energy.

He followed the method already practiced thousands of times—traversing the Blood Energy through his body's various organs, strengthening himself in the process.

Blood-colored aura didn't emerge this time. Previously, he had only been awakening its full potential to observe the complete effect of Blood Elixir III.

Tomorrow was also going to be a raid. To intercept the weapons consignment. As… The messenger had brought the intelligence and was currently relaying the details to Vector.

Better to be prepared. Though there was no need, better to be cautious than regret it later. Even if he could increase his strength by small margin.

With each circulation of the Blood Energy inside him… the time, also, rapidly passed.

Outside, Aethel’s light strengthened. The dim room brightened gradually, shadows forming inch by inch.

Then—

Crash!

The sound ripped through the air.

Sebastian’s eyes snapped open.

The circulating Blood Energy stopped instantly. Collapsing. His aura flared violently. His strength surged to its limit. Without even considering that he wouldn't be able to efficiently use Blood Elixir III's effect. He moved.

Boom!

The door to his room shattered outward, wood splintering under the force of his exit.

Sebastian did not slow.

Boom!

Another explosion of sound.

Vector’s door was obliterated as well. No knock. No hesitation.

In less than a breath, Sebastian was inside.

And Vector was in his arms.

“Uncle Sebastian…”

The faint whisper reached him just before Vector’s eyelids shut.

Sebastian froze.

He was not Vector’s blood uncle. Yet from the moment Vector was born, Sebastian had stood at his side—as his protector. His Shield. And when they were alone, Vector had always called him uncle.

He had requested many times to Vector not to do it. But the stubborn child never listened to him.

His gaze traced Vector’s body, searching desperately.

No wounds. Not even a scratch. If anything, Vector’s skin seemed to glow faintly, as though vitality lingered beneath the surface.

Sebastian pressed two fingers beneath Vector’s nose.

Breath flowed evenly.

His chest rose and fell in calm rhythm.

He checked his pulse.

Strong. Stable.

The boy’s body was warm. Breathing steady. Unconscious.

That was his conclusion.

Sigh!

Sebastian finally released the breath he’d been holding.

Still, guilt crept into his expression. Repentance. Remorse.

He had one duty—to protect Vector. And he had nearly failed in it.

Cradling him more securely, Sebastian turned toward the door.

I’ll need healers, he thought. Immediately. Just to be sure.

Before leaving, Sebastian’s eyes swept the room.

Chaos greeted him.

Shattered vases lay strewn across the floor. Pots lay broken, dusts spilled like dried blood. Chairs had cracked apart, tables lay crippled—legs snapped cleanly from their bodies.

His brows drew together.

Not because of the destruction itself, but because of what hadn’t been damaged.

The walls stood intact. The ceiling bore no cracks. Even the windows remained unbroken. Only the furnishings had suffered, as though some invisible hand had torn through the room and spared everything else.

His gaze looked down.

The floor, however, told a different story.

A straight path of ruined marble cut across it—beginning where he stood and stretching back toward the entrance. The same damage would mark his own room as well. He didn’t need to look to know that.

That path had been carved by him.

By his instantaneous movement.

Like a Teleportation.

One could argue that.

The mythical movement he had heard of. But his movement was not teleportation. Teleportation was like disappearing and appearing. But the damage clearly described that he had traversed on his feet. Distance crossed by force, not magic. And in this world no such magic exists. Those were all the mythology-stories to entertain humans.

Though the short instantaneous movement could be seen as teleportation.

Yet… that wasn’t what unsettled him most.

The furniture had been destroyed simultaneously. Not one by one. Not scattered by impact. Together. As if to alarm him. By creating the sound through the shattering and colliding of objects.

Even a Fourth Stage warrior would have damaged the walls and ceiling doing this. If together, doing one by one was totally different matter. He knew that better than anyone. His own instantaneous movement had caused damage. The destroyed marble…

And yet…

This force had not.

Sebastian’s mind replayed the moment he entered the room.

The assassin’s face—frozen. Shocked. As though he had stared directly into death.

Then—

A memory of winds that had died down the instant he entered the room.

Wind.

Is the weather windy today?

A powerful gale swirling violently through the room.

His eyes shifted toward the windows.

Outside, the sky was clear. Blue. Morning light poured in undisturbed, Aethel’s rays steady and calm.

No storm.

No wind.

Supernatural Power?

The thought felt absurd even as it formed.

Strength came from Body Refinement. Through training. Through elixirs. Through pain and endurance. That was the law of this world. Supernatural powers existed only in mythology. In Legends.

He shook his head.

Adjusting his hold on Vector, carrying him securely in his arms. He Moved forward. To had Vector checked.

But he stopped mid-way.

Seeing the blood.

His eyes moved to see its source.

The assassin entered his vision again.

Sebastian forced himself not to react. As rage simmered beneath his calm.

The man’s face was frozen in terror—eyes wide, mouth half-open, skin drained of all color. Death had claimed him before fear could fade.

Sebastian stepped forward.

Another body lay nearby. No… half a body. The lower portion lay several arm-lengths away from the upper half.

Bisected.

Cleanly.

Dead.

Sebastian’s sword had done that the instant he entered the room. The blade had passed through flesh, bone, and heart in a single motion. Through the attacker’s frozen reaction, it could be concluded that he hadn’t even seen him. Or how he cut-in half. The heart itself lay severed—split into two perfect halves.

His sword still lay in its scabbard, undisturbed. Only a trace of red liquid visible at the scabbard's entrance betrayed what had occurred. He had drawn, killed, and sheathed in a single breath—so fast the blade seemed never to have left its home.

Sebastian turned away.

I’ll return, he thought coldly. And I’ll find out who sent you.

The Lionhearts were the most likely suspects. But suspicion wasn’t enough.

He moved toward the exit.

A gaping hole marked the floor near the doorway, revealing the floor below. He felt a flicker of relief. No one else lived in this two-story house. Other than him and Vector. Explaining this scene to witnesses would have been… inconvenient. He had rented this whole building in official documents. But it was the property of the Astral Alliance.

Then he moved.

To an observer, it would have looked like teleportation. But to those on the streets, it was something else entirely.

A soft gale. His soft foot swiftly traversing through the busy streets.

A violent rush of air tore through the commercial district, racing between buildings. People felt it brush past them, tugging at clothes and hair—but saw nothing.

No one realized a Third Stage Warrior had crossed the roads at that very moment. Sebastian restrained his strength, letting his steps skim the ground lightly. The stone and cobbled roads remained intact. He did not want attention.

The scenery shifted as he moved—ornate buildings giving way to neglected stone, polished streets dissolving into cracked paths.

Concrete buildings to houses made of soil. Ragged clothes.

The Slums.

Not the heart of Carnatic City, where renowned healers resided.

But the place where the Resistance truly lived. Its base in Carnatic City.

…

A short time later… still morning.

Sebastian returned to uncover the origin of the assassin. There, among the bloods and the bisected lowered coat, he found it—a seal.

Circular. Forged from an unknown metal that gleamed dully in the morning light. He didn’t have had interest to find about the unknown metal.

At one side etched with meticulous precision— a vine-wreathed ancient gate.

After seeing the image, he was instantly clear to whom it was related too. Not to Lionhearts but a mysterious organization. Who was supporting Lionhearts.

Shock… have they already infiltrated the Resistance?

But he needed to be certain. His fingers turned the seal over.

The flip confirmed everything.

A rough circular object made on the other side. Almost like a planet. The Ethereum.

They had been compromised. This was an organization almost unknown to the world. Member and Elder these two were the ranks among the Astrum forces. And only Elder ranked figure knew about them. But his father had described about them once a time. That was why he knew about them.

But thinking the assassin was from Windstone City. He also didn’t find it shocking.
