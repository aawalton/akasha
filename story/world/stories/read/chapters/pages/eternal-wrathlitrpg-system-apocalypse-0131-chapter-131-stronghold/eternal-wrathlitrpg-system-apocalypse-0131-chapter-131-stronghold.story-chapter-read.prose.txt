After healing himself, James dashed toward the other side. There, he found Andrew struggling against his enemies. He fired his mind magic, crippling one beast at a time. The sudden attack on their flank was decisive.

The lightning bolts, coupled with mind and ice magic, killed all of their archers and mages. Now that those Anubis warriors were surrounded, the shield bearers were soon overwhelmed. After clearing the west side, James and the remaining mercenaries moved south from outside the wall. They cleared that area without any issues. Those shield bearers were weak when flanked and surrounded—their shield skill was only effective in the direction they were facing.

A chime from the system notified them they had cleared the wave. It wasn’t a perfect victory, though, as two mercenaries had been shot through the chest by archers.

[You have cleared the tenth wave.Casualties: 2. Injuries: 30. Time elapsed: 96 / 120 hours.Congratulations, you have received 500 coins.You will be teleported to a designated fort for the last wave.The last wave is in 24 hours.]

“Teleported?” Andrew tilted his head. Moreover, the last line intrigued him—last wave. That meant the next one would be the final battle.

White light enveloped the group. They felt their bodies float for a moment before landing on solid cobblestone.

Andrew observed his surroundings. Knights lined the top of a wall—not the small wall they had built, but a massive one, over five meters thick. Unlike his civilians, the knights standing atop it were orderly; their spears were sharp, their gaits firm. With spears in hand and bows on their backs, they were clearly professional defenders of the fort.

“I’ll bet they have a weapon skill or two,” Sanders commented when he saw the knights. “They’re better armed than most of us, having two weapons and all.”

“Good. We don’t have to care about defending their asses,” Xiaoming, who had just lost two of his men, grumbled.

Andrew gave a wry smile. It wasn’t the civilians’ fault that his men had died—it was because he was under-leveled.

Then, a man in full plate armor arrived, welcoming Andrew and his crew. The civilians knelt before him.

“Lord Bartholomew, we are grateful to have survived. These survivors saved us.” The leader of the civilians said.

“Go to the back. You are safe now,” he said with a nod, then turned toward James. James tightened his grip on his sword, expecting the knight to turn hostile like in the previous dungeon, but instead, the man’s smile was amicable. He thanked James and the other survivors.

He then introduced himself. “Honored survivor, my name is Count Bartholomew. You have my thanks for defending my citizens. In light of your achievement, I shall lend you my knights. Use them as you see fit to defend the north gate. I trust that your wit and prowess will defend us all.”

Then, a system notice flashed before their eyes.

[Men survived: 68. Casualties: 17. You have been granted 53 knights under your command. The ten-casualty restriction will no longer apply. The time for the next wave is 24 hours. Please build siege engines to weather the assault.]

“These are my elite knights. I entrust them to you. While casualties are inevitable, please try to keep them alive. I shall reward you generously for every life you save against the dark forces.”

“Understood,” Andrew nodded.

Soon, fifty-three knights marched out from the nearby barracks and lined up before Andrew as the count trotted back to his keep. A translucent purple barrier marked the area Andrew was supposed to defend. Just as the count had said, he only needed to hold one side of the fort.

“Seventeen? There were only two casualties!” Bernard said.

“It’s from our previous dungeon run. The system counted that one as well,” Andrew spat as the knights led them back to the barracks. This northern barrack would serve as their headquarters. Andrew frowned at the odd number of knights—losing seventeen was far from ideal. These knights looked strong, probably as capable as the Knights of Mystis from the previous dungeon.

“Alright! We’ve got over a hundred people on our side, and we don’t need to worry about these knights’ lives. Let’s clear this wave with a bang. This will be a breeze!” Bernard and Xiaoming began surveying the area as one of the knights approached Andrew. They didn’t know how wrong they were. James and Andrew’s faces turned sour. They knew about video-game logic. The more generous the game was, the more suspicious you became.

“Sir, what siege engines should we build? The count has given us enough war supplies to construct four siege engines or traps. Each siege engine requires at least four knights to man it.”

[Available siege equipment and traps:

Catapult ×100 stone (tower only)

Trebuchet ×100 stone (ground only)

Ballista ×200 bolts (tower only)

Boiling pot (wall only)

Abatis ×200 (wall + ground)

Spike traps (ground only)

Explosive arrows ×200 (wall only)]

The first four on the list were siege engines, while the others were classified as traps. James looked over at the defensive structures: one tall wall over twenty meters high and two towers capable of hosting siege engines.

He then looked over the list. Catapults seemed useless since they weren’t assaulting walls. That left the ballista for the towers. For the walls, the boiling pot and explosive arrows seemed to be the best options. Of course, they weren’t limited to one tool at a time—he could ask for four boiling pots or four sets of explosive arrows.

“Could we decide later?”

“Please do so within an hour, otherwise we won’t have enough time to set them up,” the knight nodded.

“Xiaoming, Bernard, James, Sanders—any ideas?” Andrew asked around.

“Boiling pots seem the best. They were popular in medieval warfare—along with ballista and trebuchets,” Xiaoming said.

“Ballista and explosive arrows for me,” James said.

“Wait. We can’t just dismiss abatis and spike traps. If the enemy climbs the wall, we’re screwed without them. The boiling pot only works for a limited time, and it’s doubtful it could even kill the enemy,” Bernard said.

“We can’t dismiss catapults either. We could use rocks plundered from nearby houses if we run out of stones to throw,” Sanders nodded.

“One ballista on the watchtower, please. Can I have more time to decide the rest?”

“Understood. We’ll focus on building a ballista on the left tower. Please finish your discussion within three hours—we need to work posthaste.”

“Yes. In the meantime, can I have the list of knights available to us? Can they use weapon skills, or are they all engineers?”

“We, the elite knights of Count Bartholomew, are the best of the best—capable of using weapon skills with spears, swords, and bows. We can also build siege engines from war supplies. Please don’t underestimate us. If you have any task—whether it be fighting on the front line or manning the siege engines—we can do it all.” The knight gave a salute, pride filling his very being. Andrew blinked at the knight’s expertise. They really could do it all.

“Boiling pots are out then. I would prefer these knights to fight on the front line rather than man them,” Andrew muttered. James then intervened.

“Could we have a spar? I want to see your skills. Are you as good as you claim?” James challenged the knight.

“... While one of us might not match you, mighty survivor, I believe six of us could prove to be a challenge.” The knight eyed James, giving his estimation. “Weapon skills are lethal. We can show you our weapon skills, but sparring is out of the question. We need all the strength we can get—sparring with weapon skills will leave one of us wounded or worse, dead.”

“Yes, you are right. I apologize. Then could you show me the weapon skills the knights can use?” James nodded, feeling dumb for suggesting a spar involving weapon skills. They were so arrogant that James felt the need to knock them down a peg.

The knight then called five more of his comrades, and they lined up, drawing their bows in unison. A golden glow shone from the tips of their arrows, then a rain of six arrows struck a training dummy in the barracks. Afterward, the knights holstered their bows and drew their spears. Just like before, a golden glow shone from the tips as they pierced through empty air. At last, they drew short swords from their waists and performed a horizontal sword skill.

“That’s a beginner-level weapon skill…” James muttered after seeing the lukewarm display. “The Knights of Mystis in the previous dungeon were at least novice level.”

Andrew nodded. Just as James had said, they were jack-of-all-trades — even newbie survivors could reach their level for less than two thousand coins. The only thing prohibiting them from doing so was the weapons.

“Sir knights, thank you for showing us your wonderful coordination and broad array of skills. I must ask: are these weapons for sale? I would like to arm my survivors with them as well.”

“You can have these weapons at the barracks. But we needed years of training before we could use weapon skills with them. Without this bonding, these bows and spears won’t have any effect.”

“System, what do they mean?” Andrew asked.

[Weapons crafted or gained from the knights are not registered with the system. Survivors cannot use weapon skills with them.]

“Figures it would not be so easy,” Andrew sighed. “Alright. For the next siege setup, we will have catapults on the towers, explosive arrows, and trebuchets.”

James tilted his head; he didn’t mind whatever Andrew decided — he never relied on the knights in the first place. But the other two leaders didn’t share the same view.

“Why did you buy trebuchets and catapults? They’re useless.”

“Maybe, but I want all long-range equipment available. Among them, these four are the only ones with long-range ability. We can have more explosive arrows, or maybe we can place four trebuchets on the ground, but that means leaving the towers empty.”

James nodded, accepting Andrew’s logic. He wanted to thin the herd before they arrived at the wall. Andrew had a feeling each of these siege engines was geared toward specific enemies, so he would love to have them all.

“Alright. Your order has been received. I’m sorry, but this work can only be done by us — there is nothing else you can do. Would you like to have supper? You can rest inside the barracks in the meantime. We shall need your strength when the horde of dark forces arrives.”

With the strategy meeting finished, all they could do was wait. Andrew had something else in store, though, as he approached Delila and James. Supplies from the count were not the only thing they had here — they still had the shop. Time for some modern weaponry to take the field.
