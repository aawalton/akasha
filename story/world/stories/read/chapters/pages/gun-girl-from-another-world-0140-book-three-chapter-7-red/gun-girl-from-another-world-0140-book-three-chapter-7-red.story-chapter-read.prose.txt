Chapter 7

Red

I don’t think Yorin really comprehends the concept of the thermobaric effect. However, a word that she doesn’t understand, coming from me, and then followed by “detonations,” seems to have caused her close enough to actual palpitations that, after I said it, she, almost on autopilot, drank the rest of the tea and left to go home.

… I wonder what kind of image flitted through her imagination. When you actually know what something is, it's so much harder to picture it being anything else.

Well, she returns with the morning, fully refreshed and composed as if nothing had been amiss the night before. That's probably a Priest skill, allowing them to keep their cool when everyone around them is freaking out so they can be a suitable rock in the storm for their flock. Or, y'know, maybe she just has practice.

Ayre and I help Elara with the morning chores while Korrigan plays with the chickens, then the rest of us gather in the yard after the matron leaves. She needs to file her report on the morning's harvests with the guild, but we have a briefing waiting for us.

Yorin had probably intended to tell us about the troubles with the Forest Cavern Dungeon when we arrived yesterday, but catching up had consumed all of our time. While Yorin and I had some time together that evening, it was after everyone else had turned in, and she wanted to cover the details with the entire team, all at once. Well, that time has finally come.

Immediately of note is the satchel she is carrying with her, but she neither opens nor references it before she starts.

“The Forest Cavern was your first dungeon, Remmi, and only a startlingly brief time ago,” she opens, directing her attention to me. “It is the only dungeon in the Serazin Province, but it is known as a very small one of low threat. Many times, it has been considered for demotion to Iron-rank, but Lord Ettia has beaten the efforts back each time, as it would mean a great devaluing of the territory.”

She turns to motion to her side, not to anything in particular, just for emphasis. “Because it is also a source of semi-rare alchemical plants, it has economic value, as well. Indeed, it is one of the safest and most reliable sources of many flora that simply cannot be deliberately cultivated. As a consequence of all of these converging traits, it would not be controversial to say that the guild knows more about this one dungeon than it does of nearly any other. In as much as one may know well any dungeon, of course.”

Again, however, Yorin turns back to me. “You never saw it as it was, Remmi. When you came to its threshold, it was in the grips of Corruption, barren of life and struggling with the last of its strength. You purified its core, and that should have allowed it to recover.”

“Except it didn’t,” I insert myself into the lecture. “Almost as soon as it was reopened, you started getting reports from confused adventurers who weren’t finding what they expected.” I even have on my serious face. And this is serious. If something’s wrong with how I’ve been doing purification, I’ve got a major backlog of problems that's about to come toppling down on top of us.

Yorin looks serious, too, as she nods, but that’s much more common for her than it is for me. “Precisely. Accounts confirm that the verdant abundance of the cave has returned with a relish, but the specific species seem to have changed. The monsters, as well, are not the same as they have been for so long.”

Ayre visibly mulls that over for a moment before speaking up. “Is it possible it’s because of the Corruption? Maybe because it was so close to falling, its injuries ran deeper than we thought. After such a traumatic experience, anything else would almost be expected to come back a little different, wouldn’t it?”

“Oh, right, that’s a good guess!” I bob my head eagerly at that. “Like some of the data’s been damaged and had to be reconstructed, and we’re just seeing the parts where it had to fill in the gaps!”

My expression immediately collapses as I see Yorin’s. She’s nodding, but I can already tell she knows it’s not so simple. “That would be a reasonable assumption, and it was at the top of the possibilities we were considering.” And then her focus homes right back to me again. “However, when I sent you that letter, it was because we had received undeniable evidence to the contrary.”

I can’t resist my compulsion to swallow sharply underneath that gaze. “... Something that pointed in my direction?”

And only now, instead of answering with words, does she lift up that satchel and pull something from it, one of several somethings to judge from the remaining weight in the bag. It’s only about the size of a baseball, and is a brilliant, crisp red that catches the sunlight where it peeks through its mouth-watering amber curtain.

All four of us present recognize it for what it is immediately, but very nearly no one else in this world might. At the very least, no one else in this region. That’s because I was the one who introduced them, and the only ones I’ve shared them with have been my party, Yorin and the Hagasu children. Even of that group, only Yorin and Ayre know the recipe. Not that it’s particularly complex. Someone could have reverse-engineered it in fairly short order, but it wasn’t anyone here, and I seriously doubt those kids went and opened up a sweets shop.

Yorin waits until all of our eyes are on the item in her hand, and expressions of recognition have filled our faces. “An adventurer made it out of the dungeon with a stash of these. They were all confiscated, of course, and the man was interrogated. He confirmed that no one brought them into the dungeon with them, but that they were recovered from the dungeon, itself.”

Korrigan is quite lost, but Ayre was there with me in the Forest Cavern Dungeon. It was our second mission together, and our first culling. “It … remembered something like that?”

“The situation is far more dire than a mere stolen recipe,” Yorin corrects the archer, and then lobs the apple to me, confident I will catch it. “Identify it.”

I hold it up before me and do exactly that.

ARCANA CANDY APPLE

-----------------------

A beloved treat for all ages, this deceptively simple sweet takes a crisp, tart apple and submerges it in sticky, sugary caramel to create its quintessential autumn taste! Because it was created from a sample of an Essence dish, it has been infused with Arcana to imbue it with much of the same power.

Consuming this item grants the following effects:

Restores 50% of maximum health points

Restores 50% of maximum magic points

Increases maximum health points by 25% for one hour

Increases maximum magic points by 25% for one hour

Increases chance to repel doctors for twenty-four hours

Increases chance to attract dentists for twenty-four hours

I feel my gaze flatten across my face as I read the window. Yeah, sure, I’m glad you’re having your fun mocking my cultural proverbs, but do you have to do it while jabbing your finger in my face like that? You might as well just have hung a sign on the entire description that reads, It’s Remmi’s fault!

The others are clearly waiting for answers. “Well?” Korrigan finally pipes up. “What does it say?”

I turn to her, holding the apple in my hand. “It’s exactly what it looks like. The dungeon is making copies of my candy apples. The effects aren’t completely the same, but they’re very close.” I glance back to the priestess who works for the Throne. “… I hope you paid that man you rob- confiscated these from.”

“Do not change the subject, Remmi,” she immediately answers with that tone that says she’s not in the mood for jokes. “You left the equivalent of a heavenly artifact with a dungeon core, and no one has been able to cull it since.” Yorin steps right up to me and pokes me hard enough in the collarbone that it actually hurts. “The Empress sent you to this province to protect the place of her birth! She gave you her family land! Do you believe that there is anywhere you may run if you repay her by getting it consumed by an unstoppable dungeon?!”

“Hey, surely I can only be held, like, fifty percent responsible for this, at most!” I try, hands raised in a plea for mercy.

“Well, if I may fulfill my role as your advisor, I recommend very strongly that you do not cease at fifty percent of fixing it.”

Ayre’s face twists up in confusion. “Wait, if you’re only half responsible, where are you pinning the other half?”

“I don’t know!” I desperately throw my arms up in the air. “Maybe recovering from Corruption made it extra suggestible, like how you can’t have someone just coming out of anesthesia sign contracts! Or maybe it was a side-effect of the purification technique the Essence gave me that it failed to elaborate on! Maybe all of the people telling me, ‘Oh, it’s common practice to leave an offering,’ should have clarified that you’re supposed to keep it to gift cards and socks! I’m just saying that I shouldn’t be the sole scapegoat for catastrophe just because I was trying to be thoughtful with my gift-giving!”

Yorin sighs, and it seems like some of the fury dissipates. I hope that’s not just my imagination. “It is true that you did not act in malice, but with compassion and forethought to the best of the information you possessed. You do not deserve to be gallowed by the whole of that burden. Even I could not guess at such consequence when you first told me of your offering.”

She takes a step toward us again, however. “Nevertheless, that is the situation that we have found ourselves in, and you do not yet seem to have realized its gravity. I say again, Remmi, no one has successfully culled the Forest Cavern since its recovery. Do you remember what happens if that continues for too long?”

It’s not been so long since I’ve last had to consider the possibility. The mayor of Giri Village had blocked off access to the local dungeon in the process of trying to pressure the adjoining oni tribe into agreeing to his hostile takeover of their lands. When we got that out of the way, we found that the energy of the dungeon had built up, not yet to dangerous levels, but certainly getting there. If it had been allowed to overflow without being culled …

“The dungeon will reach critical mass,” I answer, “triggering an expansion into the surrounding area. Even if the first time doesn’t catch anything essential, if it’s truly unstoppable, it will continue to do so, growing stronger each time, until it swallows Dabun Village, and eventually the entire province.”

But then my own expression freezes, stiffens, and then twists into confusion. “Wait, no one has succeeded in culling it? We have silvers, golds, and even platinums at the guild, and no one has pulled it off? Not even you?”

The priestess frowns and crosses her arms. “If it were something even I could not accomplish, I assure you, we would not be sending a team of Silvers, even one headed by a Hero, to deal with it. If it exceeded even my abilities, the appropriate answer would be to try to destroy it entirely or seal it away. The devastation from a ruptured arcane font would be preferable to the inexorable creep of erasure. No, the attempts have been made by established Bronze-rank teams that are well-familiar with the dungeon’s normal mannerisms. We are fortunate that there have been no fatalities as a result, but the guild has forbidden further deployments until the matter is resolved.”

Korrigan looks between all of the taller people speaking. “Then why were we called to come take care of it? Like Hero Remmi said, there are higher ranks a lot closer than Lurei, and shouldn’t we be focusing on the barrier against the Demesne?”

“Simple,” I answer for Yorin as I shift my weight to one foot. “You break it, you buy it. Besides, if this really is a consequence of how we’ve been purifying the regions, we have to know, and we have to figure out why it went sideways, or we’re going to just reproduce the problem all the way to the northern coast. We’ll just have replaced one unstoppable expansion with another, and then Mother Dearest will never let me hear the end of it.”

“... I think the unstoppable expansion part is a little more important than the part about you getting scolded until the end of time,” Ayre criticizes my priorities with a frown.

“If you understand the existential nature of the situation,” Yorin refocuses the conversation, “then you understand what must be done.” She places her finger against me once more, though thankfully not with so much force this time. “Remmi, you must fix this. You must determine what went awry and correct it. Not just the village, but the whole of the Empire may depend on it. With all of the authority placed upon me by the Throne, Itself, I declare this your most primary and immediate mandate.”

I give her a solemn nod. For once, there are no jokes, no silly comments, no redirections. “I’ll do my best, Yorin. I promise.”
