Approximately forty-seven years have passed.

A less perceptive individual might claim it has been closer to thirteen minutes.

I reject that interpretation on principle.

In retrospect, dramatically seating myself atop the palace with ample time remaining was a strategic miscalculation.

There is nothing to do up here.

No dancing. No violence. No parkour. And certainly no dramatic explosions.

I cannot even descend temporarily to occupy myself. That would undermine the integrity of the pose.

Sitting atop a palace roof and waiting for midnight is a commitment. If I abandon it, my celestial family will absolutely accuse me of impatience.

Slander.

Everyone knows I am the embodiment of calm.

A monument to stillness.

A stone untouched by the tides of time.

Ten seconds remain.

Thank the stars.

Midnight arrives.

My ears twitch at the sudden sound of boots scraping against—

Roof tiles?

No. Surely not.

I turn my head slowly, deliberately, bracing myself for whatever nonsense the Tutorial has decided to produce.

In hindsight, I was insufficiently braced.

Because occupying the adjacent palace roof—packed shoulder-to-shoulder like a particularly grim military parade—is an entire platoon of undead.

Zombie knights in rusted plate. Skeleton spearmen, newly upgraded and somehow more ambitious than before. And—most concerning of all—what appear to be zombie mages.

The issue? The palace roofs are both slanted and not especially spacious.

Which means—

The result is immediate.

A cascading avalanche of undead pours off the roof in a waterfall of flailing limbs, clattering armor, and deeply confused expressions.

Ah.

So the horde quite literally spawns.

No dramatic emergence from the soil. No ominous fog curtain. They simply manifest in close proximity to the nearest user, like seagulls responding to the opening of a pristine bag of chips.

Tragic.

The lucky ones plummet directly to the courtyard below.

They land with wet thuds and brittle cracks, skeletons bursting apart on impact, zombies splattering with enough force to make structural reconsiderations necessary.

None of them move afterward.

At least it was quick.

The less fortunate ones fare worse.

Some bounce off angled roofs, ricocheting downward in a grotesque pinball simulation.

Others strike ledges and corners, shedding limbs with alarming enthusiasm before continuing their descent.

One particularly ambitious zombie mage manages to impale itself cleanly through the abdomen on a gilded rooftop spire several meters down. It flails weakly, suspended and deeply reconsidering its life choices.

I attempt to suppress a giggle.

I fail.

Spectacularly.

The stars are no better.

Uncontrolled laughter floods my mind, overlapping and chaotic. Mine is, unfortunately, the loudest.

I cannot even fault the System for this design choice.

That was objectively hilarious.

A handful of undead remain on the roof, clinging precariously to angled tiles.

They do not share my appreciation.

A skeleton spearman attempts to throw its spear.

Its feet slip.

It tumbles backward without succeeding.

A zombie knight, fueled entirely by spite and catastrophic confidence, charges toward me from several dozen meters away.

It takes three steps.

Then gravity asserts itself.

It disappears over the edge.

I giggle harder.

The remaining undead, having witnessed the catastrophic failure of their peers, deliberate for exactly zero seconds before making the same decision.

Forward.

They take perhaps three steps collectively.

Then gravity collects its due.

A few more meaty thuds. A scattering of bone fragments. The faint metallic clang of armor meeting courtyard stone.

And just like that, the entirety of the nightly horde has perished without me lifting a single finger.

It takes me an embarrassing number of seconds to properly calm down.

I wipe a tear from the corner of my eye.

Now that is comedy.

Well played, Tutorial.

Ten out of ten.

No points. No experience.

Frankly?

Irrelevant.

I fully intend to convert every remaining hostile entity in this poorly disguised hallway into numbers on my status screen. A few thousand lost here will not meaningfully alter the final tally.

An acceptable sacrifice.

I rise, stretch languidly, and begin descending from the palace roof.

Time to work.

Specifically: accelerating the conversion of Zone Five into a scenic ashen wasteland devoid of un-life.

I do not make it out of the city before a golden window interrupts my trajectory.

Probably.

Far ahead of me, flames consume the distant treeline with enthusiastic dedication as I sprint toward it.

Notifications stack relentlessly in my periphery—undead caught in the blaze, generously converting themselves into experience and points—but I relegate them to background noise, much like a speedrunner ignoring any sidequest containing more than three seconds of unskippable dialogue.

The real issue is Zone Four.

Healthy forest. Wet forest. Offensively hydrated forest.

No dead leaves. No fallen timber. No suspiciously brittle undergrowth. Just dense, living greenery pressed together in a smug wall of chlorophyll.

In other words: very difficult to ignite.

Tragic.

Zone Four will be the true obstacle to my entirely reasonable and well-balanced plan of erasing all biomass from the Tutorial.

A crusade motivated purely by architectural dissatisfaction.

And tutorial points.

Then—naturally—I activate my starfire shell and dive straight into it.

The world becomes orange and screaming heat for half a heartbeat.

I emerge on the other side without slowing and continue running.

Obviously.

Even at my current speed, the combination of dry forest and concentrated starfire is more than sufficient. Shrubs ignite as I pass. Bark blackens. Resin pops and hisses. Within seconds, my trail blossoms into a growing inferno that eagerly consumes everything around it.

My recent Stamina gains have pushed my regeneration into absurd territory. The thirty percent gravity reduction is effectively free, and even sustained starfire output barely dents my reserves.

Which means—

I run.

And run.

Anything unfortunate enough to exist in my path is cleanly bisected by the replacement blade I forged earlier. Roots, trunks, undead remnants, everything parts neatly before me.

Another hour passes before I reach the fog wall marking the boundary where I first entered Zone Five.

Behind me stretches a straight line of fire cutting clean through the forest like a surgical incision.

I nod in approval.

Then I pivot and begin again.

This time, I run perpendicular lines.

Then diagonals.

Then offset parallels.

A grid forms across the zone. Intersecting corridors of flame that ensure no pocket of biomass escapes eventual incineration.

It takes time.

When the stars fade with the coming dawn, I relocate to an already-burned section of forest and find a suitably large rock.

I refuse to repeat my earlier mistake of sleeping on solid stone.

A futon of starlight forms beneath me, layered, structured, and aggressively comfortable.

Nearly two thousand Stamina vanishes in the process.

Worth it.

I curl up atop my immaculate construct and close my eyes.

Behind me, the forest continues to burn.

When I open my eyes, the sun is high overhead.

Mid-afternoon, roughly.

I stare at it.

I blink once.

Then I stretch—slow, deliberate, and deeply feline—before settling back down onto my aggressively comfortable starlight futon and immediately going back to sleep.

There is, after all, nothing left to do but wait for Zone Five to finish immolating itself.

Productivity through delegation.

Unfortunate.

Nearly night again.

Good.

Plenty of time.
