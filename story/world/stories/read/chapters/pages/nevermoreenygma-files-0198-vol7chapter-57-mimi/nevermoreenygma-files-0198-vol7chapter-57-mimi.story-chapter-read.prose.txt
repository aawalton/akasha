Chapter 57

Mimi

2079.

Mai just had returned from an especially tiring mission: capturing a dragon that now remained in one of the island’s special cages because it was euphoric. Those had been difficult days.

For starters, how did it even show up?

Creatures like dragons and other animals were one of the biggest headaches on a global scale. If something small turned up, fine—no problem, governments could offer some explanations and save face. But it was a different story when a fantasy creature appeared. Headlines like “Dragon Spotted in the Atlas Mountains” wouldn’t look very good.

When something like that happened, it was best to quickly mobilize several teams and get to work. The most plausible theory was that dragons had been part of the world millions of years ago alongside the dinosaurs. The fact that there were feys who possessed characteristics of such creatures—like Rein herself—or who shared mixed traits of mammalian anthropomorphism and reptilian features clearly indicated that there were some missing links that would eventually have to be clarified.

The most likely explanation to dragon appearing from nowhere was that some kind of rift in space-time, caused by the Dark Events, could bring creatures from another time, possibility of hibernation was ruled out if there were no legends from centuries past that mentioned dragons in the place. The problem lay in calming the creature and putting it to sleep for transport.

Ancient legends claimed of a type of dragon that could control others, but these were only the ones born from blue eggs. Whether the gypsy legends were true or not, such a creature had never been found, and so they could only rely on brute force and a strategic attack to prevent the dragon from causing damage if it approached a settlement.

And that operation had taken too long. It took two days before they were finally able to find its weak spot and put it to sleep.

Mai was exhausted.

Upon returning, she decided to take a break and use the main base’s mixed showers instead of her apartment’s.

The warm water ran over her body, relaxing her and slowly easing the fatigue in her muscles. The sound of droplets hitting the floor echoed soothingly in the room. Mai closed her eyes, letting the steam envelop her, completely lost in the sensation. She really needed it, she felt like every part of her body ached. It was best for her to take a few days off.

Suddenly, she heard footsteps approaching.

She didn’t move, it was common for anyone to enter the showers at that time. The trust among Nevermore agents was so strong that mixed showers caused no discomfort. And so far, it had been the same for her.

When she opened her eyes, a girl was standing there, just a few meters away.

Mai gulped.

She recognized her.

Mimi.

A woman with red eyes, that stood out against her black-and-red hair, and a pink and red tail that swayed naturally. Seeing her, Mai felt something quicken in her chest.

“Hello,” Mimi said with a calm smile.

Mai, who hadn’t expected anyone else, blushed immediately. The water ran over her skin, yet she suddenly felt vulnerable, as if something in the air had shifted. It was hard to explain, but seeing Mimi naked stirred something in her stomach, an unsettling sensation.

She couldn’t lie to herself. It was arousal. Mai couldn’t take her eyes off her.

Mimi was undeniably beautiful, but what made her even more captivating was the calm she exuded.

“Hello,” Mai replied, almost a whisper, unsure how to continue. She felt flushed, uncertain whether it was due to the situation or the woman before her.

They remained there for a few moments in an unusual calm. Mimi was standing a little way off, but close enough that they could both use the umbrella shower. The water still fell in a gentle stream over them, but for Mai, everything seemed to center on Mimi’s presence. Occasionally, their eyes met, and Mai felt a tension she couldn’t explain.

“Mai, right? I’ve seen your photos in the files.”

“Aha, Mai Izumi.” Mai nodded.

“Sorry, I didn’t introduce myself. I’m Mii. Or Mimi. Whatever you prefer. Pleasure,” Mimi said, keeping her tone relaxed. “How are you? Back from a mission?”

“Good, just a little tired. The thing was… difficult,” Mai replied, feeling the blush on her face persist. Despite the tension, she tried to keep the conversation light, like any other trivial locker room chat.

But something in the atmosphere shifted, and Mimi, with an expression moving from calm to curious, stepped a little closer to Mai. At first, Mai didn’t understand what she was doing, but then Mimi began sniffing, as if testing something in the air. A shiver ran down Mai’s body.

Did she smell bad? No… that wasn’t it.

Was it because of the dragon she had captured? She wondered if she still carried the beast’s scent, but when Mimi’s gaze fixed on her neck, she realized the girl was just a few centimeters away. Was this normal for Mimi? Her heart started racing, and her body tensed uncontrollably.

“W-what… what are you doing?” Mai asked, her voice a little broken. The closeness made her feel vulnerable. She wasn’t used to such an invasion of her personal space.

Mimi, noticing her discomfort, quickly stepped back, as if reacting to the sudden proximity. The warmth in her face indicated she too was a little embarrassed.

“I… I’m sorry,” Mimi said softly, her voice slightly nervous.

“Do I… smell bad?”

The atmosphere was tense, stretched by the sudden closeness, but something in Mimi’s words made Mai feel that this was a misunderstanding.

“No, sorry. It’s not that… It’s just that…” Mimi hesitated. She paused, then stepped slightly closer, her eyes sparkling with peculiar curiosity. “It’s just… you have a familiar scent,” Mimi said, as if the truth had slipped out on its own. “A scent I’ve smelled a long time ago. It’s strange… but I recognize it.”

Mai felt a knot form in her throat. What did that mean?… what was Mimi referring to?

In that moment, the atmosphere grew even more uncomfortable, as if the air had thickened. Words failed her, and Mai couldn’t stop thinking about what had just happened. She didn’t understand what was going on.

Mimi, seeming to notice Mai’s confusion, smiled nervously.

“And… your hips,” Mimi said. “I love them. Reminds me of someone.”

The comment surprised Mai, leaving her even more flushed. How was one supposed to react to that? Mimi then turned, red as a tomato.

Mimi frowned as if she were confused with herself.

“W-well… I’m going,” Mimi said with a light smile, slipping out of the shower barely wrapped in a towel she took of the pile.

Mai stayed there, alone under the water, lost in a sea of confusing thoughts. What had really happened?

That same night, after another shower in her apartment, the heat and lustful thoughts Mai had felt quickly dissipated.

Calm had returned to her body, but the encounter in the showers had planted seeds of intrigue in her brain. The feeling of being so close to Mimi, had left her thinking more than she wanted. That fey girl was hot.

She’d done other things for the rest of the day, but that encounter had left her truly worried. Did she like her? Well, she couldn’t deny it. Mimi, Lizbeth, and Shin hadn’t left her mind since the day she’d seen them in those photos. But now, seeing one of those people right in front of her—and naked, to boot—had made her feel really strange.

On top of that… what did Mimi’s gesture mean? And the comment about her hips? Or did that just mean Mai had a fat ass?

“Stop thinking… dumb,” Mai sighed in bed and put the tablet in front of her face with her personal report of the mission.

She hadn’t experienced such sensations for a long time, and she didn’t know how to handle them. That was why she had been working on the personal report for the archives. Just trying to cool off her mind.

Mai carried a tablet with the data from the last mission. She needed to deliver it to Fermi, one of the Aeons responsible for compiling mission records. It was a simple task and could have been done from her room, but she needed something to occupy her mind, something to distract her from the whirlwind of emotions.

She took the elevators down, go out the building and headed toward the archive area.

As she walked down one of the hallways, she briefly stopped in front of a slightly open door. She heard voices, some familiar and others that made her stop completely.

Rein and Noki were talking to someone.

Mai, hearing the third voice, immediately recognized Mimi. For some reason, her curiosity pushed her to stay still and listen, even though she knew it wasn’t the right thing to do.

Through the crack in the door, she could make out the three feys inside the office. It seemed like an important conversation, one Mai had no right to hear, but the words that reached her were unmistakable.

“I can’t believe you left mom Liz alone all this time again,” Rein said, her tone not violent, but her worry clearly marked in her voice. Noki nodded, a similar expression of concern on her face.

Mimi, for her part, took a deep breath before responding.

“What I have to resolve with Liz is something between us. We’re not fighting, it’s just a time of rest. I need time,” Mimi replied, her voice low and sounding weary.

Mai couldn’t help tensing at that phrase. What was happening between Lizbeth and Mimi? She had heard fragments of conversations, rumors, but… She sat silently, not moving, waiting for the conversation to continue. Suddenly, she felt like that nosy neighbor from so long ago. Well, spying had also been part of her life. She couldn’t help overhearing certain things, even if she didn’t like it.

“That rest period has lasted over 70 years, mom,” Rein said, with a tone reflecting not only concern but also exhaustion.

Mimi remained silent for a moment, and it was then that Mai noticed the sadness in her voice.

“It’s not that simple, alright?” Mimi said, sighing. “I want to resolve things, but I can’t until I fix my situation. What’s happening depends on me alone, and I need time.”

Noki, always direct, didn’t take long to ask what Mai had already suspected: “What are you hiding? Why can’t you tell us what’s really going on?”

Mimi, it seemed, had anticipated the question. Her answer was a mix of sadness and frustration. “I can’t tell anyone… yet. It’s something I have to resolve on my own. If I could, I would have already done it. But I can’t.”

Rein and Noki were silent, clearly frustrated, but also worried. How could they help her if they didn’t even know what was happening? Rein, in her protective nature, insisted once again:

“But, what are you hiding? Why can’t you trust us? We’re family.”

Silence filled the room for a moment. Mimi sighed. “Because parents have secrets. We wouldn’t be parents otherwise,” she said softly.

Mai heard footsteps and noise in the room and peered in. She saw Mimi approaching Rein and Noki and hugging them, a mixture of tenderness and pain in her expression.

Mai felt a knot form in her stomach as she watched.

Seeing her there with Rein and Noki helped her take her mind off her feelings for a bit. Those girls were truly family, and they must have been going through something at the time.

Mimi was an answer that gave no answers, but in a way, it said everything. The secrets, the unspoken reasons, the weight of untold decisions… and the complicity of those who love you most. Mimi couldn’t tell them everything, whatever that was, not yet, and not in the way they expected. Parents, like everyone else, carry their own burden of secrets.

The conversation gradually faded. Rein and Noki, though they wanted to press further, knew there was nothing more to do for the moment. Mimi was clear about what she needed: time.

Mai stepped away from the door in silence, her heart somewhat heavy. She had heard too much. She felt guilty for eavesdropping on such an intimate conversation, yet at the same time, she couldn’t shake the feeling that it had also been a wake-up call for her.

Not all problems have immediate answers, just as Miho had once told her.

She walked down the hallway, the weight of what she had heard following her, thinking about the situation Mimi was facing. And Lizbeth… where did she fit into all of this? The two women seemed trapped in some kind of emotional crossroads. Well, to start with, what was Lizbeth like?

Despite having seen her in photos, Mai couldn't help wondering what Lizbeth would be like.

Mai sighed. She would have liked to meet Lizbeth, but it seemed Lizbeth did not want to be part of Nevermore. She was some kind of adventurer and traveler, having spent the past years moving through disaster-stricken areas, helping people. That was all Mai knew. Well… and that she was beautiful.

She handed the tablet to Dirac, Fermi’s twin, since Fermi was elsewhere, and returned to her apartment.

As she retraced her steps, she suddenly froze. Rounding a corner of the complex toward the building, she looked toward the plaza of the base and saw Mimi sitting on the steps leading to the fountain decorated with crows. The street was empty at that hour, and she could hear a low kind of murmur.

Mai extended her antennas and, hiding so that her glow wouldn’t give her away, activated echolocation.

“Sorry. Sorry.”

Mimi was crying for some reason. Mai froze.

What should she do? Pretend she hadn’t seen anything and return to the building? Or act casual and try to comfort her?

What the hell am I doing? she wondered.

They had exchanged barely a few words, yet Mimi seemed genuinely sad. In her own experience, crying alone sometimes brought relief, but other times she would have wished someone was nearby to console her.

She was about to deactivate her echolocation when she heard a beep. Mimi’s phone.

Mimi tried to calm herself and answered it. She still looked tearful, but her voice tried to hide it.

“Hey love. What’s wrong?”

Eh?

“No, I’m still here in the island.”

Was that Lizbeth?

“Hmm… yes, once I’m done I’ll do it. Don’t worry.”

Mimi stood up but began walking in the opposite direction from Mai. Mai was about to follow when…

“What are you doing?”

“Ah!”

“Ah! Why are you yelling?! That’s rude!”

Next to her was Alexia. The cat-girl from the technology division with black hair and blue eyes. She was wearing sports clothes and was quite sweaty—surely she had been running.

She had given her a good scare. Alexia really was silent like a cat.

“What are you doing?”

“Sorry, you scared me.”

Mai looked toward where Mimi had been, but she had already disappeared, and echolocation no longer detected her.

Three days later, Mimi left. Mai did not see her around the island, at least not for a while. It was as if she had vanished but Rein told her that she also was on missions, Mai though for herself perhaps Mimi was also in search of her own path, the solution she so desperately needed. During those days, Mai tried to ignore the emptiness Mimi had left in her mind.

Yet she knew that life on the island would not pause for anyone’s secrets or personal matters.

Mai, as always, focused on her missions, leaving her feelings… romantic? Platonic?… in the background.

Whatever she was feeling, the work helped keep those thoughts from overwhelming her too much. And so she didn't see her for a long time. Their assignments kept them in different places all the time, and Mai figured Mimi probably didn't even remember that encounter in the showers.
