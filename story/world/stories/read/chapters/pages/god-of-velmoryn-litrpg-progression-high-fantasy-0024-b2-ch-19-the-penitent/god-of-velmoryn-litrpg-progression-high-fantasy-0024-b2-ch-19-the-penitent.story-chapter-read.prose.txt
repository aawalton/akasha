“Finally, it’s over,” Gerion said, wiping the sweat from his face. “How long has it been? Six… seven months since we started building new houses?”

His friend, the one missing three fingers, nodded. His gaze swept across the three compounds they had finished. Each house had five empty rooms, still smelling of fresh timber and stone dust. The buildings stood on short columns made of stone and the strange hardened mixture Gundir had created, something like a clay that locked the stones together.

A few Velmoryns from the Brown and Green Tribes had already settled into their new homes ahead of the full migration. Most had come to help with construction, but some simply wanted to secure the best spots, believing those who arrived first would naturally be allowed to claim first.

“They’re all coming today, huh?” the two-fingered man muttered. “All five tribes united as one…”

Gerion nodded, but said nothing. He took in the sight of the new buildings, all connected by packed-earth paths, forming clean compounds. The people from different tribes would be distributed among these houses so no group of tribesmen would be allowed to gather and settle together. That way, they would open up to one another faster, and their sense of belonging would shift - less tied to their old tribes and more toward being part of Eriavel.

“Why didn’t we get new houses?” the two-fingered man grumbled after a moment. “These are so much better than our homes.”

“Stop complaining,” Gerion said with a faint smile. “I’m just glad it’s over. We can finally go back to our old work.”

“Hey, Gerion… I don’t want to go back to what I used to do,” the man said.

“Why?” Gerion frowned, but then his expression brightened as he remembered Tekla’s words. “Are you going to become a builder? But you’re missing three fingers. I had to help with everything that involved nails.”

Gerion let out a booming laugh, though he calmed quickly when his friend didn’t return it.

“I always hated gathering herbs and berries in the forest,” the two-fingered man said, smiling awkwardly. “It made me feel useless, especially when most of the other men hunted beasts or trained to protect the tribe. Gerion, have you ever felt the gaze of someone who is looking down on you? Whenever they passed by me, they’d look at me as though I was worthless… but the way our people are changing now… I never thought the day would come when we would live peacefully together. It makes me believe in myself…”

“I’m not sure about peace,” Gerion said, eyes drifting to one of the wooden plates lying on the ground. A knot in the timber, dark rings circling like a target, bothered him for reasons he couldn’t quite grasp. “Have you seen how strict Mirion is when choosing warriors?”

“What warriors? You mean the regular army everyone is talking about?”

“Yes,” Gerion said with a nod. “Why would we need an army in the forest where only our kin and the Velthwyn live… unless we’re preparing for war?”

The two fell silent. Gerion finally sighed and grabbed a brush made from bird feathers.

“Come on. We should paint the roof before sunset. If I’m late again, my woman’s going to nag me,” Gerion said with a full-body shiver, though he still smiled - one of those warm smiles that only comes when someone thinks of home and the comfort waiting there.

But before he could reach for the jar of red paint, several Velmoryns approached with quick, urgent steps. Gerion growled, expecting another group of busybodies coming to bother him, but the newcomers moved right past him without so much as a glance. Normally, they’d stop, inspect the progress, and step inside the new houses just to look around. It was something that genuinely irritated him. But being ignored for once startled him.

“Where are you rushing?” he called after them.
