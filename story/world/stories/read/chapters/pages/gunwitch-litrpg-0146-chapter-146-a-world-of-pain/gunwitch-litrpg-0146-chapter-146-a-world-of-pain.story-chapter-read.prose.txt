The gate was still open, but no one was outside. The street lights were off, but thankfully the moon provided plenty of illumination. We walked to the main square and I noticed several of the houses had lights on inside. It made me wonder in which of them Vasil would be.

My eyes drifted to the biggest and tallest building by far. It dominated the square and was made out of red brick and what appeared to be sandstone for the decorative details on the triangular face in the center. I’m sure that has a name, but I’m no [Architect], so I have no idea what that would be called. It looked like a pointy roof from the front, but wasn’t a roof, alright. On the left side it had a tower with a copper spire on top. Yes, I do know what a spire is.

Since that looked like it was probably the most important building, it made sense to me that Vasil would be there. It was probably the town hall or something similar to it, so Vasil would feel right at home, considering his experience at the king’s court.

I nodded at it. “That’s where we’re going. That’s where we’ll find Vasil.”

As we walked up to the heavy wooden doors set into a pointed arch, Vitek stopped me. I was about to say something mean, but he pushed one of the doors open and bowed.

“My Lady,” he said with a big grin.

I almost rolled my eyes, but was able to control myself. He was probably trying to be nice and gentlemanly. I mean, he actually was nice and gentlemanly. Not everyone is trying to mock you, Hecate, I reminded myself.

When I entered the building I expected to see people, but there didn’t appear to be anyone around. It also didn’t look impressive. The ceilings weren’t very high and it looked pretty plain. It smelled like stone. Odd.

I heard some murmurs, and decided to follow the sound up a grand stone staircase. It led into a great hall with tall ceilings. The walls were painted white and were filled with coats of arms. Busts of people I didn’t know lined the pillars that kept up the roof. At the end of the hall, I spotted Vasil talking to Ola and Hubert.

“Hi guys,” I said, startling them.

“Lady Aurelia,” Ola and Hubert said in unison.

So I was no longer limited to people I’d seen before. Or “persons”, as the System called them. I immediately wondered what the System considered a person. Especially since I could transform into any “person” I wanted now. Was a viwa a person? A bwotnik? Not that I wanted to transform into a bwotnik, of course, but it would be cool if I could.

And didn’t companies or organizations count as persons as well? For the law, at least. Maybe not for the System.
