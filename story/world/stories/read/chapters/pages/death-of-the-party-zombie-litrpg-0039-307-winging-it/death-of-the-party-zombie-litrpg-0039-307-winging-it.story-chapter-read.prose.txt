There was no mistaking it.

Everything from the garish colors in her peripheral vision to the slight tingling across her undead flesh. The odd pressure in the air could only mean that Sally was in Edward’s Demonic Domain.

Although that didn’t explain how she could sense it from so far from the demon himself. It had always been a localized effect, capable of stripping buffs and lowering Stats. She had been expecting to spot him past the next tree three times in a row now, and there was still no sight of him. Maybe he’d just unlocked the boosted version of the skill during his fight with the elite Radoch.

With a hop over a fallen tree, Sally stumbled over a root and almost fell into the next clearing. Iridescent colors filled her eyes as the trees surrounding the clearing shifted in muted gamer lighting, edging back and forth as if they were heavily pixelated.

In the middle of the small area stood Edward, rapier by his side as the corpse of a giant bug hissed and melted away.

“Woah.” The zombie tried to shake some color-correction into her head. “I must have missed the memo about this being an aura-farming gig.”

The demon turned his head to meet her; his usually bright blue eyes now burning pure white. “Sally,” he stated.

“I rushed over because I thought you needed a hand.” She slowed to a stop and put her hands on her hips. “Looks like you have things… under control.” He looked rough, but the dozens of decaying Radochs around him clearly had it a little worse.

Edward nodded. “I am currently possessed by our shadowy ally.”

“Ah. Say again?” She raised an eyebrow.

“My time in this condition is limited.” The demon turned his glowing eyes up to the giant spaceship hovering over the area. “I need to make the most of it while I can.”

As much as Sally was annoyed that another of her team might be returning to the Omen sooner than she wanted, that was just the cost of their venture. If each of the deployed Outsiders could just work to the best of their ability, that was truly all that she could ask of them. Exhaling through her nose, she gave him a reluctant nod of acceptance.

“Fine. Tell me what I can do to help.”

Edward glanced back at the zombie and grinned. “Did you know that demons of a certain power are supposed to have wings?”

Before she could respond, the sound of tearing flesh filled the area still humming from Domain energy. Edward’s suit tore open at the back where pitch-black tendrils broke through his body. In a matter of seconds, the reaching branches of shadow twisted and expanded, forming a pair of large wings.

Sally whistled. “I suppose that answers my question of how to get you up there.”

He stowed his rapier and rolled his shoulders to stretch out. “Until we meet again, Mrs Danger.”

With a burst of energy, Edward launched himself into the air, the wings pushing him past the treeline with ease. Sally watched as he soared further away, making up the distance to reach the spaceship in record time. She shivered as his Domain left the area, and the chill of night returned to the nearby forest.

Once the demon was just a dark smudge against the expanse of the Radoch vessel, she turned her attention back to ground level. For a constant battlefield, it had been rather quiet ever since approaching the demon’s position. Even now, there weren’t roaches waiting in the darkness. Had the Domain scared them away from this area?

After giving the treeline a quick sweep, she sent off a message to Jackie to see if there was an update. Perhaps going back to Humphrey would be the best option.

In the darkness, the movement of a small object in her peripheral vision caught her eye.

She clicked her tongue. “Oh, look what the cat dragged in.”

Edward felt more alive than he ever had. On top of the world. A pristine pinnacle of demi-godhood soaring through the skies, ready to lay waste to all that opposed him.

He was also certainly dying at a rapid rate.

While not as well-versed as some of the Outsiders when it came to dying, he was no stranger to it. After all, it was his bugged ability to respawn that had become the basis of Sanctuary’s most powerful trait - allowing anyone there to come back to life. He was only slightly sour that his defining feature had become the norm. Thankfully, it was a lot easier to plot against your closest allies if they lived longer. Every cloud has a silver lining.

The current one was just slightly more difficult to see. At a glance, the Radoch spaceship seemed several miles long. He wasn’t sure what he was able to do to benefit the Outsiders, even if he could get inside. Not before his body was fully destroyed.

[Again, my apologies for the current circumstances.]

Edward’s right eye twisted as he ascended on the shadowy wings. It was difficult to ignore the voice of the mysterious entity as it bounced around the inside of his head, but he didn’t really have a response. Possession had been a reckless and desperate attempt to gain the advantage over his opponents.

It was powerful. Every attack and skill became stronger and quicker, every cast being the boosted version of each ability. But… it was too much. His body couldn’t handle the energy this entity produced. Like putting a blowtorch to a candle, his insides were figuratively melting at breakneck speed.

The alternative—the shadow dropping Possession—would just kill him instantly.

So now he was flying through the sky on borrowed wings. Heading toward one of the headquarters of a mass alien invasion. It would be his final act in helping—or betraying—Sally in this world.

“I was never the best adventurer,” he complained, as the underside of the spaceship grew closer.

[A demon must do evil, as it is their nature.]

Edward rolled his eyes. “Is that what you are?”

[Not in the same way you are. I am several things… yet nothing.]

Perhaps an early demise wouldn’t have been so bad if the perpetrator was more sure of themselves. This wasn’t exactly the best place to have an indentity crisis, but Edward was at least thankful the shadow shared the same motivation. Or was willing to go along with what he wanted to do. There was no time to dig further, so he prompted the entity for its thoughts on their mission.

“Ideal situation would be to find their bridge, or control center. I’m not sure I have the time for that.”

[With no prior experience of their layout, it seems unlikely we could find a specific area within the all0tted timescale. As I have no connection to your allies, I would not be able to perform recon in your absence.]

“Ah, you’re not dying alongside me.”

[Your corpse will be discarded like shed skin.]

Edward pulled a face. “Then, if we are unable to learn much about the ship, the next best thing would be…”

[Destruction. Reduce them to ashes.]

Maybe it was just his brain slowly evaporating, but Edward was becoming a fan of this unknown entity. With a burst of speed, he turned toward what looked like possible entrance to the spaceship. It was time to do a little evil.

It was in his nature, after all.

Sally tapped her foot on the churned mud, arms crossed. “After all this time, where have you been? Out enjoying nature?”

The ginger cat sat on the edge of a dead Radoch and looked back at her, a blank expression across his face. “Not exactly, although I have traveled here and there.”

“With purpose, I hope?”

He nodded. “I believe I have found where the artefact is hidden.”

She breathed a sigh of relief. “Thank the Architect. Lead the way, so we can wrap up this narrative and focus on the B plot.”

Archie tilted his head. “Interesting how you always think that when you’re off adventuring you think Theo is having an equally fun time.”

“You forget I have a nose for these things, cat.” She tapped her nose. “Last time I got this feeling, Sanctuary was being attacked by one of these bugs.”

“Either way, there is no chance of us getting to the artefact while the world is so envelopped in conflict.” His emerald eyes glimmered. “Acquisition and escape would be nearly impossible.”

Sally deflated. “So I should go back to Pops and help kill all night long?”

“No. Big brother has somehow taunted all attention toward himself. It is better if you save your energy for when it is required.”

Bully hadn’t sent over the bug-eating potions yet. As soon as they arrived, she’d be able to turn the tide… she hoped. “With Ed flying away, I’m worried about our fighting force,” she admitted. “I don’t want to drag our alchemist into combat, but the times when I’ve soloed an entire planet were when I massively out-leveled everyone. Some of these Radochs are capable.”

Archie’s tail twitched back and forth. “That won’t be a worry. Once we get our hands on the artefact, the tide will turn in our favor.”

Edward watched impassively as a wave of blood and ichor ran across the deck. Red alarm lights flashed on and off, highlighting the loading dock in bloody hues. Perfect mood lighting for the demon on the prowl.

His wings returned to their position, dark knife-like protrusions softening to feathers once more. Several pierced Radochs dropped to the metal flooring, still spilling their internal fluids.

[Rails lead to the left; that must be where they store their monsters. Head to the far right door.]

The demon didn’t respond, as pain and elation wracked his body. His sharp teeth were interlocked, incapable of opening as he bared them in angered indifference. It hadn’t taken long to find one of the decks where the Radoch fleet had descended from. A large hangar of dark metal, raised platforms, and stacked crates. The skeleton crew still manning the station had only lasted long enough to call the alarm.

Another timer on his mortality.

The metal doors to the right slid open, as several Radoch guards barged through with ranged weapons in their hands. Edward was already there, rapier slicing limbs as if it were made of fresh magma. He flickered behind the group, impaling the carapace of one as his wings swept around another, decaying the bug in seconds.

Edward stepped away from the group and flicked the blood from his rapier. Before continuing, he tilted his head at what looked like a map - or directions to certain places on the ship.

He couldn’t read the odd writing, but it was clear the fleet commander and bridge was over a mile away, through scores of different rooms and corridors. He had flipped a coin, and it landed on heads.

A tendril of shadow extended from his wings and ran over the map as if it were reading Braille.

[We are close to the engines and power generation.]

With a grin that could only be described as insane, Edward turned and strode down the dark corridor.
