The walk back to the Lily House was a bit embarrassing. Ferris didn’t quite trust the slime yet, and so he decided not to carry them. He did get a few odd looks—a one-legged boy with a slime on a chain leash—but he wasn’t strange enough to get more than looks.

Back at the Lily House, Ferris casually walked—definitely not sneaked—to his room before Mother Lily saw him. He was not looking forward to explaining his bandaged hand…

Looking around the room, Ferris looked for a place to attach the chain. Frustratingly, anything he could tie it to, the slime could eat through. Thinking back to the pole the chain was tied to at the slime ranch, Ferris got confused.

Looking at his familiar, he asked “Why no dig?”

“What dig?” The slime shifted.

Ferris made a mental image of the slime melting the ground under the metal pole, pulling it free. Even if they couldn’t eat the magical pole, why not eat the dirt underneath?

“Big. No eat.” The slime sent along a confusing attempt at explaining, mixing strange emotions and indecipherable memories. Ferris was getting used to it.

The slime could only eat what was inside their body. They couldn’t just melt something by touching it, the magical acid only worked on things fully encompassed. The ground was too flat to surround.

Looking around with new eyes, Ferris looked for something too big to eat. The short dresser was bulky solid wood. The chain was about as long as he was tall, and just barely long enough to wrap around it. The slime could probably eat parts of it like a leg, but not enough to get free. He tied the chain as securely as he could.

“I touch now. No eat!” Ferris laid down just outside the slime’s reach and reinforced the command not to eat his hand. His right hand had settled into a painful ache, a constant reminder not to grow complacent again.

“No eat.” The slime rippled and rolled over to Ferris. They liked touching things. Tasting things. Then eating them.

Carefully, Ferris laid his hand on the slime, but they just rippled at his touch.

Looking around his room, the slime could still reach some of it, although not the bed. Ferris tried to send a mental image of the room, along with “Not food, no eat,” for each item. The slime seemed to understand, and responded each time, but he was wary.

Still, he’d done what he could. There wasn’t anything inside the slime’s range that couldn’t be replaced. Well, Ferris thought, except me.

It still felt a bit odd to not have a name for his familiar, but Ferris was having trouble deciding what to do.

“Do you have a name?” He asked, trying to focus on what a ‘name’ was.

By the swirls and confusion he got back over the bond, his attempt was a failure. The concept of a name was too abstract. Ferris spent a few minutes trying to explain, even giving examples of himself and various items around the room.

The slime appeared to understand the idea of designation, being able to identify that a shoe was a shoe. However, they struggled with the concept of what they ‘wanted’ their own name to be.

Ferris sighed, and resolved to keep thinking on it. Hopefully an idea would present itself as he learned more about his familiar. Names were hard.

“Meditate now,” Ferris said, but the slime just swirled in more confusion. “I show you. No eat!”

He had to lay down on the floor, as he couldn’t easily reach his familiar from the bed. His left hand rested on the slime; the only part of his body within reach.

His right hand, still wrapped in a bandage, hurt.

He closed his eyes and focused on his breathing. It was both easier and harder than it’d been earlier that day: no one was watching him anymore, but memories of the slime engulfing his hand persistently refused to drift away. It was a strange feeling: part of him was still sure his familiar would never hurt him, but he also couldn’t ignore the persistent real pain from his hand.

Even beyond the confusing dread, so much had happened that day that it was hard to clear his head. Uncle Winton had warned him about the people here, but the slime ranchers had been friendly. Elder Harper was a bit intimidating, but had shown genuine passion in her research. Plus, he thought, there’s Mother Lily's whole deal… Sometimes it felt all like too much to handle, but they seemed like good people.

Ferris let each thought happen, and then fade away. An unknown period of time later, Ferris found his soul and opened it to enter his soulspace.

His Heartfire burned contently, and the Qi Pool was larger than ever. Ferris had kept up his diligence in cultivation, spending every night gathering qi. The pool might be enough to fill one and a half bathtubs now. The statue was still attempting to lay down roots by extending white chains into the ground, but Ferris cut them back every night. He wasn’t sure what to do with it yet.

The enhanced spear had served him well, although he hadn’t gotten into any fights as bad as the three demon frogs. He’d killed two of the blood red imps and one blue frog, and their bodies lay next to the fire.

Ferris’ eyes locked onto the one new thing: a dark green stone the exact same size, shape, and appearance as the slime’s core. Lifting the core, Ferris marveled at it. Earlier, after bringing the core into his soul he’d quickly left his soulspace. Now he had the time to properly inspect it.

This core was his key to becoming a delver. Theoretically, he could cultivate without it, but it would be much harder and take years longer.

First, Ferris would need someplace to put it. Location would eventually be important, as many special techniques would need access to the core to work. If there was no room, Ferris wouldn’t be able to use that technique. After a moment of thought, he decided to place the bonded core on the opposite side of his Heartfire than his Qi Pool. Close enough to have easy access, but with some room to build around it. He could always move it later, if truly necessary.

Ferris melted down two blood red imps, mixed with liquid qi, to form a pedestal for the core. This was the same technique as his stronger spear. All it took was a clear mental picture of what he wanted, and he could make the liquid qi take any solid form

Well, any form that he could pull up and out of the pot’s opening. It was a bit comedic to pull out an object taller than the pot itself, but the same had happened with the spear.

The demons gave the construct a red tint that paired nicely with the dark green stone. The pedestal had a slot to firmly secure the core, while still allowing him to touch it. Uncle Winton had suggested the pedestal and told him to imagine the deliberate concept of locking the core down. The man had been very specific: intention while qi forming apparently mattered… somehow. The soulspace would often interpret meaning beyond physical representation.

Once satisfied with the core’s position and housing, Ferris placed his hand on top of it and closed his eyes. After a slight feeling of vertigo, he opened his eyes to an entirely new world.

The slime’s soulspace—slimespace?—was vastly different. Ferris’ soulspace was an endless, dark, barren wasteland. Here, he found himself on top of a cliff in the middle of what looked like a small island. There was a dome surrounding the island, which appeared to be submerged in a swirling ocean of green liquid. The dome was like a bubble at the bottom of a sea.

In Ferris’ soulspace, he’d been touching the core on its new pedestal. That same core and pedestal were now mirrored here in what he decided to call the slimespace. The core represented the bond in both places, and somehow the instructions from Uncle Winton had let him make the pedestal also exist here.

The dome holding back the ocean above was massive, although he could easily see the edges—perhaps a five-minute walk in any direction from the center. It was brightly lit by a soft, magical glow emitted from the dome walls. Everywhere around him was filled with things to look at.

Ferris was standing on top of a tall butte, with sharp cliffs on all sides. A Heartfire burned merrily in the middle, near where he was standing. Ferris knew that his Heartfire represented his life, so it wasn’t surprising to see that the slime had one as well. A bit to the north, a green waterfall fell from the top of the dome to the ground down below. The strange emerald liquid landed in a small pool, which flowed off into a winding river. The ground around him appeared basic, brown dirt without any vegetation.

Surrounding the edges of his butte were yellow pointed pillars. These spire-like structures poked out of the top of the cliffs around him.

Looking out between the pillars to the ground below, he saw more of the yellow spires sporadically covering the ground. The pillars were tall, about the size of a two-story building, and were almost high enough to reach his position on top of the cliffs. Their yellow color stood in contrast to all the green down there.

As he tried to look around, Ferris was immediately alarmed by swarms of small creatures everywhere—both on the ground below and up here on the butte. He backed up closer to the slime’s Heartfire, which had always been a source of safety in his soulspace. Foolishly, he hadn’t brought his spear with him.

Crawling everywhere in groups too numerous to count, the creatures looked like large grey ants. At least compared to a normal insect, they were gigantic: about the size of a dog. More intimidating than their size was their numbers.

He prepared to fight, and likely die, as several of the ants approached him. Ferris tried to kick the first one, but he missed as it deftly swerved around.

Instead of attacking, the swarm parted around his feet and passed by.

Shocked to still be alive, Ferris looked around again. The ants ignored him, weaving around both Ferris and the Heartfire. A few times he was bumped by an antenna, but only gently.

The demons in Ferris’ soulspace had always immediately attacked him on sight. They’d never directly ignored him.

Carefully, Ferris observed the slimespace some more. If the strange grey ants weren’t aggressive, he would be careful not to provoke them—at least until he was in a better position to fight. He blushed a little, thinking of his kick… the ant had dodged without even trying. More of them streamed past, going around him when needed. With a closer look, their grey exoskeleton looked almost like dull grey stone.

The book on familiar bonds had mentioned that monsters’ spiritspaces naturally had ‘spirit animals.’ This should now technically be a soulspace, but perhaps these ants were the spirit animals? He wasn’t sure, as the book hadn’t given any details.

With a shake, he refocused on looking around. Standing out from the pillars on the ground below were two large yellow towers, higher than the rest. One was as tall as the butte he was standing on, while the other extended up almost to the dome overhead.

He could only see the top of the shorter one. It had a circle of pillars surrounding the outer edge of the top, like defensive structures on a castle.

In fact, walking to the edge of his butte, Ferris saw that he was actually on top of a similar tower made of yellow stone. He wasn’t standing on a hill, but a structure with dirt on top.

So, Ferris thought, an island inside a dome, submerged in a green ocean. Three yellow towers, plus a ton of smaller yellow spires. Then a green waterfall feeding a river. And more ants than I can count. Ferris sighed, feeling more than a bit overwhelmed.

To add to his wonder, above him Ferris saw hundreds of grey birds flying around. He hadn’t noticed them at first, but now that he had a moment to look, they were everywhere. The dome’s ceiling was far above, giving them lots of space. While they flew around the entire area, the birds appeared to congregate near the tallest yellow tower. It was hard to tell from afar, but they looked larger than normal birds. In fact, they looked similar in size and color to the stone ants crawling around, coming up to his knee. Were they also spirit animals?

While several were flying almost directly above him, none approached him, so Ferris looked for a way down. The yellow stone felt sandy to the touch, and the rugged wall sloped down instead of being vertical. The surface of the wall was uneven, with many ridges and handholds. Ferris was able to climb down pretty easily.

On the ground, the first thing Ferris discovered was that the dirt was… green. It felt the same as the more normal brown dirt from above the tower, but it was very green. From afar he had mistaken it for grass, but there wasn’t any kind of plants or vegetation in sight. Just green dirt.

Ferris looked closer at the strange yellow pillars. They were made of the same hard sandy stone as the wall he’d climbed down. Each spire had holes near the top that the stone ants would crawl in and out of. He would’ve loved to see inside one, but they always had a few ants around guarding the entrances.

Seeing the ants guarding the spires, Ferris was reminded of anthills—although these walls were seamless and solid rather than piles of clumps. Perhaps the insects had some kind of natural binding agent they used?

Passing by the numerous spires, Ferris approached the tower he’d been able to see the top of—not the tall one with all the birds. It wasn’t far, and only took a minute to approach. The first tower had held the slime’s Heartfire, so he was curious about this one. Both mid-sized towers had the same sharply sloping walls with plenty of handhelds, so Ferris gingerly climbed up to the top.

There was the same circle of yellow spires emerging out of the walls, but there was plenty of room to climb up between them. While the first tower had dirt on top, this one had a black porous rock as its roof. The ever-present ants were here as well, going about some important business that Ferris couldn’t decipher.

At the center of the tower, growing out of the black rock was a large dark green crystal formation. Ferris approached the green crystal, which rose up like a small hill.

At the center, growing out of the crystal, was a solitary tree. As soon as Ferris saw it, he jumped for joy. This tree was a big reason why cultivators bonded cores. Every core—whether in a weapon or familiar—had the ability to grow this Core Tree, and they were key to providing powerful abilities.

Heading toward the tree while trying to steer clear of the ants, Ferris saw a small slime rolling around underneath it. Ferris had a mental body in his soulspace; it was no surprise his familiar had the same thing. The slime looked exactly like in the real world: a small green blob with a dark green core. The only thing missing was the magical chain latched around their core, as that only existed in the real world.

Taking another look at the glowing dome overhead, it looked suspiciously like the bulbous shape of the slime. There was a strange contrast between the slime’s acid body in the real world, and the inverse of a dome of air surrounded by ocean in the slimespace.

It felt a bit strange, but the slime’s mental body had a core, despite the same core sitting on a pedestal back near the Heartfire. Likely, the slime’s body was simply a reflection of what the slime thought they should look like, like how Ferris had two legs here.

Bonding the core had made the slime a part of Ferris. Or maybe, Ferris was a part of the slime? Either way, they were connected, which linked his soulspace with this place. He could only enter the slimespace when touching his familiar, but it was still part of him, even when not accessible. It was like a detachable hand he could only control when connected. The core still existed separately from Ferris in the real world, but it was as much a piece of him as his heart. This was why many died if their core was broken. Even if they survived, their cultivation would suffer greatly.

Watching the slime playfully bounce around the Core Tree, Ferris got closer. The ground changed from black rock to large dark green crystals growing everywhere, but he was able to climb over them. Stopping to take in the view, Ferris gazed at the tree. It was like a bold statement of defiance, inexplicably growing atop a crystal hill.

Upon the backdrop of hordes of large grey ants, the strange yellow pillars, and the ominous swirling green ocean outside the dome… the place had a magical charm.

Ferris smiled at the slime’s antics, hopping around beneath one of the branches, until he realized they were trying to jump up and eat the fruit of the tree.

“STOP! No eat no eat no eat!” he yelled as he hurried over, franticly avoiding any ants along the way.

The slime rippled and bounded happily as they rolled to Ferris. “New place. Free!” When close enough, the slime jumped straight at him, although only able to reach his knees.

Reflexively, Ferris bent down and caught the slime. He yelped in surprise, but the expected pain of burning didn’t come. The slime was peacefully rippling in his arms, not submerging him in acid.

“No eat me,” he said just to be sure, but the slime was content to be held. Ferris shrugged. I should test more physical contact anyway, better here than in the real world.

Carrying his familiar, Ferris approached the tree. It wasn’t large—barely taller than him—but it was beautiful. Up close he confirmed that it was growing directly out of the dark green crystal, roots disappearing deep down into the translucent material. Three branches hung out about eye level, each with a single plump fruit.

His heart raced as he inspected them. He’d heard tales of the fruit of a Core Tree since he was a little boy. Planting their seeds would grow special abilities that were key to Ferris surviving the rifts.

Three fruits, each a random ability.

Each would take him a single step closer to his goals.

Each of them gave a chance. Maybe an ability to regrow his leg, maybe something even better.

It was all he could do to not jump around beneath the tree himself, joining the slime in its joy.
