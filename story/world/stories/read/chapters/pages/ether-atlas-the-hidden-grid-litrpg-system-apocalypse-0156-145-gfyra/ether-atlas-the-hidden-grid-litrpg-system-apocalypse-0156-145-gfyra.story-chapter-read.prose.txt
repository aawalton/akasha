Chapter 145

It takes strong men to raise a bridge, but the first crossing needs the courage of a person willing to carry one cord to the farther bank and hold it there. — Codex of the Long Watch, Operational Directive

Eleni hadn't asked immediately how the meeting went, perhaps Sarah's face was answer enough. She had motioned for the party to sit and put some coffee on. Stavros was at the far end of the table with a length of hinge iron he was not really working on. Spyros was standing near the yard door with a coil of rope he was not really coiling either. Yiayia occupied her chair with the walking stick upright between her knees. Maria's older girl's charcoal drawing had been propped on the windowsill.

"What happened on the hill?" Eleni finally asked, setting cups on the table.

"He refused," Sarah said.

Her mother shut her eyes for a long moment but when she opened them again, the grief had hardened behind them.

"I'll put more coffee on, you will be making plans," she said.

"We will do something, we don't know what, but if you don't want to be a part of it now would be a good time to leave," Sarah said, looking over at Stavros and Spyros.

"My boy is on the hill, Re," Stavros said with a little heat in his voice. "You are not the only one with missing family."

"They took my boat and my berth. Forty years I've worked that water and they chained her. My grandfather built that boat," Spyros added.

Donna pulled out a chair but did not sit in it. She took four empty cups from the table and placed them along one edge.

"Lower approach, first pair of positions," she said.

Two saucers became the roof posts below the gate. A salt cellar marked the roster checkpoint. Steve contributed three coffee beans for runners and a brass coffee measure for the bell.

Arthur took the breadboard from beside the stove and laid it lengthways across the middle of the table.

"That's the rise towards the Beulé Gate. Not to scale, obviously."

"It doesn't need to be. We're rebuilding links and relationships, not drawing survey plans."

Donna moved one of the cups farther from the others.

"The crossbow teams were paired with the casters, but not tightly."

"It's like an overgrown tomato hedge. I think there were sixteen firing positions," Steve said.

Correct. Sixteen firing positions from what you could see and six caster signatures. Archie confirmed in their heads.

"Plus the people we didn't see, and they matter more," Donna said and set an olive near the coffee measure.

"Runner at the lower steps and there was another at the inner gate. Bell here. We saw relief files moving both ways, so there are established paths between the exterior posts and the inner garrison."

Arthur put two chunks of bread on the far side of the board.

"Propylaea and the Gatehouse. I could take down one of the reinforced sections, easy enough. Trouble is, until we know exactly where we're headed, I'd just be making a bloody great pile of rubble for us to climb over."

"And that's if we don't end up bringing it down on someone behind it," Sarah said.

"Yes."

Steve placed a row of olives behind the bread.

"Eastern barracks, according to Mavros." He then indicated the other end of the table without placing anything there. "The north-terrace workshop is somewhere over here. Again, according to Mavros."

"Those are two broad locations, not an interior map. We don't know which building he means by the eastern barracks, which room Petros is in, whether he sleeps there or merely reports there, or how many guards are between that room and the nearest exit," Donna said, pacing around the table, her eyes on the crockery and bits of food.

"And we don't know whether Maria and Milos are in the workshop all day, and if not, then where they are taken when work finishes," Arthur added, and saw Stavros stiffen at the mention of Milos.

Stavros rubbed the heel of his hand against the table in a restless gesture. "The soldiers. Do they all follow Mavros, or do some still serve the city?"

"We don't know," Donna said honestly. "Some will be true believers. Some will be following orders because that is what soldiers do and they have not been offered anything else to follow. A coup is never this or that."

Donna spoke after she had circled the table thrice in silence, stopping at each makeshift position as if she were walking the approach again.

"The obvious answer is still the obvious answer. We kill Mavros and force our way in."

Nobody objected.

"Mama, if you want to go upstairs," Sarah started, and shut up immediately as she got a withering look from Eleni.

"On paper we walk back up there tonight, go through the gate and put him down before his own guards have finished drawing a breath, and it's all sorted by morning."

"Give me ten seconds and I can clear any room on that hill. I am not worried about a few level fifteens or whatever level they are." Donna leaned forward on the table. "The problem was never whether we can win a fight in there. The problem, as the bastard said, is we don't know which room and who else is in that room."

Killing him is within your capability. Recovering every detainee alive is not the same operation.

"I could hold a corridor, if we find a good one. Long enough for the rest of you to clear rooms behind me," Arthur said.

"Hold it against what? You don't know what's coming down it. You don't know if it's a squad of conscripts who'll drop their spears the second they see your face, or the men Mavros actually trusts, the ones who knew exactly what they were signing up for. And we don't know which qualifies as a good corridor," Donna said, dismissing the wall-in-a-corridor plan.

Sarah had drifted towards the window. "We need to do something. I am not sitting in my mother's kitchen while my brother sleeps behind that man's wall."

"Sarah." Eleni's voice was sharp and level, the voice of a mother who had spent her whole life in a neighbourhood where raising your voice was a confession of weakness. "Sit. Think. Then decide."

"There is nothing to think about. He has Petros. He has Maria. He has Milos and forty other people locked inside a garrison because he decided their skills belonged to him. We have the ability to break his hold and we are choosing not to use it because we are afraid of what might happen."

"We're afraid of what might happen to those we love," Arthur said, moving up behind her and putting his hands on her shoulders. "We're not going to sit here doing nothing, Sarah. You know us better than that. But going in half-cocked won't help anyone. I know you're worried. Trust Donna."

Sarah half turned and looked up at Arthur and closed her eyes. "I'm sorry, I didn't mean that," she said, sagging against him.

Alarm propagation in a fortified position of this density follows line-of-sight signalling. A single warning reaches every occupied room within forty to sixty seconds. Uncoordinated breach carries a high probability of collateral harm to detained civilians, Archie said, and for a second Arthur thought that he wanted to tell Sarah in his own way not to rush in without a plan.

"Everything boils down to information. We need to know what is behind the walls before we go through them," Donna said.

"I need a map of the interior. Which rooms hold civilians. Which hold soldiers. Where the alarm chain runs. Guard rotations. Shift changes. Who carries the keys and which doors lock from the inside."

Donna got up and started pacing, her mind building breach models and exit strategies. "We have two broad locations and forty-three names according to a man who benefits from deciding what we see. We need every name, their current condition and somebody inside confirming where they actually are."

"And we need to know which soldiers are committed to Mavros and which are following orders because that is what they are trained to do," Arthur added. "There is a difference between a loyalist and a conscript; when an alarm goes up, one fights and the other waits for someone to tell him what to do."

"You are talking about intelligence. You want to know the inside of a fortress from the outside. How do you do this without going inside?" Stavros said, frowning.

"We don't. We need someone who walks in and out of that garrison every day without anyone looking twice. A soldier with legitimate access and a reason to help us," Arthur said.

"Or we find someone to blackmail," Donna added.

As the five on the table looked at her with shocked expressions, she held up her hands. "I'm laying out all options, not everything comes with a neat little bow tied around it."

Eleni was grinding spices in a mortar, and Arthur suspected it was more to do with keeping her hands busy than any real need for more spices. The grinding stopped.

"There is the boy," she said.

Sarah looked over. "What boy?"

"The young guard. His mother came to say thanks and get your full name for a candle. She was talking to the neighbours about the miracle of her son walking again, but everyone already knew the story because Katerina had told half the street about it the evening it had happened."

"We met his cousin, Thanasis," Sarah said.

Arthur remembered a young man at the Council office respectfully holding out a hand for their weapons. He had explained the rule and stood behind it. Arthur had liked the lad's confidence.

"Petros knew his father, something about an outboard," Arthur said.

"Petros knows everybody's father and all their broken engines," Sarah said. Her chair scraped forward. "Thanasis and his cousin work for the militia. If they could go through a controlled entrance without turning it into an attack."

Steve glanced at the overturned cup. "Can and will are rather different questions."

"And we are rather desperate," Donna said. "Soldier with legitimate access to the garrison. A personal debt to Sarah. And a family connection to Petros. And they are inside Mavros's chain of command already."

"We are asking them to risk their lives," Eleni said.

"We are asking them to carry one message," Sarah said. "One message in. One answer out. Where Petros sleeps. How many guards stand near the rooms. A small favour, nothing more, from a man whose family already owes mine."

"And if he refuses?" Spyros asked.

"Then he refuses and we find another way."

"If we're wrong about him, we don't just lose a courier, we lose the bloody lot. We would be handing Mavros the exact proof he wants, that foreigners are trying to turn his own men against him," Donna said.

"All of this is still hypothetical. We don't know the duties they have; they could well be patrolling the harbour. I need to talk to them," Sarah said.

"I will go and find where they live; Katerina will know the mother. Any of you going will look like soldiers making a house visit," Eleni said.

"I will go with her. I know a few men who will listen," Spyros said, getting up as well.

Sarah nodded, her eyes tracking the door as it swung shut behind Eleni and Spyros.

"And so it starts," Steve murmured, looking at the empty doorway.
