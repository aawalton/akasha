I fly towards where Josiah indicated he’s taken the fishing ships, fear curdling in my breast. Following behind me will be the other three ships that were at berth, but it will take time for them to get underway, time the beleaguered fleet might not have.

We really can’t afford to lose three of our ships, and for Josiah to be this panicked, it has to be serious. Perhaps a peak Tier three – one that’s only a few steps away from Tier four. Sometimes beasts gain a measure of power even before they’ve Evolved – the spider-like danaris in the other world certainly did and was an absolute pain to kill, even though it was technically still Tier two.

While I’m flying, I interrogate Josiah for the details.

It’s a sea serpent, he relays tersely. More than five times the length of the ship from bow to stern. It’s attempting to crush our ship in its coils, but water is continuously shedding from its scales as well. It’s all I and the other Water Mages can do to redirect most of the flow off the ship instead of inside it. If we don’t, we’ll sink even if we aren’t crushed.

How are you dealing with the coils if the mages are focusing on the water flow? I demand, knowing that almost all the non-Water Mages are assigned to tasks on land, and therefore are unlikely to be present.

With difficulty, Josiah replies grimly. The beast’s scales are highly physically and magically resistant – to the extent that even enchanted weapons struggle to chip a scale. We’ve been reduced to trying to use spears to push its coils off the ends of the ship, but once they settle, it’s almost impossible to do that.

Has anything worked? I ask worriedly. We can’t lose this ship – we can’t.

Alchemical fire, Josiah answers promptly, a vague note of satisfaction in his mental voice. It didn’t like the way that clung to its body. But we have little, and must use it sparingly for fear it will cling to our enchantments next. At this point, only the enchantments in our ship’s bones are preventing it from crushing us like a piece of parchment.

What of the other ships? I ask. Can they not help? The day is a little misty with the horizon being significantly closer than usual, but I can see faint forms looming through the haze – that must be them.

They’re struggling enough as it is – the beast seems capable of controlling the currents with a deftness that outperforms any of my mages. The other ships are fighting just to keep upright.

Wonderful.

Well, I suppose that gives me something to work with, I sigh as the hazy forms resolve into ships.

Three of them, specifically, clustered together. It’s immediately obvious which one Josiah is on. A massive sea serpent is coiling around it – and Josiah didn’t exaggerate. If anything, he understated it. The creature isn’t just five times the length of the ship; at its thickest point, it’s as wide as a bus. Its sheer weight is causing almost as much damage as its attempts to coil, and I can already hear the ominous cracking of strained timbers.

The surrounding waters churn violently, the currents Josiah was talking about creating a nightmare for the other ships.

While sending out a pulse of Inspect – not trying to conceal it – I call out Noir and Ivor. Sirocco is one of the minders for the group of samuran hatchlings today, and Bastet is out on a patrol, so unfortunately I won’t have their help. I could have used Bond Teleport to bring them back when I was nearer the settlement, but Bastet in particular probably would have emptied my mana pool – and then some. I didn’t deem it as the best use of my resources.

Catch anyone who falls off, I instruct the alcaorises – beyond everything, the people are my priority. But be careful. Don’t go too near its head. They send me grim acknowledgement, then arrow downwards towards where we can already see people floating in the water.

Meanwhile, information returns from my Inspect pulse. I hum as I review what it’s telling me, forming and discarding strategies in seconds. I even consider trying to negotiate, but dismiss that a moment later – Tier threes aren’t the kind of beast who will negotiate unless they fear they might lose.

Finally, I nod sharply, my decision made.

“All right, Aingeal,” I murmur to my fire elemental, who I was glad to see when he caught up with me soon after I took flight. “Let’s show this serpent why it shouldn’t have chosen us as prey.”

He doesn’t care about ships or strategy, but he’s always happy to burn something.

He dances eagerly in the air as we begin to form a fireball. Since I chose not to summon anyone, I still have almost a full pool of mana. With Aingeal’s assistance, creating a massive ball of fire taller than I am takes less than a minute.

In that time, the serpent’s coils tighten further. The deck visibly starts to buckle, the enchantments in them becoming visible as they’re strained to breaking point. Below, the crew scramble like ants, growing more frantic by the second.

Clear the decks, I send sharply to Josiah. I’ll try to control the fire, but I can’t guarantee it won’t hurt indiscriminately.

His response is immediate – grim determination, laced with relief. A loud horn splits the air and those on deck scatter.

The moment I receive his confirmation that everyone should be under cover, I release the fire.

The fireball slams into the serpent with a thunderous whoomph, followed by an ear-piercing shriek of pain as flames spread across its scales like it’s covered in oil.

I push my awareness into the fire, feeling resistance – as both Josiah and my Inspect indicated, the sea serpent’s flesh is highly magic resistant, even to this. Fire might be its weakness, but only barely.

The serpent retaliates instantly. A torrent of water crashes over its body and the deck, trying to smother the flames.

I resist, drawing on the principles of fire itself. Fire only dies when one part of the triangle is removed. With magic, I can replace what is lost.

Instead of dying, I fan the flames with concepts of magnesium burns, of tire fires – heat that water cannot easily quench.

The serpent screams, its coils tightening even more. Sharp sounds ring through the air and I hear screams from down below as a massive crack appears in the hull. Even as it continues destroying the ship, the serpent rears up to face me. Its mouth forms a narrow shape and its head rears back.

A moment later, it spits a jet of water, bullet-fast.

I dodge instinctively, but my heart lurches as I see Aingeal caught in its path.

No! Not him!

Without thinking, I reach for him with Bond Teleport.

Even as I do it, I realise this shouldn’t work. My Bond with him isn’t like that of the others – and I can’t use it with Tame Bonds at all.

But I can’t lose him and pour my desperation and determination into the Skill.

The water covers where Aingeal and my heart clenches in my chest as it falls away to reveal empty space.

Fortunately, before the grief truly hits, I feel gentle heat off to my side. I glance over and reach out a shaking hand to the glowing ball of sentient fire bobbing next to me. He might not really understand how close to non-existence he came, but I do.

And then the cost hits.

Fire explodes within me, an inferno tearing through my body. I don’t know if this is just how the spell backlashes, or because of the nature of the being I summoned, but I don’t have the presence of mind to figure it out. I cry out, the pain mirroring the serpent’s own, and instinctively channel my agony outward, feeding the flames still clinging to the Tier three.

By the time the searing fades enough for me to think clearly again, the serpent is gone. There isn’t the hint of its coils in the water, and the ships are once more rocking gently in the normal lapping of the water.

I hang in the air, weak and shaking, cursing myself for not considering the limits of Bond Teleport before using it.

Then again…. If I hadn’t acted, Aingeal might not be here.

I glance at him. He’s hovering close, radiating sulky displeasure now that the serpent – and, more importantly, the fire – are gone. Despite everything, relief settles over me. I’ll consider the pain worth the possible outcome of Aingeal hit by an angry Tier three’s water attack.

When I’ve recovered a little, I spiral gently down through the air and land on the deck near where Josiah is standing. He’s leaning over the railing, inspecting the damage with tight lips, but he looks up the moment I enter his field of vision.

“So?” I ask, a little tensely.

“It’s not good,” Josiah says, his words clipped with anxiety, “but it could be a lot worse. Now the serpent is gone, the other mages can focus on keeping us sea-worthy until the damage is fixed.” He visibly forces himself to relax. “Thank you. I don’t think we’d have survived that without a Fire Mage of your calibre.”

“Perhaps we need to focus on training more mages in fire spells,” I muse. “I doubt this will be the last attack of this kind.”

Shouts for a healer draw my attention and I turn slightly to see someone on a stretcher being carried swiftly towards someone dressed in white robes. Relieved that they brought a healer along, I turn my attention back to Josiah as he speaks.

“No,” Josiah agrees, looking out over the water, a worried crease between his brows. “Especially since that one got away. It’s probably the territory lord. It’s certainly powerful enough. And if it’s taken exception to our presence, we’re likely to see it again sooner rather than later. But can we offer such training? Do we have the means and those of the right temperament?”

He makes a good point. I’m the only one in our entire expedition force with Fire-Shaping – there are others with some fire-based abilities, but without Fire Shaping, they’re far more limited. And there are multiple reasons for that – as Nicholas explained when it occurred to me to ask.

Fire is volatile, and those who wield it are often just as bad. Impetuous, quick to take offence, overly passionate…. After gaining a Fire-based Class, even previously calm people often become problematic if their new magic isn’t directed properly. Those who previously tended towards excess just become worse. I did wonder, after speaking with Nicholas, why I hadn’t suffered the same impulsiveness common to Fire Mages. In truth, I probably did, but in subtler ways and tempered by my Willpower.

Then there’s the risk of injury or death. All magic is dangerous, but those who work with fire, whether fire mages or other classes, have the highest injury and fatality rates. Few are willing to take that risk.

Fire Mages are a danger to themselves and others, and pure Fire Mage Class stones are some of the most difficult to find – and controlled by the local authorities. The only place where Class stones are even available for sale is in the Flameborn lands where the Great House has an intimate understanding of the challenges that face Fire Mages. I think back to Jareon Flameborn and feel regretful for a moment that we didn’t become allies.

But what’s done is done – given how determined he seemed to prove himself, I doubt he would have been open to an alliance anyway.

Ultimately, that means that, unless I want to focus on guarding the ships, we won’t be able to repeat the attack from today. Not in the same way, at least.

“How far do you think its territory extends?” I ask. “All the way back to the harbour?” I’m considering how necessary it is for us to ensure the ships are safe from this serpent in particular – though, I suppose, if not this one, then something else.

Josiah hesitates.

“Hard to say,” he admits. “But…it’s possible.”

I worry my lip between my teeth. That’s bad news. If we can’t deter the Tier three, or get attacked by others, then we can’t risk sending ships out to fish. And though I consider guarding the ships, I can’t spend all my time at sea. I’m still working with the healers and Elarion on the food crisis, which in the long term is even more critical.

All of which means we need another solution.

“Perhaps we could produce more alchemical fire,” I suggest. “Was there any warning before it attacked? I understand not wanting to set your own ship alight, but if you can spot it before it coils around the deck, you might be able to drive it off. Or even kill it.”

Though, it is possible that that would make things worse – as we’ve seen with Bastet, when the territory changes hands, lots of Tier threes come out of the woodwork to throw their hats into the ring. If they don’t involve our ships in the fighting, that would be fine, but we can’t guarantee that.

“It’s possible,” Josiah admits, “but it would take a lot of alchemical fire. As you saw, it has its own defences. If the flames aren’t strong enough, it just washes them away. Anyway, if it ends up in the water then we have a different problem.”

I see his point.

Alchemical fire is a mana-based substance, something like Greek fire or napalm – burning even on water until its fuel is spent. If the serpent is clever, it could turn that against us, using the currents to push the flames back onto our own ships.

I doubt it would care much if its prey came out slightly charred.

Then again, if the Water Mages aren’t continuously having to bail out the ship to stop it from sinking, they might be able to maintain a protective ring of clear water around the ships.

“We’ll have to raise this with the others,” I decide in the end, not feeling like I know enough to be able to confidently divine a solution. “For now, we’ve driven it off. Hopefully it thinks twice before returning.”

“Unless it brings reinforcements,” Josiah replies grimly. “Tier threes are called Beast Lords for a reason.”

I sigh. I hadn’t considered that. Most of the Tier threes we’ve faced so far have operated alone making me wonder whether Tier threes gathering beasts around them was a factor on Moriax alone. Then again, can we afford to bank on that?

“You have a point,” I admit. “Alright. Let’s return to land and call a council. See what everyone else thinks.”

Josiah relays orders to the captain, who signals the other ships, and we begin the journey back. Together, we assess the damage and determine what repairs will be needed.

But at the back of my mind, a troubling thought lingers.

If we find the seas barred to us, and we still can’t eat what grows on land… what are we going to do when our stores run out? Because, at the rate that we’re eating through them – even though we’ve already started imposing rationing – that could be within a week.
