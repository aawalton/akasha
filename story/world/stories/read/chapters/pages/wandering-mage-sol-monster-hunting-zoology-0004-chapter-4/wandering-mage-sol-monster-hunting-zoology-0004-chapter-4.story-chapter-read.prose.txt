Wish I had a spell that made me invisible. Maybe I could mess around with the light runes and make one myself… though I doubt it. The last time I experimented with light magic it blew up in my face… literally. Would something like that satisfy the dragon’s trial in the first place? I feel it must be something far more significant than that…

An invisibility spell would be impressive, but not exactly novel, and it certainly wouldn’t take her five years to put together something like that. No, whatever spell the dragon wanted from her, it had to be something… big? World-changing? She shivered at that thought, feeling overwhelmed by the pressure of attaining such a feat.

Still… what I wouldn’t give to sneak in and steal one of Narella’s pastries.

Down the hall, she could smell the scent of fresh-baked bread. Her mouth watered as she thought of the hot rolls filled with goat butter and honey.

Well, if Narella is there, I’ll be able to get some food. She can keep my secret, after all. She is one of the few who don’t hound me for being different.

The smell of bread and spices only got stronger the closer she came to the kitchen, and her stomach let out a small growl.

Hmmm, yup, I’m getting one of those rolls no matter what!

She turned left instead of right, heading down the stairs and to the kitchen where she cracked open the door. Inside, she could see three elves moving about, with arms full of bowls, towels, and other items Tressa didn’t know. Then there was Narella with ladle in hand as she snapped at one of the servants who nearly dropped a pot of hot, steaming liquid.

“I’m sorry, chef!” he cried.

Then she turned to another apprentice working on pies.

“Lorek, get your head out of your arse! I have shown you a hundred times, that is not how you put the crust on that pie!” she boomed and cracked the ladle over his head.

He let out a yelp and covered his head where she had struck him, leaving bits of crust and flour in his hair.

“Hey! I’m trying!” he whined as he rubbed his head. She smacked him again, on the fingers this time, with the thick end of the ladle.

“Enough blubbering, get to work!”

He sniffled and rubbed at his hand and turned back to working on the pie crust.

Guess Narella can be pretty scary. Working for her, anyway. I’m glad she isn’t one of my maids. Tressa shuddered, thinking about what someone like Narella would be like in Maikia’s position. Maikia can be bad enough, Tressa thought.

Narella was a wood elf, as evidenced by her red hair. The fact that despite her not being a High Elf yet rising to such a position within a noble house truly spoke to how formidable she was.

Then Tressa flinched as Narella suddenly turned in her direction and placed a hand on her hip, tapping her foot. Tressa grinned innocently as Narella let out a resigned yet amused sigh she only reserved for the young mistress of House Windshade.

“I’ll be right back. You lot keep at it, or I’ll whip all your arses when I return.”

“Yes, ma’am!” they all said in unison as she turned on her heel and headed to the door.

Tressa took a step back as Narella came out and closed the door behind her.

“Going out, are ya’ mi’lady?”

“Yes ma’am,” Tressa said respectfully. “I could smell the bread and wanted to know if I could take some, as well as goat butter and honey.”

“Hmmmm…” She looked Tressa up and down, arms folded as she lightly tapped the ladle against her elbow. “Fine. Wait here, and I’ll fetch ye something. I need to know if my fine bread is all talk or if it’s really as soft and buttery as ye say.” She turned and headed back into the kitchen, returning a few minutes later with a cloth wrapping the bread.

“Thank you!” Tressa said excitedly as she opened the cloth just enough to pull out one of the steaming, freshly baked rolls. She took a bite and grinned. “So soft!” She winced as it burned her tongue slightly and blew on the role with quick breaths to prepare it for her second bite.

“Honestly, young mistress, you ought to wait for it to cool a little longer, don’t you think?”

“Mmmm but it’s so good, I couldn’t wait.”

“Hahahaha! Well, thank you, mistress. It’s always a pleasure to know my rolls are enjoyed.”

“How can I not? The bread is so airy and fluffy, and has a sweet taste. I can’t get enough of them.”

“Now, my lady, I do suspect I’m the one you’re trying to butter up, aren’t you?”

“Maybe a little, but only because your cooking is worth the effort.”

“Ha! Alright, alright, enough laying it on. I’ve got to get back in and make sure these dolts don’t burn down the estate.” She opened the door but glanced back with a knowing smile. “And ye ought to run along as well, mistress. Else, ye might get caught. Whole estate should be waking up soon.”

Tressa flinched. “I’m not… uh…”

Narella smiled. “I’m afraid it’s all too obvious. But don’t worry, I’ll keep it to myself.”

Tressa gave another awkward grin, then ran down the hall, carefully stuffing her bread into the satchel along her side as she went.

Then as she turned a corner and looked up from her satchel, her feet shuffled out of sequence with a quick shout of alarm as she ran into someone.

Aldmern caught her by her shoulders. “Well, where do you think you’re going, young mistress?”

Tressa wrinkled her nose at him. “You know I hate it when you call me that, Aldmern.” He set her back on her feet and gave her space as she said, “And who says I’m going anywhere?”

He raised an eyebrow. “Well, seeing that you are dressed in almost the same clothes as last time I was sent after you, and the fact you have food stuffed into your satchel… well, I’d say you’re off for some picnic, my lady.”

She winced. “Hehe, okay, you caught me. I was planning to go back to the lake.” She bowed her head before him. “Please pretend you didn’t see me. I really can’t be here right now.”

He raised an eyebrow. “Did something happen? Have you been hurt?”

“No, no! Nothing like that. I just… I really need some air, and I’m hoping I can see that Tuplar fish again and finish my sketch.”

“Fine, but I’m coming with you.”

She winced again. “No thanks.”

He smirked. “Well, I didn’t think I’d break it to you this way, but…”

She gave him a suspicious look. “But what?”

“Turns out I’m now your personal guard.”

“What?” she said dryly.

He laughed. “That’s about the expression I expected. But yes, your father gave me the order just last night. So, unfortunately, it seems I need to come with you.”

Tressa let out a deep groan. “You don’t have to. Just pretend you didn’t see me and there’s no difference.”

He shrugged. “You’re not wrong, but, you know, if I don’t go with you now, I’ll just end up being ordered to go find you later. And since the promotion has relieved me of most of my other duties, keeping an eye on you is all that’s left on my schedule. Might as well get off to a good start in my new role, don’t you think?”

She shot him a glare. “Fine.” Then she turned on her heel, leading him down the hall and through another room to her secret way out of the city she’d usually use when she wanted to sneak off. It was a passage leading to a small hole in the wall near her family’s estate that led out to the massive forests surrounding Fallidorn.

Unfortunately, Aldmern being around meant she wouldn’t be able to experiment with magic the way she had wanted to. Manipulating runes was dangerous and highly illegal to do outside of an approved magical study environment with the right safety precautions and so-called masters overseeing you.

“So, this is how you always slip out with nobody seeing.”

“Shows how good at your job you are,” Tressa said. “This hole could be a major security breach.”

He smirked. “Not quite my department. As such, I suppose there is no need for me to report it.”

Tressa smiled in return, appreciating that he wouldn’t ruin it for her. Then in a mocking, extravagant tone, she said, “Some head guard you are. Not what I’d expect from my esteemed protector.”

“Oh, quite the opposite. If I reported it, then it would be sealed. If I don’t, then I’ll always know where my personal charge has gotten off to when she disappears.”

“Oh my, that sounds almost treasonous.”

He laughed. “I wouldn’t go that far.”

Aldmern stepped through the narrow passageway first, and they emerged onto a rocky slope outside of Falidorn’s wall. He hopped down then turned and held a hand out to her.

Tressa stared at it a moment and then shrugged as she took his hand, allowing him to steady her as she stepped off the rock. She hardly needed the help, as the drop was barely higher than her knee, but she thought there was some benefit in indulging him once in a while. Especially if his new position meant constantly hovering over her shoulder from now on.

Tressa didn’t like it, and she supposed she shouldn’t have expected any less from her father. But if it was going to happen, at least it was Aldmern and not some other random guard she didn’t get along so well with.

After about twenty minutes, they made it to the lake. Tressa took a seat on a fallen log, then buttered up one of the rolls with goat butter and honey, and took a bite.

“Mmmmmm, sooo good,” she said as the roll was still warm. The cloth had a simple spell on it that made food remain warm while keeping the honey and butter from melting from the heat of the roll.

“I see you’ve raided the kitchen,” Aldmern remarked.

“Did not!” Tressa said through a mouthful, acting in mock offense.

“No speaking with your mouth full. That is very unladylike.”

Tressa swallowed and took a drink from her canteen. “It ruins the point of getting fresh air if you are just going to lecture me as Father does. Besides, Narella gave them to me. So no, I did not ‘raid’ the kitchen. And when do I ever act ladylike?”

He scoffed. “Fair enough. Though, I think you should practice if you are going to be on the council.”

Tressa let out a huff. “I’m not going to be joining the council, nor the temple, or library. I’m going to do what I want. I’ll leave here and explore the world.”

Tressa tossed him a roll, and he caught it easily. Not even Aldmern could resist Narella’s cooking.

“Thank you, my lady,” he said and took a bite after adding the butter to it. Once he finished chewing and swallowed, he looked at her. “If you did leave here, where would you go? You cannot speak the common tongue… so…”

“I…” She let out a frustrated sigh and took another bite of her roll. “I know some words. I learned them from the merchants. I can work out enough of the common tongue to get by and improve over time.”

She sat back, leaning against a tree, watching the forest around them as Aldmern ate the bread. He had a point, and the thought left her quiet. The language barrier alone would make setting off on her own difficult, and she knew little about the world beyond Falidorn’s borders. Monsters and the wild didn’t frighten her, but other societies could be far more dangerous. Especially for a high elf. She knew that her people were disliked throughout most of the world due to the Mad King’s actions centuries ago, even if someone her age had nothing to do with his crimes.

She reached into her bag and pulled out a leather-bound notebook and her metal quill. She could conjure a mana quill at any time, but she found a normal physical one was far better, and besides, this metal quill in particular was one of her favorites. The thing is, the little bit of attention focused on maintaining the mana quill could be slightly irritating over long uses, especially if she was deep in focus and working on precise detailing of a drawing.

She flipped open the same notebook as the day before, leaving her secret magic-related notebooks deep in the satchel for now. She couldn’t risk messing with them while Aldmern stood nearby, so she instead looked over her notes on the large fish she had seen yesterday.

Toplur Fish

A massive species of fish found in the lake within Falidorn’s borders. Based on its size, I suspect it travels through underground water passages connected to the lake.

Estimated size: Adults are roughly fifteen feet in length and around six feet in height.

The creature attacks anything that disturbs the water’s surface, which would make approaching it difficult without provoking a strike. Its mouth can open far wider than expected—possibly due to a dislocating jaw similar to snakes and certain deep-water fish.

The scales appear extremely thick and layered, likely resistant to arrows or spears. They may be as durable as metal plating. Direct strikes against the body would likely be ineffective. The eyes and mouth appear to be the most vulnerable points. I may attempt to strike its scales with an arrow next time to see.

Further study required.

She continued to sketch the fish as Aldmern wandered down to the water and searched for a rock to skip. Unfortunately it didn’t seem the Toplur fish would show itself again, so she had to guess at some of the details. But once she was satisfied it was as good as she could get it, she placed the book back into her bag, and her hand hesitated as she looked at her book of runes and spell research.

Aldermen attempted to skip a rock with little success and then searched for another. She watched as he tried to mimic her wrist movement. The next skipped only twice, then sank.

“Hmmm,” he said as he searched for more. After creating a small pile, he began trying again.

“You need to have your fingers like this.” She stood, walked to the water’s edge, found a smooth flat rock, picked it up, and placed her thumb down on the surface, curled her index around the side, and used her middle finger to balance it in the middle. “Then just flick your wrist to keep the stone flat and level with the water and—” She flicked her wrist, and the stone bounced smoothly across the surface of the lake about a dozen times.

As he picked up another rock, an overly serious look crossed his face that made Tressa want to giggle, but she didn’t want to accidentally insult him. This one skipped a few extra times, then sank. He nodded to himself as he picked up another, seeming a bit more satisfied.

As he continued flicking one sinking stone after another, Tressa went back to her forbidden notebook. She thought it over for a moment and then pulled it out.

Just a peek, she thought. Besides, it’s literally related to my trials. If an order from a divine dragon isn’t enough reason to bend the rules a little, I don’t know what is.

Some of the pages were filled with old elven script and carefully drawn runes. They were from some old experiments when she had been trying to piece them together into an offensive spell, but none of the combinations felt right the way her other spells had.

A sigh slipped from her lips as she scratched out another failed attempt and set her quill down.

She bit her bottom lip, trying to picture how such a spell might be used, but nothing came to mind. After all, she had never truly fought a day in her life. Maybe she needed to actually experience combat to understand how an offensive spell should work.

Like most elves, she had trained with a bow when she was younger. It was considered a rite of passage. During the trials, there was always the chance a young elf might have to fight another elf—or even a monster—to prove their strength.

The Thimberman Mountains lay only three days away, with the old dwarven mines another half-day beyond that. Hunting parties were often sent to keep the monsters there from wandering too close to Falidorn. Every so often, one slipped through and reached the forests around the city.

As she stared down at the open book, the dragon’s words drifted back into her thoughts.

Invent a new spell.

She frowned slightly.

“Aldmern?”

He paused with his arm half-cocked to throw a stone when he looked back at her. “Yes, my lady?”

She bit her bottom lip, trying to think how to phrase her question.

“Um, if I may be so bold as to ask, what were your trials like?” She stared at the ground, feeling guilty for asking, as it wasn't customary to share the trials nor ask unless you were close to that person. It was an important part of that elf's life and a rite of passage, and considered quite personal.

He paused, thinking.

“I’m sorry for asking. That was rude of me.”

Aldmern shook his head. “Not at all, my lady. Yours are coming up, after all. I don't blame you for wanting to be prepared. My trials took place during the Mad King's rule. So mine were more of a spectacle for him. He treated our trials like a game and a thing to make a mockery of.”

She stared at the ground, guilt spreading through her chest. Knowing his age, growing up under the Mad King, she understood how hard it must have been.

He stood staring at the lake, his face turning into a tight scowl. A cloud passed overhead, shrouding the sun and making his expression seem even more intense.

“I’m sorry,” Tressa muttered.

He let out a sigh, and his face softened. “The Mad King wasn’t always cruel. At least that’s what my father used to say. When he was a young prince, he was kinder than most among our people. He helped where he could and was known as a diplomat among the other races. Trade flourished during those years, and many believed he would become one of the greatest kings our people had ever known.”

Aldmern paused, his gaze drifting across the lake.

“But he was also curious—always chasing knowledge. So he left to study the wider world. He was gone for nearly two centuries. And when he returned… he wasn’t the same.

“My grandfather said the friend he once knew was gone. The prince who returned seemed darker. Like some sickness had taken root in him. There was madness in his eyes. And not long after his return, his father—the king then—was murdered. Stabbed in his sleep. Or so the story goes. His body was burned without the honors a king should have received. And with that, the prince who would become known as the Mad King took the throne.”

Aldmern’s expression hardened.

“The years that followed were… very dark. He grew more violent as time passed, and few dared oppose him. He was too powerful.”

He shrugged slightly.

“Eventually, Faldin was born. Many say the boy resembled the man the king had once been before the madness took him. Centuries later, after my own trials, Faldin finally killed him and took the throne.”

Aldmern trailed off, lost in his memories.

Tressa had heard that before, that the Mad King had once been kind. But that had changed after he left and came back. Killing his father and anyone who stood against him. He had been powerful. Her father said he had dragged young male elves into the court, forcing them to learn to fight and creating a group of assassins. He claimed any woman he fancied, and they were forced into the castle and unable to leave, doing nothing more than his bidding in bed, and kept locked within the palace like decorations. He even had devices created that locked around their necks and prevented them from leaving.

…Tressa was aware that her mother had been one of them.

The Mad King had taken her while she was already carrying Tressa. Or at least, that was what she had uncovered. Nobody ever told her directly, but she found it mentioned in some old documents and letters she was researching a while ago and was able to put the pieces together herself.

Tressa had been born in the castle, though she didn’t remember much or try to think of those early years of her life, then the Mad King was killed when she was only fourteen years. The Mad King had despised the fact that she was another elf’s child and not his, and her mother feared he would have her killed.

But that’s when her father joined Faldin in a coup against the Mad King and put an end to him.

“My trials,” Aldmern said, drawing her out of the dark thoughts.

“Yes?”

He sighed again. “Well, I had to fight a Mournstag.”

Tressa paled. She had read of them. They were gigantic, antlered beasts from the north, similar to forest stags but built with the bulk and muscle of a warhorse. Their hides were as hard to penetrate as reinforced leather, and their antlers were strong enough to tear through armor.

“It was almost impossible to beat that thing. I almost lost my life,” he said. “Thank the goddess it hadn't been a Sether Skin. Or I truly wouldn't have survived. But that was my first trial. Luckily, I only had two. My second was to research an old spell in the library and use it in a way that could benefit the people.”

“What spell?” Tressa asked carefully.

“The very one that you have imbued into that cloth,” he said smugly, pointing at the wrapped bread. “It's a preservation spell. It took a long time for me to find and create a way to advance it. And I passed. Though Falgin was mad and treated our trials like a spectacle, he still was sane enough not to interfere and allow the dragon to give them to us.”

Tressa nodded and nearly bit the end of her quill.

“I'm just glad the Mad King is gone,” Aldmern said. He leaned over and picked up a rock.

Tressa watched as he skipped it across the water. This time, it went farther than any of his previous attempts.

She nodded slightly. “My Mother is free as well.”

“As are you, my lady,” he said and came over to her side.

“I don't remember anything from that time,” she said. She looked back down at her notebook, letting her thoughts drift as her eyes traced the sketches of runes.

“If I may ask, my lady, what are you doing?”

“I'm..." She hesitated, preparing to close the notebook and change the subject. But she stopped. She knew Aldmern had some ability as a mage, though his true tallent lay in archery and tracking. She wished she had someone to talk to about this with, and who else could there be if not Aldmern?

"Tressa?" he asked.

She let a smirk touch her lips as she returned her focus to the inside of the notebook. “I’m... thinking about how to join runes with the old elven script to create an offensive spell. Though I don't know what I'm doing with it, as I've never been in battle. I have studied spells in the library and can use them. But I can’t seem to find the proper combination.”

“Why are you looking for an offensive spell…” He trailed off, catching her words. “Wait, my lady. Runes?”

She moved her quill along a line, darkening it. “I’ve come close to creating new spells before, but none are what the dragon is looking for, I think. Just minor variations of what already exists…” Then, she tapped her chin in thought as she muttered, “Surely the dragon won’t accept something so simple…”

“Impossible,” Aldmern muttered. “No one has created new spells since Faldor. The third king.”

“Hmmm.” Tressa lightly sketched some adjustments to the runes, moving them around. Then, she let out another frustrated grunt as they once again didn't feel right. Not like they had with her other spells.

“My lady.”

“Hm?” She was unwilling to look up and draw her eyes away from her book. She was already fixated on what she was doing.

“Tressa, show me what you have there.” He reached down to grab her wrist to stop her from writing. When she looked up, she saw his face hardened, anger creeping into his eyes. “Right now.”
