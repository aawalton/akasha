After the feast, Elynara crept back to her borrowed room and let the door click shut behind her.

Then she lowered herself onto the mat, knees folding in with practiced ease, and laid her staff across her lap. Her fingers rested lightly on the wood, tracing the familiar grain.

She exhaled once. Then began her training routine again.

It really was becoming her daily partner. Less training she must do, and more the training she did while relaxing, meditating.

The cycles of breathing in and out helping her relax as much as the training helped her grow.

It was a pleasant balance, she found.

Starpuff hovered just out of reach, pulsing a faint gold-green. Quiet for once. Watching.

“Not now,” she murmured.

The puff twirled lazily in reply, neither encouraging nor mocking, and floated toward the beam above her bed.

Elynara reached for her notebook instead.

Her pencil scratched the paper in short strokes, the numbers uneven but firm.

Mana capacity: 142.She stared at it for a while.

Then, below it, she added a line in smaller script:Goal: moving cycle. Mana breathing + gathering + shaping. Make it feel natural. Like walking.

She tapped the pencil against her chin.

If she could practice out a continuous rhythm she could train while on the move. Make her entire day part of her practice.

Even her failures had hinted at it.

She could feel the gap between techniques, the slack her mind always needed to re-center. If she could tie them together, that gap might vanish.

A cycle in motion was a cycle alive.

Well, that was the idea at least.

But one she already partly put into practice!

Her notebook slid shut with a soft thump, and she leaned back onto the bedding.

The blanket held the day’s warmth faintly, and she tucked it close. Her body had worked. Her mind had planned. And tomorrow would take her another step closer to her newest goal.

The wind outside pressed against the wooden walls. The beams creaked now and then, steady and calm.

Elynara smiled at the sound.

She didn’t know yet how long the journey would take. Or what she would find in the next village. Or whatever it was the ruins might be hiding.

The future was a list of uncertainties.

Yet each of her steps would dispel them all, one by one.

She whispered a goodnight to Starpuff, and let the quiet carry her down into sleep.

The morning air bit at her fingers.

Mist curled along the fields, low and damp, tugging at her boots as she crossed the village square one last time. The sky above was the color of old tin, the sun barely a pale smear behind it, but Elynara found herself walking lighter than the chill would suggest.

Her satchel bulged at her hip, fuller than when she arrived.

The villagers hadn’t let her leave empty-handed. Smoked meats wrapped in oiled cloth. And a herb bundle from a kind grandmother ‘just in case’. A carved button in the shape of a leaf, its back still warm from the woodcarver’s pocket. For luck, the man had said, even though he looked like he didn’t believe in much anymore.

Elynara bowed and thanked them all the same.

“Thank you, truly,” she said again, voice soft but clear. “I’ll make use of all of it.”

And she meant it.

Even the button!

The elder followed the goodbye with a blessing.

“May Celestia watch over you,” he said, voice carrying despite its quiet.

She nodded at that, and then was on her way.

Though before she even went beyond the village, Starpuff had managed to try and nibble on the herb bundle twice already, and had to be swatted away both times. It now floated with studied innocence just behind her head, pulsing a faint yellow that meant it was bored.

Or just wanted to play. She wasn’t sure which.

The mist was beginning to lift when she circled the edge of the village.

She turned and walked, the weight of her satchel warm against her back, the rhythm of her boots falling into place with each step.

Behind her, she heard nothing more. The elder would return to his place by the hearth. The village would start its day. And she would begin the next leg of her journey.

The road stretched ahead, softened by mist, still kissed with dew.

Starpuff darted ahead suddenly, twirling into the air before looping back over her shoulder, its glow brighter now.

Not bored anymore.

It dove once through the air above her head, spun a little spiral, then zipped off again.

Elynara didn’t chase after it.

But she did smile.

The morning nipped at her ears. Frost dusted the edge of the road, a brittle shimmer caught between the grass and stone. Although it was already melting away with the morning sun, Elynara still tugged her cloak closer and muttered into the scarf wrapped beneath her chin.

“Too early to be this cold.”

Starpuff poked its round face from the fold of her hood, all smug. It blinked green twice, then promptly tucked itself deeper into her cloak like it had found the most luxurious nest in all of Aerthis.

“Oh, you get to hide,” she grumbled, tapping her staff harder than needed. “I’m the one doing the walking.”

The road wound narrow ahead, a thin stretch of packed dirt lined by bare trees and tall grass turned gray with the season. Birds called from the higher branches, but the wind was dry. No other travelers passed.

Her breath fogged the air in short puffs. But her legs carried her onward regardless. The cold lessened after the first mile. Movement helped. The sun did too.

Still, the thought dug in.

What if I didn’t have to warm up the hard way?

She pulled her hand out of her coat and held her palm out as she walked, shaping a small ember just above the skin. It flickered weakly, no stronger than a candle. Heat bloomed against her palm.

It wasn’t quite enough.

Explosions and firebolts were easy. Flares, flashbursts, wide cones of shaped ignition – those she could do without thinking now.

But steady controlled heat? That was something she hadn’t done yet.

Elynara coaxed the ember into a thread, then folded the thread into a loose spiral, feeding it just enough mana to keep glowing. Her fingers tingled, warmth slowly crawling across her skin.

A spell for comfort.

She smiled faintly to herself.

“Let’s add it to the list of things to practice out.”

The road curved past a narrow stream, shallow and slow, its banks lined with brittle reeds. She paused to refill her waterskin, watching the surface ripple as she knelt. The spell flickered out as her focus shifted.

No matter. She would try again.

The next version used a ring instead of a thread. She drew the warmth along the inside of her cloak, weaving it loosely behind the lining. It faded quickly – not enough focus to bind it.

But it was progress.

Starpuff stirred in her hood and made a quiet chirrup of approval. Then it pretend-sneezed.

“I’m not heating you,” she said, glancing back. “You’ve got enough fluff for a puff!”

The puff rumbled with a pleased hum, wholly unrepentant. It wriggled deeper into the hood, settling itself until only the faintest wisp of its shine peeked free.

The sun reached a little higher.

By then, her hands had warmed, her cheeks pinked from wind and effort. The road leveled out, and the land opened wide again – rough brushlands bordered by forest, and to the side, the faded brown smear of hills long abandoned.

But there had once been something out there. That’s what Alicia said.

No more.

And far ahead, beyond the horizon was her destination.

The thought returned like a pulse.

Ruins meant answers. Pieces of the old world and whatever magitech still survived. Elynara’s fingers twitched at the idea.

Her feet moved faster without her noticing.

The morning chill hadn’t left entirely, but it no longer bit, all frost melting by now.

Her earlier spell attempts still itched at the edge of her thoughts, competing now with the possibilities of what lay ahead. Perhaps her comfort spell would be a step toward more. Magic shaped for daily use. Magic built into gear, into tools. Warmth, light, reinforcement, purification. And growth.

She imagined a cloak that carried its own heat. Boots that dried themselves after rain. Gloves that stayed just the right temperature.

And perhaps even a tool to provide a trickle of mana constantly?

Spirits would love that.

The idea hit with weight.

Would that even be feasible? The stable mana flow could certainly ensure the spirits are fed properly, and given mana to work their miracles.

But she knew it couldn’t be so easy. Offerings already did that, in a way. But still lost mana over time. And once it turned ‘bad’, it could no longer be consumed by spirits.

Still, it was worth exploring, another time. So she quickly noted it down and moved on.

Starpuff peeked out again, blinking into the morning light.

This time, it didn’t hide and came out to play. It even started hurrying her along, like she didn’t just carry it for hours!

Cheeky bugger.

By the time the sun crested the pale blue sky, the road no longer belonged only to her.

Boots crunched ahead of her. Voices carried. A clatter of iron and the creak of worn leather marked the return of Oltenar’s scattered hands. Adventurers – a ragtag bunch moving close yet apart. One alone, then a pair, and finally a band of 4 – emerged from the distant path, faces drawn from long work but heads held high.

Elynara eased her pace.

Some offered curt nods. Others gave cheerful waves or called her name across the distance. One even stopped entirely, recognizing her in full before she even turned.

“Well if it isn’t the Fire Queen,” said a lanky man with a short-sword on each hip, grinning wide.

“I am not answering to that,” she replied, flatly.

“Oh?” He gestured to her satchel. “Got anything to trade, Fire Queen?”

She opened it with a theatrical sigh and pulled free a strip of smoked meat.

“Meat first. Stories after.”

The exchange sparked others. Soon she stood among three – then five – huddled loosely in the middle of the road, passing goods and gossip between them.

“Collapse didn’t bury us, thankfully,” one woman muttered, flexing her wrapped wrist. “But hollow spaces it uncovered deeper in feel wrong. Air’s too dry. Sound echoes strange.”

“You echo strange! It’s probably just the shape of the rock that’s different. Don’t listen to that worrywart!”

Elynara frowned at that, though amused at their bickering.

“Any idea what could be deeper in?”

“No clue.” The man who spoke shrugged. “But I’ve been visiting those mines for thirty years. Talked to miners proper. And stone just doesn’t curl and crunch on its own that way. No siree.”

She made a mental note, then another when one of them offered her a tiny token in return for a second piece of meat – a smooth, bone-white coin with a faded emblem she didn’t recognize.

“Found that trinket in the caves,” he said. “But it’s not even made of metal. Doubt I could buy even a loaf of bread for it, so a piece of smoked meat seems more than fair an exchange.”

She rolled it between her fingers, letting its chill speak of travel and memory. Another piece of the world, now tucked safely in her bag.

The wind tugged faintly at her scarf. Starpuff emerged from her hood again, floating up to swirl lazily above the group.

It drifted in loose loops, leaving thin trails of light no one else saw.

“And you got all of that just for a bit of work at the village? They must have liked you.”

“I was just lucky.”

The chatter thinned eventually. They shared their destinations, exchanged guesses about upcoming work, then began to peel away one by one. A few waved again. One tossed her a bead carved in the shape of a turtle – “for travel luck,” they said.

She really was gathering a lot of ‘lucky’ trinkets now!

And then she was alone again.

Her boots settled back into their rhythm. Her staff tapped alongside it. And somewhere between the last wave and the next bend in the path, she realized her breathing had synced to the flow.

Mana gathered smoothly, cycling without demand. The familiar tug at her chest softened, steadied. It moved with her steps.

She blinked.

And it held.

A grin pulled across her lips.

“You work better when I’m distracted?” she asked her body under her breath. “Typical.”

She didn’t stop walking.

If anything, she pressed her heel firmer into the road, breath slow and deliberate. Her heart pounded, but the pulse didn’t shake the flow. It followed.

Mana breathing, on the move. Achieved!

Imperfect. Still a little clunky. But it worked.

Starpuff let out a chirp loud enough to nearly break her stride over. She didn’t scold it this time. Instead she laughed, light and sudden, and kept moving.

The next goal built itself in her mind without needing permission: walking, gathering, breathing – and soon, training too. If she could get the whole process working while traveling, then rest would stop being the only time she grew.

Even if it only gave her a handful of points over a week, it would be hers. Earned. Unstoppable!

It would mean she never wasted a single step again.

Or well, just some.

Elynara passed the crossroads without pause this time.

The signpost leaned slightly, the paint old but legible. She didn’t need it. Alicia had drilled the route into her twice. Still, she glanced up, just long enough to check the carving.

Two villages ahead. The first was closer now. The second – the one near the ruins – waited a few days farther.

Not that it mattered.

She would reach both. And would train through both.

And if the road offered more travelers, more strange tales or carved tokens, she would gather those too.

Another step. Another breath.

Another success, another smile.

And another spell worth building for.

The road dipped gently into the next valley, and the village came into view before she expected it. Timber walls framed a scattering of houses, their roofs patched with moss and wood tar. Smoke curled from chimneys in lazy spirals.

Everything about the place was quiet, lived-in, and purposeful.

The elder met her just past the square with a tired nod and a scroll tucked under one arm. They spoke briefly – an introduction, a short explanation, and the now-familiar terms. Help in exchange for food and shelter.

Then she went to work.

The hours passed in quiet service. She carried bundles from barn to hearth, and then helped re-bind the ward post by the southern edge. The shrine near the millstone was cracked and empty, so she marked it down before moving on.

Elynara spent the rest of daylight checking on the other locations, noting them down, and feeding the mana to those spirits still clinged to.

She barely noticed the sun slipping low. But it caught her hand just right when she shaped a small butterfly from heat and light, letting it drift between her fingers. A habit now. Something to keep her touch light.

A gasp broke her focus.

The butterfly popped.

She blinked, turned – and there were children.

Five? Six? Ten by the time she looked again. They stood in a loose ring, eyes wide, mouths half-open. The smallest one stepped forward, bold and determined.

“Do it again!”

“I… I’m not performing,” Elynara said, backing half a step. “I’m working.”

Another child tugged her sleeve.

“Please?”

Starpuff chose that exact moment to leap from her shoulder, spinning three tight loops and clearly nudging her on.

“Oh no,” Elynara muttered, but her protests were already drowned by their requests. A girl clapped her hands and pointed.

“More butterflies!”

A boy raised both arms.

“Make a dragon!”

“I cannot make a dragon,” Elynara said, deadpan.

Another voice piped up.

“What about a bear?”

“Definitely not a bear.”

The requests only multiplied. She raised both hands, sighing dramatically.

“Fine! No dragons. No bears. Just one butterfly. One!”

The flame shimmered out from her palm again, delicate and slow, the wings flickering gold and orange. Breaking her own promise, she shaped a second one quickly after, then a third, adjusting the airflow to keep them hovering.

Starpuff dove through them in a tangled arc. The children shouted their delight. One reached for a glowing wing, only for the heatless flicker to vanish before it touched his skin. He blinked in surprise, then laughed and clapped again.

And so it continued.

She added mist-butterflies next, the silvery shimmer drifting between the flame-ones like ghosts in dance. Starpuff zipped through them all, leaving trails of glittering energy that faded into the night.

At some point, adults began to gather too. One leaned against a wall, arms crossed, smiling faintly. Another passed Elynara a small pouch of candied nuts with a murmured thank-you. A third handed her a mug of hot spiced juice – perfect to warm her throat with.

“You’re good with them,” someone said.

“I didn’t have a choice,” she muttered, cheeks red. But she didn’t stop.

By now she had turned the small square into a slow whirl of glowing shapes. She shifted the pressure in her spells deliberately, forcing herself to stay just beneath the strain point.

Since she’s forced to do a magic show, she might as well get some practice in!

Progress through play.

All the while her mischievous puff rolled over in the dirt and played dead, content with simply watching the show.

By the time the last spell faded and the crowd dispersed, dusk had turned to full night.

The elder met her again with quiet thanks and a promise of fresh bread in the morning. She bowed lightly, still flushed from heat and motion.

Later, seated cross-legged in the corner of the small room they’d given her, she opened her notebook. Her hand still tingled from spell shaping. Her mana wasn’t full, but it was fuller. Though she decided against training more this time.

She grinned, tucked the notebook away, and let herself rest.

Practice was good.

But laughter certainly made it better.

She had hoped to leave at dawn.

But the villagers had other ideas and she got asked to help around almost as soon as she exited. It wasn’t much, not really. Just small tasks that unfolded one after another, quiet and earnest.

Elynara found herself helping without complaint. If anything, it made the farewell easier.

When she finally slung her satchel over her shoulder, it hung heavier than before. Bundles of dried food, more candied nuts for yesterday’s show, and another lucky charm – this one carved from birch, and a tiny string of buttons tied into a loop.

“For fun,” a little girl had said. And Starpuff had absolutely tried to eat it. Or bonked it for the heck of it.

Now it dangled from her belt, one button missing its edge. She hadn’t the heart to fix it.

The children gathered at the village edge to see her off. A few pouted. One small boy folded his arms and declared, very firmly,

“You have to come back. It’s a rule now.”

Elynara gave him a bow so deep it nearly toppled her.

“Then I will.”

And with that, she turned back to the road, to the sound of stamping feet and exaggerated sighs trailing behind her. Which quickly turned into more gasps and giggles when she sent out a bird of flame to circle around them.

She smiled, leaving for the road.

The sky overhead was pale and wide, the sun already tilting westward.

She tapped her staff gently against the path, felt the rhythm of her stride settle in. The road welcomed her again – cool wind at her back, dry grass brushing at her ankles, the faintest birdsong from the hedgerows.

There were no trees here. Just space and motion and possibility.

Elynara let the familiar rhythm draw her inward.

Mana gathered easily now. The strain from the past days had faded into a kind of smooth reflex. She breathed in, slowly, and let it settle. One pulse. Then another. In through her chest, held in her core, out through the soles of her boots as she walked. She didn’t stop to brace herself. Didn’t pause for breath.

It worked.

Mana breathing on the move.

Her lips parted in surprise, then curved upward in a grin. She kept walking, not wanting to break the flow, and reached for more. Chaining it to cycling was harder. Her breath hitched. Pressure stirred behind her ribs. But she stayed upright, gait steady.

Again.

Again.

It felt like dancing on the edge of something new, though not yet stable. The burn in her shoulders warned her to stop. She ignored it. The System blink flickered faint at the edge of her awareness just then, promising skill increases.

But Elynara just pushed on.

By the time the sun slanted orange through the distant hills, her head was light and her steps had slowed, but she hadn’t broken stride once. Mana circled within her, low and clean. Her capacity had inched forward again, reaching 143.

And on the move this time!

A breath of laughter burst from her lips, sharp and bright. It startled a bird from the brush, and Starpuff twitched on her shoulder like it meant to chase it before thinking better of the idea.

“You saw that,” she told the puff. “I didn’t stop. Not even once.”

It trilled, unimpressed. Then gave her ear a smug little boop on the nose and settled back into her hood.

Her grin didn’t fade.

The road curved ahead, soft and dipping, another line toward another village. More shrines. More work. And more room to test what she could do now.

The old ache of worry still lingered at the edge of her thoughts, but it was smaller now. A quiet guest, not a master.

She had made progress. Not just in numbers either. But in herself.

It was easy, sometimes, to forget that counted too.

She paused long enough to drink from her waterskin, then looked out across the open plain. There, far beyond the curve of the hill, she spotted another line of smoke. Another home, waiting. Another page to mark.

Another chance.

Her satchel bounced against her side as she walked, and the charm of buttons clacked softly in rhythm. She reached down to touch it once, then tucked her fingers into her cloak to warm them.

The wind had teeth, but she didn’t mind.

This chill wouldn’t slow her!

Starpuff stirred again and flitted upward, spiraling once in the air before diving back into her hood. A lazy celebration.

She nodded once, to herself and to the road.

Then walked on, smile steady, eyes bright.
