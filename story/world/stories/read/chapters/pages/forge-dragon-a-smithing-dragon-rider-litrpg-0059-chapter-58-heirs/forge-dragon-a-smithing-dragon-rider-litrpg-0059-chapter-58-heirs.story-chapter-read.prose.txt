The three equites and their two magical beast companions fled from the village after that. No matter how many archers Kelia killed, more came out of the woodworks. And while none could pierce her storm, she had begun to run low on mana and Mettius was out of potions.

Mettius was covered in blood as the gore had rained down over him as he stood directly below Cassandra as she’d been cleaved in half. He was not happy with the situation but had the presence of mind to hold off on his grumbling, instead shooting mixed looks at Caleb as they limped through the forest to catch up with Grisim and the fleeing villagers on a hastily bandaged leg.

The looks were part irritation but Caleb was unsure exactly what about the situation was bugging Mettius. Not because Caleb felt himself above Mettius’ ire, but because there were multiple things it could be. Mettius wasn’t the only one covered in gore, as after killing Cassandra, Caleb had immediately gotten to work removing the magical—and not ruined—armor from her body. The work had been messy, and Caleb was not running with the dismantled gore covered armor of an heir to the empire magically adhered to his back.

Caleb had wanted to recover the armor of the warrior he’d killed but decided not to press his luck. He just managed to recover his quench steel and his hammer when reinforcements arrived some somewhere deeper in the village.

They ran through the forest, the new reborn Avis flying up above, scouting ahead and relaying the scene to Kelia with a higher level of detail than the pair had been capable of the day before. They all had leveled, some of the multiple times, but they hadn’t had time to review or discuss the developments.

Once they were free of pursuit, Caleb reviewed his messages.

He went straight to his new skill, skipping all the other updates.

Sunder: Focus the power of your attack into a single point to destroy metal.

Simple and too the point, Caleb thought, but the description lacked the nuance he felt the skill deserved.

Having created the skill himself, he knew it could do more destroy. He could focus the power of a blow into a specific area as small as his perception could perceive. He saw potential in the forge as he knew a hammer blow could be as constructive as it can be destructive in the right circumstances. He could hammer blades to a razor edge, controlling exactly where he needed the energy to go. Forge welding, something he’d already improved his abilities on well past his former master’s skills, could now be done even more precisely. He suspected he might even be able to change the direction of the energy within the steel, allowing him to push the metal in ways otherwise impossible with a hammer.

Caleb went back to the rest of the screen and paused looking at the errors on these screens after quickly looking over all the changes. While the text said error, he could get a sense for what their rank had been if he focused on them message with his Magical Perception. He suspected he was looking at the essence from their bodies that had been transferred into himself when he did so, as the same didn’t work for the archers he had no part in killing. The dark archers he had participated in fighting all felt decidedly common, while the pyromancer and the knight felt of a similar rank to himself. Cassandra however felt much more powerful than himself.

Magical Perception has increased to level 7.

Caleb smiled at the late to come bonus. It wasn’t the first time his perception had increased due to experimenting with his status screen, and he wondered how malleable from skills the system menus could be if skills themselves interacted with it.

“What rank class was Imperial Shadow Assassin?” Caleb asked as they ran through the woods, all with the glazed overlook of initialized looking at their status.

“Rare,” Mettius said absent mindedly.

“Focus on the error and let me know what you see.”

Almost immediately Mettius smiled.

“She feels like a legendary,” he said. “And I leveled Magical Perception. That hasn’t gone up in weeks.”

“Mine too,” Kelia said, focusing on the error as well.

“She wasn’t before, right?” Caleb asked.

“No,” Mettius said. “She was a rare like all the heirs.”

“So, this benefactor evolved her class?”

“Yes, it must be. She only killed eight of us,” Mettius said.

“What do you mean?” Caleb asked.

“I shouldn’t have said that,” Mettius said.

“How do you know how many she killed,” Kelia asked. “By us you mean other heirs, right?”

“You’re not going to just let this pass?” Mettius said, looking between the pair.

“Fine,” he relented. “It isn’t a state secret, but it is a secret so don’t go spreading this around—especially to your brother.”

They both nodded, and looked in the direction of Grisim who was leading the group through the woods while they brought up the rear.

“I mentioned before the that the bloodline rewards fratricide. Well, when an heir kills another heir, they get a free point,” Mettius said.

He let the silence that followed linger until Kelia spoke.

“That’s horrible,” she said, and Caleb nodded in agreement.

“It is to some but to others like my late half-sister Cassandra, it was wonderful,” Mettius said. “She killed eight of my siblings.”

“How do you know?” Caleb asked sensing the Mettius was talking around something.

He took a breath, contemplating, and then let it out with a sigh.

“When an heir kills another, they get one free point for the kill, and then get all the free points they slain heir had acquired.”

“That’s why you cast a Force Bolt at her,” Caleb said. “Because if I killed her the points would go away.”

“Actually, you would have gotten the free points along with an achievement. You wouldn’t have gotten the bloodline though and another heir could kill you for the points.”

“Elders, that’s terrible,” Caleb said. “How many are like Casandra?”

“Murderous psychopaths?” Mettius asked. “A dozen or so, but the problem is the ratio of non-psychopath to psychopath is increasing each day.”

“Why did your father give out the class Imperial Shadow Assassin to your sister then?” Kelia asked.

Mettius gave her a look like it was the most obvious thing in the world.

Caleb answered for him.

“So she could kill people.”

Mettius nodded.

“Father encourages the killing. The emperor must be strong in his eyes and he gives the classes best suited for the situation to those he thinks would best rule.”

Caleb thought about Mettius’ class, a diplomat, and decided to not point out the obvious.

The conversation died after that, and they all went back to reviewing their screens.

Caleb had a new skill to review he’d been distracted from.
