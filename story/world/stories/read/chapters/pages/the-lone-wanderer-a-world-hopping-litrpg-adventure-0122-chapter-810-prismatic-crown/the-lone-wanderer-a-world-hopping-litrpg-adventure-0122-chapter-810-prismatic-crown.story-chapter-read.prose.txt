Percy could already feel the rush of potent mana travelling between organs and being transformed from one affinity into another. Acid moved from the wasp’s second core to the gland in her abdomen, and from there to her jelly gland through the corresponding tubes. At the same time, impurities in her acid and royal jelly that didn’t contain much mana were funnelled into her ruby gland to be repurposed into powder.

Based on what Percy had been told by his mentors, only deities who had completely mastered the concepts of multiple elements could convert their mana between them without losses, yet his familiar’s body could somehow replicate the complex process automatically through the wide assortment of interlocked organs that her new mutation had granted her.

‘Any idea how this will affect your capabilities?’ he asked. Having spent a lot of time sharing the wasp’s body, Percy already had some estimates of his own, but he wouldn’t mind getting a second opinion.

‘If we’re talking just about my jelly output, it should have grown by a factor of five or six. Maybe slightly more,’ the reincarnated deity replied.

Percy failed to suppress a grin. A single promotion should have only tripled her production. Not even that, in fact, because only one of her cores had advanced and her beast affinity would have a lower proportion of acid mana to feast on. She clearly owed the rest of the boost to the improved efficiency of her biological functions.

If the wasp’s output had indeed increased as much as she claimed, she would be able to produce as much royal jelly as she and her mother had jointly secreted before, allowing Percy to resume brewing Gloomy Dawn for his grandfather and the other patriarchs. Maybe he’d finally have enough for Galahad and Elaine.

‘What about your control over the bugs?’ he asked with a tinge of hope seeping into his voice.

‘Probably a similar improvement. At the very least, I should be able to keep the peace inside the hive, so you can ask Nesha to resume her work on the mana-gathering array the next time you see her,’ Nephthys reassured him. ‘But that’s not all. I feel that this new Prismatic Crown of mine is capable of much more.’

‘Well… what are you waiting for? Try to use it!’ Percy encouraged her.

Nodding, the giant wasp redirected half of her elemental mana – the lightning half, specifically – to the colourful gem framing the top of her head while only feeding her water and beast mana into her jelly gland.

As a side effect of the new mutations, Percy noticed that Nephthys could keep her boosting art running completely effortlessly. She’d technically been able to do that for a while due to her familiarity with the spell, but her body now took care of the internal flow automatically, almost like the shape of her mana network had been built into her newly altered organs.

It only took a thought for her to redirect her magical resources into whichever body part she wanted. In practice, this didn’t really change much, because maintaining the Dance had been as easy as breathing for Nephthys, but Percy was relieved that it would be even harder for something to go wrong with her artificial promotion.

As soon as the rush of lightning entered her crown, the jewel lit up in a bright indigo colour that completely overpowered the other hues. The eight prongs began vibrating softly but rapidly, filling the wasp’s cavern with a gentle hum.

Sharing a body with his familiar, Percy could feel their mental reach expand and grow more precise, sensing the location, type, and even the current task of every single wasp in the hive. Even after borrowing all of his main body’s processing power, he and Nephthys would still struggle to micromanage more than a few bugs at once, but they at least had a better idea of what their subordinates were doing, and should be able to loosely direct them in far greater numbers.

The instincts afforded to Percy by the Command trait made the process even smoother by subconsciously storing this information in a corner of his mind and letting him tap into whatever parts he needed at will. It really was a shame that Nephthys didn’t have access to the trait, but he could still help her manage her subjects more effectively.

‘This can’t be everything…’ the Ruby Princess muttered, disappointment evident in her tone. ‘My antennae merged with the crystal housing my people, so there has to be another function we have yet to discover.’

Percy wasn’t sure whether his familiar was genuinely onto something, or whether this was just wishful thinking, but he wasn’t going to discourage a mother that yearned to reunite with her son.

‘Pick one of the wasp souls that we previously stuffed into the gem and try to do something with it,’ he suggested.

The Ruby Princess did precisely that, though the silver dot refused to so much as twitch. Even after evolving, her mutation seemed to only allow souls to be stored into it temporarily, or to be ferried back to their own bodies with Percy’s help – assuming that said bodies still existed.

Sensing the pit of despair forming across their connection, Percy couldn’t bear to accept this failure. Thinking of something, he reached into the special mana channels he had singled out for affinity fusion within his own head and extracted a droplet of dream mana to send to Nephthys.

The pink substance passed through the enriched cord and reached the wasp’s soul without issue. Next, he redirected it into his familiar’s crown, hoping to elicit a reaction.

Percy and Nephthys – who had probably realized what he was trying to do – watched with bated breaths as the Starry Soldier’s dormant soul absorbed about a fifth of the droplet as greedily as the cords tended to do, instantly doubling in size. A foreign mind awakened near their own. It was a mind of a familiar sort, since Percy had possessed countless wasps of all grades in the past, and Nephthys was used to commanding the creatures or even commandeering their bodies from afar.

They felt the disembodied bug’s attempts to communicate, though a Yellow wasp didn’t really have much to say. Even so, the implications of this breakthrough weren’t lost on them. Percy could tell that his familiar would have burst into tears already, had her compound eyes been capable of shedding them.

At last, Nephthys would be able to awaken and talk to her people!

The effects of Percy’s dream mana on the sleeping souls would likely be temporary, just like they were for his cords, but that might be for the best. They wouldn’t want to permanently awaken the Amenthei before having a way to grant them new bodies, as perpetually being trapped inside the gem would be quite unpleasant.

Either way, Nephthys was clearly elated that she could now chat with her son whenever she wanted.

‘I feel like we’re still only scratching the surface,’ Percy admitted. ‘The two mutations have both grown significantly stronger, but that doesn’t explain why they merged.’

‘Do you think there might be some way to project the stored minds into living wasps?’ Nephthys asked.

‘Only one way to find out,’ Percy replied with a mental shrug.

Picking another Starry Soldier at random – this one having a body of its own and currently patrolling the crystal-lit corridors of the hive – Percy tried to channel the disembodied bug’s thoughts into its cousin’s head.

The silver dot within the crown flickered for a second before latching onto the only available outlet for its mind. The recipient resisted the invasion for the briefest of instants, though eventually yielded to the matriarch’s authority and allowed its relative to cohabit its body.

Examining the pair of combined Starry Soldiers, Percy felt that their current state greatly resembled the method by which he possessed his own hosts. The only difference was that the soul of one of the bugs was still technically trapped within his familiar’s gem, and its mind was only being temporarily projected into the borrowed body via the power of the mutation.

‘Interesting… though stuffing a pair of Yellow wasps into the same body isn’t all that useful,’ he noted, scrambling to come up with an application for their new ability beyond giving the Amenthei a chance to stretch their limbs.

‘I think that the bugs are slightly easier to control,’ Nephthys pointed out.

Raising an eyebrow, Percy confirmed that she was right after ordering the wasps around for a few seconds. They responded to his commands faster and required less handholding, while also placing less of a strain on his mind, though he wasn’t sure why exactly that was.

Maybe it had to do with the fact that two Starry Soldiers were smarter than one, or the fact that one of them was technically still located inside Nephthys head and could receive his instructions more easily.

‘Hmmm… this ability is definitely wasted on the Yellow bugs,’ he concluded. ‘What if we channelled an already-intelligent mind – such as one of your people or a smarter wasp – into a Starry Commander?’

Starry Breeders and Commanders could also exert their wills upon their lesser kin like the royal wasps, though they were typically limited to just a handful of creatures in their immediate vicinity. Unfortunately, such pitiful control wasn’t enough for them to help an immature Princess like Nephthys make up for her shortcomings.

However, things might be different if the new mutation could expand the wasps’ range.

Eager to put Percy’s idea to the test, the reincarnated goddess poured another portion of dream mana into a second slumbering soul before channelling the awakening mind into a Blue wasp. Even the body of a two-cored Starry Commander was admittedly a bit of a downgrade for this particular soul, but beggars couldn’t be choosers. There was nobody better to help Percy and Nephthys expand their control over the creatures.

As soon as the revived wasp came to her senses in her temporary body, Percy greeted her warmly.

‘I must admit that I hadn’t expected us to reunite again this soon, but it’s good to have you back… Your Majesty.’
