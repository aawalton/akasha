The many forces and fighters arrayed against the undead champion struggled mightily against him. Every hit on his armor was a scrape or scuff, and he was as dodgy as he was strong. Grappling or entangling was nothing more than temporary, and he had continual support liches and wizards bolstering and protecting him. On top of this, the death knights around him, universally in heavy gray plate, were not slouches, nor few in number. They engaged the elites, disrupted their focus, and did not fall immediately to weapon, spell, or the Second Sun’s destructive aura. It did weaken them, at least.

The order soon came through to focus on and destroy the death knights, while keeping the champion contained.

Easier said than done. With a great cry, Zadkiel stomped the skull of a death knight after knocking him down. The metal helm became a pancake as there was a satisfying bone-smashing sound.

“Real nice,” Ba-Ra chimed sarcastically in his head. “You’re wasting time. You know your assigned role.”

“Yeah, yeah, ya nag,” Zadkiel sent back with an internal sigh. He moved on quickly, advancing once more on the shifting form of the champion as various others also collapsed on him. “Just taking what I can get.”

Zadkiel managed to kill only the one death knight because he was largely tasked with distracting the champion. Frustration caused him to lash out in anger as the opportunity presented itself. Whatever power Zadkiel directed at the champion, even his very best, was countered or avoided. He’d even tried a [Rocket Fist]! Nothing. The wraith’s wretched substance absorbed AoE and physical matter passed right through. At best, he was disrupted and knocked around by hits to his armor, or brief, continual attempts at entanglement.

And good old-fashioned terrain breaks. The earth was very hard to break up appreciably, but Zadkiel was one of the few strong enough to do it. Magic couldn’t touch it, but enough raw physical force did the trick — his specialty. All around them, the terrain was rougher, as he’d smashed into it and blown it up. The undead champion was constantly trying to avoid the poor footing, sliding the whole elite grouping to the flank at times. Meanwhile, they tried to keep him hemmed in.

Something Redberry was succeeding at somewhat. She was right in the mix with the rest of them. Perhaps too much so. A visible scratch on the breastplate was from her talons, but that particular push into melee had cost her rather dearly, slashed with the sword and raked with the whip. Thankfully, the whip had only hit her arm, and she’d detached and regrown it even as her arm disintegrated. He knew she’d expended some precious resources to pull that off, and she’d been cautious since then, even as she glared at her foe with dark murder and bared fangs.

So hot. Maybe she can take her frustrations out on me later. Hehe.

He certainly understood her sentiment. The whip had to be avoided or blocked with something expendable. It was among the most dangerous of weapons Zadkiel had ever faced. They’d tried to damage, disrupt, or dispel it, or even just get it out of his hands, anything to neutralize the threat that had killed many of their precious elites already — all to no avail.

Even disrupting him enough to evade casualties was difficult. He’d do it just to force a reaction, so he could then pivot and try for someone even more valuable. Thankfully, they’d kept him pushed back, where he couldn’t get to the Radiancers nor the Second Sun. This limited the ability of the enemy army to collapse inward toward them. There was a big pocket only filled with their elites, essentially.

A duel and a slowly shifting stalemate. Zadkiel had taken wounds, favoring the sword rather than making himself presentable to the whip. Casualties had been generated on both sides. Seraphiel and Athos, powerful servitors both, had been wiped from the field. Zadkiel’s own best had precious losses, including numerous Servitors and, infuriatingly, his second strongest war priest. Sir Remus had been gravely injured and vacated, though he would likely live. The death knights, meanwhile, were thinning out, slowly but surely.

Of all those fighting, Canmore was surprisingly accounted the most deadly to the death knights. His glowing light-blade shone brighter than the Second Sun itself as he chopped through his foes with little effort. Enough that they were trying to avoid him as much as possible, and maneuvering lesser zombies in his way to stall him.

That strange character, Odysseus, was an absurdly strong fighter and stayed on the champion like glue, constantly harrying and annoying him, seemingly without fear of the whip he kept narrowly avoiding. After a dozen attempts to trick him into getting lashed, the champion just gave up. Sadly, the mercenary knight couldn’t damage him. Instead, his efforts saved others from wounds as he forced a clash of blades with a parry.

His speed and awareness are just unreal. Can’t blindside him at all. With Deikmorn permanently weakened from the Sage, this man is stronger. Hard to swallow, but blatantly true.

Deikmorn might actually be dead if not for the man. While much more cautious, he’d tried his hand at dueling the champion, too, and barely came away. An interruption from Odysseus had done the deed. Now, Deikmorn had been ordered to execute death knights.

The Radiancers had started to focus their fire on helping kill death knights, and though the warriors were a little more resistant than the giants and had shields that seemed to help protect them, they were massively weakened from a volley and much easier to take down with another striker.

Bast was shifting from flank to flank, using his extreme AoE specialty to gore out any zombie warriors that tried to press the borders. Occasionally, he shifted to blast a grouping of death knights, though it was never enough to put them down by itself. He didn’t bother with the champion, steering clear. He’d tried his detonation-type effects on the champion, but they did nothing but get absorbed, like all other magic. He’d been smart about not getting close enough in range of the whip, too. Apparently, he had to be ‘saved’ for some special operation later to commence.

Another bloody stage, even after this. Fucking Matrazere! A pain in the ass, aren’t you? I’m glad I’ve got the Goddess of Fate on my side…

Beyond the line of their fight, another was being waged with blistering waves of magic at speeds to shame lightning, as Samantha and her team, abjuration and countermagic heavy, struck at the backline of the liches. She’d destroyed three of them already. Liches were quite notorious for dragging out a battle even against a balanced party. Each was a legend, essentially. Samantha was causing enough trouble; she’d started to pull the bulk of their attention away from supporting their precious champion, weakening the connection between them that strengthened his prowess somehow. The third she’d destroyed in a much longer frame than the first two, as everything adjusted, but that necessity was arguably better in results for their purposes.

Nearly doing it alone. All the offense against how many powerhouses? Let’s face it: she's just on another level now. Closer to Glae than to any of us.

A bone dragon from above got free enough to unleash a deadly breath attack, raking a huge portion of their elites. Zadkiel was right on the edge and avoided it, thanks to being closer to the champion.

He winced at seeing men and Naugites die, drop, or falter, creating another setback, but he turned away. He had his own role and shit to worry about.

As Zadkiel closed in on his moving target, trying right then to whip another minotaur to death, he saw his usual ‘partner’ gearing up to strike. Gelmak Por was one of the few strong enough to knock the champion with a good hit. He was also brandishing good weaponry for it too — dual-wielding a powerful axe artifact, and a specialist knocking hammer with a huge rectangular head, basically a maul or sledgehammer. Slow, but nasty. The undead champion avoided it like the plague because it had knocked him like nothing else. Not even Zadkiel’s punches had that impact. It had only connected decently once, and even that was a glancer that was rolled with, sending the champion spinning and rolling in the dirt like an acrobat.

Tough to admit. In fact, Zadkiel was just now actually doing so. It grated at his ego, but… he’d seen enough during the war to be taken down a notch, deep within. He’d come to admire many people along the way, and even to see that they were better than him at fighting, or strategy, or just sheer power. Only for the camaraderie he’d found with them could he see it and admit it, but he finally had.

Some inner part of him was demanding that he recognize things as they were, for the greater good; his brain began doing inner calculus like the devilish, pragmatic plotter he could be — the imp, when needed. It revealed what he needed to do.

Ba-Ra sensed this and was amazed. “You’re actually going to do something smart?!”

“Shut up, you!” Zadkiel retorted with a mental snarl. “I’m thinkin’, here.”

Think he did — but not for long. He connected to the team gestalt and said, “Let’s pivot and coordinate to set up Gelmak’s hammer strike. Buff the shit out of him! I’m not alone in wanting to see what a direct hit would do, am I? The rest of us disrupt and overwhelm. He’s the kill dog.”

“Now, that’s a damn good idea,” Deikmorn said. “I saw that panic roll on the hammer hit, too. A solid strike might actually dent the fucking armor!”

“I think this is wisdom, yes,” Nyomel offered.

“Go for it,” Redberry said simply. “Anything to shift matters dealing with this shitstain on reality.”

Even Dax chimed in. “Holy shit, I think you’re right, Zaddy. Do it. Let’s go all in. Our goddess is a gambler queen, after all.”

Gelmak was solemn as he replied, “I am humbled and honored by this confidence from so many. I will not let you down. For my fallen brethren, for Father Mountain and all minotaurkind, may the strike ring true.”
