The square was perfectly silent. Nobody spoke. This was the kind of silence that I had once commanded without a thought.

The boy's mother held him against her chest. His eyes were open and they stared at nothing. His chest moved, but his breathing only came in the thinnest of gasps. The Ink had left no marks on his skin, but the boy it had touched would never be the same.

The Inker watched all of this impassively. He had seen a sight like this before, and he would see it again. The soldiers flanked him with the same indifference. "Next," he said.

The crowd stirred but nobody moved forward. All of the children had received their marks. None of them were bounding on their feet any more. One of the younger girls was crying. It wasn't Sara. The Inker's gaze moved to me. When he spoke, his voice carried across the square. "The adults, then." His gaze moved from me to Ash, then back to me. "Unless, of course, they have reconsidered."

This was a challenge. One he clearly hoped I would take him up on. Ash's hand grabbed my wrist. "Are you still going through with this?" she whispered.

I did not pull away from her. "Are you?"

The Hero took in the boy and his mother. Then she took in the Inker, who was readying the needle again. "Yes," Ash said. I said nothing. She took that for what it was.

The crowd parted as Ash stepped forward. She walked with her back straight and her stride steady. I had seen Heroes walk towards death before, and I saw it now. She knelt before the Inker without being asked. Something inside me clenched as I watched her.

I did not pray, but I did hope.

"Give me your arm," the Inker said, one arm extended. Ash held out her arm. The man took it roughly, dipped the needle into the vial, and pressed it to her skin.

The needle was cold against Ash's skin for a single moment. In the next moment, she felt nothing at all.

In one breath she was in the village square, and in the next she was somewhere else. There was complete darkness around her, stretching in every direction unto infinity. It felt familiar. She had been in places like this before. Spaces between one breath and the next, where the Gods reached in and measured a man's worth. She wasn't surprised, not after what she had felt.

Ash had given her life once before. She had given it in the throne room of the Demon Queen by burning everything that she was. And then Orvyn had knelt beside her in the rubble and had taken what little was left. Yes, Ash had died once already.

There had been no fear when Ash had used the Hero's Requiem. Because she had always known it might happen, as it had to so many like her. There was fear now. Ash didn't know what to do with that.

She had changed since she had come to this far future. But what had changed her? Ash did not know. Was it the days of hauling wood and carrying water? No, Ash had done that before. Perhaps it was that first night, in the forest. Ash had sat across a fire from the Demon Queen, and heard questions she had never thought to hear. Perhaps it had happened with the plunging of that black blade. From a betrayal that had stung far worse than the death itself.

But being a Hero was not so simple as not knowing fear. It was to be willing to stand against something that might destroy her, so that the one behind her would have the chance to flee. Ash had stepped forward towards the Inker with her back straight. With no hesitation at all. Ash was ready to give her life again, if she had to.

Ash thought of Lysanthia, watching from the edge of the crowd. If this Ink killed Ash like she expected it might, then Lysanthia would see. She would have the chance to run. Even past all these soldiers, she might just make it. Part of Ash did not want her to. For what it might mean for this village and all of the people in it. A part of her wanted to anyway.

It was strange that she wished for Lysanthia to live, when she had killed her in the first place. It was a contradiction, and knowing it was one did not answer it.

All around her was darkness, stretching into infinity. Ash could not see a thing, and yet she felt something stir with instinct alone. The System appeared before her. A screen opened in the air, blue and faintly transparent. This was familiar. It was the same thing Ash had seen for twenty-three years. She knew the System almost better than she knew herself. The System still existed in this time, and now it used this strange Ink for some reason.

Ash blinked. The System was familiar, but it was not the same as it had been. The System before her flickered like an ember in the wind. The edges of it were cracked and chipped, and its blue was far fainter than she remembered it being. And there were the black lines, crawling over the surface of it. Ash had never seen those before. Text appeared on the screen, crawling across its surface.

[Assessing...]

[Calculating Potential...]

The entire screen flickered once before the text moved again.

[Gaining Insight...]

Then the errors came. They started at the bottom and slowly crawled to the top of the screen.

[WARNING: Mana signature predates current index by 984 years.]

[WARNING: Residual connection to Fate detected. Status: SEVERED. Echo strength: EXTREME.]

[WARNING: Soul-level modification detected. Origin: Hero's Requiem. Classification: UNKNOWN.]

[ERROR: Recalibrating...]

[ERROR: Recalibration failed.]

The entire screen shook. Then it dimmed, until it was so dark Ash almost couldn't make out anything at all. It brightened and steadied. It seemed the last of the errors had passed. The lines of text that scrolled now were all titles.

[The People's Champion]

[The Selfless Blade]

[Winner Of The Endless Fight]

[She Who Walked Willingly Into The Dark]

...................

Ash watched them scroll past. She understood what each of the titles was and what they were for. How could she not? Eventually, all of the lines of text moved past, leaving behind only one.

[The Last Hero]

The words sat in the dark for a long time. The Last, the title said. Not the greatest, or the mightiest. Her hands were clenched very tight.

The screen flickered again, and new text formed. The assessment was done, Ash knew. And so came her result.

[Marks: 2]

She had been given two. The Inker had been rather confident everyone would only receive one.

[Sovereign Mark: SEVER]

Her left arm burned. It felt like a blade was pressed against her skin from the inside out. Ash didn't look at it. For she was thinking of what the System had just shown her.

Sever. That was the name of her mark. It was a strange name, but its meaning was simple: cutting. It was fitting. It was a power Ash knew better than any other. What had she spent a lifetime doing but cut? She had cut through monsters, cut through hordes, cut through the Demon Queen herself. Until, in the end, something had cut through her. Yes, if any single word could sum up Ash's long life, then perhaps it would be Sever.

[Cipher Mark: VEIL]

Something warm brushed her right arm. The touch was light, almost gentle. Ash did not turn her head down to look. Sadness rose from somewhere deep inside her and pressed against the back of her eyes.

Veil. That was the name of her second mark. Ash knew what this mark meant too, almost as well as she understood the first. A power related to illusion, perhaps with concealment.

Ash glared at the screen. There must have been some mistake. The System was wrong. The mark it had given her was wrong. It finds. The Inker's words echoed in her mind. She did not believe it. Not then, and not now. She blinked past wetness and kept glaring.

The screen flickered one last time, and disappeared. Ash spent one moment in that infinite darkness. Then she was back in the square, kneeling in front of the Inker. The man's face had gone pale. He did not know anything of what happened, but perhaps he knew something of what had happened to her. If he did, then even that little something had made him go pale.

Ash looked down at her arms, and there was a single dot on each wrist. No, the word had been Spark, right? The Sever was black, and the Veil was purple. Both marks were so small she might have missed them, but she could feel them, warm beneath her skin. It was like suddenly getting new fingers.

She looked up at the Inker. He stared back at her. Ash smiled, and it was not a Hero's smile. Just a tired woman's.

"It seems I was found worthy after all."

"Sovereign!" The Inker's voice went high as he said the word. He had to say it twice before the crowd understood. "And Cipher!"

There was silence again at that, and it was deeper now than it had before. I saw widened eyes and open mouths in equal measure. Some of the soldiers shifted their weight and looked at each other.

The Inker steadied himself and I watched the effort it took. His hands were still trembling when he turned to me. "You," he said. "The half-demon. You are the last. Come."

The man had regained his composure, or most of it. It might have been an act, and if it was, the man was quite the performer. It took only a second for the sneer to come back. Ash passed me on her way back and our eyes met for just a moment. Her arm lightly brushed mine, though she could have easily walked without touching me at all.

I stepped forward. The crowd watched me the way crowds had always watched me. With fear and with fascination. It was not quite the same now, but it was similar enough.

I knelt. It was easy to bend this time because what came next would either kill me or make me something more than what I was now. Either outcome was acceptable. Either was better than being weak. I told myself that was why.

The Inker took my arm almost before I took it. "If the Ink rejects you," he said quietly, low enough that only I could hear, "I will not mourn."

"If it rejects me, I will not care either way."

His eyes narrowed, and then the needle pressed against my skin. The needle felt cold, but only for a moment. The village square vanished, and I now stood in darkness. A darkness that stretched unto infinity. I was not alone here. Something lived in this place. Something old. So old that even I, who had lived for so very long, felt small before it.

The thing that lived here noticed me. I felt its gaze. It was a gaze that came with weight. A great deal of it, as if all of the heavens had condensed down into a single stare. Perhaps they had.

Rage bubbled within me. My teeth clenched. I knew this gaze, and the thing it belonged to well. I had felt it once before, in a place that had looked almost exactly like this. It was on the day when I had first been given that mighty class and the path of my life had been set. I had not known then that it would culminate in the role that it did. I suspected this thing that watched me now had known it all along.

This was the System, or what remained of it. So it had been waiting for me after all.

A screen appeared before me, blue and transparent. I had seen it tens of thousands of times, and so I knew it well. This was not the System I remembered. This screen was cracked at the edges and lightly flickering. Black lines ran over it in no pattern I could discern.

And it was afraid of me. I could feel it in the way the screen flickered. In the way the light in it dimmed and brightened. "Perhaps you have your regrets too, you bastard," I whispered to the darkness as text appeared.

[A-Assessing...]

The screen shuddered before me.

[Calculating P-Potential...]

I felt the strange weight of this thing pull away, but only for a moment. Then it came back, and this time the touch was lighter. More careful. It seemed this System needed to look deeper to assess me, but did not want to.

[Gaining Insight...]

Another crack cut across the screen. It started in the centre-left, and cut across the whole of it. Then there was another, one that started at the top, and ran all the way down to the bottom. The flickering blue light dimmed entirely, until I could not read at all and was left in complete darkness. Then the System came back again. Text started to crawl upwards from its bottom.

The screen was shaking now, so much that the text was getting difficult to read. This thing did not want to continue but it continued anyway because that was its purpose. Its Role. Even this thing had always had its chain. I almost pitied it. The shaking steadied, and then more text started to scroll past.

[Godkiller]

[The Scourge of Stone]

[She Who Broke the Pantheon's Spine]

[The One Who Brought the Endless Night]

[Slayer of the Great Dragon Ozriel]

I knew what these lines meant. They were Titles. All of the Titles the System had given me. I had seen almost all of these before. They were summations of my entire life writ plain before me. I had seen these titles before and I had smirked. I had revelled. I saw them now and did not know what I felt, only that it was not nothing. More titles came and went, scrolling for minutes, or perhaps it only felt that way in this place. I did not look at them, not until we came to the last one. The screen paused, longer than any pause before it.

[The Last Demon Queen]

I stared at those words for some time. The System called me the last Demon Queen. Not the strongest, though I was. Not the cruelest, though I had been. The last. Of all that I had been and achieved, it thought this the thing worthy of a title.

There had been Demon Queens before me. Hundreds of them, stretching back to a time even I considered myth. All of them were links in a chain that had bound my kind since the Gods had first forged it.

The screen dimmed, and flickered no more. Some part of me knew that the assessment was done. The only thing to do now was to wait for the result.

[Marks: 1]

One mark. The System was giving me one mark, just as the Inker had said. Ash had received two. It was a small matter, depending on what the mark was. The text changed again, and this time the lettering was larger than it had been for everything else. Such that it took up a good portion of the screen.

[Sovereign Mark: REQUIEM - The Ending]

My right arm stung as pain came. The pain was sharp, and cold. Like a blade that had cut my skin from the inside. I did not look down. I was too busy staring at the text scrawled across the screen.

Requiem: The Ending. This was the power to unmake. To cease. To end.

This was a power I knew well. Not by this name, perhaps, but I knew it well. I knew it better than anyone. This was me. The woman who had ended civilizations. Ended Gods. Even in a new body, in a new world, what I was had not changed. I had hoped it would have. The Inker had said that the Ink finds. He had been more right than he could have ever known.

Sadness welled up in my chest. Sadness for…me. That surprised me. I had not expected to feel it. I had not even known I could.

The Requiem...the ending. Of course, what else would Lysanthia receive? If I was to receive one mark, and only one, then it could have only been this. The thought was bitter now. The screen flickered. I froze as fresh text appeared.

[ERROR: New Turning Detected.]

[Marks: 2]

I stared. The screen was dim and flickering, but I could read the text. There could be no mistake. Was this thing giving me a new mark? I had seen the System do many strange things in my time, but I had never seen it reassess. I braced myself, though I did not know what I was bracing for.

[Sovereign Mark: CRADLE - The Beginning]

I expected pain like there had been last time. It never came. Instead, there was warmth. A warmth that brushed my left arm. It was soft. Gentle, almost tender. The kind I had never felt before.

Cradle: The Beginning.

I read the words several times, and understood them no better for it. I knew what they meant. I did not understand why I saw them now. This mark had the power to begin things. The power to begin, given to someone who had never begun anything. I had only ever ended things. Even my own story was an ending, the story of the Last Demon Queen. What beginning could the Ink possibly have found in me? No, what beginning could this thing have found in me?

In the darkness, visions came. The first was of a flickering flame, one that had almost fizzled from a strong gust of wind. Then another came. A vase of mud. One of many. The only one that was recognizably a vase. No. I shook the thoughts away, and the visions went with them.

The screen in front of me had dimmed. The whole thing flickered once, and the light winked out entirely. As soon as it did, I felt the thing and its gaze withdraw. It withdrew quickly, the way a hand pulls away from a flame. I stayed alone in that darkness for a handful of seconds. Then I was back in the clearing, beneath the lantern light, and the Inker stood before me.

His instrument was no longer in his hand. I found it lying in the dirt at his feet, leaking ink in a small puddle that almost looked like black blood. I looked from it to the man himself. He was so pale he looked like a ghost. His marks were flickering on his arms. His mouth moved open and closed but no sound came out.

I looked down at my arms. There was one small Spark on each. Both Sparks were so small, so insignificant to look upon. The left pulsed with warmth. The right pulsed with cold.

Nobody spoke. Nobody moved. Perhaps nobody even breathed, not even the Inker. His lips finally moved. "Sovereign," he whispered. Then, perhaps because the word alone was not enough, "Both of them. Both marks are Sovereign. Both how-"

The man's mouth closed. He took a single step back, until his hand touched the edge of his case. One of the many soldiers took a step forward. Then another. Most stood perfectly still. Too still. I rose from my knees slowly, the way a queen rises.

I looked at the Inker, then at the crowd of small, frightened people who had fed me and sheltered me. Most of all, at the little girl, who had accepted something from me that hadn't been worth taking. I looked at Ash. The Hero watched me from the edge of the crowd.

I felt lighter. Something inside me had loosened in the forest, when I had first seen the sky. I felt it again now, and this time it was set free entirely.

"It seems," I said quietly, "that I might be worthy too."

The lanterns flickered in a breeze that came from nowhere. Nobody spoke, even now. It was so quiet I could hear the sound of my own heartbeat. On my left arm, the Cradle pulsed warmth and on my right, the Requiem pulsed cold.

And between them, the world was silent.
