After launching their first successful mission against The Council of Death, the team broke for the rest of the day to relax and focus on their own individual trainings. Moxy ran off to find Christian and try to convince the captain of the royal guards to stop pulling his punches during their spars once again. Rulfar went out into the wilderness, telling them he’d be back late and to not worry. Samantha wanted to stick with Asher at first, but after hearing how he was intending to speak with Donvath and fill him in on everything, she decided to head out and continue practicing hitting her targets while in mid-flight. She wasn’t quite as terrified of the High Prince as she used to be, but she was still rather uncomfortable simply holding a conversation with the man in charge of their entire kingdom.

So it was that Asher found himself all on his own as he walked through the palace halls on his way to find Donvath. Strangely, he’d never really had any issues trying to find him for a conversation before. One of the nice things about having a Time element was that Donvath often seemed to know exactly when Asher wanted to talk to him, and the High Prince didn’t have any qualms about interrupting breakfast or simply popping in when it was convenient for him.

Other than the strange occurrences where Donvath isolated himself down in the palace basement for a few days at a time, the High Prince always seemed to be out and about.

Not for the first time, Asher contemplated slipping into the astral and heading down into the basement just to see what was down there. The longer he thought about whatever it was Donvath might be hiding, the more his curiosity burned. He was mildly ashamed to admit that the main thing that stopped him wasn’t respect for the High Prince or because it was forbidden, but because he was worried if he even tried, he’d get in trouble and still not know what was down there. He knew Donvath possessed the ability to rewind time in short bursts. There was every chance that if Asher poked his head down there to check it out, Donvath would rewind time to before he even made the attempt, and then chew him out.

Time was a stupid element.

Thus, instead of going down into the basement, Asher made his way over to the High Prince’s office. He grinned as he took in the royal guard standing in front of the office door, recognizing her in an instant.

“Hey, Lily,” he said, waving with his one arm at the royal guard. “How’s it going?”

Lily, the royal guard who had helped heal him after the first greater demon he’d killed ripped him in half, gave him a curt nod, continuing to gaze up and down the corridor. He knew Lily took her job as a royal guard seriously, despite the fact that she really didn’t have to. Donvath had already explained to Asher that upon evolving his Timeline skill to the third tier, the trigger had become automatic. If anything actually managed to kill the High Prince, he’d simply slip back in time to before he was murdered.

Time was a really stupid element.

“Asher,” Lily said, barely sparing him a glance as she maintained her vigilant watch. “Do you have business with the High Prince?”

“Yeah. You mind if I head in?”

“Most people need an appointment, but he’s already told us to let you in whenever you want,” Lily admitted gruffly, as though the absence of routine and decorum annoyed her. “Just try and be quick. He’s up to his neck in work already as it is.”

“I’ll do my best,” Asher promised. After a quick knock, Lily opened the door and allowed him inside. Stepping past the royal guard, Asher’s brow shot up as he took in the current state of the High Prince’s personal office.

He was well aware by now that Donvath wasn’t exactly the neatest of people, but he didn’t have the slightest idea how the High Prince managed to find anything in the mountain of papers actively slipping off his giant desk. Even now, there were loose papers decorating the floor here and there, and the High Prince barely even spared him a glance before going back to furtively scribbling at something, rubbing at the dark bags under his eyes.

“Asher… how are you?” Donvath asked, stifling a yawn as he kept working, his eyes glued to his desk. “How’s the taskforce going? You four almost ready for your first mission?”

“Donvath…” Asher said, growing more concerned by the moment as he watched the High Prince keep writing. “…we just had our first mission. I killed one of the members of The Council of Death.”

Donvath’s quill slipped out of his fingers as the High Prince blinked. Rubbing his eyes, he finally looked up, squinting at Asher as if that would help him hear better. “…Come again?”

“We tracked down one of the liches, found their base on the edge of the kingdom in the Malani Mountain Range, and fought our way through a small army of demons,” Asher said, watching as the High Prince’s eyes only grew wider as he spoke. “I didn’t just kill one of the liches, I killed another greater demon as well. It was an eventful day,” he added, gesturing to his missing arm.

“And I didn’t see a single moment of it,” Donvath whispered, his throat hoarse.

“Is that so weird? You told me before that wizards, greater demons, and myself all mess with your ability to see critical points in time. This attack involved all three, so that’s not crazy you missed it, right?”

“You don’t understand,” Donvath said, still looking like he was staring at a ghost. “There’s a big difference between not fully being able to make out a critical point in the timestream, and not seeing one at all. You killing one of the liches making up the Council absolutely should have been a critical point. This doesn’t make any sense!”

“Would it make more sense if the lich that owned the base we fought in had a Void element?” Asher asked, wondering just how pale he could make Donvath’s face. After learning he’d missed a critical point, the High Prince had looked horrified. After learning that one of the liches might have a Void element, Donvath looked like he might actually pass out and throw up at the same time.

“Explain,” Donvath managed after a few seconds.

Lamenting his inability to share memories associated with Zygrul, Asher did his best to describe everything. Donvath nodded along through the story, grilling him for details on just about everything. It took over an hour before the High Prince was satisfied, and despite the fact that a bit of color had returned to his face, he let out a world-weary sigh as he leaned back in his chair. He didn’t even seem to care when his breath knocked a stack of papers off his desk, simply choosing to stare up at the ceiling instead.

“Welp… This is bad.”

“I sort of got that vibe already,” Asher said. “Nobody’s actually going to be able to do anything so long as Zygrul stays in the void, are they?”

“Nope,” Donvath said, scrunching up his face as he thought. “There are a few countries that have been conducting heavily monitored research into the void, but nobody has discovered any method of truly surviving within it for more than a few minutes. Not to mention the void is infinite. Forget dealing with Zygrul, even if we could survive in there, we wouldn’t be able to find the lich.”

“So what? Zygrul’s just going to be left alone?”

“Hardly,” Donvath snorted. “It’s an immortal lich with the most devastating power this realm or any other has ever seen. We’re not just going to sit around and do nothing while we wait for another Gostoria situation. I can promise you right now every country in the world with dedicated realm walkers will put their best members forward to try and figure out a way to take the lich down.”

“Why wasn’t a response like this put forward toward The Council of Death in the first place?” Asher demanded. “If the world can just assemble a small army like this, wouldn’t taking down threats be that simple?”

“Asher… You’re learning, but you just don’t understand the scope of the troubles the world faces,” Donvath sighed, looking at him with tired eyes. “There are five continents on this world, and ours is the smallest of all of them. Even if you counted up every single living soul on our continent, it wouldn’t equal the entire population of some of the larger countries you’d find across the seas. Put frankly, we’re small fry. The Council of Death? Hah!” Donvath laughed, startling Asher for a moment. “They might seem like a big deal to us, but those liches are nothing compared to some of the true terrors of this world.”

“If that’s the case, why can’t some of those super God-tier warriors swing by and take care of them for us?” Asher asked, more confused than ever by Donvath’s admittance. “I don’t understand!”

“You’re finally starting to ask the right questions,” Donvath smiled. “I’d be more than happy to tell you… Once you reach the Stage of Power.”

“What does hitting a certain stage have to do with this?” Asher growled, not at all a fan of being kept in the dark. “The Stage of Power? Evolving all of my skills to the third tier? Do you have any idea how long that might take?”

“At the rate you’re going? I wouldn’t be surprised if you hit it within a few months,” Donvath said, sounding just the smallest bit annoyed. Clearing his throat, he continued. “Sorry, Asher, but this is actually above me. There are certain rules agreed upon by the heads of nations on our continent that even I have to follow. I can’t bend this one for you.”

“Fine. You take care of spreading the word to the other leaders about Zygrul, and I guess I’ll just work on taking down the rest of the small fry making up The Council of Death,” Asher said, going to cross his arms before remembering he was down one. He’d already regrown an inch of shoulder, but he had a long way to go.

“Oh, and as far as that void-knife goes,” Donvath said, grinning at him. “I think you should take care of finding that again.”

“What?! Isn’t this the perfect use of all your truthseekers?”

“Admittedly, I’m a little worried about what an artifact imbued with the Void element is capable of,” Donvath explained. “I really can’t think of anyone more suited to handle it than someone with an Eternity element. You’re like the void’s natural opposite. It endlessly consumes, and you endlessly regenerate. Water versus Fire, Life versus Death. I think we just found your element’s main counter.”

“It could take us days to find that thing again,” Asher complained, already imagining how boring it was going to be flying in a straight line, staring at the ground all the while.

“It’s going to take your arm days to regrow anyway,” Donvath pointed out. “I highly doubt you’ll be doing much fighting during that time.”

“Fine. But if I go through all the effort of finding it, I’m not just going to give it up!” Asher said, turning and going to walk out of the room. “I’ve got a dagger collection, if you didn’t know, and I can’t think of a more fun addition to it than a dagger enchanted with Void!”

“We can have that conversation after you actually find the dagger,” Donvath said, waving him off as he begrudgingly went back to his mountains of paper work. “We’ll see if you start singing a different tune when the rest of the world comes after your head.”

“Let them come,” Asher said, his hand on the doorknob as he flashed one last grin over his shoulder at Donvath that Moxy would have been proud of. “Sounds like a fun challenge!”
