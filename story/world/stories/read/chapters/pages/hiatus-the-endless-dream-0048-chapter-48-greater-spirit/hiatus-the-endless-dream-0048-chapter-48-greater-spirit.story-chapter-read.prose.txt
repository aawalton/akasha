Dreamlight

Jeren's stomach was knotted. A twisting string of emotions that constricted every step's confidence, leaving only uncertainty. He thought of the Visitor child, manipulated like everyone else; of Maerin, who had killed a fellow Survivor; of having Alder, a vile man, in the group at all, and most of all, Dorian. He could hardly meet the boy's eyes at the end, and that's what Dorian was, a boy. Not even twenty. Everything he could have been before the Dream was no longer an option.

He thought of the lower levels and how peaceful it seemed compared to the upper layers. The potential Visitors there were trained, guided, and looked after inside and outside the Dream. Safeguards, Presence banks, mentors, time, healers, both physical and mental. They had it all. The higher layers were dangerous, but there was also an assumption that the worlds on the higher layers were more prepared. Evidently, that wasn't the case.

Momentarily roused from his thoughts, Jeren looked at the two people flanking him. One was Alder, the other Paltra. Alder hadn't been given back the gun, and the crossbow sat ready in Maerin's arms. All the Survivors stayed silent. The tension drained Jeren's already-strained mental state. All it would take was one wrong word from Alder, and Maerin would shoot him. Alder's silence had saved his life innumerable times since leaving the ship. Jeren picked the left path, which led deeper down into the earth.

After the Instance ended, they'd be ejected from their world and need a new one to settle in. Surviving in the Dream alone wasn't possible, and since they'd be expelled, there was no chance of them being a plant from a malicious Instance Holder. Thus, Jeren could safely bring them with the caravan. There, he could give them the help they needed. He couldn't do the same for Dorian.

Even if Dorian tore the connection from his world, he could never learn where their world was. The kid was another Wanderer waiting to happen. Only on his second Instance, he was already weighing lives and making the inhumane 'optimal' decision. Jeren shuddered to think what another two, much less a hundred, would do to him, and he'd be alone through it all. Jeren wanted to help, but---God above forgive him---this Instance was as far as his courage went. He hoped the boy would never go into the Dream again and that the Dream had gotten what it wanted from him already.

Something brushed against the back of his skull, begging for acknowledgment, but it was antithesis to everything Jeren believed in. He would never act against Dorian, but a shameful instinct screamed at him to ensure the Dream consumed the boy before he got real experience under him. He calmed that part of his brain by saying he'd consider more extreme measures if the third Visitor ended up dying in the Instance. No matter what horrors there were in this forsaken place, the kid was still a kid, and while he knew they wouldn't directly die if they were killed in the Instance, he was no fool. If the kid were dead, he'd wake up back in his world and likely be killed by the Instance Holder. It's why Jeren had invited him to the party, so instead he'd wake up in the corn field with them.

The sound of running water drew the man forward until they reached a cavern. Upon reaching the entrance, Jeren's brow raised, surprised at the shape and smooth, ridged stone in front of him. He'd have to squeeze through the strange stone and the jagged wall to get a better view of the rest of the cavern, but as he approached closer, his breath caught. The ridges were familiar from his first loop. He hadn't seen the monster for long, but quickly recognized the worm transport creature.

He fought between the instinct to kill his lights and the fear that whatever monster Dorian had seen in the forest was waiting for darkness. Everyone froze behind him, eyes uncertain as they didn't understand the thing in front of them. Jeren's eyes flicked from left to right, up then down, and saw something that made his posture loosen before tightening straight. The creature was dead. Towards the top of the cavern entrance, he could see that the body partially blocking the entrance had been torn apart, much like the frog from above ground. Something had killed it. He moved forward carefully, pressing himself around its body into the space beyond. His heart sank. It looked like a localized explosion had gone off; the walls weren't affected, only the creature and all the statues it carried in its belly, all now reduced to various sizes of shrapnel.

After a deep breath, Jeren shouted to the Survivors. "It's dead. Come in, it's safe."

Krevith, or at least the thing puppeting him, moved forward. Jeren knew the man underneath wasn't unconscious. [Cruel] ensured that no one could pass out before death. On some level, Krevith was perfectly aware of what was happening, just powerless to influence the outcome. The parasite picked up a harsh-looking stone, opened Krevith's mouth, and dragged it across. The jagged bumps of the tongue created enough friction for a spark to fly out. Jeren turned away, not wanting to think about how that would be possible … the moisture alone. He shook his head.

The Survivors spread out. The parasite had placed three lit torches at each entrance to the cave, and Jeren had to appreciate just how defensible the location was. There was even a thin stream of fresh water that could provide the Survivors with much-needed hydration. They all practically collapsed to the ground, huddled up in one way or another---spent physically, spiritually, and emotionally.

Jeren gave them a few minutes to catch their breath, but didn't wait so long that the new reality of their situation could sink in. They needed hope.

"Everyone," he called out. A few heads turned to him, but most stared off into space. "We're more than halfway through. Less than two hours remaining. I know things have been difficult, but when all of this is done, I can guarantee three things. A warm bed, a filling meal, and a house to stay in for as long as you wish. You'll be cared for, so please. Look forward to what comes after, don't give up." He couldn't end it on that. It would be too raw, too aimless for some of them, so he continued. "In the meantime, if you haven't yet, drink some water. I'll keep watch, but stay diligent. If you hear, see, or even feel something is wrong, say something. Even if you think it was a trick of the light, or just nerves, I want to know. Do your best, for yourselves, for me, and each other." Then, setting the example, he strode forward, filled his water skin from the stream, and took a much-needed gulp.

Twenty minutes passed without incident. There was no chatter, only dangerous glares thrown. The [Communication Stone] quietly crackled into life, so faint he almost missed it. He palmed it and pressed the smooth stone to his ear to listen.

"My [Stalker] just teleported your way. She has my kit, Boons and Restrictions. She's dangerous." Jeren pressed his eyes shut, something inside of him losing a little light.

He considered Dorian's kit. [Untrustworthy] didn't matter for an enemy, [No Free Kill] would be active regardless, and [Stalker], well, he didn't know how that interacted, but he assumed it would be null considering she was the Stalker. So, really, he was facing an enemy with no Restrictions who could heal themselves when they took damage. It was doable. They had a gun and a crossbow, and the Stalker had no offensive Boons.

"Listen. You can't let her know where you are," Dorian continued, "and if she finds you, someone has to get as close to her as fast as possible." There was a level of urgency in Dorian's voice that he hadn't heard before, which made him pause.

"Why?" Jeren asked.

"Because if I was her I'd use [No Good Deed] to heal myself and take [At The Hip] to turn myself into a stone statue."

The rest of the idea clicked in Jeren's mind, and he paled. They could summon one of the stronger enemies to them. It was bad, but still manageable. As long as they found the Stalker before the enemy came through, they could kill either in their weakened state. The Worm and Frog were dead, leaving only Rend and that Flying creature, which Jeren was confident he could beat, but Dorian's following words stopped him in his tracks.

"The Instance Holder will use it to transport the moon underground. He sacrificed Rend and the bird to it already. If that Title works how you think, you'll all die."

Another piece of light inside Jeren died. He wished the boy had better bedside manner. He laughed at his own joke, a stale, hopeless laugh.

"If it gets bad tell me. I have a plan, but I'd prefer to wait until the very end of the Instance … if it can't be helped though you have to tell me. I can stop it."

Jeren nodded to himself, wondering what the hell that meant. "Heard. Good luck, Dorian." He killed the line, having nothing else to say. He wondered where Dorian was. If he wasn't taking care of any of the Spirits or the Stalker, what could he be doing that was so important? With difficulty, Jeren pushed the thought from his mind.

Pacing back and forth, he began to think of a solution. He came up short of good plans and so turned to Paltra for help. He explained the situation to her, and the plan they came up with was marginally better. There was no point in splitting up. There weren't enough Survivors for half of them to die and still win the Instance, so instead, three Survivors would take torches deep into each tunnel until an intersection and keep watch. If they saw the Stalker, they'd scream and urge the others to run. It was, in essence, sacrificing someone. He was about to reject the idea entirely before Alder volunteered as one of the three to hold the tunnels.

Jeren considered Alder for a long moment, unsure what to think of the man. An awful person, no doubt, but one who had been manipulated the same, and had chosen to go against Nico during the Survivors' betrayal. Jeren didn't know if it was fair to judge him or if there was a chance at redemption, but he felt it would be appropriate if the Stalker found Alder, and he had to face the Stalker.

With the plan in place, they split up, each volunteer---Paltra, Alder, and Jeren---taking a torch and disappearing into the halls.

[Time Remaining Until Daybreak: 1 Hours 30 Minutes]

Not even a minute passed before the darkness encroached on Jeren, no longer in a large grouping of light to hold it back. It pushed against the light, condensing and reflecting it in the small cage it created. It slammed against, tried to constrict, but couldn't easily break through.

Instead, it whispered. Then Screamed. Human-like shades appeared at the edges, on their knees, begging for safety.

"Jeren, please."

"Help us."

"Save us."

He ignored them. Aware of the mental attacks.

"Come back."

"Don't go."

"Hi, lover boy."

The last one made him still. The voice came from behind him; he had never heard something so sweet or bitter.

"I've been waiting for you for so long. Let me in? You were gone so long."

"Hey, love. Sorry, I had a hell of a walk." Jeren laughed, but his breath hitched. He stared at the ground.

"That's alright. The kids were worried sick."

Jeren closed his eyes and continued to step forward.

"Where are you going? Jeren! Please. Please don't go."

Another step. Eyes still closed.

"Dad?"

A young, feminine voice. Confused.

"Dad!" Then excited.

"Ow." The sound of something hitting the barrier of light from the torch. "Why can't I get in? Dad?" A pause. "Are- are you leaving again? No. No, you can't go! Dad, don't go!"

Weakness drowned Jeren's limbs, and even the next step was hard to take, but he took it anyway. Steady and even. It wasn't his wife or his kids. They were dead. Long dead.

The creature changed tactics, displeased at the lack of reaction.

It took the forms of his children, rising from the graves and asking why. It became Quinn, being dragged away by the other shades. Yet with each passing attempt, Jeren became more certain until his steps became easy. He opened his eyes and continued forward.

The creature stopped the mental attacks after twenty-seven minutes. The mist bristled angrily and gathered itself into a denser, deeper darkness. It created massive spikes that grabbed for the torch, seeking to tear it from its place. Jeren was forced to dodge and duck, and at one point, light an entirely different torch to give himself more breathing room.

After another thirteen, the aggressiveness tripled, and it was all he could do to hold on with bursts of [Wayfarer]. Fear gripped him as he considered that their entire plan must've been a mistake. The two others were surely dead from the onslaught of attacks. Those who stayed in the cavern no doubt were still alive, having numerous sources of light. So many potential issues, but he couldn't do anything about them. All he could do was his best here and trust the others would find some way to live.

Then his face paled deeper as he let out a pained scream. A tendril of shadows pierced his legs and drew blood before dissipating. The pain wasn't the reason for his fear. No, he realized that the shadows were blocking out sound. For all he knew, the others had already screamed.

Another distraction: the creature thrust a spike of black mist out, a strange bulb-like protrusion near the tip. So fast and sharp was the point that Jeren didn't notice the attack until his leg gave out and he toppled to the ground. The bulb crawled forward, squeezing through opened skin until it found shade in its cradle of flesh, and finding purchase in the dark, began to grow up the inside of Jeren's leg—burrowing through flesh like a worm.

Jeren reached for the torch, but found it too far to grasp. It had clattered far when he fell, and now he was at the circle's edge. The monster sought to punish him for this transgression. It speared forward with hands instead of needles. One to the back of the skull, four more for each limb.

With a scream, Jeren activated [Wayfarer] and from each of his bones golden light grew, pushing an enemy in a straight line outwards to the edge: the wording of the Boon. So as the implanted bulb of the creature dug up his inner thigh, it was forcibly shunted through muscles, veins, sinew, bone, and flesh. The ball was crushed between the barrier of light and the stone wall, splattering half of Jeren's leg. Blood sloughed from the wall, hissing as chunks punched into the torch below, closing in on its increasingly meager light. It poured from Jeren's leg, and it was all he could do to scream. He crawled to the torch, pulling out another one to light it, and only when both were roaring did he deactivate his Boon.

The light seemed to grow and fade in equal measure. Pulling himself up, Jeren stared at his blown-off leg in a state of resigned shock. He barely noticed when a human hand grabbed one of the torches.

[Wayfarer] launched out again, and the hand went flying back with the torch into the hall. It stayed lit, tracing the person's retreat. He squinted, heart-dropping momentarily as he considered if that had been one of the Survivors. When the woman stood, he understood it not to be so. It was the Stalker. She stared at him with a tremendous hatred, not even taking solace in their destroyed leg.

"I'm glad it's you. I've a bone to pick." She said, stepping up to the edge of the golden film.

"Here I thought you were Dorian's Stalker, not mine." Jeren coughed weakly, his mind unable to fully comprehend what was happening.

"I am. That's how I know every stupid decision you've made here. I'm just happy that I can give you a true death before the Dream takes you." She pulled a water bottle from her side and tossed it in her hand. "I've been watching you closely, closer than Dorian was. It's how I know how weak [Wayfarer] can be. For one, it requires your concentration. You know what the other one is?" She asked.

"I'm sure you'll tell me." Jeren spat back.

She unscrewed the lid of the water bottle. "It doesn't stop ranged attacks." Then she threw the water onto the one remaining torch and jumped back.

Jeren's concentration lapsed. The barrier broke. He was left in darkness for only a moment. It took one second to reactivate [Wayfarer]. The water consumed the torch, and the black mist moved to do the the same to Jeren.

It pierced his skin and eyes, burrowing deeper, but not deep enough to kill him in that one second. On instinct, Jeren activated [Wayfarer] the moment he could and the creature was launched through his skull, eyes, and every layer of skin. For an agonizing second afterward, Jeren stayed alive, nothing more than a brain on the ground and nerve endings. Fire, overload, then oblivion.

Moonlight

The Stalker, torch in hand, strode over to the corpse, picked up the smooth stone, and activated it as she walked further into the tunnel.

"Hey Dorian. Me again. I remembered that Jeren had one of these. You still at the Spire?" she asked.

Nothing.

"Oh, come on, answer me! I'm going through so much trouble here."

Nothing, but only for a moment.

"What do you want." Dorian's voice crackled over the stone.

"Call me by my name." The Stalker said.

"What do you want, Ruby?" he capitulated.

"You still in the spire?" she asked.

"No. There'd be no reason to be." A short response.

A chill went up Ruby's back as she stared at the stone incredulously, as if Dorian could see. "Then where?"

"Not telling." Dorian responded.

Ruby glowered, continuing down the winding cave. "That worries me. I don't like not knowing what you're planning."

A snort came through the speaker, "Wonder what that's like." After a pause, Dorian continued, "Was Jeren alone?"

"Not telling." Ruby shot back, her pettiness on full display.

The banter went back and forth, both seemingly content to just talk.

Miles away, on the other side of the [Communication Stone], Dorian was curled up in a ball of Void, sinking to the bottom of an infinite lake.
