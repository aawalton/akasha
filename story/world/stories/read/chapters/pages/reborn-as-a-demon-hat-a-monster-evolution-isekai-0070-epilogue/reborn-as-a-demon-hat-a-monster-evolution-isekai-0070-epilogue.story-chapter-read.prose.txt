In a tiny hovel nestled at the edge of Sanctum, a Hopla was cooking up a storm.

She was expecting guests – today was Skylamp night, after all. The day when humans and Hybrids both came together to celebrate the end of the Cycle and the rejuvenation of the world. The tradition had been altered in a number of ways from its past incarnation. Nowadays, the fireworks that had been prepared were made to celebrate the joint victories of Hybrids and humans against the old order that kept them separate and oppressed in ways they never truly realized before Sys had been vanquished.

Though Fauna had to admit that Fraxx’s fireworks were getting louder, brighter, and just a tad more volatile every year…she hoped that Tara would be able to calm him down a little this time.

The night was fast approaching, and Fauna wasn’t about to be caught with her proverbial pants down. Tradition held that a meal should be shared by all families of Westerweald a few hours before the fireworks themselves. And each year, Fauna had been only a consumer – never the cook. This year would be her first debut as a cook for everyone who came to her place at the foot of the Hopla farmstead.

She checked her carrot broth stew and chopped up a couple more bushels of Mooncarrot carefully just before leaving to gather some more salad. Tara had turned vegetarian, recently, much to Klax’s amusement. She’d also been sworn off wine after much pestering by her mate. It seemed that she was finally beginning to take her new nature as a mother-to-be seriously after all this time.

Fauna spared a look across the verdant fields before her, smiling meekly at the bio-luminescent fauna that coated the cavern that had once been burnt to cinders b the Lightborn. Now, it housed one of the most popular  (and high-achieving) magic schools in the entire continent.

“Which reminds me!” she exclaimed to herself. “I must check on the apple pie for Mara!”

She ran inside to double check her stove, ensuring that its knobs and levers were adjusted correctly to properly heat the doughy mixture she’d left inside. Borlor had shown her how this new contraption worked a few months back, but honestly, she just couldn’t quite get used to it even now.

She went about setting the table, preparing some extra tables and chairs outside for the new friends she knew Tara and Klax would be bringing with them. She’d heard that Langley might even be making an appearance, and she hoped that this time he’d at least ask just a few less questions…

The house was in order. The meal already smelled good, and so she allowed herself just a flicker of a break before the time came for her to welcome her guests proper.

In her simple cottage, she was so used to the quiet. She’d grown rather fond of it, in fact. Though her mind still conjured up pictures of her old adventures these days, she was happy enough to have found a little corner for herself in the world – for herself, and for the family that she’d chosen.

And just as she had settled into the armchair at the edge of her living room, there was a knock at her door.

She blinked, rolled her eyes.

Probably Tara, she thought. No doubt thinking I need some help in the kitchen! Honestly. I’m a Wildglance, not a Wildcook!

The knock came again – more urgent this time.

“Coming!” Fauna shouted.

She ran to the door and threw it open with one fluid motion, ready to show Tara just how well she’d managed on her own.

But when she revealed who was standing there, her performative pride died just as soon as it had come.

And in its place was total disbelief.

She looked at what she knew was impossible. She looked at a dream made manifest – at a hope that she’d tossed away. Her first thought was to exclaim that it wasn’t even real. Perhaps a mischievous student was playing an ill-conceived prank.

But those disheveled locks of hair, that slightly pudgy face, and those staring sunken eyes – they were things that only she’d seen. A form that had been shown only to her.

His mouth twitched as he tried to find words. For once, he was tongue tied.

But there was little that had to be said. She knew that just as well as he.

And so she smiled at him, choked back tears, and said the only thing she needed to:

“Welcome home, Ethan.”
