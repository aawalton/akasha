The ten-thousandth failure felt exactly like the first. Which was, of course, the whole Ash-damned problem.

Vir stood at the base of the great machine in the silence of his mindscape, staring up at it in frustration.

Failure was no stranger to him. He'd been knocked down over the years more times than he could count. Had lost fights, shattered his soul, and watched his plans crumble, but in every case, there had been some small measure of progress to point to.

Some evidence that proved the task would be overcome given enough effort. Knowing that persistence could wear down anything was what had always given him the motivation to continue.

But there was no progress here. Not even the faintest trace of it.

The moment the machine channeled prana into his body, he died. It was painless, to be sure, and not even especially traumatic given how quickly it happened.

Yet that made it no less maddening when he perished every time, without lasting even a fraction of a second longer than the attempt before.

It was simply impossible to acclimatize to that obscene volume of prana, and even if such a thing were possible, it would take thousands of years of exposure. Of actual exposure to real prana. Not the simulated thing that existed in his Crown Chakra mindscape, no matter how real it felt.

There had to be another way. Some angle he hadn't yet seen, or perhaps some crucial concept of Janak had neglected to mention. But if it existed, he certainly had not found it.

Consulting the god himself had proven fruitless in that regard. Janak had listened to his account of the failures with the patient expression of someone who had expected nothing different, then told him what he'd already known. That he would need to leverage every Ultimate Art simultaneously, that the process couldn't be properly simulated without his own sacrifice, and that the full weight of what was being asked of Vir couldn't be rehearsed, only learned when the moment came.

He was confident, he said, that Vir could at least reach the final stage, could practice creating the illusion of a new world and seeding it with life, and that this would have to be enough.

Forget the final stage—he was yet to broach even the first!

In frustration, Vir collapsed his mindscape and opened his eyes.

The bedchamber materialized around him, and he noticed Maiya seated cross-legged on their bed, a book open in her lap that she clearly hadn't been reading.

She looked up the moment he surfaced.

"Any luck?" she asked.

“None at all,” he said with a sigh.

“It’s only been a few days here,” she said. “There’s still time. How long did you spend in your mindscape?”

“Months,” he said disgustedly. When had he ever in his life spent this much time and thrown himself so fully into a task, only to have nothing to show for it.

Entire months of entering the lift, riding it to the top, activating the sequence, watching the prana build to its terrible density, and dying. Over and over, with nothing to show for it except a growing and intimate familiarity with the precise sensation of being vaporized.

Maiya set the book aside. "You need a break."

"What I need is to perfect this task. Every moment is another where people are dying."

“Er, have you forgotten where we are?" She asked, arching her eyebrow and giving him a knowing smile. “We're in the deepest part of Mahādi. The time effects here are pretty wild. A day here is probably no more than a minute in the human and demon realm."

“Even still. On this scale, even that is enough to claim lives. Especially now, with neither I nor Ashani there to close the Tears."

“The Asura are well equipped to destabilize those.. They might not be able to close them, but that just means more cordons. An inconvenience, nothing more. Everyone who’d die when the Tears open would still die whether you were there or not,” she said, staring him in the eyes.

Vir held her gaze for a few seconds before sighing, raising his palms in defeat. He’d never won when she had her mind set on something. After all these years, he truly ought to have known better than to argue.He wasn’t even sure why he’d tried to argue. “What did you have in mind?”

A flicker of something passed over her face, but it was gone in an instant, and Vir thought he’d simply imagined it… until her cheeks went slightly pink.

"Ashani showed me something," she said, carefully choosing her words. "A contraption they, er…"

She mumbled the last of it, trailing off in a way that was so thoroughly unlike her that Vir straightened up.

“Mai? What’s wrong?”

"You'll see soon enough,” she said, grabbing his hand and pulling him out of their chambers before he could respond.

Vir didn’t resist, and as they rounded the corridor to the lift, he didn't particularly mind being led around like this. Enjoyed it, even.

And gods knew there was precious little enjoyment left to be had before the end. He was resolved not to cherish every bit of what remained. Thinking of that, though, squashed what happiness he’d felt in that fleeting moment.

Part of the reason he'd kept throwing himself into the machine, he suspected, was to have something to focus on besides what was coming.

It wasn’t that he feared death. Vir had lost that fear so long ago.

What galled him was that, like Maiya, he'd thought he'd have so much time!

That was the thing that kept surfacing when his mind wandered.

Dying in combat seemed like a distant concern these days. Not only was he more powerful than most mortals, as Raja, he'd been forbidden from anything that put his life in unnecessary jeopardy.

He’d spent the last several years behind desks or lobbying other Rajas. He’d only taken to the front lines once the Ash Tears began to open.

Only months ago, Vir had assumed he would live a thousand years, at least. That he might outlive the realms at the rate things were deteriorating,

He'd begun to make plans for centuries ahead, with Maiya in it and all the time in the world to figure out how to keep her there forever.

Life, as always, had a way of ruining plans.

The lift carried them down the spire to somewhere deep within its middle levels, opening into a chamber Vir hadn't seen before. It was cylindrical and white, two stories in height.

At its center stood a column-like contraption that reminded him more than a little of the machine upstairs, though it was smaller, and instead of Janak’s twisted machine, it was filled with a different, smaller one.

Despite also possessing a central space that looked designed to accommodate a person, despite a striking resemblance to the ancient god’s contraption, the pipes attached to this machine were elegant rather than industrial, and the Imperium inscriptions that ran across its surface looked like the work of creators who clearly cared about the device’s aesthetics as much as its function.

It looked designed rather than haphazardly thrown together, though Vir couldn’t exactly fault the dying god for not paying any attention to beauty.

Maiya had stopped in the center of the room and was standing with her arms crossed, looking away from him.

Finally, she turned around and cleared her throat.

"We're, um, supposed to undress."

Vir raised an eyebrow. “Undress?”

"Just trust me," she said, already undoing her robes.

Vir glanced around the chamber in panic, but his eyesight, Clarity, and the Third Eye Chakra all confirmed they were truly alone.

Reluctantly, he did the same, albeit more slowly than Maiya.

Growing impatient, Maiya moved to do the task for him, but Vir got the message.

Once they were both entirely naked, Maiya took his hand and pulled him into the capsule despite Vir’s reservations, the two of them fitting inside it with just enough room not to be pressed together.

“Well?” Vir asked, barely concealing a smirk at Maiya’s red cheeks. “Care to tell me what this is all about now?”

She coughed awkwardly. "Well, you know how sometimes there just aren't words to convey what you feel?" she muttered. "Stuff like love, loss… all kinds of things? Well, apparently the Imperium found a way. Lovers apparently used these machines to, um." She paused. "Become one with each other."

"And what exactly does that entail?" Vir asked, gesturing to the machine. “Given the context, that is.”

Maiya's cheeks were furiously red by now, and Vir guessed one reason she leaned against his chest was to hide her face.

"You'll see," she said softly as the capsule's doors swiveled shut around them, sealing them inside.

The pranic inscriptions came alive, crisscrossing every surface of the capsule in the hues of every Affinity.

And then… something happened. Something that Vir could only explain because he’d been one of the few demons across the history of demonkind to have used an Art that did something similar.

Demonic Overlord.

Despite his hatred of the Chitran art, Vir had been convinced by Cirayus to practice it with his troops, lest the day come when he might need it.

And needed it he had, coordinating and controlling Asura to find and close Ash Tears when the Fateweavers launched offensives against major cities in the human and demon realms.

Like with Demonic Overlord, Vir’s consciousness separated from his body, but that was where all similarities ended.

Instead of assuming control of other life forms, his consciousness simply drifted. Free of its mortal shackle.

It didn't hurt, nor was it even especially uncomfortable, but that was only because Vir had prior experience and knew what to expect. For all that she wanted to bring him here, he wondered how Maiya felt… and somehow, he became aware of Maiya near him, and he no longer had to wonder. He just knew, as though the feelings were his own.

She was afraid, of course. Of what would happen to her and the realms if he were to fail. But that fear was incomparable to the fear of what would happen should he succeed.

It terrified her just as much as it would terrify him, were their positions swapped.

They drifted closer, drawn together by some almighty force, and Vir didn’t even try to resist. He accepted it completely.

They touched, and for a brief moment, he felt Maiya’s soul flinch. But just as he was about to break off the communion, she opened herself fully, allowing him in.

Two beings became one, and a lifetime of emotion washed over him.

For a moment, he was but a lifeboat, lost in the sea that was Maiya’s soul.

But a singular feeling rose through the fear and the anxiety and the happiness and the sorrows that Maiya had known in her life.

It was a simple feeling, yet by far the most profound.

It was, he realized, her love for him, crystalized into a tangible and physical thing, and Vir knew then that she’d been right when she said no words could convey such feelings.

In that moment, Vir understood all of what Maiya was, and the depth of the feelings she held for him. In that moment, he came to know her in a way he never had, and never would have, even if he’d lived with her for a thousand years.

He only became aware of the tears streaming down his cheeks when Maiya’s spiritual form brushed against it. Vir’s tears became full-blown sobs as he held her hand to his cheek in that transcendent space.

All the conflict that warred in Maiya’s heart as she tried to come to terms with Vir’s destiny.

Just a day had passed since Janak’s revelation, but it seemed Maiya had spent much of that time in her own Mindscape—a perfect reflection of his own Godshollow meadow—and from the deluge of emotions he was hit with, she couldn’t have spent any less than a week cloistered inside.

Crying, mostly.

For him. For the life he was about to give for the world. A sacrifice far too few would ever know, and fewer still would fully appreciate.

And he felt her pain. The pain of losing the love of her life. At the terrifying thought of living years, decades, perhaps centuries without him. Of living in a world he gave up his life to create.

The guilt nearly crushed her, and when Vir felt what she felt, it was as though his heart was ripped in half. He yearned to tell her not to feel that way, that he was doing this for all life, yes, but first and foremost for her. That it gave him eternal joy to know she would live on in a healed world.

He realized, as she had said, that no words could convey such feelings.

And so, as she had just done for him, he opened himself entirely to her in return, holding nothing back.

Two beings once again became one, and in that moment, true magic manifested.

As his own feelings flooded into Maiya’s soul, as she became a boat upon his ocean, he was suddenly free.

Free from the weight of duty and from the consequences of his actions. From the fate of the realms. His looming death and the thousand years of life he would never have.

It all fell away, until there was only him and Maiya, and the extraordinary feeling of being truly together with the one he loved more than life itself.

Maiya, as ever, had been right.

For the first time in a very long time, Vir was content.
