The stone construct dashed at me, eager to protect Max. As it waved its arm, dozens of stone projectiles flew out of it, flying directly at the serpent.

Instinctively, I leaped back, unsummoning the weapon.

What the hell, old man?! Serp shouted in my mind. Why’d you do that?

You can’t block those, I replied.

If one or two burst into smaller projectiles, I might have been able to handle them. If more of them did, I’d end up an instant porcupine.

Keeping my distance from anything made of stone, I reevaluated the situation. My attack had achieved everything I had hoped for, and still it had proven not enough. Even with a quarter of him missing, my opponent was still clinging to his existence, like a rotten tooth.

I knew from personal experience that the deities took care of their own, but this was too much. Two revivals weren’t unheard of, but why would they waste them on a mere participant? And, for all intents and purposes, that was precisely what Max was. The deity behind him hadn’t sent him to hunt me or the others down. He was merely a jailer, guarding the siren, while hiding in a low-level trial. He was lucky to have a Grand Arcana, let alone anything else.

Meanwhile, on the other side of the chamber, Eleonore collapsed on the floor.

“I should have gone for the head,” I said loudly.

Nine times out of ten, people reacted irrationally towards those who had hurt them. I was very much counting on that. One order was enough for any of the statues to crush the goth on the spot. I didn’t have the cards to reach her in time to prevent that.

Are you crazy, old man? Serp shouted again. She could have been the distraction!

“That would be cheating,” I forced a smirk on my face.

All three statues surrounded Max. Even if still alive—in a manner of speaking—the injury had made him fear for his own mortality. That gave me a glimmer of hope.

Threads of sand flowed from his construct onto him, composing something new.

“You think you’re so clever,” he spat as he turned around. “The big shot who has it all. Well, you just blew it!”

Stone segments formed where his arm had been, creating a futuristic copy. At the same time, plates of marble covered the rest of his body, adding a new layer of protection.

“All you had to do was accept the offer, and you’d have been set for life!” he continued. “That’s over now. I’ll kill you and then I’ll kill your family, your friends, and everyone you’ve known!” A hateful smile formed on his face, through the pain. “Not your sister, though. I’ll take her just for the fun of it. And when I’m done, I’ll tear her apart and put her back together again. I’ll never kill her. Never ever!”

“So, you’ve spent some time with the aelves.” I didn’t take his threat to heart.

I knew perfectly well that he intended every word he said, just as I knew that losing my concentration wouldn’t do a thing to prevent it. Harlequin had severed the trial from the influence of the deities, so Max had no way of telling what was going on here. I strongly suspected that was the reason the siren had ventured into Eleonor’s dreams. Anything that happened here would remain secret forever. The powers behind Max and Hyena could speculate all they wanted, but they would never know for certain.

“Can you imagine it?” he asked. “Your sister will go through so much pain that she’d beg to join the Grand Arcana!”

The construct had completely vanished at this point, transformed into a set of solid stone armor. Max had done a good job covering himself with a nearly invulnerable shell.

So, you can be killed, I thought.

Only a person terrified of dying would go to such lengths to protect himself.

Serp, can you eat through stone? I asked.

That again? He sighed. Maybe. But only if I have to.

Both statues of Anubis remained static, their attention fixed squarely on me. It would have been nice for Sparta or any of the others to rush in now and join the fight, but I knew that wouldn’t happen. Even with the Listener card, I couldn’t hear any sounds of fighting or running from outside.

“I’ll make you a deal,” I said, activating the Lover and Bard cards. Some might call it a desperate move, but I needed time to come up with something. “Tell me who’s pulling your strings and I’ll share the name of my puppet master.”

Even hidden beneath a helmet of stone, I noticed his eyelids twitch.

Got you! “You’re curious, aren’t you?” I pressed on. “How come I know so much without making a pact? Why are certain deities protecting me for no apparent reason?”

“You’re lying.”

The fact that he had chosen to respond at all told me that he was more than interested. Hatred echoed in every syllable he uttered, but there was also something else—something that surpassed his desire to kill me.

“One of us will die.” I sidestepped the accusation by stating the obvious. “This way the winner will have something to show for it.” I paused for just long enough to make him think I was finished. “Of course, I think that’ll be me.”

Back in my previous run, an old veteran had taught me everything there was about trash talk and insults. She was quite the character who had the uncanny ability to get under anyone’s skin in less than a minute. I used to think that she had a card or special to achieve that, though she claimed to be like that by nature. The only reason she had confided in me was because I seemed to remind her of her son.

Insults, she would say, were never about mockery or coming up with the most disgusting combination of words. What they were really about was taking something from the other’s mind that they believed was theirs by right.

Even after everything, Maximillian was so confident in his victory that he couldn’t imagine defeat. Challenging that notion was bound to get a reaction.

“You first,” he hissed through gritted teeth.

“And you’ll keep your word?” I smirked.

It was obvious that he wouldn’t. Even this was a performance, making myself out to be more desperate than I actually was. Ironically, I was rather desperate, just not in the way I was letting on.

The Anubis statues are red herrings, I thought.

In his current state of mind, Max would never deny himself the lethal blow. If anything, he’d use them to explode and fill the entire chamber with shrapnel. That could prove to be an issue. I had a few remaining tricks to protect myself, but Eleonor was a sitting duck.

I could listen to Serp and sacrifice her, though that wasn’t my first choice.

How much blood will you need to grow as large as before? I asked the saber.

Depends. Is that all you want me to do?

Serp…

A tenth of a liter, he said. Maybe a bit more.

A tenth was bearable. Would that get me closer to a kill, though? As powerful as he had become, I didn’t think he could kill a chosen of Anubis. Only a divine weapon had the power to do that. For that purpose, I had to get close enough to Max to use my divine blades… and not die in the process.

“Why would I lie to a dead man?” Max laughed. At this point, he wasn’t even trying to pretend he was telling the truth. Or maybe he was just terrible at it?

“Alright.” I blanked my mind again. The plan was made. Now it was just a matter of seeing it through and reacting to anything Max threw at me. “The one behind it all…"

I saw Max narrow his eyes. The moment I uttered the name, he was going to make his move in full force.

“…the Jester,” I finished the sentence.

The man’s eyes widened in shock. A split second later, he dashed right at me.

I summoned Serp and threw him at Max’s head.

Expecting that, the man dodged, stepping to the side.

The saber flew inches from his head, yet that was never my real target.

Shield her! I mentally shouted as the blade struck Eleonor’s thigh.

Gaining access to a new food source, the blade quickly transformed into the head of a cobra, covering the goth’s entire body. Barely a moment later, the Anubis statues burst into thousands of sharp fragments.

Earth! Water!

I cast two spell circles on the ground. Max must have forgotten I still had that ability, for he instinctively covered his face, bracing for the inevitable impact.

Too late. I thought.

Walls of earth rose up from the floor, surrounding me with a protective ring. They were followed by a new burst of water that thrust us toward the ceiling. I had taken special care to ensure that the circles stopped short of Eleonor.

Stone shards rained onto the outer area of the chamber. Hundreds of them shattered in Serp, like hail striking a reinforced bunker.

Meanwhile, all projectiles within the giant column of water lost their inertia, starting their descent to the ground.

Acrobat! I activated the card as I drew the divine blades from my inventory.

I was going to get one chance at this.

Spell circles formed on the soles of my feet, creating chunks of hardened soil. Using every ounce of strength I had, I pushed against them, propelling myself forward right at Max.

Thread strike!

I sliced the water in front of me. The attack struck my enemy’s fake arm, causing him to inadvertently turn to the side in an effort to protect it. Unfortunately, the inflicted trauma was far less than I had hoped. The stone segments of his arm extended forward, forming a blade along his other arm.

Old man, the poison! Serp shouted.

Multi-attack! I triggered the Ten of Swords.

There was no point in wasting time. I knew perfectly well that I was being poisoned this very instant. The only way forward was to kill Max before the poison could take effect.

The stone blade made its way toward me. The water made the motion slow enough for me to react.

Restrict! I parried with both blades, using its momentum to push myself to the side.

More spikes shot out from the stone armor.

That was a mistake. If he had had any experience, Max would have made them into projectiles. That way they had a chance of wounding me at least. Instead, he had just given me the upper hand.

Restrict! I stuck several of the spikes.

Instantly they froze, unable to grow any further.

In his state of unadulterated hatred, it was inevitable that Max gave me a glance. I had seen it happen often enough, even among veterans. Working at reduced capacity, he no doubt assumed that my actions would be just as slow as his. Sadly for him, water didn’t affect the speed of my blades one bit.

The moment his head moved, I had already started my strike. For a second, our eyes locked on one another. A dull gold glow emerged from the irises as the chosen of Anubis attempted to summon whatever power his patron deity provided. Before he could, my blade cut into his eye.

Spell circles covered my weapon, blasting air in all directions, until it effectively found itself in a bubble.

Quickly, I let go, pulling my hand back.

Zap! I ordered.

Electricity ran down the weapon, sliding like eels on ice. It was enough to kill any ordinary person, yet I remained on guard.

ANUBIS boon activated.

MAXIMILLIAN SWANSEN has been reconstructed.

The message appeared again. Apparently, being undead of Anubis ensured that any wound, no matter how severe, was instantly reconstructed. It was to be expected. However, I still hadn’t ended my attack.

“Zap!” I ordered once more.

The blade lit up along with part of Max’s head. Sections of his helmet broke off, dissolving in the water like clumps of dirt.

You’re losing strength, aren’t you? “Zap!” I thrust the second blade into his head. “Zap! Zap! Zap!”

With each flash, more and more stone segments broke off, leaving the amalgamation of bone and flesh beneath. Had he been in a clear state of mind, Max would have used his undead ability to continue the fight. That moment had passed, though. Mentally, he had already accepted defeat and was now praying for survival, hoping against hope that he would somehow manage to escape.

Rookie. I thought.

That was the problem with them, especially those that had been bestowed exceptional gifts. They had no idea what it meant to strive for survival and had no idea how to act when it happened to them.

“Zap!” I ceased maintaining my mega spell circle, ending the water’s upward momentum.

A loud splash followed, bringing us back to the chamber floor. The only difference was that I remained standing.

“Zap,” I said without mercy.

The fight had rendered Max in a pathetic state. There was no trace of the cockiness that emanated from him once I had first entered the room. All that was left was a disfigured creature, clinging to whatever pitiful existence it still had.

I was just about to keep on with the zapping until the final vestiges of strength were gone, severing the link to Anubis’ boon, when I spotted something on the man’s arm. Tattooed in black ink on his only remaining biceps was the image of a key.
