Chapter 103

I cut the spiral into the wave-stone in the autumn the fishermen called the year of no tuna. My hands were steady then. Thirty-one years I have kept the socket clean of salt and barnacle and never once felt it stir. We are not told what wakes it. We are told only to keep it ready, and to be standing well back on the day it is. — Codex of the Long Watch, Field Observation, 11th Century, Cape Sounion

For a moment, nobody moved. Then, the water began to drain off the marble, running in thin streams down the cliff face and back into the sea below.

The only sounds left were the steady rush of the wind, the crash of the waves against the base of the cape and the settling stone debris. Donna deactivated her technique. Arthur watched a thin line of blood leak from the corners of her eyes. She stood frozen on the platform, her limbs twitching violently as her nervous system struggled to process the biological friction of the extended overclock.

She was trying her best to control her body, but she had stayed in the Drive too long. Arthur stepped forward, gently gripping her elbow to steer her toward the front of the temple, where Steve was already kneeling over Sarah.

Donna slumped heavily against the base of a surviving column and closed her eyes. Her muscles were still firing against her will, tiny spasms rippling under her skin. She forced a weak, strained smile without opening her eyes.

“That was close,” she said.

“Too close,” Arthur agreed.

Steve had used his knife to cut away the torn fabric from the top left of Sarah’s shirt. Sarah sat with her right hand pressed lightly over the injury, trying to stabilise the bone.

“It’ll be some time before I can move this arm,” she said, wincing as she shifted. “And even longer before I’m able to heal it completely. My Matrix seems to be splitting its resources between my body’s natural recovery and the technique itself.”

When a Mender applies a healing technique to an external target, they project Ether directly into a separate biological circuit, Archie said, manifesting over Sarah. It results in a clean net gain. In your case, your Matrix must ration its limited output between feeding your raw Constitution threshold and fuelling the active skill. Menders mending themselves is terribly inefficient. Try not to get hit next time.

Arthur squatted beside Sarah, his eyes fixed on her mangled shoulder, which was now coated in a thin film of Ether. He placed a hand lightly on her uninjured shoulder, just to reassure himself that she was still there.

“I’ll be fine,” she said, offering him a weak smile. “I’ve healed worse than this using regular, run-of-the-mill medicine back in the old world.” She tried to look past him toward the pillar and gave up with a sharp hiss. “Arthur, bring Donna over. She needs the healing too.”

“You’re in no shape to heal anybody,” Donna said from the column, her head still resting against the stone.

“I’m going to be useless for a few days at least,” Sarah countered, her voice tight but absolute. “You, on the other hand, will be up and running with just a fraction of my remaining Ether. Do the maths, Donna. Having you in fighting shape gives all of us a chance to survive the road back to Athens.”

“She’s got you there,” Steve muttered.

“Shut up, Steve.”

But the logic was undeniable. A fully functional Donna with Threshold Drive ready was their primary force multiplier, and they still had an entire return journey through hostile territory to think about.

Donna gave a small, defeated huff. She forced herself up, walking unsteadily across the fractured marble before slumping down into the dirt right next to Sarah.

Sarah pulled her bloody hand away from her own collarbone and laid her palm gently over the back of Donna’s hand. The Ether began to transfer, passing into Donna in a slow, steady stream.

They sat in silence for nearly ten minutes before Donna abruptly pulled her hand back, breaking the contact.

“Enough,” Donna said, her breathing finally smoothing out as the muscle twitches subsided. “I’m functional now. You can try again once your own shoulder stops looking like a howitzer clipped it.”

“That shield is done,” Donna said, motioning toward the shattered iron. The entire bottom half of the shield was missing, leaving a jagged, ruined edge.

“It is,” Arthur agreed, looking down at his grip. “I just hope it holds together through the trip back.”

“Good thing we still have a lot of iron sitting with Stavros,” Donna said. “He’s going to be a busy man once we get back to Athens.”

She pushed herself up from the base of the column and walked toward the spot where the guardian had fallen. There was no proper corpse left to pick through; the creature had disintegrated into a wet, melting lump of dark flesh and decaying scales. Donna moved the mass around until she caught the glint of what she was looking for.

It was a blue core, larger than anything they had harvested so far. It was the size of a fist, dense and dark, with a slow whorl of deep blue light turning brilliant at its centre.

“Risk and reward,” Donna muttered to herself. She picked it up and looked back over her shoulder at Arthur. “Looks like you’re getting an upgrade.”

She tossed the heavy stone over to Steve, who caught it and slid it into his core pouch carefully.

* * *

“Arthur, mate, give me a hand here,” Steve called out from the shadows at the back of the temple. He was tugging fruitlessly at a heavy piece of debris. “That single point I just popped into Strength doesn’t seem to be doing a bloody thing yet.”

“What are you trying to do?” Arthur asked, pushing himself up and walking over.

“Just help me move this slab,” Steve said, straining against a massive, broken piece of the collapsed column.

“Mate, you won’t budge that on your own.” Arthur stepped inside, braced his shoulders against the marble slab and pushed. It took some heavy manoeuvring, the stone grinding loudly against the floor, but he managed to shove it aside far enough to reveal what Steve had been digging for.

Steve reached down and pulled the heavy, barnacled trident out from beneath the rubble.

“Loot,” Steve said, a wide smile breaking across his face. “This, my dear fellow, is the raw vessel material for a new focus for Sarah and me.”

“That trident is seven feet long, Steve,” Donna said, walking over. “I want a new blunt weapon out of it. It’s time to retire the prybar.”

“You see them vultures circling, Arthur?” Steve said, grunting as he hefted the weight of the metal shaft. “That’s precisely why you should never tell your relatives if you win the lottery.”

“I’m doing you a favour,” Donna shot back. “You wouldn’t even be able to lift a focus made out of half of that thing.”

“And I don’t need a focus,” Sarah called out from her spot by the pillar, pausing briefly during her quiet self-mending session. “I heal by touch, remember?”

“That’s something I’ve been thinking about,” Steve said, dropping the heavy trident onto a clear patch of the marble floor. “We know Ether channels better with a focus. I’ve burned my hands enough to know that it’s true.”

He kicked a small stone out of the way and looked back over his shoulder. “Healing, at its core, is Ether manipulation as well. I manipulate it for external effects. You manipulate your Ether reserves straight into someone else. But at the end of the day, it’s still manipulation, so I’d reckon a focus would help.”

You are partially correct, but a rod or staff is entirely the wrong implement, Archie’s voice drifted down from the shadows, flat and unbothered. A focus rod or a staff is an efficient tool for ranged Ether manipulation, especially when you require stability over longer distances. Holding a focus rod against an injured person is rather idiotic when you can simply touch them instead.

“So a healer would need something that enhances control at short or touch range,” Arthur mused, leaning his weight against a column.

“Makes sense, I suppose. A concussive force or a lance of flame has a much larger margin for error, control-wise, than knitting living muscle fibres,” Steve agreed. “A holy focus is out, since Sarah has no patron deity. So no holy symbols or reliquaries.”

“Druids use totems, like carved wands,” Arthur added, “but that’s mostly living wood.”

“Bards can use instruments to heal,” Steve said, tapping his fingers against his knee. “Hey, Sarah, do you play anything?”

“Scalpel Roulette,” Sarah responded from her place by the pillar, without missing a beat.

Donna gave a short bark and moved further into the temple.

Steve stared at her for a second and shrugged. “Never mind. Who needs a bard anyway.”

“But here’s the thing. The focus can be anything, can’t it? As long as it can channel Ether for control,” Arthur said, his tone sharpening with sudden realisation. “What’s always closest to her hands when she touches someone?”

“A glove!” Steve exclaimed, snapping his fingers. “She can use a glove as a focus.”

“I’m not wearing a glove all the time,” Sarah replied, glaring at them. “And you should probably be consulting me for what seems to be a detailed discussion about my own focus.”

Arthur and Steve looked at her for a long, silent moment, entirely caught up in the engineering puzzle.

“She’s right that she can’t wear a heavy gauntlet all the time,” Steve continued, turning to Arthur and dropping his voice. “So either she needs a completely new glove design, or we need to find something like Ether-infused cloth or leather.”

“I can still hear you, and didn’t I just ask to be included? You guys are impossible,” Sarah sighed. “And since I don’t want to end up wearing a gardening glove with a core bound to it for the rest of my life, the thing you actually want is a hand bracelet.”

“A bracelet could work.” Arthur nodded.

“Not just a bracelet, a hand bracelet,” Sarah explained. “It’s a piece of jewellery with a chain that clasps at the wrist and has two to five fine chains running from it. These can then be clasped right onto the fingers.” She paused for a brief moment. “I wouldn’t mind a hand bracelet, now that I think about it. I had a pretty baciamano from before.”

“So it’s like a prosthetic one might wear if they shatter their hand?” Arthur asked.

“Yes, and thanks for completely ruining the image,” Sarah replied.

“Well, I’m not sure if Stavros can do delicate work like that,” Steve mused, looking toward the heavy metal of the trident on the stone floor. “You’d need a goldsmith for something that precise.”

“Once you nerds are done nerding, there’s something glowing here,” Donna called out from the tip of the cape.

“Bollocks, forgot about the node activation puzzle,” Arthur said, forcing himself up to his feet.

Arthur and Steve made their way back to the very end of the cape, which was now noticeably more slender than it had been minutes ago. Massive chunks from the sheer sides of the cliff had been sheared away during the fight, swallowed completely by the churning sea below.

They stopped right before the wave-cut socket Arthur had touched before the ambush, looking up at the glowing, violet-blue lines of the puzzle text hovering directly over the ancient spiral symbol.

Cross the grey water where the maiden leapt and fled,

to the isle of the vanished, the grey-eyed’s hidden bed;

let the golden measure, not her name, run true —

the second hand waits where the line carries through.

THE ETHER ATLAS: FIELD NOTES OF STEVEN PARR

Cape Sounion

THE SOUNION GUARDIAN / OANNES

It was Triton. I am writing that down because nobody else will. Barnacled human torso, the lower body a knot of scale and muscle, a trident longer than Donna is tall, and it carried the sea up the cliff with it and kept a skin of brine around itself that drank into the stone and vanished. That is not a merman. That is the exact iconographic package, down to the three prongs.

Here is the thing I have spent twenty years being too rigorous to say out loud. The being that comes out of the water is everywhere. Oannes rose from the Persian Gulf in Berossus to teach the Sumerians their letters and went back under at nightfall. Atargatis stood fish-tailed over Ascalon. Matsya is the first form Vishnu takes in the Shatapatha Brahmana, the fish that towed the first man clear of the flood. And the trident walks from Poseidon’s fist to Shiva’s trishula across four thousand miles while the textbooks file the resemblance under coincidence.

I reviewed a paper in 2009 that argued for a single origin behind the whole fish-god complex. I recommended rejection. I used the word hyperdiffusionist, which is how my field calls a man a crank without being sued. I was right about his mechanism and wrong about everything that mattered. He thought a Bronze Age prophet sailed the story around the world. There was no prophet.

There was a creature, stationed at the water’s edge, in more coasts than one, asleep until something woke it. The people who found a sleeping one and lived wrote down a teacher. The people who woke one wrote down a monster. Same animal. Different morning.

I keep calling these things memos and blueprints and a distribution network. This one is not a memo. This one is the same witness statement taken in nine languages, and I sat on the panel that threw it out for being too strange to be true.

✎ The fish-god is not a metaphor for the sea and not a story that travelled. It is the same creature, seen at nine coastlines, written down as a teacher where it slept and a monster where it woke.
