Lira wandered through the outer gardens at an unhurried pace, the picture of an ordinary maid enjoying a short break between duties. She paused now and then to admire the carefully trimmed hedges or the first flowers of spring, making certain anyone watching would see nothing unusual. The castle bustled around her with its endless rhythm of servants, messengers, and passing nobles. It was the perfect place for someone to disappear in plain sight.

This was her second attempt at spying on Kavard. The first one happened two days ago, when she tried it, her focused hearing spell had fizzled out the instant her remote point of hearing reached the Main Hall. The sudden collapse had sent her into a panic. She had immediately abandoned the gardens and calmly walked toward the kitchens, where she spent the next couple of hours helping Old Marga. After all, who would think to look for a spy among the cooks?

Fortunately, no one had come looking for her.

After calming down and thinking it through, she concluded that the Main Hall, the Royal Chambers, and the audience room were probably protected by some kind of ward or magical barrier designed to prevent unauthorized use of magic. Whether an alarm existed or not, she didn’t know, and she didn’t intend to find out.

This time, she had a different approach.

Once she reached a quiet stretch of path shielded by tall shrubs, she lowered her eyes as though admiring the flowers.

"Pika. Cipos. Hishar. Kaldris. Velar."

The spell settled over her mind.

Rather than focusing on Kavard's chambers directly, she aimed at the empty space outside his window, intending to catch the sound waves as they left the room and let the amplification aspect of the spell do the hard work.

The invisible point of hearing drifted through the air until the sounds of a discussion reached her ears.

"...You are a prince. The heir to the kingdom. You need this."

The voice was deep, rough, and carried the authority of someone accustomed to giving orders.

“I. Don’t. Care!” Kavard answered, sounding more like a petulant child than the future king. "I'll pay someone to do it for me."

"You'll pay someone to cast a Detect Poison spell instead of learning it yourself?" the older man asked in disbelief. "You don't see any problem with that logic?"

"I don't."

"What if the mage you hired is the one poisoning you?"

"I'll hire two mages and let some commoner taste it first."

There was a long pause.

"I don't understand it. Why do you have such an aversion to learning magic?"

"I don't like it. The primers are too long, and the trance state you enter while reading them is uncomfortable."

"This is ridiculous." The man's voice hardened. "This is an Archmage Lorant's primer. You only need to read a single page to imprint the spell."

"I don't care." Kavard's tone became even more defiant. "Why should I waste time learning a few spells when I can hire someone who already knows a hundred?"

Another silence followed.

"Aric learned this spell when he was ten," said the older man. "Prince Lyth knows it, and he is thirteen."

"Don't you dare compare me to that traitor."

"Traitor?"

"Aric!"

"I am sorry, I think there is a misunderstanding here."

Lira heard the sound of a chair moving, followed by the rhythmic footsteps of someone beginning to pace.

"Do you know why you're being reeducated by me?"

"Because Aric ratted me out."

"No." The answer came instantly. "It's because you're incompetent."

"What did you call me?"

"A king can afford to be cruel if he is feared and respected." The older man ignored the question entirely.

"A king can afford to be a womanizer if he is charming." His voice remained perfectly calm. "A king can afford to be lazy if he is intelligent."

"But a king cannot afford to be incompetent."

"I am not incompetent!" Kavard shouted. "I had my fun and cleaned up after myself."

"No, Kavard." For the first time, the older man's voice carried genuine disappointment. "A competent man can satisfy his urges without becoming controlled by them. You, on the other hand, are ruled by every impulse that crosses your mind."

Lira listened in complete silence.

"You are short-sighted and refuse to invest in the future. An hour of discomfort to learn a spell that could later save your life is already too much for you," the man continued. "Your father kept a harem of women during his youth, and all of them still respect him to this day, even though he hasn't been involved with them for years."

The next sentence was spoken without a hint of emotion.

"You, on the other hand, managed to kill three of your lovers, two of your own children, and then got beaten by a maid."

"You go too far, Estophon."

A sharp crack echoed through the room, as if someone had been struck.

"It's Sir Estophon to you."

Another impact followed immediately afterward, accompanied by the sound of someone stumbling into furniture.

"You dare strike your future king? I'll have your head!"

A third blow landed.

"And what exactly are you going to do about it?"

"I'll tell Father!"

"He gave me permission," scoffed the older man, Sir Estophon.

"I'll have you executed once I'm king!"

Lira heard another strike, this time sharper, like a slap across the face.

"You..."

Another strike.

"are..."

And again.

"being..."

Then again, for good measure.

"Incompetent."

The room fell silent except for Kavard's ragged breathing.

Remembering that she had been standing still for too long, Lira resumed walking and slowly circled the garden, careful not to draw attention to herself.

"Why would you threaten me instead of keeping that thought to yourself?" Estophon's voice was almost curious. "Are you stupid?"

He answered his own question before Kavard could.

"No. I already know you are."

"I am not!"

"You've just admitted that you intend to execute me once you become king."

"So what?"

"So now I'm a dead man." His tone remained frighteningly matter-of-fact. "If I'm going to die anyway, I have nothing left to lose."

Lira found herself unconsciously leaning her magical ear closer to the conversation.

"I might as well kill you first."

"What?"

"If my fate is sealed either way, why shouldn't I?"

"You can't!" Kavard blurted out. "I am the prince."

"There are two more princes, while there is only one of me."

"My father would execute you!"

"And you'll execute me if I don't," laughed the older man. "I will take my chances with Argon. He and I have history. I saved his life seven times, you know."

Another silence settled over the room.

Lira could almost hear the gears turning inside the prince's head.

Finally, he spoke again.

"I was joking..." His voice had become noticeably smaller. "There's no way I'd execute my dear teacher."

"Better." Sir Estophon sounded almost satisfied, like a proud teacher pleased that his lesson had finally sunk in. "But next time, skip the threats altogether."

His footsteps began moving toward the door.

"A king doesn't posture."

He paused one last time.

"And for the love of the gods..." His voice became cold enough to send a chill down Lira's spine. "Start thinking with your head instead of your cock."

The door opened, then closed.

Several seconds of complete silence followed before they were interrupted by a scream.

"AAAAAHHH!"

Lira heard something heavy smash into a wall as glass shattered across the floor.

"I swear you'll pay for this!" Kavard screamed.

Lira quietly released the spell, then immediately started moving away from the garden. If Sir Estophon possessed any method of detecting the magical disturbance her spell created, she wanted to be nowhere near its origin when he came looking.

She took the longest route back she could justify, walking through the crowded corridors near the visiting nobles' quarters before passing the laundry courtyard, then the kitchens, blending into the constant flow of servants carrying trays, linens, and bundles of firewood. Only after several minutes did she finally return to the library, where she calmly took her place behind the front desk as though she had only spent a short break enjoying the sunshine.

She let out a quiet sigh.

Lord Estophon, commander of the Royal Guard, was far more formidable than she had imagined.

She hadn't expected him to speak to the heir to the throne as though he were a misbehaving recruit. She certainly hadn't expected him to strike the future king repeatedly with the king's own permission.

She had to admit, hearing it had been deeply satisfying.

The conversation had also revealed something unexpected about King Argon. Lira had assumed the king simply tolerated Kavard's behavior because he was his eldest son. Instead, it appeared he was fully aware of his son's shortcomings and had deliberately placed Sir Estophon in charge of correcting them.

Whether that meant the king was a better ruler than she had given him credit for remained to be seen.

After all, they seemed far more concerned with Kavard's lack of competence and foresight than with the dead maids left in his wake.

Still, the morning had its fair share of useful discoveries.

If Sir Estophon regularly visited Kavard's chambers, then assassinating the prince had just become significantly more complicated. The commander was known to be an exceptional knight, and she just confirmed his intelligence and fearlessness for herself. He also apparently enjoyed enough authority to strike the heir to the throne without hesitation.

Lira had no desire whatsoever to go through someone like that to get to Kavard, or worse, to have someone like him hunting her in the aftermath.

Perhaps it would be wiser to shift her attention elsewhere for now. Lord Kelroth still hadn't appeared at court for this year's social season, which made Sergeant Salvek the only remaining target.

She rested her chin on one hand.

Who would have thought that killing nobles and royals would be such a hassle?
