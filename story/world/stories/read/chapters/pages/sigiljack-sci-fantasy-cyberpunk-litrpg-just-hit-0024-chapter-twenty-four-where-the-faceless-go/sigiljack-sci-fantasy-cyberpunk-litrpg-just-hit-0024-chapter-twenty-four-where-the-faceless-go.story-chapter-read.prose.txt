White.

Blinding, soul-flaying white. Digital dissolution consumed him as his mind was condensed and pulsed through the data cable sticking out of his cybernetic arm.

His existence became signal. Flesh dissolved into bandwidth. Thought stretched through his cyberdeck and into the net.

He wasn’t falling, he was transmitting.

Being unwound into light. And carried into the Grin datafortress.

For a few stretching moments he felt the free-floating embrace of nothingness. No direction. No self.

Then sensation returned.

He had form and shape again. Only abruptly and partially at first, but then fully.

The blinding glare faded from his eyes. Shadows returned. Edges framed the world once more--even if they were now only simulated.

He found himself standing calf-deep in clear, mirror-still water.

The horizon split before him: an ocean's edge kissing the skyline of a perfect city.

No smoke. No decay. None of the rot of the real world arcologies.

The buildings before him were made of luminous and beautifully-rendered geometry--symmetrical and perfect, but yet also so cold in an indescribable way.

Clean in a way only the threadnet could be.

John looked down.

He still 'wore' his combat armor. But he had no weapons.

His body had been reconstructed here as a visual abstraction of himself, by his cyberdeck, but his guns hadn’t followed him into the net.

Then he saw them.

All around him.

Hundreds of them. All almost entirely faceless, but not quite.Men, women, children. Blank-white flesh. Eyeless, featureless ovals for faces--bearing smiling toothy mouths that looked like they’d been carved not born.

And they walked.

Single file in multiple forever-stretching lines. No sound, no speaking, no hint that they had any souls to speak of. All moving in one direction:

Out from the city streets and their edge, and into the water that John stood within.

Into the ocean.

Marching forward wordlessly.

And drowning themselves.

John turned to look over his shoulder. He watched a whole row of the not-people step calmly beneath the still water. Another followed. Then three more. They each disappeared in turn--and were replaced by others willingly being consumed by the ocean as if to a metronome's timing.

John's skin crawled.

The existence of something like this required intent.

Someone had designed this. But why?

“Athena?” he murmured aloud. “You up?”

A caw cut through the air. One of <Umbra>'s digital raven constructs dove from the sky, updating his netspace map in his HUD before swooping back toward the digital city ahead.

Seconds later, Athena's facorite code panthers emerged from the water beside him, their limbs made of codelight and mirrored packet trails.

“I’m here, John,” Athena’s voice answered in his mind. “Stability in this cyberspace node is consistent. Give me time and I’ll map it.”

John eyed the drowning crowd.

“You think you can find the right path in this mess?”

“I believe so. With the aid of Umbra’s constructs. But it may not be safe to stay here in the open."

John watched one of the faceless children walk by. Its mouth locked in that perfect, awful smile.

He fell into step against the lines' flow, moving slowly through their perfectly spaced numbers--never taking his eyes of the closest faceless constructs as he passed them.

“This place gives me the fucking creeps,” he muttered.

“There is a certain… macabre aspect,” Athena said.

“Is that your way of agreeing that it’s fucked as all hell?”

“It’s too clean and yet too horrible in other ways,” Athena replied. “And those ‘things’… they feel like remnants. Each of them carries what feels like a deadened thread-echo, but one that fails to stretch beyond the net and into the threadway itself."

John slowed. His eyes tracked another child. Just a little girl. Hollow. Smiling like the other kid had. Appearing oblivious to her own existence.

“Athena… you’re not saying these are actually people, are you?”

“Perhaps… fragments. Leftovers of the Grin's victims."

He stopped walking.

His hands curled into fists.

“And they’re here why? Trophies? Fucking art pieces? Why the hell would the Grin waste bandwidth simulating this—making them drown themselves?”

“John, I don’t know. I can’t parse the rationale. Why are you so upset? Talk to me."

His voice dropped.

“You know why.”Images of Claire passed through his mind. Mostly of when she was younger, of the scared little girl who was slowing losing her mother to a disease of the mind.

The sound of water lapped around his ankles. He was nearly on the concrete shore now. The distant footsteps of the not-people never stopped.

"You feel empathy? That is reasonable, John."

“Adults die every day. Most probably don't deserve it. But I get it. That’s our world. No one owes us shit. But hurting children?” His jaw tightened. “That always pisses me off.”

“You don’t just hate seeing children hurt,” Athena challenged him in a kind voice. “You didn't just kill the threadrunner for the sake of them. You hate anyone suffering."

John shook his head. “I’m not here to play hero. I’m a merc getting paid.”

“Can’t you be both?”

He sighed. “Shit… who knows—”

A shriek interrupted him. Causing him to snap his head towards it.

Glitching pixels flashed in the periphery of his vision. A faceless figure to his right warped--its neck snapping sideways, arms twisting, hands forming into webbed claws.

It lunged.

“Fuck!”

John stumbled back—into another smiling figure—just as one of <Umbra>’s panthers pounced.

The data-cat tackled the Black Ice monster mid-lunge, tore into it. And the faceless abomination exploded into binary shards that screamed.

But the construct behind him, the one he'd bumped into, twitched next. Its own neck jerked.

And its hands snapped up and gripped him.

“John. They're Black Ice. They can damage your neural scaffold directly through your net-uplink. Arm yourself!”

<Program-Deployed: Black Fang><Cyberdeck RAM Remaining: 0>

His hand snapped open as an obsidian blade took shape in it.

The construct shrieked and tackled him into the now ankle-deep code-water. Its claws tore into John's shoulder, ripping through his avatar’s armor, slashing through the net-data body he was piloting and triggering a screaming perception of pain within him.

He gritted his teeth. And swung his weapon across his flattened torso.

<Black Fang> sliced through the warped monster's midsection. It disintegrated mid-howl, disappearing into a number cloud atop him.

He rolled to one knee. Shoulder burning. Avatar flickering.

No time to breathe. He pushed himself to his feet.

And just in time.

Three more faceless constructs began twitching.

Then morphing as the first had before it'd become rabid.

Then all three broke ranks with their individual lines of bodies and charged.

John moved too, unwilling to be easy or docile prey.

He slashed through the first enemy before it could fully establish itself as a threat. Then he turned on the second and skewered it through its chest. Finally, he spun with a sword-arc that carved through the approaching third monster. Their bodies disassembled instantly under <Black Fang>’s geometry-cutting edge.

Apparently, the faceless constructs--even turned into Black Ice--were no match for <Black Fang>.

Each swing of the code-katana sang in his hand. The weapon learned as it killed. He felt it through his cyberdeck's link to his brain—his geist blade adjusting, optimizing its structure. And understanding what it hunted better with each dispatch.

Athena’s voice flickered and all but one of <Umbra>'s constructs, a code panther to be exact, bolted off and left him. “Hold out, John. I’m pushing Umbra’s constructs forward. I'm still tyring to find the access-point for the next node.”

“I know you’ve got this.”

Another monster lunged. He sidestepped. Slashed upward. It burst like glass. "But maybe hurry anyway?"

A second swiped at him, but this one caught his arm. More pain bled through him. He didn't lose blood though, his wounds flashed with blue light--and instead bled consciousness.

He didn’t stop though, he eviscerated another construct as his panther ally tackled the one who had wounded him. Then John cut through two more enemies just as they became threats instead of scenery.

But three more hungry data-beasts appeared from out of the soulless lines of bodies for every one he killed.

Then four.

Then five.

Before a large majority of the lines had broken around him. Glitched smiling constructs twitching into monsters and lunging like static-born nightmares.

For a moment, as he fought the growing horde—John realized what Athena meant.

He was angry.

Not just at the monsters.

At what they represented.

Leftovers. Fragments, she'd said. The last shadows of people who never got a choice.

And he was killing the only thing they'd left behind, just to survive. And no one would probably ever know or care.

He slashed through another.

But she'd still been wrong about one thing: he didn’t feel like a hero because of it.

Athena’s voice hit again:

“John. I think I’ve found the next node’s access point, but something’s wrong. The source code of this fortress-layer is shifting.”John cut through three more faceless constructs at once, the lightest touch of <Black Fang> now destroying them. Before they stopped coming entirely."Athena... what's happening here..."

There was silence.

The water at his ankles ceased rippling.

The footsteps of the city stopped altogether. As every single construct in all of the lines froze around him.

All of the remaining faceless creatures turned to face him.

Hundreds of them, not just the ones who had changed into offensive programs designed to kill him. All still smiling. All eyeless.

All looking through him.

“What the fuck—”

He raised <Black Fang>. Braced for a truly endless swarm.

But they didn’t attack.

They melted.

Every line. Every man. Every woman. Every child. Every body with a stolen face.

They collapsed into binary ooze, pooling across the digital pavement and top-layers of water in every direction.

Athena’s voice snapped through. “John, you need to move—”

“Think it’s too late—”

The pools of combining people-code churned.

Swirled.

Then rose back up as one shape.

From the coalescing code emerged limbs. Faces. A hundred faces—twisting over a swollen, heaving body the size of a truck. Four sturdy and literally screaming legs held horse-like lower legs up. Atop the bottom half, a twisted and deformed and genderless human-torso emerged. It had four arms, each clawed and webbed and deadly-looking. Writhing tendrils of ligaments and teeth extended from its back. Its massive main-mouth opened upon what served as its head, its jaw snapped, and from in-between its huge shining teeth--

Another human face, stretched into a fleshy beak, emerged and screamed.

John stepped back.

A tooth-covered tentacle launched from the back of the code-centaur at him. Fast.

He raised his geist blade, just in time. He swiped through the horror-born tendril that struck at him. He severed it before it could knock him down, but only the part he had cut off dissolved into code.

He realized that <black fang> wouldn't be immediately killing the hulking titan before him.

And the real fight began.
