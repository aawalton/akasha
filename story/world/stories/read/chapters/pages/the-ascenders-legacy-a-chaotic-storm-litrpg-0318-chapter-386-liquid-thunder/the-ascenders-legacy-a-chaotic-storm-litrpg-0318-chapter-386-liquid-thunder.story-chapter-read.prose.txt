It took nearly nine hours to melt the voltrite ores.

And throughout almost all of it, Aodhán never stopped moving.

After creating the chaos strips, he had promptly moved to Elder Gumi’s side, watching as she sketched the outline for the new design. The bracelets had been simple enough to understand, but even on paper, the boots were absurdly complex… so complex that Aodhán genuinely struggled to understand what use they would be to a newly awakened cultivator.

At one point, Elder Gumi had handed him a parchment and told him to try sketching one of the bracelets himself and, well… let’s just say that had gone poorly.

Aodhán had burned through more than a dozen parchments before even managing to get the base design remotely correct. The lines were uneven, the symmetry was terrible, and there was almost no chance the thing would actually work.

But after nearly two hours—and two dozen parchments—Aodhán was finally beginning to grasp the basics.

Barely.

He was halfway through his third dozen parchments when Elder Kobi suddenly suggested that Elder Gumi test her "brilliant" idea for the boots on a cheaper metal first before risking the voltrite.

“It’s not necessary,” Elder Gumi replied without even looking up. “After sketching it, I’m already confident of success.”

But Elder Kobi remained adamant, skepticism practically dripping from every word. Eventually, after several minutes of arguing, Elder Gumi reluctantly agreed—though the scowl she shot Kobi made it abundantly clear she was reminding him that she was still his superior.

Aodhán chuckled quietly at their bickering, amused.

But that amusement died a brutal death moments later when Elder Gumi abruptly placed a heavy tray of electric steel ore into his arms and pointed toward the second forge.

“Smelt it.”

“What?!” Aodhán’s eyes widened. He looked from the tray to the forge, then looked hopefully toward Elder Kobi in search of salvation before turning back to her. “I—I have no experience with forging.”

But rather than sympathy—or understanding, at the very least—Elder Gumi simply stared at him as though he had said something unbelievably stupid.

“How then do you intend to become a runesmith?” she asked flatly. “Or better yet, a runic architect?”

Aodhán opened his mouth, then hesitated.

“I…” he stammered awkwardly. “I always thought I’d just buy the equipment and carve runes onto them afterward.”

Elder Gumi’s scowl deepened.

“What about inside carving?” she demanded, one brow arching sharply. “It is more powerful, more efficient, and significantly harder to disrupt than its counterpart. Or do you intend to cling to outer carving forever while the rest of the world continues advancing?”

Aodhán cringed. “Uh… no.”

“Then get on with it.” She jabbed two fingers toward the forge, and Aodhán reluctantly turned toward it.

Crafted from rune-etched obsidian and blackened steel, the circular forge glowed dimly with layered runes far more intricate than any other construct he had ever seen. Thick vents lined its lower sides while silver-gold arrays spiraled around the central crucible, pulsing faintly with dormant heat.

It was much smaller than the forge Elder Kobi was using, but the size hardly mattered in the face of his complete inexperience.

Big or small, the fact remained that he had never done this before.

Sure, he had watched Elder Kobi and Sedgwick use a forge from time to time, but seeing was very different from doing. And from the little he had seen, he knew enough to understand just how dangerous the work could become.

He glanced toward Elder Gumi.

“I… need you to explain the process,” he admitted. “I genuinely don’t know how to do this.”

Exasperation plain on her face, she pointed toward Kobi without even bothering to look up from her parchments. “You can see what he’s doing, can’t you?”

Aodhán sputtered. “I… yes.”

“Copy him.”

She walked away immediately afterward, returning to her worktable as though the matter had already been settled.

Aodhán stared after her in disbelief. That was the explanation?

He glanced toward Elder Kobi, only to find the old runesmith chuckling quietly under his breath.

Aodhán scowled. “It’s not even funny.”

“I disagree.” Elder Kobi grinned. “But electric steel is far lighter than voltrite. Cheaper too. If you destroy it, I’m sure Elder Gumi won’t care too much.”

Aodhán glanced down at the tray of crackling blue-gray ore in his arms and grimaced. “Yeah… that still doesn’t change the fact that I have absolutely no idea what I’m doing here.” He hesitated briefly, “Do I just pour the ores into the crucible and fire up the forge?”

“Not blindly, no.” Elder Kobi replied, shaking his head, while Elder Gumi snorted from the opposite end of the workshop without even bothering to look in their direction.

Elder Kobi sighed sympathetically. “Sorry, Aodhán. I would show you properly, but I’m busy with the voltrite right now, so explaining it will have to do.”

Aodhán grimaced. He was about to ask what would happen if he messed things up when Elder Gumi suddenly looked over, her expression stern.

“Do not blow up my forge, Aodhán,” she said, fixing him with a glare. “I am already disappointed enough to discover I have taken on an aspiring runesmith who cannot actually smith. Do not give me reason to add anger to the list.”

She turned away again, and Aodhán felt the weight of her disappointment settle in his chest.

And worse still… she was right. What exactly had he been thinking?

But the truth was that he hadn’t been thinking. Forging—real forging—had always been something he had kept at arm’s length. The heat had always made him feel sick.

Until now.

Until here, where the heat was finally manageable.

He looked up at Elder Kobi and found the man watching him with obvious sympathy.

Aodhán inhaled deeply, forcing down his nerves as his expression slowly hardened with resolve. Then he straightened to his full height and gave a single nod.

“Walk me through it.”

Elder Kobi returned the nod. “The first step is to pour the electric steel ore into the crucible and awaken the forge.”

“Okay.”

Aodhán lifted the tray and carefully poured the electric steel into the crucible, watching as the jagged chunks clattered against the black metal interior, blue arcs crackling between them.

He crouched next, reaching for the lower vents beneath the forge where the activation rings were located. Working mostly on instinct, he twisted one of the rings.

The forge hummed to life beneath his palms.

Runes carved into the blackened steel frame flickered awake one after another, while a faint warmth began spreading through the structure.

Subconsciously, Aodhán smiled and glanced toward Elder Kobi. “Is this the part where I channel energy into the crucible?”

Elder Kobi nodded. “A small amount, yes. The forge converts your energy into thermal energy, but control is extremely important here. Feed too much energy into it too quickly and the outer layer of the ore will melt before the core softens.”

“Or I waste energy trying to overheat it,” Aodhán said, already getting the gist of it. He rested a hand against the side of the forge again and asked, “What temperature am I aiming for?”

“At least twenty-two hundred Vehrs.”

This time, it was Elder Gumi who answered, and Aodhán turned to find her watching him intently.

A subtle beat of silence passed before she continued.

“The initial softening of electric steel occurs between nine hundred and eleven hundred Vehrs. Full liquefaction begins between nineteen hundred and twenty-two hundred.”

Elder Kobi nodded and picked up where she left off. “The moment you channel energy into the crucible; the temperature counter will appear. Watch it carefully and control the rise gradually. The forge responds more slowly than your energy does.”

Aodhán nodded, holding Elder Gumi’s gaze now that he had her attention. “What happens if my control slips?”

She stared at him for a moment before giving a slight shake of her head. “Let’s not find out, shall we?”

Aodhán held her gaze for another second before exhaling quietly. “Okay. Here I go.”

Inhaling deeply to steady himself, he placed a hand against the side of the forge, directly over the hand-shaped runic array, and pushed a thin thread of energy into it.

The forge responded immediately.

The dim runes carved throughout the blackened steel frame flared to life one after another, molten orange light racing through their channels like flowing lava.

A low hum rippled through the workshop floor. The vents beneath the crucible opened with a sharp hiss, and a heartbeat later, dark red flames burst to life beneath the forge—low and heavy at first before gradually brightening into orange-gold tongues that curled around the crucible's base like living things.

Heat rolled outward in shimmering waves.

Aodhán felt it against his face almost immediately, and despite himself, his lips slowly curled into a grin as a strange, almost childlike excitement stirred within him.

A sharp inhale crushed the budding excitement before it could become recklessness, and he immediately shifted his attention to the temperature counter hovering above the forge.

47 Vehrs…

48 Vehrs…

He exhaled softly without taking his eyes off the counter.

“How will I know when to increase my energy output?” he asked. “Or when to decrease it?”

“You wouldn't need to decrease it if you used your instincts,” Elder Gumi replied immediately. Even without looking at her, Aodhán could practically hear the scowl in her voice.

From the opposite end of the workshop, Elder Kobi chuckled.

“That question is the trickiest part of forging,” he admitted. “Knowing when. And the truth is…” He smiled wryly. “Most of it comes down to instinct.”

Aodhán glanced toward him briefly before returning his attention to the steadily rising counter.

“Experienced smiths usually judge it by the color of the ore as it heats,” Elder Kobi continued. “But the counter makes things easier for beginners like you.” He gestured toward the forge. “As long as you continue feeding energy into it, the temperature will keep rising. But eventually, it’ll stop climbing after reaching the maximum heat your current output can sustain. When that happens, hold it there. Let the heat spread evenly through the ore before increasing your output again.”

“How long?”

“Depends on the metal,” Kobi replied. “Electric steel conducts heat well, so ten minutes should be enough for the lower stages.”

Elder Gumi snorted. “Don’t teach him shortcuts, Kobi.”

Aodhán smiled faintly at that and returned his full attention to the forge.

97 Vehrs.

Even without the counter, he could already feel the temperature climbing. He could feel the sweat gathering along his brow as waves of heat pressed relentlessly against his skin and robes.

It should have been uncomfortable. Suffocating.

And it was.

Yet as he stared at the still-dormant ore resting within the crucible, Aodhán found himself fascinated by the rising heat instead.

What would it look like to watch impurities leave a metal? To watch something solid slowly change color, soften, and eventually collapse into liquid?

His gaze flicked back to the counter, and a faint smile tugged at his lips once more.

176 Vehrs.

The temperature was rising quickly, but aside from a few thin wisps of smoke beginning to curl from the crucible, nothing had changed yet.

Apparently, forging was also a lesson in patience.

The counter climbed to 250 Vehrs over the next several seconds, and despite watching it like a hawk, it still took Aodhán a few seconds to realize the numbers had stopped ticking upward.

He glanced briefly at the time displayed in the corner of his vision, waited the ten minutes Elder Kobi had instructed, then carefully increased his output by another thin thread.

Nearly a minute later, the counter doubled, and Aodhán felt sweat begin to trickle down his face and back almost immediately.

However, despite the uncomfortable nature of the task, Aodhán found himself thoroughly enjoying it—his mind gradually settling into a strange sense of calm as he watched the ores slowly begin to redden while blue sparks snapped more frequently across their surfaces.

At 500 Vehrs, his focus narrowed fully onto the smoking crucible before him as he activated Axis of Ruin.

Sound dulled into the background. Colors, blurred. And the discomfort vanished.

He barely registered the voices of Elder Gumi and Elder Kobi as the former began inscribing the chaos strips nearby in preparation for the next stage of the process. He barely even noticed the dull ache spreading through his arms from maintaining such delicate control over his energy flow.

It was far harder than he would have expected, but all those endless hours spent creating energized willpower strands had improved his control immensely.

The energy never dipped. Never flared.

It simply flowed.

And at 720 Vehrs, Aodhán was finally rewarded with the sight of the electric steel's internal structure beginning to collapse.

It was like watching crystalline ice slowly fracture beneath invisible pressure, its glowing surface sweating dark impurities that hissed and burned away the moment they surfaced. Veins of molten orange spread through the ore, each one lingering for only an instant before being swallowed by the deepening glow.

The sight sparked excitement within him and nearly caused his output to flare violently, but through sheer instinct and stubborn concentration, Aodhán managed to steady the flow before disaster struck.

Seconds passed in that manner. Then minutes. Then hours.

At 1000 Vehrs, the ores had already begun softening, their surfaces glowing a deep crimson as the once-solid chunks gradually became pliable.

At 1500 Vehrs, the ores had dissolved into what Aodhán could only describe as silver-blue magma, lightning arcing wildly through the viscous liquid like trapped serpents.

It was bright. So bright it was nearly blinding.

Yet somehow, it still wasn't enough.

Exhaling slowly, Aodhán increased his output again, a fifth strand of energy joining the others feeding the forge.

By this point, there was already a noticeable drain on his core from the sheer volume of energy leaving him, but it was nowhere near unsustainable. If anything, Aodhán suspected he could maintain this pace for several more days before truly exhausting himself.

More time passed. And despite the suffocating heat, despite the sweat-soaked robes clinging to his skin and the persistent ache spreading through his arms, Aodhán found himself grinning as the temperature counter slowly approached another threshold.

2000 Vehrs.

At this point, the electric steel no longer resembled metal. Rather, it resembled a churning storm forced into liquid form.

Silver-blue magma rotated slowly within the crucible, thick currents folding over one another while arcs of blue-white lightning snapped endlessly beneath the glowing surface like trapped serpents caught beneath molten glass, alive yet unable to escape. The crucible itself had begun glowing a dull crimson beneath the sheer intensity of the heat, while the runes lining the forge pulsed brighter and brighter with every passing second.

Aodhán's grin widened.

The deeper the liquefaction became, the more controlled the lightning grew, sinking deeper into the molten steel and flowing beneath its surface in branching streams of light that illuminated the crucible from within.

It was absolutely beautiful.

And the fact that he could watch it all happen in real time—the purification, the refinement, the gradual transformation of rough ore into something cleaner, stronger, more stable...

Aodhán found himself utterly speechless.

His gaze flicked toward the counter, anticipation bubbling within him.

2150 Vehrs.

They were close now. So close he could almost taste it.

At this point, the molten steel had become almost mirror-like, its silver-blue surface glowing with an eerie radiance while currents of lightning flowed through it in smooth, rhythmic pulses.

The entire crucible looked as though it contained liquid thunder, a sight that teased countless fragments of inspiration within his mind.

His water aspect hummed quietly within him, resonating faintly with the flowing liquid, but Aodhán barely noticed, his full attention fixed on the steadily climbing counter.

2180 Vehrs...

2190 Vehrs...

And then, finally, after nearly three exhausting hours, the electric steel reached full liquefaction.

The molten metal bubbled softly, a radiant fusion of molten steel and liquid starlight, so dazzling that Aodhán suddenly found himself wanting to preserve the sight forever.

But he couldn't.

He stared at the smelted metal for a long moment, committing every shimmering detail to memory before finally glancing up.

"It's ready."

But Elder Gumi had already realized that.

He found her standing directly opposite him, her expression unreadable as the silver-blue glow of the molten steel danced across her features.

In her hands rested a circular casting frame fashioned from blackened steel and layered inscription arrays. Embedded within its inner channels were the six chaos strips he had created, each one already locked carefully into position.

She smiled and extended the frame toward him, her gaze fixed on the molten electric steel still bubbling softly within the crucible like liquid lightning.

"Good work, Aodhán," she said quietly. "Now pour it in. It's time to put our theory to the test."

Aodhán smiled, lifted the crucible carefully from the forge, and poured the molten electric steel into the casting frame.

The brilliant silver-blue stream flowed like a captured bolt of lightning, filling every carved channel as the inscription arrays flickered to life one after another.

He couldn't wait to see how the finished boot turned out.
