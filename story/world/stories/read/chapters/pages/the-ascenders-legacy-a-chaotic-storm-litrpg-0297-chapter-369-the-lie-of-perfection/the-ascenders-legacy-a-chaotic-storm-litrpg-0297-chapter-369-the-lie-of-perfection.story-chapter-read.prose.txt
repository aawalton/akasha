Far away from Elias’s crumbling kingdom, Aodhán stood panting atop a mound of corpses.

Hundreds of them.

All real. All illusions.

Some were human. Others were monstrous creatures that had no business existing, possessing far more limbs, faces, and fangs than any sane thing should.

Their corpses littered the earth around him now, each one glitching and flickering like an illusion, yet solid beneath his feet. Warm against his palms.

And when he stabbed his spear into what could only be described as the lovechild of a monkey and a dragon, warm red blood spurted out.

This was the conundrum of Lysandra Quinn’s path.

Illusions so perfect that even reality was forced to believe them.

And that made it infinitely harder for Aodhán to differentiate between what was real and what was imagined.

Because against this particular opponent, they were one and the same.

Another wave of monsters rushed him, this time bird-human hybrids so perfectly beautiful that the sight of them made his mind shudder.

They screeched, unleashing a burst of sound that sent imaginary blades stabbing into his mind. But Aodhán had seen this dance before.

Hidden behind the screeching abominations, Lysandra attacked, a host of gleaming blades slicing towards him with deadly intent.

But Aodhán didn’t retreat.

Instead, he teleported forward, phasing right through the abominations to appear before Lysandra’s hauntingly perfect form.

Even before he struck, he knew it was futile.

This version, despite the core blazing within its spirit, was not the real Lysandra.

Her head flew off, but the bird-like abominations barely even glitched.

Neither did the swords.

One stabbed into his shin, so sharp it burst out the other side. Another grazed his shoulder and—

Aodhán phased again, destroying the attacks with a violent activation of Lightning Surge.

But it didn’t matter.

With each abomination he destroyed, more simply took their places.

It was deeply confusing. But more than that, it was maddening.

Aodhán had no time to rest, no time to think. No time at all to search for his real enemy.

Everything around him attacked. Even the very air twisted within his nostrils like snakes. And the fact that such a path had been born from illusion made Aodhán question everything he thought he knew about the affinity.

He had known many illusionists in his life—Marcellan, Brennon, and countless others who bent light, glass, or ice to create their illusions. He had fought many of them before. He knew how the affinity worked.

But this—this was something else.

Something closer to Azul Fetherson’s imagination, only far more sinister because the one wielding it did not lack for willpower as Azul did.

She was a transmigrant, and Aodhán was certain the enemy could not have found a better counter.

Roaring, he raised his hands toward the roiling storm above, Lightning Descent and Chaos Layering activating as he called ruin down upon the horde of abominations rushing toward him.

Lightning struck like judgment itself, obliterating swaths of illusions in an instant.

Storm essence drenched the air in overwhelming abundance, diluting the essence of illusion, and for the briefest second—barely a window—core sense picked up the blazing core of the real Lysandra.

He dove into the chaos without hesitation, latching onto her core with Leech and draining it violently.

But Lysandra was too strong to be held down by the skill.

She vanished before he could reach her, three new replicas—all with blazing cores—appearing in her stead.

Furious, Aodhán sliced off their heads in an instant, roaring in frustration as another simply took their place, chuckling softly in perfect ecstasy.

“Oh, how savagely perfect,” the illusion laughed. “Are you not enjoying this, Aodhán? Isn't it grotesquely beautiful?”

“It would be if you simply died,” he growled, using the brief intermission to search for the real Lysandra.

Core sense bloomed, expanding outward in a wave more precise than anything he had ever achieved before.

Yet nothing.

Lysandra’s voice echoed again, this time from behind him.

“Oh, foolish boy,” she chuckled. “You cannot kill me. I know all your weaknesses. All your strengths. All your skills. And you”—the illusion grinned—“have barely even understood the premise of my path yet.”

She laughed again, the mocking tone grating against his nerves.

“Did you not know that you can never kill what you do not understand?”

Aodhán ignored her, deepening his focus further.

They had been at this dance for three hours now. Lysandra threw all that she had at him—illusions defying comprehension—and he killed them. Any time he found the opportunity, he drained her too.

Until he found a flaw in her path, that was the only strategy he had that even seemed to work.

It was a desperately simple plan: hope he could drain her dry before she did him in. But at this point, it was all he had.

Fortunately, despite its simplicity, it was working.

After using Leech over two dozen times in the last few hours, he had gained enough energy to push himself up a tier and was already approaching the second.

That should have scared Lysandra.

Yet she seemed completely unbothered by his growing strength, more concerned with convincing him that this abominable dance was awe-inspiringly perfect.

“Do you not feel it?” she asked again, a drunken urgency creeping into her voice. “This perfect dance we are having. It’s like a masterpiece painting itself while the canvas screams. Beautiful. Impossible. Perfect.”

“You are crazy,” Aodhán growled.

“I am perfect!” she snapped, a hint of anger entering her voice as a new wave of abominations poured into existence, each one more twisted than the last. “Admit it. You’ve never fought a more perfect battle.”

“I don’t care about your obsession with perfection.”

“Lies,” the illusion snarled, its smile sharpening. “You are just as much a perfectionist as I am. Your skills scream of it.” Its gaze locked onto his. “Admit it.”

Aodhán stared at her in revulsion, chest heaving, and for the first time in three hours...

He stopped fighting.

Lysandra was right. This battle was futile.

He couldn’t kill her, and she sure as hell couldn’t kill him.

But if she already knew that, why had she chosen to be his opponent? What was the point of this battle?

His gaze hardened, golden-black irises flashing as he lifted his head.

“What is the point of all this? What is the point of this battle if you believe neither of us can win?”

“Perfection.” The illusion replied without hesitation, its eyes gleaming as though she wanted so desperately for him to share her obsession.

From the corner of his vision, Aodhán caught a flicker of the real Lysandra.

He waited, his eyes fixed on the illusion as continued speaking.

“Have you never craved it?” it gushed. “A perfect battle? A perfect rhythm?” It leaned forward eagerly. “Have you never—”

“No,” Aodhán replied firmly. “I haven’t. Because I’m human. Humans are imperfect. And you are too.”

His words landed like a slap across her face, and for the first time since the battle began, he saw the real Lysandra flinch in pain. Then fury as she snapped.

“I am perfect. Just look at me—”

“Nothing is perfect,” Aodhán cut in, a sneer tugging at his lips as he turned to face her fully. “Not your illusions. Not this battle.” He tilted his head slightly. “And certainly not you.”

Lysandra stared at him in stunned silence, her eyes widening in disbelief.

Then something within her broke, and fury flooded her expression, raw and unrestrained.

The illusion she wore cracked beneath the weight of it, and for a brief moment, Aodhán saw the scarred, burnt flesh underneath.

It was startling.

But he barely had time to process it before Lysandra attacked, this time in person. Hundreds of monstrous illusions surged alongside her, their claws reaching and fangs descending as the world around him collapsed into violence.

Still, Aodhán didn’t move.

He had seen the imperfection beneath her façade—the scarred form marked by old burns and years of neglect.

And he finally understood the reason for her obsession with perfection.

But just like her façade had proven, her understanding of perfection was a lie.

Because if her illusions were truly perfect—if they were truly real—then they wouldn't need her to sustain them. Perfect things didn't require maintenance. They simply existed. Independently. Eternally.

Lysandra’s illusions didn’t. They flickered. They unraveled. They returned to her… dependent. Flawed.

And if he could show her that flaw—if he could force her to see that the world she'd built around herself was a lie—then he could destroy her from within.

His eyes narrowed, his mind racing through a maelstrom of possibilities until, at last, it settled on one.

Another desperately simple plan.

But if it worked—if it forced Lysandra to confront the imperfection she had spent years running from—then he might yet win this battle.

Jaw tightening with determination, he summoned Varéc into existence and pointed him at the incoming horde.

Varéc didn’t need to be told twice.

He surged forward with a thunderous roar, midnight scales rippling as he rushed toward the attacking illusions, lightning flashing.

Aodhán didn’t join him. Instead, he turned his gaze to Lysandra, his mind piercing through the illusion of beauty to see the woman underneath. The woman Lysandra herself never allowed herself to see.

And then he struck thirty-nine strands of energized willpower surging through his pathways as he activated Synaptic Ruin—not on her nervous system this time, but on her pathways themselves—the nervous network of her magical power.

And squeezed.

Lysandra staggered, her eyes widening as the flow of energy within her spirit suddenly stuttered and died.

Her illusions shattered instantly—not just the monsters, but her façade too.

Everything shattered in a violent cascade. Reality snapped back. And for the first time—

Lysandra—the real Lysandra—was exposed to the world, stripped of every illusion she had hidden behind.

“No!” she screamed, hands trembling over her face as if she were scared to touch such imperfection. “What have you done to me?!”

“A magical paralysis,” Aodhán said, his voice cutting through the chaos. “You hide beneath perfection because you can't accept what you really are.”

“No—” Lysandra shook her head violently, but Aodhán didn’t stop.

"You create perfect copies because you can't accept your own body." He appeared before her in a blink, authority rolling off him in suffocating waves.

“You are not perfect.” He sneered, his voice dropping. “You’re not even imperfect.”

“Shut up,” Lysandra gasped, the words tearing out of her like something sharp and painful.

Aodhán should have stopped there. He’d won already.

But he didn’t.

Instead, he leaned closer, his voice low, merciless. “You’re human. And I will show it to you.”

His arms spread wide as he created a massive storm mirror and pointed it straight at her. “Look at yourself.”

The mirror was as dark as the storm it had been forged from, yet it reflected her with perfect clarity, revealing Lysandra’s true body. Not the platinum-haired beauty she projected, but the reality she'd been hiding for years.

Flesh scarred and puckered. Colors uneven. Old burns that had never properly healed.

Lysandra stared at her reflection with unmistakable vulnerability, and for a moment—just a moment—something in her expression shifted.

Not horror. Not rage.

Acceptance.

As if some part of her had been waiting for this. Wanting it. To finally stop running.

"I'm perfect," she whispered one last time, but her voice carried no conviction. Only exhaustion. "I'm perfect. I have to be—”

“No,” Aodhán said, appearing before her in an instant. “You’re human.”

His claws closed around her real throat—scarred, puckered skin beneath his touch—

"And I am ruin."

He sliced her throat open, lightning claws ripping through her soft skin like knives through butter. Another tore into her abdomen, and a third into her heart.

Her head flew off to the side, and her body fell to the earth, blood spilling from her throat and body.

This time, she didn't reappear.

Because he hadn't just killed an illusion. He'd killed the truth she'd been running from.

Lysandra was dead.

Elder Malverin staggered, a choked sound escaping her lips as she watched Lysandra give up the ghost.

The other elders stared at her, their gazes filled with sympathy, and because of that, Malverin tried to compose herself. Tried to wear a brave face.

She couldn't.

Knuckles whitening, she stared at the screen, half wishing she could scream I told you so into Lysandra's dead face, and half wishing she had fought harder—pressed harder—for the foolish girl to listen.

This was her fault. This was—

“Malverin,” Elder Mournwind’s voice came from behind her, heavy with sorrow. “I’m sorry for your loss.”

But rather than comfort, his words sparked a burning rage within her.

She wanted to lash out, to blame him for this. To blame them all.

Instead, she drew in a slow, shaking breath.

“Call back my troops,” she said coldly. “We are going home. We have lost enough already.”

“Malverin—” Aspwick began, but she ignored him, doing everything in her power to hold herself together.

Without another word, she snatched up her cloak and turned toward the exit, regret churning like poison in her gut. Why had they joined this war in the first place? Why hadn't they remained behind like the Sect of Silence?

The silence inside the command tent pressed in from every side, thick and suffocating, but Malverin kept walking, desperate to escape before her anger finally broke loose.

But just as she reached the exit, Varkas spoke, his voice dripping with disdain. “You should be proud. She died honorably.”

His words were like gasoline, and the fury boiling within her erupted at once, spilling over in a white-hot rage as she spun to face the gathered alliance.

“This is your fault,” she snarled. “Every one of you. I knew this would happen. I was against this spirit-forsaken idea from the beginning.” Her voice rose. “But I let you talk me into it. I let you convince me!”

Aspwick winced. “Malverin—"

She rounded on him immediately. “I hope you're satisfied. We have become the sect without transmigrants. We have become a laughing stock.” She turned to glare at Elder Mournwind. “We have lost enough. We cannot afford to lose any more. The least you can do is let us grieve without cheapening our sacrifice with talk of honor.”

Varkas sighed, and after a long moment, Elder Mournwind gave a slow nod. “You may leave.”

Malverin stormed out immediately, bitter hatred boiling in her chest. As the doors slammed shut behind her, she wished their transmigrants the same fate as Lysandra’s—unaware of how soon that wish would be answered.

***

Back in the Elder Hall, where the High Elders and Calamities had gathered to watch the war unfold, High Elder Zarxan watched Aodhán's screen with newfound admiration.

The boy had always been powerful, exceedingly so for his tier, but now that he knew who his mother was, that power suddenly made sense. He may not have inherited her legacy outright, but her blood still ran through his veins—her instincts, her influence, and perhaps even gifts yet dormant.

They would awaken one day, and if Aodhán believed himself strong now, he would be shocked by what awaited him when that happened.

A small smile touched Zarxan's lips as he leaned back into his throne, quietly pleased while the severed head of the illusion transmigrant tumbled across the screen.

All that shallow perfection, gone.

“Oh, what a waste,” High Elder Mystral said with a disgusted shake of her head. “He should have drained far more power from her before killing her.”

“Perhaps he understands the concept of honor,” High Elder Tharn replied, amusement curling at his lips. “He certainly never disappoints.”

“Indeed,” Zarxan said. “Though I doubt our enemies see it that way. The girl was their only transmigrant.”

Velora let out a soft chuckle. “If they were so weak, they should not have joined the war in the first place.”

“Whatever they think of us concerns us little,” the First Seat said quietly, his gaze still fixed on the screen. “Though I suspect Aodhán is still furious over our little game.” He glanced toward Velora. “I believe you, Tharn, and Kaelthorn still owe him an imbuement.”

Velora grimaced. “It was the right thing to do. He is a prime candidate for the position of High Elder.”

Tharn nodded. “He is skilled, respected, compassionate, and a threader of the Path of Ruinous Authority. He would cast a shadow over whomever we choose, so we may as well choose him.”

“Indeed,” Mystral agreed. “But he is not the only prime candidate.”

As one, their gazes shifted to the screen on the left, where Nymera battled Alaric Thorneveil. The boy was barely holding on, his power a small bastion of serenity against the overwhelming brilliance of Nymera's radiance.

Zarxan nodded. “Nymera would make a fine candidate. I would gladly give her my vote.”

“But not your protégé?” High Elder Kaelthorn asked.

Zarxan grimaced. “I have my reasons.”

“Of course you do,” Tharn replied dryly before turning his attention to the next screen, which showed Ashscar's ongoing battle against Butcher.

But was it really a battle?

Butcher was powerful; of that there was no doubt. He was a hunter, a starved predator who grew stronger the longer the hunt continued. Against any of the others, he would have been a nightmare.

Even Nymera would have struggled.

Against Ashscar, however, it was less a battle than an execution delayed by stubbornness.

Butcher tore across the battlefield in a frenzy of crimson mist, striking from every angle with the savagery of a starving beast, yet not once did he reach his target. Lightning struck him mid-charge, thunder shattered his footing, and walls of shrieking wind hurled him back whenever he drew close.

Ashscar barely even seemed involved. His gaze remained lifted humbly to the storm as he quietly prophesied destruction upon the battlefield.

It had been four hours since their battle began, and not once had Butcher landed a strike.

The storm guarded its messenger, and Butcher, for all his strength and persistence, couldn't fight the storm.

Tharn watched the astounding display for a long moment before sighing. “Ashscar is also a prime candidate.”

This time, no one responded. They simply nodded, each silently awed by the boy's abilities. If he was this formidable now, how much greater would he become when he finally stepped into the Mythic Class?

The silence lingered for a moment before High Elder Velora spoke. “They are all prime candidates. Sophran himself. I wager he would make a fine First Seat when the time comes.”

The First Seat glared at her.

“That time is not soon. But you are correct. He would make a worthy First Seat.”

Silence settled over the hall once more, and Zarxan exhaled softly.

How magnificent it would be to see all six seats filled by transmigrants. The power the Storm Sect would wield then. The authority they would command.

It was a vision fit for legend, yet Zarxan knew such a future belonged more to dreams than reality.

He glanced back at Aodhán's screen, wondering how much the boy's affection for the sect had grown in recent months.

Perhaps convincing him to stay would no longer be so difficult.

Perhaps that dream was not so impossible after all.
