Chapter 67 — What We Carry

ARC IV: THE THINGS THAT STAY

SYNOPSIS: The walk back is longer than the walk out. They pass the Verdant Expanse border before they reach the guild hall. Livia sees it first. Then Yuki checks the server feed. Then they walk the rest of the way in silence. The guild hall is empty when they arrive. It doesn’t stay that way.

VERDANT EXPANSE BORDER — 19:04

They smelled it before they saw it.

Not the smoke from the battlefield — that had been with them for hours, absorbed into their clothes, their hair, the back of their throats. This was different. The absence of something. The specific quality of air that had, until this morning, carried the layered green weight of three hundred square kilometers of old growth, and now carried something quieter and much harder to name.

Livia stopped walking.

The Verdant Expanse spread out ahead of them, to the left, stretching west as far as the zone boundaries allowed. She had walked past its edge dozens of times. She knew what it looked like at dusk — the way the deep greens deepened further, the way the canopy moved in patterns that had their own logic, the way even standing at the border you felt the specific density of something that had been growing for longer than the server had been running.

The trees were still there.

They just weren’t green.

Gray. Not the gray of winter trees, stripped of leaves, waiting — something more final than that. The gray of things that had given everything they had and were now resting in whatever came after. It stretched from the border all the way to the far edge, as far as she could see in the failing light, except for one place at the center where something stood taller and brighter than everything around it, catching the last of the evening sun in a way that had nothing to do with being alive in the usual sense and everything to do with being something the forest had chosen not to touch.

94 percent. That was the number. Three hundred square kilometers and she knew the number without having to be told.

Beside her, Margo had stopped too. She was holding something — a wildflower, small, slightly bent, the kind that grew at the edges of contested zones where no one planted anything on purpose. She had found it somewhere on the walk back. Livia hadn’t noticed when. It was still alive, which felt improbable.

Blorbo had turned from the path ahead and was looking at the Expanse with the particular stillness he had when something registered in whatever way things registered to him.

Nobody said anything. There was nothing to say that the gray hadn’t already said.

They stood there for a little while. Then they kept walking.

Yuki’s stream had been off since she’d stepped into the battlefield. It stayed off on the walk back — no indicator, no audience, just the four of them and the path and the weight of a day that wasn’t finished yet.

She checked the server feed about twenty minutes into the walk. Not for her audience. For herself.

Livia watched her face while she read it.

Yuki had the kind of face that was professionally controlled most of the time — she had been in front of cameras long enough that expressions happened on purpose rather than by accident. But there was a moment, reading whatever was in the feed, where something moved across it that hadn’t been decided in advance. Something that was simply what it was.

She turned the screen toward Livia without saying anything.

The log read:

[SERVER LOG — 19:02]

Botanical mass transfer event. Conduit: Alondra Fayeblossom, Saint of Bloom Lv.91. Status: depleted, critical. Transfer radius: 294.7 km². Current location: contested zone eastern recovery perimeter. Attended by: Fayeblossom, Seraphina; Fayeblossom, Seraphina’s companion entity. Vital status: stable.

[Note: The system notes that this is the largest single botanical transfer event in server history. The system notes: the system did not anticipate it. The system notes: the system is still processing what it saw.]

Livia read it once.

She handed the screen back.

She started walking again. The others followed. Nobody had questions that the walk couldn’t hold better than the standing still.

B&B GUILD HALL — 19:31

The guild hall was empty when they arrived.

Not dark — the automated systems had brought the lights up at dusk, the way they always did, the warm yellow of a building that had been built to be occupied and was operating on the assumption that it would be again shortly. But empty. Still. The specific silence of a place between uses, holding the shape of everyone who had left it this morning and waiting for them to come back and fill it again.

Livia stood in the main hall doorway for a moment.

She went in. Found the lamp in the planning room. Turned it on. The warm light spread across the room, across the armchair, across the desk where her notepad sat exactly where she’d left it before dawn.

She came back to the main hall and sat on the bench nearest the door.

She waited.

Alaric came first.

She heard him before she saw him — the specific gait of someone whose right leg was favoring something it was being polite about, the dice clicking faintly in his closed fist. He came around the corner of the east path and stopped when he saw the light in the hall window.

Livia was already at the door before he reached it. She crossed the courtyard at something between a walk and a run, the kind of pace that knew where it was going and had stopped being careful about it, and she got to him when he was still ten meters out.

He held up the cracked die, first thing, like a report.

"Still rolls true," he said. Voice quieter than usual. The grin was there but it was a different version of itself — thinner, realer, less performed.

"Good," Livia said, and pulled him into a hug that he received with the specific surprise of someone who had forgotten, for a moment, that this was something that happened between people.

He held on a little longer than expected. Then let go. Cleared his throat.

"The leg’s fine," he said, preemptively. "The ankle is fine. The die is fine. Everything is fine."

"I know," Livia said.

"Good," he said again. Pocketed the die. Walked inside.

Nyx came next.

He was holding the two halves of the flashlight at the break point, not trying to press them back together, just — keeping them adjacent. Some unconscious refusal to let them be two separate things yet.

He stopped when he saw Livia coming across the courtyard and his face did something complicated that resolved into nothing in particular, which was how Nyx expressed most things that actually mattered.

She hugged him. He was stiff for exactly two seconds and then he wasn’t, and she felt the two halves of the flashlight against her shoulder blade where his arms came around her.

"I didn’t fall apart," he said, into the air near her ear. Said it like he was still checking that this was true.

"I know," Livia said. "I heard."

He pulled back. Looked at the flashlight halves in his hands. Then at her.

"I’m keeping it," he said.

"I know that too," she said.

He almost smiled. Went inside.

They came in ones and twos after that.

Livia stayed in the courtyard.

Not at the door — partway across, close enough that she could see people coming around the path before they reached the gate, far enough that the meeting happened before they were inside, before the building absorbed them and the day became something being recovered from rather than something still being carried.

She hugged most of them. Not all. Some of them she just put a hand on an arm, a shoulder. Some of them she just met with her eyes and nodded and that was what was needed. Ten months of sessions meant she knew which people needed which kind of contact and she moved through it without thinking about it, without deciding, the way you did the things you had done long enough to know.

Bron came around the path carrying the shield fragment in his hand, Boris walking close at his side, and he saw her and his face did the thing it always did when the relief arrived faster than he knew what to do with it. She crossed to him and he wrapped his arms around her and lifted her slightly off the ground, which she had not anticipated, and when he put her back down his eyes were very bright.

"You’re okay," he said. Not a question. Like he needed to hear himself say it.

"You’re okay too," she said.

He looked at the shield fragment in his hand. "Shield’s not okay."

"I know. You kept a piece."

"Yeah," he said, in a voice that didn’t have an explanation for why. "Didn’t feel right not to."

Boris pressed his large head against Livia’s side. She put a hand on him. He was warm and smelled like scorched ground and like himself, and that was enough.

Thorne arrived with Rigor on his shoulder and his field notebook in his hand, which meant he had been taking notes during the walk back, which meant he was processing in the only way he had and she was not going to say anything about it. Rigor regarded her with the crow’s usual assessment and made a small sound that she had come to understand, over eight months, as something like: satisfactory.

Thorne did not hug her. He stopped two feet away and looked at her with the expression of someone confirming data.

"You’re uninjured," he said.

"Yes."

"Good." He looked at the Verdant Expanse, visible over the guild hall’s eastern wall, its gray canopy catching the last of the light. "Tobren will be out there already."

"I assumed," Livia said.

Rigor made another sound. Thorne translated nothing. He went inside, and Rigor went with him, and Livia understood that he had said what he had come to say.

Morty came alone. Staff in two pieces, carried in one hand, the dark crystal in the other, its surface cold and closed. His heart-shaped pupils had not come back. His crown was still on but the stars on it had gone dark.

He stopped when he saw her and the expression on his skull did something she didn’t have a name for yet, something that would need time to be properly categorized. Something large and tired and, underneath both of those, something that was quietly proud in the specific way of someone who had done the right thing in front of no audience and knew that it had mattered.

She walked to him.

She put her hand on the arm that wasn’t holding the broken staff. He looked at her hand. Then at her.

"847," he said.

"847," she agreed.

He nodded once. Went inside.

He doesn’t need me to say anything else yet. He needs to sit with it first. Tomorrow is for words.

The stretcher came around the path with two B&B members carrying it and Wren walking beside it.

Wren had been moving all day. Livia could see it in the specific way she was holding herself upright — not because she still had energy to hold herself upright, but because she had decided to and her body was complying through a combination of stubbornness and the particular grace of someone who had learned to keep going after the point where keeping going stopped being easy. Her hands were still casting — small maintenance heals, barely visible, the kind that kept vital signs stable without asking anything of the recipient. She had been doing this for hours. She was still doing it now.

Alondra lay on the stretcher with the stillness of someone whose body had stopped negotiating.

She came. She came and she gave three hundred square kilometers and she is still here and she is stable and I was three hours away when she did it and I am not going to spend tonight wishing I had been here because that is not useful and she would not want that.

Tomorrow. Tomorrow is for everything else.

Livia stepped aside to let the stretcher through the gate. She put a hand briefly on Wren’s arm as she passed.

Wren looked at her. Her face had the quality of someone who had spent so much of herself today that the social layer had worn thin, and what was underneath it was just tired and real.

"She’s stable," Wren said. Her voice was steady. "She’ll need time. But she’s stable."

"Thank you," Livia said. "For today. For everything before today too."

Wren looked at her for a moment with something that was not surprise but was adjacent to being moved. Then she nodded and went through the gate after the stretcher.

Phina came through the gate with Glimmer cupped in her arms like something she wasn’t going to put down yet, and her face had the particular composed devastation of someone who had held it together through the part that required holding it together and was now, in the specific safety of having arrived somewhere, allowing some of what the day had cost to surface.

She stopped when she saw Livia.

Glimmer pulsed once — dim, warm, the way he pulsed when language wasn’t the right tool.

Livia opened her arms.

Phina walked into them with none of her usual elegance, which was the most honest thing Livia had ever seen her do. The hug lasted a while. Glimmer, between them, was very still.

"She’s going to be all right," Livia said. Quietly. Not as a promise. As the thing she believed.

"I know," Phina said. Her voice was steady. "I know. I just —" She stopped. "I softened the ground. That was all I had left. The ground."

"It was enough," Livia said.

A silence. Then Phina straightened, drew back, rearranged herself into something closer to her usual posture. Her eyes were bright. She did not acknowledge this.

"Go inside," Livia said. "Rest."

Phina looked at the gate, then at Livia. "Come in soon."

"I will," Livia said.

Phina went inside.

Miles was the last person through the gate.

He had Emberwing on his shoulder, the fire low and steady — not the blazing it had been on the battlefield, not the candle-flame of his lowest HP, just the ordinary fire of something that was simply present and not performing either extreme. His coat was scorched in two places. He had not repaired it.

He stopped on the other side of the gate and looked at her across the courtyard.

Neither of them said anything.

Livia walked toward him. He walked toward her. They met somewhere in the middle of the courtyard in the lamp’s warm light from the planning room window, and she put her arms around him, and after a half-second he put his around her, and they stood there in the courtyard in the quiet of a guild hall that was full now, finally, everyone accounted for.

Emberwing’s fire shifted on his shoulder. She could feel the warmth of it near her cheek.

She didn’t say I’m glad you’re okay or come back like I asked or after the war, you promised. She didn’t say anything. The courtyard was doing that for her.

After a while, Miles said, very quietly: "You’re still glowing."

Livia looked at her hands. The faint gold of the Luxemira connection, still there, still quiet.

"A little," she agreed.

"Good," he said. The word had weight she didn’t try to unpack right now.

They walked inside.
