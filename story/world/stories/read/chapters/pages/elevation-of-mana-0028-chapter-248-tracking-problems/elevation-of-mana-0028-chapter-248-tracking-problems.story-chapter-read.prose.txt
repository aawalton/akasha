The new elves left about a month after they came, and while they were here they were busy, extremely busy. They had added to the city, and not just here and there. It now had a functional sewer system, light-posts, and several fields in the area had been turned.

That however had been a few years ago, and now I sat with the other kids, helping to take care of the small fields around the burgeoning city. Well, take care of was a bit of a misnomer, play in and with was probably more correct, but we were gathering food as we went. Most of that was picking the berries growing on the bushes there as we chased each other through.

There was no grain, at least not yet. That made me sad, no grain meant no real bread. Sure, we had something kind of like bread made from crushed nuts and roots, but it just wasn't the same. Of course we didn't have any cows or the like either, so, well, no butter for the bread, kind of a shame.

“Come along everyone, it's time for class,” we heard the call come over the fields, one of the elves, a woman, miss Kida, who'd been sent to manage us and had some magic that boosted her voice, allowing it to be heard where we were. She didn't sound excited.

It wasn't long until I and about fifteen other boys found ourselves sitting in a small room near the center of the city itself.

“Hello everyone, again, how are you all today?” she asked, looking out at us.

“Good teacher,” we said, what we knew she wanted to hear, or at least part of what she wanted to hear.

“Okay, today we're going back to reading.” There was a collective sigh at that.

Personally I found the elvish? Elven? Whatever, the elf language was fairly straightforward. It was fairly simple on some levels and the alphabet system was clearly designed to be phonetic rather than anything else. We were learning both the language and how to read it, or at least trying to.

“Um...” the boy in front of me said, trying to sound out the word he'd been given. “Under...stood?” he guessed.

“Understood,” the teacher confirmed, rubbing her temples. “You should know this, we covered it three days ago, four days before that, eight days before that, and twice in the month proceeding.”“I... don't remember?” he tried.

“Search your memories, look through them one at a time for the word and find it. I know many of you struggle with this, but you must learn to do so. If not we'll have to repeat these again. This should be a short lesson, back home we teach the children everything we're teaching you in under a month,” she complained, not for the first time.

We'd been at this for months now, struggling. At first she'd thought we were messing with her, or playing some joke.

“Your memory is different,” I said, not for the first time.

“It can't be that different,” she replied. “We still speak the same way, and though you're slower than I'd like you remember some things well enough.”“You remember everything don't you?” I asked.

“Yes, we've been through that, Op,” she replied, again.

“I know, but we need to say it again. What is the first thing you remember?” I asked.

“The first? Hmm...” she thought for a few moments. “When I was a baby I remember looking at a fire my mother held me near and thinking it was scary.”“How old were you?” I asked, actually curious on that. I had a few blurry memories from my own first childhood, when I'd been three or so, but not much before that.

“A few weeks old probably?” she said, and began looking at the air, seeming to do something mentally. “I was roughly five weeks old, if I had to guess.”

“None of us remember that teacher. Most of us don't remember what we had for breakfast three days ago,” I told her.

“That's silly, go into your mind and look back,” she said, again, still frustrated. “I don't know how to tell you how to do it better than that.”

“We can't,” another boy said, rubbing his eyes.

This went back and forth for about five minutes before she began trying to drill us on our letters again, hoping that this time it would take. For some of us it helped, or we already had it, for some of us it was just frustrating, there were no mnemonics, no games to help us remember, just charts and her telling us to remember it.

Once the time allotted was finally over and I had the chance to leave, headache and all. It was now much later, and I was ready for a chance to go home and enjoy my evening. Though of course the city wasn't empty now, there were perhaps several hundred people here now, and growing as more and more of the little family groups learned it was here and slowly gathered.

On the way I found someone I'd seen many times before, she'd done my training personally since I'd been one of the first humans here, and I was a healer much like her.

“Miss Adia, we need a new teacher,” I said as I saw her.

The elven woman turned and raised an eyebrow at me. “Why do you say that Op?” she asked. “She did a very good job with the kids back home in Atal, highly regarded.”“Humans aren't elves,” I said.

That got her to blinking briefly. “I know that Op, but I think you mean something different.”“We learn different, we need games, and time, and rhymes, or the people she's trying to teach will never learn what she's trying to teach them.”

“Can you tell me more about that?” she asked, one finger bouncing on her lip as she considered.
