Finding Hannah wasn't as easy as closing his eyes and reaching out to the weave, but that was where his search would have to begin. Opening his senses, he felt more than saw the many different echo threads representing those he had healed since getting the Weaver's Echo skill. Hers wasn't among them. Several others were missing as well, those belonging to people who were now dead. Logic would dictate that Hannah's thread not being present meant that she was no longer among the living. Perhaps that was true, but it didn't mean she was dead. Luke refused to believe that. In her shadow form, there was little that could even hurt her. So, what could? The void, certainly.

Was that it? Had she left not just the system, but even The Greater System? If she was beyond the weave, having entered the void, if such a thing was even possible, then perhaps that was where he needed to begin his search. Before going down that risky, dangerous route, however, there was one more thing Luke wanted to try. Echo threads were not the only way to locate others in the weave. The threads were not just representations of people and objects, but of connections of different sorts. If Luke looked hard enough, he could see threads running from himself, from his Meta-Heart to be precise, heading off into the weave and toward those he had made connections with in the past. They all looked the same, but there was a sense about them that made it possible to distinguish one from the other. The first one Luke picked out with his senses, not by touching it, mind you, was from someone with great care for her companions and almost overpowering gratitude for being alive and in a new place: Lemira. Next was Curtis, with his usual, but these days somewhat good-natured, grumpiness. Something was different about it, though. A hunger. It had been there since his class change. Becoming a Hollow rather than a Reaver had changed aspects of his darkness into something that looked a little too close to the void for comfort. Luke would have to talk to him about that. Penny's thread was easy to pick out, full of youthful energy and pep, though with a longing for something he couldn't make out even if he wanted to.

One by one, Luke went through the threads. Hannah wasn't among them. It was as if she'd been cut out of the weave. What that meant, he didn't have a clue, but if she wasn't in the weave, that meant she was outside of it. The void. There was no helping it. Luke would have to extend his search past the boundaries of The Greater System. For that, he would need to gather himself.

Opening his eyes, Luke bounced off the bed and walked the few steps over to the shower in his Shelter. Removing his clothes and the robe by taking them into his inventory, Luke stepped in and cleaned himself, washing away the meeting with the Singers and closing their portals, as well as the prior day's muck and grime. The robe protected him from most of it, but the symbolic nature of cleansing himself provided an air of tranquility. This wouldn't be the first time he peeked into the void, but each time he did so, he couldn't help but be afraid of what he would find. Steeling himself for the experience, he re-dressed and sat down on the floor rather than the bed. Comfort would not help with what he was about to attempt. The stronger Luke got, the more he'd come to understand that it was his understanding of, and power over, the weave that made a Lifeweaver strong, not the healing or potential for destroying other Integrated or monsters. Using the weave to kill Ezezal by hanging him from a noose crafted from a thread had been a crude attempt at harnessing that power, but it had been a first step. Still, Luke had a long way to walk and a good many steps before he could truly call himself a weaver. That didn't mean he should be afraid to start off on that journey, and it was time for the next step.

Once he'd seated himself in the lotus position, Luke closed his eyes and breathed. A clear sense of his own Meta-Heart and mana channels filled him. In truth, those inner workings were mere extensions of the weave itself. It reached down into every Integrated and, well, integrated them into the system. That system was nothing more than a sub-weave of sorts, part of The Greater System. In that way, they were all one. Sure, some Integrated didn't have mana in the traditional sense, and instead used stamina or just straight-up health, but they were just different aspects of mana. Perhaps it was more correct to say that health, stamina, and mana were all just aspects of the system. That truth made him consider reaching out to the weave through himself and not by pushing the meditation deep enough that he saw the threads. It was no surprise to him when it worked, but he let out a low curse at himself for not thinking of it earlier. What was done was done, so Luke continued questing outward as the system weave presented itself to him. With this sort of exploration, there were no issues with range or other restrictions keeping him back. For a moment, he mused on the difference and how there should be none, but then shelved that question for later. Now he had a mission to accomplish. Luke needed to find Hannah.

The act of passing out of the local system, through the barrier, and into The Greater System was not one of distance, but one of shifting layers of existence. Even so, he had to travel along the weave and find his way through it in ways that made his head hurt if he thought about it too much. No, this was not a problem for the logical mind, but one for his senses and intuition. Even after having passed through that barrier several times, the path was never clear and simple. That didn't mean it was beyond Luke's ability. Quite the opposite. Finding the way through was easier than ever, perhaps on account of his new way into the weave, but it might also just be greater experience. Once through and in The Greater System Weave, it was difficult not to feel a sense of overwhelm. While his local system spanned unfathomable depths and distance, with its weave mirroring it, all that was a mere blip in The Greater System and the weave that built it. Searching through it all would take several lifetimes, if not longer, and there were secrets, knowledge, and power out there to be had, so much of it that Luke felt as if he had to pull his senses back, like he was staring straight into the sun. Rather than look away, Luke shifted toward his goal. The Greater System Weave was not the intended destination. Not this time.

Guide would not like him skirting the edge of The Greater System Weave, but there was no helping it this time. Last time, the void and those in it had called to him, had bid him to join them, and Luke, the fool that he had been, hadn't been able to turn away. He wouldn't make that mistake twice. Nothing could make him enter the void. The risk was too great, and it would eat him alive. But maybe, just maybe, he could pull someone back if he just found the right leverage.

Going straight through The Greater System Weave, Luke no longer felt his own body. Out there in the endlessness of metaphysical space, there was no room for physical matter. It was just thought and senses, nothing more. Floating toward the edge, he felt attention pass over him. Not the first time that had happened, but it was the first time that attention had come from inside The Greater System. Those other times, it had always been the void being, but Luke wasn't near the edge. Not yet. Someone or something within the boundaries of the system had taken notice of him. Unsure if that was a good or a bad thing, probably bad, he continued. Like with the crossing from the local system to The Greater System, moving outside of that was not a matter of distance. This wasn't like a map of geography where the local system was a country, The Greater System was a planet, and the void was space. All three were on top of each other and kind of occupied the same space, but not really. Perhaps he'd find someone one day who could give a proper explanation, but Luke was not that person.

Either way, finding his way through The Greater System was a matter of perspective, and Luke traversed the weave until he found what he saw as an imperfect stitch, or a fray of the weave, if you will, and dove his consciousness through it. There, he found the edge, the line where it all ended and nothing began, the selvage, if you will.

Staring at it, perceiving the change from something to nothing, was a strange experience. The other times, it had been in moments of panic or at least with near-death hanging over his head. Now, he found himself calm and collected. A rainbow of threads spanned infinity in all directions but one, where the weave ended. If the weave was infinite, then the void was larger still. Those concepts were too large for him to grapple with, and Luke found his will pulling in all directions, as if his mind wanted to destroy itself rather than go down that line of thinking, so he pulled back and reined himself in. Light and darkness. It was beautiful beyond description, but also as mundane as a beginner’s actual weave of threads on someone's kitchen table. The ends were fraying, threatening to unravel the whole thing. That was it, what Guide had informed them of. The void was coming. Even now, it ate into the weave, destroying it little by little. How they would ever stop that, Luke didn't have a clue, but it wasn't why he had come. No, he had come for Hannah, and to search for her, he needed to look.

To that end, Luke stared into the abyss.
