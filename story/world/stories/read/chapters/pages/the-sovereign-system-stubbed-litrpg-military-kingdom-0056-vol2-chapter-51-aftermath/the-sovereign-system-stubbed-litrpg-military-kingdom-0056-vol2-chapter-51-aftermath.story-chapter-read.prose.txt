Some time later, Luke awoke to the murmur of voices outside his tent, rousing him from a night of broken sleep. His eyes felt gritty, and his body ached—stiff and heavy from the cold, uneven ground.

Suppressing a curse, he sat up and glanced toward the damp robe draped over the stool. It was still soaked. With no other option, he reached for it. Greeting his officers half-naked would hardly inspire confidence.

Thankfully, sunlight streamed through the fabric of the tent. If he stayed in the sun long enough, he might dry out enough to avoid looking like a drowned rat.

He dressed quickly, tugging on his wet, mud-caked boots with a grimace before stepping outside. The warmth of the morning sun hit him like a balm, forcing his eyes into a squint but easing some of the chill lodged deep in his bones.

Commander Bridges stood at attention nearby and cupped his fist in greeting the moment Luke appeared.

“General, I hope you slept well.”

Luke considered a biting remark, but one glance at the officer’s haggard face told him the man hadn’t slept a wink. He cleared his throat instead, brushing past the comment.

“Where is the Vice General?”

“He turned in shortly after you, sir. Shall I wake him?”

“No, let him rest. Did you inventory everything we secured in the raid last night?” Luke asked as he stepped fully into the light. The heat soaked into his skin, divine after the cold misery of the night.

“Yes, sir,” Bridges replied promptly. “We collected over two thousand weapons, a decent supply of armor, and five full carts of grain. It’s not much, but it should buy us time—at least until we reach a settlement along the river.”

Five carts… Less than I’d hoped for, Luke thought bitterly.

The men were already surviving on scraps. Even if he rationed only half a pound of grain per soldier, it wouldn’t be enough to last a full day.

Oblivious to Luke’s grim calculations, Bridges continued his report. “We lost twenty men. Another forty sustained minor injuries. The Vice General had the dead buried at first light and ordered us to leave the enemy where they lay.”

Luke nodded. “Make sure we keep a full record of the fallen and the wounded. I’ll ensure their families receive the compensation I promised once we return to the capital.”

He paused, then added, “Start the cookfires. We’ll eat the food now. Our men deserve a warm meal after what they’ve been through. We move out in two hours.”

Bridges saluted and turned away. Luke watched him go, then exhaled through his nose, fingers pinching the bridge of it in frustration.

What was I thinking? In hindsight, the plan had been too shortsighted. Yes, the system quest had been completed. But in doing so, he’d potentially doomed ten thousand men to starve within days.

Maybe I should’ve just failed the quest… he thought grimly.

But he shook the thought off just as quickly. Doubt was a luxury he couldn’t afford.

If I hadn’t come here, we never would’ve discovered the Lhair forces heading toward Ralis.

Still, fatigue clung to his mind like cobwebs. He hadn’t yet gathered himself enough to formulate a plan. Usually, with the help of his Scholarly Pursuit skill, he could analyze the facts and generate a dozen strategies—even if half were implausible.

Right now, though, he felt like he was dragging his thoughts through mud.

But everything felt far too complicated right now. Beneath the surface of his exhaustion, a deep bitterness gnawed at his heart.

From the very beginning, it had felt like an invisible hand was pulling the strings—a hidden force manipulating every piece on the board to its own design. The murder of Viscount Nero, the assassinations across the Marxx Kingdom, and now Lhair’s invasion so soon after war was declared…

It was all far too calculated to be coincidence.

Luke rubbed the crust from the corner of his eye and smothered a sigh.

It feels like I’m fighting a losing battle.

Is saving the Marxx Kingdom even worth it?

The questions circled endlessly in his mind, like vultures over a dying beast.

And then there was Victoria…

He opened his system window and stared at the latest notification. He’d tasked her with protecting the Crown Prince. If she had been gravely injured, then that could only mean someone had struck at the prince directly.

Luke bit down on his lip, hard enough to draw blood.

He felt helpless.

If the Crown Prince has been killed, then only Prince Lyon remains as heir.If there's a traitor in the palace working with Lhair, then they must be backing the second prince.But if that's the case… why send a force to attack Ralis?

He frowned, the weight of it all pressing down on his already burdened shoulders. Perhaps if he were well-rested, the pieces might have fallen into place. But clarity eluded him.

The chill of his damp robe clung to his back, sending an involuntary shiver through him. He stepped further into the sun, trying to soak in its warmth. Nearby, Bridges barked out orders to the weary soldiers. Despite their exhaustion, they responded not with groans or slumped shoulders—but with eagerness.

The sight reinforced Luke’s earlier decision. Using the rations now, though risky, was necessary. While the food wouldn’t stretch far, even a partial meal could mean the difference between collapse and perseverance. His Scholarly Pursuit skill had etched the map into his mind, but it didn’t update in real time.

There was a modest fishing town further north along the river. He recalled its placement clearly. But how long it would take to reach, or whether it could feed ten thousand starving soldiers—those were questions he had no answers for.

Just as his darker thoughts reached a crescendo, he felt a presence hovering nearby.

Turning his head, Luke spotted a bald soldier with scarred features standing quietly at attention.

“Christian? What is it?”

The old-looking young man flinched slightly, and Luke realized his tone might’ve come across harsher than intended. He cleared his throat and steadied his voice. Just because he was exhausted didn’t mean he had to take it out on his men.

“General…” Christian said quietly, cupping his fist and performing a deep, respectful bow. He bent at a full ninety-degree angle—an honor typically reserved for teachers, masters, or someone who had irrevocably changed one’s life.

Taken aback, Luke raised an eyebrow. “I’m not sure I’ve done anything to warrant that kind of gratitude, Lieutenant Commander. Save it for when we make it back home alive.”

But Christian didn’t move. He remained bowing, still and reverent, as if Luke’s words had gone unheard—or perhaps, hadn’t changed what was in his heart.

“I thought I would die in Xiandong, sir. Every day I live now feels like a borrowed one…” Christian paused, his deep voice turning husky. “This new life you've given me—I dedicate it to you, General.”

Huh!?

[DING]

“Hey, no fair! I wanted to be the first to submit to the General,” a voice called from nearby.

It was Blake, one of the other Lieutenant Commanders Luke had appointed recently. The veteran soldier wore a bright grin, the expression almost absurd against the backdrop of his drenched uniform and wearied eyes.

He stepped beside Christian and bowed deeply as well. “I submit to the General.”

[DING]

“Argh! I told you those bastards wouldn’t wait for us!” came an irritated shout from the back of the crowd.

By now, most of the surrounding soldiers had turned to watch the strange scene unfold, and Luke could feel his face warming under the weight of their attention.

Sure enough, Zion and Jason appeared a moment later, taking their places beside their fellow officers. They offered no elaborate speeches—only firm, resolute words of loyalty followed by a deep bow.

Luke’s eyes flicked to the system window as the notifications stacked in rapid succession:

[Christian Hays has declared his loyalty][Blake Adams has declared his loyalty][Zion Mason has declared his loyalty][Jason Jacks has declared his loyalty][Accept?] [Y/N]

Baffling. That was the only word he could muster to describe the situation.

Moments ago, he’d been questioning everything—his surrender at Xiandong, the choice to cast his men into the wilderness with barely any supplies, and the wisdom of completing the system quest at all.

And now… here these four stood, pledging their loyalty without hesitation. It was as if they were answering the unspoken doubts in his heart, telling him: You did the right thing.

Luke stared at them—at their bowed heads, unmoving and upright with the pride of soldiers who had chosen their path. His throat tightened.

Is this okay? Can I really accept them like this?

His current subordinates had all been friends—people he trusted, peers he didn’t hesitate to command. But these men… would accepting them change that balance? Would he struggle to lead them the same way?

The questions clawed at his mind—until a calming wave swept through him, cool and sure. Luke recognized the effect immediately.

Dragon’s Heart.

The skill dulled his inner turmoil, cutting through the emotional fog like sunlight through mist. His breathing slowed. His heart steadied.

A faint smile tugged at his lips.

He raised his hand and selected [Y], accepting all four declarations.

“I accept your vow of loyalty,” Luke said clearly. “Now raise your heads. We’ve still got a long road ahead before we make it back to Ralis.”

[DING]

Only then did the four men lift their heads. Their eyes gleamed—not with fanaticism, but with quiet resolve. Each of them wore the same broad, satisfied grin.

Luke waved them off, giving orders for them to join the others for breakfast. No one objected. The atmosphere had shifted.

And as the crowd dispersed, Luke finally found himself alone once more.

Luke sank onto a fallen tree, letting the sun soak into his damp skin. He shuddered unconsciously at the warmth—an involuntary reaction from a body long starved of comfort.

Without a word, he opened the system and navigated to the Subordinate window to check the latest updates.

[User has accepted Blake Adams as a subordinate][Congratulations. Uncommon-tier subordinate acquired]

[User has accepted Zion Mason as a subordinate][Congratulations. Uncommon-tier subordinate acquired]

[User has accepted Jason Jacks as a subordinate][Congratulations. Uncommon-tier subordinate acquired]

Uncommon, huh? He hadn’t expected them to be epic-tier like Kayson and the others—but still, it was something. Not everyone could be a prodigy. Sometimes, loyalty mattered more than raw talent.

But as he scrolled down, his smile dimmed.

No skill selection.

Figures. The system likely didn’t want him gaming it—recruiting dozens just to stack skills like some broken gacha exploit.

He jolted so hard he nearly fell off the log.

What!?

The wording was... ominous. Like a subtle whisper from the system saying, You’ve done it now.

Does this have anything to do with last night?

The warning says I’ve caught the attention of “others.”Does that mean it’s not just the Bishop I have to worry about?

Why did everything have to be so complicated?Wasn’t it enough to be trapped in a web of war and treason?

What if he was the only one without the full picture?

What if he was just another piece on someone else’s board?

“That bad, huh?”

Luke whipped around, startled.

A bedraggled old man stood nearby, hunched and dripping like a half-drowned rodent. His robe clung to his wiry frame, and his goatee had surrendered to the rain in sad clumps.

Luke sighed for what felt like the hundredth time since waking.
