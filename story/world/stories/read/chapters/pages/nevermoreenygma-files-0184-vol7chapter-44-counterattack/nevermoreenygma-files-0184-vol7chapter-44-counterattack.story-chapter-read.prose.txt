Chapter 44

Counterattack

March 2009

The weak light from the lamps barely illuminated the luxurious decorations of the study.

A study that could have been a place of reflection and serenity, but at that moment was only a scene of deadly tension. The sound of distant gunfire echoed in the distance, a warning that the chaos was everywhere, ready to devour everything.

Mai stood there, staring at the man in front of her in a military uniform, his face tense, his hands raised in a gesture of desperation.

Mai wore a black tactical uniform, which blended into the half-shadow of the study, but her silver hair caught the moonlight streaming through the window.

“Please… don’t… I didn’t mean to…,” he murmured, with his voice trembling, pleading with a palpable desperation that hung in the heavy air. Yet there was something in his plea that didn’t match. His face wore a smile, like that of a madman.

Mai watched him with cold eyes, not an ounce of compassion. She knew him, she had seen him on television, in the files photos. But in that moment, it didn’t matter. The man’s fate was already sealed, and she was merely the hand to deliver the inevitable. The reason behind her presence there was him. All she knew was that the target had to be neutralized—without questions, no hesitation.

She had seen too much in those months. This was something that should have been done a long time ago.

Her hand closed around the magical pistol, the cold metal gripped firmly. Not a second of doubt. The trigger pressed, and the shot rang through the air with a sharp sound that made the man collapse to the floor, his lifeless body leaving an irreversible mark in the room. For a moment, his body glowed red, until dark patterns appeared across his skin and his face aged as if undergoing instant mummification. First the wrinkles, then the skin clung to the bones, leaving a dry corpse that never decayed. It had simply dried completely.

The silence that followed was absolute, broken only by the pounding of Mai’s own heart, fast and deep, as if the echo of the shot had reached the earth itself.

She had done it. She had taken her revenge.

They said one usually felt emptiness in those moments, but she felt relief. That kind of trash didn’t deserve to walk among the living.

But before she could process what had happened, a door creaked open, and a small head peeked inside.

A little girl entered, her small delicate figure illuminated by the faint light of the study and the hallway behind her, amidst approaching screams.

“Dad, what’s happening?” she asked, her soft voice full of innocent confusion, which instantly shattered when she saw the figure on the floor. Her face transformed from uncertainty to horror, and her large round eyes quickly filled with tears.

Mai felt a pang in her chest, as if an invisible hand had gripped her heart.

The girl ran toward her father, but her body froze as she looked at him, staring at the scene with a cold, lost gaze. It was as if the girl recognized the body, yet those features on the still-warm corpse resembled those of an old man. Time seemed to slow, and silence thickened in the room as Mai watched, unable to move, while chaos knocked at the door.

Then two soldiers burst into the study, their boots thundering, weapons raised toward Mai. “Stop! Hands where we can see them!” they shouted firmly in Korean, their presence as imposing as destiny itself.

Mai didn’t hesitate for a second. The image of the girl pinned to the corpse, eyes wide with terror, pushed her to act.

Mai leapt toward the window.

While doing so, she pressed the button on his device to signal that the objective had been achieved. The others who had infiltrated the house did not need to go to the study.

A battle of big proportions was unfolding throughout the entire area. Explosions in the distance, the sounds of machine guns, swords, and pistols.

The air struck her fiercely as she jumped, falling three stories in a graceful motion, the world spinning around her. The sound of gunfire from the study reached her in a breath, but it was too late. Mai hit the ground, her feet touching the earth with the speed of an animal escaping its cage, without looking back.

Bullets whizzed past, slicing through the air, but Mai had already moved ahead, propelled by the speed that only adrenaline and the will to survive can grant. A few shots grazed her, but none hit. There was no time to think, only to run.

Night closed around her, the city lights flickering like distant points, oblivious to the carnage that was happening in the place. Mai didn’t know about who the little girl was or what had happened to her. The only certainty was that she had crossed a line from which there was no return months ago. And now, all she could do was run.

But that was fine. It was necessary.

After all, all the ones there were there for one reason.

The fight erupted in the back of the mansion, among the gardens that had once been a place of calm, meticulously decorated, now transformed into a battlefield. The moon faintly lit the shadows of the struggle, but nothing could soften the chaos spilling across the grounds. The tactical swords clashed against soldiers’ gunfire echoed through the air, shattering the night with its metallic roar.

The members of the Wishper team moved with the fluidity of a wild river, their shadows entwined in a deadly dance that only the most trained could execute.

Sari, the blonde-and-green-haired siren, leapt from side to side, her trident in one hand and a flash of fury in her eyes. Each strike dismembered soldiers with lethal precision, her grace turning her into a ghost among the shadows.

Beside her, Milena, with her purple hair, glided like a specter, launching magical projectiles from the big gloves in her hands that exploded in bursts of blue and red light, obliterating everything in their path. It had taken both mermaids months to master the magical weapons made especially for them, but now they wielded them with frightening ease.

In Mai's case, the magic weapon was similar to a pistol, except that it fired magic bullets. Many of those involved in the operation carried them, because it was not until the operation was well underway that night that Mai and seven others were chosen to go up and execute the leader, as her short stature and speed were quite advantageous. In the end, Mai was the fastest to reach the study meanwhile the others were attacking different parts in the mansion.

The soldiers, mostly well-trained and equipped humans, retaliated furiously, firing volleys of bullets at the fast-moving figures that seemed to vanish before the bullets could reach them. But bullets weren’t their only enemy.

In the air, the team’s espers, moved their hands in endless rapid gestures, controlling the elements around them. The air twisted with the energy they released, and rocks, trees, and even the ground itself were lifted by telekinetic force, hurled like missiles at the soldiers.

A thundering explosion followed the impact of a grenade thrown by one of the soldiers. Dust and debris flew into the air, while the Wishper team dodged the blast with superhuman speed.

Mai was among them, her senses alert, watching coldly and calculatingly as the combat unfolded. In her hands, the air seemed to vibrate, charged with contained energy. Every movement was an extension of the same imperturbable calm that had accompanied her recently.

This is necessary, she told herself, glancing at Sari.

“You did it?”

Mai nodded. Sari smiled.

The mermaid, amidst her fury while fighting, seemed to be releasing all the contained grief.

Amid the chaos, Sari’s words from the day they met echoed in her mind: “This isn’t just a fight, Mai. It’s a message. A message of what could happen to all of us.”

She knew what was at stake. The mission that had brought them here was critical, and the consequences of failure were far greater than any physical battle. Though the purpose of this confrontation was clear, something else was at play—something only the future could reveal with certainty.

Grenade explosions briefly illuminated the battlefield, and the clashing of swords against soldiers’ armor, along with the shouts of combatants, filled the air. But amidst that chaos, Mai had a moment to remember as she took the tactical sword from her belt.

It had seemed like a simple mission at first, but the truth was far darker than she had imagined. They had come here to stop a secret global operation that has been running for years, even decades, something the soldiers there were protecting at all costs. Yet the information they had was incomplete. A larger mystery lay behind this confrontation, one they could not ignore.

The attack on the man she had killed, the girl in the study… everything was connected. Everything happening in that garden at that moment went beyond a simple battle for power. It was the end to something much larger and far darker.

Mai pushed that memory to the corner of her mind as she faced a soldier charging directly at her with a blade. His sword clashed against hers. With a fluid movement, Mai deflected the strike and, in a single precise motion, bypassed the soldier’s defense and struck him in the neck, leaving him unconscious on the ground.

As Mai fought, dodging a projectile fired by a soldier, her mind briefly drifted to the memory that had invaded her consciousness. Her eyes flashed for an instant—a fraction of a second sufficient for the memory to seize her completely.

It was the night Sari and Milena had come to her door, seeking feys and espers willing to join a mission. In the north, an unknown group, part of a larger cult, was kidnapping feys and humans too, using their bodies and magical essence to create weapons and summon creatures like the one that had caused the Shinjuku disaster. And the rest… selling them on the black market if their bodies didn’t disintegrate upon death.

Not only was their magic or abilities extracted, but also their physical parts: flesh, bones, organs… an indescribable horror. Secret cults believed that the essence of feys could grant them eternal life, and they were willing to do anything to obtain it.

A commercial agreement that could later expand into something larger was something these groups could not afford to allow.

That cult, the one calling themselves the Star Worshippers, had formed an alliance years earlier with the so-called Octogonus Circle—a radical anti-technology terrorist group that nevertheless used technology to spread fear across the world. Around the turn of the new century, the cult offered their assistance to Octogonus, hoping to push both groups toward their goals. Both the cult and the Octogonus Circle had suffered heavy losses in the final decades of the twentieth century, and mutual aid had seemed like a profitable path forward.

With Octogonus’s support, the cult broadened its reach, weaving a network capable of supplying the most grotesque desires to anyone willing to pay for them—maintaining a chain of demand that only continued to grow.

Mai had listened to that account with the same focused coldness she always displayed when dealing with valuable information, but what struck her most was the direct link to the events of 2006—to the attack she had been involved in.

Gladius, the vampire responsible for the chaos of that attack, wasn’t just a mere pawn of the cult. He had been involved with these groups almost from the beginning, plotting to eliminate enemies and eventually climb his way to the top of the R&R pharmaceutical conglomerate, from where he could gain absolute control over major clans, direct the flow of money, and assist the cult’s expansion.

He had supplied the sleeper agents used by Octogonus in their terror attacks, brainwashing them through the cult’s rituals. Those who never realized they were living bombs came from these same circles—manipulated into becoming pieces in a game far larger than anyone had imagined.

Gladius had also been the one who provided the tomes from which the creature in that attack had been summoned. The cult had taken control of several such creatures using their most skilled necromancers.

With the help of Franz Rackhenhell and other interested parties—such as the magnate behind the ZAIEN multinational and a number of powerful fey families whose influence had grown over recent decades—Wishper had secured the budget to carry out a global operation. More magicians, espers, and feys were hired, along with former intelligence officers who were sent across multiple regions to gather scattered information, pulling at the threads one by one.

And so the operation began: an unstoppable offensive that swept first through northern Japan, uncovering scattered clues throughout the winter. Then South Korea and northern China in January and February. Until finally, in March, a group of more than a hundred operatives—Mai among them—slipped into The Separatist Republic of North Korea by sea, carrying standard tactical equipment and weapons Wishper had developed in recent years.

What they found there was something none of them had ever wanted to witness.

The sites they were looking for were close to the coast, which would make certain things easier if they managed to carry out their plan. But what they discovered forced them to reconsider everything.

A slaughterhouse for imprisoned espers and feys.

Their regeneration—normally what kept them alive—had been disabled due to the extreme conditions they were subjected to. Those few who survived the torture did so at the cost of their sanity. And the nightmare didn’t end there.

The slave and prostitution network—selling captives to the highest bidder anywhere in the world—was also there.

The reason was simple: Asia’s protected fey and esper territories were a perfect hunting ground for the cult, capable of supplying an enormous chain of wealthy clients through Octogonus.

Basements where espers and fey were hung upside down, cut open to be drained of blood for a variety of rituals.

It was a market where nothing was wasted—flesh, bones, fat, everything had a use.

In the case of attractive men or women, they were kept alive and well-maintained for sale—but not before being passed through the cult’s hands or through those of the low-ranking workers. Then came the mental conditioning, designed to shatter their minds and render them obedient.

The infiltrators did not act recklessly. They infiltrated quietly, first stealing data and transmitting it to other teams in Europe and the Americas. And only then—once the network was mapped—would the strike come. A simultaneous blow.

They split into two teams, joined by a third sent by a certain alchemist in China. In total, they were roughly one hundred and fifty operatives prepared to strike three separate targets.

The slaughterhouse and factory was the first. A prison complex where a five-hundred-meter section of the facility was to be demolished with explosives, while they fought through more than three hundred workers collaborating with the cult, plus a garrison of about a hundred soldiers and several cult members acting as Plant Chiefs.

The second target was the area where the captives arrived and also were distributed worldwide: the airport, where they would have to face nearly three hundred soldiers and other personnel working on-site, including feys and espers acting as security guards. At least the team sent from China had three young Canglongs for that purpose, as with spring approaching, they were already strong at their station.

At that point, they had missed something.

If they had they arrived days earlier, they could have captured the leaders of the cult’s mid-level branch, but they had already departed for an unknown destination. With some luck, other groups around the world might be able to locate them—whether in southeastern Russia, northern Germany, the Arabian Peninsula, or the other two bases in the Americas, in Peru and the Middle East of the Kingdom, where the main headquarters were believed to be according to the collected data. The information that was being leaked from the network was horrific and reached the kingdom's government and spread to several countries too.

And finally, the third group: the mission brought the team to a mansion. Nearly seven hundred soldiers.

But it was no ordinary mansion. That enormous property belonged to the country’s leader—a man Mai would never have imagined as the true mastermind behind this entire nightmare. Throughout all that time, he had been involved in the cults’ activities and the black market, moving pieces from the shadows and ruling the country with an iron fist for forty years, yet barely showing any signs of aging.

The reason for that last part was obvious. The cult must have supplied him with some elixir to prolong his life. But rumors said that anything involving eternal life or longevity gained through ritual, magic, or alchemy came at a price few were willing to pay. In his case, it could well have been the madness with which he had ruled his country—implementing a policy of isolation toward neighboring nations and the West. It was clear that behind that isolation also lay the trafficking network he had kept secure for so many years, distributing his “products” worldwide.

While the Wishper team took care of the final leaders of that section, including high-ranking regime agents involved, Mai was tasked with entering the operation leader’s office—the president himself. She knew what she had to do. She knew he was responsible for so many lost lives. Without hesitation, she pulled the trigger.

It wasn't the first time she had done so. Her first killing was when Miho was still alive during a case, she had had to use the weapon provided by the Inoue Section when she completed her training to serve as a special consultant. Since then she had pulled the trigger many times.

The first time Mai ever shot someone, it hadn’t been with the intent to kill—not like in combat, not like a soldier or an enemy. It had been a calculated act, though one that had failed. She had executed a serial killer in Tokyo when he had lunged at Miho. At that moment, she had felt relief at protecting Miho, though the man’s face as he choked on his own blood haunted her for months.

The dance between life and death was also part of what could happen in that dangerous line of work.

But in this case, she had done it without remorse. What she hadn’t known, what she had never anticipated, was that in that instant, as the man’s life ended with his fall, the leader’s daughter ran into the office, asking in an innocent voice, “Dad, what’s happening?”

The daughter of the man she had just killed. She had taken a life, and somehow, that little girl’s life had become entwined with her own—unintentionally. She could only wish the best for her.

The sound of explosions and the crash of impacts against the walls shattered the bubble of memories and doubts Mai had been carrying. The roar snapped her out of her trance, and with a swift movement, she refocused on the battle. There was no time to think—not when chaos continued to crumble the surroundings.

Quickly, Mai crouched, dodging a grenade that exploded almost beside her. Dust and smoke filled the air, but she didn’t hesitate. She advanced, ears ringing, with the precision of a predator, swiftly eliminating soldiers in her path with short and swift sword blows until the blade broke. With a swing of her arm, she grabbed a sword on the ground and cut through the air with fury, cutting a soldier who had tried to stop her. Another shot, this time with her magical pistol, brought down one of higher rank heading straight for her, his body crashing violently to the ground.

The soldiers’ screams echoed across the battlefield, but she remained unstoppable, moving like a shadow among the combatants. An explosion to her left sent debris flying towards her again, but even that didn’t stop her. There was no turning back now. No room for doubt.

Suddenly, the sound of an alarm pierced the chaos. A flicker of confusion crossed her thoughts, and then she understood: the alarm one of the infiltrators had deactivated had been reactivated and with that comms too. Military reinforcements were approaching. The mission time was over. Evacuation had to be immediate.

But before she could make a single move, a figure appeared in front of her, cutting the line of fire. Half horse, half female human body, and wings, it looked like a mixture of a pegasus and a centaur, but it wasn't exactly that. An iridescent, helix-shaped horn adorned her forehead. Miria, the green and blonde-haired helicorn fey, appeared like a flash, dressed in tactical gear that blended perfectly with the environment.

“Hold on, Mothy!” ordered Miria, with her voice firm and quick.

Mai didn’t speak—there was no time. Without hesitation, she leapt onto Miria’s back, gripping the vest of her armor tightly. No sooner had she touched the fey’s back than Miria surged forward, running at supernatural speed, carrying Mai out of the chaos of the battle in mere seconds.

Miria was the only Fey Mai knew who could switch between four different forms, so her equipment also had to be customized so that her fighting style could switch between those forms.

Miria’s wings beat forcefully, propelling Mai with a gust of air that made the wind slice across her face as they sped away from the massacre.

Around them, the rest of the team also began to escape. Some feys, using the speed of their legs, vanished in the blink of an eye. Others transformed into shadows, dissipating into smoke through magic, leaving only the trace of their energy behind. A couple of espers took advantage of the chaos to commandeer vehicles, jumping into cars as if it had been planned all along.

The terrain became a succession of distorted images, explosions lighting up the night, gunfire slicing through the air. Mai could hear in the distance the screams of soldiers trying to reorganize to cut off the escape routes, but it didn’t matter. Miria moved with inhuman skill, zigzagging between obstacles, avoiding the bullets flying around them.

Mai held tightly to the helicorn fey, her body tense with adrenaline, muscles primed for anything. Reinforcements were already close. If they didn’t escape now, everything they had fought for would fall in minutes. There was no time to look back. Yet deep in her mind, she knew this wasn’t the end. Something else, something far larger, awaited them on the horizon if they failed to capture the leaders wherever they were.

“Hold on to my chest, I'm going to change!”

Mai obeyed and changed her position to hold on to Miria's waist as Miria changed her form. The horse form disappeared and the wings now flapped against Mai's cheek as they appeared on Miria's back.

“Are you okay?”

“Yes! Let's go!”

And as the sound of sirens approached, Mai could only wonder if this chaos, this mission, would lead them back to a darkness that neither she nor her companions could avoid.

As news arrived through comms that the other two teams had achieved their objectives and even escaped with four dozen prisoners, Mai at least breathed a sigh of relief.

Half an hour later, they were moving away from the coasts of the Republic of North Korea.

Their leader, Han Il-sung, had just died as a result of a coup, according to the news that would be published in the morning.

***

Night had fallen over a calm sea, but in her mind, it was still chaos. She had taken several lives in one night—a father, someone who had caused more damage than she could imagine, yet someone who had once been human. Someone who had a daughter, someone who, despite being evil, still had a story to tell.

Mai leaned against the wall near a door, motionless, listening to the echoes of the conversation inside. She didn’t need to be in the room. Adrenaline prevented her from deactivating her antennae, allowing her to catch everything happening around her, reverberating through the ship’s metal. The captured fey, hands cuffed and covered in wounds, seemed to mock his captors. His voice was defiant, even as torture became apparent in the air. The screams of the interrogators and the sound of blows reached her through the door, but the man maintained a challenging attitude.

“Come on, tell me what you want to know! I don’t know anything! I saw nothing, I only know that… I was in the wrong place at the wrong time!” His laughter was bitter, as if nothing mattered anymore. “What I did… it was just a job.”

The shouting intensified for a moment, and then a fey interrogator, his face reflecting years of suffering and rage, erupted. “Job?! Job?! Everything was taken from you, they took your daughter, they stole your life, and you still call it a job! You simply sold yourself to the killers.” The words filled the room, and Mai felt herself tense, the interrogator’s hatred palpable in his tone.

Through the door, Mai could hear the captured fey continue to taunt them, with his voice full of mockery and arrogance. He was definitely insane.

“And what are you going to do? Kill me? I’m not the only one who knows what’s happened. You’re guilty too. You, the heroes, the ones who call us monsters. But in the end, it’s all the same game. Who cares in this rotten world which side you’re on? In the end, it’s all about survival!” A sound of spitting echoed.

Then another voice spoke, cold and calculated. “Have you heard of Project AFT?”

The interrogated fey immediately fell silent. Mai felt it. Something in his tone had changed.

“AFT… Project AFT…” he repeated weakly, in the tone of a bad actor.

The interrogator who had been beating him stared intently, his eyes filled with fury. “And what do you know about the feys used in that project? What do you know about those who disappeared? What do you know about the Muryan Syndrome? Does it have anything to do with it?”

“I don’t know anything about that… I don’t know anything else! I was just following orders!”

The fey interrogator said nothing. He simply stepped forward, the cold expression on his face saying it all.

Mai heard a woman’s voice in the room, calm, as if nothing else mattered. “Nerea,” said the interrogator’s voice, and immediately Mai heard the sound of slow, heavy footsteps, the creak of doors opening, and then the screams. Piercing, gut-wrenching screams.

“Leave him! We need him alive! Jinat and the others will interrogate him more later.”

Mai stepped back, moving away from the door. The sound of the screams still reached her as she made her way to the deck, but fortunately, the roar of the waves and the sea breeze offered at least some calm.

She stopped at the edge of the deck, looking out at the vast sea stretching to the horizon. The wind struck her face, and for a moment, Mai felt as if she could escape everything.

She was tired—she couldn’t deny it.

She gazed up at the stars in the night sky, and in that silence, a single thought surfaced in her mind. Maybe it was time to return to Tokyo. It was time to leave this mission behind. To leave behind everything she had done, even knowing that the truth would not disappear. That battle could very well end here.
