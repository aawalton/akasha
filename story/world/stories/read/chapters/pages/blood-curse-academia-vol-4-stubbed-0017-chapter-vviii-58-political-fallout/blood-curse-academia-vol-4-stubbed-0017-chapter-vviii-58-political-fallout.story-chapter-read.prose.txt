Chapter V.VIII (5.8) - Political Fallout

“As it is topical, today we will be discussing the political fallout seen throughout Tross as a result of the country’s recent schism,” Professor Krimpit’s cold voice spoke at the front of the lecturehall.

Despite saving his life during the Drama Club’s performance, Krimpit did not appear any better inclined towards Kizu this quarter. Well, maybe he treated him slightly better. The professor displayed a passive apathy towards him now, which was preferable to the open disdain from previous class sessions.

Krimpit adjusted his spectacles as he frowned down at his pupils. Today he wore a maroon bow tie along with his usual professor’s uniform, stylized a tweed brown. Kizu recognized it as the very same uniform the man had worn the night of Necro’s attack. When he looked closely, he could spot the meticulously precise stitching along the stretches of fabric where it had been torn. Kizu wondered if the professor himself had stitched it or if he'd asked someone to assist with the repairs. For some reason, sewing seemed like a skill well within Professor Krimpit’s capabilities.

Then he started to wonder about how he might go about magically repairing split cloth. Obviously, temporal spells were off the table. That branch of spellcraft had been forgotten to history. But could an enchanted sewing needle stitch with more precision than a human hand? Or was there a spell for controlling the fibers of threads themselves?

Only after he refocused on Krimpit’s lesson about trading route disruption and political fallout did Kizu realize the irony of him wondering about magical fabric. His parents were silk merchants. If he had any questions, he could ask them about sewing.

As Kizu shook off his internal tangential thoughts, he considered exactly the consequences of what Professor Krimpit was explaining to the class about Tross. A strife between the nation’s capital and the major port cities had been building for the last decade, ever since a resource allocation and taxation effort after the Ooze invasion to the south. Then further stress as the Dragon invasion sent tens of thousands of Tainted refugees to the nation’s west coast.

Kizu was just about at a conclusion when the professor spelled it out for them all.

“While conjecture in many respects, I believe it prudent for all to understand that the Tross Civil War has all but begun. Port Kallis and the surrounding towns in particular are using this physical divide in the nation as a means in which to politically distance themselves from the main body of power and legislation without Tross having the capabilities to enforce their control over them.

A student raised a hand. A rare sight in the Politics F class. Only then did Kizu realize that every single student in the lecture hall was paying attention to Professor Krimpit’s words.

“Yes.” Krimpit waved a hand in the student’s direction. He didn’t call on his name, likely having never bothered to learn the names of those in a class that he’d more than once publicly declared ‘worthless degenerates.’

“Doesn’t Tross have military members based on the coast?” the student asked. “I know there’s a major outpost in the west to watch over the sea towards Edgeland.”

“There are a few outposts. However the main body of Tross’ loyalists are stationed along the south eastern coast, near Hon. Historically, they’ve been more worried about invasion from the Hon Empire than the peoples of Edgeland. And with good reason. As we discussed in history class last semester. The minority of you who bothered to attend with open ears will know that there is a long pattern of skirmishes in the strait between the nations that stretches back thousands of years. In contrast, Edgeland has been largely consumed in internal struggles for power and only unified recently. While yes, there have been many scouts placed to watch the movements of the Dragons, Tross still relied on its infrastructure in the east and the network of spatial transportation and World Dungeon access designed to move troops across the nation quickly and efficiently. All of which is now rendered useless due to the rash decisions and actions of the Hon Emperor.”

“So they don’t have enough people on the outside to squash a rebellion?” the student asked.

“That is mere speculation. Tross does not announce its troop positions or numbers to the general public. Only a general or individual in a higher station in the Tross military could supply reliable information on that subject.”

“But the diviners will know, right?” another student spoke up.

“But they definitely have their own diviners to counter intelligence gathering,” the first student said.

Professor Krimpit’s frown deepened at the pattern of student outbursts. Gone were the raised hands. He waited for silence to once again fall on the class before continuing his lecture. When he spoke again, his tone was colder than before.

“By the end of the year, I expect the loyalists to the Tross government to have been rounded up and either put to death or put under strict security. The future is far messier than the clarity of the past. However, they always echo one another. Perhaps this time will be different, though I believe it unlikely.”

Kizu nodded along, considering the situation. There was also the political stand off between Tross and Hon to consider. Would they work together while sealed off from the rest of the world? Or would there be a struggle for power and control while they were isolated? More than ever, Kizu wished he knew about the political powers across the known world. Though, he supposed modern political knowledge was becoming more outdated by the day as the Dragons destroyed and seized control of the remains of the majority of the governments in Edgeland. One way or another, in a matter of a year, the entire known world would be tossed on its head.

“Your bath water is still working,” Ione said.

Kizu turned his head to face her. Unlike every other Politics F class, Ione was alert and actually listening to Krimpit’s lesson.

“My bath water?” Kizu asked.

“Krimpit says that all transportation in and out of Hon is cut off. But your bath water is still running. I took a bath just yesterday on Owl’s Respite and it was the same as ever.”

“You took a bath on…actually, that doesn’t matter.” Despite his words, Kizu was wracking his mind about how Ione would have gotten on the boat without him knowing. Definitely not through the portal in Taroe’s home. He’d had it deactivated and she had no spatial spell knowledge. She must have used a summoned creature to ferry her over to the ship. But that was a lot of effort for just a bath.

“My dorm is having plumbing issues,” Ione explained. “Didn’t think you’d care.”

“No, no, you’re welcome to bathe anytime, anywhere. I mean, anywhere that I have ownership of. And when other people aren’t in the bath….” Kizu verbally floundered as his mind stuttered on the image. And his flustered voice was slightly louder than he intended, attracting weird looks. Thankfully Krimpit hadn’t seemed to notice yet.

His face burning red, Kizu lowered his voice to address her initial query. “I need to do more research on the spatial limitations of my portal, but I believe that because I left it active the day Hon cut itself off, it remained functioning.”

“Interesting. Are there others like it?”

Kizu slowly shook his head. “I doubt it. Most people don’t bother with portals that size. It was an experiment for me. I kept it small enough that I could leave it active and just do maintenance on the portal’s enchantment wards every few days. That way other people, like you, could come use the bath without needing me to activate it every time.”

“You realize how valuable that is, right? You have access to the sole known link to Hon. Potential for a smuggling empire.”

Kizu hadn’t really considered that option. It was a portal to the pipes of a ghost town. Not especially accessible for the average black-market merchant. Though…he was willing to bet his father might be over the moon if he learned of Kizu’s little portal.

Something to consider for the future. He wrote down a note for himself on a scrap of parchment, reminding him to discuss it with Aoi. If she thought it was important and worth potentially exposing the undead inn over, she could send a message to Emperor Sasaki about the portal. Sadly, they had no means in which to directly contact the ghost innkeeper in the town. But Aoi, with her vast political knowledge, could probably make a better judgement call than him on the matter.

He let the portal fade to the back of his mind as he refocused on Professor Krimpit’s lecture on the current political tensions. Every day the world felt just a smidge more dangerous and on the brink of chaos. But, for now, Shinzou Academy remained a safe haven.
