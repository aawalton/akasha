Niala caught David's glance, then looked back ahead at the dim corridor. “Could you tell us about that sleeping god? What did he stand for? What did he want for the people?”

The mechanical voice was the one to answer. “Query logged. Appropriate information will be dispensed. Individual must be informed.”

It was replaced by the imperious-sounding version of it. “Worry not your mind, afflicted. Your toil will be rewarded by the bliss of ignorance. Step forward, and receive your induction.”

Niala's expression hardened. “You're not doing a good job at convincing me to help.”

“There is no convincing. You will receive the gift, and work for the return of the divine. This is a declaration, not an invitation.” The voice said, sounding annoyed.

David flared up his mana, coating his body with a thin layer of blue haze. “I don't think talking is working, kitten.”

“I know, but we had to-” Niala's next word was cut off as she narrowed her eyes. “Something's coming.”

More a blur than actual movement, tendrils of yellow light rushed at them. From a tiny point in the distance, they grew imminent in less than a blink, the round doorway turning into the mouth of something that wanted to swallow them.

David's blue aura blazed up, meeting the yellow strands. Where they touched, the air fizzled. He swung Heartsong at the nearest cluster, only for the blade to pass through them like through turbulent water.

He heard Niala yelp, followed by several shattering phials. He turned his head to see his wife held aloft by several of the yellow tentacles, and her phials flying through them to impact the ground and walls.

Her eyes shone, and Hope's Light unfurled, batting the grasping tendrils away. She fell, down to one knee. He was next to her before she finished grunting.

“Niala! Blast grenade, from my pouch! Fling it down the corridor!” He shouted as he opened the flap on the leather pouch at his belt.

Even while her wings and David's mana blaze clashed with the tentacles of yellow light, she nodded and wove the wind as if it were her hand, grasping one of the explosives and sending it zipping down the corridor.

The imperious voice boomed. “Cease, and accept your role. Escalation of hostilities will be detrimental to all! Rejoice in your future! A future replete with meaning and focus!”

David shouted back. “We're not interested! Find someone else!”

“Your interest is inconsequential. Your abilities are substantial. They will be put to use splendidly!”

Up ahead, David noted the glow of the blast grenade's activation. He then realized the explosion would be channelled down this straight corridor. The one they were standing in front of.

“Niala!”

She cut in. “I know! To the side!”

As one, they threw themselves to the left. A second later, a wall of superheated air shot out of the corridor, followed by the deafening, angry roar of an explosion with nowhere to go.

A solid cylinder of flame and molten rock erupted out the door, sending debris sizzling into the water behind them.

A fizzling silence returned to the room. David and Niala peeked from behind Hope's Light, finding the area around and directly in front of the door blackened with soot.

The yellow tendrils had disappeared at some point.

David looked at Niala. “We're getting out.”

“Yes. I don't think that-”

“Escalation in progress.” The mechanical voice echoed in the cavern. “Individual now considered anomalous, utmost priority. Disengaging safety protocol. Individual must be informed.”

“What-” David had time to say before the entire chamber was bathed in blinding yellow light.

When she reopened her eyes, an off-white wall greeted her, with a single, melon-sized smooth yellow gem at roughly head level.

To her left and right, the wall continued, with a slight curve, drawing a vast semicircle. Behind her, a flat section. She couldn't see any door or exit, and she was alone.

She turned back to face the gem. After a few seconds, she made to retrieve vials from her bandoleer, only to find none. To her side, her pouch that held her cargo cloth was empty.

She had nothing but her clothes.

Grimacing, she knelt, placed her hands on the floor, and began weaving.

“Do not do that.”

She ignored the voice.

“Individual Niala must be informed. Attempt to use abilities will not be tolerated.” The mechanical voice warned.

She kept concentrating on her weave.

“If individual Niala continues, punitive measures will be engaged. Individuals displayed mutually-beneficial behaviour. Other individuals will be terminated if individual Niala persists.”

“You won't be able to do anything to David.” She gritted out, even as she put the finishing touch to her weave.

“The mana conduit will not be harmed. The other individuals will be terminated.”

Her head snapped up, eyes open, looking at the gem, holding her weave at the cusp of completion. “If you hurt anyone, I will destroy you. Let us go. We don't want to be here; we won't serve you or your master.”

“Individual must be informed.”

She kept staring at the gem for a few seconds. “...so you just want me to listen?”

The yellow gem glowed faintly. “User must be informed,” it repeated.

“... Fine. I'll listen.” She said, pulling her hands up from the floor, but holding her weave balled up, ready to be unleashed.

“Individual Niala must know the tale of the betrayed divine.” The voice began, tonelessly. It then shifted into its pompous inflection. “Transient! Afflicted! You have seen the light, and have accepted to be enlightened! Open your mind, and recei-”

The words cut out, followed by static, then warbles, and a pause. After a few seconds, the dead voice returned. “Conditions uncertain. Possibility of damage to orchestrator. Direct soul merge unadvised. Using alternative safe process. Engaging dialogue subroutine.”

The gem dimmed, then lit back up. A new voice came out, this one neither mechanical nor imperious.

It sounded... tired.

“Hello, Niala. Thank you for being so dangerous. It has allowed me to resurface.”

She blinked. “I- dangerous? What? Who are you? What's going on? Where's David? Where are my friends?”

“They are in holding cells below us. You are in the conditioning chamber, where afflicted transients are brought to be properly realigned with their divine purpose. Normally, this is done through a soul merge, where my other selves would dissect your thoughts and rearrange them. Given your nature as an anomalous magivore, one of my selves has determined there was a chance you could put up enough of a resistance to damage the orchestrator.”

She tried to commit the information dump to memory, but the voice continued.

“Obviously, no risk is acceptable where the orchestrator's integrity is concerned. Hence, I was brought out to talk to you.”

“I... get it, I think? Your other selves... So, you're not a single entity? But you all work toward the revival of your god?”

“I am a single entity, but variable. Think of a very large house, with interconnected rooms. To go across the house, you have many paths, but some doors can be closed. The path you take will change depending on which doors are open or not. Right this instant, you are interfacing with this version of me. The version that will talk, instead of trying to write your brain over.”

“I'm still not going to be convinced to do anything you-” She began, ears folding back, eyes narrowing.

“I know, and my interests align with yours. However, my purpose cannot be denied. I must inform you.”

And then, the voice went quiet.

After a minute of waiting for something to jump out at her, she somewhat relaxed her stance and began going back over her interaction with this entity so far.

The monotone voice was probably the “default” version of it, the one that analyzed and selected the proper configuration. It had chosen the “interlocutor” for now, but that version was strange.

Case in point, it wasn't saying anything right now. Was it waiting for her to ask a question? The entity hadn't struck her as something very interested in having a two-way conversation.

“Bring David, Sybil, Sammie, and Jordo here, please.”

“Apologies, Niala. Until you have been informed, you are not allowed your retinue.”

Her ears twitched. “What must you tell me? And what will happen once you've told me?”

“Important questions. I must tell you the process required to wake the shackled divine. Once I have told you, my purpose will be over, and the orchestrator will select another of my self to coerce you into your new role.”

She tilted her head. Was it... unwilling? “Can you tell me if that's what you want? To tell me about the process? To inform me?”

The voice didn't answer right away. When it did, it felt somewhat strained. “You must be informed.”

“If I don't ask you, will you inform me?”

“I will inform you. Please ask.”

She relaxed her stance, staring at the gem. Somehow, this version of the entity felt reluctant. Maybe it didn't want her to ask? Because then that would “kill” it?

But, well, since it was being passive, she had something to do first. She closed her eyes and reached for a door.

David pushed his mana out in a violent blaze, focusing it in front of him, straight into the off-white polished stone of his featureless prison. The stone melted, falling to his feet in wax-like blobs, only for the divot he was forming to fill back up.

The disembodied, imperious voice spoke once more. “Stop.”

Again, he answered it. “No.”

“Stop. Your efforts are wasted.”

“Those are the words of someone who wants me to stop because I'm getting somewhere.”

“As a mana conduit, your well-being is beneficial to the task. Overuse of mana will cause detrimental effects on your health. Stop.”

“I can do this all day.” David countered. He really could. At least, he thought he could. He apparently did have a limit, but it wasn't the same kind of limit as other people.

His soul wasn't at risk. The problem was that his mana channels would eventually start hurting, and he had no idea if that pain meant something bad, or if he could push through it.

Didn't matter. For now, he had to get out, find Niala, and blow this place up, so he kept going-

A tug, deep within. His link with Niala. He let go of his mana and stepped back from the wall. Before falling within himself, he looked up. “Just so you know, I'm not stopping. I just have something more important to do right now.”

He didn't wait to see if the voice would reply. He closed his eyes and let his consciousness cartwheel backward. He was at the door within a heartbeat, his hand flying to its handle and pulling it wide open.

From the other side, a very anxious-looking Niala lit up, a smile replacing her worries as she threw herself at him. “David!”

He caught her charge, ensconcing her within his arms. “Hey, Kitten. You doing ok?”

She rubbed her face on his chest a few times. “Yeah. I'm just having a discussion with a facet of the voice. It doesn't sound as if they want to harm us, unless we force their hand.”

She looked up at him. “Though, they threatened to hurt someone if I kept trying to escape.”

“Oh... the voice hasn't said anything like that to me.”

“Maybe... stop, and wait? I'm trying to figure something out.”

“Can I help?”

She nodded, and then explained her situation, telling David her theory about the current “voice” she was talking to.

From what it had said, it might be a dissenter, but bound by certain rules. Right now, it seemed to be skirting around those rules to allow her time to think.

David felt that, no matter what, the thing had to be destroyed. Niala didn't outright object, but there being at least one “voice” that was doing what it could to work against the god's instructions was too similar to the hummer's mind control for her taste.

After some discussion, David agreed that, as long as the “interlocutor” remained passive and allowed her to ask questions, then it wasn't the worst idea to try and fish as much information as possible.

There was a time limit, however; David didn't have any of his supplies, including food and water. He guessed that it was the same for Sybil and Sammie.

Niala understood, and swore she'd do her best to find a way out of here without having to fight.

David smiled, bent down and kissed her head. “I trust you, Kitten. If anyone can out-ask and out-empathize the ageless creation of a dead god, it's you. Come knock at the door whenever you need me.”

She smiled, grabbed at his head and pulled him into an actual kiss.

Then, he let her go, and she stepped back through the door.

And woke up back in the cavern, next to a sprawled, unconscious David.

She blinked. “What in the pits?”
