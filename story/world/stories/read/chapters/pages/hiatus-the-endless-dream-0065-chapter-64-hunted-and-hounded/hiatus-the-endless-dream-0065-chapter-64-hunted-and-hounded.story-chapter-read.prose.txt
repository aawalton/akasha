Dreamlight - Carlson

Carlson’s bones ache. The soles of his boots drink deeply from the ruptured blisters on the man’s heels. The blood would dry later, when he took them off, leaving behind a corrosive, ever-darkening stain on the leather. Far past the point of yawning, Carlson feels his eyes droop. He stumbles forward along the abandoned cobbled streets towards the guild. Full flower beds remain in windows like an untouched dream. He slaps himself hard with a gauntleted hand, drawing yet more blood. His guild-mates always said he had more strength than his constitution could keep up with. They had been teasing him back then, but as the memory echoed, he couldn’t help but regret his past stat allocations. His eyes flick open as his face comes level with the supports of a market stall. He stares at the wooden beam, thinner than his forearm, and bile rushes out of his stomach, marring the previously untouched street. He falls backward, the metal armor scraping against stone as he lands hard. He shivers, knowing how close to endless death he had come. The market stall had saved him. Not so for its owner.

Sitting in a chair, overseeing rows of crops, is a tall, burly man. A simple straw farmer's hat adorns him. His overalls rise and fall in a hypnotic rhythmic pattern. He doesn’t lift his sword to end the sleeping man; the death of those afflicted invites only disaster. Carlson feels his eyes begin to fall again, and he crawls back, practically pulling out paving stones as he goes. His breath is harsh and hoarse. He turns his head and stares at the sun, basking in its warm glow and painful rays boring into his eyes. It’s all he can do to stay awake. He walks sideways, blinding himself until he reaches the guildhall.

Three people are in the guildhall. Carlson makes four. Minerva is doing even worse than him, but only just. She shows it more. The bags under her eyes, the sluggish, zombie-like way her head turns to the door. She doesn’t even prepare a spell or reach for a weapon in her exhaustion. An abomination could’ve barreled through the door, and she’d have had the same reaction, slight surprise.

“Kill me,” she begs upon laying eyes on Carlson, “please kill me.”

The armored paladin is not surprised to hear this. He was beat to the request by only a moment, but he could not bring himself to pull rank, not to Minerva. He agrees, and with a smile, she lays her head onto the wooden table. Carlson raises his sword. The blow is messy. He cleaves into the back of her skull instead of her neck. Her body flails and spasms in death throes for six seconds before she dies. The others do not react to this. If anything, they appear jealous.

Anything to not fall asleep. If that means continually killing and resurrecting one another for the meager amount of rest such a thing imparts, so be it.

After a long silence, a raven-haired man with salt-laden hair speaks up, "How much longer till port?"

He directs the question at a woman studying maps across the table, Cammie. She withers slightly, not at the question or even the man, but at just being addressed at the moment. "Little over a week. We're three-fourths of the way there."

They'd been doing this for almost a month. Ever since the curse grew. People fell asleep and never woke up. Not even resurrection would drag their consciousness back. The curse had been heard of before, but never this powerful and never on a scale like this. Every city and town they traveled to was full of nothing but sleeping bodies.

Carlson’s eyes lurch open at the barely audible pop that echoes down the barren street. In normal times, the sound may have been dampened or overwhelmed by others, even a footstep, but in the absence of it all, the noise deafens the paladin and wakes him from a growing stupor.

The noise is unmistakable. Someone sleeping had just died.

He stands at attention, carefully bringing himself to the door. With the sword out in front of him, he opens the door to the street and peeks out. Just over the rooftop, he can see a discolored tendril descend down a chimney and then-

Pop

Another one a ways down and Carlson pales. One abomination is hard enough to deal with in his semi-delirious state, much less two.

Pop

Pop

Pop

Carlson stills. Everyone’s eyes widen as the number of harsh, whistling sounds grows at an increasing rate. They realize what’s happening simultaneously: the abominations are killing others in the throes of the curse and creating more abominations.

“Safe room. Every guild has one.” Cammie quietly hisses out. There is no argument. Carlson gathers Minerva’s body, and they all descend.

It’s dark in the safe room. They haven't allowed themselves to sit in darkness after the first week, when they lost another of their group to the curse. A simple light spell rectifies the lack of sight, but it doesn't last with the energy drain. There are no torches to light, and not enough air even if there were. The room is bare and uninteresting. There are supplies and a deck of cards, but besides that, not much to do. Carlson is forced to dispel the magic after a half hour. After an hour of struggling to stay awake, Carlson suggests that they try to cut a path through, as they will surely not make it through the night down in the safe room. There is no response. Everyone has fallen asleep.

He revives Minverva.

“Why am I blind?” is the first words out of her mouth.

Carlson laughs at that, despite the situation. “I wish that was the bad news I had for you. We’re in a safe room. Barrow and the others succumbed to the curse.”

Minerva hisses out a curse and collapses against the wall, “It was just a matter of time. In truth. I think this is the end. I don’t think there’s a place not affected by this curse.”

“Conjecture?” Carlson questions, “From you?”

“Oh, my Paladin. Can’t I reminiscence here at the end?” A note of acceptance in her tone.

“No, because it’s not the end. I refuse.”

Minerva leans forward at that, although its dark so the interest is lost on Carlson. “How? Or do you get my hopes up for nothing?”

“It’s a secret. One I didn’t intend to tell, but there’s little point in keeping it quiet now. The curse has a will.”

“Huh?” Minverva questioned blankly.

“The curse has existed for years, but was smaller in scale. It only affected two, maybe three people a year. Those people never survived for more than a year, but they woke up occasionally, and when they died they didn’t turn into monsters. That parts new.”

“How do you know it has a will?”

“Because it asks those affected by it to do something in their sleep. It summons away the soul and gives it tasks to complete and if completed the cursed wakes up. I don’t know what’s different now or why things changed, but I’m hoping that part remains.”

“So you’re just hoping. Should’ve expected that from you.”

I am not so sure if I am hopeful or desperate here.”

She sighs, “Only one way then. Forward. Good riddance. I could use the rest.”

Carlson chuckles. Within a minute, Minerva is asleep. Despite his bodies attempts for the past few days Carlson does not sleep right away. He tries, yet still finds he can’t. Thoughts race too fast in his head, warring with doubt and fear. He’s still having those thoughts an hour later when he finally fades.

He wakes up, refreshed. Overhead is a starry, multicolored sky, broken up by jagged, pure white lines.

Moonlight - Dorian

We sat in Joey’s duplex. Except it wasn’t really Joey’s duplex, just a copy that the Dream made. Joey was set up by an oversized window, staring up at the sky like it might snatch him up if he looked away. Maerin was somewhere doing something.

“It’s not going anywhere you know,” I tried to quip.

“Huh?” The word was more like a noise that escaped Joey’s mouth than an actual question, and he continued a moment later, “It looks broken. I don’t like it.”

I raised an eyebrow, but looked out. I often described the sight as a shattered sky, so some part of me recognized what Joey felt. I didn’t really get it, though. I thought it looked rather beautiful. “There’s a lot of stuff you aren’t going to like here. The System talk to you yet?”

“Nah,” Joey said, “I can pull up my stats and stuff like you said, but nothing from this Ananke you told me about.”

I didn’t know whether to breathe a sigh of relief or curse at that. It seemed like my introduction and oversight by Ananke really had been special. It made sense with the context that my brother and she were … colleagues? The thought still didn’t sit right with me.

Joey spoke up again, “So what now?”

“Now we look for the others that came over from the Dream. There’s four of them including her. Big, one eyed guy named Krevith. Gladiator type. Lithe, tan woman, she was wearing pelts last time I saw her. Name’s Paltra. The last two are … less friendly or reliable, but could be helpful. One’s a parasite inhabiting the body of a woman named Illia and the other is Alder. Bad dude. Probably won’t be around for too much longer.”

“Did you say Illia?” Joey’s voice broke, and he held his head in his hands, finally breaking eye contact with the sky.

“Yeah, wh—“ the memory hit me again. Illia. She was at the diner. She had woken me up in the hammock. Except. That wasn’t her. Another replacement. I thought about the last member of our little group, Alexi. I frowned. Damn, why couldn’t he have gotten replaced instead? I considered the thought and shrugged. I genuinely only put up with the guy because of everyone else. It was cruel, but if someone were to be replaced, he would rather it have been Alexi. It would have made dealing with Alder a lot simpler, too. “Damn,” I continued, “well, that sucks.”

Silence stretched between us for a time before Joey spoke, “It bothers me more that they’re not here more than these other people took their spot. They’re just dead right?”

My heart seized. I didn’t want to think about it. The thoughts kept swirling, and I tried to release some of that building panic, “I think it’s more non-existence. Can’t kill something that just isn’t there.”

“That’s the same thing. Either way its the same. Everything they could’ve been is just … gone and no one would have even mourned them. I … I still don’t think I’m mourning them the way I should. Because when I look at Maerin that’s who I see and all the memories that go along with it.”

I just nodded lamely. I didn’t expect Joey to bounce back as fast as he did, and now I understand why, in part, he had. He was still in disbelief. I wanted to chide myself for letting him make a decision this important while in such a state, but I knew better, at least with Joey. The man hadn’t been unsure of a single decision in his life. “Yeah, shit’s fucked.”

Joey burst out laughing. He wiped a tear from his eye. A few seconds later, “Gods, your bedside manner sucks, Dorian.”

“Yeah?” I asked.

“Yeah,” he confirmed.

“Alright, well, let’s get moving, harder to wallow while being active. Time to find my strays and then try to figure out how to expel all the Instances from our world.” I pushed, standing and heading for the exit. Joey followed me, and we quickly found Maerin fixing up a car.

She tossed Joey the keys as we approached. “Should be good to drive?”

I stared incredulously between the car and Maerin. I was really questioning the level of technology in her world. “Does your world even have cars?” I asked.

“Yep,” Maerin answered.

“You know how to fix them?” I pressed.

A touch too long of silence, “yeah,” she confirmed.

I started to doubt her words stronger and then stopped myself. I cycled through thoughts that it was entirely plausible that, in fact, there were so many ways it was possible, it’d be silly to think it wasn’t possible. Because if she was lying, but used belief to fix or get the car running, and I started interfering with that, even passively, with my own Presence, the car could break down mid-drive. Safe to say I didn’t want that to happen. So I pretended to believe.

“Neat, thanks b- Maerin,” Joey said before unlocking the car and sliding in.

Maerin gave me a look and flicked her eyes to the passenger seat. I took the hint and got in the front with Joey. “The car will only be useful for so long. After a point the Dream starts getting a little nebulous in its landscapes. The further we travel from here, the more true that gets,” I said.

“That’s fine. Should be true for the others then. So if we start getting to those weird places, we know we’ve gone too far.

Joey started up the car and began driving. He flicked on the radio shortly after, and the Beatles started playing. I frowned, not quite understanding how we were getting radio in the Dream when the song began to break down into Static.

I caught Maerin, who had been gently bobbing her head along to the new music, glaring at me. Despite her expression, she managed to fake a level of cheeriness in her voice, “Could you fix that Dorian?”

I sighed. Yeah, yeah. My disbelief in things was getting in the way. Still, this was good practice.

I began thinking about the radio and about the Dream. How could it do that? Fuck it, there were a thousand different ways. Let’s just pretend that it taps into a spot in our world to get radio stations. Surprisingly, the song came back into focus.

Joey turned his head to me to ask a question. It ended up being a mistake as the moment he looked away, someone ran out of an alleyway.

“BRAKE!” I screamed.

To Joey’s credit, he immediately stomped on the gas. It wasn’t enough. We were going to hit the person. I bit down on the thought as stupid. I had power now; we’d hit the person if nothing was done, but there was a possibility here. I opted for a simple one. I yanked the jaywalker up into the air, seven feet off the ground. The car slid another fifteen feet after the brakes were hit. I softly placed the stranger, a woman in robes, onto the ground. Her eyes were wide and confused as she looked at us. I could see the questions rapidly flick through her tongue before she settled on a simple one, “Hello, my name is Minerva. I’m looking for some people. Would you have happened to see them?”
