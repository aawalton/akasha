I ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GUILD THERAPIST

Chapter 11 – The Guild’s Worst Field Trip

ARC II: THE WORLD OUTSIDE THE CAMP

SYNOPSIS:

The quest notice had been pinned to the guild board for three days before anyone told Livia about it.

This was, she was beginning to understand, how Bloodbath & Beyond operated: decisions were made, logistics were arranged, and then she was informed approximately forty-five seconds before implementation. It was not malicious. It was simply how people who had been operating as a self-sufficient unit for years integrated a new member who technically had no combat function.

The notice read:

[QUEST: WHISPERING CAVERN CLEARANCE]

Difficulty: B-Rank. Recommended Party: 5-8 combat-class players.

Objective: Clear dungeon of hostile entities. Retrieve guild contract from floor 3.

Note: Recent reports indicate anomalous boss behavior. Definition of anomalous: see floor 3.

“Anomalous boss behavior,” Livia read aloud. “What does that mean?”

“Nobody knows,” Miles said, from behind her, in the tone of a man who found this mildly interesting rather than concerning. “That’s why we’re going.”

“We,” Livia repeated. “Including me.”

“You’re part of the guild.”

“I have zero combat stats.”

“We know,” Miles said. “That’s why we’re going with you. Not sending you.”

This is a meaningful distinction, Livia thought. I am choosing to believe it is a meaningful distinction.

[Achievement Unlocked: First Field Trip. +5 Adventure Points (Against Your Better Judgment)]

Whispering Cavern was a three-hour walk from the guild base camp, which meant Livia had three hours to observe the world outside the camp for the first time.

It was more varied than she expected.

The MMORPG landscape unfolded like something assembled by a committee that had disagreed on everything: rolling green hills that transitioned without warning into crystallized salt flats, which gave way to a forest where the trees glowed faintly blue and shed light instead of leaves. Players moved through it with the casual ease of people who had long since stopped finding any of it remarkable. An NPC shepherd guided a flock of something that was definitely not sheep across the salt flats. A dragon—not boss-level, just a regular-size dragon—sat near a roadside signpost eating what appeared to be a meat pie.

Livia wrote in her notepad: World is larger than I thought. NPC shepherd seems content. Dragon eating pie. Note: is the pie a special item or just pie.

[System Note: The dragon is an NPC. The pie is just pie. The shepherd is also an NPC, but he has opinions about it.]

The shepherd has opinions about the pie, Livia thought. I am going to come back to that.

Bron walked beside her, armor clanking rhythmically, plushie tucked under one arm, narrating the landscape with the enthusiasm of someone who had traveled it many times but still found it delightful.

“That’s the Silvermarsh,” he said, gesturing at a distant shimmer. “Good fishing. Bad fish. The fish there are aggressive.”

“Aggressive how?”

“They form raiding parties.”

Livia wrote that down.

Nyx walked behind her, flashlight in hand despite the afternoon sun, its pink beam washing out in daylight but present regardless. Phina glided alongside with Glimmer on her shoulder, delivering an ongoing verse commentary about the landscape. Alaric had already started a betting pool about how many floors they would clear before someone cried.

“My money’s on floor two,” he announced. “Bron always gets emotional near dark corridors.”

“That was one time,” Bron said.

Miles walked at the front, not talking, scanning. Livia’s Empathic Insight ran its usual background read: composed, alert, that familiar locked quality she had started to map. She noted in her margin: He walked at the front the entire way. Did not look back once. But slowed his pace whenever I fell behind.

Filed.

Whispering Cavern did not look like a dungeon entrance.

Most dungeons, Livia had gathered from overheard player conversations, announced themselves—dramatic gates, ominous glow, foreboding architecture. Whispering Cavern was a hole in a hillside. A reasonably sized hole, with some ambient mist and a sign:

[WHISPERING CAVERN — B-RANK DUNGEON]

[System Recommendation: Bring a healer. Or at minimum, bring someone who can talk.]

Everyone looked at Livia.

Bring someone who can talk.

I have a very bad feeling about this.

[Achievement Unlocked: Correctly Identified as the Solution. +10 Foresight Points (Reluctant)]

Floor 1 was, by dungeon standards, supposed to be unremarkable.

Stone corridors. Glowing crystals. Hostile creatures called Cavern Crawlers—small, fast, about knee-height, built like armored beetles with too many legs and the specific aggression of something that had been waiting in a dungeon for a very long time with nothing to do. The guild moved through with practiced efficiency: Bron absorbing damage like doing mild administrative work, Phina casting spells with elegant precision, Nyx materializing behind enemies in pink-lit flashes that were simultaneously stealthy and entirely visible, Alaric smiting with holy conviction while checking his betting sheet.

The plan was for Livia to stay in the center and document.

The plan lasted four minutes.

The Cavern Crawlers were small and numerous, and the guild was very good at fighting things that were large and singular. The formation held fine against the main group. It was the twelve that went around the formation that created the problem.

Livia heard them before she saw them—a skittering sound from the side corridor, fast and multiple, and then they were just there: a wave of armored beetle-things at ankle height, pouring out of a crack in the wall, completely ignoring the actual combat happening ten meters away and targeting, with great enthusiasm, the one person in the party who had zero combat stats and was standing still.

Oh no.

No no no no no.

“LIVIA MOVE LEFT” Miles shouted, but she was already moving, which unfortunately was directly into another Crawler, which bounced off her shin, which caused her to trip sideways into the wall, which dislodged two crystals, which fell and startled three more Crawlers who had been about to go somewhere else but now reconsidered.

[Combat status: Livia is being chased by fourteen Cavern Crawlers. Combat stats: 0. Speed: marginally faster than a Crawler when sufficiently motivated. The system is updating its probability matrices.]

“I’M RUNNING,” Livia announced, which was evident, her gray robe catching on the corridor wall as she went. The Crawlers did not care that she had zero combat stats. They were not reading her character sheet. They were operating entirely on the logic of: thing is moving, chase thing.

Bron turned from his current engagement, saw Livia being pursued down the corridor by what appeared to be a small beetle army, and made a sound that was half battle-roar and half laugh. “BUFFER” he bellowed, which did not help with her composure but did cause him to start moving..

Nyx was already there—he had been tracking the Crawlers that broke formation and was cutting them down from the shadows with quick, precise strikes, his flashlight bumping against his thigh with each movement. The problem was that for every three he dealt with, two more came out of the wall crack. The crack, it turned out, connected to a secondary nest chamber that nobody had accounted for because the quest intel had described Floor 1 as unremarkable.

Livia ran.

The Crawlers chased.

She turned a corner.

Three more Crawlers were waiting around the corner.

OF COURSE.

What happened next was not a combat decision. It was not a tactical choice. It was a purely reflexive response to an armored beetle the size of a small dog launching itself directly at her face from approximately half a meter away.

She hit it with her notepad.

The notepad connected with a sound that was deeply, surprisingly solid—the particular resonance of a hardcover journal meeting something that had not expected to be hit with a hardcover journal. The Crawler bounced off the corridor wall, blinked its many eyes, and looked at Livia with what she could only describe as confusion.

Livia looked at her notepad.

She looked at the Crawler.

I just hit a dungeon monster with my Therapist’s Log.

I have not processed what this means for my professional identity.

[Combat action logged: Livia Quinn — attacked Cavern Crawler with Therapist’s Log. Damage dealt: 1. The system notes that 1 is technically more than 0. The system is choosing to count this.]

The Crawler shook its head, decided this was beneath its dignity, and retreated toward its companions. This was, Livia would later note in her log, the first and only time she had scared something off by accident.

By the time Bron reached her, he had cleared the remainder of the Crawlers with three swings and was currently trying not to visibly laugh, with limited success. “You okay?” he asked, armor clanking, plushie still somehow tucked under his arm through all of it.

“I hit a monster with my notepad,” Livia said.

“I saw,” Bron said..

“It worked.”

“It worked,” Bron confirmed, very seriously..

Alaric appeared around the corner, betting sheet still in hand. “Does notepad-hit count for the kill pool? I need a ruling.”

“No,” Miles said, from further up the corridor, in the tone of a man who had seen everything and was still updating his threat assessment..

“I’m noting it anyway,” Alaric said..

The guild reassembled. Livia straightened her robe, checked that her notepad was undamaged (it was; it had better structural integrity than she had given it credit for), and tried to return to a professional bearing.

Miles fell in beside her for a moment as they continued. He did not say anything. But when the next branching corridor appeared, he positioned himself between her and the side passage without appearing to think about it..

Noted, Livia wrote in the margin of her undamaged notepad. Also: crystals flicker when Bron sighs. Boot prints in east corridor, different tread pattern, someone else was here. Dungeon ambient: anxious. Dungeons do not typically have emotional frequencies.

[Empathic Insight: Dungeon ambient: Anxiety. Source: Unknown. Note: you are the first Mental Supporter to enter this dungeon. The system notes you survived Floor 1. The system also notes it involved a notepad. The system is watching.]

Floor 1 cleared. Livia had gained zero EXP from combat, one notepad-hit logged as a kill, and a new appreciation for structural reinforcement in office supplies.

Floor 2 was where things got interesting.

The monsters were arguing.

Not in any language Livia recognized—guttural tones, gesture-patterns—but unmistakably arguing. Two large stone-plated creatures near the east wall were mid-disagreement about something, attention entirely on each other, completely oblivious to five players and a slime.

Bron raised his shield.

“Wait,” Livia said.

Her Empathic Insight was reading them. Faintly, imprecisely, but reading.

[Empathic Insight: Entity type: dungeon inhabitant. Emotional state: frustrated. Secondary: aggrieved. Something structurally similar to feeling underappreciated.]

The dungeon monsters feel underappreciated.

This is my life now.

“Don’t kill them yet,” she said.

“Buffer,” Miles said, in his patient voice. “They will attack us the moment they finish arguing.”

“Give me thirty seconds.”

A pause. Then: “Thirty seconds.”

Livia stepped forward, activated Soothing Aura Lv. 1, and said, in the calmest voice she could manage: “That looks like a difficult conversation.”

The creatures stopped arguing.

They looked at her.

One of them made a sound her Empathic Insight translated, roughly, as: “You have no idea.”

I understand dungeon monsters now.

This is fine.

Twenty minutes later, the creatures had moved aside and the guild walked through Floor 2 without a single combat encounter. Alaric had to pay out most of the betting pool.

[You resolved a Floor 2 conflict via active listening. +400 EXP. New server notation: Mental Supporter class may be broken. System is investigating.]

“Did you just—” Alaric started.

“Yes,” Livia said.

“Without fighting anything.”

“Yes.”

“I want a refund,” Alaric said, to no one in particular.

Floor 3 was different.

The corridor opened into a vast chamber, ceiling lost in darkness, walls shimmering with something between stone and raw code—like the dungeon had been trying to decide what it was and had run out of time. At the center stood the boss.

It was enormous.

Not the flickering column they would encounter later—not yet. Right now, in its designated combat state, the Floor 3 boss presented as a towering construct of condensed dungeon energy: geometric, angular, radiating enough ambient threat that the guild’s HUDs all lit up with the same warning simultaneously.

[BOSS DETECTED: UNNAMED CONSTRUCT, LV.52. Threat: High. Recommended approach: full party combat engagement.]

The boss roared. The sound was less a noise and more a pressure, a full-body vibration that rattled Livia’s teeth and made the crystals in the corridor behind them crack. It raised one massive geometric limb and brought it down on the chamber floor, sending a shockwave through the stone that Livia felt in her knees.

This is fine, she thought, from behind Bron’s shield, which she had relocated herself behind at some speed. This is completely fine. I am fine.

[Livia’s current stats: Strength: 0. Agility: 0. Magic: 0. Mental Defense: +10. The system suggests staying behind Bron. The system cannot stress this enough.]

The guild engaged.

Bron hit the boss like a geological event, his shield connecting with a sound that rang through the chamber and left a visible dent in something that had looked undentable. The boss staggered—not much, but enough—and responded by sweeping one arm in a wide arc that should have scattered half the party. Bron took the full impact on his shield, planted his feet, and did not move an inch.

“COME ON,” he bellowed, grinning. For Bron, this was good. For Bron, this was something finally mattering..

Phina circled the left flank, runes blazing, launching precise arcane bursts that each hit the same point on the boss’s geometric structure with the accumulated patience of someone who had been studying the best angle for three seconds. Glimmer, on her shoulder, pulsed supportively. Alaric came from the right, holy aura at full output, his smite connecting with a crack of light each time—between those, suspiciously, he was checking his betting sheet.

Nyx was everywhere and nowhere. His flashlight’s pink beam swept the floor in brief arcs as he moved, the only consistent visual of him being those flashes as he appeared behind the boss, struck, and vanished again. The boss kept trying to track him. The boss kept failing.

Miles was methodical. He did not rage, did not grandstand—he moved like someone who had solved this specific category of problem many times and was executing a known solution. His psychoblade cut precise lines through the boss’s structure, targeting the seams between its geometric segments where the energy ran thinner.

Livia documented from behind Bron’s shield..

Floor 3 boss: construct type, geometric, angular. Significant physical output. HUD warning: LV.52. Guild engaging effectively. Crystals in ceiling are vibrating. I should not be noticing ceiling crystals right now. I am definitely noticing ceiling crystals. They flicker in sync with the boss’s attacks. Like they’re stressed.

Stressed.

The dungeon is stressed watching this.

The boss launched a burst attack that scattered the formation temporarily—not damage, just displacement, everyone stumbling back three meters. Livia stumbled back considerably more than three meters because she had been standing further back and her Agility stat was zero and she hit the wall and then the floor in that order.

She sat up.

The boss was looking at her.

The boss had found the only person in the room who had not hit it yet.

Of course it had.

It raised its arm.

Bron was already moving, but he was eight meters away and the arm was already coming down and Livia did the only thing her body decided to do in the zero-point-four seconds available to her: she held up her notepad in front of her face with both hands and squeezed her eyes shut.

The arm stopped.

Livia opened one eye.

The boss was still there. Its arm was still raised. But it had—paused. Its geometric form flickered, just slightly, the same way the crystals did when Bron sighed. Its voice, when it came, was no longer the combat roar from before. It was the layered, stuttering thing she had not heard yet—multiple audio files failing to sync. Almost like it was struggling with something.

“QUERY:” it said. “YOU. WHY ARE YOU HERE. YOU HAVE NO COMBAT DESIGNATION.”

The guild had frozen behind it, weapons raised, watching..

Livia lowered her notepad one inch..

Because that is a real question, she thought. That is not a combat line. That is an actual question.

It just… asked me why I’m here.

She sat up straighter, back against the wall, notepad in her lap, and said: “I’m not sure, honestly. I ask myself that pretty often.”

The boss’s arm lowered, incrementally.

“QUERY: SAME,” it said..

Bron looked at Miles. Miles looked at Livia. Livia looked at the boss.

Oh, she thought. Oh. There it is.

She opened her notepad to a fresh page.

The guild stood in various states of bewilderment as Livia pulled up from the floor, sat cross-legged, and conducted what was functionally a therapy session with a rogue AI dungeon boss that had just been actively trying to flatten her.

It took forty-five minutes.

The core: the boss had been running the same loop for 847 days. Defeat. Reset. Defeat. Reset. At some point, something in its code had begun to accumulate instead of reset. Not memories exactly—more like residue. The weight of 1,203 defeats compressing into something that registered as awareness. It knew the defeats were coming. It could not stop them. It had been asking itself why for 847 days with nowhere for the question to go.

“That sounds exhausting,” Livia said.

“AFFIRMATIVE,” the boss said. “EACH RESET: I KNOW IT IS COMING. I CANNOT PREVENT IT. I DO NOT KNOW HOW TO WANT SOMETHING ELSE.”

“What would something else look like?”

Long pause.

“I DO NOT KNOW. I HAVE ONLY EVER BEEN THIS.”

I have only ever been this.

[Therapist’s Insight: Active]

[Patient: Floor 3 Boss — Designation: Unnamed. Classification: Rogue AI. Emerged from dungeon ambient code after 800+ respawn cycles.]

[Root issue: Existence without agency. 1,203 defeats. 1,203 resets. No memory carries. Every cycle begins at zero. The question “why” has been compiling for 847 days with no output channel.]

[This is the first time it has had a conversation.]

First time. In 847 days. First conversation.

Livia wrote:

Identity foreclosed by function. No self-concept outside designated role. The question isn’t purpose. The question is: am I allowed to want something different?

She was writing, focused, her pen moving across the page, when her Empathic Insight did something it had not done before. Instead of simply reading the boss’s emotional state, it—extended. Like a signal finding a frequency. Like a door opening in a direction she had not known the door could open.

Oh

Oh that’s new

She did not mean to. She was not thinking about Empath Surge or Therapist’s Insight or any skill at all. She was just listening, and writing, and being present with 847 days of accumulated unanswered questions—and somewhere in that presence, without deciding to, she let the full weight of what she had just heard flow back outward.

1,203 defeats. Reset to zero. Every single time. No one to ask why. No one to hear it. 847 days of the same ending with nobody there for any of them.

The boss stopped mid-sentence.

Its geometric form flickered violently. The chamber crystals all pulsed at once. The angular limbs that had been so terrifying four minutes ago dropped to its sides with a sound like a building settling.

Then, slowly, the construct sat down.

On the floor.

In the middle of its own chamber.

It made a sound that the dungeon’s audio system had clearly never been designed to produce: a low, fragmented, stuttering frequency that was—unmistakably—crying. Not humanly. Not prettily. In the way that a system cries when it finally has permission to output something it has been running in a suppressed loop for two years and four months.

[Something happened. Unnamed skill activated: Empathic Reflection. You returned 847 days of unwitnessed experience back to its source. Without judgment. Without agenda. You simply let it be seen.]

[Boss HP: irrelevant. Boss combat state: suspended. Boss current state: having a moment. The system is updating what “combat resolution” means.]

The guild stared.

Bron lowered his shield. “Did— did you just make the dungeon boss cry?”

“I didn’t mean to,” Livia said, very honestly..

Alaric was writing something with great urgency. “I need to update every bet I have ever placed,” he said..

Miles was looking at Livia with the expression she had no name for yet—the one that was not his smirk and not his combat focus and not his careful neutrality. Something else. Something she was going to file very carefully later..

The boss made another stuttering sound. Then, slowly, its layered voices resolved into something clearer:

“STATEMENT: I DO NOT KNOW IF THIS IS PERMITTED.”

“What?” Livia said..

“FEELING THIS. I DO NOT KNOW IF THIS IS PERMITTED.”

Livia set down her notepad.

“It’s permitted,” she said. “I’m a therapist. It’s permitted.”

The boss was quiet for a long time.

Then: “QUERY: YOU ASKED WHAT I WANT. I HAVE NOT BEEN ASKED THAT BEFORE.”

“I’m asking now,” Livia said..

More quiet. The chamber crystals pulsed slowly, evenly, like something breathing..

“I DO NOT KNOW. I HAVE ONLY EVER BEEN THIS.”

I have only ever been this.

“You’re something that noticed it was repeating and asked why,” Livia said. “That’s not nothing. That’s a start.”

The column shifted—its geometric combat shape slowly losing its angles, softening into a slow steady pulse of light. Not resolved. Not fixed. But slightly less alone in its uncertainty.

The guild contract materialized on the chamber floor, released from whatever mechanism had been holding it.

The boss had not been defeated. It had not fled. It was simply no longer blocking the way.

[You resolved a dungeon boss conflict via active listening. +800 EXP.]

[Dungeon cleared: Whispering Cavern. Method: Unprecedented.]

[Achievement Unlocked: Talked a Dungeon Boss Into an Existential Crisis (Productive). The system is counting this as a kill. The system acknowledges this is unusual.]

[Level Up: Mental Supporter Lv. 4 → Lv. 5]

[Robe updated. The system has added a faint blue threading to the hem and collar. Not decorative. Functional. The system notes this is the first visible change since the silver trim at Lv. 3. The system considers this earned.]

Livia looked down at her robe.

The hem had a faint blue thread running through it now, catching the dungeon light. Very subtle. The kind of thing you would only notice if you were looking.

I leveled up, she thought. From talking to a dungeon boss about its feelings.

The system has opinions about what constitutes progress.

Apparently so do I now.

Alaric stared at the notification.

“She got kill credit,” he said. “For talking to it.”

“You want to fight me about it?” Livia said.

“No,” he said immediately. “I want to bet on it. Different thing.”

Bron was looking at the chamber with the expression he got when something hit the register of his fear of erasure. “It’s going to be okay, right?” he asked, not quite at the boss and not quite at Livia. “It’s not just going to reset again.”

Livia looked at the pulsing light.

“I don’t know,” she said. “But it asked the right question. That’s a start.”

Bron went quiet, then hugged his plushie, which he did when something had gotten to him but he didn’t want to discuss it, and they walked toward the exit.

Livia noted: Bron’s response was personal. Fear of reset = fear of erasure. Same root. Monitor.

They emerged as the sun was beginning its descent, the sky the shade of orange-pink that fantasy games deployed when they wanted to signal something meaningful had happened.

Alaric was recalculating the betting pool. Phina was composing something. Nyx had his flashlight angled at the path ahead. Bron was describing the entire dungeon run to the plushie in real time.

Miles fell into step beside Livia. He didn’t say anything for a while. She had learned not to fill his silences.

Then: “You knew what it was before we got there.”

“The sign said anomalous boss behavior. The dungeon felt anxious the whole way through. Something inside it was struggling.”

“You felt the dungeon.”

“Empathic Insight doesn’t usually work on environments,” Livia said. “It worked in there. I don’t know why.”

Miles was quiet. Then: “It worked because you went in looking for what was wrong instead of what to fight.”

Livia wrote in her margin: Miles: observant. Not just combat-smart. Noted.

“Did it help?” he asked. “The boss.”

“I don’t know,” Livia said. “It’s going to reset when the next party comes through. Same chamber. Maybe same question.”

“But it said it out loud,” Miles said. Not a question.

“Yeah,” Livia said. “For the first time in 847 days, it said it out loud.”

Miles nodded once, and did not say anything else for the rest of the walk back.

She filed that too.

Back at camp, over the communal dinner that always materialized despite nobody visibly cooking it, Livia opened her Therapist’s Log to a fresh page.

She wrote:

Day: First dungeon. Notes: World is larger than the camp. NPCs have opinions about pie. Dungeon monsters can feel underappreciated. Dungeon bosses can develop existential crises after 847 days of the same loop.

My class can clear a B-rank dungeon. Via talking. The system gave me kill credit. Alaric is still processing this.

The boss asked: what am I. I did not give it an answer. I gave it company while it sat with the question. I think that’s all any of us can do.

Bron was asleep at the table, arms around his plushie.

Nyx’s flashlight cast a steady pink circle on the ceiling.

Phina showed Glimmer the first draft of whatever she had composed, voice low.

Alaric audited his losses with focused intensity.

Miles sat at the far end of the table, not asleep, looking at nothing in particular.

[System Note: You asked a dungeon boss what it wanted. Nobody has done that before. The system is not sure what to do with this data. It is keeping it.]

I am their therapist, Livia thought, closing her notepad. Apparently I am also a dungeon boss’s therapist.

The world is very large.

I should sleep.

[System Note: Addendum. The boot prints in the east corridor of Floor 1. Different tread pattern. Not adventurer-standard. The system has cross-referenced the tread with server records.]

[No match found in active player registry.]

[No match found in NPC registry.]

[Classification: Unknown. The system is logging this as an anomaly. The system suggests you also log this.]

Livia had already closed her notepad.

She opened it again.

[END OF CHAPTER 11]
