Later in what was in my current ‘night’ cycle, I returned to the rooftop where I had constructed my Thaumaturgic ritual.

With me was Sylvia. We had set out alone to accomplish my task, partly because it was just easier that way…and partly for some alone time. It's been hard for us to find a stretch of time when the two of us could be really, truly alone these days. We were both so busy with our respective duties right now. Me as the overall leader of the expedition and the Polaris Reach. And Sylvia in her capacity as the second in command to Nyx, as the leader of the remnants of the Order of the Eclipsed Dawn and the other Orders. This was all doubly so because we were, well.

Currently preparing for a siege from the Savoy led faction of the Principality, of course. That didn't exactly lead to a calm, or even romantic mood.

I was even here in totality. I had left behind my Core as a clone to pick up the duties I’d neglected during my little tour of vengeance. Most people in the expedition didn’t even know that had happened. Instead, all they knew was that I had disappeared for half a day, returned covered in blood, and then vanished into a closed-door session with my officers.

I’d already heard all kinds of wild theories about where I’d been and what I was doing.

Some of them were even right.

But that didn’t matter.

What did was that I was standing over the remnants of my chalked ritual, while Sylvia bent down and inspected the runic strings upon the rooftop. I was quiet as she did, still feeling a bit…chagrinned over the whole mess with the Elves. I may have been forgiven by my fellow Polarians, but the rest of the leadership had been less understanding. I had been told in no uncertain terms that if this happened again, they would start considering a vote to change the overall leader of the expedition.

Three guesses who had put forth that notion. They certainly weren’t a furry pain in the ass.

Not at all.

I had taken it on the chin, though. I may have realized I would do it again, but I could be regretful for the necessity of my instinct towards a campaign of rip-roaring revenge.

Maybe I’d just be more circumspect when the time came.

I was knocked out of my introspection when Sylvia stood from her crouch and turned to face me, a slight, slanted smile on her lips. “It’s decent work…for a Neophyte.”

I snorted at her tease, approaching and laying my hands on her hips. Almost instinctively, she returned the favor, and we stood there almost nose to nose in a pseudo-embrace. “You’re a Neophyte as well, you know,” I shot back. “You only have a semester's worth of experience over me in the field, Miss Illusionist.”

Sylvia’s answer was a short, throaty laugh and a shake of her head. “Perhaps,” She acknowledged. “But you’re a Magi, and I a Cultivator. Our exact methods of going about Thaumaturgy may be different, but in the end, there are enough similarities that I can appreciate your skill in the school. You’ve…always possessed a knack for picking up Mysticality that I lack,” Sylvia admitted freely. “What you seem to possess in a natural talent for, I’ve needed to struggle to attain.”

I blinked at her frank statement. I…suppose that was true, from a certain point of view. It was odd for Sylvia and I to even be in the same course in the first place, considering our differing foundations towards Mysticality. But there were certain levels of overlap between Cultivation and Magic. Both were capable of Healing, even if they were approaching from the differing directions. Both were capable of Warding, even though that was considered a subset of the Sealing School of Cultivation Arts.

And both Mana and Ki were capable of Thaumaturgy. Though once again, it was considered a subset of Sealing.

It was all about the runes, in the end. Most Cultivation Arts that involved runes in any manner seemed to be folded into that School. It was…different than what I was used to as a Mage, though.

More chanting was involved as the sigils were inscribed. Something about verbal intent?

Cultivation was weird, man.

The point was, though, I had seen Sylvia as she learned alongside me and everyone else in the Thaumaturgy classroom. Most people, I’d learned, seemed to inherently struggle with the various forms of runic magic. Something about runes and the structured manner in which you had to array them almost tripped up most people. They struggled to learn the language said to have been passed down by the ‘gods’ that could influence Mana and Ki as it passed through them, in much the way electrical current did a circuit.

Not me, though. I could admit that I had a certain inherent understanding of runes. Something that might just be an innate talent of mine. Maybe I had a flair for languages or something?

I’d certainly done well in my High School Spanish classes, though there was a far cry from runes and Español.

I’d noticed how Sylvia seemed to struggle in her own way with the material. Not to an unreasonable degree, of course. She was certainly better than ninety percent of the class. Sylvia was still the daughter of a world famous Arch-Mage, and even in her short years had been downright immersed in an environment rife with them.

But…it didn’t come almost naturally to her as it did to me when it came to runic Magics like Abjuration and Thaumaturgy.

I tightened my grip on Sylvia’s waist. “I can help you stud-”

Sylvia cut me off with a laugh, shaking her head. She surprised me by leaning in and brushing her lips over mine in a brief kiss, before pulling away. She smiled at my confused look. “No, Nathan,” She said fondly. “Beyond the fact we’re not currently at the Academy and actually in the middle of a long-deployment military campaign…I don’t want that help.”

I blinked at her for a moment, but accepted it. I could understand wanting to accomplish something on your own, without the overt support of another. Something you could say you earned through your own blood, sweat, and tears.

It was why I had chosen to build an Order without the explicit help of Grey.

After a moment, Sylvia’s smile faltered, though. “Besides…I doubt we would even have the time, soon,” She looked off into the distance, gaze landing on the wide expanse of the Fiume d’Oro that Rhoscara bearded. “They should be here soon.”

She didn’t even need to specify who. I understood.

I followed her gaze and nodded. “Any day now.”

Soon, the Principality would be here. I…doubted we would have time to even speak to each other, once things began in earnest. If-no, when we triumphed over the Velancian army, we would be moving out as soon as we could, bound for Smaragd and accompanied by the forces of this city. It promised to be a constant slog of battle and death as we fought our way through the forces of Lasgach.

That is…if we could even find a way through the shield covering the western half of the Barren Forest. I hadn’t forgotten about it, and the barrier troubled me.

I shook those thoughts away and instead focused back on Sylvia, giving her the best smile I could muster. “Enough of that. For now, we have other things to be thinking about.” I waggled my eyebrows at her.

Sylvia gave me a tolerant, fond look, and reached to grasp the back of my neck. As she pulled me down into another kiss, I felt the cool sensation of one of her illusions fall over us, vanishing from the sight of any would be watchers.

Privacy at last.

I was deeply glad I'd thought to bring along a small rug.

……………………………………….

When we’d had our fill, Sylvia turned to me with a serious, expectant look on her face, adjusting her cloak as she did. “I’ll speak to you later, Nathan. I must return to my duties, and you…I know what today is,” She paused for a moment before continuing. “Give her my love.”

I reached down and grabbed one of her hands, raising it to my lips and brushing them against the back of it. “I will, don’t worry. I’ll see you later, yeah?”

Sylvia shifted that hand and patted my cheek with it, and then…disappeared.

I almost wanted to laugh. That little trick looked very much like the same one I’d seen the Rangers pull on me multiple times now. Someone was a little inspired by her peers.

Good for her.

And then…I sobered up. I glance down at the wristwatch I had so painstakingly crafted all those months ago, judging the time. It’s not like I had Tarus to tell night and day apart anymore, so it was difficult to do so beyond this little thing.

She…should be awake by now, I’m thinking? I believe it was around mid-morning around there, and she was a surprisingly early riser for her age. I had certainly never been one to wake up before I needed to be dragged out of bed at that age.

It was probably a habit she’d picked up from her Mother.

Bah, I was just dragging things out because I was anxious about doing this. I’d promised I would, months ago now, but I was a little scared it wouldn’t work. It was likely to be very draining on me, and I’m not sure just how much time I’d get before I had to return to my duties. But…it was important that I at least try.

I took a deep breath, slapped my cheeks with both hands to focus myself, and nodded firmly.

Let’s do this.

I sank down into a cross-legged position and closed my eyes, folding my hands before me. Evening my breath, I fell into a meditative state almost immediately, long since used to the practice. The pulse of Vereden’s Aether could be felt and ‘seen’ in my mind's eye all around me. The crests and troughs of it surrounded and crashed against me like the beating of an enormous heart. Almost absentmindedly, I felt the attention of Anima fall on me as my awareness floated upon those waves. Through our Aether, we exchanged wordless greetings, and yet didn’t linger longer than that. We were both busy in our own ways, and I had nothing new to update her on.

The Great Spirit of Life returned to her…experimentations, and we bothered each other no more.

Instead…I traced something else with my senses. One of the Aetherial tethers that connected me to the myriad, inactive Sprites I had taken up the habit of leaving in circumspect locations. These little constructs barely took more than a single mote of Mana to create, and were entirely self-sustaining when they had been. They didn’t have a will of their own, not in the dormant state I’d created them. But I could access them with a flex of my will whenever I wanted. It took a far larger investment in Mana to do so than it had taken to create them, but when I did…

I could see, and hear, and even speak through them if I so chose. This type of Sprite couldn’t be transformed into a clone, but they could be my vessel for…many things.

I traced the threads that bound me to my Sprites, carefully picking through them, until I found one in particular. This thread was by far the longest one I had. It had been one of the very first of these new Sprites I’d created, after I had evolved Ringed Mind into The Lattice of Heaven and Earth.

One that I had left in Blutstein.

I poured Mana into that connection, more and more and more, until I was starting to run dangerously low on Mana. Nothing happened, and I was about to run out. Gritting my teeth, I wordlessly reached out along the Familiar Bond I shared with Fade and asked for help. Understanding my need, Fade started flooding his own connection with the Mana he had converted for just this purpose.

Thankfully…I didn’t need to drain him dry. I felt it when I had finally tripped the thread connecting me to the Sprite into an activated state.

Connection.

I closed my physical eyes…

And opened the eyes of the Sprite, miles and miles and miles away. Flicking them around, I was…a little amused at what I found.

I was sitting on a doll shelf. One that I had made for someone very special to me, not long after we had moved into the headquarters of the Order, in the immediate aftermath of the Skyfall. On either side of me rested several of the porcelain dolls which my resting spot had been made for. I…appeared to have been squished in between two of them, tightly enough that I needed to squirm my way out.

This must be what a rat felt like, trying to escape a trap.

I suppose the noise I was making was loud enough to catch attention, however. Below me, I heard a childlike, inquisitive noise of confusion, and then…

An enormous human face appeared in my field of vision. The difference in scale between us was enough that I could easily make out individual pores in the feminine face looming before me. Yet, I was far from scared.

Instead, I smiled widely at the titanic pre-teen in front of me, ridiculously happy to see her. “Happ-” I tried to croak joyfully, crushed between two porcelain dolls. With one last push of my Spritely strength, I popped out from between them to hover before the little girl. “Happy Birthday, Aveline!”

The massive (in comparison) face of Aveline, the daughter I hadn’t seen in months, immediately stretched out into a wide, toothy smile at my words.

I’d kept my promise. Despite the distance between us…

I had managed to show up for her birthday.

God, I love Magic.
