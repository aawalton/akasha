Ace and his team weren’t alone—and not just because their sadistic host was floating nearby.

Using his newly enhanced senses, thanks to his new gear, Ace listened carefully as something slithered through the forest. He felt it pause, its attention shifting toward the group as they gathered around the rapidly disintegrating Behemoth, and Ace tensed for a fight.

Before he could warn the others, however, the thing slithered back into the forest, and it faded from his perception.

He let out a sigh of relief.

That had been close.

They others, oblivious to the near-miss with yet another monster, continued to rifle through their new loot.

"Sylvan Grace Essence," Olivia read from somewhere nearby, satisfaction in her voice as a glowing vial materialized in her palm. The liquid inside pulsed with emerald luminescence, organic patterns shifting beneath its surface. "Rare quality."

The System giggled. "Perfect for a dancer, don't you think? All those fluid, powerful movements—just like the ancient forest spirits! Though they tended to eat people rather than dance with them."

Olivia ignored the commentary, pocketing the vial before collecting a cluster of small coins.

“Yay!” the System said happily. “Money!”

Still ignoring the floating girl, Olivia grabbed all fourteen Iron Chits that materialized at her feet. The currency clinked together, their surfaces etched with arcane symbols.

Beside Tara, Marcus winced and grabbed his side as he read his own screen. "Twisted Root Bands. What the hell are those?”

Before anyone could respond, leather wrappings materialized around his forearms, automatically securing themselves to his skin.

"I guess it…” Marcus squinted as he read his screen. “I guess it enhances Mind Drinker abilities?"

“Mmhmm,” the System said. “As a bonus, it hides those hideous new strips of bark that just grew across your skin. Yay!”

“What—”

Marcus unwrapped one of the strips of leather. Sure enough, beneath the bands, patches of his skin had begun transforming into something resembling the Behemoth's bark plates—a permanent marker of his encounter with the creature's blood.

“Aw man!” he yelled with a grimace. “Not again!”

The System giggled mercilessly.

Marcus frowned. “I mean, do they at least do anything this time?”

The System smiled. “Nope!”

“Ugh.”

As Tara read her own screen, she glanced at Ace with concern. He nodded reassuringly, though he noted how pale she remained from letting him drink her blood.

Not good.

"Viridian Healing Sap," she read, accepting a container of glowing green fluid that appeared instantly before her and hummed with energy.

“Too bad Rachel isn’t here, huh?” the System asked without a shred of sadness in her voice. “What’s the Fang Gang without their little analyst?”

Instantly, everyone went silent.

“Oh, my, I seem to have struck a nerve.” The System clicked her tongue in mock disappointment. “My apologies.”

Ace glanced back at his combat boots, which sat on a Highland boulder a short ways off.

“Ah,” the System said knowingly. “I knew the nostalgia would get the best of you. I guess you want to bring them?”

“Wouldn’t mind it.”

“I guess you can access your inventory…” she said with a shrug.

“How?”

“How do you think, silly goose?” she asked.

He frowned and summoned his stats screen, trying not to pause and read his stats just yet. He scanned the edges of the screen. With a sudden burst of light, a new tab appeared labeled “inventory.”

Huh.

Handy.

He grabbed his boots and held them in his hands, still not quite sure what to do. He tapped the inventory button and, in a flash of blue light, the boots disappeared from his palms. A small icon with his boots appeared in his inventory. He did the same with his other loot, and sure enough, each appeared as an icon safely tucked away in a limited inventory space.

“There isn’t much room in here,” Ace pointed out.

“It grows as you level,” the System explained. “Duh.”

In answer, Ace just sighed.

“Might want to add those chits,” the System said with a wink. “There are plenty of pickpockets in my world.”

“I thought stealing came with a gamble,” he pointed out. “That’s why we have to use your damned trading system.”

“Oh, yes,” she said cheerfully. “But there are some classes with special thieving powers. It takes death off the table, as well as some of the more… shall we say, unsavory risks of a life of petty crime.”

Noted.

He fished the chits out of his pocket and, with another flash of light, they slipped into his inventory.

Ace backed out of the inventory screen, pausing momentarily as he noted the growth he’d gotten from their fight.

Level 18.

“Not bad,” he said. “That’s quite a level jump.”

“Not bad?!” The System laughed. “Everyone else is at least Level 20 by now, Sergeant! Even Marcus, and he spent half the fight unconscious!"

"What?" Ace frowned, scanning the readout again.

"You're in last place," she continued. "Tsk. Shouldn’t have set up your little team, huh? Sharing all that experience is slowing you down, Sergeant. Quite the tactical disadvantage, wouldn't you say?"

She was baiting him.

It wouldn’t work.

Without responding to her, he set to work allocating his new AP. With 36 to allocate, and with his Strength, Intelligence, and Wisdom stats starting to lag behind Vitality and Dexterity, he had some hard choices to make.

Intelligence would make his Soul Meter, which he clearly needed. But Wisdom would also be quite a benefit because it would make his Soul Meter regenerate faster.

Of course, it was always useful to have more Strength, too.

This was tough.

After the lessons he’d learned today, he allocated the points evenly between Intelligence and Wisdom. His Strength Ring gave him enough of a Strength boost for now. He would have to use his next AP to make his Strength catch up with his other stats.

As he finished with the stats screen, the System floated closer. Her grin widened, and she looked at him expectantly.

“Is there something you want to say?” he prodded.

“Always,” she answered.

Ace crossed his hands over his chest. “Alright, then. Out with it.”

"You could always disband the party, you know." The System twirled a lock of blonde hair around her finger with feigned innocence. "Hoard all that delicious EXP for yourself."

Across the clearing, Tara caught his eye. Marcus still struggled with his injuries despite the healing. Only Olivia seemed combat-ready, and even she showed signs of fatigue.

"No," Ace said simply.

The System raised an eyebrow. "No?"

"We stick together." He slipped a few Iron Chits that had dropped from the beast into his pocket, feeling the weight of the metal against his thigh. "That's how we survive this place."

A sudden prickling sensation crawled up Ace's spine, bypassing conscious thought to trigger primal alarm systems.

They were being watched—and not just by the System.

No, this was something else.

Something older.

The Nightmare Bear EXP solute he'd absorbed just a few days ago enhanced his situational awareness, granting him perception beyond normal vampire senses. He felt the observation like physical pressure against his skin—eyes from elsewhere, attention from the space between spaces.

Without moving his head, Ace activated Shadow Realm Immersion just enough to peer through the membrane separating dimensions without fully crossing over. The skill drained his Soul Meter at an alarming rate, but he needed to know for certain if his hunch was right.

Reality thinned around him, the colors inverting and bleeding away at the edges of his vision. Sound compressed to distant echoes as the cold of the Shadow Realm brushed against his consciousness.

What he saw froze him in place.

A massive entity studied him through the veil, its form blocking out a massive chunk of the forest. This wasn’t the humanoid figure that had reached into his chest during his first immersion—no, this was something else entirely.

Something primal.

Something powerful.

The entity's attention wasn't focused on the team or even the Behemoth's remains. It watched him specifically with patient interest. It didn’t come closer. It didn’t even have eyes. It simply stood far out of reach, watching him.

Waiting.

In a rush of smoke, it disappeared. The forest behind it appeared again as the shadows thinned. Within seconds, it was like the thing had never been there at all.

Ace released Shadow Immersion. Color and sound rushed back with disorienting intensity. The System continued her childish taunting, showing no sign that she'd noticed the entity's presence or Ace's brief glimpse beyond the veil.

She hadn't seen it.

From the looks of it, she hadn't even sensed the thing.

The realization had chilling implications. There were forces at work beyond even the System's knowledge or control—ancient entities moving through the shadow spaces between worlds, watching his progress with calculated interest.

Her presence shifted, and he felt her appear behind him. Though he turned his head, he wasn’t fast enough, and she leaned in close to his face.

Too close.

“I can’t wait to see what you become,” she whispered.

The hair on the back of his neck stood on end, and he glared at her with the full force of his barely restrained rage.

But she was already gone—as was what remained of Victor’s body. She had claimed yet another one of them, and her deadly little game continued.

With Ace at the center of it all.

Tara circled the still-decaying corpse and knelt beside him. Her voice dropped to the barest whisper, and she leaned in close to him. "You okay?"

To his surprise, he wanted to tell her everything. He wanted her to know what they were up against, but the System stood too close. The little girl’s wide eyes missed nothing, despite her apparent disinterest. And Tara... she'd already given him enough today. She was weak, vulnerable, in no condition to process existential threats beyond their immediate survival.

"I'm fine," he said, the lie sitting uncomfortably in his gut. "Just planning our next move."

She nodded, though a small frown tugged on the corner of her mouth.

Their healer didn’t believe him.

For now, that didn’t matter. They needed shelter, security, and time to recover. He, however, needed answers. About the shadow man who had reached into his chest. About the ancient entity watching from beyond the veil. About Darkmoor.

All connected, and all focused on him specifically.

As the team gathered their loot and prepared to move out, Ace made his decision. The next time they were in a secure location, he would go deeper into the Shadow Realm for a full exploration of whatever lay beyond the veil—and it was time for Darkmoor to give him more answers.

Someone—or something—was stalking him from the shadows, and it was likely they had information crucial to his squad’s survival.

The System. Darkmoor. The figures in the Shadow Realm. The longer Ace was here in the System’s twisted world, these big players treated him more and more like a pawn.

No more.

He was sick of the games.

It was time to find out what Darkmoor wanted from him—and this time, Ace wouldn’t leave until he had answers.
