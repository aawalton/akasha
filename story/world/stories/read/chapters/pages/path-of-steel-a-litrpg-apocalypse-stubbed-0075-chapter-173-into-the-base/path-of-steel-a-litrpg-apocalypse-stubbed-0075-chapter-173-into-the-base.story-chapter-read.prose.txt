The moment Gary entered the base, something changed. It wasn’t just the light, which suddenly felt brighter, though the entrance had looked dark from the outside. It wasn’t the smell, either—the thick brim and smoke covering the stench of open flesh.

No, it was something deeper. It was the order.

Outside, Gary had seen towering spirals of mortal and tentacle, undead sprinting on all fours, titans howling to the sky. In here, the terrain was almost clean. Efficient. Scientific.

Pipes of pulsing flesh crawled up and down the walls, engineered so they wouldn’t touch each other. A few reached up to the top of the spirals, venting black smoke into the air and creating the smog which covered this entire area. Others spawned from lit furnaces and traveled to vats of purple liquid, of which some held chimeras, others different experiments, and a few were opaque. Thin smoke swam in the purple, anyway, creating illusions of tentacles where there were none.

Such vats covered the floor of this space, spread thinly and with no discernible pattern. Their bases pulsed like beating hearts. Because of the line of furnaces, the heat was staggering.

There must have been no battle in this room, because only a few vats were broken. Most were still intact.

“What is this place…” Gary muttered, slowing his steps.

“It’s almost pretty,” Goldie said.

“What? No! It’s grotesque!”

“I think it has a certain kind of harmony.”

Gary shook his head, but try as he might, he couldn’t see it. The pipes pulsed on the walls, the vats were arranged without pattern. Everything worked like clockwork, or so it seemed, but the lack of definitive arrangement made his head hurt. It was like a terribly written function that somehow worked—or, rather, like a codebase written by some mad genius, sacrificing good practices for extreme efficiency.

Another metaphor floated in the back of his mind, something much more apt, but he couldn’t quite place it.

A loud explosion coming from deeper into the base shook him awake.

“We need to hurry,” he said, rushing out. He zipped between the vats, avoiding the sight of the tentacle-flesh abominations that lay inside to dive into one of the three tunnels connecting this space to the rest of the base. This tunnel was where the explosion had come from.

“We didn’t see any chimeras outside, did we?” Goldie said.

“They must be hiding with the puppeteer,” Gary replied.

“Yeah, but why? Wouldn’t they want to create a lot of zombies on the battlefield?”

He had no answer to that. “Let’s be careful. This isn’t nearly over,” he said.

They shot through a narrow tunnel decorated with real eyes, which followed Gary as he passed. The tunnel ended at another wide, factory-like room, this one ruined completely. Whatever had existed on the floor was now a mess of sweet-smelling fluid, tentacles, and glass. The pipes didn’t just crawl on the walls, they shot through the air across the room’s higher half. At the very end lay a door, half-embedded into the stone wall. It was massive and made of dark wood, with a large red orb in its center, while its surface was covered in murals that weren’t yet complete. What little Gary could glean resembled a handful of vaguely humanoid figures fighting giants. One of the humanoids had a core inside it, or rather, multiple overlapping cores around the same center.

“What is it with bosses and murals? Like, can’t you just tell us your story?” Goldie said.

“I don’t understand those murals. I don’t understand anything. It feels like we know too little about the puppeteer. For Sui Duang, at least we knew he was a dick, but this is all a mystery,” Gary said.

Another explosion dragged them to reality. They weren’t alone in the room. The brothers were staging a retreat to the door, holding off the vicious offensive of Feng Shen and a dozen other Core Formations. None of them were weak. Gary was flabbergasted when Feng Shen unleashed his Yin Yang Collapse, the move which had defeated Gary in the finals, only to have it blocked by Moe of the four brothers. The black-and-white disk ground against a red, runic ring. The explosion rattled the entire factory, but to Gary’s shock, both techniques had been nullified.

The rest of the fighters weren’t holding back, either. Powerful techniques clashed left and right. Alpha smashed a punch which felt like a sonic missile—the ground around his feet cratered, and the air shook by just the recoil. The legionary facing him was catapulted backward, shield cracking, so quickly it was like he disappeared. He flew past Gary like an illusion.

For the first time, Gary realized that Alpha was a body cultivator.

Jin was a flurry of blue, his strikes so rapid they seemed like a net falling on his opponents. The wrestling champ jumped forward, taking the cuts with the fat of his sumo-like body. He smashed his palms together, and a vortex of aura appeared, dragging Jin in along with all the debris around the champ. It lasted but a moment. The assassin’s gears whirled, the sound like a laptop overheating, and he barely outran the vortex. All the debris was pulverized between the wrestler’s hands.

“Stop running!” the champ roared. Cracks ran down Jin’s body as he reappeared behind Moe, next to a cloaked figure. A white light flashed, and he was good as new.

These all take time to describe, but they happened in the same instant. Gary was still rushing the battle, his spear ready to bite, when a climber shot out of another tunnel. He wore white robes and carried the signature purple sword of the swordsman clan, the Sevenfold Sky clan. His steps were light, as if treading on clouds, but his speed was real. He’d reach the battle at the same time as Gary. Both pretended to ignore each other until their paths crossed. Without warning or so much as glancing over, they attacked.

Gary’s spear thrust with the full power of his black lightning, backed with his great strength and the stars in his veins. It was like a rocket. The sword slashed so rapidly it was almost invisible. The auras of their strikes clashed before their actual weapons, creating a black and purple explosion which tossed them both back. They landed on their feet.

“My name is Liu Dong of the Sevenfold Sky clan,” the swordsman declared, assuming a battle stance.

Gary was already feeling the shivers. He could sense the strength of his opponent’s aura. The swordsman he’d faced outside, a field boss who put up a bit of a fight, was a candle to this man’s bonfire. Something warned Gary that he was facing a real genius this time.

But wasn’t he, too, a genius?

“Gary Steel of Heaven Reaching. Well met,” he replied, ready to thrust. The swordsman arrived while he was still an afterimage. A blur was all Gary saw. He thrust on instinct, spreading his aura to cover a wider area than just his spear and force the swordsman out, but he was fast. Too fast. He blinked behind Gary.

“Thunder Step!” Gary roared. The ground where he used to stand was sliced open. Flickers of purple rained after him, and he retreated while desperately trying to defend. Where the sword’s aura nicked him—not even the actual blade—deep red lines appeared on his skin.

“Nail Gun!” Gary yelled.

“Starry Night!” the swordsman replied.

They clashed a dozen times in less than the blink of an eye. Gary’s strikes were stronger than his opponent’s, but the swordsman had made more slashes than Gary had made thrusts, the interval between them so short that the strikes overlapped. The dozen clashes merged into an explosion, but neither opponent was still there. They flew across the room, a black and purple blur clashing, their path indicated by delayed explosions.

Gary knew he had to control the distance. His range was far greater than his opponent’s. If the swordsman couldn’t approach, Gary would win—but he was too damn fast.

I was making everyone outside seem slow. I have a hundred and twenty Speed. How can he be this much faster? Gary cursed, fighting his hardest.

They clashed over and over. Gary found himself losing ground. Liu Dong wasn’t just fast—he flowed, somehow, in a way that surpassed mere technique. It felt like watching a constellation, with every star a blade. These stars were everywhere. His attack, his defense, his movement… All glowed with astral patterns, transcending mortal limitations. Worse, they created a battle tempo different than Gary’s black lightning, which he couldn’t help but get sucked in. The more they fought, the more their tempo approached the swordsman’s, and the greater Gary’s disadvantage.

It’s his vibration, Gary realized. That’s his advantage. It isn’t speed at all, it’s everything.

Gary had always held a vibration more powerful than his peers. This was the first time he found it lacking, but there was nothing he could say. Liu Dong’s vibration, whatever it was, was simply superior to Gary’s.

To find himself lacking in something he usually took pride was a blow to his ego.

Then again, how long had it been since he worked on his vibration? The songs, the stats, the breakthrough, the body cultivation… Gary’s black lightning had been overwhelming when he’d first broken into Birth of Aura, but now he was an entire realm higher, and his vibration was basically the same.

Slashes rained on him. The enemy wouldn’t wait for Gary’s pondering. Stars fell swift, and the more he was pushed back, the closer the stars came to his body. They weren’t the same stars as the energy in his veins, he knew that with clarity, but they were similar. As they approached, he was shocked to find his veins activating by themselves. They didn’t just hold stars. They held hunger. An invisible tendril of energy crept towards Liu Dong’s stars, and Gary panicked. This wasn’t part of the Starvein Immortal Body Manual. It was supposed to only absorb pure starlight. He had a feeling that if he let this tendril eat Liu Dong’s star-shaped aura, he’d harm his own foundation.

He hastily drew back, taking a deep slash to the arm while shooting a thrust himself.

Their weapons clashed. The explosion pushed them both away. Gary remained wary, panting, while Liu Dong pointed his sword between Gary’s forehead.

“You’ve just broken through, haven’t you?” he asked.

“It’s more than enough to kick your ass,” Gary replied.

A smile played on the swordsman’s lips. “There is no need for geniuses to fight to the death over a fourth-floor event. Let this swordsman give you a bit of advice. Your vibration is powerful, but potential without work is like a carriage without a glyan.”

He didn’t see the tendril, Gary realized. “Let this spear-wielder help you, too. Whoever doesn’t see is bound to lose.”

“Do you imply I missed something? Nonsense,” the swordsman said, his face morphing into distaste. “You are too prideful, Gary Steel. A climber must know when to admit defeat. May I go?”

Gary glanced at the massive door, where the brothers had almost completed their fighting retreat. The door was ajar to let them in. No one had fallen on either side. There is no need for geniuses to fight to the death, Liu Dong had said. Was everyone following that? Was this a new rule? The weak got slaughtered, but geniuses worked together? Was he part of the corrupt top of the world?

I hate this place. I hate it so fucking bad.

He glanced at Liu Dong. Should he fight, or should he not? Of course he should.

Before he could act, the swordsman glanced warily at the door. “Fine. You drive a hard bargain. Here,” he said, tossing Gary a sack he just drew from his space ring. Then, without waiting for a response, he turned into a ray of starlight and dashed sideways at incredible speed. He passed around the battle, not attacking anyone, and dove into the ajar door. The brothers completed their retreat, then. Everyone was in. The door shut, at the same time that Feng Shen’s full-power Yin Yang Collapse crashed into it. The entire base shook. Pipes broke, and stone fell from the ceiling as the ground wailed. The door, however, held. It hadn’t even been scratched.

Feng Shen floated to the ground while the rest of the allies panted. They exchanged some words. Gary jogged closer when a notification rang in his ears.

Mission success! We have conquered the enemy base of operations and successfully pushed them into their last refuge. Victory is one step away. Everyone, rejoice! For the Gymnasium!

Gary sighed in relief. Given how the war missions worked, they’d earned a respite until the next round. The other mission hadn’t ended yet, since he’d have received a notification, but it was far from his problem.

“Did you sense that?” he asked Goldie.

“Sense what?” she replied.

“That hunger in my veins. I think they tried to eat Liu Dong’s stars.”

“Maybe you haven’t been feeding them enough?”

“I don’t know.”

“It’s important to take care of your body, Gary.”

“That’s not—” He cut himself off, sighing. “Nevermind.”

He joined the rest of the elites in front of the door. Up close, he could see its murals better. They were half-formed, as if the battle interrupted the carvers in the middle of their work. The half that was there showed nine humanoids fighting giants. Gary had assumed they were fighting all the giants, but taking a closer look, he could tell they were only fighting one giant, while the rest of them waited behind it, roaring. Like they formed a line. There were also two giant corpses piled behind the humanoids.

The nine humanoids were only rough figures, as if their features hadn’t been carved yet. Only the middle one held a little more detail. Its face wasn’t clear, but it contained a core where its heart should be, which seemed like it was pulsing, or like multiple cores overlapping each other with the same center. Gary counted four of them. This figure held a hammer in one hand, swinging it at the giant, but its other hand was low behind it as if sneakily dropping something to the ground. That something was the only part of the mural that was completely drawn.

It was a small red orb, with tiny red tentacles sticking out. The Seed of Eternal Flesh.
