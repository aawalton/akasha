The hodag attacked Kyle with a ferocity he’d never experienced.  The regular C Grade versions were slow, relying on landing individual heavy strikes.  The variants tended to lean into their specific skillset, but still relied on big attacks to try and finish him.  This creature, despite its immense size, attacked relentlessly.  Claw swipes were followed up by lunging bite attacks and thrusts with its horns, while any attempt Kyle made to create more distance resulted in mid-range strikes with its tail or swift leaps to try and crush him outright.

He was keeping up thanks to Genesis Engine, but the status quo wasn’t.  It was more like being hunted by a large cat than fighting a lumbering titan.  What’s worse, the hodag still hadn’t shown any of its skills.  Part of Kyle hoped that the ridiculous durability and properties of its scales meant that it didn’t have the same offensive tools as the others.  That part of Kyle also knew that it was wishful thinking.  This was a true mid-tier C Grade creature, and an extraordinary variant to boot.  Making any such assumptions would prove deadly.

He’d already made a quick shift back to Model: Spear on his prosthesis, and made quick jabs where he could.  The bamboo weapon had been enhanced with conceptual sharpness and infused with the concept of Immutability, making it a dangerous tool against most C Grades.  Unfortunately, the counterattacks Kyle made with the weapon struggled to leave more than light scratches against the hodag’s scales.  The only meaningful damage he’d been able to leave on the creature was from Model: Cannon’s explosion.  The scales on one side were cracked and scorched, but the injury hardly slowed the hodag down.  Even worse, he didn’t have another shot grown and ready to use.

“C.H.A.D.D., can you tell if the resistance is an active ability the hodag is using, or just a passive effect of the scales?” Kyle asked as he ducked under a lunging bite, only to sidestep a descending claw before pushing himself out of the way of a swipe of the monster’s horns.

[I AM NOT COMPLETELY SURE DR. MAYHEW.  THERE ARE SEVERAL POSSIBILITIES, WHICH-]

“Give me the quick version, C.H.A.D.D.!” Kyle called back.

[BEST TO ASSUME IT IS A PASSIVE EFFECT, DR. MAYHEW.]

Without another word, Kyle withdrew his staff from the World Tree Seed, as well as pulling out a handful of seeds, primarily those for his modified durian fruits.  He waited for the creature to attempt another bite.  A quick use of Cultivate grew the fruits to maturity, and Aura Force smashed them directly into the hodag’s face.

The cloud of stinking pollen and fetid juice caused the creature to rear back for just a moment, and Kyle seized the opportunity to point the tip of his staff at the hodag, activating Cultivate alongside Heart of Creation, even going so far as to include the concept of Adaptation.  Hundreds of scarlet petals erupted from the staff as the wood cracked, drops of acidic sap sizzling against the stone ground as they lashed out toward the hodag.  Even taken off-guard by the stink bombs and shrouding pollen, the hodag reacted swiftly with a backward leap.  The grasping petals only managed to wrap around the front right forelimb before being ripped away, the staff splintering as it couldn’t take the sudden shock.

Still, the damage was done.  The binding petals were slowly eating through the scales on that arm, the concept of Adaptation making the acid constantly iterate and improve as it fought to dissolve the mana-resistant material.  The hodag roared in pain, thrashing and clawing at its arm to release the deadly petals.  It was the opportunity Kyle needed.

The broken remains of his staff floated near him, held in place by Aura Force.  Parasitic Resonance was connected to each shard as he pushed Infuse to its limit, weaving in the concepts of Immutability and Timelessness to the best of his ability.

“Phaegos, now!” Kyle snarled, the effort of maintaining this level of focus pushing him to his extremes.

Done, the entity replied as a flood of conceptual Death flooded through his body and into the fragments of his staff.

The fragments, despite all the enhancements Kyle provided, began to deteriorate and rot the moment Death entered them.  They were doomed to crumble in mere moments.  And it would be enough.

Aura Force shot the darts of rot and decay directly at the injured arm, which was only now beginning to get free of the potent acid.  The hodag hardly seemed to notice the impacts, until the pieces finally disintegrated and released their deadly payload.  Conceptual Death flooded the injured arm, ravaging flesh as it sought to extinguish the hodag’s overwhelming Vitality.

Even though Kyle knew it wasn’t going to be enough for a finishing blow, he looked on with satisfaction as the creature let out a pained cry and stumbled back another half-step.  One more push, he thought.  Gritting his teeth against the exertion, he swapped Model: Spear out for Model: Shrike, launching a flurry of razor-sharp leaves, followed by two crescent blades of pure mana directly at the struggling monster.

It roared again, this time its echoing voice filled with power.  Stone cracked around it, and Kyle was thrown back nearly twenty meters, despite trying to stabilize himself with Aura Force.  When he got a good look at the hodag, he saw its scales glowing with emerald light as their mana-resistant properties were pushed to even greater heights.  The leaves had been blown away entirely, and the crescent blades that boasted some of Kyle’s highest attack power barely did more than crack a few scales.  The remnants of the sundew petals were similarly gone, and the progress of the conceptual Death had all but halted.

For a moment, Kyle was reevaluating whether or not this was an opponent that he could truly fight.  He still had a few cards to play, but it had taken some of his best shots and still looked to be in the fight.  That is, until its injured arm dangled uselessly to its side, and the scales lost much of their luster.  It was a one-time move to deal with the attacks, Kyle realized as a grin spread across his face.  The grin was quickly wiped off when C.H.A.D.D. spoke.

[DR. MAYHEW, WITH THE MANA-RESISTANCE REMOVED, I HAVE A BETTER SENSE OF THE CREATURE’S ABILITIES.  THERE ARE POTENT NEUROTOXINS IN ITS TAIL AND FANGS THAT HAVE BEGUN TO SECRETE AFTER ITS SKILL ACTIVATED.  FURTHERMORE -]

C.H.A.D.D. was interrupted as the creature lowered its torso and lifted its hindquarters, shooting a storm of barbed fur at Kyle from its lower back.  He responded with Aura Force while throwing himself to the side, only to find that the barbs almost entirely ignored his skill.  He barely managed to switch to Model: Shield, and tucked behind it as best he could.

Two nasty cuts opened on his right leg which didn’t make it behind the shield in time.  It was impressive enough that the mana-rejecting properties of the barbs ignored Aura Force and managed to cut through Enhanced Carapace and Iron Will, but his eyes widened when he tried healing the injuries with Adaptive Regeneration.  The wounds were far more difficult to mend than they should have been, reminding him of the cuts from the shrike matriarch.  Conceptual sharpness?  No, that’s not it…  A wounding concept, perhaps?

Whatever it was, it took time to heal.  Time they couldn’t afford as the hodag leapt at them once again, pushing off with its hind legs in an attempt to gore Kyle with its horns.  Mind racing, Kyle raised his shield as he withdrew one of the barriers he made to fit inside Storm Shelter.  The spiral construct wrapped around him, and the creature smashed through it, striking Model: Shield and breaking through the reinforced wood.  Kyle already launched himself backward with the force of the blow to lessen the effect, gladly trading the prosthesis for its hidden ability.

More scarlet petals exploded out from within the broken barrier, wrapping around the hodag’s horns and head.  With Cultivate still unavailable from Heart of Creation’s use, he couldn’t empower the acid like before, but it still burned and sizzled against the hodag’s weakened scales and vulnerable eyes.  A quick use of Aura Force at the same time sent the splinters from the construct and Model: Shield flying into the now-scaleless arm, shoving the hodag off balance as the wood bit into the vulnerable flesh.

The hodag seemed unbothered by the damage, instead using the momentum from its stumble to lash out with its tail, now stinging like a scorpion trying to impale him.  Without another option, Kyle used Storm Shelter.  He could have attempted to dodge, but if the tail was using the same concepts as the barbs, any direct hits could have been debilitating.  Coupled with a neurotoxin that would take time to neutralize, it simply wasn’t a risk that he could afford.

Seeing its tail skitter off the barrier with limited effect, the hodag didn’t waste more than a couple of attacks before changing tactics.  Mana began to build in the creature’s chest as it prepared for the other signature skill of the hodags.  The roar.

Without a connection from Parasitic Resonance, Kyle had no way to disrupt the skill.  Activating Heart of Creation, he focused on the concept of Immutability as the hodag let out its scream.  A focused shockwave ripped into Storm Shelter and the surrounding area, pulverizing rock and shredding the few small shrubs in its path for over a kilometer.  More than just the destructive properties of the attack, it also carried with it a mana-rejecting component.  Environmental energies were entirely forced away, and even Storm Shelter struggled to maintain its shape and intent as the hodag’s scream tried to make the skill’s form unravel.

This was where Storm Shelter still being a D Grade skill showed its weakness.  While it was an incredible defensive skill, only made better by Kyle’s Willpower, it struggled to hold on against mid-tier C Grade attacks, particularly at this level.  Originally he’d considered upgrading it after his healing skills.  This fight reinforced that it needed to be prioritized alongside Parasitic Resonance.  He didn’t need it all the time, but when he did, he needed it to be able to hold on.

As the dust settled, Kyle dropped the barrier to begin its cooldown period from using Heart of Creation.  The hodag appeared to be about spent after using its roar, and Kyle needed to seize this opportunity to make a decisive blow.  Fortunately, he had all the setup he needed.  Throughout their fight, the hodag had inhaled pollen and seeds from the modified durians, had been struck with Model: Cannon, and its arm had been peppered with debris from all the wooden constructs it destroyed.  When its scales were working at full capacity, that wasn’t necessarily a problem for the creature.  Now, with the mana-resistant properties all but expended, Kyle had a window.

Reaching out with Aspect of the Tree: Branch, Kyle activated Cultivate.  The skill’s cooldown just ended, and he once again called on Heart of Creation to empower the ability.  The hodag let out a cry of pain and surprise as roots and vines erupted from all of the plant life that struck it, using its own body and energy as fuel for growth.  As it was now, it didn’t have the fight in it to resist.  Letting out a deep breath, Kyle extended tendrils of Parasitic Resonance toward the creature.  The fight was over.

At least, it should have been.  C.H.A.D.D. flashed a warning, and Kyle leapt out of the way just in time to avoid being cleaved head to toe by a massive blade.  Mangalica crashed to the ground like a meteor, covered in wounds that wriggled and writhed as they knit themselves together.  The orc was hardly recognizable, having swollen to nearly twice his already gargantuan size, and all semblance of reason was gone from his face.  He swung his sword at Kyle, sending a pressure wave that knocked him off his feet and out of range of Parasitic Resonance.

“What happened to Duroc?” Kyle asked, not daring to take his eyes off the battlefield.

[HE IS COMING,] C.H.A.D.D. replied, calming Kyle’s nerves.

Taking a defensive stance, Kyle watched the renegade War Chieftain, ready for any attack.  With the hodag nearly dead, he just needed to hold on until Duroc arrived, and they could finish this fight for good.  At least, that was Kyle’s plan.

Mangalica turned to the dying creature behind him, and in an entirely unexpected move, thrust his arm into its chest up to the elbow.  Kyle’s look of shock matched the hodag’s as it let out a cry of pain, then went limp.  Its body began to wriggle and writhe, and Kyle looked on in horror as the hodag’s flesh and remaining mana was drawn into Mangalica, leaving only a withered husk behind.

The massive orc swelled to even greater size, and his blade, which Kyle now recognized was made from bone, grew alongside him.  Mangalica turned toward Kyle, letting out a snarl before charging directly at him with speed entirely out of place for its size.

Crap.
