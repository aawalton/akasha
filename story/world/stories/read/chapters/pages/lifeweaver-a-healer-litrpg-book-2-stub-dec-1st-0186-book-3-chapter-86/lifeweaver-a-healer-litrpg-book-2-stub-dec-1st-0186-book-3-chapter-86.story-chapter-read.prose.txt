The strange wave of energy that followed the glowing line passed over the group just as the hole into what was basically the underworld opened. Luke watched the others react, the life going out of them. Ray, Mateo, and Penny collapsed, and he found, so did Luke. The experience reminded him of some mech movie he'd seen once. When the mech piloted by the main character took serious damage, all manner of lights flashed red with warning inside the cockpit, and alarms blared from every speaker. This was like that, only inside. It affected mana, of course, not the physical body, but after becoming Integrated, mana was as important as the blood flowing through their arteries and veins. Without it, everything shut down. Consciousness fled from the others in the blink of an eye, but not Luke's, not right away. The Greater System Weave spread out before him, as well as the weave of their own system, but he ignored them, unable to focus on much of anything.

The healing mana in his Meta-Heart rushed out, converting back to regular mana on its own in a way he hadn't ever seen before. That bought him enough time to pull out the Mana Orb he'd been filling up. It didn't contain much, but it was enough to keep everything from going dark. Once he'd taken in everything, drinking greedily from it like he was dying of thirst, a sound broke through. Someone screaming.

"Luke!"

Blinking, he pushed himself off the floor.

"Luke, please!"

"Hmm?" he grunted, unable to focus his vision.

It was Lemira. She was calling out to him. "Luke! The portal is closing! We have to go!"

He sat up, looked around dumbly, and blinked. It was as if he'd been hit on the head with a sledgehammer. There was something else, too, something different. He couldn't quite say what it was, but something had happened to his mind. That wasn't all. In his current state, his body was pretty much degrading by the second. He needed to fix that, and fix it soon, or his friends would start to feel it too. "What happened?"

Then he saw the others. They were not doing so well. As if on instinct, he reached out to Lemira with a thread of mana. For some reason, perhaps because she wasn't human, she'd been spared. She didn't have much mana, but he took as much as he dared, added more from himself, and distributed it to the others. They were all fine and alive, but with their mana zeroed, it would have taken them a long while to get back up on their own. Even with this little boost, they were looking at hours of unconsciousness, he thought. And what about everyone else? They'd be even worse off. This was the first time he'd seen someone with nothing on their mana bar. Until that moment, he'd thought it meant death. He had toed that line a few times, but never like this.

To his horror, he saw Lemira was right. The portal was closing. If they missed this opportunity, they'd need to find and kill nine more greater demons. That could take days, or even weeks. Unacceptable.

"Stay with them," Luke croaked.

Lemira stopped yelling. "What?"

"Pour mana potions down their throats. When they wake up, go back to the guild. You have to help them, Lemira. Use Penny's orbs!"

"You can't go down there alone," she said, shaking her head.

"I'll make it. This was the Singers' win condition. They told me so themselves. They'll push now, and we can't let them."

"But-"

"No. You told me we need to beat the demons first."

"Yes, well-"

"Then do as I say. I'll beat the demons, but I don't want to return to everyone I know dead or taken captive by the Singers. Do you understand? You need to stay here, or we'll lose everything."

She stood there for a moment, looking down at her hands. "What if I fail?"

"You won't."

"My father-"

"Fuck your father, Lemira. You're with us now. I believe in you. Tell me you can do this."

She looked up at him, her gaze first full of shock, then determination. "I can do it."

"Good," Luke said. "I'll see you soon. Help them, Lemira!"

With that, he crawled over to the ever-diminishing hole connecting Earth to Infernal Flux.

New Quest: Find a way to make demonkind retreat from your home system.

Reward: A title befitting your accomplishment.

In his near-death state, Luke landed with little to no grace, thudding against what turned out to be dark-brown cobblestones. At least he didn't hit his head this time. Since his senses didn't detect immediate danger, he closed his eyes for a little while and waited for mana to regenerate. The smell of the place was what got to him first. Not sulphur and brimstone, like you might expect. No, this was different, almost like the sour stink of unwashed bodies and dirt, maybe even with hints of blood. Not a good first scent. In the far distance, Luke picked up sounds. Groans, he thought, and the clanging of metal. A faint voice shouted something, and another cried out. This was not a good place.

Before even opening his eyes, Luke reached out to the system weave and found that he, with a little concentration, could reach the echo threads leading back to his friends. That was a relief. The hole had closed behind him, but Luke wasn't trapped. He could get out if he ran out of options. In doing so, he would fail the quest, and the consequences of that would be felt throughout New York and, if Lemira was to be believed, the rest of the world. It was a heavy weight of responsibility to carry, and Luke was glad he'd increased his physical attributes enough that his shoulders had strengthened a little.

Like a teen having to get out of bed in the morning before school, Luke groaned and, with an immense push of willpower, sat up, opening his eyes. He wasn't sure what he expected, but it wasn't this. A cell. Maybe four strides across, the room was all stone, except for a door of some unknown metal set in one wall. The door looked solid, except for a small opening showing the corridor beyond. It might have been large enough for someone smaller than Luke to crawl through, but metal bars made that an impossibility.

"Weird," Luke whispered as he got to his feet, swaying a little.

There was nothing about a time limit in the brief quest description, but he felt a powerful urge to be quick about it. All his friends were half-dead up there, with both demons and Singers about. A bad time all around. In the dungeon he'd just left, with the pink portal, Luke hadn't felt the same need to get through it as fast as possible. He wasn't sure why, but this time he couldn't linger. He wished for a few hours to meditate and bring his mana back up to acceptable levels, and he wished for his friends to be there with him. More than anything, he wished he knew how Hannah was doing. There had been nothing but silence from her since she appeared for that brief moment in Luke's dungeon. No echo, no nothing. It was beginning to worry him.

That was when it hit him. Like a wave of dismay, it all came back to him. Pain. He'd caused so much pain with his healing before he learned to numb it. The time he had failed. Oh, Nate. Nate, Nate, Nate. The first friend Luke had lost, and it had been due to incompetence. If he had known then what he knew now, healing Nate would have been as easy as snapping his fingers. Luke sank to his knees and brought his hands up to cradle his face, panting as everything crashed into him at once. Not just the healing, but the hurt. He'd hurt so many people. Killed. Several times he'd killed, calling it justice. Leslie, the Solarian, and the pixie he didn't even recall the name of. Executions, plain and simple. Again and again, he saw their deaths, felt the life drain out of them. Other Integrated. Not humans, but sapient species nonetheless. Lifeweavers, so many Lifeweavers. What little food he had in him threatened to come back up, but he struggled to keep it back, swallowing again and again as tears streamed down his cheeks. This was painful. Too painful. And his parents? It was like he had forgotten about them the moment he touched the orb, as if something had blocked them, had kept everything out.

That was it.

The mental block. Guide's gift to humanity, a way to cope with their new absurd reality. It was gone. That was the Singers' weapon. Not draining their mana. That would have been too simple. No, this was worse. So much worse. It could disable everyone forever, could drive them mad. Luke fought against the dread and hopelessness of his own actions and Earth's future. His valiant effort to keep food down failed, but he gritted his teeth afterwards. No, even with everything he had done, Luke would not give up. Could not give up. Pushing, he got a foot under him. The fight was not over. His friends still needed him. He could still go on.
