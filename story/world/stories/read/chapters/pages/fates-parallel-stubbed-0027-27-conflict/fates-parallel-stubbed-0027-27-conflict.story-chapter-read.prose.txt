It didn’t take Lee Jia much convincing to get Xin Wei and Guan Yi onto her side.

“We’d be glad to join your group, Miss Lee. Yan Zhihao is insufferable, and the mockery I’ve received from my countrymen for practicing body cultivation has gotten tiresome.”

Lee Jia was a bit taken aback by how easily Xin had agreed. She didn’t know him that well, and thought it would take some negotiation.

“Mockery? I thought that Yan and his friends were boycotting Ienaga’s classes because they think she’s messing with them about how to awaken.”

Xin chuckled wryly and gave Lee Jia a condescending look.

“Of course they are, Miss Lee, but they aren’t going to say that. It would be tantamount to admitting that they have given up on body cultivation because you defeated Yan in a duel. No, in order to save face they are now pretending that it was never worth their time in the first place. Yan challenging you was a show of magnanimity, you returned the favor by cheating and making unreasonable demands—that’s their story now.”

Lee Jia stared incredulously at Xin, looking between him and Guan Yi to see if he was joking. Guan Yi was, of course, impassive.

“You’re kidding right? That was the third most stressful week of my life and he’s pretending that he was doing me a favor? Rrrgh, I could just go over there and kick his ass all over again.”

“Welcome to the social norms of the Empire, Miss Lee. I assure you that we find them just as irritating as you do—we’ve simply learned to live with it. Now why don’t you introduce us to the rest of your group?”

Lee Jia returned with Xin and Guan to see that Kim Yongsun had been integrated officially into their group. Xin Wei’s eyebrows rose as she introduced Eunae and Dae.

“My, you keep rather impressive company, Miss Lee. Pleased to meet you all, I am Xin Wei and my stone-faced companion here is Guan Yi, though I understand many of you are already acquainted.”

Once introductions were finished, they discussed how to split their rewards, and the group composition. They agreed that since the offered rewards were unknown, they would decide that when the time came. Any cores they obtained they would distribute as evenly as they could after the expedition had ended.

Their group had eight people altogether—Lee Jia, Takeda Rika, and Guan Yi would be the front line engaging foes directly, while Hyeong Daesung, Seong Eunae, and Kim Yongsun supported from a distance with spells and ranged attacks. Xin Wei and An Eui were given special roles.

Since Xin’s spiritual arts were focused on support and defense, he would use his powers to control the battlefield and provide support as needed. Eui was the heaviest hitter, but the backlash of her power meant that she had to be held in reserve until the right moment. In the meantime, she had an eclectic skill set, and could freely move between roles as needed.

As they finalized their battle plans, Xin Wei brought up a final issue.

“If Yan Zhihao leads the Qin group, and Miss Hayakawa leads the Yamato group, who leads ours? Also, does that make us the Goryeo group? We’ve nearly got a two to one ratio of half-spirits to humans.”

Lee Jia brought a palm to her face and shook her head.

“Close, Xin, but we’re humans too, remember?”

Xin shrugged, his expression not quite apologetic.

“I’m trying. At least I remembered not to call you beastkin, this time.”

Before Lee Jia could get into a yelling match with Xin, Dae stepped forward.

“I don’t think we necessarily need a leader, do we? As long as we understand our roles, we can simply make decisions by council, no?”

Lee Jia shook her head.

“Actually, it’s probably best if we do have someone who can arbitrate any disagreements and make emergency decisions.”

As much as Lee Jia wanted to agree with Dae, she’d seen too many street gangs fall apart to infighting and losing cohesion at the first sign of trouble.

“What about Eunae? She’s a—um, from a family of leaders.”

Lee Jia caught herself before bringing up Eunae’s royalty. Kim Yongsun nodded in agreement.

“I will follow Miss Seong.”

Lee Jia frowned, glancing between Kim Yongsun and Guan Yi. She wondered which of the two boys was more taciturn. Eunae grimaced and stared at the ground as the group's eyes all fell on her.

“I actually get flustered rather easily in stressful situations. I don’t think I would be able to keep my composure in an emergency. I’d be a poor leader, regardless of my status.”

Lee Jia raised an eyebrow at Eunae’s demurral. Eunae’s charisma was like a force of nature, and even Eui was using her given name within the first day of their formal introductions. Lee Jia really had thought that she was the best option.

“What about Jia?”

Jia’s head jerked around to stare at Eui in shock. The rest of the group was also looking at her curiously. Eui shrugged.

“Think about it. Xin doesn’t really know anyone else here, and I don’t give a fuck about any of you. But I do trust Jia to have my back, and she’s the only one here who knows everyone. Well, everyone but Kim, but he only cares what Eunae thinks.”

Eui’s suggestion was met with mixed reactions. Eunae and Takeda smiled, while Dae and Xin looked thoughtful. Kim Yongsun seemed affronted, and Lee Jia was blushing furiously at all the attention. Guan Yi didn’t react at all—damn him—but he was the first to speak.

“It is an unusual suggestion, but there is merit in it. As you pointed out, we are a diverse group and Miss Lee is the common connection between us. I propose that we simply vote between Miss Seong and Miss Lee.”

“Tsk, you were supposed to nominate me, Stoneface you traitorous brute. I’ve also been groomed for leadership, and my role as a supporter makes me an ideal strategist.”

Guan shrugged impassively and Xin Wei was added to the list of options. With a quick show of hands, the group cast their votes. Only two voted in favor of Eunae—Lee Jia and Kim Yongsun. The rest voted for Lee Jia, except for Xin who received his only vote from himself.

“Stoneface, I’m going to slap you.”

Xin Wei’s grumbling went unheard as Lee Jia’s skin prickled with anxiety. How did this happen? In this group that had multiple nobles and a literal princess, how was she the leader? Who was she? Just Lee Jia—just some girl from the streets of Nayeong. Such a nobody that she had to borrow the family name of a gang boss because it was the closest she had to family. Jia was startled by the feeling of Eui’s tail poking her in the side.

“Stop panicking, Jia. You’re gonna do fine.”

Jia’s throat felt dry, but as Eui gave her hand an encouraging squeeze, her expression became resolute. If her friends were willing to trust her with leadership, then she would just have to do her best not to let them down.

The next morning they gathered outside the walls of the Academy campus and prepared to set out. Lee Jia looked out at the wilderness surrounding them and scratched her head.

“So I guess we should just head north towards the mountain? How do you track down magical beasts anyway? I was just a regular mortal before coming here, so my only experience with beasts and spirits was avoiding them at all cost.”

Eui nodded in agreement.

“Same. If I felt so much as a weird shiver or prickly skin I would turn around and run.”

Eui had much more hands-on experience than Jia, having lived nearly a year by herself in the wilderness. Kim Yongsun gave them a strange look.

“You mean you were only mages, right? Actually, I’ve been meaning to ask—Miss An, isn’t that a mark of exile? I’ve never heard of an exiled mage—rogue mages are generally considered too dangerous. If you were exiled, how did you get invited to the academy? Does nobody else find it strange?”

Lee Jia blinked at Kim Yongsun. That was more than she had ever heard him speak before. Also, she had forgotten that he was the only one here who didn’t know that she had been unawakened before attending the academy. Jia glanced nervously at Eui, but it was Dae who spoke up first.

“U-um, I imagine that’s a bit of a sensitive subject, hehe. It’s a bit rude to ask directly like that, don’t you think?”

Eui pre-empted a reply from Kim.

“I don’t mind. I’ll answer if he wants to know.”

Her tone was a bit confrontational and Jia was worried that she might pick a fight. Her worries only increased when Eui took out one of her knives and pointed it at Kim.

“I won’t speak for Jia, but I was unawakened before I arrived here—this is a mark of exile, and I wasn’t invited. I was exiled for killing a mage, I found this place by killing a mage, and I’ll kill as many mages as I have to in order to stay. Is that good enough for you?”

Eui stared down the edge of her knife at Kim Yongsun with a cold expression. It was a side of her that she hadn’t seen since the first week, and Jia stood by her friend nervously as she checked the group’s reactions.

Takeda and Xin seemed the most at ease, looking on with quiet amusement. Dae had gone pale, and was looking frantically between Eui and Kim with a panicked expression. Eunae had a carefully neutral expression, and Guan just looked like that normally.

Kim Yongsun for his part stood his ground with arms crossed, his tail whipping angrily and his ears set back against his head as he snarled.

“It certainly explains a lot. And you, Lee? Are you a pretender as well?”

Lee Jia felt a cold sweat forming as Kim confronted her. She hadn’t expected to face conflict like this so soon, but she steeled her resolve and glared up at Kim Yongsun, silently cursing her diminutive stature.

“Pretender!? What’s that supposed to mean? If you’re asking if I was unawakened, yes I was. And so what!? Eui and I went through hardships that a noble like you couldn’t even imagine to get here! If you have a problem with either of us you can feel free to stay behind and meditate. The rest of us will go on without you.”

Kim flicked his tail and scoffed.

“I was willing to tolerate being led by some lesser cat clansman out of deference to Mistress Seong, but I will not subordinate myself to petty crimin—”

“Enough!”

Eunae’s shout pierced through the air and commanded absolute attention. Her eyes had opened slightly and she was staring directly at Kim Yongsun’s nose, as close to eye-contact as Lee Jia had ever seen her get, intentionally.

“Kim Yongsun, Jia and Eui are my friends. Every insult you give to them is an insult to me, and to the entire Seong family. Am I understood?”

Kim hesitated for a moment, teeth clenched, but after a moment his deference to Eunae won out over his disdain for Jia and Eui. He bowed to Eunae.

“My apologies. I spoke out of turn.”

It occurred to Lee Jia that it was Eui who he should be apologizing to, but didn’t want to press the issue and cause more conflict. She smiled and nodded gratefully to Eunae before getting the group moving towards the mountains, eager to move on before any more incidents.

“I don’t recall agreeing to be your damn friend, princ—OW!”

Eui’s grumbling was stopped by Jia poking her in the side hard, with claws. Jia’s cheeks were puffed out as she glared furiously at Eui.

“S-sorry…”

Eui apologized while rubbing her side. Those claws were seriously sharp.

“Ow…”

Once they had gotten moving, Dae was more than happy to explain the intricacies of tracking magical beasts.

“Magical beasts and elementals are formed by—and attracted to—high concentrations of mana. Spirits are a bit more complicated, but they are also usually found where mana is plentiful. With our natural mana sense, mages are at a bit of an advantage when it comes to finding such places but mana is important to all forms of cultivation, so the other disciplines have also developed ways of sensing the ambient mana.”

Lee Jia nodded along, wondering when exactly the academy disciples had started using the word ‘cultivation’ to refer to all three disciplines. She blamed the Qin majority.

“Since everyone here has mana sense, we can just rely on that. The trick to finding high concentrations is following the flow of mana. It doesn’t move quite the way you would expect a fluid to, so there’s some complicated nuance to—”

“So we go that way?”

Lee Jia was pointing along a concentration of mana that flowed between the mountain and the academy campus. As Dae had said, mana didn’t flow like liquid or gas—it would fluctuate in strange ways that didn’t respond to any physical or metaphysical phenomenon that Lee Jia could identify. The one exception was that the flow would consistently bend toward immortal practitioners.

Dae scratched his cheek and chuckled.

“Ehehe, that’s, uh, correct. You’ve identified a leyline, Miss Lee. Most likely artificial, being drawn in from the source by the academy’s collection formati—”

“Ok, let’s go that way then.”

Lee Jia knew better by now than to let Dae ramble past the point too much.

“I-Indeed, hehe. Do you mind if I ask how you knew to distinguish the leyline from the ambient mana?”

“I don’t know. It was moving differently, I guess. Like most mana is just there—kind of flowing aimlessly and moving around without purpose. The leyline felt like it was going somewhere, like it had a purpose.”

“I see. That’s some rather impressive insight.”

Dae scratched his chin thoughtfully while Takeda whispered something about talented people.

“Miss Lee, have you ever felt an impression like that before you awakened? Like there was a path that felt safer or more dangerous somehow?”

Lee Jia nodded, recalling the countless times her intuition had saved her in the past.

“Sure. I could usually guess if somebody was a mage, or if there was a magical beast on the road ahead, and when I left Nayeong I just kind of knew which road I should take.”

Dae nodded sagely.

“Latent mana sense. You probably sensed the connection between the token and the shield formation. I doubt that I could have tracked my token here without a formation, though. You did it as a mortal through raw intuition. That’s—”

Before Dae could finish his rather loud thinking, Lee Jia held up a hand to signal the group to stop. She sniffed the air briefly, frowning.

“I smell blood. Fresh, I think—this way.”

Lee Jia led the group towards the smell. The terrain between the academy and the mountain to the north was hilly, and while most of the vegetation had shrivelled away in the dead of winter, there were still small cliffs and hills that could hide predators. As the group crept over a nearby hill, they spotted the source of the blood.

Several deer corpses were strewn about at the bottom of the hill. They had been brutally savaged, some of them dismembered beyond recognition. Lee Jia could see signs that there had been more, but that they had scattered and fled into the trees, most likely in response to whatever had done this.

With Lee Jia leading the way, the group approached slowly, keeping an eye out for any sign of whatever had attacked the deer. Lee Jia examined the first corpse, finding that it was still warm despite the freezing winter air. In fact, it was beyond warm, it was smoldering hot. Now that she looked closer, there wasn’t as much blood on the ground as she would have expected. Inspecting the wounds, she saw that they had been cauterized and were still painfully hot to the touch.

“Whatever did this is still close. Some kind of magical beast using fire. I don’t get it, how come none of them have been eaten?”

While Lee Jia had expected an answer from their resident know-it-all Dae, it was actually Takeda who answered first.

“Beast youkai don’t need food, but they keep the instincts of whatever animal they awakened from. They tend to be aggressively territorial, but mindless. They’ll kill anything they come across, even other youkai. The better question is—if they did this recently, where did they go?”

Lee Jia closed her eyes and focused on the sounds and smells. Her ears twitched at every rustle of leaves or crack of a twig as her allies searched the area. The smell of blood was strong, as was the smell of the deer who had been grazing here before they were attacked, but there was another smell lingering—harder to place, but strange and unnatural. Like a combination of smoldering coals and burnt meat with a slight tinge of...rotten eggs?

She tried to track the smell, but something wasn’t right. It was as if it had appeared out of nowhere, right in the midst of the deer, and then vanished without a trace. Lee Jia felt her hair start to stand on end as she felt a pervasive sense of wrongness. The same intuition she’d been discussing with Dae earlier was screaming danger at her. On a hunch, she focused exclusively on her mana sense, spreading it as much as she could.

Almost immediately she sensed something out of the ordinary. Not an aura, but the mana around her was flowing strangely. It was being pulled towards the ground in the same way that it was normally attracted towards her and the others. The effect seemed to be growing stronger by the second, almost as if—Lee Jia’s eyes widened and she shouted a warning.

“Underground! They’re still here!”

Lee Jia barely had time to dive out of the way as the ground erupted underneath her. The creature’s teeth and claws lashed out violently at the space that she had just vacated before it landed back on the ground and snarled at her.

The creature resembled a wolf, with ashen fur and glowing red eyes. It was several sizes too large for a regular wolf, and its front paws had too many claws, which were long, black, and bladed. The inside of the beast’s mouth and the edges of the obsidian claws were glowing red-hot, and white-hot liquid fire seemed to seep from between the wolf’s black teeth and drip from its maw.

Lee Jia felt a mixture of dread and excitement as she faced the beast. A confusing mix of anticipation for her first battle, the intimidation of the massive creature, and the surge of adrenaline from her narrow escape of the wolf’s ambush.

Lee Jia was about to order her team to get into formation when her thoughts were interrupted by the sound of several more earthy eruptions.

All her other emotions were overwhelmed by fear as four more beasts—slightly smaller than the first, but otherwise identical—burst from the ground, surrounding them. There was a brief, tense standoff as the wolf-like creatures hemmed them in, and then…

Chaos.
