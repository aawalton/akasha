I chewed my lower lip as I stared at my reflection, frustration continuing to build within me. I’d tried on basically every single combination of leggings and t-shirts that I owned, and not a single one felt right.

It wasn’t. That was why. I wanted to wear something nice to Duncan’s house, something that I looked good in, but I couldn’t. I’d made the mistake of telling Mom I was going to the gym, so I had to choose something I could work out in. Otherwise, she’d realise I’d lied to her.

My stomach turned. That would be bad. If she suspected I wasn’t actually going to the gym, she might follow me when I went out, I realised. Or perhaps she’d swing by the gym to check whether I was there, and I wasn’t sure what would happen when she figured out that I wasn’t, but I knew it wouldn’t be good.

I had to make sure that wouldn’t happen. I couldn’t give her any reason to become suspicious, and that meant I had to wear leggings and a t-shirt even though I really wanted to wear a dress.

A disappointed sigh slipped from my lips. It wasn’t that bad, I tried to tell myself. I was wearing the same outfit I pretty much always wore, and Duncan had seen me in it so many times before. It wasn’t a big deal that I wasn’t wearing a dress.

But it felt like it. I wasn’t going to Duncan’s just for a normal hangout. It was maybe a date, and that meant I wanted to look better than I normally did. I wanted to look pretty for a change, and I wasn’t sure if that was even possible. It definitely wasn’t if I went with the outfit I had on.

I pulled the shirt over my head with an irritated huff and threw it onto my bed, where it joined the rest of the ones I’d tried on and decided against. My eyes narrowed as I took in the mess. I’d need to tidy everything away and make my bed before I went out, I realised. It was still early. I had a few hours until I had to leave, but I had to try to remember to do it because Mom would get annoyed if I didn’t.

Normally, I tried to make the bed when I got up, but by the time I finally dragged myself out of bed, I hadn’t had the energy. It was too much of a mess, and all I could do was stare blankly at the sheet that I’d somehow managed to pull almost completely off the mattress before making my way into the bathroom.

Then, I almost fell asleep in the shower. I hadn’t been that tired when I was still in bed. Granted, I was exhausted, but that was different. I wasn’t actually sleepy. I was more just… drained.

I hadn’t slept well. It felt like I’d only managed to get an hour or so, if that. For some reason, I just kept waking up, and I couldn’t fall back to sleep. I tried, but it wasn’t that easy. Sleep seemed to elude me. I’d grabbed at it desperately, but it had slipped through my fingers.

My normal distractions didn’t help either. I’d tried going to the other world and spending some time at the Academy, but I could barely focus when I was there. I kept getting preoccupied by my own thoughts, my worries about what would happen when I got to Duncan’s house, and I could hardly keep track of what was going on around me.

Seth had noticed it. He’d seen just how distracted I was, and he’d asked me if I was alright a few times. I managed to play it off at first, but he always paid too much attention to me, and I wasn’t used to it. He knew something was wrong, and I didn’t know what to do, so I just told him I wasn’t feeling well.

The lie made me feel guilty. Even as I stood in my room, staring down at the small mountain of clothes on my bed, I felt bad, but I hadn’t known what else to do. I just needed an excuse for why I was so quiet, and I thought that would work.

It had worked, though. Kind of. He’d nodded as if it made sense and asked me if I needed to see the doctor. I’d said I was fine, but he still looked concerned, and that made me feel even worse.

Guilt pulled at me again. Seth had been paying even more attention to me since then. I hadn’t gone back to the world; I didn’t want to risk him realising there was something else going on, but I knew he’d been more attentive than normal. He was worried about me, and I hated that.

Dizziness rose within me, making the room sway around me. The urge to let it pull me into the other world tempted me. Part of me longed to go back to the Academy and hang out with Seth. I wanted to show him that I was normal, that everything was alright, and he didn’t need to worry, but I pushed it away.

It wouldn’t work. I knew it wouldn’t. No matter how much I wanted everything to be fine, I would still be distracted. I still needed to figure out what I was going to wear to Duncan’s, and even though I didn’t need to be at his until three, I had to get ready.

Pressing my lips together into a scowl, I stared down at my clothes again. I could do better than that, I decided before spinning on my heel and marching towards my chest of drawers. I had to be able to do better than the outfits I’d already tried on. They were so boring, so normal, and I wanted something else.

There must be something I could wear that looks good and won’t make Mom suspicious, I told myself as I reached into the drawer. My fingers trailed over the shirts inside before finding a hoodie, and an idea began to take form in my mind.

Maybe that would work. If I wore a hoodie, especially one of the baggier ones, I could also wear a dress and hide it underneath. It wouldn’t be too hard to tuck it up into my leggings, and I could leave the house without Mom realising what I was actually wearing. Then, once I got out of sight of the house, I could take off the hoodie and my leggings and stash them in my bag or something.

Excitement fluttered in my chest, but it didn’t last long. Moments later, it was crushed as I glanced out the window at the sky. There was not a cloud in sight, and normally, I would have been happy about that, but it just disappointed me.

It was the middle of summer, and although it wasn’t as hot as it had been a few weeks ago, it was still too warm for me to go out in a jumper. If I did, I was pretty sure I’d be sweating before I reached the end of the drive, and that would be bad. Even if I took off the hoodie, I’d still be sweaty and shiny by the time I got to Duncan’s.

My eyes widened, and I bit my lip again. Wait… I told myself as a new plan began to form. It was still pretty hot outside, and a dress would be cooler than leggings and a t-shirt. Maybe I could just pretend I’d decided to wear a dress because I was too hot, and I had my workout clothes in my bag. I would need to actually pack them, just in case Mom demanded I show her, but she might buy that excuse.

Plus, she had told me I should put more effort into looking feminine. Apparently, I looked like a slob when I wore leggings, and they did nothing to help my figure. They make my legs look chunky or spindly, depending on Mom’s mood, and boys don’t like that. She’d told me on multiple occasions that I should wear dresses more, so if she asked, I could always say I was just taking that on board.

It was risky. I knew it was. Mom might still get suspicious, and I’d have to handle it carefully, but I’d already decided that was what I was going to do. The thought of being able to wear something nice was too tempting.

A grin tugged at my lips as I moved towards my wardrobe and pulled the doors open. Luckily, Dad had already washed most of the clothes I’d brought with me to Scotland, so I had lots of options. Too many, if anything.

My gaze roamed the row, taking them all in. I couldn’t wear anything too nice, I told myself. That would make it harder to convince Mom I was just going to the gym, and it might make things weird with Duncan too, especially if he had just been joking when he called it a date.

Anxiety caused my muscles to clench, and nausea bubbled in the pit of my stomach. That was one of the things that kept distracting me when I was at the Academy. I found myself recalling every single text Duncan had sent me over the month I was in Scotland and trying to work out whether he’d been flirting with me.

He had. That much was obvious, but he was a flirty person. Maybe it was just normal, and I was reading too much into it. I’d almost managed to convince myself of that during the night, but I couldn’t quite do it. There was a lingering doubt that stopped me, and I wasn’t sure if that was just because I didn’t want to believe it. I wanted to believe that Duncan might actually be into me, despite how unlikely I knew that was.

A buzzing came from my bed, and my head snapped around. Fear spiked in my heart as I rushed towards the bed, throwing clothes out of the way as I searched for my phone. Immediately, I was worried that it was a message from Duncan. I was so scared that he’d texted me saying that he’d changed his mind and didn’t actually want to see me or ever talk to me again.

My hands trembled as I finally found my phone and lifted it quickly, but it was just a text from Phoebe. Relief slammed into me, making my knees weak, and I sank onto my bed as I read the message.

Okay, I’m back. How’s it going?

I hesitated, glancing towards my wardrobe before typing a reply.

Not toooo bad, I wrote. I think I might be able to get away with wearing a dress.

Oh yay! read Phoebe’s immediate response. Which one are you thinking?

My fingers twitched over my phone screen, and I paused before writing, No idea. I’ve not even started looking yet.

Ohhhh what about that blue one with the flowers???

I knew exactly which dress Phoebe was referring to, but there was no way I could wear that. It was too… short? Too tight, maybe? I’d felt kind of weird the last time I’d worn it, and I wasn’t entirely sure why. It just made me uncomfortable, and I didn’t want to feel like that. I was already nervous enough. I didn’t need anything else to add to that.

Mmmmm I don’t know. It doesn’t really fit me anymore, I typed back.

That felt like an excuse, and it was. I had grown a bit since I’d bought it during a shopping trip with Phoebe a few months before. I doubted my body had changed all that drastically since then, but it still felt different.

Okay, okay… how about that yellow one? That’s super cute, no? Phoebe suggested.

I looked towards my wardrobe again, my eyes seeking out the yellow dress. Phoebe was right. It was really cute. I’d worn it when I was in Scotland, and it had made me feel good about how I looked, so maybe I could wear that.

But the moment my gaze landed on it, I recoiled away. An image flashed behind my eyes, and I shook my head, trying to push it away, but it just sank its claws in deeper and refused to leave. I clenched my eyes shut, hoping that would help, but nothing did.

The dress was too similar to what I’d been wearing then. It was almost the exact same shade as my uniform for the Shooting Stars. It was another world, I told myself. It wasn’t real, and none of that actually happened. I tried desperately to convince myself of that, but I couldn’t stop seeing Phoebe’s dead body in my arms.

It had felt real. It had all felt so real.

Pain speared my heart as the image pulled me closer, sucking me into the world again. I’d held her as she died. I’d just watched as the life drained out of her, powerless to do anything to stop it. I’d tried. Warm crept over my hands at the memory of her blood flowing over my fingers. I’d tried to stop the bleeding, but it didn’t work.

My phone vibrated, dragging me back into reality. I opened my eyes, blinking away the tears that had begun to form in them, and glanced down at the screen.

Oh wait, no. What about that other blue one? The one that’s kind of denim-y?

I sniffed quietly and stared down at the text from Phoebe, my chest still aching slightly. For a moment, I couldn’t recall which dress she was talking about. Hazy memories of various blue dresses floated through my mind, the styles vastly different. Some were long, with full skirts and corseted bodices, but others were short and tight, the material a strange, almost reflective fabric that changed colours as the light hit it.

Some version of me had owned every single one of the dresses I was remembering, but not the real me. I’d never worn or even seen most of those dresses in my reality. The memories must have just been bleeding in from the other worlds, I realised as I shook my head and pushed myself off the bed.

My legs felt a little unsteady as I crossed my room, gripping my phone tightly in one hand to stop myself from dropping it. I wasn’t fully recovered from the heartbreak that had gripped me moments before, and my body felt weak, but I forced myself to keep moving until I reached my wardrobe. I flicked through the clothes hanging there, searching for Phoebe’s suggestion.

It didn’t take long for me to find it. Within seconds, my hand had closed around the hanger, and I pulled the dress out of the wardrobe, hooking it over the back of the door, so I could look at it properly.

How had I forgotten about it? I’d worn that dress so many times since I’d bought it a few months ago, and I knew I felt okay in it. It wasn’t too short or tight, but the waist was still cinched, and the neckline was kind of low. Not low enough for my mom to comment on it, but it showed a bit.

My fingers touched the skirt lightly as I cocked my head to the side. It was light and summery, I told myself. And I’d worn it a few times when we were in Scotland, so maybe that would help Mom believe I was just wearing it just for comfort.

Ohhh, I do like this one, I texted Phoebe, hesitating before adding. You don’t think he’ll think it’s weird that I’m wearing a dress, do you? I mean, I don’t normally wear one when I go over to his unless it’s a party or something.

I chewed my lip again, staring down at my phone as the three dots appeared to show Phoebe was typing. Anxiety simmered in my stomach as I waited for her response, but it didn’t take long.

No, not at all! I mean, you wear one sometimes, and I doubt he’s even noticed before. Like, I don’t think guys pay much attention to what we wear.

Yeah, that’s fair I guess, I replied.

I’d never really considered it too much, but Phoebe was probably right. I doubted they really cared that much, and Duncan had never mentioned it before. I couldn’t remember him ever saying anything about my clothes, so it was probably fine.

I hooked my hands into the waistband of my leggings, starting to pull them down before pausing and glancing up at the dress again. There was no point in putting it on before doing my hair and makeup, I realised. If I somehow managed to get makeup on it, I’d be so annoyed, and then I’d have to find a backup outfit to wear. That would be even harder than it had been to decide on the dress in the first place.

But I was only wearing a bra and leggings. I couldn’t exactly just do my hair and makeup whilst half-naked. I was alone in my room, and I had no idea what Mom was doing, so the chances of anyone seeing me were low, but I still didn’t want to do it. I knew if I spent that long staring at my reflection without a top on, my mood would be ruined. I’d get swept into a cycle of overthinking and criticising my body, and that would be bad.

Grabbing the first top I could reach from my bed, I pulled it on before dropping to the ground in front of my mirror and eyeing my reflection. There wasn’t much I could do about it. I was meant to be going to the gym, and there was no point in wearing makeup there. I’d just sweat it all off, so Mom would start asking questions if she realised I was wearing any.

Still, I had to do something about the bags under my eyes. The lack of sleep had made them even darker than they usually were, and I was starting to look like I’d been in a fight. Actually, I was pretty sure a black eye would be lighter than my bags.

I checked the time, hesitating before reaching for my moisturiser. It wasn’t even one yet, and Duncan had said to get to his for three. I had so my time to waste before I needed to leave, and it felt like it was too early for me to put any makeup on, but I was running out of things to do. I needed to stay busy, to keep moving. Otherwise, I knew I’d start overthinking again.

It was fine, I told myself, trying to take a deep breath. If I needed to, I could always touch up my makeup before I left, so it was okay. Actually, maybe it was better. I might see my mom at some point in the next few hours, and then she’d realise I’d been wearing makeup all day. Then, she wouldn’t think I’d put it on specifically to leave the house.

I looked up at the dress again as another realisation washed over me. It would be best if I changed into that soon. Then, I could wear it all day as well, and it would seem more normal. Mom would be less likely to question it when I went out.

But reluctance pulled at me. The thought of putting it on just to lounge around in my bed worried me. I could easily spill something on it, but I couldn’t exactly continue to wear what I was in. I was already dressed in workout clothes, and it would have been so suspicious if I changed out of them just to put them on again once I got to the gym.

My eyes flicked towards the door. That was a good point. I didn’t know what Mom was doing or even if she was at home, but I couldn’t risk it. I threw myself off the floor and pulled my clothes off before spotting my pyjamas in a ball next to my bed.

I felt myself grin as I grabbed them, moving as quietly as I could, just in case Mom was somewhere nearby. That was a better option. I could just wear my pyjamas until I was almost ready to go out, and then I’d get changed into the dress, so there was no chance of it getting dirty until then.

The smile remained on my face as I sat down in front of my mirror again and squinted at my reflection. That was better, I told myself before picking up my mascara. I should be able to get away with wearing some, but that was probably it. If I wore eyeliner or anything like that, Mom would definitely notice.

My eyes flicked back and forth as I leant back and scrutinised my face. I looked alright. Not good, obviously. The smudges beneath my eyes were still far too visible, and I had a spot in the middle of my forehead that had appeared overnight, but there was nothing else I could do about it.

I sighed before giving up and tearing my gaze away from the mirror. I’d finished everything I could do to prepare, and that was good, but it also meant I’d run out of things to do. I had nothing else to distract myself from the anxiety that clawed at me, and I grabbed my phone again.

Texting Phoebe would help. I wouldn’t tell her I was nervous or anything, even though I was pretty sure she already knew, but I’d just talk to her about other stuff, and that would take my mind off everything. There was a guy she’d met the other day, and she’d started to tell me about him before getting distracted, I recalled. I could ask her about him again.

I tapped on the messages app, my finger moving towards Phoebe’s name, but something stopped me from clicking on it. Something else caught my eye, causing my breath to catch in my chest. There were three dots next to Duncan’s name. He was typing.
