Bart stared at the glowing blue text on the main slate.

TRANSFER MANIFEST: EXISTS

MANIFEST STRUCTURE: AVAILABLE

DECLARED TRANSFER CLASSES: AVAILABLE

His right index finger hovered a fraction of an inch above the interface. Before he could decide …

“Keeper.”

Bart did not move his hand or look up.

“Yes?”

“Patron Services has 19 active requests,3 escalations, 1 disputed retrieval priority, and someone has submitted an accessibility complaint against a staircase,” Nell said from the opposite end of the desk.

Bart slowly lowered his hand.

Gregor looked up from a stack of incoming folios. “Can a staircase receive a complaint?”

“Well someone does have grievances against it” Nell confirmed.

Bart closed the historical research screen. The record disappeared, replaced by the Archive's active daily management dashboard.

He opened the accessibility complaint. It was absurd, but was entirely legitimate.

An elongated serpentine scholar of reptilian linguistics had been directed by an old catalogue route to a specialised reading room. The reading room was located at the top of a narrow, tightly wound spiral staircase designed exclusively for bipedal navigation.

HOLDING: AVAILABLE

PATRON ACCESS: PERMITTED

ROUTE: CERTIFIED

ACCESSIBILITY: INCOMPATIBLE WITH REGISTERED BODY FORMAT

Bart immediately recognised the distinction. The holding itself was perfectly accessible but  route was not. Earlier in his tenure, he might have treated this as a structural emergency or a retrieval crisis requiring an elaborate physical workaround.

Nell however preempted him. “We can bring the item here.”

Bart checked the holding restrictions.

STAFF RETRIEVAL: AVAILABLE

“Yes,” Bart said and was glad for once there was no crisis.

Gregor reached across the desk and took the freshly printed retrieval slip. He studied the text quietly for a moment.

WESTERN CIVIC STACKS - LEVEL 3

ACCESS ROUTE B

SHELF 14-K

“14 K,” Gregor said. “Route B.”

“Correct,do you need Albert to help you” Bart said.

“I know the way…” Gregor replied with a slight smile. He turned and headed for the stacks.

While Gregor was navigating the western wing, an elderly scholar in robes trimmed with silver threads approached the desk. He carried the distinct, impatient posture of someone accustomed to deference.

“Excuse me,” the scholar said loudly. “I submitted a retrieval request 20 minutes ago. I was informed my text is available, yet three other patrons have received their materials before me.”

Nell did not look up from her ledger. “There are older requests currently holding priority in the queue.”

The scholar drew himself up slightly. “The Silver Spire has just entered into a formal support arrangement with your institution.”

Bart heard every word and looked at the scholar.

“Yes,” the scholar continued, sensing the Keeper's attention. “Surely that financial relationship permits some priority in routine service.”

Bart checked the daily queue on his side, finding the man’s request safely buffered behind a visiting dwarf and two university undergraduates.

“It permits quarterly reporting, but that's the extent of it” Bart said flatly.

The scholar bristled, opening his mouth to argue the point with him.

Nell stepped in smoothly, her voice carrying the absolute, immovable authority of an experienced assistant librarian.

“Retrieval priority remains based on request age, accessibility need, and operational efficiency,” Nell stated “Funding status is not a service category and you may approach and check with Silver Spire upstairs as well based on our agreement” but do try our special green tea its great while your waiting, it's actually from the Keeper’s own special personal stock winking at Bart. This seemed to mollify the scholar a bit.

Bart had started to stand up to enforce the policy however Nell did not need him. Across the foyer, Mabel had paused in her security rounds, watching the exchange with mild interest. If the scholar's behaviour escalated into a physical or other nuisance, Mabel was already positioned to remove him.

Bart sat back down when Albert passed behind the desk, silently pushing a cart of supplies.

“Everything satisfactory, sir?” the Chief Custodian murmured.

Bart watched Nell calmly explain the queue policy while offering him the tea.

“Apparently,” Bart said.

A moment later, a new request chimed in the interlibrary queue.

INTERLIBRARY REQUEST

REQUESTED ITEM: A Concise History of Extremely Loud Civilisations

FORMAT REQUIRED: AUDIO

PATRON ACCOMMODATION: SOUND-SENSITIVE

Bart stared at the request for just a moment.

“Nell.”

“Already requested a transcript,” Nell said, stamping a return slip.

Bart closed the request.

Gregor emerged from the stack doors a few minutes later. He was slightly dusty across the shoulders but that's about it, There had been no monstrous ambush or hidden clues leading to forgotten archive parts.

He placed a volume onto the counter.

“14-K,” Gregor said.

Bart checked the slip against the spine. It was exactly the requested text.

“Well done,” Bart said.

Gregor shrugged, as though crossing the Infinite Archive to locate a specific text was the most ordinary thing in the world.

“It's my job,” Gregor said and grinned.

They delivered the text to the serpentine scholar at an accessible reading station on the ground floor.

Once the patron was settled, Nell logged the interaction to ensure the structural failure was permanently documented.

ACCESS ROUTE DEFICIENCY: LOGGED

TEMPORARY ACCOMMODATION: STAFF RETRIEVAL

PERMANENT REMEDIATION: FACILITIES REVIEW REQUESTED

Ada arrived 20 minutes later to collect the facilities ticket, already muttering about load-bearing masonry and spiral geometry. It was the Infinite Archive functioning exactly as a normal library should.

Bart looked across the vast entrance hall. Nell was serving patrons at the desk. Gregor was processing the morning’s returns. Mabel was managing admissions. Albert was methodically straightening a display trolley.No one was waiting for Bart to solve an ancient crisis or needed him to save them.

Albert adjusted a brass fitting on the trolley and looked at him. “You seem troubled, sir.”

“Nobody seems to need me,” Bart observed.

Albert considered the bustling, orderly room. “An unfortunate consequence of competent staffing.” Bart looked at him while Albert simply continued dusting.

Before Bart could dwell on his own institutional irrelevance, a young human patron in a slightly oversized coat approached the desk.

“Excuse me,” the patron said tentatively. “Where are the toilets?”

Bart immediately pointed toward the doors at the rear of the foyer. “Through the Staff Restroom” And froze immediately.

The Staff Restroom was currently functioning as an active interdimensional transit route.

Nell spoke without pausing her writing. “Public facilities are through the east corridor.”

Bart slowly lowered his arm and changed direction, pointing toward the opposite side of the hall. “East corridor.”

“Thank you,” the patron said, hurrying away.

Gregor watched the patron leave. “You nearly sent them to another world.”

“Well i did correct myself before they left,” Bart said defensively.

Late in the afternoon, the queue finally fell empty. The last of the interlibrary requests were dispatched, the reading rooms began to clear, and the daily audits were filed.

Bart once again sat at his desk.

The historical research screen was still minimised in the corner of his display.

TRANSFER MANIFEST: AVAILABLE

He looked at it. The answers to Keeper #4,091’s actions were waiting right there, however there were still two late returns sitting in the drop-box, and his dashboard showed a pending calendar reminder to draft the first quarterly reporting framework for the Silver Spire.

Bart processed the two returns then he drafted the reporting framework. He filed both without any administrative drama.

At exactly closing time, the System generated the daily operating report.

TODAY'S SERVICE SUMMARY

PATRONS SERVED: 43

RETRIEVALS COMPLETED: 27

INTERLIBRARY REQUESTS PROCESSED: 11

ACCESSIBILITY ACCOMMODATIONS: 3

INCIDENTS REQUIRING KEEPER AUTHORITY: 0

Bart continued to stare at the final line.

Gregor finished locking some doors and came to look at the screen.

“Zero seems good,” Gregor decided.

“It is,” Bart said.

Albert meanwhile emerged from the reading room carrying the last abandoned cup and placed it neatly on his trolley.He stopped and for once, he did not immediately move on to the next task.His pale blue eye-lights travelled slowly across the entrance hall. Empty chairs stood pushed neatly beneath the tables. Returned books waited on the processing cart. Reading lamps were being extinguished one by one.

Bart noticed though.

“Albert?”

The skeleton remained still for another moment.

“I had almost forgotten this part, sir.”

Bart looked around. “Closing time?”

Albert rested both skeletal hands on the handle of his trolley.

“Yes, but not because there was an emergency where the archive was nearly destroyed or because everyone had gone.”

His gaze settled on the reading tables.

“Simply because it signalled a the day's work was complete.”

Bart decided not to say anything,

Albert straightened one chair that was already almost perfectly aligned.

“It is good to have that problem again.”

Then he pushed his trolley toward the supply room.

Gregor noticed the minimised manifest blinking quietly in the corner of the display.

“You gonna open that?” Gregor asked.

Bart looked at the clock on the wall. The library was secure. The funding was diversified. The staff were safe atleast for now.

He closed the display.

“Tomorrow.”

Gregor looked surprised. “Really?”

Bart looked across the library. Nell was locking up the final stuff on her desk, Mabel was completing the last checks and Albert was wheeling his pristine trolley into the supply closet.

“It will still be there,” Bart replied.
