The information about Neera had done little to assuage Isha's concerns, but it had at least made her smile. Hearing that Adia was returning soon had done more for that particular face too. She loved our daughter nearly as much as I did, or at least that was the running joke.

Our child's return to the city came on a rainy day, sheets of water falling from the sky as she slipped in through a gate with a handful of her companions. I was of course there to greet her, along with Isha.

“I'm home,” she said with a smile as we beckoned her to join us, a small shield from myself keeping the falling rain from bothering us at all. “Father isn't that wasteful?”“Hardly, it takes almost nothing to keep up at this point,” I informed her.

Truly the years had been kind in that regard. I did kinetic magic almost constantly, to the point that little effects were now almost second nature. Moving things about here and there, projecting shields, all those little things came to me as naturally as breathing. It was odd, but very welcome.

“Isn't showing off rude?” she chided.

“That's not showing off,” I responded. “You've yet to see your old man showing off,” I added with a smile.

With a roll of her eyes and a shake of her head she walked with us. Our relationship was... odd, she was fully adult, and looked as old as we did, she wasn't quite as powerful yet, but she was getting there too. In a hundred or so years would the difference in our time training even change things? Perhaps, perhaps not, it was hard to tell. Regardless she looked like our equal, and so we treated her like one, as best we could at least.

“Your hair has gotten so long,” Isha noted, reaching out to play with Adia's long tresses.

“Mom...”

“Oh let her have a bit of fun,” I laughed. “I'd be asking about your trip, but I'll get it from you later. I even had your favorite blend brought out, don't get the call for it often.”

That was my ritual, and an old one at that. We had always had tea while she told me about her day as a child, and it was something I loved. The memories of those days were bright and shining to me now, moments to treasure that I couldn't go back to, but didn't need to. Our little girl wasn't so little anymore, but I could still smile at the thought of tea with her again.

“I'm not a child anymore,” she pouted when I mentioned it.

“You will always be my child Adia,” I chided in return.

We chatted as we moved back through the city and towards the palace, for it was at this point decidedly a palace, people kept their distance, respectful, cautious. It was odd to me, who'd walked these streets long ago, almost invisible to people to now walk them as their ruler. It was nice that nobody disturbed us though, seeming to understand that we were busy. I needed to go on walks like this more.

As we moved into our home Adia took Isha by the arm. “I'm going to talk with mother for a bit,” she declared.

“Oh, well come find me when you're done,” I told them, seeing she wanted some privacy for that particular conversation. “And let me know if you need anything.”I wasn't worried, for if they spoke of anything I needed or wanted to know Isha would almost certainly tell me later. It was something children almost always missed about their parents, that we'd known each other and been in one another's confidence far longer than they had. Then again there were things I didn't need or want to know, and if it was one of those, then so be it, I trusted them both.

So instead I went down to one of my workrooms, I had quite a lot of these.

“Good afternoon your Majesty,” a voice called out to me.

“Good afternoon, reports?” I requested of the golem, it was a very, very simple thing.

This particular one had no real body to speak of, instead worked into the room itself, but it did begin printing. It wasn't an AI, not like the ones that had been gaining in popularity back on Earth, though I was really interested in one of those. No, this was simpler, it had a large number of commands and canned responses, along with the ability to tell me that none of those fit. I wasn't sure that building an actual AI would be helpful, but it would be fun at least.

I scanned the reports, though there was nothing that really needed too much attention. A few pieces of paperwork done here and there while I waited on the girls, eventually it was Adia that came to see me.

“Ah, done already?” I asked.

“She's not happy with what I told her,” she informed me.

“Which was?”“There's nothing wrong, at least physically, she thinks it's deeper.”“I know, thank you for speaking with her. What about you though, find anything for yourself while you were out and about in the world?” I inquired as I summoned over the tea pot and began brewing things for us. “New friends? Experiences? Found a husband perhaps?” I asked.

“Dad...” her face turned red.

“Oh pah, your mother already asked I'm sure,” I chided.

“She did...”

“And? Don't keep me waiting, I may not be getting any older, but I am eager to hear.”“Honestly I'm not sure I'm really meant to settle down like you two did,” she admitted. “Maybe one day I'll find someone, but I like the road, and staying in one place with one person seems...”“Difficult? We all have our quirks dear. I for example can lose myself in work. Your mother tends to put too much on her own shoulders, Chien is a pervert and always has been. They don't improve as we get older either I'm afraid, only worse. Though you may find that settling somewhere for short periods becomes easier as you get older. To me these days your childhood was so short... almost like a single day was when I was younger...”

“Really? It doesn't seem like that though, I remember you always being there, almost every day, like it was a constant thing. Sometimes I miss it.”“Adia dear, there's no need to miss it, if you ever want tea and talks with me all you ever have to do is ask. It makes me happier than I can say when you do.” I gave her a bright smile, seeing for just a moment the child she once was one more time.

“I'll remember that, but what are you up to? I've been hearing all kinds of things, a new research lab, a whole new setup, what's next?”“Mapping the world,” I answered.

“We already have maps...” That much was true, though there were some limits to them. I'd brought in enough geometry for our maps to be pretty good even, but I had something bigger in mind.

“Yes, but what if there's something far from us, another land, more people?” I asked. “Shouldn't we meet them?”

She blinked at me in confusion. She of course hadn't lived on Earth, where there were multiple continents.

“Why would there be more land dad?”“Why wouldn't there?” I enthused.

“Sea monsters get big if you go too far out.”“Then we'll need bigger weapons and ships, balloons too.”

She shook her head and I just laughed.
