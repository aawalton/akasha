Seeing as Nash, Priyah, and the others had a good handle on the situation for the time being, despite the overwhelming numbers of goblins, Luke decided to go see to its source. The monsters were coming down a hill, so the natural course of action was to go around it and see what the hell was going on. As he skirted the frothing horde, keeping a wide berth, not wanting to drag them toward himself, he noted something interesting. Even from a distance, Luke saw how many of the goblins seemed to repeat. Even with just those closest to him, running on the edge of the massing short-statured berserkers, the pattern was clear. First, a short one wearing a battered bronze helm while hefting what looked like a spear tipped with stone, then a taller goblin with a club, followed by one riding a creature similar to a badger, except with fangs, and then one with a huge nose, wielding a crossbow. After that, another one with a spear again. Again and again, the pattern repeated.

Luke narrowed his eyes. "What the hell?"

Even if the group below did an admirable job at keeping the goblins at bay, no one would be able to keep standing forever. Cresting the hill showed no difference in the landscape, but it did give some clues as to what was going on. Four open portals. Small ones. Their color was light blue enough to almost be gray. Goblins streamed out of them at quite the rate, screaming in rage as they crossed over to this world.

Beside each portal stood a goblin in white robes. Their arms pointed up to the sky, and they kept making different signs with their fingers. Spellweaves danced before their faces without pause. Interesting. As far as Luke could tell from that distance, each portal had a set number of goblins that repeated over and over again in some endless march of madness.

"Traitorous vermin?" Luke asked himself, observing the white-robed goblin mages perform their unending summoning. If those were the monsters the quest referred to, this wouldn't take long.

He made as if to move farther along the field of grass to put the portals between himself and the mages, so he could get closer without being spotted, but found someone already had that idea. Curtis strode toward them. The tall grass swayed in the gentle breeze, moving this way and that across the Reaver's black jeans, reaching almost all the way up to his knees.

Waiting for Curtis to make his move, Luke looked around in the grass he himself stood in, hoping ticks didn't exist in this particular world. Getting some foreign version of Lyme disease wouldn't be fun. Then again, he could just heal it. As his thoughts drifted, Luke spotted something above Curtis.

It was that same formation of moving darkness he'd been playing with on the bench before they entered the portal. Only this time, it was much larger. Darkness streamed from Curtis in free-floating little blobs that joined in and gathered, strengthening the pattern forming even as it moved, spinning around itself as if involved in a complex dance.

Something about it felt to Luke as though there should be sound, but the darkness was silent. Rather than screaming terror, there was nothing to break the constant murmur of the goblins' battle cries and the gentle breeze.

The monotony of sound changed the moment one of the goblin mages spotted Curtis. Or more like, spotted his spell or whatever it was. It was a miracle it took that much time, since the pattern was as large as a bus, but discover it they did. A goblin mage let out a cry of alarm as it turned and pointed. The portal next to it wavered for a moment, but then snapped back into place without a moment's pause in the stream of goblins stepping through.

Now that Curtis had been discovered, those goblins changed course. Turning to face the new threat, the goblin mages seemed to have agreed to divert a fourth of their forces, because the rest of them kept going up the hill and then back down to charge against Nash.

Luke wondered how that would change the battlefield below, but he didn't check. It would have to be something spectacular to make him look away from whatever Curtis was cooking with that huge thing. Every speck of darkness within him must have been fed into it. With the goblins closing in, it was sure to be a sight to behold.

The goblins moved closer and closer, running for all they were worth without a care for their own safety. Meanwhile, Curtis just stood there, glaring at them without moving a muscle, at least not as far as Luke could see from a distance. Once the goblins got close enough, the darkness over Curtis's head seemed to tremble before reaching out toward the approaching monsters. Before anything happened, however, the tendrils withdrew, trembled, and without doing anything more, fell apart. It was what happened at the bench all over again, except this time, Curtis collapsed, falling to the ground to join the countless puddles of darkness.

"Shit!" Luke shouted as he set off.

The goblins noticed him, but he didn't care. It would be a near thing if Luke reached Curtis before the monsters. It didn't help that two of the remaining streams of goblins turned away from their original path to target him instead. Arrows filled the air before Luke had crossed half the distance. Most, he swatted out of the air with Threads of Mana, but some made it through. Cursing, Luke pulled one free of his thigh mid-stride. Another struck his shoulder. This one, he pushed out with another thread. Using that same thread, he stitched himself back together, ignoring the pain rather than dampening it. He'd need the use of his arm soon enough. Dealing with that and keeping himself upright and running drew his attention from the approaching goblins, and Luke missed a spell as it crackled through the air.

Fire exploded against the fabric of his robe, almost the entire left side of it apart. Enough force was packed into it that Luke found himself stumbling to the side. That had hurt more than the arrows, but the pain subsided as soon as the stored healing mana in his Meta-Heart dealt with the injury. If he wanted to keep some of that healing mana in reserve, he would need to be calm and not lose focus. Getting hit with a spell like that was a rookie mistake, and Luke was far past the point where those were acceptable. This wasn't the Tutorial Dungeon.

Getting back up, Luke withdrew his staff and felt the increased attributes it provided boost his physical fitness. Rather than use those to fight, he leapt over the first group of goblins coming to meet him. If he stopped to fight, Curtis wouldn't have a chance.

He charged forward at top speed. Like that, it didn't take long to reach the mages. Slicing through two spellweaves, he closed the distance to the closest one and slammed the metal staff down with a huge overhead swing. It was met with a barrier, but not a very powerful one. The barrier cracked under the weight and gave way. While that attack didn't do much harm, having lost much of its momentum, it still put the goblin mage off balance. That was enough for Luke, who allowed himself to fall over the robed figure.

Standing back up with a new monster core in hand, Luke continued forward. More spells came his way, explosions of fire and ice, but he tore through them before they drew close enough to hurt. He feinted a blow at the next mage, then ran past, ignoring them in favor of reaching Curtis faster.

Luke needn't have bothered. Curtis might've dropped before, but he was back on his feet before the goblins reached him, tearing into them with his signature two-handed sword of darkness. The Reaver was nothing more than a blur of pure darkness, his entire form looking like a humanoid-formed blob holding a weapon. It was almost frightening to see the efficacy with which he tore through the goblins, slicing them apart, cutting off several limbs or even heads with every swing.

The goblins appeared to agree on the frightening aspect of the man they faced, because, summoned or not, they broke and fled, running back toward the portal they'd emerged from. The problem was, Luke saw, not all of them ran. Well, all the goblins did, but not all of them. Not their shadows. The shadows stood there, frozen. Or was it shadows? They looked like nothing more than dark figures remaining behind when their corporeal counterparts fled.

Curtis didn't stop to consider what to do with those. He cut them down. When the Reaver's sword sliced through them, the goblins died. It didn't matter how far they'd run. A ...blob of darkness meant a dead goblin. The goblins themselves didn't show any wounds. They just fell, their eyes wide open even in death, as if they were surprised by their lives ending.

Something sharp and cold struck Luke from behind. Right, the goblins pursuing him. Curtis's display had caused Luke to forget he was in the middle of a fight, so much for paying better attention.

Reaching out behind him with Threads of Mana, Luke flexed the skill's reach to latch onto the closest goblin, the one that'd just thrust a spear into his side. With Weaver's Perception, he didn't even need to turn to see the ones he killed, and kill them he did.
