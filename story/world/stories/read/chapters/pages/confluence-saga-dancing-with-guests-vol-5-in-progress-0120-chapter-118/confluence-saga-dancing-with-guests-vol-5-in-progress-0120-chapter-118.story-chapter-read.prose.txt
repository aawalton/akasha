They're going to help me pass the wall.

There was a moment where I thought they were saying no. Where his hesitation his clarification of the expense let me know I wouldn't have the answer I hoped for. Yes the cost was more than I expected to pay, I didn't plan to become a retainer, but they're a lovely couple and they did reach out.

They did their research too, or rather that Seer of theirs did. To think I was eating breakfast with them all and none of them clarified who she was until I put my foot in my mouth. Lovely couple? Couple of pranksters. They're all a bit crazy, we're all a bit crazy I guess I should say.

But soon enough I'll be past the wall, I'll finally be able to help people again, to be useful outside the small niche I'd built myself. I can't change what happened, no one can, especially not all these years later.

Next time I won't have to let it.

They were late. They were never late. They were late that one time but only by a few hours and only compared to when she'd expected them. This time they were actually late, late enough that Fay was getting worried.

When they hadn't shown up for lunch she'd figured they'd gotten caught up and lost track of time. They'd only done it once in the six and a half years she'd known them, but once was enough that it might happen. When they didn't arrive for dinner she'd made up excuses for them in her head.

That was two days ago. She'd insisted on sleeping in the house, door to the Holding open so that if they did get home she'd hear them right away. Briana had rolled her eyes before curling up against her, the bed was comfortable and they were still alone.

She knew they'd be back, she knew they'd be fine. There was no doubt in her mind. They'd made it back despite Seb being barely able to walk, they'd made it back despite distractions, they'd made it back after months of pushing to find their Affinities. They'd make it back this time.

Knowing they'd be back didn't help. It didn't stop her imagination from running wild, her knowledge of all the mishaps a Diving party could get into a curse in this situation. She didn't need to know the odds, she didn't need to remember the reports describing negative outcomes, horrible outcomes, tragic outcomes. She didn't want to know, not right now.

"Coffee?" Briana asked.

Fay glanced up from where she'd been tracing patterns on the tabletop with her fingertip in the few drips of coffee she'd spilled. Her mug was empty, the table nearly dry at this point.

"I should be at the Holding."

"They'll come looking for you the moment they get home. If you're not in your office or the house they know you'll be here with me."

"If they can come looking," Fay said.

"When they make it back to the Holding they'll be able to come looking."

A fresh cup of coffee bumped into her hand right before a plate of pastries joined it on the table.

"You didn't eat breakfast, you barely ate last night."

"I'm not hungry."

She felt Briana's Energy press against her. She frowned, she wasn't lying. She didn't need to- She shook her head against the thoughts and let the band establish, let her girlfriend's concern and love fill her thoughts.

Eat, Briana sent. They're your favorite.

She sipped her coffee and took a nibble of the pastry. Less than a minute later the plate was spotless and she was licking her fingers clean. She blushed and put her hand down firmly on the table. The pastries were good but that hardly meant she needed to lick the bits of sugar and flour off her fingertips, that she had to stare at the plate as though it still held traces of flavour.

More? Briana sent, her tone filled with amused satisfaction.

I'm not hungry, she answered despite grabbing the plate being held out to her.

"It's been over a week," Fay said anxiously. "They're over a week late."

"That isn't like them," Alex said. "They've always seemed responsible."

"That's not the point Alex," she answered. "Everyone knows they're responsible, that they make it back on time, that they do what they commit to."

"Fay," Briana said gently.

"Sorry."

"I get it," Alex said. "I don't know how Briana is staying so calm."

Fay glanced at her girlfriend, flushed then chuckled.

"She isn't. She's just better at distracting herself."

"I got kicked out of the back room at Kaf-Fay," Briana admitted. "Apparently we don't need to keep preparing beans when we have more than a six month supply ready to use."

"Did they make more-" Fay began.

"No, they said you're cut off. The rest of the customers have to have a chance to eat pastries too."

"It's their Holding. I'm their sister. I should- No. I don't know how I managed to eat as many of them as I did."

Fay heard the Holding Entrance open and turned. Sandra stepped in and locked eyes with her. Fay watched the disappointment fill her face even as she felt herself wilt at the ongoing lack of Seb and Anya.

"There's bacon," she said.

"No," Sandra said even as she walked over and took a piece.

"It's not the same," she continued as she took the smallest bite. "It doesn't taste right without her here to tell me I can't have it."

"They'll be back," Fay said.

"It's been a week," Sandra said, blinking fiercely. "What if-"

"They'll be back," Fay repeated sharply enough everyone froze. "They will."

"Fay…" Briana said.

"I know Bri, I know. But they will. I know them."

~ * ~

"I'm sorry Seer, we have no one available who can help."

Fay forced herself to remain calm as she stared at her desk. What good were the other Diving Administrations in Deep Earth if they couldn't send someone to go looking. She knew there were Divers capable of handling the Depths that Seb and Anya would be at. She'd looked into what every other group offered when they were setting up.

For them to say they had no one available meant they had no one willing to go into uncharted Depths. It meant everyone they had played it safe, kept to the paths, didn't take risks. It meant they weren't Elite, that they weren't the right people to send. Especially when you were sending them to look for people who were missing.

She knew the Council could find someone in a heartbeat, that Valene could have a team assembled within the hour, could have enough teams to search in every direction and map far enough out to find any trace. But she wasn't part of the Council, she wasn't Valene's protege. They had no reason, no obligation.

Anya's Godparents had not responded when she reached out. Or rather they had responded with the most useless answer. They looked forward to the wedding, they were glad to attend, happy to be invited. They knew it would be an interesting occasion.

There wouldn't be a wedding if they didn't come back. There wouldn't be any interesting occasions if they stayed missing. She didn't need their confirmation, they'd already confirmed months ago. She needed their help, she needed them to send some of their retainers, or even go themselves. They were high enough level that-

They were high enough level. They were retired, they were Combat Pathers who didn't dive any longer, or if they did so rarely that it made no difference. But they were the Keepers, they were higher level than she was. The threats that Anya and Seb were facing wouldn't be able to scratch them, unless they allowed them to.

What level would you have to be for those Depths to be low risk? How much progression would it take for the difference to be so significant that skill was irrelevant?

Fay stood up and started pacing. She'd seen Seb and Anya train, she knew what they'd been capable of a few levels ago. If they'd hit her, it would have hurt. They were a threat if she just stood there. But she'd hurt Anya on her birthday, she'd panicked and nearly broken her sister's hand by accident. Anya had forgiven her, she'd even forgiven herself, but the fact remained.

She left her office, heading through the hallways to go find Alex. He'd be able to tell her if she was wrong. He wouldn't be able to talk her out of it, only answer her question.

"Alex."

"Fay? Are they-?"

"If they were would I be standing here alone? Would I look like a disaster?"

"You don't look…"

"Briana said that too. She wouldn't accept a band connection after she said it. I know how I look."

She'd barely slept in the month they'd been missing. She still ate, still drank as much coffee as Briana would let her have, still went through her daily routines. Her hair and clothing told another story. No one in the Seer dorms would have recognized her. She barely dressed, settling for robes and house clothes that only barely coordinated. Her hair was more knots and tangles than anything else, she wasn't sure if anyone would be able to salvage it. She didn't care, not at the moment.

"You look terrible," Alex said. "Happy?"

"No," she answered. "But thank you for being honest."

She took a deep breath, hesitating despite knowing her idea was right.

"Level 16 Depths. What level would an Awakened have to be to be safe there regardless of their Path and experience?"

"That's not how it works. Combat Pathers go into the Depths. Non-Combat grow by focusing on their specialization."

"Alex. I don't mean to become a Combat Pather. What level would make survival trivial as long as the Awakened wasn't being foolish."

"No," Alex said. "Fay, no."

"Arbiter Alex," she said. "In your professional opinion and knowledge of Awakened progression, what level."

"I- you- no."

She didn't want it to come to this. She didn't want to use the words but he wasn't answering. He wasn't going to answer. She could see his determination, his knowledge of the answer.

"As the only currently available member of House Syl-"

"Really? You're going to use that?" Alex snarled. "Twenty-seven. Twenty-five would probably be safe, twenty-six would have to remain alert but twenty-seven should be fine in almost every conceivable situation."

"Thank you," she said and turned to leave. "I'm sorry, I truly am. I had to make sure you came to the same answer."

"Are you going to tell Briana?"

She didn't answer. She didn't have an answer. If she spoke to Bri, if she told Bri, she'd have to explain. She'd have to tell her why she knew it was safe, why she was willing to take the risk. But if she didn't tell her… Would Bri forgive her?
