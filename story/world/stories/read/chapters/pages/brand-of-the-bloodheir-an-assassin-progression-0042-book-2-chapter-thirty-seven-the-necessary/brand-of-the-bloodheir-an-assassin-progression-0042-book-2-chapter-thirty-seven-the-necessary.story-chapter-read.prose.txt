As with the Roost, each and every doorway was a mystery, its destination hidden behind an impenetrable wall of blackness. He was relieved to find more familiar territory as he exited the doorway from the circular structure onto the small, rounded chunk of land floating in the endless void.

How many chambers remained hidden and undiscovered inside the seemingly innocuous building that was his first introduction to the Under? Appearances had proven to be infinitely deceiving as he knew full well. An entire world likely lurked within the pathways beyond those he’d barely begun to explore.

His mind struggled to comprehend the sheer unbelievable truth that now challenged every conceivable norm. With nothing left for him to do at present, he strode through the portal into the Barren.

Though the scenery was intimately familiar, the differences were stark as the dramatic view of his new home came to light. When last he’d inhabited the space—only a short time ago—the atmosphere had been dim and dreary on his small floating island. Now, the light overhead shone as it had during his first visit. Like the sun, unimpeded by clouds, burned high above, his shard of land was illuminated in an expected afternoon glow.

There was no floating orb of light hanging in the void above, nor did there appear to be any light source shining from below. Even so, light seemed to fill the space as if the land itself emanated the glory of the gods.

At first glance, the house and grounds remained unchanged. Still, he approached with caution.

“Even when safe, this one trembles.”

“And this was the Rightmaker? This is the one who has been called?”

The Raven Talons mocked him, and he had no retort. Curious, however, he found it that they had referred to his position as Rightmaker in the past tense. Was…

Was he the only one left standing who believed he still served the crown, even if only by title?

“The trees are so thick that the forest goes unseen.”

“The only thing thick here is his head.”

“Enough,” Risens demanded.

“Now he grows a pair.”

He ignored the cackling blades and stepped inside. This was the first time that something of consequence had not been altered. When entering the portal, he had harbored no thoughts of changes to be made or needs to be fulfilled. So far, the changes had been inadvertent, responding to his desires more than his direct commands.

He gazed at the exit that would take him to the Roost. He needed to understand the full extent of his abilities and his dominion over this place.

Risens felt comfortable in the sudden chill and crushing weight of passing through the doorway to the Roost, though that was the physical. He bore the weight of an internal struggle as his boots struck solid ground once more. Traveling to the hallowed space for the sole purpose of testing a hypothesis seemed almost blasphemous. The lure of the secrets and skills that lay hidden there pulled at him, yet in the moment, he was merely passing through.

As if his intentions were announced before his arrival, the judgmental eyes of the stone ravens burdened him with an extraordinary heft. He offered a reverent nod before reopening his return portal to the Baren with the careful motion of the Quillkey. Though he had no desire to elongate the feeling of clawing disrespect, he paused before stepping through into the world beyond. If it was his thoughts that bore the control for the changes in design and function, what desire would be next to follow?

Risens needed little in terms of material goods. Many of the essentials were already accounted for. He had a bed and storage for his gear and the documents he’d secreted from the Gilded Cage. Beyond that, he’d never given much thought to what comforts he might require. He thought back to his quarters in the castle. A bath was the only other amenity that his scarcely adorned private chambers provided.

No longer would he have need of the training rooms. The world around him, the various realms he now had no access to,would offer more than enough practice for the time being. The Under, as it seemed, would serve well as a test of his martial skills. The Roost, at times, did so as well, though its trials had, to this point, been primarily exercises of the mind. If an outdoor setting were called for, the wild grass and narrow, overgrown fields that surrounded his haggard home in the Barren would work. Perhaps a garden would do for him to grow his food or herbs, though now, the cleansing comforts of having a bath readily at hand seized the forefront of his mind.

Beyond the small vessel that contained the Tears of the Under, there were no water sources to be seen. If he controlled the ability to spark fire to life with merely a thought, he expected that this fact would be of little consequence. Risens pictured his simple bathing quarters in the castle—smoothed, rectangular stone inset into the tiled floor. Polished metal handles set into the wall controlled the flow of both chilled and alchemically heated water—though here, there were no magi.

“Fool.”

“Do you not listen at all?”

“What?”

“You’re as good as a magi now.”

“I wouldn’t go that far.”

“Fine. You have the potential to be something like a magi.”

“That’s better.”

Huh. No matter how hard Risens tried, he could never picture himself as a magus. Perhaps that would come in time.

Aside from the tub, a small basin extended from the wall with similar controls, and, unless he were planning to fertilizethe fields, a toilet would be a required amenity.

Maintaining his mental vision of his castle quarters’ bathroom, Risens blinked his eyes open as he emerged from the wavering black passage of the portal. There was, in fact, a change, though it was a far cry from what he’d expected. The sky was noticeably darker than it had been when he’d crossed the portal to the Roost, even though his departure had only taken a few moments.

Risens chided himself for ignoring the house’s crude state. That should have been among his chief concerns. Aside from the peculiar changes to the light, the weather here had remained a pleasant constant, so he’d overlooked the holes in the roof for the expansive views of the blackened void that surrounded him. It was an oversight he vowed to remedy at the next opportunity if his theory proved to be true.

A grin of excitement and expectation pulled up on the corners of his lips as he stepped inside. While the crumbling, weathered facade had remained unchanged, the interior was obviously different. To the side of the shelves, a doorway stood open, exiting from the main chamber. Peering into the new room, the smile turned to one of immense satisfaction. Just as the image had been etched into his mind’s eye, an inset stone bath, a small sink, and a toilet greeted him. He wasted no time retreating into this new addition to his home.

As he did so, he wondered if there were any limitations. If he could conjure up a brand new room with a mere thought, what was stopping him from creating a house to rival even the nobles back in Windwake?

He would give it more thought at another time. For now, a bath awaited him.

Giving the knobs a gentle twist, a steady stream of clean, crisp water poured forth. Running his hand under the stream, the temperature shifted from pleasantly cool to a potent heat.

His trust and belief in Mother Raven were bolstered. She had, so far, given him no reasons to mistrust her, yet he was still coming to terms with the meteoric changes that had reshaped his life. It was one thing to be told he now controlled the power to alter the form of a structure with mere thoughts. It was another thing altogether to understand the profound truth of that statement implicitly and to see it with his own eyes.

The Barren was truly his to command.

Wiping his hands dry on the front of his cloak, he brushed against the wrapped vial that hung by a cord around his chest. The warmth that had permeated the thin glass tube, creating a soothing balm, had now chilled. Lifting it carefully to inspect its foul contents, it was as if he was gripping an icicle. The container was noticeably lighter now, and gently sloshing the liquid inside revealed a tube that was less than a quarter full.

Correlation struck him with a palpable force. Within moments, his understanding of the Barren had swelled. The process had strangely consumed no physical materials; neither lumber, nails, stone, metal, nor running water had been wasted in the construction, though the results were finite. Raw materials aside, he was now certain of the cost.

The vital essence, the lifeblood that sustained the carrion in the Under was thekey.

The connection between the contents of the thin vial that hung around his chest resolved into a perfect focus in his mind. It was the blood—the true essence of the Under—that propelled the use of magic here in the Barren. What sustained the tainted creatures bent on death and destruction in the endless challenges below meant life for this realm. It was the necessary ingredient for his creations.

Then another thought struck him. Perhaps the light within the Barren—which had so quickly shifted to darkness—was also tied to his supply of carrion blood. While night now nearly prevailed, he knew it was something remedied, if only at a gory, putrid cost.
