"Yeah, it's like there's a bubble around us, and when anyone is close to one of us they're normal, but if they're out of that bubble. It's like— It's like they're no even there. Is this normal? If this is a false alarm I'm sorry, but that Dream place is creepy if everyone's like that."

Maerin and I shared a glance.

"No," she said, "that certainly is not normal."

I tasted ash in my mouth; the thought of every single one of those five hundred people blankly staring at my back as soon as I turned around made me shiver. I imagined them swaying lightly in place, waiting for me. Waiting for Maerin or Joey. I was going to leave Maerin there alone. Could she kill them? Could I, for that matter? If they're not what they seemed, maybe they had more Presence than I thought. My heart beat in my throat.

"We should abandon them then," I said.

The others stared at me. I didn't look closely at either of them, but I thought I saw a sour look on Joey's face. I didn't address my words; instead, I opted to spawn three plates of French toast and scrambled eggs. Joey's mouth hung open in surprise, and I gave him an apologetic smile. "One of the things I need to fill you in on, but don't worry about it."

He nodded, the sour expression never quite leaving him.

"It's too soon to stop helping," Joey finally said.

"Maybe, I might agree, except the Instance they're looking for is apparently mine."

"What?" Maerin chimed in. "How?"

"I have no clue," I raised my hands in defeat. "If I had to guess, inheritance from Rowan. Look. I was down to help them when they were just people down on their luck, but not if they're some sort of fucked up hivemind."

"We don't know that, and whatever they are they act the same way around Carlson and his group," Joey said.

"They're from the same world," I retorted.

"So are we, and we're disagreeing right now."

I didn't actually have an argument for that, so I pretended I didn't hear him. "It's a risk. We don't know what or why they are, and with our world being eaten—in case you forgot—we don't really have time to figure it out. Not worth the ache."

"We don't even know if they're a hive mind! They could just be staring. We can't jump to conclusion based on just what I thought I saw!"

"We can and should. The Dream is fucked. It's basically just hell Joey. If you have a gut feeling it's probably right. If you think something is dangerous you're underestimating it."

"Then let's confirm before we just burn bridges. You can't get rid of all the Instances by yourself. Get them to help."

"I did. I have a deal with Carlson. I'll direct him at as many Instances and hope the problem handles itself."

"There's so many people there that need our help. We can't just abandon them."

"Yes. We can. There's nothing we can do about it."

Joey locked eyes with me. He looked more tired than anything else. "When are you going to stop acting like you're powerless in this?"

"I am powerless. You are powerless. No matter how many Instance Holders I end up killing I will still be powerless in the face of the Dream. Because whenever it wants it can just decide it's done thinking about me and I'll be gone as if I never existed."

Joey grimaced and winced, and I clamped down on that part of me that felt bad for pointing out his girlfriend was basically dead.

Joey just sighed, "Maybe. Maybe that's true. I don't know. From where I'm sitting I don't see it."

"See what?" I said, not trying to hide the sarcasm.

"How those people could possibly be dangerous. Being used by someone dangerous? Sure, but Dorian, isn't that the type of thing you hate? If something is off with them I doubt it's their vault. It's not right to leave them like that. Five hundred people being used like that?"

Anger flared in me, and I didn't know what it was directed at. "What do you suppose we do then?" I half mocked.

"What we can." The silence filled the room.

I didn't have anything to say. So, I stood up and pretended I needed to get a drink of water.

Maerin and I separated from Joey soon after. We made fake old western wanted posters with the other Survivors' faces in case he stumbled across any of them. He put Illia's wanted poster back down.

From there, it was just Maerin and I calling our respective parents so they didn't panic and send a search party out. Since neither of us knew the password to Maerin's phone, she had the idea to call using Dream magic, as she had taken to calling it. One, no doubt heavily simplified, explanation of how phones worked later, and Maerin was making the call out of thin air. Her understanding/belief of how phones worked was likely riddled with bad assumptions and errors, but the only person who could fight her on it right now was me, and I didn't even know what she did. Still, it must have been detailed enough, since my misgivings didn't make the call drop.

My parents were surprisingly understanding, maybe even a little relieved that I was well enough to be around other people. I took it at face value, just happy that I could go back into the Dream right away. Maerin's cared even less than mine, unaware that their real daughter no longer existed.

I couldn't help but stare at the empty space Maerin spoke into, like there was something actually there even though there wasn't. No matter how often I said it, I couldn't get over it. I was basically a God—however minor—within my world. The change was so sudden that it was hard to process. In four days, I had gone from some random shmuck to a shaper of reality. Except I wasn't a random shmuck, not really. My brother was special somehow. Ananke was giving me extra attention. Instance Holders had vendettas against me.

Infinite power at my fingertips. I could shape the world as I saw fit. The thought made me shudder. I never bought into the ideology of power means responsibility. That if you can do something, you should, but I also thought having power didn't mean you could walk around like a pompous asshole. I didn't know what was right, and I didn't know how most things worked. How does an entire government function in reality with competing interests? How did a judge get their job? I didn't know at all, so any tweaks or changes would just be a shot in the dark towards precariously stacked cups. If I were going to take over the world, it would have to be as some sort of god-emperor, and I didn't care for that either.

I could eat them all. Take their innate Presence for myself and start the world anew in my image. I thought about it, and that reality created a hollow pit of disappointment in my chest. I kept coming to the same decision. I didn't want to be a God, but I was, and I needed to be or else we'd be eaten by other Gods. Which led to something I really didn't want to admit. I had to become an Instance Holder, an actual one.

Beings with more Presence could probably forget me out of existence, and there were so many Instances nearby, chipping away at my world. Distracting Carlson and his group by pointing them at the Instances would help, but only to a certain extent, and that was the limit of their abilities. Joining them would only be an exercise in wasted resources. I thought back to how Orlan had described my brother's attack on the other world. Rowan had probably gained so much Presence from that.

He let static take his vision, and then, like he was taught, formed it into words.

[Alright, Ananke. You win. I'll do it.]

Nothing for a moment, and then almost tentatively the static moved.

[When?]

[I want to go into another Instance with some people. Then I'll start.]

[A waste. No amount of exploring would equal the abundance of an Instance.]

[It's a question of design, not utility.]

[I will teach you design. Leave the delving to your other … to your friends.]

[I want to make sure they know what they're doing.]

I got a snort of amusement from the static, and I froze. I hadn't been able to feel anything over the static before. Was this new on my and her part, I wondered.

[Maerin will be fine. She is close to the Instance Holder threshold, if you wish to allow that. Regardless, no Instance would drain that much in a single delve. The others you brought are also at no risk given their rewards.]

[I have a lot of questions.]

[Of course.]

[I want assurances. I want my mental bridge with Maerin to stay, and I want to leave at any time if things go to shit.]

[Curious, but acceptable.]

I bit my lip, but ultimately nodded.

"Maerin. You'll be going in alone. I need to talk to Ananke. Go with them to an Instance. If they start acting weird or draining your Presence just stall."

Maerin gave me a long look. "Okay, how will you know."

"She's agreed to let us keep the mental connection when you're in an Instance."

She nodded slowly, "Alright. I'm off then."

And with a pop, she ragdolled onto the floor unconscious. I grumbled, picking her up and dumping her on the couch before returning to my conversation with Ananke.

[Alright. Let's get started.] I wrote in the static.

I didn't have to wait. In an instant, I was in the Dream. My surroundings hadn't changed, but I could feel the shift in my chest. No one stepped from the static to greet me, but from the center of the room, a blank white expansive began slowly expanding, and as it expanded, it encompassed and took over the room.

Static filled the air in front of me as Ananke 'spoke'.

[You're right to think of Instance Holders like Gods. One of the first things I teach each of them is what I'm about to teach you. It's a long lesson, one we can't complete in one sitting, but this is how you create a world.]
