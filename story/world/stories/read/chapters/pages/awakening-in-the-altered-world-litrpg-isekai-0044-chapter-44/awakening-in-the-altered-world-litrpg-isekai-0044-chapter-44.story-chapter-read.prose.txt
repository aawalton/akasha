Blood was leaking from Beckett’s lips. He didn’t bounce back up instantly, and when he glanced back up, his eyes were unfocused.

Is he shell-shocked?

Beckett’s head was bleeding as well.

Beckett hacked a little, spat a little more blood; by his eyes, he barely recognized where he was.

Alex went decisively for his emergency crystal, which he’d taken out of his Interspatial Ring so he’d have on his person.

It was secured to his steel belt—or it had been, but when he went for it, he discovered shattered, iced-up steel.

The crystal was gone.

Alex’s teeth clenched, hard. You’ve got to be fucking kidding me.

He glanced around, but it was impossible to distinguish it in all the ice shards.

Why didn’t I keep it in my ring?!

Of course the second or so he’d be able to access it wasn’t worth the risk. He wanted to slap himself, but there wasn’t time to self-criticize.

The Frigid Knight was slashing for Beckett, its cleaver overflowing with ice mana.

Alex lurched toward the two, throwing up a hand; twin Mana Shields filled the space in front of Beckett, absorbing the ice.

Beckett still had the wherewithal to lunge sideways, haphazardly, and Alex was relieved to see the guy seemed to be regaining his senses a little.

And the Frigid Knight stepped, once, twice—in that instant the warping, stained-glass shadow it cast darkened Beckett’s body to a strange blue.

It slashed, its giant cleaver gleaming with ice, Alex was certain what saved Beckett was pure instinct.

Steel met steel; Beckett gasped, and for an instant the two weapons wrestled. By his face Beckett seemed astonished by the forces his body was experiencing.

Then the cleaver turned sideways, wrenching Beckett off-balance, and struck Beckett on the side of his helm, incredibly fast—CRACK!

Beckett’s body hit the ice.

Alex screamed, a desperate try to get the Frigid Knight’s attention as he ran.

Beckett, call the fucking crystal! But it felt like a vain hope. He wasn’t even certain Beckett knew where his crystal was.

The Frigid Knight raised that giant cleaver over Beckett’s body.

Beckett barely reacted; Alex knew he was still conscious because the guy cringed instinctively.

Two Mana Spears were already surging at the Frigid Knight.

It’s too slow.

Alex knew it with a cold certainty, even as his Spears released.

I need to hit that thing, NOW.

So he did.

Swordforce formed on the blade of Alex’s sword.

Alex slashed.

An arc of Swordforce, backed by a sudden thrust of Force, whipped across the clearing and struck the Frigid Knight in its icy helm.

The Frigid Knight froze, and in this time Alex slashed twice, wildly, still screaming; two more Swordforce slashes broke against it.

The Frigid Knight turned toward him, and its eyes went to mirthful crescents—does this thing have a freaking sense of humor?

“Yeah,” said Alex, breathing heavy, meeting its gaze. “That is your slash. What are you going to do about it?”

As he said it, he felt shocked; he was as surprised as the Frigid Knight was.

His speaking was nonsensical, obviously, since he sincerely doubted the thing knew English, but he hoped the feeling, at least—

And Alex saw what he imagined Beckett saw.

He saw the Ice mana within the Frigid Knight’s lower body brighten, sharply—and then it was a giant, blinding blur; a lesser blur filled the air, and Alex reflexively blocked.

There were very few moments in Alex’s life he felt fully, 100% present, so present he was aware of every little detail his senses perceived. He’d felt it against the Templars, briefly, and he’d felt it as he slashed.

The giant sword descended.

Alex’s body surged with Force.

Two swords slashed, a giant icy sword bearing down on what seemed a toothpick by comparison.

Alex screamed.

It was like his body was one great gong, and it’d just been struck incredibly hard; his vision blurred with tears.

CLANG!

He staggered back three steps.

He wasn’t sprawled against the ice. One flow of force absorbed what was incoming, while a second augmented the forces of his muscles—precisely like he’d achieved against the minion earlier.

If he’d failed, he was certain the blade would’ve followed through and sliced through his neck.

It was his second successful, if instantaneous, try at Force cycling. A skill with in which he’d succeeded once, and failed twice, up until that last strike.

The chances were one in three.

But Alex wasn’t thinking with the past in mind; his eyes were gazing at the sword as it descended, and he felt he was seeing it utterly clearly.

The sword descended again.

CLANG!

Alex staggered back. His body felt very relaxed, even as new Forces surged through.

CLANG! CLANG! CLANG! CLANG!

Six strikes; and to take all six, he needed his Force to succeed, all six times. He needed to be perfect. The instant required he was perfect.

Alex was.

His eyes were very wide, but it wasn’t in surprise.

He wasn’t just seeing the Frigid Knight very clearly; he felt like, in that instant, he simply saw.

The minions, off to the side, were slowed significantly; likely it was due to the buffs vanishing, but they were also weighed down by the Frigid Knight’s hostile ice.

He was also feeling the way the forces were seeping into his body, into his bones, the tiny cracks that were being formed; it all felt startlingly clear.

His Force cycling was helping, and he felt it was improving, but in a few strikes, it wouldn’t matter.

It’s going to break me regardless, he thought, weirdly serenely.

There wasn’t time or space to Mana Shield, and his Mana Spears were utterly insufficient.

A seventh exchange almost forced him to a knee; ice glazed the surface of his sword despite his flows of Swordforce. It’s also overpowering my mana.

It’s too much. It wasn’t despair, but clear analysis.

He felt, behind his clarity, a sense of increasing urgency, but it was almost like his mentality at present was working against him.

He was trying to execute as perfectly as he could, but in this instance he could recognize he needed to break out; he realized perfection wouldn’t save him.

But the Frigid Knight was entirely a blur, slashing so quickly, so violently, it was taking all of Alex’s force of will to stay clear-eyed, to refuse to let terror in.

If I’m not perfect I die. If I keep being perfect, I die.

The Knight slashed, and this time as their blades met, a wave of icy mana rushed through, striking and crystallizing the mana flowing inside Alex’s body.

Alex’s legs folded beneath him.

He glanced up, seeing a dull, distant false sun behind the translucent blade as it reared back. A blade that was destined for his neck.

He felt a sense of indignation, of raw anger, as he clenched his sword with tremblings arms; the feeling pierced his clarity.

Fuck that.

All the Force Alex could muster rose within him.

The giant sword reddened, and Alex’s lips pursed.

CRACK-CRACK!

The Frigid Knight staggered forward, and fire and lightning blossomed against its back, the furious colors visible and watery through its translucent chest.

The two most intense fire-lightning lances Alex had seen, back to back.

But Frigid Knight still didn’t seem badly hurt.

Behind the Knight, Alex saw Beckett.

The guy’s expression, in that instant, was very raw; his eyes were bloodshot and reddened, and his body was still trembling.

Beckett seemed very angry, and anguished, at once.

“Crystal!” gasped Alex.

“I can’t,” said Beckett, teeth set, and Alex saw the crystal on Beckett’s belt was plated with Ice mana.

A third fire-lightning lance surged through the air, which the Frigid Knight slashed away easily—but Alex realized, as with his own Force, the variance in Beckett’s skill had vanished. Every last lance was potent.

He’s best when he’s emotional, Alex realized. Unlike me.

A wealth of lightning surged through Beckett’s body, and Alex saw Beckett’s pupils flicker, briefly, lightning-white, before returning to their original blue.

His lightning infusion’s peaking as well…

But Beckett’s body was still wrecked, and he was still trembling; how much can this actually compensate, and for how long? Alex was genuinely concerned. It can’t be sustainable.

Beckett’s eyes met his own, and Alex knew he was asking a silent question.

He was asking—will you help me?

Alex decided, Yes.
