Mr Gump didn’t have shit on me.  For every stride my pursuers took, I needed to take forty, and they still couldn’t catch me.  Bushes, trees, farms, they all just blurred past me as I sprinted for my life.

The childhood refrain of “run rabbit run” had echoed in my mind for a while, but Wrath and Vanity had quashed all such thoughts.  I was no puny mammal.  Poikilothermic for the win.  That means cold-blooded, in case you didn’t know.

As titanic as those chasing me might be, they were but mammals.  I had a vast reserve of biomass and no need to eat.  Sleep was for the weak.  While I would probably sleep for a week after this was done, nestled on my pile of shinies or wrapped in Esme’s warm embrace, it wasn’t high on my hierarchy of needs at the moment.

Three days had passed as I foot-slogged my way across the empire.  I’d never seen so much of it up close.  Steering clear of the towns and villages had been a must; the giants had simply stomped the first one I ran through flat.   For all their stoned rambling about karma, they apparently paid very little regard for the little people if you stole their ganja.

I was finishing my fifty-eighth run through of “just keep swimming”, which offended me less because the protagonist was not a squishy mammal, as I panted to a stop and looked back behind me.  The big bastards were still coming, but I’d bought myself a moment to catch my breath.

There was an uneasy stalemate.  I could outrun them or dodge their projectiles if they got too close, but they never stopped.  My life had become a marathon where, instead of cyclists trailing you with water bottles and medical kits, I had stompy death on my heels.

Even Wrath was willing to admit that this was a fight I couldn’t win.  My only choice was to flee and lead the big bastards to something meaner than me.  Hopefully, the fight would distract them enough that I could slip away and run off again to gather more troops.

It is really something when the reptilian parts of a dragon’s mind are going “nah dude, leg it.”  I had bitten off more than I could chew, another first for my new life as a dragon.  Well maybe not a first, but definitely a second or third.

“You guys getting tired yet?”  I yelled back at the dozens of giants stomping their way towards me.

“Stop running and find out!” bellowed Gnarly.

“We just want the gear back!” added Wave.

“You wanna smoke, you’ve got to catch me!”

Putting deeds to words, I turned and jogged on.  As the ground-shaking steps grew closer behind me, I put on a burst of speed and leapt across a river,  landing with one knee touching the ground, then kicked up clods of grass as I sped away.  I was pretty sure that was my river, the one that threaded down from Mount Bob to distant Ankmapak.  A hundred feet of still blue water, unpolluted by the hundreds of thousands of kidneys it was destined to pass through, zipped past in a blur beneath me.

Navigation was not my strong suit.  Every morning I oriented myself to put the dawn behind me and tried to aim for a distant landmark.  By noon I had inevitably passed said landmark and would struggle to find another to keep me heading in the right direction.  Bugging out back to the dungeon and grabbing a map wasn’t viable; god knows where the detoxing giants would wander off to.  I’d hardly be able to tell where the portal was anyway.  I needed to keep them focused on me.

This also meant I couldn’t get too far ahead of them.  Couldn’t risk them losing the trail.  So I was left playing a game of giant stoned, but rapidly sobering, cats vs scaly mouse in a mammal suit and hoping I was heading towards my hole.  Figuratively speaking.

“Stupid bloody—Beloved and kindly system that has deemed fit not to give me a bloody minimap,” I grumbled as I loped off across some farmland.  The peasants fled as soon as they saw my tail, the big bastards hot on my heels, and I got to appreciate the sheer work involved in carving agricultural space from the wilderness.

Weeds, pests, soil pH… the last one probably wouldn’t mean anything to the farmers, but nature was an aggressive thing.  Any space for something to carve out a life for itself was always going to be crowded, and the law of the jungle applied.  Back on Earth, I’d skived off gardening for a year, and my little plot of green had been filled with vines and creepers.  I’d broken two strimmers wrestling the grass back under control.

My point is these acres of wheat or barley or bunglegrains, whatever the locals called their primary seed crops, were hard won and required constant maintenance.  And here I was leading creatures whose every footstep pounded the dirt to something akin to stone.  One footstep undid generations of work.  I was racking up bad karma by trying to help.

The locals wouldn’t understand; most of them won’t have even heard about the Changer yet.  They might have heard about the civil war and my accidental rebellion.  But news travelled slowly on Helstat, at least for the common folk.  I made a note to ask Kat to record some news sections to go at the start of her movies so we could spread the good news of Emperor Bob and boost my reputation.

I paused to make sure the giants weren’t going to stomp right through the middle of a town again, then headed for the wilderness.  Once again, that lack of a minimap set me to cursing.  Navigation was easy when you’re flying.  You can see for miles and miles.  Sprinting across the ground in my skivvies (pink polka dots on purple this time; Esme had weird taste in gentlemen's undergarments), every tree, large bush and minor hillock was a problem.  You might wonder why I hadn’t gotten dressed; I could have taken the time, but after my first tunic and trews combo got sweat-soaked and torn to shreds by passing vegetation, I’d given up on the idea of arriving at my destination fashionably attired.

Up in the sky, a hill was just a possible place to set down and roost for a little while.  Down here it was a bloody great pile of dirt that blocked my line of sight. Bushes were a problem if they got big enough, especially the spiky ones.  I’d learned early on in this marathon that sometimes going round was the easier option.  Hence my running around in my undies.

Forests presented another challenge.  I could thread my way through the trees; hell, I could just run straight through the smaller ones if I wanted to.  But I went from being easily tracked by my pursuers to being lost among the leaves and fronds, which is what is commonly referred to as a not-good situation.

Days and nights blurred past like the grass, farms and small towns.  I kept the rising sun at my back and the setting sun ahead of me.  The obstacles in my path deflected my course somewhat, and I took to shifting back to a dragon and flying up to check for sights I could recognise.

The mountains that were home to the dankest dwarves and where Dagrun and the TOTS made their home had appeared in the distance yesterday.  The peaks gradually grew to loom, to dominate the skyline.  It was testament to how much ground I could cover that they went from pimple on the horizon to the teeth of the earth reaching for the heavens so quickly.

The forests had grown thick as we left what passed for civilisation behind.  I’d been forced to remain a dragon and fly very slowly.  Long as their legs were, the giants couldn’t hope to match me on the wing.

“You guys must be jonesing for a smoke about now!” I called back, making sure to keep outside of rock-lobbing range.

“I gots the twitches!” Chef-bro shouted.  “C’mon, Bob.  Help a big brother out!”

Remorse was a strange thing to run through my mind.  Being a dragon seemed to have largely removed that level of self-awareness.  The weakest and tiniest of my mental menagerie had been growing stronger over the last few days.

Regret was puny in comparison to Vanity, Wrath or Greed.  Barely a blip on my mental radar.  Even with its increased power, it was relatively unimportant.  Was I being a monster?  The stupid hippy, stoner giants hadn’t done anything wrong other than say “no thanks, mate, we’re good,” when I asked them to help.  So perhaps I was, but I found I didn’t care too much.

Setting aside the crushed farmland and minor villages, which had hopefully been loyal to the Madson, it was yet another karmic weight pressing down on my very limited conscience.  That did concern me.

For the greater good was an argument that historically didn’t hold up to hindsight.  I was forcing beings to act in ways they would prefer not to, sans consent, without volition.  The other, far more pressing, worry was that I would force an evolution in the Changer’s tactics.

Thus far, it had met us blow for blow; whatever we tried to do to move the battle in our favour was countered with a new combat form.  Usually purple, furry and weirdly cute, bar the fact they were all trying to eat my troops' faces.  Beyond the flyers and the heavier models it used to counter my BB-209s and the dwarves' steam-mechs, it hadn’t been innovating that much.

How did one fight what was, to all intents and purposes, a kaiju?  Well, traditionally there were two routes to take that I could think of.  Swarms of small, low-value and highly disposable units, or one big bad-ass mother who could meet something like a giant on its own terms.  Please let it take the second option, I prayed fervently to any god who might be scanning my thoughts at that moment.

If the Changer switched to spawning titanic monsters, all our regular forces became chaff, and we didn’t have an infinite supply of little critters to throw at it in waves.  Or did we?  Maybe there was a way…  If it came to that.  Which I was confident it wouldn’t. Yes sir.

I caught the first hint of silvery leaves off to the north.  My dodging the spinier bushes, forests, and poor, benighted peasants had led me too far south.  On the plus side, most of the southern lands were nominally loyal to Madson, so I probably hadn’t accidentally flattened my own farmers' fields.

“Nearly there!  I’ll just drop your gear off behind the Changer’s lines and then all you have to do is go grab it before the thing converts it into more monsters!”

“You have wronged us!” Gnarly yelled.

“You have betrayed us!” Wave added from the other shoulder.

“Needs must when the devil vomits in your kettle, dude!”  I dodged another barrage of stones.  “Just don’t tread on too many of the good guys!”

Ah shit.  How the hell was I going to get these big lumbering oafs past my own troops?
