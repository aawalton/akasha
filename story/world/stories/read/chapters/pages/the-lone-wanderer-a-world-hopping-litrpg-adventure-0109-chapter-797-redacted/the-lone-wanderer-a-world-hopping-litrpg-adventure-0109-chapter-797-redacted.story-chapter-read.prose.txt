A few days after registering Supercharged Cognition, Percy decided to take a short break from training to test how his accelerated thinking speed would affect various tasks. He’d originally wanted to wait until after he completed his first two promotions as well, but his curiosity eventually won.

As it turned out, having as much brainpower as dozens of people didn’t directly translate to mastering concepts dozens of times faster. Had that been the case, the universe would have been dominated by mind deities already.

His new mindsets did help with practicing his Elemental Control spells, but he was still bottlenecked by other resources, such as his mana regeneration and his body’s reaction speed. Even with the ability to think several times faster than normal, he would have to play around with his mana extensively to fully understand its properties and learn how to reconstruct it from scratch.

According to Zoris and Nephthys, other mind mages had it worse than Percy, because comprehending a concept usually involved understanding various ideas in a certain sequence, as well as a lot of trial and error. A mindset like Supercharged Cognition greatly helped with the first problem, but wasn’t nearly as effective in dealing with the second.

Due to the synergy between Percy’s bloodline and new affinity, he had a unique advantage in both serial and parallel processing, allowing him to circumvent some of those obstacles.

The effect was still rather modest, though Nephthys expected it to eventually surpass that of her own Divine Spark mutation. Once Percy’s third core was closer to the Clear grade, his mindsets might prove even more effective than an Elemental Source, on top of not being restricted to a single affinity.

Outside of his long-term goals, Percy’s cognitive upgrades were extremely helpful in both alchemy and combat.

Parallel Minds didn’t directly raise his yield, but Percy found that his mind clones could each control one of Micky’s person-shaped sentries to brew side by side like a small army of trained alchemists. His cauldron – the original one; not the monstrosity that he had designed to compress his potions to Violet – could fit a hundred times as many ingredients, so Percy and Micky could brew over a thousand times faster than any of the Guild’s elders.

Better still, while Percy’s mental pockets hadn’t directly improved his yield, his Supercharged Cognition appeared to have done precisely that, elevating it to around sixty-eight percent when brewing regular elixirs. A two-point improvement was admittedly quite modest, and he expected the gains to keep slowing down as he approached the coveted concept of alchemy.

However, his upcoming promotions would hopefully push him a few additional steps up the ladder, and his ability to accumulate brewing experience faster through multiple minds and bodies would also play a role once he had enough time to invest into the fine art.

Percy asked Micky to spar with him again, lending him half of his mental pockets while keeping the rest to himself.

Unfortunately, no amount of raw brainpower could make their bodies move faster, but it gave them time to carefully consider each attack and plan ahead. Training like this for a few months would revolutionize their fighting style, ironing out the few persistent wrinkles that the Huehuan’s decades in the coliseum had failed to eliminate.

The biggest improvements, however, were to their sensory domain.

As they had predicted, Percy’s mind clones allowed them to control half of Micky’s fist-sized sentries even without pumping mana into Supercharged Cognition. With their newest mindset at full power, they could easily control the entire army, giving them access to a hundred sensory domains.

Supercharged Cognition also happened to more than double the volume of their Sage’s Pond, giving it a radius of nearly seven and a half kilometres. By spreading the constructs out on a flat plane and combining the resulting bubbles of information, Percy and Micky could scan everything within seventy-five kilometres.

The radius dropped to just over thirty-three kilometres if they arranged their minions into a spherical formation, though there was rarely a need to stretch their senses that far vertically.

Funnily enough, Micky’s capacity had now become their new bottleneck, so Percy hoped that his friend would be able to produce more sentries after a few promotions of his own. Even if he didn’t, it wouldn’t be long before they could scan an entire province with a single glance.

If they focused their attention on a single opponent, they could double the predictive window of their foresight.

The jump from seeing one second ahead to two might not sound like much, but it almost guaranteed that no opponent would be able to land a hit, unless Percy and Micky were completely outclassed. As things stood, that would only happen if they fought a Tenth Circle demigod, a true paragon, or an actual deity.

Expanding the predictive window further wouldn’t be easy, because every additional second compounded the number of branching futures that they had to sift through. A handful of projections after a single second would easily turn into dozens after two seconds, and hundreds after three. The fact that Percy would also have to constantly choose moves that effectively countered those possibilities further restricted his advantage, though he expected his limits to keep increasing with the grade of his third core.

The one thing that Percy didn’t experiment with as much as he would have liked was magiscript.

His recent gains should have greatly raised the maximum complexity of his unit cells, though he decided to not worry about that until it was time to redesign his and Micky’s equipment. That would have to wait until the Huehuan awakened his third core and underwent the next round of mutations, which was still some time away.

In theory, Percy should also be able to handle more powerful instantaneous formations, but those would be restricted by the output of his other cores and his domain – which the new mindsets hadn’t affected.

All things considered, he was quite satisfied with his improvements, as his third affinity had proven tailor-made for him. Had he known how well it would synergize with his entire arsenal, he would have been tempted to activate the Void Decree even without Baphomet’s meddling.

‘Four thousand years to prepare might actually be overkill,’ Micky joked. ‘I almost feel bad for the guy.’

Percy chuckled, though he didn’t say anything.

Redirecting most of his mental resources to mindset creation, he only kept a single clone working on the Dance, another on affinity fusion, and lent a third to Micky to help him get twice as much training with his sentries. Percy’s work with the boosting art was progressing reasonably well, though he still wasn’t sure how to mix the two mana types.

His usual approach of mimicking the behaviour of a common element with soul mana until the resources merged didn’t seem to work for rare affinities. The fact that his new core was separated from the others by three grades wasn’t helping, but he’d still expected better results after four months.

He’d tried a bunch of things, such as circulating mana of each type through a different core, suppressing both of his blessings in case they got in the way, and repeating all his experiments with both soul and phantom mana, since he had no idea whether the presence of pure mana in the mixture would make things easier or harder.

Nothing had worked, until he resigned himself to filtering vast quantities of soul and mind mana through one another.

He had done that by picking a pair of neighbouring channels inside his head – one from each mana network – which shared a common junction. Then, he had simply kept the two resources flowing through one another twenty-four seven. He hadn’t expected that to work, but it was a relatively effortless state to maintain whenever he lacked better ideas.

His silly experiment hadn’t yielded any results at first, as the two elements had mostly phased through one another without interacting.

Against all odds, a few minuscule particles had started forming at some point, scattered across the two channels. Percy had only noticed their existence a couple of weeks before upgrading his Enhanced Cognition mindset. The pitiful quantity and faint pink colour of the new resource had made it difficult to spot even with his powerful Internal Sight.

After ensuring that the two channels looped around themselves in isolation from the rest of his mana network to prevent any leakage, he had started to carefully monitor the accumulation of the fused mana.

Gathering the pink traces together, he realized that he had only produced about ten droplets after months of endless circulation, with an extra droplet currently coalescing about once per week.

Percy wasn’t sure what this mana type could be used for, nor how to accelerate its production. Closing the gap between his cores’ grades would presumably help, but his only other option was to keep circulating his mana and hope that sheer exposure made the process slightly quicker or gave him some other clue.

Even experimenting with the resource would be difficult until he accumulated more.

So far, he had tried shaping it into small constructs like needles, though they appeared somewhat inert. The mana could touch both his soul and mind – which was admittedly a rather unusual property – but didn’t do much more than that. The constructs were too fragile to so much as scratch him, and neither his mind nor his soul wanted to consume the mana to mend themselves faster.

Regardless, Percy was glad that his efforts had generated at least something worth investigating, and quite eager to uncover its purpose. For now, he decided to name it something that he felt was quite fitting with its colour and origin:

Dream mana.
