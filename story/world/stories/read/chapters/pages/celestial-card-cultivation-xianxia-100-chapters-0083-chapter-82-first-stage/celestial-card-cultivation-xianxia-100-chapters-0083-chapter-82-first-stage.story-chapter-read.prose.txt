The Divine Dragon Sect—despite its diminished numbers—took first place in the Southeast province tournaments this year!

— Diary of Xu Ziqing, Imperial Scribe, Tianqiu Plane – now known as The Origin, Outer Realms

The moment he started pushing the energy of the Earth-affinity body cultivation elixir into his spine, he felt himself tilt to the side from his seated position, and his head hit the ground.

He didn’t pay that any mind, though, but instead worked his way slowly from the bottom up all the way to his neck.

The pain was incredible, but he didn’t relent.

Another ragged breath, and he started on his skull. There was a brief moment where he might have blacked out, but his will brought him back, and he finished up, consuming the last of the energy that had permeated his body.

Once he was done, he felt a slight thrumming from his lower dantian, a pulse emanating from his Celestial Core Card flushing through all his bones.

It felt as if it had helped settle the changes that the cultivation potion had brought with it.

From one moment to the next, he was ripped from his trance-like state by an enormous stench.

Opening his eyes, he found that his whole body was covered in the same dark gunk that had clogged his meridians: impurities. This time, he hadn’t needed the yellow pills Elder Sun had given him to expel them, though.

His robes were unusable forevermore. Maybe they could be burnt, but not at their campfire.

Minghao yelled at him. “Run to the stream and don’t come out until you are clean!”

Jianyi did as the older man said, shedding his robes along the way, jumping into the stream nearby as naked as on the day he had been born.

The water was freezing, but he had the feeling that it affected him slightly less than it had in the past, and once he started to cycle his qi through his body, he could focus on cleaning himself.

After the worst of the impurities was gone, he started to focus on himself and how he felt.

Solid—was the first word that came to mind.

He somehow felt heavier, without being encumbered.

Testing was needed, but he felt like it would take a lot to break a bone—just like a rock, he felt like he could withstand a lot of force.

It took a while to get rid of all the stink, but eventually he returned to Minghao and the campfire, dressed in the last of the sect robes he hadn’t destroyed yet.

The retainer looked at him with a grin. “Next time, you are not allowed to be anywhere near me!”

Jianyi laughed. “That seems fair. The manual didn’t say that it would have this effect.”

“I think you have grown a bit.”

“Really?” Jianyi looked himself up and down with a grin. A finger-width more was always welcome. Only because it increased his reach when fighting, of course.

“Can we do some testing tomorrow?” he asked, eagerly.

“Naturally. I would suggest that you focus on familiarizing yourself with the changes in your body for the next couple of days. Spear training should be high up on your priorities.”

Jianyi nodded with enthusiasm.

***

Jianyi moved his spear tip in a constantly looping motion, just like he had done hundreds, if not thousands, of times before.

Yet, it was different.

He hadn’t figured out what specifically was different, though.

Having already established that while his bones felt sturdier, they were not heavier, he struggled to see what it could be. He scowled.

“Are you still trying to figure it out?” Minghao asked exasperatedly.

Jianyi looked at him, eyes wide. “Do you know?”

“Describe the problem!” his teacher demanded.

“Everything feels slightly off. Not in a bad way, just different, more… energetic, maybe?” he said reluctantly.

“And why is that surprising after you progressed your body cultivation?” Minghao asked, eyebrow raised.

“Well, I strengthened my bones, not my muscles. So why is anything else different?” Jianyi was genuinely at a loss.

“Ah, now I understand the problem. You have too simplistic an understanding of how body cultivation works—or the whole body, really. There are two effects at play. First, while the majority of the change happened in your bones, the qi in the elixir did infuse your whole body to some degree. Much less than the bones that you worked on specifically, but it did affect everything.”

That Jianyi could understand. After all, the elixir had gone into his stomach and then traveled through his whole body via the bloodstream; it could have had a halo effect on everything it touched along the way.

“The second effect is more important, though. Your body is not a group of independent components. Everything is connected to everything else in some way. Yes, primarily your bones transformed, but did you know that the bone marrow is key in creating blood?”

Jianyi had not known that at all.

Minghao continued. “Your muscles are connected to your bones and are now infused with blood created by your bones, and can exert more pressure without being able to damage your bones. You haven’t noticed yet, but you are in fact heavier than you were before; you are just also stronger than prior to the procedure, so you haven’t noticed it that much yet.”

Jianyi scratched his head. “How heavy will I become?”

“That’s your takeaway from all this? Your weight?” Minghao asked, chuckling. “But don’t worry, you will get quite a bit heavier as you progress through the realms, but it is primarily limited to the bone transformation, so the other steps within the realm won’t change much, and before it becomes an issue, you should be able to manage your presence on this plane with your domain, so that your actual weight doesn’t matter that much—at least not in a negative sense.”

For a moment, Jianyi had worried he would crush through his mother’s house’s floor, and was relieved that wouldn’t be the case.

Returning to the more important parts of Minghao’s explanation, he started to smile. He was stronger, probably healthier. He briefly wondered how he looked, but abandoned that undignified thought quickly.

“Can we spar?” he asked, full of hope.

“No. Not yet. First, every movement, then every form, then Circling Sky. Everything until it is at least as good as it was before your advancement. Only then can we think about a spar. And you probably want to work on the Eternal Spear Meditation before that as well.”

It wasn’t that Jianyi didn’t want to do it, but it would have been nice to test his strength properly. “Will I have to relearn things every time I advance?”

Minghao shrugged. “That depends on the type of advancement and your base proficiency. You are still at a stage where small changes can throw you off quite a bit. At the same time, the bone-strengthening part is quite an impactful change as well. I would assume that if you get to the fifth stage, strengthening your skin, the impact on your martial arts will be less.”

Jianyi nodded and began with Open, just like half a year ago when Minghao had started to teach him.

***

After an hour or so of going through all the movements and forms, he could say with certainty that things had changed.

It wasn’t hard to adjust, and adjust he must.

What was really interesting, though, was that the process of re-learning taught him a lot about his imperfections and how to improve them.

He was sure that at the end of this, he would not just be stronger physically, but also a better fighter, and his ability to adjust to future changes would grow as well.

As advised by Minghao, he then went through the same process for the Eternal Spear Meditation. Similar adjustments were needed, but the big surprise was that with his body cultivation strengthening his every move, his spirit cultivation benefited as well.

The changes were not large, but with every movement being executed just a tiny bit more forceful, his dantians opened a little wider and sucked in more qi from the environment.

He looked with a sense of wonder at the different components in his body, spirit, and soul, and how they all strengthened each other.

Finally, Minghao relented and sparred with him. The first thing Jianyi noted was that the gap between his own abilities and his teacher’s was unchanged. No matter how hard he pushed, he couldn’t overwhelm the man physically or with his technique.

The only possible explanation was that Minghao was still restricting his own abilities to a large extent. That was humbling and exciting at the same time.

There was lots to learn, still.

However, the other observation was that he was sure that he was significantly stronger than before. The mix of a stronger body, the relearning of forms, and, last but certainly not least, the experience he had gathered from his fights with the spirit beasts had improved his abilities significantly.

Was he as strong as Lu Shan or Miyao?

He wasn’t sure. Overcoming sixteen years of privilege was hard. But he certainly was closer than ever before.

The mid-year assessment wasn’t that far away, and he looked forward to testing himself against his fellow disciples.

Thinking about the privilege that some of the other youths had brought him back to the question of why so few people engaged in body and spirit cultivation at the same time.

“Minghao, is the limitation on qi cards really the reason why people don’t engage in body cultivation?” he asked, still at a loss as to why people would give up on something as wondrous as this.

The retainer stroked his chin, thinking. “Things have changed over time. In Elder Sun’s generation, it was still more common to undergo at least a few stages or realms of body cultivation. Even I dabbled a bit in it… But you must not forget that a thousand years of war change a lot, even for cultivators. Especially for cultivators, given how many die all the time on the frontlines.”

Jianyi swallowed hard, thinking of his father.

“I think that before the Celestial Confrontation, cultivators were a lot freer to find and experiment with their path to ascension. These days, if we are being honest, true ascension beyond the Nascent Soul Realm is not even something that people think about that much. The sects are more like a constant recruiting and training ground for the battlefield.”

All of that sounded bleak, and Jianyi was reminded of the hate- and desperation-filled words of the young female demonic cultists they had captured in the sect. She had been convinced that the war was already lost.

Minghao continued, now maybe talking to himself more than answering Jianyi’s question. “I sometimes wonder whether the much simpler approach of splitting people into spirit and body cultivators to get them ready to fight as quickly as possible is one of the reasons why we produce few people that can compete with the old monsters like Elder Sun anymore…” He clapped his hands. “But there is no need to worry about that for you. Continue on your own path and rest assured that Elder Sun will do whatever it takes to have you unlock your full potential.”

Warmth filled Jianyi’s chest, thinking about what the two men had already done for him and continued to do every day. With the warmth came commitment. Commitment to work as hard as he could to prove worthy of their effort.
