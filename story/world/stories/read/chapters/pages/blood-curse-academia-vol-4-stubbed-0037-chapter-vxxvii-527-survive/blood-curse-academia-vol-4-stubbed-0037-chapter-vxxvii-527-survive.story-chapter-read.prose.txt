Chapter V.XXVII (5.27) - Survive

“Well done, all!” Arclight boomed down at the entire student body from her place on the roof of a nearby building. All 800 students at Shinzou Academy had been instructed to meet in the largest courtyard for a mandatory school assembly.

Kizu stood off to the side of the main crowd with Ione and Basil at his side. Originally, he’d entered the courtyard alongside just Ione and they both had the intention to slip away as quickly as possible after whatever announcement was completed. But before Arclight started the assembly, Basil had stumbled out of the throng and joined them. He’d come here with Aoi but her friend, Sophia, had bumped into them. The Tainted girl had never hidden her disdain for Basil, so the shapeshifter had made the tactical decision to retreat over to Kizu where he was more welcome.

“As you all know, your results for the token exchange have all been marked,” Arclight continued. “I am proud of each of you and the creative strategies you utilized to receive results! Now, two banners will deploy below me with a listing of each of your placements. One, with the results of the first tournament challenge. And the second, as a visual of how your tournament results have altered the combat rankings.”

At the end of her sentence, a pair of massive scrolls unfurled from the eaves of the building she stood upon.

Kizu was listed alphabetically as tied among the 34 students to get the token within the first week while simultaneously keeping a hold of their own token for the entire challenge. Ione was, likewise, in that listing while Sene, as a result of losing her token, was absent. As he reviewed the list, Kizu spotted a few others he knew but curiously not Harvey. It took half a minute of scanning names before he found the Tainted boy at number 433.

“Odd,” he muttered to himself.

“That Ione holds a top spot?” Basil asked. “Yes, I too find that odd. With how often you spend time sleeping I would have assumed your token would be stolen within the first two days.”

“I ate it,” Ione replied.

“You swallowed your token?” Basil replied dryly. He and Kizu exchanged a look. “And after that? Surely it didn’t stay in your stomach for the last few weeks.”

“Of course not. It was to buy me time to figure out a plan. After that, I buried it in Kizu’s garden. I figured that even if someone divined its location, the skeletons onboard Owl’s Respite would keep them out.”

Kizu nodded. That last bit had been something he’d considered himself. But since he only slept for a few hours everyday, he had deemed keeping the tokens on his person a more prudent solution.

“These results will be publicized and available for you to peruse at your leisure at a later time,” Arclight announced, her booming voice oppressing the chatter. “Now, I know many of you are curious as to what the next challenge will be. You all know that I like simple things. So I stripped away many of the complexities of Headmaster Ballarfulur’s tournament proposal.”

The headmaster stepped out from behind Arclight. How they’d missed him earlier when his top hat reached so far skyward could only be explained through magic. He smiled at them all, his white teeth glinting in the sunlight overhead.

“While at first reluctant, I’ve been convinced the loss of nuances brings out the true essence of the challenge,” the headmaster agreed. “It really boils down to a children’s game at its heart.”

Then, one by one, each professor stepped out and stood on the rooftop ledge, staring down at the student body. Of them all, only Arclight and Headmaster Ballarfulur had anything resembling a pleasant face. Professor Krimpit glared down at them with contempt, as if they were to blame for him being dragged up to the roof. Professor Oasaji was as stoic as any turkey could be. Professor Grove’s face was unreadable as a wisp. Professor Ignis's posture was resigned and sloppy. Taroe frowned with irritation though he wasn’t even looking at the students. And Professor Knoff’s face twitched between a polite smile and a mad grin.

The one common thread among all the professors was that they each had a hand slathered in a glittering blue paint. Well, sans Professor Grove who lacked hands. And Oasaji had a taloned foot in place of a hand. That paint though was an abnormality and doubtlessly involved the next challenge.

“You’ve gotten to know each of our academy’s professors these last few months or years attending our academy,” Headmaster Ballarfulur continued. “They all have their own individual strengths and foibles. However, I do not hire inept staff. And if you did not already aware of that fact, then I promise it will be impossible for you to hold your flawed world view after today.”

“Are we going to fight the professors?” a student nearby whispered to her companion. Her face was pale and she trembled slightly at even the suggestion. She opened her mouth to reply to her friend but only a pathetic squeal escaped her throat. She hurriedly looked back up to the professors on the roof.

Kizu exchanged a look with Ione. It was like the headmaster said, each of the professors had their own strengths. Still, they’d managed to overpower Professor Kateshi back in their first semester. If given an advantageous match up, they might be able to overcome a challenge that involved fighting. If blessed with a healthy dose of luck.

Kizu’s memory flashed back to the sight of Professor Oasaji facing the Dragon. The flaming bird riding the scales of a summoned dire shark in an orb of suspended sea.

Some professors seemed more of a challenge than others.

“The entire academy grounds are at your disposal,” the headmaster said. “However, step foot outside our campus and it will be considered a forfeit. Cause serious harm to a fellow student, and it will also be considered a forfeit. And let it be known that in fairness of the contest’s spirit, Professor Grove will act solely as an auxiliary assistant for me.”

Kizu quickly glanced around his surroundings. Were they expected to brawl the professors here? Would it be best to attack all at once as a group? Or try to escape and ambush them in the hallways? Attacking now would require coordination and fleeing to a more advantageous battlefield would definitely be what most students would choose. Was it the right choice for him though? He scanned the crowd for Sene. With her, Basil, and Ione, Kizu thought they stood a decent chance in a fight. They needed to buy Ione time to get out some of her bigger summons. And, likewise, it might be prudent to target Professor Oasaji before the turkey could get minions of his own on the field. Even with eight-hundred teammates though, their odds felt lopsided. Most of the students here would be little more than fodder for the professors to cut down like a farmer reaping rice. He considered that maybe the blue painted hands on the professors might serve as a handicap.

“We’ve outsourced one additional chore required to help make this challenge shine flawlessly,” the headmaster said. “Please, step forward, my friend.”

Wakino appeared. Her face remained hooded and Kizu noted that both of her cloak’s sleeves sagged, empty. There was no telling how much of the spatial mage was under that cloak.

Her presence could mean any number of things. Like a plan to box them in with barriers to fight the professors. But why would the headmaster have included that rule about leaving the academy if he planned for them all to remain in this courtyard in boxes?

The answer finally clicked for Kizu right as the spatial mage cast her spell. The headmaster had described it as a ‘children’s game.’ This wasn’t a fight at all. Not at its heart, at least. Just as the first challenge hadn’t focused on fighting, this was a challenge parallel to combat. As Wakino’s cloak’s empty sleeves raised up, magic surrounding the gathering, Kizu turned and almost managed to grab ahold of Ione’s shoulder.

“The task is simple.” Headmaster Ballarfulur twisted his curly mustache between two fingers and grinned down at them. “Survive.”

And then every student in the academy jumped.
