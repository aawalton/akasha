The world had gone to shit.

Tara held on tight to her Echo Staff as the sounds of battle intensified by the blackened lakeshore. In her periphery, Olivia and Marcus fought desperately against Raven’s shadow tendrils, buying her precious seconds that slipped through her fingers like water. Meanwhile, Ace was bleeding out. Shadow corruption pulsed from the ragged hole in his chest with each labored breath.

Kneeling beside him in blood-soaked clothing, Tara felt her conventional healing magic failing against the darkness that pushed back like a sentient void, undoing her work almost as quickly as she performed it. Her Soul Meter had depleted at an alarming rate.

Through it all, her grip on the Echo Staff tightened until her knuckles bleached white. What she was about to do was little more than a desperate gambit that might amplify her healing enough to save Ace, even if the cost would be her own HP.

Now or never.

To her delight, the response was immediate and overwhelming. The Echo Staff’s crystal core flared with crimson light, resonating with her blood magic. Energy flowed from Tara into the staff, then rebounded back through her and into Ace in an amplified wave.

But the cost was equally severe.

As her Soul Meter ticked closer and closer to zero, the magic drew not just from her Soul Meter but from her very life force.  Tara felt her own vitality draining away, and she could practically see her HP ticking downward. The staff allowed her more bandwidth to heal him, sure, but it was still coming at a hefty cost.

One she might not be able to pay—but she didn’t care, and she wouldn’t stop.

The shadow corruption in Ace’s wound receded under the amplified healing, and the inky darkness finally gave way to her light. Severed vessels beneath Ace’s skin finally knitted together. Damaged tissue regenerated. The punctured lung sealed itself.

Black spots danced at the edges of Tara’s vision. Her arms felt heavy. Her eyelids drooped. She swayed, struggling to stay upright as her strength waned.

Hold on, she told herself.

She was so close to bringing Ace back from the brink of death.

So close.

She couldn’t quit now.

Through gritted teeth, Tara maintained her grip on the staff despite the agony coursing through her body. It was a desperate act, one she had committed to, one she would see through to the end even if it killed her.

And it might.

Across the battlefield, Olivia danced between Raven’s increasingly frenzied attacks. The Blood Dancer spun effortlessly between attacks, her body flowing through her practiced defensive positions with inhuman speed. Each time a tendril broke through her guard, she countered it perfectly, Hydra’s Bane carving through shadow matter as though it were paper.

Marcus supported her from a distance, his psychic abilities disrupting Raven’s concentration at critical moments. Blood streamed freely from his nose and ears now, the price of pushing his powers beyond their limits.

But Tara couldn’t watch them.

She couldn’t protect them.

She could only focus on what lay before her.

“Tara!” Olivia called, concern evident in her voice as she glanced toward the healer. “What are you doing?”

The woman’s voice was distant.

Echoing.

Muffled, like a scream under water.

Tara couldn’t spare the breath to answer. The Echo Staff pulsed with increasing intensity, the crimson glow spreading outward in waves that illuminated the entire battlefield. Each pulse drew more of her life force, the drain becoming more severe with each passing second.

Where the amplified healing energy touched the shadow corruption, it sizzled and cracked. The darkness retreated, pushed back by the relentless light. Beneath Tara’s hands, Ace’s body warmed again, and color slowly returned to his face.

His eyes, however, refused to open, and his breathing remained ragged as ever.

Almost there.

The final surge of power came at devastating cost. Tara’s spine arched backward as the Echo Staff pulled from reserves she didn’t know existed—not just her Soul Meter now, but the very essence of who she was. Crimson light pulsed beneath her skin, mapping her veins and arteries in stark relief against her deathly pale skin. The connection between staff and healer became a visual symphony of agony as, with each passing second, another wave of her own life force slipped through the staff and into Ace’s still-closing wound.

Her body convulsed in violent tremors, her muscles seizing as the Echo Staff drained her beyond safe thresholds. Her now blue-tinged lips parted in a silent scream, but there wasn’t any air left in her lungs to make a single sound. Tara’s fingers, once steady from years of emergency medicine, curled into rigid claws around the staff’s shaft. The skin beneath her nails blanched white, then darkened to a mottled purple as small veins ruptured under the strain. A sickly numbness crept up her arms like winter frost across a windowpane, deadening all sensations inch by excruciating inch, until she couldn’t feel her hands at all.

Shadows encroached on her vision, and the world narrowed to a single point of focus—Ace. Even as her lungs struggled for air in shallow, halting gasps, she felt a terrible satisfaction watching the shadow corruption retreat before her wild and beautiful magic.

The shadow corruption had been purged from Ace’s wound. His breathing had stabilized. The ragged hole in his chest had closed entirely, and he was already stirring.

With trembling hands, Tara pulled the Echo Staff from the ground, breaking the feedback loop before it could drain her completely. Still, her paramedic’s instincts catalogued her own symptoms with clinical detachment: acute magical exsanguination, imminent systemic failure, probable death

Worth it, she thought as darkness claimed the periphery of her awareness. This is what I was made for.

In that final moment of consciousness, as her grip on the staff loosened and her body began to collapse, Tara experienced perfect clarity. She hadn’t merely pushed her powers beyond their limits. She had transcended them. In choosing Ace’s life over her own safety, she had discovered the true essence of her Blood Saint class: not just in healing through sacrifice, but salvation through surrender.

It was the paramedic’s curse, really, to heal others at the expense of oneself.

Her staff clattered to the blood-soaked ground as her fingers finally lost their grip. Her chest barely moved now, each breath a fragile whisper against the void spreading through her veins—a void left behind as the last reserves of her magic fueled one final, perfect healing pulse.

But the work was done, and she had succeeded.

As she fell, someone caught her. Strong arms held her back, and someone’s hand cradled her head. Her eyes fluttered open, but she couldn’t make sense of the world around her. Purple hazes on the sky. Green blurs somewhere beyond. A stretch of black fog that made no sense.

And damn, did her head hurt.

“Tara.” Ace’s voice reached her through the haze, sharp with urgency despite his own wounds. “Stay with me.”

She tried to respond, but her lips formed only silent shapes, no breath left to carry sound. The void had claimed too much of her, an inky emptiness spreading where vibrant power once flowed. Her eyelids fluttered, too heavy to keep open as consciousness slipped through her fingers like water.

Ace’s face swam above her, his features hardening with resolve as he took in her fading state.

“No one dies for me,” Ace growled.

In answer, Tara’s eyes fluttered closed. She just couldn’t keep them open anymore.

Something warm brushed her mouth—skin, followed by the sweet tang of something wet rolling across her cracked lips.

Blood.

She tried to push him away. He needed to save his strength. She hadn’t just risked her own life for him to give it right back.

Don’t be stupid, she wanted to say, but she didn’t have the strength.

“Drink,” he commanded, his palm cradling the back of her head. “That’s an order.”

As he spoke, the first drops hit her tongue like lightning striking parched earth. Tara’s body responded instinctively, her vampire nature asserting itself over her sanity or medical training. Her hand weakly reached for him, and her fingers coiled around his forearm. With a rough yank, she pulled it closer, and her fangs extended without a conscious thought.

Ace’s blood carried warmth, strength, and something else—fragments of his restored life force, the very essence she had sacrificed herself to save. It flowed into her empty reserves, lighting small fires in the void along the way. Each drop sent a wave of renewal through her shattered body.

The exchange lasted only seconds, but Tara experienced it as an eternity. Through the blood connection, she caught glimpses of Ace’s raw determination—his absolute refusal to allow her sacrifice, his unwavering belief in their unity.

With the growing awareness came clarity. The intoxicating power in his blood called to her, offering a swift path to full recovery if she simply took in enough. Her fangs pressed deeper into his skin as her vampire instincts urged her to drink it all.

To drain him completely.

The thought hit her like a sledgehammer to the brain, and she recoiled in disgust at having even allowed the idea into her head. She fell to one elbow and coughed into the grass, blood sputtering from her mouth as she fought to control herself.

“Good, you’re back,” he said.

Her head craned as she lifted her gaze to meet his. She hadn’t taken nearly enough to restore herself completely, but she’d taken precisely what she needed to survive.

Relief flooded through her, and she let out a weak chuckle. “Thanks.”

“Don’t mention it,” Ace said with a wry grin. “Did I miss anything important?”

“Oh, you know, nothing major,” Tara said with a weak gesture toward the others as they fought Raven in a desperate battle on the lakeshore.

“Fun,” Ace said under his breath.

“Don’t try to move yet,” she managed to say, even as a surge of nausea burned the back of her throat.

“You need to focus on yourself,” he said quietly. “I’ll be fine.”

She tried to nod, but she was too weak.

There was a pause, and she could practically feel the concern radiating off of him.

“You need more blood, Tara.”

“You need more,” he pointed out.

“No,” she said, her voice cracked and hoarse.

“C’mon. We need you back in the game, too.”

“It’s you or me, Ace,” she whispered with a shake of her head.

He pressed his forearm to her lips once again, but she weakly pushed him away.

“Go help the others, idiot,” she said.

“Tara, stop being a stubborn—”

“Go. Fight. That. Jackass,” she ordered, her voice firm and commanding despite her waning strength.

Though she still couldn’t open her eyes, she heard Ace sigh in exasperation.

“I mean it, Ace,” she said as she laid in his arms. “The best thing you can do for us right now is to kill him once and for all.”

“Alright, you win,” Ace replied.

The grass rustled, and the strong arms laid her gently on the ground. Her head spun, and she briefly opened her eyes long enough to see a towering blur hovering over her.

Ace.

Gently, Tara rolled onto her stomach and managed to lift herself up onto her elbows. Though her head felt like a bowling ball, she managed to hold it upright long enough to catch a glimpse of him walking away.

Ace raced toward Raven with renewed purpose with Moonfall gleaming in his hand. The blade caught the eerie purple sunlight, and a beam flashed across the metal as he raised it to strike. Raven had his back to Ace, apparently oblivious to the Dusk Emperor’s approach.

Tara sensed the change in him, something she had seen a few times on the ambulance. He wasn’t just healed, this time—he had been transformed by the experience of a brush with death.

Nothing like a close call to wake you up.

He was their leader. Their vanguard against the darkness. And she had given him the second chance he needed to finish what they started.

“Slice his throat open,” Tara said softly, as she collapsed once more onto the blackened grass. “And beat the shit out of him while you’re at it.”
