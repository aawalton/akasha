The cave opened above them like a cathedral made by water and time.

Sixty metres across at its widest point, the ceiling rising fifteen metres above the pool in a natural dome. Arthur’s eye caught the limestone vault first and ran the load. Water had worked this space over thousands of years, calcite growing down the walls in slow sheets. The Ether glow from the pool threw violet patterns across the walls.

And every wall was painted.

The party pulled themselves onto the dry stone ledge and stood in the middle of twenty-seven thousand years of human art. Water dripped from their wetsuits as they gazed in awe onto stone that Gravettian feet had walked on when mammoths grazed the steppe above.

When Steve spoke, his voice was quiet.

“Those are Przewalski horses. Stocky, short at the withers, the kind that ran the steppe when this was grassland. Charcoal laid down first, ochre over the top.”

“The bison here and here, heads lowered,” he continued. “That's a Solutrean convention. Nineteen thousand years ago. But the hands….”

He took a step closer to the east wall, ungloving one palm like a man approaching something sacred. “The hands are Gravettian. Black and red. Forty-four black, twenty-one red. This cave was painted twice. Different hands. Different millennia. Both came here on purpose.”

He turned in a slow half-circle. The chemlight swept past ibex leaping across a calcite flow that had sealed over them, the mineral deposit preserving the image beneath a translucent veil. Past seals and past great auks: three of them, one on the ceiling and two on the east wall, drawn with anatomical precision by someone who had watched them swim.

Then the light caught a child’s handprint.

Small and perfect. Placed at a height by someone who had been lifted up to reach the wall.

THE ETHER ATLAS: FIELD NOTES OF STEVEN PARR

Cosquer Cave, Marseille

THE PAINTED HALL

Can't sleep. If the Cosquer pattern holds, and patterns this clean usually do, then I am in trouble professionally and we are in trouble operationally. Working list, off the top of my head. Lascaux. Chauvet. Altamira. Niaux. El Castillo. Tito Bustillo. The whole Cantabrian coast. Pech Merle. Font-de-Gaume. Cussac. Coliboaia in Romania. Magura in Bulgaria. Apollo 11 in Namibia at 27,000 BP, same window as the Cosquer hands. Sulawesi at 45,500 BP. The Maros-Pangkep panels. Leang Tedongnge.

The Murujuga petroglyphs in Australia, which we used to date at 30,000 and now think might run older. Cueva de las Manos in Argentina. Serra da Capivara in Brazil. Every documented Paleolithic art site on Earth. Candidates, all of them. The hands at Cosquer were drawn 27,000 years ago by people who walked into a cave that already had Seeder infrastructure waiting under the stone. They put their hands on it because they could feel it or because they were guided there by someone.

The harder problem is the personal one and I am writing it down because if I don't write it down I will keep turning it over until morning. Twenty years of fieldwork. Twenty years of trying to reconstruct how Paleolithic humans thought. Why they painted what they painted, where they painted it, in the configurations they chose. Every paper I ever wrote assumed the artists were responding to the world they could see. Their lives, their hunts, their dead, their gods. I’m sure some of it was just that but a lot of it could be them responding to something I couldn't see and they couldn't name.

Or the world they could see was bigger than the world I was trained to look at. I don't know which of those is worse.
