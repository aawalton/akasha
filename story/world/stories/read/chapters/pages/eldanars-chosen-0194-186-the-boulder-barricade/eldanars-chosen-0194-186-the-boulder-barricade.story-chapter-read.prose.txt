The walk back from the Adventurers' Guild to the Bull's Head Inn was mercifully uneventful. The freezing night air had driven the vast majority of the refugees into their makeshift tents, leaving the cobblestone streets quiet save for the heavy, rhythmic clanking of Josh's plate armour and the soft crunch of frost beneath their boots.

When they pushed through the heavy wooden doors of the inn, the common room was dark and largely silent. Only a single oil lamp flickered near the bar. Garrik was standing behind the counter, wiping down the scarred wood with a damp cloth. The old fighter looked up as they entered, a weary but genuine smile breaking through his thick, grizzled beard.

"I was beginning to wonder if you had found a warmer floor to sleep on," Garrik murmured, keeping his deep voice low to avoid waking the families huddled near the cooling hearth.

"Not a chance," Josh smiled tiredly, walking over to the bar. He unbuckled his heavy sword belt and rested it against a stool. "We just spent the evening at the guild. Mich helped us secure our next contract. We are heading out again tomorrow morning."

Garrik stopped wiping the counter, his brow furrowing with sudden concern. "Tomorrow? You just survived a siege. You have only had one night of proper rest. The city guard is still terrified the walls are going to be breached any moment."

"Which is exactly why we need to leave," Josh explained patiently. "We are heading east to clear the goblin dungeon. We will probably be gone for a few days. We need to start pushing back against the surges. If we can stabilise the local portals and cull the wandering hordes, the craziness in the wilderness might finally start to die down. It might allow some of these families to actually go back to their farms, or at least allow merchants to move safely outside the city walls again."

Garrik looked at the young human fighter, his eyes shining with a mixture of pride and quiet apprehension. "You are taking a lot onto those broad shoulders, lad. Just ensure you keep your shield up. Goblins are weak, but in large numbers, they will drag down a heavily armoured knight just to strip the steel from his corpse."

"We will be careful," Josh promised. "Is our spot by the fire still open?"

"Always," Garrik nodded, gesturing to the corner. "Get some sleep. I will have a hot pot of porridge ready before you leave."

The party spent their second night in Ashenfall sleeping on thin straw pallets laid across the hard wooden floorboards. Despite the total lack of comfort, the profound exhaustion in their bones demanded rest, and sleep claimed them quickly.

The next morning broke with a bitterly cold, grey light. After a hasty breakfast of watery oats, they gathered their gear and returned to the guild hall.

The atmosphere was already buzzing with nervous energy. Mich was waiting for them near the front entrance, standing beside a surprisingly large assembly of adventurers. There were roughly twenty fighters, rogues, and spellcasters gathered in the hall, divided into four distinct groups. They were all wearing a mix of boiled leather and cheap chainmail, looking eager but noticeably anxious.

"Morning!" Mich greeted them briskly, her ink-stained fingers clutching a clipboard. "These are the other parties I mentioned. They are all hovering around level fifteen to twenty. All four of these groups will be accompanying you to the eastern forest."

Brett raised a highly sceptical eyebrow, his new drake-scale gloves glowing faintly in the dim light. "We are escorting them?"

"They are assisting you," Mich corrected smoothly. "Once you have cleared the immediate threat and stabilised the portal, two of these groups will remain behind to secure the perimeter and establish a camp. The other two groups will then return here to Ashenfall to escort the novice tier adventurers out there. The city needs capable fighters, and we cannot train them safely if the low-level zones are overrun with massive hordes."

Josh looked over the assembled fighters. They were a mixed bag. Some looked competent enough, but others held their weapons with the clumsy, hesitant grip of raw recruits who had never faced a true life-or-death situation. Coordinating a group of five was second nature now, but managing a small militia of twenty-five was a different challenge entirely.

"We will take the lead," Josh told Mich firmly, pitching his voice so the other parties could hear him clearly. "They follow our orders, they stick to the path, and they do not engage unless we tell them to. Is that understood?"

A tall, broad-shouldered human fighter from one of the accompanying parties stepped forward. He wore a decent set of iron chainmail and carried a large, two-handed broadsword. He had a proud, arrogant set to his jaw.

"My name is Thorne," the fighter announced, his tone bordering on abrasive. "My party and I have cleared that dungeon several times before the surge. We know how to kill goblins. We do not need a babysitter."

Josh met Thorne's aggressive stare with unwavering calm. He simply let the silence stretch for a few seconds, his storm-grey eyes boring into the other man. The sheer physical presence of Josh in his heavy plate armour, combined with the latent, humming energy of a tier three class advancement, was intimidating. Thorne shifted uncomfortably, the bravado slipping slightly.

"Good," Josh said quietly. "Then you will know exactly how to follow my orders. Let's move."

The large, mixed group left the guild hall and exited Ashenfall through the eastern gates. The morning frost crunched loudly beneath the boots of two dozen adventurers as they hit the muddy frontier road.

For the first few hours, the journey was a simple, tedious march. They followed the same deeply rutted tracks they had used during their very first outing as a party. The open plains stretched endlessly on their left, while the dark, ancient treeline of the eastern forest loomed on their right.

When the road finally curved, Josh signalled for the group to cut directly into the dense woods.

The atmosphere shifted immediately. The bright morning light was swallowed by the thick pine canopy, plunging them into a damp, oppressive twilight. The air smelled of rotting wood, wet earth, and something far more foul. Moving twenty-five people through thick undergrowth was a noisy, clumsy affair. Branches snapped loudly, and the less experienced fighters cursed as they tripped over hidden roots.

"Perberos," Josh called out softly, halting the column. "Take the point. Take any competent scouts from the other parties with you. I want a mile buffer between us and any horde."

Perberos nodded silently, though his silver eyes betrayed a hint of frustration at the noise the group was making. He gestured to a small group of nervous-looking rogues and a young beastkin ranger from Thorne's party. The elf melted into the undergrowth without making a single sound. The other scouts scrambled to follow him, their movements clumsy and loud in comparison to the elf.

It did not take long for Perberos to find the signs.

The forest floor was littered with the crude, disgusting markers of a goblin infestation. The elf pointed out massive piles of rotting, half-eaten animal carcasses left carelessly near the roots of ancient trees. The smell of raw sewage was overpowering in certain hollows. Every few hundred yards, they found crude, terrifying totems constructed from snapped branches, rusty wire, and the shattered skulls of unfortunate forest hounds.

"The density is staggering," Perberos whispered to a young human rogue beside him, pausing behind a thick fern. "They are destroying the local ecosystem. There are too many mouths to feed."

Suddenly, Perberos held up a single, leather-clad hand. The scouts behind him froze instantly.

In a small clearing just ahead, a patrol of six goblins was arguing violently over the bloody remains of a deer. They were small, ugly, and clad in filthy scraps of leather, their jagged, rusted blades completely neglected.

Before the human rogue could even draw his daggers, Perberos moved.

The elf did not bother with stealth. The goblins were so low level, and so distracted, they simply did not register as a threat to a tier three ranger. Perberos stepped out from the ferns, drawing his longbow in a fluid, blurring motion.

The bowstring hummed a deadly, rapid tune. Three arrows materialised in the throats of three goblins before the first body even hit the moss. The remaining three creatures shrieked in terror and lunged forward.

Perberos dropped his bow and drew his twin curved blades. He stepped directly into the path of the frantic, rushing goblins. He ducked under a wild swing from a rusted cleaver, spun seamlessly, and decapitated two of the monsters in a single, perfectly executed horizontal strike. The final goblin turned to flee, but Perberos simply hurled his left blade like a throwing knife. The elven steel buried itself deep between the creature's shoulder blades, dropping it instantly.

The entire skirmish lasted less than five seconds.

Perberos calmly walked over, retrieved his blade and arrows, wiped them clean on a patch of moss, and turned back to the wide-eyed, completely stunned scouts behind him.

"Clear the path," Perberos instructed mildly, picking up his bow. "The main column will be coming through shortly."

They encountered several more of these small, wandering patrols over the next few hours. Every single encounter was laughably easy. The sheer difference in power between a level twenty-five party and a roaming pack of level five goblins was astronomical. Josh did not even need to draw his sword. Bhel spent most of the journey complaining loudly that his axes were getting bored, occasionally backhanding a leaping goblin out of the air with his heavy gauntlet just to feel useful.

The adventurers following them watched in absolute awe. The terror of the frontier was completely negated by the overwhelming, brutal efficiency of the experienced fighters leading them.

But the easy confidence vanished when they finally reached their destination.

The group broke through a final, dense thicket of thorny bushes and stepped out onto a high, rocky ridge. Below them lay the valley that housed the portal to the Goblin Caves.

Josh stepped up to the edge of the ridge, and his breath completely left his lungs.

If they had thought the Kobold siege inside the town walls was massive, it was an absolute drop in the ocean compared to the nightmare unfolding beneath them. Apparently, this is what happens when a broken portal is allowed to spill for several days.

The valley floor was completely covered. There was no grass visible, no mud, and no rocks. There was only a writhing, chaotic ocean of dark green flesh, rusted iron, and crude bone armour. There were several thousand common goblins, their shrill, shrieking voices combining into a deafening, continuous roar that vibrated in the stone beneath their feet.

But it was not just goblins.

Standing head and shoulders above the smaller creatures were thousands of heavily muscled orcs. They carried massive, jagged war axes and wore thick plates of bolted iron. Lumbering slowly through the dense crowds, easily parting the sea of smaller monsters, were hundreds of hulking, grey-skinned cave trolls, their massive tusks gleaming dully in the weak sunlight.

Scattered throughout the horde were specialised, terrifying variants. Josh could see goblin shamans waving bone staves that sparked with erratic, dangerous green magic, and massive orc bosses clad entirely in heavy, spiked steel armour.

At the very back of the valley, the entrance to the cave was a pitch-black, gaping maw in the rock face, practically vomiting a continuous stream of new monsters into the already overflowing basin.

"By the Mother Tree," Carcan whispered, her hands flying to her mouth in sheer horror. She gripped her amber-topped staff tightly. "There are tens of thousands of them."

"This is not a dungeon break," Bhel breathed, his dark eyes wide beneath his bushy brow. The dwarf had completely stopped complaining about being bored. "This is an invading army."

Thorne, the arrogant human fighter from the secondary party, pushed his way to the front of the ridge. He stared down at the massive, writhing horde, his chest heaving with sudden adrenaline. He drew his massive broadsword, the steel scraping loudly against its scabbard.

"We have the high ground," Thorne announced, his voice practically trembling with reckless excitement. He looked at Josh and his party. "You guys are tier three. We are all nearing level twenty. These are just basic goblins and standard orcs. If we charge down there right now, we can slaughter them. We can break their lines before they even know what hit them!"

Brett, who had been silently analysing the valley with cold, academic precision, snapped his head around and glared at Thorne as if the man was completely insane.

"Put that sword away before you get us all killed, you absolute fool," Brett commanded, his voice dripping with venomous authority.

Thorne bristled, gripping his weapon tighter. "Excuse me? We out-level them massively. We can cut through them like wheat!"

"In a one-on-one fight? Yes," Brett argued, stepping aggressively towards the larger man, completely unintimidated by the drawn sword. His dark red gloves sparked with a tiny, threatening flash of raw plasma. "In a one-on-ten fight? We would crush them without breaking a sweat. But that down there is a one-on-a-thousand scenario. You will swing that heavy piece of iron until your arms tear themselves from their sockets, and there will still be five thousand angry beasts waiting to rip your head off."

Thorne glared down at the mage, his face flushing red. "We have healers. We have magic."

"And we have absolutely no intelligence regarding their elite modifiers," Brett continued mercilessly, gesturing down to the massive orc bosses and the shamans. "We do not know if the portal surge has naturally increased their base levels. A level fifteen orc boss is entirely capable of cleaving you in half if you are exhausted from killing a thousand of his weaker minions. A blind charge is suicide."

"He is afraid," Thorne scoffed, looking at the other younger adventurers for support. "The great tier three mage is afraid of a few goblins."

Josh stepped forward. He did not draw a weapon. He simply placed himself squarely between Thorne and Brett. His presence was like a physical wall of solid steel.

"Listen to me very carefully," Josh said, his voice entirely calm, devoid of any anger, which made it infinitely more terrifying. He looked Thorne directly in the eyes. "We survive because we use our heads. We dictate the terms of the engagement. If you charge down that hill right now, I will not stop you. But know this. I will not follow you. My party will not follow you. And when you are surrounded by a hundred orcs, screaming for a healer, you will die. Alone. In the mud, covered in your own blood and piss. Make your choice."

The silence on the ridge was absolute. The young adventurers stared at Josh, completely terrified by the cold, uncompromising reality of his words.

Thorne looked down at the massive, terrifying horde in the valley, then back at Josh's impassive, storm-grey eyes. The bravado completely drained from the younger fighter. He slowly, reluctantly lowered his broadsword and slid it back into its scabbard.

"Fine," Thorne muttered, looking away. "What is the plan, then?"

Josh turned back to look out over the valley. He pointed to the far edge of the basin, where the rocky walls grew steep and incredibly narrow.

"We need to negate their numbers," Josh explained, slipping into his tactical mindset. "Just like we did at the riverbed after the siege. We need a choke point. We find a narrow pass, we fortify it heavily, and we draw them to us. We force them to fight us head on, where their bodies count for absolutely nothing."

"Perberos," Josh called out, not looking away from the valley. "Take the scouts. Find me a strong point."

The elven ranger nodded and vanished along the rocky ridgeline, taking the rogues with him. The rest of the party waited in tense, uncomfortable silence, watching the horrific, writhing mass of monsters below.

It took thirty agonising minutes for Perberos to return.

"I have found it," the elf announced, materialising beside Josh. He pointed towards the southern edge of the valley. "There is a deep, naturally formed rocky defile. The walls are sheer stone, rising forty feet high on either side. It is completely impassable from the flanks. The entrance is incredibly narrow, perhaps wide enough for only ten or twelve orcs to stand shoulder to shoulder. The corridor extends back for nearly fifty yards before opening up into a wider ravine."

"It is perfect," Josh said, a fierce, predatory grin touching his lips. "We plug the gap with heavy armour. We put the ranged fighters on the high ledges. We turn that narrow corridor into a meat grinder."

"There is a surplus of loose, heavy boulders near the entrance," Perberos added. "If we move them, we can artificially narrow the gap even further."

"Let's get to work," Bhel cracked his massive knuckles, a wide smile splitting his braided beard. "I love a bit of heavy lifting."

They moved as quietly as possible, slipping along the ridgeline and descending carefully into the wider ravine behind the chosen choke point. When they reached the narrow defile, Josh immediately saw why Perberos had chosen it. It was a flawless, natural fortress. The steep, jagged stone walls offered excellent, protected vantage points for the archers and spellcasters, looking directly down into the narrow, muddy corridor.

The next hour was spent in gruelling manual labour.

Bhel, Josh, and Thorne led the heavy fighters in rolling massive, jagged boulders towards the mouth of the defile. Having twenty extra adventurers made the work significantly faster, though coordinating them required constant supervision from Bhel to ensure they did not make a sound that would alert the horde in the valley beyond. They stacked the heavy stones to create a solid, waist-high barricade on either side of the entrance, effectively narrowing the fighting space by half.

"This is good," Bhel grunted, wiping sweat from his brow as he wedged a final rock into place. He patted the sturdy barricade. "It will break their charge. They will trip over the stones, and they won't be able to flank us."

"Positions," Josh ordered, stepping back to survey their handiwork.

He moved to the centre of the narrow gap, planting his heavy boots firmly in the dirt. He drew his longsword and raised his battered, gouged steel shield, locking it securely against his left arm. The helmet Garrik had given him sat heavily on his head, the magical stat boost humming reassuringly against his skull.

Bhel stepped up to his immediate right, his twin axes drawn and resting comfortably in his massive hands. Thorne, looking slightly paler now that the reality of the impending battle had set in, took up a position on Josh's left, raising his broadsword.

Behind them, filling out the rest of the narrow line, stood nine other armoured fighters from the secondary parties. The combined presence of four distinct groups created a solid, unbroken wall of steel and iron across the mouth of the defile.

"Ranged fighters and support, take the high ground!" Josh commanded.

Perberos scrambled up the sheer rock face with impossible agility, finding a secure, shadowed ledge about twenty feet above the frontline. He unslung his beautiful longbow, nocking an arrow and waiting in silence. The other rogues and archers quickly found their own perches along the jagged walls.

Carcan stood directly behind Josh, safely protected by the wall of fighters. Her hands were already glowing with a soft, warm golden light, her amber-topped staff resting lightly on the ground. The younger, inexperienced healers gathered nervously around her, drawing comfort from her serene, professional presence.

Finally, Brett walked up to stand just behind the front line. The mage did not seek the high ground. He preferred to be close enough to feel the heat of the battle.

He raised his hands, his long fingers spread wide. The deep red drake-scale gloves sparked violently. Brett snapped his fingers, and a tiny, serpentine flame familiar darted from his sleeve. The creature was made entirely of concentrated, roaring fire. It looped through the air, leaving a trail of intense heat, before diving directly into the dirt at Brett's feet.

A wave of pure, shimmering thermal energy exploded outward from the mage. The magical aura washed over Josh, Bhel, Thorne, and the other fighters in the front line.

Instantly, the steel weapons of every single fighter ignited.

Josh's longsword erupted into roaring, orange flames. Bhel's twin axes burned with a fierce, intense heat, the fire dancing along the scarred iron edges. Thorne stared at his flaming broadsword in absolute, terrified awe.

"Do not fear the fire," Brett announced smoothly, his voice carrying clearly over the crackle of the flames. "It will not burn you. It will only incinerate whatever you strike. You are wielding raw devastation. Make it count."

Josh looked at his flaming sword, then reached deep into his own core. He tapped into the latent energy of his class. Static electricity erupted across his heavy plate armour, bright, jagged arcs of blue lightning hissing and snapping in the cold air. The air instantly smelled heavily of ozone and burning fuel.

He was a terrifying sight. A massive, heavily armoured knight wreathed in blue lightning, wielding a blade of roaring fire.

Josh raised his flaming sword high into the air, the intense light casting long, dramatic shadows against the stone walls of the defile. He looked at Bhel, then at Thorne, and finally up at Perberos on the ledge.

"Perberos," Josh yelled, his voice booming like thunder, intentionally shattering the silence of the valley. "Wake them up!"

The elf did not hesitate. He drew his bowstring back to his ear and fired a single, whistling arrow high over the barricade, directly into the massive, writhing horde of monsters waiting in the valley beyond.

The battle for the Goblin Caves had begun.
