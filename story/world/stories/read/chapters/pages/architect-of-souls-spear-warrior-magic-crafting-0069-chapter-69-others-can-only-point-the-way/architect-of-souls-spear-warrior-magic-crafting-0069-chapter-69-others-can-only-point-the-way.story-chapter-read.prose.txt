Ferris swapped the jade slip over to Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting, then went back to meditation.

Inside the jade slip, Ferris once again found himself in an elegant library filled with pedestals containing books. Ferris had been in a rush the first time he’d explored this jade slip, worried about the stolen oath statue. He’d found the information he needed and ran. Since then, he’d spent almost every day either working or recovering from the loss of a mental body. He had wanted to visit the library and review the information more closely, but it wasn’t a priority as he knew he needed more foundational knowledge before he could progress his crossbow to a better version.

Still, Ferris decided to spend the rest of the morning looking through the jade slip on crossbow construction. First, he went to his soulspace to create a new stack of paper. Returning to the jade slip, Ferris transcribed three of the books from the pedestals as closely as possible.

He called them books, but they were much shorter than the one in the Acid Resistance jade slip. All three of them combined only took about two dozen pages—much more affordable. It would take Ferris a lot more qi to copy everything, but that was the nice part about the progressive series of designs. He could leave the more advanced knowledge for later.

The first book he copied was an introduction to proper form when shooting a crossbow. Ferris had read it briefly before, but seeing it again after all his practice made certain parts make a lot more sense. He also saw where he had started to diverge from the instruction, forming bad habits. The book had a lot of pictures of proper poses, and Ferris tried to take it slow and recreate them as best as possible.

Next, he copied the crossbow blueprint that he had already made. Ferris had a good grasp on this design, as he had made it twice. It was fairly simple altogether, although he still didn’t feel satisfied with his work. The crossbow he’d built functioned, but his construction failed to match the size specifications. To claim he had completed even this first, most basic level, he would need to get other basic technical manuals that showed how to craft more precisely with qi. Hopefully, Mental Materials would have a starting point, but he expected precision to be a long-term struggle.

Last, he moved on to copy the next crossbow blueprint. The main difference was that it had more pieces, and more places where two parts needed to fit together. The first design had thankfully avoided that as much as possible, but this one didn’t. One of the primary areas that got more complex was the trigger. Before, it was just a lever Ferris pressed up through a hole, which pushed the string off a hook to fire. This trigger instead held the string taunt itself and would lower to fire the weapon.

Overall, the second design felt very similar to the first, just more… complete. It was as if someone had taken the second design and ruthlessly simplified everything about it to make the first blueprint.

Looking ahead, the third crossbow design changed the arming method. Instead of bracing the bottom of the stock with a foot and lifting up, it had a lever that was pulled back.

He did his best to copy the three books as closely as possible, and then took the pages back to his soulspace to store for later. It would have been nice to have some kind of binding to hold the pages together, but it wasn’t worth the qi.

After leaving the library, Ferris and Jade found a bench nearby to eat the lunch Mother Lily had packed for them. It was a set of sandwiches layered with pickled cabbage and grilled mushrooms. Jade was back to rating everything a ten out of ten. They had forgotten the scale after the tests with Serena. Ferris hadn’t been too sure about turning the game into a magic detection ability—wanting to keep the game fun for the slime—but he was starting to see the advantage of teaching Jade a more commonly used measurement.

After lunch, they headed for the training hall to meet Serena. It was a relief that he wouldn’t need to apologize again for not being ready to continue.

Ferris was early again, although this time he didn’t have any special reason. He just didn’t have any other plans after lunch before the meeting.

When Serena arrived—also early, just less so than Ferris—she found him playing a coordination game with Jade. He didn’t get as much out of the games as he used to, but it was fun to direct the slime around in various ways.

“Greetings, Ferris and Jade,” Serena said as she entered. She was once again carrying a large sack that clanked whenever she moved.

“Hello, Serena,” Ferris said, and Jade rolled over to bounce around a bit near her. The slime had committed her to permanent memory after the many times she had fed them.

“I am happy to report that I was able to purchase Acid Resistance Ability Construction this morning from the library,” he said with a smile, sitting at the table.

“Excellent,” she said, setting the sack down next to her and sitting across from him.

Jade asked Ferris to be lifted up, and then started rolling around the table as they talked.

“I am grateful for your help,” Ferris said, “but having reviewed the instructions, I worry that this will be harder than I expected. I should mention that I have been focusing all my efforts on healing my leg, and I have not yet been able to purchase Mental Materials. I have had some guidance from my mentor, who has helped me so far, but the instructions for Acid Resistance don’t match what I know.”

Serena seemed a bit surprised, but appeared to understand more after learning I had Uncle Winton’s teachings. She considered the words for a few moments before responding.

“I hesitate to say it, but that shouldn’t be a problem. That manual teaches the correct way to learn the ability, but by asking me to assist you, Elder Harper has given me tacit approval to help you skip some of the work. This is usually considered a bad thing,” she shrugged, “but that is because learning to build your own abilities helps ease you into delving and is eventually needed anyway, or so I’m told. You, on the other hand, need Acid Reconstruction on top of everything everyone else’s needs, so ‘easing you in’ is redundant.”

“However,” she looked off to the side, “I didn’t realize exactly how… early… you are. I expected you to still be working on learning the techniques, but not even having Mental Materials surprised me.”

Ferris could only sigh. He didn’t regret any of the merit point purchases he had made. The chain helped safeguard him before he understood the bond and Jade’s memory, and now it protected the core. The crossbow crafting manual was key to preventing the destruction of the oath. There was a little regret that he had to pay the fine for destroying his bed with acid, but that was better than letting the scarabs infest the slimespace, potentially killing Jade.

“The first question I have,” Ferris asked, “is if I need to purchase the manual before we continue?”

She spent a moment in thought before answering.

“No, with my method, there is only one technique you need to learn, and I can talk you through it. You should purchase it soon, though; you’ll need time to practice its techniques.” She was looking at her hand as she spoke, likely feeling both a desire for him to progress faster and a worry that she was taking advantage of him.

For his part… he wanted to delve. He understood that taking shortcuts would eventually hurt him, but he had a long road ahead to find the ability to heal his leg. He expected he would have nothing but time to learn things the correct way while stuck at later points of the research project.

“Then,” Ferris said, “I presume the next purchase I will need to make will be for the magical resources required for the ability.” He paused for a second, a bit of a blush appearing on his face. The jade slip specified he needed a handheld metal pyramid (32mp) and a lizard’s tail (32mp). “I must admit, I am not familiar with how these things work. The manual listed two item names, with some numbers and alternatives, but I have never purchased magical resources before.”

Serena nodded and thankfully didn’t comment on his lack of knowledge. Instead, she just explained.

“It is a simple concept,” she said, taking out her knife and a bushel of carrots. She had Jade’s immediate attention as she lifted one up. He didn’t believe they had any plans for more tests involving food… had she brought them just because she wanted to feed Jade?

“This carrot is mundane,” Serena continued talking, eyes locked on the slime, “but some carrots that are found out in the rifts have a magical nature to them.”

Ferris nodded. He knew that things like the slime ranch’s berries or the items Uncle Winton had fed Jade were ‘magical,’ but no one explained it further.

Serena cut off a part of the carrot and tossed it to Jade. She was well-practiced at managing such tasks with only one hand.

“This can be detected with different abilities,” she continued, “and a numerical standard is used to determine how strong the magic is. Our clan uses ‘mp’ or magical potential. Thus, if you had an ability that required a magical carrot, any carrot would work as long as its mp was equal to or higher than the number listed. This carrot, being mundane, would be considered to have an mp of zero.”

Ferris frowned, then asked, “By potential, you mean it has the capability for holding a certain amount of magic?”

“Sort of,” Serena nodded, “although the word potential is used for a reason. It is common to also find abilities to measure ‘magical strength,’ which can be charged up to the item’s potential. At full strength, the item itself is more magically powerful. It is advised not to think of it as a container with capacity, as that metaphor breaks down quickly. In general, knowing the current ‘strength’ is both more common and less useful than the upper limit. Usually, magic items will lose strength over time. They can be recharged by feeding them qi, although some can power themselves. Magical potential is the strength of a magic item at full power, where it won’t accept any more qi.”

Ferris remembered the magical berries from the slime ranch. Foreman Carter warned him that they would lose potency over time, and to use them quickly. Could he have fed qi to the berry? When the man had said ‘potency,’ was that the same as strength? Why couldn’t you just feed an item an endless amount of qi? How did the magic items that powered themselves work? Did they make their own qi?

A stream of questions ran through his head, until he picked one to ask.

“What is the difference between, say, a magical carrot or a magical potato?” Ferris asked.

“That,” she said with a shrug, “gets into the study of enchantment. If you want to know more, you will need to purchase an introduction at the library.”

Ferris did want to know more, especially because of Nyoka’s hint to investigate the process of forging a bonded weapon.

He could see the relation between the concepts of connectivity and sacrifice to Acid Resistance’s required items of a pyramid and a lizard’s tail, but it felt like there must be something more. Conceptual relations did matter in the soul, so that must be at least part of it, but perhaps not the entire explanation.

Serena chopped up a few more pieces of carrots and fed them to Jade while he thought.

“Then, what about a magical object like your knife?” or, he thought to himself, Jade’s magical chain and cage protecting their core.

“This,” she said, waving it a bit, “was forged and then enchanted. It also has a magical potential rating, but its magic strength won’t fade anywhere near as quickly as a raw magical resource. Equally important is the skill and technique that went into its construction. The value of an enchanted weapon cannot be easily determined by mp, although it can provide a clue. For example, if second-tier resources had been used, the mp would be much higher.”

So, Ferris thought, Jade’s chain is likely the same way. He noted that she said, ‘won’t fade as quickly,’ not that it wouldn’t lose strength at all. Ferris hadn’t known that was something to look out for; was it worse being constantly submerged in Jade’s body? Maybe his uncle could tell him more.

After a moment of consideration, she continued.

“The same could be said for the value of special items found in the rifts.” She cut off two pieces of carrot. With a flourish, she lifted them both up, wedged between different fingers of her hand. “One carrot might have a special nature worth much more than others with the same mp. If a carrot with a special nature is commonly found in a specific keystone, then it usually gets a more unique name.” She tossed them both over to Jade, who happily ate them.

Ferris thought that might be the source of some of the more… elaborate… names he had heard in stories.

“Then, the required items for Acid Resistance can be anything that matches the description and meets the mp requirement. What about the alternatives?”

“Those,” she answered, “are listed in case you cannot find the required item. The primary item is picked partly for being the most suitable type, but also for its availability to our clan. For example, there are a lot of scaled monsters around here, so magical lizard’s tails are pretty common.”

“There is a rift around here that regularly has small magical geometrical shapes like pyramids?” Ferris asked.

“Ah,” she said, “that is different. If you take something like a magical metal ore, a skilled craftsman can forge it into other shapes while maintaining the magical potential. The pyramid is the primary choice because we can just special-order it made for you. If a resource can be created by our clan and still work, it is almost always the first choice over monster parts or items found in the rifts. Unless, of course, it is something like magical claws or teeth, there are a ton of those.”

“I see,” said Ferris, although he wasn’t sure he did. What was magic, that someone could take a bar of metal and form it into another shape, changing the significance and use in his soul? He could see how the conceptual representation could be changed to suit the need; was that all it was?

“When you purchase the magical resources from the clan, they will likely not be fully charged. They will have bled off strength while in storage. After you move them to your soul, it is best to pour liquid qi over them until they stop absorbing any more.”

Ferris cringed at that. Yet another thing to spend qi on. Plus, how did you recharge magical items in the real world, like Serena’s knife or Jade’s chain? His best guess was a special ability or technique.

“Yes,” Serena said, seeing his expression. “Thankfully, anything in your soul won’t lose strength over time, so you only need to do it once.”

Interesting, Ferris thought, does the soul hold the magic in, or is it somehow passively recharging it? I could test that by waiting and seeing if a resource slowly gained strength over time. Although I would need a technique to measure the strength first…

“Then,” Ferris moved on to his next question, “the rest of the construction is made from qi?”

“Nope,” Serena said with a big grin, pulling over the large, clanking bag. “That is where we cheat.”
