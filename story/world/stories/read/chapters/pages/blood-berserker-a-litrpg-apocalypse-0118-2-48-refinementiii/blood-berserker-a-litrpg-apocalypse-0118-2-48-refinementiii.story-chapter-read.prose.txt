Nathan's eyes narrowed in a fit of rage as he ripped Oblivion free from the head of the demon. His eyes locked on the text that hovered at the corner of his eyes.

Someone, or something, had seen fit to attack his mine while he was away. That was something he couldn't have.

Even worse was the fact that he was still here, trapped in the stronghold of this demon, while whatever it was, attacked those who were residing in his mine..

A flash of anger crossed the berserker's face as he dismissed the system notification. And picked up the corpse of the pit fiend. Clutching it in a tight grip, he rushed at the walls of the maze.

His mind in total focus, he needed to get out of here as soon as possible. There was no telling exactly what had attacked his mine, or whether Ciara and the others could handle it. And he wasn't going to risk taking a chance, especially if he could clear this and be on his way as soon as possible.

That was seemingly going to be his biggest issue: leaving the demon’s stronghold.

Nathan knew that the only way he was going to be able to leave the stronghold was by fighting the demon prince Kabash. It meant that he’d have to do that as soon as possible, or he risked having his time wasted by fighting fodder like the pit fiend or the Xharnton while Ciara and the others fought something much worse.

Or maybe I’m just overreacting, and they can handle the intruders.

It wasn’t absurd for him to assume that Ciara, Ruben, and Tasha could handle the intruders. Of course, the berserker had to accept that thoughts like that bordered on wishful thinking, especially in an apocalypse.

That was why Nathan focused on the worst possibility, which was that everyone in his stronghold and mine could lose their lives, and he, on the other hand, could die before even getting a chance to return to the island.

Keeping in line with his pessimistic view of things, he had a lot less than forty-eight hours, considering the 3-hour-to-a-day time dilation in the demon prince’s stronghold.

It was possible that the system had factored in the demon’s time dilation, and it would give him his forty-eight hours, but he highly doubted that.

Wishful thinking.

He knew he couldn’t afford to be optimistic around anything that concerned the system; he’d have to be grounded in his current reality. They might’ve found a way to put him in a tight spot, but Nathan would overcome and adapt just like he always had.

First things first, I have to figure out which direction the demon prince was in.

Nathan had a hunch that Kabash was at the center of the maze which should be at the last spot he’d seen the demon prince but with the constant walking through the twists and turns of the maze, he’d lost his sense of direction, and even if his guess that the demon was at the center was correct, he didn’t have a clue which way the center was.

But Nathan did have something.

He closed his eyes and sucked in a deep breath, doing his best to find a connection to the wind, and once he did, he exhaled while silently asking the wind for assistance.

A light breeze blew across his face as the wind showed its eagerness to help.

Nathan kept his eyes closed as he connected to the wind in a way that words could not describe, and in a few moments, he had an inkling of where the wind wanted him to go. There!

He opened his eyes and immediately turned to the maze word to his right. Without wasting time, he picked up the husk of the demon, using it as a light shield, and he rammed through wall after wall, breaking through them like they were nothing. He no longer had time to play games.

He needed to get out of here as fast as possible. And with the time dilation in the stronghold, Nathan could not afford to waste any more time.

It took a while, but Nathan eventually broke through the final wall, emerging into a rounded clearing. A quick look around revealed that the clearing was in a ring shape, with multiple maze endings at different sides.

So this is where all paths lead to.

The clearing would have been bare save for the demon prince Kabash standing in the center, its eyes closed as it seemed to be muttering.

Kabash was surrounded by the bodies of some priests strewn about in a rough, haphazard circle, all unconscious and ignorant of where they were.

Nathan could easily see the ecstasy in the hideous face of the demon prince Kabash. His eyes flitted to the priests strewn about until he finally caught sight of the priestess, and he quickly averted his eyes in case the demon was paying any attention.

The priests strewn about seemed to be losing color by the second, and it was undoubtedly tied to whatever the demon prince was trying to do to them.

Done taking in the sight, Nathan flung the scraps that remained from the corpse of the pit fiend after he’d utilized it to pummel through the walls.

Nathan's eyes locked in on the form of the demon, still eyes closed, ecstasy showing on his face, but his eyes quickly shifted to the side as another entity emerged from one of the open ends of the maze in the clearing—the ranger.

The ranger looked worse for wear, and his clothes were burnt in different directions, but the ranger still had that intense look in his eyes, even as he studied the situation, and he quickly notched an arrow and aimed in the direction of the Demon Prince Kabash.

It was only then that Kabash opened his eyes, which flitted between the berserker and the ranger at opposite ends, a look of disgust deeply etched on his face. “I'd assumed that you both would have been dead by now, but you both have proven to be stubborn, more stubborn than the lesser demons of hell.”

“It seems I will have to end you both before I can enjoy my meal.” The demon closed his eyes once again. “Although I believe it would be better if you both kill each other.”

[Hidden Mind (I)] Triggered.

A heavy wave descended, a budding pressure descended on Nathan's skull, but as quickly as it started, it simply dissipated into nothingness as his skill,  [Hidden Mind (I)], activated. The same couldn't be said for the ranger, whose eyes had turned glossy as he turned his gaze to Nathan, his notched arrow now aimed at the berserker.

The demon grunted. “I see you managed to evade my mental attacks.” He turned to Tristan. “Kill him.”

Nathan cursed as he instinctively moved out of the way, narrowly avoiding an arrow to the throat.

The arrow that the ranger had just fired landed with a solid thwack, as it stood erect, embedded at the spot Nathan had stood a moment ago.

Nathan knew that fighting the ranger was going to be a lot harder than any of the two demons he had fought previously. He’d seen the ranger in action before the maze had been created. He knew how fast the ranger could loose arrows, and it was clear that this fight would be more difficult than the other fights he’d had today, especially since this fight was more suited to the ranger than him, as there was a lot of distance between them.

Glancing out the corner of his eyes, he saw that Kabash had closed back his eyes, the look of ecstasy returning as the priests strewn about resumed losing color.

Nathan spat on the floor in frustration, then sprinted towards the ranger. To avoid stepping on the bodies strewn about, he had to go around them, which was worse for his case, as it was a clearing and the ranger was obviously better suited to fighting this way than he was.

Yet he continued to run, even as the ranger pelted him with a volley of arrows.  But the berserker, with the help of the wind, narrowly avoided each strike—Nathan was on high alert as he continued to close the distance between him and the ranger.

The situation he’d found himself in wasn’t ideal, but Nathan knew that his best chance of taking on the demon was putting down the ranger—he couldn’t do both at the same time. Nathan didn’t believe that he needed to kill the ranger, which meant, at the very least, he had to figure out a way to knock the ranger unconscious.

Or if push came to shove, he would have to incapacitate the ranger by breaking one or both of his hands.

That way, if they survived the fight against the demon prince, a priest or a level up would be enough to return the ranger to prime shape. Probably.

Unfortunately for Nathan, the ranger did not share the same sentiments of preserving life as most of the arrows loosed targeted fatal spots.

It was a weird situation to be in, because while the ranger was actively trying to kill him, Nathan was trying to find a way not to kill the ranger. And for someone like the berserker—who wasn't well-versed in the act of sparring—he wasn't quite sure that he could do this without killing the ranger.

But regardless of his thoughts on the matter, he kept on the charge, avoiding arrows that were fired at him. "Is it just me, or has he gotten a lot slower since the last time?" Nathan mused.

While the rangers' arrows were deadly, with most of them targeted at his eyes, the berserker could have sworn that the dead ranger wasn't as fast as he had been earlier, which made Nathan a bit confused.

Was the ranger trying to fight the demon's mind control, or had something else happened to the ranger that was affecting his firing speed? Either way, Nathan really couldn't focus on that.

The ranger, even as mind-controlled as he was, was smart enough to try increasing the distance that the berserker was trying to cut.

But he wasn’t doing much in terms of distance as compared to Nathan, who was rapidly eating up the ground between them, even though he had to run in zig-zags and other weird patterns, which were slowing him down.

And Nathan, for the most part, had to close his eyes even as he ran because the ranger could easily get a free hit in there and wasn't about to take any risk by dying by the very move that had saved his life a couple of times.

His people needed him. He couldn't die here, and not especially not to the ranger.

Multiple arrows had managed to hit him. More often than not, they bounced off without doing much apart from bruising his skin. And the berserker had to depend on the wind blowing past his ears for direction most of the time.

Although he occasionally missed his footing as he continued his blind charge, his ability to perceive the world through the wind was amateur level at best.

But Nathan knew that he needed to get up close to the ranger, with his eyes closed, or else he risked the ranger being able to pull off a good shot, which would undoubtedly kill him on the spot, which meant he had to be careful with the way he moved.

So, despite how odd it looked, running around in a zig-zag pattern with his eyes closed, the berserker put full trust in the wind, letting it guide him since he had to keep his eyes closed.

It took some time, but Nathan eventually cursed as an arrow hit his eyelid, but he kept his eyes shut even as the arrow fell to the floor.  The berserker continued, his fingers tightly wrapped around Oblivion's hilt.

That hit proved that Nathan was right to keep charging with his eyes closed, even though he occasionally had to peek to make sure he wasn't running in the wrong direction—his trust in the wind wasn’t 100%.

Nathan was more than halfway close to the ranger when the wind urged him to crack open an eye, and once he did, he was treated to the sight of a bunch of vines erupting from beneath him, wrapping around the berserker's arms and legs. But Nathan was unfazed as he closed his eyes, casually uprooting the vines, as he continued his blind charge at the ranger.
