His hair was a tussled light-brown mane and his eyes were a dull blue with star-shaped irises, marking him as a worshipper of Messimer. The events of his last loop are definitely still affecting him.

I need to make sure he keeps his level-headedness.

Adam pulled out his challenge stone and aimed it at the Necromancer.

< You have forced Player James Carteaux into fighting you >

Adam was teleported to the arena alongside his target, landing on his feet atop a wooden floor like the one inside the Tome Keeper’s giant library. The seating around him looked like it was cut from bookshelves, showing old dusty tomes and scrolls between the legs of the spectators on each level of the seats curving around his half of the arena.

Akin to the Absolute’s abode, the spectators were flesh-wrought creatures, shadow-and-bone humanoid birds, flesh smiths wearing skin robes, bone constructs that defied logic with their anatomy, and things made of metal that were best described as robots.

Most surprising of all, however, was the fact that there was a special booth right behind Adam wherein the Tome Keeper and his bodyguard, Heskel, appeared. The boyish-looking Absolute had his enormous tome in front of him and looked up in apparent surprise.

“So, this is what it looks like. I see,” he remarked. One of the shadowy bird scribes jotted something down on a scroll using a pen.

Heskel grunted something that Adam assumed was akin to a personal assistant telling their boss they didn’t have time to play around.

“It should only be a moment,” the Tome Keeper told his servant.

On the other half of the arena, everything was made of obsidian dotted with blinking galaxies visible within. It was remarkably similar to the shard that Adam had lodged in his heart, but not exactly the same. On the stands were various creatures with upside-down pyramid bodies and heads. Some of them had business suits and togas over their human bodies extending below the strange floating heads, while the others were entirely just pyramids, with tentacles and whiskers below. They were all dotted with eyes. Even one of the business men had a tie with an eyeball looking around.

Unlike Adam’s part of the arena, Messimer did not appear behind his adherent.

Maybe it’s because he is not yet chosen.

In the act of Adam switching Absolutes to the Tome Keeper, he had technically been chosen by him, although he hadn’t yet gained a second boon, so it felt like an in-between state. Still, it was interesting that the Absolutes might appear to watch their chosen ones fight.

Although, it’s likely that the Tome Keeper just appeared because he’s curious.

James Carteaux looked around where he stood at the other end of the arena. He wore the gluttonous aristocrat’s crimson shirt, the rat whistle necklace, the mask of convergence from the Ritualist weapon, mage aspirant’s cowl, spring boots, the fetish of sloth and wraith lantern trinkets, some black pants with silver stitching, and eyeball rings Adam did not recognise.

“You challenged me,” James said, his voice flat and dull. “Why?”

Adam didn’t respond and instead activated his lucky charm and barrage master’s coat, before flinging his kunai straight for James’ centre of mass.

The throwing dagger split into twenty-one, then duplicated into three two times thanks to his barrage gloves, half of the kunai becoming phantoms, and finally the Ghost evolution tripled the number with even more phantoms.

Thanks to the lucky charm, all of the kunai honed directly in on the same spot on James’ body, but the Necromancer was quick to react to the overwhelming attack, triggering the eyeball ring on his left hand, which sent a shimmering field outward from his finger. As it washed over all of the many projectiles, they suddenly switched directions, randomly picking somewhere new to target.

James smirked, confident that he’d defused Adam’s strongest attack, but then Adam triggered his mentalist’s locket and swept the relic’s control across all of the projectiles, pulling them towards the same spot on James’ body.

Before the Necromancer could activate any other relic, he was pierced through by nearly 400 projectiles, utterly destroying his body. His crimson shirt released a blood nova from the first impact, but it did nothing to protect him, and he was dead before the first tick of Adam’s stacking bleed affliction could go off. All that remained of him was scattered bits of flesh, bone, skin, and hair.

Adam retrieved his kunai as the fight came to a close.

“Well done,” the Tome Keeper praised him.

Adam wasn’t sure what to think about using future actions to justify killing someone, especially when he’d managed to steer Kat away from such a dark path by intervening.

But I get it. It’s impossible to tell if someone will change completely if all the cards in their hand are swapped.

Someone destined to become a cannibal player-killer seems impossible to fix, especially if going to Nharlla’s realm is the thing that breaks him and turns him down that path.

It was part of beating stage ten that players would go to their patron’s domain, and Emelia had told him long ago that those who went to Nharlla’s realm were fundamentally changed by the experience, losing their sense of self. The only way to potentially help such a person from repeating that fate would be to have them swap patrons, but that too was a dubious prospect.

If I stayed in this universe, maybe I would end up justifying things in the same way. After all, if I had dealt with Katherine Joan Williams before stage six, then she wouldn’t be able to charm me, Mikhail, and Sarah…

Adam stuck a piece of fried chicken in his mouth to quell the maelstrom of complicated thoughts this topic spawned. He decided to hold off on worrying about this until he was actually in the situation to make such a decision.

Adam had thought to talk to Seo-Yoon and Natalia, as well as Sarah Ayala, but since they had another day tomorrow, he wasn’t in a hurry, and the way Kat looked at him made him forget the many plans he had in mind.

They went up the stairs to the player house and enjoyed each other’s company until early morning before sleep found them.
