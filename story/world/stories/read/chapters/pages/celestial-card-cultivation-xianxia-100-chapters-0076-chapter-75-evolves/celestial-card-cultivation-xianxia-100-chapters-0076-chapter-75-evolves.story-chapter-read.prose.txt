The anomaly seems to be focused on the Divine Dragon Sect – I will observe this for another year or so; if things don’t change, I have to inform the seneschal.

— Diary of Xu Ziqing, Imperial Scribe, Tianqiu Plane – now known as The Origin, Outer Realms

Jianyi held his latest card close to his temple and reached out with a thin thread of qi. As the qi touched the green jade card, carrying his intent, the card dissolved into a sphere of qi that travelled along the thread still connected to his neck dantian and entered his pathways.

If Jianyi were a normal cultivator, the card should now travel slowly from neck to chest until it reached the middle dantian, where it would establish itself again as a card that would passively help his body cultivation.

He wasn’t a normal cultivator, though.

The card had barely traveled through his spirit for a few seconds when he felt the Celestial Core Card pulse once, twice.

Then, with the force of a riptide, a large ball of qi—probably everything he had gathered since reaching the second stage of the Qi Gathering Realm—flooded out of the Core Card, into his lower dantian. Without pausing, it entered a pathway that took it past the middle dantian and onto the road through his spirit that led to his neck.

The Cinnamon Tree Sprout card stood no chance.

The ball of qi enveloped the disassembled card and ripped it along on its circulatory path that led back to the lower dantian. Back to the Celestial Core Card.

Once there, qi and card merged with the Core Card—and for a moment, there was nothing.

Jianyi held his breath.

Then.

The icy cold freshness of a raging mountain river.

The unbridled vitality of thick vines connecting dark, looming trees.

The teasing, flickering light of a just-lit candle flame.

The slow, inexorable march of a tectonic plate.

The gleaming heaviness of a bar of Sun Iron.

The endless circle of the elements took Jianyi from experience to experience—he could feel it, taste it, smell it.

Wuxing.

It was the most majestic, intense experience of Jianyi’s life.

The only constant in the permanence of change was his Core Card, hovering in the air, larger than life.

Behind it, Yin and Yang.

Jianyi saw the qi, still encompassing the card he had just added to his spirit, being absorbed by the Celestial Core Card.

A moment later, he was diving through a white-hot forest fire, the Yin Yang symbol spun up to rotate. First slow, then fast.

Then it pulsed.

The pulse infused the Core Card and, for the first time since the Bamboo Shoot Grove had been absorbed, the card regained its solidity and lost the translucency that had gone hand in hand with it not being accessible to him.

With a final pulse, he was kicked out of this realm—whatever it was—out of his spirit.

He opened his eyes.

Looking around, he was still safely at the campfire in the middle of a forest.

The whole process probably hadn’t taken more than a few seconds, minutes at most.

Yet, the intensity of it all had been overwhelming, and his palms were sweaty.

After a couple of deep breaths, Jianyi delved back into his spirit, his lower dantian, and there was the Celestial Core Card. He could feel that he could channel qi through it again.

His shoulders sagged with relief—the inability to fully access his powers had weighed on him more than he had realized.

Squinting his eyes, he looked at his Core Card to see whether anything had changed.

Celestial Core Card

Unique

Affinity – Core Card

Converts cultivator’s qi into any affinity.The genuine version of this novel can be found on another site. Support the author by reading it there.Moderate increase in active cultivation.Evolves.

Jianyi looked at the card, eyes wide.

From what he had learned in one of the lectures at the sect, the effectiveness of Qi Cards grew with rarity. An uncommon card, the lowest level of rarity for this type of card, provided a ‘minor’ increase in cultivation.

That had been true for both the Bamboo Shoot Grove as well as Cinnamon Tree Sprout.

Further rarities would increase the impact to moderate, notable, and substantial for legendary cards.

It seemed like the Celestial Core Card had taken his two Qi Cards—and a bunch of qi that he had cultivated—and merged them into itself, and strengthened them.

This was an incredible boon in two ways.

First, and most obviously, moderate was above minor, which would mean that his cultivation speed should accelerate—hopefully, he would gain back the qi he had lost in the transformation.

Second, and maybe as important, there was one major drawback to Qi Cards, whether body or spirit: They were part of the cards drawn from a deck when a cultivator was in combat.

That meant that it could happen that you didn’t draw the life-saving technique you needed because the limited number of cards in your hand was taken up by one or two of your Qi Cards.

It was one of several reasons why it made sense to try to bring the number of techniques in your upper dantian close to the maximum that one could take on in any given cultivation realm.

It was less of an issue for body cultivators, but still…

Jianyi didn’t know what to think about this. Didn’t know how he deserved to have received the Celestial Core Card.

The fact that at the bottom of the card, Evolving had changed back to Evolves was almost a minor point given the revelations.

Jianyi shook himself and left his spirit world and settled down at the campfire, munching on a couple of nuts he still had in his storage ring.

After a few minutes, he got curious to check whether he could see the increase in his passive cultivation.

He delved back into his spirit and looked at the gate on his right palm.

Motes of qi were constantly entering his spirit. It was a satisfying sight. Knowing that he got stronger every moment, just by existing, was still incredible to him, and he had to smile.

Though if he was being honest, he didn’t notice a meaningful increase in the speed with which qi entered his spirit before the change happened.

Something stirred in the back of his mind.

There was something that he was missing.

Something that increasingly bothered him.

Wait.

He frowned.

No.

It couldn’t be.

He closed his eyes, directly appearing before his Core Card.

Celestial Core Card

Unique

Affinity – Core Card

Converts cultivator’s qi into any affinity.Moderate increase in active cultivation.Evolves.

The shock was too all-encompassing. He jumped up, not being able to contain the nervous energy in his body.

Active.

It said active.

That was not how qi cards worked.

They improved passive cultivation—made it so that one could sit at a campfire and take in qi while enjoying one’s life.

That was…

What?

What was that?

He took a couple of breaths and tried to think it through.

So, he had lost that advantage of getting stronger faster while not doing anything—which also helped when recovering from fights and injuries.

On the other hand, he still had the benefit of not having Qi Cards mess up his combat; he had gotten a moderate strengthening, instead of the minor one that was normal.

And, maybe most importantly, the Celestial Core Card would reward him for working hard.

Every time he would go through the Eternal Spear Meditation, it would yield more; every body cultivation session—and everybody knew that those were torture—would make him more powerful than he would have otherwise.

The smile returned to Jianyi’s face.

He didn’t know how these effects all evened out; maybe they did, maybe they didn’t, but he did know that it felt right. It felt like… him.

He had to try this right away.

It was late, but he had planned to stay awake all night anyway.

His spear in hand, he began: Eternal Sun. Eternal Dawn. Dusk. Moon. Expanse. Axis. Veil. Horizon.

In sequence, meridian after meridian opened and sucked qi out of the environment, into his spirit.

He felt the difference.

There was no way to quantify it, but after half an hour, he felt like he had made progress. It wasn’t a lot—nobody could expect that—but he was sure that it was more than he would have gained before.

Jianyi felt warmth spreading from his gut through his body. It wasn’t the qi that he had just harvested. It was happiness.

After the progress of weeks of cultivation had been ripped away from him, it felt so good to be back at it now.

He wondered how far disciples like Lu Shan were at this stage?

It shouldn’t matter, of course, but he couldn’t completely free himself from the desire to compete with the best—how else was he supposed to make a difference on the frontlines?

He suspected that there were disciples who had started with him who were at least on the third stage of Qi Gathering, maybe even the fourth.

Either way, achieving the third stage in a month, as per Elder Sun’s goal, felt now much more doable than just a day or two ago.

The next question was, what kind of impact would the new ability of the Celestial Core Card have on his body cultivation? Given that he had no baseline to compare it to, it would be hard to judge, but given that he had proven that it worked on the spirit, he had faith that it would work on the body as well.

Heavens, I promise I won’t squander this opportunity. I will work with discipline towards my purpose—and bring down the demons! He promised himself with conviction.

He felt something inside of him stir, resonate.

Was it his will? The as-yet-unexplored resource sitting on the bottom of his spirit lake?

It didn’t feel like it.

But it reminded him of something that he had experienced before.

He put a few sticks of wood on the campfire and settled back down.

Maybe meditating on the sensation would help.

After a few minutes of deep breaths, he found himself in his soul space. He dove into his spirit lake, but it was undisturbed, and none of the blue light that he had seen a few times by now erupted out of the granite at the lake’s bottom.

Emerging from the lake, he settled into a lotus position, hovering over the calm blue surface.

The fortress of spears surrounded him, undisturbed. He didn’t even want to think about what would happen to him should it ever fail without the protection of Elder Sun around him.

He looked up. The Heavens were calm. But beyond… beyond lay the Dao, endless and unfathomable.

Now he remembered.

The feeling he had just had was a weak reflection of the revelation he experienced in Elder Mo’s study when she had helped him choose the right cultivation technique.

That meant that his promise had some resonance with his Dao.

He shuddered.

It was such a fundamental concept. He felt like he was too young to even consider it.

Nobody discussed it in detail, but from what he had gathered, cultivators rarely truly engaged with their Dao before the second, often only the third cultivation realm.

Apparently, it became harder and harder to progress with just cultivating qi. It needed a deeper understanding of one's purpose to be guided in the right direction.

Fortunately, he didn’t need it right now, not to meet Elder Sun’s goals, not to make it to the Qi Condensation Realm—and even if he did, killing all demons was a worthy Dao, anyway.
