Being invisible was still as cool as the day Ray had first selected the Assassin class. Stealth as a game mechanic was often overpowered and just straight-up kicked ass in most games, especially in MMO:s. It was the main reason he chose the class to begin with and the single reason he hadn’t rage-quit just yet. Except for that one part, the class sucked ass. Other than a few movement skills, it offered almost nothing of worth. Each skill was just some variation of poking people harder with daggers. Boring and shitty! So, in a careful move full of wisdom and cunning, he had selected Alchemist as his profession. It made sense. Alchemists make potions and stuff, but more importantly, they make poison. Assassination and poison were, like, the thing. For a while, it had worked out great! Health potions always came in handy when your healer was off somewhere doing everything except healing, and the liquid fire was badass. Poison helped, and the paralyze ones were fun, sure, but then what?

All that work only to watch a former cheerleader dash through the air and play discount Thor by bringing down lightning and shit. Nick was a damn knight, but even he had some AOE skills, and that shield of his was way too good. What kind of shield makes monsters explode when they touch it? Damn. At least that guy wasn’t around at the moment, but Martin was even worse. Dude couldn’t even go invisible, but nothing could touch him. Attacks just slid right through without harming him. Way too good. Also, those laser thingies he could shoot? Holy hell. And Curtis? What was that about? Mr. Depressed walking around, grunting one-syllable answers and looking at you like you kicked his dog or something? Also, the dude has turned into a small void of his own. Ray wouldn’t be surprised if that bastard turned out to be the final boss or something. He didn’t even want to watch Scrubs anymore! Fucking quitter.

And then there was Priyah! Ray glanced down at her coming over the wall. Well… maybe her class wasn’t that great either. She raised her hands and snapped a screaming peasant’s neck from a distance, like a Sith.

“Well, shit,” Ray hissed between his teeth as he leapt down from the thatched roof of the building where he had perched to get an overview of what the hell was going on. The townspeople were still screaming their damn heads off, and it looked like their eyes were about to pop out of their skulls, but that didn’t stop them from fighting. Good thing they sucked at it, especially if their arrows turned you into the frickin’ Deep Dweller or whatever. Craning his neck, he tried to get a glimpse of Curtis dealing with the now-dead Healer, but the wall was in the way. There was always something in the way.

Ray sighed as he rammed his dagger into the forehead of the nearest peasant. At least a good stab shut them up. Who would have thought you’d need to bring earplugs to a dungeon? Not him. The peasant’s soul drifted into the weapon, and Ray noted the rather large amount of, well, soul, leaking out of the corpse as it fell to the ground. A small cluster of female archers raised their bows and took aim at him, screaming like banshees.

“Dammit,” he cursed, leaping into stealth and using his short-distance teleport skill to end up right in the middle of them. They were unwashed and wore old clothes that smelled of mud. Gross. Not that he himself had much high ground in matters of personal hygiene. He was better at taking showers now than he’d ever been before touching the orb, which was kind of funny considering the fall of civilization and everything, but it hadn’t seemed to matter much when he spent most of his time in front of the computer. That was another pro of becoming Integrated. He was lean and springy now, rather than fat and wheezing. An improvement, even if it hadn’t helped much in the girlfriend department. Women didn’t like him. They were a mystery to him.

Appearing out of stealth in the middle of the group, he asked for their input. “What do you look for in a man?”

They just screamed in his face, so they got a taste of his dagger. Each kill made it stronger, and Ray had used it to kill a lot. A lot. Ramming it through bone was nothing. At this point, he was pretty sure he could shear through steel if he needed to. The archers died, but not before one of them started melting in that same way Kenny had died. Ray wanted nothing to do with that, so he thrust the dagger into the transforming woman’s chest, piercing her heart and monster core, or meta-heart, whatever was in there. The dagger worked like that and didn’t just inflict physical pain. Damn, how he loved that dagger. Even so, he hadn’t expected it to work against the void, but it did. It really did. As it turned out, void creatures, even the ones in the middle of transforming, had souls. Who woulda thunk it?

Penny was doing her thing, racing around in the sky and killing everything that moved while non-stop shouting little haikus or whatever. Martin killed a bunch but couldn’t deal with the ones transforming, and Priyah was getting mobbed. Ray sighed and rushed over to her, shouldering past the mob of peasants, killing as he went. When he got there, she was on the ground, bleeding from three large punctures in her upper right arm. A man stood over her with the offending weapon raised for another strike, a damn hayfork. The peasant dropped it when he died, and Ray offered Priyah a hand, dragging her to her feet.

“Maybe you shouldn’t be in the middle of this,” he said, offering her a health potion. Attacks rained down on him, but they were slow and uncoordinated, nothing he couldn’t dodge without even moving his feet. It didn’t take the enemies long to die, but he had missed her answer.

“What?” he asked, turning around to see her emptying the potion.

She shuddered at the taste. He still didn’t have a good grasp of how to deal with that aspect of potion brewing. “And where am I supposed to go?”

“I don’t know,” Ray said with a shrug, then pointed at the wall. “How about up there?”

“Behind you,” Priyah said.

“Huh?”

He turned and saw a blackened peasant frozen in time mid-lunge, even as it turned into a void creature. It shuddered and groaned, but couldn’t move at all.

“That’s your doing?” he asked.

“Uh-huh.”

“Kind of cool,” he admitted as he drove the dagger into its center, killing it.

The weapon shone bright and Ray thought the transforming void creatures were paying it a little too much attention. Were they afraid of the weapon because it could harm them? He didn’t think so. They seemed… hungry.

“Hng!”

Ray swung back to see Priyah on the ground, holding her injured arm. The potion hadn’t helped much, even though her health was almost full.

“You’re not doing a Kenny, are you?” Ray asked, hearing himself sound like an asshole. He cleared his throat. “I mean… How are you?”

“Heh,” Priyah breathed. “Don’t a Kenny. Good one.”

“Well, are you?”

She nodded, face drenched in sweat. “I think so. It’s like an infection, except it isn’t physical. I can feel it taking over.”

“Can you hold it with your Sith powers?”

Even through the pain, she burst out laughing, but desperation hid underneath. “I’m holding it, but it’s slipping.”

A trio of young male peasants charged out from behind a building. Ray gave them a taste of liquid fire before they got near, and they all fell down on the ground, writhing in pain as the fire consumed them. He got down and grabbed Priyah’s shoulders. “Fight it. You’ve got to fight it! Luke can fix you up. I’m sure he can.”

“He’s too busy,” she said, her voice little more than a whisper. “I’m not strong enough.”

“Bullshit,” Ray said. “You’re here, aren’t you? That shows you’ve got some balls! Eh, lady balls. Use them and hold on. Push it out.”

Lightning slammed into the ground nearby, throwing a cluster of archers off their feet and into early graves before they could loose arrows on Ray and Priyah. Penny was keeping the enemy off their backs, at least for the moment.

“Can’t push it out. Can barely hold it back.”

Something dark and massive crashed past behind a pair of buildings, like a wave of darkness sweeping over a large group of peasants. When it receded, they were all gone. Curtis appeared a moment later. Several arrows slammed into his chest, but he just laughed and kept going. There was actual joy in that laugh. Ray shuddered. He had wanted to make his friend laugh again, but now he wasn’t so sure it was a good thing. Curtis disappeared again and reappeared behind a mixed group of almost thirty peasants and town guards. Some were turning, but not all. Once Curtis had passed through, none remained. After that, he angled himself toward the small hill where Ray and Priyah sat.

“He’s not slowing down,” Ray breathed.

Priyah was in no condition to speak. She was barely holding on.

Ray got to his feet. “Curtis! Stop!”

The hungry darkness didn’t react. It kept coming. Not sure what else to do, Ray stepped between Priyah and Curtis and held up the dagger, tip pointed at the coming wave. That seemed to bring Curtis out of it, and the darkness pulled in on itself, eventually taking the form of a smiling Curtis. It was eerie as hell.

“What the hell are you doing?” Ray asked, still not lowering the knife.

Curtis nodded at Priyah, who sat behind Ray. “She’s becoming one of them. The girl has to go.”

“She’s holding on,” Ray said.

“Not for long,” Curtis said, not raising his voice as he gave the dagger in Ray’s hand a long look. “You should be careful with that thing. It’s looking very tasty from where I’m standing.”

Ray looked from Curtis to the dagger and back, frowning. “Dude, be less weird. You’re not killing Priyah.”

“Fine,” Curtis sighed, his form melting into darkness again. It swept past and over Ray, enveloping Priyah before continuing on to another cluster of peasants. When he was gone, so was Priyah’s arm.

“It’s gone,” she said, her voice tinged with panic. “It’s all gone.”

She laughed with what sounded like relief. Relief!

“Heh,” Ray chuckled nervously, unsure how to react. “What the fuck?”
