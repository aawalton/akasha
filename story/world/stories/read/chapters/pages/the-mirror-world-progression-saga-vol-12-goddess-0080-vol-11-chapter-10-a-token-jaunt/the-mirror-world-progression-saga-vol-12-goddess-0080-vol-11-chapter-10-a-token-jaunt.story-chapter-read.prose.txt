Samantha frowned and sighed long-sufferingly at the pitiable comment of her lover. “What happened, Azure? What did you find out from the vision?”

Azure closed her eyes with her hand on her head, shaking it. “Please… I don’t want to talk about it…”

“You don’t think I need to know? Deserve to?”

“Deserve.” Azure scowled and exhaled with a bitter demeanor. “We just can’t get away from that word, can we?”

Samantha shifted to put a hand on her lover’s shoulder and said more gently, “Whatever happened, I will help you through it, my love. As before.”

Azure looked at Samantha’s hand, and her expression was miserable. “It’s more that I’d rather you not know, for how you’ll see me, after.”

Samantha screwed up her face in bewilderment. “There’s nothing that could change that, Azure. I’m surprised you’ve contorted your thinking enough to believe otherwise. After all we’ve been through? After the revelations of what we are, already revealed in all the horrifying details?”

Azure didn’t reply, still not meeting Samantha’s… well. Those weren’t open, after all.

Samantha stopped flying and lowered herself onto the bed. With some effort, she opened her eyes, first squinting at the garish and crude details of the physical world but widening quickly enough. Her eyes ran more with blood, so she cleaned them briefly, before deliberately turning Azure to look her in the eyes, which the Naugite did… reluctantly, fearfully, shamefully, helplessly.

“It’s so unfair,” Azure whispered, eyes brimming with tears. “To look into these, now… you’ve made them like rare treasures…”

Samantha smiled faintly. “And you can’t resist?”

Azure nodded. Swallowed. With trembling lips, she said, “I am the Mirror Demon, Samantha. It wasn’t a past life, it… I’m the same being. I wasn’t killed, I was captured. Contained. Seemingly left to rot. Her sins, all of them, all of those visions, they’re mine.”

Samantha searched her lover’s face calmly as she spoke, and took a deep breath after. Nodded. “It fits. Like the puzzle pieces of a face fitted finally into recognizability.”

Azure’s eyes were wide with incredulity. “That’s all you have to say about it? Do you not get-”

Samantha put both her hands on Azure’s shoulders, shook her slightly, and leaned in insistently. “I do, Azure. And it doesn’t matter.” She smiled. “You lived a new life, regardless, you know. You chose something different, you turned away from that dark beast and walked a path back to the light. So you cast a lot of shadows in your wake, so what? I needed that, I always will, and you already know this. You’ve said as much yourself and embraced the role. Nothing has changed, Azure. You’re still the wonderful, broken, and put-back-together crystalline flower I love and cherish. As I said, nothing can change that.”

Azure’s face continued to be incredulous as she shook her head. “I-I don’t know… I am devoid of details on the matter of the choice, Samantha. I don’t know who broke me out of the tomb, or what happened after.”

“Which tomb? Actually, I think you should probably share the vision with me, so we both know the same details. Oh! And your impressions of where this was. I need to forge the Transition Point there soon.”

Azure winced and nodded reluctantly. “As you say, Your Majesty…”

“Aw, come on, don’t say it like that!”

Briefly, Azure shared the information through their mental bond, able to bestow the imagery and such directly.

When it was done, Samantha was sitting cross-legged on the bed, holding Azure’s hand in one of hers and pondering it all.

“A vision of the Sage way back when,” Samantha mused. “Looks like you were one of his first experiments, and must’ve been used in some way to forge the magic of Matrazere.”

Azure sighed miserably. “Yeah. Couldn’t have been too essential, though. They didn’t need me there to keep being assholes for the last thirty years, or whatever it has been. Not like I know, after all. My life is a big fucking lie.”

“So, you really think Goldie will have the answers to the rest?”

“So that snake of hers claimed, more or less. Not that I trust snakes at this point, but surely we’ll get some useful information.”

“Mmn. Do you want to go as soon as possible then?”

Azure frowned. “I still find it a bit disturbing how okay you are with this.”

“You still aren’t that being, Azure, even if you share a reshaped outer sleeve. She’s dead, and you’re the one who drove the final knife in her soul. That act in the darkness, love. That was the end.”

Azure stared back at Samantha for a moment, then looked away. “The monster put behind me… more literally than I realized. I hope so.”

“You’ve taken occupation and possession of what was once hers. If anything, you’re a corpse bloom sprouting out of her decrepit carcass.”

“Ah, what an analogy. I’m basking in you, my sunshine…”

Samantha groaned playfully, with a false cringe. “You’re so corny! See? An evil lady could never drop a line that lame.”

Azure squinted over at her dangerously. “You’re lucky I’m feeling uninspired toward dark deeds, or I’d make you pay for that comment.”

Laughing, Samantha bent to kiss her betrothed on the cheek and then floated upward off the bed. A mirror materialized out of the air. “I’m going to officially anchor the Transition Point here real quick, and then we can use that coin. And yes, I’m going, whether I need to for the coin to be utilized or not.”

Azure slipped her legs off the bed and blew air out of her mouth. “Okay. Let me get some food in me, and maybe I’ll feel a little less shit.”

Samantha nodded and then dematerialized to the Bridge of Edges. With all the information provided by Azure, she found it rather easy to locate the hollow to fall into and pop up deep underground, in the collapsed tomb area familiar from Azure’s transferred imagery.

The area was a bit high off the ground, with a pillar formed up to it. That would need some work if they were going to use it as another ‘base’ of sorts. But she’d let others handle that unless she was particularly needed.

Samantha was drawn to the collapsed area. She floated over it and focused on it with her spirit sight.

The entire zone was expertly ‘sanitized,’ or smudged into a lack of any real spiritual information. She couldn’t determine exactly when it was done — it could’ve been yesterday or thirty years ago. Not a century, though. Less than that, definitely.

Interesting. Did whoever was responsible for this want to hide the act, or what was here? Both?

She had no answers, nor any way to get them, except by asking Goldie. The evidence had been scrubbed away, whatever it would’ve revealed.

She soon left by mirror-walking. While Azure got herself ready for the trip, Samantha arranged how to use the new Transition Point and how to secure it. New structure around the entrance and the destroyed area, and a faster natural route out to the surface without disturbing any remains they didn’t need to.

We need to find one more of these, somewhere. Then, we can finally complete Glae’s stupid quest.

When the time came to use the token, Samantha simply invited Azure into the spirit world. For a long while, she intrinsically knew in the back of her mind where she needed to be to activate the token’s special conditions: nowhere, basically. The fog of Limbo.

When Azure appeared next to her in the fog, Samantha took her hand, nodded, and pulled the token out of her dimensional sack, where it had been waiting since the time she’d visited the last of the nagi, the one called ‘Goldie,’ in seeking out who could help Azure out of her coma.

The token was gold with a many-headed snake imprinted on it. Holding it out, she indeed sensed it could be ‘used’ in a magical sense.

“Ready?” she asked Azure. At a simple nod, Samantha activated the token.

It flashed with a great pulse of magical energy that enveloped them, and when it abated, they were elsewhere.

A grand temple hall fit for the highest royalty was splayed out before them. Colorful, ornate pillars in rows lined it all the way down, and in front of these, framing a path of intricate rug, were continuous rows of naga statues in varying poses, numbers of heads, and arms. They were fully colored and lifelike, as if they’d jump out at you, though Samantha already understood they wouldn’t.

Azure’s hand gripped that of her lover’s very tightly as she watched the statues warily, and Samantha had to suppress a grin. “It’s alright, Azure,” she said instead. “They’re not alive.”

However, in the spirit, Samantha saw something more. Eyes quietly and calmly watched her from within the stone, showing the guardians had life within them… if necessary.

Oh. Well, we’ll just pretend we didn’t see that.

“The lighting is odd, here,” Azure whispered. “Ultraviolet, I think.”

“Yes,” Samantha said vaguely, and led her lover by the hand down the hall.

Further down, a tall marble dais, like a pyramid, rose, painted with an incredible, multifaceted mural of intricate drawings, runes, pictograms, mathematical formulae, and strange 3-dimensional models. There was no space uncovered on it, though some things were faded.

To Samantha’s Third Eye, the imagery was almost alive. She could see some of the meanings within the layers, indicating signs and omens. She saw six meteors falling onto four towers, she saw planetary and star alignments she knew had happened… she saw confusing formulae that somehow translated the end result to her: apocalypse was near.

Indeed, it is.

At the very top of the pyramid was a throne, circular, with a great plush cushion and a flaring back. Sitting was a four-armed, snake-like nagi coiled up around herself, watching them with a hint of a smile. Her skin was gleaming bronze in soft scale patterns, her serpentine bottom half longer, with golden scales on the outside.

She wore a patterned, multicolored semi-robe over her torso, a single piece of fabric that wrapped her but left her arms, stomach, and most of her upper torso exposed. She was absolutely decked out in jewelry of all kinds of precious metals and stones, from necklaces and bracelets to belt-like waistwear.

She wore an elaborate headdress, like a big, flared leaf, made of gold and white gold, inset with rubies that formed floral designs. Her hair, made into several intricate braids, was an iridescent, pearl white.

“Welcome back, Precious Goddess!” she called down, her voice sweet, musical honey flowing evenly and echoing through the great hall. Her eyes turned to regard Azure, and her smile brightened. “And the lover she sought me out to save the other day! I’m so glad you’ve come, Azure! You may call me Goldie! I’m sure you have many questions, and that’s all just fine. I’ve forgotten much, but I am an oracle, after all.”
