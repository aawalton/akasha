Grey blood squirted as the quarrel skewered the old man, exiting through his eye socket and splattering his kids in a lavish amount of spent anatomy. People screamed. The girl's knees buckled as the lifeless body pulled her under while the boy stood frozen next to her, mouth open, uncomprehending.

Kalsin grabbed for the azTéls to drag them away in the opposite direction, but Áron jerked from his grasp, making them stumble. Kalsin cursed when his knee hit the ground painfully, and frantically ran his fingers over his side to make sure his lantern was still in one piece. If they survived the next hours, he was going to have a serious talk with the man's grandeur of useless heroics. The old man was dead, the hell was he running there to help for?

In a few short but crucial seconds Kalsin was back on his feet, but the scene before him had turned into a luminant nightmare. Figures slipped in and out of his pocket of vision, running and disappearing into the white. Gray shadows wrestled further out, hard to make out in the hazy lantern-shaded distance. The clatter of steel on steel rang in the biting air, screams and death gurgles, crowned by the occasional whizz and thud of crossbow bolts.

There's only one word that describes what happens when you put together a random assortment of strangers, pull them nice and snug together, then butcher one in a gory display; chaos. If there's no established order to control human instincts, panic takes root.

Treyant's now-familiar abyssal power slammed into Kalsin's mind, and suddenly it felt like a mountain's weight had been taken off his shoulders, his fears and doubts replaced by hope and stubborn defiance. He would not die today. The tremble of his body retreated. He took a deep breath before adjusting his scabbard to his side, pulled his sword with full composure, and started walking.

A Clenn man Kalsin didn't recognize was moaning on all-fours, impaled to the ground by a sword above his ankle, eyes unseeing and delirious from being left unprotected from Blindlight's glare. Kalsin plunged his sword through his back and into his heart, barely slowing. It was mercy killing. A justified act. At least that's what he told himself.

Kalsin jerked around when a figure rushed into his lantern's radius, then disappeared back into the white just as quickly. Not the people he was looking for. Hesitating only for a moment, he turned away and continued ahead, his bahten close behind. His eyes watered as he stared forward unblinking, trying to make out barely distinguishable silhouettes not even ten feet away.

Kalsin ran the remaining distance just in time to see one of the cowled priests wrestling with a woman, while a man circled their struggling forms, trying to find an angle to skewer the priest at spearpoint, the éj-lantern on his belt swaying as he moved. The other priest was lying face-down on the ground with a bolt in their back. How the hell was that possible, an unseen crossbowman firing and finding targets at range during Blindlight?

The remaining priest screamed, voice wild and high, and lashed out with a claw-like weapon attached to the knuckles. The woman reared back for a second, then surged, tackling the priest in a blur. But Kalsin lost sight of their fight, having other priorities.

He had snuck up on the circling man undetected. As he brought down his sword, blade barely an inch away from the sweaty neck, the man became aware of his presence. Perhaps a sound gave him away, or it was the tickle of moving air. The man surged with quickness Kalsin did not think possible, turning and deflecting his strike with the haft of his spear. He then staggered, the backlash from overstraining his surge hitting him.

Kalsin cursed silently, swatting aside a weak jab and pushing his advantage as he kept cutting at his opponent's defenses. Kalsin avoided fighting humans as much as he could. Without a surge of his own, he was always at a severe disadvantage, even against those with weaker physical powers. People above nails and dirts could still access surges below their rank, but without constant use, it atrophied like a weakened muscle.

The man facing Kalsin must've had incredible skill with physical surge in the past. His technique was sublime. But his endurance had been exhausted in that single shot of a life-saving move, and his surge needed long minutes to recover.

Kalsin slashed high overhead. As the man raised his weapon to block, Kalsin grabbed the spear shaft for purchase, and kicked the man hard in the stomach. With a savage downward wrench, Kalsin brought both the spear and the man holding it to the ground. His next slash found its way deep in the side of his neck, nearly decapitating him on the spot.

Kalsin looked up and saw the priest finish gutting the woman with that strange weapon. It looked like brawlers' brass knuckles, loops hooked snug onto the wearer's fingers, but with steel claws instead of blunt knots. A short, curved blade extended from the thumb, making the weapon look like a crude knife with a partial glove attached.

The hostile woman's lifeless body hit the ground, and Kalsin's eyes met the priest's. Her cowl had fallen back during the fight to reveal the face of a girl with skin bleached to an ashen shade under Blindlight. Her lower face still had the cloth wrapped around it tightly, but there was nothing to conceal the space above—Giving Kalsin a sight that was unlike anything he had ever seen before.

Intricate patterns decorated every inch of her skin. Rivers of ink were drawn on her forehead, around her dark eyelashes, across her bald head like an ancient script of a foreign language.

Though her expression was hard to make out under the scrawlings, she looked panicked to a point where her feverish breaths fluttered her face mask. Her wide and colorless eyes were skewering Kalsin with their baffled intensity, and with her claws still brandished toward him, it looked as if she would attack at any moment.

Kalsin pulled himself from his perpetual state of shock. They needed to focus.

He flashed her what he hoped would be a reassuring smile, then promptly ignored her and went to inspect the unmoving priest. The quarrel was embedded deep through the ribs, having shattered a handful of bones from the looks of it. The grass beneath and around was soaked in red. Dead, no two ways about it.

"I hope you’re not religious, because there’s no time for a burial ceremony," Kalsin said.

The girl-priest stared at him with eyes unable to settle between wide and narrow, surprised or suspicious, though it was hard to make out under her inked and half-covered features. For some reason, she'd expected Kalsin—someone she had traveled alongside—to be suddenly hostile.

Curiosity tugged at Kalsin to see if the dead priest was painted in a similar manner, but he stood instead. "Alright then, but only a short prayer, and I suggest you do it quickly and devoutly. There is violence to do and all that."

He stepped away to let her deal with her dead companion, instead going over the belongings of their attackers. He grimaced when seeing the man's éj-lantern had broken to little pieces during his fall, but pocketed a satisfactory amount of silver from the both of them. Remallon-pressed coinage. They were dressed similarly to the collector's men in the workshop, lawmen trying and failing to pass as civilians. And like the stranger he'd finished off a minute ago, both of them were Clenn.

Damn it. Damn it, damn it, damn it. He swallowed bile threatening to rise. Shut it out. There'll be time to lament later.

The priest had redonned her cowl and was kneeling at the deceased body with her head bowed, though she'd positioned herself to keep Kalsin in her peripheral vision. She looked the same as before, except for the flecks of washed out blood staining the sleeve of her robes. She stood as Kalsin finished his corpse-robbing activities in good old hunters' fashion, and fell in line several feet behind him. Kalsin didn't expect her to do anything differently. He had no illusions that she suddenly trusted him, but he knew their current situation was not unlike a parasitic relationship between flea and dog. Leaving the safety of a lantern would've been suicide.

Several things were off about her, and he couldn't put a finger on all of them. He'd never heard about priests of Vadász—or anyone in the Republic for that matter—using their own skin as canvas.

Just the idea was absurd. Being Scarrd was enough of a burden already, so why would someone go ahead and craft similarly dark lines upon themselves? It was wrong. He didn't know how to feel about it. What implications it carried.

Another wave of hive-surge brushed Kalsin’s consciousness, coming from somewhere beyond the white curtain, and he shook himself. Focus.

Kalsin resumed his approach, expecting to see the worst with each step he took. Shadows danced around him in tempo with his lantern's rattle, unveiling their path bit by bloody bit. A trail of grayed syrup congregated with another in a river of blood sneaking around tiny pebbles, arriving at a maimed horse carcass. One of the warrior women was sprawled atop her mount with a fist-sized hole in her chest, glassy eyes staring skyward.

"Death by mace," Kalsin muttered, shuddering. In his line of profession, he'd seen his fair share of injuries that blunt force was able to cause. They weren't pretty. Especially not if you got your own armor dented and caked right past your innards.

A loud clang of steel, then a thrum of vibration rose from nearby. Kalsin picked up his pace, pulling Sap and the priest into a run. She moved surprisingly nimble in her layered robes.

They broke into a glade covered by a copse of thin trees and more dead bodies than all the others he'd seen combined. Áron fought beside Erazza as a trio of men pressured them with flashing clubs, making the pair lose ground by the second. Jack was positioned between the skirmishers and the unseen shooter, a quarrel sticking out her shield and several more having bounced off, now laying broken at her feet. She pulsed with frustrated tension from being pinned down and unable to put her sword to use. Hollow stood at the clearing's edge, nostrils flaring in agitation.

Kalsin went for the man closest to him; two long steps, a thrust between the ribs, and the Clenn collapsed with a grunt. But when Kalsin tried drawing back his sword, it stuck fast with the dead weight keeping it pinned at a downward angle. His eyes met that of his next opponent not three feet away. He pulled harder. The man charged Kalsin with a triumphant thirst etched on his face.

Only to be swept away by the barreling mass of a fifteen-thousand pound warlander bahten, shattering every bone in his body on contact as Hollow crushed, then brought under and trampled the unprepared fighter into the ground. Bloody gouges decorated the spent flesh like boot prints in soggy mud.

The last man turned to flee—gods only knew where, without a lantern—but Erazza quickly finished him off with a well-aimed hatchet in the neck.

Another bolt whistled through the air and Jack hissed as the projectile barely squeezed past her shield and scraped along the meat of her shoulder. With one free hand, she tore out a piece of fabric from her shirt and held it to the wound, then caught Kalsin's eyes and jerked her head in a direction. Towards the trees.

Kalsin called out to the others to get in cover, already running. Valuing their own hides as they should, nobody objected to making a quick retreat.

In seconds they were loping through the miniature excuse of a forest as dried sticks crunched underfoot. Jack came last, covering Hollow's backside with her shield until the foliage became adequate enough to hide them. They ran deeper into the theater of ghastly trees.

"Pathfinder Treyant?" Kalsin asked several minutes later, once they'd slowed from a desperate sprint to an uneasy jog. The trees had fallen off behind them and rocky slopes took their place. The azTéls must've known where they were going, because they quickly found a narrow stone path weaving between the mounds.

"Don't know. We haven't seen her since the fight broke out," Áron said.

"She can take care of herself," Erazza said without pulling her eyes off their path. She cut through a dense bush with her hatchet and hurriedly stepped on the trail behind it. "Used to serve in the military. Officer in the Old. And I've no reason to doubt her word."

Kalsin grunted, focusing on walking. The Old was short for the outer line of defense. Trenches and blockades stretching for hundreds of miles across the Eastern Expanse. Basically wormfood for the Veiled invaders. It took hard men and women to survive there. Allegedly.

Áron drew back to quickly walk next to Kalsin. "Got any idea how they were shooting us, and more importantly, hitting us in the current conditions?" he asked with clear meaning at their white surroundings.

Kalsin watched the pattern of blood on the priest's flowing robe, thinking. The answer came to him in the image of a curtly written collector's note. "Optics advised," he muttered out loud, glancing back.

Jack, her shield now slung on her back as she covered the end of the line, gave a reluctant nod. The makeshift bandage she'd tied on her shoulder had soaked through a little, but the bleeding seemed to have stopped. An inch lower and she could've lost an arm, two inches to the side and her neck would've been gone. She'd gotten lucky.

"I warned you two. We expected the collector to make a move sooner or later," Kalsin said. "Unfortunately for us, it happened sooner."

"But Blindlight should equalize the field for us all. Éj-lanterns are one thing, but they're worthless if you're outside its radius, looking in from afar."

Kalsin nodded, glad for the common sense the man was showing. Most people didn't get so far when having to rely on their own observations under duress. "The letter we've found mentioned something called 'optics'. Now, I don't have a cocking clue what it could be, exactly, but the result speaks for itself. A means for long-distance view during Blindlight."

The words sounded ridiculous, but nobody spoke on the contrary. There was no denying what they'd just experienced.

"Shit," Áron declared, wise beyond his station.

"The good news is, this piece of equipment seems like a product of the elite, a fancy new invention only the rich can afford. Exorbitant enough that it's reserved for the use of a single man."

"Twenty seconds between shots, give or take," Erezza said with a nod. She had a keen presence of mind if she really did count the seconds and wasn't just bullshitting them. Kalsin thought she might be bullshitting them. "Crank mechanism, and a powerful one at that."

She descended a step as the path led them across a shallow gully, stepping with the purpose of someone with a familiar destination in mind. When Kalsin asked, Áron was the one who spoke. "How do you think we came to Remallon in the first place?" And that was all he said.

Fifteen minutes of heavy marching followed as they tried to widen the distance between them and the lawmen. The ground slowly evened out until they were walking on mirror-smooth soil dusted by dirt, the smooth powder wafting under their heels. Both bahten gave anticipatory neighs and loped ahead, just barely keeping within the lantern's vision, only to jump back as they kicked up dust in excited circles.

The fracture came in sight abruptly from the white glare. As if a giant had sliced a razor-sharp sickle through the earth, the land was split by a narrow river, the water-level completely even with the ground. Despite the stream being only five feet wide, however, one would never stop sinking if they couldn’t swim; fractures were deep. Infinitely deep. They did not have a bottom, continuing endlessly into the earth. While the surface was relatively safe to travel, the depths were inhabited by creatures, as well as rare minerals. The lower the depth, the larger both the monstrosities and the value that divers could hope to find.

Sap jumped headfirst into the fracture with a loud splash, thankfully far enough to keep from soaking them, while Hollow slipped under the waters with his dignity intact. They submerged and spurted in satisfaction upon breaking the surface. Their water-proof saddles on their backs gleamed invitingly.

Kalsin had been busy watching the playful animals, and he turned to notice the azTéls dragging a dirty, but sturdy looking boat towards the fracture. They must've hid it somewhere nearby, because the spear-shaped boat was covered in dry leaves and bits of tangled moss. The design was standard for fracture traveling; sleek enough to traverse narrower passages, and long enough to carry half a dozen people if they squeezed together enough.

Jack stopped beside Kalsin and they watched the elegantly curving river as it disappeared outside their lantern's shade. She slid a hand into his and squeezed, looking at him with a soft smile. One that he returned.

The collector would not catch them today.
