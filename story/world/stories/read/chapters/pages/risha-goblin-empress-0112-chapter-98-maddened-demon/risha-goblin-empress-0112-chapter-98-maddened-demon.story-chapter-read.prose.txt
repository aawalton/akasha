Risha held the crazed gaze, not looking away. Damien had turned into a wild animal, and if she looked away, or backed down, he would cut her down. She knew this with absolute certainty

She was Empress Risha, Mother Of Monsters, Alpha of the Kobolds, and she would not let this maddened demon strike fear into her.

A slight shift in his body weight, and Risha felt the power gathering behind an attack.

“She needs you.” Risha’s voice paused the power she felt gathering. “The one whose name carries weight in your heart, the Mother. Freera needs you now.”

The mana dispersed, and both swords fell to the ground, dispersing into mana as a semblance of sanity returned to the demon.

“Igni.”

The spirit jumped from her and into the demon where she held his arm. The madness was everywhere, and Risha did not look away from the gaze of The Brother, because she knew if she did, the semblance of sanity the demon held would disappear.

Igni’s work was slow, and Risha felt that Nyx wouldn’t be able to help, so they stood there, the blood of a dead god’s Echo pooling around their feet.

Slowly, Damien returned to himself. His muscles relaxed, and he sagged into Risha.

“Igni is terrifying.” Damien chuckled to himself.

“He’s a strong spirit.” Risha said, her voice tight with concentration as she continued to provide her energy to the Fire Spirit.

“You have no idea, Risha.” Damien straightened, taking his weight away from her. “There was truth to your words, that Freera needs me. Tell me what you meant.”

Risha looked at the dead Echo, as her mind went to Siyah in the city above. She looked up, her eyes narrowing as she imagined the Angel Mother.  “I don’t like saying it aloud.”

“No one can hear us here. Tell me.” Damien’s voice was sharp, his fists clenching.

Risha took a step back, noticing the remnants of madness that Igni still burned.

Damien looked away, his hands clenching and unclenching, “Sorry. But this place is strongly warded. No one can listen without me noticing.”

Risha studied Damien for a long moment before nodding, “The Demoness I was traveling with. She has gained the Class; Inheritor Of Freera.”

Damien paused, his eyes going distant as he thought. Silence stayed with them for the next several minutes as Igni finished his work and returned to Risha.

A notification came up, telling her that she’d leveled, placing her at level 62. She dismissed it to focus back on Damien.

“Freera came to her in a vision.”

Damien looked up sharply.

“She told her to come to Freerala.”

Damien shook his head, a mirthless chuckle escaping him, “I should’ve known. She knew death was coming.” He closed his eyes as he tilted his head up. He breathed deep, then let it out. “We should head back. I know what Freera wanted her to find.”

He opened his eyes, and took a step towards the exit.

Damien paused, his eyes turning to Risha. He bowed his head, “Thank you, Mother Risha. My debt to you grows.”

He rose, then continued towards the exit, where Klu watched with unreadable eyes.

Risha looked at the corpse in the middle of the room, then turned to follow the Demon.

As Damien closed the doors behind them, a flash of spirit made her turn back to look in the room. She saw the dead Echo collapsing before the doors slammed shut.

In front of them, Siyah stood on top of a small raised platform. She sang a soft song, spreading over the gathering of demons like magic. Orcs and goblins sat on nearby crates, close enough to protect her if needed, but allowing her to interact with her people.

Risha spotted the demon beggar that had guided them, watching Siyah with awe in his eyes.

Damien’s steps slowed as they approached, until they stopped completely. The three of them listened to Siyah sing of two ancient Demons and their love in the face of oppression.

The song ended, and Siyah caught sight of the three of them. A smile spread across her face.

Damien walked forward slowly, and Risha spotted a bittersweet sadness in the set of his shoulders and the tilt of his head.

Siyah got down from the crate she stood on, and ran over to them.

Damien raised his hand, but she went right past him to Risha.

“Are you alright, Mother? No injuries?”

Risha shook her head, “No. But I think we know why you were guided here.”

Siyah tilted her head curiously.

Risha nodded to Damien.

He walked up to them as if he hadn’t been completely ignored. “We should return to the Delver’s guild. Erin is better at explaining these things than I am.”

Siyah looked at Risha, and Risha realized that Siyah’s hood had been down in front of so many demons. Siyah had been self conscious about her broken horns the entire trip, but here, she’d forgotten.

It brought a smile to Risha’s face.

Risha focused back on the Demoness’s face. “You can trust him.”

Siyah looked over Risha, then turned back to Damien, “Can it wait till tomorrow? You all look exhausted.”

At her words, Risha realized the weariness in her bones. She was tired from her constant use of spirit, and they’d just arrived in town that morning, or was that yesterday? It was hard to tell time in the eternal darkness.

Klu’s hand on her shoulder made her blink, “That seems like a good idea. I will escort the mother to her room.”

Risha was pulled towards the inn, leaving the two demons on their own in the street.

“How are you feeling Klu?” Risha asked as she looked at his spirit. He’d managed to cleanse it on his own, but it must have been equally as exhausting for him.

“I will be fine, Mother.” Klu sent a glance back to the two demons who were talking to each other now. “I worry more for the Demon. He hides his pain behind his smile.”

“I’m glad that there’s someone for Siyah.” Risha said as Klu led her to a room with two guards standing outside of it. They opened the door, and Risha yawned at the sight of her bed.  “I’ll get some rest for now. We’ll stay for two more days, then we must return home.”

“Yes, Mother.” Klu paused in the doorway. “Did you sense it too? When the doors were closing?”

Risha nodded. “Something left to the Spirit Realm.”

“But Damien killed the creature’s spirit?”

Risha sat on her bed, “Yes, the body was empty when I entered the room.”

Klu frowned, his eyes lost in thought. “I’ll consult some of the more knowledgeable spirits I know.”

Risha fell backwards, and looked up at the ceiling. “I’ll do the same. I don’t like something so powerful… getting away.”

Klu nodded, his eyes narrowing. “Rest well, Mother.”

“I’ll try.” Risha said, then tapped the post of her bed three times. Moments later, a purple spider crawled into her hair.

Risha smiled, and closed her eyes, hoping that sleep would come without bloody nightmares.
