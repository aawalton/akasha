Dust.

It was a common theme of living in Phoenix. Even behind the 303 Wall, the massive dust storms that ripped across the Sonoran Desert caked the city with it once or twice a year. Maybe that’s why, despite noticing all the dust, I hadn’t really thought about it.

The other thing was, Retan’s Path was obviously something to do with light. His gold-light Avatar aura made that obvious. It wasn’t unthinkable that at least one of his servants might be in on this, but I’d assumed that he’d want to keep his inevitable betrayal as sudden as possible. Telling the sect’s S-Rankers that he planned to harvest their cores would be akin to committing suicide.

But as the dust drained from the room and spiraled out the windows and cracks between cedar wall panels, I second-guessed that assumption. A dust mage was involved—at least in defending Retan’s personal sanctum—and he’d just noticed me.

I readied the Storm’s Blade. The various treasures scattered around the room had no value—not to me, and almost certainly not to Retan.  They were simple decorations, and I needed to…to what? Avoid contact?

That’d be best for Ellen and Tanat. The longer I stalled, the better off they’d be. I needed to avoid Retan and his teachers as long as possible, and I needed to find that evidence. Two Lightning Charges, one Rainfall Charge, and one Wind Charge. That wasn’t a ton of resources. I couldn’t turn immaterial; that’d be the easiest escape, but I hadn’t used it to break in, either. The risk of getting trapped had been too high, and now, I didn’t have the Charges.

Instead, I retreated back into Retan’s study. The dust had all disappeared there, too. I took one look around and winced. Yes, everything was old and falling apart, but nothing was a mess. This room was still used. The dust had been an alarm.

Lightning Dance. I threw myself through the air, repositioning up through the gap in the ceiling as the dust returned. This time, it swirled and coalesced into a thin, long-limbed figure in black leather armor. His helmet matched Tanat’s, but the metal components looked worn, his cloak and clothes were patched and threadbare, and his lips and chin were weather-beaten. He held a ‘sword’ in one hand, though I had no idea what a sword made from floating dust would do.

Walar: S-Rank

I could take him.

I knew that, for sure. He wasn’t any stronger than me, and I still had half of my Charges. I could use most of my skills from the get-go. Or, even better, I could shift to Cyclone Forms and start a Storm Sigil combo. If I was fast enough, it wouldn’t even need to be a full cast to disable Walar.

The trouble was…

“Intrude, you have stumbled upon Honored Retan’s inner sanctum. You were not invited, you are not welcome, and you must now face his judgment,” Walar said. He didn’t look up.

“I don’t think so,” I whispered to myself as I crept away from the hole in the ceiling.

The trouble was, if I started fighting, that would only give up my exact location. I could beat Walar. I didn’t know if I could beat Walar and five other teachers, or if I could beat Retan. And worse, the moment I started fighting, that’d be the end for Tanat and Ellen’s attempt to fix her broken core.

I kept creeping away from the hole. Lightning Dance threw me back across the roof, toward the canyon wall. It took me further from an escape, but the last thing I wanted was to escape.

I just wanted to stall for time.

Walar erupted from the gap I’d cut into the roof in a whirlwind of swirling dust. Tiny sparks arced from each individual mote, and I nodded appreciatively as I took another few steps to hide behind the great hall’s central spire. One of each Charge type wasn’t much, but it did give me a very limited selection of tools to use.

I just had to be clever with how I used them.

Kade was doing something stupid again, that maniac.

Ellen watched Retan carefully as Tanat led her by the hand through the crowds of students. It was the safest move she could make. Everyone was watching Retan, and though her eyes and lips didn’t look like the others’, it was still a natural thing to do. Outwardly, she was the picture of calm, drawing on her experience in high-stakes galas with Bob.

But inside, she was redlining.

Kade was definitely doing something stupid. Both of the sanctioned dueling circles had shut down, and most of Retan’s teachers—the only armored people on the arena floor—had started moving toward the great hall’s gates and the garden beyond them.

“Tanat, how quickly can—“

“Quiet, please. Something has gone wrong. We need to move quickly, and we have no time for unnecessary words,” Tanat said. She picked up the pace. “If we’re lucky, the A-Rankers will not notice right away.”

At the door to the treasure house, Retan himself continued as if nothing was wrong. The current core—one shrouded in a light, pleasant green and blue that mixed to an almost tropical color—was the center of a bidding war between three students. The current bid was two S-Rank cores and three A-Rank ones, including a Paragon’s core. Ellen stared. If the shadow core got that high, they’d need serious help acquiring it.

“Give me your water core and wait here. I will be back soon,” Tanat said. “My potential partner is in this group, but we cannot allow her to know the lengths of your desperation.”

“I’m not desperate,” Ellen said, but she handed the core to Tanat, and a moment later, the swordswoman was gone, leaving Ellen standing on the dueling pitch as the green-and-blue core went to the victor and the other two stalked sullenly away.

Ellen let herself watch as Retan disappeared into his treasure house. No doubt, he was going for another core. Or, maybe, he was ready to shut down the entire auction. If he knew about Kade—and he almost certainly did—the only way he’d keep going was if he was truly confident in his own indestructibility.

A blot of darkness appeared on the treasure house’s roof, and a split second later, something disappeared into it. Ellen looked away. That had been Darkness, and that meant either that Kade was on the move or that someone else had the spell. The latter was likely. It was a common spell. But Ellen’s money was on Kade, doing something even more stupid. The Darkness hung on the edge of the treasure house, obscuring him, and Ellen relaxed. She’d only noticed it because she was in sync with the shadows and on high alert for Kade. Chances were good he’d be alright.

Then Retan appeared, directly below him. One of the unarmored man’s hands rested on his white sword’s hilt. The other carried a small object wrapped in cloth. “Our next core is a special one. Sister Tanat brought it to us from her Lonely Gate, and today, I offer it up for auction. This is the core of Kaloran, Lord of the Black Tower. An S-Rank Paragon, walking a Path of shadow.”

Ellen’s blood ran cold. The core she needed was right there.

And Kade was less than fifteen feet from the SS-Ranked Paragon holding it.

The last of the Lightning Dance uses brought me behind Walar as he stalked the roof, searching for me.

My last Wind Charge went into Stormrunner, and I quickly cast Darkness as I flew through the multi-colored sheets of cloth overhead, weaving in between glowing, hovering torches of pure magic. I had a single Rainfall Charge to my name. After that, I’d be down to spells until the fighting started. And it would start.

Retan emerged from below me as I landed on the roof. He started talking, his voice echoing across the dueling pitch, and it didn’t take long to realize he had the core.

Ellen’s core.

The Storm’s Edge was unsummoned, and in the moment it took me to decide to summon it, Ellen’s eyes caught me from the crowd. Somehow, they grabbed mine through the Darkness, and her head shook slightly. I nodded, and she looked away. Her meaning was clear. The core was her responsibility—hers and Tanat’s. I had a different task.

Retan didn’t look up. I was pretty sure he had no idea I was here. But instead of attacking him, I waited until the bidding picked up. Four different students wanted the shadow core. None of them was Tanat. But one was standing next to her, and he had a significant stockpile of cores to spend. The seconds ticked by. Bids came in faster and faster, and Retan’s aura dropped just a little.

And in that moment, I slipped into the treasure house under the cover of Darkness.

The entire building screamed trap from the moment I started down the stairs. I recognized some of them as traps from Dark Citadel worlds, but others were different. Flares of light that would blind me and sear my skin. Walls made of pure brightness so intense it was like a laser beam. The entire treasure hall was a labyrinth so winding I couldn’t even see the treasures within yet. But Retan had only been gone for a handful of seconds.

He had a bypass for all the traps, or a skill that let him ignore them. It was the only explanation that made sense. That made what I was doing exceptionally dangerous.

But at the same time, I didn’t have a choice. Every teacher in the sect was hunting me, and the last place they’d seen me was the great hall. I had exactly two hiding places left. Either I headed for the surface of the canyon, or I went into the treasure house.

And only one of those had even a chance of proof that Retan was trying to play the ascension game—and that Tanat’s life was in grave danger.

It took time and effort to bypass the traps—even more so because of the menace of Retan. If I wasn’t fast enough, he’d come up behind me on his way to grab another core. And, worse, I noticed piles of dust here and there, in the corners. Walar could be on the prowl, too. Any missing grain, and he might be alerted.

Then, after working my way past one final wall of light, I stepped into a wide, open room.

I’d expected mounds of cores. Piles of them. Hundreds of different swords on the wall-mounted racks. Armor and items from dozens of different portal worlds.

Instead, I was greeted by a wide, echoing room. Three cores sat on the floor, along with two A-Rank weapons and a single, battered S-Rank greatsword. Aside from those six items, only a single object disturbed the pristine, dust-and-shadow-free room. A hexagonal column of stone, simultaneously dull gray and glowing bright white, filled the room’s center from top to bottom like a pillar in the Carlsbad Caverns. It was absolutely perfect; every edge gleamed with suppressed, brilliant energy. And there was so much of it. I knew exactly what it was the moment I saw it, though. I could feel it.

This was the beginning.

Compared to the God of Thunder’s massive Mana batteries or to the central pillar in the Crone’s Ghostmarket, it was an almost meaningless amount of energy. It would have taken four or five of them to equal one of the floating spires. But compared to what I could access, even with the nascent Eye of the Storm over the Black Tower, it was almost unfathomable. Incredible amounts of power. Almost overwhelming. Almost intoxicating.

I could use this power. I could add it to the Eye of the Storm and use it against the God of Thunder. It would give me the edge I needed—I could feel it. It’d represent a huge amount of firepower. A huge increase in my chance of surviving the next few days.

But the Crone’s words made me hesitate. She claimed that, at some point, ascension became the only thing that mattered to Paragons pushing past S-Rank. The extra power would be useful, yes—but only if I could safely deploy it. Otherwise…otherwise, I’d either become someone obsessed with the pressure of ascension, or I’d be dead.

That was enough. I cleared my head, walked carefully to the back of the treasure room behind the hexagonal column, and waited for Retan to come back.

If I was lucky, it’d be enough of a hiding place to last until the end of the auction, and then, I’d be able to plot my escape. And in the meantime, I had the proof I needed. If it was a real treasure house, Retan would have more cores and more weapons. Instead, all the power he gathered was poured straight into the stone pillar.

The moment Ellen’s hand closed on the shadow core, she started whispering. “We need to get this done, and we need to get it done fast. Kade’s doing something incredibly stupid.”

“What?” Tanat asked, but to her credit, she started maneuvering through the crowd, hand wrapped around Ellen’s free one. Ellen didn’t relax. She wouldn’t relax until this was over, and both she and Kade were back on Earth or at the Black Tower. If Tanat wanted to come along, so be it, but right now, Kade was in danger, and her core was so close to being repaired. She was so close to regaining her power.

She didn’t say anything until they arrived at the base of the narrow stairs leading up out of the canyon and started climbing. Then, quietly, she whispered, “Kade’s in the treasure room.”

“What? Why would he enter Retan’s treasure room?” Tanat stopped suddenly, eyes narrowing. Her hand dropped to her sword. Then she slowly lifted it back off the grip. “This is about what he believes Retan is doing, isn’t it?”

“Probably. I doubt he’s trying to steal more cores or anything. That’s not like him. He’s looking to prove to you that Retan is a danger to you.”

Silence. Ellen counted to fifteen before Tanat moved again. “You are Kade Noelstra’s partner, and so I owe you this. But there will be a reckoning between him and me. Keep moving.”

Ellen nodded, and they started to climb again. It took longer than she’d have liked—but then again, she didn’t want to live with a breaking core for another moment, and she wanted Kade to be here when it was fixed.

Then she followed Tanat into a wooden house and a cavern carved into the stone beyond it. And there, deep in the dim, damp cave, she saw a device of portal metal, bronze, and copper.

Ellen swallowed. Then she stepped into the room and steeled herself for what was to come. Her core surgery was about to begin.

She would be whole again.
