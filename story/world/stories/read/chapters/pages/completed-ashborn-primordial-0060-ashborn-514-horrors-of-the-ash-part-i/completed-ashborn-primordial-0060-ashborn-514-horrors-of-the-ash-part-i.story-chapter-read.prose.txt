They spent the next few days making preparations, though it was mostly for the wolves to recover their strength. Shan and his brothers had been spooked ever since reality shifted, and Vir wasn't about to venture into the Ash with companions who weren't at their peak. He’d inscribed the recycler tattoo on them all, but they’d taken longer to fully regain their prana.

As for equipment, they already had the best weapons and armor they were ever going to have. Any one of Vir's Artifact Chakrams would have nations bankrupting themselves to acquire, to say nothing of his katar. Maiya, for her part, had her orbs and her storage pocket that allowed her to fine-tune the effects of her spells.

A power she’d finally be able to wield effectively away from the prying eyes of Alt Ashani.

As for goodbyes, there really were none to be said. They hadn’t known many in this world before the shift. Now, there were even fewer.

On the morning of the third day, Vir and Maiya stood at the edge of Alt Ashani, staring out at the shimmering barrier that separated civilization from oblivion. The wolves gathered around them, tense and ready despite their lingering fear.

“Last chance to turn back,” Vir said quietly.

Maiya squeezed his hand. “And do what? Sit in our room and wait for the end? No. If we're going to die, let's at least die trying.”

Vir nodded. He took one last look at the city and its spires and crowded streets. A doomed city, filled with doomed people, perhaps, but it was the last piece of civilization they’d encounter.

They turned their backs on it and walked hand in hand into the Ash.

The Boundary offered no resistance, as usual. They could have ended up anywhere in all the realms, scattered across thousands of miles, but it didn’t matter. By consuming all the realms, travel became easier. Well, for Vir, that was.

The moment they crossed the threshold, reality changed. An endless expanse of black stretched beyond them in every direction.

Billowing torrents of soot blasted them like a hurricane, forcing them to cover their mouths. The air was so thick with prana, it formed liquid drops of black, floating eerily from the ground up to the sky.

If Vir’s visible black flames were a sign of burgeoning prana, this was in the next realm entirely.

Vir felt it pressing against his skin, trying to force its way into every pore of his body.

A sensation he’d felt innumerable times before.

And yet, he fell to his knees, gasping. Beside him, Maiya did the same, her face twisted in pain. The wolves fared no better, whimpering and pressing themselves flat against the grey earth, trying to escape the all-consuming weight.

“This is insane,” Maiya rasped through gritted teeth.

And indeed it was. Far greater than even Mahādi had been in their universe, and that city had been lethal to even Vir until only recently.

No, this was something else entirely. An environment so saturated with raw magical energy that, had they been anyone else, their bodies would have imploded already.

“Cycle!” Vir commanded loudly enough for the wolves to hear. “And fire your magic continuously! Don’t stop. If you stop, you will die.”

They obeyed, cycling prana through their bodies as fast as they could, blasting all manner of attacks, from Maiya’s continuous use of B-rank orbs—anything greater would have posed a danger to their party—to the wolves firing off prana blasts as fast as they could.

As for Vir, he had any number of ways of discharging excess prana, be it burning them off as flames or activating any of his Ultimate Arts.

In this case, it was good that Yuma’s Embrace consumed so much prana. Not only did it act as a sink for the torrential downpour trying to enter his body, it healed whatever damage Vir was unable to.

Even then, it took a full hour before Vir could even move at all and another three for Maiya to follow suit. By then, by the time he was able to answer the wolves’ pleading whimpers, the damage had already been done.

Doggedly pulling himself through the Ash with his arms, Vir crawled over to the wolf that lay inert on the soot, her body already half-buried with Ash.

Vir put his hands on her but knew in an instant it was too late.

Not even Yuma’s Embrace could bring back the dead.

A grey-furred beast Ashani had named Jiya. His brothers and Shan couldn’t even move to help, so crippled by the prana they were.

Shan threw back his head and howled a howl of pure anguish that filled the soot-stained air. The other wolves joined him, their voices rising in a chorus of grief.

Vir felt a real loss settle into his chest. These were wolves who'd kept Ashani company for hundreds upon hundreds of years. Faithful companions who'd followed them across realms and universes alike without question or complaint. And now one of them was gone, claimed by this nightmare of a realm before their journey had even begun.

“I’m sorry,” Vir whispered, kneeling beside Jiya’s body. “You deserved better than this.”

But there was no time to mourn as they should. The Ash was hostile to all life, and lingering here would only claim more of them. It was a miracle some great beast hadn’t found them while they were vulnerable.

The only path was forward, and so Vir went around healing the others with Yuma’s Embrace.

The moment Maiya could move, she leveraged her A-grade Life orbs to do the same, and both continuously healed the wolves until they’d acclimatized.

Lastly, Vir had Maiya set the dead wolf’s corpse ablaze. Its ashes would join the rest. It was no place for a cremation, but it was the only choice they had.

They departed when it was done, with Vir holding Maiya in his arms while the wolves bounded beside him. While none of them had fully acclimatized, they could at least move, and the more energy they expended, the less the pain they were forced to endure.

The power of Haste coursed through Vir’s body, accelerating his perception and movement. The prana density that had nearly killed them now supercharged his abilities, giving him more raw power than he'd ever wielded before.

Cycling Balancer of Scales to its maximum output, Vir bounded through the Ash, each leap covering hundreds of yards. The monotonous landscape blurred around them, with its gray earth and monstrous trees that had been corrupted into grotesque parodies of life.

The wolves kept pace, the exertion helping them bleed off excess prana as they adapted.

Hours passed in this manner. Or perhaps days—if this realm’s Ash underwent a similar time shift, then they could likely spend months in here with only a handful of hours passing outside.

Vir navigated by Prana Vision—which he had to underpower to be able to see through the intense prana.

Eventually, Vir spotted the remnants of a city. Broken walls jutted from the ground, and crumbled sections of wall traced what used to be a ring around what had once been streets. He recognized the architectural style immediately, and the size could only mean one thing.

“Sonam,” he said, setting Maiya down. “We're in what used to be Kin’jal territory.”

“So, not too far from Daha, then,” Maiya commented.

Vir oriented himself, calculating distances based on his memories of the old maps. “Maybe two hundred miles. We're not exactly where we need to be, but it could have been worse, all things considered.”

They could have been dumped out in the Demon Realm, where the journey would have taken weeks.

They continued on, skirting the ruins with caution. Jatan Lake was nowhere to be seen, no doubt drowned long ago by the ever-falling Ash.

The ruins of Sonam showed signs of recent habitation, though not by anything human. Massive claw marks could be seen in the city’s towering walls, and acid hissed as it ate through the soot. Something had passed through here recently, and Vir had no desire to encounter it unprepared.

Using what she knew of the city’s layout, Maiya identified several key structures that were still standing, allowing them to orient themselves north.

Once again, vast fields of nothing greeted them, but as they pushed deeper, they began to see Ash Tears bend in on themselves, creating impossible geometries that hurt to look at. Some led to other parts of the wasteland, while others led to what appeared to be the tattered remains of spaces where reality itself had been broken.

Vir spotted a black whirlpool in the distance, which upon inspection proved to be massive vortexes of Ash prana that pulled everything toward them.

Even light bent around them, creating spheres of absolute darkness against the grey landscape. They had to divert well around their sphere of influence, keeping miles between themselves lest they get pulled in.

Terrifyingly, the abominations only grew more and more frequent the deeper they pressed.

“What are those?” Maiya muttered.

“Prana collapsed under its own weight, most likely,” Vir said. “I guess that’s what happens when prana gets that dense. I doubt even Dance of the Shadow Demon would let me escape if we got stuck in that.”

Vir felt like he was threading a needle as he traveled, and only when they left those terrifying phenomena behind did he dare to breathe more easily.

His relief was short-lived.

The creature emerged from the Ash without warning. A massive, serpentine thing with too many legs and a head that split open to reveal concentric rings of teeth. Like a Wyrm that had become mutated and disfigured, its body was covered in chitinous plates that glistened as if coated in oil.

By far the most terrifying aspect, however, was its eyes. Its many, many eyes strewn haphazardly around its body burned with a crazed malevolence that gave even Vir pause.

Vir didn't recognize the species. It might have been something native to this realm, but more likely than not, the abomination was something else entirely, warped beyond recognition by the Ash's corruption.

It didn't matter. The creature lunged, and Vir barely had time to respond.
