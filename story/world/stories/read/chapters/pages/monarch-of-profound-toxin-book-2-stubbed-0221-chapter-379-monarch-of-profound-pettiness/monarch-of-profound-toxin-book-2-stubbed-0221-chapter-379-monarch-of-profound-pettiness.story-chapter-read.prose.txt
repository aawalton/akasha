Chapter 379

Life sucks, and then you still don’t die.

Multiple times a day, Eik was harvested for the parts needed to create the grade 7 potions and pills, each session running for between twenty and forty harvests.

Any time he ran out of ingredients, he had to get more, and, unfortunately, that required him to ask the guild master to come down and cut him some more. He swore he wasn’t a masochist, but the definition was becoming blurry. He didn’t like it, but he still kept asking for it.

Honestly, he didn’t know whether to celebrate or curse the fact that they couldn’t just do a thousand harvests per sitting. It would have been a true trial, but at least it would have been more condensed. The reality was that a Living Manifestation cutting parts out of him required regeneration, and regeneration required energy. And that wasn’t an infinite resource. He needed to rest in between, if only a little.

And even after the Toxic Initiation, Guild Master Ganyak hir Gawak was not a young man. He might have only been a butcher by proxy, but it required immense concentration on his part.

But besides that, his personal crafting hell was going as well as he could have hoped. With a grasp on both common potion creation and creation of the Legendary Mystery Medicine, all of the grade 7 potions were being completed in a careful but steady stream.

Chop the Blade had already been by to demand his first Potion of Mighty Strength. Immediately, he had snatched it out of Eik’s hand and disappeared off to the same remote world where Eik had tested the first of them.

Nearly drooling with excitement, the old geezer had returned not even an hour later full of demands for more potions. The guy just wanted to have fun. Eik gave in and handed over four more, plus a Potion of the Metal Body, just to get the annoyance off his back. The rest he was determined to keep out of Chop’s hands.

After all, Eik couldn’t make this stuff sitting on his ass.

A knock on the double doors of the laboratory pulled him out of a near trance-like state of concentration. At his call, the door cracked open slightly and Robert poked his head inside. “Hey, boss. Do you have a moment?”

“Yes, please! Come in, come in! I’ve been hoping for someone to show up and give me a damned break!”

Robert snickered. “I thought as much.”

“Take a seat wherever,” Eik said, gesturing to the messy space. “What’s up?”

“We’ve had kind of a… fascinating request that I figured you’d be interested in hearing about.”

“Is it important?”

The administrator tilted his head to the side. “Not particularly, no.”

Eik breathed in relief. “Oh, perfect.”

“So, we had a visitor upstairs just a couple of hours ago,” Robert said as he moved some documents from a stool and sat down.

“A visitor? Who?”

“Merchant Lord Greggers.”

Eik narrowed his eyes. “Who?”

“You’re kidding,” Robert groaned. “That guy was such a pain in the ass back when you were getting started! He tried to straight up rob your shit to sell it himself!”

“Of course I’m kidding, dude,” Eik chuckled and slapped Robert’s shoulder carefully. “But is that guy seriously alive? I figured he’d annoyed someone to the point of murder at this point. I know I was close a few times at least.”

“Honestly, I didn’t really know either. He’d just grown so insignificant that his very existence had faded from my mind. I can’t believe he used to be important. You could literally breathe on him and he’d die.”

“Hey, I brush! Twice a day!”

“Yeah, because Ihasu and your dad say you have to.”

“Uuh, not true!”

“Yeah, right.”

“You’re getting a pay cut for that, homie,” Eik threatened.

“I quit.”

“Okay, I’ll give you a raise. Please stay.”

Robert held out a hand without a word and Eik begrudgingly forked over a grade 6 Legendary Mystery Medicine, muttering darkly under his breath. “Freaking monster.”

“Anyway,” the administrator continued as he stuffed the pill into his own bag of holding. “He’s hoping to buy a license to distribute.”

“License to distribute what?”

“You know, potions.”

“What kind?”

“According to him, all of them,” Robert incredulously. “This is an opportunity you would be foolish to let slip, is what he said, if I remember correctly.”

For a moment, Eik was dumbstruck. “Does this guy not know what our company value is?”

“I mean, he must, right?”

“You didn’t ask what he was thinking?”

“My brain was obviously occupied with the effort of trying not to laugh out loud.”

“That’s fair enough, actually.”

“And he’s in business with Boulder Fist Gary, if you remember him as well.”

“You can’t be serious,” Eik questioned. “One of my first villains!” he uttered with a flourish. “I thought he fell into invisibility after I killed his beloved son Rock Fist Bart.”

“Apparently not.”

“And these two thought they would try to get a bite of my big ass cake?”

“Uh huh. What do you want to do about it?”

Eik scratched his stubbled chin as he contemplated it for a few seconds. “Tell them… that we will work with them, draw up a contract and— Wait, what’s the smallest font you can print on a contract?”

Robert looked at him with puzzlement. “I don’t know, like, really small.”

“Small enough to need a magnifying glass to read it?”

“Yeah, of course.”

“All right, perfect. So, draw up a nice contract, get them to sign, and write in really tiny letters that all profits they make from any source must be won off me in honorable duel.”

“Wow, that’s… pretty mean.”

“Yeah, well, they both tried to both ruin and kill me, so, there. Are you saying you won’t do it?”

“I’m saying I can’t wait.”

“Amazing! Now, I have work to do, so get your ass out of here.”

“Yes, boss!” Robert said and headed for the door, skipping giddily at the thought of getting to dish out some good ol’ cold revenge.

“And call me when you show them the small print! No way I’m missing that!”

The last thing to disappear through the door was Robert’s upturned thumb.

***

Taking only single, short breaks at a time every couple of days, Eik continued his crafting hell, enduring both the physical discomfort from the frequent harvests and mental exhaustion from the constant high focus, monotonous work.

With every pill completed and ingested, his power grew steadily, surprising even himself. To his joy, his new evolutionary trait, Supreme Vessel worked not only on normal potions but also on Legendary Mystery Medicine, intensifying the permanent boost to his stats by something in the area of a hundred percent.

But while these creations, along with the elevation of True Toxic Harmony—Omnicide—would be the difference that might just make it possible to put up a fight against the returned Worldbreaker, Eik had no illusions of their chances.

The fiend was unfathomable.

He was afraid. Most of his career as an Awakened had been a train made of recklessness, driving on tracks of close-call survival against his superiors. But this was different.

Ihasu, the kids, his father, sister, friends… Everyone depended on his success in this. If he fell in battle, life in the Unified Mass would become a buffet for the Lord of the Moon.

Yes, there were other X-rankers living in seclusion or hiding, but if Eik and Chop couldn’t do it, then why should they be able to? Or even be interested? They could well be waiting for an excuse to put an end to their centuries of life.

It was days later when Andihar burst through the double doors to the laboratory, ripping one of the sides off its hinges and sending it flying through the room to crack against the opposite wall and shatter into pieces. “Eik!”

Having felt the large elf coming since before he even entered the offices upstairs, Eik didn’t bother to look up from his cauldron as he spoke quietly, tongue peeking out of the corner of his mouth in concentration, small fragments of stone flying around him. Only a barrier of aura kept pebbles from ending up in his mixture. “I’m actually trying to… focus here, Andi.”

“There no time for that crap!” the elf shouted, drawing Eik’s attention in earnest. Looking at him properly for the first time, Eik noticed the expression on the elf’s face. Dread.

A heavy lead ball of worry tumbled into his stomach. “Andihar, what’s going on?”

“The Lord of the Moon has attacked,” Andihar told him, lowering his voice to a normal speaking level. “It’s really bad this time, Eik.”

Blood drained from his face as Eik forgot all about the potion in the cauldron. Spectral limbs of azure reached up for him, but he annihilated it in an instant without bothering to look at it. He followed Andihar out of the room.

They were outside by the time he got in his second question, Eik slowing down to match the S-ranker’s speed. “Did he go directly for headquarters, or is it a city-wide invasion?”

“It’s not Gimleh this time. The attack hit Yanglith about four and a half minutes ago.”

“Wait, what?” Eik asked with a frown. “What’s Yanglith?”

“It’s the native world of the Yanglithic civilization. They’re a relatively minor civilization in the Nidafjeld Alliance. They only drifted into the Unified Mass about two and a half thousand years ago.”

“I think I’ve met some of them before, maybe. They have the little things coming out of their foreheads, no? Like feelers, or something like that.”

“That’s them, yes.”

“There aren’t many of them.”

“They lack the resources and territory to expand their population as massively as some of the more influential civilizations. As far as I know, they only have their home world as well as one more smaller world.”

“How many spawn?”

“Thousands. And the Lord of the Moon is there in the flesh.”

Eik let out an involuntary gasp. “What? He’s there? Why is he attacking such a minor civilization? He could have caught us off guard with an attack on a much more significant target!”

Andihar’s jaw flexed as he clicked his tongue. “We don’t know. It doesn’t make sense.”

“Fuck!” Eik hissed as they passed through the fracture to Gimleh. Abruptly, he wrapped his arm around Andihar and exploded forward at extreme speed. “Guide me to a fracture to Yanglith!”

Andihar immediately pointed him forward.

At the prospect of another clash with the Lord of the Moon, Profound Toxin hopped around like an excited, aggressive dog inside his spirit. Through their deep connection, even some of Eik’s own fear was overshadowed by that powerful wave of excitement. Good. He seriously needed at least some of that battle craze that always took him.

“That bastard has to die today!” he snarled.

Gimleh had had hundreds of thousands of troops on standby for the moment anything happened, and it showed. Warriors of B-rank and above stood in line to go through fractures to aid Yanglith. Even if the weakest of them stood no chance against the foes, they could still assist in recovery and rescue.

Eik just hoped there was something left to find.
