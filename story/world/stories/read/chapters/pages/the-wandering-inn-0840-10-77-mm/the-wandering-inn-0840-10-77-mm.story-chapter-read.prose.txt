Benoix was no Talenqual. It was neither as large nor had a company of even moderate renown to hold it. Nor did it have a Fraerling city nearby and the potential to play into the war of the Great Companies.
This was a good thing for Benoix. It was allowed to be smaller and, still, interesting.
Certainly, once you had won clear of the deep jungle to find one of the closest port-cities to the Illaxian Jungle—notable for stretching for over five hundred unbroken miles southwards along the eastern coast of Baleros along with the presence of the Synectic Maze—this glimpse of civilization would feel like a mirage.
Benoix had cleared the encroaching trees and vegetation for a thousand feet from the trade road that stretched northwards. The city? Stone walls. Buildings made of barnacle-encrusted wood, so old that generations of Lizardfolk had lived and died within the walls.
More stone than Talenqual had been. That city of sprawling Lizardfolk had been four times bigger, but consequently, far more wooden. The walls not made of stones mortared into place, and the streets dirt instead of Benoix’s stone. Paradoxically to the eye, richer.
Any thriving Lizardfolk city pushed at the seams. Benoix was not nearly as well-placed as Talenqual. It was not growing; when Lizardfolk or Nagas sought ambition, fortune, a change in their status, they did not embark on enterprise here.
They just left. By sea or land. So Benoix was a stable population of sixty-eight thousand—small as Lizardfolk cities went. Its main exports were sea catches, seaweed especially, and bananas. It had several plantations that the small army had marched through and ransacked.
This was upsetting to the local Naga council, but within ten minutes, they had been assured that they would be compensated. On top of the handsome fee for lodging, healers, transport for the dead…
Discretion.
Benoix had a standing army instead of a mercenary company. Barely two thousand strong, though they could call on the militia and swell that number mightily if the enemy came calling. As they watched the mostly-Human procession coming their way, there was a thought.
Could they capture them instead? Everyone knew these were Terandrians. And a royal.
Survivors of the calamitous battle at sea. This lot had, if rumor served, shipwrecked on the coast south of here, then marched north through the jungles.
Already a feat that would gain you the sympathy of any actual native who knew how perilous and hard that trek was. For a bunch of thin-skinned Terandrians? Almost made you feel bad for them.
But they had also, in fleeing Dullahan and Lizardfolk armies from two of the Great Companies, Iron Vanguard and Jungle Tails, gone to the worst place ever.
The Synectic Maze. A death-zone. A kind of dungeon that even Named-ranks avoided. They had gone in and survived. Then fought their way clear of a Jungle Tails army and marched for ages to get here.
Very impressive. Very sympathetic, if you had any for those Terandrians who’d set out to claim the New Lands. It also meant they were definitely weary; malnourished, if how fast they’d stripped that banana plantation was any indication; and depleted in numbers.
The Calanferians didn’t really shine with the light of the Eternal Throne. More like the mud and grime of weeks in the jungle over chafed skin, bug bites, and blisters. They had come in a giant Citadel-class ship, but there were barely a few thousand of them, sailors, soldiers, and colonists. Terrible, what a tragedy.
How much were they worth as ransoms?
Benoix’s [Commander], a Lamia, was making that calculation atop Harbor Precipice, which hung over the bay proper. The city was in fact one giant cliff; the harbor below was a miniature lake where water rushed into the bay. A [Geomancer] had made this city ages ago, allowing Lizardfolk to build on the stone and have ample space for fishing and trade with the sea below.
The Lamia trained his spyglass on the army marching his way. Sure, there’d be more Terandrians in a bit, but if they took this group hostage, a dagger at the throat of the [Princess] or whatever was worth a lot of gold. It wasn’t personal; she had no relationship with any Great Company. It was just survival in Baleros.
Two thoughts made the Lamia hesitate. He swept his spyglass over the people marching towards the city. They were, oddly, in formation.
“They’re not soldiers, Commander. Some are; I can see their armor, but the rest look like irregulars. But they’re marching in close to parade formation. What, are they trying to look like they’re a real army?”
“Good lines for amateurs if they weren’t all soldiers before. It makes no sense; they’re not going to train that while they’re desperate to get out of the jungle. If it were a feint, I’d screen their front with those [Knights] and [Soldiers] to make it appear like they’re more organized. Those civilians…they’re armed.”
The Lamia saw weapons and armor on the Calanferians. Hodgepodge and not readied; just strapped to shoulders, hanging off them as they walked. It made him think of village militias who’d survived a warzone. The Medusa peering at the group murmured.
“They survived the Synectic Maze.”
“Yeah. I don’t think we want to know how they levelled. Still…”
He was thinking, using all of the brainpower that his specific evolution afforded him. The Lamia spoke.
“The Council’s gotten information requests from multiple Great Companies. Their tails are all knotted up about it. Well, two requests. Jungle Tails wants to know everything.”
“Ergh. Are we joining them? We’re on the wrong side of the continent…”
“Doesn’t hurt to play nice. The other one was the Iron Vanguard. We told them to eat their armor.”
“Hah! I heard Jungle Tails spanked them and sent them packing. There’s something. They don’t have an army near us, right?”
A moment of worry; the Lamia shrugged.
“Not landed. Might be trouble from the sea, but we’re independent. I think that’s the best move. The last Great Company interested in this lot is Forgotten Wing.”
“You said we had two information requests—”
“Last one wasn’t a request. The Titan just said to leave ‘em be. Or he’ll toss a Tier 5 artillery spell into our town. Too busy for War Walkers, he said.”
The Medusa shivered as she curled up.
“Nagas. So why are we thinking of taking them on!?”
“[Princesses] are worth a lot of money. The Titan’s busy. A couple of Tier 5 spells? Just get the ransom to rebuild all the buildings and put us ahead.”
Balerosian logic. Everyone knew it, from the Titan on down. It wasn’t personal like other continents. You didn’t let opportunity like this pass you by without at least thinking…but the Titan knew that.
He was a smart guy. The Lamia grunted as his spyglass swept over the army, and he made up his mind.
“Think I saw that [Princess]. They’ve got [Knights] on her, or a body double. Smart. Tell the army to stand down. Disband the militia, throw the doors open, yadda yadda.”
The Medusa peered across the army.
“That fast?”
“Found a third reason we’re not engaging.”
“What’s the third r—Temples!”
The Lamia lowered his spyglass. Even from a distance, he could see Reason #3. The beleaguered Calanfarians were clearing the jungle, still emerging from the hellish terrain that made each step a fight to push through thorns, vines, and clouds of biting gnats. It must have been a raiding group which had looted the banana plantations.
Hungry enough to grab food even within sight of the city. But the main body was just fighting out of the trees. He’d wondered how they’d made such good time.
‘Good time’ meaning they’d taken probably a month to go barely a hundred miles. In the Illaxian Jungle? For non-natives? That was insanely good. He’d put it down to marching Skills, perhaps a native guide, luck, and counterlevelling.
All these things were probably true, but another reason was likely the glowing magical spells clearing the undergrowth ahead of the Calanferians. Bright little orbs of red, deceptively small, that expanded into flashes of light. Smoke, dust…
[Fireballs]. Someone was blowing the undergrowth to pieces ahead of the Calanferians. Even now, the [Fireballs] kept flying. The Lamia [Commander] shook his head.
“That’s not even a [Mage] division. That’s one damn [Mage].”
They were still throwing [Fireballs] as the Calanferians cleared the trees. Just shooting them backwards into the jungle. He shuddered.
Fighting high-levels sucked. The rule was you took a hundred for every high-level combatant if they were ten levels higher than you. A thousand if they were nastier. Then you let someone else fight that battle because you weren’t going to survive it if you were the enemy commander.
Benoix would stay poor, and he’d stay a low-level [Commander]. What a pity.
It beat being Talenqual.

They made it to Benoix without incident.
Yes, without incident.
If you wanted to call losing over a hundred people to infected wounds after the Synectic Maze, illness from bug bites, attacks from monsters ‘without incident’.
Compared to what they’d been through, it had been…quiet.
No more armies. No more great incidents.
Jungle Tails hadn’t come back for round two after their defeat. They’d just fallen back, let the Calanferian army escape northwards, picked up their dead, counted their losses, and declined to re-engage.
Mercenary logic. They’d seen the survivors of the Synectic Maze, and they didn’t want to pay the cost of fighting them.
Funny thing—the Iron Vanguard had also vanished. No other armies had come calling.
It was almost like there weren’t that many damn roving mercenary groups in a jungle where no one lived. Almost like the Dullahans, for all their ire, didn’t want to try a jungle landing and campaign after their own losses.
If there were other groups in the jungle, they’d been raiders, villages—no one keen on taking on several thousand Humans. Let alone how they’d made their way through the jungle.
[Fireball] solved a lot of problems. Tree? [Fireball]. Insect swarm? [Fireball]. Animals? [Fireball]. Free charred meat.
Even the city didn’t want a fight. Which of course meant they were now in the position of fearing these visitors had the upper hand. They might easily take the city hostage…
Well, that was where Calanferian diplomacy came in.

Benoix did not throw open the gates for every soldier and colonist as they marched towards the walls. But they did have tents set up and a lot of people outside the gates.
“Esteemed guests! Hello, welcome! Calanfer has already been in contact with us. We have rooms for your officers, [Healers], food—and I understand a few ships of yours are coming this way. The rest of you may camp outside our city, and we have provisions aplenty, even water and heating spells.”
It was a rather clever suggestion to put Benoix at ease. Inviting the entire army within the walls would have been disastrous, but this was far more amenable. The colonists could enter the city in rotations, and despite being barred from access, most of the weary Humans didn’t seem to care; half were setting up their own ragged tents or just heading for the baths, food—or lying down on the spot. The Naga was prepared for some objections or comments, but the weary group was so quiet he flicked a tongue out and coughed.
“We have plenty of amenities we are willing to offer, and the costs have already been taken care of. Just say the word if you need more. Can we…get you anything?”
“A [Time Travel] spell?”
The Naga slithering next to a weary-looking [Lord] laughed. Hundredlord Cortese did not. He strode ahead, limping a bit—his visible flesh was riddled with mosquito bites. He still had his dueling sword, visible magic armor, but his underclothes, his entire bearing, seemed worn down.
Like everyone else. The overly-proud, confident look of a man stalking his prey, a lion amongst equals, was gone. But more than that? He just appeared lost. Lost.
The Guilty Man.
The Nagas did not know him by that name. He introduced himself as Hundredlord Cortese of Kaaz, a powerful nobleman, and they treated him as such. But he would forever be The Guilty Man in their memories of the Synectic Maze. Purple, uncertain, borne down by his sins. A blade held aloft in trembling grip.
They walked with him, even now. Not his people; there hadn’t been many Kaazians aboard The Throne’s Will when it ran aground. Yet his people were with him.
The Guilty Man’s…followers. The people who had borne his color, had known him, walked with Hundredlord Cortese. In formation—they unnerved the Lizardfolk who didn’t understand why they stood together, moved together. They listened to Cortese. When he elected to get a [Healer] to check up on him and anyone in need of it, they all walked with him.
It was the same for the others. A few were more…independent.
Captain Ennerev, for instance, looked like he’d aged a decade. He hobbled towards the Council, favoring his good leg; he’d lost the other. In true [Sailor]’s fashion, he’d had a replacement peg leg made.
“I’m Captain Ennerev of Calanfer, by way of bein’ one of the leaders here. How does the Eternal Throne know we’re here? We kept out of contact to avoid pursuers…”
The others slowed to listen as the Naga chuckled.
“Er, hard to miss you lot coming north, Captain. Benoix is the only city you’d reach if you were hugging the coast. Our [Hunters] noticed wildlife fleeing you a week back; it was probably then.”
“Ah. Then the Eternal Throne has protected us.”
“Yep, they’ve arranged everything. You people are really good at diplomacy, you know that? Hence the warm welcome! Believe me, we’d have taken you lot for [Mercenaries] and given you the old Balerosian welcome until we got a good look at you. Come in, let’s get you a seat. And some cream for those bug bites. Dead gods, you look horrible!”
The Captain nodded. But he half-turned even as his knees buckled at the sight of a chair.
“First, Her Highness of Calanfer. Seraph—Your Highness?”
He caught himself as the vagaries of civilization returned to him. But the woman who walked forwards didn’t seem to even notice. She was like the others, footsore, yet less bug-bitten and worn.
It was that damn circlet. It kept the bugs off her, mostly, and even some of the rain and rot. Her servants were probably why her wardrobe was at all feasible. She was still visibly exhausted, but she was stumbling forwards.
“We should rest. We should all rest…but first, Ser Solstice. Markus…”
Captain Ennerev tugged down the cap over his head. The Nagas didn’t understand until they glanced back and saw the last figure. He stood at the back of the procession, bearing a white shroud in his arms. The Nagas closed their mouths.
“Oh. That. Yes, there’s already, um, a ship.”
Princess Seraphel, no, The Weeping Princess turned. Like Cortese, she had her own followers, though she was only half…the other woman who stood next to her whispered.
“How?”
“Ser Thilowen.”
A [Knight] stepped forwards.
The Grim Knight. He bowed.
“I have informed Their Majesties of the particulars of our needs. I thought, given the circumstances…”
“Yes, yes. Go. Tell Rabbiteater…I see.”
Seraphel’s head hung, and she breathed in and out. Then she turned.
“We must rest. But after we—we deliver the remains—we had to leave so many behind.”
She might have wept, but she was too tired to. They had needed every drop of water on their trek. So the [Princess] just closed her eyes, swaying, until someone steadied her.
Not her maidservants. Mariel and Beacle were as worn as their mistress, but they had leafy fans they were used to smacking mosquitos with. They hit one now, slapping Dame Neranthei across the cheek.
She didn’t even blink, just wiped the bug guts away. Better a blow from the fans than sickness or the damn bites. No, the woman who caught Seraphel was—
A nobody. The council of Benoix were clearly trying to figure out who she was. She was neither a handmaiden nor a Thronebearer. Nor even the undead duo, Strategist Veine and Clatterface, who stood next to Seraphel, eternal bodyguards.
This woman had rougher clothing, no royal training or visible magic about her. She did have a spear she had been using as a walking stick, and some looted leather armor, but she was…what?
“Thank you, Gilthisa.”
Seraphel thanked the woman, and Gilthisa, a [Commoner], nodded.
“Seraphel, does this mean we’re going home? Or to the New Lands?”
She murmured—then saw the Nagas eying her and flushed.
“I meant Your Highness—”
The [Princess] half-turned to the woman who had also been The Weeping Princess. Theirs was an odd case, because Seraphel had vanished, but it meant that two women were defined by that painful blue, a shower of tears.
“Even now, you may call me Seraphel. You must. It is an order, Gilthisa. I couldn’t bear it if we were to become strangers just because of formalities. Not you, a friend.”
They smiled at each other, a pair who had not even known one another before landing in Baleros, never exchanged a word. Gilthisa shook with trepidation as she glanced around; she was a nervous woman. Wracked with her own guilt and regrets. She had killed her husband in self-defense. Been a pariah—
Her life’s story was one everyone knew. Any one of the Calanferians could have told you elements of her life by heart. Not all of it. Just the most painful ones.
The reasons she wept.
Each leader to their own company.
Ser Thilowen, filled with discipline. Cortese, lingering, glancing back at the silent figure standing there with his brother in his arms, guilt on his back. The Weeping Princesses two, too tired to cry. They just hugged each other as Seraphel whispered.
“To the ship, then. He won’t put his brother down until he’s sent back home.”
And—the last of them watched it all. Even he, the [Knight], had his own followers, though they stayed back out of respect for his grief.
The Lonely Warrior.
No longer alone, for countless people knew him; if not what lay behind his helmet, then his strength, his loss, his force of will. He walked forwards at last, past the others.
A collective. Individuals slowly waking from the Synectic Maze’s madness. Some who fought to become their own people again, like the [Summoner], Revi, who stood with Vofea, Ksmvr, adventurers of their own names and natures.
Others who continued to follow, even if they no longer shared thoughts. Who found meaning in being a follower of The Guilty Man, like a pair of [Archers], a weary [Storm Sailor], and so on. Calanferian [Soldiers] and would-be colonists who had fought and levelled and now didn’t seem to know if they were truly going home or if this was just a dream.
And one other.
The Angry Woman stood alone. Alone, of all the others. Her arms were still covered by the ruined magical clothing that marked her as a noblewoman of Tourvecall, the Kingdom of Incantations. Yet she alone had no followers. Her dented helmet hiding her visage slowly swivelled to follow Rabbiteater as he walked past. Bearing his brother’s body.
Lady Menrise said nothing. A wand was smoking from the countless [Fireballs] it had fired off. Her blood was almost boiling with mana burn. Her stomach longed to vomit, and she was malnourished, exhausted.
She was still—
Furious.

It was a custom of [Knights], seldom talked about, to send charter ships for their fallen. They were used to it.
Brave [Knights] went on quests abroad. They embarked on crusades, and some came back covered in glory. Some came back covered in shrouds.
He was not the only one who had died. He was just one man amidst thousands of casualties. But Ser Markus of the Spring, of the Order of Seasons, had been Rabbiteater’s friend. He had died in the Synectic Maze before they had ever seen the sky. The Goblin hadn’t known it. He had fought his way free of the madness, won a victory, then learned his brother was dead.
The first man to embrace him after learning his identity, the companion who had rode through a war and fought his way across the sea—Ser Solstice’s grief had not ended even after all this time.
The Naga council of Benoix was eager to learn anything they could about their strange visitors and would have joined the procession headed to the docks, except for the—smell. It came from the pale white shroud that the [Knight] held, and the Nagas recoiled, paled, drew back.
“Is that—?”
“It’s his brother. Please, allow him a moment. He has to—is the ship ready?”
“I, er, don’t know. But we have—look, we have mortuaries. A [Graveyard Keeper]. We could have the body preserved or put in a coffin—”
It was not a terrible stench. Nor were there flies. Menrise had enchanted the shroud after that had begun. It was just that odor that was unmistakable.
Death.
Seraphel du Marquin shook her head. She murmured as she slowed.
“He must lay his brother to rest. He—he hasn’t put him down since…he has borne him all this way. Please.”
The Nagas stopped slithering after the others. They turned to each other, and Menrise brushed past them. Cortese followed. An army followed. Ser Solstice walked down the stone steps towards one of the ships in the harbor. It bore a unique strip of paint on the hull with the flags and crest of countless Terandrian kingdoms upon it.
Expensive markings for a reason. They denoted it as a barge for the dead. No [Pirates] or regular ships would seek to loot it; Terandrian kingdoms and [Knight] orders vowed undying vengeance upon those who did.
The procession stopped, and Princess Seraphel, Cortese, and a few others walked forwards alone. Another limping Spring Knight. The Ivory Four. Those who had known him.
Lady Menrise was last to approach. Ser Solstice said nothing at first as the [Captain] of the ship herself disembarked.
“Ser Knights. Your Highness, Lords and Ladies—I am Captain Wisaindre of Home’s Repose. It is my sworn duty to bear the fallen of Terandria’s children home. Is this the honorable [Knight] I have been sent to collect?”
“Yes.”
Seraphel answered when Rabbiteater did not. She half-turned.
“We have other bodies. And—and a list of Thronebearers. Many whose remains were not collected.”
Captain Wisaindre’s eyes widened at the list that Ser Thilowen presented. It was long. Over a hundred names?
Entire battles in war had not seen so many. Yet she composed herself, bowed deeply.
“If possible, their remains shall be recovered. I will return their names to their kingdoms. I—it is customary to list how each fell, their deeds—”
“They fell in battle. In the Synectic Maze, a death-zone. List that for each and every one marked thusly. Those who did not fall there died at sea, battling the Bloodtear Pirates.”
Ser Thilowen’s voice was not cold, but edged. Hard steel, that man. Harder than anyone had dreamed from one of the silly Thronebearers. Menrise’s gloves were clenched by her sides as Captain Wisaindre bowed again.
“We have attempted to recover as many bodies from the sea battle as possible. This [Knight]…”
“Ser Markus of the Order of Seasons. Spring.”
At last, Rabbiteater spoke. His voice was grating, thick from the long journey without speech. Seraphel turned to him, but hesitated as he lifted the shrouded body.
“Where do I put him? He’s tired.”
“We can take him, Ser Knight…”
“No. Show me.”
After some whispering, one of the crew led Rabbiteater up the gangplank. There was momentary silence, and Menrise stood for minutes on the dock as the Captain talked to the [Princess] and others.
“…Thought lost, Your Highness. Those that did survive, well. The Iron Vanguard has boarded or captured all those who did not immediately flee to the New Lands or sailed to northern Izrilian cities.”
“All?”
“Their pursuit was relentless, and they had a full navy. Not all were sunk…many were ransomed wholesale. As of yet, no fleet has set sail to repulse or address the Dullahans. There is much talk of it, but…the survivors are the focus. Those who reached the New Lands still numbered thousands, but they fled their ships as the Dullahans sank them. Then they lost contact, of course.”
“Why ‘of course’?”
Lord Cortese demanded, growing slightly more animated. Menrise shifted; she had wanted to know that too. But she was holding onto her tongue for fear of what might come out. The Captain gave him a look as if he were mad, then spoke.
“Because of the mana-drain, Hundredlord. It is…lantern lights, you have been out of contact so long. It is known, now, that the New Lands are both draining mana of all who step foot there—artifacts, [Mages]—and that the ground is salted. Nothing grows that is not native.”
“Dead gods.”
Seraphel whispered. The [Captain] bowed deeper and turned.
“If there is any news I might impart…I would offer you passage homewards, Your Highness, but this ship is sworn to take none but the dead. Not even royalty.”
And I don’t want to be attacked for having a damn [Princess] on board. Menrise filled in the little social niceties of Terandrians with ease. They all knew this language, this game. Ser Thilowen bowed.
“A more suitable escort shall arrive in due time. I am aware of some developments—is there any news of major import we might have missed?”
The [Captain] thought as Rabbiteater returned. Everyone fell silent, but he just walked down the gangplank and stood there. So they gossiped about the news in front of the dead. That was Terandria for you.
“I…oh, there is one piece of news. The Singer of Terandria was headed for Baleros. Rumor has it she intended to begin a tour here or—”
“Cara? Cara came here?”
Seraphel’s face lit up. She almost began to smile until the [Captain] hesitated.
“I…you are a fan of hers, Your Highness?”
“I know her personally. She can’t have come here for me? Could she?”
That statement made even Menrise’s head turn. The Singer of Terandria knew the 4th Princess of Calanfer well enough to…? She was no [Heroine]. Why would someone sail all the way to Baleros?
However, the [Captain] had turned pale, and she was licking her lips.
“I, ah, perhaps this is not fit news, then—”
“What? Why? Did something happen to her?”
“I—I—there are still vigils being held across Terandria. You see, it was a remnant of the Bloodtear Pirates. Her ship was sunk, and there were no survivors found by the Blighted Kingdom, which had—”
Silence. Seraphel took in the news of the Singer of Terandria’s presumed death—then she began to scream. Her handmaidens and bodyguards rushed to her as the [Captain] stepped back.
“Your Highness!”
“Not Cara! No—no, I refuse to believe it! She’s survived—she is alive.”
“Of course, Your Highness. Her body was never found, and they are searching. I am terribly sorry—”
Please don’t ask me about anything else. The [Captain] seemed ready to sail back to Terandria that moment. However, Seraphel didn’t remain in hysterics long.
Gilthisa had come running as the [Princess] clung to Dame Neranthei, weeping. She hesitated, but Seraphel embraced her.
“She is alive! I know it! Oh, dead gods. This ordeal never ends. But I shall not lose her. I swear that by the Eternal Throne and Noelictus and—Gilthisa, I have to tell you about Cara! She’s another friend. Between you and she—I have few friends in this world; I shall not lose another. If I may call you that?”
The sight of a Calanferian [Peasant] embracing a [Princess] had the rest of the Terandrians agog, but Gilthisa was smiling as she took Seraphel’s hands.
“If I can do anything, Your Highness, we are sworn to you by our shared bonds. Friends? Of course.”
“Friends. I had always wanted some. And she is alive. My friend—”
Seraphel was composing herself. Then someone spoke in a mocking, piercing tone that carried across the silent docks.
“Friends? Fwens. Oh, glorious day! Hooray, hooray! I’m a thirty-some [Princess] and I have a fwen. That makes it all worth it. Me and my fwens against the world.”
The sarcastic voice dripping with bitterness made Seraphel jump. Then turn, biting her lip. Everyone else turned, and Lady Menrise kept talking.
“What a sacrifice! Two friends, the Singer, and Ser Markus dead. So much more than the rest of us have lost. Can your hearts bear to lose a third friend? I should hope not. Let ten thousand more lie dead and rotting in the maze and Baleros’ sands before such a heartache should come to pass.”
“Menrise. I didn’t mean—”
The Angry Woman’s throat burned. Her chest was heaving, and her tongue felt like a viper striking the flinching [Princess], the silent Rabbiteater, again and again. At last, she caught hold of it. Covered her visor with one hand, turning her head away.
“I’m…sorry. I just—”
“No, I understand. Let’s…Ser Solstice? Rabbiteater? Is there anything else to…?”
“It’s done.”
He didn’t look at Menrise or Seraphel. Rabbiteater turned away as the aghast [Captain] eyed the [Lady], the company, and backed towards her ship. The Goblin whispered.
“I’m tired. I’m going to sleep.”
He walked off as the rest of the weary Terandrians followed him slowly. Lady Menrise stood alone on the docks. Then she followed them. They had emerged from the Synectic Maze with levels, alive, against all odds.
But they were not well.
Least of all…her.

She did not apologize again for her outburst on the docks. She should have, but by now, it was easier just to let it go. Move on.
They were used to that. It did not mean the words had not hurt; Princess Seraphel did seem to recall her higher station and composed herself as they found their lodgings. An inn. Benoix had thrown open all its housing to the Terandrians, who would strain the city, but the soldiers and colonists would sleep in houses, the barracks, anywhere space could be found.
Gilthisa got to lodge with the most important people, such as the [Knights], command staff, and high-level members of the entourage like Admiral Dakelos.
His leg was still healing, so they’d carried him off to a [Healer] to make sure the bone was properly set and there was no infection. He was a hated Ailendamus [Admiral]…a sworn comrade.
Funny. Before the sea voyage, Menrise would have said that the Ivory Five, herself included, were the only real friends of Princess Seraphel. Even to her subjects, she was an aloof, distant personage. Heroic at times, beloved or pitied, the Cursed Princess.
But today, she had admirers in this weary group, at least. She would have probably trekked a hundred more miles in the jungle than abandoned Dakelos, broken leg or not.
Menrise would have done the same.
…Probably. She didn’t know sometimes. She wondered whether that damned man and the ship crewed by the [Innkeeper] were responsible for more good Terandrian ships going down, or if they’d saved people.
