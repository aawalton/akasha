Under the chief's orders, the whole village sprang into action, searching for the frost caterpillars. He had informed everyone that the caterpillars would eat the frost indoors, so it was crucial to gather as many of them as possible to keep warm and firewood indoors in a burnable condition.

Mom was sent home to get Dad, since he was the one who could actually do fire magic. She would have died from nervousness while she waited if it weren't for Granny Ethel.

"It's alright. You can do it, Callie," the elderly woman soothed.

"B-But what if somebody discovers that I have… magic…" Callie asked. She feared that the villagers might tell dragonbloods about her abilities, and then… Then she would never see her family again.

"Don't worry. I'm sure the chief will do everything in his power to prevent that from happening. Trust him. He's always thinking of what is best for everyone in the village, does he not?" Granny Ethel said.

That was true. As stern as the chief was, he always knew what to do in a crisis. Callie feared imagining what would have happened in the winter two years ago if they didn't have their chief. He made sure that everyone survived that tough winter, when initially it had seemed that many would starve.

Callie nodded. In this crisis, too, she had to trust the chief. Trust that he would be able to lead them down the right path. Most importantly, that everyone would survive. Callie would do her best to help, even if she was still scared.

After some time, Dad came to the chief house covered to head to toe with snow. He rested briefly in the chief's house, and then, he, Callie, and the chief were off. She was surprised to see that Chief Aemon would be going with them, but apparently, he would be doing his best to keep Callie's power a secret.

She wasn't sure exactly how the chief intended to do that, but she figured out that with him around, the chances of her revealing her secret to others would be small.

The first house they went to was Gerda's family's house. It was the second largest in the village, and Silas had taken a semi-permanent residence in their guest room. When they entered the house, it was cold, and everyone inside was dressed in coats.

The merchant was busily running around, distributing oil lamps, kindling, flint, and steel as well as warm clothes. It wasn't for free, but from what Callie understood, he was distributing them at a rather generous discount.

"My, my. Little Callie, what are you doing here in such cold?" The merchant asked when he noticed her coming into the house.

"Callie discovered a way to get rid of the frost, so she'll be going from house to house to make sure that everyone is treating the frost caterpillars right," Chief Aemon said.

"Frost caterpillars? Those little bugs? I never would have imagined that they could have such a use," Silas's eyes widened in surprise, then leaned closer to Callie. "How did you even discover that?"

"I just saw them eating the frost in our coop, that's it," Callie said nervously. For some reason, she worried that Silas would somehow be able to figure out that the only reason she discovered that was that they were arcane beasts, not regular bugs.

"Nature is most fascinating, isn't it? We discover something new every day," Silas smiled and returned to his work, as there were more people outside requesting his goods.

Meanwhile, Callie, Dad, and the chief hurried to the main fireplace. The fire had gone out completely, and a light layer of frost covered the firewood.

"Oh, you've brought Rowan! Thank you, Chief!" Gerda's mom sighed with relief, while Gerda clutched to her mother's side, likely trying to remain warm by sticking close to her.

"I make no promises, but I'll do my best," Dad said.

"Has the frost reached any other place in the house?" The chief.

"Yeah, it got into my room! It's so cold in there, I can't sleep!" Gerda complained.

"Could you please show me? We'll leave Rowan to his task. It'll be easier for him to concentrate without an audience," Chief Aemon said, urging Gerda and her mom out of the main room.

So this is what the chief intended to do. With nobody else in the room, Callie could do her magic undisturbed.

"Go ahead. Do try to hurry, though," Dad whispered.

Callie nodded and walked up to the fireplace. Her magic was still roiling about in waves, coming in strong at some times and retreating within her at others. She really, really hated this feeling. Most likely, it was reacting to the mana in the storm, but Callie wished it could settle down for only a minute.

Breathing deeply and slowly, Callie once again imagined the warmth of fire. Safe and warm and contained. It was difficult to keep the steady image, with her emotions being tugged constantly by the storm itself. More than once, she felt like her magic would burst out uncontrollably.

But she waited for the right moment, for the exact time when the magic was neither trying to push its way out, nor was it too deep inside of her. She released her magic, and the frost disappeared from the firewood, and it caught fire.

The small flames staggered weakly at first, looking like they would go out at any moment. But at that moment, Dad extended his hand with a decisive, sharp motion, producing a steady stream of fire. It joined Callie's smaller fire, and soon the fireplace was crackling, the cold in the room retreating.

Callie and Dad shared a look and smiled at each other. They had managed to do it together.

A few minutes later, the chief and Gerda's mother came back into the room. Their eyes lit up with joy the moment they saw the fire going.

"R-Rowan, you actually did it!" Gerda's mother said in disbelief.

"Finally!" Gerda cheered and hurried to the fireplace, stretching out her hands and keeping them close to the fire.

"I don't know how to thank you," Gerda's mother said, then turned to Callie. "Both you, really. The caterpillars really do eat the frost. I mean… It was kind of obvious when you think about it, they always have, but I never thought of actually bringing them indoors."

"I'm just happy that everyone is alright, Auntie," Callie said.

"Excellent. We shall be moving onto the next house then. I hope you don't mind if your house acts like a shop for a little while," the chief said.

"Of course not! Silas has helped us a lot, too. I'm not so heartless as to have him do business outside in this weather. If anyone needs something from him, just send them here," Gerda's mother confirmed.

With that being said, Callie, Dad, and the chief headed to the next house. As soon as they managed to get past the bustle of people who were trying to get to the merchant. The poor man looked to be buried in the crowd, distributing goods left and right.

At that moment, Callie felt like she had heavily misjudged Silas. Because he was outside, she had thought that he was the one who stole the glauxis's egg. But he helped Callen follow his dream, told them many stories of the outside world, and was now helping the villagers in their time of need. Surely a person like that couldn't be bad.

In any case, it wasn't the time to think about the thief anyway. She had to worry and light a fire in everyone's home.

The day was difficult and long. The chief always said that Callie was there to make sure that the frost caterpillars were treated properly. She did check that, but afterwards, the chief would always distract the inhabitants of the house for Callie to have time to light the fire along with Dad.

With each fire she lit, Callie felt more exhausted. It wasn't that the magic energy within her was draining. In fact, she felt like she was constantly full of it. But it became increasingly more difficult for her to control it.

After lighting the fire in the fourth house, Callie shook her head at the chief, indicating that she couldn't do it anymore. Not without burning the whole house down at least. Uncontrollable fire, she felt like she couldn't manage very well.

With the swell of the storm, she actually felt like she could easily burn a whole house down. That's how overwhelming the power was. It was terrifying, actually. She didn't want to cause such a horrible accident when the village was already suffering.

Dad crouched down next to Callie. "Are you alright, Callie?"

"I… I just don't think I can control it anymore. It feels like the power will just… explode out," Callie said.

The chief, having listened, nodded. "If you believe you can't control it anymore, we'll stop. I'm going to move the families without the fire into the houses that have the fire going for the night. We should be fine. Thank you very much, Calluna! Our village owes you a great debt."

Callie couldn't believe her eyes. Chief Aemon bowed before her. Was she dreaming? The big, powerful chief was actually bowing. For her, out of all people.

"Oh… No! I r-really didn't do a-anything special," Callie stammered out in panic.

"Don't be modest. This is something only you could have done. Thanks to you, we might all just make it out of this crisis," the chief said, then turned to Dad. "Take her home for a good, long rest."

"You got it, Chief," Dad said and picked Callie up.

She didn't even protest to be carried. She was too tired, and they had to get home through the blizzard. Despite that, at some point on the way, Callie had fallen asleep in her father's arms.

The next few days, the blizzard continued, and Callie continued helping villagers as much as she could. The little, hungry caterpillars were doing quite a lot of the work on their own, devouring the frost. Some houses had managed to get the fire going on their own, all thanks to the tireless efforts of the little bugs.

Frost moths were already well-liked among the villagers. Their cocoons provided them with precious material that could be sold, but now the villagers loved them even more, calling them the saviors of Greyfen.

Callie even heard one of the villagers mutter, "I don't care if they are arcane beasts or whatever. I like these little guys!"

Initially, there was fear within her. The villagers had figured out that the frost caterpillars were, in fact, arcane beasts. Callie always feared that it was a matter of time, since their unusual nature was rather obvious once you looked at them more closely.

But instead of fearing the little bugs, the villagers felt grateful towards them. Arcane beasts or not, they had saved many people from freezing to death, and even the villagers, most of whom were hunters, could recognize that.

Once the mana snowstorm finally passed, five days later, the frost caterpillars had eaten so much that many of them were hurrying outside to spin their cocoon. The villagers helped them.

With Callie in the lead, they found the coziest places for frost caterpillars to pupate. High enough so ground animals couldn't reach them, and hidden enough so they wouldn't be snatched by birds. Some people even suggested throwing a celebration to honor the frost caterpillars.

"Grow up big and strong," Callie said to the frost caterpillars that she placed on a branch of a spruce tree. It should provide ample hiding space.

Thank you!

Callie was surprised to hear the caterpillar's voice. They had always talked only about food. Yet this one was thanking her.

At that moment, Callie felt something on her arm. Oh no… She had hoped that because caterpillars were so simplistic in their way of thinking, they wouldn't have such feelings of gratitude towards Callie as Snowglow and the glauxis had.

Even Mom and Dad had dropped their guard, since nothing had happened despite her handling so many of the frost caterpillars.

But Callie could clearly feel how her magic was once again growing stronger, and it was threatening to break out. Another petal was undoubtedly growing on her mark.
