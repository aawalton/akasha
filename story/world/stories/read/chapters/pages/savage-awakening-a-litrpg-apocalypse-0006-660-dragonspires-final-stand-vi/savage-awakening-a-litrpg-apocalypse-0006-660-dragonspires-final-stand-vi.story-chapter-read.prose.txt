The Sage crushed the elixir's flask and grinned.

Those Laws swirling around him grew denser; but even if Zane couldn't quite place them instantly, he had a feeling he knew what they were.

They were some of the rarest Laws in Dragonspire.

Malzareth's eyes narrowed as it considered them.

"I was thinking about what you said earlier," said the Sage. "No one here's worthy of your respect, 'cept the lad—hells, but you'll regret touching him, by the way—and this ol' bag of bones, back when I was in my prime."

The Laws thickened as the Sage spoke. "As I see it it's not so simple. Might be there's things I can't do physically at my age—with the things I've put my body through. But I'd daresay my spear's the strongest it's ever been! And I've learned a thing or two it takes a little time to learn."

The Laws were time Laws. But at a higher tier than the Time Laws the King Astrolith had wielded. Time Laws concentrated by Reina's best Grand Alchemists in absolute secrecy, for a singular purpose.

"You know—I'd say I'm at peace," the Sage said affably. "Might be my body was different in another time, but I didn't miss it."

Zane saw time-runes settle onto the Sage's physique, settling down from the air; they shone a brilliant bright blue, each of them. A brilliance that started to fade the moment they landed.

But their effect was immediate.

"Then... well," chuckled the Sage. "This clever lady came to me with an idea. Expensive as hells, and just on the edge of the possible... but hells, if it wasn't a chance I couldn't refuse!"

And the Sage started to change.

His scars melted away, revealing nothing but incredibly dense, corded muscles beneath—muscles that swelled, brightening the Astral Plane as they did—his hair streamed out behind him, as wild as it ever was, but in thick locks—locks that went from white to black.

The Sage kind of looked like Zane, physique-wise, by the time the rune-Laws finished their work. He was taller and leaner but still fleshed with dense muscles.

In the Astral Plane his physique was made of cords of pure shining silver... he could only imagine the Creation-count the young Sage had put in. It had to be a nearly maxed Fifth Titanform.

The Universe around the Sage was a sphere of pure nothing. And it might've been the most striking aspect of all.

Zane was almost certain he was glimpsing what a thousand shards of Destruction felt like.

Like some locked, scarred-away portion of the Sage's reserves was finally unlocked.

The Sage stood there, and his mere existence exerted a faint pressure on this world, even in this locked, high-quality space...

The Sage's eyes were the Sage, Zane noted. His grin was the same too—wild and utterly carefree—even if his transformation was limited to these few moments, to the time until those Time-runes lost their color, the Sage seemed in no hurry.

He seemed like a man with utter confidence regarding what he was about to do. Like it was utterly impossible to him that he wouldn't do it, even as he faced down the creature that downed him once before...

In that moment the Sage's aura felt incredibly sharp, knife-like.

Malzareth hadn't said a word.

Even as the Sage's transformation was completing, Reina's avatar was casting buffs—Zane recognized Sanctified Physique and one other he didn't know, settling over the Sage's chest—and then the Sage's aura surged until it hit some kind of wall... some upper limit beyond which he could no longer be boosted.

"Right," said the Sage, still grinning. "I'd say it's high time we took care of a little unfinished business, you and I..."

He clenched his fist around his spear.

"This f'king spear's ages in the making!"

The remainder of Dragonspire's elites contributed their Supremes at the same time, but all eyes were on the Sage as he made his move.

The Sage simply attacked.

Zane found the Sage's Supreme quite striking in that moment. It felt like the pure defiance he'd always known from the Sage, the sheer feeling that the Sage would exude in the Astral Plane. But at the same time there was an incredible energy to it, almost a vigor, an undeniable life that the Sage must've had in his youth... then there was the weightiness he'd felt in the Sage's spear lately, the sense that it just couldn't be stopped—that old-man's strength—

He realized, startled, that this might just be the one chance the Sage ever got to realize this.

The best of his Supremes. Young and old in one...

Zane saw Malzareth's eyes. Just took a long, hard look at them and was surprised again.

It brought to mind that one anecdote the Sage had said—that rather than face the Sage that first time they met, Malzareth had chosen to stab him in the back.

Twenty Greater Endbringer-strength Monsters tried getting in the Sage's path, but the others' Supremes mowed them down, and the Sage carved through the rest like they were nothing.

His spear shone silver with his Supreme.

A Supreme that was still gathering strength as he carved through those Endbringers—a spear that carved through each of their eyes and throats, the most vulnerable of their weak points, a single slash dispatching each of them—an utterly ruthless spear.

The Sage's spear erupted with silver again, like it hit a second level. Then again, as it hit the next—

The Primes stood in the Sage's path now, and the Sage let out a battle cry.

There was little other way to describe what he did to them. Two strikes for one of the Primes, three for the other, these pristine, utterly domineering slashes, all but beheading one and slicing through the skull of the other.

It was the most effortless Zane had ever seen anyone dispatch a Prime. It also explained the look in Malzareth's eyes.

It was impossible to know Malzareth's thinking for certain.

But just glancing at it, Zane knew.

This Sage put the fear of god in it.

The dragon let out a screech; another Prime pounced from its universe, and this Prime looked like the Mirror Dragon, just a dim, dark mirror—easily the strongest Prime it'd summoned; Malzareth granted it its own dark Blessing—

The Sage struck it even as it was coming into existence, and he had it in pieces in the space of two seconds.

Then he stood in front of Malzareth, spear in fist, and there was nothing in between them anymore.

Half Malzareth's own spears had been dispatched in an effort to catch him, and the Sage had blitzed out of the way with purely mechanical speed—simply sheer physique, laughing as he did.

The Sage's spear hit a fifth—a sixth 'peak'—that brilliant outburst of silver every time it seemed to hit a new threshold—

Then Malzareth's eyes flared crimson.

It wasn't a Supreme. Instead it drew on another of its weapons... everything around the Sage, including the Sage itself, seemed to slow.

At first Zane thought it was another kind of time Law, but it wasn't that, exactly. The reality around the Sage simply seemed incredibly dense all of a sudden, difficult to move through.

Then Malzareth let out a glass-shattering cry.

All its nearest summons winked out; their shards all clustered into its remaining few spears, instantly.

Its soul went blinding too. It had to be some desperate weapon. Something it pulled out since it knew its life hung in the balance.

Zane saw it imbue those few remaining spears—six spears—with as much Destruction as they could carry; drawing deep of its soul to do so—pushing those spears, already thick with Destruction from the summons, over the top...

Those spears struck at once.

They were the only things that seemed to move quickly in that moment. They moved almost instantaneously.

Six lines of Destruction—white, covering six spears of Creation—so thick they were almost shards condensed to matter—carved through the space.

Carved through the Barbarian Sage's body.

One sliced right through his still-beating heart.

An ultimate, life-saving attack from a half-step Overgod.

And then, there was a moment. A singular moment where everything seemed to ground to a halt, and the faces of all those elites below were reflected pale in the Destruction-light...

That was that.

That should've been that.

Except they all saw the Sage's expression.

They saw the look in the Barbarian Sage's eyes, and all those who knew the Sage knew—

The Sage was not accepting this for an answer.

In that moment, they all knew exactly what would happen, even as it did.

The Sage's Supreme hit one final peak. A silver supernova in the Astral Plane.

With the Fate of the Galaxy on the line, the Sage went for the throat.

And the single most devastating attack that had ever graced Dragonspire landed on Malzareth's body.

In retrospect, Malzareth's mistake was clear from the start—from the moment it spoke of its Fate Signs and its destiny...

Did the Barbarian Sage seem like a man who gave a single damn about what Fate wanted?

***

There was a single scream—a soul pierced all the way through.

Space rent from the sheer force of the blow, despite its locked status.

The spatial cage nearly succumbed too.

Then there was a flash of pure Destruction, lancing at the cage—a wild, wanton expulsion, and the cage shattered.

There was a shattering of the void.

Malzareth had vanished. And in its wake there were thick rivers of golden blood.

An avatar of Reina's instantly dashed for the Barbarian Sage, faster than anyone else could react; the body fell and THUDDED into an asteroid.

At the same time her true body touched her speaking charm, focused on a very different direction. At the shattered man still crushed under a pile of 20+ Iron Gold.

"Zane!" she called.

But before she could say more, she heard the sound—they all did.

THUMP.

THUMP—THUMP—

And another, an instant later, from beneath that stack of some of Malzareth's strongest—a howl, a sound of pure agony. But it was a sound Zane would never make from a physical wound.

And then—

His aura went ballistic.

His aura was a geyser in the Astral Plane. An absolute geyser, skyrocketing seemingly infinitely, a wellspring of essence and life that kept on giving—

His aura hit a peak.

And in that moment, Zane’s eyes went silver.
