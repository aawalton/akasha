The mental response of the serpentine entity was much like a sleepy old reptile waking up from slumber. There was a feeling of a streeeetch, and then his voice boomed, “What’s this about, then? A summoning you say?”

Samantha felt like a candle she was holding had been snuffed out. She struggled to control herself from answering angrily. Deep breaths, deep breaths. “There’s an apocalyptic battle going on, Levi? You know, the struggle of everyone else against the Sage? And you offered to assist at the right time, despite limited energy?”

A great serpent blinked a few times. “Oh. That.” He seemed to fully rise up at this point, beginning to loom over reality. The light behind his eyes flared into new awareness; a steam kettle began to cook. “I can’t miss this, not my paramount moment, nor can I fail in a promise made.”

His serpentine eyes suddenly flashed, and she felt her own third eye flash in response as energy poured out through it, seeking information, grasping, wriggling its way for purchase in reality.

“Show me the time, show me the place, oh exalted one, oh goddess, oh summoner! Point the way, open yourself as the conduit, and unleash me!”

Samantha, feeling a kettle beginning to boil over within her, swiftly flew over the frontlines and pointed at the enemy. Somehow, someway, Dax had gotten the signal and was staging a retreat, namely by a sudden network of force walls spawned in the way, keeping the enemy from pursuing temporarily.

The goddess declared ominously, “Our enemy, from there to the walls of the greatest of heathens — destroy them, wash them away, throw them into the abyss!”

A low rumbling began as Samantha felt the boil begin in her blood, and especially in her gut, where power flowed from afar and condensed. Soon, she realized it was the laughter of a dragon, of the leviathan.

The laughter became more obvious as it echoed through dimensional spaces into the imminent reality. Gleeful, baleful laughter bounced off the ground and the sky and the walls as the great serpent, surely among the greatest and most ancient of dragons, seemed to relish what was to come. “Hahahahaha! So I will… with pleasure.”

The power and pressure in her belly became uncontainable, and her mouth opened to explosively spew forth a great torrent of water. Rather than chaotically spraying, however, it formed into an undulating great snake flowing out and away to the target spot.

In mere moments, a small river was contorting itself into shape, as the water spout finally ceased abruptly and Samantha discernibly sagged in her flight, gasping and sputtering.

Whoa. That was intense…

Samantha re-cloaked herself and moved elsewhere, otherwise using mirrors to observe what was to follow.

The floating river slowly began to take on definition, forming the semblance of scales and a draconic head. As this occurred, the enemy was not idle; all of the tower building up and unleashing a massive series of entropic detonations directly into the watery buildup. The Counter Crew, as well as Samantha herself, countered what they could, but the sheer concentration was too much from so many towers.

Nonetheless, when the smoke cleared, the water had simply shifted shape from the damage. Steam rose into the air, carrying away the blackness with it as it evaporated.

The serpent made of water did not stop forming, nor did it stop growing all the while. Levi’s distinctive head became evident, and his eyes, glowing and steaming balefully, became the most evident thing of all.

The colossal dragon scowled down at the army before him, and even still, kept on laughing, now with mocking derision. His voice finally boomed out across the battlefield. “You think your insignificant little weapons can harm me, who has prepared for this moment for aeons beyond your understanding?!”

As he spoke, the volleys of magic blasting him didn’t stop, detonating inside him and merely displacing matter that constantly regenerated and replenished, like an endless font. “Do you not know, do you not see the structure of the walls I built against you? Can you fathom the pain, the agony, to unmake flesh for such a thing as this? All. For. You.” He snarled with flagrant hatred. “You would need years to tear down what I built up! This, the mountain of cairnstones stacked to the heavens, names smudged out; this, the last testament to all the corpses made of my kind in your name.”

Samantha called off wasting energy defending Levi — all of it was immaterial to him and a desperate waste on the enemy’s part. It was like when she had tried pushing against the giant demon-thing. It had been present for a specific purpose, only, and there was no denying it, only enduring it.

The shoe was on the other foot in that respect, today.

Levi scoffed, as steam rose in torrents from his nostrils, and he continued to grow, and grow, and grow. “Know this, sages: everything that begins becomes a circle spiraling to an end…” With a flinch, with a flick of his chin, a massive wall of water began rising rapidly from the ground, stretching itself up to him, widening and widening into an impossibly held-up tidal wave that cast a shadow over the enemy army. It formed a semi-circle that largely represented the entire southern front the alliance army was carving out.

Men who’d before been stubborn and staunch against even overwhelming force were now breaking and trying to flee, pushing in a concentrated mass. Once more, though, they had nowhere to go.

“I am yours.” Like a giant ribbon of green-blue lightning, Levi dove into the water and a ripple shot through it.

It fell. An apocalyptic tidal wave crashed down on the enemy.

The first hit shook the ground like an earthquake significant enough to make alliance soldiers lose their footing. Samantha felt it even while flying above their heads, as the air itself was not spared the vibration. Thousands of enemy soldiers surely died on impact, and the waves rolled on from that point, killing scores and scores with every passing second.

This is not just water. It’s infused and charged with pneuma, lending further gravity in the spirit…

Towers fared no better against the unstoppable tidal wave, smashed and blown over, their protections unable to weather the sheer force.

Down, down, down, the numbers of the destroyed fortifications ticked.

Efforts against the rolling waves at their most intense and massive beginnings were completely futile, like tossing pebbles and firecrackers into an ocean. The waves invariably swept away everything in their path. In moments, perhaps a hundred thousand were swallowed, and a hundred towers with them.

Yeah… good call, Dax. Can’t beat this kind of AoE with anything. They never expected water to come for them here.

A mass flight ensued from wizards, taking to the air on a thousand mass or individual spells, before the oncoming waves took them. The alliance forces did what they could to disrupt, but there was great difficulty due to the range restrictions past a certain point.

Fortunately, Levi’s attack was not limited, likely because this whole wave was him, not just an attack. Samantha’s sharp senses caught his darting, swimming motions beneath the waves, pushing it, accelerating it, even as he appeared to be gobbling up soldiers along the way. Samantha couldn’t be sure, but she thought he was likely identifying those caught within who were otherwise surviving the waves — champions and so forth.

Now that’s what I call a thorough trouncing.

Halfway again to the great walls of the city, hundreds of thousands engulfed and the equivalent in towers toppled, the efforts of the enemy against depleting and ‘lower-lying’ waves began to see some returns. Force magic came like an endless series of battering rams, and fire magic caused explosions of steam, all from collectives of wizards and the towers pooling and organizing their efforts. Meanwhile, the water further back and curling toward the flanks was ankle deep, draining itself to the south immediately around the alliance’s lines.

Dax ordered movement to capture the space. Though it was muddy, their whole army had footing and terrain-crossing buffs, so they navigated far better than they should have.

The soldiers were somewhat amazed to see mostly bare, ripped up, and muddy earth, the bodies left behind only scattered here and there.

Samantha understood, though. Avenging Leviathan had swallowed them. The reference to ‘casting to an abyss’ was not entirely abstract.

While this went on, Samantha arranged for a swift aerial resurgence, as the enemy towers would be far too busy blocking the now slow-rolling waves in the hopes they wouldn’t reach the walls. The flanks were less covered by Levi and could be hit up during the onslaught. They needed to distribute rather than concentrate their forces, just in case, but why not take advantage, after all?

To reinforce them, Samantha decided to splurge a bit on mana, summoning her Firebirds via multiple castings. The spell had no caveats about ‘maximums’ beyond the limits of thirteen per spell, so it seemed wise to leverage a rather broken technicality Ozra left for her. She dumped almost a thousand MP from temp item rotations provided by the army, thereby summoning over a hundred firebreathing little menaces. The hawk-like entities were sent far and wide like mini-dragons to support, one volley of them after the other.

Levi’s waves began losing momentum, but he’d halved the battlefield in one fell swoop, giving them what amounted to a badly-needed shortcut. Soldiers tramped across the hollow space left in the wake, dragging their feet through standing water and mud, as flyers and shock troops concentrated on the flanks, gouging out new firing positions for the spellcasters.

They were certainly making progress, and they had more cards up their sleeves, to say the least.
