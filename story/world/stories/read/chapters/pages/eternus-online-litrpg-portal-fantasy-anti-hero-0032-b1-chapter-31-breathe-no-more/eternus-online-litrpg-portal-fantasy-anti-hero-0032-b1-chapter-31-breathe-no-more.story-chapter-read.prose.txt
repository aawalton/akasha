Romulus took a steadying breath when Bonnie led them to the access for the compound, and felt Lightsbane's pulse of enthusiasm in his hand. The blade knew they were destined for the death of Hyperion’s faithful, and there was a kind of inarguable joy emanating from the sword at the prospect.

His eyes peered through the bars of the wrought-iron gate itself toward the interior courtyard of the large compound, and he saw limited activity within. The lights were on, casting a wash of illumination across the ‘square’ that was more than sufficient for his enhanced vision to work with. He could see cooks, cleaners, and servants walking around within—though none appeared to be paying attention to the entrance.

Several buildings framed the square: what appeared to be two housing areas to the right, a large communal hall to the left, with an attached bathhouse of some description. The chapel lay at the furthest part of the square opposite the gate, with its own living quarters building attached.

More lifesigns pulsed at the far edge of his vision, within the Chapel and its attached quarters, and he even noted what appeared to be two bodies entwined in a bed within one of the domiciles. A slight blush of embarrassment filled his hidden cheeks, but he chose to avoid thinking about the implications of sex in Eternus while returning his gaze to the courtyard.

“Eight in immediate reach,” he said to Kate softly. “Just the unarmed servants, easy to subdue. We could probably apprehend them nonlethally and—”

“Do not be naive, Romulus,” Mortarius cut in with a voice of cold steel and lifted a hand to tap his ghostly right forefinger against the wrought iron. “This is Hyperion’s Paladin. None of the people serving here are ‘just servants’. Each of them will be blessed by the Radiant Lord’s power. They are not just enemies of convenience, they are your foil. If you permit them to, they will bring magic and arms to bear that can end even your existence—runeblade and all.”

“They look unarmed to me,” Romulus muttered back.

“And for any other Chosen of the Pantheon, they would be fine to consider as such. You are the Revenant-King, and a Sanguine to boot. Their magic will ignite your body and boil your blood. Do not make the mistake of believing these seemingly humble people to be weak, Romulus. It will be your final death.”

“He’s right, Lulu,” Kate whispered at his other side. “It’s just like when we used to raid the Tower of Kuratan in Corpsebreeze Passage. Remember the ballroom? All the seemingly innocuous attendants that turned out to be brutal enemies? It’s like a mini-dungeon in there, and those are the trash mobs.”

“Bonnie, can these people use magic?” he asked the guardswoman quietly instead of replying to his companions.

“They do all be offerin’ healing, milord. Not right sure about nothing else.”

“The risk outweighs your reticence, Romulus,” Mortarius said steadily. “You know the answer.”

“Carpe diem, quam minimum credula postero,” Kate quoted to him with a slight smile.

Seize the day, put very little trust in tomorrow.

Romulus let out a quiet sigh and felt his hemoforce ignite in answer to his pull on its power. “Very well,” he said simply. “No quarter.”

Mortarius dipped his head in approval, and Kate drew an arrow to nock.

Romulus glanced at his redheaded companion and then looked back toward Bonnie. “Go make us our distraction, Bonnie.”

The guardswoman blinked, and then dipped her head and hurried away with a quick “Yes, milord” and no more. Romulus watched her go and then turned to Kate, his eyes glancing at the top of the wall. “We can jump over,” he said to her after a moment’s thought. “I can toss you if needed. It’s about six feet up. I’m pretty sure I can clear that easily.”

“As fun as being tossed by you would be, Lulu, I’ll just climb,” Kate said with a slight grin. “Nightstalkers have the shadowstride ability. I can walk on any surface that is sufficiently darkened. It just costs a lot of mana to sustain.”

“Whatever works,” he agreed and edged backward to remain out of line of sight while they waited for Bonnie.

One minute turned into five, five into ten, and ten into twenty while they waited—and Romulus very nearly decided to go before the woman could give them whatever diversion she’d promised, until he heard the sudden sound of bells. His gaze snapped toward the main gate of the city, and he smiled grimly at the echoing alarm. The cacophony and chaos would be a fine cover, and it would probably have every available guard rushing toward it.

What it signified, he had no idea, but it was undoubtedly going to draw attention.

“Ready?” he asked Kate.

“Ready,” she confirmed with a steely look in her jade eyes.

Romulus nodded to her, raised his gaze, bent his legs, and poured his Strength and Agility into his movement, launching himself up and over the wall in a flare of his cloak to land within the courtyard of the religious compound. His impact was a heavy, metallic THUD that no amount of stealth could conceal, and Lightsbane’s naked blade pulsed with its cardinal runes as he appeared over the wall.

The first person to notice him was a girl in serving attire, her sandy hair tied up in a bun, and her blue eyes wide with shock. Confusion and trepidation crossed her features, and she eyed him uncertainly while approaching.

“Sir? Is everything ok—?”

Romulus sliced with Lightsbane before she could finish, and forced himself to watch the woman’s eyes widen in shock as the runeblade bisected her from waist to shoulder. His gaze lingered only long enough to see the life vanish from her eyes before he turned away and surged toward the other members of the compound already gathering.

A scream broke the night, and Romulus felt himself fall into a cold, predatory trance.

Seven targets.

Three people tried to run, with one making it two steps before an arrow wrapped in black flame punched through his neck and out of his throat to impale itself into the spine of the woman in front of him.

Romulus paid the death no heed, other than to count down two possible targets, and charged toward a group of four nearest to him. An elderly man at the forefront attempted to generate a spark of some radiant magic as he closed. Still, Romulus didn’t give the old man time: slamming his right foot down to arrest his motion, sliding forward, and pivoting off the same foot to duck and rise in a spinning upward slash that cut the old man apart diagonally from waist to neck.

The three women with him screamed in horror at the scene, and Romulus launched himself toward them without abatement. The clearly elder of the three women he caught with his left hand, dragging her backward and sinking in his fangs to rip out her throat. The taste of fear filled him, and he felt something dark and savage roar to life in his chest as he left her to die.

“Stop! Please! Why are you doing this?! Who are y—?!”

The pleas of the second of the three women were cut short when Romulus swiped up his hand and sent a Blood Lance punching through her diaphragm, while the third woman, now openly weeping, was run down and impaled by Lightsbane. The living weapon punched through her spine and out of her solar plexus, and Romulus twisted the blade inside of her and tore it out sideways, sending gore and viscera erupting across the freshly-swept stone.

Lightsbane vibrated with pure ecstasy in his grip, and Romulus turned.

His [Blood Hunter] vision told him more people were hiding within the communal hall—not to mention the couple that were still entwined in their bed. His left hand rose to summon the blood staining the floor in an ever-spreading tide, and it answered his call as he strode through the carnage. A double-helix of vitae forged itself into being around him, and Romulus remembered something that Mortarius had told him.

His eyes locked in on the life signs within the communal hall, and he pulled the double-helix of blood toward him, compressing it into a crescent arc of condensed blood that he forged to rotate along its edges at ever-increasing speed. Along its forward edge, he added small serrations for greater effect, and then compressed it further after that, while stoking the speed of its rotation, until it became a liquid buzzsaw.

A glance at Kate told him she was out of the line of fire, and then Romulus lifted his hand and pointed at the building.

Like a bullet from a rifle, the arc of blood shot forward at unnatural speed, slicing clean through the woodwork of the building with splatters of crimson and then eviscerating everything within. Screams of terror and agony filtered from the building as it sliced through, and then he felt the blood leave his range of influence and go inert.

Romulus walked forward and into the ruined doors silently, kicking them out of the way and entering the building. Within lay a field of gore, with smashed glasses, clay pots, mugs, tables, and destroyed support struts. Bodies littered the floor, with the sound of weeping men and women rising above the groans of the building.

His footsteps were heavy, harsh thuds upon the woodwork as he strode within, and his [Blood Hunter] directed him to the remaining three lifesigns.

A young man, a young woman, and a boy barely out of his teens came into view.

They sheltered behind a stone counter, panting and weeping with terror.

None of them were seriously wounded, and they seemed to be shielding the boy between them with their arms. Romulus walked until he came to a stop before them, and then hesitated. The girl’s eyes were green as emeralds, just like Kate’s, and she had a very similar shade of auburn hair. His gaze met hers in surprise, and he looked her over in disconcerted silence.

“Why?” the young man asked, and pulled Romulus’ attention from the woman. “Why are you… Why are you doing this?”

“Because you are my enemy,” Romulus replied automatically, while [Indomitable] distantly kicked into play. “I am only doing what is necessary.”

“We don’t even know who you are!” the man spat back, his tear-stained features twisted in rage. “You’ve killed our p-parents, our friends, everyone! What the fuck kind of monster are you?!”

Romulus paused at the man’s desperate shout and set Lightsbane’s tip to the wooden floor, while kneeling to look him in the eyes. Any number of dramatic one-liners came to mind, but there was a sincerity to the desperation in the man’s eyes that brought him up short. Once again, his discussion with Kate returned to him, and the Descartian travails that had quietly haunted the back of his mind: Cogito, ergo sum.

His eyes seemed so damn real.

Romulus took a breath to answer, and then abruptly, his body caught fire.

A scream tore from his throat before he realized it was happening, and his hemoforce dropped by thirty percent as something blazing hot washed through his body. Romulus turned his gaze toward the trio, and particularly, to the teenage boy in the middle. His hands were together, and radiant shimmers of light were drifting from where he’d sent something out of his palms.

Romulus felt his limbs shaking, smoking, and spasming at the remembered pain of whatever it was the boy had done to him, his ears were filled with ringing pain, and he could feel burnt and destroyed flesh across the left side of his body and face. Pain and hatred overrode all else in a primal rush of predatory reactivity, and in place of his shock, a new emotion took root.

Pure, unfiltered rage.

The young man tried to lift his left hand toward Romulus, and in response, he slammed Lightsbane into his mouth, ramming the runeblade deep into the stone counter behind him, and then slashing outward to half-bisect his skull and destroy the counter in the process. The teenager and the woman were screaming, but Romulus paid them no heed. The boy’s hands started to shine again, and Romulus reacted instinctively: dropping Lightsbane to reach out and grip his wrists.

The young woman tried to pry his hand off the youngest of the three, and Romulus instinctively backhanded his left hand into her face, smashing her head and body against the floor with a dull thud that was only barely audible to his damaged hearing. His right hand squeezed the boy’s wrists, and he compressed and twisted until the teen screamed and his bones popped under Romulus’ hold.

Then, he released his hands and reached out to grab the adolescent by the throat.

Hatred, fury, and even self-loathing writhed within Romulus, and he stared at the boy.

“Why?” he asked in a voice that was a monstrous growl to his own ears.

“Y-you’re evil!” the teen said snottily, between sobs. “Hyperion says we have to fight all evil, e-even if it costs us our lives!”

Romulus stared at the boy for a long, silent moment, and then felt cold understanding settle on him like a cloak.

“Mortarius was right,” he growled softly. “I was careless.”

He didn’t bother expanding on his words, and instead, he crushed the boy’s throat like paper mache. The youth’s legs kicked feebly at the air, and Romulus threw his dying body to the side while turning to the woman who had tried to protect him. Lightsbane was retrieved, and he picked her up by the hair, throwing her against the wall and slamming his fangs into her neck.

She cried out when he did, but he could already feel her blood healing him, and closed his eyes against the bliss. Her legs kicked uselessly at his shins, and Romulus realized he’d been wrong: she could never have held a candle to Kate’s beauty.

These people were mindwashed zealots, and he had been an absolute idiot to let his remnant humanity convince him otherwise. The Dark demanded ferocity, might, and utter ruthlessness—it was a lesson hard-earned, and it had taken a boy with barely any magic to show him how truly vulnerable he was to his lingering mortal sentiments.

He would drain and butcher every last person in the compound.

Then, he would go after the Paladin, and put Lightsbane through his righteous skull.

Blood would flow, and he would dedicate it all to Lilith.
