Not…exactly, Kalanthia responds, then expands as I level her with a look. Ensuring that an Ascendent didn’t pursue me is why I was ultimately captured.

I had been wondering how that fit into the story. Though I still don’t see how the pieces work together.

“The Tier six wanted you to do something else in recompense?” I guess, a frown creasing my brow.

Yes. The emergency teleport can only work once before it needs recharging. The only place where it can be recharged is a special rift.

“And let me guess,” I conclude, realisation dawning. “That’s right in the heart of the Blood Fang clan’s territory.”

Not in the heart…but protected by them, yes.

“And so, what, they were trying to get information out of you? Find out who sent you?”

They would have liked that, but no. They wanted the teleport back. They were attempting to Tame me to force me to give it up.

She cuts herself off, looking at me warily. I grit my teeth, pushing back the surge of rage that someone would try to Tame one of my companions. And in such an awful way.

I remind myself that it clearly didn’t work – and that I want to find out more details which I can’t while I’m still battling with my own anger. Again, I’m briefly glad that the Blood Bastards will be following Kalanthia’s trail – I dearly hope this Tamer will be one of those who come so we can chat.

“I’m alright,” I tell her, “I’m in control. Just…didn’t they already realise you have a Bond to someone else?”

This Tamer attempted to use his equivalent of your Binding Skill, but was unable to see it. He assumed that his failure to succeed against me was because he had not broken my will enough.

I snort in disgust.

“Which he tried to do by torturing you.”

Yes.

Maybe I shouldn’t destroy that muzzle, I think vindictively. See how he feels about being so utterly at someone else’s mercy! Something occurs to me.

“So, OK, the Tamer couldn’t see the Bond, but why didn’t you tell him about it?”

Kalanthia hesitates.

I…did not want him to focus his attentions on convincing me to break it, she admits finally. And…when I came to understand just how vulnerable and weak I was under the enchanted muzzle, I hoped that– She cuts herself off, sending me a look even warier than before. I wait her out, sensing that she’s not done speaking, and just needs a little time.

Finally, I’m rewarded when she sighs and continues in tones of shameful confession. I hoped that you might find me there and free me.

All the anger inside me disappears as Kalanthia looks away from me. I understand why this is so difficult for her to say – she has great pride in being an apex predator, not needing anyone’s help. During the previous times I’ve helped her, we have been roughly equal. This is perhaps the first time she’s been in a situation where she was utterly powerless and unable to help herself in the slightest.

The closest other time to this was when she was poisoned by the samuran leader Tree-whisperer and her heart would have been destroyed without my help. But there, we were comrades in a battle, and I was the party healer. It’s not the same thing as needing to be rescued like this.

But perhaps learning that she can rely on us, on me, is a good thing.

I push myself to my feet and close the short distance between me and where Kalanthia’s lying. It almost breaks my heart to see her gaze at me almost fearfully – whether because traces of her recent torment remain or because she fears that I will use her vulnerability against her. But I don’t hesitate, instead stepping forward to start scratching behind her ears and down her jawline, a more thorough scratch than earlier in the conversation.

She is tense for a moment, and then relaxes, leaning against me heavily enough that I almost stagger, even with the twenty-five points I have in Power.

We share a peaceful moment for a time. I luxuriate in having the time just to stroke one of my companions – and one I haven’t seen for too long.

Unfortunately, my mind doesn’t allow me to be off duty for too long – even with the Tier four threats dealt with, there are still far too many things to do.

Kalanthia seems to sense it as my mind begins ticking over my to-do list – no shorter than ever before, but at least not all of it is as urgent as it has been in the last few days.

She pulls away.

Thank you, Markus Wolfe, she tells me whole-heartedly. I don’t know whether it’s for rescuing her, for the scratches, or for simply not taking advantage of her vulnerable belly when she showed it to me, but I don’t need to.

“You’re welcome,” I tell her simply. Then, curious about a question that I thought of during the pause, “You said they wanted the teleport back. Couldn’t they just take it off you?” Though, as I ask the question, I consider how I’ve never seen her wearing anything. That said, her coat is far fluffier than the average African leopard – if it is small, perhaps it could have hidden.

No, she replies, then flips her right paw over to reveal the pad. A moment later, something golden gleams upon it.

I lean forward automatically and see what looks like a fancy old-fashioned golden key, studded with jewels all down its spine. It looks gaudy as all heck, but otherwise doesn’t seem like anything special. However, Inspect Item makes it clear that it’s a treasure indeed.

Sanctuary Key (legendary)

This key, made by a Grandmaster with deep insights into stealth and spatial movement, allows any touching it to be untraceably teleported away to a safe place (definition determined by unconscious desires of users) as long as it is charged. Return activation varies between 50 – 70 years after initial activation, depending on Energy environment during the time away. For safety, this key may be held within the body of one of the users. While it is so, it is insubstantial and can only be removed if the user willingly summons it to physical form in their grasp. Note: if the key is insubstantial when it activates for the return journey, it will return all of the original users who still live. Upon arrival, the key will rematerialise in physical form, willed or not. If it is in physical form, all those who wish to be transported, must be touching it or one in contact with it at the time of activation.

Charges 0/1 (Outbound complete, inbound incomplete. Activation time: more than a local year.)

Enchantments: ?

I find it interesting that there’s only a question mark for enchantments – I guess that my Skill just isn’t high enough level to gain that information about a legendary item. But at least my level in the Skill is giving me plenty of other details, and the description pretty much answers all of my initial questions. And at least it has an activation time written in the description, even if it’s very inaccurate right now. That gives me hope that we’ll have some notice.

When I step back, Kalanthia makes the key disappear again, melting into her paw pad as if it had never existed. Weird.

“Couldn’t they have just waited for you to be teleported back which would cause the key to drop out of your skin anyway?”

I will not be in their grasp, then, Kalanthia reminds me. I will be returned to where I initially left – deep in the Ascendant’s den.

“Good point,” I admit. And, thinking about the description, there’s no indication that anyone would be able to go along with her with the key inside her body even if they were touching her. And the teleport is supposed to be ‘untraceable’.

I wonder what they were hoping to happen if they got it out of Kalanthia’s body, but a second look at the description gives me a good theory – it indicates that, if the key is in physical form, anyone who wishes to be transported must be touching it or in contact with it at the time it activates. Perhaps that means Kalanthia wouldn’t be transported if she weren’t touching it.

“I wonder what they intended to do when it activates on the inbound journey,” I wonder out loud. “Even if they managed to get it out of you, presumably the key itself will teleport regardless. Maybe they didn’t realise it would take them to the den of a Tier six.”

I did not inform them of that, Kalanthia replies with just a touch of blood-thirsty satisfaction. So most likely not.

I frown.

“Then why not give it to them and save yourself? It’s not like you need to teleport again, right?”

Kalanthia gives me a severe look.

And disappoint the Ascendant again? I do not wish to be hunted by him.

“Ah. Good point,” I admit. I’d overlooked that particular complication.

I step back as she pushes herself to her feet and stretches. I am hungry, Markus. I wish to hunt with my mate. I have given you the gist of what has happened in the time we were apart – and before we met. Can you save any further questions for later?

“Sure,” I agree. Then my eyes snag on the frayed ropes still around her back legs. “Actually, wait a moment?” She gives me an impatient glance.

For what?

I raise an eyebrow, gesturing at the bindings.

“Well, unless you want to keep wearing those very fetching anklets.”

She follows my gesture, then snarls angrily.

I had forgotten. I cannot even feel them anymore, she admits. Yes. Remove them.

“I would have done so before, but Tilathis’ threat meant the muzzle took precedence, and then there was no more time,” I explain, though her only response is an impatient rumble.

Using Inspect Item on the ropes comes back with no result – apparently they’re not classed as an item. Perhaps because they’re broken. However, my other range of skills – and Skill – for inspection indicate that they’re a type of woven plant fibre, filled with stable magic and reinforced with, what I guess, is some type of potion that sets the magic and increases the item’s durability.

It’s a fascinating exploration and I decide not to use fire to weaken them enough to break them. Even though I could protect Kalanthia from being burned, I’m certain that the enchanters and alchemists would salivate to get their hands on these – as far as I know, it’s not a technique that they are familiar with. Though maybe the tanner would be – I recall that he used different alchemical mixtures to set my magic when making my armour.

There are no knots – the fibres where the two pieces join seem to have been woven together somehow, and the weaving is just as protected as any of it. But, with Flesh-Shaping at my command, there’s more than one way to skin a cat – or remove the bindings from one.

Kalanthia isn’t happy with the plan of temporarily removing parts of her paw to allow the bindings to slip off, but when I demonstrate how quickly and painlessly I’m able to do it, she becomes a little less tense.

It isn’t long before Kalanthia’s finally free of any bindings and I have two looped around my wrist – I won’t put them in my Inventory just in case that messes something up.

“Alright, happy hunting,” I tell her. She barely spares me a backwards glance as she begins running into the forest, but I don’t hold it against her. I feel the fierce joy that pulses down the Bond at being free once more – an apex predator in her natural environment.

I watch until she disappears, but even then don’t stand up immediately.

Though I’m grateful that I’ve got Kalanthia back and we don’t have to fend off a powerful Tier four just after killing the last one, her return has certainly added new complications.

Elves. Humans. A whole civilisation with clans and guilds and who knows what else just on the other side of the mountains. And it’s unlikely they’ll just be willing to ignore us.

Perhaps if we make an example of the Blood Fang bastards it will offer some sort of deterrent. The thought makes me swallow, because a message strong enough to be a deterrent will require the kind of merciless slaughter that doesn’t come naturally to me.

Yet if it will save more of the people under my protection, how can I put my own peace of mind ahead of them? I find myself praying that everyone who chases Kalanthia was complicit in her capture and torture – it would certainly make it easier to justify strong measures to myself if I know from the outset what kind of people they are. But I need to prepare mentally for if that isn’t the case.

Loran, how are things? I ask my faithful manservant and friend, hoping to distract myself from my thoughts. Is there anything I need to deal with right now?

It takes a moment for him to respond – I hope that he isn’t in the middle of something.

There are many things that need your guidance, but none that are immediately urgent, my lord.

Loran…

Markus, I meant, he sighs.

What do I need to deal with? Do the healers need help? I question.

The last I heard, the healers were finally getting on top of things. Though they didn’t die automatically, the parasites lost a good deal of their power with the death of their controller. Their stealth isn’t as comprehensive, and they don’t tend to self-destruct anymore. I guess that they are now considered progeny of the Tier four, rather than extensions of its will. And, like any being, have an instinctive desire to survive. That’s good because it means that even the other healers are able to detect and remove the parasites now without the kind of risk they took before we killed the Tire four.

Not immediately, though Healer Thessalanor has requested you to demonstrate healing the scarring left by the parasites – they’re still struggling with that.

Alright. Is that everything? I ask hopefully, while strongly suspecting the reverse.

Sure enough….

That’s the most urgent matter, though there’s still the question of food. The recent bounty has helped everyone significantly, but eventually even this will run out, and the hunters are concerned that there may be limited prey for a while until the forest replenishes with beasts moving in from surrounding areas.

I nod unconsciously, then glance over at the farm area. I see Elarion and a bunch of people I recognise as farmers and Plant Mages. They’re bustling about busily, doing something, though what it is, I can’t tell.

I know, but Elarion seems to think that he has some ideas to try. Hopefully at least one of them will come to fruition soon. Anyway, we have the sea to draw on again and Nessie should be helping the fishermen to find and catch the shoals of fish.

That’s good to hear, Loran replies with some relief. I’ll make sure Sarran knows.

I know we also need to have the defences repaired, though I’m hoping to get Tilathis to help with that – if only by finding the reagents. After all, he’s the one who broke them. If he isn’t willing, I’ll bring out the big guns – Kalanthia and Lathani. I doubt he’ll be able to say no to them.

That would be helpful. Would you like me to organise the enchanters?

Not yet, I decide after a moment of thought. We don’t know when the nundas will be back.

As you wish. Loran hesitates a bit.

What?

I know you don’t really like meetings…but I really think we should have one with all the leaders of the settlement. I had one earlier with them that I can brief you on, but I think that they need to speak to you.

They weren’t rude to you or something, were they? I ask, sitting up. Because if so….

Not at all, Loran reassures me. I just think that they would feel more reassured to speak to the lord of the settlement. They want to know where the settlement is going, and I didn’t feel like I could speak on your behalf.

I lean back against the rock again. Where the settlement is going. Now that’s a question. We’ve been so busy just trying to keep our heads above water that I’ve barely spared a thought for the direction in which to paddle.

OK, I reply, trying not to let my sigh come through the link. Set a meeting. Perhaps for after dinner? That way we’re not interrupting anyone’s daily work. You and Sarran can brief me during dinner on where we are currently.

As you wish, my…Markus, Loran agrees. Is there anything else for now?

Not for now. I’ll see you later.

Even once I feel his attention shift away from the Bond, I continue staring sightlessly at the sky.

Because Loran is right. Now the most urgent matters have been dealt with – or are heading towards that way – and our population has actually settled into a permanent home – this is the time to start setting longer-term objectives in motion. Education. Empowerment. Innovation. Creating a settlement of which to be proud.

Set in that context, this new threat of the Blood Fang elven clan seems almost irrelevant. Something to prepare for, sure, but that’s all.

The heart of the settlement, the core of who we are…that’s what we need to concentrate on now.
