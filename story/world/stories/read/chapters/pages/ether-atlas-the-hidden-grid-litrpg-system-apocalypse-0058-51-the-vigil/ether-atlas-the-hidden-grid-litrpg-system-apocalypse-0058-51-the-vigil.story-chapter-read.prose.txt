Chapter 51

"The candidate must allocate before contact. The vessel must be tuned before the load is applied. The system rewards preparation and punishes its absence with a consistency the operator should consider personal." - Codex of the Long Watch, Candidate Preparation Protocol

They climbed back to the plateau in silence, and nobody spoke further until they were within a hundred metres of Azzopardi's perimeter.

"Three weeks," Steve said, quietly. He was looking at the ridge ahead. "He's been operating that for three weeks. Forty fighters, a healer, territorial control across the southern coast. That's not improvisation. That's an organisation he had before Day Zero, scaled up."

"Smuggler," Donna said, flat. "Gulf of Sidra to Sicily, probably. He didn't build that network in a fortnight."

"Customs went down. The infrastructure stayed."

"The infrastructure was always the operation. Customs were an inconvenience."

Arthur let them talk and kept walking. He was running the negotiation backward in his head, looking for the part where he should have said something different. He couldn't find it. The deal was the deal that could be made with the cards on the table. He just hadn't liked the table.

Azzopardi was where they'd left him. Same camp table, same perimeter sweep, same expression when he looked up. He took the news of the agreement with a brief working of the jaw and offered no opinion on it. He gave them the corner tent at the eastern edge of the camp, a pouch of dried mutton, and a polite nod.

The tent was small with three sleeping rolls already laid out, courtesy of Azzopardi's quartermaster. It also had a folding table and a lamp with the wick trimmed for economy.

Donna closed the tent flap.

"We need to talk about allocation."

Arthur sat on the edge of his roll. The shield came off his back, and his shoulder told him about it.

"Inside the camp?" Arthur asked.

"Inside the camp."

"The temple's a ten-minute walk," Steve offered. "We could allocate there."

"On an open plateau, in line of sight from the watchtower and the Baron's patrol track." Donna pulled the pry bar from her belt and laid it across her knees. "While we lock up one at a time for thirty seconds each. That's better, is it?"

Steve considered. "I'm not committed to that suggestion."

"Mela," Donna said dryly.

"Six points each," Arthur said.

"Yes," Donna said.

"My last threshold cost me a minute." She rolled the pry bar between her palms once, tested the balance. "DEX recalibration at twenty-five. I went from wanting to move to actually moving, and there was a gap in the middle where my brain and my body were arguing about which one had authority. A minute is manageable in a controlled environment.”

"Mine was three minutes," Steve said. "ACU 25. The room came in louder before it came in clearer. I couldn't speak for the first ninety seconds because my own voice was distracting me." He glanced at the lamp. "I am aware that 'I went non-verbal for ninety seconds' sounds like a reward to Donna."

Arthur gave him a warm smile.

"The temple," he said. "Probably means a guardian. Whatever's in there is going to be operating at a Sanctum Zone density we haven't faced before. It’s a sound choice.”

Arthur looked at the tent flap. The flap faced inward, away from the watchtower and the camp's central fire. From outside, the canvas would show three silhouettes by lamplight and nothing more readable than that.

"Two on watch inside the tent," Donna said. "Weapons within reach but undrawn. I don’t want weapon silhouettes. The tent flap stays closed. We don't need Azzopardi's people seeing a man lock up for a minute and wondering."

Archie's geometry brightened in the corner of the tent. He had not been asked, but Tier 3 Archie had opinions on operational logic.

"Allocation in a defensible position with two combatants on overwatch is consistent with risk management protocols observed across multiple engagements." He paused for a moment and then continued. "Allocation in front of a node guardian is not a protocol that has been observed, because the candidates who attempted it did not return to be studied."

"Helpful," Donna said.

"I am calibrating the risk asymmetry."

"Arthur first," Donna said. "Your STR threshold is the largest unknown. If you go down, we need it to be ready for it. Then I will, and Steve last."

"Agreed."

* * *

Six points unallocated. Three from the Level 10 unlock back in Marseille, three from Level 11 on the plateau.

Steve was already cross-legged opposite him, guidebook open to the allocation tracking pages. "Donna's threshold was Dexterity. Mine was Acuity. Both at twenty-five. Both restructured the relevant architecture." He uncapped a pen with his teeth. "I want to observe yours."

"Glad my nervous system is scientifically valuable."

"It is. Very."

Donna had moved to the seam of the tent flap, her axe across her thighs. She pushed the focus rod within Steve’s reach.

“Steve, you better drop the pen and pick up the rod, if someone comes through there,” she said, motioning at the flap.

Arthur went with Constitution first. Five points. The monsters were getting deadlier and his body needed to tank hits. He was the shield of the party and he would make sure he stood his ground.

Each restructuring rolled through Arthur in a thirty-second wave: connective tissue tightening, bone matrix densifying, the thermal flush of a body adjusting to new tolerances. By CON 23 his body was a different proposition. This was the biggest allotment in Constitution he had made and he could feel his body humming. He pressed his fingers against his chest and his ribs felt like steel.

He gave a quick smile. "I was going to say this beats going to the gym, but then I realised I had to put myself in mortal danger for these points, and the gym feels like the better alternative."

Then came the big one. Arthur took a breath to steady himself.

"Don’t dally," Steve said, pen ready.

“Lie down. I fell on my face during mine,” Donna said without moving her eyes from the flap.

Arthur did and focused. STR 25.

The restructuring started the same as always. Then the warmth became heat and then the heat locked. His body became an inferno. His face a rictus mask.

The right arm seized. Then his left. Then his legs, his core, his jaw. Every muscle fibre fired at once and held. He couldn't breathe. His skeleton felt like it was being measured by something that needed the numbers exact.

Blue-white light traced his Nadi Matrix through his skin. It pulsed once and started the journey again before pulsing a second time. On the third pulse his muscle fibres reorganised. Growing denser. Stronger.

Force vectors realigned along paths his skeleton hadn't supported before, load distribution rewriting itself from the ground up. His body had been using the architecture it was born with. Now it was using the one the system had designed.

The lock released and he gasped.

ATTRIBUTE THRESHOLD: STR 25

NEURAL SYNCHRONISATION ACHIEVED

He sat there for a moment, breathing. Then he held up his right hand. He sat looking at it for a time, clenching and unclenching his fist. Then he threw his fist forward and it made a snapping sound.

"It's unified," he said. "Before, I was strong because individual muscles were strong. Now they're one system. The force doesn't start in the arm or the shoulder." He closed his fist again. "It starts everywhere and arrives at the hand."

He picked up the lump hammer. It was the same tool, but the wielder could now force a lot more force through it.

"Donna said the lag disappeared," Steve said, writing fast. "Between wanting to move and moving. You're describing the same principle for force generation. At twenty-five, the Matrix stops enhancing individual components and integrates them." He looked up. "Three for three. Every attribute does this at twenty-five."

He was already flipping to a fresh page. "If I'd had a control group and proper instrumentation, this would be publishable. Assuming any journal survived. Assuming anyone would believe a paper titled…"

"You're doing it again, Steve," Donna said from the tent flap. She had not turned.

"Zatar had a similar experience," Archie chimed in. "Strength and Constitution focus. His threshold restructuring lasted approximately ninety seconds longer than yours. I attribute this to skeletal density differences between your respective species."

"Was Zatar able to finish? Find out what’s coming?" Arthur asked.

"He was consumed by a subterranean predator on day eight hundred and ninety-seven. His shield positioning was excellent right up until the ground opened beneath him." Archie's geometry rotated once. "I mention this as context for environmental awareness."

"Reassuring," Donna said.

Donna allocated next. Four Dexterity, one in Strength and one in Constitution. The threshold was behind her. After the DEX points landed, her eyes changed. She tracked a moth circling the lamp and her head moved with a smoothness that didn't look voluntary. DEX 29. The neural synchronisation deepening with every point past the threshold.

Arthur held the tent flap closed. Steve held the focus rod ready.

Steve put two into Acuity, two into Resonance, and two in Constitution. His restructuring involved sounds at the edge of hearing. His sensory pathways recalibrating. When it finished, he sat very still for a moment with his eyes closed.

"What?" Donna asked.

"The lamp wick," Steve said. "It's burning at three different temperatures across its length. I can hear them separately. The hot patch sounds different from the cold edges."

"Many environmental signals will be available to you that were previously below your detection threshold. You may experience this as either insight or insomnia. The distinction is largely a matter of attitude," Archie said.

“It does have the added advantage of finer control of Ether manipulation.”

* * *

The bindings went last. They had three beetle cores from Marseille, small and dull and walnut-sized. Low-grade, but low-grade was still beyond any Earth metals.

They had debated using the larger cores but had decided to wait till they could find better bases to mould the ether-saturated steel plates they had found in Marseille.

Arthur pressed the first against the lump hammer and focused.

His intent was simple. Dense and heavy, weight in the head. Pound stuff.

The core dissolved into the steel over fifteen seconds, its surface rippling. When it settled, the head had darkened and the grain of the metal had tightened. The balance point shifted forward. In his newly integrated grip, the hammer sat in his hand as if forged for it.

Steve bound the second to his stab vest. His intent was - protect the torso, maintain casting flexibility. The core dissolved into the fabric and the weave restructured, tightening across the chest and back while staying loose at the shoulders and elbows. The material developed a subtle grain that caught the lamplight.

The third went to Donna's pry bar. Arthur held the core against the steel and focused. This was to be Donna’s backup crushing weapon.

The intent was impact and crush, not unlike his hammer. The dissolution was quick. The bar's curved head straightened a little and grew thicker. Nothing major but the bulk of the weight now sat at the top. Arthur tapped it against the tent's centre pole and the ring was deep. Denser than steel had a right to be.

He tossed it to her. She caught it one-handed, giving it a twirl to test the weight.

"Works," she said.

* * *

Outside, the camp had gone quiet. Inside the tent the three of them sat with bodies that were slightly different from the ones they'd worn when they went inside.

"How long until first light?" Arthur asked.

"Six hours," Steve said. He had been watching his own hand under the lamp, opening and closing the fingers as if trying to catch the moment a tendon moved. "Maybe a little under."

"Let’s sleep," Arthur said. "Single watch. Steve first, then me, then Donna. Four hours sleep each."

"Wake earlier if anything moves outside this tent," Donna said, settling down.

Arthur laid the hammer along the edge of his sleeping roll and the shield against the centre pole and let the new weight of his ribs press into the canvas under him and closed his eyes.

Tomorrow they would walk into a temple that had waited five thousand years to be woken.
