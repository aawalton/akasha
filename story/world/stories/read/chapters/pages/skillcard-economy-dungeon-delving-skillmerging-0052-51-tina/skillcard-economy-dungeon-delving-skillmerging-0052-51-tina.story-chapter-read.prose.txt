“How?” I asked, stunned and confused. NPCs couldn’t get out of Rifts. If they were forced out, they disappeared and respawned back inside. So how? How was one here?

Toby moved to another cell. There was another NPC. Then the next one, and the next.

“Every one of these cells,” Toby said, “has an NPC.”

“All of their eyes are the same. Empty, devoid of all life,” I said. “Where’s the door? I have to go and check inside.”

There was a glass door with a number lock, but we didn’t know the password.

“Hey, Cerise,” I called her. “Can you hack this?”

—No. That area is on a closed server. I can’t access it remotely. Did you find anything?

“I—I will tell you later.”

This was really hard to believe. NPCs, in the real world.

“Use your skill to go inside.”

In my shock, I had forgotten about it until Toby reminded me. I channeled my Magika and focused on Shadowstride. I chose the chair’s shadow and hopped.

I was inside.

I looked towards the glass, but instead of Toby, I saw myself. The chair and desk had no dust. The floor was shining. I moved towards the bed, where a woman in a loose white shirt and pants sat.

Elara Horndale

Eldest daughter of Rihan Horndale, Count of Aklas. She is the first in line to the throne. Elara is afraid of those responsibilities, but still, she tries her hardest to become someone fit to rule.

Recorded in the Akashic System in the year 779.

Her eyes were similarly distant, devoid of any life.

“Elara,” I called her.

She kept staring straight ahead, at her reflection in the mirror.

“Elara. Elara.”

She did not budge and kept staring at her own reflection.

What have they done to her?

I hopped beside Toby. He had seen everything through the one-way glass and had a deep frown on his face.

“It’s like they are dead from the inside,” I said.

“Have you heard of any Rift which had a city named Aklas in it?” Toby asked.

“Can’t remember anything like that.”

Toby punched the glass hard. “Just what the fuck is this, and where is Tina?” He shouted the last part loud enough for it to echo throughout the hallway.

“Toby!”

Someone shouted from the far end of the hallway.

“Toby, is that you?”

Toby turned and ran towards the voice. I followed him.

“Tina! Tina, where are you?”

“Shit, Toby, it really is you.”

Running past many more cells, we reached the one with Tina. Her cell was the exact same as every other.

A woman leaned against the glass. Her short silver hair was dirty, as if she hadn’t bathed in a while. She was as tall as me, wearing denim shorts and a top with the left sleeve missing.

It was SalamanderEater, the RiftTuber whose videos I watched sometimes.

“Toby, is that really you?”

“Tina.”

Toby reached for her through the glass.

“Why are you here?" Tina said. "I don’t need your help to escape.”

“As blunt as ever. Well, we have some work to do and need you.”

“What work could be as important as learning how Life’s Last Hope was closed? Can you believe it Toby? A Rift was actually closed.” She said it excitedly.

“More important than learning how the Rift was closed? Hmm. Lemme think. Closing one yourself?”

Tina’s face fell open. She composed herself and spoke.

“Toby, are you standing right in front of me?”

“Yeah.”

“Move aside. I’m going to cut it open.”

Toby moved and stood beside me.

Tina’s hand glowed. It went from yellow to orange to red. She touched the glass, and it melted. She carved a full circle, then pushed it with her other hand. The circular piece of glass fell to the ground but did not break.

Tina stepped out of it.

Before she could say so much as a word, Toby rushed towards her and took her in an embrace.

“Jeez, Toby, stop clin—”

Toby didn’t let her finish her sentence and kissed her.

I thought it was Cerise being Cerise and not being worried about Tina, but now I see how it is.

A long moment passed. I averted my gaze, then looked back, expecting them to be done, but they kept going. I coughed, trying to get their attention. They didn’t stop. I coughed harder, and Tina pushed Toby away. She was dazed.

“Oh, ah, hmm. Fuck, who are you?” she squealed after seeing me.

“He is Clark,” Toby said, wiping his lips with the back of his hand. “He is the one who closed Life’s Last Hope.”

I was sure I saw a sparkle in Tina’s eyes. She covered the distance between us in a step, clasped my hands between hers, and bombarded me with questions.

“How did you do it? How did you know you could do it? Did you do it alone? Are you going to do it again?”

“A-ah-umm,” I stammered.

I didn’t know which question to answer first. And I shouldn’t say it, but she smelled. I don’t know how Toby was able to endure it for that long.

“Easy, Tina.”

Toby pulled her back, and I finally took a breath of relief.

“You can ask all the questions you like once we get out of here.”

Something exploded, and the whole building shook.

“What’s happening?” I shouted.

A couple of seconds later, the shaking stopped.

Then another explosion. Screaming. Cars beeping. Those noises shouldn’t have been able to reach this deep into the building, but somehow they did.

“Cerise, what is happening?” Toby shouted into his intercom.

—Monsters.

“What monsters?” I asked her.

—Rift monsters. It’s a dungeon break.
