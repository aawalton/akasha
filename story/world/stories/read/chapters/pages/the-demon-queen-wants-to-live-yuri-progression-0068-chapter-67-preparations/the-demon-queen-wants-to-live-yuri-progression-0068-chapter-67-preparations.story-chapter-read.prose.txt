The Athenaeum was the same as it had been the last time I visited it. And yet it was not the same, because this time Ash was with me.

Ash stopped three steps past its door and stared upwards. She looked at every balcony. I followed her gaze. A book lifted from a shelf two floors up and drifted down into a waiting hand. Ash's eyes followed it the whole way. "Wow," she said.

I had stood where she stood now. I had felt what she felt, though I had not admitted it then and would admit it less now. It was strange. The sight no longer inspired anything within me, but seeing Ash witness what I once had, in the same way I had, was not an unwelcome sight.

"Yes," I said. "It is rather impressive. By this world's standards."

Ash glanced at me. "As if you didn't do the same."

"Do what?"

"Didn't stare just like this. There was nothing like this before, Lys." She said it lightly, which robbed it of any sting at all.

She was wrong. I had not gawked. A Queen merely surveyed, with appropriate appreciation, the better works of lesser civilizations. The distinction was considerable. Perhaps I should have said so.

I had wanted to bring Ash here for some time. I had wanted to bring her here alone, which had proven more difficult than the task warranted. The girl and the boy had both made motions to follow when I announced where I was going. I had informed them, with great dignity, that the Hero and I required some time in solitude. It had not been a particularly good excuse. I had expected to be questioned on it.

Instead, Ren's eyes had gone very wide. Her face had reddened to the color of her hair, and then she had nodded so furiously it was a wonder her neck had not snapped. She seized Corin by the collar, and dragged him off down the corridor before he could so much as open his mouth.

I still did not understand it. Perhaps my excuse had been better than I had credited? Either way, we were now here. In less favorable circumstances than I would have liked, but regardless, we were here. I took Ash's arm and led her inside.

Madam Oris sat behind her desk near the entrance, exactly as she had the first time. There was a ledger open before her. She did not look up. From this distance I could see that she was reading, again. I was fairly certain it was the same ledger as last time. The Crest on her forearm caught the light. A black Sovereign mark that looked like a book. The curiosity tugged at me, but now wasn't the time.

I stopped before the desk and held out my hand. "You have something of mine."

She looked up. Her gaze moved over me without ever finding my horns. "Ah," she said. "You're that one." She set down her pen. "Why didn't you come back earlier? I said three days, didn't I?"

"That is not something you need to know. Where is my card?"

She muttered something and reached behind her desk. Madam Oris produced a small wooden card and slid it across the desk. It was rather similar to the ones these students used to find their classes. A slightly darker shade, but similar enough. "This card lets you take out five books," she said. "Bring them here and I'll register them. You can take out five, no more. You can take them for seven days. Other than that, don't do anything to a book you wouldn't want being done to you."

They were concise rules, and appropriate for a place such as this. I took the card and turned it in my fingers. I had walked out of this place with Zarvok's journal beneath my arm, with no card. I had simply walked, and no one had stopped me. "And if I do not bring them to you first?"

The woman snorted. "Then this whole place lights up like a thousand suns and you'll wish you had. So don't." She returned to her ledger. "You can't take anything that belongs here willy-nilly."

Anything that belongs here. I did not ask. I took Ash's arm again and led her deeper in.

I explained the Athenaeum to her as we walked, because she had not asked and because I wished to. I explained everything that Ren had explained to me on that day. How one had only to think of a thing and channel the smallest thread of Essence, and the place would understand. How the books came when called. How the staircases moved.

"You think of where you want to go before you step on," I said, as we reached one of the spiral stairs. "It does the rest."

I stepped on and Ash stepped with me. Nothing happened. The stair remained exactly where it was, and so did we. I glared at the offending contraption, then glared up at a balcony above us. The contraption moved. The lower steps held firm while the upper flight detached, swung through the open air, and reconnected to the floor above. We rose. Ash said nothing about the pause. She was wise for a Hero.

We found an empty alcove, nestled in the upper reaches of the Athenaeum. One where there were no students to gawk and point. There had been much of that on the way here.

"Is there anything in particular you would like?" I asked.

Ash looked at me as though I had said something in a language she did not speak. "Aren't you the one who's dueling?"

I glared at her. "Answer the question."

She looked around at the shelves. They were so high I was sure she could not read the ones on the uppermost shelves. Her jaw worked. "I wouldn't even know where to start, honestly," she said. "I haven't been in many places like this."

I stared at her. "You have not been to many places of…knowledge?"

"No." She rubbed the back of her neck. "I was always training and there wasn't a lot of time. And I didn't really see the point. The places I've been to weren't really like this." She looked at my face, and her hands came up at once. They were making a gesture that meant nothing to me. "That doesn't mean I don't like reading. Or that I wouldn't. I just didn't get to, before. It doesn't mean I can't start."

I held the disappointment for only a moment. How could one call it life, if one were not able to read and read freely? I could hardly call it such. No matter. Such deficiencies could be corrected now. I would make sure to correct them. "Very well," I said. "Then you will read what I read, when I first came here."

I took the wooden slab and channeled Essence and thought of the volume -the title of the general history I had read on my own first visit. I waited. A book drifted down from somewhere far above and settled into the air at arm's reach. I caught it and held it out to her.

Ash took it. It was an enormous thing, thick enough to be used as a weapon in its own right, and she eyed it the way one eyes a beast that has broken its cage. "This…is what you read?"

"It was the first of what I read."

Ash said nothing. She looked around, and sat at the nearest empty table. I joined her, and channeled more essence into the slab.

I groaned at the page in front of me. If I could have stared Ruin into existence by will alone, the book before me would not have remained on the table. It would have been ash, and I would have considered the world improved by its absence.

It was a collected account of those who had reached the Sigil, and the circumstances in which they had reached it. I had thought, reasonably, that such a thing would hold the clue to this Insight everyone spoke of and no one explained. I had thought wrong. Each account was more impenetrable than the last.

One man wrote that his Line had branched into a Sigil on the day he abandoned his family's farm and set out for a great city named Etheos, there to apprentice himself to something called a gunsmith. The very next account was a woman who wrote that hers had branched on the day she abandoned the life of a merchant and returned home to care for her ailing mother. It did not make sense. Those were two opposite choices and they had led to the same result. The author compiling these seemingly did not find this strange.

Many of these accounts made no sense at all. Some were not accounts but poetic verse, and the verse was worse than the accounts. I read one that claimed to concern the true nature of water. Presumably the one giving the account had a water-related mark.

Water knows no form but the one it is given, and so it is given every form, and keeps none. I sought to hold it. It held me instead. On the morning I stopped reaching, the line branched.

I snorted. Whoever had written this had spent more effort on the poetry than on saying anything useful. I turned the page.

The accounts that made some sense were no more useful for it. Many people trained for years, even decades, and had nothing to show for it. Then one day, a thing would happen and their marks would advance. One man went from a Line to a Sigil while he faced a beast he called a manticore, in the instant he was certain he would die and did not. I read that one twice.

I did not know what a manticore was. I doubted very much there was one to be found within these walls, or anywhere I might reach in two days. I looked over at Ash. She was reading.

This in itself was not remarkable. What was remarkable was the gaze with which she read. It was intense. Focused. That was strange, for all through my own reading, I had watched her from the edge of my sight. She had set the book down nearly a dozen times by now, and each time she had picked it back up minutes later. I did not understand. She was not driven by the same hunger I had. She was reading as if she did not find the task enjoyable at all, and yet she read anyway. "Why are you still reading that?" I asked.

She looked up. "Why not?"

I pointed at the book. "You do not find it interesting. I have been watching you fail to read it for an hour."

"It's good to know the history of where we are," she said.

I nodded. It was a lie, clearly. I might not have noticed it at all once but I knew the Hero well by now. "That is not your reason."

Ash looked away. She murmured something under her breath I did not hear. "Ash. I want to know."

She looked at me, and her cheeks were faintly red. "You said you read this," she said. "Didn't you?"

I did not understand the point of her question. "Yes?"

"Well." She turned the book a little in her hands. "I wanted to read something you read. That's all. How else am I supposed to explain it?"

I felt warm. There was, for once, nothing I wished to say. The book sat open in her hands. Ash looked back down at the page she had failed to read a dozen times and this time she did not drop it.

I let the silence be. It was a good silence. I let it be for some time. Almost none of ours needed filling anymore. I reached for more books. The ones on Insight were indecipherable, but the books on cycling and conditioning were not. Cycling or Breathing was the term for techniques used to absorb mana from the air and turn it into Essence. At higher stages it involved purifying said Essence.

Body Conditioning I had experienced firsthand, though the books had stated that the Conditioning needed to reach Sigil from Line was a small thing. That run in the desert had been a small thing, apparently, compared to what a Sigil had to do to reach Crest.

Then there was the book on Tenets, and this one I did not want to set down. I had come looking for information on Lena's power, and I had found it. The problem was that I had found far too much. I read about five lightning marks that all managed to reach a Tenet, and yet all five of them were different from each other. Some of them did not even seem related, to the point where I wondered if perhaps the writer of this account was a fool for putting them together.

One of them, the most common, was called the Lightning Armor. It wrapped close around the body, and offered protection from all attacks. It worked the same way as Ren's Tenet of flame did -she'd called it a 'Wreathe of Flame', with some embarrassment. It was the same Tenet my Ruin had not breached. The same type of Tenet Lena most likely had.

For a time, we both simply read. It was enjoyable.

It was Sol who broke the silence. The bond tugged, and then I reached for it without thinking, and found her awake. Awake, and -I sat up straighter- rather furious. It was the annoyance of a small creature who had been left somewhere beneath her station for longer than she had agreed to bear. "We should go," I said, and rose. "I doubt I will learn more here in any case."

Ash rose with me, gathering several of the books we had been reading. "Sol?"

How did she know? "She is displeased." I checked the bond again. "Considerably." I checked it a third time, in case the displeasure had worsened in the time it took to mention it. It had not, but a Queen is thorough.

Sol let out an outraged squawk the moment I lifted her from the beast hall, and did not stop for the length of three corridors. I suspected she only considered stopping at all because it was tiring her.

"You did not wait so long," I told her, adjusting her between my horns. "It was an afternoon, Solara. A single afternoon." She squawked again, this time with the full weight of her small body. "Yes. I apologize. It will not happen again." It would happen again. We both knew it.

"Is that bird ever nice?" Ren asked, staring between my horns.

"She is perfectly agreeable and 'nice', as you put it," I said. "She simply has high and exacting standards."

Ren made a face at this. It seemed she disagreed with my answer. Beside her, Corin walked with his hands folded and his eyes down, though he glanced up at Sol every few steps.

Ash had found the two with little effort. How she had known where they were, and how to find them, was a mystery. She had led me straight to their rooms, taking each of the Academy's many confusing turns with full confidence. By now, I had given up trying to understand this little trick. It had to be some kind of innate [Trait].

Ren led us to the door, which, like everything of value in this Academy, was at the end of a descending corridor.

The door was a great slab of black and across its whole face ran etchings in branching patterns. There were lines that split and split again, in every color I could name and several I could not. The air here felt heavy even with the door firmly closed.

"Still can't believe he's just letting you stay in here," Ren said. "Like, for three whole days. You're damn lucky, you know that? Do you have any idea what I'd have to do to-"

"It is fitting my station."

Ren's mouth closed and she rolled her eyes so completely that I half expected them to come loose. She pointed. "Pretty sure the Archon didn't mean you could bring the bird in too."

"The Archon would not dare refuse me." Sol chirped from atop my head. It was, unmistakably, agreement.

Ren sighed and put her shoulder to the door. I tasted Essence. The door swung open.

"Woah," Corin breathed. He stood at the mouth of the door. For a moment, his hand had risen halfway, as if to follow Ren in. He stayed where he was.

The Sigil resonance chamber was a vast white room, and it was lined with black pillars -pillar after pillar as far as my eyes could see, and likely farther still. The mana in it was so thick, breathing the air felt like drinking it. It was r even denser here than the time we had come from. Not unlike wading upstream into a molten river. I might have fallen down to my knees, had I not used Essence to keep my back straight and my stride even.

The Ember Core groaned in protest, but I ignored it. Ash managed to stay on her feet in the same way, with rather more grace than I suspected I had shown.

"How the hell," Ren said, staring at us both, "can you two just walk around like that?" Then she paused, and looked at us with her eyes squinted. "I forgot you both were monsters. That much Essence just to move, really? That's just bullshit."

"Don't think too hard about it," Ash said.

I crossed to a space between two of the nearest pillars. The mana here felt thickest of all. Sol was very still on my head. And through the bond, I could feel that she was…pleased. I knew she had standards.

I slowly reached for her and set her down. Sol started to waddle back and forth. She was handling this place with more ease than Ash and I had. How exactly she was managing this was a mystery.

"You will bring our meals at the required times," I said aloud to both, loud enough that Corin could hear from where he stood.

Ren's head whipped around. "What do you think we are, your servants?!"

"Hmm. Not yet. Perhaps if your service is adequate I will consider you both for the positions," I answered. "It is no small thing to be considered even my servant." Ren's mouth opened wide. No doubt she was preparing herself to say something base.

"Okay," Corin called back, head half-bowed.

"Don't encourage her!" Ren said.

I let them argue. Ash moved to one of the black pillars on my left with the tomes we had borrowed. She set them down at the pillar's base. She was staring straight ahead at the unending rows of pillars, and so she did not see Ren coming up behind her.

Ren sidled up beside her. She looked at me and pressed something into Ash's hands. She whispered something that did not carry. Which even I knew was odd for Ren.

Ash looked down at what had been put in her hands. It was a book. Or at least something that resembled a book. I could not make out the cover from where I stood, but I saw what the cover did to her. The color climbed from her collar to the tips of her ears in the span of a single breath. Her eyes went wide.

Ash said something back that I almost but not quite heard. Ren glanced around the chamber. Then she made a gesture with her hands. It was a gesture I had seen the girl make before. Her fingers laced together, then waggled.

I did not understand any of it. I understood only that there was a book, that it had turned my retainer the color of a Hamel sunset.

"What is that?" I stepped closer and asked.

Ash spun. She put her front to me, both hands behind her, the book pressed flat against her spine. "Nothing."

"It did not look like nothing."

"It's a book. It's just a book!"

"Then I would see it." I took another step.

"You can't." Her chin had come up. A stubborn gesture I had seen often on Heroes. I had rarely seen it on Ash. Her face was very red. "Not yet, Lys. Just- not yet."

I clicked my tongue and folded my arms. She had said the word 'yet', which implied that she would show me eventually, in time. But the way she had said the word made me suspect that time might never come. There were a great many ways a Demon Queen could make a Hero part with a book. I would do none of them. Ash was unwilling. And I would not force her, no matter how my curiosity burned. "Very well," I said. "I will not pry." I paused. "For now."

Ash did not relax until she had crossed to Sol's satchel and tucked the book away inside it, next to the food, and even then she kept herself between it and me. What, did she think I would do something as uncouth as go back on my word?

Ash walked towards me again. "So…this place." Her voice was tight. "I guess it's pretty much perfect for what we're planning." She did not meet my gaze.

I looked at Ren. The girl was back at the threshold of the chamber, and was presently pulling Corin's cheeks from both sides. Only the Gods could guess why.

Her Tenet of fire was a thing my Ruin alone could not pass. Just like Isabelle's power was something my Ruin had not breached. Ren had not known the particulars of Lena's Tenet, but I would assume hers was similar, while planning for if it was not.

I would deal with all of that, in time. With preparation I would make here. I would attempt to reach Sigil, to fight the girl on a more even field.

Besides, this gave me an excuse to answer a question that I had carried all day. "You want to see which of us would win," I said. "With our marks. Do you not?"

Ash finally met my gaze and she smiled. It was not a Hero's smile. She looked around the white room, at the pillars stretching away into forever.

"Yeah," she said. "Let's hope we leave this place in a better state than we left the last."
