The party's departure to Lysara met with delay when Aylin arrived at their inn with a royal missive. Apparently, Duke Ledkin had learned that the wayfarers responsible for discovering the initial demon incursion and halting their corespace ritual were staying in his city and wished to meet with them. Jade wasn't sure what he wanted to get from their group, Fenrin had visited the royal keep with news of the demons' plans while Jade and her friends were back on Earth, but supposed it wasn't unusual that he wanted to stay fully apprised of the situation. When dealing with the fate of the world, you didn't get second chances.

Heeding Aylin's advice, Jade did not attend the audience with the rest of the party. While her upgraded shapechange feature masked her true nature from most detection magic, the royal keep was the most fortified place in the island city. Jade's magic might be able to fool the standard detection implements used by city guards monitoring the city gates, but they weren't taking any chances with real defenses. While too many people knew Jade's true nature for it to remain a secret forever, this would be a poor time for her disguise to be uncovered. With the uptick in demon activity and general public fear, Jade was certain that the exemptions she'd received in Estelhelm would not be granted here. She wondered if it would be better to start avoiding cities altogether, just in case.

The rest of the party, minus Siora, who stayed behind with Jade to treat the latest batch of alchemy ingredients she'd gathered, took several hours to return. By the time they did it was too late in the evening to leave for Lysara, putting Jade in something of a bad mood.

"What did the duke want?" She asked, doing her best to hold in her frustration. "A play by play of everything we've done in the Labyrinth? That took forever."

"You're telling me." Luis took off his newly purchased formal jacket and threw it onto the couch before sprawling across it, face down. "I thought it would never end."

"Luis, get off that." Naomi said, giving him a disapproving look. "You just bought it."Luis shifted, pulling the jacket out from under his large frame and throwing it up to drape over the back of the couch without a word. Naomi rolled her eyes, retrieving and hanging it on the rack by the door.

"No, I'm with Luis." Tara said, tugging at the neckline of her long, blue and white dress. It was the first time Jade had seen her wear anything other than her combat suit or rugged adventuring gear and thought it was an excellent look for her. The tek'kalla, apparently, did not share her view. "We didn't tell them anything they didn't already know." She continued, clomping into the washroom. "I'm getting back into some real clothes."

"You need to take this seriously." Naomi told Luis. "There's a reason we went through so many training sessions in Washington. We're going to be treated as representatives of our entire planet. How we act now could color our ties to the people of the Labyrinth for years."

"I know." Luis groaned. "And I was polite and respectful to everybody. The duke, the chancellor, random nobleman number one, random nobleman number two…"

"Luis."

"I think they were making it up at the end. How many nobles can one city even have?"

"Luis!""I get it." He said, rolling onto his back and running a hand over his face. "But if one more person looks down on us because the world we come from doesn't have magic, they're going to find out what a strength score of thirty feels like."

"I don't think you internalized much of those diplomacy lessons." Jade said dryly.

"He was fine." Fenrin reassured her. "I've been around those types before. Some of the provocation was intentional. They wanted to gauge your reaction. When you didn't rise to the bait, they learned that you couldn't be so easily manipulated. You did well."

"See?" Luis grinned up at Jade. "I'm a natural.""Why were you meeting with so many people? I thought you just went to see the duke.""Everything has to be a ceremony with the elite." Fenrin said. "I've been dealing with it my whole life. It's part of the reason I liked spending time on expeditions with my father so much more than staying home. Merchants are much easier to deal with than nobles.""He did have a lot of questions about what happened in the corespaces we've been to. Both of them." Naomi said. "And it wasn't a waste of time. Afterwards, he renewed his pledge to provide top quality equipment to any Earth wayfarers passing through Illysport, as well as support from local wayfarer guilds."

"Well, that's something." Jade said, lowering herself into a chair. "With the deals the Ebon Eye will be providing our people, we should make sure as many of us come through here as we can…"

"People are worried, Jade." Naomi said, sitting across from her. "The people in charge. This isn't just about Earth anymore. They were talking about raising armies.""I'm glad they're taking it seriously. Too bad they can't take on the Cores for us."

Jade thought about the quest the Labyrinth had given them upon stopping the demon's first summoning event. There were more ritual sites out there, on the next floor. Right now, demon ritualists could be opening a door to whatever abyssal realm they came from, letting loose a tide of bloodthirsty monsters. For some reason, the memory of when she'd touched the splinter of death surfaced. It was during her fight with the vampire on their first journey to Estelhelm, transporting the relic they'd uncovered at the Lysara stronghold. Touching that sliver of cosmic energy had conjured up visions of war and doom that culminated in a sinister, looming presence, one that cast the land in shadow. Whoever or whatever that was, Jade was certain it was what lay in the center of the Labyrinth. This entire world was a seal, and it had been created to trap that… thing. She didn't understand why she was so convinced of this but knew in her gut she was right.

Jade blinked, realizing everyone was looking at her. She'd zoned out, missing part of the conversation.

"Sorry, what?"

"I said, we're still planning to leave for Lysara in the morning, right?" Naomi asked.

"If everyone is okay with it." Jade said, perking up. "I know it doesn't really help us make progress, but-""You don't have to sell us on anything." Luis said, finally sitting up properly. "You've done more than anyone else. It's time to help you."

"We're all on your side, Jade." Naomi said. "We want Alan to be okay, too."

Everyone nodded their agreement. Even Tara, who stuck her head back out of the washroom.

"And if Siora's potion doesn't work, I'll find a way to get your brother aboard the Intrepid. My clan has some of the best healers in the Labyrinth!"

"Are you suggesting my potion won't be effective, Tara?" Siora asked. The elf's tone was as sweet as ever, but something about the smile she turned on the tek'kalla made Jade shiver. "If you aren't satisfied with the quality of my elixirs, then maybe you'd rather equip yourself with a different supply."

"No! No, of course not." Tara backpedaled, her face turning a lighter shade of lavender. "Your potions are the best, Siora."

"I'm glad you like them." Siora said, turning back to her alchemy station. Jade had meant for it to be her desk, but the elf's beakers, pouches, and other implements had quickly taken it over.

"Rule one of adventuring, Tara. Always be nice to the healers." Luis said. "Didn't that academy you went to teach you that?"

"Luis, I don't think the Labyrinth runs on RPG tropes."

"No, he's right." Tara said, emerging from the washroom wearing a more casual outfit. "They did. I just never got good at it. After all, damage dealers are clearly the most important role on a team."

"I take it back." Jade sighed. "It really is the same."

Returning to Lysara felt surprisingly like a homecoming. After the chaotic dungeons, monster-filled swamps, and overcrowded cities, the woodland home of Siora's people was a welcome respite. Jade and her friends had spent weeks here training under the renowned Wayfarer Serrethis Rhysten before continuing their journey through the Labyrinth. Jade had no doubt that his training, as harsh and exhausting as it had been, was the only reason they'd survived their recent trials.

While the rest of her companions settled back into the guest home the elves provided for visitors, she and Siora went to visit the elderly sword master. The pair rode a gondola up through the treetops, ascending to the top of the cliff that split the village. The verdant hues of the forest surrounded them on all sides, so thick it nearly hid the ground from view. Only the cascading waterfall broke through the world of leaves and tree trunks, slashing through it like an azure blade as it crashed into the pool far below.

"I'd almost forgotten how beautiful it is, here." Jade said, leaning against the railing and watching the sunlight shimmer in the mist. "It's good to be back."

She expected Siora to agree, but the elf remained silent. She bore an uncharacteristically sad expression on her face, seeming to hardly register Jade's words.

"Hey, you okay?"

"Huh? Oh…" Siora shook her head, smiling apologetically. "Just lost in thought.""About what?"

Siora didn't respond for several moments. Finally, she sighed.

"I'm worried about the demons."

Now Jade understood. Earth had been threatened since the moment the Labyrinth smashed its way into their world. Siora's home, barring the unexpected stronghold manifestation, had been a safe haven for her people for who knew how many centuries. But soon, that might change.

"We're going to stop them, Siora." Jade said softly. "We aren't going to let them into our world. Either of them.

"When you say it like that, it's hard not to believe you." She smiled softly. "How do you do it? How are you so sure of everything?"

"You have to believe something to make it happen. I told you before that I was an athlete before all of this, remember?""Yes, you said something about big competitions you were in."

"I wasn't just in them; I won some of them. Faced off against some of the most skilled, dedicated women in the country. Honestly, all of us were at about the same level of skill, you don't make it to an arena like that if you aren't one of the best."

"Then what made the difference?"

"Belief." Jade said, simply. "That's what I think, anyway. To achieve something great you need more than just dedication and effort. You need those, too, but without a foundation they aren't enough on their own. Before you can truly master something, you need to believe you can do it. That’s what gives you the confidence to take the leaps no one else will. It's the same here. If we don't believe we can stop them, we'll never have the chance to. We'll make a half-assed try, then give up and tell ourselves we did everything we could. People are good at telling themselves lies like that. But when you have the confidence, the resolve to see things through to the end…" She gazed out across the treetops, verdant foliage extending to a distant horizon. "There's almost nothing you can't do."

"I think I understand you a bit better, now." Siora gave her a considering look. "Thank you. I feel a little better now."

"Sometimes, though, that confidence needs to be reined in a bit." Jade said, giving the elf a rueful smile. "That's a lesson I learned the hard way. So, I'm relying on you to tell me if I ever take things too far. Take too many risks."

"I think I understand. Even the sweetest nectar turns bitter when overdrawn."

"My people have a similar saying." Jade laughed. "Too much of a good thing… Anyway, we'll keep fighting, Siora. For your people, for mine, and for everyone else's."

"Isn't it tempting to sit back and let someone else take care of it?"

"Of course it is. But if everyone did that…" Jade shook her head. "That's not the real reason, though. I don't want to be powerless, Siora. I spent a couple of years like that, and I can't go back to it. I'd rather risk my life to change my fate than wait on Earth, wondering each day if it would be the last for my world."

They rode in silence for another minute. Then, just as the clifftop came into view and the gondola began to slow, Siora spoke up.

"I've made a decision." The elf began, her eyes alight with uncharacteristic determination. "Don't ask master Rhysten about an apprentice mark for me."

"Why not?" Jade asked, surprised. "Are you planning on staying here?"

If she kept travelling with them without any means of accelerating her experience gain, Siora would keep falling further behind. Travelling together was dangerous enough already for her.

"No." Siora said, glancing down at the unblemished skin of the back of her hand. She set her shoulders. "I'm going to visit the sanctuary tonight. To get my wayfarer mark."
