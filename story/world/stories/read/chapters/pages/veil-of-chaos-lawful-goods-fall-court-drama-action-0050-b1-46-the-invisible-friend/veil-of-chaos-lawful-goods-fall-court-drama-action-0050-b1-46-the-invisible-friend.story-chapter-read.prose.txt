The ice lake stretched behind Sophia, oversized and overwhelming, like a covered mirror, showing futures she couldn’t face. She moved away from its sparkling shine, the battle covered in thick powder infused with a billion diamonds…blanketing the blood and horror it had seen.

Still, tucked safely inside her [Pocket Dimension], Lily rested, frozen, preserved, and awaiting resurrection to live and love again. To enjoy a long, fulfilling life in a way Sophia couldn’t anymore. She had to live and love. Sophia had to protect that precious treasure.

Her many eyes tracked the slowly settling snow as Floor 7 mourned its fallen Champion. Soon, it would welcome a new terror to guard its frozen halls. But that was a problem she could deal with another time.

Floor 6. I have to reach Floor 6.

She didn’t see the frosted walls or gentle powder as she pressed forward. In her mind’s eye, she saw hope dangling on a string, like slow-spinning redemption. It roped her throbbing heart in, drowning out any dark murmurs seeping out of the walls, attempting to corrupt her.

Lily will see her family again… Lily will share a kiss again… Lily will live again!

Crawling across the ice, she’d never felt so isolated, yet so motivated. She…she was so flawed, but this dream was so mesmerizing and vindicating. She was certain now that she was vindicated in becoming this creature, because now she was seeing things Damon always said he saw in her… Things she could never see before.

This was her choice. Her free will. Her selfish need.

So she followed that dangling string of hope, slowly spinning her toward redemption.

That feeling lasted exactly as long as it took her to reach the pathway between floors, when the pain hit without warning.

“Ghhk—”

It wasn’t clean and not sharp. It stretched, like her muscle fibers being pulled past their tolerance or sinew separating from bone in slow, grinding increments. Something clawed deep inside her at something she hadn’t known existed until this moment.

“Ah. I think we’ve finally found our beat,” The voice returned, softer now. Gentler. “There it is. I should have warned you about that.”

Her form rippled, eyes swiveling in every direction, yet she wasn’t looking at her surroundings.

You again! Wincing, Sophia pressed forward. I’m not your puppet!

“Me. And I’m not pulling any strings, despite what you think. Hmm… Perhaps we started off on the wrong foot earlier.”

His pause almost felt apologetic.

“I shouldn’t have tried to command you. I’m fairly new to this bothersome existence, as well, you know. I made a mistake. I can admit that. You’re not the kind of woman who responds well to orders, are you? I can see that now.”

Get out of my head.

His voice carried no malice as she followed the tiny ice crystals her little fox had made, following the path of the adventurer party.

“Sophia, please, I’m not in your head. I’m…quite literally everything. It’s rather disorienting, actually. The stone beneath your… Well, I don’t think I can call them feet. Weight? No, that sounds pedantic. We’re such a mess. Aren’t we?”

She didn’t respond, focusing on breathing as needles dug into her lungs with every lunge forward. Sohpia didn’t stop, and neither did the chatty Core.

“I’m the air filling whatever passes for your lungs now, and I know they hurt. I truly don’t mean to hurt you… But it’s just my body. Certain bodily functions for…whatever I am, are currently outside of my control. You’re inside me, Sohpia—a part of me—not the other way around. That distinction matters, I think. For both of us.”

Another wave of stretching agony pulsed through her, and she nearly collapsed against the frozen wall, tongue pressing against the roof of her mouth.

What is this? What are you doing to me?

“You’re not listening,” he groaned, breathing into her mind as a sharp draft ran through the frozen halls. “I believe we are soul tethered. I’m trying to help you understand. It’s simply…my biology. You are my Raid Boss. My extension into floors that I cannot directly control. My, eh…enforcer? No, that sounds terrible.”

He paused as she blinked, trying to regain her bearings. Her body or soul was recovering to whatever he’d done, and rapidly.

“Bah. In any case, the point is, when you move away from my center—Floor 10—it is as if you are trying to pull your soul out of mine. That’s painful. I think, hmm… A comparison you’d understand would be…an umbilical cord, with options about distance?”

Gross…

“Hey, I’m trying to work with you here. Don’t bite my head off…not that I have a head. Or are you my head? So weird.”

You are weird, Sohpia internally grumbled, struggling up and shifting into an ice ball to roll down a hill and conserve energy. I don’t care what you say. Just leave me alone. I’m getting this girl to the surface, and you’re not going to stop me!

“Stop you? Who said anything about stopping you? I’m trying to clear your way.”

The ice ball she’d become stuttered mid-roll.

What?

“Uh, you’re smarter than that, Sophia… What else? The creatures between here and the surface. I’m keeping up with you. Keep up with me!”

She almost shivered at how many feathers that tone ruffled, yet she didn’t know how to respond to that. The disembodied chatter-box didn’t give her a chance to.

“Obviously, the monsters are mine, in a sense… Kind of a part of my body. I can…encourage them to give you space. Really, it’s rather pathetic, to be honest,” he grumbled. “It’s like a stern finger wagging, if anything. But I’m trying.”

I—what? Sophia momentarily paused, unraveling into her full size right in front of a terrified snow bunny, who couldn’t even move. She ignored it, though. Why would you do that?

“Uh, well, because I need you, and you seem like the kind of woman who is going to do what she wants regardless of what I want. That much is obvious. So, I’m just trying to figure things out now that we can finally talk—I mean, really talk. This is a first for me, too!”

A whistling sound vibrated through the frozen halls, and the bunny quivered before darting away. “Ugh… It’s so hard to make those sounds. Anyway, I’d rather you succeed without tearing yourself apart in the process, so could you take a little more care of yourself? You’re far too interesting to lose to stubbornness.”

She shrugged her frame, unfurling at the bottom of the hill as her massive form reformed with a wet crack of reshaping bone and sinew.

Sure, whatever. I still don’t trust you.

“You wouldn’t have a brain if you did. I’m a dungeon. My entire existence is built around consuming things.” He paused, and Sohpia shivered at that admission; it almost sounded contemplative.

“It wasn’t like I was always a dungeon, just like you weren’t always a Mimic. I’m also…curious. About you. About what you’re becoming. About why you care so much about a girl you’ve never spoken to. I’m curious about myself, even! We both woke up in alien bodies.”

She’s a child.

The words came out before Sophia could stop them as she transitioned from a frozen cave to a damp, black-stoned tunnel.

“Mmm. Is she, though? She’s sixteen. Hardly a child by this world’s standards. Culturally mature, as they say. Though your Earth memories are…extraordinarily confusing and contradictory.”

She’s a child! Do you have no regard for life? Sophia repeated before catching his slip. You’re reading my memories?!

The venom in her mental voice surprised even her. Something raw and bleeding had torn open inside her chest, and she couldn’t stuff it back down.

Silence. Long. Heavy. The kind that felt like being watched through a microscope.

“Ah,” the Core finally whispered, and his voice had changed. Softer. More careful. “We are all the same, Sophia. Living in all our ways and pain… I feel you crying from a place so much deeper than ‘she’s a child.’ Sophia, what you did…has nothing to do with this halfling girl.”

Shut up! Shut up! Shut up!

Her gut knotted, and she slammed her body against the wall. The fissures ran up the wall like frozen lightning, stone cracking under the force of her grief.

Silence answered her for several seconds as she trembled and snarled at the wall, not having anywhere else to take out her frustration. Yet, when he spoke again, his voice was impossibly gentle.

“I’m sorry. That was clumsy of me. I forget sometimes that humans need…time. I used to be an elf, you see. At least, I think I was… Part of me wonders sometimes. But all that matters is what we believe, right? We both have…different customs. Vastly different customs.”

She was shaking. All forty and a half thousand pounds of her, quaking like a leaf in a hurricane. Don’t you dare talk about my grief! Just because you can see my memories doesn’t mean you know me!

“No, you’re right… I’m not here to push you, Sophia. Whatever this is—whatever you did—it’s yours. Your pain. Your memory. I shouldn’t have reached for it without asking.”

Then why did you? No! No! She breathed out her jitters and refocused, dragging herself through the rough cave floor. Blood and tears were scattered across a rare dry area, where the halfling boy had awakened to harsh reality. Just…leave me alone.

A melancholy, almost teary voice brushed against her mind. “…I can’t do that, Sophia… Do you know what it is like being alone? I can’t return to silence.”

The words burrowed into her chest like fishhooks.

She wanted to ignore them. Wanted to push forward without acknowledging the ache they’d opened up. But she’d spent what felt like years in this dungeon with nothing but hunger and instinct and the fading memory of hazel eyes.

She knew silence. She knew what it was to scream and have nothing answer her.

Sophia continued her path, slower now.

…How long?

“Hmm?”

How long have you been alone?

It took a moment for him to speak again, his voice stripped of its theatrical warmth.

“I don’t know. Do dungeons grow, like humans? I think I was an elf… I felt isolated even then, I think. Then, once I woke up here…time moves strangely. Maybe decades? Sometimes a single hour can stretch into eternity for me as I watch everything inside me.”

There was a pause as she turned down another tunnel, feeling the emotions burning inside her simmering. It helped when he wasn’t talking about her.

“Adventurers talk to each other. They laugh, argue, and make promises they won’t keep. I hear all of it. Every whispered fear, every desperate prayer. But none of it is for me. I’m just…the horrific thing that must be purged. Those shrill, alarmist cries. I’m just…evil to be destroyed. But I bet you’ll just scoff and roll your eyes.”

Honestly, kind of, yeah… Sophia pressed on, letting the stress inside her ebb out in slow streams as the temperature rose with each new junction in her pursuit. Look, from my point of view, you’re just trying to manipulate me. But I’d rather you talk about yourself than me… So talk away, dungeon elf man. Talk away…

There was a blissful stillness for several seconds during her transition between Floors 6 and 7. In that blank space, the pain bloomed again, making her vision go white for a heartbeat, but it quickly passed. When awareness returned, she was in a different place.

Ahead, she smelled the fertile scent of life, took the place of where frozen isolation had been. Ancient trees with bark that glowed in soft bioluminescence, branches forming a canopy that filtered light into perpetual twilight, and what had to be a false clouded sky overhead.

“And so you arrive at my Twilight Woodland,” the voice mumbled, sounding somewhat dejected. “I won’t say I’m not hurt by your suspicion, but I understand the hesitation. It’s not like you’re a monster now, the same as me, but by all means, bury your head in the irony.”

Sophia’s many eyes tracked the twilight canopy, processing the accusation buried in his sulking. It was becoming hard to remain focused on her mission. Luckily, her little fox had left more ice crystals, but they appeared to be weakening—he was out of his element.

…That’s not the same thing. How the crap did they move so fast? I guess they know the path out, and the hard part was finding the path that leads to the lower floors…

“Isn’t it the same?” the Core countered, making her tongue twitch with agitation. “You killed plenty of other creatures here to survive, and would kill to protect your man without hesitation. Humans consumed animals and plants in your previous world to exist. We do the same here. You didn’t choose to be born a human then as much as a monster now. Much as I woke up as…this.”

Sure. Maybe that’s true, but it still doesn’t change the fact you ‘need’ to control me, she snorted, shaking off the uncomfortable vibes and the logic vine he was creating. You were the one who said I was like your hands… Well, who wants unruly hands?

A sound like leaves rustling in the wind rustled around her as she transformed into a paper plane to use the breeze to her advantage.

“Right, why contend or empathize with me when you can just flatten me into a manipulator. I’m the evil that must be purged. And you’re just…what? A victim? You chose to be born inside me. You cannot lie to me. I didn’t choose you. You chose me through Titania as a self-punishment. So who is the victim here?”

Sophia’s wings fluttered at the accusation, hanging with her in the bioluminescent air as she flowed around branches and vines. Monsters were everywhere, hidden and watching, yet, true to the Core’s earlier statement, they appeared to be afraid to act.

…As if you or I are victims. As if… I’m sorry, but we’re both monsters.

“Then why apologize?” His voice sharpened, then softened just as quickly. “Don’t apologize for being cautious. I’d rather you suspect me and survive than trust me and die, and I am left alone without a conscious soul to argue with. I’m just…tired, Sophia. Tired of being the monster in everyone’s story. Even yours. It’s why I’m helping you. You want that party to live? What you want, I want! So, monsters or not, let’s help them.”

Her eyes narrowed across her paper body, leaning into an air stream she wasn’t sure was deliberately provided or not. Below, she saw the frantic escape of the party, not even bothering to be subtle; the fact that they saw her, the literal dungeon raid boss, on the seventh floor probably told them nowhere was safe.

Great, we’re moving on from the manipulations to something more helpful. So, how far away are they?

“Cute…but not really. You are the most selfish little thing in—no, deep breaths! Anyway, if helping you will at least keep you talking to me, then why not? You lost a good chunk of time there transitioning floors. You may not have realized it, but even you, with all your structural advantages, need time to adapt.”

Perfect. Still didn’t answer me, she grumbled back.

“Okay, maybe I won’t help if that’s how you want to talk to me. Maybe I just stop twisting myself up inside to let them go on their safe way and see how long they last.”

Sophia growled out a low rumble as she unfolded mid-flight, landing on a giant branch near a flock of giant three-eyed birds that properly flipped out at her appearance. In a flurry of squawks, they abandoned their perch, flying into the canopy as her mouth curled back.

What is with your hot-and-cold attitude? Pick a frame.

“I’m sorry I don’t fit neatly into your little box—eh, haha, I didn’t mean for that to be so literal for a Mimic. I made my first joke! Progress, no?”

Sophia didn’t respond, causing another long sigh to whistle through the forest.

“Very well, I may be acting a tad childish, though you can hardly blame me. You are not the most cooperative first friend. All I ask for is a tad more respect. I’m on your side, after all. Or…I can be, if you’ll let me. Your fox buddy may be entering a rather vulnerable state soon.”

Her jaw worked around, long tongue sliding out to glaze her large teeth as she considered who she was dealing with. It wasn’t like she could trust this thing as much as she could trust herself when her Intelligence was barely out of the single digits—it was the monster of monsters. Then again, if she did want a smoother time, she may have to play ball.

Damon, help me… Okay, elf dude, I’m listening.

“Good!” The dark twilight parted, sending bright beams into the forest canopy. “Monsters typically are locked to their individual floors. It takes a very special creature inside my system that can break those rules…many of which I am bound to, as well. You happen to be one of those exceptions, though, as we’ve established, with your limits. Your little buddy, however…well, he’s attached to you now, not the floor. So…”

Like my soul stretches when getting further from you, the further he gets from me…the harder it is for him to survive? Shit.

“Best hurry, they’re nearing the transition point to the fifth floor. Because what matters to you matters to me. That’s what partnership means, isn’t it?”

She wanted to lurch forward, but those words caused something in her chest to seize, and words echoed from her distant past, words from another life in their cramped apartment, after she’d lost another job and was spiraling into self-hatred.

Damon’s words as he’d held her face in both hands, hazel eyes meeting hers with absolute certainty:

“Your problems are my problems, Soph. I know you worry a lot and maybe you cry too much, you’re too impatient… especially with yourself, by the way, but we’re a team. We’re partners. This is why I’m here, right? I’ve got you. I’ll listen, so let it out, and get out there again. My shoulder’s always open.”

Her heart breaking, she felt her golden blood running down her tongue as she released her bitten organ and let the jitters run out. We’ll…unpack this later, bud. Dammit! Just…stay out of my memories.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about. Are you thinking about him? My, the way your heart rate changed just now when I said those words. Did he say them?”

Bastard… Respect goes both ways. Leave my memories of Damon alone.

Returning to her paper plane form, she followed the breeze skyward until she was soaring over the seemingly endless canopy that showed on all sides. The Core wasn’t done prodding for more information, as if it was truly curious.

“Why? You have this anxious feeling all the time, but I can feel it go away when you think of him. Why would you want to bottle up and separate that part of yourself?”

Just…leave it alone, or I’m never talking to you again.

“…Understood. We’ll shelve that for another day.”

A silence followed that was actually more agonizing than Sophia wanted to admit, as words she had written in her diary and heart rematerialized…words she put to pen to get out of her chest, yet they persisted like a cancer:

I need to listen to Damon more… I think I try too hard… How I look, what I do, what I’m saying… Are my lips unkissable? Are my eyes unlookable? Is my skin untouchable? Am I unlovable? Am I good enough? I spend too much time explaining myself. I hope there’s time to change…before he realizes I’m not the girl he thinks I am.

Yet, Damon’s loving, always supportive words were a balm and a searing iron that said she was good enough…and that scared her, and she didn’t know why.

But this wasn’t about her. This was about a young woman inside her stomach, just on the cusp of finding love, about to blossom into adulthood. It was about something beautiful taken by something savage, and she could make it right—it was about Lily.

A girl she didn’t know, and she didn’t need to know her. She just needed this girl to live and love…like she wanted to live and love.

The dungeon went quiet after that.

Sophia didn’t know whether it was respect, compliance, or simply calculation—but the Core stopped pressing. The canopy rushed beneath her paper-thin form as she rode the air currents forward, every second stretching tight with urgency.

The rules were simple now.

Monsters were bound to their floors.

She was not.

The fox, however, was bound to her after she’d claimed it.

Every yard of distance pulled at the tether between them, a pressure like stretched sinew deep in her chest. It wasn’t lethal—not yet—but it was wrong, and she felt it worsen with every floor crossed.

She didn’t need to be told where the transitions between floors were; intuitively, she could find every secret, hidden transition point. A fog enclosed her, and a towering structure emerged from a shimmering haze, a giant tree without branches, merely a trunk that seemed to vanish abruptly a mile up—she’d reached one of the paths to the next floor.

Slipping into a split in the bark, she found the interior hollow with geyser-like elevator shafts that brought platforms up and down. Insects buzzed around the edges, honeycomb stuck to the walls, yet not one approached her, almost terrified of her, and the Champion queen hovered with its guard, her hive clustered around her protectively.

Her eyes widened as she saw the party on one of the platforms, clearly exhausted, covered in minor wounds. They’d just made it to the upper platform to disembark. She was too late to reach them—only close enough to see them—when she landed on a rising tube that must have been partially fashioned by the bees for transporting larvae.

As she rose, her consciousness began to fail her, and she barely managed to crawl off the rise as her mind faded. The transition to Floor Five cost her another hour, she assumed—maybe more—and she screamed through each waking moment of it.

The tether screamed with her—hooks biting into her soul, dragging backward even as she forced herself forward. By the time the world reassembled, she was shaking, mana burned low, body protesting the strain of being somewhere a Raid Boss had no business being—on the upper floors.

“I told you it would be painful. You’ve lost even longer this time, though I’ve made sure their path has been less…smooth on this floor. I’m doing my part. Better hurry.”

It feels like you’re enjoying my suffering… she snarled back, shaking away her spotty vision and realizing she was in the safe transition zone.

She sighed, lacking the energy to fight him. Floor Five was a fungal maze of glowing caps and drifting spores. Hallucinogenic. Less toxically hostile, like the Eight Floor, and more annoying. Still, it was lethal to anything unprepared with sticky pits that seemed to move on their own due to them actually moving on their own…being giant slimes.

She tore through it without any trouble after adapting to the lower floors.

The fox had been here—she could feel him. Ice markers traced the party’s path, small, clever signs left at junctions, each one easing the pressure in her chest just a fraction.

Why are you still following them? I guess I told you to make sure they were safe, but… I’m right behind you. Ugh… I should be more careful about my instructions.

When she crossed into Floor Four, the pain eased—not gone, but manageable. Enough to breathe and think, so she slowed.

The Bone Hollow, as the Core named it, opened before her: a cathedral built from the remains of titans long dead, or that was the theme he was so proudly going with. Fossilized ribs arched overhead. Massive skulls formed natural shelters, their empty sockets watching over the living. It was here that the party had finally run out of steam and had stopped.

They’d made camp beneath one of the giant skulls.

They were clearly exhausted. The halfling boy lay unconscious, having cried himself to sleep. The Witch slumped against a bone pillar with her mana spent. The Ranger elf stood guard, her weary eyes scanning the zone out of sheer stubborn will as the Fighter rested beside her, ready to take up the next watch when called to.

And at the center of it all was her fox; frost-light pulsed softly from his body as he stood near the halfling boy. Sophia breathed a sigh of relief before melting into the shadows, her massive form hidden amongst the casual debris that she took the shape of.

They’re alive.

“I told you, I made sure to pull as many creatures as I could away from their path. Though a couple of hungry Bone Rats were too ravenous to hear my probes, they managed to dispatch them with only minor injuries.”

Whatever… All I care about is that I caught up. But now…how do I return Lily to them?

“You’re overthinking it…”

She ignored him, her focus narrowing as she studied the camp. The dungeon boss they’d watched tear apart a Seventh Floor Guardian appearing on Floor Four—carrying their dead Cleric—would look like a trap.

“Oh. You’re ignoring me. Fine. I’ll just be watching…”

When no one was looking but her fox, she signaled for him to join her. The fluffy boy’s fur puffed up when he saw her in the shadows, and he followed her direction to circle around to join her. The sharp action jolted the Ranger from her half-dozing state, muscles tensing and bow at the ready.

“Garrath! Garrath!”

“Hmm? I’m up—” The Fighter snatched his sword, bloodshot eyes peering around. “What is it?”

“The Arctic Fox left. What does that mean?”

“How should I know? The bloody thing’s supposed to steal, kill, and eat us, not act like a damn guard hound. You’re the Ranger.”

“I’m trained in environment, not animal or monster behavior,” she growled back, peering into the shadow and sliding right over her with [Stealth] active. “Should we wake Meredith and Thomas up? Maybe we should press toward Floor Three?”

“No… No, we need the rest. I practically had to drag the boy up the last few floors. Meredith’s dealing with enough as it is with making the call to leave Lily. There’s something off about this place, Sylvana. I know Red Dungeons are supposed to be odd, but…”

“You’re right,” she mumbled, settling back but not dropping her guard. “I’ve never heard of a Raid Boss fighting a Floor Guardian like that… It almost feels like that Mimic was trying to save us. It even sent that fox to support us… It killed some of those Bone Rats and Arctic Foxes—its own kind.”

“Bah… Let’s just get another hour’s rest and then keep going. I’ll take the next watch. We need your eyes sharp without that fox warning us about danger.”

The elf shivered and huddled closer to the Fighter subconsciously while scanning the shadows. “If the fox left… You don’t think—is that Mimic out there?”

“Yeah, best not to think about that, because,” the man’s tone darkened, “if there’s a Raid Boss out there, it doesn’t matter what we do. We’re screwed.”

“True… Best not to jump at shadows that might not even be there. Mimics are terrifying…”

“There’s something we can raise a glass to when we’re back in the city.”

She snuggled closer to him nonetheless, closing her eyes and keeping her partially strung bow in her lap as her head lulled against his shoulder.

Sophia decided it would be better to make the handoff later, when they were at least a little more rested. They needed to be somewhere they would feel safe, not trapped, and not violate their safe campsite.

Her fox prowled around, huddling closer to her to watch them, practically vibrating with excitement as she privately praised him.

Everything was fine now. Everything was perfect.

She’d beaten the Seventh Floor Guardian, and practically everything else in the dungeon would be terrified of her after her recent levels. The only real threat left to her was on Floor Eight.

An hour passed, and then thirty more minutes before the Fighter awoke his companions. Sophia tried to distract herself by speculating on the Fighter and Ranger’s relationship, but, as much as she wanted it to be romantic, it appeared to be just a close friendship.

She followed them for hours as they made their way up a giant spine to the next floor above. There she stalked them through the Beast Warrens like a protective shadow, watching with Foxy, their unseen guardians.

Even they were starting to notice that monsters were avoiding them. Predators scattered as she went ahead of them, blending into the shadows and appearing as ordinary objects to hear them mumble in passing. Even territorial creatures melted into their dens when her shrunken form materialized near them.

Yet, through it all, she was painfully careful not to be discovered by the party. It was even getting to the point where she was discovering signs of other parties nearby. She was nearing the more populated floors.

As they neared the transition point to the Second Floor, she knew it was time. They were starting to meet other Adventurer parties and warn them about what they’d been through on the Seventh Floor.

She instructed Foxy to remain hidden as the party talked with a group of young Level 3 Adventurers, attempting to get some goods on the floor to make a little extra coin. Thomas leaned heavily on Garrath’s shoulder, fist shaking and white as Meredith explained the encounter to the group of three. Partway through, she trailed off.

Sophia’s massive form materialized from the darkness.

Meredith’s scream was immediate, Sylvana’s arrow flew wide, hands shaking too badly to aim, and Garrath shoved Thomas behind him, sword raised against something his eyes couldn’t process.

She knew she was a thing to behold—too many teeth, too many eyes, a shape that violated every comfortable category—and her size was enormous now. Sophia was a Titan Mimic, but they called her something different.

“Raid Boss!”

“Meredith,” Garrath shouted, “get them to the next floor and inform The Guild. Sylv and I will hold it off until—”

Sophia didn’t speak. Instead, she lowered herself to the stone floor, movements slow and deliberate, and opened her maw.

The party flinched, and despite the warning, the young Level 3s were petrified behind the White Witch. And Sophia simply released her [Pocket Dimension].

Lily’s frozen form slid out first—ice sculpture perfectly preserved, the curse’s spread halted by the void’s timeless embrace. Then came the treasure.

Coins and weapons. Everything she’d collected that might have value, everything that could cover resurrection costs and healing supplies, or so she hoped. After all, all this treasure was nothing to the treasure of a young girl with a heart in love. That was her treasure.

Sophia closed her giant mouth, hopped back once, twice, paused…and then shrank rapidly into a large enough rock to be seen to roll away. Soon after, she vanished into the shadows.

“That was beautiful,” the Core murmured as she retreated to watch them. “They’ll tell stories about this for generations, no doubt. Bards will sing of the mysterious Mimic of the dungeon… It will certainly increase traffic for me. Is that a good or bad thing, do you think?”

I don’t want stories. I want her to live. And I couldn’t care less about traffic right now. I’m just happy I could save her… I saved her.

Relief flooded through Sophia’s chest as the tears finally fell. That captivating hope that dangled on a string had found its shine in her eye. It wasn’t forgiveness. Wasn’t absolution. She was selfish. She was a monster. But, at least in this, she was right. She was vindicated, as flawed as she was…she did something right.

There, slipping into the catharsis of this moment, she watched the tears flow from the two parties, thanking their gods. Thomas was being restrained by Meredith, preventing him from contracting the same curse via touch, and instructing them to wrap her up for transport.

Words were passed between them, defense paper-thin in the stress and impossibility of this situation, yet one part of their conversation changed everything as she listened:

“…It’s not possible,” Garrath mumbled, wrapping cloaks around the frozen halfling teen, careful not to directly interact with the ice. “A Mimic saved us… The Dungeon Boss saved us and brought back Lily.”

One of the Level 3 Adventurers laughed through her tears. “Impossible, like Prince Damon—our kingdom’s youngest prince—being a disguised dark elf who serves Titania? Impossible, like a Lady Aria—a legendary sun elf—entering into a divine servitude contract with him under the Holy Emperor’s seal? Or impossible, like him being exiled and they’re now in our city out of all cities?”

“What? Our city?” Sylvana was keeping her gaze on their surroundings, bow ready to loose an arrow. “A sun elf and dark elf are in our city right now? That has to be an omen.”

“But what kind of omen?” Meredith muttered. “A sun elf brings fortune, a dark elf misfortune. Do you know why they’re here?”

The young woman shrugged. “From what I heard, they’re asking about this dungeon.”

Sophia didn’t hear much more as her world shrank into a tiny pocket in her mind.

Damon and Aria are here? No… No, Titania, you can’t—he’s coming for me. Damon’s…coming for me. I can’t… You can’t…

Backing away, she forgot secrecy, frame unraveling, and silencing the stunned party. Yet, she didn’t see them. All she saw were hazel eyes, looking at her with such devotion it transcended death itself.

I’m not worth it, Damon. I’m a monster. I’m…not your Sophia anymore. Please… I can’t let you see me like this. I can’t… I can’t!

Turning, she ran. Foxy ran with her, but Sohpia barely noticed him.

All she saw was him.

Please, Damon, don’t save me! If I see you, I know…I’ll break. I’ll break.

“Sophia?” the Core whispered. “He’s searching for you. Whatever you’ve become, whatever you’ve done—he’s still searching.”

Shut up. He doesn’t know.

Her breath hitched. Golden tears blurred the world as she stumbled past stunned adventurers, Foxy padding after her.

He would have been a good father.

The thought shattered her.

“You’re running from him again…and toward me?”

I know.

She was always running—parents, friends, love—anything that might close around her, even when the cage was made of warmth.

I said I would die for him…

Her heart faltered.

But why… Why can’t I live for him?

She plunged deeper into the darkness. She was just a monster. She had to show him she was just a monster…so he’d forget about her and find someone better.
