Three Days Later"I think I've decided on Dagger Expert as my first Class," Esen announced.Simon was about to respond when their carriage hit a nasty bump in the road, sending everyone jumping an inch in their seats. Artifact-powered transportation may have been a marvel of Valtian engineering, capable of auto-piloting between cities without needing a driver, but it was still lacking in many respects.Like shock absorbers and suspension. Simon wasn't a big fan of cars – it came with the territory when your parents died in an 'accidental' crash – but after days of bruises forming, healing from Transmigrator's Body, then forming again, he would've traded the carriage for its Earth counterpart in a heartbeat."I hate this damnable contraption," Katarina spat, with a surprising amount of venom. "The last carriage we rode in wasn't nearly so unpleasant.""That was a luxury caravan funded by the slave trade," Simon said. And it still sucked compared to Earth's creature comforts. "We looted it from Lucette and her gang, remember? Something like that would be too high-profile for what we're doing here."Staying under the radar was their top priority, and humans were essentially second-class citizens in Ashora. If they rolled up to Fellrig City in a vehicle typically reserved for wealthy merchants, it would result in too many questions that they couldn't properly answer.At one point during their planning, Simon had jokingly suggested installing a Waystation Crystal at Proxia to cut down on travel time. Queen Ashora had responded, quite seriously, that humans were barred from utilizing Waystations unless explicitly permitted by a higher-ranking Demon. Without the forged authorization papers she'd given them to let their group enter Fellrig, they would've been arrested on-sight for being 'off their leash'."Suppose you're right." Katarina let out an exaggerated sigh, sinking into an ineffectual pile of cushions that were more of a placebo than anything. "We couldn't have used that carriage for this trip anyway. Not after someone stole it from us."Bastian massaged his temples. "Never going to let me forget that, are you?""Why would I? You try living with barely a coin to your name for years, finally obtaining an object of real value – then having it vanish in the dead of night."Despite her unamused tone of voice, she was clearly holding back a smile. "How much did that merchant give you for our carriage, again? Should charge you an equal amount. With interest.""Don't think that'll do you any good in Ashora," Bastian remarked. "They use different currency here."Of which Simon already had plenty. The Demon Queen couldn't give them allies, or even a half-decent carriage, but she'd at least been generous when funding their escapade. And thanks to Inventory, he could keep it hidden from the locals – who would definitely have wondered why humans were walking around with a small fortune in their pockets.Kat sent Bastian an evil grin. "I can wait until we're home to collect my due compensation from you. The 'salaries' that Simon implemented will allow my father and I to live well for the rest of our days, but some extra to help fill the ledgers won't hurt."Cyna chuckled. "Loath as I am to dispel the only form of entertainment I've witnessed since we departed from the Isles, please stop tormenting the love of my life.""Oh? But that carriage he stole was used to fund your rescue, if you'll recall. Aren't you interested in how much money he was carelessly throwing around? The desperate lengths he went to try and save your life? How he would've given the very sword off his back for just one more minute with you?"Note to self, Simon thought. Don't draw Kat's attention when she's bored.After a long pause, Cyna looked at Bastian with a wordless, questioning, and deeply curious gaze."...Esen!" Bastian exclaimed, a hint of embarrassment creeping into his voice. Which Simon didn't fully understand, as the Swordsman had nothing to be embarrassed about, but Katarina had somehow reframed his heroism and dedication as the behavior of a lovesick romantic. "You said you've selected Dagger Expert as your first Class. A fine choice! Would you care to explain the reasoning behind your decision?"Esen's gaze shifted between everyone else in the carriage. Whatever he'd expected from being temporarily inducted into the Rainbringer's inner circle, it wasn't this."Just thought it made sense," he muttered. "Wanted something with power. So I can fight. Other choices couldn't. Infiltrator, Thief...not enough combat Skills."He frowned. "And why Con Artist? Does this 'system' think so poorly of me?"Simon shook his head. "We all got Con Artist too. It's a default choice for members of the Hurricane." Or for a transmigrator who'd averaged one new identity per week since coming to Valtia.Esen flinched, still unused to hearing Simon speak to him directly. "I...see. Won't take offense then, Lord Rainbringer.""I'll take offense if you keep calling me Lord. If the people of Ashora hear you say that, it could blow our cover."Katarina smirked at him. "You're just sick of people fawning over you like a noble.""Bit of Column A, bit of Column B."Simon wished there was a way to seize power without being treated like he had power. The way some people changed their behavior around him made his skin crawl. It would be one thing if they were listening to him because they genuinely believed in his ideas, but the vast majority had mentally slotted him into the same role as Duke Helmund; a tyrant to be heeded lest he rain down suffering on what little remained of their brief, miserable lives.Which could still be fun if he was intimidating someone who deserved it. Not so much when trying to get his aides to understand that he wouldn't bite their heads off for pointing out a flaw in his policies."So – Dagger Expert," he continued. "Weren't Archer and Swordsman options as well, Esen? Not criticizing, just asking."The youth stiffened his posture, then relaxed when Simon clarified he wasn't in trouble. "Archer was a strange option for the Boon to give me. I've only held a bow once. Swordsman, maybe, but I'm nowhere near the virtuoso with the blade that Bastian is. Always felt more comfortable with knives."You certainly are, Simon mused, recalling how quickly Esen had been to stab him after the mana-bomb devastated Castle Helmund. Good reflexes...and the shortest hair-trigger in the Hurricane. Excellent for combat, less so for social situations. He won't be handling diplomacy anytime soon.Katarina nodded. "I think it's wise to play to your strengths," she said. "Admittedly, I went in the opposite direction by focusing on fire magic instead of my proficiency with a crossbow, but Bastian and Cyna are proof that the idea holds merit."Bastian had adopted a no-frills Class that emphasized his innate swordsmanship expertise. Cyna went for one that bolstered her stats if she was fighting for a cause she truly, passionately believed in. Both choices had served them well thus far."I still have my second Class Advancement to choose from," Cyna added. "Not sure if I should continue down the Executioner path or pivot to a different specialization. Wouldn't have survived Duke Helmund without it, but – tree!"Her abrupt shout prompted a flurry of motion as everyone except Simon scurried over to one side of the carriage. About a dozen of them. Barely enough to be considered a grove.To Simon, they looked...a tad sickly, if he was being honest. He'd seen more colorful trees back on Earth. These appeared slightly out-of-season, even though it was apparently spring in Ashora right now.But to his allies?It was like they'd stepped into another world.Grass. Trees. Wildlife. Things they'd heard of only in stories about distant, far-off lands – things they'd scarcely believed to be real at all.It didn't matter that Ashora was in partial decline from Helmund's proliferation of Mana Harvesters. Compared to the barren, desiccated wastelands of the Severed Isles, this was practically the Garden of Eden itself.His companions had cycled through a series of powerful emotions over the past few days. First was the sense of childlike wonder, which even now had yet to completely fade. Cyna would never own up to it, but she'd openly shed tears when she initially laid eyes on Ashora's rolling fields of grass. Her reaction had probably been the most stoic of the four of them.Next came the anger. It was one thing to be told, objectively speaking, that your lands were dying – especially when its death had been so gradual and drawn-out. With Helmund's demise, no human remained who could recall the Isles in its full splendor. For the people living there now, its current state of degradation was all they'd ever known.Unless they were given a point of reference. Shown what had been taken from them.By the end of Day 1 of their journey, Simon's Boon-Bearers were all in agreement that Duke Helmund – whose body literally melted as he was barraged with flesh-searing Kills – had gotten off easy with his punishment.The childlike wonder returned by Day 2. No one could stay mad after seeing squirrels for the first time. Until they remembered why they were excited to see squirrels. And so the cycle continued.Simon was exhausted just from watching their emotional highs and lows. He couldn't imagine how it felt to them. Maybe a better leader than him would've pulled an inspirational speech out of his ass, helped soothe their weary hearts, but that was completely out of his wheelhouse.He could only work towards a brighter future and let his results speak for themselves.Speaking of – time for more community service."Hold on to something." Simon waited for his allies to steady themselves, then moved to adjust the carriage's navigation Artifact. The vehicle rapidly slowed, almost sending everyone into a tangled heap on the floor."Why are we stopping?" Katarina fought hard to meet his gaze, as if tearing her eyes away from the quaint little grove felt physically painful. "If it's to let us savor this sight for a while longer...I appreciate the thought, but we don't have the luxury of time right now.""This'll be quick." Simon gestured out the window. "We have company."A shadow was stretching out from underneath one of the trees, cast by the mid-morning sun. At a glance – or if you were distracted by seeing more green than you ever had before – you wouldn't have noticed anything wrong.It was only when his allies took a second look that they recoiled in disgust, finally catching how the shadow's hue was shaded just a bit too dark, how it seemed to wriggle with anticipation as their carriage stopped nearby.Simon couldn't blame them for failing to notice. Without Ancient-Attuned pinging him, he would've missed it too."Is that a Fell Beast?" Cyna hissed. She sounded morally offended that her beloved garden had been defiled by an abomination, her hands already clasping the hilt of her greatsword."Two of them, actually." Simon opened a side door and hopped out of the carriage. "One down in the shadows, one up in the leaf canopy. Camouflage hunters. Met another just like them about...five weeks ago? Would've gutted me if I hadn't found a Sanctuary Tree for shelter."He eyed the shadow with distaste, observing how its excited wriggling went still as he came into view. Was it lying in wait, ready to strike if he drew closer? Was this a fear response, the creature having sensed that if it wasn't careful, it might go from the hunter to the hunted? Could be either or."Those aren't Sanctuary Trees, though, so this is a great spot for enterprising Beasts to stage an ambush. It's an enticing pit stop for people on the road to Fellrig – maybe set up camp, take a nap under the shade. And not everyone travels with as many Warding Orbs as they should. Only takes one instance of cutting costs to lose your life."In fact, that was how Simon had been introduced to the wonderful world of Valtia: amidst a demolished caravan, where an enormous, mutated rat was munching on fresh corpses. They hadn't replaced their protective Warding Orbs before journeying into the Severed Isles wastelands, and they'd paid the price for it.Kat, Cyna, and Bastian all started to get up, hatred glimmering in their eyes. They really weren't happy with the notion of Fell Beasts infesting their precious trees.Before they could leap out of the carriage, though, Simon held up a hand to forestall them. "You guys hold back. Esen – you're up."The newly-minted Dagger Expert winced at the sound of his name. "Me, Lord Rain– um, Sir Rainbringer?""Just 'Simon' is fine. Hop to it. You need to adapt to the changes that Levels and stats have caused in your body, and there's no better practice than real combat."Esen took one look at the squirming, sentient shadow and visibly shuddered. "...Must I? Can't I simply fight Demons instead?"You'd rather fight a Demon than a Fell Beast? Well, I guess the former is more humanoid, while the latter is usually a full-blown horror movie monster. Plus he's spent enough time around me for the concept of Demons as a whole to be somewhat demystified."Relax," Simon assured. "These two Beasts are weak."
