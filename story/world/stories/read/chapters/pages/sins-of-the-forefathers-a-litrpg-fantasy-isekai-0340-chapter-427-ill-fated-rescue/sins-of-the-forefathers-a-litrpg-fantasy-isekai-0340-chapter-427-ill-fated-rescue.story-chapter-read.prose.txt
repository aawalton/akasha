When I heard the door to my cell open, I was…a little surprised. It had barely been thirty minutes since Anguis and the mentally captive Elysael had left me alone to wallow. Surely he hadn’t already finished his business out in the city before he started on his fucking genocide?

If he was, I’d tell him to fuck off. I’d been busy.

But no.

What I saw standing in the open doorway of my cell was a much, much more welcome sight.

A thoroughly confused-looking Sylvia.

I blinked rapidly for a moment before a smile instantly crossed my face, and I understood what was happening.

My love had come to rescue me.

I laughed and rose to my feet as I sent out a quick mental message. After all, it would have been rude to leave without saying goodbye.

“We’ll continue this later, Anima,” I mentally shot to the Veredenese Great Spirit of Life, across the vast distances between us. “I’m getting rescued.”

“…very well, Nathan,” Anima returned in a distinctly troubled tone. “I…shall consider what you have told me. Until then, please…be safe.”

As the connection between us cut out, I shot Sylvia a wide smile. It only grew wider when a few other familiar faces peered around the edge of the doorway into my cell. “I see,” I said, breaking the stunned silence in the cell. “There’s a whole squad of you here to rescue little old me, eh?”

“I…” Sylvia blinked once more before rapidly shaking her head. “Yes. Nathan, what’s going on? I…wasn’t expecting you to be so…”

My smile started to fade as the reality of everything that was happening reasserted itself over my mood. “Uninjured?” I sighed, a sense of immediate impatience starting to roll over me. The elation of my apparent rescue was fading, and now…

Now we needed to move.

“That was the point,” I said briskly, striding straight towards her. Sylvia quickly stepped aside as I entered the prison hallway outside my cell. I quickly scanned my surroundings, recognizing everyone present. Despite the circumstances, I was still thankful for their loyalty.

My eyes lingered on the collapsed, unconscious forms of the two Corvid Knights Anguis had left behind to guard me. I was thankful that my comrades hadn’t killed them. These poor suckers weren’t willingly part of Anguis’s mad plot.

The mind-controlled didn’t deserve death.

My attention was stolen when three people exited the cell directly to my left. From it emerged Maria and…Kierla?

The oddly excitable Elven Scout who had led the expedition to the Elves? What? I…whatever. I suppose she had come to rescue the third person with them. The one that the other two were holding, hanging between them, their arms holding him up underneath his armpits.

Alveron.

The Elf looked even older than he had when this long, terrible day had started. Exhaustion was written into every line of his face, every concentric wrinkle reflecting his despair. He hung limply in between the two young women supporting him. Not because he was injured, no. At least not physically.

I think, instead, that Anguis had crushed Alveron’s spirit.

Still, the ancient Paragon was able to meet my eyes when I sought his out. “Did he visit you, too?” I asked quietly.

Alveron closed his eyes…

And nodded.

“I see…” I sighed, shaking my head. “I can guess what he told you. Alveron…are you going to be able to help us? You know what we need to do.”

The space between us, here in this underground prison, was still and silent for a moment. I think Alveron was actually, seriously considering my question.

Could he continue? Did he have the will to, in the face of the revelations that Anguis had imparted to us? Both in public…

And in private?

Alveron drew in a long, shuddering breath, and as he did, I swore I could feel the ambient Aether in the air being drawn into his lungs. When he was finished, he held his breath for a moment before letting it out, opening his eyes…

And nodding.

“I can…” Alveron whispered in a dry, brittle tone. He straightened up, and when they noticed he could stand on his own two feet once more, Kierla and Maria stepped away from him. “I can…one last time…”

“What is going on?” Sylvia burst in, casting a concerned gaze between both Alveron and me. “What do you mean, did ‘he’-” She abruptly paused, narrowing her eyes at me. “Are you talking about…”

As she trailed off, eyes focusing across my shoulder, I felt a new heart abruptly appear on the edge of my blood sense. I recognized it from its slow, deep beats, and honestly, I was a bit surprised to find the owner down here with us. I hadn’t thought she cared enough to assist in my rescue. She was moving fast, and in only moments had sprinted up to our group standing outside of my former cell, appearing out of the darkness of the hallway that I believed led upwards.

Meia Itzelan took a single moment to catch her breath, allowing herself to exchange a nod of acknowledgment with me before she spoke. “We need to leave,” She said, blunt as always. “Now.”

Sylvia tensed and walked up to the Half-Orc woman. “Have the Dwarves discovered us?”

“No,” Meia shook her head impatiently. “That’s not it. We have to go, Sylvia. Hart, we can’t stay here. We’re needed elsewhere. The others can fill you in on why we came to get you like this on the way.”

Why they came to get me like this? What did she mean by that?

I wanted to ask her, but with her message delivered, Meia was done standing around. She spun on her heel and took back off down the tunnel, her grey concealing cloak snapping in the wind caused by her passage behind her.

I exchanged glances with the rest of my rescuers, from Sylvia, to Maria, to Renauld, and even Walter and Kierla. I shook my head. “Fill me in on what’s happened as we move. Let’s go.”

With that, as a group, we took off down the corridor.

…………………………..

I have to admit a certain level of…hurt from what I was told by Sylvia just what had happened in the outside world, since my capture. I had, sincerely, thought that I had been making at least a little progress with Marcel, over the last months we’d worked together. There had been plenty of times he had challenged me before the other Captains, yes, but not egregiously so. None of it had struck me as a blatant grab for power and influence. Rather…more like he had been helping, genuinely, in his own way by pointing out errors in my leadership and judgment.

I’d known for a long time that Renauld’s father would have preferred if he had been the leader of the expedition. But…

I hadn’t expected him to try and cut me off like this.

It…didn’t matter, in the end. I wasn’t going to let him suborn the expedition out from under me. There was too much riding on the forces of the expedition being able to stand united against Savoy, against Anguis, and most importantly…

Whatever had actually become of Fynneas.

Anguis had left me alone so I could come to an answer to his question.

Would I be willing to join up with him? To forsake everyone and everything I believed in, for a mere chance to survive the ‘purging’ of Fynneas’s Mantle of Divinity, when the corruption of the Skyfall intensified? To flee with my tail between my legs back to the safety of Blutstein’s wards?

Of course not.

I had reached an answer, in that cell, with the help of Anima.

Seeking out Fynneas within the heart of Smaragd was still the best chance we had to end all of this. It didn’t matter that Anguis and Leonard had ripped the Mad God’s Mantle of Divinity out of the Concord and cast it into the sky of Vereden to be cleansed.

Fynneas was still the rightful owner of the Mantle. He still possessed the greatest link to it of anyone on Vereden.

If he had been cleansed of his madness…

Then maybe we didn’t even need to kill him to put a stop to this. But first, we had to survive the trap that Rhoscara had become. No…

That wasn’t quite right.

We needed to win the battle for Rhoscara. We needed to drive Anguis and his forces out of this city, preferably killing the Serpent in the process. Hell, maybe I’d even get a shot at Leonard, too.

Besides. As a Precursor, I was meant to kill Anguis. The entire purpose of The Scintillant Blade was to act as a conduit to reclaim sparks of Divinity for the System. And wouldn’t you know it?

By his own admission…he had two.

Hah.

Maybe I’d get two Skills from killing him. The System had yet to scam me, for all of its reduced state.

And so, I had a plan. Get out of the prison, link back up with the expedition, reassert my control over it, and then combine forces with Bleddyn. Together, the two of us just might be able to put together a good enough defense. There wasn’t a single Dwarf in Rhoscara we could trust anymore, not with the revelation that Anguis had access to their collective soul, or perhaps their collective unconscious. Whatever it was, the only way to break the Serpent’s hold on them…

Was to kill him.

At least…that’s what I had thought the plan was.

As we burst into the dull, grey light of the outside, I immediately noticed we were…nearly alone. The only people still out here with us were Meia, impatiently tapping her foot, and Fade.

Who immediately perked up at the sight of me and rushed to bump his head into my stomach, nearly goring me with his antlers as he did. “Nate!” He said excitedly, over our nearly reconnected Familiar Bond. It was odd how I had been cut off from him during my stay in the cell, but not Anima. It might have something to do with their respective strengths as Spirits, though. I hadn’t had time to examine the ward schemata to be sure. “Are you alright?!”

I smiled down at Fade, resting a hand between his antlers. “Yes, I’m fine,” I said aloud, flickering my eyes up to meet Meia’s. “Don’t worry about me. Meia…what’s going on? Why do we need to leave?”

The Half-Orc woman’s shoulders tensed, and the scowl on her face only grew. Her next words caused the bottom of my stomach to drop out.

“The fighting has started.”

………………………………

I needed to see this for myself, and Meia had almost instinctively realized that. She told us that the rest of the apparent squad that had come to rescue me were waiting for us, not far from where we were.

In fact, they were above us.

The main complex of the Scarlet Palace possessed its own series of watch towers, strategically arrayed upon the roof of the crimson shingles. It only made sense, after all, considering that the palace was situated at the very top of the massive, river-bordering hill that Rhoscara had grown up around. The central tower, at the very heart of the palace, was the single tallest point in the entire city.

It was there that I found the rest of the expedition members who can come to rescue me, watching what was happening out in the city with far-eye’s.

It was, in a word…

“A massacre,” The man lying on his stomach next to me murmured, handing me the device. A grim frown on my face, I took it and saw for myself…

That he wasn’t exaggerating.

Sylvia had briefly mentioned the explosion that Bleddyn’s men had set off to create a distraction, and that had been reason enough alone to cause me to grimace. They couldn’t have known such a thing wasn’t even needed. Anguis had deliberately left me minimally guarded, and his forces were all so brainwashed that distractions weren’t necessary. The only thing it had done was destroy the port and kill soldiers. That was…decent in a strategic sense, but it didn’t matter in the end.

The fire that was raging out now in the city did.

Bleddyn and Syvlia hadn’t known that Anguis’s plan had been to slaughter Rhoscara down to the last man, woman, and child, before razing it to the ground and salting the ashes. They had likely thought that maybe the Rhoscarans alone would have tried to douse the flames, if only to save their city.

They hadn’t known.

Every Dwarf in this city could be already compromised by Anguis’s stolen Divinity. And so…

It almost looked like the entire city was now ablaze. There were only small pockets of Rhoscara that weren’t actively burning to the ground. And moving through all of it were soldiers.

Dwarven soldiers of both forces.

Blank-faced Rhoscaran soldiers led the way through the streets, seemingly trying to evacuate scared civilians from their boarded-up, fortified homes. The people of Rhoscara had been expecting open battle in their streets, but it was fire that came for them instead. Relieved, they tore down their boards and moved their piled up furniture from their door, expecting their defenders to be here to lead them to safety.

Only for the Savoy forces, trailing in the Rhoscaran’s wake, to cut them down where they stood, in the doorway of their homes. And then, when the Savoy were done with the innocent, they turned their blades upon the soldiers of Rhoscara.

And thus were Anguis’s words fulfilled.

Not a promise, or a malediction, or a curse.

A certainty.

The Serpent wasn’t one to turn away a perfectly good disaster like this conflagration. It was, instead, an opportunity.

This was happening…all over the city.

En-masse.

Every second I stood here watching, hundreds of innocent people were being cut down by their own defenders. I felt feckless and stupid, played for a fool by a master of deception, helpless to fulfil the promise of my Order.

As a snarl crept over my face, I noticed something.

There was still resistance in the city. Anguis wasn’t rolling over it totally, as of yet.

And so it goes.

I tossed the far-eye aside carelessly and turned to face the grim, horrified faces of those who had come to rescue me. They, too, had seen the grotesquerie that Anguis was determined to turn Rhoscara into.

“No more,” I said coldly, meeting the eyes of every person in this tower.

“Move out.”
