The suffocating tension in the Guildhall slowly began to thin as the exhausted adventurers resigned themselves to their grim reality. The massive roaring hearth in the centre of the room was finally stoked back to life, casting a warm, flickering orange glow over the scarred wooden tables and the battered fighters nursing their wounds.

Josh, Brett, and Carcan remained at their isolated table in the corner. They had managed to flag down a completely overwhelmed tavern maid, successfully ordering a massive platter of hot food on the Guild's newly promised tab.

When the food arrived, it was not delicate or refined. It was heavy, utilitarian survival fare designed entirely to replenish depleted physical bodies. The maid dropped three thick, steaming bowls of hearty root vegetable stew onto the scarred wood, accompanied by massive hunks of crusty brown bread and an entire roasted fowl dripping with greasy, salted juices.

Josh descended upon the food with terrifying, single-minded focus. The physical toll of his armour, the brutal combat at the bottom of the sinkhole, and the frantic, sprinting retreat had completely hollowed out his energy reserves. He tore into the roasted fowl with his bare hands, his enhanced metabolism desperately crying out for calories to fuel his newly acquired level and his rapidly evolving muscle density.

"Pace yourself, Josh," Brett advised dryly, taking a much more measured, careful sip of his hot stew.

The elemental mage had finally stopped shivering. The ambient heat of the crowded room and the steaming broth were finally chasing the freezing, necrotic chill from his bones. He pushed his non-existent spectacles up his nose with a slightly cleaner hand. "If you choke on a chicken bone before the undead even reach the walls, it will be a highly embarrassing footnote in our grand heroic saga."

Josh simply grunted a muffled response, using a massive, torn hunk of crusty bread to mop up the thick, savoury gravy at the bottom of his wooden bowl. He was easily eating the lion's share of the meal, consuming enough calories for three normal men, but neither Brett nor Carcan begrudged him the extra rations. They had both felt the impossible, anchoring weight of his defensive shield during the retreat. He had literally carried the squad upon his steel-clad shoulders, and he needed the fuel.

Carcan ate slowly and methodically, her aristocratic features a mask of serene, quiet exhaustion. She broke off small, delicate pieces of bread, her silver eyes staring blankly at the flickering flames of the distant hearth. The sheer volume of concentrated holy mana she had expended over the last twelve hours had left her physically and spiritually drained.

"Are you alright, Carcan?" Brett asked softly, noting her distant stare.

"I’m trying to figure out the recovery time for my mana core," Carcan replied, her melodic voice lacking its usual vibrant energy. "The siege will likely begin soon. I need to ensure I am capable of maintaining a mass healing aura for an extended duration. Bhel's injuries took a significant toll, as did the rescue."

Before Brett could offer a reassuring remark, Perberos silently slipped back into the seat beside his twin sister.

The elven scout looked deeply drained, a stark contrast to his usual aura of effortless, liquid confidence. The ethereal, aristocratic glow of his pale skin was entirely muted, covered in a thin layer of grime, and the dark circles under his luminous silver eyes looked like bruised, heavy shadows.

"Ronald has finalised the scouting roster," Perberos announced quietly, reaching out to tear a small piece of crusty bread from the communal loaf. "We are leaving the city gates at nightfall."

"Nightfall?" Josh questioned, finally pausing his ravenous eating to look at the ranger. "You are going back into the corrupted plains in the pitch black?"

"We are going to push deep into the northern plains to locate the front line of their marching column," Perberos confirmed, his tone perfectly level and devoid of fear. "We need to know what we are dealing with. We need to measure their marching speed, estimate their numbers, and identify any siege weapons they might have salvaged. We cannot defend the walls if we are entirely blind to their formation."

"That is a suicide mission," Brett pointed out, his pale brow furrowing with genuine, deep concern for the scout. "If you get caught out in the open plains by a pack of Crypt Ghouls or skeletal hounds in the dark, there is nowhere to hide. The entire landscape is flat, frozen grass."

"That is exactly why the squad is comprised entirely of rangers and stealth-focused rogues," Perberos replied smoothly. "We do not intend to fight. If we draw our blades, we have already failed. We are strictly observing and reporting back to the city guard. But I need to get some sleep before the sun sets."

The ranger turned to his twin sister. He pulled a small, crumpled piece of parchment from his leather belt and slid it across the scarred wooden table.

"The Guild administration has temporarily requisitioned the upper rooms of the hall for the scouting teams," Perberos explained, suppressing a massive, jaw-cracking yawn. "Carcan, I need you to procure some essential supplies for me while I rest. Get as many heavy steel-tipped broadheads as you can physically carry, along with specialized armour-piercing bodkins if the fletchers have any left. The standard iron arrows will simply bounce off the heavier ghouls."

Carcan took the list, her silver eyes scanning the messy, hurried handwriting.

"I also need concentrated stamina draughts and high-grade health potions," Perberos added, standing up slowly from the wooden bench. "We are going to be running for hours in the freezing cold. Drain the Guild's promissory note if you have to. Cost is entirely irrelevant right now."

"Consider it done, brother," Carcan promised softly, carefully folding the parchment and tucking it into the folds of her pale green robes. "Go and rest. You will need all your strength for the dark."

Perberos gave Josh and Brett a tired, respectful nod of farewell before turning away. He melted seamlessly into the crowded, noisy hall, heading for the heavy wooden stairs leading to the upper dormitories.

"Right then," Brett said, clapping his hands together briskly as he finished the last spoonful of his hot stew. "A shopping trip on the Guild's dime. It is a terrible shame the circumstances are so apocalyptic, because I could really use a new silk lining for cloak."

"We are prioritising steel, potions, and immediate repairs, Brett," Josh stated firmly.

He finally pushed his empty wooden bowl away and wiped his mouth with the back of his hand. The heavy meal sat comfortably in his stomach, the calories already working to repair his bruised and battered muscles. "But before we hit the blacksmiths and the alchemists in the artisan district, we need to check on Bhel. He was threatening the medical acolytes when we loaded him into the wagon, and I want to make sure he has not tried to escape the temple."

The trio quickly gathered their remaining gear. Josh strapped his gouged steel shield back onto his arm, and they left the loud, crowded warmth of the Guildhall behind.

The walk to the grand temple of the Light took them away from the noisy, chaotic commercial centre of Ashenfall. They walked through the winding, cobblestone streets of the upper district, where the architecture shifted from heavy, practical timber to polished white stone. The air here was much cleaner, carrying the faint, pleasant scent of expensive burning incense and fresh pine needles.

The temple itself was a towering, magnificent structure constructed entirely of brilliant white marble. Its massive, intricate stained-glass windows caught the pale morning light, casting long, colourful geometric shadows across the pristine stone steps leading to the heavy oak entrance doors.

Stepping inside, the atmosphere was a stark, incredibly jarring contrast to the muddy, blood-stained reality of the adventurers' guild.

The grand infirmary was a massive, vaulted hall filled with perfectly aligned rows of pristine white beds. The air smelled sharply of bitter medicinal herbs, burning sage bundles used to ward off infection, and clean, freshly sanitised linens. Acolytes dressed in pale yellow robes moved quietly and methodically between the beds, whispering soft prayers of healing and carefully changing heavy bandages. It was a place of profound, dedicated quiet.

However, the serene, peaceful atmosphere of the healing ward was currently being violently, loudly disrupted.

Josh, Brett, and Carcan could hear Bhel long before they actually saw him. The dwarf's deep, booming, gravelly voice was echoing loudly down the pristine white corridors, entirely devoid of any indoor volume control or respect for the recovering patients.

"I am not an invalid!" Bhel roared, the sound bouncing off the high vaulted ceilings and causing several yellow-robed acolytes to jump in fright. "I have fought through cavern collapses that would crush a normal human man into a fine paste! A little bit of black sludge is not going to keep me strapped to a soft bed whilst I should be out preparing for a siege!"

Josh winced, exchanging a highly concerned, entirely sympathetic look with Brett.

They hurried down the pristine corridor, following the booming noise to a private recovery room located near the very back of the ward. Carcan took the lead. Her aristocratic elven features set into a mask of pure, terrifying stone. The serene, quiet exhaustion she had displayed in the Guildhall completely vanished, instantly replaced by a cold, sharp fury.

She pushed the heavy oak door open and marched directly into the room.

Bhel was sitting upright in his bed, throwing his thick woollen blankets aside. His bristling beard was wildly unkempt, sticking out in several odd directions, and his broad, barrel-like chest was heavily wrapped in thick layers of pristine white bandages. He was currently pointing a thick, calloused finger aggressively at a senior city healer.

The healer was an older human man wearing elaborate white and gold robes. He looked completely exhausted, rubbing his temples, and entirely out of his depth when dealing with the stubborn mountain warrior.

"I need my boots, and I need my axes brought to me immediately!" Bhel demanded loudly, ignoring the senior healer's frantic, physical attempts to push him back down against the soft pillows. "There is fighting to do, and I am not doing it in this ridiculous, drafty, open-backed hospital gown!"

"Bheldur Giantguard," Carcan stated.

She did not shout. She did not raise her melodic voice above a normal conversational volume. But the sheer, freezing menace dripping from those two words caused the ambient temperature in the small room to instantly plummet.

Bhel froze entirely. His thick, calloused finger remained suspended awkwardly in the air.

Carcan stepped fully into the room, her luminous silver eyes burning with a terrifying, uncompromising fury. She looked exactly like an avenging angel of the Light, and she unleashed absolute, unmitigated hell upon the stubborn dwarven fighter.

"Do you have any concept of the sheer volume of raw, concentrated mana I had to pour directly into your chest simply to stop that necrotic sludge from liquefying your heart?" Carcan demanded, her voice razor-sharp, slicing cleanly through Bhel's booming bravado.

She took another step forward, her pale green robes swishing ominously. "You are not made of invincible granite, Bhel! You are meat and bone, and right now, that meat is barely knitted together by a very fragile, incredibly taxed magical thread! I can’t believe I am having to have this conversation with you again! I am very disappointed in you!”

Bhel slowly lowered his hand, his dark eyes widening in genuine, profound alarm. He had never, in all the long, bloody months they had travelled together, seen the serene elven healer lose her temper this badly.

"I... I just wanted to check on the condition of my gear," Bhel stammered weakly, entirely losing his booming volume and suddenly finding the white bedsheets incredibly interesting.

"You will not check on anything!" Carcan snapped, stepping right up to the very edge of his mattress and leaning over him. "I spent far too long performing painstaking, exhausting magical surgery on your chest. I had to physically weave golden light through your severed arteries to keep you from drowning in your own corrupted blood!"

Josh and Brett, standing in the doorway, simultaneously took a very slow, very quiet step backwards. They pressed themselves completely flat against the clean white wall of the corridor, desperately trying to blend into the plaster and avoid drawing her ire. The senior city healer took the golden opportunity to silently slip past the two heavily armoured humans, escaping the room with a look of profound, heartfelt relief.

"If you tear those stitches," Carcan continued, her voice trembling slightly with the sheer force of her anger, "if you undo the work I nearly burned my own mana core out to complete, I swear by the roots of the Mother Tree I will never cast a single healing spell on you again. I will let you bleed out in the mud for all I care, and I will walk right past your stubborn corpse!"

She punctuated the final threat by poking her slender, elegant finger sharply against the very edge of his thick, white bandages.

"Do I make myself completely, undeniably clear?" Carcan demanded.

Bhel physically shrank. The massive, towering dwarven warrior, who routinely traded physical blows with giant monsters, who laughed in the face of charging orcs and defied the crushing weight of the undead horde, sank further and further down into his soft white pillows. All the fight completely left his dense, muscular body. He pulled the thick white bedsheet up to his chest, looking exactly like a thoroughly scolded child facing the wrath of a strict tutor.

"Yes, Carcan," Bhel mumbled into his magnificent beard, completely and utterly cowed. "Crystal clear."

Carcan held his gaze for three long, terrifying seconds, ensuring her absolute dominance was firmly established and that he would not attempt to leave the bed the moment she turned her back.

Then, exactly like flipping a magical switch, the terrifying fury completely vanished from her face.

Her aristocratic elven features softened back into their usual mask of serene, gentle calm. The freezing aura dissipating entirely. She clasped her hands neatly together in front of her pale green robes, tilting her head slightly.

"Excellent," Carcan smiled sweetly, her melodic voice returning to its normal, soothing pitch as if she had not just threatened to let him die in the mud. "Now, the boys and I are heading down to the artisan district to do some essential shopping. Is there anything specific you need us to pick up for you?"

Bhel blinked rapidly, severe emotional whiplash clearly evident on his pale, greyish face. He looked past Carcan, desperately seeking help or solidarity from Josh and Brett in the doorway. The two humans simply shook their heads frantically, their eyes wide with fear, completely refusing to get involved in the domestic dispute.

"Right. Um. Yes," Bhel cleared his throat nervously, pointing a trembling finger toward a pile of ruined, mud-caked leather and iron sitting sadly in the corner of the pristine room. "My chest piece is completely shattered. If you could take it to the blacksmiths and see if they can hammer the iron plates back into shape, I would appreciate it. And... maybe a new set of throwing axes? If it is not too much trouble."

He paused, swallowing hard, looking at the elven healer warily. "...please."

Carcan offered a genuinely warm, radiant smile. She reached out, placing her slender, elegant hand gently over the dwarf's thick, calloused forearm resting on the white blankets.

"Of course, Bhel," Carcan said softly, her silver eyes filling with a sudden, touching vulnerability that completely betrayed the terrifying rant she had just delivered. "I will make sure your gear is perfect and ready for you. I just want you to live. I could not bear to lose you."

With that completely disarming, highly emotional final statement, Carcan turned on her heel. Her pale green robes swished gracefully against the floor tiles, and she marched straight out of the room, leaving a stunned, heavy silence in her wake.

Josh and Brett remained frozen against the wall in the doorway for another long moment. They stared dumbfounded at the dwarf in the bed.

"Well," Brett whispered, his pale eyes completely wide. "That was utterly terrifying."

"Absolutely chilling," Josh agreed in a hushed tone, adjusting his grip on his heavy steel shield. "I am never, ever going to complain about a healing spell again."

They both raised their hands, giving Bhel a highly awkward, silent wave goodbye. Josh opened his mouth, just about to offer a few quiet words of masculine solidarity to the utterly defeated dwarven warrior.

"Josh! Brett!" Carcan’s voice called out from further down the pristine white corridor. The sweet, vulnerable tone was entirely gone, completely replaced by a sharp, stern, military authority. "Are we going shopping or not? Do not dawdle!"

Josh and Brett physically jumped, heavy armour clanking loudly. They did not say another word to Bhel. They simply scrambled over each other to get through the doorway, rushing down the corridor after the elven healer exactly like two severely scolded children terrified of being left behind.
