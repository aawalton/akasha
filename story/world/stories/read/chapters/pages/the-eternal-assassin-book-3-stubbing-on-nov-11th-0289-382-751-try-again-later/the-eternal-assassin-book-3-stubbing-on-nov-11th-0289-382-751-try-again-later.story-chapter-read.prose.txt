Admittedly, Asher was more than a little confused by what Kree told him as they raced toward the spot Draken had apparently taken up. For one, Draken was supposed to be placed under permanent house arrest. In exchange for being allowed to live within the city despite his forbidden Death element, he wasn’t allowed to leave his manor without express permission of his ‘handler’ back in the capital.

Though Asher supposed the fact that his manor was no longer in one piece and the city was collapsing might be a good point to argue in his favor if this ever went to court.

Even so, that wasn’t the most confusing bit. While Draken was a terrifying force to go up against due to his ability to kill anyone around with him little more than an errant thought, his Death Aura would be utterly useless against the undead horde flooding into the city. Try as he might, Asher struggled to think of a way Draken could meaningfully contribute to the city’s defenses that would require him to leave his manor.

Right up until they reached the main market for the second ring and he spotted another familiar face dashing around so fast he could barely even keep track of him.

“Asher!” Smiles shouted. The undead was laughing with child-like glee as he ping-ponged between a dozen different undead, his shining sword moving so fast that it left a jagged afterimage in the air as he stuck them all down in a split second. Despite the fact that Smiles had lost a lot of his speed after being severed from the King of Death, he was still the fastest thing Asher had ever witnessed. A fact that was made readily apparent as Smiles raced around the square and killed another thirty or so zombies before stopping before them, all before Asher could even say anything. Laughing at the look on his face, Smiles grinned. “Guess what? I’m the ambush now!”

“Reap!” Thunder shouted, sending one of the large undead flying with a crack of electricity as her mace smashed into the bottom of its head and blasted it open. “What are you doing here?!” Draken was standing with the fountain at his back, doing his best not to look worried while Crush worked to defend him from all sides. The large man was shining like a newly waxed statue, his entire body covered in iron as he smashed apart any zombie that dared get anywhere close to his boss.

“I should be asking you that!” Asher shouted, racing over to them with Kree at his side as Smiles took off and went back to ripping his way through the undead horde. “Why did you leave the manor?!”

“Draken knew this would be the place we could do the most good, and Smiles can’t go more than a hundred feet from him,” Thunder explained, turning and clapping her hands together. A thin, narrow shockwave rocketed out from her position, smashing into a zombie and blowing the thing up before it could sink its teeth into a man who had fallen to the ground. “Would you believe there’s apparently a bylaws section of the contract he agreed to regarding defending the city? According to Draken, he’s allowed to leave the manor in the event something like this is happening. Though I think he would have done so anyway.”

“No time for that, I need your help,” Asher said. Yanking Rosh’s crumpled body out of his rift, he gently placed the man on the ground. Kree’s breath caught as he took in Rosh’s current state, and Thunder’s lips formed a thin line.

“Reap… I don’t—”

“Zap him!” Asher demanded, pausing to hurl one of his daggers at a distant zombie going after the same fallen man Thunder had just saved. The man seemed to have broken down, and was curled up in a ball on the ground, frozen in fear. “And Kree, would you go save that idiot over there?!”

“On it!” Kree said, vanishing into his own shadow and shooting off across the ground as a black smudge. After he left, Thunder sighed. Shaking her head, she leaned down and placed a hand on Rosh’s chest.

“Here goes,” she said, grunting as she sent a wave of electricity coursing through his body. Rosh’s entire body jerked under the power of her skill, and he gasped, coughing as he tried and failed to sit up with a shattered spine. Before he could lose consciousness again, Asher screamed in his ear.

“Use your skill!”

As if Asher’s shout had shocked him more than Thunder’s electricity, Rosh’s head recoiled. Thankfully, he still listened. A brilliant, violet light erupted from Rosh’s ruined body, blinding everyone nearby and causing them to look away as wooden shards and stone fragments were pushed out of healing flesh and his limbs cracked and twisted back into position. The light lasted a few seconds, before winking out just as quickly as it had come. Just like when he’d used the skill to mend his shattered legs down in Loratta’s hideout, Rosh sat there, blinking as he looked up at them, without so much as a scratch on his body.

“…Eighteen thousand, one hundred and sixty,” Rosh muttered, giving Asher a crooked grin. “…You were a little over.”

“Keep the change,” Asher said. Letting out a sigh of relief, he then yanked fifty thousand shards from his core and shoved them into Rosh’s chest. “In fact, have some more change. Whikoga is a warzone right now, and if you continue to refuse to sit things out within my Personal Rift, then at least be ready to defend yourself.”

“That skill of yours scares me more than anything,” Rosh said, absorbing the shards as he took Asher’s hand and got to his feet. “…Though I think I’ll accept your charity just this once.”

“Oh, are people getting free stuff?” Smiles asked. The undead stopped before them so suddenly that bits of undead viscera shot off him and splattered all over Thunder, much to the woman’s annoyance. “Can I have my old sword back? I’ve barely killed a few hundred zombies and I’m already on my fifth sword! They keep cracking!”

“You know what… Merry Christmas,” Asher said, yanking the undead’s old soulsteel shortsword out of his rift and tossing it to him. “Don’t make me regret this. Or, you know, putting so much effort into keeping you ‘alive’ in the first place.”

“I’ll do my best!” Smiles grinned, before blitzing away to keep carving through the new undead.

“I think after the dragon flying around up there, Smiles has probably killed more undead than anyone else,” Thunder muttered as she wiped bits of flesh out of her face. “He’s a real piece of work… but damn is he good at killing. Thousands of people are going to owe him their lives when all this is said and done, and Draken by proxy.”

“Maybe Draken will get a little more wiggle room regarding his house arrest,” Asher said, making a mental note to have another conversation with Donvath about that. He’d asked about Draken’s position a long time ago, but Donvath had simply given him an odd look before shrugging and stating that it was more so up to the Frent family than him. Most of the cities in the Noala Kingdom were more under the rule of the noble families in charge of them than his own, as much as he hated to admit it. But now that Asher was doing Elnor a favor by saving her city…

Speaking of Elnor, Asher blinked when he realized the giant dragon in the sky was now hovering in one place, its head craning this way and that as if hunting for something. Wondering if that something might be him they were hunting for, Asher clapped Thunder on the back.

“Thanks for your help with Rosh. Keep him safe?”

The two of them watched as timid-little Rosh walked up to a large zombie that was roaring and barreling down on him. Clenching his eyes shut, Rosh’s fist glowed a brilliant violet, and he swung with quite possibly the worst form Asher had ever seen. Despite that, the moment his glowing fist connected with the eight-foot-tall zombie, the undead abomination practically evaporated. Its entire chest caved in instantly, and the remains of the zombie were sent flying back over the wall dividing the first and second ring.

“…I think he’s doing a fine job of that himself,” Thunder muttered as she stared at the logistician with new respect.

Strength of Shards… Asher thought, shaking his head before triggering Recall and appearing on the back of the dragon.

“There you are!” Leighann shouted, grabbing him to make sure he didn’t fall off. “I found the lich! I know where it’s hiding!”

“Fantastic!” Asher replied. Gripping his daggers, he glanced at Elnor in confusion. “Why didn’t you go after it yet?”

“Donvath already told me how you are the only one who can truly kill them,” Elnor explained, flapping heavily to keep them from plummeting to the ground. “As much as I want to fly down there and roast that monster alive, I’m willing to leave the job to you. I’ve got my hands full defending the city as it is. We need the full third and fourth ring intact in order to hold all the refugees. If the zombies overtake the second ring and break into the third, there won’t be any rebuilding Whikoga. The city and everyone in it will be killed for good.”

“Then let’s make sure that doesn’t happen,” Asher said, looking toward Leighann. “Where is the lich hiding?”

“There’s only a single pocket of space in the entire city that I can’t see through, no matter how hard I try,” Leighann explained, pointing down toward the first ring. “Between those three collapsed buildings. It hasn’t moved in all the time we were waiting for you to come back.”

“They know that within the city’s walls is the only place safe from Elnor’s dragon’s fire,” Asher muttered, nodding as he prepared himself. “Elnor, can you fly above the spot? I can take care of the dismount myself.”

“Good luck,” she said, her words rumbling through his chest as she flapped and they shot off toward the first ring. “Oh, and Reaper. Once we’re done here and the kingdom isn’t at risk of utter destruction… I’d enjoy having a little chat with you sometime soon. There are a number of things I would like to discuss.”

“Oh,” Asher said. He felt appropriately nervous when the dragon large enough to eat him in a single bite glanced over her shoulder at him, her large eye feeling as though it were peering into his soul. “Sure, that’s fine.”

“Good. We are here.”

With that, Elnor did a barrel roll of all things, tossing Asher off her back and sending him careening down toward the ground even as Leighann hooped and hollered from her safe spot secured in her saddle. Seeing as he was already preparing to leap off the dragon, Asher merely rolled his eyes and angled his body so he was falling correctly. He could see the large undead hiding in between the buildings Leighann had pointed out, and he narrowed his eyes as he shot toward it.

The fact that the undead was the only one out of countless thousands that was staying put and not rushing toward the center of the city was all the confirmation he needed that something was up with this one.

Placing an Eternal Mark on one of his daggers, Asher hurled it toward the undead, throwing out his own arms and legs to increase air resistance and ensure the dagger would land before he did. The moment the dagger impacted the ground, he Recalled, cancelling his momentum as he appeared directly in front of the large undead. The monster didn’t even have a chance to react before Asher cleaved it in half with an elongated Crescent, sending it toppling to the ground. Throwing up a Spatial Lock to make sure it couldn’t teleport away in case there was some trick, Asher frowned while he took in the weird undead.

Now that he was up close to it, it was obvious this large undead was different from the others. While most of the zombies looked like corpses with plants enhancing them, this one looked like the opposite. It almost looked like a tree that had been grown into the shape of a large human. What was really freaky was the fact that it seemed to have a skeleton and musculature, and Asher shivered as he turned it over with his foot.

Whatever this thing was, it definitely wasn’t a lich. However, there was a giant, glowing crystal almost as large as Asher’s own head embedded in its chest.

“That doesn’t sound good,” Asher muttered. He hesitated for only a moment before yanking out his void dagger and plunging it into the control node. The moment he did so, the crystal’s glow vanished as it went inert and died in his hands. Sheathing his void dagger, he turned to look at the top of Whikoga’s outer wall. The seemingly endless wave of zombies pouring over the wall came to a grinding halt as the zombies stopped moving as one. Some of them still stumbled over and crashed down on the other side, but just as many began wandering around in confusion, now acting far more like regular zombies that one might see in a classic horror film back on Earth.

Before Asher could wonder what that meant, he jumped when he felt a vibrating in his soul. Whipping out his sending stone, he pressed the rune bound to Rulfar.

“Rulfar? What happened?”

“We took down the golem,” Rulfar explained, panting as though he himself had been the one to wrestle the colossal construct to the ground. “Only… there’s no lich. Instead—”

“You found a control node?” Asher asked, his stomach sinking as he looked at the inert crystal in his hand.

“Yeah. How did you know?”

“I found one too,” Asher said, tossing the crystal into his rift for the time being. Rulfar was silent for a moment, no doubt wondering the same thing he was.

“But in that case… Where are the last two liches?”

“I have a bad feeling we’re going to learn the answer to that question soon enough,” Asher sighed.
