Chapter II.X (2.10)

Sterling was pissed. Mitsuko hadn’t seen him this angry ever. Not even when her ex-fiancé had stabbed him and forced him into the body of a dying mouse.

“Listen to me! I know what I’m saying!” He paced back and forth on the deck. He’d waited until after she’d saved The Selcouth Sable before launching into a lecture. “Have I done nothing to earn your trust?

“I’m sor—”

“You acted like a juvenile simpleton who insists on disobeying out of principle! I know actual toddlers with better listening skills! Just because a sword is new and shiny doesn’t mean you immediately shove it down your throat!”

Mitsuko crossed her arms. “What happened? I’m using a question.”

Sterling continued to pace and mumble to himself. Then he turned to glare at her. “You summoned the attention of a Titan. Taiyo, Helios, Sol, the Radiant One. Whatever you want to call him. He’s one of the original Titans. He is the sun that brings life to our solar system.”

Mitsuko frowned. “I’ve heard the term Titan before. Isn’t…The World Dungeon a Titan?”

“Yes! And would you like to jump down an abyss of the Great Labyrinth Sekai and start pissing him off? NO! You wouldn’t. Because that would result in your immediate death!”

“Are they related? Sekai and Taiyo?” Mitsuko asked. She squinted and stared up at the sun overhead. She raised a hand instinctively to shade her eyes, but of course that blocked the sun from view. “How did the sun kill us?”

“Yes, they’re related. They’re two of the original Titans. And he created a solar flare directed at our location. It would have eviscerated us and the entire archipelago if I hadn’t pulled us from the loop. We’re lucky he hasn’t bothered to repeat the action. He likely slapped you down out of instinct and has already forgotten us.”

“Wait. The sun can remember between loops?”

“Of course he can! Listen when I speak! This is a Titan you decided to pick a fight with. Why do you think light continues to pour down on us despite time being frozen on the outside world? If he didn’t have authority still we’d be dragging around imprints of shadow everywhere we went as we pushed the light aside.”

“What? Is that how light works?”

“It’s one theory we sages discussed as a hypothetical back when we designed the Prismatic Spiral. The point was moot, as we knew Taiyo wouldn’t be deterred by our experiment, but it was an interesting thought experiment.”

“Why can I cast Deteriorate but not Mend?”

“Both are foolhardy. Attracting Taiyo’s attention in any way is a task for dead idiots. But Deteriorate pulls on Taiyo to give more. It’s his natural state of being so he likely didn’t even notice at your level of power. But Mend sends that light back. That he will undoubtedly notice.”

“Okay…what about light not from Taiyo? Could I cast Mend on a candle in a dark cave?”

“It would result only in momentary darkness. You’re welcome to experiment with candles all you want. But keep Taiyo away from your spell.”

Mitsuko leaned on the ship's gunwales and tapped her fingers on the wood. She felt like she should be taking notes right now. Several of the sailors were giving her odd glances as she held what must sound like a one-sided conversation with the ship’s cat. She likely appeared a lunatic in their eyes. An extremely dangerous lunatic who just single-handidly chased off a kraken. But their opinion mattered very little to her at the moment. So long as Holly could still convince the captain to bring her over to Porcelain Island that was all she cared about.

“So the Prismatic Spiral can shut down a minor immortal like my friend Wan. But it stands no chance at stopping something as powerful as the sun.”

“Is that another question?” Sterling asked. There was still irritation in his tone, but at least he was starting to relax a bit.

“No. Simply a comment.” She currently had a surplus of questions on hand. Five more if she counted correctly. But she didn’t want to waste them all.

Eventually, Mitsuko retired back to her cabin to repeat the conversation she’d had with Holly. Already the same questions, responses, and statements in their conversation felt stale and old. Mitsuko couldn’t imagine going through the motions of this thirty more times.

But, once again, Holly was more than happy to help out and secure the captain’s ship for her. Mitsuko was confident in the gnome’s silver tongue. After all, she’d already accomplished it once in the previous loop, it shouldn’t be all that different this time around.

When they docked at Mauve Island, Mitsuko split ways with everyone. Instead of retracing her steps and returning to the clocktower or shops, Mitsuko walked beyond the town. As she passed the inn, Fairy’s Grotto, she stopped and bowed her head. Inside, resting on a bed on the second floor, her friend Wan lay comatose. It might even be a condition worse than comatose. It seemed to be a state indistinguishable from death. She hoped he felt no pain.

“If you’re hoping your Mend spell will have advanced enough to bring your friend back to consciousness, I am saddened that I must be the one to tell you that is not the case,” Sterling said. He had been with her last time she visited this inn and discovered Wan’s state.

She gazed up at the purple vines of ivy that grew across the stone inn’s exterior. A little poof of purple smoke puffed out of the chimney at the roof’s apex. A peaceful inn with a lovely garden. The ivy matched the aesthetic while simultaneously functioning as an extremely effective security measure, as Mitsuko distinctly recalled from her break-in. Every one of those vines could be activated at a moment’s notice to lock down intruders.

But Mitsuko wasn’t here to break into the inn this loop. Instead, she continued on, traveling down a winding path into the forest behind Fairy’s Grotto.

“Oh! I understand your intention now,” Sterling exclaimed as he padded after her. “I made mention of using your spells in combat to enhance their levels further.”

“You did,” Mitsuko agreed.

She was about to use her blade to hack apart foliage in her way, but stopped herself before creating an ice sword. Instead, she cast Mend and sent the violet plants. They receded, cast back in time to a state of growth where they were mere sprouts.

“I unlocked ‘Mend Others’ in an earlier loop,” she said. “But as far as I’ve experimented, it’s remained pretty limited. These plants though I can send months back in time.”

“An interesting observation,” Sterling said. “Yes, different living creatures have different metrics in regards to my spell. Plants tend to be far more cooperative than humans. You’ll be able to control them with far more ease than humanoids. And should you ever attempt to use a spell on a Dragon…well, let’s just say you should avoid it.”

“Just like how the sun is unaffected by the Prismatic Spiral.”

Sterling hesitated and clearly considered his words carefully before speaking. “I…suppose that it is along the same vein.”

Mitsuko continued deeper into the forest until she reached the edge of a familiar collapsed stone bridge. The forest split in half, a gorge at the center of it. Not a particularly deep gorge, but one that still required a bridge to pass over.

She tapped a foot on the crumbling stone structure. A chunk tipped over the edge and fell into the ravine.

“Wha’s that?” a voice called out from below. “Someone there? Pay my toll! You pay to cross!”

Mitsuko smiled. She’d found herself a reliable sparring partner.
