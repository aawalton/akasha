James waited with bated breath. He had spent over three thousand coins buying bullets and grenades. He also bought one high potion for personal emergency use, leaving him with only a thousand coins. Delila and the mercs followed suit, mostly buying grenades.

Andrew, the ever-sly genius, had James buy bullets. Without gun barrels, James wondered what use those bullets were, but Andrew explained that bullets could still explode without a gun barrel—you just needed to ignite the primer.

“So?”

“These walls have braziers on them. With hot coals, we can light up the bullets. Look.” Andrew picked up a glowing coal with his gloves and placed it on a bullet’s primer. The bullet exploded, leaving a tiny hole in the wall.

“It’s only effective at short range, though. What a shame. I should have brought the guns you gave to the guild.”

“...Silas didn’t let you take them, huh? They were my guns, you know. At the very least, two of them were.” James recalled giving the guild his Remington rifle and Thompson Contender handgun.

“Sorry. They belong to the guild, and technically the UN owns the guild. We can bring only our own personal weapons. I argued they wouldn’t have any use for registered guns since they weren’t conquering dungeons, but they didn’t listen. That fucking traitor.” Andrew’s face scrunched up like he had swallowed a bitter bug at James’s complaint.

“Well, my arrows work better with weapon skills than guns would because they have homing abilities, so I don’t mind losing those guns. I already have three weapons—no, four if you count the two swords. I’m not planning to bring another. In fact, I might need to lose the axe in the future. You don’t owe me anything,” James muttered.

“Thank you, James. That eased my heart a lot. Damn, I wish I had a teleporting spear. That would make my skill much more powerful,” Andrew said.

“Keep dreaming. The most you’d get is an elemental weapon. Even if it could return to your hand, I doubt it would teleport. Maybe it could float back to you—but it’d be useless if it got lodged in an enemy’s corpse.” James said as he recalled his boomerang axe skill.

“The siege weapons and traps are finished. We also have twelve men to man all the siege engines on the ground and on the towers. They’re ready to fire at your command,” a knight reported to James and Andrew.

“So, we’ll have forty-one men on the wall?”

“Each tower can house ten more men, if you’d like. I believe they can kill more enemies by shooting arrows from the towers rather than from the wall. We can also bring the explosive arrows you purchased up there. So, what shall the plan be, Sir Andrew and Sir James?”

“That’s a good idea,” Andrew nodded.

“Wait a minute, Andrew! Are you serious? What about the wall? You expect us and twenty-one men to hold it by ourselves?” Xiaoming and Bernard complained.

“We have better weapon skills than they do. You guys are at the novice rank, right? Moreover, some of us also have magic. It’d be best to put us at the front. It isn’t discrimination—just putting the best men in the best positions. Is there anyone who can use bow skills? I could put you up there. James, would you prefer to be up there?” Andrew nodded and turned to James.

He also recalled how the count had promised them rewards: if his knights returned safe and sound, he would grant them more rewards. If possible, Andrew aimed for a perfect clear with no deaths in this last floor.

“I ain’t sitting up there,” James scoffed. He might have bow skills, but if the enemies were anubis warriors like before, he’d prefer to get up close and personal with his fire breath and sword skills. He could kill more that way.

“Good. You’ll save more lives by sitting in the front,” Andrew nodded.

“...” Bernard and Xiaoming spat but agreed. They rallied their army; each man carried ten grenades hanging across their hips.

As the timer reached zero, a massive portal—bigger than James had ever seen—spawned across the battlefield. James looked over the plain. It was flat ground with nothing in sight. He thought he would have nothing to worry about, but he was proven wrong soon enough.

Hundreds of hawks and eagles spawned across the battlefield. Along with the minotaurs James had encountered before, anubis warriors also appeared beside them—thousands of them, against a mere army of 121 men. They rallied at a distance, far beyond the range of the trebuchets and ballistas. James had never seen such numbers in his previous dungeon runs.

Massive minotaurs and chariots followed on their flanks. All of them chanted in an unknown language; despair and fear seeped into the mercenaries as they watched the army rally against their cause.

James didn’t wait for the theatrics to finish. He drew his bow and fired an arrow at the crowd, imitating that one archer from the famous movie who fired the first shot against the enemies besieging a fortress.

The golden arrow traced an arc in the sky. James couldn’t see the enemy clearly, but with so many of them lined up before him, he assumed it wouldn’t matter where the arrow landed. It jabbed right into the head of one minotaur. Complete silence followed as the creature dropped onto the plain, unmoving.

“Yeah! Headshot!” James pumped his fist—only for the soldiers and mercs to glare at him.

“What? They’re enemies, right? Why aren’t you shooting?”

The knights readied their arrows, but Andrew stopped them.

“Belay that order. Don’t shoot. They’re still a bit too far away,” Andrew said, raising his hand. “If James can hit them, fine, but we have limited ammunition. Don’t waste it.”

The chanting stopped. The thousands-strong enemy began marching toward their position. James drew another arrow and kept shooting, each shot reaping one monster’s life.

Then came the ballista—their bolts not only reaped one life but sometimes three in a single shot. Then came the trebuchets and catapults; like meteors, the stones smashed against the monsters’ hides. After that came the knights’ bow skills—all forty-one of them raining golden meteors on the approaching horde.

Alas, it was but a trickle. What could a mere hundred men do against thousands of enemies? The beasts came upon the fort like a tsunami. James wondered how they would scale the wall without ladders, but these monsters didn’t need any. Their teeth gripped their rugged weapons, and their sharp claws dug into the stone, propelling their climb.

“They are coming, prepare your weapon skills!”

James kept shooting arrows as long as he could. When their claws peeked out from the wall, they were met with mind and ice magic. Again, the mind magic was effective at dislodging the dogs and bulls from the wall. James drew his sword and lunged at another creature that had made it to the top.

Other mercenaries and knights didn’t stay still. The knights used their spear skills in harmony, defending their frontline from the mad horde. Then there were the mercenaries. They had some teamwork except for James’s. But their firepower was lacking without magic. Sanders was crucial in keeping Andrew’s team afloat.

Andrew was amazed at the knights’ efficiency. He would love to have such discipline, but survivors were given random weapons from the system.

James used fire breath on the climbing dogs and bulls. His confidence rose with every monster he dropped from the wall. Golden arrows from the towers rained down on the black mass.

James turned when a shout from his volunteer told him that the wave hadn’t even finished spawning.

“They are bringing the siege tower next!” the volunteer said.

“Knights! Destroy those siege towers! Prepare the explosive arrows! They are bringing the bulk of their forces!” Andrew said. He then turned toward his men. “Grenades!”

“Throw the brazier!” Xiaoming ordered his men to throw the brazier filled with bullets over the wall toward the dark wave of the enemy below. The resulting explosion was like a delayed frag grenade, killing several monsters below while the catapults on the tower brought down the siege towers.

Minutes passed. The waves kept on coming. After the siege towers, came the beetle that had destroyed the settlement before. James could only look at it while clenching his teeth, frustrated. That beetle was impervious to all damage. It marched toward the gate, and once it reached it, the gate would be destroyed and those spawns from hell would come in.

There was nothing he could do now but persevere. James rampaged on the top of the wall as mercenaries and soldiers began giving ground.

“Drink your potion! We have to defend this wall!” Andrew said as fatigue began creeping in. “The last of the enemy has come out from the gate. The end is within sight.”

Andrew rallied his mercenaries as he drove a spear into another creature and threw it over the wall. He then activated a life magic to boost his mercenaries’ stats. In a short amount of time, he could double their stats, but it had cons: the fatigue would also double. This new magic proved to tilt the scale back when the enemy had gained ground.

“We should retreat. The towers are still standing — we could bottleneck them on the main street instead of on the wall.” A knight said as he saw his fellow knights falter as fatigue seeped in. The wave was relentless. The defenders buckled under pressure.

“All right! Sound the retreat! Throw your remaining grenades at them!” Andrew shouted as another breach formed on the wall. From within, countless soldiers poured in. It was only because of the modern weaponry the mercenaries had that they could stave off the wave. Andrew spat at the difficulty spike this dungeon had. This assault was even harsher compared to when they tried to reignite the star in the previous dungeon.

As they retreated to the main street toward the castle, the beetle reached the gate and crushed it with a single charge. It continued, barreling down the main street until James barraged its eye with his ice spells and a lightning bolt and it turned its eye to him, enraged.

“Didn’t think it would work,” James muttered as the beetle turned toward him. It was at that moment the trebuchet struck its legs, incapacitating it. It was a lucky strike—or was it? What would have happened if the group hadn’t purchased the trebuchets?

James didn’t let the thoughts linger and dropped down to the main street, being the last one to retreat. Knights lined up beside Andrew, their bows drawn, and they let loose their arrows.

James didn’t lag behind. Using his new bow skill, Triple Shot, he killed three warriors with each shot as he rendezvoused with Andrew and the knights. Hail of explosions from the explosive arrows still rained down from the towers. With the beetle gone, it would take some time before they climbed the towers within the wall.

“We have to save the guy in the tower…” Andrew said.

“Don’t joke around, Andrew! Just think about how to survive this! You know that a perfect clear is no longer possible with those numbers!” Xiaoming and Bernard scolded him.

“Yes. You are right. All right. I have no more tricks. Let’s just give everything we’ve got. Everyone! Today! We shall live or we shall die! Fight with everything you’ve got! Show them no mercy! Let’s show these bastards what it feels like to face humanity! We are survivors and dungeon conquerors! Charge!”

There were no more tricks, only grit and guts. Andrew accepted the fact as he rallied his men for the final battle.
