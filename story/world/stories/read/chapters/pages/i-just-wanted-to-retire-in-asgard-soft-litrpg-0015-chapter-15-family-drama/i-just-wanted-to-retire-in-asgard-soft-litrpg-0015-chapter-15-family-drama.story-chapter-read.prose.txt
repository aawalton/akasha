Thor had already lost track of Loki. He stood at the edge of Jotunheim, winded, turning in a slow circle.

"If only I had my hammer," he muttered. His hand drifted to the empty space at his hip, where it should have hung. He had no idea whether Loki had taken it or Barry had. And if it was Barry, could he still trust the man at all?

Even if the answer was no, what could he actually do about it? He'd been circling that question ever since Barry first stepped out of that portal.

He shoved the thought away again. Finding Loki came first. After that, keeping her away from Barry, or from whatever he'd become.

He looked around for a flash of green. "Loki! Show yourself!"

Behind him, a portal opened, spilling blue light across the ground. Thor turned. He'd seen this exact one before, in Barry's underground workshop.

"Barry?"

No answer. He waited a moment longer, and still nothing. His empty hand flexed at his hip, already a habit, and without thinking too hard about it, he stepped through anyway.

He came out in a trench at the bottom of a valley and finally saw it: that familiar uru metal, gleaming even in the dark. His hammer, lodged in the stone a few paces ahead, its handle tilted toward him.

"There you are..."

A few long strides took him across the distance. He closed his fist around the handle and worked it free, and the weight of it settled back into his grip, right where it belonged. "Missed you, buddy."

He didn't stop to wonder how it had gotten here. Already, his eyes were on the trench walls, searching for a way out.

Another portal opened at his back almost at once, and the scent reached him before he even turned: something burnt and sweet that coated the back of his throat. Cursed magic. Loki's cursed magic.

"Loookiiiii." The name came out as more growl than word. His tattoos lit beneath his skin, the hammer already crackling in his hand, and he walked through.

Something felt off right away. The ground still held the heat of recent spellwork, and a few paces ahead, there she stood, breathing hard enough that he could hear it across the gap.

"Hello, brother. You found me."

She stood easy anyway, weight balanced, hands loose at her sides, wearing that same half-smile as always, one corner of her mouth lifting while her eyes stayed flat.

Thor started toward her, his legs growing heavier with every step, the ground trembling faintly under each one. "I told you to stay away from him. You never listen."

"Brother..." She stepped back, unhurried, watching the hammer more than his face. "I'm not the problem here. You saw what he did. You were standing right there."

He kept walking. Lightning gathered along the hammer's head, crackling blue, sparking more than usual.

"Thor. That man is not some simple mortal. Whatever he is, he's already surpassed both of us. Both of us, Thor. Us gods."

Thor said nothing. He kept closing the distance.

"So we stop fighting each other and deal with him instead. Kill him before he gets any stronger. Or, if you can't stomach that, we bind him. Put him to work fixing every realm, and make sure he never walks out of whatever cage we build. Because I'm not spending the rest of my life waiting to see what he does next."

"Don't... just don't."

"You saw what he did on Jotunheim, brother. A century of unanswered prayers, and he built something that answers them in an afternoon. We never managed that, not in all that time. Forgive me if it doesn't make me feel safe."

Thor stopped walking. He couldn't argue with any of it. All he could see was Jotunheim minutes after Barry had finished, life moving across ground that had been nothing but frost and ash. He had no yardstick for someone who could do that. It still didn't change his mind.

"I will not hunt the man who just kept a world from collapsing. And I will not put chains on him."

Loki kept her distance, eyes on him, seven spells already glowing in a slow ring behind her shoulders. Waiting.

"You think this ends in gratitude. It doesn't. He answers to no one. Fear that, if you've got any sense left in you."

"I'm more afraid of us deciding who gets to stay free than I am of him." He raised the hammer, and the sky above them answered, clouds pulling in tight and dark. "That's not our call, Loki. So I'm taking you back, and I'm done talking about it."

Loki sucked in a breath to set all seven spells off at once, and the ground under her feet lit blue. She looked down. Then the portal took her.

"Loki!" Thor went in after her without hesitating.

He fell for far longer than any portal should have taken, and landed hard.

He got his feet under him and found himself standing on something gold and mirror-bright, humming with more power than the Bifrost had ever carried.

"Where are we?"

His voice rang back at him from every direction.

"The realm in between, you idiot." Loki's reply came from somewhere off to his side, closer than he'd expected.

They were standing on a branch of Yggdrasil. Threads of light rose off the gold and hung in the air, turning slowly, and the hum of it made Thor's teeth ache. His skin crawled, as if the place were pulling at every part of him at once.

"The place Father used to talk about," Thor said quietly, looking out at it.

"See?" Loki said. "He already has access to realms we didn't even know existed."

Barry huffed, set another timer, and marked a fresh set of coordinates in the realm in between. A quick look at his screen gave him their vitals. He raised the recorder.

"Thor, vitals steady but dropping. Loki, vitals approaching dangerous levels." He clicked the recorder off.

Not being able to see what was happening in there was driving him crazy. Too many restrictions. He could bypass them, but that would take time.

"If you two survive in there, that place isn't exclusive to Odin."

He turned back to his board. The map of the nine realms was much clearer now, every location and coordinate written out. He'd read nearly every book he could get his hands on, everything except the ones Odin kept hidden. Those were probably about the tree, judging by the gaping holes in everything else he'd found on it. Barry understood why Odin would lock them away. Whoever held that information held the keys to every realm, and Odin wasn't about to hand out admin access.

He slapped himself, hard, and regretted it immediately. His cheek was already throbbing, his eyes watering.

He looked at himself in the mirror. Dark hair, dead brown eyes.

"You," he said, jabbing a finger at the reflection, "need to retire. No godhood, no crown, none of that glorious-king nonsense. You've already done enough. Go back to that one-eyed king, ask him about Ragnarok, and solve it. Or do whatever the fix turns out to be."

He trailed off, rubbing his stinging cheek. "Fine. Once it's solved, you ask about the forbidden knowledge, and then you're done. That's it. Done."

He nodded at the mirror, tugged his jacket straight, and smoothed down his hair. "Okay. Let's go."

He dialed in another set of coordinates, this time for Odin's throne room, and stepped through the portal.

The air in Asgard was different after Jotunheim. Cleaner. No frost, no ash, no scream of prayer energy. Just cool, dry air that smelled of polished stone.

He walked the long halls with his shoulders level and his pace even, telling himself the mission was done. He said it twice before he reached the great doors, and it still didn't sound true.

Odin was already seated, his one eye fixed on the entrance as if he'd been holding that exact posture for hours. Barry didn't bow. He stopped at the marked distance and started talking.

"Mission's done. The realm is stable, just like you asked."

Odin's voice stayed low. "I felt it. The prayer streams out of Jotunheim shifted the moment your so-called second phase went live, and not by a little. Something fundamental moved in one of my realms without my leave. I would like to know what."

"Like I said, the realm is stable. Your prayers are handled."

"I did not ask for the surface results. I asked what you built."

Barry knew that tone. He'd heard it from plenty of managers, and it had never once meant thank you.

"You got the outcome you needed. Why does it matter how I got there?"

Barry kept his hands still. Odin's eye never left his face, and his fingers tightened slowly on the arm of the throne. Somewhere down the hall, a torch popped. Neither of them looked.

"Then tell me, engineer. What did it cost?"

"Some sleep and a lot of bad coffee. I fixed what you gods left broken, that's all."

Barry nodded and turned to leave, then stopped halfway. "If that disease reaches your heart, you're done. And since you wired everything into yourself, the rest goes down with you. Single point of failure, old man."
