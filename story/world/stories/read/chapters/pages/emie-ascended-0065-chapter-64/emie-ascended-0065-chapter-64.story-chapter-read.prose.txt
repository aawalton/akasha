“I’m glad you arrived early,” Veridia greeted me as soon as I arrived in the classroom the next day. “I just received a request that I think you will benefit from assisting with.”

“What kind of request?”

“The local dungeon of one of the smaller cities I manage as a Porter was destroyed by demons a few hours ago,” Veridia explained. “I’ve been tasked with retrieving a beginner dungeon core from a nursery we maintain to replace the one that was destroyed.”

“And you want me to observe?” I guessed.

“Yes. I felt that observing the process would be far more beneficial than merely discussing the topic. I initially planned to demonstrate the process and let you practice with some of the newer cores in the nursery later this week, but an opportunity like this is simply too good to pass up.”

I hoped that didn’t mean she expected me to assist with the process already. Though I already had a general idea of the process, I didn’t feel nearly prepared enough to attempt it myself.

Given barely a moment to say goodbye to Stephen, Veridia teleported us to a location that was both familiar and unfamiliar.

“Are we on the Memorial Retreat peninsula?” I asked.

Looking surprised, Veridia exclaimed, “Oh! You know of it already?”

“Yes. I visited with Stephen not that long ago. We were talking about doing our own little mini-trek through the bronze-ranked dungeons.”

“I’m honestly surprised you’re even aware of the concept of a trek,” Veridia admitted. “It isn’t something that I would expect a relatively new Ascender to know about.”

While I had nothing against Veridia, I didn’t feel inclined to share too much about my background or the advantages I’d been given before ascending. Thankfully, before I needed to come up with a response, my instructor started speaking again.

“In any case, we don’t have enough time to have you practice locating dungeons. We’ll have to do that when we come back later this week. For now, I just want you to pay attention and try to understand as much as you can.”

We’d been walking as we talked, and it didn’t take long to reach a small outcropping of buildings.

I listened as Veridia explained the plan to the dungeon’s caretaker. I was a little worried that the man would push back and try to stop us from removing the dungeon, but it seemed he’d already been informed of our arrival.

Though I was not directly involved in the retrieval and storage of the dungeon core, I was able to observe the process very closely. Veridia also explained every step of the process as she worked, which really helped to reaffirm what I’d already learned.

“Did the dungeon lose a few floors?” I asked once the dungeon was safely stored in an anchored dimensional space that Veridia had created.

It wasn’t a true storage device since the effect was maintained directly instead of being enchanted, but the effect was pretty much the same.

“Yes. That’s not uncommon. The entire process is stressful for a dungeon core, even with the efforts I took to minimize the strain. It’s actually noteworthy that I managed to retain as many floors as I did.”

I could understand that, especially after witnessing the process.

“Alright. We need to hurry up and get this dungeon to Harborton. Without a dungeon in place, the demons are able to open incursion breaches inside the city.”

“How did they even destroy the dungeon in the first place?” I asked.

“Probably the same way it always happens. The defenders didn’t do a good enough job of repelling the demons, and some of them slipped into the dungeon. The last demon attack was several weeks ago, so nobody realized demons were still inside until the dungeon core was destroyed.”

“Really?” I gasped. “I thought the dungeon would be destroyed quickly if it was going to happen.”

Shaking her head, Veridia said, “Not at all. Demons still have to work their way through the dungeon. Most dungeons will throw everything they can at the attacking demons to survive, but they are not always successful.”

“Wouldn’t the other delvers notice?”

“Not always,” Veridia replied with a shrug. “While we don’t believe that demons are sapient, that doesn’t mean they aren’t clever. There are plenty of recorded instances where demons used stealth in numbers or found other ways to remain undetected for extended periods of time.”

Thinking back to the first demon attack that I’d experienced and how it had occurred without warning, I immediately understood what she was saying. In that case, higher-evolution demons had managed to conceal a small army of demons from both direct and indirect observation, and had attacked the city without any warning.

Exactly how they’d bypassed the wards and other protections had never been fully explained, but it had undeniably happened.

The teleport to Harborton was not quite as smooth as normal, though I chalked that up to the difficulty of maintaining a very large dimensional space at the same time.

The city was in absolute disarray when we arrived, and there were several bodies still strewn around the damaged Dungeon Complex.

If I had to guess, the demons who’d destroyed the core had probably wreaked as much havoc as possible before eventually being killed by the city’s protectors. However, I didn’t actually see any demon bodies, so it was also possible the demons had escaped.

I felt the impulse to start healing those in need, but I forced the sentiment down since that was not my role at the moment. There were also several Healers working to heal the injured, which helped smooth the ripple of guilt.

“Placing a dungeon core isn’t all that different from retrieving one, except you do everything in reverse,” Veridia explained as she reached the damaged markers denoting where the old dungeon’s entrance used to be.

Though I caught most of what she was doing, there were some things that I missed since I didn’t have a true sensory skill with which to observe. By reaching out to the ambient essence in the environment, I was able to perceive a little more than I would have otherwise, but it still wasn’t anywhere close to what I’d been able to perceive before ascending.

In times like this, I felt the loss of Spatial Sense distinctly. I’d need to make it a point to find a way to recover the skill with my next evolution if I didn’t manage to acquire a skillbook for it beforehand.

“How long before the dungeon is accessible?” I asked when Veridia stepped away from the newly manifested dungeon entrance.

“It’s accessible now,” she replied.

“That fast?”

“Yes. As soon as the dungeon reset its anchor, it was good to go.”

“What rank was the old dungeon?” I asked.

“I believe it was late steel-ranked, nearing silver.”

If it had been close to evolving, that would explain why it had taken the demons several weeks to reach and destroy the dungeon’s core.

“Is having the dungeon replaced by a beginner dungeon going to cause problems for the city?”

“Undoubtedly,” Veridia replied. “However, it’s still a better result than not replacing the dungeon. It is very likely many of the city’s residents will migrate to other cities, which means I’ll likely be very busy in the coming weeks and months.”

“Would it help if I set a waypoint here?”

“You can if you’d like. Right now, I’m the only Porter with access to the Portal skill covering the city. Having a backup probably wouldn’t be a bad thing,” Veridia conceded.

“Okay. Then I’ll make sure to do that.”

In truth, the small city had already made enough of an impression on me that I was pretty sure I wouldn’t forget it anytime soon, but just to be sure, I went ahead and used my circlet to create an imprint of the city’s arrival platform.

“Your day was a lot more exciting than mine,” Stephen told me after I explained how I’d spent the day. “Believe it or not, they had me doing Archery training with weighted arrows meant to simulate the artifacts they use to close incursion breaches.”

“I thought they were having you train in how to serve as a guard while I located and retrieved dungeon cores?”

“They were going to do that, but after I told them about my shapeshifting ability, they changed their mind.”

“So, you told them, then?”

Stephen nodded. “Yes. It made more sense considering it’s a matter of your safety. I don’t really care if people react badly to my skill as long as it keeps you safe.”

Stephen’s declaration made my chest feel warm, even if a small part of me wanted to insist that I did not need that kind of protection. However, I was rational enough to recognize that safety came before pride.

“Still, what does Archery have to do with shapeshifting?” I asked curiously.

“Someone suggested that I practice shooting an artifact after shifting from one of my flying forms. Once they realized that I could keep watch from above, the decision was made to shift my focus in that direction, and you know how good idea fairies work,” he said with a grin.

I’d told him about the concept of ‘good idea fairies’– a slang term I’d picked up from my military father. I wasn’t sure if the term had ever really been popularized widely, but as a young girl, I’d always found the term funny.

Suppressing a grin of my own, I asked, “Wouldn’t that basically be the same thing as just teleporting to a location and shooting? I don’t really see the point.”

Stephen shrugged. “I can’t say that I see it either, but it was good training, regardless.”

“So, are you not going to serve as my bodyguard then?”

“Probably not directly,” Stephen replied. “But we knew that was likely, given how weak we both are. At least this way I’ll actually be useful.”

I didn’t bring up the fact that his usefulness as overwatch was somewhat limited by his inability to clearly communicate in beast form. Still, even a warning of impending danger was better than nothing.
