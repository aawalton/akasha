Chapter V.XL (5.40) - Just a Bedtime Story

“Just told a bedtime story from my childhood. If he found it relatable, that’s not my problem,” Kizu replied.

The students were spread out on the beach, sitting on the blankets they’d brought along and eating their packed meals. This was not the intended lunch spot, but it was good enough and something to keep the children distracted while Kizu spoke to Ms. Reads.

He faced Ms. Reads and Dean’s mother. The former’s posture was hunched and eyes weary while the latter looked ready to pull out his spinal cord, her fingers curled like gnarled claws.

“You goaded him,” the angry mother growled.

Kizu rolled his eyes. “Hinata said he wanted to hear a story so I told one. It’s not that deep.”

“Yes, we’ve heard the retelling of your cautionary tale from three different students,” Ms. Reads said. “And… is it my understanding you were raised by a witch?”

Kizu chuckled. “What gave it away? The green hair or the monkey familiar? I’ve not kept my origin a secret from anyone. If my stories are too mature for children, then I’m sorry. They’re all I know. I’m used to addressing more…” Kizu paused, searching for the right words for a few seconds. Then he found them. “Emotionally grounded children. Ones less ruled by base instinct.”

“You dare!” The mother stepped forward, right in Kizu’s face. Or… she would be if she didn’t stand nearly a head shorter than him.

“Mrs. Harrow, your child did punch Mr. Kaga,” Ms. Reads said. “He has a right to say that Dean was out of line. Violence is never an acceptable response in my classroom.”

“We’re not in your classroom.” Harrow seethed, not removing her glare from Kizu.

Ms. Reads rubbed her temples with her thumbs. The poor woman looked at the verge of collapsing. “I am a basic education teacher for children. Not a law professor. I don’t have the energy for semantics.”

Harrow turned on a heel to shift her glare. “You don’t have the energy for—”

Whatever nonsense the woman was about to spout, it was cut off by a child’s scream.

All three of them shifted their attention in an instant. Surprisingly, while it involved Dean, it wasn’t Anata, Mae, or Rui involved. The three of them had been sitting on a flora patterned blanket off to the side with Kon and Mort. No, the girl Dean was currently pummeling was the know-it-all with pigtails. She was curled up on the sand, Dean over her, kicking her downed body.

Kizu stepped past the two adults, about to jump over to Dean and remove him from the confrontation. But, strangely, he wasn’t fast enough.

Rui hurled himself at Dean. The bully’s eyes bulged as Rui’s head took him in the gut and he toppled over.

“YOU DON’T HIT GIRLS!”

The two of them rolled in the sand, a bundle of flailing limbs as they punched and kicked while they wrestled.

It only lasted a few seconds. Kizu successfully separated the two boys and put Dean in a box of barriers. The angry boy screamed and fruitlessly punched the walls. Using a technique from a book Wakino had lent him, Kizu reduced the texture of the barriers, making them a bit softer so as to not let the boy stupidly break his own hand in his tantrum.

Turning his back on the imprisoned boy, Kizu’s attention next went to the downed girl, huddled in a ball in the sand. He knelt down next to her.

“Hey, I know that was an, er, frightening experience,” he said. Not the strongest start and the girl just whimpered, but Kizu decided to barrel forward. “Listen, I’ve been trained as a healer. Show me where you’re hurt and I’ll—”

“RELEASE MY SON THIS INSTANT!”

Kizu stiffened. He took a deep breath. In the corner of his eye, he saw the angry mother stomping up to him, her steps leaving divots in the sand. Rather than deal with her, Kizu sealed himself in a box of barriers.

The woman, completely oblivious to the very visible barriers, smacked into the box like a bird into a window. She fell on her butt and continued to rampage. Kizu added a sound bubble for good measure. Best not to freak out the girl further.

Simultaneously maintaining two barrier boxes and a sound bubble was enough for Kizu to feel a slight bit of strain on his blood reserves. Especially since he was about to start performing healing magic, which wasn’t nearly as efficient as his spatial spells. He’d need to ask Anata for help after all this.

“Listen, nobody will hurt you in here. Can you tell me where you’re hurt?” Kizu coaxed.

The girl finally uncurled herself and looked up at him. Her face was a mess of scratches mixed with wet blood, tears, and sand.

Kizu tore the sleeve off his awful chaperone uniform and wet it with an elemental water spell. The girl flinched as he gently scrubbed off the sand.

“Relax. You’re safe.” Holding the dirty cloth to the side, Kizu washed it with another elemental spell. Then he resumed, dabbing her skin with it. “Tell me what happened. What set Dean off like that?”

“I.…” The girl sniffled loudly and sounded on the verge of crying again. Kizu patiently waited for her to get herself back together.

He silently noted that both Anata and Mae controlled their emotions far better than their classmates. He wondered if that was just their personalities or if their heritage played a role in it as well.

“I said to Dean that the story sounded like him. I pointed out similarities to things he did. He got really angry. Then I said he needs to look out for witches. And… and….” Her eyes watered again at the memory. “I just wanted him to be careful. Mae… she’s a… I mean, she said that she….”

Kizu understood enough. The girl had been scared for her friend and wanted to warn him to avoid Mae, in case the Kitsune girl cast witch magic on him. He clearly hadn’t taken the warning very rationally. Recalling how this girl spoke down to her fellow classmates on their walk, Kizu assumed the delivery hadn’t been the most polite.

Next, now that girl was a bit more relaxed and her face cleaned up, Kizu placed his hand on her forehead and channeled. Scratches and scrapes were easy to repair. They mended together just like during his practice with the square of artificial skin. Those patched up with ease. The bruises forming on her stomach where Dean had punched her were a bit trickier since he had only read theory on bruising. Still, the skin returned to its natural color. Even with that success, Kizu didn’t dare try to repair the black eye slowly coloring around her left eye. Messing with the face was dangerous territory. He felt confident in repairing the scratches there, less so the bruise. It probably would be fine. But there was a slight risk that he might accidentally seal the skin over the eye closed or something similar. The last thing he wanted was a repeat of his experience with Raygen, with himself in her place. No, a bit of bruising around the eye for a couple weeks wasn’t worth the risk of her losing an eye. He explained as much to the girl and she nodded eagerly when he mentioned the risk of messing up his eye.

With that out of the way, Kizu took a deep breath and dropped the sound bubble and winced at the shrill screaming that pierced his ears. He turned around to see how the rest of the class was doing while he’d been working with this girl.

Nothing was on fire. That was about the best thing that could be said.
