Henry had some personal issues to deal with. That’s what the last couple of days had boldly underlined to him. Having spent months fighting for his life, surviving, then reaching for the top had kept him focused and busy. But now that the “top” was merely a couple steps away, he couldn’t see much past it. Like climbing up a peak, only to find his surroundings obscured by fog, rendering him aimless. Of course, he was very aware that there were other peaks to reach for, namely, whatever had created the System and the door, but it was too late for that. He’d stared into the abyss, and the abyss had stared back and asked him, “What now?”

Henry had no answer. Not then, at least. But it was coming along. Between the talk with Zerathstra about grounding himself and not getting lost in his own thousands of fragments, and considering that last chat he had with Maurice, Henry believed he was working toward some sort of new balance. He still felt off-kilter when it came to what the future looked like, but he didn’t need to see too far to know he had to deal with the crisis right in front of his nose first. As for after, well, exploring was always an interesting proposal. Henry had seen many wondrous sights ever since he’d gotten a new lease on life, and he was looking forward to seeing more. Even this stretch of the ocean, no matter how bare it was, was still majestic in its own way.

As Henry and Maurice sank in the depths, chasing after the last orb, there was no sign of the storm above. No flashes of lights, no sudden, massive waves. It was all quiet and calm. The blue stretched endlessly around them, only disturbed by the occasional school of one kind of fish or another. It took Henry a few minutes to see anything below, and now as they approached, the path to the orb curved a little, telling him that they’d need to move east a little as they approached, but it would be easy to correct. As for the seabed, it was nothing like what they’d seen in the Current. Out here, there was no magical source of power and light that kept everything growing. The sandy dunes were a little bare, aside from the occasional reefs that swarmed with gray, richly branching and undulating sieve-like corals that must have adapted to the depths, and after a quick examination, Henry realized that it wasn’t stone or rock that housed such micro-reefs, but skeletons. Some were smaller than others, but the yellowed flashes he could see here and there—especially where part of the reef broke—made it obvious.

“[This place is pretty. But spooky,]” Maurice said, and Henry could only agree as he watched a little pale crab creep its way toward a small, sinewy red fish. He was sorely tempted to stop and explore these odd formations a bit more, but they were on a mission, and he could always come back. So the two kept going, and soon, the two began finding more signs of life. And of civilization.

At first, Henry thought it was more ecosystems that grew off the bone of something large. But unlike their earlier findings, these new reefs were growing on stone, and when he came to a stop to examine one, scraping away the corals to the pleasure of some scavengers and to the dismay of others, he found pocked, but clearly worked stone below.

This only lasted until they reached the chasm.

Henry stared for a moment, sending his Avatars along the edges of the darkness which Find the Path was conveniently pointing them toward while he tried to parse what he was seeing. When considering the story he’d heard, Henry had thought this Merfolk city had been sitting near a trench or something of that sort. But this didn’t look like it was the case. There were no mountainous formations around, and the “hole” in the seabed was only limited to this area right here. As if something had dug out everything below the city just to sink it. As if a sinkhole had suddenly formed below it and swallowed it.

“[You sure the orb’s down there?]” Maurice asked after a few seconds, and Henry confirmed. It was. Find the Path, empowered by the ring that’d been summoned by the other orbs was pointing straight down.

“[It is. I’ll leave some Avatars around for a quick escape if we need to. And you don’t have to come if you’d rather not,]” Henry said, though this time not because he was trying to isolate himself or anything of the sort. The hole was really scary, and the water seemed to darken faster than it should. Already, Henry could vaguely sense something off about the area, but he couldn’t tell exactly what it was. But it was familiar. And it only took him a few more seconds to narrow it down.

It was aura. But it wasn’t actively directed or anything of the sort. It just had a hint of it, like Thalis and its trees had a hint of Zerathstra. This… presence felt like it was asleep. It wasn’t hostile, nor was it paying attention. It was just there, in the darkness itself. It felt like stepping into something’s home. Its domain.

Henry hadn’t known aura could be used in such a way. Or maybe it was a passive effect from spending so much time in a single spot?

“[Alright, then,]” Henry said, peering into the darkness ahead. “[Let’s get to it.]”

***

Even though there had been only the merest hint of light at the lip of the chasm, the light quickly fled as the two dove into the sinkhole. The water quickly turned glacial, then, for a lack of a better word, it became… different. It wasn’t about the cold, or pressure. Henry had faced and endured harsher environments. But it was almost as if the cold and darkness itself were seeping into his body and spirit. Numbing his skin and flesh. Muffling his thoughts—or at least, trying to. Still, Henry found himself focusing further on the path still leading straight down, and as he swam, he began seeing things. At first, he thought the darkness was playing tricks on him as with every second that went by down here, it was feeling more and more like Henry had dove headfirst into a sense-deprivation tank. But no. It was bits of lights that he was seeing. Barely there, mostly along the edges. And clearly, they were skill-based, because he could feel how they tugged at his attention, attempting to lure him closer.

So Henry approached one of them. Not because it had succeeded in affecting him, but because he’d been swimming right in the middle of the sinkhole, and well, this was someone else’s home. So better to come in along the walls. Straight in the middle felt too much like barging in, and Henry wasn’t sure if he wanted to start a fight against whatever was waiting down there for them. Not when he could feel their presence so strongly in every breath of water, in every drop of mana, when he couldn’t even sense their actual body anywhere.

“[Henry?]”

Maurice’s voice came out weak, as if a wall had been between them. But this was telepathy, and no such nonsense should be taking place.

“[I’m good. Not taking the bait. Just opting to approach closer to the wall.]”

“[Oh. Okay. Good.]”

The light near the wall blinked a couple of times, growing brighter as they approached and then—

A spike of alarm. Then a shape bolted out of the crevice and into the darkness. Whatever it was that had been attempting to lure them, it didn’t seem like it really wanted to deal with them now that they’d gotten close.

The descent continued. Time seemed to stretch and lose all meaning down here until finally, nearly half an hour later—by Henry’s approximation—they reached the bottom.

They were here. The illusory light of Find the Path had now leveled, pointing straight forward in the inky darkness. Henry tentatively reached forward with his arms, as his eyes were completely blinded, and he sensed stone. Broken stone, but unmistakably worked and shaped stone.

This was the sunken city of the Merfolk. Where the last orb would be, and where the creature that had pulled it down lived. Because now that he was down there, a skill Henry hadn’t felt activate in a while rose from slumber.

Sense Foe (Kraken) had triggered, and it was pointing everywhere around them.
