"That's exactly the problem, stone-dumb fool!" Khanorrh raged, his voice rumbling like an avalanche. "The mountain's memory blurred in those moments! During this chaos the aberrant unleashed, anything could have happened while our senses lay shattered like river stones! The earth itself trembled beneath her touch!"

You and your friends are spies for Wentouk, seeking to undermine our power and take what is ours! And I… I thought you'd help us! I believed in your destiny! What an old fool I am!"

"A stone's silence isn't mercy—it's waiting," Khanorrh said, his voice dropping to a dangerous rumble as he visibly reined in his rage. "I don't know whether you're following Ainorrh's old instructions or if the wind carries your whispers to her even now. Roots run deeper than the tallest peaks, and hers have been spreading for centuries."

His eyes narrowed to glittering slits. "That doesn't matter. What matters is that you're nothing but tools in her hand—foreign rot spreading through our soil. This is clearly a plot to destabilize our position and concentrate all power in one city! In Ainorrh's grasping hands, while the rest of us wither like moss in drought!"

Aira stepped forward, raising her hands in a placating gesture. "Khanorrh," she said, sure that Lila would pass her words, "we are not your enemies. Our roots may be different, but we drink from the same stream of knowledge. You yourself said that the mountain remembers what the wind forgets—your path is to seek understanding across centuries, not isolation. And that's exactly what we've done."

But her words fell on deaf ears. Khanorrh's paranoia had already taken root, burrowing deep into his mind where reason could no longer reach. His eyes, which had momentarily widened at the Splinter's reaction to her words, now narrowed with cold certainty.

"Our roots crack the bedrock," he hissed, his voice barely audible yet somehow filling the entire space. The guards tensed as one organism, their bodies shifting from stillness to coiled readiness at his gesture—a single, cutting motion that seemed to slice through the very air.

He turned his back on them, silhouetted in the doorway like a mountain against the sky. "The earth doesn't weep—it swallows. We will deal with their treachery accordingly. And when we do," he glanced back, his profile hard as flint, "the mountain will remember."

As Khanorrh's silhouette disappeared through the doorway, the guards following like shadows in his wake, the treehouse seemed to exhale—a subtle tremor running through its wooden bones. The rectangular opening that had formed to permit their entry began to transform once more.

The process was a perfect reversal of its creation: the green luminescence returned, tracing the edges of the doorway in pulsing veins that grew brighter with each heartbeat. The wood at the margins began to ripple and flow, living matter responding to an unspoken command. Grain patterns that had been severed now reached toward each other like seeking fingers, reconnecting strand by strand.

Layer upon layer, the wood knitted itself together, filling the space from the edges inward. What had peeled back now folded forward again, the material growing and thickening until the last sliver of outside light was extinguished. Within moments, where the doorway had stood was nothing but seamless wood once more, the grain patterns swirling in that same unnatural vortex as if no breach had ever existed.

The only evidence of their visitors was the lingering tension in the air and the faint, earthy scent of Khanorrh's presence—the smell of stone warmed by the summer sun. They were sealed in again, prisoners of a living cage that obeyed only the will of Ziemrot and its guardians.

"By the fox's tail, this is just great," Lila muttered, pacing the confined space like a caged animal. "We're stuck here tighter than sap in winter. Any ideas on how to shadowfoot our way out of this tree prison?"

Alliot, ever the pragmatist, sat down cross-legged in the center of the cell. "First, we need to calm down. We have to think clearly. Panicking won't help us. Let's use our skills and knowledge to find a solution. Any use from your level-ups?"

"By the twisted branches, Alliot!" Lila snapped, her eyes flashing. "I'm not sure we're ready to take commands from you right now. Or even any guidance on how to behave in this situation. You've got more cracks than a cliffside with this ambush of information! Don't expect us to turn around so fast—we're not leaves in your wind!"

"But I… I apologized," said Alliot. "What else are you expecting from me?"

"That's a great question, solid as oak!" exclaimed Lila, green hair swinging as she turned to face him. "First, maybe something similar to what we expected from you when you caught up with us near Wentouk. A bit of explanation? More trust? Maybe stop keeping your roots showing and actually share your plans? How about that?"

She took a few steps and hit the wood where there was an opening moments ago.

"Ouch! Cold as the cliffs, this wood is strong!" Lila said, nursing her reddened fist. "But also, we'd like to see some actions that would show us that you are a part of this team. And not just a spy, made to snoop on us by Ainorrh. Apologies are like hollow stumps without actions behind them. Start acting like you mean it—roots and all."

"I'll… I'll think what I could do," said Alliot.

"While you are thinking, tell us about this method of contacting Ainorrh," said Aira. "Did you really keep her in the dark or was it all a ruse?"

Alliot stood up and started pacing. As most of the communication was non-verbal and passed through their party chat, it was hard sometimes to pick up non-verbal aspects of the situation. Especially with Alliot. But not this time. Their enlightened defender was visibly agitated. "I never lied to you," Alliot said. "I may have omitted some things. Under command from my superiors, I should say."

"So, were you in contact with your superiors?" asked Lila. "Or were you not?"

"Not since I talked to Ainorrh during my brief stay on Wentouk," Alliot said, his shoulders tensing visibly beneath his shirt.

"When you left us without a warning?" Aira said, her eyes fixed on Alliot's face.

"Right. Then," Alliot said, exhaling slowly. "This method demands a lot of energy. More than you'd imagine. You need to boost the signal. Wentouk is far. It is alike our standard way of talking. Just boosted to cover long distance. The Source of the city, as Khanorrh alluded, is good enough. It can work in the field as well. But it can almost fully deplete a person's energy pool. It isn't used lightheartedly just because someone wants to talk to their boss."

"Well, at least you didn't seem to have a reason to telltale about us yet," said Lila.

"In any case," he continued, glancing at Lila, "until now there was no reason to contact them. And it's not like they can provide us any help. Moreover, I'm sure Khanorrh will treat it as a further act of aggression. And we don't really know what consequences would there be."

"It's not like we have a lot of hope as we are now," said Aira. "It really doesn't seem they would just let us go."

"Let's wait and see what they decide," said Alliot. "The High Shaman isn't the single ruler. There always is a council."

Aira stepped closer to Lila and examined the walls, her fingers tracing the grain patterns. "Alright, Alliot is with us for now, whether we want it or not," she said. "But he's right, we need to figure out what powers would be useful now. These trees are infused with powerful protections. You saw how they reacted to Khanorrh's power. To the power of the Source. We need to find a way to disrupt their energy without causing too much damage. And unless I'm mistaken, we have a perfect tool for that."

At closer inspection, in some places, the light of the day filtered dimly through the wood grain—not from any opening but through the translucent spots where the living treehouse had grown thinnest. Maybe it was a result of the way the tree was growing. Aira stood before the seamless wall, the Splinter of the Worldroot cradled in her palms. Its emerald glow intensified as she raised it toward the barrier, casting eerie shadows across her determined face.

"Worth a try," she muttered, pressing the crystalline fragment against the living wood. "The treehouse responded to its energy before. Maybe it can communicate our intentions more directly."

The moment the Splinter made contact, the connection flared through her Energy Manipulation skill—a vivid network of power suddenly visible to her magical senses. Every fiber of the wood lit up like a map of glowing veins, pulsing with elemental energy that flowed outward from where the Shard touched the barrier. Beneath her fingertips, the wood shuddered and softened, the grain patterns swirling and shifting like ripples in disturbed water.

Encouraged, Aira pressed harder, channeling her focus through the Splinter. The wood groaned—a low, almost pained sound—as a slight depression formed around the crystal, the material drawing back from its touch like flesh from a hot brand. Fibers within the wall structure retracted, creating a tiny hollow no bigger than her fist.

"It's working!" Lila exclaimed, moving to Aira's side.

"Don't celebrate yet," Aira replied, her brow furrowed with concentration. "There's resistance—like pushing against a living current."

She shifted her stance, applying more pressure and willing the opening to expand. The wood yielded another few inches before suddenly stiffening. Fine tendrils of wood snaked outward from the edges of the depression, weaving a complex lattice that began to close the gap almost as quickly as it had formed. For every bit of progress Aira made, the treehouse's defensive response doubled in strength.

Al squeaked in excitement, scampering forward to investigate the small hollow. His tiny nose twitched at the edge of the opening, whiskers quivering as he measured the space. But before he could attempt to squeeze through, the gap constricted further, nearly catching his whiskers as he darted backward.

Sweat beaded on Aira's forehead as she fought to maintain even this small victory. Through her magical senses, she could feel the entire structure working against her—not just the wall before her, but the floor, the ceiling, every fiber of the treehouse coordinated in resistance. This wasn't just a physical barrier; it was a manifestation of Ziemrot's will itself, responding to any attempt at escape with increasingly determined opposition.

After several more minutes of struggle, Aira's arms trembled with effort. The Splinter's glow flickered, then dimmed as if its power was temporarily exhausted against the overwhelming force arrayed against it. The tiny hollow remained—a shallow indent barely deep enough to insert two fingers—but nothing that would allow even the smallest rat passage, let alone three adults.

She lowered the Shard, breathing heavily. "It's too strong," she admitted. "The Splinter can affect it, but the entire treehouse is drawing power from the Source. We're not just fighting this wall—we're fighting the entire mountain."

Lila placed a hand on Aira's shoulder. "Maybe that's not the approach then—we're barking up the wrong root," she said, studying the small indent thoughtfully. "If we can't break through, maybe we need to catch a different leaf. Work with the treehouse somehow instead of against it, like moving through the forest instead of chopping it down."

Aira nodded, examining the faint luminous trails the Splinter had left in the wood. They hadn't disappeared completely but lingered like words in a language she was just beginning to understand—a conversation between elemental powers that she'd only caught fragments of.

"You might be right," she said, turning the Splinter in her hands. "This isn't about raw power. It's about communication. The Source responded to me before—maybe there's another way to reach it, even through these walls."
