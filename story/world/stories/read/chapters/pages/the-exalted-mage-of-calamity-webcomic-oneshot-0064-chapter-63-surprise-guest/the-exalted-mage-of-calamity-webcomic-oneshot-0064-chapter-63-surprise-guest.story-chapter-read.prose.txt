“Surrender if you don’t want her to die,” the man growled.

His blade edged closer to Gwen’s throat, drawing a thin line of blood. His arm was no longer bleeding. He had sealed it with aura. Even so, his condition was far from stable. He knew he was losing.

“Put the dagger down,” Veronica said.

“Stop! Move any closer and she dies,” he barked.

“You kill her, and you die,” Veronica shot back. Her arm barely responded. It felt heavy, as if it had turned to stone again. The damage was severe—torn muscle, fractured bone—but the pain was muted, dulled to a sharp sting rather than a piercing agony.

That was when she noticed. Even now, Gwen was still casting healing magic on her.

It wasn’t necessary. Sage could suppress the pain, adjust her body as needed. The gesture however, wouldn’t go unforgotten.

“…Alright. I surrender,” she said.

Her gaze locked onto the man’s face. He stood inches from Gwen, his expression tight, his breathing uneven.

Her pupils narrowed. A phantom trajectory aligned in her view. Her arms lowered to her sides. The energy gathered around her settled, the faint lift in her hair fading as the charge dispersed.

The man hesitated. “Smart choice,” he muttered. “Don’t try anything.”

Veronica said nothing. Her gaze only sharpened, fixed entirely on him. Her mana rings turned slowly, steady and controlled. All she needed was to wait for the perfect moment. One clean opening.

“Turn around and—”

An invisible burst of kinetic force slammed into his head, quicker than anyone could react to.

“—Argh!” he cried, staggering back as the dagger slipped from Gwen’s throat.

"Run!" Veronica shouted.

Gwen yelped and dropped, rolling across the ground to put distance between them.

The man blinked hard, growling as he tried to refocus on Veronica.

But he never got the chance. A flash of light split the sky, blinding his vision. It descended in an instant, almost like—

A lightning bolt crashed down onto him, the impact scorching the earth and blasting dirt into the air.

The explosion thundered through the clearing, the shockwave rolling outward as the ground blackened beneath it.

For a brief moment, only smoke and crackling embers remained where he had stood. Then, she spotted the man’s charred body, laying on the ground.

Gwen breathed heavily, staring at the spot of billowing smoke where the man had been standing. She glanced back over at Veronica. She noticed her hair glowing purple, a dense field of mana surrounding her. Even with her limited experience as a mage, she’d never seen anything like this before.

“Is… is it over?” she asked.

Veronica exhaled lightly. She raised her palm, summoning another set of triple wings. Another lightning bolt crackled in the sky.

“Yeah. But I’m not taking another chance if he’s still alive.”

“Are you sure it wouldn’t be better to get a city healer?” Gwen asked.

Her hands moved steadily over Veronica’s arm, pouring soft, restorative light into the injured area.

“It’s fine. This is good practice for you. Better than working on a rock monster.”

They were back in Veronica’s room at the inn. She had borrowed a small table and a pair of chairs from the innkeeper. Her arm rested across the tabletop while Gwen sat opposite her, focused on the healing.

“Just remember—Tier-2 variables won’t always behave like lower-tier ones. Sometimes they will, but not always,” Veronica said. “You can shape a similar equation, but that doesn’t mean it will produce the same result. You need to find the exact combination that achieves what you want.”

She watched Gwen’s casting carefully.

“This is called Dynamic Casting—the ability to adjust your spell to match the situation in real time. Out of all the paths, Blooming is the most suited for it.”

Gwen worked through several spells in sequence. Each one served a different purpose. For Blooming mages, there was no single spell that could handle everything.

At least not until the higher tiers.

She began with Gentle Touch, a Tier-1 spell meant to soothe the affected area, reducing swelling and easing circulation. The tension in Veronica’s arm eased slightly as the inflammation dulled.

Next came Renew, a Tier-2 spell that accelerated natural healing. Bruises, cuts, and surface damage began to mend more quickly. However, its effectiveness dropped the deeper the injury went.

Then she applied Mend, a subtle aura that guided the bones back into alignment, encouraging them to repair themselves. The reinforced regeneration would take over from there.

Finally, she used Reinvigorate, increasing cellular regeneration to restore the muscles and tissues beneath the skin. The torn fibers slowly began to knit together, though the process resisted her pace, uneven and sluggish.

Part of that was due to the spell’s Tier.

The rest came from her secondary path: Longevity.

The combination favored gradual strength over immediate effect. The healing would deepen over time, lasting longer, but it lacked upfront impact.

Unlike the Path of Ruin, other paths of magic usually involved a much more complicated, involved process in spellcasting. Healing magic took several spells to heal an injury—until one learned concentric spell layering: the ability to combine multiple spells into one.

That skill would be something she’d teach Gwen later. Once she reached Tier-4.

“For now, just focus on control,” Veronica said. “If needed, I’ll get a potion or see a healer. But this is good experience for you.”

Gwen nodded, sweat forming along her brow as she concentrated.

“Is it better now?” Gwen asked.

Veronica tried to flex her fingers. A sharp pain flared as she did, though Sage dulled most of it.

Her fingers twitched, but the damage was obvious.

“Mm. I can feel it healing,” Veronica said.

If the assassins had been mages, she would have handled them far more easily. Unfortunately, she lacked experience in direct combat—especially at her current tier. She had only been in a handful of real fights, and those came after she had already surpassed most mages.

Even an archer with thousands of kills would struggle in close combat if all they had ever done was fight from a distance.

She could have avoided the injury by entering Exalted Form immediately. But the habit of holding back still lingered. That power used to come with risks when she had Medusa’s curse. And while she could use it now, she couldn’t sustain it for long. At Tier-2, she had nearly crippled her cores in less than twenty minutes.

She was also reserving her strength, just in case there were more hidden enemies out there.

Luckily, her worries were unwarranted.

“What about you?” Veronica asked, glancing at her. “Are you alright?”

Gwen lifted a hand to her neck. A small bandage was wrapped around it. The wound had already been healed, the bleeding stopped. But that wasn’t what Veronica was asking.

“I’m fine. I’ve been in worse situations with Lorence,” Gwen said.

Veronica’s expression didn’t change, but something in her chest tightened.

“But… what now?” Gwen continued. “You killed those assassins. Won’t they send more?”

“Maybe,” Veronica said. “If they do, it just means I need to move faster. I set up a meeting with Dain’s men tomorrow at noon. He’s sending two people to handle things. Once they arrive, I’ll ambush them and question them.”

She rested her hand lightly on the table. “The ones who attacked us today were probably just grunts and knew little about their actual operations. They were only dispatched to get rid of me. That’s mainly why I killed them instead of capturing one of them alive. Tomorrow will be the big catch.”

“What’s your plan?” she asked. “This is all for… that Galland guy, right? The merchant? You think he’s been set up and is being used as a pawn to cover up their tracks, right?”

Veronica nodded. “The details of that shipment are probably stored somewhere in the notary office in the city. Besides getting the lackeys to talk tomorrow, I’ll still need to grab some incriminating documents from the office.”

She sighed before continuing. “The only problem with that is the place is guarded by the Scavengers. Apparently, the owner is related to one of the higher-ups.”

“How do you know all of this?” Gwen asked.

“I’ve been doing my own investigations. I spoke with Helm, one of Galland’s partners who was imprisoned. He’s the one who told me that the evidence might be in the notary’s office.”

She paused for a moment. “And… I have my own reasons for believing it’s there.”

[Someone is approaching.]

A knock sounded at the door.

Both of them turned, eyes fixed on it. Gwen stiffened.

“Is that…” she whispered.

Veronica narrowed her eyes. “No… probably not an assassin,” she said, rising from her seat and walking toward the door. “What assassin knocks?”

Her hand settled on the handle. “Who is it?”

“My name is Emio. I’m from City Hall. Is Veronica Everwells staying here?”

City Hall?

Veronica opened the door.

A young man in uniform stood outside.

“I’m Veronica. What can I do for you?”

He smiled. “It’s nice to meet you, Miss Veronica,” he said. “I’ve been sent to inform you that Lord Tomas is requesting your presence at City Hall. Same meeting room as before. He wishes to speak with you about something related to a guild commission and IDs? I wasn’t given much more information.”

“Ah.”

The matter with those fake merchants. But IDs? Was her identification finished that quickly?

“Alright. Thank you. I’ll head over shortly.”

The man bowed. “Then have a good day, Miss Veronica.”

He turned and left. Veronica closed the door, glancing back at Gwen.

“Who was that?” Gwen asked.

“A messenger from City Hall. The Viscount’s son wants to speak with me about those merchants who staged the request.”

“The ones in the forest? Where Nasha got injured?”

Veronica nodded. “That one.”

She paused for a moment. “Actually… come with me. You were there. You can serve as a witness.”

“Sure,” Gwen said. “And… I should stay close, in case your arm gets worse. I’m not confident I did everything right.”

“That’s fine. Let’s get this over with, then figure out tomorrow.”

After a quick wash and gathering what they needed, they left the inn and headed toward City Hall.

Veronica had Sage divert its focus entirely toward detection, scanning for anyone watching or following them. The two assassins earlier had been tracking her for some time. In a crowded city, it made sense Sage might have struggled to isolate them.

[No hidden threats detected.]

Good. Just keep that up. Don’t worry about maintaining the passive barrier. As long as we’re in the city, they won’t dare attack me out in the open.

They arrived at City Hall and approached the front desk. It was the same receptionist from before.

“Welcome to City Hall. Lord Tomas and his guest are waiting upstairs,” she said politely. “Would you like someone to guide you?”

His guest? Who else was here? Viscount Leopold?

“No, that’s alright. I know the way,” Veronica replied.

She headed upstairs, Gwen following closely behind.

Reaching the door, Veronica pushed it open and stepped inside.

Lord Tomas sat across the room on a couch. Opposite him sat another figure, only the top of his head visible from her angle.

The hair… it looked familiar.

“Ah, Miss Everwells. You’re here,” Tomas said.

He stood and approached, extending his hand. Veronica stepped forward to meet him as Gwen closed the door behind them.

As she crossed the threshold and clasped his hand, her eyes shifted past him.

Then they widened.

The man across from Tomas looked up. The same scrawny frame. The same hunched posture. He stood and extended his hand as well.

“We meet again.”

Veronica reached out and shook it. For a moment, she could only stare.

“Galland?”
