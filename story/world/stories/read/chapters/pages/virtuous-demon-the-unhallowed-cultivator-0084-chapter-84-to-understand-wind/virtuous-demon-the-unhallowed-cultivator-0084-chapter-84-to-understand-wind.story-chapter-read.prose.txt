A wizened old man, with a great white beard going down his chest, watched the world beneath him. But he was not alone.

At the peak of Zhenren’s Throat, he could see it all. Even the great Liu Clan was nothing but a grain of sand in the tapestry of this land. He saw the Obsidian Salt Steeple that stood still for milleniums, perched in the western corner of the ocean, though it occasionally flickered in and out. He saw the unending tornardo that tore half the Crestfell Continent apart, the Fulgurin Chrostrom, drill through the earth endlessly. He saw the foundation of the young divine order, the boundless tower that floated above the Great Nine, looming over the world.

The Divine Sword Saint could even gleam through the schemes of the Revered Beast, his feathers hidden in the realm of his people.

And yet, he knew there was more to the world.

And not just the New World, where Immortals took to the skies as their domains after half the world was destroyed during the Scourge of the Abyssal Worm.

The end of the Third Grand Era.

He looked beneath, to the secrets of the world buried by time.

The vastness and grandeur of this world meant little to the one who could cleave it all in two.

“Worthless corpses latching unto the living. But I understand it all to well, for that is the Grand Testament of Ego,” with a deep gaze, he whispered to the world. When the soft hands dug into his hair, the wrinkles on his face were washed away with a deep smile.

“Little Shu, let me tell you about a small thing about swordsmen,” with a grand voice, the Sword Saint began to teach the young boy atop of his shoulders.

The young boy of a few years listened attentively, while watching the clouds beneath him.

“If you want to truly understand it, you must learn the process of forging. Mankind has forged blades throughout all of time. Whether to cause strife, or preserve life, to reach the skies or conquer the world.”

The clouds rolled over, spanning across the skies beneath Zhenren’s Throat. The numinous flowers grew, uninterrupted by the divine winds. The single tree grew, bearing another fruit.

The flower bed swayed, a beautiful grave for a beautiful person.

“To understand the sword, you must first forge yourself. Like you’re an unpolished piece of metal, you must shape yourself with the flames of Qi, temper your will with the hot and cold vicissitudes of life, grind and polish your edge with the understanding of the world, and mounted with a guard so that you may grasp it without cutting yourself. Only then can you consider yourself a beginner in the way of the sword." With a finger pointed out towards the cloud, the Divine Sword Saint continued, And with a single wave of that index finger, all of the clouds beneath Shuren’s eyes were cleaved in two.

And though the boy didn’t talk, the shine in his eyes at the sight was apparent.

"This is the basics that many don’t understand, for what they see in the sword is a mere tool; a weapon to do their bidding. So first, always begin with yourself.”

That scene had been imprinted in the boy’s mind for life.

So that one day, he would reach the peak, and with a single wave of a grass blade, cut everything in sight.

Shuren felt the drops of water bounce from the waterfall into his body, but each drop would burn before evaporating. He felt each grass blade beneath his feet soften and blacken, and each breath he took made his innards begin to boil.

“I need to gain a greater understanding of his teachings. Using the internal heat of my body as reference, I can slowly change the properties of my Qi to induce a shift in temperature," Shuren talked to himself out of exhilaration, ignoring the discomfort of the heat inside of him.

“If I can achieve Metal Qi, then… Then there may be hope left.”

Though many would prefer meditating until their Spirit Meridians became full, and slowly breaking through with the chance of inspiration or trial and error, and even more would go out into the open, facing enemies to fight and hone their body; Shuren was taught another method from his grandfather.

To forge one’s body, using the reserves of Qi in their Spirit Meridians as flames, the mastery of Qi to shape your body, and the Liu Breathing Style to temper yourself.

First, he would test the limits of his current power in the embroiling heat.

Sweat evaporated off his body before even forming, but his body and muscles didn’t tremble. Inhaling the cold air from the atmosphere, Shuren exhaled a misty breath of abated heat. But his eyes, radiating and almost glowing under the hymn of his Qi, would remain open, without a blink.

Shuren tensed his right arm, and over 90 percent of the Qi Aura shifted to that single spot. Slowly, he spooled out even more Qi, even while maintaining his Ruyi Staff in the distance to remain vertical. Until there was only a thin, glassy veil of Qi on everything else except his right arm, so as to not injure himself by protecting the rest of his muscles.

He’d practiced this single stance at least a million times at this point. He didn’t need to put any more thought into it.

The air cracked as his arm whipped, blurring to the naked eye. For a moment, the waterfall was slightly interrupted, with a flurry of water drops flying everywhere.

Growing red, Shuren’s right arm trembled as he brought it back, relaxing his stance.

“Again,” was all he said with an emotionless voice.

Though his Qi aura had dissipated, all Shuren needed to do was tranfer the Qi that filled his Spirit Meridians out to fuel his body once more. Once more, he brought his body to the brink of exhaustion, following up with the left hand. The right leg. The left leg.

And once he had brought his body to its limit, he would test the limits of his staff next.

But Shuren tried one last movement, in remembrance.

With both arms lifted above his head, his right hand stacked on the left, as if holding an invisible blade, Shuren forced himself to stay still as his Qi aura concentrated as much as possible on his arms, balling at his fists.

With each muscle, with each thought, with each memory, with each sense, Shuren used everything in his body.

He couldn’t hear the chirping of the birds, nor the pattering of the waterfall. He couldn’t feel the whistles of the wind, nor the sweat evaporating off his body. His eyes were wide open, yet he couldn’t perceive the world.

Everything on the line, for one single downward motion.

A slash.

But the world wouldn’t move.

The waterfall continued to slam into the river.

Unencumbered, uninterrupted, unending.

The leaves on the trees hanging off the mountain side continued to sway. The pool of water in the river remained undisturbed.

Shuren took a moment to breathe, ignoring his shaking arms. With a stoic expression Shuren stared at the waterfall, but in the end just scoffed at himself.

“Come,” with an outstretched hand, and the Ruyi Staff twisted and whirled into his hand.

The next step was to use his exhausted body to still bring out the depths of his powers with the staff. And comprehend the barrier between his own Qi and Wind Qi. Through this, it would be easier for him to understand Metal Qi.

I’ve come up with a few simple techniques using my own comprehension and abilities. The staff disperses and controls the air, thus tapping into Wind Qi.

Though Shuren had learnt the ways of the sword, the Seven Clouds Sect focused on teaching him the ways of mastering Qi, using it in unconventional ways. This is how they focused on everyone breaking through to Path Foundation with their own unique style, with the various teachers and elders that pursued different Paths.

A good chunk would still end up pursuing the Cloud, Water, and Wind Path either way in the sect, as it was their specialties.

Shuren twirled the Ruyi Staff on his right fingers adeptly, before grasping it in his hand with a stance. This time, he manipulated his Qi Aura to circle around the weapon and his right hand. At the same time, the capabilities of a Spiritude Artifact allowed him to leave a portion of his Qi; his will, within the staff, which used it as its own reserves. This was how he developed Wind Burst and Staff Purge.

With a single step forward, he twisted his body and threw out a stab in the air, the Ruyi Staff reverberating yet still within the firm grasp of Shuren.

Wind Burst!

An invisible force flew out from the staff, and a large hole formed in the middle of the waterfall.

Then he repeated with his left arm, then again with both arms. And as always, he retained to keep his body on the edge of collapse, while exhausting as much power as possible.

Remember it! The sensation of Wind Qi, the change of my own Qi, and the realm between the two! Make it under my control, and I’ve finally grasped that aspect of the world!

What he needed to do now was to take out the Ruyi Staff as the mediator, and directly grasp Wind Qi himself. By now, half of Shuren’s Spirit Meridians had already emptied.

Now, Shuren returned to his martial stance, the Ruyi Staff this time had fallen to the floor, rolling across the grass. With his Qi aura swelling around his right arm, he focused on the sensation of Wind Qi. He etched the feeling of the staff, the force of the wind, the strength of his Iron Talisbody. Yet, this time he didn’t have more than 90% of his aura around his arm, but only a bit more than the rest of his body.

Once more, the wind cracked like a whip as he punched out, but the force was far weaker. The pool of water remained undisturbed once more. Once more, he pushed the limit of his body, focusing on the sensation between his own Qi and the aspects of the wind.

He felt it, the unchanging, hot Qi that burned his body cooling down for a split second, feeling into a cool sensation, wrapping around the bones and muscles in his arm.

Thin lines ripped out of the skin of his arm, with blood spurting out. The sensation of wind slicing through his arm broke Shuren’s concentration, holding his arm to his face as it trembled.

But the expression on his face was not of shock, but of glee. “Ha! To think I already managed to make Wind Qi directly, without even a weapon catalyst! My only problem was transmuting it too early, causing wind to burst out of my arm instead of my fist,” he exclaimed, a deep grin forming on his face.

Stages 9 to 12 focused entirely on learning the basics of using Qi to tap into the paths, understanding how to change the quality and properties of your Qi to something else entirely. The average person would require catalysts, charms, and weapons that incorporated specific elements to aid a person. Even if you keep expanding your Spirit Meridians slowly through meditation; the absorption and refinement of Qi, you still had to master the usage of Qi.

With that sensation branded in his mind, his eyes glimmered with confidence.

“Again!” he yelled with vigour.

This time he wrapped more Qi around his palm, and lessened it around his arms. With a solid stance, one foot forward, and sending his right arm back, he punched with complete force and rotation.

A wave of water crashed around Shuren, as a massive hole interrupted the entire stream at the end of the waterfall. Thinking of nothing but the method he used, Shuren ignored being doused in water and shifted to punch with his left arm.

“Wind Blast!”

As the Qi pooled around his left palm, it shot out, turning into a force of Wind Qi, continuing to fly across the air to slam into the waterfall once more. An explosion of water drops flew everywhere, and a strange sensation erupted within his body.

All of his Spirit Meridians had slightly expanded, able to store at least a third more Qi than before. When Shuren absorbed the Qi in the vicinity, he could sense the reservoirs feeling a bit more emptier.

“I can see the gate to Path Foundation, it shouldn’t be far off! Stage 12 should be easy enough if I just focus on fueling and expanding my Spirit Meridians!”

With his left hand grabbing on his right shoulder to stretch his body, Shuren held a deep grin as all of his plans were falling into pieces.

“All that’s left is to understand Metal Qi to the point I can control it. If I can’t use the sword, then I’ll just have to break the rules! If I can control Metal Qi to the point of condensing it freely, I can just form a blade made of Qi itself!”

Things were looking great again. If this would succeed, then there was still hope.

Hope to overcome his Cursed Anomaly, and take control over his life again.

“And once I hit Stage 12, I can finally negotiate with Elder Spiritweaver. To become a Core Disciple.”
