Elynara stepped out of the guild half-floating, half-fuming.

The talk about Magitech Civilization ruins got her all excited. But she was also mad at Alicia for all the teasing. Really, why string her along at all? It’s not like she would have left immediately if told.

…Though perhaps it would have been a wee bit distracting.

And indeed, her legs wanted to run the moment her boots hit the stone. Her head spun with spell ideas, magic stuff she might find, maps, materials she would need. And all of it tangled around Alicia’s smug little smile still haunting her thoughts. That twinkle in her eyes. That calm voice. That tray of tasty snacks, and the cookies.

Alicia was a menace.

“She planned this, planned it all,” Elynara muttered to herself.

Of course she did. Alicia always budgets for ingredients, people, and patience. She was far too professional not to. And it showed with her work.

She knew just how to bribe with sugar, reveal with timing, and then vanish for effect.

But even her few half-remembered memories from her past life offered no good solution here. Elynara didn’t know how to deal with people so well-prepared. Other than to avoid playing their games entirely, if possible.

But when would such a thing actually work when her curiosity was involved?

Starpuff trilled loudly from above, looping through the air in quick, tight circles. Its glee was unmistakable.

“Don’t you start,” she said, flicking a hand toward him. “You weren’t the one being toyed with.”

It dipped around her wrist and bonked her elbow before spinning higher, leaving a faint twist of color behind. She couldn’t even tell if it was an apology or an encore.

Starpuff was probably just showing off, either way.

Still, her mouth tugged upward at the antics.

Then, Elynara took the corner toward the church a little faster than she meant to, her scarf trailing behind her. The rhythm of her boots on the cobbles matched her pulse – light and quick. Even her thoughts raced ahead, already at the destination despite the journey forward still to come.

Pondering what to pack, a few tests she could try, and what to refuse to tell Alicia about in revenge. At least until it’s fully confirmed.

She was nice like that!

The bells chimed once as she slipped through the stone archway. Evening shadows reached across the courtyard. Someone had lit the lanterns early, their glow already pooling gold across the walkway.

Elynara moved up to her room, to a protest of a few children demanding butterflies. There she dropped her bag on her cot, barely slowed long enough to close everything back up again, and bee-lined to the dining hall.

Warm stew scent curled through the air, but it barely registered.

Which was a tragedy, but inevitably her appetite could not win with her curiosity, especially when it came to magic. And whatever in the world ‘magitech’ was even supposed to be. To say she was interested would be a severe understatement.

Entering the hall, Father Alaric greeted her with a nod. She returned the greeting, Alaric’s calm presence making her subconsciously relax a little too.

She found Mina waving from across the nearby table, with Pufflet balanced proudly atop a basket of folded cloth. He looked like he had done all the work himself.

Elynara got in line, scooped half a bowl of stew, and then slid into her usual seat. But instead of digging in right away, she stirred it idly with her spoon.

With others busy eating and a moment of quiet… Why not? Why not note down some things first?

Her bowl quickly forgotten, she opened up her notebook on the next empty page. Got her pencil ready. And started jotting her thoughts down.

It started with a quick heading:

"Ruins: Initial Prep."

Then a short checklist. Then a longer one when she felt the short one wasn’t quite enough. Then whatever else she might expect. And stuff. Which somehow turned into several increasingly chaotic sketches of Starpuff wearing a tiny explorer’s hat.

The idea of a green floating puffball in a hat and a lasso proudly buzzing to explain the greatness of Magitech Ruins tickled her funny. Especially once she realized any listeners would just be very confused, unable to understand the buzzy fluff at all.

She didn’t even notice until Mina leaned closer.

“You look possessed.”

“I’m possessed by knowledge of many things,” Elynara said without looking up. “It’s different.”

Mina tilted her head.

“Is that why you’re drawing angry clouds in hats yelling at some ruins?”

Elynara paused. Then glanced down to take in just what she had been drawing up.

There it was, an earlier doodle of hers.

A squiggly brick ruin with eyes and a speech bubble that read, “Not enough mana!”

“I might have gotten a little carried away. Maybe.”

“You think?”

She flipped the page and started a cleaner list. Rations. Pencils. Travel gear. She would need a few more changes of clothes if she wanted that trip to be a comfortable one. And maybe a second notebook.

Mina nudged her with an elbow.

“Are you actually going into danger again?”

Elynara didn’t answer right away, but thought about the possibility nonetheless.

“I don’t really know yet,” she said after a moment. “But even if I do, I’m going to be careful. And I won’t be alone. And I’m better prepared now, stronger. My spells are quite a force to be reckoned with, don’t you think?”

“Hmm.”

That wasn’t doubt in Mina’s voice. And she certainly didn’t sound fearful either. She was still lost in thoughts however.

Elynara turned slightly.

“Worried?”

Mina shrugged, eyes dropping to the table betraying her true feelings.

“Not really. You always come back. I will miss you being here though.”

The words landed quietly, stirring something in Elynara’s chest. And hitting hard enough to actually make her stop thinking about ruins for now.

She had her lovely friend-made-apprentice to spoil and reassure right now.

Elynara smiled, leaning closer in to hug the little girl.

“I always will, and I will miss you too,” she said, soft and sure.

Starpuff floated down and settled at the edge of her bowl, bouncing up and down for the zooms of it. Then it peered at the untouched stew. Trilled disapprovingly and tried to give her the look.

Or so she assumed, the green puffball was hard to read at the best of times. But its intent was clear enough this time around.

“I wasn’t ignoring it,” Elynara said. “Just… forgetting it temporarily.”

The sprite trilled again and began trying to fish out a carrot piece, to no avail.

Mina giggled at Elynara’s words. All while Pufflet perked up and leapt from his perch, but missed the bench and flopped into the basket he was guarding earlier instead.

The girls both laughed at its antics. And after Mina fished Pufflet out, they spent the rest of dinner time chatting happily.

They finished eating just before the final bell. Most of the children had already been herded off to bed. Though the girls lingered in the hall until the last plate had been cleaned and the stew pot scraped bare, helping out together.

Then she said her goodbyes and slipped away.

Back in her room she cracked open her notebook again. This time, her sketches were a little cleaner. She mapped what she remembered from Alicia’s report, then drew out the possible locations, and the way to get there from Oltenar.

Somewhere around the fifth version of a hypothetical mana reactor chamber, her pencil slowed.

Starpuff floated lazily beside her, moving back and forth in small flowing arcs.

“Do you think there will be much still intact?” she whispered.

The sprite bobbed once. That probably wasn’t an answer, but she took it anyway.

Her hand hovered over the page. She added one last note in the corner:

Start from the outer edge. Carefully and slowly. There was no rush.

Then she closed the book.

The lamp flickered gently above her cot. Quiet wrapped around her shoulders like a familiar shawl.

Ruins.

Her excitement has died down a lot by now, but… She was going to see ruins.

Not crumbled stone or abandoned wood – but real, hidden pieces of the old world. Full of untold stories from ages past. And this time, she wouldn’t be reading about it in a library, but exploring and learning on her own.

She would be walking through it.

Starpuff blinked once and curled up above her pillow.

Elynara lay back, eyes wide even as her body pulled toward sleep.

It would take a week to reach the village, with all the planned stops.

And two more days she had left to prepare.

She was going to make them count.

Elynara was halfway through reorganizing her notes when somebody knocked on her door.

Surprised, she got up and opened up. But seeing Mina on the other side, whatever thoughts she might have had about a sudden emergency evaporated.

She quickly invited her friend in. And after a little bit of awkward talking – ending with Mina getting pulled into another hug – they climbed onto the bedding together, careful not to disturb the loose pages spread across the blanket. Pufflet was already there, stretched between them like a sleepy, overgrown scarf. His tail twitched against Elynara’s thigh, lazy and warm.

“You’re going to chase this, aren’t you?” Mina’s voice was soft, but the question came without hesitation.

Elynara’s pencil paused.

She glanced up and met the girl’s eyes.

“Of course. Wouldn’t you?”

Mina frowned. Her fingers picked at a loose thread in the blanket, gaze flicking down for a moment before settling again.

“I don’t know… Probably?” she said. “But I would also worry about you. From what you told me about where you’re going… The Deadzone isn’t safe. We were taught never to go anywhere near there. That the edges alone can twist things.”

The pencil rolled in Elynara’s fingers. Her hand fell still.

Yes, it would be a little dangerous. But the idea of stepping into a place not yet catalogued stirred something eager and bright beneath her ribs. Though truthfully… it wasn’t the history that interested her. It was the potential. And magic.

“I’ll be careful,” she said. “Alicia wouldn’t send me if it wasn’t manageable.”

“You would still go even if she hadn’t,” Mina replied, pulling her knees up.

That earned a short laugh.

“Okay. Maybe.”

Starpuff zipped into view, spinning above them before dropping with full puff-weight into Pufflet’s fur.

The little beast twitched, let out a questioning chirrup – promptly exploded into a sneeze. Then it launched off the bedding and dashed toward the far wall, tail fluffed in full betrayal mode.

Elynara choked on a laugh. Starpuff sat innocently in the middle of the abandoned blanket, looking far too pleased with itself.

“You’re awful,” Elynara told it.

“Prrt.”

Mina giggled, then broke into a full laugh that curled her shoulders forward.

Elynara tried to keep her scowl, but it broke after just a moment of trying. Her cheeks puffed.

“Traitors. All of you.”

“Pufflet is the victim!”

“If your pet wasn’t so fluffy, Starpuff wouldn’t try to ride him! He’s totally complicit.”

Another giggle. Mina leaned back, finally relaxing into the heap of bedding. Her eyes crinkled at the edges, that quiet sparkle Elynara had come to read as comfort.

Silence settled between them again. The girls just laying down, enjoying each other’s company for a while.

Then, gently, Mina spoke up.

“You’ll come back, right?”

Elynara didn’t flinch this time. She gave the question its proper pause, then answered plainly.

“Yes. Always.”

Mina nodded, small but certain.

“Then it’s okay.”

They lay there a while longer, the sounds of the hall thinning as the rest of the church quieted down. Starpuff floated near the rafters now, tracing small loops that left fading lines in the dim glow of the lanterns. Elynara watched the pattern curve once, then double back. A spiral. It really didn’t feel random at all.

“Have you named any of your spells yet?” Mina asked out of nowhere.

Elynara blinked.

“Named them?”

“Yeah. You’re always writing weird things in your notebook. I figured some of them had cool names.”

“...Most of them are still labeled ‘Test 3’ or start off from some idea and then develop into something completely different.”

“That’s awful.”

“They’re descriptive! If not entirely accurate.”

“They’re boring,” Mina said. “Call one Splash Strike or something.”

Elynara narrowed her eyes.

“That’s worse.”

“Okay, what about Aqua Drill? Or Zappy Mist?”

“But my water spells don’t do any drilling – or zapping!” Elynara protested.

Mina burst into another round of giggles.

“You’re no fun.”

“I have plenty of fun. You just aren’t allowed near the naming process.”

“Rude.”

“Your carved offering looked like a duck last week.”

“It worked, didn’t it?”

Elynara snorted, amused.

“The spirits really aren’t too picky, I suppose. Perhaps I should make some one day too.”

“Better make it quack.”

Pufflet padded back into their corner, now thoroughly composed, and flopped against Mina’s side. She scratched under his chin absentmindedly.

The warm air wrapped around them, thick with the faint scent of herbs and old wood. It felt steady, and invited them to sleep.

Elynara pulled her notebook back into her lap, then added a line beneath her last list: Do some more mana training before departure. She paused, then added: Pack cookies.

Mina peeked over.

“Cookies?”

“It is fuel. For spellcasting.”

“Sounds fake.”

“Sounds delicious. And cookie-driven Elynara is definitely stronger than cookie-less!”

They grinned at each other.

Starpuff fluttered down and nudged the edge of the notebook closed, then curled into a ball atop it without asking permission.

Elynara tried to lift it. The puffball expanded and flopped again.

“You are not the boss of me.”

It purred, unconvinced.

Mina yawned, stretching her arms overhead. Her hair stuck to one cheek.

“I’m sleeping now.”

“That’s fair.”

She tugged her blanket closer and settled down, Pufflet shifting to cover her feet.

Elynara stayed upright a little longer, watching the quiet curve of the rafters, the shimmer trails Starpuff had left behind, the slow rise and fall of her friend’s breath.

Then she shifted, pillow tucked behind her back, and finally let her own eyes drift closed.

The ruins would wait a little longer.

For now she had some dreams to catch up on..

The morning light filtered soft and golden through the church’s windows, spilling across the stone floor in quiet shapes. Elynara sat near the courtyard steps, staff balanced across her knees, both hands resting lightly on the carved wood. Her breathing was slow, measured. Mana flowed in slow, deliberate circuits, rising through her chest and sinking again with the rhythm of her breath.

The hum kept her steady and focused on the familiar exercise.

Mostly.

Footsteps approached behind her, not loud, but not hesitant either. She opened one eye and caught the sway of familiar robes in the edge of her vision.

“You’ve heard, then,” Father Alaric said, voice low and warm.

Elynara blinked and sat up just a little straighter.

“You knew?”

He smiled, lines creasing gently at the corners of his eyes.

“I listen. Alicia doesn’t hide things half as well as she believes.” He stepped past the last row of benches and came to stand beside her. “And you hum when you’re thinking too hard.”

“I do not hum.”

“You do,” he said without skipping a beat.

She narrowed her eyes, but the protest died in her throat.

Alaric lowered himself to sit beside her, movements slow but practiced. His knees clicked faintly. He didn’t seem to mind.

“Are you afraid?” he asked.

The question hit her more directly than she would have expected. But Father Alaric could be very to the point when he wanted to be. She decided to answer honestly.

“I don’t know,” she said honestly. “It’s ruins full of wonder and things I have never seen before. Ruins from an era long past. But being so near the Deadzone is…”

She didn’t finish.

He nodded, understanding in the silence.

“The forest holds memory,” he said. “But the Deadzone holds scars. Do not mistake one for the other. Neither is safe without preparation.”

The words landed without worry. Instead speaking only of being cautious.

He placed a hand gently on her shoulder. His palm was warm, his grip light, but steady enough to hold her center. She breathed in through her nose and let the tension slip out with the exhale.

“Go with open eyes,” he said. “And when you return, share what you have seen. That is how memory becomes understanding. And how we learn as people.”

“Mhm. I will,” she promised.

The quiet stretched again.

Then, right in the middle of the peaceful silence, Starpuff – traitor that it was – zipped in from above and bonked her cleanly on the temple.

“Prrt.”

She flailed, staff wobbling, and almost smacked herself in the shin.

“Hey–!”

Alaric chuckled, deep and amused.

“Even your companion agrees, I see.”

Starpuff spiraled away smugly, trailing a brief loop of glittering light behind him.

Elynara rubbed the side of her head and muttered,

“Hmph. It’s threading on thin air.”

But she was smiling now, and Alaric could see it.

They sat a little longer, the only sound the creak of the wood and the quiet hum of the mana-laced air. A breeze slipped in from the open chapel door and tugged at the edge of her robe.

She stood at last, brushing off her skirt, then offered a short bow.

“Thank you.”

Alaric rose slower, leaning briefly on one of the pews.

“No thanks needed. But do return safe and sound. This town is smaller without you.”

She flushed.

“I’m only going for a few days.”

“Then don’t take too long,” he said with a knowing smile.

He turned back toward the hallway. Elynara hesitated a moment longer, gaze lifting to the carved crest above the entrance. It had been worn smooth by time and dust, but she knew every curve by now.

Then she turned and followed the path out.

Starpuff floated ahead already, tail flicking in lazy arcs. Its loops were slower than usual today.

Outside, the sunlight had deepened into late morning brightness. Children ran past toward the garden, laughing at something Pufflet had apparently done to their shared laundry. Mina’s voice shouted after them with just enough authority to make them scatter.

Elynara laughed softly to herself.

She didn’t feel much braver now, yet her mood had lifted a little.

And that was plenty enough. A steadier mind and calmer thoughts lead to better results. Or so she believed. So she had seen and practiced.

The ruins were waiting. So were the nerves.

But first, she needed some supplies. Like twine. And a new waterskin perhaps.

Tomorrow would be packing day.

Today, she could still breathe.

The moon hung low over Oltenar, soft and full, casting pale light across the quiet rooftops. Most of the town had long since gone still. The chapel bells had marked midnight, and even the wind seemed to respect the hush.

Elynara sat by the window, legs tucked beneath her, the wood cool against her thighs.

Her notebook lay open in her lap, a half-sketched diagram curling across the page – a ruin-shaped wedge stacked with question marks and little doodles of possible magical things she might find there. Not that she knew any of them, really. But it was fun to guess.

One of them looked like a sideways potato. She frowned at it.

Then grinned amused.

Starpuff hovered nearby, barely glowing. The light of it waxed and waned in time with her breath. For once, it didn’t pester her or prod for attention. It just lingered around, casting a soft presence, a lot quieter than usual.

Unusual, but Elynara had long since learned there was more to her spirit companion than met the eye. And being considerate was apparently one of those things.

Its hum was almost thoughtful, fitting her own mood.

She stared down at the sketches. A few of them were serious, her attempts to guess what the ruins might contain. But many simply… weren’t. One was simply a stick figure pointing at a rock with dramatic emphasis. Her handwriting beside it read: "Could be a mana battery?!" with a very aggressive underline. Three times.

Her fingers drummed once against the edge of the page, then stilled. The world outside the window was silent. With nothing to disturb the peace. Just the wind brushing lightly over roof tiles and through the narrow alleys.

Elynara didn’t speak for a while, appreciating the quiet. Simply watched the empty street and the distant glimmer of the town watch’s torchlight far down the slope. Then, quietly, more to herself than anyone else, she whispered–

“If the world really is fading… maybe the answers are in those ruins. Or at least a hint of them.”

Starpuff blinked once. Its glow pulsing a faint green.

“Maybe I’ll find something worth bringing back,” she added.

The words weren’t loud. Yet they echoed inside her chest the way good promises did. She didn’t really know what she would find. But she would still go and explore.

She would learn.

After a while, she opened up her notebook again. And doodled a tiny campfire, then a note to herself not to forget socks. Then three socks, in case the first one got eaten by mice. She paused, added a question mark next to that. Then underlined it for good measure.

It would be best not to forget.

Starpuff trilled once, quietly pleased, and nudged her hand. Elynara reached out and let her fingers rest against the warm pulse of it. There was always something oddly steady about its presence. Annoying most of the time.

But now, at this hour, it felt grounding.

Across the room, Mina stirred. Her blanket shifted. One leg kicked out into the air before she muttered, “Don’t… chase ghosts…” and flopped her arm over Pufflet’s belly. The little beast responded with a sleepy snort.

Elynara smiled.

“Too late,” she murmured.

She carefully closed her notebook and held it to her chest for a moment. Then reached down and set it beside her pack, which she had already half-filled earlier that evening – pencils, string, field notes, and an overly aggressive amount of dried bread rolls Alicia insisted on sneaking into her bag when she wasn’t looking.

Tomorrow would start early. She had supplies to check. Mana to cycle. Alicia to glare at again. Probably.

But for now, the window stayed open, and the breeze was gentle. The scent of evening blossoms drifted in from the planter outside. Her eyes began to blur around the edges, and her head tilted slightly toward the curve of the wooden frame.

Starpuff stayed close, pulsing gently, the slow rhythm of its glow matching her breathing. Not entirely asleep. Not entirely awake. Watching.

Waiting.

The next journey was coming, and soon.

Tonight, however, the town was quiet, and the promises and hopes she had made to herself still echoed softly in the dark.
