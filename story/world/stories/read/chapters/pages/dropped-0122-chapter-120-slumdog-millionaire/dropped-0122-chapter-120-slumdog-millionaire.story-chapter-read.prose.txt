Chapter 120: Slumdog Millionaire

Going out for some jollies in the poor part of town was a time-honoured tradition back home. Granted, back in London, there tended to be more cars and nitrous oxide canisters involved, the latter being so popular that it spawned an entire cottage industry of ‘laughing gas parties’ in the late 70s, before the government finally took notice and began to crack down on illicit use. That just moved the scene underground, leading to a slight reduction of use, but nothing substantial enough to move the needle in the national statistics.

Looking closer though, I couldn’t see any of the telltale signs of drug users back home: the sunken and red eyes, the persistent tremor in their limbs, or the aching desperation in their faces as the craving for another hit intensified. Since I knew for a fact that drugs here could be just as addictive as on Earth, that probably meant they were slumming it for another reason. Certainly, they belonged in the upper classes, those whose livelihood was assured enough that they’d be wandering around without a care after a major incident, not having to worry about anything so pedestrian as a career. Moreover, they were open to inspection something that I’d quickly realised was unusual among people who mattered.

[Aaron Ford - Level 3 Scion]

The first name I saw nearly made me trip, until I recalled that a ford could refer to a shallow spot in a river, and not just the behemoth of a car manufacturer whose cars carried me across the nation for the better part of of six decades. He was the lowest levelled by a substantial margin, and paradoxically the one taking the lead, strutting around like a peacock with a chip on his shoulder. He was the only one wearing ornamentation, a cloak of feathers that reminded me of a few particularly spicy clubs in Soho, but must have cost a fortune in this world, where such embroidery either required painstaking manual labour or enchantment to achieve. His class, combined with his immaculately pressed blonde hair and general posture, a bright smile on his face, indicated a high chance that he was the one nominally in charge of the trio. Nominally, because he also looked the youngest, and de jure authority often meant little against the realities of the field.

[Erin Reid - Level 15 Warrior]

His bodyguard, for instance, was far more likely to have the final word should things get dangerous; she was built like a brick house, a massive greatsword on her back that was thicker than my forearm and properly ten times as heavy, wrought as it was in a single massive slab of steel. She was armoured, that much was obvious despite the outer cloak, because only metal jingled the way she did with every step, and I was very good at noticing such sounds, whether they came from clothing or coin. The permanent scowl on her face only added to the effect, projecting an aura of menace that kept bystanders well away from the party of three.

[Siobhan Grace - Level 9 Scholar]

Rounding up the party was a twig in human form, so thin that I could spot the contours of each individual rib against his servant’s garb. A pair of thick rimmed glasses, heavyset in the manner that predated the invention of lighter plastic compounds, and the ink staining his fingers made him the very image of a harried scholar, the kind I saw by the dozen every time I walked past University College London on my way to work. Frankly, he looked like Pumpkin could bowl him over with a light touch, but that didn’t mean he wasn’t dangerous; in a fantasy setting like this, where formal education ended in the teens for most children, a Scholar class indicated significantly more dedicated to study than on Earth in the 2020s, and often went hand in hand with magic.

Altogether, they made for a motley trio, trying so hard to blend in that they instead stuck out like a sore thumb. That didn’t deter me, because I knew that whatever they were up to, it was unlikely to be boring.

“A nice day to be out on a stroll, isn’t it?” I opened, approaching them with the social weapon of choice in offices across the world: small talk.

Aaron yelped, having completely blanked my approach, which reminded me that I was still wearing Silent Night, and thus moving a lot more quietly than either of my classes would suggest. Erin was a lot more alert, stepping forward protectively to bar my way to her charge, while Siobhan stammered as he backed off to the side, which would have been funny if I hadn’t seen his hand reach for a hidden pocket, likely grabbing a casting aid or reagent in case things kicked off.

”Concealment off,” Erin barked, a no-nonsense growl that demanded immediate obedience.

[Intimidating Roar failed to affect you!]

Literally, as I felt a wave of fear wash over me, demanding that I submit to her instructions.

”Now that’s just rude,” I scoffed instead, because I knew myself, better than I did anyone else, and had experienced all the ups and downs of life over six decades and more; such fake, forced emotions simply didn’t have the resonance to move me, not at this level. “Do you greet your mother with that mouth?”

That was also a British classic, albeit far ruder than discussing the weather. Surprisingly, that made Erin grin, a small thing, barely a lift of the lip, but noticeable all the same.

”Maybe the skill use was overkill,” she agreed, somehow more amiable after being insulted. “But my instructions stand. Show me what I’m working with, or clear off.”

I thought it over, just for a second, before concluding that there was little harm in doing so. She was a bodyguard, not a strikebreaker, and seemed unlikely to try and murder me in broad daylight without provocation. I slowly reached for my Blackened Bracelet and slid it off, placing it on a pocket.

[Blackened Bracelet Stored.]

I also edited my false Class, setting it to a Level 6 Soldier instead of the usual weak merchant, because the latter would be hard to believe after my silent approach, whereas a bit of experience in the quieter side of warfare would easily explain it. Sure enough, the trio all relaxed after seeing my status, high enough to speak of some competence, but not so high as to be threatening own my own.

”What do you want?” Aaron asked, not nearly so hostile as his bodyguard.

That was the question, wasn’t it?
