The steam pipe is long and twisting, and every step becomes more difficult as I fight against the wind coming from the other end. At first, it was a light wind. Then a stiff breeze, and finally a howling storm. A violent gust pushes me back despite my weight, and the roar of ceramic-on-steel is quickly swallowed by the scream of a radioactive hurricane.

Traces of molten corium have quickly intensified into what has become a superheated sandstorm, scouring my armor. A Typical would have been sandblasted into pieces by the force of it alone. I’m lucky that it’s moving too fast to stick to my armor.

This might be one of my stupider ideas.

Still, I’m committed, and I continue to push through the storm, braving the radioactive rain. Eventually a large opening looms through the gloom, though through the nightmarish environment, I can barely make anything out. Beta radiation is thick here, and it tastes like Fireball whisky. A spicy, overwhelming flavor that fills the mouth and dries it out. A kick at the end that makes me want to choke.

God, I can’t believe I used to drink that stuff.

Trying to see through the roaring gale is near-impossible. The visual spectrum is worthless, being clouded by molten corium and water vapor. LIDAR isn’t any better, and sonar is equally jammed by the howling winds. I scan up and down the radio frequencies, trying to get a view into where the opening leads to. I finally land on a frequency similar to the band used in ground penetrating radar, though it’s simply the least bad option rather than something good.

The room ahead housed probably what were heat exchangers at some point. It’s only a best guess, as there is nothing close to recognizable as a piece of machinery. Everything has been melted down in little islands of slag in a toxic lake. Water is pouring from hundreds of tiny rivulets in the outer walls as well as a few pipes. It’s only then I realize that the slick metal walls are not the walls of a room.

This is the inside of a boiler.

The water is boiling as fast as it can pour in, and is at least the source of the water vapor and most of the roaring storm. But not all of it. A few of the islands of slag have large holes in their center. The corium and radio-isotope rich winds come from them.

Nuclear volcanoes. Fantastic.

Fortunately for me, the top half of the boiler has been blown off, actually breaking open the roof and exposing the sky. It reminds me of the sky from The Matrix. Black, boiling, evil looking clouds block out the sun or stars. The winds of a dead hurricane screams and whistles over the jagged hole in the concrete room that contains the heat exchanger and boiler.

It gives me a way out that isn’t a direct line to a reactor core. Assuming one still exists. The damage to every part of the plant is beyond catastrophic and well into apocalyptic territory. The only semi-reasonable explanation would be a reactor detonation. Though not even Chernobyl’s core explosion caused this level of system-wide damage.

My duplicate’s words from Amiens’ mimic Fracture come to mind, as deranged as they were. It called the Fracture a story, and a dream. This is more akin to a nightmare, but I guess monsters would find nightmares comforting.

I slowly climb my way out of the exploded boiler, and into the desolate sky. My fingers dig deep into the thick, red tinted metal that everything seems to be made out of. I make my own handholds, digging out chunks and kicking holes into the boiler’s hull.

I clamber out over the edge and onto the roof of the plant. Actinides and heavy metal-laden winds scour at my armor. They move too fast to stick, and grains of contaminated graphite and boron control rods sandblast anything too large that sticks to my armor.

Lucky that the worst of it doesn’t stick.

Still, dust does find its way into crevices in between plates and the chainmail beneath. It’s less toxic and radioactive than the molten corium and reactor materials. But ‘less’ radiation in this context merely means death for a Typical in less than thirty seconds than near instant.

The scene outside of the plant is truly horrific. The black clouds that blot out the sky are far from the worst of it.

I guess this would count as a nightmare.

The plant itself, a sprawling, concrete monstrosity is blackened and scorched. Huge rents and holes dot the roof, and in multiple places there are huge chunks of the building simply gone.

Black specks dot the roof, what little of it remains intact. My sensor suits identify them as chunks of moderator material. In a few places, they’re shards of half melted reactor rods or casings. Beyond the plant itself, the Fracture extends out to the horizon. Whether that’s real or a mirage is up for debate. The landscape is desolate and covered in gray snow. The blackened skeletons of long dead trees jut up out of the heavy metal snow.

Drifts of sickly green-gray snow blanket piles of debris. They’re either parts of the plant blasted free and thrown away, or maybe outer lying supporting infrastructure flattened by the blast.

Even farther in the distance is a much larger city. Broken skyscrapers lean over, steel spines broken by time and weather. Lightning deep in the clouds above throws strange shadows on the ground for brief moments, casted by the concrete and steel skeletons of a murdered city.

Looking back over the plant, I notice a massive crater not far away. One of several huge, vibrant green pillars of light fires into the sky from it. They even penetrate a little bit into the dark gloom above.

I catch a hint of motion partially illuminated by lightning and magical Cherenkov radiation. My focus snaps to it in an instant, trying to capture everything and anything. It only lasts for an instant before I lose it.

While the outside environment is less intense than inside the boiler, it’s still difficult to make out anything. Magical heavy metals and radioactive materials occlude both my mundane and esoteric sensor systems. It could just be a trick of the light and the violent storm. It could be something else, but I can’t hear anything. The howling, screaming winds swallow everything, even the sound of my heavy footsteps.

I keep a thread focused on the sky, but I keep most of my attention on the path before me as I walk over the roof. The roof is clear of snow, likely kept clear by all the hot heavy metals coating everything. Chunks of radioactive materials and irradiated reactor parts are everywhere. It looks like this plant had at least eight reactors, given by the numbers of pillars of light. It’s possible it had more that either didn’t explode or were too small to punch through the thick concrete building.

Eight cooling towers are on the other side of the plant, and I make my way towards them. I’m careful to avoid the more heavily damaged sections of the roof, but given just how damaged the entire facility, it makes it slow going. Worse, everything being coated in radioactive, magical materials means I can’t get good scans of the roof until I’m basically on top of it. I have to go slow, and even have to backtrack multiple times.

Unfortunately, my luck runs out. A particularly radioactive area meant I realized the roof’s structural integrity was too low to support my weight too late to do anything about it. The concrete roof gives way under my boots, and I fall into the darkness.

“Oh, shit!”

I crash through a rat’s nest of pipes, beams, and wires. Fluids of all kinds coat my armor. My mundane sensors are shot full of radiological jamming. Waste water filled with sediments full of heavy metals and saturnium chips pours out of a snapped water pipe. I slam through a concrete floor, my weight and speed snapping it like a twig. I blow through two more, rusted rebar screaming in metallic agony. I finally slam into a heavy foundation with a boom.

I quickly roll out of the way as an avalanche of rubble follows me down the hole I made. It clatters to the floor with a crash that shakes the floor. A waterfall of radioactive waste water pours down from above, though it abates after less than a minute.

Looking around, I find myself in an octagonal control room of some kind. It’s a multi-level room, with four levels. On each wall is a diagram of a reactor, with each control rod and their status listed on the map. Every single light is red, and they flicker in the dim light. A siren whispers weakly from a speaker on the ceiling.

The consoles themselves make little sense, and are a random assortment of buttons with a variety of Chinese characters on them. None of them make any sense or have anything to do with the operations of a nuclear reactor.

Some buttons are labeled as “heaven” or “eat”. Others aren’t even real characters, or the markings have been eaten away by the decay that has devoured almost everything else. Like the rest of the plant, there are no bodies. No monsters yet, either, which is concerning.

All the other Fractures I’ve been in have had scores of fodder enemies, generally ones that used to be bosses in weaker ones. But I have yet to see anything, barring the strange movement in the cloud. I’m still not convinced it was anything other than an optical illusion and bad sensor returns.

Drifting among the consoles and control stations offers no answers to what happened here. The computer systems are varied and a mess. Some of them are analog systems that look like they came from the early 60s, while others are cutting edge modern day processors. All of them are fried and contaminated. Whether they were destroyed by the radiation tearing them apart at the atomic level or something else, I’ll never know.

I end up taking nothing with me. Everything is just too heavily contaminated to risk it. At the very least, I find a map of the facility, or at least part of one. The metal plaque it was engraved on is heavily damaged like most things, but what I can make out matches with what I’ve seen so far.

The Winchester Mystery house of nuclear power plants. What a mess.

There are a few more storage pools shown on the map, but all of them are located in the missing areas, separate from where the reactors were. It’s possible the fuel went critical in the storage pools and caused a massive steam explosion after flash boiling the water. It wouldn’t have been a nuclear explosion like from a bomb. Nuclear fuel isn’t enriched enough to do that.

Who knows how magic changes that equation. Maybe it’s more reactive?

At least it can guide me out. The map shows the plant as a maze of hallways and countless rooms, most of them without purpose. I can’t imagine how long it would take me to find my own way out—With a standard maze solving algorithm, approximately eleven hours.

Ah, my pathing algorithms. Useful as ever.

I still want to check out the cooling towers. I need to find the boss before the Fracture ruptures. At the very least there won’t be many monsters if it does. I still have weeks before it becomes a problem, so I can’t say I’m concerned about that. The facility might be immense, but it’s not infinite.

About eleven hours worth of searching, to be exact.

On the other hand, a lack of monsters is a concern for other reasons. This Fracture is different from what I’m used to, and I’ve dived into more Fractures than probably anyone else. A Fracture being so completely desolate is unnerving.

The walk out of the plant is the same as my initial forays into it. I trudge through pools of toxic water, over piles of rubble and machines so damaged as to be rendered unrecognizable. Their twisted metal corpses lie in the pools of water, rusting away. The only victims left behind by a manufactured catastrophe.

With the help of the map, I’m able to make my way out, though it did require multiple detours. Many hallways collapsed or had huge craters punched into them. Some of them were deep enough I couldn’t see the bottom. I was not in the mood to try to vault them, especially after already falling through the roof.

After exiting the main facility, I make my way to the cooling towers. Most of them still stand, though damaged. One has collapsed into a pit, likely what was maintenance or engineering spaces for the coolant lines.

Shouldn’t be deep enough to swallow most of a cooling tower. Especially given how tall they are.

While cooling towers at nuclear plants are massive structures, these ones dwarf even sky scrapers. All eight of them are at least a thousand feet tall and four hundred feet wide at the base. They are easily the largest structures in the entire facility, and it’s only once I get close I get to understand the full scale of them.

Most of the towers have open bases, designed to let in air from below. One of the standing towers has the rubble of the collapsed ones scattered around it, forming a berm. I leap over the fifty foot tall berm with a boom, and land with a second one.

In the center of the tower, with one vibrant green eye cracked open, is a black scaled dragon.
