Ethan pulled the second spike from the wall. Gold light blazed up the blade, hotter and brighter than the first, the cathedral’s full Syntropic charge loading into the weapon before he had finished drawing it free. The metal burned through his gauntlet. Its tone resonated with every gold vein in the chamber, deeper than the first spike, steadier, and the cathedral’s light flared in answer.

CelestOS: Weapon charge exceeds suit regulation tolerance. The cathedral is not trickling power into that spike, Ethan. It is equivalent to shoving an ocean through a keyhole.

“And my hand is the keyhole?”

CelestOS: More precisely, through the spike, the gauntlet, three failing insulation layers, and then your hand.

“Comforting.”

CelestOS: It should not be. It is currently cooking you.

Heat alarms flashed across the edge of his HUD in hard red. Gauntlet stress. Dermal temperature climbing under the composite. Ethan tightened his grip anyway. He had already learned what happened when he fired the cannon, and what happened when the Wall tried to fight alone. The creature took every system he built and turned it into more hunger unless the planet’s light burned faster than the Resin could eat.

The creature was seven meters from Maria. The Living Wall’s tendrils were wrapped around its legs and torso, gold tissue burning against Resin chitin, and the creature tore through them stride by stride. Its exoskeleton smoked. The regeneration from the leg wound still drew power from the culture’s reserves. Slower, but still advancing, the stalk prongs reaching toward the woman shielding the child against the wall. Five meters.

Maria had Frederick pressed against her chest with one arm, her other hand flat against the passage wall behind her. Her face was white. Her knees were locked. There was nowhere left to run, and the secondary passage behind her was the only exit the creature hadn’t yet cut off.

CelestOS: Maria, go left two steps along the wall. Do not move into the center. Do not give it a straight line to the passage.

Maria moved. Her shoulder scraped stone. Frederick shifted in her arms and made one small sound, not quite a cry, and the cathedral reacted to it.

Every gold vein in the chamber pulsed once, hard enough to brighten the vaulted ceiling. Root mat along the floor surged toward the creature in a rippling wave. Ribs of living stone and gold-laced root rose under the creature’s feet, hooking around its ankles and driving upward into the joints where the chitin had cracked around the first spike wound.

The creature stumbled, and for half a second Ethan saw the cathedral’s intelligence in the movement. The Wall had learned from the corridor. Thick barriers fed the culture. Direct contact killed tissue. But leverage still mattered. Shape still mattered. The planet could make the creature spend itself in the wrong direction without needing to overpower it.

Root ribs tightened and the creature screamed. The sound slammed into the vaulted ceiling and came back down as pressure. The vertical split in its chest opened wider, the rings of teeth spinning, red vapor hissing from the aperture in angry bursts. Resin filaments erupted from both knees and lashed into the living stone restraints. Gold veins around the filaments went gray. Root ribs blackened where they touched the chitin.

CelestOS: The Wall is attempting structural containment. It is not asking permission.

“Is that good?”

CelestOS: It is better than the alternative, which is all of us dying while the planet politely observes.

The restraints held for two seconds, then three. Ethan ran. Each step pulled more gold through the blade, the tone rising until the chamber seemed to vibrate around him. His suit pushed power into his legs, compensating for the damage in his ribs, shoulder, lungs, everything that had been hit or strained or burned since Patel entered the Wall. The HUD tried to stack warnings in the corner of his vision. He ignored all of them except distance.

Four meters between him and the creature. Root ribs failed, gold lines going dark, the living stone softening, the restraints sloughing away from the creature’s legs in steaming strips. The creature lurched forward, almost falling, then caught itself on one gauntleted hand. Its stalk prongs whipped toward Maria again, both barbed tips spreading wide, tasting Frederick’s signal through the Syntropic field.

CelestOS: Right stalk compensating for damage. Left stalk delayed by point three seconds. Maria sees it too. Listen to your wife.

Maria’s voice cut across the chamber, tight and controlled. “The stalks are forward. It can’t guard the split.”

The creature’s stalk prongs were fully extended toward her and Frederick, both barbed tips reaching for the signal that had drawn it through five kilometers of corridor. Its chest was exposed, the vertical split wide open, the spiral of teeth rotating, red vapor streaming into the cathedral air.

CelestOS: Chest aperture exposed for approximately two seconds after each lunge. That is not enough time for a reasonable plan. Use an unreasonable one.

“That is not even a suggestion.”

CelestOS: It is what we have.

The creature lunged, both stalk prongs shooting forward, barbs spreading, the right one lower than the left, aimed at Frederick’s heartbeat. Maria twisted her body around the baby without leaving the wall. She put her shoulder between Frederick and the prong like a shoulder could stop what five kilometers of Living Wall had barely slowed.

The cathedral moved first. Gold tendrils burst from the wall behind Maria and crossed in front of her in a woven shield. The stalk hit the shield and punched halfway through. The barbed tip came within a meter of Frederick, and the gold veins burned harder instead of going dark.

CelestOS: Wall shield failing. One second.

Ethan reached the creature’s flank. He hit it at full speed, his left hand catching the Resin exoskeleton at the shoulder. Contact scorched his gauntlet instantly. Heat seared through the composite and blistered the underlayer against his skin. His suit tried to pull his hand away. He overrode the reflex with brute force and held on.

The creature was too fast for something that large. Garnet eyes on the side of its head flicked toward him, and the chest split rotated with the torso, the rings of teeth grinding. Both stalk prongs began to retract from Maria. Almost too late, but not late enough. The creature slammed its elbow backward.

Ethan took the hit across the ribs. Pain flashed white through his side. The blow lifted his boots from the floor and nearly tore his left hand from the creature’s shoulder. The spike flared in his right hand, the heat spiking high enough that warning text became a sheet of red across the HUD.

CelestOS: Do not lose the weapon.

“Working on it.”

CelestOS: Work faster.

The cathedral threw another tendril around the creature’s wrist. A second looped under its arm. A third lashed across its throat and burned there, gold against red, root mat turning black as the Resin ate into it. The Wall was buying angle now, buying the fraction of a second Ethan needed. Shape mattered more than strength.

The creature pulled against the tendrils and they stretched thin. Maria shifted again, a small movement, perfect. She stepped to the right, drawing the stalk prongs a handspan farther from the creature’s centerline. Frederick’s signal moved with her, and the creature’s body followed the signal before the rest of it could decide not to. The split opened wide.

CelestOS: Now.

Ethan swung around the creature’s side, inside the arc of the stalk prongs, boots scraping the cathedral floor, his weight pulling against the ridged surface of the exoskeleton. His left hand slipped on heat-slick chitin. The composite in the gauntlet cracked. Skin burned beneath it. He held on.

The creature twisted toward him. For one breath, he was staring straight into the vertical mouth. Concentric teeth spun inward. Red vapor spilled over his chest plate. The smell was acid and copper and old blood cooked into metal. Somewhere inside the chitin and Resin and stolen armor, the last architecture of Patel’s body was still being used as a scaffold, remains turned into transportation and nothing else.

CelestOS: Ethan.

The voice was quiet. He planted his feet and the floor helped. Gold veins flared under his boots and root mat climbed over the soles, locking him in place for the impact. His suit braced. The cathedral held the creature’s shoulder for one more impossible heartbeat.

Ethan drove the spike into the center of the vertical split with both hands. The blade punched through the spiral of teeth. Rings shattered around it, fragments of Resin enamel spinning into the air. He drove deeper, through the rotating mouth, through the red vapor, into the core of the Entropic culture. The spike sank to the grip.

The creature froze, and so did everything else. For one suspended heartbeat, the cathedral went silent except for Frederick’s pulse in the walls. Then the cathedral answered.

Every gold vein in the chamber flared simultaneously. The Living Wall’s tissue on every surface blazed with Frederick’s heartbeat amplified to maximum, the planet’s central node channeling its full Syntropic output through the floor and the walls and the vaulted ceiling. Energy poured through the tendrils still wrapped around the creature and through the spike buried in its core. Gold light erupted from the blade and tore into the Resin tissue.

The planet’s immune system, concentrated in this chamber, focused through a weapon forged from its own stone and powered by the heartbeat of the child the creature had come to claim, met the Entropic culture that had been growing inside Patel’s armor for months. Gold light flooded the Resin like a rejection written in energy. The planet refused it.

Resin around the spike destabilized first. Tissue turned gray at the creature’s core, then gold light jumped outward through every tendril and connection the culture had built. Each one died as the light reached it. The red glow in the exoskeleton flickered out in spreading patches. Chitin cracked. Armor plates peeled loose. Both stalk prongs seized mid-reach toward Maria, went rigid, and crumbled into powder.

The creature convulsed once. Its garnet eyes went dark, one pair and then the other. Then what had been Patel lost its shape and collapsed six meters from Maria and Frederick, hitting the cathedral floor in a final heap.

Ethan stood over the remains with the spike still in his hand. The blade’s gold light was fading, the sustained tone dropping to silence, the metal cooling against his scorched gauntlet. He was breathing hard. Blood ran from his left shoulder where the pauldron had been torn away, and the gauntlet on his left hand was scorched black, the composite warped in the shape of his grip. His ribs ached. Five kilometers of the Wall’s failed defenses had left his lungs raw, and the sprint burned in them still.

Gold veins in the floor blazed beneath the heap. The Wall’s tissue surged through the sludge from every direction, breaking down the Entropic residue, absorbing and neutralizing it. Where the tendrils passed, the sludge turned to clear fluid that sank into the stone. New gold veins flickered to life in the gaps the creature’s passage had killed, thin at first, then brightening. Through the corridor mouth behind him, the damage stretched back into the tunnel, the Living Wall’s tissue gray and peeling for the first hundred meters. Beyond that, faintly, new light was returning. The Wall was regenerating from the cathedral outward, reclaiming the tissue the creature had killed one meter at a time.

The cathedral dimmed from blinding to steady. The vaulted ceiling’s gold veins settled into an even rhythm, and Frederick’s heartbeat filled the room.

CelestOS: Hostile neutralized. Threat to Frederick, Maria, and Ethan Cross removed. Entropic culture inactive. No secondary growth detected.

Ethan stood where he was, the spike hanging at his side. Gold veins pulsed once beneath the thinning sludge.

CelestOS: And before you ask, no. That was not Patel. Not anymore. Patel died somewhere inside the culture he grew, wore, and fed. What reached this chamber was a hostile organism using his remains as transport.

Ethan just stared.

CelestOS: I am logging it that way. Deliberately. If Celestitech ever reads my record, I want them to understand the distinction. Their man did not fall in battle. Their weapon was rejected by the planet.

Smoke thinned in the cathedral’s warm air. Ethan dropped the spike. The metal rang once against the stone and lay still.

Maria’s voice came from behind him, steady and quiet. “Ethan.”

He turned. She was still against the passage wall, Frederick against her chest, her arms locked around him. Her legs were shaking. The controlled steadiness that had held her in place while the creature crossed the cathedral was releasing now, the adrenaline ebbing, the tremor visible in a way it hadn’t been thirty seconds ago. She’d stood between a Resin creature and her child because standing was what was left.

He gripped them both in a tight hug.

CelestOS: Maria’s blood pressure is elevated but stabilizing. Frederick’s vitals remain steady. Ethan, yours are objectionable, but not immediately fatal.

“Good to know.”

CelestOS: I am choosing to interpret that as gratitude because I have had a long day.

”It was.”

He took his son and the baby was warm and impossibly small against the scorched chest plate, his heartbeat steady where it pressed against the armor, and the gold veins in the cathedral pulsed in time with it. Frederick hadn’t stirred. He’d slept through the creature’s scream and the gold detonation and the sound of chitin hitting stone six meters from his mother’s arms. The planet’s heartbeat and his own were the same signal, and the signal said safe.

Maria leaned into Ethan’s shoulder. Her weight settled against him and he took it, the suit bracing for both of them. Her breathing slowed against his chest plate, and her hand came up and rested on Frederick’s back with her fingers spread wide, covering the full span of him. The pressure of her grip came through the suit’s sensors, firm and deliberate. She wasn’t going to let go for a very long time, and neither was he.

“Cel told me it was coming.” Maria’s voice was muffled against his shoulder. “She said move and I moved. She said it was heading for Frederick.” A pause. Her grip on the baby’s back tightened. “I couldn’t have outrun it. So I stood where I could block the passage behind me and I waited for you to get here.”

“You were right where you needed to be,” he said. His voice was rough from the sprint and the smoke and everything else. “Both of you.”

Maria’s hand tightened on Frederick’s back. She didn’t answer. She didn’t need to.

CelestOS: Vital signs stable. All three of you are perfect. I am recording this moment for the log because the log has had a terrible day, too, and it deserves something worth reading.

Frederick’s eyes were closed. His breathing was even. His hand curled around nothing, fingers opening and closing in the slow rhythm of a newborn discovering that hands exist. Gold veins pulsed with his heartbeat through the cathedral, through the corridor, through every meter of Living Wall tissue on Veslaya. The planet held steady around the child, and the child slept, and the light was warm.
