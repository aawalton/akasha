Aida left Ernie feeling hollow, instead of feeling gratified at witnessing a miracle. Fortunately, Levi didn’t let her spiral into overthinking, promptly whipping out his itinerary again and shuffling Aida along.

He took her to Charity Temple, which was located not far from Buddington Market.

It seemed to be as much of a community hub as the Market, with mothers with young children as well as those of a more venerable age making up the bulk of the demographic.

The reason soon became clear: Charity Temple had a small restaurant nestled in a corner of their grounds, where they were serving meals for one copper baen.

“This is one place you should definitely remember,” Levi said quietly to Aida as they stood in the long line. “As kids growing up, we’d always come to temples like this for the cheap meals. That way we can save our baen for the more exciting activities.”

“Are these temples and services common?” Aida whispered.

“Every temple will sell something at cost, though the larger the organization the more options they have. So if you’re ever short on baen, there’s no shame in going to a temple. However, it is courteous to donate when you can.”

After a humble meal of rice, soup, and vegetables, Levi took Aida into the main building where a large statue of a faceless deity was erected. In the backdrop were carvings depicting each of the five elements, and in front was an altar carrying bowls with each element’s material in it.

Levi taught her the basic ritual of praying.

“You’ll need to know this for when you go to Moonglade,” he said beside her. The two of them were kneeling on thick cushions in front of a large incense burner. “They’re a lot more serious about spirituality.”

As Levi talked Aida through the prayer, she began to appreciate just how deeply the five elements were embedded into the foundation of Wyndian culture, if only because it gave her a more obvious template to follow for when she had to improvise a native Wyndian reaction. Before beginning a prayer, they had to bow five times: once to acknowledge and appreciate each element’s significance in their lives. To conclude their prayer, they would remain prostrated for five breaths, to signify their submission to the elements.

When in doubt, just defer to five of everything.

“As an aside, this is also the temple where Agnes volunteers regularly,” Levi said to Aida as they walked out of the prayer hall. His eyes scanned the volunteers who were on the temple grounds, serving, sweeping, or guiding visitors who looked lost. The volunteers all wore simple brightly-colored vests, distinguishing them from the monks who did the more important activities in maintaining the temple’s upkeep: keeping the prayer hall free of dust, adding oil to the bowl with flames, guiding groups of patrons in meditation.

Aida tugged on Levi’s sleeve, slowing him to a stop at the edge of what appeared to be a guided meditation class.

Unlike Professor Bruce’s meditation classes, which were a lot more freeform and basically boiled down to “empty your mind, follow your mana,” the monk seated on a small raised platform in front of the patrons was humming as he knocked on what appeared to be a wooden fish at slow, steady intervals.

Even though it was obvious those in the crowd didn’t have enough mana to be classified as a practitioner, Aida could still feel some who had more mana than others. Relative to those surrounding them, their pricks of energy were like candle flames in twilight.

“Is it possible for nonpractitioners to cultivate their mana like this?”

“Anyone can cultivate their mana. The main distinguisher between a practitioner and a nonpractitioner is the capacity and control one has.” Levi crossed his arms as he watched the group. “Caleb’s mom is an example. She developed her mana capacity and control pretty late in life—not enough to be able to do much more than assist her clients’ own mana flows, but she has some ability.”

“So the few people who have enough mana to be sensed…”

“They were already predisposed to having a respectable amount of mana, but that doesn’t guarantee they can control it.” Levi glanced down at her. “We may have looked down on Aida in school, but out in society, her ability level wouldn’t be sneezed at.”

Aida continued watching the meditation class, fascinated. The rhythmic clacking of the wood did much more to calm her mind than Bruce’s constant, monotonous refrains to empty their minds of any thoughts and emotions.

“You think Agnes participated in these group meditations?” Aida asked abruptly.

Levi’s breath hissed as he exhaled. “I was wondering if you would start thinking in that direction.”

Gently grasping her elbow, Levi pulled her away from the group to an empty bench below a tree in full bloom.

“You think so too, don’t you?” Aida asked once they sat down. It was quite a good bench: far enough removed from the main path to not be disturbed by the constant flow of new visitors, yet not too far that she couldn’t people watch.

“I guess it’s lucky that Charity Temple was on my itinerary today,” Levi said softly. His bronze eyes moved steadily through the crowd, as if he was looking for someone. “I don’t think you were paying attention, but I was talking to Ernie about Agnes’s habits while we were drinking tea. Before you woke her up. What did you do, by the way?”

Aida shook her head hopelessly. “I really don’t think I did anything. I just Detoxed the spirit tea from her body, but as you saw, it took a long time before anything happened. It didn’t feel quite right, honestly.”

Levi was silent as he digested her words. “If you weren’t from another world, I would have thought Agnes’s predicament and miraculous recovery were just coincidences. But it seems like ever since you showed up, more and more odd circumstances have been cropping up… everything seems to be connected.”

“Should we ask if they’ve heard of anyone else falling into an eternal slumber?” Aida suggested halfheartedly. Part of her was hoping Levi would come up with some logical excuse not to do so; she had enough concerns just in dealing with the logistics of starting a new job in less than a week. She couldn’t afford to take on a mystery.

After all, Agnes entering a mysterious coma seemed unlikely to be related to Ezra’s situation; he had the ability to self-induce a coma, and the Professors seemed reasonably certain that was what he had done. So until she went to Moonglade and underwent more advanced meditation training, so that she could… what, lure Ezra back to life? When he presumably had the chance to, but turned it down?

Suddenly, Aida’s optimistically simplistic view of her future seemed precarious.

But it seemed like he wanted to tell you something before… so at the very least, you need to get confirmation that he doesn’t want to come back.

Aida’s head started hurting again. Like Levi, she had the strong feeling that everything weird they had noted was connected. But she couldn’t quite see the connection. She just didn’t have enough information, and honestly, the scope was too broad for her to even know where to start looking for answers. Her best lead right now was to follow the already-set path Professor Bruce had laid out for her.

However, seeing how happy Ernie was to have his wife back, and at the same time seeing that flash of contempt Agnes had when she looked at him…

She shook her head. She had already promised herself to take care of the Lorehs; she couldn’t afford to take on fixing a pair of strangers’ marital troubles.

“Nevermind. I don’t think we can do much on our own.” Aida stood up from the bench, dusting off the seat of her pants. “I think we’re better off letting others know. The people whose job it is to get to the bottom of all these uncertainties.”

Levi gave her an approving smile. “Delegating? You’ve learned. I’m proud of you.”

~ * ~ * ~ * ~

Aida sat in her bed later that night, fingering Ezra’s blank bead on her bracelet. Everyone else’s bead glowed merrily with their mana, reassuring her they were all alive and well.

It had only been two days since she had seen Ezra—not that any of her visits had been productive, since he was sleeping—but it felt so much longer than that.

Despite her decision to hand over the investigation of Agnes’s case to the Gullbeaks, which Aida was very confident they could do since they were highly regarded professionals who solved the local community’s most pressing problems, she still couldn’t entirely let go of the feeling that she had to be involved.

Seeing Agnes in that same still slumber magnified the severity of Ezra’s condition. Or rather, reminded Aida just how little anybody knew about Ezra’s state.

Professor Bruce’s guess was just that: a guess. Nobody knew for sure Ezra was the one refusing to return to his body; he very well could be desperate to return, but was being stopped, for one reason or another. Just because he was under Healer Luk’s care didn’t mean he was safe.

She had initially been looking forward to going to Burston City to work, and considered the training at Moonglade to be a bother… but now she was more anxious to go to Moonglade to commence her Meditation training.

She was certain, now more than ever, there was some sort of connection between Agnes’s coma and Ezra’s.
