“So… I think these pillars are some sort of warning,” Asher called out as they approached the large depression in the ground up ahead Samantha was leading them toward. “Which is alarming for a couple of reasons. The wild realm is supposed to be pure chaos. How are there written warnings?”

“Nobody knows what the wild realm even is, right?” Rulfar pointed out, frowning as Moxy kicked into his back and tried to order him to go faster. The warrior looked like she was having entirely too much fun riding atop his shoulders, and Asher had a feeling Rulfar was about three more kicks shy of tossing her into the freezing mists himself. “Maybe it really is fragments of different worlds slammed together into one impossible collage. Or maybe this was left behind by other realm wanderers.”

“Maybe…” Asher muttered, focusing on his own realm wanderer class. He’d already made the class switch immediately upon landing on the realm, and he was trusting the class to be the early warning system they needed in the event something too powerful for them to handle came their way. He hadn’t gotten too much use out of it just yet, but that seemed to be what the class did. At least in the wild realm.

Now that they’d gotten close enough to the large depression, the team slowed down and approached the dip a bit more cautiously. Whatever was giving off the death energy reading wasn’t at risk of changing, and it was still there. The depression itself looked to be about a hundred feet wide, and was curiously a perfect circle. The mist seeped down into it, giving the illusion that the sunken earth was one giant pool of blood now that it had turned red.

“So I’m just gonna say it if nobody else will,” Moxy said, raising an eyebrow at the swirling hole of mist. “That looks evil as hell.”

“It is a source of death energy we’re hunting for,” Samantha called down, slowly flying a loop around the depression as she examined it from above. “I’m not very surprised.”

“Kroult, do you have any sort of detection spells you can use?” Asher asked. “If we’re lucky there’s some powerful artifact just sitting in that depression that we can snag and get out of here, but we’re never that lucky. It’s probably going to be attached to a monster, and I’d like to know what we’re dealing with before we hop in there and wake it up.”

“I have a spell for sensing small critters I used to use to find lost pets… I do not know how applicable that will be to the situation,” Kroult admitted nervously. “Should I try it?”

“...You know what, why not,” Asher decided, figuring it was better than nothing. “Give it a shot. If we’re about to go up against an uber-powerful rabbit that wants to tear us to shreds, might as well get a heads up.”

Sadly, Kroult’s spell didn’t end up giving them anything to go off of, leaving them just as much in the dark as to what awaited them underneath the swirling mist as they were before. However, the lich did end up revealing that he’d had a warming spell all this time he could have used to help combat the cold, much to Rulfar’s annoyance. It was clear to Asher that the lich was still hesitant to offer to use his magic without them directly asking him for it, but he figured that sort of confidence would just have to build over time. Their team had never had a dedicated buffer either, so the growing pains went both ways.

Once Moxy was on her own two feet again, shivering, but capable of fighting, Asher motioned for them all to gather round and brought all of them into his rift. Until he could confirm exactly what he was going up against, letting them stand beside him was simply too risky. While Samantha technically could survive a single lethal hit thanks to Winged Savior, the others definitely couldn’t. He even pulled Kroult into his rift as well, just in case he needed to make a quick getaway. Leaving the lich behind in another realm right after he’d joined the team would be a bit of a rude thing to do.

With Crescent in one hand and his flaming dagger in the other, Asher hopped into the depression, wincing as the thick, red mist reached up to his chest. The effect grew stronger than it had been before, to the point where even with Complete Resilience, his dagger, and Kroult’s spell, he was still shivering as he stalked forward. He was pretty certain the awakened panther made of pure ice that he’d fought all those months ago would have frozen solid if it had tried to spend any time in here.

The closer Asher got to the center of the depression, the more the scarlet mist began to swirl, and the tighter he gripped his daggers. He knew something was going to jump out at him at any moment now, and honestly, the anticipation was far worse than the freezing mist. He just wished—

Something grabbed his leg below the mist, and Asher was glad his friends were all tucked away within his rift because he screamed as he lashed out and stabbed whatever it was with his flaming dagger. Even as what felt like a cold, rough hand released its grip, two more replaced it, one grasping at his thigh and the other going for his ankle.

“No, no, hell no!” Asher shouted, lashing out and stabbing each of the hands the moment he felt them. He tried to spin around and start slashing at arms or bodies, but his daggers hit nothing but swirling mist. Yet even after confirming that there were no undead anywhere near him, again, he felt multiple hands start clawing at his body, actively working to drag him down into the mist.

Is it the mist itself that’s the monster? He wondered, shuddering as he continued stabbing at the invisible hands hidden beneath the mist. Every time he stabbed at one the crimson mist seemed to swirl a bit more than it had been before, and he swore he could hear faint whispering just on the edge of his awareness. He knew his attacks were eternal, but he had no idea how much damage he was actually inflicting. He’d never fought against what was functionally a giant cloud before.

At the very least, he still didn’t feel any sort of overwhelming death aura. Even while immune to such energy, he could still tell if it was present, so that was a good sign. Changing tactics, Asher fought his way back to the edge of the depression, hopping out with Distortion and shuddering as the mist returned to drifting around just his lower calves.

“Alright, I need some help!” he said, reaching into his rift and dragging the rest of the team back out. “Good news, I think you guys are safe from instant death. Bad news, we need to kill an evil cloud.”

“How evil we talking?” Moxy asked, leaning over the lip of the depression and tilting her head as if trying to hear something. “Is it… whispering to us? That feels pretty evil.”

“It has to have some sort of core,” Samantha said, looking down at her artifact to confirm what it was telling her. “Dead center of the depression, that’s where the death energy is strongest. Want me to fly you over and drop you onto it?”

“That’s not a bad idea… The tricky thing is we can’t risk damaging whatever’s giving off the death energy. If we do that, this whole expedition becomes a massive waste of time,” Asher said. “I just wish we could see what we needed to grab.”

“Well, there’s the obvious solution,” Rulfar said, turning toward Moxy. “Chuck a ball of air toward the center and blast the mist away. So long as the core isn’t particularly fragile, that should get us a good view of it.”

“I knew we kept Carrot around for a reason,” Moxy grinned, ignoring Rulfar’s grumbling at his hated nickname as she began packing air into a ball. Once it was ready, she took aim and hurled it toward the center of the depression, the ball vanishing for just a moment as it entered the mists before exploding and sending crimson mist flying in all directions.

As the mist parted, Moxy's attack revealed a withered corpse of someone curled in the fetal position in the dead center of the depression, clutching a throbbing, dark gem tightly against their chest. Despite the fact that the fragile corpse looked like it should have been blasted away by Moxy’s skill, it didn’t move so much as an inch. The gem continued to pulse even as the mist quickly flowed back together to cover it once more, and Moxy grunted.

“Definitely evil. Also, is it just me or is the mist growing… larger?”

“It’s not just you,” Asher muttered, motioning for them all to take a step back as the mist began swirling faster and faster within the depression. Smoky, red hands began grasping the edges as wispy constructs pulled themselves free from the mist. Faceless humanoids emerged one after another, not making so much as a sound as they slowly drifted toward them. The whispering seemed to increase in volume with every figure that emerged from the mist, to the point where Asher was beginning to find it difficult to think.

“I think you made it mad!” Rulfar called out, cleaving his battle axe through the closest of the drifting figures. Weirdly enough, his attack actually seemed to work as the being of mist was cut in two before dispersing, the mist seeping down to join the layer still drifting around their feet.

“It was your idea!” Moxy laughed, already blasting the figures apart with a wild barrage of punches. Her air strikes easily dispersed multiple figures at a time as they left the depression, for all the good it did them.

“Asher, what’s the plan?!” Samantha called down, safe from the horde of enemies clawing their way out of the depression as she began firing down into them. Her arrows were breaking the figures apart just like Rulfar’s battle axe was, but for every one or two that fell to her well-placed shots, ten more seemed to take their place. Within seconds of them first appearing, there were already hundreds of whispering mist figures drifting toward them, their sheer number threatening to overwhelm them before they could make a decision.

“These things seem endless... Get me to the gem!” Asher decided, using his boots to levitate straight up as he held up his arms. Just like they’d practiced, Samantha shouldered her bow and shot over to him, grabbing his wrists and yanking him along for the ride as she flew over the swirling sea of crimson mist. Looking down, Asher could now see the thousands of bodies filling the depression, each one pressing and crawling over the next as they tried to force their way toward Moxy, Rulfar, and Kroult. Despite the fact that this was the only way he saw of putting an end to this before they were overwhelmed, Asher shuddered as he realized what he was about to do. The moment Samantha reached the center of the depression, where the mist was thickest and dozens of wispy bodies whispered as they reached up toward them, he nodded.

Dropping from her grasp as he fell straight into the thickest of the mist.

Before he’d even hit the ground, Asher started slashing as he searched the mist for the one solid corpse Moxy had briefly revealed. Already there were countless hands grabbing at him and pressing in on him from all directions, but he tried to ignore them as he fought his way through the horde. Elongating his flaming dagger with Distortion and using that as his primary weapon bought him a little space, enough that he was able to force the wispy figures back. After a few terrifying seconds, he spotted the huddled corpse once more, and he let out a sigh of relief as he leapt at it and reached out to grab the pulsing gem it clutched against its chest.

Only for the corpse’s hand to lash out and grab him by the wrist, stopping him dead in his tracks. Before Asher could even recoil, the corpse’s withered head snapped up to look at him, its empty sockets pooling dark red mist like bloody tears as it opened its mouth and screamed.
