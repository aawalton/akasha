It was time to let an undead sentinel loose.

This probably wasn’t going to go well, but they didn’t exactly have a choice in the matter.

Deep in the Shadow Realm, at the edge of the void-black lake, Ace held his bleeding hand against the central groove of the altar. He watched in silence as his blood flowed along the channels. As it progressed, the runes glowed brighter, pulsing in a steady rhythm. The shadow vines around the sentinel’s body writhed violently as Ace’s blood traveled toward them, as though they were thirsty for a taste.

At first, they pulsed like living arteries, swelling with darkness before compressing once again. Then, as the ritual gained momentum, the vines loosened their hold—not letting go entirely, but giving the man enough room to move. The thinner tendrils recoiled first, curling away from the man’s body like scorched serpents. The larger, more deeply embedded growths shuddered, their surfaces cracking to reveal glimpses of pale light beneath. The remaining vines, desperate to maintain their hold, coiled tighter around his chest in a final, desperate clinging before they, too, began to disintegrate.

As the last of Ace’s blood reached the central point of the altar, the largest vine—a thick, rope-like mass that had wrapped around the man’s neck—convulsed once and burst apart, showering the stone with particles of shadow that hissed and evaporated like water on hot metal.

None of the vines loosened their hold after the biggest one had dissolved into dust, but they were loose enough that the man could now move.

And yet, nothing happened.

There was just…

…silence.

Ace furrowed his brows in confusion and lifted his hand off the altar. The wound in his palm healed instantly, stitched together by his vampiric abilities, and he wiped the lingering smears of blood onto his pant leg.

All the while, his attention remained on the immobile sentinel.

Huh.

He didn’t really know what to do at this point.

Uncertain of his options—and why the man wasn’t moving—Ace placed his hand firmly on the sentinel’s shoulder.

The reaction was instantaneous and savage. Shadow energy arced between them like black lightning, sending a fresh wave of raw agony coursing through Ace’s body. He tried to pull away, but his hand had fused to the man’s shoulder. The sentinel’s eyes snapped open, and there were no whites to his eyes. Instead, they swirled with void energy—not the vampiric crimson that Ace had grown accustomed to, but the absolute darkness of something that had lived too long in the shadows.

Reality twisted.

The sky folded down on him.

And, with one final snap, the Shadow Realm shattered like glass.

Ace stumbled backward as both he and the sentinel were violently ejected from the Shadow Realm. They had been shot out onto the blackened grass directly in front of his startled team.

The trio reacted instantly. Tara knelt at Ace’s side and checked for injuries. Olivia drew her shiny new shadow dagger. Marcus flinched and sank into a defensive stance, his hands raised and ready to deliver a blow if need be.

Behind them, sitting once more on her branch, the System watched with undisguised glee and clapped her hands like a child at a puppet show.

“Goddamn,” the sentinel said, his voice hoarse and raspy from years of disuse. “Fuck, that hurts.”

The coiled vines remained wrapped around the sentinel’s body. Even as he stood, they clung to him like armor, slowly loosening by the second. The trapped man stumbled, nearly falling, but he caught himself at the last second. His movements were jerky and uncoordinated, and he ultimately landed hard on one knee. His hands pressed into the grass, and he sucked in breath after breath as he tried to stabilize himself. Shadow energy still crackled across his skin, highlighting the hollows of his gaunt face.

A single vine wilted away from him, but many more still clung desperately to his frame.

“What…” The man’s voice scraped from his throat, and his wild eyes darted between the four of them. “How… long? How long… have I been… imprisoned?”

“I don’t know,” Ace confessed. “A while, probably. Do you remember your name?”

“My name?” The man looked down at the ground, his eyes fading in and out of focus as he fought to keep himself upright.

The question seemed to confuse him. His brow furrowed in concentration, the shadows swirling around his temples as though his thoughts themselves had physical form.

“I was... I am... Raven,” the man said. “I’m the last of my team. The only one who survived the Crucible.” His eyes focused on Ace with sudden clarity. “But I didn’t escape. None of us ever escape.”

The shadows around Raven’s body began to pulse and writhe with growing intensity, responding to his agitation.

The sentinel’s gaze locked on Ace, recognition flaring. “I know what you are.”

“And that is?” Ace pressed.

“Another batch of lambs led to the slaughter.” He laughed—a hollow, broken sound. “There’s always more of you. Always.”

Tara took a cautious step forward, as though sudden movements might spook him. “What—”

“It’s because of her,” the man interrupted as he glared up at the giggling girl in the trees. “It’s always her.”

“Guilty,” she said cheerfully.

“We need to get in the Crucible,” Ace said, redirecting the conversation to what mattered most. “We don’t want to fight you, but we will. We can make this amicable.”

“No, we can’t.” The man pushed himself to his feet, and though he swayed, he stayed upright this time. “The Sisters will destroy you just like all the others.” He paused. “Just like me.”

Marcus suddenly stiffened, his eyes widening.

“Ace,” he whispered urgently. “Something’s wrong with his mind. I’m picking up fragmented memories—dozens, maybe hundreds of encounters like this. He’s been through this cycle before, many times.”

Raven’s head snapped toward Marcus, and the man creased his nose in disgust. “Stay out of my thoughts, Mind Drinker.”

Marcus took an involuntary step back.

“Ace,” he said quietly. “Ace, he doesn’t realize he’s been trapped here for years. His memories are fragmented—he remembers awakening before, fighting other teams, but there are huge gaps. It’s like he’s been caught in a time loop.”

Raven’s face twisted with rage. “STAY OUT OF MY HEAD!”

Raven’s words spilled out in broken fragments, each of them fighting against the corruption that clearly clouded his mind. His eyes darted between Ace’s team members, never settling on one person for long, and black shadows leaked from the corners of his mouth like rabid foam. Thick vines clung to his body, wrapped around his limbs and torso, slowly loosening their hold with each of his movements. Dead leaves and moss fell away with each gesture, revealing patches of corrupted skin beneath.

“Oh…” he said absently, and his eyes fogged with a memory. “There were five of us. We wanted power. We wanted… something… what was it?” His voice rattled, as dry as autumn leaves. “Something called the Keystone. The Sisters broke us. Turned us against each other. They’re all dead. I’m the only one to survive.”

Ace maintained his defensive stance, noting how the shadows around Raven’s feet pulsed with each emotional spike in his story. The vines covering Raven’s body shifted and writhed, as if they possessed a life of their own. Marcus and Olivia flanked Ace on either side, weapons at the ready.

Yep.

This wasn’t going well.

“And now you work for the Sisters?” Olivia asked, her disgust evident.

“Work for them?” Raven barked out a harsh laugh. “No, I’m their prisoner. Their pet.” He raised his arms, showing arcane symbols burned into his flesh beneath patches of torn vines. “Bound by blood and shadow to eliminate anyone who tries to enter. The doors only open one way now—over a living corpse.”

Ace stepped forward, his blade lowered in a final attempt to reason with this guy. “Maybe we can help you break free. We don’t have to fight, Raven.”

“Awww, no fun!” the System pouted. “I want punchy, punchy!”

Bitter laughter erupted from Raven’s throat. “Seventeen teams have stood where you stand. Each one thought they were different. Special.” His eyes met Ace’s. “Each one died screaming.”

“But none of them had a healer like me,” Tara said.

She stepped forward, and though Ace reached a hand out to stop her, she slipped out of reach. Her focus remained on Raven, and crimson healing energy pulsed at her fingertips. “Let me help you.”

For a breath, hope flickered across Raven’s face—genuine and human.

It didn’t last.

The man shut his eyes and shook his head. “You can’t fix me. No one can. They’re watching us, even now, and they’ll only let me go if I kill twenty teams. I’m so close.” His voice dropped to a whisper, and his chin lifted skyward. “So close.”

Apparently, this guy wasn’t going to even try to take a bargain.

Time to hit him before he could kill Ace’s team. The man was still weak, still stumbling, and Ace wasn’t about to lose this chance.

He drew Moonfall, and its silver edge gleamed in the purple sunlight. In one fluid motion, he launched himself forward and aimed for Raven’s neck. Moonfall was sharp enough to cut it clean off, and Ace wanted to end this with as little bloodshed as possible.

Two feet away, and the man’s eyes were still closed. The vines around him pulsed with life, but Raven apparently couldn’t hear Ace’s silent approach.

He could do this.

As much as he didn’t want to kill a fellow survivor of this place, Raven didn’t stand a chance.

The blade whistled through the air—and though Raven never once opened his eyes, a thick vine intercepted Ace’s sword.

Ace balked. “What the—”

The vine hardened instantly, catching Moonfall’s edge. The impact reverberated through Ace’s arms as the vine coiled and snapped, launching him backward through the air.

He landed in a crouch, weapon still ready.

Raven’s distorted face twisted into a cruel smile, and he finally opened his eyes to look right at Ace. The vines around his body writhed with new purpose, no longer falling away but actively reinforcing his corrupted form.

“Nice try,” the sentinel said lazily. “But the Sisters know their sentinels are weak when they first awake. You really think they wouldn’t have a failsafe to protect me as I wake up?”

Ace raised Moonfall, its silver edge gleaming against the darkness. “Form up,” he ordered his team quietly. “This is going to be worse than the Alpha Slug.”

Sure enough, black tendrils erupted from Raven’s skin, ripping through his clothes and the remaining vines. His body contorted as shadow energy poured from his eyes, nose, and mouth. When he spoke again, his voice carried an unnatural echo, as though multiple voices spoke through him.

“I’ve tried to resist before. It doesn’t work.”

The shadow tendrils extended into blade-like appendages that writhed around him. His transformation accelerated—bones cracked and reformed, his spine elongating as he grew taller, darkness condensing around his limbs to form armor-like plates.

The air around them grew thick with shadow essence as Raven’s body finished its horrific transformation. Where a man had stood moments before now loomed a nightmare of shadow and bone—a guardian forged from corruption and bound by ancient magic. The last of the vines still clung to parts of his body, pulsing with a sickly green energy. Raven’s monstrous form flexed its new appendages, and the shadow blades extended from every limb.

“The Sisters are watching,” he said, his voice now completely inhuman. “Try to give them a good show, will you?”

With that, his eyes burned suddenly amber—and he attacked.
