By the time we reached Joey's place, I had 6,031 Presence. I didn't know any of my neighbors. I nodded to some of them in the halls, but hadn't shared more than a few words with any of them. There was an old lady on the second floor who I'd hold the door open for sometimes. I hoped she wasn't one of the twelve people who had been killed so far. I carried their blood on my hands. I remembered the wail of a mother calling out to a son, no doubt half eaten by the spider. I shuddered, pulling into myself further, in the back of Joey's truck.

I had a pit in my stomach. A void where every stray emotion pushed itself, leaving very little behind. I wished I felt worse. The only thing that slowed those negative emotions lay in the fact that I knew this nightmare would never stop. I thought about the Being in the Static— Ananke's deal. Becoming an Instance Holder and collecting Presence through Visitors, and apparently normal people.

People were also made of Presence. The realization made me sick. With only twelve kills, I gained 360 Presence. I had gained almost as much Presence from a torturous Instance. If I killed everyone in the world, how much would I have? Seven billion times thirty. How much did Rowan gain by travelling to other worlds and waylaying them? I thought of the story of the Lone Wanderer and how he had done the same to his friend's world. Was that story about Rowan, or was this just what the Dream did to people?

The reason for denying the position of Instance Holder felt so far away.

I had to search for it again, but once I found it, everything snapped back into sharp focus.

I couldn't succumb to nihilism. What I had done with the spider was an accident. Instance Holders played God and hurt innocent people on purpose. I needed to ground myself in something. To focus on the few things that mattered to me. People. People mattered, yet I couldn't protect everyone. I hadn't even tried to last Instance.

Three people. I could protect at least three. Mom, Dad, and Joey. Even if my entire world fell apart, if the Dream broke into my world and ate it from the inside, I'd make sure they were safe.

The truck parked in Joey's driveway, a rental duplex. He hopped out of the car, hands on his head as he paced towards me. I extricated myself from the bed and put my hand on his shoulder. I knew him well. Right now, he needed three things: assurance, a goal, and a deadline. "We're safe for now. Get Maerin inside. I have until around 11 at night to explain everything." Joey nodded, patting my hand before stepping around the car to let Maerin out.

I felt a little bad. Maerin was one of the more capable survivors, and she certainly didn't shut down in dangerous situations. Hell, she had the wherewithal to get us running when the spider appeared, yet here I was asking for Joey to take care of her like she was made of porcelain for his benefit more than hers. I frowned. It didn't sit right with me.

We silently settled into the living room. The room had sparse decoration, holding only a couch, a number of chairs, and a large flat screen TV. The kitchen sat next to the stairs leading to the basement level, and my eyes caught the shelf with lines of alcohol bottles common among college kids. I grabbed a vodka bottle and a cup, "Either of you want a drink?" I asked.

"Please,"

"Absolutely,"

Maerin and Joey affirmed. They settled into the couch, Joey whispered to Maerin, and she nodded along, but her eyes were locked on me. The fact that they were now Gray instead of Green left me feeling uneasy, and I remembered that the position she had taken as Joey's girlfriend did not belong to her.

I poured myself a shot and quickly downed it. I held my breath, avoiding the taste, but the burn still hit my tongue. I poured a double for good measure and downed that as well. Before breathing again, I blew out, trying to get as much of the fumes out. I had learned that trick senior year of high school. It didn't get rid of the vague, burning, sour taste, but it certainly helped. Only after feeling the flush on my cheeks did I go about opening the fridge and making the other two drinks. I went for a classic. Vodka lemonade with ice, I wasn't about to mix anything fancy.

I brought the pair their drinks before going back for the alcohol and mixer, along with a chair. I sat across from them, unsure of how to start the conversation. I hoped that one of them would start it for me, but neither did. Eventually, I found the words, "Joey, what I'm about to tell you is going to sound insane, and it's going to be uncomfortable, but I need you to know I would never mess with you like this and everything I say is absolutely true in so far as I know it. Okay?"

My friend looked between Maerin and I worriedly, eyes darting too fast for my liking, but nodded. "I trust you Dorian, but I don't understand anything that we just saw. You said it had to do with the journals .. Uh, your brother's VHS tapes and the like." He eyed me, perhaps worried that bringing up Rowan would lead to a bad reaction. It did, but it wasn't an outburst like he might've expected.

"Yeah, in part. Rowan found out about another place that he could go when he slept called The Endless Dream. He could visit other worlds and there are … death games called Instances that give rewards."

Joey said nothing, just nodded along. I wished he'd go hysterical and start yelling. I could deal with that, but he just looked at me, waiting patiently as if I had all the answers. I could feel his nervousness, apprehension, and worry. I could see his eyes glazing over slightly. I just didn't know how to address this in a way good for him. I knew him, but I didn't know how to tell him about this next part. "Our world isn't as continuous as we usually experience it. Changes are made and we believe them to have always been that way. The first Instance I entered rewrote my memories, and I'm pretty sure those memories turned into reality."

Joey's mouth flailed like a suffocating fish, "How do you know that?"

I paused, "I don't, but no one's corrected me on anything, and if it were just my memories, someone should have noticed," I looked to Maerin and took a deep breath, "recent events also point to that."

Joey followed my gaze, and I felt his heart sink. I turned to Maerin, "Tell me exactly what's happened since you came to today."

Maerin wore a slight scowl at the command, but didn't say anything more. "I woke up here, tried to find out what was going on for a few hours looking through the" she paused, searching for a word, "… phone, and then we went to hang out with one of Joey's friends. Who ended up being you."

I raised my brow at Joey, "Buddy, you left out a lot of important details there."

It got a forced laugh out of him, "Well, I know you two haven't always gotten along. I know it's gotten better … but last time you were looking through your brother's journals," he trailed off, and I winced. Yeah, I hadn't exactly been pleasant back then, but the one I hadn't been pleasant to wasn't Maerin, it was someone else. In that moment, I couldn't bring myself to tell Joey the truth. That one of the most important people in his life disappeared from existence and was replaced by a girl who I had met yesterday and who didn't know a single thing about him. He'd figure it out eventually, if I just let it all run its course.

Then I looked at Maerin and realized I was doing it again. She wasn't one of the three people I'd do anything to protect, but I had brought her here, and I owed her. She deserved this much help. At the very least, she didn't deserve to be stuck in such a compromising position, not after what she had been through in her world.

I paused and checked in mentally. I considered Maerin's second Boon and briefly thought she may have used it on me to do this. I quickly rejected the idea. She couldn't use Boons outside of Instances like I could, at least not yet.

Joey stared at me expectantly, and I could stall no longer. I did anyway and took another drag of vodka straight from the bottle. "Maerin isn't your girlfriend. She's from The Endless Dream. I don't know what happened to the one who came before her."

Joey's hand slowly slid off Maerin's, and she quickly retreated to the other side of the couch. Her muscles tensed, as if prepared for the worst, but Joey just sat there. "That's… that's not possible," he said. "That's not possible," he reaffirmed, "I remember meeting her freshman year, asking her out, meeting her parents. We did that play together too." He turned to Maerin, whose expression had softened considerably at Joey's shaking voice. "I remember it, you do too, Dorian! We've talked about this, it's not true, right Maerin?" he begged.

"I'm sorry. It's true," she looked away.

His eyes were wild, not dangerous, but certainly desperate. "Dorian, you promised not to fuck with me here. You promised! Do not fuck with me man."

I moved forward and wrapped Joey in a hug. I didn't know what else to do, and it made my skin crawl, and I didn't want anyone to fucking touch me, even if I initiated it, but he needed this right now. He went limp in my grasp, but his chin rested forward on my shoulder. "What the fuck is happening, Dorian? What was with the spiders?"

I broke the hug, feeling awkward and unsure. I went back to my chair, but scooted it closer. "They're from the Dream. I don't know why they're appearing, but they're not here now. They're only by my apartment and the school."

Maerin had said something about God protecting the world from Instances leaking through, so I threw her a questioning glance. She nodded, but opted not to say anything as she looked at Joey with a cautious expression. I picked up on the cue, a conversation for another time.

"Are you real? Do you remember the same as me?" Joey asked. The question broke my heart, and I was infinitely thankful for the answer.

"Yeah. I'm real. I remember it the same as you, everything since we were kids. The good and the bad." I assured him and myself in the same breath.

Silence filled the room, so thick I could choke on it. It originated from Joey, and I knew that silence intimately.

Joey suddenly stood up, "I need a bit."

"Joey," I started. He held up a hand, eyes closed.

"I'm fine— actually no I'm not, but I will be. I need a second. If I go for a run am I going to be safe?" he asked.

I looked to Maerin. She nodded. I nodded to Joey.

"I'll be back in a little bit," he said. His eyes clamped tighter, and his breath hitched, "Dorian. You promise you're not fucking with me. Right?"

"I wish I was," I replied, "I really wish I was."

"Alright," his voice cracked, and he started walking towards the door.

"Be safe," the words came out of my mouth from nowhere, "please."

He didn't turn to me, but I saw his shoulders shudder more. There were no words, just a heavy nod of his head. He left. The door shut, and I took another deep swig of vodka. Maerin moved over on the couch and reached out for the bottle as well. I handed it to her, and she followed suit. Twenty minutes passed, and we had drunk more than half, and the alcohol started to kick in.

It felt like the beginning of a series of decisions.
