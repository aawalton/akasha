“Hello dear wife,” Caleb said to Kelia who sat in a snow drift in the middle of the training yard.

Avis, now a brilliant white with blue accents sat perched beside he basking in the chill of her aura. The bird’s attitude and general demeanor remained generally unchanged after her transformation into what Kelia said was a frost phoenix, but with two exceptions. Her opinion of Caleb had warmed, and all the ire she’d once had for Caleb was added to the hate she had for Bog.

The two beasts had not gotten along before, but now that their innate magics were at odds, the only thing keeping them from fighting were the combined wills of Caleb and Kelia pushed through their bonds.

“What do you need?” Kelia asked, in a knowing yet amused tone.

“I just wanted to see how your training was going,” Caleb said. “Is that so unbelievable?”

“What’s in that pouch then?” she asked, pointing to the sack of quenching steel in his hand.

“Oh this? Well, now that you mention it, my quenching steel seems to be out of its… quenchyness.”

Kelia gave him a look, and then extended her hand. Caleb lifted the bag with his magic and it drifted over the distance to her. She snatched it out of the air and looked inside before pulling a pinch out and rubbing it between her fingers.

“It’s absorbing the cold mana from my aura,” She said. “I think it depleted it’s mana when you used it on that pyromancer.”

“Oh, sorry—“ he said going to reach it back from her. He turned his attention to his Magical Perception and saw she was right, the cold mana that was most of her aura was streaming into the bag.

“No, it’s fine,” she said, pulling the bag away. “It’s useful. I’m struggling to generate mana when my pool is full, so I’ve been emptying it by using my Imperium to craft things. This is like my ring, I can push all my mana into it to practice generating it.”

“And that means…”

“That you will have this back at the end of the day with enough cold mana in it to make your silly little crafts.” The words she said were in a gentle teasing.

He gave her a rather chilly kiss, and they parted to go back to their own training.

***

Back at his forge he found Mettius examining the armor that once belonged to his sister while Drusus awkwardly tried to look busy despite the fact that there was very little work for him to do.

“Have you gotten the chance to examine this yet?” he asked Caleb after a greeting.

“No, not yet,” Caleb said, “I know once I do I’ll get absorbed by it to the exclusion of all else and we are moving out soon. I decided to do some work that would be more immediately helpful for the legion.”

“Admirable, but that’s not how an equites is supposed to behave,” Mettius said. “Your growth should be your sole focus to the exclusion of all else. The legionnaires will appreciate timely repairs to their equipment sure, but what they really want from you is to step in an save the day when an enemy hero shows up on the battlefield.”

Caleb nodded, seeing the sense in the words, though he suspected that the motivation for more equites had less to do with the potential aid they could render and more to do with a general sense of self importance. Still, he was glad for the work he did. He was unique in that he had a craft that used his magic. Many times now he’d learned a technique forging that manifested as a skill in battle, and just today he’d leveled Sunder outside of battle while working in a forge. While he might not gain essence and advance in levels as a crafting class might, he had the next best thing—potentially a better thing.

He'd dreamed of being a lowly Blacksmith, but he struggled to see how any system granted skills could have elevated his crafting more than those he developed himself.

“It helps me develop new skills,” Caleb said. “Even today I leveled my new Sunder skill repairing my blade.”

“You repaired a blade with Sunder?” Mettius said surprised.

The skill was a known one to him and he’d discussed it at length with Caleb already.

Caleb nodded.

“All it does is let me direct the force of a blow into flaws. If seeks the flaws out itself, but I can direct it if I apply my concentration to it.”

Mettius looked at Caleb with a new appreciation in his eyes.

“When you evolve your class, I think you will do just fine,” he said. “But that’s not why I’m here. If you are going to study that armor, you need to do it sooner rather than later.”

“Why?”

“While I gave up my inheritance to get my equipment, Cassandra accepted hers as gifts. Many senators and patricians give gifts to the heirs to curry favor with the potential next emperor. They will want it back now that the investment didn’t pan out.”

“But isn’t it yours now? Since you killed her.”

“Yes… I killed her,” Mettius said, though they both knew Caleb had done most of the actual killing. “But I don’t wish to make an enemy of the senator that gave her that armor. And besides, its ruined. I couldn’t afford to repair it.”

“I could—” Caleb began to say he could fix it, but Mettius shot him a meaningful look, jerking his head toward Drusus.

“Let’s go check on Kelia’s progress,” Mettius said, and Caleb sensed it would be unwise to point out that he’d just been talking to her.

They left the forge, and once alone Mettius spoke.

“I know you have more than an affinity for steel,” he said plainly.

“How?” Caleb said, realizing even then that he’d just confirmed it.

“You’re more subtle than your brother, but you should both take lessons from your wife,” Mettius said. “Aside from the fact that there’s no way you could have detected Cassandra in the darkness without the ability to detect more than steel, you also could detect the magic in the gauntlets and didn’t balk at the suggestion you could do the same with that armor. Armor that I might add is a combination of a few varieties of mage metals, one of which is mithril and the rest nearly as rare.”

“And having more than a steel affinity is bad?”

Mettius nodded.

“If you value your freedom it is. For most, it wouldn’t matter, many Blacksmiths develop it after decades of work but you already have it and are still young, and are a gifted smith on top of it and a bit of a magical prodigy.”

“I am?” Caleb asked, genuinely surprised at the assessment.

“You are, and your wife might be as well. The reason no one crafts skills is that its hard, and the fear of loosing the System’s assistance is crippling for most. Forging your own path is hard. It’s high risk with high reward, but it takes skill and luck. Something most lack. The empire tightly controls anyone who could potentially make magical equipment beyond the simple aspected gear most capable smiths can craft. Even those enchanted ingots you’ve been trying to hide so poorly are beneath their care.”

Caleb instinctively reached for his pouch of ingots and then winced. He was bad at deception.

Mettius smiled.

“But you have shown a potential for growth and have a remarkable start. I have no doubt you could one day repair my half-sister’s armor, but you can never let it be known. Your value to the empire on the battlefield is nothing compared to what they would have you do collared and chained to a forge.”

“Collared?” Caleb asked, as the chained to a forge was admittedly not too daunting—assuming it was a slight hyperbole.

“Yes. They’d still need you to level but they couldn’t risk you falling into enemy hands or escaping. The only way for that would be to collar you and place you in the arena. You’d loose your dragon rider class and gain a gladiator class, but you’d keep your affinities and all your natural talent.”

Caleb was suddenly both very grateful in his initial instinct to hide his magic, and simultaneously mad at himself for doing such a poor job. Suddenly, he realized how much power Mettius had over him, and as usual the thought was visible on his face.

“Don’t worry about me,” Mettius said. “I won’t betray you to my father’s servants. There is a lack of good men in the equites.”

“I sense that doesn’t mean you don’t want something from me,” Caleb said.

He might be bad at hiding things but he was very perceptive.

“I won’t extort you or blackmail you, but I would like to propose a trade.”

Well Caleb didn’t protest, he continued.

“I will let you study my equipment, and supply you with materials, and you will make me equipment.”

Caleb had to stop himself from agreeing on the spot.

Free magic metal to experiment with and examples to study?

“What’s the catch?” he asked.

“Why does there have to be a catch?” Mettius asked sincerely. “You have a skill I need, and I have resources you lack. I’ll pay you for your work with metals you can use to outfit yourself and your family. It’s a mutually beneficial arrangement.”

Mettius was right, and from what he knew of the man so far, he seemed to be trust worthy and honest.

“I’ll agree on one condition,” Caleb said.

When Mettius only looked expectantly, he continued.

“Tell me what your goal is.”

Mettius considered for a moment and then looked around.

“That’s fair. I have a rather large bit of leverage over you. I’ll tell you, but should you tell another, I’ll not keep your secret as I’m hauled off in chains.”

“Deal,” Caleb said.

Mettius took a deep breath.

“I have no wish to be emperor, and I don’t think any of my siblings should be either. I don’t think there should be an emperor.”

Caleb’s eyes grew wide.

“You’re a rebel?”

“No, those fools are ants raging against a tiger that stepped on their ant hill. They want to tear down a system they don’t understand and have nothing replace it but chaos. I don’t want to destroy the empire. I want to change it. Have you heard of democracy?”
