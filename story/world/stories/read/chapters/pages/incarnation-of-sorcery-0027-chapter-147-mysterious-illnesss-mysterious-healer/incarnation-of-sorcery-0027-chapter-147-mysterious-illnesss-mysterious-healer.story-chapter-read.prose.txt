Suspicious as the sudden elimination of the trouble the Rhellian Institute was facing seemed, there wasn’t a whole lot he could do about it. He had been specifically requested to not interfere by the very woman who had initially asked for his help.

All because the Demiurge had gotten spooked at Ryland’s potential involvement and had levied a harsh threat that he still wasn’t quite sure about.

What exactly had that Guardian meant by needing to use force? Would he really have taken measures that would have harmed the very children Wyla and her compatriots were desperately seeking to safeguard in the first place?

Ryland wasn’t certain.

What he was certain about, however, was a certain fishiness in how everything was resolved so easily.

The Rhellian Institute had invited everyone who were doubting them into a large meeting with the institute’s council of High Academs. Yulkaviel had been part of the meeting, which was why Ryland had even heard about what had gone on in there.

Essentially, after considerable talking, the Rhellians who had doubted their own academy’s ability to protect their students had been convinced they were hasty in their harsh judgment. They were willing to give the institute another opportunity to prove themselves. They would allow their children to remain and continue learning.

Of course, they had technically exacted a lot of promises from the academy first. Things like consulting all parents on specific locations regarding field trips, modifying the curriculum to include more practical lessons regarding magic and its applications, and things of that nature. It just all felt like lip service at best to Ryland.

The real point of that event was the fact that Jure’s delegation no longer possessed any real teeth. They had come here with the intention of retrieving their students from the Rhellian Institute, and had gone on to receive surprising reinforcements in the form of other disgruntled Rhellians.

Only for said reinforcements to cave so easily at just one little meeting.

One could be forgiven for thinking that had been the point of them in the first place. As though their belligerence had been planted as a ploy to make the Jure delegation’s intentions seem hasty and uncalled for.

Except, of course, Ryland had seen that woman’s true desperation to safeguard the youth of her world. She had changed her tune very rapidly at a threat from a Rhellian Guardian, but before that, she had been wholly sincere in her efforts.

Ryland wasn’t quite sure what had happened there, so for the time being, he stayed out of the matter. As he had been asked to do.

But fixing the issue of those standing against the Rhellian Institute didn’t suddenly allow the institute administration to focus on the upcoming awarding ceremony and nothing else. A new complication was rising. At this rate, Ryland was almost starting to think the institute was cursed.

For several of the institute students had contracted a mysterious and seemingly incurable disease.

Viren hadn’t expected he’d make a Rhellian friend as easily as he’d had.

“I don’t know…” Tezar said. For all that the other boy was a few years older than Viren, he neither looked nor acted like it. “They won’t allow it easily.”

“Right, but are you going to let that stop you?” Viren asked.

Viren hadn’t gotten through to Tezar as easily as he felt like he should have. They had both heard the news. They had both seen the absence.

A bunch of Rhellians were fatally ill. No one not immediately attending to the students was allowed to even know what they were suffering. They lay insensate on their beds, tended to by the best Healer Mages available on Rhel, yet there hadn’t been a lick of improvement in two days. If anything, Viren had heard that the condition of the afflicted was growing worse.

No one knew where it had come from. Even the Healer Mages were unable to diagnose whatever the horrid illness was, much less actually start treating it properly.

Viren had failed to get a glimpse of what was going on. He felt rightfully awful about the whole situation of course. No one should need to go through a terminal illness. But more than moping about feeling horrified, what he actually wanted was to fix the Calamity-cursed problem.

“Are you really going to wait until one of them dies?” Viren asked since Tezar hadn’t replied to his earlier question.

The Rhellian winced, then looked offended at the notion. Maybe Viren was pushing things a little. They had only gotten to know each other for a few weeks now. He and Tezar were nowhere near as close as he and Acrypha were, much as he’d never admit it out loud.

But this was a situation that didn’t leave room for a subtler approach. People were about to die.

“Fine,” Tezar said. “You want to see why I keep hesitating? Come on.”

He led the way and Viren followed eagerly. There. Things should be handled, right?

Well, it turned out nothing was so simple. They couldn’t even get close to the area where they were keeping the ill students.

“This quarter is restricted,” said the armoured Rhellian Academ barring the hallway. He didn’t have a helm on, so his haggard face was openly scowling down at the two boys. “Haven’t you heard what’s going on?”

“Yes, sir,” Tezar said. He had hesitated at seeing the teacher before them, but had forged onwards at a little nudge from Viren. “But we think we know how to help them.”

The Academ’s brows climbed nearly to his skull. “You think you know how to help?”

Tezar backed away, shaking his hands in disagreement. “Not us! Not exactly, I mean. But we know someone who can probably figure out what’s going on—”

Apparently, that was entirely the wrong thing to say because the Academ’s drawn face now tightened with lines of anger. “Are you insane? You think two boys have figured out what’s going on when the entire Realm of Rhel is stumped? Have you no sense of respect?”

“Sir, I—”

“And I could excuse some foreign brat not understanding anything, but you! You’re a Rhellian. You’ve lived and breathed the same air as me for your entire life. How could you truly think that you know something we don’t?”

Viren was so taken aback by the burst of anger, he was also momentarily recoiling. His thoughts caught back up with him within a few moments, however. It was natural for the man to be frustrated at his current circumstances, but to take it out on two students like this was beyond uncalled for. And what kind of weird point was he even trying to make?

“Sir,” Viren said. “We’re only trying to help. Can’t you at least hear us out? Just because we’re a couple of students doesn’t mean we’re clueless about everything.”

“I did not ask for your opinion, foreigner,” the Academ said rather viciously. “And I will request that you stop instigating such devious acts among my fellow Rhellians as well. Otherwise, I will be forced to complain to your Academs that you’ve been causing us a great deal of trouble.”

It was Viren’s turn to feel outraged. “Trouble? We literally want to help!”

“And all you’ll be doing is wasting our precious time. So for the last time, get out!”

Tezar grabbed Viren’s arm and more or less dragged the younger boy away. Viren didn’t want to give up the fight so easily. Once, he might have quailed against someone so superior standing like an impassable wall before him. But not anymore.

Not after he had met Ryland. Not after everything he had gone through.

Not after the ferocious battle with the Calamity and its supporters.

“I told you,” Tezar was saying. “I told you it wasn’t going to work. They won’t trust us. They’re completely sure that nothing we can do is going to help.”

“I’m not giving up,” Viren said adamantly. He looked at his new friend a little harshly. “And you shouldn’t either! I don’t know why you keep hesitating, Tezar, but we have to be more resilient about this.”

“But that assumes we know what to do. And we don’t! All we know is that someone might know a way to fix this.”

“Oh, I see. So you don’t think my plan is going to work?”

“Well, we did just get stopped…”

“That wasn’t part of the plan! See, they don’t actually know anything about our plan, they’re only assuming things because we’re just students.”

Tezar didn’t answer.

Viren came to a halt with a frown. His expression was clouding over. “They don’t actually know, right?”

There was something going on. Something hinted at by Tezar’s weird recalcitrance ever since Viren had gotten to know him. But direct questions weren’t going to help. It hadn’t before and Viren wasn’t even sure why he was bothering with it now, since Tezar was choosing to remain silent.

“Alright, fine,” Viren huffed, before continuing his purposeful walk. “Keep your secrets.”

Tezar shook his head. “What are you even thinking of doing?”

“Getting some reinforcements.”

“What are you even thinking of doing?” Acrypha asked when Viren finally found her. She hadn’t been in her room. Rather, she had chosen to visit the library and look up the history of Rhel of all things.

“Getting some reinforcements,” Viren said. “Which happens to be you.”

Acrypha didn’t look up from her old book. “What am I supposed to do about an illness, Viren?”

“It’s not just an illness, Acrypha.”

Acrypha finally frowned up at him. “Don’t believe every silly rumour.” She looked over at Tezar. “Thanks for handling him. It’s given me a lot of time that I otherwise wouldn’t have for myself. He’s not actually right, though, is he?”

Tezar once again looked noncommittal about it all. “He’s not wrong, but he’s also…”

“He’s Viren. Yes, I’m well aware. It’s why I was thanking you for taking care of him.”

“You’re not taking this seriously!” Viren said. “It might get those students killed!”

Acrypha sighed. “What exactly is your plan? How am I supposed to help? Didn’t you just say sir has been banned from helping?”

“Well, yes, but you’re the most brilliant student I know. They’ll listen to you.”

“Really? A student like me? Did you just hear yourself speak? A student. Shouldn’t you be going to High Academ Tyra or one of the Mage Knights or someone like that?”

Tezar looked between the two of them and shrank a little like he wanted to disappear.

“They were going to be my next option, yes,” Viren said, raising his head haughtily, like he had seen Acrypha herself do so many times now. “I was just hoping I’d be able to count on you.”

Acrypha sighed and closed her book. “Fine. Let’s go.”

Viren couldn’t help the grin that popped up on his face. “Right. Where are we going?”

“You’ll see. Come on. Let’s not waste any time.”

Viren wanted to think she was now taking the issue seriously, but a scowling part of him couldn’t stop believing that she just wanted to get back to her studies. Nevertheless, her strides were purposeful so he didn’t complain.

Even though he did want to voice the question of what in the world they were doing in front of High Academ Zevo’s office.

“Acrypha…” Viren began, but she cut him off.

“High Academ,” Acrypha said, knocking hard enough to make the door shake on its hinges. “We know the cure for the ill students.”

For a moment, Viren wondered if the door would be opened by one angry High Academ who would only respond by blasting the three of them with magic. Maybe they’d all be turned to frogs and forced to ribbit all the way back to their rooms. It certainly looked like Tezar was being turned into a frog, going by his reaction to Acrypha’s forceful knocks.

But then the door opened. High Academ Zevo was indeed scowling at them all, enough to make Tezar retreat behind Viren. He hadn’t blasted them with a spell, though. That was something.

“Are you mad?” High Academ Zevo asked.

Viren more or less grabbed the opportunity he had been handed. The man had taken a good look at them and hadn’t asked who they were. “I am mad. Mad that a bunch of students might just end up dead because of negligence.”

The High Academ’s nostrils flared. “There has been no negligence in how we have treated our students to the best of our capabilities. Day or night, no matter the hour, we’ve treated their boils, pulled the poison out of their blood, forced their clenched hearts to keep pumping, always seeking the source that’s been keeping them in their current state. This isn’t negligence.”

“Then what are we supposed to call it when someone doesn’t use all their options?”

“It’s called being careful. Something I wouldn’t expect a bunch of children to understand.”

“Careful about what?”

Viren thought he almost—almost—had it. That he had nearly made the High Academ reveal the real reason behind their recalcitrance. But Zevo wasn’t about to let his feelings run away from him.

At Viren’s blunt question, the High Academ had clamped up, content to only stare down at Viren with growing sharpness.

“Thank you, sir,” Acrypha said with a bow. “We’ll be taking our leave for now.”

Viren turned to her with a surprised frown. “We will?”

“We will.”

“But we haven’t gotten permission yet.”

Acrypha glanced over at the suddenly tight-lipped High Academ. “Oh, I think we have everything we need. Come on, let’s not bother the High Academ any further.”

Viren wasn’t quite sure what exactly had just transpired between them all, especially when their little meeting had been so short. But he did trust Acrypha. Especially because their very next destination was the real lynchpin of this entire operation.

“…so you see, sir,” Acrypha was saying inside Ryland’s room. “I figured I’d gain just enough information about the illness to see if you can diagnose what’s going on. And we went to High Academ Zevo very specifically because if there’s one person in this entire institute who has even a modicum of faith in you, then it’s the High Academ you smacked into a wall.”

Viren beamed. There. He had always known that going to Acrypha would be an amazing idea.

Ryland, however, wasn’t beaming. If anything, he was trying not to frown, though Viren had grown accustomed to his foibles enough to note the bottom line. Things weren’t particularly good.

“You know what it is, don’t you, sir?” Viren asked.

Tezar looked on like he didn’t dare hope.

Ryland got up with a little nod. “I believe I do. Serious as the issue is, I didn’t think it would be this. You all did an excellent thing to glean some more information, because now I know that I must act.”

Viren found himself swallowing a little. “Why? What happened to them, sir?”

“I suspect,” Ryland said. “That they’ve been infected by a piece of me.”
