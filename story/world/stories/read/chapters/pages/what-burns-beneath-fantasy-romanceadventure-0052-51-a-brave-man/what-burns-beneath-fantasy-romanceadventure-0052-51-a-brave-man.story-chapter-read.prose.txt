Ice wrenched through my insides. I cried out, doubling over, as the Fae’s heat withdrew.

Then… silence.

I buried my face in my hands, thoroughly defeated. I’d failed. All this work, every step of this entire nightmarish journey… for nothing. I’d failed.

He’d won.

I’m such a fool. I can’t believe… I’m such a fool.

I couldn’t bear to look up. I didn’t want to see the sharp smile above me, or the hatred in Renner’s eyes. I drew my knees tight, pressing my forehead into them. Wrapped trembling arms around my legs.

I don’t know how long I stayed like that. It could have been hours. But eventually the silence became too stifling, so I dared to peek up through my lashes.

The cave was a haze of dust and smoke, illuminated by smoldering embers and that putrid green glow.

He was gone.

I unclenched my fists. They’d been so tight that I could feel warm drops of blood beneath my nails.

Gone. He’s gone. We’re… safe.

It wasn’t much, but I breathed a little easier. Raised my head a bit more.

Renner was on his feet. Pale. Staring down at mottled, crimson-streaked hands that no longer wept fresh blood.

He’s alright.

As if he could feel my gaze, his own lifted. Latched onto me. The hatred in it was scorching.

Will he kill me? That’s what he… does. The thought struck like a physical blow. I was everything he hated. I dealt with Fae. I was going to help one.

I have to… if I don’t bring the shard later, it’ll be undone. Renner’s hands, his head, Teela… If he wants to kill me, he’s going to have to wait a bit.

I lifted my chin, ready to tell him as much, just as Teela groaned.

I stumbled to her on numb legs. “Teela! You’re alright! You’re… Are you? Alright? Does anything hurt?” My gaze swept down frantically, searching for any injury, but she did indeed seem to be unharmed. The only trace of her ordeal was a faded, toothy scar on one forearm.

“Me? Yeah, of course I- where are we?” She winced, looking me up and down. “Salt, what happened to you?” Her red eyes cut towards Renner, growing impossibly round. “Salt, and you?!”

She lurched off the stone and spun in a panicked circle. Took in, with obvious and increasing alarm, the nightmarish scene she had woken to. Smoke, smoldering flames, stones smeared by crimson and black, a basin of bloody witchwood, and dozens of long-dead, macabre little forest creatures. “What in the realms is all this?!”

I caught her hand. Mine was trembling. “It’s okay. It is. We’re… everything’s alright.” I could feel Renner’s glare boring into me as I managed a weak, tearful smile. “You’re… s-safe.”

She goggled at Renner, with his blood-smeared hands, neck, jaw, and the spattered earth behind him. “Yeah, uh, I don’t feel safe.”

An exhausted titter escaped my lips, and it quickly turned into a sob. I pressed my palms against my eyes. “You are. S-safe. I promise. Do you… do you remember anything?”

I followed her gaze. Baltha’s head lay turned away from us, a mound of scraggly hair and blood-smeared flesh. Her enormous body was crumpled nearby, limbs bent at unnatural angles.

Vomit rose in the back of my throat. I ducked my head, unwilling to look at the grisly sight.

Teela actually crept towards the body, jaw hanging low. “What happened?!”

“Fae.” Renner’s voice was hoarse. He was still staring at me. His gaze drifted down, settling on my mouth, and his jaw tightened.

“It’s awful. It’s… huge. It’s… dead? You killed it? You two actually killed a Fae? She’s all… burnt.” She practically gagged on the word. “Why don’t I remember anything except going to sleep?”

What? March through the forest in the middle of the night? Sneak into an inn full of people who tried to kill me, who nearly did kill me, and take Renner’s bag without them noticing?

My shoulders slumped. Foul, stagnant air filled my mouth. It was an impossible task under normal circumstances, and right now… I was barefoot. Exhausted. Reeling. Drained of energy and of magic, and far too frightened to even think of trying more runes; not when doing so could leave me comatose and I wouldn’t have the shard and he’d be there and angry and covered in blood and I’d fail again and Teela would collapse and Renner’s head would-

“Brin? Brin?”

Teela’s bony fingers dug into my shoulders. “H-hey, breathe, okay? You look like you’re gonna faint.”

My mouth. I lifted trembling fingers and gasped, flinching away from my companions. Warm, fresh blood was smeared along my lips. Across my unbruised throat. I felt dirty. Unclean. From the cold that had snaked through my insides, from his very touch. Slick fingers on my skin.  Cruel. Trapping me.

It felt like something vile had crawled inside of me. Something I wasn’t sure I could wash away.

But I could certainly try. I seized the bottom of my tunic. Humid air brushed my stomach as I lifted and scrubbed, so hard that it was nearly painful. Baltha’s blood was slick and warm, and I felt it rub away quickly. I kept scrubbing. Pressed the thick fabric furiously against my mouth, trying to tear away every trace of his touch.

“B-Brin?” Teela whispered after several moments. “Hey, hey, are you… what can I…?” She trailed off, voice falling to a murmur. One hand settled between my shoulderblades.

I shook my head. Kept my face buried in the soft cloth. Choked down tears. There’d be time for that later.

Breathe. Keep it together. Just… just a little bit longer. Need to get the shard.

Stillness pressed in, threatening to crush me in this rank, sweltering pit. I lowered my hands, but couldn’t seem to stop wringing my hem. “R-Renner.” I looked at him pleadingly. “You… don’t have to help. I… I don’t want you to. But… but the inn. There’s f-five of them. How-”

“Four.”

I sniffled. “N-no. Agnes, Josiah, Wa-”

“Four.” His voice was flat. Lethal. “The guy on you? I know where I hit him. He was dead before the kid.”

Teela looked back and forth between us, red eyes rounded and shining with barely-contained panic. Her tail snaked through the hazy air.

“O-oh. Oh.” I sagged, exhaustion creeping through my limbs. “Four. We’re not… y-you don’t, but I… I have to go. I…” my voice broke. I buried my face in my hands again, head shaking back and forth. “I have to get… I don’t know how to-”

“I do,” he growled. Soft thuds echoed as he moved forwards. Towards me.

I flinched back again, stifling a cry. “Don’t-!”

Renner froze for a step. Then he continued, with very deliberate slowness, around me. He stopped, grimacing down at Baltha’s battered head. Mottled, blood-stained fingers gripped ragged hair and lifted.

“What is going on?” Teela sounded like she was about to cry.

I tried to think of something comforting to say, something reassuring, but all the words died in my throat. I looked towards Renner.

As if he’d offer comfort.

He was stiff. Muscles in his jaw working, free hand clenching and unclenching. Then he turned towards us. “You said the captain was trustworthy?” He winced. “At least, seemed to be?”

I nodded.

“Right. Probably would’ve insisted on going with you if…” Thick brows furrowed as he trailed off, lost in thought. His chest swelled with a sigh. “Come on.”
