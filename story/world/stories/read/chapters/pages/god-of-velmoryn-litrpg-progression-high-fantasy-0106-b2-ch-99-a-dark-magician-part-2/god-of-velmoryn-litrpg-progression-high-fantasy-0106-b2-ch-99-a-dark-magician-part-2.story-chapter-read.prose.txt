The night was still young when Aria, Lucas, and about a dozen other velmoryns surrounded Nelya’s house.

It was quiet outside. Almost all of Eriavel remained gathered before the Crimson Guardian while Tekla prepared for the ritual. She had chosen to stay and perform her priestly duties rather than accompany Aria and Lucas. In part, it was her way of showing how deeply disappointed she was, but mostly, she wanted to keep the matter quiet for now. Her departure would certainly not have gone unnoticed.

“Remember, Lucas,” Aria said. “We came to capture them alive. Especially Freya. We cannot let Dariel…”

“I know,” Lucas cut her off, his crimson eyes fixed on the only window glowing with dim light.

Aria pressed her lips together and gave him one last scrutinizing look. Then she stepped out from beneath the veil of darkness and approached the house.

The street was empty, so Aria was difficult to miss. A group already on high alert certainly did not miss her.

The door opened, and a flustered man stepped outside.

“Lady Aria?” he exclaimed, quickly pulling the door shut behind him.

It failed to close properly. He opened and shut it several more times, each attempt harder than the last.

“I should replace the hinges,” he muttered.

“Are you leaving, Keleign?” Aria asked, ignoring his words as she continued toward him.

“Yes, I am,” the man said hastily, finally managing to close the door. “I intend to attend the ritual. Nelya is already waiting there. Would you care to join us?”

“No. I do not wish to partake in rituals that are not dedicated to our Lord.”

Aria leaned slightly to one side and nodded toward the window.

“You seem to have left the crystals lit. I would advise against that. Unless they overheat and, our Lord forbid, something happens that harms your small family… or our entire settlement.”

“Huh?”

“The light,” Aria said. “I can see it through the window.”

“Ah, that…” Keleign chuckled hoarsely. “Those are not crystals, Lady Aria. They are candles. Our Lord’s son taught us how to make them while you were away.”

“Oh?”

Aria leaned farther to the side, as though trying to see the candles through the window.

“I would love to examine them. Anything created by our Lord is precious to behold.”

“Eriavel is full of them, Lady Aria,” Keleign replied, refusing to yield. “I am certain we can find some on our way to the Divine Tree.”

“Why go to such lengths when one is already so close? You could offer me wine as well. I hear the Silver Tribe is famous for its liquor.”

Aria stepped onto the narrow wooden staircase leading to the raised platform before the house.

“We are indeed, but for a man to entertain a woman alone with wine so late at night…” Keleign said. “It would hardly be proper, Lady Aria.”

“I am certain there are worse things that could tarnish a person’s name.”

Aria was already standing beside him, reaching for the door handle.

“Wait!”

Keleign reached for her hand, but an invisible force stopped him before he could touch her.

“It is time we spoke plainly, Keleign,” Aria said coldly.

The force pushed him half a step away from her.

“I know exactly who is inside and why they are here. Believe me, it would be better for you to open the door willingly and allow me to enter peacefully. There is still a chance this situation has not become irreparable. Do not waste it.”

Keleign went rigid. His knuckles whitened and his teeth ground together, but in the end, he did not dare attack Aria, assuming he had even considered it.

Aria had been regarded as a formidable mage even before I blessed her. Now, only I knew just how absurdly powerful she had become.

“I’m not sure what you…” Keleign began, but before he could finish, the door opened from within.

“Come in,” Nelya said weakly.

Aria entered and quickly examined the velmoryns gathered inside.

“Freya is missing,” she thought with a sigh.

Was that… pity?

I was surprised to sense an emotion so closely tied to compassion and empathy coming from the dark magician.

Once she reached the center of the room, Aria released her mana.

“Wait!” Keleign yelled, lunging toward her.

An invisible force caught him before he could take another step and hurled him aside. He crashed into the wall and remained pinned against it.

A crimson tide erupted from Aria and surged through the room, enveloping every velmoryn inside. Whether they tried to flee or move toward her made no difference. All of them were immobilized before they could accomplish either.

She did not stop there.

Her mana, so dense it was almost tangible, pressed against the door leading into the adjoining room. The wood groaned, then cracked as the hinges tore free. The door collapsed inward, revealing a bedroom - and Freya leaning against the far wall, her eyes wide and her lips pale.

Aria extended one hand, and her mana obeyed.

Crimson energy coiled tightly around Freya. She made no attempt to escape or resist as it lifted her from the ground and carried her into the living room.

Only then did Aria allow her mana to fade. The scarlet light dimmed, allowing the room to return to normal color, though her presence remained just as overwhelming. She sat on the wooden couch covered in soft fur, crossed her legs, and leaned back.

“Why?” she asked.

Her voice echoed faintly through the room, where the silence was broken only by a chorus of rapid, heavy breaths.

“What do you mean, Lady Aria?” Nelya tried.

“Do not deny it! I know everything. Our Lord’s Emissary showed me what you whispered behind these walls. I know about your plans. Your schemes.” Her gaze moved across the gathered velmoryns before settling on Freya. “Do you understand what this means for Eriavel? Is your husband aware?”

She didn’t answer.

“To question the Priestess’s decisions is to question our Lord’s,” Aria continued. “Did you truly believe this small group could overthrow the order High Father set upon us? Do you not understand how dependent we are on His mercy?”

“We did not question our Lord, nor did any thought of disobedience enter our minds!” Nelya retorted when no one else dared speak against Aria. “Is questioning the Priestess now blasphemy? The Priestess herself admitted that she can make mistakes. It is our duty to question her.”

Her voice rose as she spoke, her eyes gleaming with a fire that showed she truly believed every word.

Whether those words were true was another matter.

“Should we remain silent when faced with injustice? Should we look away while only our brothers and sisters die on the battlefield?” Nelya sneered, a menacing smile spreading across her face. “You would not understand. Not one member of the Crimson Tribe perished. All of you survived and rose even higher, while we were left to mourn our dead. You could never understand what it feels like to watch those weaker than you rise while you are denied every oppor…”

“I would not understand?” Aria whispered. Her voice was quiet, yet it silenced Nelya at once.

“I would not understand what it means to lose my brethren? For decades… no! For centuries, it was my tribe that was crushed and suppressed. Your Vael killed my master when she questioned the ways of the Goddess. You stole our resources, our weapons, and our potions. You forced our alchemists to leave us and serve your tribes. You stole our future. You even kidnapped our children.”

She rose from the couch, her eyes settling on Freya.

Freya refused to meet her gaze.

“I would not understand what it feels like to be denied recognition?” Aria snorted. “I was the strongest warrior in my tribe when High Father first blessed us with His mercy. Since then, I have watched those who were once weaker than me surpass me and continue to grow. I endured my own tribesmen whispering that I had been punished for my lack of faith.”

Her fingers curled at her sides.

“But I never, not even once, disobeyed High Father or acted against my people. I waited. I cast aside every doubt and every poisonous thought within me, and I placed my complete faith in our Lord.”

Her mana swelled around her.

“And He rewarded me.”

The furniture, the floor, even the walls… everything trembled.

“That is what faith means. To trust in what you cannot see and what you fail to comprehend. Otherwise, it is not faith. It is merely recognition.”

I was speechless.

So were the velmoryns gathered in the room, though for entirely different reasons. As Aria’s emotions flared, her mana pressed down on everyone around her until even drawing breath became a struggle.

Then Aria sighed.

With that single exhale, she released the anger that had built within her. Her mana receded as well, and the velmoryns collapsed to their knees, breathing heavily.

Aria stared down at them.

There was no satisfaction in her eyes. Not even the smallest trace of pleasure at seeing the power she now possessed.

“Is this what I am meant to use it for? Was I blessed so I would intimidate and crush my own kin?” she thought, but quickly discarded the thought.

“You will follow me willingly and wait until the Priestess has finished the ritual,” she said, dismissing the last traces of her crimson presence. “After that, your fates will be decided.”

She turned toward the door and began walking, as though their consent had never been required.

The moment she reached the threshold, she flinched.

Goosebumps spread across her skin as every one of her senses warned her that something had just happened, though she had no idea what.

She could not even see the cyan glow as it washed over all of Eriavel.

Then I heard a familiar voice.

“Velmoryn God… Pintre came to Velmoryn God.”
