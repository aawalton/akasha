“Uh, hi.” Lexie didn’t know quite what to say. As much as she’d replayed the scenario in her head before getting here, she didn’t think much about greetings and introductions or really anything beyond telling him to knock off whatever he was doing.

Mostly because she hadn’t thought he would be this…unassuming. She’d been expecting someone a little kooky, like the Alchemist, or arrogant and bloodthirsty like the Emperor. Or somewhere in between, a scorned mad scientist who was ready to pull the trigger and let the world descend into madness simply because he had his life's work stolen from him. But this man simply looked like a weary older man, staring at them with a type of expectation.

“Well?” he asked. “Are you going to say something?”

“It sounds like you were expecting us,” Aiden spoke up.

“Of course,” he said. “You are very unpredictable, Lexie Sparrowfoot, but there’s a method to even your madness.”

A warning skittered down her spine as Lexie stared at the man, feeling like something was wrong somewhere.

“You knew we would be coming?”

“I knew you were investigating me,” he said. “And spying on my communications. So I figured at some point you would be here. Would you like to come in?” The last sentence was such a mundane shift from everything else he had said that it took her a moment to recognize the invitation.

“I think we can probably talk out here,” she said.

“I’m not standing out here in the hot sun and talking to you for an hour or so. My hip can’t take it anymore." He turned to climb the steps of his cabin. “Come in or don’t come in. It’s up to you. You’re the ones who need me anyway.”

Lexie turned back to her father, wondering what to do. They had to go in. It was what they were here for anyway. And if it was a trap, well, they should be powerful enough to fight their way out.

“I don’t sense any traps in the area,” Aiden said.

“Me either,” Tate concurred.

“I think two of us should go in and two should stand outside and keep watch," Lexie said. It was pretty easy to see where the split should be. “Dad and I will go in, and you two keep watch.”

Tate nodded, but Torin protested. “I have questions for him as well. About the Emperor.”

“We’ll take shifts,” Lexie said, so that they didn’t argue. “After we’re done with him, then we’ll let you in to ask whatever you want.”

That finally had Torin jerking his head into a nod.

"Call your Uncle," Lexie told Torin. "The Association should be on alert. Just in case."

He nodded again, and Lexie and Aiden headed inside.

Mikhail had left the door cracked open as though knowing they would come in. His home was cramped yet homey, filled eclectically with horse trinkets and sunny colors. Once again, there was something familiar about it that Lexie couldn’t quite put her finger on.

Mikhail stood by the kitchen sink, washing some plates. A kettle whistled in the air.

“Muan tea?” he asked as he turned around with a knowing gaze

“Have I been here before?” she asked.

“Not in this room, no,” he responded. “But it likely seems familiar, doesn’t it?”

Lexie swallowed. “Yes.”

He chuckled but didn't elaborate.

Lexie peeked at her father, and he was looking around watchfully, likely scanning for traps.

This was not how she expected this to go. This was all unsettling for her, and she felt the curious desire to run away, but she restrained it. She wasn't a little girl anymore, running from her problems, and neither was she a powerless human.

"No tea," she finally answered as he was waiting.

“Aiden? How about you?”

Aiden stiffened when his name was called, because it was said in such a familiar tone.

“I’m fine, too.”

“Good,” Mikhail said, and he poured himself a cup, sifting the herbs into the tea before he came back to settle on his threadbare couch.

“Now, go ahead. Tell me whatever you came here to tell me.”

“We want you to call off Saint-0,” Lexie said instantly. She knew that whatever was supposed to happen hadn’t happened yet. Aiden 2-0 was on standby in Indigo. Torin should be calling his Uncle by now.

Lexie had also led Pvilycht closer to the Emperor’s side, with the order to take him if it looked like he was going to push the big red button.

“And why should I do that?” Mikhail asked.

“Because what you’re doing is going to destroy the world,” Lexie didn’t know why he had done it so early. The Fae weren’t here yet, and all he would be doing was laying waste to human life.

“Tell me,” he said. “What exactly do you think Saint-0 is?”

Lexie raised an eyebrow. “I know everything. About your experiments. About the fact that you made an undying soul and tried to imbue it into a bunch of child soldiers, but it didn't work. I know that there has been one success so far, and you plan on making more to use against the Fae.”

He smiled. “You are pretty close, but you’re not entirely accurate. Why don’t you have a seat while I tell you the whole story? That way, you can get the full picture of what you demand.”

“Look, we don't have time. You have to–”

“We have time if I say we have time." His voice didn't harden, but it was commanding nonetheless. “Nothing is going to happen in the next hour or so anyway, and there’s still time for me to call it off. But to craft a proper argument and convince me not to do what I’m doing, you’ll have to know the full story, and I’m not going to do that with you two looming over me. So have a seat."

Aiden and Lexie shared a look. With every passing second, this situation got stranger and stranger, and Lexie got more and more uncomfortable. She wasn't scared of him, per se. He didn't appear menacing or malicious.

But there was something about him that felt so...disconcerting was the only word she could use.

Lexie wondered if she should threaten him. She could hang him by his neck in the air and threaten to torture him if he didn't give them what they wanted. She wouldn't actually do it, but he didn’t know that. Unless he did. He seemed to know a lot about her.

But she had a feeling that wouldn’t work here. He didn’t have an ounce of fear in his eyes, even knowing what she could do. Which meant that he likely had a counter prepared or that he didn’t care if he died.

After all, his soul would last forever.

There was really no option here apart from diplomacy. So Lexie slid into the seat, and her father sat next to her.

Mikhail gave a smile of satisfaction that wasn’t menacing but also wasn’t comforting.

“Good,” he said. “I’ve been waiting for this for a long time, you know. But I suppose it all had to happen like this for it to make sense.”  He spread his fingers apart. “Okay, so where do I start?”

“Saint-0.” Lexie said. “Why did you activate him now? I thought he was supposed to be your tool against the Fae?”

A smile tickled the side of his lips. “What exactly do you think Saint-0 is, Lexie Sparrowfoot?”

“I think you were working on a way to create undying souls,” she said.

"Yes," he answered. "Do you want to know how I did it?"

"By splicing souls and implanting them–"

"No." He chuckled. "That way wouldn't work, and it's far too messy. It's simple. A virus. Nice. Efficient. Beautiful. After all, that's how humans evolve, right? Through strife, famine, and illness. Through war. We become better versions of ourselves when we put ourselves through the wringer. It's a shame that we only realized it too late, after we already sacrificed our powers to the Fae invaders."

They stared at him in horror as he took another sip of tea.

"So tell me," he said, unrepetantly. "What happens next?"

"They died," Lexie's voice was hard. "You killed those children in Khamen."

He gave neither admission nor denial.

"But somehow Saint-0 lived.”

He put down his cup. "You're very close to the truth, I’m impressed. You have one thing wrong, though." He pinned her with his gaze. "None of my experiments failed."

"What?"

“We thought it did,” he admitted while sipping his tea. “I couldn’t figure out why all the children died. The virus was safe for animals, even though it never did what it was supposed to do. But it killed humans. Why? It drove me crazy for weeks as I tried to splice and fix whatever was wrong with it. But there was never anything wrong with it. We didn’t realize it until one of our agents died in the field and came back to life in another body.”

He pinned them with a look. “You see, what happened that day was that we had been trying to control chaos. We had tried to choose our strongest soldiers for the task, the youngest and best. But the virus prefers to make the choice itself. The children who had been killed by it had not resurrected because they’d not been chosen. Instead, the soul had jumped into other bodies in the room and lay dormant until it was time. Until they died and became my soldiers."

“The Emperor?” Lexie whispered.

“He’s one of them,” he admitted. "And the reason why he hasn't ascended his fleshly limits yet is that I told him not to. Because the Saints, as I call them, are obedient only to me.”

“I see.” Lexie swallowed. She placed her hands on her legs, her brain hurting.

“There are far more of us than you know, Lexie," he said. "There’s someone you’re not asking about. Someone who you should have been asking about."

His eyes showed that he knew the directions her thoughts travelled in, and he knew what she wanted to say, even though she never voiced it out.

“The answer is yes,” he said. “She was one too.”

Lexie’s breath caught. Her hands trembled. Aiden turned stiff as a stone beside her as Lexie looked out the window, spotting Torin patrolling.

She wondered if her ears were ringing or if someone was screaming from far away.

“Her capture by District 9 wasn’t an accident,” he said. “It was planned from the beginning. She was to be a double agent operating there, activated upon her death. And her death was the thing that activates you, Archmage, while the second part of her mission begins.” He said. “That was how it went in the iterations. But then we had a lucky surprise. We had a new unexpected Chosen… who appeared right unexpectedly. Unassuming at first. A simple error, a bug to be ignored, Until we realized what you were...” He said. "When I heard of your existence, I almost couldn’t believe it. Especially when you began to change the story in significant ways. It was clear that fate was curious about you, too, and so was chaos. You became a merge. A progenitor.”

Lexie didn't understand a whole lot of what he was saying. She was still stuck on her mother being an undying soul.

“I knew we could use you," he said. "So I tried to meet you. Through your mother and some old associates. But she'd cut me off. She refused to follow orders, even her mind fought me. Because of you." He shook his head. "Knowing she could do that, that you could make her do that, was incredible. Even as an infant, you'd interrupted my magic with something of your own. Caught between fate and chaos, somehow merging them both. I knew that you would certainly be the last step in my plan."

"What plan?"

"You already know, I'm sure," he said. "You might have the details a little foggy, but you know what I want. After all, you want the same thing. Which is exactly why the ISTS tried so hard to get rid of you. But they couldn't because by the time they realized what a threat you were, you were already outside of their control."

"They tried to get rid of me?"

"Yes, but they couldn't. They couldn't even get rid of people in your orbit whom you had illegally told the truth to. You didn't play by their rules. You had inadvertently become the focal point of the narrative, and the control was now in your hands. In other words, you won the game of the Chosen without even playing."

She blinked. "I see."

"Wonderful, right?"

"Why was it a game in the first place?" she asked.

"Because it was Janus' test," he said. "He couldn't simply give them the world they were searching for for nothing. Every Guardian gives a test before you attain the reward. Earth then chooses its champions who will decide its fate. The Chosen are Earth's champions."

“Why from another dimension?"

"Because then you wouldn't be bound by this dimension's limits. Your souls aren't bound by the Fae contract. You're the only ones who could do it. Champions of Janus." He leaned forward. "You’re going to help me reset everything to the way it’s supposed to be.”

“Uh, okay.” Even though that was exactly what Lexie had come here to offer, she was still confused. Putting everything else aside, since he already knew her, he should have known that he didn’t need to do all this to convince her to do it. He could have just come to her house and told her what he knew, and she would have been down to destroy the system.

Except for the fact that he was a crazy, child-murdering scientist.

Even knowing that it was so hard to connect that with the man sitting in front of her, who looked like a tired grandpa if anything.

There had to be something more going on here.

“Listen, we’re on the same page here,” she told him with her hand out placatingly. “I’m completely willing to help you with what you need to do, but this isn’t the way to go about it. In fact, you didn’t need to do all this to get my attention.”

“But I did,” he said. “You see, you’re not predictable, but there are things I can predict about you. There is a workaround."

"There is?" She remembered Vulcan had mentioned it before he’d been captured. He said that he’d found a workaround for her unpredictability.

Yet he was still dead.

"Vulcan failed because he failed to fundamentally understand who you were as a person,” Mikhail said. "He thinks too much like a computer scientist and thought you were ones and zeroes, and as long as he could hedge his bet...But he didn’t know you, didn’t see what you wanted from the drives you had. He didn't watch you, and when he did, he didn't truly comprehend what he was watching. But I did. I understood it all, and I knew you as though you were my own child."

Aiden didn’t like that statement. He didn’t like it at all.

"So you see, I know that this is the only way it could happen. And the only way you would agree to what I'm about to tell you is if you and everyone you love is in abject danger. I didn't know exactly how it would happen, so I tried this a few different ways. The Alchemist was one. Irina another. Even Neqal."

"What?"

"I thought it would be the hope of seeing your mother again that would bring you here, knowing that she was alive and that I could bring her back."

"You can?"

"Possibly. But you refused to hear him out. Then Duru...I thought that was a particularly clever one. But you defied that, too. But alas, after much trial and error, here you are."

Lexie wanted to leave. She wanted to leave badly.

Something was wrong.

"So what do you need me to do for you to call off your Saints?"

"The Fae will be arriving soon," he said. "Things have changed. You don't have time anymore. We must break this prison that we're in."

"I agree," she said. "So again, what do we do?"

He gave her a smile that was almost sad, almost regretful. "I'm so sorry about this, Lexie."
