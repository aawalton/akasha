Lee Jia and Eui sat meditating together within the pavilion, surrounded by the verdant beauty of the park surrounding the river. Lee Jia was unable to enjoy the scenery at the moment, however, as her concentration was focused solely on pushing her qi against the corruption clogging the meridian. Slowly, bit by bit, the corruption began to—

“Stop!”

Lee Jia was jolted out of her meditation by Elder Qin’s voice. He had a way of infusing his voice with qi that smashed through her meditative state like a hammer through glass.

“As I have already told you, this requires precision. Your own qi cannot usually harm you, but that protection does not extend to others. You are a rusty nail where you must be an acupuncturist's finest needle.”

Lee Jia growled in frustration. Elder Qin was very quick to interrupt her when she did something wrong, but his explanations of what she was doing wrong were vague and metaphorical, and his explanations of how to correct her mistakes were non-existent.

“I don’t understand. I just need to clear the corruption from Eui’s meridian the same way the awakening stone did for me, right? It didn’t seem all that precise to me.”

Qin scoffed and stroked his beard, sneering down his nose at Lee Jia.

“The sliver of my illustrious ancestor’s intent that dwells in an awakening stone is infinitely more precise than you could hope to comprehend. If that tiny fraction of the God-Emperor’s power was applied to a mortal with your clumsiness it would annihilate them, body and soul.”

Lee Jia blanched at Elder Qin’s words. Just how powerful was that innocuous little jade slip?

“The greater one’s power, the greater the precision required to use it delicately. To forcefully awaken a mortal, one requires the utmost precision. If your power is a nail, it requires relatively little refinement to produce from it a needle. Mine is a mighty tree towering towards the heavens. The God-Emperor’s? A mountain.”

Despite Qin’s flowery metaphorical way of describing things, she thought she understood what he was trying to get across. The only reason she stood a chance at doing this without hurting Eui was because she was so weak, and the only reason the awakening stone could do it was because of the God-Emperor’s superlative ability.

“Does it really have to be me, though? Couldn’t you do it? And is this really the way the great sects awaken their disciples?”

“The great sects do not accept unawakened disciples. They must be proven, either by awakening stone or peerage. The most common method employed by the noble clans is not an option, unless you plan on travelling back in time to give birth to Miss An yourself.”

Lee Jia gave Elder Qin a confused look. He scoffed and shook his head.

“Indeed. As for why I couldn’t do it myself, my reasons are three—First, at my power, that level of precision would strain even my ability. Second, it requires a level of trust that I doubt Miss An even has for you, much less myself. Third, I do not want to. I know what the brand on her soul means, I know that her body carries the power of death dormant within it, and I am fairly certain that the awakening stone would have burned away her meridians.”

“Wait, you knew that and you still tried to get her to use it!?”

Qin ignored Lee Jia’s interjection as he continued.

“An Eui’s fate is as inauspicious as yours is auspicious, Miss Lee. Your fortune is impressive, and with your blessings I have no doubt you have a grand destiny before you. Miss An, however, will only hold you back from that greatness. She is destined for nothing but death, destruction, and misfortune.”

Lee Jia glanced nervously at Eui, who was still calmly meditating, practicing the qi breathing methods. Frustratingly, the little fragment of Eui’s mind in her head was agreeing with everything Qin said. She glared defiantly at Elder Qin.

“I’ve never felt lucky in my entire life. Everything that I have, everything that I am, I fought tooth and claw for. I don’t believe in fate or destiny—Eui and I will make our destinies, and we will do it together no matter what your stupid fate thinks.”

Qin gazed impassively at Lee Jia as he stroked his beard.

“Do it, then. Turn your words into action. Show me the strength of your conviction to defy fate—I certainly won’t do it for you.”

Lee Jia clenched her teeth, her tail was lashing back and forth furiously. Elder Qin was right and she hated it. The smug superiority, the absolute confidence behind every word he said, it made her feel like she was losing an argument to Yan Zhihao, and she wanted to scream. Instead she sat down, took a calming breath, and got back to meditating.

As Elder Qin Zhao watched Lee Jia angrily return to her practice, he allowed himself a tiny smirk. Seldom did he ever have the opportunity to work with such a raw bundle of potential as this. All she needed was a little bit of refinement.

“Stop!”

Qin Zhao watched with a frown as his new pupil screeched angrily, pulling at her ears and whipping her tail back and forth.

…

Perhaps more than a little refinement.

Jia was fuming as she returned home with Eui that night. They had trained until dark, and it was the most frustrating experience of her entire life. Nothing she did was ever good enough, every attempt had another mistake to point out, every correction was just another mistake, and never, ever did Elder Qin actually offer any sort of positive advice. He was determined to make her figure things out on her own, only ever interrupting her to tell her what she was doing wrong.

“Was it really that bad? I just spent the whole time meditating. I think I cleared a bit of the meridian myself, but honestly I don’t know if I’m even exaggerating when I say it’s gonna take ten years to get there on my own.”

Jia groaned with frustration, rubbing agitatedly at her face.

“Yes! I think he just left you alone because he doesn’t consider you a student. I got the feeling he was just using you as a tool to teach me a lesson—he’s kind of a jerk like that. He gives me the impression that everything he says has three different meanings.”

Eui nodded, staring up at the sky contemplatively as she rested her arms behind her head.

“Makes sense. You’re the one who made a big fuss beating Yan Zhihao in a duel, and you’re probably now the only person in the world who practices all three core disciplines. Compared to that, I’m just some tag-along profiting off of your fortune.”

Jia froze—she hadn’t even considered the implications of being the first omnidisciplinary practitioner. Unfortunately she had something more important that needed addressing right now.

“Eui, that’s not true! I couldn’t have done any of that without you supporting me the entire time.”

Eui raised a skeptical brow at Jia and shook her head.

“What have I done for you? Told you to give up when we first met, read you some letters, distracted you with my bullshit mental problems, stabbed your combat tutor? Hey, remember when I tried to kill you when we first met and then didn’t let you sleep in the bedroom for half the week? No, Jia. Elder Qin is right, I’m nothing but a chain around your neck.”

Jia winced.

“You were listening to that?”

“Yeah, I heard it. I appreciate your confidence, Jia, I really do, but I have to be honest with myself. Everything you have, you fought for. Everything I have, you fought for.”

Jia shook her head in protest.

“That’s not true! We both worked together! I wouldn’t have learned writing so quickly without you sharing that knowledge—I know you spent extra time practicing your calligraphy just to share that experience when we meditated. You were always there to help me practice my sparring, too, and I know I wouldn’t have been able to develop my mana so much without your help.”

Eui sighed and shrugged.

“Maybe I helped a little, but I think you’re overestimating my value. Takeda, Hyeong—hell even that Stoneface guy and the princess bitch helped you more than I did.”

Jia frowned, but she knew that getting Eui to admit that she had helped at all was already enough of a win.

Once the pair arrived home, they shared a quick meal before preparing for Eui’s awakening. Elder Qin hadn’t sent them home because it was dark, after all. Jia was worried, though—Elder Qin had warned them that this experimental method was essentially a crude replication of the awakening stone, and not a tried and true technique.

“You’re sure you’re ready?”

Eui shrugged off Jia’s concerns.

“I just spent all day meditating, Jia. If I’m not ready now, I’m never going to be. Let’s just do this.”

Jia nodded and they took up position facing each other in much the same way they did during their joint mana meditation sessions. With practiced ease, they settled into the spiritual breathing patterns and focused on their souls.

The first hurdle was one of the most difficult—Jia had no way of sensing Eui’s soul, and thus had nowhere to send her qi. According to Qin, they would have to achieve a state of perfect synchronization and mutual trust. That, or perform some kind of degenerate ritual that he refused to elaborate on.

With each breath, Jia released a small portion of her qi and tried to focus on maintaining her control over it. It was difficult to maintain her connection to the qi once it was outside of her body, but she had been practicing fine control of her qi all day and as frustrating as Elder Qin’s methods were, his instruction had been invaluable.

Soon, Eui had drawn the portion of qi that Jia held control over into her own soul, creating a link between them. Jia became aware of the labyrinth of corrupted meridians within Eui’s soul. It was a much more vague sensation than her own, like the difference between seeing a labyrinth from above and being stuck within it.

Carefully, very carefully, Jia began to push against Eui’s corrupt qi with her own, slowly increasing the pressure while being extremely mindful not to spill over the boundaries of the meridian. While Jia’s power was too weak to burn out Eui’s meridians like the awakening stone threatened to, one slip could still cause significant damage.

Unlike the awakening stone, Jia was only able to move the corruption, rather than annihilate it entirely. Elder Qin had explained that this shouldn’t be a problem, as once a route to the dantian was opened, Eui’s own qi supply would be able to break apart and flush out the loose corruption.

This was in fact one of the very basics of spiritual arts. All spiritual techniques began with opening meridians in a specific pattern that would align the qi with a certain element and allow for the expression of desired effects. As Magus Hwang had explained, it was essentially a method of carving formations into one’s soul.

Normally, this would be done by the individual, pushing the corruption outward from the dantian. In this case, Jia and Eui worked together, with Jia providing the qi and the control, while Eui guided the energy through her meridians to push the corruption inward towards her dantian.

Eui’s role was essentially the same as Jia’s had been when she used the awakening stone, but there were some key differences—First, unlike the awakening stone, there was no life and death panic or intense pain, which made it much easier to maintain a relaxed emotional state. Second, if Eui’s emotional state fluctuated, Jia could simply stop applying pressure and wait for her to relax again.

The process was slow—much slower than Jia’s awakening had been. The corrupt meridian was cleansed bit by bit, with a lot of stopping and starting. Sometimes, Eui’s focus would slip and Jia would need to wait. Sometimes it was Jia’s fault—her control was good, but not perfect, and every small slip caused Eui intense pain that would force them to stop for a few moments.

As always, it was nearly impossible to tell how much time was passing while they meditated. Because of that, Jia had no idea how long it took to finally open the path to Eui’s dantian. It was completely impossible to miss the moment that it happened, however.

Once Jia’s qi entered Eui’s dantian she immediately lost her control over the qi within Eui’s soul. Her control, but not the connection. Before Jia could react, the qi circulated within Eui’s dantian and surged back through the now-open meridian, crossed the link between them, and returned to Jia’s soul before repeating the process and completing the circuit.

As with the mana in their auras before, it suddenly became impossible to distinguish where Jia’s qi ended and Eui’s qi began. As the energy in their dantians balanced out, Jia was struck with an overwhelming flood of emotion.

It was almost too much to bear as every single feeling crashed into her at once. Anxiety, relief, elation, anger, jealousy, rage, love, all without any thoughts to guide or contextualize the feelings. Worse, as both Jia and Eui felt overwhelmed by the sudden surge of emotion, that emotion fed straight back into them in a positive feedback loop.

They were almost immediately broken from their meditative states, hyperventilating, sobbing, and clutching each other’s hands so tightly that Jia’s claws dug into Eui’s skin and drew blood. Neither of them noticed as they both wept uncontrollably, still overwhelmed by the remnants of the unexpected flood of emotion.

Without hesitation, Jia drew her friend into a tight embrace and Eui returned it. They stayed that way for a while, sobbing into each others’ shoulders and slowly coming down off of the emotional high that they had just shared.

Eventually, they got their breathing under control and separated, though Eui refused to let go of one of Jia’s hands. She held up her other trembling hand and regarded the bloody claw marks.

“Ancestors, Jia! Those things are sharp.”

Jia didn’t know why—perhaps it was a left-over part of the heightened emotions, or perhaps it was the incongruous nature of the comment—but she laughed. That set off Eui and soon they were both giggling uncontrollably as they sat hand-in-hand and watched the sun rise outside of their window.
