It took the pair about fifteen minutes to get to the spot that the mage had had in mind. They hadn’t talked for the most part, neither trusting each other enough to have a casual conversation. Both synced had their guards up Levii looked around the place, staring at the early risers who walked the streets. He could pick out a few homeless people here and there, but he had to admit that they looked a lot better than those back in Gantus. It seemed that the quality of life, even for the poor, was a lot better in the big city.

“We’re here,” Xara said. Gesturing to Levi as she stepped into the unassuming front. Levi looked at the front of the store. On the outside, it looked nothing more than a rundown shop; there were a few cracks in the wall, fading paint, and a sign that marked this place as a tea shop. Levi wasn’t much of a tea drinker, but even if he were, he highly doubted that this was the kind of place he’d come for tea, which was the entire point, he guessed. It was designed to deter people while providing a reasonable enough cover for arms dealing. At least that was what he thought about the whole thing; then again, the mage really could be interested in having some tea since it was early in the morning. If that were the case, then the mage either had terrible taste or was a penny-pincher.

“You coming?” Xara asked from inside the store.

Levi sighed as he walked in, coming to a stop just beside the mage. It was a bit smaller on the inside than he’d anticipated; the dimensions just didn’t fit. His amateur architectural senses cried foul, and so he got to scanning. His eyes scanned the room, taking in everything and everyone in it. Apart from him and Xara, there was a girl behind the counter who was serving drinks. Two burly men sat on two different tables, sipping tea quietly. All in all, the place was scanty, which was to be expected considering the time of the day and the outward appearance of the Shop.

“Lady Xara, what would you have today?” The barita asked.

“The usual… and so will he.” She said, gesturing to Levi.

The usual? I didn’t come here for tea. Levi thought, but kept his mouth shut.

“The usual, coming right up,” The barista said. Not a moment too soon, a panel wall, between the two men drinking tea, slid to the side, revealing a hallway. White glowstones on the walls of the hall provide light in the absence of windows. Levi's musings were broken as Xara grabbed him by the arm after nodding to the barista. The mage led him into the hall, and the wall panel clicked in place once they’d walked through the space that had been there. Levi followed Xara’s lead, curious to what exactly the hidden entrance led to. The pair turned a corner at the end of the hall and were greeted by a sight that made Leiv’s eyes widen.

Grakkking Hell

The missing space of the tea shop he’d been searching was right in front of him, and it was glorious. The entire place was covered in gear. Cloaks, Robes, daggers, Swords, Bows, arrows, quivers, and even weapons he didn't know about. Everything he needed was right here. His mind raced at how much gold had been used to stock this place with supplies. This place could pay a king's ransom. Xara’s grip on his arm tightened as she led him through the store, heading straight for the counter, which was at the back of the store. A bit counterintuitive if he was asked. It would be easier for a thief to escape with goods if the counter were at the opposite end of the exit. At the same time, he didn’t know what safety measures the store owner had in place.

“Quit ogling,” Xara whispered, as they got closer to the store. “You’ll stand out as a fresher, and believe me when I say that this is not the kind of place that you want to stand out. Word of advice: Do not steal anything from Sersil's shop. If you value your life, that is.”

Levi nodded at the mage's words. This place was definitely a black market, and it would do him no good to stand out amongst everyone else who was shopping. It turned out that they weren’t the only people shopping today. The Hidden store had a lot more customers milling about than the tea shop did. To be frank, there was an argument to be made that the tea shop didn't have any customers, seeing as the two burly men beside the hidden wall panel hadn’t even flinched when it had moved.

He had to admit that he was impressed by how clean and organized the business was from the inside. The entire setting had him feeling… excited. Hanging from the walls and racks were equipment that he couldn’t imagine purchasing. Everything about this black market blew his mind, but he centered himself; it'd do him no good to spend this much time analyzing their business. He wasn’t here for that; no, he was here to purchase new gear.

“Xara,” a grandmotherly voice said. The voice had come from beyond the counter. Levi thanked Cerpital that his hood covered the top half of his face because of the way his eyes widened once he’d glanced at the woman behind the counter. Old was an understatement; the woman was definitely on her last legs, and he didn't miss the way the woman had addressed Xara. She’d addressed her by her first name only, and to Levi, it made a bit of sense, considering the kind of place they were in and the age of the woman.

“Sersil,” The mage greeted.”I’m here to get some equipment for me and my friend.”

“Hmmm, friend. I’m glad that you finally have one of those.” Sersil hummed, looking in Levi's direction, totally missing the look of embarrassment on the mage's face. “Perna, come over here and get our customers what they want.”

A girl came out of nowhere, no doubt the Perna that Sersil had been talking about. She nodded at Sersil, then at them, before Xara started rattling off a list of things that they both needed. The number of things the mage listed made Levi's head spin; there was no way that the mage should be able to purchase all those things. It was more likely that she wanted to see all the items and their costs before picking the ones she could afford. “...Oh, add an uncommon hooded cloak and a tier five storage ring.”

Storage ring?

Levi had heard about that back in Gantus, but he hadn’t believed it because it sounded absurd; there was no way that anyone could have a ring that could store things in Cerpital knows where, and bring those things out of thin air when needed. It made absolutely no sense to him, especially since he hadn’t seen one person in Gantus in possession of such an artifact. If there were even a possibility of such an artifact being present in Gantus, then he would have caught wind of it, but he hadn't. Besides, even Killian, the wealthiest person he'd known in Gantus, hadn't possessed such a ring, and if the taskmaster had, he would have been very careful with it.

Perna left them as soon as Xara finished rattling off the list of things they needed. The attendant was undoubtedly better than they at finding the things they needed in a shorter amount of time. That left Sersil, Xara, and Levi at the counter; the ladies broke into light chatter, but Levi remained quiet. He had nothing to say in their conversations. Sersil must've known that he had no interest in speaking, judging by the way she refrained from involving him in the conversation.

Fantastic customer service.

The hidden store was well-run for a black market; it was so organized that Levi had to assume it was just a black market for the rich. There was no rowdiness, no loud noises. No arguments. It seemed like a respectable establishment, which unnerved him a little because this wasn't what he’d expected when he walked through the panel. To be fair, his only other experience with black markets was the one back in Gantus. A rundown shop that blended well with the environment, it was low-key, just like Sersils' store. Unfortunately, that was where the similarities ended. The inside of the black market actually reeked just the same as the outside. The place was crawling with shady figures who haggled at the top of their voices. Customers and sellers often suffered severe injuries while conducting business. Compared to that, Sersil's store looked like heaven.

“Here are the items you asked for,” Perna said as she dropped their order on the counter. “Will that be all?”

“Yes,” Xara replied. Her eyes went over the items on the table, confirming that it was all there. “How much does all this cost?” She asked Sersil.

“Three thousand five hundred gold. But since you’re such a good client of mine. I’ll let you take it for three thousand four hundred gold.”

“That's nice of you, but I'll only pay three thousand gold for them.”

“Add three hundred gold and you have a deal,”

“That's still too much. I’ll add a hundred gold,”

“Make it two hundred and fifty.“

“I can do two hundred more. That's my final offer.”

“Deal”

“It’s a deal, your total is three thousand two hundred gold only.”

Only? Three thousand and two hundred gold coins was an unbelievable amount of money, and Sersil had said only. What was wrong with these people? Was this how rich people behaved?

“Okay, I’ve sent it to your account,” Xara said, after a moment had passed. No doubt the mage had used the system bank to transfer the money.

“Seen,” Sersil replied. “Oh, and dearie, you might want to haggle a bit better next time. I would've sold the loot to you for three thousand gold coins. Although I have to admit that you’ve gotten better than the first time you came here.”

“Ohh, I’ll do better next time, Sersil. Thank you,” Xara said sheepishly.

What is going on here? Did Sersil just admit to swindling Xara of two hundred gold? And did the mage just say thank you?

“I would hope so,” she said before abruptly turning to face Levi. “And you. You'd better keep her safe. She doesn’t have that many friends. So I’m sure that you’ll keep her safe.”

As confused as he was about what was going on, he didn't need the conversation to last longer than necessary, so he simply told the woman what she wanted to hear. “I will.”

“Alright, see yourselves out." She said. And before his very eyes, she waved a hand over the items they'd purchased, and it was all bagged. Sersil and the mage nodded at each other to signal the end of their conversation.

“Let's go,” Xara said, as the bagged items disappeared into thin air.
