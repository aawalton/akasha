I spent the next week alternating between training, exploring, and spending time with both Daryn and Maya.

Daryn wasn’t around all of the time, as he apparently had other obligations in the Drassian Defense Service and it’d been determined that I could at least take care of myself enough to walk around on my own. If I ever needed him, I could call, and he or another nearby solider would come to assist me.

As for Maya, she alternated between quiet days and loud ones. She wasn’t always super talkative, but when she was, she had a million questions for me. She always wanted to hear more about Tellos and the magic I used. When I showed her that I could heat the air near her, doing it gently enough that I wouldn’t hurt her by proximity, she’d been completely astounded.

The last couple of days, she’d been complaining about belly aches. Apparently, the operation she had to undergo upon returning was to do with a sharp pain in her abdomen around the area that the Drassians believed their dormant core organ resided in.

The leading theory right now was that contact with the mist-like substance within the rift activated dormant Drassian cores, but that waking them up too quickly caused them to mutate and corrupted the subject’s mind and body.

Apparently, Maya’s core had been flooded with some kind of substance that was supposed to slow her core’s expansion, and while her core hadn’t receded to its previous size, she was showing no worrying symptoms.

That being said, I’d noticed that her mana signature was somewhat more dense than other Drassians I’d encountered, which excited her immensely.

The whole thing had led Maya to wonder if she might be able to use magic herself one day. Hence her never shutting up about it.

Not that I minded. It was fun to answer her questions, and occasionally, her asking me one to do with the mechanics or the ‘how’ or magic made me scratch my head and wonder.

I realised that despite mana being a constant and a given in my own world, I’d never really put a lot of thought into the ‘how’ of everything. I’d always just accepted mana to be a thing that simply existed, like air, and the process of casting to be something akin to breathing for mages.

But that wasn’t quite the case. Everyone knew how to breathe. Magic had to be learned, and it was far more intrinsic for some than others.

That being said, I’d learned to use magic without any real training. Maybe I wasn’t adept at any of it yet, but I’d still made a start. I wonder how much further I’d be able to go with a stronger knowledge base?

While I knew that there weren’t likely to be any books explaining the mysteries of magic on Drassia, something I did know was that systems seemed to be governed by rules. Many things inside of a system were determined using numbers, and some of the skills I possessed gave explanations regarding their use that left little room for interpretation, such as [Hoard]’s range, as well as hard limits, such as [Hoard]’s current inability to store living creatures.

Some skills, like [Kinetic Shell], were more open with their wording, which was likely why I’d already found there were a variety of ways I could place and use the barriers. Did the lack of written restrictions mean there were none? I doubted it, but I also thought I shouldn’t discount a skill’s ability to let me do a thing unless I’d already tried it. I didn’t know if I could ever make a weildable mana shield out of [Kinetic Shell]’s barriers, but that didn’t mean it was impossible.

Maya had spoken to me about the ‘scientific process’ during our discussion around magic, and from the simplified explanation she’d given me, I got the impression I’d been applying something similar for a while now. I wanted to keep doing that, but learning more about science and mathematics would likely bode well for me in better understanding the rules of the system I used, as well as how to test things.

And, possibly, it might tell me a little about the workings of magic itself.

So it was that I’d ended up in a library. I didn’t receive my 96% discount here, sadly, and the books were anywhere between 300-800 sarcs each,  but I ended up picking up two basic books regarding physics, one history book about Drassia, a couple of books about finance, one book regarding biology, and a statistics schoolbook which turned out to be far more complicated than I’d originally realised skimming a couple of pages.

Altogether, the total for the books I bought was around 4000 sarcs. When I placed the books into my [Hoard], I checked to see how much each book was valued at, curious about the currency conversion between sarcs and gold.

One book was considered to be worth 3 gold, one was four silvers, another was ten gold, and one was only worth coppers. It seemed completely random. The value here couldn’t have been determined based on the size of the books or the material used, as most of them were pretty uniform. I wondered if it had something to do with the information inside?

Maybe some of this knowledge was more valuable on average to someone on my planet than it was on Drassia. That made sense. The book that had been valued lowest had been the Drassian history book.

That said, I found it the easiest to read. Learning about how they’d first built lightning rods to harness the power of electricity due to the world’s frequent storms, and then built large buildings around those and established cities in frequent storm sites was really interesting.

The physics books were both pretty dense, but Maya had started coaching me a little on the easier one. I’d been learning a little about kinetic energy, which had perked my ears simply because of the skill I had. Maya had posited that the use of mana itself was a form of kinetics, which I was beginning to wrap my head around, at least in a basic sense.

The statistics book was most useful to me as a weight right now. I was being literal. I lifted it when I got bored in my hotel room. The thing was inordinately big.

Speaking of weights, I’d raised, or rather, lifted [Weightlifting] to level two over the past week.

[Weightlifting: 1 >> 2.]

Lats had been my favourite workout, but I was also beginning to experiment with pull ups. I could do a couple of sets of fifteen before my arms felt tired, but I was sure I could manage a few more if I pushed myself hard.

I wasn’t becoming a powerhouse any time soon, at least in the physical sense, but I wanted to keep working at it. Consistent practice was what it took. Still, I had more at my disposal than just brawn.

The gym had something called a ‘boxing ring’ that I’d seen some Drassians practicing in. It took me a couple of days to convince Daryn to spar with me. He thought I was being overzealous—his words—but when he finally got in a ring with me, he saw that I, despite our immense size difference, could fight.

Well, he was clearly pulling his punches, and even then I was struggling to confront him head-on, but I was fast, and I only got faster and more agile retreating.

The [Deer in Headlights] buff was no joke. I felt about twice as confident in my footwork and bodily motions while on the defensive, and while that didn’t result in a literal doubling of my speed, it was still a noticeable boost in my ability to duck, weave, and sidestep.

It was going on the offensive that was difficult. I’d yet to reveal any of my magic to Daryn, partially out of my desire to hone my physical skills alone before attempting to incorporate it, and otherwise because as much as I liked Daryn, I wasn’t sure how comfortable I was sharing my abilities with Drassians other than Maya.

Even Maya, who I fully trusted at this point, I’d yet to reveal [Hoard] to. It was natural for me to try and hide it at this point; I’d already been doing so for months. I was almost sure nothing bad would come of me telling her, and she’d just add it to the list of ‘amazing’ things I could do, but I still had yet to explain to her the means by which I’d done my first magic trick.

That same reluctance to use my abilities, at least the visible ones, was exactly why I could never really strike Daryn back. I could still benefit from [Deer in Headlights]’ speed boost while going for a counterattack, but with a lack of coordination, it was difficult to get past his guard, and with a lack of power, it was impossible to break it.

Still we trained daily. It took days of him gently smacking me around, increasing his speed rather than his power to push me without hurting me, until I finally got a notification to tell me that I was on the right path.

[Deer in Headlights: 1 (7) >> 2 (8).]

The effect of the increase felt immediate. It was slight, but my defence was faster now. Better put together. Even when he rushed me with jabs, his gloves flying at me, I was able to fend off more of the blows and even sneak a couple of return strikes into his hard stomach.

Still not enough to even wind him, but I earned an uppercut that knocked me to the floor.

It hadn’t been hard, and my helmet and jawguard prevented it doing more than ringing my head a little, but I still felt dizzy after. Once I fell, I watched as Daryn rushed over me.

“Shit! I’m sorry, buddy. I think I went a little far there.”

I laughed as I pulled myself back to my feet, spitting the guard from my mouth. “I dunno what you’re talking about. I’m fine.”

He blinked. “Seriously?”

“I barely felt that,” I said, pushing my gloves up. “Let’s keep going.”

It was slow, but over the course of the day, Daryn began to realise that he didn’t need to hold back with me so much. That I wasn’t quite as fragile as he might have expected me to be.

He still definitely wasn’t even breaking a sweat fighting me, but I started to receive punches that might leave bruises, smacks that knocked me to the floor, and gut checks that knocked the air from my lungs. Daryn had repeatedly asked me if it was too much, but after me repeatedly assuring him, he’d stopped asking.

After three days of ‘real’ sparring with the man, I’d not only progressed my [Deer in Headlights] skill another time, but I’d learned a new skill.

[Recovery: level 1 acquired!]

The skill was common and didn’t carry a description, but it was pretty clear what it was noting. I was getting better at getting back up, my dizziness abating faster, my balance correcting itself when I received a strike that would usually send me tumbling.

By the time we’d been at it a week, I was able to fight Daryn on this level abouit half as well as I had been when he’d been completely going easy, which was saying a lot. I couldn’t easily scale Daryn to someone from Tellos as he didn’t have an active core, but in terms of physical strength I imagined he might be about Mansol’s level.

Honestly, a couple of times when we’d been sparring I’d dissociated and imagined I was being attacked by the tiger again. At one point, Daryn had noticed me flinching or not properly putting my guard up, and he’d asked if I needed to stop.

Later on, I explained what had happened to him. Or at least a heavily simplified version missing a lot of details.

“That’s why you wanted to spar with me so bad?”  Daryn had eventually asked. “Because you think you might have to fight a dude like me once you leave here?”

It wasn’t exactly the reason—I’d mainly wanted to get stronger. But I couldn’t pretend that it wasn’t a part of it.

“Well, I can always escort you back,” Daryn said. “Then, if you point that guy out to me, I can knock him on his ass.”

I snickered at the idea. Wouldn’t that be something?

“No,” I said, shaking my head. “I’d get in trouble for attacking staff, even if it wasn’t me directly that did it.”

“I don’t understand why you’d wanna go back there.”

We were sitting in a diner. I was eating blueberry ice cream, Daryn’s was chocolate and fudge. It was a substance I remembered from Maya’s dream, and it tasted every bit as delicious in real life.

That said, at his question, the spoonful in my hand was melting and dripping, and I’d scarcely noticed.

“I think I have to go back,” I finally ended up saying.

“Yeah? Why’s that?” Daryn propped his head up with an elbow. “From everything you’ve told me, the place you came from sounds pretty tragic.”

“I’ve barely told you anything.”

“That’s the point!” Daryn took another spoonful of ice cream. “Surely, if you came from some place super amazing, you’d be really eager to go back. But you never say you miss anything. So what’s waiting for you back home?”

I put my spoon down. I didn’t answer.

“Family? Friends?”

I kept silent.

“Then what?”

“Do you know much about medicine?” I asked Daryn.

“I mean, as much as any soldier. So not a lot. Why?”

“I’ve got a friend who’s sick. She got poisoned, and it damaged her immune system. I don’t know all the details.”

It wasn’t technically my friend, but Ceri’s. I didn’t bother explaining that.

“I mean, Drassia’s wiped out basically all major diseases at this point. It’s our leading field. If you have something wrong with you, there’s probably a drug or a therapy for it. About the only thing we can’t do is revive the dead.”

Huh. To think Tellos had something over this planet.

He bit his lip. “I dunno what your friend has, but I know a few pills exist that are specifically designed with repairing poison damage in mind. Pills for the brain, pills for organ damage, for the nervous system… And of course, extremely versatile antivenoms.”

“Have they been tested on anything other than Drassians?”

“I’m probably not supposed to tell you, but we’ve actually sold shipments to a human colony before,” Daryn said. “Honestly, I’m not even sure I’m supposed to know that. I heard a CO talking about it once while I was eating lunch.”

I blinked. “Can you… get me pills like that?”

“For your friend?”

Daryn hummed for a moment. His spoon trailed the rim of his cup.

“Yeah. I think I can get it cleared if I explain the situation.”

“That’s incredible.”

It gave me even more of a reason to go back. Honestly, I’d been ready to hear that there was nothing that could be done to help Ceri’s plight, but would that have made any difference?

“That’s why you’ve gotta go back?” Daryn asked. “For your friend?”

“It’s not just one friend.”

It wasn’t. I’d been resisting thinking about it at first, but the longer I’d been left waiting while the way home was prepared, the more realisation I’d had that I’d left the rest of my group in a shitty position.

I was meant to be their leader, after all. I’d planned to take over, and I’d taken steps to get that group productive and on-track. Everyone there had taken risks for both me and each other. We’d fought and worked together, and without me, without Toar, even if Toar returned…

Was I supposed to let that asshole take over again? Reap the benefits of my hard work? Leave them alone to deal with him and his bullshit?

Or just as bad, leave them leaderless. Let some other guy muscle in and take over, or watch the group fall apart.

I didn’t know every one of their circumstances for getting here, but I knew at least Jackal, Maisie, and Finn didn’t come from money, and I assumed their circumstances weren’t much better than mine. It wasn’t fair to leave people who’d helped me to fend for themselves while I lived in this happy place, eating ice cream and riding rollercoasters.

I’d worked in the mines for months. Most of them had been at it for years, and they had nothing to show for it. I couldn’t live with myself reaping all of these rewards while my companions rotted away.

“If you did decide to leave, you know they’d probably let you come back, right?”

I honestly hadn’t considered the possibility.

“You don’t think they’ll close the portal?”

“Negative. There’s way too much vested interest in the contents of that rift, especially now that we know how to make it safe to work in, partially thanks to you. I imagine that portal will be open for a long time.”

That was a big deal. That might even make a difference to my plans.

What if, once I’d served out the remainder of my contract and helped my friends become stable, I decided to return to Drassia?

Hell, what if I took them here? Would that be allowed?

I floated the idea past Daryn. He shrugged his shoulders.

“That’s hard for me to say. You’d probably have to ask Alai, and then she’d have to ask someone else. I don’t wanna get your hopes up.”

It was a non-answer, but I didn’t exactly mind that. I imagined even if Daryn thought a ‘yes’ was likely, there was no way he was going to risk telling me, just on the off-chance.

My mind flashed back to pills and medicines. I wondered what else the Drassians had made, and how it compared to the potions and salves on my planet. Did they have elixirs that could make me stronger? Maybe smarter? Stuff that could make me not need to sleep, or fight at full potential for hours?

“Nothing I could get you,” Daryn had said with a laugh. “Half that stuff’s illegal. Steroids, stimulants… if anyone found out I’d grabbed that stuff for you, losing my job would be the least of it.”

I thanked him for explaining and picked at my ice cream.

Hearing that was a touch disappointing, but the conversation had opened my eyes to new possibilities.

That [Lead Belly] perk was sounding increasingly enticing by the day. It wasn’t just the ability to consume things like Earth Stones or the Electrostatic Matrix I’d found that made me want the ability, it was the idea of being able to chug any potion or pill I picked up with less worries as to the side effects.

For someone like me, who could read exactly what something did and get a real-time assessment on the risks, [Lead Belly] seemed almost good enough to take already, and I was almost ready to commit to it now, if only for the fact that I wanted to decide which other perk I was taking.

[Heightened Growth] offered me a 20 percent growth boost in a single subset of skills. I wasn’t interested, mainly because I already developed skills quickly. Anything a boost like that would provide could already be attained just by me working harder. There were better options.

[Reduced Restrictions] wasn’t one of them. As it was, I was already finding means to break my level ten restriction. I didn’t need this ability to help me do it further. There would likely only be a small portion of abilities I wanted to hang onto until higher levels before refining or combining, possibly either while waiting to get other skills high enough or while waiting for suitable sacrifice components. I imagined such skills would be rare enough that I didn’t need to worry about this.

[Mana Matrix] was interesting. It was something I’d considered picking already, as ‘extreme’ resistance to a mana type sounded spectacular, and I imagined that opening myself up to a new element could give me a bunch of new potential in spellcraft.

That being said, the main thing staying my hand was indecision on what element to commit to. If I did take it, which mana type did I want to be able to generate?

[Diamond Hands] offered me a Legendary skin-hardening skill upon my ascension to Tier 3.

I imagined Tier 3 was way off. Vulrak, the massive lion I’d met in the mines, had been Tier 3 at eighteen, and that was considered prodigal. Even if I managed to reach it at Vulrak’s age, that was over four years from now. If I managed to stay alive with no benefit until then, I got a great skill. Not so great if I died in between.

I’m sure someone would’ve told me I was a fool not to take a path toward a Legendary skill. I disagreed. I was already travelling that path, whether or not I chose that perk. I’d get one sooner or later.

[Living Battery] increased my mana generation in line with my Tier, as well as the ‘potency’, but it also increased the cost of using mana. I wasn’t sure about it. It was a strong consideration, but the idea of exponentially stacking costs with each tier increase worried me somewhat.

[Channel Cleansing] sounded useless. I was already doing that with Spirit Stones. Speaking of which, I’d managed to bump my mana control from 37 percent of the mana in my body to thirty-eight since this time last week.

Finally, there was [Intuition], which also interested me. The idea of learning things faster through passive observation sounded powerful, especially considering I could apply the boost to multiple subsets of skills. That being said, I had no idea if the actual boost was substantial.

The remainder of my ice cream had fully melted by the time Daryn waved me back into reality. I’d been pretty lost in staring at my system screen.

[Small Gun Handling: Level 1 Acquired!]

Me and Daryn didn’t see much of each other during my last evening, which I spent buying and storing food—something I’d been doing with my spare cash for a week now. He’d said something about coming back to see me off in the morning and yet it’d been Alai who’d picked me up. Honestly, I felt a little sad.

“Daryn’s inside the facility,” Alai explained when I couldn’t bite my tongue. “He’s going to be receiving you when you arrive.”

“Oh!” I realised how relieved I sounded. I coughed. “I see.”

“Did you decide upon what kind of reward you wanted?” Alai asked.

“Well, I already asked for medicine to help my friend.”

“You can have something else. I spoke with Daryn a little about your situation, and I think it’s appropriate to send you home with this.”

She reached ahead in her seat and handed me a box. Inside, there was a blue set of body armour, lightweight and comfortable, the suit itself covering my upper legs, chest, and upper arms. Like a cuirass.

“Go ahead and put it on over your regular clothes.”

I did just that. It slid on and off easily, still feeling too big for me. Before I could complain, I noticed the adjustable straps.

I pulled on a couple, and within ten seconds, it was well-fitted and snug. It felt warm, but not suffocating, and the material itself was as soft as my Drassian day clothes.

“That’s gel armor, made up of ceramic particles that thicken upon impact. It’s great at blocking knives and bullets, but you’ll still feel it if you get hit. It’ll hurt.”

I stared at the armor. The padding on it was extremely thin. Honestly, I imagined I could put this on under long clothes and no one would be any the wiser that I was wearing it, unless they were extremely perceptive.

“We want you to be safe,” Alai said. “If this helps at all, then we’re happy. We know we cannot keep you here, but…”

“Thank you,” I said. “For everything.”

Alai nodded. We hadn’t spoken many times, but I could tell this was a big deal to her. It was a big deal to me too, what I was doing. I might have felt more prepared than I ever had been to go back to the mines, but after weeks of safety and comfort…

I took a deep breath. I knew what I was doing.

We arrived at Drassia’s end of the facility. I was waved in and taken to the portal. It looked as loud and crazy as it had when I’d first stepped through it.

I was louder and crazier.

I stepped through. The world spun.

I didn’t throw up this time; I’d gotten used to this.

I was back in the rift.
