Dormitory Assignments at Awakened Academies are seen as a rite of passage.

You arrive, you are judged on your merits, you are assigned to a dorm. No student knows the criteria, no teacher has ever shared the truth. Are you assigned to fit with those most like you, those you will come into conflict with from the start? Is your personality and demeanor a factor?

Everyone agrees there are only a handful of outcomes from your time in a dorm. You become friends, or you become enemies. Sometimes both at the same time.

~ * ~

Seb glanced around the dining hall as they entered. Even without seeing the Hammerswells already seating themselves it would have been obvious where they were expected to be. Five long tables, each with the name of a dorm on display at their foot, took up maybe half the room, with the Dean sitting at the center of the table set perpendicular to them.

He doubted this was the usual layout. The space was too large, the tables made up of a collection of smaller ones hinting at a more typical isolated arrangement. He glanced at their assigned table with seats for maybe twenty people. The Hammerswells had sat themselves in the very middle while a single hooded individual sat at the far end. The other tables were filling as groups slipped past them, they'd need to make a choice if they didn't want to have it made for them.

Near or far end?

Near?

Seb pulled a chair out for her then sat down on the other side of the table in the chair she kicked out for him. He could feel how amused Anya was at the situation, she hadn't expected his gesture and wasn't going to let him have all the fun.

Think they're 3b? she sent.

Maybe? Splitting the floor would make sense if we're only three people between two groups.

From what he'd seen of the first and second floors as they passed them on the stairs there was only one door per floor. He knew how quickly Engineers could build something new, he doubted it would take them all that long to re-organize layouts to support various sized groups.

We could have sat next to them and asked, he sent.

We'd have had to walk past them.

Seb stretched out his hand, Anya's fingers wrapping around his in a brief squeeze before they both smiled. They'd probably have to deal with that group at some point, but why force the issue sooner than necessary.

~ * ~

Anya glanced up as a group slid past them. Two men, two women, all of them bantering back and forth as they went to sit on the far side of the Hammerswells. The looks the two groups exchanged told another story, she'd seen that look between squads growing up, they knew enough about one another to have issues, but knew better than to say anything in the moment. At least most of them did, from the glare she caught Marie giving Sasha something had been said once more.

She knew Seb was watching the interplay, she could feel his attention shifting from one classmate to the next. He'd let his attention wander a moment ago and she'd gotten a sense of him making note of everyone within range. Not enough to do anything with but sufficient to remember having been in the same room.

She felt more than saw the Dean stand up at the head table. A wave of silence rushing through the room as the movement was noticed. Hopefully there would be no speech, that they would be served dinner and allowed to leave. They couldn't be expected to socialize on the night of their arrival before they knew who they'd ever actually interact with, could they?

"Now then," the Dean began before the doors swung open with a muffled thud.

Anya spun to look, her eyes crossing Seb's as he did the same.

A giant ducked through the doorway before straightening. Sasha had not been a small man, yet she doubted he'd come to this one's shoulder. After he gave the entire room a glare that swept from side to side he stepped aside, allowing a much shorter woman to follow and step to the opposite side of the door. Neither moved beyond that.

Guards, she sent.

Only groups of elites were invited, not their guards.

Seb… They're watching the room. They are waiting for someone.

Before Seb could respond a trio stepped through the doorway. The first looked so average she doubted she'd remember seeing him without access to Seb's gift. Brown hair over a forgettable face and a posture that seemed much too relaxed to be with the pair he preceded.

Their eyes, Seb sent.

You and eyes, she sent back teasingly before her thoughts nearly froze.

In any other situation the pair's eyes would have been attention grabbing. When combined with the sight she saw in the mirror, that she knew Seb saw every time they looked at one another, their glow was something else entirely.

The woman's eyes looked like amethysts framed by vibrant red curls while the man at her side's eyes could have been sapphires under a silver Mohawk. Anya glanced back and forth between them, her thoughts filled with questions, only to keep filling as she realized that she could have stood next to either of them and been mistaken for their sibling. Fay was as slender as she was, plenty of people were, but she had never seen anyone else whose slenderness was quite so sharp.

Who- Who are they? she sent, knowing Seb had no answer.

"Apologies for our tardiness," Sapphire said smoothly. "We were mistaken as to the time-frame given and Keldren found the stairs to be too narrow to hurry."

"What Bern means is he almost overslept because he couldn't read your clocks right," Amethyst answered, her voice almost singsong with what had to be repressed mirth.

"Gwen," Bern said without turning his head whatsoever nor changing his tone. "Must you?"

"Sit," the Dean said. "You are late. This once your reason will be accepted."

Anya nearly found herself pressed into the table as Keldren made his way past her chair, Bern following a step behind while the others took their seats next to Seb. She thought she caught Gwen's eyes lingering on her however the Dean spoke the moment everyone was settled.

"Now that everyone has arrived." The Dean paused as though expecting another interruption. "Welcome to the three thousand one hundred and forty second cohort at Scattered Creek Training Hall. You are all skilled, talented, exceptional, yet as of this moment none of you are. The bar you passed, that of Elite progression, is one that every single student who has come through these halls has met. Outside a Hall you are rare, inside you must demonstrate it when compared to your actual peers."

Ten years, Seb sent. That was the bar.

We barely made it.

She felt his disagreement. She knew she was right, they'd had a deadline. He'd had more time, he rushed because he wanted to be with her. Feeling his thoughts follow hers she smiled.

Eight, we did it in eight.

"You will meet your instructors over the next days, possibly weeks. Those you already met will continue to evaluate you and will ensure you are further directed to those whose specializations most closely match. You will learn, you will train, you will be evaluated. You will be pushed. I know you believe you have been, but you will be pushed by those who know what your skills and limits can achieve. Who will not step in to save you from true harm."

"Your dorm mates are your allies and your competition. You will be judged relative to them, but also as a dorm against the others. You have an opportunity few can lay claim to. Enjoy."

The Dean sat and servants flooded the hall. Within moments everyone had a meal in front of them with additional servings within easy reach. Anya took a bite, then another, her hands moving mechanically as her eyes kept flitting to the group at their side. She wanted answers, Seb wanted answers. But there were no good questions, no way to ask them why they looked like her, why their eyes were the color they were. It wasn't as if she could just-

She looked up from her food to find Gwen staring at her with a strange look on her face.

"You're not any cousin I know of," she said before nudging the last of the trio. "Morgan, whose line still has green eyes?"

"None I've heard of. Especially not bred true," Morgan said, his voice somehow both crystal clear and unremarkable at the same time.

"She must be a throwback," Bern said, his tone implying more than the words could ever say on their own.

"Looking like that?" Gwen answer. "Not a chance."

Anya set her fork down with a firm thump and waited for them to all turn towards her.

"I was taught it was proper to introduce yourself before asking questions of new acquaintances. And to speak to them if they are present."

"She speaks," Bern said.

"Bern," Gwen said before turning her attention back to Anya.

"Introductions later, when names can be spoken politely. Did you grow up among humans?"
