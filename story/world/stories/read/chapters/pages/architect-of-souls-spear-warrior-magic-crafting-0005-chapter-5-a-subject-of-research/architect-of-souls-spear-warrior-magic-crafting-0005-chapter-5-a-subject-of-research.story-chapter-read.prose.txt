Sooner than he would have liked, a bell rang through the house announcing supper time. After a minor struggle, hunger won out and he pulled himself out of bed.

At the end of the hall Ferris found a large room filled with chairs, a couch, a fireplace, and a large table. The area was cozy, with blankets or pillows strewn about. The head of some type of monster hung above the fireplace. A large clock stood in the corner, and various exotic items decorated the room. Three people were already sitting around the table as Mother Lily carried food from another room—probably the kitchen.

“Ferris, dear! Come take a seat.” Mother Lily set down the platter of food and pulled out a chair at the table for him.

Ferris approached the table and greeted the other residents before sitting down. “Hello, I am Adept Ferris Bluereef. I will be staying here for a while.”

He had introduced himself a few times that day, but each time it felt odd to call himself ‘Bluereef.’ Yes, it was the clan he had grown up belonging to, and it was his name. But, before today he’d never actually needed to say it.

Back home, everyone was a Bluereef. There were at least a thousand people sharing the same name. Only the clan’s head family actually introduced themselves as Bluereef when talking to fellow clan members. Everyone else used family names, which would be chosen by a newlywed couple together upon marriage.

Ferris’ parents had picked the name Charla, inspired by a red pepper plant that grew in the waters outside the bastion. It didn’t have the prettiest flowers, but the fruit were a spicy delicacy that both loved to cook with. Thus, on the day of their wedding, they had declared themselves Alan and Marris Charla Bluereef. Fitting, for a pair of chefs.

Ferris’ uncle had never married, at least not yet, and still held the family name he and Ferris’ father had been born with: Winton Zale Bluereef.

Of course, the family name was a private matter, internal to the clan. He would never introduce himself to an outsider with it. That would be improper. To the rest of the world, he was simply Ferris Bluereef.

“Adept Karl Goldhand, at your service,” said the man sitting on Ferris’ left. Karl lifted his right hand as he spoke, encased in an elaborate gauntlet that appeared very… gold. The man’s left hand was bare. Combined with his name, that almost certainly meant the gauntlet was his bonded weapon. Many martial families that specialized in a single type of weapon would adopt it as their surname. Ferris was not in a position to judge, as Clan Bluereef lived in Bluereef Bastion, surrounded by beautiful blue coral reefs in the Wilds.

“I am Adept Cyrus Wade, researcher extraordinaire,” said the boy across from Ferris and then gestured to the girl on Ferris’ right. “This is Adept Brixten Holder.” They were both around Ferris’ age, about seventeen years old, much younger than Karl.

“Call me Brix,” she said.

Cyrus had a strap from a satchel around his shoulder, but instead of a pouch it was tied to a large, metal bound book. Instead of a title, the book had an eyeball embossed on the cover. Something so ornate and eye catching was probably his bonded item. Books were a common choice. Ferris had heard they gave a large amount of utility. They usually lacked direct attack abilities like a piercing spear, but they could be devastating in other ways.

Brix, on the other hand, had a large metal… something on the ground next to her. It was a bulky box with several tubes. Ferris had never seen anything like it. He didn’t see anything else, so whatever the thing was could be her bond. Of course, she might have something small enough to hide, or was still unbonded like him. Once bonded, a cultivator would never leave their item behind—although at the higher tiers they could store it in their soul.

Although he wasn’t sure yet if it was her bonded item, Ferris made a mental note to be extra careful walking around her. The metal box thing was bulky, sticking out. It would be easy to accidentally bump into it, which would be a grave insult to any Adept. Even touching a bonded item was paramount to a slap in the face. Ferris didn’t think Brix would appreciate him stubbing his toe on it.

“So, we’ve got a bet going.” Brix said with a nod, “Are you a test subject or minion?”

“A what?” Ferris asked, confused.

“Damnation, yet another test subject.” Cyrus sighed, a bit dramatically. “I am cursed to be surrounded by meatheads.”

Brix, meanwhile, smiled like the cat that got away with the fish—before hitting Cyrus on the shoulder. “Don’t call me a meathead, you birdbrain.”

Cyrus rubbed his arm, again a bit too dramatically, and explained. “The fourth branch of Clan Hollowfield focuses on research. Thus, they typically hire new retainers to fill one of two purposes. Option one is a brilliant,” he gestured to himself, “research assistant.” Cyrus grabbed his book and held it up. It magically opened, pages flipping by. “Recruited by the clan for their superior intellect and ability,” the book snapped shut dramatically. “If this applied to you, you would have known. Option two,” he glanced at Brix, “is someone worth studying: a test subject.”

Still smiling, Brix turned to Ferris. “Oh, don’t mind him, he is just sad he lost the bet. Also, the ‘brilliant research assistant’ is just a lackey they dump the boring work on—aka a minion.”

“Enough of this,” Mother Lily said. The woman had disappeared and then returned from the kitchen after his introduction. “That is no way to welcome a new member to our house.” She placed a bowl of some type of soup in front of Ferris.

It smelled delicious, although he didn’t recognize the meat. That was odd, as he had grown up helping his parents at work. They ran a small restaurant, along with a few extended family members. It had originally been run by his grandmother, on his mother’s side. Their family had gotten a clan charter for the building and business generations ago, which was a point of pride and obligation.

His father had been hired as temporary worker, but then started dating his mom… The rest was history.

A small family business had no end of work, so Ferris and his sisters had practically been raised helping out. Cleaning dishes, waiting tables, and running errands. Gutting an endless amount of fish. Perhaps he shouldn’t be surprised at the foreign meat. Ferris was most familiar with what was commonly available around home, while this looked like some kind of bird meat. Who made soup with bird?

Some of that endless work had lessened when Ferris got into a more intensive martial training program, but he had never stopped helping out. Not even when he lost his leg—at least when he hadn’t been sulking about.

Well, maybe the work had stopped when he became an Adept. Looking down at the strange soup, Ferris wasn’t sure how to feel about that. Just earlier he’d been happily thinking about not to have chores, but now… Would he ever cook again?

Tasting a small spoonful, Ferris couldn’t place the seasoning. Some of it was almost familiar, but not quite right. The soup also lacked any fiery spice. While it wasn’t bad, he did start looking around for a sauce to add some heat.

There were none.

By the time he emerged from his thoughts, he saw that Brix and Cyrus had listened to Mother Lily, focusing on their own food. Apparently, the woman had them well trained. That was impressive. Young new Adepts away from home were a common trope for trouble. Although maybe that was just in the stories. He’d certainly had a healthy dose of respect drilled into him through training.

“I appreciate the information.” Ferris said, mollifying Mother Lily and turning to Cyrus. “Yes, I guess I am a test subject.” The term matched his expectations for Elder Harper’s research project. At least, if these two could joke about it, then it might not be as bad as he feared.

Ferris gestured to his leg, “I will be working with Elder Harper on a healing ability.” Ferris looked at Brix and asked, “what do they have you in for?”

The question may have been too personal to ask a stranger, but they would be living in the same house. It also helped that Ferris had told them his research project first.

Brix got a large grin and grabbed the metal box from the ground next to her. Standing up, she put her arms through a pair of straps, making the box more of metal backpack. She placed one foot dramatically on the chair, grabbed one of the tubes from the backpack, pulled it out, and pointed it forward like a wand. The tube had a hose leading back to the backpack.

“My family has no cultivators or martial tradition, but my father works with mundane pumps and pressure systems. After I managed to become a cultivator, we designed this alchemical cauldron together into a weapon capable of shooting concoctions in battle.”

Ferris was a bit in shock, both at the dramatic pose, and the idea. Bonding a cauldron was nothing new, but modifying it to propel liquid sounded crazy. Usually, an alchemist would carefully brew in safety, only needing to use the final product in battle. Also… cauldrons usually looked like fancy pots, not metal boxes and tubes.

“Unfortunately, starting a new path is hard. We had the physical design, but needed it spiritually forged to be bonded. Elder Tarr agreed to help me, providing a crafter and guidance, and in turn I help him on a project dealing with brewing alchemy potions in battle,” Brix sat back down, placing the metal backpack back on the ground next to her.

“Have you used it in the middle of fighting a monster? That sounds…” Ferris was a bit lost for words.

“Crazy?” Cyrus asked, with a smile.

“I was going to say amazing,” Ferris shrugged, looking from the contraption back to Brix, who grinned at Cyrus, “but yeah, that too.”

“Unfortunately,” Brix looked back at Ferris and threw her hands up in the air, “I haven’t been able to delve yet. I have been working on battle abilities, but I have to make it up as I go.” She lowered her arms and sighed. “There is also a ton I have to learn about traditional alchemy as well.”

“There is a test you have to take before you are allowed to join the clan delving grounds,” Cyrus added, “Karl is the only one of us that has delved a rift.”

Ferris looked at Karl with excitement. “What did it take to become a delver for the clan?” He was here to fix his leg, but he also needed to be able to help his clan after his contract was over. For that, he needed to delve.

“Brix could tell you more about the tests than I could.” Karl answered. “She is currently training to pass the clan prerequisites, while I was already an experienced delver before joining the clan.”

For a ‘test subject’, Brix was a fount of information. The rest of the meal—and sometime after it—was spent listening to Brix explain her training, while Cyrus interjected almost helpful comments and Karl gave rare, yet insightful, advice. Ferris enjoyed it thoroughly, and not just because no one had mentioned his missing leg the entire time.

When he got back to his room, it was dark outside. He considered more meditation, but the lingering pain in his side insisted there would not be any benefit in continuing tonight. Sleep would help him more.

Laying down, Ferris thoughts went to tomorrow. He would finally learn his options for a bond. Imagination running wild, he drifted off to sleep.
