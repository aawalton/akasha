I expected debauchery from the Korvoks; that was certain. Everything we had seen from the mad Dwarves up to this point had been depraved to an extreme I had never once experienced.

But when Korvakh the Steward drew back the curtain separating us from the rest of the Inner Heart, I wasn’t expecting the kind of debauchery that greeted my eyes.

On the other side of it was a scene that I could only describe as bacchanalian.

There were other Korvoks in the massive, circular room on the other side of the curtain. Quite a few of them, actually. Several dozen at the very least. I almost wanted to call them Karvokar, from the basic proportions. But that wasn’t quite right. Most of the Karvokar we had encountered up to this point were, in one way or another, off-putting. It wasn’t about the hardened, flayed muscle, or even the numerous mutilations that they had inflicted upon themselves. It was the mutations, the oddities in form that they had somehow manifested. Strange proportions in limbs or extremities, extra growths where there shouldn’t be any. Oddly intentional-seeming defilements of the bipedal humanoid form that all sapient people of Vereden, no matter their species, seemed to share.

None of that was present on any of the Korvoks present in this room. They were all so…so…

“Ah…” Korvakh breathed. “Behold the Immaculata. It’s always such a treat to see them once again.”

I mouthed the word to myself, understanding the implicit message within the name.

Immaculata.

These people thought they were literally perfect, immaculate beings. And what was worse?

I could see why adherents of the Red Truth could believe that.

If the Skarned were the discarded failures of the process that created Karvokar, as Bleddyn had told me, then the Immaculata were the artisanal work. There were none of the odd mutations present within the Karvokar here. Each and every one of them was a lithe example of Dwarven kind, with all of the correct proportions that I would have seen on any random citizen in the streets of lost Rhoscara, if not tending to be more lithe. Svelte, almost, thin and pinpoint physically poised in a way most random mortals were completely incapable of. They were, like the Rendarchs we had encountered up to this point, completely and totally flayed down to the muscle, relieved of all their skin. In its place was an almost refined example of the hardened, steel-tough exposed muscle that most other Korvoks seemed to possess. Except, theirs seemed to have been polished into a literal mirror sheen. It glimmered in the clear white light cast by the mana lights of the room, reflecting the polished white marble and gleaming gold it was constructed from. They weren’t shy of this display of ‘skin’, so to speak, either. Every single example of the Immaculata was wearing what seemed to be thin, translucent robes of what looked to be either lace or silk.

Where had they even gotten that? The only place I had ever reliably seen silk was Kawamara.

The Immaculata were all over the room, lounging, or perhaps I should say posing intentionally. Their form of dress revealed absolutely everything about their form, and to someone who wasn’t a Korvok, there was nothing titillating about it.

All of that had been severed, root and stem, leaving only smooth, reflective musculature in its wake.

And the wealth. The sheer, mind boggling wealth on display, of a kind that I would expected real Dwarven aristocrats to display. The amount of golden jewelry on display was enough that I suspected you could fund the works of Blutstein for a year if you melted it all down and minted coins from it. Rings, and necklaces, and bangles galore, either upon their body or within it in some manner. They chimed and jingled with every languid stretch or gesture made, as they lounged in deliberately artistic ways. In fact, artful leisure seemed to be the point of this all. Every movement, every moment they existed seemed to be solely for the goal being…aesthetically pleasing.

Low conversation filled the air, as did the clattering of what seemed to be bone-wrought board game pieces, as they competed against each other. Some painted appropriately horrifying scenes that appealed to Korvokian ideals of beauty; some even played light instruments, their sound having been somehow blocked by the curtain.

All the while the room was ringed by the same high inlet waterfalls of blood along the edges of the room, while in the center of it…

In the center of the room was a fountain. A statuette fountain at that, bearing the form of the same joyous, flayed figure we had glimpsed in the cathedral, blood streaming from its mouth to flow down into a pool at its feet.

I took a deep breath, taking all of this in.

If it wasn’t for all of the blood, and the intentional mutilations of all the attendees of this little soiree, I would have said this really wasn’t out of the ordinary for any regular Herztalian function.

But with it, it struck me as a profound violation.

I alternatively wanted to be absolutely anywhere else…and wanted to rampage through the room, killing all of these monsters where they sat, tittering amongst themselves as they posed.

Some of my people weren’t able to keep their cool at this fresh new fright, and either audibly cursed or wretched quietly. I managed to maintain my own composure, and turned to face the quietly attentive Korvakh, seemingly watching our reaction with a strange attention. “Is the Korvokhan here?” I asked shortly.

Please say yes. If he was, then we could just kill the bastard and get this over with.

Sadly, Korvakh just shook their head. “Heavens no. These are merely the Immaculata, his most prized attendants. A portion of them, at least. Kind Ul-Korveth is deeper into the ventricles of the Inner Heart, overseeing an…important function. He bade me to escort you to him. Please, follow me.”

With that, our guide fully stepped foot into this ‘ventricle’ of the Inner Heart, winding his way between elaborate chaise lounges and indolent Korvoks like he was meant to be here. It was…a bit harder for not only me, but the rest of our forces to do the same. This room was large, yes, but the Immaculata didn’t seem to care a bit about keeping a neat and organized space for themselves. All of us were forced to stream through all of their leisure.

I would have expected some kind of pushback from them, but no. Instead, the Immaculata barely even acknowledged our existence. They were pretending not to notice us in a frankly bizarre fashion. It wasn’t just that they would only give us the most cursory of glances; it was that they were posing. I flat-out saw dozens of them ‘subtly’ angle their freakish bodies on display for me and my people, as we passed. It didn’t matter what they were doing at the time. They had to make sure we were ‘appreciating’ their literally sculpted forms as we followed Korvakh to one of the far walls, where another covered entrance lay.

I could see that this, more than even the gratuitous amounts of slaughter we had engaged in today, was getting to some of my soldiers. And frankly, I didn’t blame this.

This was beyond weird. I was having a hard time even considering the Immaculata as people. They were more like…cats.

Trophy cats. Well-bred, well-groomed pets whose only purpose in life was to lie around and be admired.

You could just tell that they considered themselves as above us. There was a disdain even in their posing, like we were unworthy of the right to admire them. Honestly, the wordless attitude reminded me distinctly of some nobles I had encountered in the past.

The kind that I always had to repress a longing desire to throw from the highest point on Kyronkar.

Alas, although I suspected this would all end in a bloodbath, the time wasn’t right for such a thing. We still had yet to meet with this ‘Ul-Korveth’, and more importantly…

We still didn’t know where any of the captives were. Until then, I would just have to stomach freaks.

Odd thing, though. To my senses, not a single one of the Immaculata…

Felt any stronger than even the most common Skarned.

I suppose they really were more of a trophy than a weapon.

…………………………….

To my distaste, that first room wasn’t the only one where we encountered the Immaculata. We had to wind our way through four more of the Inner Heart’s ventricles before we began to reach anything close to a serious installation.

There really was such an odd number of the Immaculata. I couldn’t help but wonder what the point of them was. They weren’t soldiers, and they weren’t administrators. They certainly weren’t priests, either.

Why…why keep this many of them around? There had to be hundreds alone in just the chambers we threaded through. And there were more of those ventricles packed full of the Immaculata, beyond the ones we’d seen. In nearly every room we found, there were other passages blocked from sight, that I was sure led to more and more of these pleasure domes.

Who knows just how far the Inner Heart stretched? It was almost like another city, before the pulsating spires of Karag Korvok itself. Not anywhere near as big, of course…but notable.

And that noteworthiness made me uneasy.

Eventually, Korvakh stopped just before one more of the curtains, and turned to face us. “Past here you shall find Gracious Ul-Korveth and, perhaps, those you were expecting. Please, I would ask-”

I didn’t pay the steward another mote of attention. Instead, I stalked forward, brushing aside the curtain with far more force I likely needed.

All I had needed to hear was that the captives were on the other side, and I was fully ready to charge in and save them. I did not intend to let either any of the Varlayans, my captured people, or Azarus become sacrifices in some sick Korvokian ritual. Those who had been close enough to hear Korvakh’s words surged in behind me, just as eager to finally, finally fulfill our objective in this hellhole.

Only…

I stopped not far inside the room, staring blankly at what I found. Everyone else who streamed in after me did the same, gradually filling up the entrance to simply…stare.

In all of my wildest dreams, and through all the expectations I had ever, ever held for anything on Vereden…

This had never once even crossed my mind.

The world narrowed into a single view, and I simply…stopped caring. I barely noticed the sudden industrially forged metal that the floor had abruptly transitioned to, of a suspiciously familiar white shade. I was only slightly conscious of just how large this room was, being, I think, itself nearly a square mile in diameter. I barely flickered my eyes away to notice anything within it, seeing a great many things and people inside, and yet paying them no mind. Even recognizing many of the people within this great room was only able to cause a brief shift in my interest, many of whom had been missing for a short, long, or fraught time.

The only thing that mattered to me was the great glass window that spanned the complete, massive length of the far wall.

And what I thought, I hoped, I dreamed of was on the other side of that glass.

It must have taken me several minutes of fast walking to reach it, and when I did, I only tore my gaze away from the glass for a moment to consider who was standing to my side.

I say standing, but what I really mean was floating.

Rafael, my missing Lich, had folded the desiccated arms of his original, deceased body behind his back as he stared out of the window that had so enraptured me. There was a complex expression on his undead face. I didn’t know the Necromancer anywhere near as well as I did someone like Grey, but to my eyes…

There was a curious mix of dread, wonder, and hope on that nearly bare skull. Our eyes met for a single moment, and then the both of us were right back to staring through the window.

I was the one to break the silence between us.

“Do you know what that is?” I asked quietly. I was only able to bring myself to little more than a whisper, as if speaking louder would dispel the illusion on the other side of the glass.

Slowly, creakingly, Rafael nodded. “Yes. I had…suspicions, even before the master of this hall sought to illuminate me. The Great Sage had a great many things to say, to wonder about, in the years I studied under him. Yes. I do know what that is. Do you?”

I nodded, reaching up to rest a hand on the glass, as if I could simply reach out and take what lay in the titanic room contained by it.

“Yes. It’s…” I shuddered, unwilling to even consider the possibility for a moment. But eventually, I managed to force it out in a single breath.

“It's a spaceship.”
