Caleb thought, taking it in.

His Wind Affinity was screaming at him that this storm was barely contained. That it would only take a small disruption to whatever controlled it to let it dissipate into the mountains.

“The storm wants to escape,” Caleb told his brother. “It’s small. I think I can sense its center if we go in. If we can kill whoever’s holding it together we might give everyone a fighting chance.

Grisim didn’t argue, he just started checking that all his gear was securely strapped after the run, and then nodded his readiness at Caleb who’d done the same.

“Bog,” Caleb said, “Don’t use any spit attacks unless we are desperate. I think they might be able to sense the heat of it.”

Bog nodded reluctantly, and they ran off into the storm.

The cold hit him like a punch to the chest, and he had to stop to catch his breath. It was like diving into the icy river behind the mill. Something he’d done on occasion and regretted every time. Each inhalation hurt, but he found he could still breathe. He was aware of the cold and the pain, but there was a mental hand on the pain of it holding it at bay from becoming all consuming.

My enhanced constitution, Caleb realized, thanking Kelia for her foresight.

Caleb’s metal sense gave him a warning a moment before the strike landed. He’d grown familiar with the quenching steel over the past few days, and now in the metal barren wasteland that was the storm, the presence of the flecks of steel within the armor and weapons of the Frigidi shone to Caleb senses—at least within the 8 meters of himself where he had metal senses.

Caleb ducked the icy spear thrust at his chested, moving to the side, he swung his hammer down on the weapon, shattering it with his drastically harder hammer. Grisim followed into the gap opened by Caleb, and thrust his spear with a Power Attack following the line of the now broken weapon.

The clash of steel on ice was just one more amongst the tumult and the ice shattered beneath the stamina enhanced blow.

Caleb stepped after Grisim and threw his hammer with at Arcane Power Attack at the flecks of steel he sensed just beside it. As soon as he sensed his hammer’s momentum stop, he pulled the hammer to himself, using part of the force of the move to pull himself to meet it. He snatched the hammer out of the air, distantly aware that the gloves between himself and his new metal handle were limiting his control.

Hammer in hand, he ran at the surprised Frigidi and struck him on his ice armored head, breaking his neck and the armor along with it.

Only then did it come into focus for Caleb. This wasn’t a Frigidi with their clean crafted frost weapons and armor that mimicked the shape of steel equipment in ice. This was the rough grown armor of the Frost Troglodytes

He checked his ignored notifications.

Adolescent Frigidi level 3 has been killed.

Adolescent Frost Troglodyte (uncommon) has been killed.

It wasn’t just the Frigidi. They’d come with the Troglodytes.

“Elders,” Caleb whispered, barely audible over the storm. “This is definitely our fault.”

Grisim didn’t hear.

“Why are the Trogs here?” Grisim asked. “Nevermind, it doesn’t matter. Where to?”

He’s right. It doesn’t matter for now.

Caleb pointed to where he sensed the wind was the weakest in the center, and they moved that way. The frost people had some ability to see within the storm that Caleb and Grisim lacked. When up close in combat the brothers were at a disadvantage in they could barely keep their eyes open against the snow let alone could their gaze pierce through it. But, that advantage was limited to only a few meters while Caleb could pick up on the presence of their steel from further out.

They ambushed three more mixed groups of adolescent Frigidi and trogs as they made their way to the center, navigating their hometown blindly and using the terrain to their advantage. Master Dimel’s forge was on their way to the center, and Caleb ducked in quickly, picking up another spear and a bag of nails—all cold-aspected. He’d lost his enchanted spear down the mountain after the wyvern attack, and not had the time to go looking for it. As crazy as it would have sounded to him a few weeks prior, the loss was a small one. Less than a day of work, and already outdated. Until he improved, he planned to use ingots fully for his stats, allowing the aspect of his weapons to more fully manifest without the interference of his enchantments.

Gris grabbed a helmet while they were in there, and Bog took mouthfuls of coal from the smoldering forges. He didn’t seem harmed by the level of cold of the storm, but he clearly was not a fan of it.

After restocking, they moved back out into the storm. They ambushed all the groups they found unaware, but not all their opening blows were decisive, and skirmishes broke out. Caleb and Grisim each took a few blows, but every time the enemy got the chance to make a counterattack, it was followed by Bog pouncing on them and killing them with the weight of his body alone.

They moved on in this wait until Caleb sensed they neared the heart of the storm. The wind stood still ahead, not absent, however. Normally still air was a hazy awareness in Caleb’s perception, but within this circle Caleb sensed wind held prisoner. Like the heart of a storm had been placed in a bubble it sought to escape.

Caleb signaled to Grisim by tugging at the buckles on his coat and they stopped. Caleb sensed no metal within that sphere, but if the storm’s jailors were like the white clad troglodytes, that didn’t mean no one stood protecting it.

They waited just long enough for Caleb’s mana to top off, aided as he was by both the very high ambient mana within the storm, and his last mouthful of his mana regenerating mushroom.

Then, they charged.
