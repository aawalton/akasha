Sakura did not remember when she broke into a run. She raced through the ruins, catching her shoulder on cold stone, stumbling over cracks in the floor, and hearing that terrifying roar behind her. The monster was not chasing her in blind fury. It was not about to let its chosen prey get away. Its huge obsidian body slammed into what was left of a wall, scattering black shards across the courtyard. Sakura was already choking on how fast she was running. Her legs felt weak, her chest burned, but fear drove her forward, not letting her stop and think about how pointless this all was. Behind her was a creature that had just torn apart one of its own kind and grown twice as large. Ahead of her was another heap of ruins where Sakura hoped to find shelter. Because if she did not, then... She simply could not let herself think about that.

She ducked under a half-collapsed arch and rushed through a dark corridor lined with broken windows and fallen columns. When she saw a dead end ahead, she cut sharply to the side, nearly falling as her shoe slid on a layer of black stone grit. Something crashed into the arch behind her, and the walls shuddered. The monster was not moving as fast as she had first thought, but it was relentless. It was not hurrying because it had no reason to hurry. To it, she was prey, weak and lost, driven into a maze of ruins, and that realization made everything inside Sakura go even colder. She slipped into the next passage, almost pitch-black, smelling of dust, dampness, and something stale, and stopped for a second to figure out where to run next.

The space around her was more complicated than it had looked from outside: jagged passages, collapsed stairways, inner courtyards, short corridors, surviving pieces of wall, and between them the wind still whispered something she could not make out, and that whisper was starting to irritate her almost to the point of madness, because Sakura did not care about anyone else’s secrets right now. She needed an exit. Or shelter.

She darted right and a few seconds later ran into a round roofless chamber half-buried in sand. It had probably once been an inner hall or a watchtower, but now all that remained were the walls, the narrow gap she had come through, and a broken floor hidden under an uneven layer of sand and debris. Sakura made it almost to the middle, already about to dash farther, then stopped because there was nowhere else to go. Only a smooth black wall, marked here and there with cracks and faded runes that stretched across the stone in a complicated pattern. Her heart dropped. She had run into an enclosed space, maybe even a trap. She spun around sharply as the sound of claws scraping against stone came from the narrow opening.

“No,” she breathed hoarsely. “No, no...”

She rushed to the wall and touched it with her hand. The growling came closer. A few more seconds and the creature would burst in. Sakura squeezed her eyes shut for a brief moment, and then something cold and hard seemed to click into place inside her. The panic did not disappear, but it stepped aside. Her breathing was still ragged, her hands were shaking, and her knees felt weak, yet her thoughts suddenly became clearer, just enough for her to use them. Sakura slowly opened her eyes and forced herself to look around, studying the cracks, the dust, and the runes.

Something was wrong under her feet. When Sakura took half a step back, the surface under her sole answered with a dull, too-empty sound. Hollow. She dropped to her knees at once, ignoring the pain in her scraped legs, and shoved the sand aside with both hands. The outline of a square stone slab quickly appeared beneath it, set tightly into the floor. Nearly worn-out runes ran along its edges, and near one corner Sakura felt a narrow indentation, as if fingers or a metal hook had once been meant to fit there. Her heart kicked into a wild rhythm again, but now it had a purpose. It was either a hatch or another trap, and right now that did not matter. She had to try to use it if she did not want to become a snack for that fanged thing.

A shadow had already appeared in the passage.

Sakura dug her fingers into the edge of the slab and pulled. Nothing. The stone did not even budge. A crash sounded behind her. The monster hit the wall as it forced itself through the narrow opening, and small obsidian shards rained into the chamber. Sakura changed her grip, braced one foot against the floor, and yanked again, feeling the skin tear under her nails. The slab creaked. Barely. Just a fraction. That was enough to ignite a nearly insane hope inside her. She pulled again, then again, no longer thinking about how weak her arms were, how the System had apparently described her attributes honestly, and how under any other circumstances she would not even have tried to move something that heavy. But death was crawling toward her from behind, and sometimes that was enough to make a person do the impossible for a few seconds.

At last the slab gave way.

Not fully, but enough for a narrow black gap to open between it and the floor, and something rotten drifted up from below at once. It was not enough. The monster had already burst into the chamber, smashing through what was left of the arch as if it were a dry branch. Sakura jerked her head up and saw it lowering its huge maw, saw the crimson light pulsing in the cracks of its body, saw its sharp obsidian claws cutting into the sand. She had no time left for fear or hesitation. She slid her fingers into the gap and pulled the slab even harder. It lurched, opening the dark drop wider, and Sakura lost her balance. She fell through at the exact moment the monster lunged forward.

She did not fall far, only six or seven feet, but the landing still knocked the air out of her. Pain flared in her shoulder, her elbow scraped against stone, and the taste of blood filled her mouth. A dull crash sounded above. The slab she had shoved halfway aside slid back under its own weight, almost sealing the opening and leaving only a thin slanted slit of light. Sakura lay there for several seconds, too afraid to move, listening to the monster scraping at the stone overhead, growling, slamming its heavy body against the floor, unable to understand right away where its prey had gone. Dust slowly settled over her hair and face. Her lungs burned. Her whole body shook from the fall and the delayed horror. But she was alive.

Sakura forced herself to sit up and look around. The basement she had fallen into was bigger than she had expected. Not a cramped pit, but an old stone chamber with a low ceiling, cracked columns, and walls covered in the same runes as above, only here they were easier to see. There were too many of them. They ran across the stone in rings and broken lines, spreading even onto the floor. Looking at them made her skin crawl, as if she were seeing not just writing, but traces of something very old and very alien.

Three skeletons hung in chains on the far wall. Their bones had yellowed and blackened in places, and their empty skulls hung low, as if even after death they had been unable to lift their eyes from the floor.

Off to one side, half-grown into the ground, lay the obsidian body of another monster. It looked long dead: its shell was cracked, its red glow had gone out, and one of its limbs had been torn off. Through the cracks in the floor and right between the slabs, the same dead flowers were pushing through: gray, dry, with stiff petals. In the center of the chamber stood an empty altar of black stone, and a slanted beam of light from the crack in the ceiling fell across it.

“A creepy place, but it looks... safe,” she whispered.

She let herself exhale. It felt strange to calm down in a place where someone’s bones were chained to the walls. Sakura almost smiled. It was a nervous little laugh.

Then she got up and slowly moved closer to the altar to get a better look. Its surface was covered in runes too, carved thin and deep, and in some grooves dust had gathered so long ago that it seemed like part of the stone itself. Small chips were breaking off the altar and falling to the ground. Time spared nothing.

“Just... just give me a little time,” she muttered into the emptiness, not knowing whether she was speaking to the world, the System, the monster above, or herself.

The thought that she might be able to wait it out down here came almost at once, and it seemed so reasonable that Sakura seized on it with the desperation of a drowning person. The System had said: chance of surviving the next twenty-four hours — 3.8%. So she needed to survive twenty-four hours. If this was a trial, if the whole point was simply not dying in the first day, then maybe hiding would be enough. The monster would leave. It would lose her trail. It would stop throwing itself against stone. Sakura would stay here as long as she had to, and then... Then she would think about what came next. It was not a good plan, but it was the only one she had for now.

For a while longer she could still hear impacts and scraping from above, then everything went quiet.

Sakura sat down at the base of the altar, rested her hands on her knees, and stared at the floor. Time moved strangely here. Without sun, without clocks, without the sounds of a city, it seemed to spread out and lose its shape. Sakura had no idea whether ten minutes had passed or an hour. Several times she felt herself on the verge of slipping into a shaky half-sleep from exhaustion, but the moment her eyelids lowered, the subway rose before her eyes at once: red lights, smoke, suffocation, people crushed by the crowd. She would snap her eyes open and stare again at the altar, the skeletons, the dead obsidian body, the slits of light above, forcing herself to believe that this was her new reality.

When a scraping sound came from the far corner of the basement, at first she thought she had imagined it. Then she heard it again. Not from the hatch. From somewhere off to the side, from the darkness behind a fallen column and the overgrowth of dead flowers. Sakura slowly rose to her feet, feeling icy fear crawl up her spine.

“No,” she said very quietly. “No. You couldn’t...”

The scraping grew louder, unbearably close, and in the next second the stone in the far corner of the basement cracked. A dark slit split open in the wall, half-hidden by rubble and dry flowers. Black grit and small chunks of obsidian spilled out, and then a huge gleaming limb forced its way through, its claws sharp as knives. The maw came next, then a shoulder, and a moment later the monster was already inside. Scratched up, furious, breathing hard, even more terrifying in the tight enclosed space. Sakura backed away without taking her eyes off it, feeling horror crawl cold along her spine and lock up her legs. The monster slowly lowered its head, found her with its gaze, and let out a low, almost satisfied growl, as if it liked the fact that now she truly had nowhere left to run.

Panic hit all at once, so hard that for a second Sakura’s vision went dark. The safe shelter had been a lie. A stupid, pathetic hope she had managed to clutch with both hands.

She darted to the side, trying to get around the altar, nearly slammed her hip into it, but there was a wall behind her, chains with skeletons to the right, the dead obsidian monster to the left, and a scatter of sharp stone fragments. The creature came at her slowly, heavily placing one paw after another, as if it already knew how this would end. It was not hurrying. Why hurry when the prey was cornered? Sakura sucked in a shaky breath, feeling herself start to tremble from fear and helplessness.

“No... no,” slipped from her almost soundlessly.

The monster took another step, then one more, and suddenly lunged forward without warning with a hoarse roar that made everything inside Sakura tighten into one icy knot. She flinched back, barely managing to avoid the paw that slammed into the edge of the altar with a crash and broke off a chunk of black stone. The shard skidded almost to her foot. Sakura dropped her gaze, saw it, and snatched it up before she even had time to think about why she was doing it.

The jagged sharp edges bit into her left palm at once. Pain burned through her skin instantly. She clenched her teeth but did not let go. A new cut opened across her already scratched hand, and warm blood ran down her wrist. Sakura instinctively backed toward the altar and raised the shard in front of her, even though she knew how pitiful it looked against a thing like this, and at that same moment blood slipped from her fingers and dripped onto the edge of the altar and the dry flowers at its base.

For one short, terrible moment, nothing happened. Sakura had time to think only that this was the end.

Then the runes flared.

First one, then dozens at once, and the whole chamber flooded with a thick crimson light rising from below, from the cracks, from the lines carved into the stone, from the dried petals that suddenly seemed to come alive, filling with dark red color and trembling. The monster jerked, roared, and squeezed its eyes shut for a second, blinded by the light. Sakura had no time to be afraid or surprised. Everything happened faster than thought. She saw the familiar vulnerable gap beneath the monster’s throat between the obsidian plates, and her body lunged forward before her mind could stop it. Sakura drove the sharp altar shard into its weak point with both hands, putting everything she had left into that blow: fear, pain, helplessness, and fury that she was being driven into a corner again and forced to die again.

The shard went in.

The monster howled so violently that the walls trembled. Sakura felt the sharp edge of the stone slide deeper, felt her bloody fingers touch the hot, pulsing gap in the creature’s body along with it, and at that same instant something seemed to strike back through her hand from the inside. Something older and greedier. The monster convulsed, trying to tear itself free, but no light was spraying from the wound now the way it had from the creature above. The monster crumbled apart right in front of her eyes. Its obsidian body cracked, crimson light burst outward, and then the whole thing collapsed into a stream of dark ash that twisted once and was pulled into her palm.

Sakura screamed.

Burning pain sank into her bones, while a terrible cold spread through her blood. She recoiled, dropped the shard, and fell to her knees by the altar, clutching her hand, but it changed nothing. Blackness was spreading up her skin from her palm, over her wrist, over her forearm, as if her arm were being coated in obsidian dust that was becoming part of her. Inside, everything burned and tore. Sakura felt that one more second of that impossible foreign sensation would tear her apart. Then, just as the pain reached its limit, something else wove through it. Warm, thick, almost sweet. Not relief, but a strange, terrifying pleasure, as if her body, after nearly breaking, had suddenly understood exactly how much it liked this power. That made it even worse. She bent lower, breathing hard, clenching her teeth until her jaw hurt, and simply waited for the wave to pass.

The light in the basement slowly began to fade. The runes dimmed one after another. The crack in the ceiling again became the only source of pale light. Sakura sat by the altar, trembling, covered in dust, sweat, and the creature’s ash. The monster was gone. Nothing remained of its body except dark powder scattered across the floor, and even that seemed to have already sunk into the cracks in the stone. Sakura slowly lifted her right hand from her left and stared at the blackened arm.

The skin was black up to the elbow, with a faint glassy sheen, as if the same obsidian the monsters were made of had briefly shown through beneath her human skin. Sakura gasped, nearly choking on a new surge of panic, but in the next moment the blackness began to recede, slowly and reluctantly. It drew down from her elbow to her forearm, then to her wrist and hand, dissolving like ash in water until it clung only to the tips of her fingers before vanishing almost completely. On the inside of her wrist, there remained a barely visible mark, thin, ash-obsidian in color, shaped like a petal with a sharp, slightly curved edge.

Sakura stared at it for a long time, not daring to touch it.

That familiar pale glow flared before her eyes.

CONTACT WITH ALTAR CONFIRMED.ASH ABSORPTION: SUCCESSFUL.

Sakura froze, breathing hard, and lifted her gaze.

HIDDEN PATH ACTIVATED.CLASS DETERMINED.

For a second the lines vanished, then returned in a different form.

CURRENT CLASS: OBSIDIAN HIEROPHANT.
