Chapter 210 / B3-55: Shape of the Ship

The rains continued for one more soggy day. Joe had fun doing not much of anything for once. Kenda, having the day off, came over with Wen to hang out. The four of them, including Yuk, spent most of the day swapping stories and bantering about build ideas. They also taught him an Illuminarian board game, which Joe mastered far too quickly in the eyes of the two competitive girls.

They lost Yuk and Kenda for at least an hour when the raggamuffin finally explained to the skill savant how their pattern worked. Yuk glossed over their origin, leaving out how they were actually a fragment of the primordial swarm monstrosity, Khimbar. Even so, a swarm-based life build was a completely new concept for Kenda, and she was utterly enthralled by the concept.

While those two were huddled together discussing Yuk’s Monster pattern, Joe and Wen hung out with the hounds. The elf’s expression was one of wonder as she gaped at the three spectral hounds lounging around Joe's living room.

“They are magnificent,” she beamed. “There are legends about Callum the Bounder and his Wild Pack. My father met the Bounder during the War of Winter.”

Wen's voice carried the reverence of someone standing before living history. The hounds appeared canine at first glance, but no mortal dog possessed their strange beauty. Their forms shimmered with pale silver light, edges fading into drifting mist.

Baru raised her head from where she rested beside the apartment door. While Mahq and Mojo were downstairs with the young druid’s grandparents, Joe had no doubt the big girl was on watch, listening for any troubles that might befall her charges below.

Near the kitchen lay Bayu, who, in complete opposition to her bigger sister, had conked out and was snoring. Every so often, the napping hound responded to the storm, releasing a low spectral rumble of her own that mimicked distant thunder rolling across hills.

Meanwhile, Ranu was impossible to keep track of. One moment, he lay beside the coffee table. Next, he appeared near the bookshelf. A heartbeat later, he was back at the window, gazing out at the storm. His movements were so fleet and silent that it was almost as if he was teleporting around the room.

Wen reached over to run a hand down the back of the restless runner. Her emerald eyes opened wide. Joe knew exactly the sensation she was feeling. Their coats, being barely substantial and slightly chilly to the touch, made one think of heavy fog drifting through a cool night.

"My father told stories about them. ‘Ware the softer baying, for the Hounds are nearing’ was one of his favorites," Wen said. “One of the tricks of the hounds is that their howls are louder farther away than they are up close."

“Bayu is likely the exception to that rule. When she barks next to me, it's so loud I jump out of my skin every time she does it.”

"I can't believe these are the hounds of Callum the Bounder. He was a legend, too."

“What can you tell me about him?” Joe asked.

Wen smiled widely, both at Joe’s excitement and the fact that Ranu had practically climbed into her lap for more pets. If the spectral beast were solid, he would have completely swamped the slight elven archer. In the flesh, the deerhound would have weighed almost as much as Wen.

"My father knew Callum well. ‘Vault the cauldron’, which means to move fast enough to avoid troubles, originated with the Bounder. Callum was a harrgwerin, similar to the brer of Illuminaria.” Joe recalled the rabbit-like brers he had met so far.

"Callum was a hedge-knight, one who was not aligned to any court, but honored by all of them. Which mostly meant Winter and Summer, since the Kings have been silent for the last two hundred years. He was a force to be reckoned with. Clever. Fast. Stubborn beyond reason."

“During the War of Winter," she continued, “he fought for Queen Gloriana, not because he had any love for Summer, but because he loved roads. The Morrigu, as a tactic, was closing them, sealing the pathways through the Gossamer Lands and the gates to Illuminaria. The stories say Callum and his hounds carried messages through battle lines no one else could cross. Baru guarded him from assassins. Bayu's bark could rally entire companies. And Ranu..."

The spectral dog's tail wagged.

"...could outrun a winter storm."

"That’s them," Joe murmured, looking about the apartment at each of the three ghost hounds: the protective guardian by the door, the thunder-voiced sleeper in the kitchen, and the vibrating runner who was barely able to lie still stretched across Wen’s lap.

Joe wasn't sure what the best thing to come out of Glen Cumha was. Margen wasn't even in the top two. It was a toss-up between his fast friendship with Yuk and the constant comfort these three ghostly pups had been bringing him and Mojo.

The sky finally cleared on Windsday, leaving Joe just two more days until the cooldown on [Anyone] was up. The world outside was still dripping, and any street not running downhill toward the shore was likely filled with ankle-deep puddles. Even so, Yuk was too excited to wait for the city to dry out a bit before heading for the shipyards.

The pack, this time without Mahq, splashed their way across the plaza, with the discorporated swarm riding on Joe’s shoulders and back. Thankfully, Founders Plaza had wards that cleansed the standing pools. Joe and Mojo just got soaked, not splattered with filth.

With the plan to go see the newly named Shellter, Joe found himself humming the classic Rolling Stones’ song of a similar name, ‘Gimme Shelter’, though by the time he reached the coast, the song in his head had morphed into ‘Street Fighting Man’.

The main shipyards were off to Joe’s right, but that was not where they were headed. Unlike most sea vessels, his hull did not need to be assembled on dry land. His hull was already a single massive shell. Balance in the water was far more important. To this end, the Shellter was being assembled at a floating platform out on the waters of the harbor.

“All aboard,” Joe called out as he spotted the rowboat that he had been given permission to use whenever he wanted to view the progress. Mojo zoomed to his spot at the bow of the dory, and Yuk flowed down off Joe as he settled onto the bench and took up the oars. Joe felt his back and shoulders swell with muscle as [Morphic Form] built up the structure that his athletics-based skill, [Hunter’s Haste], would use to drive them across the waters of Highmast Harbor. So empowered, their dinghy was fast enough to pass fishing boats that were under sail.

In a matter of minutes, they were hopping out onto the large floating construction raft. Yuk resumed their perch on Joe’s back and shoulders while Mojo trotted along beside them. There were a few other projects being built beside the floating platform, but none could compare to the Shellter. Even though she was barely started, the vessel was already amazing.

The dragon turtle shell that formed her hull rose from the harbor like a scintillating basin that had once belonged to a great titan. Time had polished portions of the ancient shell smooth as glass, while other sections retained deep ridges and weathered grooves. The shell's natural growth patterns spiraled across the scutes like the rings of a mighty tree. Flecks of color drew the eye, even while the shell itself seemed to want to drive you back. A looming sense of danger lurked within the draconic carapace. Most ships that plied Illuminaria’s deep waters were warded either to escape notice or to exude an aura of dread. Given the monstrous material into which the Stellter was being built, Joe’s options could only be the latter.

Inside the shell was a rootwork lattice that hugged the walls of the hull, leaving the center open. These barky tendrils led back to the brineroot mangrove tree that stood towards the back of the boat. At the moment, dozens of heavy sandbags filled the bow of the vessel to balance out the small tree. The roots had been fused into the shell, turning the whole structure into a living organism. Joe could feel life coursing through the hull. Myllo’s plan was working perfectly. This was a ship that Joe would be able to heal, not one he would need to repair should it be damaged.

The tree itself was unlike what Joe thought mangrove trees on Earth looked like. The trunk was far thicker than the spindly image he had in his head. It was not huge by any means, not even as thick as his thigh. The bole was also twisted and bendy, reminding Joe far more of a Japanese maple than a mangrove tree. Even the leaves seemed to lean in that direction, appearing more golden than green. The bark was smooth and silvery, looking nothing like either of those two trees.

The tree was maybe twenty feet tall, not counting its incredible root system, with a canopy of roughly the same diameter. The seemingly endless roots even grew over the edge of the shell, filling the small irregularities in the ridge of the carapace. These roots hung into the sea, drinking up the seawater. After drawing out salts, minerals, and nutrients to feed the living tree, the fresh water emerged from the base of the trunk and roots, where it could be collected. Joe planned to learn the SweetWater ward to ensure they always had fresh water, but it never hurt to have a backup source of drinking water.

Shipwrights bustled about carrying long planks, measuring and fitting them atop the growing framework inside the shell. These would form the deck and the cabins. Joe’s lifesense did not extend into the boards, meaning he would still need mending charms for the woodwork.

The deck builders greeted them with friendly waves before returning to their work. They stayed out of the way of the shipwrights, walking back and forth across the platform. As Joe paced out the sixty-foot shell, he described to Yuk where everything was going to be placed.

Up front would be the helm room, followed by a large great room: part living room, part library, part dining room. The kitchen, or in nautical terms, the galley, would be off the great room. Bedrooms on the next floor up. There would be a bathroom or head on each of these floors, the downstairs one containing a shower to rinse off the sea salt after a swim.

Most storage would be below, with additional space on the top floor. The rest of the third floor belonged to Yuk. As Joe described the low-ceilinged rooms and storage spaces, he felt a pulse of anticipation through the bond. Yuk was interjecting ideas on how they planned to fill the space.

The big back deck would open, allowing Finn and a friend to come aboard.

Before the left, Joe met up with Orrick Tidemaker, Myllo’s foreman on this project. The dwarf reminded Joe of Dunny, the ship carpenter from the Tidedancer. They each had that same straight brown hair and the sloping shoulders of the coastal dwarf heritage. Orrick perked up when Joe described Yuk’s plans for a large forward-facing cannon.

“That be just what this beauty needs,” Orrick barked enthusiastically. “She be one tough brute. She deserves to have a snapper.”

‘Ooh! That is what we are naming the cannon,’ Yuk blurted into Joe’s head. ‘Snapper! I love it. Whatta ya think, Joe?’

Joe sent his full agreement to his friend while he flared [Crystal Mind]. Master Orrick was spouting off a list of specifications he would need to get from Myllo regarding the cannon’s weight, size, power, and more in order for the shipwright to build the proper base for the weapon.

With the numbers locked in his head, they spent the next few hours just hanging around, watching the work unfold. The larger beams were already fixed in place. By the time they left, the framework no longer looked like a collection of beams. Joe could finally see the shape of their home.
