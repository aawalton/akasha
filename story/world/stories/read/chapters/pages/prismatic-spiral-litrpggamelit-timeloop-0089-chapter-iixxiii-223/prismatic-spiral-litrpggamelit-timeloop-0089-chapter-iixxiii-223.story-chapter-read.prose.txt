Chapter II.XXIII (2.23)

After thoroughly trouncing Zeaqu at cards for a few hours, the little skiff puttered up to a wall of yellow mist. If Zeaqu was concerned about the lack of visibility, he showed no sign of it as he shuffled his deck and muttered what she assumed to be archaic obscenities in a foreign language.

“You truly know them?” the wizard asked.

They disappeared into the fog. Despite the thick yellow density, he had no unique scent.

“Who?” Mitsukoa asked.

“The creators of the card game. After showcasing your vast knowledge for the game’s strategy, I assume you must have been speaking honestly earlier with your boast.”

“Yeah,” Mitsuko replied absentmindedly as she swiped at the fog. “The summoner cards were meant to act as a bestiary with a general blood tax based on the summoner who was designing them. Then a necromancer saw them and wanted to replicate it. Some children stole the cards and were spotted playing with them later. It cascaded from there and suddenly a game formed out of it.”

“It’s taken Amber by storm these last few months. If you have insider information about strategies designed by the creators, then you could use the information as a bribe. I know many who’d trade much to dominate and embarrass their foes.”

“Really? It’s just a card game.”

“A popular one. On an island where knowledge is treasured.”

Mitsuko leaned back in her seat and drummed her fingers on the wooden table. As she pondered about card game strategies, the wizened wizard lifted the deck of cards to his lips, opened his mouth wide and swallowed them.

Mitsuko blinked.

“Did you just… eat those?”

The old man grinned, showing a dazzling set of teeth while his eyes twinkled, despite the fog.

“Oh, they were so delicious! I could not help myself!” Zeaqu said with hammed up acting. He thumped an open palm against his belly. “What will I do now? Oh, dear. Lich’s dungbeetles, I am in a pickle.”

Mitsuko rolled her eyes. Then she focused on his wide smirk, thoroughly examining it. “Do you have a spatial storage inside your mouth?”

The wizard spluttered. “What? You ruined my joke!”

“Back molar?” she guessed.

“Second to back,” he muttered. “There’s a crown there.”

“Why would you set a spatial storage on a tooth of all things? Is this just for a single gag?”

“A colleague of mine went through a spatial magic phase. We traded a few knick-knacks and this one looked fun. Helps me avoid foods I don’t like as well without being rude. Plus I store soup in there. An automatic soup dispenser whenever I please!”

Mitsuko scoffed and smiled at the liberal use of dangerous magic. It was normal for these sorts of powerful spellcasters to be just a bit nutty. But, then again… wasn’t she now a powerful spellcaster? She surely was nothing like them. Except… she did just burrow into a leviathan and cut it apart from the inside with very little intent on self-preservation. Perhaps people might consider her a tiny bit nutty.

“What flavor?” she asked.

“Pumpkin!”

“Good choice. Though I personally prefer miso.”

The wizard nodded, his beard bobbing as he accepted her answer.

“Were you just out at sea looking at the barrier?” Mitsuko asked.

“Partially! I also needed to pick up a supply of materials for my next experiment. I met with one of my contacts and we performed a trade. They’re not allowed on Amber so all business needed to be conducted at sea.”

“Blacklisted from the island?” Mitsuko frowned. “What sort of materials required you to contact someone—”

“Hush!” Zeaqu raised a finger to his lips. Then he spun around and flicked his hands into the air sporadically.

A few seconds later and the fog split open, revealing a massive yellow castle with spires that reached skyward. She could spot dozens of people flying around the castle, like little gnats around a donkey. Most rode on enchanted equipment, but a few simply ran through the air. Several of the windows of the castle were lit up in different bright colors that stood out even in the daytime. Under one window, the words ‘ENTER AT YOUR OWN PERIL’ were written in bright pink glitter paint. As she stared, fireworks went off on the other side of the castle, the pops of which could be heard all the way out here.

The island itself slowly pushed its way through the sea and dragged along the mystical fog that surrounded it in a ring. The sea churned at the base. An island of perpetual movement.

“Amazing,” Mitsuko mumbled, star struck. She had to remind herself to breathe again. This was what magic truly looked like. The wonder. The mystery. It sucked in every bit of her attention. The sight filled her very being as gooseprickles dotted her skin.

The little skiff puttered alongside the island until they arrived at the docks. An old lady in a purple bathrobe desperately waved at them with both arms.

“Zeaqu, is that you?”

“Oh no.” Zeaqu dropped low to hide behind the gunwales.

` Mitsuko crouched down as well. “Everything fine?”

“That’s Piano Pipper.”

“Piano… Pipper,” Mitsuko repeated.

“She’s been chasing me for weeks now ever since that one drunken night. Quick! My new friend, I must request a favor of your services.”

“What sort of favor?”

“Ummm….” The wizard tugged his beard as his eyes darted around the skiff. “Oh! I have a crate of bananas over there. I need you to say you’re delivering bananas to Wizard Zeaqu.”

“Who do I say that to?”

“Whoever asks.” The elderly man crawled over to the crate and dragged it over. “Bibbly boff, bibbly boof,” he muttered. Then he tapped the side of the crate with his walking stick and it opened up, allowing a hundred bushels of bananas to pour out. “I’m counting on you!”

Mitsuko watched as the wizard crawled into the crate and it sealed around him.

Sighing, she stood up. And then nearly fell over in surprise. The woman, Piano Pipper, was staring with unnaturally wide eyes at Mitsuko.

Mitsuko raised a hand and offered a weak wave. The woman continued to stare, not blinking once.

The skiff continued along the coast until it reached a dock.

A human woman wearing the Sailor’s Guild uniform looked Mitsuko up and down.

“Welcome to Amber Island. State your business.” Unlike the two wizards Mitsuko had interacted with so far, this woman thankfully seemed normal.

“I’m supposed to deliver that crate of bananas to Wizard Zeaqu.”

“Why are the bananas spilled across the… you know what? I don’t care. You’re on one of the special boats and that wizard is, indeed, supposed to receive a crate of bananas. Here, can you sign this?” She thrust a clipboard with a paper that was filled top to bottom with lines of text. Mitsuko was by no means illiterate, but this went far beyond her comprehension. If in Hon, this would have been exactly the sort of document she would hand off to Wan for review.

Normally, Mitsuko might have some trepidation about signing a massive, near indecipherable contract. But the benefit of the timeloop was that nothing was legally binding.

She started to scratch her name on the line at the bottom but paused halfway through as a dangerous thought popped into her head. Perhaps some things could still be legally binding.

“Does this bond to my soul?” she asked, gesturing at the clipboard.

“No.” The woman sighed and her shoulders fell. “There likely is someone on this island who specializes in soul binding contracts. So I suppose the question is fair. But I work for the Sailor’s Guild, not Amber Island itself. This contract was written up by the guild. It mostly just wants to confirm you’re not here to harm any residents or expose the secrets of the island to the outside world. If you break your word then you’re liable to face the wrath of not only Amber Island, but the guild as a whole. Since we have world-wide influence, it’s best not to break any of the island’s rules.”

“No worries there. I’ll be on my best behavior. Don’t want to cause any ripples.” At least, not this loop. But nobody needed to know that except her. Mitsuko finished her signature and handed the paper to the guild woman. Then she stepped off the skiff.

“Welcome to Amber Island!”
