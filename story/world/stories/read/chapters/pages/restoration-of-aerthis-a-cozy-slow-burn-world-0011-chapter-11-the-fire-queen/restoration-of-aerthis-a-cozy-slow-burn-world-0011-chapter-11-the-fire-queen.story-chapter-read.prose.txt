Light crept across the ceiling, pale and slow. Elynara blinked, eyes unfocused, letting the shapes blur until the beams overhead looked like rivers of soft gold.

She let out a breath, rolling onto her back. The quilt tangled around her legs as she stretched, toes flexing until a quiet pop eased the tension in her calves.

Her arms felt heavy, but not in a bad way. It was just the weight of yesterday’s training letting itself be known, a reminder that progress had a price.

Starpuff hovered into view upside down, its glow blinking in the half-light before it dropped, landing squarely on her nose.

“Mmmpf–”

She scrunched her face, crossing her eyes to look at the fuzzy green menace.

“Seriously?”

It trilled, unbothered, then zipped to the foot of the bed, dancing in a lazy circle.

A small laugh slipped out of her mouth at its antics.

“Yeah, yeah. Good morning to you too.”

She sat up, pushing back hair as it fell into her eyes.

Staying like that for a while longer, she decided to start her day with a bit of thinking. She bought her notebook for a reason after all!

She quickly liberated it from its resting place, and her trusty pencil with it. For a moment she wasn’t sure what to begin with, but organizing her thoughts was as good a place to start as any.

Flipping the notebook open, she tapped the pencil once against the page with her personal notes before writing:

What I can do now:

Using the staff for mana shaping is nearly effortless now.

My fire spell is stable, lands where it should, with decent power. Reliable and strong. Holding it on the ready still drains mana steadily, needs training, I think.

Sense faint mana flows near objects and trees.

Can dismantle Bonefangs for basic materials. Will have to go buy some tools for that though.

She paused, letting the morning sounds trickle in. A few birds chirping from afar, and a distant voice calling out in the hall, a soft clatter of pots, the muted rustle of wind against the window.

Her eyes wandered to Starpuff who was still lazily fumbling around the bed, content to snuggle into the pillow. She wasn’t even sure if her little spirit needed to sleep, but it was certainly content to laze about as much as it was happy to cause mischief around.

The pencil moved again.

The things I have learned:

Bonefangs can be handled with some planning and clear shots.

Planning helps, but steady practice is equally important.

Mana regenerates, but slowly, and with the growing pool, waiting around won’t work for long. Active meditation helps a lot.

Killing one monster isn’t the same as surviving many, so I’ll limit quests for now.

She scratched at the back of her head, hair sticking up where it had flattened in sleep.

Starpuff hovered closer, circling the tip of her pencil, as if reading over her shoulder.

“Nosy.” she muttered, though a small smile tugged at the corner of her mouth.

She turned to a fresh line.

Limits:

I only know one offensive spell so far.

My mana pool is still quite small. A few spells and I would be out.

Recovery takes time, so running out at the wrong time remains an issue.

Leaving town alone for larger expeditions would be reckless to do still.

Her lips pressed together for a moment as she reread the last line, then let her shoulders drop.

“Not forever, though.”

She was certain of that.

Letting the quiet settle in, her gaze drifted to the window. Light edged over the rooftops, slow and steady, the same way it had in… somewhere else, a place she couldn’t name but could almost feel.

Mornings were different here. Cooler air, the scent of woodsmoke and damp earth, mana humming faintly at the edge of her senses. But the way the light painted the world gold, that felt the same.

Memories slipped in sometimes. Flashes of a sky she used to watch, laughter without faces, warmth without names. They came and went like half-remembered dreams, and most days she let them pass.

It was easier to look forward than backward, especially here, in a place that gave her space to grow, to learn who she was becoming, not just who she had been.

Her mind wandered to the lovely people she had met here: Father Alaric’s quiet patience, Alicia’s sharp practicality, Josh’s easy grin, Mina’s calm presence as Pufflet curled around her shoulders. People who felt real, solid, like anchors she hadn’t known she needed.

And then there was Starpuff, bobbing in the air like a small, smug promise that she wasn’t alone. That she could still find small wonders, even on mornings like this.

A faint glow brushed her cheek. Starpuff bobbed once, as if in agreement, then darted up to spin near the ceiling beam, flickering like a particularly fuzzy firefly.

She shook her head, setting the pencil down.

Closing the notebook, she hugged it to her chest for a breath before tucking it into her satchel. The leather’s familiar weight felt reassuring.

The light through the window was stronger now, painting soft gold across the wooden floor. Elynara swung her legs off the bed, feet finding the cool boards as she stood.

She stretched again, reaching toward the ceiling until her spine cracked, letting out a soft, satisfied “ah” as she dropped her arms.

“Today will be a good day.”

Starpuff zipped past her ear, trailing a sparkle of light that faded into the morning.

Yeah.

Today would be a good day.

The scent of warm bread and lightly spiced porridge drifted through the hallway as Elynara stepped out, the cool air of her room replaced by the soft hush of the church waking around her.

She moved quietly, fingers brushing along the edge of her satchel as it tapped against her hip with each step, the boards beneath her feet creaking in the familiar way they did each morning.

At the end of the hall, near the dining entrance, Father Alaric was bent slightly, gently checking the bandage on a small boy’s hand. The child sniffled but didn’t pull away, wide eyes fixed on the careful, practiced movements.

Hesitating, Elynara paused as the warm memory of the gentle pat on her head and the quiet praise from yesterday arose, cheeks prickling with heat. She shifted her grip on the strap of her satchel before dipping her head in a small, careful bow.

“Good morning, Father.”

Alaric glanced up, and his smile came immediately, lines near his eyes catching in the soft morning light.

“Good morning, Elynara.”

Her flush deepened, and she quickly dipped her head again, slipping past with a polite nod before the warmth could spread further.

The dining hall was already alive with small sounds of spoons clinking and soft chatter. The morning hush too fresh for the later, louder chaos the children would bring.

Mina sat at one of the long tables, her wooden bowl steaming, Pufflet curled around her feet like a fluffy, overgrown slipper, tail twitching lightly with each breath.

Elynara took her own bowl of porridge, added a drizzle of honey, the scent rising sweet and warm. She moved toward Mina’s table and, after only a breath of hesitation, took a seat.

Mina looked up, a half-smirk playing at the edges of her mouth.

“Morning, Fire Queen.”

Elynara let out a groan, sinking into her seat.

“Please don’t call me that…”

A soft snicker escaped Mina as she returned to her bowl, while at her feet, Pufflet’s ears perked, nose twitching at the sound. It uncurled and stood with a stretch that gave the impression it was a fluffy cloud that came to life. The nippy creature cautiously sniffed at Elynara's boots with curiosity as it waddled over.

Above her shoulder, Starpuff flickered into view, a faint pulse of green light like a smug lantern. It hovered, leaning closer as if inspecting Pufflet with exaggerated seriousness.

Elynara raised an eyebrow.

“Behave.”

Of course, Starpuff did not. It dipped lower, drifting nose to nose with Pufflet before giving a mischievous little nudge.

Pufflet sneezed, the sound small but sharp, stumbling back with a startled squeak before shaking its head, ears flopping in protest.

Mina choked on a laugh, covering her mouth with the back of her hand as her shoulders shook.

Elynara rolled her eyes but couldn’t stop the small laugh that bubbled up, the sound catching softly between them before fading into the quiet of the morning.

They ate in comfortable silence, the clink of their spoons against wood steady and grounding.

Between bites, Mina glanced up.

“I’m helping Sister Lyra with laundry later.”

She made a face.

“Pufflet hates the steam.”

Elynara swallowed, giving a small nod.

“I’ve got errands for the guild today. Just simple runs, nothing exciting.”

“Guild errands, soo~ grown-up.”

Elynara poked at her porridge, lips tugging upward.

“Better than moping around.”

“Mm.”

Mina hummed, but there was a soft smile hidden there.

Just as they chatted, Pufflet had returned to Mina’s side, leaning heavily against her leg while sniffing the air with quiet suspicion. While Starpuff, entirely unfazed, bobbed above Elynara’s shoulder, drifting in small circles as if proudly declaring victory.

Mina’s laughter came again, softer now, as she bent to scoop Pufflet into her arms, the creature’s fluffy tail curling against her wrist.

“Come on, you silly thing.”

After wishing Mina a good day, Elynara soon scraped the last bit of honey-slicked porridge from her bowl, letting the warmth settle in her chest as she stood to rinse it at the side basin.

Her fingers brushed the wood of the table lightly before she left, tracing a line in it with a satisfied hum.

The day waited, and she had errands to run, tasks to complete.

But for a moment, the morning warmth of the church, the scent of bread and honey, and the small laughter of a friend made the world feel gentle.

After breakfast, Elynara stepped into the quiet hall, the warmth of porridge still lingering faintly on her breath. As she turned toward the small wooden door at the end of the hall, her fingers brushed the strap of her satchel, feeling the notebook's corner lightly bump against her hip.

The library greeted her with its usual hush, but it felt different in the morning light. The sun streamed through the narrow windows, falling in bright, soft lines that made the dust motes dance above the shelves, turning them into drifting sparks.

She paused for a moment, letting her eyes adjust, letting the scent of paper and old wood settle around her. Starpuff drifted in beside her, flickering gently before perching on a shelf, its glow a quiet pulse in the warm air.

It felt peaceful here.

She stepped forward, boots making the softest thump against the boards, and glanced at the nun seated at the desk near the front. The woman looked up, adjusting her spectacles, her eyes curious but kind.

Elynara hesitated, shifting her weight, then lifted her notebook.

“I was wondering…” She cleared her throat.

“Do you have any recommendations? For someone trying to learn about the world, I mean.”

The nun’s brows lifted, her expression warming into a soft, approving smile.

“First time asking for help, hmm? That’s good. We don’t know what we don’t know.”

Elynara ducked her head, cheeks warm.

“I’ve been reading some of the travelogues, and found an old map, and… it’s a lot.”

The nun nodded once, then gestured toward a corner shelf.

“There. Local history, old guild reports, some odd collections. The ledgers are strange, but sometimes they have notes about herbs or creatures. It’s a place to start.”

“Thank you.”

She moved toward the indicated shelf, Starpuff following in a lazy spiral above her head, pausing to peek between the stacks as if searching for trouble - or snacks.

Elynara picked a few titles that seemed interesting, but before starting on them she first pulled down the travelogue she had started before.

She settled onto the low stool in the sunlight, flipping to the marked pages. Her eyes scanned mentions of slimes, Bristlebacks, and the Crooktail lynx, her pencil tapping lightly against the margins as she cross-checked notes, before continuing her reading.

There wasn’t much left of it, and she didn’t note out anything new either. Still, with that one done with she could start on the next with clear conscience!

Deciding to start with the newest looking one out of those she picked, she was soon engrossed in her reading again.

She made a small, thoughtful noise, Starpuff swooping down to hover near the page, its glow brightening as if demanding a share of her attention.

“Yeah, it’s confusing,” she murmured, tapping the book. “Even for you.”

Starpuff let out a soft, warbling hum before drifting over to the next shelf, nosing at a dangling ribbon bookmark.

She flipped to another section, skimming a passage where someone had scrawled in the margin:

Mana abundance once allowed even simple folk to work minor cantrips and spells. Now, even the smallest spells are a luxury.

Her pencil hovered, then carefully wrote the line into her notebook. She stared at it for a moment, letting the meaning sink in.

Books were valuable. The knowledge in them was real, or at least, it had been. But dates scribbled in corners showed how many decades old these notes were, how many pages were copies of copies, filled with cautious handwriting and smudged ink.

A world that had been shifting, leaving the words behind.

She flipped between pages, eyes moving from sketches of herbs she recognized to creatures she hadn’t seen yet, to vague warnings of corrupted places she had only heard rumors about at the guild.

Even the newest pages felt like fragments of something bigger, stories waiting to be tested outside these walls.

Her pencil scratched across her notes, her letters small, deliberate.

Books don’t agree. Some of this is likely wrong. Some are just old and no longer relevant. And quite a lot is missing.

She paused, letting the quiet fill the space between her thoughts, the warm sunlight resting on her hands, the air smelling of paper and dust.

While she didn’t write it down, there was probably a lot more she couldn’t even tell was missing. But this was a small frontier town, and just the start of her life here.

She would learn and find the missing pieces, definitely!

A whisper escaped her lips, a grin pulling at the corner of her mouth.

“Knowledge is scattered. Test everything.”

Starpuff swooped down at that, giving her a gentle flick against the ear before darting away to circle the lantern, pulsing like it was laughing too.

Elynara closed the book in her lap, hugging it for a moment before rising. She moved carefully, returning each book to its shelf, letting her fingers linger on the warm wood before stepping away.

The nun glanced up as she passed, and Elynara offered a small, quiet smile.

“Thank you.”

The nun nodded, approving, before returning to her ledger.

Elynara stepped outside, closing the door softly behind her. The morning had warmed, the air bright with promise, the world alive in the light as she let her breath out, steady.

There was still so much to learn. So much to test and practice.

And she would.

But for now, she had stuff to do, and a trip to what has become her workplace as of late.

The guild sign creaked softly above her as Elynara stepped inside, the warm breeze brushing past her back before the door closed.

The hall felt calm, holding that late-morning hush where voices carried easily, snippets of conversation about road conditions, a passing mention of wolves near the northern fence, the soft clink of coin as a request was finalized.

The scent of paper, ink, and the faint tang of oiled leather settled around her, grounding in its familiarity.

Alicia was at the desk, head bent slightly as her quill tapped against a fresh stack of forms. She moved with quick, precise motions, the pages flipping with practiced ease, the faint scratch of ink a steady counterpoint to the quiet conversations around them.

Elynara approached, pausing to scan the small quest postings pinned along the quest board. She plucked one free, reading it over - a small delivery to the tannery, an errand simple enough to complete before noon.

She stepped forward, offering it to Alicia without interrupting the quiet rhythm of the guild.

Alicia took the slip, eyes scanning the text before she nodded, reaching for the small guild stamp and pressing it down with a clean thump that left a neat mark on the corner.

“Accepted.”

Alicia said, her voice calm, moving to file the slip into a thin ledger.

Elynara lingered, tucking the approved request into her satchel. She glanced around; the hall felt almost empty aside from a pair of adventurers quietly sorting gear near the window and a runner resting on a bench, head tilted back as he dozed.

But Alicia kept moving, sorting a new stack of forms with steady efficiency, quill flicking, paper rustling in tidy stacks.

Elynara tilted her head, curiosity bubbling up despite herself.

“You’re always busy, even when there aren’t that many people here.”

Alicia let out a soft snort, not bothering to glance up.

“Because there are many people. Just not here.”

She stamped a form, the mark clean and final.

“Most adventurers don’t linger unless they need something. They’re out on the roads, in fields, or sending reports from further settlements. I handle quest data, supply forms, runner logs, and communications between towns.”

Elynara’s eyebrows rose slightly.

“Communications? Between towns? The nearest is beyond the wasteland to the south, isn’t it?”

That earned her a pause. Alicia looked up, the faintest edge of pride flickering across her expression before it settled back into calm professionalism.

“We use something called Guild Communication Tablets,” she said, setting the papers down for a moment.

Elynara’s interest sparked, leaning in slightly.

“Guild Communication Tablets?”

“They’re Ancient Era artifacts. Guild halls across the world have them, or used to. We use them for emergencies, urgent updates, or coordination when needed.”

Her eyes returned to the papers, flipping to the next page, quill in hand.

“They’re costly to operate. We can’t waste them on small updates about broken cart wheels or minor herb runs.”

Elynara’s gaze drifted toward the back rooms of the guild, picturing a rune-etched slab resting in the dark, pulsing softly with faint mana whenever a message arrived from somewhere far beyond Oltenar.

Alicia caught her look and smirked faintly, one eyebrow lifting.

“Don’t get your hopes up about seeing it. Unless something goes terribly wrong, it stays put.”

A small laugh escaped Elynara before she could stop it. She adjusted her satchel, nodding once.

“Got it. Thanks.”

Alicia was already turning back to the next stack, quill tapping once against her thumb before she continued writing.

Elynara stepped out of the guild, the door closing softly behind her. The breeze caught at her hair, the morning bright and alive as she tucked her quest slip deeper into her satchel.

Adding it to the quiet list of things to research when she found the right book or person who was willing to elaborate, she made a small mental note - Guild Communication Tablets: Ancient Era, emergencies only.

For now, there was a delivery to make.

And the day was still young.

Quite a bit of time had passed by the time she finished her work, which involved a slightly more complicated but still manageable "simple delivery".

All that running around put her into a mood for some snacks, so a market trip it was.

With no reason to hurry, Elynara enjoyed the warm afternoon air, carrying the scent of smoke, sizzling meat, and sharp herbs drifting between the busy rows of stalls.

She walked at a slow pace, letting the sounds of the market’s clattering pans, haggling voices, and children’s laughter fill the space around her.

Spices pricked at her nose, teasing memories she couldn’t fully place. Fleeting flashes of dishes layered with flavor, steam rising from bowls, warmth against her palms. Simple food, wondrous food, both distant now. Here, it was hearty fare first, filling bellies before anything else.

Her steps slowed near a stall where skewers turned over glowing coals, their juices hissing softly as the vendor brushed on a thin glaze. She watched the steam curl, the smell pulling a quiet sigh from her chest before she could help it.

She bought two, passing over a few coins, letting the warmth seep into her fingers as she took a slow breath of the smoky air.

With salt, a hint of pepper, and a spice she couldn't quite place, the first bite was just as satisfying as she had hoped. The char at the edges gave way to tender meat that loosened something tense in her shoulders.

“You’re going to pay for two but eat them both yourself, huh?”

She froze mid-chew, jaw tightening before she turned, leveling a flat look at the source of the voice.

Josh stood there, arms folded lazily across his chest, that familiar smirk tugging at his lips as he raised an eyebrow.

“Figures. Little miss mage too fancy to share–”

She narrowed her eyes, then, without a word, shoved the second skewer straight into his mouth, cutting him off mid-breath.

He blinked, eyes crossing for a moment, before the skewer shifted and he managed a bite.

Elynara leaned in, voice sweet, sharp as a blade.

“Gosh, Mister Josh, you sure know how to treat a lady.”

He froze, blinking again, before a startled laugh escaped him, muffled around the food.

“Yeah, yeah,” he managed, chewing as the skewer vendor chuckled, calling out over the counter.

“She got you good, boy.”

Josh gave a mock glare, but the corner of his mouth twitched upward as he took another bite.

They walked a few steps, the market sounds pressing gently around them, bits of conversation floating past as Elynara’s eyes drifted over a table of bright peppers, another stall selling thin cuts of smoked fish, a basket of crisp apples stacked under a faded cloth.

From over her shoulder, Starpuff darted forward, drifting toward a hanging strand of dried herbs. Its nose, or whatever it had, nearly brushed the leaves while circling in tight loops. Almost like it was inspecting the smells before zipping back.

“Don’t think I didn’t see that.” Elynara muttered, clicking her tongue softly.

Josh glanced sideways, chewing the last of his skewer, brow raised.

“You really do talk to ghosts, huh?”

She shot him a look, but he just grinned, wiping his hand on his trousers.

“Hey, no judgment. Just saying, you’ll scare the merchants talking to air like that.”

Starpuff flickered once in response, darting a short distance away before coming back to hover near her shoulder, a faint green glow that seemed almost smug.

Josh let out a short huff of a laugh, clearly amused at her incredulous face.

“You know… That spirit of yours reminds me of the old stories you sometimes hear from the forest peoples.”

She glanced at him with quiet curiosity.

“Some families out by the farms still leave out offerings. Bits of bread, milk, hoping the spirits notice. Supposed to help with harvest and all that.”

He rolled his shoulders, flicking the skewer stick into a barrel as they passed.

“If you really see them, you might find people wanting you to check if theirs are still around.”

Elynara took the last bite of her skewer, chewing slowly as she considered it. She nodded once, brushing her fingers clean.

“Thanks. I’ll think about it.”

Josh shrugged, lifting a hand to make a small circling gesture near his temple, smirking.

“Don’t let it go to your head.”

She rolled her eyes, shifting her satchel against her side as she turned away, but a small, quiet smile tugged at the edge of her lips.

Starpuff floated in close, following her.

It circled once, then settled near her shoulder as the noise of the market carried on, warm and alive as they stepped back into the flow of the street.

By the time Elynara made her way back, the streets had begun to quiet, the sky softening into the warm hues of late afternoon. She paused near the church gates, brushing ash from her fingers where the skewer had burned low, letting the last traces of the market fade from mind.

Inside, the scent of baking bread and simmering herbs met her like an old, gentle promise. Reminding her tummy that a certain pesky somebody took her second skewer away.

Huffing, she rolled her shoulders once, adjusting her grip on her staff before stepping fully into the familiar calm of the church, ready to start on her daily chores.

Late afternoon light angled through the tall windows, catching on floating dust motes as Elynara swept the hallway. The soft scrape of the broom was a comforting sound, mixing with the low hum of voices from the kitchen and the warm scent of herbs and bread drifting through the air.

She moved to the tables next, wiping down their worn surfaces until they shone softly in the light. A small stack of laundry waited in the corner; she folded each piece carefully, smoothing the fabric with her palms before placing it in neat piles for the nuns to collect.

By the time she was setting the last folded cloth aside, her shoulders rolled with some satisfaction. It wasn’t grand, but it was work worth doing.

A muffled giggle broke the calm, followed by another, then the sound of quick, soft footsteps.

She turned just in time to see a group of children peeking around the doorway, eyes wide and bright. One, bolder than the rest, stepped forward, chin lifted.

“You promised to show us magic!”

Elynara blinked, tucking a stray strand of hair behind her ear, her shoulders dropping as she sighed.

“I did, didn’t I…”

Before she could add anything else, small hands grabbed at her sleeves, tugging insistently, the soft patter of feet echoing as they pulled her along. She let herself be dragged, boots scuffing lightly against the floor, out into the small courtyard behind the church.

They spilled out into the open air, the children forming a loose semicircle, eyes fixed on her with unblinking excitement.

She held up her hands, shifting her staff lightly to the side.

“Stay back,” she said, voice firm but gentle, “and no getting too close. Understand?”

They nodded, some bobbing eagerly, others clutching each other’s sleeves, trying to hush their giggles.

Elynara took a breath, grounding herself as she felt for the mana in her core, letting it slide into her palm in a slow, measured stream. It was familiar now, the warmth under her skin, the soft hum only she could feel.

At the staff’s tip, a small flame flickered to life, steady and warm, swaying like a candle in a gentle breeze.

The children gasped, one whispering.

“She’s really doing it…”

She kept it small, shifting her wrist so the flame moved in a gentle arc, dancing back and forth in the air as she guided it carefully.

A few claps rang out, tiny hands coming together, eyes wide with awe. A giggle slipped through the group, quickly hushed, only to spark another ripple of laughter.

Elynara rolled her wrist, letting the flame sway in a playful rhythm before pretending to toss it lightly in the air. It hovered for a breath before she caught it again, moving as if she were juggling, the flame splitting for a heartbeat into smaller sparks that danced before fading away.

A series of soft “woah”s rippled through the children as she repeated the motion, leaving behind tiny, short-lived orbs of flame that flickered out on their own, harmless against the dirt.

Finally, she closed her hand around the flame, the warmth folding in on itself before vanishing cleanly.

“That’s all for today,” she said, lowering her staff. “Magic isn’t a toy, okay?”

They groaned in chorus, but their faces were bright, some already turning to run off, voices rising in excited chatter as they rushed to tell the others.

She let out a breath she hadn’t realized she’d been holding, the courtyard quiet again except for the faint rustle of the breeze.

A small warmth settled in her chest as she turned, stepping back inside to help finish preparations for the evening meal, the comforting hum of work waiting for her once more.

After helping serve the evening meal, Elynara stayed behind to clear the tables. The bowls stacked neatly under her hands, the soft clink of wood against wood oddly comforting as the voices around her settled into the low, tired hush of the day winding down.

She was halfway to slipping out when small hands grabbed at her sleeves.

“Play with us, play with us, Miss Elynara!”

She stopped, blinking as a half-dozen eager faces once again stared up at her, eyes bright, bouncing on their toes.

She let out a long breath, pinching the bridge of her nose.

“Only for a little.”

They didn’t need to be told twice, dragging her outside into the soft dusk air, the warm scent of earth and grass meeting her as the last of the light clung to the edges of the courtyard.

They ran around her, laughing, shrieking with delight as she pointed her staff and let innocuous flickers of mana spark harmlessly in the air, small bursts of warm light that faded before they touched the ground.

She turned slowly in place, pretending to dodge as the children darted by, calling out spells that didn’t exist while they tried to “catch” her.

It didn’t take long for their energy to outpace hers. After ten minutes, she raised a hand, calling.

“Time’s up! Go on, it’s time to gather for sleep.”

They groaned in unison, but she shooed them away with a firm wave, watching as they scattered, giggling, back toward the church.

She stood alone for a moment, staff braced lightly against the ground, catching her breath, before moving on to another part of her daily routine she was getting intimately familiar with.

By the time she moved to her usual corner near the outer wall, the shadows were already getting quite long, and even the stars began to peek through the indigo sky.

The staff felt solid in her grip as she settled onto the bench, rolling her shoulders back.

Mana capacity training. Just one more push before the day ended.

Slowly she drew in mana, allowing it to fill her core. Holding on until it pushed against the limits of her capacity, she then released it in a steady, controlled flow. One cycle.

Then again. And again.

She kept count in her mind, each cycle sliding into the next with a comforting rhythm. It was easier, somehow, under the stars. The world was quiet, her focus narrow.

It was almost relaxing.

Until it wasn’t.

At cycle twenty-three, her breath caught, sweat beading on her forehead. Her arms trembled as she gripped the staff tighter, pulse pounding in her ears as the mana pressed against her core, refusing to settle as easily as before.

She forced herself through two more cycles, jaw tight, until her vision blurred and she let out a sharp exhale, dropping onto the bench with a soft thump.

The world tilted as she closed her eyes, letting her breath slow, the ache in her limbs pulsing with each heartbeat until it faded into a dull, heavy calm.

Back in her room, she pulled off her boots, letting them drop softly to the floor as she leaned her staff against the wall. The small space felt cooler now, the air settling as she sat on the edge of her bed, pulling her notebook from her satchel.

She flipped to her mana training log, the pages marked with a mixture of tidy and messy lines of dates, numbers, and small notes. Her pencil hovered for a breath before she wrote clearly at the bottom:

New total: 41.

She stared at the number, frowning, mind moving despite the heaviness in her limbs.

She traced a finger along earlier notes, eyes flicking across each increase she recorded - and whatever else she could remember from her earlier practice.

It wasn’t obvious at first, but at twenty-five mana, it took about five cycles per increase.

At thirty-six, it was six cycles per.

Before that… she couldn’t remember exactly, but it had been faster. Easier.

She noticed a pattern there, her brow furrowing.

Each increase was taking more cycles, roughly following the square root of her current capacity, rounded down. Pausing mid-training and not resuming quickly enough seemed to break the pattern. She distinctly remembered having to start over after falling asleep before finishing.

But it wasn’t all bad. Each bit of practice made the next cycle a little easier, the mana moving a little smoother.

She closed the notebook firmly, sliding it under her pillow before laying back on the bed, pulling the thin blanket over her chest as she let out a slow, exhausted breath.

The weight in her limbs wasn’t unpleasant. It was proof of progress.

Her eyes drifted shut, her mind replaying the flickers of flame in the courtyard, the quiet cycles of mana under the stars, the number forty-one etched in her thoughts.

Next…

Sleep pulled her under before she could finish the thought.
