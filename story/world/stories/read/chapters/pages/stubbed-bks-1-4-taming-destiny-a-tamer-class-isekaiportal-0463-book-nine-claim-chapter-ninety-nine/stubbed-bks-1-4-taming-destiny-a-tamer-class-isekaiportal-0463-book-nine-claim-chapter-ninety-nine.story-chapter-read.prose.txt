For a moment, we’re frozen, then all hell breaks loose. Spells wash over the space around Layton, forcing the beast that’s just attacked him to show itself. Shock pulses through me – I recognise the humanoid creature with spider-like mandibles and webbed ears.

So does Bastet.

The original territory lord! she exclaims, then leaps forwards. You shall not hurt my pack! she growls loudly. At her tail, the male larnatis hesitates for a moment, then surges in her wake.

I fully agree with her sentiments, but my focus is on Layton. The Tier three has dropped him to the ground to free up its claws to defend itself from everyone now surging to attack it.

Not wanting my ally to be trampled, I grab him and pull him out.

He’s alive, but the wounds are serious, even for a Classer. The claws pierced him from back to front – they must have damaged his lungs as he’s barely able to breathe. Not only that, but they’re poisoned – as I suspected in our first meeting, the Tier three indeed has venom, and not only in its mandibles. As a primarily mage Classer, I doubt Layton has put that many points into Constitution – certainly, his body is quickly losing the fight against the injury and venom.

I reach for a healing potion, then swear as I realise that I gave my last one to Thiakar earlier.

Pressing my magic into Layton’s body, I meet with far too much resistance to be able to heal him in time.

I look wildly around. We didn’t bring a dedicated healer, but there are several Warriors here with Lay-on-Hands or similar Skills. However, Murphy’s law strikes again and I can’t see any of them close at hand. The Tier three is defending itself with a reckless viciousness that I wouldn’t have expected from it based on our last meeting and everyone is already flagging from the other battles we’ve fought today. This is the last thing we needed.

Damn it.

I bite the bullet.

“Layton, open your eyes.”

No response, only a weak panting that’s getting weaker every moment.

Doing my best to be as gentle as I can, I force one of his eyelids open, then trigger my Skill.

Layton’s Willpower is far stronger than Thiakar’s was a moment ago, but it’s fluctuating as his image on the other side flickers. I swear again, knowing what that means.

Markus? he asks weakly as I press my way through, taking advantage of the moments it weakens, but continuing to press even when it’s strong – he might be stronger than Thiakar, but he’s still much weaker than I am.

“You’re dying Layton,” I tell him bluntly, “and I can’t get hold of a healer. Your only hope is me, and I need a Dominate Bond to be able to heal you fast enough.”

I press my way forwards to stand right in front of him. He flickers out and ice grips my heart.

Then he reappears and bone-melting relief goes through me.

“Please, please give me permission,” I practically beg.

Will you force it if I don’t? Layton asks weakly.

Before I can answer, he flickers out again, and when he doesn’t reappear, that icy grip returns.

In that moment, I decide that I will Bind him if – when – he reappears, agreement or no agreement. I’ve already committed a crime worthy of execution today – Binding a free citizen of Moriax without his permission. Why not add a noble to the list? Layton can do what he likes afterwards – at least he’ll be alive to do it.

I almost sigh in relief as he flickers back into place.

Fine, I agree, I hear just as I trigger the Bond.

The moment the real world reforms around me, I dive into Layton’s body, not economising my mana in the slightest as I pour it into the wound, forcing the blood to remain in his body and continue circulating oxygen to his critical organs while limiting the spread of the hemotoxic venom. I spare a single hand to grab a mana potion, ignoring the sensation of weakness potion poisoning gives me. I’ll live. Layton won’t if I don’t do this.

Seconds pass and Layton hovers on the edge between life and death.

Then, finally, I start making progress.

Perhaps three minutes later, I sit back on my heels, feeling utterly spent, like everything has been drained out of me and I’m nothing but a husk. But Layton is stable. Not entirely healed, but I’ve managed to pull all the venom out of him and deal with the worst of the impalement.

Wearily, I look at the battle that still rages on with the Tier three. That, too, is reaching its end – and fortunately so since some of the others in the fight look almost as bad as I feel.

One of the Warriors with Lay-on-Hands is actually off to the side, treating three people whose shields obviously failed at the wrong moment. Where were you when I needed you? I grumble mentally, though I don’t truly blame them. They’re warriors first, healers last, after all.

And I guess that he has only taken the time off because the battle is decided – the Tier three has lost both mandibles, one arm, and is covered in its own blood from a myriad of painful-looking cuts and slashes. I’m honestly surprised that it’s still fighting. Tier threes normally have more self-preservation than this.

Then I frown, forcing my head to clear – potion poisoning is awful. I consider taking a clearing potion, but then decide not to. As long as I don’t take any more potions, the effects should start reducing soon. Unfortunately, they’re not something I can clear from my bloodstream with magic.

I shake my head, my thoughts are wandering. Focus. I was thinking something. About the Tier three.

Then I blink as I put the pieces together.

If I weren’t feeling so bad, I’d have leaped to my feet. As is, it’s more of a stagger.

Glancing down at Layton, I hesitate, then carefully bend down and pull him into my spatial tattoo too – he’s unconscious and defenceless right now. And, even though the Tier three finally crumples to the ground, if my guess is right, there may be more enemies.

“Scout Talis,” I call in a voice that’s weaker than I’d like it. Nonetheless, the scout has excellent hearing.

“Lord Markus?” she responds, hurrying to my side.

“The device!” I urge her.

She blinks in confusion.

“The device, my lord? Which device?”

I wave my hand in the air, almost overbalancing myself in the process.

“The instrument that tracks Tier four signatures.”

She blinks again, then her eyes widen, horror creeping over her expression as she correctly interprets my implications.

Pulling out the device, she fiddles with the dials a moment, then I see the lines around her mouth tighten.

“You’re right, my lord. The Tier four is still alive.”

Curses ring out from those who have gathered around us – almost everyone.

I close my eyes for a moment, my own curses silent. Clever, I compliment the Tier four grimly. Explode, making us think you’re dead, then use the Tier three as a distraction to make your escape.

Then, opening my eyes again, I fix them on the scout.

“Can you track it?”

She nods firmly.

“It’s concealing its presence, but this tracker is perfectly calibrated to its signature now. It won’t get away.”

Talis quickly leads us out of the chamber. I pause briefly on the threshold, looking back at the dead Tier three with a little regret. If I’d known it was controlled….

Well, perhaps it’s for the best. I’d already warned it once and even if I would have made allowances for it being controlled, there’s no guarantee it wouldn’t have taken that mercy as a sign of weakness.

We follow the Tier four through the tunnels. It’s fast, but Talis assure us we’re slowly catching up, especially as it keeps needing to double back on its routes. It’s clearly searching for an exit, and I’m increasingly glad that I had the forethought to use the Earth Mages. From what Sirocco reported back from them, they have blocked off every exit they’ve found. Unless they’ve missed one somewhere, the only exit should be the tunnel through which we entered.

At that thought, I smile.

“Let’s set up a little trap, shall we?”

Half our party – led by Talis – continues pursuing the Tier four through the tunnels, not allowing it to rest or regain any of its strength. The rest of us head to the entrance where we spend the time recovering. I even manage to level up to thirty-five, putting all my points into Willpower as usual and washing away the minimal amount of disgusting liquid with my water-magic.

Though the Tier four has an extremely powerful stealth Skill that only the instrument tracking its very signature is able to bypass, we know where it has to go to get out – and magic allows us to create a trap it’s unable to avoid.

As it zips across the magical tripline, the trap activates instantly, a hemi-spherical shield springing up from the stone floor of the tunnel. Dropping its stealth, the Tier four, now only the size of a tennis ball, writhes around, slamming against the walls of the shield. But its strengths were never in the physical, and with it so reduced, it has little effect.

I see it pause, and then its domain explodes out of it. We take an involuntary step back as the wave crashes over us, but quickly regain our equilibrium – it’s only a pale echo of the power that we fought against earlier.

“Layton, would you like to do the honours?” I invite. Since he almost died to this creature’s final gambit, I thought he might appreciate being its end.

Pale and weak, but with a mask of concentration on his face, Layton steps forward. For a minute, nothing seems to happen. Then I see the air inside the bubble shimmer and the Tier four becomes increasingly agitated.

It starts throwing itself against the shimmering walls again, its movements more and more frantic.

And then, suddenly, it loses all cohesion. Its slime melts away from its body – a mess of organs almost identical to the ones I found in the parasites, only a little more complex. The organs start steaming too, then shrivel up to a fraction of their size. Finally, the shrivelled bits of flesh begin burning with a low flame, blackening into charcoal.

A rush of warmth goes through me and I briefly pull up my status screen to see that my Energy pool has already increased almost halfway to level thirty-six – this Tier four is obviously worth a lot more than even the Wind Roc. Or perhaps it’s that the system counts only those of us who fought the controller directly as deserving of Energy from its death.

Still, at least this time we have the confirmation that it’s dead. I watch the remains in curiosity as they shimmer and suddenly transform into a Core that is almost the size of the reduced parasite controller itself. I’d already suspected that the Core doesn’t become physical until the beast is killed, since I’ve never found it within the body of one of my companions, but it’s interesting to see the transition happen before my eyes.

It’s also an interesting Core – faceted in ways that I haven’t seen before. I step forward in alarm as it seems to crumble into shards before my eyes. Has the heat destroyed it? I question fearfully even as part of me is sure that can’t be the case – Cores aren’t crystals, for all they resemble them.

But as I look at the mess of small shards that now litter the floor of the trap, I realise that they’re far too regular to be accidental. A very strange Core, I conclude – one made out of many, many little ones. But it suits the beast that was most likely itself spread among thousands of beasts.

Did you kill the Tier four? Elarion asks through the Alliance Bond, distracting me from my contemplation. The beasts here have suddenly stopped moving. I’m about to answer when he continues. Wait. They’re moving again. I tense. They’re running away.

New alarm flashes through me.

Try to stop them if you can – I don’t know if the parasites are still alive inside them, or if they could cause problems later.

We’ll do our best, Elarion confirms. There are few remaining anyway.

Layton finally lowers his hand, his gaze far away from the present. He looks completely spent.

“Are you OK?” I ask him quietly, stepping closer, and placing my hand on his shoulder. He flinches, so I quickly pull it away. We no longer have a Bond – the moment I finished healing his wounds, I cut that. The same with Thiakar – he’s currently being updated by one of the fighters he’s obviously friendly with.

Layton just continues staring at the bubble with a far-away look, not responding to my question.

Bond or no Bond, I can tell that Layton is struggling with something, so I just keep him company, fixing my eyes on the shrivelled and blackened remains of the beast that’s caused so much issue. I know that we should head up to the surface – there are beasts to either kill or heal, and all the rest of the post-battle clean up to deal with. Ah. It’s one thing to know logically that my Willpower is monstrous by most people’s standards; it’s something else to experience it.
