Out we went into the plot of land we designated for our greenhouse build, the very same we had shown Vera the other day. Nadia pretty much dragged me there, while I envied Vera and her cozy computer setup on the sofa, lounging there warmed by the fire AND the heater, with Nero’s occasional company whenever the cat felt like showing up.

At least the supplies were already there, in a pile under a tarp near the house. It hadn’t rained or snowed in a while, but the morning dew made everything wet, hence the tarp.

“I’ll admit, it was a good idea of yours. Not bad, my prince,” Nadia said.

I rolled my eyes at her, grabbed her waist and kissed her. “I only have good ideas.”

“Let’s settle on often, shall we?”

“I’ll take it.”

We lifted the tarp and set to work, hauling the timber and plexiglass sheets. We got an occasional glimpse of Kimberly working on the cameras, but he was on the far side of the house so there was almost no trace of him save for when he went to his car to grab some random tool or knickknack.

Our tools, instead, were inside the shed. The strange, eerie shed that defied the laws of physics. I looked at Nadia, and she looked at me.

“He’s not around,” she whispered conspiratorially. “It’s now or never.”

We moved quickly. The less the mad scientist saw of my shed, the better. Wouldn’t want to wake up one morning and find out he had snuck in during the night to take a look at it because it had caught his interest, and now I had no shed anymore.

In and out we went, several times, gathering all we needed so that we could lock the door behind us. It was fun, I’ll admit–I felt like a spy in action.

“Practice in case we decide to take the snatch-and-grab job,” I joked.

“You make it sound like a heist.”

“You sure it isn’t one?”

She shook her head. “Wouldn’t be in the official jobs roster, silly.”

“You call me silly?” I demanded. It deserved a round of tickles.

They got express delivered, and after I turned Nadia into a puddle, I made the last trip into the shed to get the last few things. Yep, definitely too big on the inside. A shed this cluttered should have felt cramped, and it did, mind you… just not enough. Not nearly enough.

“Damn,” I cursed as a hammer fell on the ground. Clumsy.

I bent down to pick it up and felt that the floor was strangely warm. It was winter, and these were supposed to be wooden boards on concrete, and yet it was pleasantly warm to the touch. Hmm. Strange evidence stacking in.

I let go of the hammer, grabbed my phone and used the torch to see between the seams of the wooden boards. And lo and behold! Nothing.

Absolutely nothing. And not darkness-nothing. Just the regular concrete you’d expect kind of nothing. Nothing to see save for dull grey. Huh. Here I’d hoped it would reveal the shed’s secrets, but there had to be a hot water pipe or some shit running down there, the boring explanation, and nothing else.

“Whatever,” I grumbled, my breath fogging and reminding me that outside, it was winter-cold.

It was time to build.

“Took you a while,” Nadia complained.

“Did it?” I asked, hands on my hips, mirroring her posture. I stared at her, and she melted a little. “Someone just can’t wait to get building, can she?”

“Mayyyybe,” she cooed. “Maybe I like doing things with my handsome prince.”

“I don’t buy it,” I said. “I think you are doing this for the veggies and fruit.”

“Ohno! You found out!”

I chuckled. “No, really. A hammer fell and then I found a strangely hot floorboard and went to examine.”

“You and your strange conspiracies. What did you find this time?”

“Concrete.”

She laughed. “Yeah, well. What did you expect?”

I shrugged, and we moved on to the actual build. “Alright,” I said. “According to the tutorial I watched, we should begin by marking the layout…”

We did. And it was harder than the guy in the video made it look. We almost suspected he was using powers, but the video was actually rather old, and he was old too, too old to have awakened during the Onset or later.

Which meant it was us who sucked. But you know what? We did it in the end. We placed stakes on the hard, half-frozen ground and drew taut strings between them to mark the area. Now it looked like a proper construction site!

“Adrian,” Nadia decided to rain on my parade. “The diagonals don’t match. It’s not a square.”

I grumbled. “Figures…”

We fixed it, then it was time to fight the frozen soil again, this time to level the ground. And then again to anchor the base of the frame we’d build with the treated timber we bought, because of course we couldn’t use just random wood or it’d rot in no time. Nature became something like ten times more aggressive after the Onset, and untreated organic material just rotted faster than it used to.

Then… then it was getting dark, and we were all sweaty under the many layers of clothes. We had made a mess, but, we decided as we surveyed our own work under the blue light of evening, we had made good progress. Solid progress, and it was ours.

“Feels good, doesn’t it?” Nadia asked.

“Oh yeah,” I agreed.

Although now came the boring part: putting all the supplies away for the night, back in their stack against the house and under the tarp. As we did, we heard Kimberly call Vera to check a camera angle, and our manager rushed outside with way too few clothes to withstand the colder air.

She rushed back inside, then outside, then inside a few times to tell the man how to adjust the angle after checking the view on her computer. She could just… carry it outside, couldn’t she?

As I thought that, I touched a broken stake and cursed.

“What happened?” Nadia asked, immediately by my side.

“Splinter.”

“Nuuu. Let me see!”

Before I could even stop her, her hands were glowing and she was pulling the splinter out. My finger was fine in no time, but all I could do was stare at my girl. “The cameras,” I said. “Vera’s looking.”

“Ohshit,” she said, stunned for a moment. Then I saw her mind kick into gear. “She’s not inside yet, is she? Let me check. Vera!” she called. “Can you come over?”

“One moment!” our manager’s voice came from outside. Thank god she was outside. “I need to grab the computer! I can’t keep going back and forth!”

Then she went to Kimberly, told him how to fix the camera once and for all, and then she came over. “What’s up?”

“I…” Nadia began. “Right, I wanted to show you our progress! Ah, but… aren’t you a little bit underdressed?”

“Mm, yes,” she said distractedly. “It’s a bit cold but I can survive. Show me what you built!”

Even though she had been watching us through the cameras (and thankfully must have missed that one moment when Nadia slipped), she was as excited as Nadia was about showing her our progress.

“And with this,” my girl told her. “We’ll have produce all year round. Who knows, maybe we’ll even have surplus to sell! I know it won’t be much compared to hero work, but it’s a little dream of mine, to be able to just…”

She trailed off, staring at the plot. She had begun to talk to cover up her mistake, but sometime during it, it had become genuine.

“To call this place ours,” I finished for her. “To work the land, and enjoy its fruits. Even if we are just a city boy and a girl who have no idea what they are doing.”

Vera smiled. “That’s very sweet.”

Kimberly was done sometime later, and after Nadia and I finished tidying everything up outside, we found Vera back at her laptop with Nero on her lap.

“Aww,” Nadia squealed, and the two gave the cat more attentions than he bargained for. He didn’t leave, though, so perhaps not too many after all.

“As you can see,” Vera said after she and Nadia were done, “camera coverage is all set up.”

She turned the laptop around to let me see, as I was on the other side feeding the fire.

“That’s good,” I said, still thinking about that little almost-incident earlier.

“Indeed,” Vera said. “I hope this makes you feel safer.”

“It does,” I said.

“But it’s not enough,” Nadia added. She went on to propose we check in daily with each other, Vera included, and I was surprised by how quickly she agreed with the protocol. “And remember, if you see any oddity…”

“You two will be the first to know, yes,” she said, smiling–although perhaps it was a bit forced.

I noticed and decided it was time to change the topic. “Well, onto other matters. You got some more jobs for us?”

“Nothing new,” she said with a sigh. “We have the two leftovers, the cellar pests and the quote-unquote heist, as you put it.”

“The one suspiciously well-paid,” I said.

“I looked into it, and it should be fine.”

“Should, she says,” I said, looking at Nadia and making exaggerated faces.

My girlfriend smiled warmly at my antics. “How about we do the cellar pests? A couple time-stops, they won’t even know what hit them.”

“Sure,” Vera said, not even asking me. I pretended to be offended and she withered me with one look, as if to say that she knew who really decided those things around here.

Which was fair, but only those things.

“Alright, that’s settled,” she said, scribbling something down. “And one more thing. Kimberly was adamant I tell you he’d be down to have a look at the rest of your place, at your convenience. I warned him about his extravaganzas, but he insisted.”

“We’ll… take it into consideration,” I said, scratching the back of my head.

“Somehow, I knew you’d say that. Well then,” she got up. “It’s late, and I wouldn’t want to impose.”

“Do impose,” Nadia said. “We insist. We have some veggies from the winter garden, and meat. Adrian and I make a mean stew when we set our minds to it. Which, with you here, we would.”

She winked at her.

Vera shook her head. “I wish I could. Maybe another time, how about that?”

“Sure,” Nadia said, shooting me a quick look.

“It’s alright,” I told her after our manager was gone. “She can’t stay all day here, can she?”

My girl pouted. “I like her, though.”

“She has her work.”

“She could have stayed. What’s so urgent she can’t stay for dinner?”

I cupped Nadia’s face. “You little cutie. Feeling like you need some affection?”

“Yeshplease,” she said.

We ate quite late, that night. We were still basking in the afterglow of it all when Nadia’s eyes suddenly widened. “The cameras!”

“Huh? Are you talking about earlier? Vera wasn’t at her computer, remember?”

“What if they recorded it? What if she saw and that’s why she ran away?”

I blinked. Moments later we were at our computer, clumsily inputting our credentials to check. We held our breath as we found the folder and clicked play, then skimmed through the recording until…

“There,” Nadia said. “That’s the time. More or less.”

We watched it.

Nothing. The camera over the plot came online after the heal, it seemed, together with a whole set of them on this side of the house. Probably how Kimberly wired them.

“Wheew,” Nadia whistled. “That was close. Then Vera didn’t watch us at all!”

“Better this way,” I said. We had been more than a little lovey-dovey while working.

“But she said she’d keep an eye on us!”

“Sweetieeee,” I shushed her with a kiss. She let the matter go as she melted into me.

A bit later, as the fire became embers but–and I couldn’t get enough of this–the house wasn’t getting cold anymore (heaters!), Nadia pouted again. “I wish we could enjoy this more.”

“Why can’t we?”

“You know why.”

“The guy asking around, yeah,” I said, sighing.

“And all that it might mean. I know you are thinking the same thing as me.”

“Which would be?”

“Are you going to make me say it?”

“Say it, Nadia.”

“Very well, asshole prince. It’s the lab. The fucking lab under the cellar, okay? I’m thinking, if I knew there was something in his house when I first came, and guess what, I was right, someone else might know. They are asking questions. Is it too big of a stretch to assume it’s about the lab? You were the conspiracy theorist here after all.”

“…yeah, you have a point,” I conceded.

“There. I’m worried, Adrian.”

I squeezed her to me. “Me too,” I said. “But we have each other, and we have a fantastic manager.”

This made her smirk at me. “We do, don’t we?”

I scoffed at her antics. She mock-punched me, we play fought, and then I had her pinned below me. I made her turn around, ready to deliver some spanks that might turn the evening spicy, when I saw her catch her own reflection in the mirror behind the sofa.

That damn mirror every time. I was sure I had moved it away, but then with the heater installation it must have been moved again and I forgot.

She paused, tried to recover, but I was done skirting around the topic.

Still above her, I let go of her hips and made her face me again.

“Hey, master, what’s up?” she asked, still in character.

“Pineapple. Let’s pause for a moment.”

She immediately went serious at the mention of our safe word. “Sure, my love. Is everything alright?”

I grimaced. “I should be asking you, sweetie. I saw you look at yourself and wince. I want to know. Do you miss how you used to look?”

“I…” She paused, looking away. Her body language shifted so quickly, it was like she was the scared, jumpy woman from before all over again. “Let’s not talk about it, okay? I will talk about it, I swear, just not now. Is that okay?”

“Of course, my love.”

“Hold me, please?”

I did. I did until she calmed down. And I did not brush the topic again that night, no matter how sad seeing her like this made me feel.

Or so I planned on doing, and I would have done it, but then she started sobbing quietly. Little hiccups, tears wetting my shoulder as she trembled like a leaf.

No. It wasn’t right. I had to tell her.

“Nadia,” I said, lifting her chin gently so that her eyes would meet mine. “Your old look. I know how much even talking about it hurts you, and I didn’t ask because I’m mean or clueless about these things. You see, there’s something I haven’t told you.”

She looked at me with wide, wet, brown eyes that were so fragile and beautiful, waiting for my next words like a little puppy.

“When I gave you your power back, stripped of your mutation… I kept that part of you. I didn’t get rid of it. I have been holding onto it all this time, Nadia.”
