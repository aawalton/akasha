Ning strolled through the lively market, where the air buzzed with the cries of disciples hawking their wares, each trying to outshout the next.

With spirit stones finally jingling in his pouch, Ning felt a rare sense of financial security.

After all, as the saying went, "With a weapon in hand, murderous intent arises."

He wasn't a murderhobo, of course. But after being broke for so long, having a bit of wealth made his hands itch to spend it.

"Behold! Feathers of the majestic Flamingo! A rare and coveted ingredient for fire practitioners! Get them now, only fifty spirit stones!" one disciple called out, displaying a bundle of vibrant, fiery feathers.

"Sister, your beauty can only be matched by this dress made from Heavenly Silkworm silk! The material is of the highest quality, and it's incredibly smooth... Ah, this handsome senior brother! Would you like to buy this dress for your lover? It would make a perfect gift! Eh, you're single? Poor you~"

The senior brother fell silent.

"Hair Growing Pill! Hair Growing Pill! The latest creation of the renowned alchemist Ling Xin! Brother, I can see that you're bald, but with your exquisite temperament, imagine how magnificent you'd look with long, flowing hair! Take this pill, and a dragon among men shall be born~"

"...I'm sorry, but this bald head is just a wig."

"?!"

It was Ning's first time visiting the sect market, and the sight before him was far less immortal-like than he'd imagined.

Disciples had spread cloth, wooden boards, and even animal hides across the bluestone ground, shouting prices at passing customers and bargaining loudly enough to make the entire place resemble a vegetable market.

Spirit herbs and medicinal pills occupied some stalls, while others displayed talismans, weapons, and cultivation materials. There were also plenty of peculiar objects whose purposes weren't immediately obvious, many of which came with suspiciously impressive claims about their origins.

Ning wandered between the stalls, examining whatever caught his interest.

"Senior Brother, what's this?" he asked, picking up an oddly shaped piece of black metal.

"Ah! Junior Brother has a good eye!" The vendor immediately brightened. "That's an ancient fragment recovered from a powerful cultivator's cave dwelling. Nobody has managed to uncover its secrets, but I'm certain it contains a great opportunity!"

"Oh? How much?"

"Only three hundred spirit stones!"

Ning carefully returned the fragment to its original position.

A few stalls later, he came across a cracked jade pendant.

"What about this?"

"An extraordinary treasure discovered in an ancient ruin!" the seller declared proudly. "Even a Foundation Building cultivator couldn't identify its origins!"

"And the price?"

"Five hundred spirit stones!"

"..."

Ning thanked the man and continued walking.

He'd read enough cultivation novels to know that protagonists often stumbled upon extraordinary treasures disguised as worthless junk. A rusty sword might contain the inheritance of an ancient sword immortal, while an ordinary-looking stone could turn out to be some heaven-defying treasure.

Naturally, he'd wanted to try his luck.

The problem was that every merchant here seemed to have read the same novels.

They were already selling their suspicious-looking junk at prices befitting ancient immortal inheritances.

What happened to finding an overlooked treasure for three copper coins?

At these prices, even if something really did contain an extraordinary inheritance, he'd probably go bankrupt before discovering it. Worse, he could easily end up paying hundreds of spirit stones for an ordinary piece of scrap metal.

Yeah, let's leave treasure hunting to the protagonists.

Having satisfied his curiosity, Ning turned his attention to the medicinal pills displayed at several stalls. He asked about their effects, prices, and impurities, comparing the answers given by different vendors without committing to a purchase.

After all, these were cultivation pills. In his previous life, swallowing mysterious substances bought from strangers would've been an excellent way to end up in a hospital. Here, the consequences could be even worse.

Ning might be willing to take drugs for cultivation, but at the very least, he wanted the good stuff.

That was why he'd arranged for someone more experienced to accompany him.

"Junior Brother Ning!"

A familiar voice came from behind him.

Ning turned and saw Fang Zhu approaching through the crowd.

Fang Zhu was the steward responsible for Sector Three, and Ning had met him several times since being assigned his spiritual field. Most of those meetings hadn't even been official. Fang Zhu and Old Zhou were longtime friends, so whenever Fang Zhu visited the old man to chat, drink, or shamelessly freeload off his wine, Ning inevitably ended up seeing him as well.

"Senior Brother Fang." Ning cupped his hands in greeting. "You're here."

"Of course. You invited me, didn't you?" Fang Zhu chuckled. "Besides, after that meal you treated me to, how could I refuse such a small favor?"

Ning smiled. A little hospitality was a small price to pay for reliable advice, especially when he was about to spend nearly all his borrowed spirit stones.

"So, how's your first visit to the market going?" Fang Zhu asked.

"Quite educational."

"Oh? Found anything interesting?"

"Several ancient treasures, supposedly. The problem is that they're all priced as though their owners have already discovered the secrets inside."

Fang Zhu burst out laughing.

"Hahaha! You actually went looking through those stalls? Junior Brother, half those fellows would call a broken chamber pot an ancient treasure if they thought someone would pay for it."

"That's what I was beginning to suspect." At least now Ning knew he didn't have the protagonist's halo.

"Well, at least you didn't buy anything." Fang Zhu shook his head in amusement. "Anyway, where's Old Zhou? I thought he'd be the one bringing you here."

"Senior Zhou was supposed to accompany me," Ning admitted. "But after I asked him a few questions about cultivation pills, he told me to find someone else."

"A few questions?"

"Well, I wanted to know how the pills worked, what ingredients they contained, how impurities affected the meridians, and whether different pills could interfere with each other."

Fang Zhu nodded, finding the questions reasonable enough.

"Then I asked about the absorption process, possible side effects, and how to recognize poor-quality pills."

Fang Zhu fell silent.

"He threw me out before I could finish."

Fang Zhu couldn't help laughing. "Hahaha! No wonder that old fellow couldn't stand it!"

"I was only trying to be careful," Ning protested. "I'm going to swallow these things, after all."

"Fair enough, fair enough." Fang Zhu waved a hand, still chuckling. "Come on, then. I'll take you somewhere reliable."

Ning followed him, relieved to finally have someone familiar with the market to guide him.

"You're lucky you asked me," Fang Zhu continued cheerfully. "The place I'm taking you to is excellent. Unlike some of these stalls, nobody there will sell you powdered grass in a pill bottle and call it an ancient prescription."

"That actually happens?"

"Only if you're unlucky or stupid."

Ning decided that inviting Fang Zhu had been a wise investment.

During their walk, Fang Zhu continued praising the place with growing enthusiasm. The pills were properly refined, the prices were fair, the ingredients came from reliable suppliers, and even the after-sales service was supposedly excellent.

Eventually, Fang Zhu led him to one of the larger stalls near the inner section of the market. A broad brown beast hide covered the bluestone ground, while shelves and wooden tables displayed talisman paper, inks, pills, herbs, minerals, and various cultivation materials in neat rows.

Compared to the surrounding stalls, the place looked professionally managed.

The middle-aged shopkeeper behind the counter immediately straightened upon seeing them.

"Owner Fang! You're here."

Fang Zhu's smile stiffened slightly. "Don't call me that in public. It makes me blush."

Ning turned to look at him, then glanced around the neatly arranged stall as realization slowly dawned.

So, this entire journey, while Senior Brother Fang had been enthusiastically praising the trustworthy goods, reasonable prices, reliable suppliers, and excellent service, this guy had actually been advertising his own store.

He'd even done it with a completely straight face.

"So, Senior Brother is the owner of this place, huh?"

"Ah, you got me." Fang Zhu coughed lightly, though there wasn't the slightest trace of shame on his face. "In my defense, I wasn't lying about any of it. The goods really are reliable."

Ning nodded. That much he could believe. Fang Zhu had managed Sector Three for years, and Old Zhou trusted him enough to be a longtime drinking companion. That alone was a better guarantee than some random disciple on the roadside shouting about a once-in-a-century clearance sale.

Now that Ning thought about it, this also explained why Old Zhou was always so sarcastic whenever Fang Zhu came around.

This guy owned what appeared to be a lucrative business, yet every time he visited Old Zhou, he somehow ended up drinking the old man's wine.

Ning had previously assumed it was simply the shamelessness of an old friend. Turns out, it was the shamelessness of a wealthy old friend.

Somehow, that made it worse.

Fang Zhu coughed, having noticed the judgment in Ning's gaze. "Anyway, Junior Brother, didn't you want pills?"

"Of course, Senior Brother." Ning turned his attention to the shopkeeper.

The shopkeeper produced two porcelain bottles.

"For an early-stage Qi Condensation cultivator, I'd recommend either the Red Bud Pill or the Blue Dew Pill. The Red Bud Pill contains fewer impurities and generally takes three days to fully absorb. The Blue Dew Pill is cheaper, though it takes around five days and leaves more impurities behind."

Ning looked at the red and blue bottles.

Red pill or blue pill?

He couldn't explain why, but being presented with those two choices by a suspiciously friendly merchant made him feel as though there should be far greater consequences attached to this decision.

Thankfully, the only truth waiting for him was poverty.

Still, he had a few things to confirm before buying them.

Ning asked about the impurities, possible side effects, and whether the pills were compatible with the Pure Qi Sutra. The shopkeeper explained that both were suitable for early-stage Qi Condensation cultivators, though taking another pill before fully refining the previous one's medicinal energy could cause impurities to accumulate in the meridians.

"What about the absorption time?" Ning asked. "Does that mean the medicinal energy is released gradually over several days?"

"That's right. You'll absorb a portion during each cultivation session, though the exact amount depends on your cultivation speed."

Ning nodded thoughtfully before asking how to recognize when the medicinal energy had been fully absorbed.

Fang Zhu, who had been listening beside them, slowly turned toward him. "...Junior Brother."

"Yes?"

"I think I'm beginning to understand why Old Zhou threw you out."

Ning looked at him innocently. "What? I'm only asking a few questions."

The shopkeeper chuckled, seeming amused by it all.

Satisfied, Ning finally asked, "Could you tell me the prices?"

"Fifteen spirit stones for a Red Bud Pill and eleven for a Blue Dew Pill."

Ning mentally went through his finances. Wei Zhusang had lent him a hundred spirit stones. Normally, he would've preferred to save a portion for emergencies, but cultivation was the foundation of everything in this world.

Spirit stones sitting inside his pouch wouldn't make him any stronger, so he might as well put them to proper use.

"I'd like five Red Bud Pills and two Blue Dew Pills. Also, could you add two sticks of Spirit Incense? I heard they can improve the quality of meditation."

"Certainly. The incense is one spirit stone per stick." The shopkeeper quickly calculated the total. "Five Red Bud Pills come to seventy-five, two Blue Dew Pills to twenty-two, and two sticks of Spirit Incense make another two. Ninety-nine spirit stones altogether."

"Yes, thank you."

While the shopkeeper prepared his purchase, Fang Zhu raised an eyebrow.

"That's quite a sum. Last time we spoke, weren't you asking me whether there was anywhere in the sect you could borrow spirit stones?"

Ning remembered that conversation vividly. Fang Zhu had indeed known several lenders, but their interest rates had possessed distinctly demonic characteristics; one could even say they were as high as the heavens themselves.

"I borrowed some from my friends," Ning said with a smile.

"It seems Junior Brother has quite the silver tongue." Fang Zhu knew that most of Ning's friends had only known him for a short time. Convincing them to lend him nearly a hundred spirit stones was no small feat.

"It's nothing like that. I just reached Minor Accomplishment in the Small Cloud Rain Technique," Ning replied modestly.

Well, he was bragging a little, but occasionally receiving some appreciation for one's hard work was good for motivation.

"Seriously?" Fang Zhu looked genuinely surprised. "No wonder Old Zhou keeps calling you the Holy Farming Body. With your speed in mastering farming spells, you're starting to live up to that ridiculous name."

Ning's face went blank.

So the nickname really was going to stick, wasn't it?

Fang Zhu chuckled at his expression before remembering something.

"Right. If your Small Cloud Rain has reached Minor Accomplishment, you're eligible to apply for another farming spell."

Ning's suffering over the nickname vanished immediately.

"Another spell?"

Thanks to Old Zhou's guidance, he was already familiar with the available choices.

There was Scorching Purge, which burned away weeds and pests; Soil Refining, which improved and balanced the condition of spiritual soil; and Golden Finger, a precise planting technique that could pierce the earth and bury seeds at the proper depth.

Together with Small Cloud Rain and the more advanced Withering and Flourishing Technique, which required mid-stage Qi Condensation, they formed the core spells used by ordinary spiritual farmers.

Ning desperately wanted Scorching Purge.

No more crouching beneath the sun and pulling weeds by hand. No more spending hours hunting spiritual insects one plant at a time. One spell, a little fire, and his problems would disappear in cleansing flames.

Unfortunately, Ning still possessed common sense.

Improving the soil would increase the health and eventual yield of his entire field. Compared to saving himself some physical labor, the long-term benefit was obvious.

Ning mourned the death of convenience for several breaths before looking at Fang Zhu with painful determination.

"Senior Brother Fang, please help me apply for the Soil Refining Technique."

Fang Zhu nodded approvingly.

"Good choice."

Ning knew.

That was what made it hurt.

...

Returning home, Ning glanced at the weeds that had grown tall once again and almost couldn't resist the urge to march back and ask for the Scorching Purge spell.

He quickly restrained himself. Now that he had the pills, his priority was cultivation, and he wouldn't have enough time to devote to a new spell the way he had with Small Cloud Rain.

Choosing Scorching Purge just to deal with weeds would be shortsighted. Soil Refining would do far more for the field's fertility in the long run.

Sigh.

"What's done is done. Let's see what these pills can do."

Ning pushed the matter aside and took out a Red Bud Pill.

The pill looked harmless enough, a smooth, glossy red sphere that was faintly translucent under the light, with a soft herbal fragrance rising from it. It almost resembled one of those fancy vitamin capsules back on Earth.

Ning already knew how to consume it. He simply had to swallow it whole.

The pill's spiritual essence was sealed inside a thin shell that dissolved best during spiritual circulation. Chewing it could cause some of the medicinal qi to leak out or stick to his teeth and gums before it could be properly absorbed.

Even wasting a fraction of it would be disrespectful to those spirit stones.

After psyching himself up for a moment, Ning popped the pill into his mouth and swallowed it with a gulp.

The moment it reached his stomach, warmth spread through his body, as though he'd just swallowed a mouthful of molten honey. His meridians tingled, and a faint buzzing sensation began to pulse behind his navel.

Boom!

A burst of spiritual energy surged through his body. Ning immediately circulated the Pure Qi Sutra, guiding the influx through his meridians and refining it. His body swelled slightly under the sudden pressure, but he quickly steadied the flow and brought it under control.

Exactly one hour and eleven minutes later, Ning opened his eyes and let out a turbid breath.

Panel.

[1st Stage of Qi Condensation (42 → 43/100)]

Ning's eyes lit up at the improvement.

The efficiency had almost doubled!

Oh, this was so going to make his numbers go brr...

"Alas! One pill in, and I'm already addicted."
