A few minutes earlier.

Claire couldn’t look away from the portal. Tears were trailing from her cheek.

She sniffled.

Claire loved her husband more than life. He was a good and kind man, a selfless man.

She knew him better than he knew himself. So it hadn’t surprised her one bit when he confessed what he’d done.

If anything, it made her love him even more.

She couldn’t stand the thought of her little boy in danger. New Boise’s fate was sealed, that was it. Layton didn’t need to join them.

And yet—

She would’ve loved to see his face just one last time. Hold him like she did when he was young, reassure him everything was going to be okay.

Maybe this wouldn’t be the end? Maybe once things were over here, she’d finally get to see him.

Clinging to that thought, she stepped off the platform and started to join the rest of her faction.

As her foot reached the last step leading from the portal, her hand lightly grazed the stone shard. And a prompt appeared in her vision.

A faction has requested access to your teleporter.

Would you like to grant access to Faction Layton Mischief?

Yes or No?

Claire stared at the words. She couldn’t move, couldn’t breathe.

That was her son’s name. Visions played in her mind of birthdays, summer days, laughter, tears, graduation, disappointments, celebrations. Layton’s goofy smile that reminded her so much of his father’s.

She knew what she should do, what her husband had done.

Her finger lingered in the air over the answer she knew she should choose.

But no matter how hard she tried, she couldn’t do it.

Her shoulders began to quake, tears rolling in streams.

She chose, praying her husband could forgive her.

The moment she chose, her knees gave out and she fell forward, the last step sprawling onto the floor.

There was light buzzing that filled the room. Footsteps. Then two strong hands reached down, lifting her softly to her feet.

“Mom?”

***

Boretek almost admired the human faction leader.

It took courage to challenge a being such as Boretek. He could tell Martin was scared. He saw it in the way his shield trembled on his arm.

This was a man who knew his fate, but defied it anyway. Boretek knew what was coming. He even welcomed it when Martin swung his mace.

The weak needed hope.

But—even if he might respect the man’s bravery, there was no way he could abide such a sloppy attack.

He raised a hand to casually slap away the mace, but right as his hand was about to connect, the attack accelerated, slipping past his guard.

Boretek stared at his hand in stunned disbelief.

The attack did no harm. It was easily rebuffed by his mages’ defensive spells without ever reaching his body. That wasn’t the problem.

The problem was that Boretek was level 52. He was the faction leader of Gree-Cek. The human had no right to avoid his guard.

Before Martin could even twitch, Boretek ripped the man’s shield from his arm, then plunged his spear into his gut and out his back.

The man’s feet dangled as he was lifted into the air. Had he really admired this pitiful figure just moments before?

Disgusting.

“Return to your city and die with the rest of them.”

In a single flick of the wrist, he flung the man high into the air, his body sailing back to the walls where he came from.

He’d die before he even hit the ground.

Or so he thought.

A small figure blasted from the walls—colliding with Martin right at the pinnacle of his fall. The two figures spun in the air, plummeting together back to the earth.

Boretek watched them fall, eyes growing wide as gilded golden-and-grey-feathered wings sprouted from the newcomer’s back.

Together they glided downward and landed just a little ways off. Boretek was finally able to get a clear look at the newcomer.

Whoever it was wore loose grey healer robes with intricate golden embroidery that spun its way throughout the fabric, with a silver and gold helmet that split for the eyes and down its center. Four golden horns lay above the brow.

He carried a light-blue longsword on his hip.

Now that the two men were safely on the earth, the newcomer set Martin down. The spear wound that should’ve been open and bleeding was now fully healed.

Boretek listened as the man in grey robes spoke quietly.

“Stay here, Dad. I promise, everything is going to be alright now. You have to trust me.”

Martin listened and stayed still as a statue as the man in grey shifted his attention to Boretek. The wings disappeared into the robe and the helmet evaporated as the young man strode to stand directly in front of him.

“My name is Layton Shepard—and you are not welcome here. If you leave now, I’ll let you live. You have my word.”

“You’ll let me live?” Boretek stared down at the youth, hardly able to believe what he was hearing.

The boy didn’t respond.

Boretek looked around confused.

“That choice isn’t yours to make.”

The boy’s head tilted up, holding Boretek’s eyes in his own. Unbidden, a shiver crawled up his spine.

“It is my choice.”

Boretek felt more than he heard the words. They seemed heavy in the air. For some reason he seemed to believe they were true. But that couldn’t be right.

He raised his spear above his head, signaling for his men to resume their assault. Wind tousled Boretek’s hair at the release of his people’s immense power. He would break down New Boise’s defenses, his men would paint the streets with blood.

Boretek had to shout over the sounds of war. “Then show me.”

The boy drew his sword.

The entire blade seemed wreathed in a hellish blue flame. His helmet reappeared, blue eyes glowing from within.

A dark cloud rose from the city, billowing and expanding outward.

“So you won’t leave?”

Boretek glared down upon the boy. His blood began to boil. He wanted to strike him down without another word. Only… his limbs refused to move.

He felt a surge of panic. His teeth ground, vein pulsing in his forehead as he fought the invisible force that bound him.

All the while, the boy waited calmly as the cloud of darkness grew closer.

“I will not leave.” Spittle drizzled down his chin as he hissed the words. The pressure released in an instant and Boretek stumbled forward.

“If that’s the case—then I condemn you to death.”

Just as before, he felt the words, like a lock clicking shut.

Boretek had heard enough.

Three more spears appeared, one in each of his hands. His skill activated and each movement left an afterimage in its wake.

Four spears became hundreds as all the movements blurred together.

***

Martin was still struggling to process everything that just happened. One minute he was flying through the air with a hole in his gut, arm broken—the next he was in the arms of his son, wounds healed, gliding back down to earth.

Straight back to the same monster that had just stabbed him and thrown him in the first place.

Now all he could do was watch in horror while his son, the one he’d sacrificed so much for to keep from facing this monster, stood before a literal wall of a thousand spears.

He needed to do something, he couldn’t just stand by and watch his son be murdered. He took a step forward.

Four glowing blades coalesced in front of Layton, near-exact replicas of the burning blue longsword he held at his side.

It wouldn’t be near enough, he would need a shield, but Boretek already stole Martin’s and threw it who knew where. He reached into his storage, locating his first shield, the one he’d been given in the tutorial.

The wood seemed brittle and flimsy in comparison. It was better than nothing. He set his shoulders and took another step.

Martin couldn’t believe what happened next.

Layton actually turned his back on Boretek. As if he couldn’t even be bothered with the Gree-Cek faction leader.

His blue eyes smoldered from beneath his helm.

“Dad, don’t worry.”

From behind, Boretek roared. Martin could barely track the spears as they flew, afterimages leaving trails confusing his eyes. It was impossible for him to know which were real and which were fake.

The phantom blades weren’t confused, though. They moved with exact precision deflecting each and every attack with ease.

“This will be over soon.”

“Layton, I can help…” He knew the words were hollow, though. If Boretek attacked him like this, he would already be dead.

And yet his son didn’t even pay him attention. When Layton finally did move, Martin wasn’t even sure what really happened.

There was a flash of blue light and then Boretek stumbled back, looking as surprised as Martin felt.

An arm flopped into the grass.

“You’re like a cheap imitation of what strength really is.”

The words came from Layton’s mouth, they sounded so familiar. But they carried with it a force that he couldn’t deny.

“You’re just like so many others. You would take so much, without offering anything in return.”

Boretek prodded at the bloody stump where his arm used to be. His mages didn’t take the assault on their leader lying down. A dozen or so in hooded purple and gold swept in to surround Boretek.

Layton’s body completely vanished in a barrage of magic spells.

Martin reached out, feeling helpless.

The aftershock of the many powerful attacks buffeted his clothes. The spells created an explosion so bright he was forced to shield his face.

When the light died down, he squinted through watery eyes fearing what he might find. What he saw made no sense.

Two more people had arrived. One in dark-blue plate mail armor who rivaled even Boretek in height, and the other a dark man with pointed ears.

They both glowed a deep shade of blue.

And they were completely laying waste to Boretek’s mages.

Layton appeared in the midst of all the carnage completely unblemished, pressing the attack on a retreating Boretek.

As if the scene weren’t already strange enough—colors faded and the world became shades of grey and black.

Everything was happening faster than Martin could track.

Not wanting to be left behind, he jogged to keep up with the fleeing army of the Gree-Cek.

He didn’t have to jog far.

Each and every member of the Gree-Cek was stumbling and falling over themselves, each attempting to flee in a different direction. Some rubbed at their eyes, while others fell and prodded at the ground.

What?

Layton and the two others, who now simply seemed to glow grey, moved with absolute confidence.

Boretek was swinging away at nothing, while Layton just waited, taking it all in.

Martin didn’t know what to think. Who were these people who were with his son? Friends? They didn’t seem human, though.

“You must be Layton’s father.” A deep, youthful voice appeared in Martin’s mind.

The darkness twisted and coalesced in front of him, becoming material, taking form.

“I can see the resemblance.”

Directly in front of him, close enough to reach out and touch, was a pitch-black panther the size of a horse.

“You can call me Mischief.”

***

Boretek didn’t understand.

His mages layered him in different defensive spells. What happened should’ve been impossible.

But there was no denying his arm was on the ground instead of his body where it belonged. To make matters worse, this young man hadn’t just blocked his Thousand Arms skill, he’d ignored it.

Boretek stumbled backwards into the ranks of his mages, allowing them to engulf him in their midst.

He needed to reset, gather his bearings. He’d been caught off guard, but that wouldn’t happen again. A potion, he needed a potion.

There was a crunch to his right, and his entire right side was splashed with something warm and thick.

He wanted to spare a glance but was too concerned with the blue-eyed monster walking after him. He downed a potion and immediately felt his arm being regrown.

There. Nothing was so bad he couldn’t recover. He’d need to be more careful, though.

That was when he finally noticed: the cloud of black had finally arrived. It swallowed the boy, his mages and then him.

Boretek tried to run, but he stumbled and fell backwards. He couldn’t see anything. His heart was beating fast, he didn’t know which way to run anymore.

“Wait, please.” He groped at the mists, dropping his weapons. “I’ll leave, stop. I’ll leave, you won’t see my face as long as you live.”

“They told me about you, Boretek. You’re a murderer. Yet you beg for mercy.”

That voice again. “Tell me, did you grant my father any mercy?”

“Your father? I only did what others have always done before me!”

The vapors parted and the boy strode from the darkness, black mist licking at the golden hems of his robes.

Blue eyes burning.

“If you were stronger, would you have shown these people mercy?”

“Yes! Yes! What are we without mercy?”

Layton’s face contorted in a look of disgust.

“You beg for mercy, when your victims cry for justice.”

“No—” His voice cut off.

A flash of blue was the last thing Boretek, leader of the Gree-Cek, ever saw on earth.
