As interesting as Harmstead was, the fact that Vin was on a time-sensitive mission meant he couldn’t really stay around for any longer than he already had. He had five days left before the festival, and then who knew how many more after that before the battle for wave eight. The battle for wave seven had ended up coming fifteen days early, at the fourty-five day mark rather than sixty, which meant this next one would presumably be quite early as well. John should be able to pick out a pretty solid estimate as to the exact day of the battle soon enough, but according to Spur, he didn’t have one just yet.

Of course, whenever Vin visited people in their dreams with Dream Walk they tended to be a little bit… loopy. Only fellow dream mages such as the pixies or Lumel tended to retain their regular mental state when visited in a dream. Spur should remember the gist of their conversation, but Vin wouldn’t put it past the Commander to have forgotten a thing or two.

With that in mind, he and Alka set out the next morning bright and early, eager to hit the last two fragments on their list and make it back to Terra with plenty of time to prepare.

“Drintus told me that the fragment directly south of them is home to a bunch of dwarves,” Vin said as they hiked down the side of the mountain. The mountain was steep enough that movement was a bit slow going compared to how fast they usually ran, but Vin was enjoying the simple act of watching the wildlife and taking in the environment of another world. Even if they didn’t have the time to kick back and enjoy the town, the least he could do was take in the scenery.

“Dwarves, huh?” Alka asked, and Vin didn’t even have to ask to know where her mind had gone. “How do you think Deorer’s doing? Haven’t spoken to the old smith in a good bit now. Not since he helped patch me up after our battle with the ranker king.”

“You know him better than I do,” Vin shrugged. He smiled at the sight of a tiny family of mice-like creatures as they scurried across a tree branch and slipped into a tiny hollow. “But seeing as he hit his second prestige ages ago, I imagine from a leveling perspective, he’s probably got to be pretty high up there.”

“Maybe I’ll ask him about it the next time we meet.”

The trip down the mountain was quiet for the first hour or two, but naturally, Edregon couldn’t just sit by and give them a leisurely morning hike without interruption. While the two of them were crossing over a small creek running through one of the flatter portions of the mountain that was filled with trees, Vin’s sensitive ears picked up on the sound of a deep growling, and he immediately held up a hand for Alka to pause.

“Monster,” he warned. Summoning his staff to his hand, he then cast Sense Life and easily pinpointed the large creature. The fact that it was barreling toward them at a rather alarming speed made his spell largely unnecessary, as one small tree after another was splintered and knocked down while the monster raced toward them.

“A bit bigger than that overinflated rat we took out the other day,” Alka said, her sword waiting at the ready for the monster to reach them. Each tree that fell sent a small quake through the sparse forest when it hit the ground, but neither of them were worried. “You want this one?”

“You sure? I know monsters are sort of your whole thing.”

“Eh, I like fighting dangerous monsters,” Alka shrugged. “And we are on a bit of a schedule. Go nuts.”

“If you say so." Throwing together the runic formation for Meteor, Vin summoned a large, flaming chunk of stone overhead. Just as the projectile finished forming, the monster smashed through the latest tree with a roar, revealing itself to be some sort of dense, dog-looking creature with hardened plates on the front of its head. To Vin’s surprise, it wasn’t even an elite monster. Just a strong-looking normal one.

New monster discovered! 200 exp gained.

“It’s just a regular monster,” Vin said, gesturing with his finger and completing his spell. The poor monster didn’t even make it another dozen feet toward them before his Meteor smashed into its body, blasting the thing backwards and killing it near instantly. As the monster’s corpse tumbled to a stop, Alka sighed and sheathed her sword.

“You know, not that I would ever wish for the world to have more powerful monsters for us to deal with. But I never really thought about how boring taking them down would become as time went on. Guess I always thought monsters would just scale with the strength of the Slayer.”

“That’s probably how it did work for Slayers back on your world for the most part,” Vin shrugged, leading them down the mountain once more as they walked past the smoking corpse of the monster. “I imagine the lower-level Slayers would deal with the weaker monsters, and the high-level Slayers would get called in for the really dangerous stuff. The big difference here of course being that as we explore Edregon, it’s up to us to take on everything.”

“Good point. I should start dragging around one of my trainees to make them deal with this sort of thing.”

Vin merely shook his head while Alka laughed, focusing on not tripping over his own feet as the mountain grew steep once more.

The two of them only had to deal with another three monsters on their way down the rest of the mountain, each one taken out with the same speed and efficiency as the first. Even as good as Alka was, she wasn’t able to finish off the monsters quite as quickly as he could. Vin didn’t think that was anything surprising, as it was tough to kill something faster than by blasting it with a giant piece of flaming rock, but Alka seemed mildly annoyed at the fact.

Eventually, they hit the fragment border, and Vin peered curiously into the land beyond. In stark contrast to the mountain they’d spent the morning hiking down, this next fragment was largely flat. There were thick, earthen columns jutting out of the ground here and there the size of entire buildings, but the ground itself was devoid of any elevation change.

“Did Drintus happen to tell you where we could actually find these dwarves?” Alka asked while they walked into the fragment.

Third ring fragment discovered! 1,500 exp gained.

“He did not,” Vin sighed. Angling them toward the center, he took off at a brisk run. “Let’s just aim for the middle and see what we can find. I doubt we’ll have too much trouble finding them.”

In fact, the two of them barely made it a full mile into the fragment itself before they ended up running into their first handful of dwarves. A large plot of farmland carefully positioned between a number of those earthen pillars was currently being tended to by a few dozen people, and Vin slowed to a stop as he watched the Farmers at work.

Not only did the Farmers utilize the ground for their crops, they had clearly chosen this location for a reason. The edges of the pillars had plenty of things growing out of them that Vin had never seen before, and his mind immediately went back to Agne, the new leader of the infernals. While it had been some time since the two of them had last spoken, he knew she loved gardening, and he’d made a point to try and bring her some unique crops from some of the different fragments he hit.

“Look alive, they spotted us,” Alka said, and Vin focused as he spotted one of the Farmers wipe her hands off and walk toward them.

“Can I help you?” she asked, her voice gruff as she stopped a good two dozen feet away. Despite working in the field, she had a hammer slung on the side of her belt that Vin had no doubt she knew how to use.

“Hello! I’m Vin, an Explorer,” he said, before gesturing toward Alka. “This is my companion, a warrior who specializes in killing monsters. We’re just passing through and were curious if you could point us in the direction of your town.”

“I could, but it wouldn’t do you any good,” she said. Her frown lightened up a touch as she looked over them and seemed to come to the conclusion they weren’t there to cause trouble. “Ruckont closed its borders months ago, shortly after we came to Edregon. We do some trade with the surrounding fragments, but only our people are allowed inside. Sorry to disappoint.”

“Aren’t some of your people staying and working in the fragment to the north-west?” Alka asked pointedly. “You’ll move into other towns but won’t let people visit yours? Seems a bit unfair, don’t you think?”

“So it would seem,” the woman shrugged. “What do you want from me? I’m a Farmer, not the governor. Not like I have any say in any of that crap, I just make sure people don’t go hungry.”

“And your work is incredibly important,” Vin said, shooting a glare at Alka to shut her up before she said anything else. “Actually, I was hoping I could maybe take one or two of your crops with me, if that would be okay? I’ve got a friend who loves gardening and getting her hands on interesting new plants.”

“Take whatever you want from the reject bin,” the dwarf shrugged, waving a hand dismissively. “They’re not fit to eat, but the seeds should be fine. Just don’t touch anything still in the ground. And that goes for the pillars of earth as well.”

As the dwarf left them to their own devices, Vin did just that, wandering over to the large, earthen crate that was sitting off to the side of the farm. A few of the dwarves threw him curious looks, but none of them stopped what they were doing to approach. While he sifted through the bin, pulling out a crop that looked like a bulbous, blue carrot and another one that reminded him of a tiny, fist-sized watermelon, Alka grunted quietly.

“It just annoys me when towns do this sort of thing. Take advantage of their neighbors without giving them the same privileges, you know?”

“I mean, they are still trading with their neighbors," Vin pointed out as he tucked the crops into his pack. Even if Agne wouldn’t be able to cook with these, she’d definitely still enjoy getting her hands on the seeds. He was planning to give some to Terra’s Farmers as well of course, but Agne came first. “It’s not like it’s another Citadel situation where they’ve battened down the hatches and aren’t doing anything with anyone. They’ve got every right to close their doors to others if that’s what they want.”

“I suppose,” Alka said, glancing up at the midday sun. “Well, that makes this a pretty fast fragment. Plenty of time to hit up the last one on the list. Do we know anything about it?”

“Drintus said it was ‘difficult to traverse,’” Vin explained, securing his pack and making sure it sat comfortably on his back. While things like travel sores didn’t really happen any more thanks to his high endurance, a blessing he was eternally grateful for after all his wandering around back on Earth, old habits died hard. “When I asked him for more details, he just laughed and told me I should see for myself.”

“That sounds like Drintus alright,” Alka snorted. “A minotaur after my own heart. In that case, no sense in wasting time. Want to hit the last fragment and head home? I know we’ve both got plenty of things on our plates with the festival and the battle for wave eight slowly approaching.”

“Sounds like a plan,” Vin said, waving goodbye at the Farmers before taking off toward the final fragment on their list. After all these months on Edregon having explored countless fragments, he was finally about to finish the third ring surrounding Terra. It was a feat that had been a long time in the making, and he was more than a little excited to add that last fragment to his ever-growing map.

Edregon was probably too large and complex for anyone to ever truly map it in its entirety, but that certainly wouldn’t stop him from trying!
