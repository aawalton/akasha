An hour later I sat and admired my gains. Thirty-four Chaos Spawn downed. My loot piles? Huge.

Pile one, teeth, eyes, meat. Disgusting, but expected.

Pile two, fourteen more Chaos Fragments.

And pile three? Weird random crap.

I scanned the last pile. Crude Serpent Dagger, cast-iron pot, wooden flute, broken ceramic plate.

…What the hell am I supposed to do with this stuff? Hopefully, stronger monsters drop better loot.

For now, it is what it is.

I still had thirty minutes before Wave 2. There were two things I still needed to do. First, use the other Minor Chaos Shard. And second? Have a long-overdue heart-to-heart with Mischief.

Priorities first.

I grabbed the Crude Serpent Dagger and the chaos shard and repeated the process. Channel mana. Infuse the shard. Press it to the dagger.

Pop.

Zzzt.

Pain. Sharp, brutal, electric pain.

“MOTHER OF—”

I yelled and dropped the knife. My hand burned like I just grabbed a live wire.

What the hell just happened?! I shook out my fingers, glaring at the dagger. Did it just electrocute me?!

Heart pounding, I pulled up my status screen and analyzed the dagger in the dirt.

Crude Serpent Dagger of Shocking—This dagger doesn’t discriminate—it will shock any creature it touches, including the wielder.

…

What.

WHAT?!

What kind of absolute trash-tier upgrade was this?!

I glared at the dagger like it personally insulted my ancestors. This wasn’t an upgrade—this was sabotage! Was that even allowed?!

I thought back to the chaos shard’s description. What did it say again?

“Can be combined with items to produce unknown effects.”

…Right. Unknown effects. Nowhere did it promise “good” effects.

No safety net. No guarantees. No “Are you sure?” pop-ups. Just raw chaos.

And—stupidly enough—that actually sounded kind of fun.

I gave my shocking snake dagger a rueful smile. Yeah, I’m not touching you again, Mr. Shocky.

With the shard spent and my “upgraded” dagger in time-out, that left one last issue before Wave 2.

Mischief.

Snatching my club up, I turned toward where I left the oversized menace—only to freeze. Mischief was no longer sprawled out, weak and dying.

He was sitting. Alert. Healthy. Watching me. My grip tightened around the club.

How? How was he already back to full strength?

I replayed the last hour. I cast Weak Heal. Then Weak Regen. That’s at least 17 HP recovered, maybe more, since I boosted Intelligence.

That meant Mischief had been at fighting strength for at least twenty minutes.

And he didn’t move. I narrowed my eyes. Mischief tilted his head slightly, watching me. Sizing me up.

I exhaled slowly, but it was forced—like my body was trying to steady itself before my brain caught up. What the hell was going on here?

No reaction.

“Didn’t you try to eat me earlier?” I pressed. “You… not hungry anymore?” I had left my back completely exposed to this natural predator at full health for at least twenty minutes, maybe longer.

It hadn’t attacked.

I crossed my arms. Fine. Let’s see if this thing really understands anything.

“You saw what happened last time? Little creepy chaos dudes running all over?”

Mischief’s tail twitched, but he didn’t move besides that.

“Another wave is coming.” My voice is steady, but my pulse isn’t. Was I really trying to communicate with a mountain lion?

I gestured my hand back and forth between us. “We fight, or we die.”

Neither of us moved.

I must be crazy. Do I really think any of this is getting through?

Mischief stood. I stayed frozen. Mischief took a cautious step forward—the tension in the air was suffocating.

My muscles coiled, every instinct screaming at me to raise my weapon. But something—maybe our shared fate—told me to hold my ground.

Step by measured step Mischief closes the gap. I held my breath. Slowly he sauntered up to me. So close now that I could hear his breath softly move in and out.

If I wanted I could reach out and rest my hand on his head. But something told me taking the initiative and patting the large predator on the head wasn’t the right move.

I waited, one hand resting on my club, the other hanging at my side. Mischief walked right past me, but as he did, his head moved just slightly.

Soft hair tickled my fingers.

Then, as if I didn’t exist, he padded to the loot pile, sniffing through the mess. He paused at the meat, and actually looked back at me. Expectant?

I blinked.

Is he asking for permission?

I hesitated. Then shrugged. “Go ahead. I’m not exactly dying to eat grey, stinky monster meat.”

Mischief didn’t wait. He leaned down and tore into the Chaos Spawn flesh. The whole situation felt so strange. I knew that my words likely meant nothing to this wild animal, and yet I felt a strange kinship.

I didn’t have time to dwell on this. For now I was just happy it was eating the Chaos Spawn meat and not me. A system notification flashed.

1 minute until Wave 2. Prepare yourself.

My stomach dropped.

Mischief jumped slightly. His ears flicked toward me. His tail twitched.

Did he just get the system notification? I narrowed my eyes. “Did you see that?”

Mischief flicked his tail. Looked at me. Didn’t confirm or deny it. I mean, what did I expect him to do?

If he did see it… what language did he get it in? Did the system adjust for him? Did he even think in words? This was all so weird.

I shook my head. No time for this.

“I’m moving to the flattened grass to fight—if you understand me, don’t get too close.” I swung my club in a wide arc to illustrate my point.

Mischief watched. Fine. That’d have to do.

It was an uneasy alliance. At least, I knew I was uneasy about it.

But what choice did I have? The wave was coming.

I jogged back to the clearing. The once-tall grass had been trampled, leaving me with a rough twenty-foot oval to work with.

It’ll do.

Wave 2 starts in 10… 9… 8…

I checked my stats one last time.

HP: 28/28 MP: 25/30

My MP had regenerated 10 points since healing Mischief. That meant mana recharged naturally.

Good to know.

3… 2… 1…

I exhaled. Here we go.

Wave 2 of 5 has begun.

Just like last time, the wave started from the far side of the clearing.

The trampled field offered me a better view then when the grass stood tall and untouched, and—

Damn.

It wasn’t fifty Chaos Spawn. Not one hundred. It was way more.

My stomach knotted. I was hoping the waves would double in size each time. This wasn’t double. This was easily quadruple.

“Looks like we’ve got our work cut out for us,” I muttered, and glanced back toward the meat pile.

Mischief wasn’t there. I turned in a full circle, scanning the area.

“Hey—what the hell?!” I snapped. “I thought we were working together! Did you seriously run away?!”

Turning about, I looked everywhere. Nothing. There was no movement. Did Mischief bolt?

I cursed under my breath. It felt good to think I wasn’t alone.

Whatever.

I adjusted my grip on the club. If I had to do this alone, so be it. The first wave nearly killed me because I let them come to me. Not this time.

This time, I was bringing the fight to them.

The first couple of Chaos Spawn crossed the twenty-foot mark. I unleashed the best battle cry I could muster and ran to meet the horde. The first Chaos Spawn barely had time to react before—

CRACK.

The hit exploded against its skull. A spray of black ichor splattered across the grass as its head whipped sideways at an unnatural angle.

The body didn’t just drop—it flipped twice before crashing into the dirt.

The horrible image of the mangled Chaos Spawn stayed tattooed on my brain. But I kept moving. A second Chaos Spawn lunged for me. I twisted mid-swing and brought the club back around.

THWACCCK.

The spiked wood buried itself in its ribs with a wet crunch. The Chaos Spawn was impaled on the club’s spikes, the momentum of the strike not enough to detach the body. Its tiny limbs flopped and swayed as I took another baseball swing.

Two down. More came pouring into the small clearing.

I gritted my teeth—swinging my club in wide arcs. The monsters didn’t stop coming.

Bone, cartilage, flesh—it all crumpled under the impact.

Five Chaos Spawn were smashed aside in a single swing. Their bodies collapsed in a grotesque heap, limbs twitching.

You have killed Level 3 Chaos Spawn. You have killed Level 3 Chaos Spawn. You have killed Level 4 Chaos Spawn. You have gained extra XP for killing a monster above your level.

Three dead, two more crumpling.

I yanked the club back and took another horizontal swipe.

You have killed Level 3 Chaos Spawn. You have killed Level 2 Chaos Spawn.

My breathing was sharp, controlled. This club was a gift. Wide swings let me clear entire groups, and these freaks were paper-thin when it came to durability.

They kept coming. I kept swinging.

You have killed Chaos Spawn (Level 3, 4, 4, 3).

Level up.

This wave was different. The first wave? All level 1s. This one? They ranged from 2 to 5.

At first, I didn’t notice much of a difference between them. Level 5s died just as easily as level 2s—maybe they were slightly tougher, but not enough to matter.

Then I spotted something.

One of the Chaos Spawn—level 5, from the notification—was gripping a weapon.

To its tiny, twisted hands, it was a shortsword.

To me? It looked like a dagger, not too dissimilar from my own shocky knife.

I caved in its rib cage with a single swing. Bones crunched, ichor sprayed—but the sight of that weapon made me wonder.

They’re getting stronger. They’re getting weapons. Why are their bodies as flimsy as ever?

I should be struggling. But I’m not.

Why?

I should’ve been winded. My arms should feel like dead weight from swinging a blunt-force weapon for this long. Instead?

I felt trained. Conditioned. Like I’d been doing this for months.

My endurance wasn’t just better. It was unnatural. And that wasn’t all. My arms and legs felt powerful. The stats were clearly having an impact as I leveled.

Why did it feel like the Chaos Spawn were barely improving?

I glanced up, expecting a swarm. With their numbers, even at my enhanced speed and power, they should be able to overwhelm me.

They weren’t. Instead, the Chaos Spawn came in small, disorganized clusters.

Why weren’t they mobbing me? By my estimation, they should be overwhelming me with sheer numbers. That was when I saw it.

A blur of orange fur.

Mischief was rampaging through the Chaos Spawn ranks, carving through them like a ghost of claws and teeth.

His movements were fast, strikes precise. I was probably three levels higher, but it’d still be a hard-pressed fight if it came to it.

Every swipe of his claws didn’t just wound—it obliterated.

A Chaos Spawn leaped—he caught it midair.

His teeth crunched down on its throat. One second, it was alive. The next, its head hung on by a thread.

The other Chaos Spawn noticed. They tried to turn on him, swarm him.

They couldn’t. He was too fast. He was a living weapon.

Instead, they abandoned strategy and rushed toward me. A desperate charge. A suicide run.

And without overwhelming numbers? They were nothing. I clubbed them down.

Congratulations. You have reached Level 5. Choose a new skill. You have reached Level 6.

The last Chaos Spawn fell. I staggered back, chest heaving. The club felt heavier now.

Even with my weirdly boosted endurance, twenty straight minutes of combat added up.

Still… I should be worse off than this. I fought over a hundred monsters—most my level or higher—and yet? I was still standing.

How?

A Chaos Spawn with 5 Strength didn’t seem to have the same power as me with 5 Strength.

Meaning… that stats might not be everything. Were there other factors? Mischief’s speed compared to mine pre-stats would obviously be much greater.

I let that settle, exhaling slowly. It’s something to think about.
