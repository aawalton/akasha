Clutching the sword at his side, Luke sprinted into the throng of people crowding the main road. The bustling streets offered decent cover—if he was lucky, he might even lose his pursuers.

It didn’t sit right, using civilians as a shield, but he had no other choice. Besides, he doubted the black-robed attackers would risk open slaughter in the heart of the capital.

Shoving his way through the masses, Luke’s ears perked at a sharp scream. He turned just in time to see a woman collapse, an arrow lodged deep in her neck. She grasped at the wound briefly, blood bubbling from her lips before she crumpled to the ground.

Chaos erupted. Screams filled the air as the crowd scattered, trampling over one another in a desperate attempt to escape. Panic spread like wildfire.

A pit formed in Luke’s stomach. ‘They’re attacking civilians? In broad daylight?’ Where were the city guards?

He didn’t have time to dwell on the thought. The panicked flood of people nearly knocked him off his feet, and it took everything he had to stay upright.

Luke cursed inwardly and drew his sword. “GET THE FUCK OUT OF MY WAY!” he roared, brandishing the blade.

The effect was immediate—civilians scrambled to avoid him, veering away in their rush to flee. But the outburst came at a cost. He’d just broadcast his location.

Gritting his teeth, Luke sheathed his weapon and darted into the nearest alley. It was away from the palace, but he had no better option.

The ground was slick with grime and filth, making each step treacherous. Still, he pushed forward, boots slipping slightly with every stride. At the next junction, a chill raced up his spine. Danger.

His instinct screamed.

Out of the corner of his eye, he saw a blade slicing toward him—aimed straight for his neck.

Time slowed.

Luke dropped to his knees and leaned back, sliding across the filthy stone. The sword whistled inches above his face, missing him by a hair’s breadth.

In one fluid motion, he sprang to his feet, drew his sword, and lunged. His blade tore through the attacker’s robe, burying itself deep into the man’s heart.

The man crumpled, lifeless.

Luke didn’t stop to watch him fall. He kept running. He didn’t know how many were chasing him or how close they were—only that they hadn’t given up.

An arrow hissed past his shoulder, thudding into a wooden door ahead. The gust of displaced air tousled his hair, a testament to the force behind the shot.

‘Shit! They’re definitely trying to kill me.’

If the earlier ambush hadn’t made that clear, the arrow certainly did. These weren’t bounty hunters or captors—they were assassins. They didn’t want him captured. They wanted him dead.

And Luke had no doubt why.

The gunpowder recipe.

Whoever these people served, they were no friends of the Marxx Kingdom. But right now, none of that mattered. All he could do was run—twisting through the winding alleys, praying to lose them in the maze of stone and shadow.

Soon, his body began to falter. His legs felt heavier with each step, and his breathing turned ragged. It had only been five months since he’d transmigrated to this godforsaken world, and he’d only begun training seriously halfway through that time.

There was no way his body could sustain this pace.

'I need to hide—somewhere, anywhere.' Panic edged his thoughts.

Luke’s eyes darted across the alley, scanning for shelter before exhaustion overtook him completely. Every house he passed had its door shut tight, and he couldn’t afford the time it would take to force one open.

He looked ahead—and froze.

A dead end.

‘Shit.’

His instincts screamed. If he didn’t find cover now, he’d die like a stray dog in the street.

He cursed under his breath, desperately searching for anything—an alcove, a pile of refuse, a loose grate in the cobbled street. Even a sewer... If it meant survival, he’d crawl through filth. Dignity meant nothing to the dead.

Slowing down, he turned to glance behind him. His pursuers weren’t in sight yet, but the sound of pounding footsteps echoed through the alley, growing louder by the second.

He had no escape.

Drawing a deep breath, Luke steadied his ragged lungs and gripped his sword. If he was going to die, he wouldn’t do it cowering.

He unsheathed the blade and forced his heart to calm. If this was his final stand, then he’d make it count.

‘Is this what those heroes on the battlefield feel before they charge to their deaths?’ he wondered.

In that still moment, something strange happened. His fear dulled. The anxiety clinging to his chest fell away, replaced by a curious calm. A clarity bloomed inside him—quiet and unnerving.

He narrowed his gaze toward the mouth of the alley just as three figures rounded the corner. Unlike the assassins he’d encountered earlier, these men wore no masks.

They slowed their approach, chests heaving, sweat glistening on their brows. Luke recognized them immediately—they were the ones chasing him all along.

His eyes narrowed as sunlight caught his pupils.

‘There’s one missing…’

He’d counted five during his escape. One he’d already killed. That left one unaccounted for.

Luke’s eyes flicked upward, scanning the rooftops—and then he saw him.

A shadow crouched on a rooftop about a hundred feet away, bow in hand, an arrow nocked and drawn. The archer had him dead in his sights.

“You’re a slippery fucker,” the man in the lead muttered, wiping the sweat from his brow. He had beady eyes, a thin goatee, and a jagged scar along his left cheek. His rough features and sun-scorched skin marked him as a commoner, not a trained assassin.

“Who sent you?” Luke asked, voice calm, sword still steady in his right hand.

The man snorted and spat to the side. “Someone who wants you dead, obviously.”

“Boss, he killed Andrew… Let’s fucking kill him already,” one of the men behind the leader growled, stepping forward with fire in his eyes.

However, the leader raised a hand, halting his companion.

“Don’t worry—we’ll kill him,” he said, his lips curling into a sly grin. “But I want to do it slowly.”

Then, with the same hand, he gave a subtle signal.

The twang of a bowstring rang out from the rooftop. An arrow was loosed from a hundred feet away.

'Sly bastard!' Luke cursed.

Despite his words, the leader had no intention of a slow kill—not when he had the element of surprise. The signal had been for the archer to fire the moment Luke let his guard down. This man wasn’t just cruel—he was clever.

But Luke hadn’t been idle either.

Just as the arrow was fired, he kicked off the ground. The projectile screamed past his head, missing by mere inches and striking the stone ten feet behind him.

“Kill him!” the leader barked, drawing his sword.

Luke charged. His blade was held low, angled to strike from below. The moment he reached his enemy, he launched an upward slash from an awkward angle, fast and unpredictable.

Steel clashed in a ringing screech. The leader’s sword flew upward from the impact, leaving his torso wide open and knocking him off balance.

Luke didn’t hesitate.

He lunged in and drove the point of his sword straight into the man’s throat. The blade tore through flesh and cartilage, silencing the man with a gurgling gasp as he dropped like a sack of meat.

But victory came at a cost.

With both arms extended from the thrust, Luke left himself vulnerable. The attacker to his right seized the opportunity and slashed at his exposed side.

Pain exploded through Luke’s hip. He gritted his teeth, stifling a cry, and twisted with a backhanded slash.

In the narrow alleyway, he lacked room to fully swing, but it was enough. His blade sank into the man’s right arm. The attacker screamed.

Luke yanked the blade free, slicing clean through flesh and bone. The severed arm fell to the ground, blood splattering across the grime-streaked cobblestones.

“Y-You bastard!”

Now only one enemy remained standing on the ground. Fueled by rage and panic, the man charged wildly.

Luke braced for the incoming blow—but then his ears twitched.

‘Another arrow?’

He twisted, deflecting the thrust with a sharp parry. Then, using the enemy’s momentum, Luke turned his body and yanked the man forward.

A dull thud sounded and the attacker went limp mid-stride.

An arrow had buried itself in the back of his skull, planting itself deeply. He collapsed in Luke’s arms like a broken doll.

Luke shoved the body aside and snapped his head toward the rooftop. The bowman still stood there, but even from this distance, Luke could see hesitation in his stance. The man’s hands trembled as he reached for another arrow.

Then Luke turned to the final enemy still alive—the one clutching his bleeding stump, shrieking in agony.

A bow was slung across his back.

“Sit tight,” Luke said coldly, stepping toward him. “I’ll be borrowing this for now.”

Without waiting for a response, he slid the bow from the man’s shoulder.

Luke set his sword down beside him and strode over to the body of the man struck down by the rooftop arrow. Without hesitation, he gripped the bloodied shaft and yanked it free from the man’s skull. The sickening squelch didn’t faze him.

He nocked the arrow with care, drawing the string taut as he trained his gaze on the final enemy—the rooftop archer.

But before Luke could fire, another twang echoed through the alley.

The enemy’s arrow whistled past Luke’s head, close enough to ruffle his hair. He didn’t flinch.

It was as if he already knew it would miss.

Panic seized the bowman. He dropped his weapon and turned to flee, scrambling across the rooftop like a startled animal.

Luke exhaled slowly, steadying his breath. He adjusted his aim and loosed the arrow.

His eyes narrowed as he tracked its flight.

A sharp cry rang out. The arrow struck true, piercing the fleeing man clean through the heart. His body tumbled forward, rolling off the rooftop—and landed with a dull thud in the alley below.

“Ah… much better,” Luke muttered with a sigh, the tension leaving his shoulders.

His mind was still crystal clear, though pain still flared from his side. He glanced down and inspected the wound at his hip. A shallow cut. Painful, but not life-threatening. A few stitches would be enough.

Satisfied, he turned to the last man still alive.

The wounded attacker was curled on the ground, clutching the raw, bleeding stump where his arm had been. His pitiful whimpers echoed in the narrow space. Once, Luke might’ve felt sympathy—but not for someone who’d tried to kill him.

Luke crouched beside him and smiled faintly. “Hey. Thanks for lending me your bow earlier. I really appreciate it.”

He didn’t wait for a reply.

“You see… I’m a man who always repays favors,” Luke continued, voice calm and cold. “So don’t worry. I’ll take great care of you… for however long—and painful—that might be.”

As he finished speaking, Luke activated Domineering Air.

A crushing pressure filled the alley.

The man gasped, eyes going wide in terror. His body trembled violently. Then came the stench—the acrid smell of urine as he lost control, overwhelmed by pure fear.

“W-What is it you want?” he sobbed. “I’ll tell you anything—just please don’t kill me!”

Luke's voice dropped into a chilling stillness. “Who sent you?”

The man’s lips trembled. “I-It was the Black Tiger Gang! They—they offered us money to kill you. That’s all I know, I swear!”

Luke’s expression didn’t change, but a flicker of intrigue passed through his eyes. ‘Black Tiger Gang…’ He hadn’t heard of them before. It was likely an underworld faction operating out of the capital.

“And where can I find them?”

“They run the Jade Silk Pavilion,” the man blurted. “It’s the biggest brothel in the city. That’s their base. I swear, that’s all I know!”

He began inching backward, tears and snot streaming down his dirt-streaked face.

“I told you everything. Will… will you let me go now?” he pleaded.

Luke was silent for a long moment. Then he nodded.

“Sure. I’ll let you go,” he said, standing and dusting off his robe.

“R-Really? Thank you! Thank you so mu—”

His gratitude turned to horror mid-sentence.

Luke’s sword plunged into his throat, silencing him instantly. The man gurgled, eyes wide in disbelief, before crumpling into the blood-soaked alley.

“I’ll let you go…” Luke said coldly, pulling his blade free, “…to meet your friends. In hell.”
