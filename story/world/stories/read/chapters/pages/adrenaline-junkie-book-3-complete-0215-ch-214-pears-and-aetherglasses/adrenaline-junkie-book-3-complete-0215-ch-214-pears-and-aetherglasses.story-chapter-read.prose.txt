“Ah," Archie exhaled, voice low but laced with that thin note of annoyance as his eyes lingered on the glowing text box in front of him. D-Grade. Level 105 minimum. The last line in particular stabbed at him like a finger poking his chest, considering this was the first time he’d been under leveled to enter a dungeon.

Five levels. Five racial levels were all that stood between him and walking through those giant carved doors with the rest of them… well, and five Silver coins ironically.

Immediately, Archie felt five or so nudges at his soul.

His jaw flexed as he flicked his gaze across the platform again, snapping towards five general locations from whom he felt used Identify on him. However, due to the sheer number of people he assumed to be Adventurers and the constant shifting they did within place, he couldn’t identify exactly who Identified him. With that in mind, he took a couple of steps forward and merged with a large crowd and began Identiying people left and right while making sure to constantly keep moving and not keeping his eyes in one place.

[Human Lv 105]…[Dwarf Lv 107]…[Human Lv 127]

After Archie’s mass Identify spree, he noticed that there were actually quite a few others who didn’t fit the mold of being Level 105+. Said people were the vendors who had set up shop; most were Level 100 to Level 103, seemingly setting up shop here immediately after evolving.

While exploring the ring-shaped platform that circled the dungeon, Archie’s ears picked up the sound of steel being hammered. Normally, he wouldn’t have paid much attention, as there were smiths scattered about the platform edge trying to sell and forge wares, but compared to the other smiths’, this person’s hammering was in a league of its own.

Hell, it sounded just as good as his own, maybe even better.

I need to see it personally to see how it compares to my own before I go off to hunt for money, he thought to himself as his eyes locked onto the source.

The smith was a redhead, her hair tied back in a messy ponytail, loose strands plastered to her sweat-slicked temple. She worked with incredible focus to the point where it was visible to him, even with the crowd of people between them.

As sparks spat up into the air, clattering on the planks of her stand that showed off her armors and weapons, and bouncing off her apron. Her green eyes narrowed under the glow of her forge as she brought the hammer down again, again, again, trying to fix the shape of a reheated dagger.

She was so focused that she didn’t so much as twitch when a group of rowdy adventurers laughed too loudly beside her stand or when the dungeon doors would loudly get pushed open.

[Human – Lv 100]

“Eh, screw it,” he muttered, keeping his hands at his side and moving toward the red-haired female smith’s stand. “Social embarrassment be damned; if I get ignored, then it is what it is.”

As Archie was making his way to the red-haired female smith, he saw that she was setting down her hammer atop her anvil. Lifting the no longer glowing red dagger with a pair of tongs, she turned it under the light of her forge and rotated it in order to look at all of its angles – and judging by what she was seeing, she didn’t like it as her lips were now pressing thin and her eyes lingered on quite a few impurities that lingered within the metal.

She let out a click of her tongue before she tossed it back into her forge.

By the time she straightened up and reached for a nearby rag, she caught sight of Archie standing across from her stall.

“Hello,” Archie said, keeping his tone even, casual.

“Hullo,” she returned, voice carrying that lilting accent as she rubbed soot off her cheek with the rag. “Wot can I do fur ye?”

Archie’s brows furrowed slightly at the sound. While she was speaking in USL, the rhythm and twist in her words were heavy, rough, and to his ears sounded very Scottish. Could she be from Earth? Is she also a Tutorial Participant?

“Ah,” Archie started, raising his hands a little. “I was just wondering if you could help out another smith with a question. I’d offer a metal for payment.”

He reached into his pouch and pulled free an ingot, its sky-blue sheen catching the light of the forge. Two large Aetherium ingots. He held them out just enough for her to get a good look, giving the both of them a little wave.

Bralmir bless, Bralmir for the many different crates of metals he put inside my Spatial Rings, Archie thought, watching her eyes locked onto the metal like a hawk spotting prey.

“They’re attuned to Wind and Lightning,” he added, rolling the ingots in his palm so that their facets shimmered with faint static. “Really durable too,” he added.

Her gaze flicked towards his face at his words, only to snap back to the metal bars again as she activated Identify on them both and read their System Descriptions. Archie could visibly see her jaw clenching down, as if she was holding back the urge to snatch it straight from his hands and immediately start forging with it to see what she could do with it.

Still, after a long beat, she tore her eyes from the ingots and looked back at Archie’s face.

Her expression shifted ever so slightly. A crease formed between her brows as she tilted her head, squinting at his face that was partially hidden behind the rim of his Headdress of the Savage Ursine.

“…Ye look a wee bit famli’r,” she said slowly, her voice dipping lower, cautious now. “Ya’ mind takin’ off dat dress on ye noggin?”

Archie blinked once. Twice. He raised a hand and gave the edge of the fur-lined headdress a tug, lips quirking at her choice of words.

“Dress?” Archie asked dryly.

“Aye,” she said, crossing her arms, green eyes narrowing with a bit more bite. “Dat silly bear rug ye got strapped to yer skull. Take it off, eh? I wanna see ye face proper.”

“Anyways, I only deal wi’ folk proper if they show their faces. That’s one of me rules.”

Archie hesitated, fingers still on the rim of his headdress. “Okay...”

Confused at her request but not seeing any reason to make a fuss about it, Archie tugged the fur-lined headdress off his head, shaking free his dark brown hair that was as unkempt as ever and sending the headdress into the Spatial Storage Cube in order to keep appearances that that was the only Spatial Storage he had on hand.

The smith’s eyes widened for the briefest moment, a flicker of recognition dancing across her face before her brows pulled together.

“Oi,” she said, voice sharp with surprise. “Do ye know some lad called ‘Enry? ’Bout yer height, always looks like he’s in a foul mood like someone woke ‘im up.”

Archie froze at her words at both the name she mentioned and the fitting description of Henry’s newly gained mood after having gone through his Group Tutorial. His eyes narrowed just slightly, though not out of hostility, but more out of shock. “Henry?” he repeated, slower this time. “As in, Henry Gracefield? That Henry?”

“Ye, ye,” she nodded quickly, pointing a soot-smeared finger toward him, eyes widening as she had mentally overlapped Henry’s face over Archie’s. “Yose ‘is brudder or somethin’? Ya look like ‘im.”

Archie let out a breath he hadn’t realized he was holding, shoulders loosening a little. “Yeah. I am. Are you from Earth too? Or did you meet him on that Dwelf planet he was on, or something like that?”

That question made her lips twitch into a quick smirk, like she’d been expecting it. “Ye, I’m an Earthlin’,” she said simply, bringing up the rag to a large pocket just in front of her bosom. “Me name’s Liv. Liv Baird. I used tah be in da Group Tutorial with ‘Enry. Real fucked up one, that was. But–” she gave a little shrug as she stopped for a moment to look off at the side for a moment before focusing back on him, “–me ’n the rest made it out alive thanks tah ’im, killin’ all dem kidnappers an’ slaver like the bastards they were.”

Archie rubbed the back of his neck, exhaling slowly. “Yeah, I heard from Henry that it was pretty fucked up.” He shifted, then reached forward and set both of the Aetherium ingots onto her anvil with twin muted thunks.

“Now that I think about it…” Archie tilted his head, studying her again as a few memories began popping up and solidifying his view of her. “I do remember my brother talking about a red-haired Scottish smith who forged him some decent equipment back in the first half of the Tutorial,  and again at the end just before… You know...  He said you were one of the few he trusted to not give him junk after what had happened, and how everyone who focused on their Professions distrusted those who didn’t, as they believed him to be working with those who kidnapped and enslaved them.”

Liv snorted, though she looked faintly pleased all the same. She tugged her ponytail tighter and planted her fists on her hips. “Aye, tha’ was me. Though if ye ask me, yer brudder wouldn’t know a proper tool if it smacked ’im in the face, wi’ how often he just nabbed somethin’ off the ground an’ fought wi’ it. Still – aye, I wos dere. Me name’s Liv Baird, like I said. And before ye ask, I wos visitin’ mah sistah, but just like ‘Enry ‘n Maria, I got tossed inta a differ’nt Tut Group than dem. And aye, I’m Scottish, born ’n’ raised there.”

Archie gave a slow nod, his expression softening as she confirmed who she was. “Well, Liv, I owe you for that. For looking after his gear when I couldn’t. So, if there’s anything at all I can do for you, just say it.”

He dug into his belt pouch, scribbling something quick on a scrap of parchment before sliding it across the anvil. “That’s my Aetherglass number. Shoot me a message if you ever need help outside the Event. And if you need something else in the meantime, you can find me or Henry in his shop back in Creavenboor. Place is called ‘Woods R Us.’”

Liv chuckled at the name, shaking her head as she plucked the slip of paper from his hand. From her own spatial storage, she pulled out her own Aetherglass she bought from the System Bazaar, a little messy but practically in perfect condition, and started tapping away.

“I’ll keep dat in mind,” she said with a grin that was all teeth. “Havin’ da num’er one of da Tutorial owe me a favor sounds… ah, real nice.”

Archie gave her a crooked smile, but before he could say more, Liv tilted her head.

“Oh, by da way, did ya know dat if ye put two Aetherglasses togetha in a closed-off zone, dey ‘reate a wee link? I learned dat from an elf friend back in a swamp. Handy trick.”

Archie blinked. “Wait… really?” He fished out his own Aetherglass immediately, holding it up to hers and pressing them together experimentally.

“Does it work like… a peer-to-peer network?” he asked, brow furrowing as his mind immediately went technical. “Like, they communicate directly when cut off from the towers that the System’s scattered around?”

Liv tilted her head, eyes narrowing as she repeated his words with the same cadence but twice the confusion. “Peer ta what?”

Archie coughed into his fist. “Uh. It’s… a type of–”

“Da fuck does a pear gotta do wit’ Aetherglass?” she interrupted, looking at him as though he’d told her that the sun was green.
