Stalker

Dorian. I needed to find Dorian. He wouldn't remember me, not if Ananke actually did her job. Last we parted, it had been in heated violence, and I was left hoping that any residual feeling would be scrubbed clean. I didn't like starting fresh, especially not with how volatile he could be, but I knew him well enough by now that I could make it right.

Meeting him in [The Fields Have Eyes] hadn't been possible. I tried, but I wasn't let through. Thankfully, he hadn't left the Dream proper, so there was still time to get him on the right path. I looked at my options for Boons and Restrictions and balked. He really did have a shitty kit this time.

[Open the Path] was good, but not good enough to warrant it as a one-time use. No Good Deed was plain awful. A full heal was good, but there were hundreds of Boons that would do something similar without incurring an additional Restriction.

Most egregious of all were the Restrictions. Stalker was perfect. He should never unequip that. Untrustworthy crippled him. Dorian filled the Face portion of a party. In a more competent group, he could get away with Flex, but this was not a competent group. He was best when deciding where others froze. The decisions sometimes sucked, but at least he made them. More often than not, making any decision trumped making no decision in the Dream. Taking away his ability to interact and trick people properly weakened him considerably.

I sighed in the liminal space of existence. Ananke was kind enough to make it an actual space instead of an empty void, and then I took care of the rest. Kicking my feet up on the coffee table, I sank deeper into the couch and turned up the volume on the TV. From it, I continued to watch over Dorian's shoulder, as I had from the very start of the Instance.

I had lost hope when he had a shit spawn.

I despaired when Jeren made the decision to frame him as a Fiend.

I wanted to strangle Krevith and Maerin when I met them.

Dorian had really only started acting like himself towards the end, there with the third Visitor. It wavered. The Dorian I knew would've strong-armed his way into leading the group underground, but that was fine. He didn't need to be that person, yet there was still plenty of time to guide him.

The TV just showed Dorian and Co. making their way through the forest towards the spire. It gave me time to let my mind wander. I didn't know if I wanted to go after Dorian or the group of Survivors. Since I was part of Stalker, I was forced to try to get Dorian to fail the Instance, but I knew him better than anyone else—including Rowan. Even if I went after the group of Survivors, there was a good chance Dorian would last an hour and come in and ruin everything.

We used to joke that Dorian always won, like it was some universal law, like Presence. The only way I could confirm that the lose condition was met was if I killed him first. It's why being in Stalker wasn't that bad a handicap for me. Because of who I was dealing with, it gave me a lot of freedom.

I had to decide what was more important to me. Dangling the carrot in front of Dorian or killing Maerin and Krevith. I checked the timer. I had another ten minutes to decide, and it wasn't going to be an easy decision. A distraction presented itself.

The group had reached the edge of the tree line, and the lurid crimson glow of the half-sentient moon gripped the space not covered by trees. Space warped in waves at the fragment of the Instance Holder's undivided attention. Dorian didn't notice it, but I could see it causing ripples in the underlying space, not just in visible sight. One moment, an unprotected sapling was next to the lake, the next it was striding on top of the water, and it was actually there, no trick of the light.

I took the thought to its conclusion, satisfaction touching my lips, and I looked up to the canopy. I was right. A ways up the branches were hanging in space, not connected to the other trees by any means. The collective forest and the tenuous memory that they were supposed to be up there held them aloft. It confirmed to me that Orlan was telling the truth about the moon containing a fragment on an Instance Holder.

I should keep that one alive. Dorian always needed competent allies. My long-time friend wasn't making that easy, though. He was mid-shrug, an exhausted expression on his face. "I don't know. Honestly, I thought both of you would be dead by now."

Orlan fidgeted awkwardly at that, but was brave enough to keep Theon behind him—another point in his favor. "So … so are we free to go then?"

I shook my head, clicking my teeth. Wrong decision. The best thing to do when dealing with Dorian was to just do the thing when he was feeling numb. If you asked or gave him time to consider the consequences, he'd… well, consider the consequences, I suppose. I moved myself to have a better view of the conversation. They were by the lake. There weren't many trees leading to the spire, and even fewer on the route they were on. [Shroud of Nothing] would stop the light from hurting Dorian if he submerged himself. The other two would be shit out of luck, though.

"No." He finally said.

"W-well then how are we all supposed to get there?" Orlan asked in turn. Damn. Yeah, if he survived for the next … seven minutes, I'd have to tell the old man to never end another sentence with a question.

Dorian continued to stare the pair down, no doubt contemplating murder. To my surprise, he didn't just attack the two. "The only other option we have then is to find an underground tunnel or me tying you up here."

Theon countered, voice barely above a whisper and seeming more to speak for the desire for a third option more than the belief it would work, "Can we swim under the water?"

"Not unless we can make a safety raft with an air pocket."

"There's enough sticks and—"

"Not enough time. Tied up or tunnels. Pick." A smile touched my face at Dorian's words. I missed him. Hearing his voice again. It was like nothing else. I had started to give up hope that I'd ever see him again, and it spurned the very essence of my heart that he wouldn't know me like I knew him. Yet. I reminded myself. We were both still here. There was time to correct that flaw.

Theon completely shrank back, which only left Orlan to answer. "You can tie us up here."

"I changed my mind," Dorian shook his head. I couldn't tell if he considered something else or didn't like the answer. "We're finding tunnels instead."

There was brief arguing, but the outcomes were already determined, so instead I looked across the couch to my roommate. I had completely stripped the figure of all of his stats and Presence. He was no more than the outline of a person. Referring to him as 'he' was too much. That was only valid in the memory of their existence, and as far as I was aware, I was the only one who met them. Cases like this gave a certain power of definition. Hmm. I considered it more of an 'it' than a 'he', so it became an it. It had been amenable to sharing the space inside [Stalker], taking turns and working together, but I hadn't been. I wasn't really a sharer.

I looked at my roommate with a dissatisfied purse to my lips. I wanted a buddy I could talk to, at least someone to quip at when nothing interesting was happening. A project for another time, I didn't have the bandwidth to design a personality right now, not to my satisfaction.

My fingers drummed nervously on my knee. Just three more minutes. My heart drummed in my chest to the point of being painful. I wanted to see him again so badly that I couldn't contain it much longer. That thought confirmed it. No matter how I wanted to kill Maerin and Krevith, I wanted to talk to Dorian more. I had so many things to tell him, so many strings to lay out in front of him to get what I wanted and—

Something Orlan said drew my attention. "If you leave us here I'll tell you why the Instance Holder hates you so much." He looked at Theon hard, "God refereed to him as a brother of the Dreamspawn? You're sure of it?" Theon nodded.

I flipped the table into the TV. It crashed against it, but didn't shatter its surface. I hadn't designed it to shatter.

Fuckers. They were getting rid of my leverage, and they didn't even know it.

I breathed deeply. I still had a lot, and maybe he'd get something wrong, maybe even I would learn something new. I leaned in close and listened as the old man began recounting events.

Orlan

"Not many people remember it. Our God wiped the event from most people's memories. Only those of sufficient import were allowed to keep the knowledge." I watched the boy carefully, ready to throw myself at him or away at a moment's notice. His brow twitched, but other than that, he made no other moves.

"Many years ago, a Visitor came to the world, but he didn't come in through an Instance. All the safeguards put into place to stop an invasion were reduced to nothing. We don't know how long he was there for. He watched and waited, for what I don't know.

When he acted, it was impossible to ignore. The sky sundered apart, leaving a vibrant crystalline lattice of stars so dense it looked like broken glass. From the tear in reality, he summoned flaming meteors that impacted every major building." That got a stronger reaction. Something about the stars and the sky had bothered him, but instead of a violent response, he seemed cowed, fearful almost. I continued.

"They were naught but shells for the swarms inside of them. They acted more like a tide than a group of bugs. They fell on us, stripping people so thoroughly that not even their souls returned to the River. Those bugs. It's where we came up with the idea of Fiends, and popularized it through text into our worlds zeitgeist.

The Visitor, your brother," another flicker of emotion from the Visitor, this time sadness, "floated in the air, just staring at the events unfold. He was expressionless the whole time. When our God … the Instance Holder finally arrived, they both disappeared into a white box. Sometime later, the box disappeared, and after a week, the Instance Holder came back. He repaired the land, erased people's memories, gave us new directives, and then shattered himself into multiple pieces.

I don't know why your brother did such a thing, but our Instance Holder referred to him as Dreamspawn, and he said it with so much hatred. The only thing I can imagine is that he's taking it out on you. This term, Dreamspawn, does it mean anything to you?"

I saw the Visitor stare off into space, looking through me completely. I held my breath, unwilling to make a single move. Only two things gave me solace. That this Visitor seemed nowhere as powerful as his brother, and that the protections of this being an Instance would protect us from the reality manipulations his brother had done.

"Alright," The boy finally said, "you can go."

Then, with that, the Visitor turned and walked away, not even bothering to tie either of us up.
