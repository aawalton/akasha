“Oh, Quinn.” Rhea’s voice lifted with a note of pleasant surprise as she caught sight of Viktor coming through the main doors of the Guild. “What are you doing here?”

He gave her a shrug. “Came to see if there’s any news. You’re still working?”

The girl nodded, a few stray locks of hair slipping free as she did, and she brushed them back behind her ear. “Just a little more to do.” She glanced down at the ledger she was holding. “Twenty minutes, perhaps, and I’ll be finished.”

Looked like she had been “promoted” from a normal receptionist to a receptionist-who-also-did-the-work-of-a-Junior-Secretary, much like Claire before her.

“I’ll go look around, then. We can walk back to the Sanctuary together once you’re done.”

“Ah... yes. That... that sounds like a fine plan. Yes, let’s do it.”

Was it his imagination, or had her cheeks gone faintly pink? Oh, well. Whatever. With a wave of his hand, he left the girl to her work and made his way toward the mess hall.

Celeste was still chewing through the endless babble the quill had been scratching out, most of which was worth less than the parchment it was written on. Empty gossip, idle banter, the type of meaningless chatter that didn’t amount to anything of substance. Good thing he had delegated the tedious work of sorting wheat from that mountain of chaff to someone else. Nevertheless, it would likely take quite a bit of time before she fully parsed it all and arrived at anything remotely useful.

Of course, he had no intention of simply standing around and waiting for the information she might or might not eventually manage to extract. There were other ways for him to gather intelligence, after all.

Cedric was serious about winning this competition, and Isadora, after being horrified that her son had once again put himself in danger, would certainly make full use of her connections to learn as much as she could about the team Iskora was assembling, in order to give him the best possible advantage. And that was why Viktor had come here today. He was hoping they might have stumbled upon something worth knowing.

He stopped just past the entrance of the mess hall, letting his gaze slowly sweep across the chamber and take in every familiar detail. Still the same rowdy adventurers, talking too loudly and laughing too easily over their drinks, packed in tight rows along the weathered wooden tables that had seen more spilled ale than anyone here would care to admit. It hadn’t changed at all, not in any way that mattered, compared to the time he used to come here to have lunch with Claire.

His eyes roamed the room, looking for a face he recognized. And they did find one, though not who he had expected at all.

So, with a grin, he made his way toward a corner where a bald, gaunt man hunched over a table by himself, dark beard bristling, eyes completely fixed on his cup as though the rest of the mess hall simply didn’t exist.

“So you’ve finally left the inn, huh?”

“You...” Azran glanced up at him. “Cedric must have already told you that I’m now doing some jobs for the Guild, investigating that strange place, right?”

The man before Viktor looked quite different from the last time he had seen him. His unkempt beard was a bit less wild, his eyes were a bit less bloodshot, and his clothes were a bit cleaner. Sure, he was still sitting in a corner, drinking alone without caring about the world around him, exactly as he always had. Nevertheless, this was an improvement. A start. And anyone had to start somewhere.

“Yes, he did,” Viktor said, dropping into the chair beside the Riftwalker without waiting to be invited. “So, have you found anything, I don’t know, unusual about that place?”

“Unusual?” Azran snorted. “The whole damned place is unusual. I’ve never seen anything like it before.”

“But it’s something created by magic of your discipline, right?”

“It is. But asking my opinion of it is like asking a pyromancer his thoughts on someone who conjures the freaking sun. I recognize the patterns, yes, and I recognize the framework underneath it all. But the scale of the bloody thing is just unfathomable. The first time I entered that place, I found myself standing there like an idiot, just staring at it, unable to make sense of what my own eyes were telling me.”

“Well, maybe it’s not the work of one mage, but a coordinated effort of many,” Viktor said.

Azran snorted again. “So you’re telling me a thousand-odd Riftwalkers gathered on Daelin’s doorstep to conduct some grand ritual, and no one noticed anything? Fine, for the sake of argument, let’s say it did happen somehow. But there’s still one thing that remains unexplained. What, exactly, is maintaining the spell right now?”

“You mean...?”

“We mages can create magical artifacts. Like the Bottomless Bags, or the Waystones for the portal I’ve told you before. And that strange space... it’s like a gigantic artifact. The principles are the same; only the scale has been blown up to a whole new level. The thing is, every artifact decays and needs upkeep to stay functional. And something that size ought to need a mountain of upkeep. Yet, as far as I could tell, there’s nothing to maintain it. It simply keeps existing.”

Viktor nodded, turning the words over in his mind. It was true that all magical artifacts deteriorated over time. Even the ringed crosses Khenemhotep made were no exception. No matter whether they were actually used or not, they started bleeding power after a single day, and ran dry completely after three.

So what, then, was keeping the Folded Chamber standing? He had theorized before that it was created by the power of the Abyss. But how about keeping it running day after day without so much as a flicker of instability? Could it be that the Abyss was somehow reaching across the impossible distance, sustaining the thing from thousands of miles away as easily as it had once brought the place into existence? Or maybe the Folded Chamber did deteriorate over time after all, just as any other artifact, only slowly enough that no one had noticed the difference.

But if that were the case, perhaps one day its formation would give out and the whole thing would collapse without warning. When that happened, when the barrier between the real world and that warped pocket of space vanished, everything held inside would come spilling out all at once. All that water, released in a single catastrophic moment. That patch of forest would be flooded, and maybe even Daelin itself might not escape unscathed either.

“Another thing,” Azran continued. “The interior of that space is very similar to a floor of a dungeon. Can you believe it? A dungeon without a Dungeon Core that has only one floor and sits above the ground. So tell me, what is not unusual about that place? Go on. I’m waiting.” He reached for his cup and took a slow sip. “Still, if it’s indeed related to dungeons somehow, then maybe that explains the lack of a need for maintenance.”

“What do you mean?” Viktor asked.

“You may or may not already know this, but the inside of a dungeon can contain much more space than should physically fit under the ground above it. Yes, the same with that strange place we’re talking about. Now, do you know what happens to a dungeon when you remove the Core?”

Viktor had to suppress a small chuckle at that. Azran was a Dungeon Reaver; the guy knew better than anyone else what happened to a dungeon once its Core was gone.

“I heard that it would gradually diminish. Becomes nothing but an empty cave.”

“That’s what happens to the contents, yes, to everything the Core had been sustaining up until the moment it was taken away. The stonework erodes and crumbles. The still water turns stagnant, then foul. Anything kept alive by the Core’s mana slowly dies off. The monsters either turn on each other or abandon the place to find food elsewhere. But this is strictly about the things inside the dungeon. The dungeon itself, as in the boundaries that hold the space together and keep it from collapsing, remains exactly the same. Thousands of years later, it’s still there, as solid and intact as the day it was first formed.”

That much was true. People talked about the dungeon being reduced to an empty cave as if it were the most normal thing in the world. But it was anything but. There should not be a cave remaining; the whole place should explode the moment the Dungeon Core was taken out. Yet, that was not what happened in reality. The shape of dungeons was preserved even after their Cores had long since been taken or destroyed. This differed completely from the magical bags the Riftwalkers created. The moment such a bag lost its enchantment, it would instantly tear apart as every item stored inside spilled out at once.

“Strange, isn’t it?” Azran said. “The Dungeon Core is what builds the dungeon in the first place, so naturally it’s what maintains everything once the construction is done. So why doesn’t pulling the Core out cause the whole thing to collapse in on itself?”

Strange, indeed. When the Dungeon Core was no longer there, everything that had once benefited from it was cut off from the mana it provided. Everything except the barrier that isolated the folded space within from the world outside.

“In other words,” Viktor said, “whatever maintains the boundaries isn’t the Core at all, but something else altogether?”

“That’s my thinking. Don’t ask me what that something else is, though. I have no idea.”

Viktor found he took no comfort whatsoever from that answer. So there was something powerful enough to maintain its grip on every dungeon that had ever existed, across every corner of the world, throughout the entire breadth of recorded history. What was it?

Is it... the Abyss?

But.

Putting aside the question of why, if the Abyss was capable of something like that, then it meant its power operated on a scale that was barely distinguishable from the Gods themselves.

And it was an enemy he would inevitably have to fight one day.
