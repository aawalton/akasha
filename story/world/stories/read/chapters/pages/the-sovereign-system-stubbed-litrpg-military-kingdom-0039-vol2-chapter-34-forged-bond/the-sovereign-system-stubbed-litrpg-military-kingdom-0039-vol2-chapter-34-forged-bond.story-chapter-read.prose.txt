Luke emerged from sleep just as the sun crested over the horizon. A breeze kissed his face, and the damp chill of morning dew clung to the air as he stepped out of the hide tent that had shielded him through the night.

The camp was already stirring with quiet efficiency. Soldiers moved with practiced rhythm, packing their belongings and fastening gear to their horses. Many of the hardened men acknowledged Luke with respectful nods or brief greetings. He returned them with a simple nod and a faint smile.

Not far off, Stanley Prichard was securing the saddle on his mount. When he noticed Luke, the older soldier stood at attention and cupped his fist in salute, his posture exuding deep respect.

“Good morning, General,” he said.

Luke smiled and offered a silent pat on the shoulder as he passed. The memory of their conversation the night before lingered. Even now, he found the man somewhat pitiable.

After hearing Stanley’s story, Luke had been relieved. He’d feared Lhair had embedded a spy within the expedition, and that their plans had already been compromised. That fear had passed—but it had only given way to a deeper concern.

The Black Tiger Gang, it seemed, would stop at nothing to further their ambitions. Even demanding an outrageous dowry for an aging courtesan showed their shameless cruelty.

Luke might have understood if the courtesan had been young and still profitable to the Jade Silk Pavilion. But Rose was nearing her late thirties—an age when most in her profession quietly retired, no longer able to draw the crowds they once had.

The sheer disregard for human dignity made Luke’s stomach turn.

He knew how ironic that sentiment was, considering his own hands had ushered gunpowder into this world—a weapon that would inevitably take countless lives.

But to him, there was a difference. Soldiers entered the battlefield knowing death might await. The Black Tiger Gang, on the other hand, preyed on the weak, bleeding the lifeblood of Ralis City's most helpless.

Luke recalled the woman and her child he’d seen huddled in the alley when he first arrived in the capital. People like them were starving in the streets while Ming Hu, the gang’s leader, grew fat on their suffering.

“Good morning, General,” came a voice.

Prince Lyon gave a respectful nod, his tone calm.

“Morning,” Luke replied.

Over the past few days, the prince’s demeanor had shifted dramatically. The bratty arrogance was gone, replaced by respectful composure. He no longer spoke out of turn and addressed Luke with the decorum expected of nobility—something unheard of on their first day.

Luke had initially been wary. Even Richard, the prince’s loyal knight, had been baffled. By his account, not even the king had ever seen his son act so properly.

Still, Luke saw no harm in accepting the change at face value—so long as it worked in his favor.

“I see Mr. Prichard is still alive and well,” said a voice from Luke’s side.

Tristan stepped forward, rubbing sleep from his eyes. Fatigue lingered on his face.

“I take it he’s not a threat?” he asked.

“No,” Luke replied. “In fact, he might prove to be a valuable asset.”

Tristan chuckled and shook his head. He chose not to press further.

“Shall we depart? I’m eager to find out just what we’ve gotten ourselves into.”

Luke nodded silently, his gaze sweeping across the bustling camp.

“Saddle up—we're moving out,” he called, voice loud and clear.

“Yes, General!”

The party quickly mobilized, hoisting gear onto horses and tightening saddles. Soon, they were back on the trail. It was the fifth day of the expedition, and they had already spent much of that time traveling along the southern border.

Without the burden of the royal carriage, their originally estimated fifteen-day journey had been trimmed down to just eight.

On the final day, with only ten miles left before reaching the valley beneath Xiandong, the group came to a halt. Instead of pressing onward, Luke, Richard, Tristan, and the second prince stood still, staring intently at the ground before them.

Something was wrong.

“This isn’t good,” Tristan muttered, his brows pulled tightly together.

Only Prince Lyon looked puzzled. “What’s the matter?” he asked, glancing between them for clarity.

“Troops passed through here—thousands of them,” Luke said, pointing toward the worn slope. “Based on the tracks, I’d say at least a thousand were mounted. The rest marched on foot.”

“Could it be our men?” the prince asked, though his voice wavered.

Richard answered before Luke could. “No, Your Highness. We’re at the southernmost edge of the kingdom. Any troops traveling this path wouldn’t be ours,” he added grimly.

“Then whose?”

“There are only two nations south of us,” Tristan said flatly. “Lhair and Selis. Not hard to guess which army marched through here.”

Luke opened his system window, eyes flicking to the mission log.

Progress: 80% remaining.

The number had been decreasing steadily over the past few days. He needed to complete the retreat with at least seventy percent of the army still intact, or the mission would fail. Every delay chipped away at that slim margin.

“Richard,” Luke ordered, “choose your best scouts and send them northeast. The rest of us will press on to the valley and wait for word there.”

“Yes, General.”

As Luke turned toward his horse, Tristan followed, lowering his voice. “Wait, Luke—you're not seriously thinking of scaling the cliff face to reach the fortress, are you?”

Luke didn’t answer immediately. He reached up and stroked the neck of his mount, the silence between them stretching across the dust-choked plain.

“Do you trust me, Tristan?” he finally asked.

The question hung heavy in the air.

“I—I do. Of course I trust you, young Luke,” the old librarian replied, his voice unsure. “If anyone can succeed in this mission, it’s you.”

“But—”

“Good,” Luke interrupted, turning at last to meet Tristan’s eyes. “Then swear your loyalty to me.”

Tristan blinked. “Sorry?”

Luke saw the confusion flash across the older man’s face and had to fight back a smirk. His tone remained calm, his eyes calculating.

He had been considering ways to scale the cliff from within the valley—but now a better plan had surfaced. One that could also serve another purpose.

“Become my subordinate,” he said, placing a hand firmly on Tristan’s shoulder, “and I will grant you power. But your vow must be sincere—otherwise, the gods will not recognize it.”

Tristan looked utterly dumbfounded.

“I’m employed by the Royal Academy,” he said, struggling to find his footing in the conversation. “Swearing loyalty to a student is against the law. I could lose my position—possibly even be thrown into the dungeons.”

Luke nodded, understanding the hesitation.

The Royal Academy was meant to remain above noble politics—a sanctuary of knowledge unsullied by bloodlines or factional games. But he also knew that this rule was more ideal than reality. Most simply ensured they weren’t caught bending it.

“While I say ‘subordinate,’ it’s really more of a friendship,” he said, offering Tristan a warm smile. “In fact, nothing needs to change between us. You won’t need to treat me any differently than you do now.”

“I… still don’t understand what you want me to do,” Tristan said, his voice uncertain.

Luke placed his other hand on the man’s opposite shoulder and looked him directly in the eyes.

“Swear your loyalty to me—with sincerity—and I’ll take you to heights you never imagined possible.”

He saw the flicker of doubt cross the older man’s face. Perhaps he had pushed too hard.

But Luke couldn’t afford to back off now. After learning that Tristan Gale was a Legendary-tier subordinate, securing the man’s loyalty had become a top priority. Not only would it boost his stats, it could be the difference between success and failure in scaling the cliff—and surviving the expedition.

Maybe acquiring a Legendary subordinate is harder than I thought, he sighed inwardly.

Just as he was about to withdraw his request, he noticed a shift in Tristan’s expression. The tension in his brow eased, and a faint, almost nostalgic smile appeared. It softened the lines on his face, making him seem younger—less burdened by time.

“It would be an honor to be regarded as your friend, young Luke,” Tristan said, voice calm. “If it pleases you, these old bones will be happy to follow you from this point onward.”

His words weren’t loud, but they struck Luke with surprising force—deep and resonant, as if they echoed in his very soul.

[DING]

[Tristan Gale has declared his loyalty. Do you accept?]

[Y/N]

A grin crept onto Luke’s lips as the system prompt materialized before him. There was no hesitation.

He mentally selected Yes.

“Good. Now go get ready—we’ll be departing shortly,” he said, patting Tristan’s shoulder with renewed confidence.

“Wait, that’s it?” Tristan asked, blinking.

“Mmm. We’ll talk more later,” Luke replied casually, turning back to his horse.

Only after Tristan walked away did Luke return his focus to the glowing system window before him.

[User has accepted Tristan Gale as a subordinate]

[Congratulations, Legendary tier subordinate acquired]

[Please select a skill]

Will of Conquest (Passive): For every Epic or higher-tier subordinate under the user’s command, all existing skills receive minor boosts in effectiveness. With enough high-tier subordinates, certain skills may evolve into Sovereign-tier variants.

Monarch’s Presence (Passive): Grants a passive intimidation and leadership bonus in both social and combat scenarios. Weak-willed individuals may misjudge the user’s strength as far higher than it is..

Insight of the Throne (Passive): Grants the user a passive ability to detect subtle social dynamics—loyalty, fear, envy, deception—especially in conversations involving multiple parties.

Luke drew in a cold breath as he scanned the list. Each skill was powerful, passive, and strategically valuable in different ways.

Truly rewards befitting a Legendary tier subordinate…

The one that stood out initially was Insight of the Throne. It would prove invaluable in places like Ralis City, where power plays simmered beneath every conversation. With it, Luke could navigate political undercurrents more easily and discern hidden motives with far greater accuracy.

Monarch’s Presence bore a striking resemblance to his existing Domineering Air skill—albeit on a more advanced level. But that similarity was precisely why Luke dismissed it without a second thought.

His attention shifted to the first option on the list.

Will of Conquest…

A passive skill that amplified the power of all his current abilities depending on how many high-tier subordinates he commanded—and with the potential to evolve them into Sovereign-tier forms. Though the term Sovereign-tier was unfamiliar, it didn’t take much imagination to grasp just how powerful such skills might be.

If Scholarly Pursuit were to evolve… would I be able to absorb an entire floor of books in a single sitting? Luke thought, his pulse quickening with excitement.

For someone who valued knowledge so highly, just this prospect alone was worth selecting the skill.

[You have selected: Will of Conquest]

He dismissed the glowing window, expecting some sort of follow-up. A surge of power, perhaps. A secondary prompt. But nothing came.

Several seconds passed in silence. Still nothing.

He let out a quiet sigh, the corner of his mouth twitching with faint disappointment. Mounting his gelding, he turned toward the camp.

Around him, the soldiers were nearly ready—most already mounted, checking their straps and tightening gear. But one figure near the rear was still struggling.

A young soldier fumbled clumsily with his horse’s tack, sweat beading on his brow despite the morning chill.

Luke spurred his mount forward and pulled up beside him. The boy visibly panicked at his approach.

Without a word, Luke dismounted, placing a hand on the restless horse and murmuring soft, calming words. The mare relaxed at his touch.

“G-General, I’m sorry!” the youth stammered, wide-eyed and stiff with fear.

“Calm down. You’ll spook the horse,” Luke said evenly, his focus still on the animal. He tightened the saddle, double-checked the bridle, then inspected the harness with swift, practiced hands.

Once finished, he turned his attention to the young man, who looked only slightly younger than himself.

“What’s your name, soldier?”

The color drained from the boy’s face. But he answered without hesitation.

“Ch-Chase Martin, General!” he barked, cupping his fist and bowing so quickly that he cracked his elbow on the mare’s flank.

The horse flinched and let loose a rear kick that narrowly missed the boy’s ribs.

Luke sighed, pressing his fingers to the space between his eyes. This kid’s going to get himself—or someone else—killed if he’s not watched carefully.

“Chase,” Luke began, placing a steady hand on the young soldier’s scaled armor. The contact made him flinch again, but Luke ignored it.

“You see that man over there?”

Chase lifted his head, following Luke’s pointing finger toward Stanley Prichard.

“From now on, you’ll stick to him like glue. I want you to act as his shadow for the remainder of the mission. Think you can do that?”

“Yes, General!”

“Good. Now calm your horse before you mount. She’s more scared of you than you are of her,” Luke added, giving the boy a reassuring pat on the shoulder.

With the camp finally in order, Luke climbed back onto his own horse and rode toward the front. The two scouts had already departed, leaving him with forty-eight soldiers, along with Prince Lyon, Richard, and Tristan.

He turned to face the column, lifting his voice.

“Ride out!”
