“Here.” I offered Eleonor one of my last plastic bottles.

She pushed it away, leaning against the wall. The effects of the Emperor were harsh, even if done by someone on your own side.

“Drink,” I said in a firm tone.

I didn’t have time for her acting as a child. Less than a minute in the well, and we had already been attacked by a truly indestructible creature. I didn’t know that statues would be so efficient as guards, although I could see the logic. They were reliable, difficult to defeat, and didn’t drop any rewards. One could say that they were perfect for the job. Also, as I had seen, they were unbribable.

With an atypical frown, Eleonor twisted the cap. Her face twisted as she forced a sip down.

“What else will we come across?” I asked.

“I don’t know,” she replied. Her eyes focused on the bottle for a few seconds, after which she took another sip. “Worse than this.”

“Lots of pain for no reward,” I said, looking down the new corridor. “It better be worth it.”

“It’s always worth it,” she muttered.

Nine times out of ten she would be correct. There was a reason for something to be guarded, yet there were always the annoying ten percent of cases that went against the general rule. It wasn’t much, but just enough to fuck things up for everyone, as Avon used to say.

“Do you plan on using… that again?” The goth’s voice trembled as she asked.

“Yes.” There was no point beating about the bush.

The woman took another gulp, then pushed off the wall.

“I’ll take point.” I entered the tunnel before her. “Careful where you step.”

The motionless centipede remained on the floor like a broken ornament. The temptation to smash it echoed in the back of my mind, only to be quickly ignored. I wasn’t the sort of person to get into fights for the sake of it. As long as there was nothing to be gained, avoidance remained the best strategy.

Going through the tunnels of the well was very much like war: long stretches of boredom peppered with moments of absolute terror. Centipedes, spiders, and stone insects would leap out from the walls in a spontaneous attack. Either deliberate or not, they would always focus on me, attempting to carve me up. Thankfully, that made things easier.

Combining the Gatekeeper with the multi-attack of the Ten of Swords proved quite the useful combination.

After what felt like an hour, I decided we stop.

“You’re quiet,” I said as I summoned my cup of water.

“I don’t want to attract attention.”

I downed my cup, then waited for it to fill up again.

“They know we’re here,” I replied. “So, what’s this place?”

“Echoes of the past,” she said with a stern expression. “Actually, I have no idea,” she added a few seconds later. “I just know they decided to keep her here. No idea about the ruins.”

That seemed to be the common theme.

“Any other guards apart from the insects?”

“These aren’t the guards,” she said in a low voice. “You’ll know when you see them.”

Comments such as this never made me feel good. One thing was for certain—the “real” guards had to be stronger than the statues we had come across so far, otherwise they would have been killed by now.

After a series of twists and turns, we reached another dead end. Taking her time, Eleonor pressed the tiles on the wall in a specific order, then stepped back as a new opening formed. Luckily, there were no surprises. We entered a wider tunnel with a large canal of murky water in the middle. The amount of aether glowing beneath the surface was unusual. It wasn’t concentrated, flowing about in large streaks, as if leaking from some underwater container further up.

The divine blades moved in the direction the water was coming from.

“Upstream?” I turned to Eleonor.

“Yes, that— Look out!”

I leaped to the side just in time to avoid a heavy strike that whooshed by me, smashing the marble tiles I had been standing on.

Red Stone Guardian Statue

Level: 25

Cards: NONE

Special: Overburdening Strength, Copycat

Weapon: War Hammer

Another level twenty-five? I used Harlequin’s Sight on my attacker.

Appearance-wise, he was no different from a standard late medieval knight made of red marble, wearing plate armor. Several things stood out, though. For starters, Harlequin’s Sight wasn’t supposed to work on statues. They weren’t considered alive, so they followed their own set of rules.

Another oddity was the level of persistence. Given the different natures and sizes of the statues, there were bound to be variations.

Salvos of aether strikes bombarded the statue from the side. Both had taken the initiative on this one. I quickly joined in with a multitude of thread strikes.

The damage inflicted on the statue was negligible, but pushed it back several steps.

Shit! Something in my mind clicked.

“It’s not a statue!” I shouted as I charged at the stone entity. Of all the enemies I had to face, I dreaded this type the most.

Stoneweaver, I thought. That was the card that was being used against us right now. All the creatures we had faced since going down the well were created by one person using that card. I had seen his work many times in the past, but didn’t know anyone who had actually seen him.

At present, I couldn’t be certain that it was the same entity. Cards, even unique ones, changed hands more often than one might think. The monster I knew who used that card had been present in several trials. Like now, the first time we had come across their handiwork, my group had assumed we were dealing with statues. As it turned out, they were merely similar.

The Stoneweaver granted the ability to mold entities of stone. Insects, spiders, people, even entire buildings could be constructed through its skills. Half of my last group had died trying to keep them at bay. I had lost friends more than once and still had nothing to show for it.

“Copy this!” I combined my multi-attack with a power thrust.

The point wasn’t to injure the statue—I knew that we didn’t have the strength for that—but to push him back. As Avon liked to say, there was more than one way to win a fight.

Restrict! Restrict! Restrict!

I doubled my efforts, increasing the intensity of my attacks as much as my body would allow.

Sparks filled the air as marble clashed against indestructible silver. Then, the statue took a step back. It didn’t seem like much, but it was the start I needed. My attacks combined with those of the goth were already more than the entity could endure.

“Obey!” I used the Emperor’s skill again.

For a split second, the statue lost concentration. The resistance he was putting up faltered, letting the strikes landing on him shove him into the canal.

“Ele!” I returned the blades to my inventory.

The woman hadn’t fallen to the floor, but it was obvious that she wasn’t all there. Her attacks had stopped, leaving her standing like a puppet with half its strings cut.

We don’t have time for this! “Ele!” I grabbed her with one hand, placing her on my shoulder.

There was no sign from the red guardian. Only ripples from the splash could be seen. One of the issues with entities of stone was that it took a lot of effort to make them swim. I had no doubt that it was still in one piece. Given enough time, it would climb out and resume the fight.

“Snap out of it!” I ran forward.

“Why…” she muttered, her words clear thanks to my Listener card, “did… you use…”

“Can you swim?” I asked, ignoring everything she was saying.

No answer.

“Can you swim?!” I repeated.

“Yes.” Her voice was stronger, although she didn’t sound like she had fully recovered.

That was why I hated working with genius rookies. They always broke down facing the unexpected and took three times more time to recover. Back at the bottom of the well, and even before when we had faced the Scorpion Queen, it had taken her roughly a minute to snap back to reality. We didn’t have that much time here.

“Get ready!” I held her tightly, then swerved, jumping into the canal.

The state of the water seemed terrible from the outside. One could only imagine what lay within. It couldn’t be called sewer water, but it came rather close.

I used the Magician card to try to form a pocket of air around us. That didn’t work, I resorted to the Ten of Chalice, covering the upper part of my face with a layer of ice.

Eleonor struggled. I felt her kicks on the left side of my body. Even with the water reducing their impact, the pain was significant.

I released my grip, letting her swim away. My greatest fear was that I’d have to step in again and save her from herself, but thankfully she came to terms with the whole situation rather fast.

At least you’re not an idiot. I pointed forward, checking if she was able to see in the muck. Her reaction told me that she could.

For a full minute we kept on swimming beneath the surface, before the goth moved up to get some air.

Not even a minute? I grumbled in my mind. That was less good. It meant that the risk of us being seen just tripled.

Removing the ice barrier on my face, I swam up as well.

“What was the point of that?” the woman asked. Her voice was shivering, possibly due to the cold.

I looked around, making sure there were no threats. There didn’t seem to be anything dangerous in sight. Just walls, not even an opening to other corridors. The canal we were in must have been the major sewer of a prosperous city at some point. The city must have been reduced to ruins millennia ago, but the underground section had remained.

“Climb up,” I said. It was a bad idea, but if I didn’t deal with her hypothermia, things would get far worse later on.

Serp, do you sense anything? I asked.

Just broken dreams and promises, the blade replied. There won’t be a single sphere out of this. You’re risking our lives for nothing.

Can you take on Hypnos?

The conversation abruptly ended. As rebellious as Serp was, even he knew that he had no chance against a deity.

“Hold a bit,” I went up to Eleonor.

Reaching down, I placed my hand on the floor and cast a fire spell. Flames came into being, burning on the marble. They didn’t need any wood or paper to keep going. Sadly, given my low magic, they were no larger than a small bucket.

I added a few more circles on the wall and did the same.

“Stand here,” I said. “You should feel better.”

Trembling, the woman did so.

“It’ll be faster if you undress,” I said. “Your choice, though.”

“What about you?”

As she said it, steam came off every part of my clothes—one of the benefits of wearing a gift from the deities.

“I don’t need to,” I said. It also helped that I could use spell circles.

Her reluctance was apparent, but ultimately, the cold proved too strong to ignore. The goth undressed, piling the clothes on the floor, then moved closer to the flames. All the time she kept her back to me.

“Those weren’t statues,” I said, remaining on guard. “We’re dealing with a Grand Arcana holder.” I paused for a moment. “I know that it can’t be the one we’re hunting, or we wouldn’t have come here alone. So, what’s really going on?”

“I told you.” She glanced at me over her shoulder. “We’re here to obtain information. I… knew that she’d be heavily guarded, but not that a Grand would be here.”

“A level twenty-five,” I added. The only reason our opponent wasn’t higher was because the trial didn’t allow it. “Can’t you use your powers?”

“If it were simple, don’t you think Harlequin would have found them?” she snapped back at me.

This was nothing more than the usual backlash, so I let it pass. If nothing else, finally I was looking at part of the real Eleonor, not the fake layers of confidence and deceit she usually wrapped herself in.

“My power doesn’t always work on deities,” she said after a while. “I’ve tried. That’s how I found her.”

“Who?”

“Another seer. She’s been imprisoned here by the same person we’re hunting.”

So, that was it. “You made a deal.” I wasn’t sure whether to be pissed or impressed. Pissed because I didn’t know what games she was playing and impressed that she had managed to pull it off. “What do you get in return?”

“I told you—information.”

“Right.” I didn’t believe that for a second.
