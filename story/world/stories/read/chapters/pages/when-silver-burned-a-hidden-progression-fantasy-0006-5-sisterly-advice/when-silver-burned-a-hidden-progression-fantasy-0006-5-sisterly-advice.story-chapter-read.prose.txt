People intent on making it in this world are varied, but in the end, only three types fall through the sieve: the resolute, the weary, and the charitable. The first are those who think they have all the necessary information at their disposal and pursue their coin one at a time, focused, obsessed to master a singular trail. The second always seeks new paths to lead them toward wealth, no matter how much they have cobbled together in their nest, the backup plan of a backup plan of a backup plan formulating before the first stage had taken its course. The third, well; the third is the exception. The unlikely few who—in the grand scheme of things—don't exist.

Kalsin thought himself resolute, so perhaps that's why, for his benefit, Cinder took great lengths to stand among the weary.

"A goat?"

"A goat."

Kalsin eyed the white, horned head of a dead mountain-hopper strapped behind Sap's saddle. "Now you've just got me confused. You're tracking a mythos with—the severed head of a goat."

"No, not tracking it, I've got that part figured out. This, we'll need for later." Cinder shrugged. "Superstitious folk call it a summoning. That's all nice and easy if you believe some such deity watches over every nook of the world at every given moment. Summoning? Please. So before the discussion turned sour—with me being the respectful cynic that I am—I compromised with my own, somewhat less far-fetched definition. Bait, I call it."

"How diplomatically pragmatic of you."

Cinder was waving a map she'd bought when they crossed into the Republic's region of Grenstel. It had been fresh paper in the beginning, but by now the map could've been mistaken for a half-hearted attempt at abstract art, crowded by circles and lines and scribbled words in no apparent order. Kalsin could just about make out Remallon around the center.

"Right there." Her one functional hand full holding the map, Cinder automatically raised her stump before she caught herself trying to point. "Uh, slightly north-west from the city. See? This fracture leads right along a forest-island. Rumored to be haunted, as of two Blindlights past. A small piece of land with no documented inhabitants, and with only a few smaller settlements bordering the general area. A smaller fracture branches off and into the forest, leading to an idyllic little waterfall. Apparently folk used to visit it, back in the day."

No inhabitants meant no coins left behind, but Kalsin held his mouth to hear Cinder out.

"You're thinking what I was thinking, but. But. I stayed around the hunter’s respite long enough to catch another piece of news. Tragedy befell one of those settlements I mentioned. An entire family just poof, disappeared, gone. Until the father's body was found farther down the fracture, carried by the current. With a very... peculiar cause of death."

Kalsin gave an anticipatory hum as he gently patted Sap's neck, taking care to avoid the vertically slitted gills. The bahten gave a low neigh, pushing his long snout into his face. A serpentine tongue nibbled his ear.

"Tendons, Kal. The man had his ankles gaping open, and he was missing his tendons. Like something had sliced the skin, grabbed the cords, and wrenched ‘em right out of his body."

His sister’s fervent excitement only made Kalsin silently shake his head. Come on, Cinder. That was just fucking disgusting. "I assume you're about to make a point with all this?"

"Forest. Rivers. Waterfalls, "Cinder listed on her fingers. "A mysterious disappearance. Mythos being mythos. All the events line up with each other. I'd bet it's a fossegrim."

"That doesn't tell me anything," Kalsin said. "Look, I'm about to stand face-to-face with someone who might have the answers we've been seeking for years. Answers about a cure to being a Scarrd. And things could go ugly, or they could go deadly."

"So what, am I better off arranging your funeral instead? Let me finish," Cinder growled. She paced below the stable's rickety planks like a caged lioness, in and out of the beam of moonlight shining through the hole above hazardous remains of the long abandoned place. "So—yes. The family, they were Clenn and quite wealthy. The wedding ring they found on the man's body was worth a fortune. White gold with deep-pearl accents—you could imagine the drama for the spoils. Which means, there's another one of this same ring..."

"...resting with the—likely deceased—wife," Kalsin concluded. The story did have potential. But if Cinder figured it out, others would soon see and follow her line of reasoning.

Maybe a job for a later time. While getting their hands on fresh coin was important, it wasn't their highest priority at the moment.

Cinder, weary to a fault.

Kalsin voiced his concerns about her plan. Cinder waved them away fast as they came. "Hunters tend to overlook legends about benevolent creatures, and the fossegrim falls in that category. But we know better, don't we? To sword-point with every last one of them. We..." She trailed off for a moment, eyes falling on her severed hand. She cleared her throat. Shrugged. "Can't let you have all the excitement. You go right ahead sniffing after the cure, and while you're at it, I'll make sure we have everything to summon—excuse me, lure this creature. We're hunters by profession, after all. And we've got time."

Kalsin hummed thoughtfully. He didn’t answer otherwise, though. Just breathed slowly for a moment or two, eyeing the moon above.

Cinder stepped up to him. She put her good arm around his waist and, after a moment of hesitation, rested her head on his shoulder. “How are you and Jack doing?”

“She agreed to the plan.”

“You know what I mean,” she said. “She… she’s worried about you. More than just a bodyguard. More than just a friend.”

His gaze stayed directed firmly above for a second longer. He looked at his sister. “I… I don’t know what you want me to tell you. There’s just so much to do, and you want to bring this up now?”

“There’s never a bad time to bring up matters of the heart, Kalsin.”

“You know the heart has nothing to do with this. She’s…”

Kalsin shut his eyes, trying, trying to get the image of her scar out of his mind. He let out a slow breath. “She’s incredible. Of course she is, I know that—but my body just doesn’t seem to. And I don’t think it ever will.”

Cinder drew up a brow. “Hey now, don’t talk in absolutes. We’re hunting for a cure, after all. Things could change like that,” she snapped her finger. She then squeezed her arm around him a bit stronger. “Look at you, Mister Broodster over here.”

“Are you just messing with my love life because you’re too lazy to get one of your own? For an older sibling, you sure don’t act like someone nearly a decade ahead of me.”

“Older sibling-by-home, little pal. And more years just means that I’m seeing things more clearly than you. You need to get a sweetheart, and you need to do it yesterday.”

Kalsin just shook his head. There was no point dwelling on this topic. Not until they learned more about the cure.
