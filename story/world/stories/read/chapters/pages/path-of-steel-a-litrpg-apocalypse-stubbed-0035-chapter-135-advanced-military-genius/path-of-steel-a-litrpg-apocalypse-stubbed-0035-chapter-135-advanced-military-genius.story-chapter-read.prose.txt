Oracle Jay specialized in aura control. His aura field extended a full eight feet out of his body, significantly more than Gary’s six feet. He also possessed remarkable aura quantity. His Strength and Vitality were lacking, as Gary had discovered in their previous match, which meant that his Speed was off the charts—one of the reasons he couldn’t catch him.

Should I invest in speed? he wondered. The free points from his last level-ups were still unspent. He was saving them for a time of need. In his fight against Sui Duang, he could judge what he needed most and invest there, but he could also do it now if he really had to.

The problem was, he suspected speed wouldn’t be the deciding factor against Sui Duang.

Can I catch him even if I invest in speed? he wondered. Our difference isn’t small. Sixteen points will probably make up for it, but maybe not.

What else can I do?

How the hell is he reading my mind?

Oracle Jay interpreted Gary’s silence as patience. “Not coming?” he said. “Then, don’t mind if I do!”

He launched forth, his spear blurring in a flurry of strikes that were neither strong nor fast but aimed precisely where Gary struggled to defend. He fended off the first few attacks, steadily pushed off-balance until he was forced to thunderstep away. Oracle Jay gave chase, still gliding over the sand. His energy expenditure remained lower than Gary’s.

“It’s a slow death for you!” he yelled, laughing manically.

“It really is!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed. The crowd started shouting.

“Don’t give up, Baldinator!”

“You can do this!”

“Show him our rage, Jay!”

“Teach him that fur isn’t a game!”

Gary tuned out the many cries to survive Oracle Jay’s onslaught. His spear played fast in his hands, occasionally shooting lightning, but the oracle dodged everything with casual ease. His every move prepared the next. It was impossible to fight someone who could read your mind.

If only I could use the third song… Gary thought. This was precisely the kind of opponent the Song of Rain was made for, but he hadn’t mastered it yet. To have a slim chance of success, he’d need to use the other two songs first, which would greatly consume his already depleted stamina.

I got the moves down, but I never managed to remove the lightning from my aura… Damn it!

Gary was pushed back hard. Left with no respite, he was quickly running out of breath, chased around the arena by Oracle Jay. Every time Gary tried to counterattack, his opponent was already moving back, mockery clear in his gaze, then moved ahead again when Gary stepped back. Their movements were mirrored. Gary was terribly pressured. The Baldinator cries had gone silent now, and the banners waved less spiritedly.

It really was a slow death.

Gary tried to make some distance. He sensed when Oracle Jay caught up—his opponent’s wide and powerful aura field blanketed Gary, not clashing with his own, but coexisting. Unless it attempted to restrict his movement or act against him in some other way, he couldn’t fight it—he couldn’t spare the aura.

Gary was growing tired now. His spear felt heavy. His moves had lost some sharpness, his arms moving in slightly wider arcs that telegraphed his next attack. Trapped in the area of the opponent’s aura field, one didn’t need to be an oracle to read such obvious tells.

Wait, that’s it! Gary realized. He isn’t reading my mind! He’s reading my miniscule tells before every attack! That’s why his aura field is so wide and his control so advanced—his vibration must have something to do with perception!

It made sense now. Gary felt stupid for not realizing it earlier—he might have, if his mind wasn’t numb from all the training. He thunderstepped away, leaving the Oracle’s aura field, then unleashed a storm of thrust-shaped aura attacks.

The Oracle deftly dodged them all. He wasn’t reacting ahead of time anymore, but from this distance, he had more than ample speed to dodge everything anyway.

“So you finally realized it,” the oracle said. “But what can you do about it? That’s the beauty of mind-reading!”

“It appears the Baldinator discovered how the mind-reading works…but even that knowledge can’t save him!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed.

Gary gritted his teeth. It was true. Thunderstepping back and throwing all those aura strikes had taken a lot out of him and achieved nothing. I’ll never hit him from this far away. I should have stuck to Master’s advice, he thought. Okay. Don’t panic. He can read my mind—what can I do about it?

Nothing, was the obvious reply.

No, there has to be a way. He’s only the fifth-ranked disciple. If he was impossible to beat, he’d be number one. He has to have a weakness. His stats are atrocious, besides dexterity. He must have invested in Fortitude to gain his foresight. My body is far superior, but how can I use it if I can’t catch up?

What the hell can I do if he just dodges everything? That’s cheating!

He was snapped out of his thoughts by his back touching a wall. Shit. He thunderstepped away from the oracle’s onslaught, heading for the arena center again.

“The Baldinator is on the run! There’s nothing for him to do other than stall the inevitable. Perhaps Sui Duang will have a better answer tomorrow!”

I haven’t lost yet, asshole, Gary thought, anger propping him up. I need to win. I will win. There must be a way, but what?

He made a few token, distracted attacks just to keep the Oracle off him as he thought. He got more space than he’d expected when the Oracle had to change his trajectory to dodge one attack.

What? Gary thought, snapping into focus. He didn’t read that one?

He threw more attacks, emboldened, but the Oracle read them like a book. Gary took a blow to the ribs for his efforts—thankfully, they held.

There was one attack he failed to read. Did I do something special? Gary wondered. His eyes widened. I was distracted. That’s it! If I don’t know what I’m doing, he can’t know either!

As crazy as it sounded, Gary was running out of hope. He risked it. His aura field pulsed, calming down like he wasn’t in pitched battle.

“What’s that? He’s meditating! The Baldinator is meditating mid-battle!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed. “Did he go crazy from the pressure? Is he disrespecting his opponent? Or does he know something we don’t?”

The audience gasped, cheered, and laughed. Gary ignored them. He caught the narrowing of the oracle’s eyes. It enhanced his resolve.

While distracted by his meditation, Gary stepped forth. His attacks were casual and thoughtless, using less than half of his usual potential. Yet, to his absolute elation, he saw the oracle troubled. He couldn’t read them. He really couldn’t read them.

Gary gave his opponent a smile. The Oracle snarled and attacked wildly. “How arrogant can you be?” he yelled. “Maybe I can’t read these attacks, but it’s only because they’re trash! You can’t beat me like this!”

Gary ran forward, casually flicking his spear. The Oracle wasn’t as relaxed as before. They clashed, Gary slowly gaining an advantage, but his opponent could fight back. They embarked on a fight that was the opposite of pitched.

“These are the weakest attacks we’ve ever seen in the finals!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed, but Gary didn’t care. He pressed on. He was slowly pushing his opponent back, but it was a roughly equal battle, and he remained exhausted. Maybe he’d win. Maybe he’d run out of stamina. Gary tried to keep his mind off these thoughts as he meditated and fought at the same time. It was so weird.

To keep his concentration on meditation, he focused on the most interesting thing. His own attacks. He watched them after they’d been thrown, like a spectator himself, watching the oracle struggle and get pushed away by attacks he considered childish.

There really is balance, he thought. Suddenly, something arrested his attention. Wait! Is that…

He observed another of his attacks. It struck the feline, some of its aura seeping through his block, but his fur didn’t fall. It didn’t even budge. That was because this attack…didn’t contain black lightning!

That’s it! If I relax, my vibration is much easier to control. I see. He warped an evil grin. I see.

Focusing on the aura ahead of his spear, he gathered it in a clump, then thrust through it. “First Song: Mud!” he shouted. A slow thrust left his spear. It was deceptively powerful, but Oracle Jay wasn’t an idiot to stand there and take it. He easily sidestepped the strike.

“Second Song: Life!” Gary yelled, unleashing a powerful, regenerating strike that missed the oracle and crashed into the back wall.

“Such slow attacks will never touch me!” Oracle Jay exclaimed.

Gary grinned. “Third Song: Rain.”

The aura gathered on his spearhead. It was shaped as distinct drops, none interacting with the others, each a miniature thrust. Keeping himself distracted was the exact opposite of how he usually attacked, but he achieved it with sheer willpower. He slashed. An impressive amount of aura was spent on the attack, draining most of his reserves. The aura fanned out, covering a wide area and leaving no space to dodge. It wasn’t slow. Oracle Jay cried out as miniature thrusts rained on him. His block was useless. He gathered his aura field in a barrier ahead of his body, blocking some of the drops, but even individually, they weren’t weak. Many penetrated. His body was rocked from side to side, tiny wounds opening everywhere, and he vomited a mouthful of blood. What little fur he had was in disarray.

Most importantly, to form that aura barrier before himself, he’d withdrawn his aura field.

Gary thunderstepped. He arrived before the oracle, spear pulled back to strike, and the man could not react. “Not again,” he pleaded as Gary’s spear struck him in the chest. Black lightning exploded. The oracle flew back like a broken kite, smashing hard against the wall and raising sand. His body collapsed, all his fur falling again.

The audience remained in stunned silence for a few moments. Then, all together, they erupted in cheers. Only the felines were silent.

“Gary Steel is the winner!” Rollo Frollo exclaimed. “He had that move from the start but wanted to play a bit. What a born showman! What a bold young man!”

“Fuck you,” Gary muttered under his breath.

“BALDINATOR! BALDINATOR!” the audience cheered.

“My beautiful fur…” a whimper came from across the arena. Gary hesitated a moment, then paced for his opponent. The crowd of felines who’d already jumped down from the stands glared at him as he made his way through them. Oracle Jay lay on the sand, pink and hairless like he’d just been born. Seeing Gary, he raised his gaze.

“You could have held back in the final strike. I would have admitted defeat,” he said.

“I didn’t know that,” Gary replied, extending a hand.

Oracle Jay stared at Gary’s hand, then shook his head. He stood up by himself. “Please don’t ever fight me again,” he said, jumping into the sands.

“What a show of grace from the winner! What magnanimity!” Rollo Frollo said as the audience cheered. Even the felines weren’t as pissed anymore, though they still threw him dirty gazes.

“That concludes the quarter-finals, folks! Tomorrow, Feng Shen fights Lin Xin—the battle of the generations—while Sui Duang and Gary Steel will clash in a long-promised Duel of Honor! There will be no judge, no restraints. Murder is allowed. No matter what you do—don’t miss it!!!” Rollo Frollo screamed, and the curtain fell.
