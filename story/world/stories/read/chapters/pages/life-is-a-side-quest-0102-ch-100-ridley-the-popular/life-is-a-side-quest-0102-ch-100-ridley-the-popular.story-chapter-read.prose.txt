Ridley sat in the corner of the cafeteria staring at the wall. She shivered as she felt eyes on her back. She sensed the wary attention of a pretty senior girl approaching her table. Ridley turned a glare on the girl, but the girl ignored it and strolled right up to Funk Master, who turned and beamed a smile at her.

“Hey Louis, I was wondering when your next concert is? My friends and I really want to come check y’all out.”

“Of course! You and your friends will be most welcome. Always nice to have pretty faces in the crowd to dedicate a song to!” He smiled charmingly.

He was wearing his sunglasses—an annoying trait he had picked up because they made him feel more comfortable and thus able to access his charisma with a nice barrier between him and reality.

“This Saturday at the Scat Jazz Lounge. We go on at four, that leaves plenty of time to hit up dinner with the fans if you want to join the afterparty.”

“Of course! We’ll see you there,” she said with a flirty smile as she turned and walked back to her table.

“YES!” The hefty drummer Charlie pumped his fist. “You will hit the five-hundred-fan mark soon!”

“And complete your quest!” the bass player Thomas said.

The atmosphere had certainly shifted since school started again after the impromptu state-of-emergency break. It was now a pretty well-established fact that Ridley and Funk Master were Chosen. The video of Ridley punching a hole in the arcade machine had gone viral again, this time with a vengeance—she had even made the news. The fact that she worked with orcs at Congo was now public knowledge as well.

Funk Master’s band had gained attention. He had confirmed his abilities with his bandmates, who all spread the rumors even faster. He started playing off inquiries by saying, “Maybe I am, maybe I’m not. You’ll have to ask an orc if you meet one,” obnoxiously winking afterward.

The worst part was there were so many attempts to become “buddies” with Ridley from the most random people. Some had been on the periphery of her mockers from the girls, but surprisingly, some of the former catcalling boys had approached her to ask her out. She was starting to believe that some of what she thought was bullying was actually juvenile flirting.

She decided to banish herself to the band kids’ table, as at least these guys didn’t go full sycophant on her.

“Ridley, are you going to come to the show?” Funk Master asked with a smile. “Maybe bring your boyfriend?”

“He’s not my boyfriend… yet. I just say that to get the creeps to leave me alone,” she responded, pouting.

“You should just embrace the celebrity life! You deserve it. You fight to protect these nobs,” Louis said, gesturing around him.

He had a point. It might not be so bad. Maybe she would join that manga fan club and find some friends who at least had a common interest. They certainly would get a kick out of her disguise self ability.

Wait a minute.

Ridley snatched the sunglasses off Funk Master’s face.

“Hey!” he shouted.

“Hello Louis, glad to have you back,” Ridley jeered.

“Not cool,” he said, losing some of his straight-backed posture.

“I am this close to dropping out and getting a GED,” she said.

“Well, would you be able to pass?” Louis asked.

Ridley gave him a look.

“Not saying you’re dumb!”

“I will have you know that my intelligence points have gone up, and the GED is supposed to be easy.”

“Well, it would give you more time to adventure. I’m sure you are rolling in new gear by this point.”

Ridley straightened up, thinking about how many resources she had gotten her hands on. Once people figured out their true worth, she would be filthy rich.

“Heads up… Jared is walking this way.”

Ridley turned to see Jared strolling up behind her.

“Why, the mighty Jared Malone has lowered himself to converse with us weirdos,” Ridley said, giving her mischievous smile.

Jared placed a red-stained math book on the table. “You left this in the classroom.”

“Oh? How could you tell it was mine? Did the blood stains give it away?”

Jared shrugged.

The red stains on the math book were actually from bullying—someone had put a leaking Gatorade bottle in her bag.

“Can we talk?” Jared asked, oddly morose.

“We are talking now. The noise coming from your mouth gives it away.”

Jared rolled his eyes. “Privately?”

Ridley gave it some thought. “I suppose it wouldn’t hurt… much,” she said, standing.

They walked to the cafeteria wall and Ridley leaned against it, crossing her arms.

“Can you drop the macho air?” Jared asked.

“What macho air? This is what I do. I lean now. I’m a cool leaning person. Like the Fonz.”

“The who?”

“Start talkin’, Chachi,” Ridley smiled.

“I was wondering… I’m having a party at my place this weekend. I was wondering if you wanted to come?”

“What? Why?”

“There are people that want to hang out with you that will be there, so… maybe you would have fun.”

Ridley just looked at Jared in confusion.

“I don’t know what kind of high school drama episode you think we are in, but in what world would I get along with your friends?”

“Well, if you were more you and less… macho acting…” Jared trailed off.

“Macho again? Find a new verb.”

“You mean adjective.”

“Whatever.”

Dang, maybe I should actually study for the GED, Ridley thought.

“Look. The point is you changed. A lot. I know I wasn’t exactly nice to you, but I miss that Ridley now. I didn’t appreciate that before and I’m sorry. Before I was a complete ass to you, we were friends. I’ll admit it—people pester me to get you to hang out. My mom has given me a tongue-lashing that would make you blush. The thing is… she was right. I would like to fix some of our friendship at least… if it’s possible.”

Ridley looked at Jared suspiciously, but as much as she despised it, he sounded genuine. Her cell phone decided to make a nice distraction from self-analysis by buzzing with a notification. She glanced at it.

Bru-haha: Next location confirmed. Planeteers ASSEMBLE! 👊

Louis stood at his table and pumped his fist, then looked over at Ridley. She gave him a thumbs up.

Ridley smiled and looked back at Jared. “Alright.”

“Alright?”

“How about this… you come hang out with my friends this afternoon, then I will hang out with yours,” she said, smiling evilly.

The Planeteers stood on a hilltop a mile away from the new target as Ben explained the layout. His big spy drone was currently shaped like a crow in flight—a massively oversized crow, but it was harder to tell with it so high in the sky.

“I can make out four gore monsters of various species. One looks BIG. Two shadow-cloaked humans… they are around some kind of black rock and seem to be… writing on it?”

“That’s new,” Bru-haha commented as he looked over Ben’s shoulder, sketching the layout on a whiteboard like a football coach. “This might be a coincidence. The pedestal normally targets random resources. Is it a coincidence that we stumbled on the same resource as these brainwashed Chosen?”

Ridley and Terry sat to the side as Ben did his recon. Terry pulled out a new tool he had made. Jared glanced around nervously. Ridley took perverse pleasure in the way Jared had squirmed at the mention of gore monsters.

“If this doesn’t work, you are going to reimburse me for my precious resources.”

“It’s goin’a work, lass. Just you be trustin’ me, aye?” Terry said, sitting on a rock over Ridley’s arm.

Terry affixed a light resource to something that looked like an oversized fountain pen.

“So I’da been thinking, yeah? Laser can destroy the pigment in your skin, burn it right up, yeah? That’s how they remove tattoos. So I figured this light crystal funnels down when primed and cuts through tattoos. Tried it on meself. Doesn’t hurt me none as I has the magic powers. Or at least not too bad. Actually… hurts quite a bit, but you’ll be jumpy jammers toot sweet.”

Ridley sighed. “Just do it already.”

“Righto, here we go.” Terry said as his hand wrapped around the light essence rather than the pen. He channeled his mana into it, which took the form of chi from his monk class abilities. Overcharging the crystal caused it to push out some of the light elemental magic. It funneled to the end of the pen and shot a concentrated beam of magic into Ridley’s skin.

Ridley didn’t feel much at first, but soon it started to burn. She felt like her skin was being peeled off an inch at a time.

“DAMNIT! Can you do that any faster!?” Ridley barked.

“Sorry love, this is actually a pretty advanced tatty you’ve got going here. I mean the design is simple, but the concepts are uni! I have no way o’ reading it, but it looky like spirit has to be involved in some way. Not some simple speed boost or strengthening.”

“Man up, Ridley,” Darin chided as he adjusted his Nerf sniper.

“I will take that rifle and shove it up your… AAaaOUCH!”

“Almost… should have a piece cut out. Aaaaand there.”

Ridley felt a small jolt as the magic of the tattoo snapped. She slouched on her boulder and felt nauseous from the pain.

“GAH! That… was not… pleasant.”

“Well, that’s why you don’t go round drawing on your soul willy-nilly,” Terry chided.

“Your SOUL?” Jared asked in shock.

Ridley shook her head and felt through her body to see if anything was different.

“That was only one of the three spirals. You got two more to go and I’ve used up this essence already,” Terry explained.

“Well, crap… time to see if Miss Crazy Pants is still around.”

Ridley reached up and unclipped the hairpin hesitantly. She braced for the…

“TRAITOR!!!! YOU MIGHT’N HAVE DECAPITATED MY DAUGHTER THYSELF! YOU UNGRATEFUL…”

Ridley clipped the hairpin back in place.

“Huh. Well, she’s still there… I’ll have to see if anything changed tonight during the fight.” She stood and walked over to the group, Jared following like a lost puppy.

The group looked at Ridley questioningly.

“Still possessed,” she shrugged.

“So what is his role in all of this?” Ben asked, nodding at Jared.

“Cheerleader,” Ridley responded. She turned and pointed at Jared. “Go make some pom-poms.”

“I thought Louis was the cheerleader,” Darin replied.

“Hey! No boost for you,” Louis said, offended.

“I don’t need any boosts,” Darin said as he pulled a gun out of his holster and spun it.

“Jameson said he would like a boost. He says he likes running circles around Darin,” Jada said, stroking the ferret.

Darin shrugged as if he didn’t care.

“We need everyone performing their best today,” Bru-haha said seriously as he lifted the whiteboard. “This looks like a tough fight. Let’s go standard pattern.” He wrote some X’s as he called out everyone’s positions.

“Jameson, hit and run. Nerf Kid, sniper support from this hillside. Ridley and Terry, frontline tanks, but don’t get surrounded. I will be mid-tank and support. Funk Master, back line with Sky Master and be ready with some quick wit if anything gets past us. Jada, you can stay just within radio range and communicate for Jameson.” He turned to look at Jared.

“Jared, grab some pine needle branches and wave them in the air when we kill something. Maybe do jump splits if you can manage,” Bru-haha said with a menacing face, causing Jared to hop up and start looking for branches.

Bru turned to Ridley with a smile and a thumbs up. Ridley returned the gesture with a grin.

When Jared left, Bru-haha started passing out potions. “These are free if they don’t end up getting used. If they are, I will simply take a small cut to replenish them. Drink for a rapid recovery boost. I tested them and they can heal shallow wounds instantly, so they may stabilize you if you take a serious injury.”

Murmurs of thanks went around the circle as everyone took a bottle.

Louis leaned over to talk to Ridley. “Do you want me to de-buff you if… you know… you start going crazy?”

“Yeah, go for it. The last thing I want is to hurt my team,” Ridley said, feeling a sinking sensation in her gut. She turned to speak to the group. “Let’s have Funk Master buff us here out of earshot before we charge in.”

“Excellent idea, Ridley,” Bru nodded.

Louis fired up his sax and played a marching tune before he paused.

“Let’s fire up, cats and kittens…”
