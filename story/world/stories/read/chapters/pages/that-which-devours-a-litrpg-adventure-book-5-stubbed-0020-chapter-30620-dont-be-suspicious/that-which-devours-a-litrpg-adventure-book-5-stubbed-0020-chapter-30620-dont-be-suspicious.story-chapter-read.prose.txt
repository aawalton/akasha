I pressed myself harder into the shadow near the roots of the tree.

“Did the portal just flicker?” asked the same voice.

Silence.

“You’re seeing things,” said another.

Someone chuckled. “Those rejects wouldn’t dare fly anywhere near us.”

“You have that right. We’d tear them wing from wing.”

More laughter.

Yet, none of the voices moved closer to the portal. Instead, the three dots flew off back to whatever they were doing.

I couldn’t freaking believe it. They didn’t even check out the clearing. Not close enough to really even look.

Silently, I shook my head, and touched the link to Strange.

He was close. Close enough that parts of my radar near the edges were filled in, showing me a walkway in the trees that he had definitely traveled down.

I studied my surroundings, and headed to the edges of the roots of the trees. Water lapped all around the area, not leaving me a good way to travel hidden.

The walkways were high up, stretching from tree to tree, and made out of wood.

The canopy covered the sky, blocking out even the stars. Worse, the air was so still I could feel my own movement through it. It felt dead, with no movement anywhere. Heaviness hung over everything.

Red dots were scattered all around me, mostly staying clustered in their places. Those three moved along the walkways, but moving away from the portal now, not nearer. Each of the massive trees must contain homes of some kind, and most of the red dots were inside those.

Thinking fast, I leaped up and landed quietly on a dark walkway near one of the huts that didn’t have a dot inside. The look gave me more info on the buildings. There were no windows, and thick doors. When I used second sight, mana covered each of the structures.

So, shields, more than likely.

Either way, as far as I could tell, the doors were closed right now on most of the buildings.

Watching my radar, I carefully started walking down the walkway to the next hut. It had four red dots inside, but the door remained shut.

Darkness coated everything, and no light glowed anywhere.

There were no lanterns, no torches, nothing.

Bit by bit, I uncovered more of the area, which I assumed was mostly housing. Then I reached a walkway Strange had used. This area had more movement, with red dots all leaving a massive structure that he had passed through.

Quietly, I crept closer to get a better view. Then I decided to climb up a nearby tree.

At least twenty clan members left the building through three different doors. All were below me in levels, all citizens in the mid-hundreds range.

None of them looked up. All moved in groups of three, carrying weapons, though not all of them had armor. None wore the armor that the one who’d attacked me did.

Soon, most of the people were gone. They had spread out to various buildings, though a few remained in the big meeting area.

I touched the bond with Strange, and he yanked on it.

Suddenly, I found myself seeing through his eyes.

Three of the Clan stood on a platform much higher than the walkways.

Two of them I recognized from the cavern. The last I didn’t.

“You need to leave,” said the one I didn’t know.

For a moment, I thought they were talking to me, but then Zephyr shook his head.

“I need more than that to just betray the Clan and walk away,” he said with a frown.

Vyn also shook his head.

“I cannot tell you more,” said the one I didn’t know. He stepped closer to the edge of the platform. “The Clan goes down a dark path, and the head isn’t…” He paused, before swallowing. “He is caught up in the death of his daughter.”

Strange suddenly glanced up at the stars, making my head roil, before he turned back to the others. He stood near Vyn’s foot.

“Why do you turn from the teachings?” asked Zephyr, stepping closer.

“Because they are a lie.”

The simple statement hung in the air, and both beings froze.

“You weren’t there. I know what happened, and I’m leaving this world while I still have a chance.” Again he paused, and stared at Zephyr. “I promised your parents I’d watch over you, but I won’t throw away my life while your wings stutter.”

“You're leaving then?” asked Zephyr, clearly knowing the answer, and stepping back.

“Yes, tonight. It's the…” He paused. “You three should come with me. Live. See what else is out there, and maybe, someday, we can reclaim–” His lips snapped shut, and he closed his eyes.

“Reclaim?” whispered Vyn.

“I cannot speak of it.” He shook his head again, as wings appeared behind him. “Please, for the love of your parents, leave. Tonight. Do not look back. Just go.”

He gave a sharp nod to Vyn, Zephyr, and someone I couldn’t see, before launching himself into the air. He flew fast. Faster than any others of their kind I’d seen.

Suddenly, the air felt light and Strange breathed easier.

“Zephyr? Maybe he’s right,” said someone I couldn’t see. “Maybe we should cool our wings for a few days, eat some good food, and wait. You are the one that always plays it safe.”

“We are so close to earning the next level in the Clan. Our reports gave us status…” Zephyr’s voice faded.

“Status doesn’t mean anything if our leaders flee,” whispered Vyn.

Strange nudged his foot without making a sound. That caused the winged being to glance down. Strange nodded at him.

“Even the little one agrees, and he's little. Something isn’t adding up about all of this. The call to attack any known devourer on sight is like a kit attacking us. Why should we throw our lives away? By those orders, we should have attacked the Blood Emperor, and that would have been nothing but a quick and, likely, very painful death.”

“Keep your voice down,” whispered Zephyr. He let out a long sigh. “Let’s lay low for a while, do our route off-world, but not look deeper than we need to.”

Hands clutched either side of Strange.

“Let’s get you into the pouch,” said Vyn. “We're gonna be flying.”

Strange stared at his face for a moment, then nodded.

The connection between us snapped.

He wasn’t far away. He was just on the other side of the massive building, and if I was going to reach them before they left, I needed to move.

After searching my radar, I launched myself into the air, making the leaves rustle as I left the tree. I landed rather gracefully in another tree away from the bulk of the walkways. Two more leaps, and I approached the area where Strange stood.

In the distance, the platform stood out surrounded by trees, but three figures were already soaring through the air in the distance.

I landed on the platform, and tossed a hunk of meat into my mouth. Then, I leaped up.

My wings slid out of my shoulder blades, and my center of gravity shifted.

I needed to catch up to them.

Bit by bit, I gained on them, pushing myself to fly faster than I ever had.

They were flying in a V formation, studying the ground ahead of them.

I passed over a rather large wall, made of massive tree trunks stabbed into the ground. The tops were pointed. I noticed that the three Clan with Strange had slowed when they crossed the wall, and I wasn’t sure why.

Then, my radar blazed with creatures at ground, well, swamp-level, and a creepy sensation rose up my spine. It was almost like I’d entered a higher leveled mana area. Or, like something big was staring at me.

I picked up my speed and yanked on my stealth. I blended in with the dark sky, and leaned into that as much as I could.

The three figures slowed down even more than they had when they first crossed the wall, with one pointing at something in the swamp.

Finally.

I caught up rather quickly at that point, but something caused me to slow down in my approach, rather than to just fly into their group.

Suddenly, massive tendrils shot into the air all around me. One slammed into my side before I could blink. Pain burst through my body as I crashed into murky water. Water washed into my open mouth, making me cough and splutter until I climbed to my feet.

Another tendril swiped at me, but I dove back into the waist high water.

I swam fast away from the source, trying to get my bearings.

The red dots on my map were all over, but something massive stood nearby.

By the time I put enough distance between me and it to feel slightly safe and climbed out of the water, the swamp buzzed with movement. Smaller creatures fled the area, while others hung back and watched. All of them were stronger than me.

Tendrails trailed along in the ground in various directions, but it couldn’t reach my new position. The two near me jerked back into the water, then the surface went still.

I readied myself to jump into the air, then froze.

Strange’s dot remained near the massive creature.

Yet, he wasn’t worried, as far as I could tell.

All I wanted to do was get Strange, find that mountain top, then get off this planet.

I shook my head and leaped up into the air, letting my wings take me higher than before. The cooler air made me grin. The reprieve from the hot mugginess felt good.

Four dots faced off against the tendrils, one of which was Strange.

I remained high in the sky as I approached and used Insight.

[Strange, Shielded, Courier of the Cosmos, Level 125, Bonded.]

[Zepyr-Kael, The Tether, 262, Potato Chip.]

[Vyrn-Slyte, The Talon, 231, Potato Chip.]

[Nyx-Thrum, The Shround, 230, Potato Chip.]

Power flared along the bond with Strange, then cold terror.

[Strange, Courier of the Cosmos, Level 140, Bonded.]

Strange vanished behind one of the tendrils.

[Dread, Sedge Sea Weaver, Primeval, Level 282, Predator, Unknown.]

[Warning: uncivilized creature detected.]

[Bounty issued.]

Dread suddenly became outlined in red, including all of its tendrils under the water. A massive humanoid creature rose from the swamp, formed out of many thinner vines. Out of its chest erupted a roar as rows of teeth flashed.

Yet, the vines holding it together suddenly fell apart, falling in various directions while the red outline remained.

Keeping high in the sky, I could see Strange’s dot creep ever so slowly closer to a rock. The darkness hid him, but tendrils attacked those near him, going in all directions.

The three moved back to back as weapons flashed.

I cared about none of it. I was only interested in the small black shadow blending in next to a boulder as best he could.

Strange.

That dang little guy.

I waited until the bulk of the tendrils were bashing on the three Clan warriors before darting in. Two more rose out of the water and aimed for me, but this time I was ready.

I dodged, and skidded across the top of the boulder.

Strange merged with my shadow, freeing a huge weight off my shoulders.

Then, I darted up as fast as I could fly.

Pin-pricks of light formed in the air, and suddenly my face slammed into a hardened surface. I bounced off, brain rattled, as I crashed into the swamp yet again.

I didn’t dodge the next tendril that wrapped around my legs. It lifted me out of the water and I braced as it slammed me back down. My wings vanished just before impact as my armor thickened.

One of the dots suddenly moved in my direction, and the tendril loosened.

I lurched up from the water to see Vyrn rolling back into position.

“Sync up with us,” yelled Zephyr, his wings fluttering behind him.

Heat rippled up my chest, coming from my stomach, and my mouth watered as another tendril headed my way.

My claws grew longer and I crouched down, then swiped. Golden lines flared for just a second as pieces of the vines were sliced off.

Several more all shot in my direction, all highlighted in red, including the ones beneath the water.

I almost snorted as it tried to be sneaky. I was going to feast, and it didn’t even know it yet.
