Ethan leaned back against his chair, only a little aware of the looks he was getting from the council members. He had placed sixth in the whole world. Sure, the difference in level was really small and would likely change pretty fast, but that wasn’t enough to dampen the sense of accomplishment he was feeling.

Of course, it wasn’t like he had placed first or even top 3, but it felt good to see all that blood and effort lead to something tangible, and now that New Dawn was close to being self-sufficient—relatively, that is—the faction should soon begin to climb.

“It might be worth having a little celebration. For morale’s sake,” Sarah said. The once council member of the Union had been allowed in on the High Council, though it did look like she might soon become a mainstay. Amanda had begun the investigation and so far, the woman’s record was squeaky clean. Her people—and even those from other ex-Union settlements—loved her and had little bad to say about her, so until the full investigation was done, she would be representing the new settlements along with a man by the name of Andrew, a trauma-surgeon who’d previously refused to be involved in politics within the Union until Amanda managed to convince him.

Gavin nodded, eyes bright. “That could be good. After everything that went on, I’m sure it’ll be appreciated. Though I think they already got started without us,” he added with a smile.

“They definitely are. But before we get distracted, I want to come back to the list,” spoke Sophia, a focused frown on her face as she distractedly scratched the curly brown head of one of the roaming Direhound she somehow had tamed on her way to New Dawn, “I agree a little celebration could help, but we can’t let it go on for too long. Things will not stay calm for long, I’m guessing… we’ll hear from the Everglade Bushwhackers soon, which are south of us. And maybe the Iron Saints, depending on where they are. Both are in the top 15. Plus, we shouldn’t forget that until a couple of days ago, we wouldn’t have even placed this high in the list, so there could still be plenty of powerful factions around us.”

Andrew tapped his pen on a small notebook in front of him. His eyes were present, which told Ethan he had already stopped looking at the leaderboards. “While I also agree that doing something for morale’s important, I don’t want it to delay our business too much. We have a long, long, list of urgent business to take care of, and we’re still finding new stuff to deal with. There’s also the fact that we still don’t have our logistics down. Our bodies are different and all thanks to the levels and stats and whatnot, but we still need food and water. We did manage to recover some foodstuffs from stores and warehouses, but we have no running water, and everything’s running out fast. Too many people are drinking from wells and rivers, and I’m worried we’re not the only ones who are growing stronger. Not to put a downer on the mood, but I’d really rather not add a super-powered plague on our list of worries.”

“I wouldn’t worry about this being too distracting, Andrew,” said Amanda, pen scribbling as her eyes stared into the empty air. “We can have a few volunteers in every settlement handle a little get-together. For those who are interested. Just a second…” she trailed off for a few moments, then her gaze sharpened once more. “Done. I asked my assistant to find a few volunteers and to not let things get out of hand. As for us, I’ve already summoned all the documented high-levels to New Dawn and they’re showing up. Audrey, Marcello, and Imani will handle the day-to-day of the rift-handling task force, until a better chain of command is established. But we can get them to work right away. Ethan,” she looked up from her notes toward him. “We’ll be creating teams to go and close out rifts, but ideally you’ll cooperate with them, that way we don’t double-book rifts. Is that okay with you?”

Ethan nodded. That made sense to him and this way, they would know who headed out to which rift and whether they needed to send someone after them or not. Amanda smiled. “Great. I’ll be going in along with them. You’re right about our leveling falling behind. To think people are already closing in on level 100…” she mumbled. Then looking back at him. “Also, I’m sure plenty of them would appreciate it if you swung by from time to time to give them some hints and whatnot. I don’t think a lot of settlements have a top 10 fighter to learn from. But no pressure, of course.”

“Yeah I don’t mind. It could help me work through some things as well,” he said with a shrug, wondering what those other top 10 individuals were like. More and more were showing signs of their own Prime Manifestation, and it would do him well to have some ideas of what the possible abilities were. And while he hoped not all of these were murderous pricks like the ones he’d encountered, some of them were bound to being to be on the unhinged side. Practice never hurt anyone. “But that’ll have to wait. I’m going to be running a few more rifts in the next couple of days. I might not be very available,” he added. Ethan still had a rift token he had earned. A rift that might not be like the others. And it was time he cashed it in.

The council members nodded, some even smiling as the sound of whistling and celebrating reached them from the outside. They hadn’t even begun scheduling anything, but it seemed like the citizens of New Dawn were already getting started on the fun.

With that, the rest of the meeting was spent discussing their next moves. They needed to build up the task force to handle the rifts, but they’d also need someone to defend the settlements and spread their influence. So that meant that they’d need at least three more corps. One for settlement defense, one for diplomatic outreach who could start on reaching out to find friends and families, and one for their scouting effort, though the last two might be possible to fuse.

For this as well, he let the council-members—and Amanda, mostly—take care of things. Plus, while he could assist to any meeting about the faction, he didn’t want to make a habit out of it.

“One last point, before we move on to the next order of business,” said Amanda, tapping her notebook as she turned her sharp gaze toward Ethan. “I don’t see Ember on the individual list.”

Ethan frowned, then pulled the list once more. He’d seen her yesterday, and the Ignisra had already broke through level 80. But the top 100 list ended on level 78.

“That’s… odd,” he muttered.

“We’re out of the loop, I think,” Sarah edged in, and Andrew nodded, so Amanda quickly filled them in on the Ignisra and its origin, and while it took Ethan a second to see through the freshly promoted Great Administrator’s point, his eyes widened.

“So that means… she might not be the only alien already on Earth,” said the surgeon, and Amanda nodded.

“While I’m sure it was a bit of a lucky encounter, I’m having trouble believing the ‘opportunity’ happened only once. There’s a good chance at least a handful of other entities are already among us. Observing. Learning. Ember’s friendly and proved multiple times that she’d have our back, but I wonder if that applies to all other… visitors.”

Gavin nodded, rubbing the back of his neck, lips pressed. “This is… concerning. Would they be able to bypass us to communicate with their factions without us knowing?”

“Well, Ember never showed interest in our day to day, preferring to nap and exploring rifts,” Amanda said, sighing as she leaned back and put her hands behind her head. “But it’s definitely going to be an issue, likely not with her, but it’s a scenario we can’t exclude out of hand. Though we might have some time considering the system said communication and teleportation were limited with the outside universe. But the language used in the notification was a little vague, so I’d make some preparation just in case. It had said the limitation would be reduced, but we don’t know how tight it is right now. We don’t know if it’s complete blackout with the outside universe or if there are ways to slip a message through.”

“They might have their own ways of communicating,” muttered Sarah. “We know nothing of how the rest of the universe works.”

“At the same time, the language does tell us that it is restricting us from the rest of the universe. Which is… good? It sounds like we’re on the training wheels program. At least, we’ll still be until our pylon reaches tier-5.”

Ethan listened, nodding along. He definitely agreed with everything being said, but honestly, he was happy he didn’t have to deal with all this by himself.

“Ethan, do you mind if we ask Ember to answer some questions? Her input might help us put some of these worries to rest.”

“I’ll let her know. She’ll help,” he said. He recalled how the lizardling had managed to summon her. He just had to figure out a way to bribe her.

With that, the meeting continued for a few minutes until the council-members decided to take a break, at which point they headed out to grab a bite and mingle with the still chaotic festivities outside and seeing the good mood all around them, he decided to hang about for a few minutes. Maybe grab something to eat. After that, he’d go see Ember, then the rift task force to make sure no dangerous rift is spilling and once that was done, he’d see what the special rift token was for.

It was time to get back into the action.
