Chapter 284

Twenty minutes and a lot of procedures later, Travis was finally sitting at the Central Dashboard. Around him, Michael’s inner space stretched as far as the eye could see, crisscrossed with bright white lines and the jaw-dropping sight of the black hole’s accretion disk. The room was slightly above the ecliptic, hovering what felt like mere miles above the disk, even though Travis intellectually knew it had to be much more than that.

Icarus popped into existence beside him. Unlike the times when the AI wanted to mess with him, their current instance was wearing no strange props and their clothes hid the shape of their body. Even their face, while still androgynous and with skin so smooth it almost glowed, lacked any provocative features. It looked perfect, almost uncanny like a Michelangelo statue.

“You know I could have opened a portal for you and saved you the travel time,” they said. “Given how much you care about not wasting time.”

“I need to become familiar with the proper procedure first,” Travis replied. “I’ll be sure to take you up on your offer next time.”

Pulling up a screen, he saw that the President was done delivering his message to the nation. Online chatter was being carefully monitored to gauge how the public was reacting to the news. The System, under Icarus’s careful guidance, was already nudging things along using all the resources at their disposal. They didn’t have many users they could turn into temporary operators just yet, but the Origin System–being the same thing as Icarus–was completely embedded in the world’s digital and information infrastructure and could manipulate algorithms at will. Soon enough they might even choose to drop the pretense and let Unity Corp straight up buy the social media companies.

***

After the speech, the President retreated to a quiet corner of the White House, staring out of the window in silent contemplation. People gave him a wide berth, perhaps thinking that he was lost in his own mind again, and he was fine with that. It would take a while for everyone to realize that he was no longer mentally incapacitated, and already he imagined people attempting to justify the speech he had just given in front of the cameras as one of the few moments of lucidity in the last months he had left in this world.

Fools, and he was going to play them all. For now, he was fine pretending. Let them turn over all the stones they could find in search of the powers pulling the strings to make him deliver his speech. Let them come face to face with the terrifying truth of Unity Corporation.

Then he would have the last laugh.

He opened his Origin System interface. Navigating it with his eyes felt clumsy, but even though he was sure nobody would question him if he waved his hands around interacting with empty air, he chose not to.

Quest: The Official Narrative I - Complete

A second popup screen confirmed that he now was 100XP richer, and that he now had access to the Shop. He found it in his main status easily enough, and saw that it was filled with all sorts of strange things. Before he could examine it further, however, multiple new quests appeared all at the same time.

New Quests

The Official Narrative I-end

Establish a secure communication line with Travis Tyrell to discuss the next steps in the handling of the Huntington incident.

Reward: quest line continues.

A Power Vacuum

The Huntington Incident has decapitated the chain of command. During your cognitive decline, your administration was infested with opportunists and sycophants who are now either missing, dead, or liabilities.

Initiate the executive restructuring of the federal government.

Reward: Leased Enhancement: (common) Quick Memorization I.

The Schoolboys of Daejeon

Unity Corporation has reason to believe that an anomalous event is behind the disappearance of a class of children in a school in Korea.

Facilitate diplomatic relations to allow Unity Corporation to act unimpeded.

Reward: 500XP.

Tutorial II

Open your status and examine the skill [Presidential Authority] to learn about its effects. Use the skill on a target without them realizing what you have done.

Reward: 100XP, 10 copper magic coins.

President Arthur Terris laughed. He read the first quest’s description again, and the laughter almost turned bitter. The way the System phrased the quest implied that he could choose not to contact Travis Tyrell, but he knew full well that it wasn’t true.

He didn’t mind, he decided, the laughter dying down into a chuckle that turned heads around him. In fact, reading the various rewards, he felt the gradual build-up of anticipation, laced with greed and desire. He knew that the System had him already, and so far it had been only promises and little more. He did not dare imagine just how hooked he would be on it as soon as he got to the actual magic, which by the looks of it was going to be sooner rather than later.

He imagined, briefly because the implications of it threatened to rob him of sleep for the rest of his life, what might happen when the entire world was going to be given access to the System, once it was no longer in experimental beta or whatever they called it. He imagined an army of average Joe’s, given quests that slowly turned them into deadly sleeper agents, powered up by the System and hooked up on a constant stream of XP and dopamine.

This was the future he was helping Unity Corporation to build, and he was fine with it because he would be among the most powerful, the first lucky ones who had been given the System ahead of time, a baron then duke then lord in the coming feudalistic land of the dystopian future. He was not just a random person, but a man whose quests already shaped international politics and diplomatic relations.

Pulling out his phone, he tried to call Mr Tyrell but the line did not connect. He tried again, frowned, then decided to switch to another quest for now. He sent a message to the Korean diplomat and, while he waited, he looked at what he needed to do to complete [Tutorial II].

“[Presidential Authority]? Sounds interesting,” he muttered.

An evil grin appeared on his face as he read its description, imagining all the ways he could take advantage of the skill to make others do things for him. Some of the things he imagined were more deplorable than others, but then again they were no different from the things that money and power had also allowed him to do for most of his life.

Suddenly, he felt a cold breeze at the back of his neck. Turning around, the empty rooms of the White House seemed to stretch into infinity, becoming long tunnels of darkness and smooth white surfaces. The air was grey, cold and hostile. He struggled to breathe, and there was a presence watching him, judging him, twin eyes of deep emerald, wings of fire and horns of pure evil, pointed ears and–

Then he was back, staring at the carefully manicured gardens of his presidential residence. Even turning around had been a figment of his imagination.

No. Not his imagination. He was not losing his grip on reality, not again, not this time.

His breath was quick and erratic, and his back was wet with sweat that made his suit stick to his skin, the cold fabric making him shiver. It took him a moment to gather the courage to look behind him, this time for real, but when he did he saw nothing out of the ordinary. Still, his hands continued to shake for several minutes, and he had the impression that should he even attempt to go down the same train of thought as before again, he would not be let off so easily. That this was only a warning, the first and only warning.

Outwardly, he must have looked utterly frozen. Perhaps it was nothing new to the eyes of his staff, who used to deal with him when his mind was addled. Except now, he was frozen by the unfolding realization of what the System really was. It felt like the tenth time that he had realized something utterly terrifying about it in the last hour alone, each new insight building upon the previous ones.

Was the System going to be the end of free will? The tool with which a select few were going to impose upon the world their sets of values, behaviors and boundaries? Or was he being kept under special scrutiny?

Trying to shake those thoughts from his mind, he gingerly extended a mental probe toward his magical skill. It felt utterly alien and strange, but the guiding hand of the Origin System allowed him to perform the task with uncanny ease.

***

Travis watched the President almost have a heart attack when the man used his skill to make one of the cleaning crew maids suddenly throw a silverware fork to the ground. Perhaps because he was feeling watched, the man then proceeded to calm the maid down when she inevitably broke into hysteric sobbing for having done such a thing in front of the President. When it didn’t work, he was glad to see that the man used the skill again on her, this time to calm her down, and the little accident allowed him to discover more about magic.

The reward, ten mana coins, appeared in his hand. [Tutorial III] was triggered, this time asking him to absorb a coin to refill his mana pool, teaching him more of the basics.

“You put a lot of effort into making sure people are slowly eased into the System,” he told Icarus.

The AI had been distracted by something, but they perked up and nodded. “Yes. The quest line is actually different for each user, depending on their knowledge of these things. What’s important is that by the end of it they know how to use the Origin System, and have been given the starter XP and items.”

Then Icarus’s gaze drifted to the distance again, where Travis saw that the gargantuan ring surrounding the whole inner space–visible even from a solar system away–was gathering power.

“Something going on?” he asked.

Icarus hummed without looking at him. “Might,” they said. Their attention shifted back to the President, who had completed the [Tutorial III] quest. “Giving him the fourth one.”

The new quest would take longer to complete, and it was going to open a new quest line about growing a user’s personal power. The first step was to reach a Fully Owned Main Tier of Copper (10%), which was the Status screen way of saying to reach ten percent into Copper territory. Growing a user’s power, of course, had all sorts of benefits among which was the increased power being sent back to the System itself.

The only problem was the coins they would need to give the users not in a mana-rich zone, either through rewards or through the Shop, but with now two dungeons at their disposal, Unity Corporation was ramping up their coin farming operations.

While the President fiddled with the Shop, where he found discounted–yet still unaffordable–bundles to purchase the Stats module or the Levels module for the System, Travis looked into the distance. The ring around the inner space had finished powering up, and a massive shield was growing up and down from it, coloring the backdrop of space blue.

“You need to decide if you want to stay or leave,” Icarus said in a sudden hurry. “You have three seconds.”

“I’m staying,” Travis said. “Are we under attack?”

“Might,” Icarus said, once again distracted.

“Can I help?”

“No,” they said. “Stay here. This is the safest place.”

“Will you be going to help?”

Icarus shook their head, a complicated expression on their face. “There are already enough me’s there.”

A screen appeared, showing dozens of points of view. “I’m real-time syncing with all of them,” they explained. “I am always connected to all the other instances of me. Even those doing dangerous things in quarantined areas are not really disconnected from the main me. For as long as there is only one Michael, Travis Tyrell, then there will only be one Icarus.”

The shield snapped close all around them. A second layer appeared, its orange color filtered through the blue.

“You are stuck here,” Icarus explained. “Sit back, relax and watch the show.”
