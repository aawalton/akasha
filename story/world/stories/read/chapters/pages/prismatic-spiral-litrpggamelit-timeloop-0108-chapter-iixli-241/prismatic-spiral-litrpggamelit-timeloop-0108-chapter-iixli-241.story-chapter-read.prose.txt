Chapter II.XLI (2.41)

Mitsuko let out a deep sigh of relief as the yellow fog emerged on their horizon. It signified the end of this uncomfortable journey. For the last hour Mitsuko had been playing out the events of the day and fruitlessly theorizing methods of bypassing this unproductive trip for future loops. She could go to Hamish without Fuku, but wouldn’t have a way to convince him to travel. She could steal his compass and charter The Selcouth Sable, but would be unable to penetrate the fog or convince the Sailor’s Guild to allow her portage.

“Maybe I can abduct him and force him to allow us passage,” Mitsuko muttered, staring at the back of Hamish’s head. The drunken man’s neck was a shade of pink that had slowly reddened throughout their journey.

That comment caught Fuku’s attention, though she said nothing, instead opting to simply eye Mitsuko. The witch clearly knew something strange was happening with her, though she remained mute in regards to questions. Whatever she’d seen from the doll, it must have been enough to scare her into a state of silence and submission.

They passed through the fog, the experience basically the same as the previous loop with Zeaqu. The thick cloud parted on demand for Hamish, with very little effort on his part. A minute later, they penetrated the haze and Amber Island loomed in front of her once again.

“The skiff actually made it all the way out here,” Verna commented from inside Mitsuko’s bag. “I half expected us to drown and reset the loop.”

“We made it,” Mitsuko agreed.

“So, this is Amber Island.” Verna pulled her turtle body up so that her two front legs rested on the lip of the bag. “Amazing. I always dreamed of visiting. This is my father’s home. I wonder which of the towers used to belong to him. Not to mention my uncle as well. There’s so much here. It’s wonderful.”

“Hey, Hamish,” Mitsuko called out. “Do you know of a wizard named Eustace Hrim?”

Hamish leaned against the ship wheel as he acknowledged her, veering the ship off course and nearly slamming them into the side of the island. He caught himself right before and jerked them back into a trajectory towards the port.

“Name’s familiar,” he said, pretending that he hadn’t just smashed his skiff. “Why you asking?” He burped, the excess alcohol clearly taking a toll on him.

“I wanted to know if he used to have a tower here. He existed here ages ago though, any records of him left?”

“Hm. Yeah. I think so. He’s got one of those haunted and trapped towers. Most wizards avoid it.”

“Can you point it out to me?”

“It’s the only white and blue tower on the island. Should be able to find it without my help.” With that, Hamish returned to steering his vessel. Which was probably a good thing as they were fast approaching the dock.

“Thanks for asking,” Verna said. “I know it’s not directly relevant for you, but it would be nice to know more about where I came from. My father was always… aloof.”

Mitsuko nodded in acknowledgement to the sage’s statement. Her question hadn’t just been for her friend’s benefit. Sterling had mentioned that at the very end of the loops, Mitsuko would be required to face off against all the guardians one last time. Gathering information on them now seemed prudent.

The same dock worker as in the previous loop approached their little skiff. This time though, Mitsuko didn’t need to speak to the Sailor’s Guild member. She simply sat back and listened as Fuku and Hamish dealt with her.

“I am here on behalf of the request of Wizard Zena,” Fuku said. “She wishes to speak to me on the subject of witchcraft.”

“And I’m here because I was threatened,” Hamish muttered, then he hiccuped. “You know as well as I do that I wouldn’t step foot here otherwise.”

“Threatened?” the guild member raised an eyebrow at him. “Remind me, Wizard Hamish, what is your rank?”

“Under Zena’s,” Hamish continued to mutter, not meeting anyone’s eyes. “That’s all that matters.”

“Wizard Zena is one of the highest ranked and could be a contender for the governor position if she hadn’t decided to instead back Fribbolt. Nearly every soul on the island is beneath her authority. And, if my records are correct, you didn’t used to be ranked below her.”

“Yes. Thank you for reminding me that I had colors stripped from my sleeves,” Hamish snapped.  That quick lecture seemed to have sobered him up quickly. There was suddenly a sharpness in his words and fire in his eyes that Mitsuko hadn’t seen in him before. “Why are you so well informed for a Sailor’s Guild dock worker? Who are you truly?”

She raised her clipboard defensively and stepped backwards, but there was a smile infecting her lips. “Me? No one important.”

Alarm bells sounded in Mitsuko’s head. Was this an acolyte? Or worse, the island’s guardian? She exchanged a look with Verna but the ice turtle didn’t give any obvious sign of knowing this woman.

Despite Mitsuko being out in the open, the woman hadn’t yet given her even a second glance. Her focus had been on interrogating Fuku and Hamish. Since Mitsuko hadn’t yet opened her mouth, the woman hadn’t seemed to acknowledge her as important. That was a good sign. Perhaps this was just local politics at play, not directly related to the Prismatic Spiral.

After a bit more back and forth, they were permitted to set foot on the dock. Hamish, continuing to grumble about his treatment, moored his skiff. While it had gotten them to their destination, it wasn’t as if Mitsuko and Verna’s concerns were unfounded. The little ship sagged, lopsided in the harbor. It had taken in water somewhere in its hull and was slowly being dragged under by the weight. The entire vessel seemed to be held together by barnacles and plastered on seaweed.

Arriving on Amber Island felt completely different this time around. Instead of skulking around in the back alleys to avoid causing notable ripples in the loop’s events, Mitsuko and Fuku were escorted through the center of the city by a young girl employed as a courier.

The girl clearly did not know what Fuku was, because she continued to happily yap as they followed her. Anyone experienced with witches knew to speak as little as possible when in their presence, or else risk offending them.

“Over there is where my mother got me my shoes! See how they sparkle and leave behind silvery after-images? The wizard who owns the shop is a master cobbler! He’s so nice and friendly. He gave me a piece of taffy as well when I got the shoes. Did you know that Amber Island has all sorts of amazing taffy? It’s made by the candy shops! You can get other hard candies and even expensive chocolates at them, but they’re not made on the island like the taffy is. Have you tried any? The candy shop sign says they sell overseas. You’re from overseas, right?”

“Yes. We are,” Fuku replied.

“Your candy shops over there probably get more business because you have more children in your cities. Some older adults like candies, but I remember when I was younger we used to all go crazy whenever shipments of Amber Island taffy came in. It was like a holiday! You need to try it.”

The girl continued to chatter, but neither Mitsuko nor Fuku appeared to pay much attention. Mitsuko noted that the witch was eyeing each of the nearby rooftops. Her owl fluttered from roof to roof, zigzagging his way in front of them. On the yellow shingles of a barber shop, he encountered one wizard, who offered the bird a sandwich, but no one else interrupted Rou’s path. Mitsuko understood immediately that Fuku was using her familiar as a means in which to scout ahead and sense dangers. Mitsuko had tried using the same strategy with the sages on several occasions, but they were always obstinate about handing over the information they gathered.

Mitsuko wondered if she sealed her soul to a familiar, if their bond would carry over throughout all the future timeloops. In a way, she supposed that the sages already were like familiars in how they were tied to her very soul. With her luck, bonding with an actual familiar would just mess things up further and cause the entire Prismatic Spiral to collapse and explode.

“I only recently gained permission to immigrate to the island. My mother had to beg her master wizard for the right and she finally deemed me an acceptable age to be permitted on Amber.” The courier girl had moved on from candy over to rambling about her life story. It was a follow-up to her ramble about how she was still learning the streets of the city and confession that they were confusing due to the constant changes in building locations. Apparently, some of the buildings occasionally wandered and resettled in empty lots.

“Here we are!” the courier proclaimed. “Wizard Zena’s tower!”

“I can see why she wants to speak to Fuku,” Mitsuko said flatly.

The building wasn’t a tower, not by Mitsuko’s definition at least. It was a squat black building that stood out against all the yellow stone buildings surrounding it. All of the windows were shuttered and at least half of them nailed closed with criss-crossing planks of black wood. An iron gate creaked open to reveal the walkway up to the house. It was buried in gray grass, with only the occasional stone of the path peeking through. Mitsuko thought she spotted actual charred bones in the smoking remains of a smoldering bonfire in the garden.

“I need to go take care of other courier things,” the girl said behind them. “Nice meeting you! You seemed nice so I hope you don’t get eaten!”

“As if I would allow myself to be consumed,” Fuku said darkly as the girl swiftly retreated. “I survived my mother’s wrathful raising, I will not fall now to an unknown witch.”

“Isn’t Zena a wizard?” Mitsuko asked.

“She wishes to learn the secrets of the Hon covens and combine her might with that of the legendary witches of the past. It is why she summoned me to this island.”

“And why did you agree?” Mitsuko asked. “Why not just ignore the request?”

“New location with access to otherwise forbidden information. Since burning my bridge with the Hon witch covens, this seemed the best option for expanding my power. Your father and husband offered to pay for my passage as well so long as I assisted them in locating you.”

“Akuta is not my husband.” Mitsuko bristled. “I left him behind long ago.”

“I suppose so. Since it was recently demanded of me to not interfere with your going-ons for one week, I am afraid I must renege on my promise to them to hunt you down on their behalf. Until next week, that is.”

By the time that next week eventually came, Mitsuko planned to be far too powerful for someone like Fuku to neutralize her. Though, her statement did raise the question as to why Fuku didn’t abduct her in past loops.

“Why didn’t you wipe my mind and drag me off to Akuta immediately?” Mitsuko asked. “Why give me the opportunity to even speak to you?”

“Your father was convinced that you would come willingly. I was meant to allow him an opportunity to do so before resulting to magical coercion.”

“Hm.” Mitsuko still thought it odd that Fuku had never made any hostile move against her in other loops. Though, she supposed she had murdered Akuta several times, making the abduction senseless. “What do you think of Akuta?”

“I despise him,” Fuku answered. She took a step towards the house. “During our journey here, I debated cooking his brain on many occasions; however, as we had forged a pact, I was forced to not harm him until after I delivered you to his arms.” She continued to walk down the overgrown path. “Let us speak no more of the man. Instead, I plan to finally speak to my benefactor.”
