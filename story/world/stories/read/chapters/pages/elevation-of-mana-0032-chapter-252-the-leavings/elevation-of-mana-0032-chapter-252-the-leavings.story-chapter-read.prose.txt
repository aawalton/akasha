My mother, with a bit of prompting from myself, approached the elves the day Big Op left. This was simply a matter of course, if he was there causing problems then it needed to be known, and it needed to be known to keep an eye out for him.

“He left,” Miss Adia told us a few days later. “That afternoon so far as we can tell. He and a few dozen others left the city and haven't returned.”“Will you go after them?” mother asked.

“No, no we won't. If they want to leave that is their right, and there is no evidence they did anything we would be worried about. The ones who left seem to have gone willingly, and there was no fighting or theft from the city at large beyond what could be considered theirs.”

“What I told them would happen,” I griped.

“Hah!” the older elf woman replied. “You could have gone with them you know, if you wanted.”“No Miss Adia, I think we need to be here. We still have a lot to learn, and you are trying to teach us it.”Her father and a few others were nearby giving me appraising looks. The old elf man was actually smiling as he watched on, and heard our explanations. He was nodding, pleased with the way things were going, and that was good, he could have been a real problem if he hadn't.

It didn't take long after that before our meeting ended. I had little to say during it, though a few other members of the city, both elven and human were called in to hear what needed to be said. It didn't amount to too much, but people needed to be told if any of those who left returned, not to punish them, but to make sure they were kept an eye on for trouble.

And yet, weeks passed, and there was no such trouble. The older elf left, taking his wife and most of his people with him. Many of the airships were seen coming and going, but they too didn't stay long, their own business in hand, and things went, mostly back to normal.

Teaching did in fact improve, with another teacher brought in to help and our old one slowly retiring. Eventually she left, heading back to the elven homeland with so many others who had decided not to stick around. A handful of humans went with them too, though very few, mostly older ones who wanted to see something new before they died. I could understand that, it was a simple gesture, but my place was here.
