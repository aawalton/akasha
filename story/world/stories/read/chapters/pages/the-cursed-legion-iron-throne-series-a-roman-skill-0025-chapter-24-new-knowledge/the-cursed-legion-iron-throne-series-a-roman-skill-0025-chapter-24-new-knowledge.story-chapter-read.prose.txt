Max entered through the darkness; a feeling of weightlessness assaulted his senses. The world began to spin—

Then he stood on the other side with both feet on solid ground. One moment he had been falling into a void of endless darkness, and the next he stood in the wake of a command tent. The only light was from lanterns and candles that sat in every corner; no light filtered in from the outside. The smell of incense filled it from an incense holder to the right. Banners of the Golden Claw covered every wall.

In the center was a long table covered by a single map with movable figurines.

Six men stood over the table.

"...the Umbra Pass has been under heavy assault..."

"...a distraction for a larger force coming from the Twelve Titans Pass..."

"...have the legions been mobilized?"

"No reports indicate..."

"Reports? When do we rely on reports? We should send a messenger to get clear information..."

Max gulped as he tuned out the five men who were talking amongst themselves and stared at the lone figure at the head of the table sitting on a chair. While everyone wore the legions' armor of maroon and silver, the man wore nothing but a tunic and pants. While the rest had their weapons on their hips, this man sat with nothing.

That's him. It has to be.

Max's guess proved correct when Senior Casius walked around the table, apologizing to the lieutenants he passed, and stopped by the man. He leaned over and began whispering something to him. The old man kept bowing slightly and kept his voice too low for anyone to hear.

The Centurion sitting on the chair, a man with salt and pepper hair and deep eyes, nodded along.

"Is that him?" The Centurion looked directly at Max.

Max took a step back as soon as the Centurion's eyes locked onto him. Never had he felt so small. Never had he felt so inconsequential before any person. Just being subjected to his gaze made his body scream danger and flood him with the desire to run.

That was power, unadulterated.

What he aspired to become one day, if not greater.

Senior Casius nodded.

"I see," said the Centurion and raised his hand.

The five legionaries went silent, and everyone in the tent turned to look directly at Max.

Max gulped.

The Centurion stood up and walked around the table toward Max. He stopped directly in front of him.

Max had to look up to stare at his face.

"Good. You've unlocked your resistance attribute. That makes things much easier." The Centurion looked back toward Senior Casius. "You did not mention that last time."

Casius hesitated, eyes growing wide. "I-I did not know, Lord Centurion."

"I see. Magir, get me a Patron Mage. I want to see his status page in its entirety. I will not have an unknown quantity around me writing vital information that could change the course of a war. Scribes are important, and I've asked for one, but the lives of millions are not worth having one."

Magir, one of the five lieutenants, saluted and rushed out of the command tent.

The Centurion waved him to follow.

They looped around the table. Max kept his shock in check as the pavilion opened up larger than it had any right to. Beyond the table, hidden by the clothing and shadows, was a massive office area with a larger table made of red wood with silver and gold inlays in the center, with cabinets and drawers behind it made of the same wood and design. A chair of equal quality but with heavy cushioning rested behind it.

A smaller, more average table sat in the left corner with an equally average chair.

Max took a second to look around like he had always done and found another flap that led further into different areas.

How big is this pavilion? It has to be magic, right?

The Centurion walked past the desk to the drawer furthest left, opened the third from the top, and pulled out a roll of parchment, ink, and a feathery quill that glimmered with gems on it. He set them down on the average table and raised its estimated wealth by multiples.

"While we wait for the Patron Mage, come here and show me your writing."

That was all Max did for the next hour as they waited. The Centurion made him write line after line after line until his wrist started hurting, teaching him how to better format the messages, what to always start with, how to end the messages, where to make space for his signature or stamp, and so much more. All of which was pretty simple, other than the fact that he had to keep writing with the Enchanted Parchment, ink, and quill.

It was expressly made for practice, apparently. Anything written using the three together would vanish after two minutes, regardless of what was written.

The Centurion rubbed his chin. "Much better than expected. Where did you learn to write?"

Max considered lying, but a quick look at Senior Casius standing in the other corner without having said or done anything suggested otherwise. He chose to take heed of said suggestion. "My mother, Lord Centurion. She taught me a lot and read many stories to me."

The Centurion stepped back and pulled the cushioned chair from behind the desk and closer to Max. He sat down and crossed one leg over the other, taking a moment to taste the words Max spoke, letter by letter, mulling them bit by bit until he got whatever he was looking for.

"An orphan. Reads and writes. Literate mother, who also lived in the slums when alive. Read, and I am assuming she had books, right?" He asked. Max nodded. "Ah yes. A literate mother, living in the slums, who just so happens to have books. Don't you find that odd? Casius? Do you?"

"Yes, Lord Centurion."

The man frowned at Senior Casius's response and then turned back to Max. "...but there is more. You invoked the Cursed Legion. How did you know to do that? Was it in these books you've read?" He waited for an answer. "Well?"

"I-I..."

Max remembered all the old men that helped him escape guaranteed death.

He didn't know whether the Centurion looked at it in a good or bad light, nor was he going to test it. How could he repay all the people that saved him by sending them to the same fate they helped him dodge? That was disloyal and cowardly. Max was many things, but disloyal? A coward?

No. I am neither of those!

He thought to himself, closed his eyes and grit his teeth. It was not easy being around an individual that powerful for so long. Max took a deep breath, his fate sealed. "Yes, Lord Centurion. It was mentioned in a story I vaguely remember. All I recall were those few lines."

"Huh... Is. That. So."

Max gulped and did his best to keep a stoic, straight face. He nodded.

The Centurion tapped his knee with a small smile, his eyes burning with intensity. The longer he stared, the harder it was to sit there beneath his attention.

Max couldn't believe he was thinking it, but he would rather prefer dealing with the Optio and all the exercises than this. At least there he could figure out what was happening and why it was happening to him. That was not the case here in this command tent. Then again, he had already learned something extremely important within a few minutes of being here.

He learned what the resistance attribute was for, though not entirely. The attribute apparently gave him... well, it gave him resistance against the natural power within the Centurion and likely anyone that was as strong as he was. What he wondered was how far that extended?

His first assumption had been poison resistance, or maybe something against the elements, but it clearly did more.

Yet, that did not explain its synergy with the magic attribute.

I'll ask Senior Casius later—

"That's not what I was told," said the Centurion.

Max snapped his head up.

"I heard quite the tale with climaxes, daring raids, grand sacrifices in the name of friendship, strangers whispering suggestions in cells, and..." the Centurion smiled and rose from his seat. He stepped closer toward Max and towered over him, grabbing his shoulder. "Successful heists."

Max paled and tried to move backward. The chair did not budge, held by something from behind. Max stuttered and tried to make any excuse or deny the allegations but found himself incapable of speaking any words or forming any coherent thought. All he could hear was his beating heart thunder in his chest and skull, pulsing in rhythm with it.

He had been caught—

"Indeed, your spreading title is a worthy one. Only someone with Brass Balls would..."

Senior Casius shifted in his spot.

The Centurion turned toward him. "Is something wrong?"

"Has Captain Hector or Optio Ignacious not informed you?"

"Informed me of what?"

"They're Magical Brass Balls now."

The Centurion stared at Casius for a few seconds without saying anything. Then he burst out into a boisterous, stomach-clenching guffaw that had him stumbling backward. "Magical! He said Magical! Can you imagine it? Magical Brass Balls."

A Title about you and its lore has reached 750 people!

Title Achieved!

Brass Balls II (Magical)!

Known for unwavering courage and standing against an improbable tide regardless of who stands in the opposition!

+3 vitality

+1 reaction

—

A Title about you and its lore has reached a mighty individual of great providence!

Title Achieved!

Brass Balls III (Magical)!

Known for unwavering courage and standing against an improbable tide regardless of who stands in the opposition!

+3 vitality

+1 reaction

+1 resistance

+2 magic

How strong did someone need to be for even the system to title them mighty? More importantly, how screwed was he to have been caught by someone that powerful and with such great providence. Was he doomed? Guaranteed a horrible and agonizing death for escaping with the Skill Shard.

It didn't matter how he figured it out or if Max had made a mistake at this point. Survival was the only thing he could think about.

Max looked toward the exit with his peripheral vision.

Can I make it?

He turned back toward the doubled-over Centurion and the awkward-looking Senior Casius. Neither one was looking toward him. This was the only opportunity he had to escape this place... even if he knew it was near impossible to escape the legion. He would be hunted down for sure, but that was better than giving up his head without trying. He refused to be nothing; at least they'd remember his escape attempt.

Max took a deep breath and then bolted toward the exit—

His face crunched into what felt like a solid city wall. His nose popped multiple times and the world blurred and spun as he slammed onto the earth, groaning in pain. Tears streamed from his eyes and blood leaked profusely from his nose, dripping onto his shirt and the floor.

"Ah, Magir. You finally made it back."

The voices felt mangled and distant, tainted by the pain Max was experiencing. He rolled onto his knees and held his face. He could see the blood in his hands and on the ground even in the dim light and his blurred vision. It was a lot more than he was expecting from just a crunched nose.

Max could feel his Skill Shard coming to life and giving him a boost to recovery, but it was too small to really matter.

Someone kneeled beside him and held his shoulder. "The Patron Mage needed a moment to recover their mana. He had been practicing his arts and used up much of his available energy by the time I reached him."

Magir squeezed Max's shoulder and pushed him up. His other hand grabbed Max's chin and tilted his head toward the ceiling.

Max groaned in pain.

"Be still. Let me heal you."

Magir's hands began to glow a dim blue and gold. Cold energy washed over Max's face. He screamed as the bones and torn flesh popped and reset without anything to dull the pain. Max couldn't fight Magir's grip and escape as he felt everything he had suffered in reverse and all at the same time for a few seconds.

And then it was over. Max sat there covered in his own blood and without any pain at all.

Magir let him go and moved out of the way.

"Your healing arts were always horrible, Magir," said the Centurion.

Max looked up. The most powerful being in the entire legion leaned back onto his desk and smiled as though Max hadn't just made an untimely, horrific attempt at escaping. Senior Casius was shaking his head, though he did notice that the older man was looking at him differently.

He understood why. It made sense to him too.

No one had known he had gotten away with the Skill Shard. No one except the Centurion, and if he had acquaintances he spoke to about him, had known. That meant only the people that were in the room before Magir arrived were aware of it. The issue was that Senior Casius was a Record Keeper. Max didn't quite understand the extent of that, but he had a few guesses. The most obvious one was exactly what the name implied, someone that records what they see and hear... which included this tidbit of information.

"Never been my forte, Lord Centurion," said Magir. He waved his hand and the old man Max remembered on the platform stepped in; the Patron Mage. The old man bowed deeply and moved to stand beside Senior Casius. They shared a long look that left the Patron Mage confused, but otherwise said nothing.

Max noticed it and frowned. Another person added to the list that would learn about his heist.

"Let's get this over with. Let's see what our Magical Brass Balls..." The Centurion stopped and hummed. "I don't like that title. I'll think of a better one... Where was I? Oh yes, let's see your status page. Come here."

Magir stepped out of the room without waiting.

The Patron Mage stepped closer to the Centurion and pulled out a black orb that reflected no light from the surrounding candles and lanterns.

Senior Casius closed his eyes and looked away.

Max watched it all happen, mentally noting everything as best as he could. The Patron Mage had a spatial inventory, maybe a ring, pouch, or similar to Max, a system function. Magir wasn't privy to information that dealt with Max and his situation. And then the most important one so far, if his guess was correct. The Record Keeper kept a record of everything he saw.

Max assumed that because he closed his eyes and turned away—

"Well? Come on..." the Centurion frowned and rubbed his beard with a thoughtful expression. "Little man? Big Mister? Brass Warrior? Magical Brass? Hmm... I can't think of a good title... Ah! I got one! Little Brass! It's perfect!"

The two old mages shared a look.

Max groaned.

The Centurion dutifully ignored their reactions, happy with the title—

A Title about you and its lore has evolved and been titled by a mighty individual of great providence!

Title Achieved!

Brass Balls III (Magical)! -> Little Brass I (Magical)!

Known for unwavering courage and standing against an improbable tide regardless of who stands in opposition! Titled by a mighty individual of great providence! Witnessed by a Record Keeper and a mighty individual

+1 strength

+1 perception

+1 resistance

Max stared at the notification in awe.

He gained an upgraded title just from being around this man, and that wasn't considering all the other attribute boosts he gained too. Fifteen points in less than two hours of being in the same room as the Centurion in casual conversation. Of course, all of them were based on what had already been there in the form of his original title as a foundation, but it was still a massive amount of growth.

It also included a new attribute he hadn't even known existed; perception.

Another one, similar to vitality, that was unlocked through titles and achievements rather than anything he did per se to actively break through whatever barrier hid them. Max wanted to learn what it required to do so and how he could unlock more. Especially before reaching the level 10 threshold and needing to select his class—

"Yes, Little Brass is a perfect name." The Centurion nodded to himself.

Max took that as the perfect sign to get up. He hesitated for a second, considered escaping, remembered his nose crunching on Magir's armor and chest, and then decided otherwise. He didn't want another broken nose, especially if Magir was going to heal him again.

He walked up to the Patron Mage.

The old man in black held his hand out and produced a small dagger from the same spatial inventory.

Max took a deep breath and placed his hand on the Patron Mage's. Captain Samson wasn't here to grab his arm and do the process without warning. Max had to watch as the Patron Mage grabbed his hand, slowly placed the blade on Max's palm, looked up to the Centurion for the go-ahead, and then slowly cut his palm open.

Max gritted his teeth as he watched his hand get cut open and blood flow from the wound.

The Patron Mage pulled Max forward and set his palm on the black orb. It greedily drank all the blood.

Max watched as the orb turned as red as his blood. Only then did the Patron Mage let him go. Max looked at his hand and was surprised to find the wound missing, and the only sign it had ever existed was the traces of blood that hadn't been sucked by the orb.

The Patron Mage picked up the orb and handed it to the Centurion, who took it gracefully.

The old man bowed to his superior and then left without saying another word, leaving Max alone with Casius and the Centurion once more.

The Centurion held the orb close. Blue mana rushed from his hand and into the orb. The mana and the blood swirled without mixing. And then finally, words flickered in the air in front of the Centurion for everyone to see.

Max stared at his status page in reverse—

The Centurion swiped his hand through the status page; it vanished.

Max stepped back as the air in the tent changed. Pressure descended upon the entire room and nearly drove him to his knees.

The Record Keeper gasped in shock.

Max looked toward the Centurion's darkened face. Shadows lined his expression as he stared dead at him. He couldn't help but tremble and shake—

"Casius," his voice was deeper than normal, morphed by the power he radiated. "Did you see the status page?"

Max sucked in his breath and took another step back. He could feel his teeth chatter against his will.

That was a threat. Without a shadow of doubt, it was a promise of violence.

"N-No, Lord Centurion," Senior Casius whispered.

"Leave."

"Yes, Lord Centurion."

Max watched as the Record Keeper departed, dread growing in his chest. His hands grew slick with sweat and his stomach rolled on itself. Every fiber in his being wanted to run away from the monster that was staring at him with those gray, sharp eyes that missed nothing.

Yet, he knew better.

The Centurion set the orb down on the desk and stepped forward; the pressure increased, dropping Max to his knees. "Fome?"

Max grunted as he fell, unable to fight the power that pressed down on him. He had no other reaction. That was his last name... not that he recognized what it was or what it meant.

"It means nothing to you?"

"I am an orphan—"

The Centurion grabbed his neck and lifted him up with one hand into a standing position, comfortable in the sense that he didn't need to tip-toe, not that having a monster of the Centurion's power holding your neck was in any way comfortable at all. "You've never met your father?"

"N-Never. My mother died when I was younger."

"Yet she had time to teach you to read and write so well? How?" The Centurion's grip tightened slightly.

Max groaned as the pressure kept increasing. His vision was growing darker the longer this lasted.

"Speak! How!"

"I don't know! I don't know!"

The Centurion dropped him. "You don't know? How old were you when your mother died?"

"S-Seven!"

A few seconds passed as the Centurion studied him for anything that indicated dishonesty. Only when he didn't find anything did the pressure suddenly vanish and he let Max's throat go.

Max dropped; his legs had no strength to keep him up anymore. Shakes assaulted his entire body.

The Centurion stepped away and toward the table. Max heard the orb being picked up—

The Centurion slammed it into the ground, shattering it into a thousand pieces of glass. Blood splashed onto the ground, still mixed with the liquid blue mana, drops hitting Max from where he sat.

He looked up at the scene.

A thousand questions ran through his mind.

What did Fome mean?

Why did the Centurion react so violently?

What baggage did his family name carry?

Where was his father, and why had he been raised by his mother alone?

What did that have to do with teaching him to read and write? He knew it was odd and rare for someone like him to learn those skills, but it wasn't that impossible. Right? There had to be others that became scribes and students and whatever else required the skill to read and write.

"Tell me..." The Centurion said as he placed both hands on the desk and leaned forward, leaving his back toward Max. "When did you learn your last name?"

When? I unlocked it… he thought long and hard.

His brain was foggy, and it made it difficult to remember anything so clearly.

"The day I unlocked my Status Page... err... Here in the legion. When I killed the goblin to unlock level 1," said Max.

"I see... And do you know who named you?"

Max frowned. He wasn't quite sure... but thought deeply about it anyway. His mother never truly liked his name and always cut it short to Max. Her dislike of the name had never been clear why, yet, now that he thought about it...

Maybe my father named me, and that's why she hates it? He thought.

"I-I don't know, but my mother never called me my full name—"

"Maximilian Fome," The Centurion covered his face with one hand and tilted his head up. "'The Greatest' Fome. Named by a mighty Fome—"

"What if it's not who you think it is? There can't be just one person that—"

The Centurion laughed. "Your surname is in two parentheses. It's him. It can be no other. Only the Dreaded Dragon would earn a change to the system itself from the norm."

Max took a deep breath. That was the first time he had heard anyone say anything about his father. Even his mother refused to mention his name or anything about him at all, always going dead silent whenever Max asked even the simplest questions. And now he has a title...

This was far more important to him than all the attribute points and tidbits of knowledge he had collected so far. More important than his Patron Inheritance. The Skill Shard, skills, achievements, titles, and everything else he had gained because it was his father's.

It was his family.

A-Am I secretly a dragon—

"And no. You don't have dragonblood." said the Centurion with a knowing smile as he looked back.

Max cursed under his breath.
