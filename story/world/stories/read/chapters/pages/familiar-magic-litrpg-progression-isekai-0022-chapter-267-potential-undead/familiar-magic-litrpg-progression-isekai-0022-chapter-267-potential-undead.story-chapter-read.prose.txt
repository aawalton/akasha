Ethan raced toward terrible danger…in order to save demons from the undead. He tried not to consider the complexity of that reality as he rushed right at it, throwing explosively charged knives up at the colossal gigas towering over his army. The storm raged above, and Ethan splashed through ankle-deep water as he moved, but he doubted the immense beast would have any problem with the terrain.

The Twilight menace he faced pounded two enormous demons against the ground. They were some of the strongest Ethan had managed to lure back the army, large insectoids, with powerful claws and jaws, but the gigas was bashing them to pieces. Ethan had to throw several more volleys of knives, each one lighting up the battlefield in a flash, before the four-armed giant let one of its victims free.

The other wasn’t so lucky, and with the titan’s full attention on it, Ethan arrived to the sound of it being crushed. He raced up the giga’s side, slashing away, trying to get its attention, reaching out to his team as he did so.

“We need a plan,” Selina said. “A new and better plan.”

“I’m trying to neutralize the big one,” Ethan said, “ what are you thinking?”

“I’m thinking that this is pointless if we don’t target the real undead. While you’re fighting the gigas, our army is being decimated.”

“They’ll die a lot faster with this bastard left to itself; it nearly dropped two other Twilights in a matter of minutes.” He paused to dodge massive, grasping hands. “Why is the army struggling so badly? I know you were ambushed, but a lot of the demons I’m seeing are just milling around in confusion.”

It was Angelos who answered, voice clipped and angry. “They aren’t like us, they are effectively rabid animals. I have them leashed, and I can sick them on targets, but they recognize this threat. They’re panicking and trying to escape.”

Ethan swore. “Even if you direct them personally, the storm is cutting visibility down, and in this light it isn’t easy to spot the undead. They’re only slightly paler than the red demons, and they’re all mixed together.”

“Then what do we do?” Angelos demanded.

“What do demons usually do?” Ethan wondered aloud. “How the hell are they able to survive these things on their own. What would they be doing if we weren’t here?”

“I’d have to release them to find out,” Angelos said.

“Never mind, just thinking out loud,” Ethan said. “Alright, I have a bad plan.”

“A second bad plan, you mean?” Selina asked.

“We need to isolate the sick from the healthy, triage this situation. Fable’s already a wall against the storm, can we lock out the undead entirely?”

Fable’s voice jumped in. “With our current energy reserves, that should be possible. There are some features of the landscape that will help, but ultimately we’ll be relying on exterior weapon systems to hold back the undead.”

“Do it, Fable.”

“Of course, Doctor Bishop,” she said. Ethan could see the train already transforming in the distance, the wall getting longer, and arcing away from the undead in a semi-circle.

“And are we just sacrificing the other half of the army?” Angelos demanded. “Ethan, the work we’ve put into this–”

“I know!” Ethan shouted back. “I know, damn it. One thing at a time. Get Fable to make a single gate in the wall, with a safe place for you. Let the demons in, burn away anything that doesn’t belong.”

“We’ll do it,” Apollo called back, his more controlled voice replacing that of his Familiar. “But hurry up with that gigas. As we speak, our force shrinks while theirs grow.”

Finally putting his whole attention on the battlefield, Ethan reassessed the situation. He’d spent his brief call with Apollo using the giant like a walking weapon’s platform. He’d used superior speed to stay ahead of the four arms desperately questing for him, while hurling a stream of daggers into the onrush of undead from the nearby forest.

Their numbers weren’t endless, and they were easy targets before they became lost in the army’s thousands. Revan appeared in a burst of flame, charging in the undead, catching them in a terrible inferno. Satisfied, Ethan turned back to the behemoth he was riding, and brought out the big guns.

Glistening Razor, Epic

Masterwork Twilight Greatsword

Special Effect: [Damage increases with larger targets]

King Rothavaro created this sword himself. Forged from thousands of independent shards of Twilight glass, the blade was designed to slay giants. Each swing will extend the sword’s reach as desired, allowing the user to hunt the greatest of game.

Rothavaro crafted the sword in his youth, painstakingly making each piece of glass–which looked like tiny, impossibly sharp stars–then adding them to a dimensional space inside the hilt. It was little more than a dagger at first, but the king explained to Ethan that he’d used the blade to exercise his Twilight Gift. At first it took hours to make a star, but by the time he’d taken the throne of Viridus, he could make hundreds a day.

When Ethan used the blade now, he found it remarkable how light and powerful it was. While it tore through the gigas’ undead flesh, Ethan couldn’t help but be distracted by how flashy the sword was. Its default length was six feet long, with a wide, straight blade. That blade was composed of thousands of tiny shards of glass, however, and they reflected light in thousands of different directions. With the storm’s constant lightning, Ethan was practically hosting a rave.

The Glistening Razor carved, as it was meant to. As Ethan ran and leapt, the blade would flash out, doubling, tripling its length or more depending on his whim. There was just so much flesh to cut, and the deathly silent gigas moved too quickly for him to easily line up attacks. He needed support.

Revan leapt from a nearby melee, his feline agility easily positioning him behind the creature, while Ethan scrambled out front. Fire erupted forward, a thick, heavy blast of it crashing into the gigas, and running across it in a wave.

It was disturbing when the creature showed no reaction, simply fighting as the heavy downpour smothered the flames.

Ethan swore, dismissing Revan. Somehow this thing was too hard to burn, which left slicing it to pieces. Tomo appeared in his twenty-something foot size, katanas drawn, and he looked like a toddler compared to what they faced. But Tomo had done this before, and he jumped at their foe, swords a blur.

Brutal, somewhat mechanical work took place then, as Ethan and his Familiar slowly cut down the enormous monster. Ethan could even watch the rest of the battle as he sliced and dodged.

The Fable train had done its work well, adding dozens of cars to create a safe enclosure on the east side of the battle. All along its lengths, enormous tongues of fire stretched out, while beams of blue-white energy carved through the closest undead. As suggested, a twenty pace wide gate opened in the center, and the army of demons rushed for it.

Ethan swore, seeing how much of their force they’d already lost, and how many undead were hounding the demons toward the train. At least half seemed to be former recruits.

“How do the demons normally handle this?” he wondered again.

At least the gigas was slowing. Between Ethan and Tomo, the beast had already lost two of its four arms, it was crippled and down on one knee, and it was barely holding itself together.

Both swordsmen went for the kill, racing to target its three, vulnerable heads. By instinct, or possibly some unseen controller, the gigas did an impressive job of keeping them both back for a minute, but it had been weakened too far, and one head after another crashed to the ground, severed by odachi and glass blade.

Ethan and Tomo spent a few more minutes finishing it off, making sure it couldn’t even drag its headless body around. Then they fought together across the battlefield, hounding those undead chasing the army.

“Gigas is down,” Ethan reported. “How goes your side?”

“Not well,” Apollo admitted. “Three times we’ve had to put down outbreaks over here. There’s tunneling and flying undead, and Fable keeps needing to adapt.”

“Just keep fighting,” Ethan said. “We’ll move on to finding the ones behind this attack. I can see a couple of good places they could be watching from–I’ll let you know when I find them.”

“Hopefully we still have an army, by then,” Apollo grumbled. The man was clearly becoming short-tempered, hovering around, babysitting demons all day. But there was nothing more Ethan could do right now.

He signaled, and Tomo gave a salute before disappearing into a puff of light. Deevee took his place, and Ethan jumped on the back of a three-headed hydra as a half dozen copies flew in each direction, looking for the target.

Ethan had only been airborne for a few seconds when something crashed down on them from above, taking Deevee by surprise. They slammed into the ground, Ethan rolling to his feet as Deeve flashed away. He looked up, searching for the next opponent.

He didn’t need to look far. The gigas was back.

It reformed before his eyes. Car-sized chunks floating up into the air, slotting into the right location, then sealing shut in seconds. Only half-reformed, the gigas was already attacking, using one of its own severed limbs as a club.

“We have a problem,” he sent to the group.

“What now?” Apollo demanded.

“Well, apparently undead this powerful can restore their minions from nothing more than a pile of wet chunks. The gigas is up and fighting. We’re in trouble.”
