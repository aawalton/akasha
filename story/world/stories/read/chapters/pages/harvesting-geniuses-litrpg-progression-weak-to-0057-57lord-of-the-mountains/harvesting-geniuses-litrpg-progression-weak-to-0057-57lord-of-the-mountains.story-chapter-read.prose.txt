Jinyo ran with Gretel on his back, pushing full speed through the pouring rain. The herd of bipedal deer-creatures in a blood frenzy behind them was straight out of a zombie film. The muddy ground made running treacherous—not only that, but Floral Step had been completely sealed by the mud caking his shoes. Worse still was Nephrin, swinging her arms with everything she had. Her bare feet had long since lost count of how many branches and stones they’d struck. Every step was agony and revolting.

“I swear—when I get home, I’m selling every single pair of high heels I own!”

“Don’t sell them—I feel sorry for them. But if you really are going to, can I buy them at half price? My sister likes pretty things.”

“Doctor Jinyo, I don’t think your sister wants second-hand shoes.”

“HWAK!” An Eltharok let out a bellowing roar, swinging its arms to clear branches and vines blocking its path as it accelerated. The muddy, rain-slicked ground was no obstacle at all to creatures that ruled this forest. Without even looking back, Jinyo could feel them closing in.

“I hate to say this, but running isn’t the answer. There’s no way we’re faster than these things in their own territory. The harder we run, the more energy we waste.” Nephrin panted as she ran, the pressure squeezing her mind for a way out of the crisis. Having to run from monsters while carrying a seven-year-old boy was an unbearable burden. The only reason she was still safe was Jinyo covering her rear. She hugged Gretel—whose eyes were shut tight—until the boy felt suffocated. She was genuinely terrified that a single misstep would be the end.

“I can’t fight and protect everyone alone at the same time. We need to regroup with the others at minimum.” The surprise attack had scattered the survivors in every direction. He had no idea which way Esther or Luciria had gone. Finding even Esther alone would change the entire situation.

“Bloodline Deduction!” Jinyo’s eyes bled red. He opened Esther’s information window and filtered by dominant and recessive traits matching his profile. He didn’t know which direction Esther had run, but he was willing to gamble. Bloodline Deduction’s search range extended far beyond line of sight and was unaffected by fog or rain.

“Found him.” The skill reacted—flagging a direction not far from where they stood.

“Nephrin—I’m accelerating. Follow me.” Jinyo surged from rear guard to the front, slashing branches and vines out of their path as he moved. Nephrin didn’t hesitate, clutching Gretel tight as she ran at his heels, a procession of Eltharoks in tow. As they drew close, shadows locked in combat came into view—unmistakably Esther and Luciria, holding off Eltharoks and Cursed Villagers.

Esther swung his blade with arms like iron cables. A single stroke nearly cleaved a Cursed Villager in two. The man who had once killed an Eltharok in a single form was soaked through and gasping for breath, his left hand pressed against his stomach where blood was flowing freely. Jinyo hadn’t expected to see him in a state this grim.

Peach Blossom Dance!

Jinyo channeled Planar through the blade and struck in a continuous eight-hit rhythm to create an opening in the Eltharok. The moment the window appeared, Esther didn’t hesitate—he drew back his arm to full extension and swung, targeting the neck: the kill point.

Second Detonation!

Esther forced his body through the form again. The rain-soaked blade erupted in a plume of white steam as it sliced through the neck of the nearest small Eltharok. He leapt aside, dodging a flurry of fists and kicks from the enemy. Two kills—but plenty more remained.

“What happened to your skill? Don’t try to act like a shonen protagonist. You joined our side and now you’re this weak—you’re making my life harder.”

“Shut up! Blame the rain.” Holding off Eltharoks for several minutes using only basic enhancement skills was already pushing past his limit. If he had any choice in the matter, would he want to be in this sorry state?

“Fair enough. I agree completely!” No need to ask who that was.

“Hah.” Jinyo exhaled. The two strongest combatants in the group—one of the highest-scoring exam candidates and a Divine Punishment wielder—were now more liability than help. Looking at the Planar he had left, it wasn’t enough to open the Realm of Paradise. Even though it sped up his recovery, the cost of establishing a Domain would leave his reserves too depleted to fight for any period of time.

The downside of Domain magic: once you stepped outside its radius of operation, you could do nothing except expand the existing Domain or establish a new one. He had burned far too much Planar adding the granary and the cottage.

‘There’s nowhere to run. Sven still hasn’t regained consciousness, and we’re surrounded on all sides.’ The reason Esther and Luciria hadn’t gone far was the dead weight called Sven. Without leaving him behind, breaking through an encirclement of monsters was impossible. Luciria and James would never do that—and neither would Jinyo’s own heart permit him to make that call.

Jinyo ate a raw Explosive Potato and forced himself into Moonlight Status. Moonlight Status gained by consuming agricultural produce without exposure to actual moonlight lasted only five minutes. He drew his sword and released a dense double-element Planar.

“Anyone who can still hold a weapon—fight as best you can! I’ll draw their attention—all you need to do is find a way to cut their heads off.” Jinyo poured Planar into the Sonneteer. Peach blossom petals swirled across the battlefield. Strangely, despite the heavy rain, not a single petal was washed to the ground. Jinyo broke into the sword dance—not aiming to inflict wounds with each slash, but dispersing the pink Planar as far as possible.

Multiple Eltharoks lunged at Jinyo. They ignored the presence of Esther and Luciria entirely. Jinyo jumped and executed Floral Step despite the mud caked to his ankles. His precision was severely compromised—errors could happen—but this wasn’t the moment to worry about that.

The Sonneteer responded to the Planar with a faint light. One after another, Eltharoks fell under the Bewitched status and their attention converged on Jinyo. Not every monster was affected—several continued attacking Esther and Luciria—but the bulk of the pressure had been redirected to him.

“Truly the bladework of legend. I never knew the Sonnet of a Hundred Blossoms could be used like this.” James praised Jinyo’s actions quietly. It was open suicide—drawing almost every enemy’s focus onto himself. What would happen if Jinyo’s power ran out first, or if he mistepped and found himself encircled? It was no different from throwing raw meat into a pack of starving hyenas.

“Miss Luciria—this is no time to hesitate. If we don’t do something, your teacher will die, and after him, the rest of us follow.” James seized a short spear and braced it on his fist. Luciria drew her sword from its scabbard with trembling hands. It was terrifying. The pressure and the tension made her want to cry.

Luciria channeled blue Planar through the blade and fixed her eyes on the Eltharoks, searching for an opening. Giants with necks so thick no ordinary girl’s strength could cut through—rain falling so hard no one could fight at full capacity. If she didn’t make herself useful, she couldn’t protect anyone. Not the teacher who had done so much for her. Not the half-brother who was still alive.

Wanderer’s Sword Art — First Form:

Shore Break!

Luciria lunged in a single bound. She spun mid-air, and a wave of water surged along the blade, merging with the falling rain into a cresting torrent. Water-element Planar and Sword Spirit, stacked in power, drove a devastatingly heavy cut. The keen edge found the back of the neck of an Eltharok that Jinyo had drawn into the Bewitched state.

The blade connected—but didn’t cut all the way through. It was the water wave that extended her striking range enough to finish the job. The head of the smallest Eltharok separated from its shoulders. She didn’t stop there. Luciria recalled the patterns she had drilled for hours every day in the combat simulation room under her grandfather’s demanding watch—monsters small and humanoid, resembling goblins or hobgoblins, with a reach similar to that of the Cursed Villagers.

Using her light frame, she kicked off and lunged into a second beat, launching into a continuous string of cuts.

The Wanderer’s Sword Art channeled water-element Planar—and here, it didn’t merely fail to hinder her. It strengthened her. The Planar control technique she had practiced day after day let her draw on the rainwater itself to amplify her damage. The more water or moisture in the environment, the greater Luciria’s advantage.

“Miss Luciria—this old butler is moved to tears.” James leapt and drove a spear straight through a skull. The seasoned elder butler moved in tandem with Luciria—not as strong as a young man, but thirty-plus years of honed skill made him more reliable than the two bodyguards who had ever protected her.

“Empower.” Inaction wasn’t her way. Nephrin shifted to the rear, raised her staff, and began casting enhancement spells on the others from range. She wasn’t proficient in support magic, but she had learned the basics once. She cycled through the party, distributing spells—Jinyo alone receiving five.

Strength boost. Speed boost. Fatigue reduction. Weapon-edge sharpening. Focus enhancement. All of it heaped onto Jinyo alone. Nephrin refused to let Jinyo die. Esther pursued Jinyo at a relentless sprint and fell into step beside him. Together, Jinyo and Esther synchronized their movements and killed the Eltharoks one by one.

Even injured, even operating below peak efficiency, Esther remained a superhuman who could kill an Eltharok in a single form. His raw strength and Planar reserves were high. With Jinyo using the Sonnet of a Hundred Blossoms to cloud their vision and dull their senses, decapitating them became manageable work—provided he stayed calm and found his moment.

‘It’s fine. No rushing. Take it slow.’ Jinyo repeated Peach Blossom Dance over a hundred times. He palmed a ball of Celestial Rice and a raw Explosive Potato and crammed them into his mouth to refuel as fast as he could. The supply was hemorrhaging—dwindling by the minute. The look Jinyo gave Esther said everything. Esther closed his eyes, breathing steady, focused entirely on reading the rhythm for his moment.

Time passed. Jinyo’s movements sharpened. His proficiency in Peach Blossom Dance climbed in rapid bursts. Peach Blossom Dance was the form with the highest Planar output per strike, and paired with the Sonneteer’s ability to induce the Bewitched status more easily and enhance the Sonnet of a Hundred Blossoms—it was the best tool he had. Jinyo, Esther, Luciria, and James fought together until the last Eltharok fell.

The downpour eased.

Everyone was soaked through and exhausted to the marrow—on the edge of collapsing into the mud. The experience gain had been substantial. Luciria, whose level was still low, benefited most. She had never fought this many real monsters in her life. Let alone killed at least three Eltharoks through her own effort.

Without Jinyo, she couldn’t have done it.

“Is it over?” Jinyo murmured.

“More or less.” Esther dropped himself into a puddle without a care for how it looked.

“Well done, Miss.”

“It’s because of James and the Professor. It wasn’t… as frightening as I thought.”

“The first step is the highest wall. You’ve already cleared the hardest part. From here on, nothing is as hard.” James consoled the small girl and steadied her spirit. Luciria felt both shaky and lighter at the same time. She could hardly believe she had killed an Eltharok. But the experience points and the level increase were real.

“Bro Yo.” Gretel ran to his father, who had barely enough strength left to stay standing.

“Were you scared, Gretel?”

“I wasn’t scared! I’m super tough!”

“Good. How could Rina’s son be scared?”

“Bro Yo already knows.”

“Heh…” Jinyo let himself sit down. Everything had happened so fast. What exactly had just happened?

“In the end it’s only us, isn’t it. No idea what happened to the others.” Nephrin swept her gaze over everyone still standing. Going from hundreds to seven—it was hard to make peace with. She had nearly killed herself gathering those people, and in the end they’d been reduced to the same handful she’d started with.

“Those people scattered in every direction. The poor wounded who couldn’t move—they’re probably all dead by now. Can’t help it. Who would’ve thought a herd of monsters in the hundreds would rush the base? Just surviving with my head attached is more than enough. Don’t dwell on it.” Esther was clutching his stomach. His face had gone progressively pale. The adrenaline that had driven his body through the crisis drained away the instant the danger passed.

“Hey—are you all right?” Nephrin rushed over and lifted his shirt. A large bruise was spreading, petal-shaped.

“It’s because he was protecting me. He…” Luciria dropped her gaze.

“Doctor Jinyo—we need to drain the internal bleeding immediately.”

“No! Stop! Don’t think for one second that I don’t know your twisted medical methods. I will not allow it. It’s just a bruise—it’ll heal on its own. Stay out of my business.” Sooner let you cut me open and stuff Cursed Fruit down my throat? I’d rather die right here.

“Don’t be difficult. If you die, what do we do?”

“Then let me die. I’m not weak enough to need your help!”

“!!!” Jinyo reached for the Sonneteer on reflex. The blade swept out in a fierce arc and struck a high-speed spinning spherical object that had materialized from nowhere. It was heading straight for Gretel, who was still in a weakened state—and if Jinyo had been a second slower, all that would have remained of his son was a cooling body. The energy wave radiating from the ball passed through his pulse points. The Planar inside his body was being twisted, destroying his organs one section at a time.

Bang!

Jinyo deflected the ball and sent it back. A dense clot of blood drooled from his mouth. The fingers gripping the sword shook uncontrollably from their tips all the way to the shoulder. He couldn’t stop himself from tracking the direction the ball had bounced—and the familiar presence he could never forget cried out for his full attention.

Gretel trembled and fell. His legs refused to move. His skin had reddened like scalded flesh. Jinyo shifted to place himself in front of his son as a shield. The thing was advancing slowly—stepping out of the dark forest in the silence after the rain had stopped. A grey form standing upright, as tall as a tree. A heavily muscled, inhuman body with four antlers branching from above, making it look like a walking ancient oak.

In its left hand it carried a ball made of animal hide. The same ball that had just tried to take Gretel’s life.

Esther looked at it through his golden eyes and felt his heart lurch. If Jinyo was an evil demon cloaked in darkness and revulsion, this creature—grotesquely shaped as it was—radiated sacred, exalted power. Those luminous amber eyes looking down at all seven of them were the eyes of something regarding livestock waiting to be culled.

“What the hell are you?!” It had the shape of a monster but the aura of the divine. In all his life, Esther had never encountered anything like this. An invisible pressure bore down on his very soul, willing him to surrender. There was no way he could survive a creature like this.

“As I suspected.” Jinyo had felt it coming for a long time. Nothing that had unfolded since he entered the Cursed World had been coincidence. What the system was now displaying told him everything.

[You have discovered: Lord of the Mountains — Servant of Heaven, Level 50 (?)]

The presence Jinyo had registered from the very first second. Like the Chimera Wolf or the Locust Demon. But what set this apart from everything before was this—it carried the same scent as an Annow. Yet it was not an Annow.

“I have a proposal you cannot refuse.” The Lord of the Mountains opened its mouth and spoke in human language.

“Nephrin, Roswin. James, Kranet. Esther, Kalos. Submit yourselves to Heaven—and you will be granted a long and glorious life.”
