Five days had passed since the afternoon in the dungeon with Arthur. Six since the night the system had woken back up and set its number running, and that number now stood at two months, twenty-nine days, and eighteen hours.

The preparations had been moving ever since. Castle White was the target, and getting Adom inside it with the runes laid where they needed to be was no small undertaking. Valiant had the whole of it in hand, his network feeling out the approaches, the guard rotations, the supply lines, the names of men who might be bought or bent or simply gone around. It would take what it took. But they meant to move the moment the way was clear, and not a day past it.

In the meantime, Adom had gone on living.

There was no sense in doing otherwise, and a great deal of sense in not looking like a man with a coup folded in his pocket. So he kept his hours. He taught. He sat his duties at the Magisterium, graded the work his students handed in, worked with Prof Kim on their many projects and trained with Beth.

Unsurprisingly, the runes hadn't faded. The sun still sat in his palm, banked and quiet, and the enhancement rune still marked his forearm, and neither was the sort of thing a man could walk the city wearing in plain sight. So he wore gloves now. Fine ones, soft leather, the left to cover the palm. And because one gloved hand and one bare one would have drawn every eye in any room, he wore two, and let people put it down to a new affectation of the Ghost of Arkhos, who had always been allowed his oddities.

What he hadn't counted on was the rest of the city agreeing with him; within the week he'd started spotting the same soft leather, worn in matching pairs, on students and clerks all over Arkhos, having set a fashion while trying to hide something.

It was on the fifth day, just as he finished a class, that the summons found him.

He'd sent the last of his students off with their reading and was wiping down the board when a Magisterium runner came in with a folded note. A special summit. His presence required. No subject named, no agenda, nothing but a time, a hall, and the Archmage's seal pressed into the wax.

Adom had not liked the feel of the week.

He'd spent it turning the same questions over and getting nowhere with them. What were Rayhan and Mephtilem building toward, that the system had set three months against it? What did they know, what were they moving, where would it break the surface first? He'd put Valiant onto pieces of it and chased others himself, and turned up fragments that pointed everywhere and nowhere at once. But beneath all of it, all week long, sat a low and steady unease he could not talk himself out of.

He'd mentioned it to Beth, in passing, between drills.

She hadn't been surprised. An after-effect of the training, she'd said. He was growing sensitive enough to the currents now that trouble announced itself a little ahead of arriving, the world leaning faintly toward what was coming before it came. She'd seemed almost pleased about it.

But Adom was not pleased about it. The last thing he wanted was to go through his days with a permanent weight in his gut, feeling the future tilt toward him before he could see what it carried. It was one more thing to hold, and he held enough already.

He was still turning all of that over, the note folded in his gloved hand, when he came up the long approach to the Magisterium and saw the carriage at the main gates.

It was a beautiful thing. He registered that first, before the rest of it caught up to him. Black and silver, lacquered to a mirror shine, drawn by a pair of pegasi that stood folding and refolding their great white wings, their breath steaming faintly in the afternoon air of early fall.

Then he saw the crest worked into the door. The imperial crest.

For a long moment Adom stood very still on the approach, and regretted, deeply and specifically, every bad feeling he'd had all week, and the training that had given those feelings teeth, and the whole art of divination, root and branch.

"...Fuuuuck," he said.

He did not go in right away.

He stood on the approach a moment longer and let himself feel exactly as bad about it as the thing deserved, because there was no good version of this.

The imperial crest did not roll up to the Magisterium to deliver glad tidings. It came because the Emperor wanted something, or wanted to look at something, or wanted to remind a hall full of mages whose Empire they served.

And Adom, who had burned the whole week on the question of what Rayhan and Mephtilem were preparing, did not love it landing on his doorstep the very afternoon his bad feeling came due.

He made himself walk in anyway. There was nothing else to do. Bolting from an imperial carriage would have raised more questions than facing whatever sat behind it.

The entrance hall was too quiet for the hour. He went up the steps and through the great doors into the long sunlit corridor beyond, and with every stride the unease wound a notch tighter, because he still had no idea what he was walking toward.

If this was a move, if they had come for him here, in the open, then they had come knowing exactly what he was. A first-circle mage, in his own seat, ringed by his own people. Nobody opened a fight on those terms without being very sure how it ended.

Which meant, if it came to a fight, Adom wanted Biggins.

The thought stopped him mid-stride. The dragon had told him to call the moment it came to that, and a corridor full of imperial steel might be exactly the moment he'd meant. Better early and wrong than late and dead. Adom half-turned, the call already shaping itself in his mind—

"Magus Adom Sylla!"

A big, deep voice rolled down the corridor, far too cheerful for an ambush. Adom turned back and found a man coming toward him down the length of the sunlit hall. He was tall, well past two meters, broad to match, blond hair cropped short over a clean-shaven jaw, a stride that ate the floor.

A red cape swung from steel pauldrons at his shoulders, and a sword rode at his hip.

Everything about him was built to a scale that made the corridor look modest. He was, by any honest reckoning, magnificent. He knew it, too, and carried it without a shred of arrogance.

Adom stopped where he stood.

"Oh, for God's sake," he said, under his breath.

Then he put on a smile and went to meet him.

"General. What a surprise."

Magnus Strong. The name had always struck Adom as almost unfairly on the nose, and the man did nothing whatsoever to soften it.

Supreme General of the Imperial forces. Every sword, every shield, every standing army the Empire could put into a field answered, in the end, to him. After the Emperor, the Archmage, and the Chancellor, no man in the whole of the realm stood higher than Magnus Strong.

The General's hand came out, and Adom took it.

He'd forgotten about the handshake until their palms met, and then he remembered all at once, because Magnus Strong had a habit, and the habit was this: every handshake was a quiet contest. A slow, steady tightening, a small testing pull, the whole of it conducted in silence behind a pleasant face, to see whether the other man would give.

Most men gave. Adom usually did too, because he'd never had a reason worth the bother of doing otherwise.

But today was different.

He didn't know what Magnus had come for. And if it turned out the General had come to put him in the ground, Adom decided he was not about to spend the last minutes before it losing a handshake. So when the pull came, he met it, held, and pulled back.

A flicker crossed Magnus's eyes. Interest, perhaps.

"The last time I laid eyes on you," the General said warmly, his grip firming a degree, "was your nomination. The day they put the title on you. Youngest magus the order's ever named." He gave a small, companionable tug. "You looked half terrified, if you don't mind my saying so."

"I was, a little." Adom kept his voice easy and his smile where it was, but did not yield a finger's width. "It's a lot to be handed all at once."

"And how do you find it now? The work?"

They were both pulling in earnest, hands locked, knuckles paling, neither face giving away a thing of it. This was a bit ridiculous, to be honest.

How old was this man, anyway? Supreme general of the realm, and still turning a handshake into a contest over whose was bigger.

"Busy," Adom said pleasantly. "Endlessly busy. But noble work, and gratifying, when it goes well. I wouldn't trade it."

Magnus laughed, and for a moment the pressure on Adom's hand eased, and then it came back twice as hard.

"Good. Good man."

He clapped his free hand over the back of Adom's, trapping it, while smiling. "That's the spirit of the thing. A man ought to do his work whether he loves it or no. That's duty."

His eyes stayed warm. "But loving it, now. My father always said loving the work was the whole difference between a long life and a short one. He was right about most things, the old man. Rest him."

"Speaking of which," Magnus went on, easy as ever, "how is your father? It's been too long. I don't think I've laid eyes on Arthur since he hung up his sword."

Adom couldn't have said whether it was the heat of the moment that did it, the blood up, every nerve reading the room for a knife. Maybe he was seeing threats in shadows. But the General had just spoken of his own father in the past tense, rest him, and turned in the same breath to Adom's, who was very much alive.

There was a message in that. Adom was nearly sure of it. And he did not like it one bit.

So he stopped being polite about the handshake.

[Primordial Body] had remade him years ago, well past the limits of ordinary flesh. His bare strength alone ran orders of magnitude beyond anything a normal man could bring to a grip, and he had never once needed a spell to prove it.

"He's well," Adom said, warm as anything. "Restless, you know how he is. Took me hunting last week. In fine form."

Magnus's smile held for exactly as long as it took him to understand he was losing.

Then it began, very slightly, to wither at the corners. His arm tensed. He pulled back, a real pull now, the friendliness gone out of it and a soldier's full strength behind it, and for the first time the contest was honest. Adom met it without effort. And then, because the man had gone and brought up his father, he drew the General's hand around and held it where he wanted it, still and beaten, and kept it there.

Guess I've got the bigger one, he thought, and went on smiling.

"Good," Magnus said. The friendliness had gone out of his voice. "That's... good. Glad to hear it."

He wasn't thinking about the conversation anymore. Neither of them was. Their hands were locked just like their eyes and the pleasant words had gone hollow, a courtesy neither man was paying the slightest attention to.

And then Adom felt it. Just a touch, a stirring deep under the General's skin. Fluid, beginning to move. Instinct, most likely. A fighting man's body reaching for its weapon, the same way Adom's own reached for mana when the hair went up on his neck.

His heart picked up.

Is this it, he thought. Are we really doing this? Here? Now? In the hall?

He was already past the question. The first spell sat half-woven in his mind, the thing he'd loose the instant it started, and the shape of the second was waiting behind that when he heard footsteps.

They came from somewhere behind Magnus, ordinary boots on stone, and just like that the moment came apart. Whatever had been winding tight between the two of them folded itself away in the space of a breath. The Fluid under the General's skin went still and the grip eased.

They let go of each other.

The footsteps belonged to Merlin. He came round the corner unhurried, and his eyes passed over the two of them once, taking in more than he let on.

"Adom." A nod. "General Strong."

"Magus Merlin." Magnus turned back to easy warmth in an instant. "Good to see you."

So they knew each other. Of course they did.

"And you." Merlin stopped beside them. "I had a missive just now. A summit, off the calendar, no subject named. We don't convene outside the monthly councils without good reason, so I knew this one mattered."

His eyes went to Magnus. "And the Supreme General waiting in the hall tells me it matters a great deal. Is everything all right?"

"Oh, yes… yes." Magnus was already turning toward the far end of the hall, toward the doors of the council chamber. "The rest are inside, I'm told. The whole of the Ten but you two. They're waiting on the three of us."

He gestured them ahead, the picture of good cheer again, as if the last minute had never happened.

"Shall we?"
