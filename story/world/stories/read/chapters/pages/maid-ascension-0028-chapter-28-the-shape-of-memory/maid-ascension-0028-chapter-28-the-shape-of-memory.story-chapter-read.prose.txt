Having already learned two spells that day, Lira spent the rest of the evening cleaning and organizing. The work itself was simple, but the state of the library made it feel endless. Dust, misplaced books, poorly maintained shelves, and stacks that had been left unattended for years all competed for her attention.

She worked methodically, and as she did, she began to test her new cantrips in short bursts. Every so often, she would activate both spells for a few seconds before letting them fade.

“Pika. Selan. Tureth.”

“Pika. Velar. Syneth.”

The effect was immediate each time. The chaos around her would momentarily resolve into something more structured. She could see patterns where before there had only been clutter. Books that belonged together revealed themselves through small details, whether through binding, subject matter, or placement. Even the faint traces of previous organization attempts became easier to identify.

She did not keep the spells active for long. The strain built quickly, and she had already learned not to push too far. Still, even in those short moments, she was able to make more progress than she would have otherwise.

By the end of the evening, she had assembled a small pile of books set aside for later reading. Each one held some relevance to her growing understanding, whether in magic, history, or politics.

More importantly, spells worked well together.

Satisfied with her progress, she allowed herself to rest.

The next day, she returned to her alcove with a clearer mind. Sitting in her usual spot, she began planning her final cantrip. She had spent the night considering it, turning the idea over repeatedly until it settled into something precise.

If observation was the first step of knowledge, then memory was the second.

There was little value in perceiving something clearly if it could not be retained. The Book Absorption spell she aimed to learn would flood her with information. Without a way to manage and retrieve that knowledge, much of it would be lost or become unusable.

She needed something that could explore her mind and recover what had already been stored.

A recall spell.

She opened her father’s ledger once more and began searching through it, following the same process as before.

Pika came first, as it always did.

The next word took longer, mostly because she was reluctant to touch on such a powerful concept. Eventually, she decided to go for it: Moren, meaning past. It fit clearly, representing not just time, but what had already occurred.

The final word required more thought. She needed something that, combined with past, would mean memory.

She found it in Kaldris, meaning retrieval.

Pika. Moren. Kaldris.

The structure was complete.

Creating the primer, however, proved more difficult.

Unlike meditation or observation, recollection was not something she fully understood. She knew what memory was, but not how it was stored or accessed. That knowledge was beyond her.

Instead of trying to define the mechanism, she chose a different approach.

She began writing her own memories.

Starting with her earliest memory, a lullaby, faint and incomplete, but still there. It was one of the only things she remembered about her mother. The melody came more easily than the words. Still, she wrote what she could recall.

Then came the title of the first book she had ever read.

Her father’s signature, practiced and precise, was alongside the seal he used for his work.

Words of advice he had given her, some simple, some harsh, all of them meaningful.

Fragments followed. Small, seemingly insignificant moments. The insults a neighboring child had used during their arguments. Quotes from books, the names of repeat clients, and even a few one-time customers who had left a strong enough impression to be remembered.

She sketched Lord Kelroth’s face as best as she could recall it, focusing on the features that stood out most.

The page filled quickly.

She chose to write everything by hand rather than using magic. The act itself felt important, as if the process of writing helped stir the memories into clearer form.

Between each section, she added the incantation.

Pika. Moren. Kaldris.

By the time she finished, the page was a jumble of thoughts, impressions, and fragments. It lacked the structure of her previous primers. It felt messy and chaotic.

It reminded her of the fire primer she had first read, confusing and difficult to follow.

She sat cross-legged once more, placing the paper in front of her.

“Pika. Selan. Tureth.”

The meditation spell settled over her quickly, her mind falling into stillness with practiced ease.

She began to revisit the memories she had written and others she hadn’t.

Some came back clearly. The lullaby carried a faint echo of warmth. Her father’s signature appeared in perfect detail. The words he had spoken felt almost as if they were being said again.

Others were less stable. Faces blurred, names slipped away, and moments fragmented into incomplete impressions.

She did not force them. Instead, she observed.

She considered the nature of memory, not as something fixed, but as something shaped by perception. Her sharpened mind began to notice patterns she had never considered before.

Some of the book quotes she had written down appeared in the common language, even though she had originally read them in another tongue. That inconsistency stood out immediately.

She had always assumed that memory was tied to language, that what she stored was what she had heard or read.

That was not the case, at least for her.

The realization came slowly, then all at once.

Information was not stored as words. It was stored as meaning, understanding, or perhaps concepts.

Language was applied afterward, a layer added when recalling or expressing the memory.

Her thoughts sharpened further as she followed that idea.

Memories were not static records. They were reconstructions, built from underlying concepts rather than exact copies.

That was why some details faded while others remained. The structure persisted even when the surface changed.

The moment everything aligned, she felt it.

Click.

The spell formed naturally, locking into place as the understanding settled.

“Pika. Moren. Kaldris.”

The effect was immediate.

Memories surged forward, directed by her active thought. She could reach for them, follow them, and bring them into focus with far greater clarity than before.

And then, without warning, something deeper surfaced.

For six years, she had remembered only one image clearly.

The sword.

The moment it fell toward her father’s neck.

That memory had overshadowed everything else, burning itself into her mind with a clarity that had left no room for anything before it.

Now, for the first time, something else emerged.

Her father’s smile. A perfect recollection of his face, his beard, his kind eyes.

Lira’s breath caught as more followed, faster now, each memory building upon the last: his hand resting on her shoulder, the sound of quiet laughter, and the warmth of a space that no longer existed.

It was too much.

The clarity, the density, and the sheer volume of it all pressed against her mind at once.

Even with the meditation spell anchoring her, the emotion behind those memories broke through the stillness.

Lira gasped as the world rushed back in, her body trembling as the flood of memory also receded, both spell effects ending abruptly.

Lira’s hands clenched in her lap, her breathing uneven. Then she broke down, weeping, as her tears fell without restraint.
