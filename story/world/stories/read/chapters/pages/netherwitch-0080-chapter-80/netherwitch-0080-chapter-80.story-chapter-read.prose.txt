Mighty cliffs rose from the depths of the star-filled void. The major plane, Tartarus, plowed through the dark, shedding waves of chaos. Where the flux split upon the world, sheets of white broke, revealing the shimmering light of Unus Mundus. Above, the empty void had given way to Tartarus’s logic. The sky was a gray haze, an approaching wall of crimson clouds hanging on the horizon.

Sylvia floated ten kilometers offshore, gazing down upon the city of Iacchus. Ships sailed upward through the stars, approaching the long harbor. Traffic to and from the metropolis had always been heavy. With the International Exposition of Magic taking place in Aidonopol, it was heavier still.

Her appearance was subtly different.

With every grand mutation came a host of physical changes. Awakening humanized demonic bodies while Transcendence refined an immortal’s appearance according to their ideal. For a lady like Sylvia, who’d always been beautiful and human-looking, the impact was relatively minor.

Yet, she hadn’t been willing to leave her changes to the whims of fate. And she didn’t have to.

For 300 merit points, gamers could buy the character creator feature. This tool didn’t just apply during birth. It could be used ahead of every grand mutation, allowing moderate adjustments to any gamer’s appearance. Sylvia had fiddled with the dials herself, but after a few hours of effort, she’d done the wiser thing. She’d commissioned an artist.

To be more accurate, Emmy had commissioned an artist. Multiple artists, actually. Sylvia’s wife had hired more than one. She wasn’t alone in planning her Transcendence. Emmy, too, had changes she wished to make, the foremost of which was the restoration of her original hair color. Personally, Sylvia was fond of the brown highlights left by Emily’s life, but that was nostalgia speaking. If she took a step back and examined things critically, she agreed with her wife. Esmeralda’s original hair color was more beautiful.

Being a good spouse, she’d discussed this with Emmy, then encouraged the lady to do as she wished.

The result of their planning could be seen now. The silver-haired princess stood a proud 155 centimeters tall. Her face had been tweaked, gifting her with sharper angles. The effect was a bit less cute, but granted her a more elegant and regal presence. When she smiled, her eyes were wide and adorable. When her gaze narrowed, there was a proper atmosphere of danger.

Her hair and her bust were much the same. The lavender-pink undertones of her glossy locks had been lightly touched to better emphasize her pastel pink eyes. Her legs, however, had been refined into the definition of slim and sexy.

Sylvia couldn’t wait to go home and tease her wife. It was a good thing her Starlight Striders had Ingrid-approved designs, because it’d be a crime if she never showed them off.

Her thoughts drifted back to Iacchus as she floated closer.

“When I left this city, I swore I would never return,” she murmured.

When she’d left the Timeless Beryl Wilderness, Sylvia had been a wee little first consolidation witch. The large buildings and the giant, gothic towers had revealed another face of the netherworld. Even now, the sparkle of the city lights reminded her of Earth. She wondered, how many years would it be before Viridian City showed the same grandeur?

Her gaze swept the city. There, in the Nychta Ward, was Belkis’s apartment building. Sylvia remembered when they first arrived, landing on the balcony to enter her senior sister’s apartment. On the opposite side of the city, she found the Tomes of Tartarus. Ah, and along the river, the room she’d rented for Gavin and Elroy.

So many nostalgic memories.

Then her gaze suddenly stopped…

“Is that the Rechin Sange?” Sylvia said in disbelief.

She cast farsight. Gazing through the water lens, she brought the eighty-meter astral warship into view. The Rechin Sange, confirmed by its plaque, was tied to one of Iacchus’s many piers. The ship was heavily armed and armored, with a thick hull, three cannons, six ballistas, and a pair of flame mortars.

Of course, the quality of those guns left something to be desired.

This warship was the property of Demon King Vilhelm Codrin. When Sylvia had last seen the vessel, it’d been docked at the Fortress of Dawn next to the Aripa. The Aripa was the trade ship she’d taken to Tartarus.

“I wonder if someone I know is in town?” she mused, dismissing her farsight spell.

After a moment’s consideration, Sylvia catalyzed a swirl of fate. Then, with a causal chant, she wove a chain of two hundred and eighteen runes. “■■ ■■.”

A prying eye opened in the depths of the Iacchus, its vision routed through her System. The silver-haired asteri gazed at a redheaded vampire. Iulian Codrin had chiseled features, pale skin, and a dashing uniform. Thirteen years ago, he was their captain for the Young Demon’s Tournament. Sylvia, Elroy, Gavin, and Nessa had been members underneath him.

And, apparently, Iulian was a captain once more. This time of a ship. The redhead was overseeing longshoremen shuffling boxes in a warehouse.

“I should stop by and say hello,” Sylvia said with a smile. Then her expression turned serious, humor falling. “Actually, I could make good use of this. But before we meet, I have a little shopping to do.”

Which meant it was time to put on her guise. Sylvia Swallows was not a girl who should be seen wandering the streets of Tartarus.

She called forth the shadow of her Three-Faced Visage. The tiny, wooden figure rested in her palm. Three masks hung off its head. One had the face of Sylvester Swift. The other showed the countenance of the light elf Frida. The third remained blank. Sylvia allowed the instrument a minute to breathe ether. Then she fed the statue blood-natured mana. The figurine melted into tendrils, its vines wriggling into her veins before settling somewhere in her chest.

She activated Sylvester’s mask. Her features shifted, bones and skin shuffling. The witch grew taller, adding eleven centimeters in half as many seconds. Her light gray hair shortened, losing its lavender-pink tint. The face of Cloud Princess Swallows changed, replaced by that of Sylvester Swift.

Form restored, the gentleman adjusted his top hat, making sure his pinstriped suit sat properly on his taller figure.

“Though I’m obviously a respectable man, I think it’d be better if I avoided customs,” Sylvester joked. “■■■ ■■.”

With the swirl of his cane, the warlock tore open a gate. Voomp. He stepped through, his polished dress shoes landing on scraggy, yellow grass. Tilting back his head, he drew in a deep breath, tasting the primordial ether.

“What a beautiful world,” he said. He gestured, thin wisps of energy whirling around him. “■■, ■, ■, ■■■, ■■■ ■■.”

A chain of anti-divinations rolled over the terrain, obscuring Sylvester’s presence and his recent acts. Then he casually stepped through a second gate.

Voomp.

Sovereign planes, like Tartarus, would place divinations and enchantments along their coast, hoping to catch smugglers and other illegal entrants. No doubt someone had noticed his use of an unauthorized gate to enter. They might even have detected his second gate, though that was harder to distinguish as it was perfectly legal to open portals within the plane.

“I should create a quieter version of gate at some point,” Sylvester mused. “It is one thing to use the spell near Aidonopol, where there are many magical guests. Opening a gate in less common locations might well attract unwanted attention. ■■, ■, ■.”

With a lazy chant, the warlock unleashed false trail, anonymous presence, then tangled weave. Satisfied no one would come to snoop quickly, he floated up into the air, rising three score meters above the ground. Then he fell forward. He glided through shortened space with the poise and posture of a gentleman riding on a parade float.

Flux shift was faster than a Lunam Fuga.

Between gravitational propulsion and crunched space, Sylvester could surpass 800 km/h. However, maintaining that velocity was as exhausting as a sprint. A speed of 500 km/h was more reasonable and could be sustained for a long time. The Lunam Fuga, by contrast, reached its peak at 275 km/h. But Sylvester could ride a broom all day. His mana regeneration outstripped its consumption. So brooms were greatly preferred for long-distance travel.

Of course, there was no rule stopping Sylvester from using flux shift and a broom at the same time. The problem was, he couldn’t fold space fast enough, limiting his effective velocity. Even so, he knew from experience that he could exceed 1000 km/h.

“What I need is a broom designed for flux shift from the start,” he mused.

Why, with such a tool, he might beat Windscourge in a race!

The city approached. A soft drizzle pattered off Sylvester’s top hat as the gentleman settled onto the bustling streets. Crimson clouds loomed overhead, bringing with them wind and petrichor. The scent of wet stone lingered in the air, tinged with the taste of copper.

In no great hurry, he strolled along the road, peering into windows. Here and there, demons picked up the pace, rushing to avoid the rain’s arrival. Others remained sedate, not caring one whit what was to come.

Minutes later, he opened the door to Delver’s Tools and Magical Curios.

B-dring.

A little bell rang when Sylvester entered. The small shop held a mix of magical instruments, most of which were aimed at adventurers and hunters who spelunked Tartarus’s evolving caverns. The owner, a siren named Ophelia, was behind the counter. He knew the woman vaguely. The siren was a member of Belkis’s old coven, the Deep Seekers. Many years ago, this voluptuous xemyalistra had sold to ‘Sylvia’ ‘her’ first realm ring array, Sparkling Starpack.

Nowadays, he used Hidden Hut, a realm ring array he’d wrangled from Yvonne Taubert. Come to think of it, Sylvester would have to replace it after reaching the fifth consolidation.

“Welcome to Delver’s Tools and Magical Curios,” Ophelia greeted with her beautiful voice. “Are you interested in a bit of adventure?”

“Not in the least,” he replied. “After reaching the fourth consolidation, the wilderness has lost its thrill. No, I was simply hoping to purchase some magical supplies and Aidonopol was unpleasantly crowded. Yours was the first store I came across.”

“Oh, I’ve heard the stories,” Ophelia returned with a charming laugh. “I think a third of the witches skipped town for the event. I mostly sell goods for the caverns, but I might have what you want. What are you looking for?”

“Three things,” Sylvester answered. “An elemental source for the elements sky and abyss. They must be of decent quality and hold at least 50 standard units. Then I’d like some space dust. I’m hoping for 150 droms, if you have it.”

“With regards to the elemental source, how many units per drom?” Ophelia asked, looking serious.

“Five or more will do,” the silver-haired man clarified.

What he wanted was to change the element of his ice and lightning palaces. To do so, each palace had to first be closed. Once closed, it could be opened if he sacrificed 1000 experience points and an appropriate elemental source. The process would take 24 hours.

Alternatively, he could simply die and let the palaces rebuild during resurrection. The experience point cost would remain, but the material component could be eschewed. But he wasn’t willing. His phylactery remained in the Cloud Island Wilderness, and he didn’t have one to two weeks to spare. If he waited so long, Heaven might move its prisoners.

“We have several abyss sources. They’re popular with divers,” Ophelia said. “The cheapest that’ll fit your standards sells for 45 thousand.”

“And a sky source?” Sylvester questioned.

“None,” Ophelia answered. “But I do have 80 droms of space dust in the back. Some divers like to have it on hand, in case they need an emergency gate.”

Dust wasn’t expensive. Not by Sylvester’s standard. But it was too heavy to fit in the soul. Thus, it was easily lost. Wealthier adventurers could afford the dust and the risk of leaving it behind. Hunters, who were usually first consolidation demons, could not.

“If you’d like, I can send a runner,” the siren offered. “One of the girls might have a sky source or extra dust to spare. There’s a tavern across the street. We can get a bite to eat while we wait. One pretty girl and a handsome warlock.”

Ophelia shot Sylvester a sly wink.

…

His eye twitched. What was it with witches and warlocks? Either they were as biased as hell or they wanted to bang him. Having lived as a woman for years, the silver-haired man knew the ladies could be just as wild as men, but this was ridiculous. He wasn’t that good-looking.

“I already have a girl, I’m afraid,” he said, placing 105 glints on the table. “This should cover the cost of the dust and abyss source. And please seal the materials in these bottles.”

The gentleman placed his metal briefcase on the countertop. Popping the locks, he handed the xemyalistra two glass cylinders.

“I don’t normally take gold, but I’ll do it for you as an apology for making things awkward,” Ophelia accepted. The siren flashed a genuine smile. “Who’s the lucky lady?”

“My wife,” Sylvester said blandly. “Don’t take this the wrong way, but she’s far more beautiful than you are.”

“If you dared to say otherwise, I’d be sure to send her a message.” The siren laughed, her voice like a song.

Ophelia rose from her seat, heading to the back of the shop, his empty bottles in hand. After a minute, the woman returned with one cylinder full and the other at half. The dust within glimmered with silver and indigo light. Alongside them, the witch set down a pitch-black crystal.

“For you, handsome fellow,” she said with another wink. “And tell your lady to buy you a meal on my behalf.”

“I will.” Sylvester slipped the source and the cylinders into his case. After securing the locks, he tipped his hat in the siren’s direction. “I bid you adieu, madam. Do check out the Exposition. It’s a marvelous event, and it’d be a great shame if you didn’t visit.”

“I’m heading for Aidonopol next month,” Ophelia called after him.

B-dring.

Sylvester stepped out into a downpour. Red rain ran in rivers on his pinstriped suit, the water repelled by Spring Blossom’s skintight barrier. The scent of wet stone had faded, leaving behind ozone and copper. A cold gust blew by, dragging at Sylvester’s suit and his top hat.

The silver-haired man strolled out into the center of the street, then he jumped.

Gravity flipped while space compressed, dragging him upward. His body stretched like taffy. It was a short flight before he was floating above the city, cane in his right hand and briefcase in his left. His eyes swept the skyline. One minute later, he was standing atop the spire of what was once Belkis’s apartment building. The wind howled around him.

Brruumrruumrruum. Thunder rolled in the crimson clouds above.

“A lovely storm,” Sylvester said. “But it’s time for serious business.”

A blue window popped open. Runeworks Studio had finished booting.

When seeking information, the fastest and most reliable course was divination. Every word spoken and every action taken left an imprint on the world. The threads of causality floated in the ether, gradually decaying like tracks left in the sand. Magic could reveal those imprints, erase them, hide them, or replace them with lies.

Sylvester had several spells in his grimoire that could search a city block. Muttered memories captured rumors on a specific subject, but only those spoken out loud in the last 24 hours. Way back when, Glenda Fischer had used this spell to learn the name of Dumas, the werewolf who had assaulted Riley and Sylvia in Vallen.

A city was much bigger than a single block, but the spell would make a nice foundation. Sylvester placed the base sequence into Runework Studio’s buffer. With the sigils arranged, he modified the magic.

“If I want to do this efficiently, I’ll need a larger range. Which, in turn, will demand greater efficiency,” he muttered to himself as he worked. “Also, one day is too short. I’d prefer to look back a full week. As for additional filtering? I don’t need it. My System client will act as the buffer.”

First, Sylvester increased the integrity of the spell. As it was, muttered memories would fracture if loaded with enough energy to cover the city. Even so, to cast such a monster would require 56,000 mana points. Which was well-beyond his means, great fey or no. Fixing this would require a bit of ingenuity.

He stroked his chin.

Ah. There was an idea. He’d transform the spell into carrier fragments. These fragments would then disperse throughout the city, pinging multiple times. This would allow the efficient recycling of their power. His soul sang, dropping chains of runes onto the interface like a mad hacker typing on a computer. A moment later, he stopped to review his work, tweaking and adjusting the sequences where needed.

“I’ll call you ‘ears of the city,’” Sylvester said, admiring the chain he’d created. “But, you aren’t even close to done. I need to make you reach further back in temporal space.”

Before starting the next set of changes, he ran the spell through a simulator. Runeworks Studio confirmed that it’d cover the full city at a much more acceptable cost of 2500 mana points. The real trick would be adding the missing features while retaining that performance.

Red rivulets poured off the warlock’s top hat as he considered, the storm raging all around him. Muttered memories used pure causality. To capture details seven days back, he’d need to weave in a bit of fate. Not too much, because fate was squirrely. The more Sylvester used, the less reliable the information he’d get.

“Some rune substitutions should do it,” he decided, swapping out a few chains. The length of the spell grew another fifteen percent as he made a series of modifications. “Yes, just a bit of fate to make the threads more real. Oh, and I’ll need a scrubbing feature. Otherwise, I’ll contaminate subsequent castings.”

Seven minutes later, Sylvester had a spell that ran twelve hundred runes long. Ears of the city was functionally similar to muttered memories, except it covered 10 to 25 kilometers of range. Furthermore, the spell would pull from both spoken and written gossip, so long as the words had been present within the last seven days.

Just as importantly, this was a causality divination, which meant it was reliable.

“Time to give it a try.”

Sylvester let Resonant Tear drop from his wrist, the jewel swinging wildly in the wind. Raising his cane, the silver-haired man prepared to cast. Then he paused, a sheepish expression spreading on his face.

“How silly of me, I neglected an important factor.” He laughed. “A spell with this much power will be loud.”

Thankfully, there was an easy solution. With a smirk, Sylvester released a lazy chain of one hundred and ninety-two runes before tackling a greater sigil. “■■■, ■■■ ■■■■■ ■■■ ■■■■■ ■ ■■■■■ ■■.”

A giant knot appeared in the sky, taking on a clear shape. Legrame was a compound rune of causality and fate meaning: ‘Thus, I know.’ Huge masses of energy whirled around the golden glyph. Then, pop, realm magic swept over it. The rune was cast into the sky five kilometers away.

The heavens pulsed.

A magnificent spell swept every corner of the city. Most demons couldn’t sense it. A few, who were stronger or more attuned, paused, wondering what had been cast. Causality sang, tracing the hidden threads and echoes. Information flowed back, a deluge of whispers flooding into Sylvester’s ears. The warlock reeled under an onslaught of a million voices.

Most mages would’ve lost important secrets to the torrent. He stored every scrap in his soul, his System whirling as it filtered through the mass.

Several seconds passed while Sylvester sorted through a sea of gossip.

Viscount Malik Nychta hadn’t changed one bit. Belkis was long forgotten, but the dragonling’s sick games went on. From the rumors, Sylvester could extract the current victims of Malik’s ‘hunts’: Ginevra Mancini, Mei Dai, Hoa Ngo, and Vera Holmberg.

Of these, Mei Dai’s name was most prominent. Two years ago, Viscount Nychta had grown weary of Baroness Bai Meng’s pursuit. Being the disgusting, vile creature he was, Malik turned his eyes to Lady Dai, Bai Meng’s long-time friend and companion. Apparently, Malik had kept Mei Dai for months before throwing her aside like trash.

The resulting blowup had been epic.

“Disgusting,” Sylvester spat, lips curled. “But Mei Dai’s fall happened a year and a half ago. She isn’t the girl I’m looking for.”

Viscount Nychta had already moved onto new prey. Three girls remained who could be targets. Ginevra Mancini was an oracle and a devil. Exactly the kind of strong but vulnerable girl Malik liked best. But Ginevra had been missing for months. Either she’d slipped Malik’s net, or she’d been the victim right after Lady Dai. In either case, that left Hoa Ngo and Vera Holmberg.

“I need rumors specific to those girls,” Sylvester decided. His eyes narrowed. “But I can’t neglect Miss Mancini, either. She might well have run away with my prize. ■■■, ■■■ ■■■■■ ■■■ ■■■■■ ■ ■■■■■ ■■.”

A second pulse of causality washed over the city. Information poured into his head. Sylvester let the flood flow over him, standing still and steady. When he opened his eyes two minutes later, he saw dark shapes flying up into the storm.

No doubt, they were looking for him.

“Time to depart.”

He stepped off the roof’s edge. As he fell headfirst, floor after floor flashed past him. At the last moment, he completed an easy twirl, space expanding slightly, so his dress shoes landed neatly and gently on the city streets. An ogre shot Sylvester a glance. The warlock offered a smile, tilting his hat.

The giant let out a grunt then continued on his path.

“As I suspected, he’s already done playing with Ginevra and has moved on,” Sylvester concluded, nose wrinkled as he reviewed the information. “Which leaves me with Hoa and Vera, both of whom look all the more likely.”

Know what? He deserved his indulgence. Thinking this, Sylvester stepped off the main road before entering a restaurant. The gentleman made a small swirl with his fingers before casting the red water clinging to his suit out the door.

“Welcome!” the aani shopkeeper barked. The dog-headed demon pointed at the containers of meat. “Let me know what you want, I’ll put it together for you.”

Sylvester considered for a moment. “I’ll go with your pane con la milza,” he decided.

A while later, he was seated in the back corner of the restaurant. Rain continued to pour, pattering in the side alley. Sylvester took a bite from his sandwich, the strong flavors of veal lung, spleen, and trachea playing on his tongue.

Even after his modifications, the cost of a large-scale divination was hideous. Sylvester had burned through half his mana just to cast ears of the city twice. Of course, he could’ve saved a great deal of energy if he’d used his newly purchased dust.

But there had been no need. Mana recovered on its own.

Half an hour later, Sylvester licked his fingers clean. “Since I have a pair of suspects, I should check them individually.”

He didn’t move from his seat. Instead, he tapped a lazy rhythm on the table, weaving a string of two hundred and eighteen runes. A prying eye opened in another part of the city, gazing at Vera Holmberg. Vera was a dryad, her most eye-catching feature being her unusual, bright blue hair.

Recently, the lady had attended two parties with Malik Nychta present. Rumors claimed Vera was the viscount's most recent paramour. Despite this, Lady Holmberg had studiously avoided the dragonling. The contradiction was precisely what attracted Sylvester’s interest. Though, it was hard to say whether Vera was a victim. Quite a few ladies were attracted to the vile but handsome man, and some realized his predilections.

“■■■, ■ ■.”

Rune gate was followed by an unfamiliar fifty-four rune weave. A Duat Dream Drop could only be detected with a highly specific spell. One Sylvester happened to know, thanks to Belkis’s trouble a decade prior. Vera jerked, a small bit of magic pinging in her well-lit apartment. Gazing around the room in suspicion, the woman chanted a spell. Sylvester’s video feed faded into static.

He let his prying eye die. Vera Holmberg didn’t have the dream drop.

“Hoa Ngo, let’s see if you’re the lucky girl. ■■ ■■.”

A second eye opened, its gaze partially obscured by white fuzz. Despite the wards, Sylvester saw a dark-haired yakshi hurrying through Iacchus’s streets. Pouring rain broke on a magical shield projected above the woman’s head.

Yakshi were a line of blue-blooded demons most commonly seen in the Fey Federation. According to rumor, Hoa and Malik had met only once. And that one meeting was all it took for the lady to abandon her fiancé and start mooning over the viscount. Yet, none of this matched Hoa’s prior character. Indeed, her fiancé was so convinced, he’d challenged Viscount Nychta to a duel two months past.

Sylvester was curious. Was Hoa Ngo the dragonling’s latest prey, or was she a woman full of rot?

“■■■, ■ ■.”

Unlike Vera, Miss Ngo showed no reaction when the spell was cast. Sylvester let out a sigh.

“I apologize for ever thinking ill of you, milady,” he murmured. “You and your fiancé are the victims of a vicious scheme. Alas, the only compensation I can offer is an express trip to the Timeless Beryl Wilderness. May this small vacation soothe your wounded heart.”

Rising from his seat, Sylvester placed his hat back on his head. The gentleman stepped out of the restaurant, red rain falling in a relentless downpour. He stood there for a long moment, waiting. Waiting until Hoa Ngo opened the door to her apartment.

“■■■ ■■.” Voomp.

The yakshi turned, having just thrown the bolt. Hoa’s eyes went wide, her expression one of shock and horror. Sylvester raised his cane, electricity crackling.

“Who – ”

Bang!

Lightning flashed. Then came stillness. Hoa’s corpse crumbled, her heart pierced clean through. With a flick, he cast ectoplasmic web, capturing the yakshi’s soul. Setting down his briefcase, Sylvester pulled out a lamp caged in black iron.

“Infinite pardons, Miss Ngo,” he said to the dissolving corpse as he sucked in the shadowy soul. “But you have something I want. And trust me when I say, this is for your own good.”

Not that it was fair to measure ordinary women against the threshold set by Asmodeus’s wives.

Chink!

“She’s – ”

“What a charmer,” she commented dryly. “How did you end up with her as your first mate?”

“Mihaela is quite competent in her own way, and despite her recent actions, she has mellowed over the years,” Iulian explained. “I’m just a glorified carrier of souls,” he dismissed. “As for Awakening? It’s still a good while off. I did find an oracle to consult with about my past. I’ll meet with her the next time we make port in Iacchus.”

Awakening was a major obstacle even for gamers. Demons, who rarely knew their pasts, had an additional struggle: they didn’t know their mortal desire. This was a resolvable problem, thankfully. By consulting oracles or specialized zoi, they could use divinations or dream magic to recover memories locked in the soul. With these bits and pieces, they could grasp the obstacles buried deep inside their heart.

“And what about you, Lady Swallows?” Iulian questioned.

Military Equipment of Heaven

Ljósmeðr

Attack Power: 125%

Penetration: 100

A standardized weapon issued to all the einherjar. Though most commonly a sword, Ljósmeðr can take multiple forms including those of spears, hammers, or knives. Ljósmeðr are usually used by those in the principality and power castes, though more powerful versions of the weapons exist.

Holysword: Consume 100mp to add 100 attack power for the next minute. All attacks gain a weak light aspect nature: +5% DRR and +30 AP. Against living creatures, attacks have an additional 25% SRR. Holysword applies to all compatible arts used in conjunction with the weapon. Additionally, while holysword is active, the ljósmeðr can cut spells and abilities, dealing an additional 15% damage to all magical barriers.

Sunlight: By consuming 100mp, the wielder can make a single 600 power light aspect attack. This strike has +100 penetration along with +10% DRR and 100% SRR against living creatures. Sunlight cuts through spells even more effectively than holysword, dealing an additional 30% damage to all magical barriers. If the physical blade or a compatible art strikes the foe as well, base attack power is added to the blow. Sunlight has a range of 50 meters.

Gullbrynja

Defense: 100

DR: 5% / +5%

Mp Drain: 15 / hour

The standardized armor worn by the einherjar. Like the ljósmeðr, this armor is suitable for Class III and Class IV warriors. Fundamentally, this is a martial armor relying heavily on ki consumption. However, it contains a magical formula to summon an emergency barrier when needed. The Damage Reduction boost of this armor applies to all ki-armor arts as well as to guards and parries.

Emergency Guard: When the wearer is subject to any single attack dealing more than 30% of their max Hp, the guard triggers in response. For the next second, a defensive barrier will be erected. This grants 35% Damage Reduction (multiplies with other DR) and +250 Hp. Furthermore, the wearer’s defense will be applied a second time, reducing the potency of the attack.

Iber Hatz

Unlike a ballista, the standard bolts require no enchantment. For this reason, bolt costs range from 20 to 80 soli depending on their size. When a common projectile is fired from the Iber Hatz, it has enormous curvature, allowing a full 180 degree reversal in a mere 500 ms. The pipes can be loaded with two enchanted bolt types: the ‘light lance’ and ‘burst lance’. Heaven only issues specialized bolts for the medium and long pipes.
