The group of ten thousand soldiers continued their march across the plains from Xiandong Fortress, unimpeded by the Selis army. Yet it wasn’t until half a day had passed that the tension truly began to lift.

Luke didn’t know who started it, but a cheer erupted from somewhere in the center of the formation, echoing out across the wide plains. The shout was infectious, lifting the spirits of all who heard it.

“We’re alive!”

“Long live General Drakon!”

Ten thousand voices roared in joyous unison, fists raised in triumph. Relief and euphoria swept through the ranks like a wave. They had escaped the jaws of death. Unarmed as they were, survival felt like victory. It was better than starving to death or being slaughtered in a doomed siege.

For a moment, Luke allowed himself to bask in the celebration. A smile crept onto his lips. Hearing his name shouted with such reverence stirred something deep inside him—an intoxicating blend of pride and disbelief. But it wasn’t the acclaim that made him happiest.

“There’s a good spot to camp up ahead. I think going any further would be counterproductive,” he said to Tristan, pointing toward a raised patch of terrain he had scouted earlier using Eagle Eye.

“Mmm. The men deserve a rest,” Tristan agreed with a nod. He turned and relayed the orders to Bridges and the others, who swiftly passed the word down through the Lieutenant Commanders.

With the promise of rest, morale surged even higher. Were they not disciplined soldiers, many would have broken formation entirely in their eagerness to reach the site.

An hour later, the army set up camp just outside a forested area on a gentle rise above the plains. Luke had chosen the location for two reasons: first, it offered a commanding view of the flatlands below, ideal for spotting any potential pursuers. Second, the woods might provide game—though he had no illusions about their odds. Feeding ten thousand mouths with local wildlife was near impossible.

He pulled a small strip of dried meat from his satchel and bit into it, wincing as his teeth met the leathery texture. Still, it was food—and far better than nothing.

“Your tent is ready, General.”A soldier approached with a respectful salute, eyes gleaming with fervor.

“Well done. Go get some rest,” Luke replied, dismissing him with a nod.

He and the other officers stepped into the medium-sized command tent in silence. Despite the bloodless escape from Xiandong, a heavy atmosphere lingered. No one had spoken of it aloud, but the crushing uncertainty of their situation loomed over them like a storm cloud.

Commander Bridges, his two Lieutenant Commanders, and the four newest additions to the command staff turned to Luke expectantly. They didn’t need to speak for him to understand the question in their eyes:

What now?

Without a word, Luke crouched and began scratching a crude map into the dirt floor with his finger, placing stones and sticks to represent key terrain features. His Scholarly Pursuit skill granted him a near-perfect mental map, but the others had no such advantage.

So he showed them.

“This is Xiandong, and to the north is Yunxi,” Luke said, gesturing to two distinct stones he had placed on the eastern side of his makeshift map. “We won’t reach Yunxi before we run out of food—nor can we make it back to Ralis.”

He traced a line from the bottom of the map, curving it along the western edge.“The Wu River runs south from just below the capital and into Lhair. If we want to arrive before supplies run dry, we’ll have to head southwest from here,” he explained, drawing a sharp line from their current position to the river’s course.

“Wait, General… you want us to venture into enemy territory?” Bridges asked, clearly stunned. “We don’t have any weapons. We’d be slaughtered if we’re discovered.”

“Would you rather starve, Commander?” Tristan replied, his voice calm—almost bored.

Bridges opened his mouth to retort, but no words came. He lowered his head in silent resignation.

“Fresh water’s one thing, but food will still be an issue, right?” Christian spoke up, his tone serious. “We can’t possibly find enough in the river—especially without tools. I used to fish the Wu River growing up, but I never caught more than a few small carp.”

Luke shook his head. “You fished near Ralis, didn’t you? Carp lay their eggs in vegetation-rich shallows. Once the larvae hatch, they drift downstream to grow before moving into deeper water. The wider sections of the Wu will have much bigger catches.”

Christian blinked in surprise. “General… how do you know so much about this?”

“I’m well-read, Lieutenant Commander,” Luke said with a glance, then turned his attention back to the map.“We’ll travel along the riverbank until we find a small town or a suitable spot to establish a temporary camp. From there, we’ll send a small team back to Ralis to request supplies.”

He continued, his voice firm: “Our number one priority is returning to the capital with as many men as possible. I need all of you to help maintain morale. If anyone deserts out here, they might lead Lhair straight to us—especially once we cross into their territory.”

A few officers nodded, though the weight of their circumstances still pressed down on the group.

“Who will you send to Ralis for supplies?” Tristan asked after a pause.

Luke smiled. “You, of course. I don’t know anyone else in this army with your stamina.”

The others turned to Tristan, some with confusion, others with open skepticism. With his lean frame and older age, he hardly looked the part.

“Huh!? You think I’d just leave you out here?” Tristan asked incredulously.

“Tristan,” Luke said, stepping closer and resting a hand on his friend’s shoulder. He leaned in and whispered with mock seriousness,“You can run for days on end without tiring… as long as you’re well fed. And let’s be honest—we can’t afford to feed you right now. Your appetite alone might drive us all to starvation.”

The older man scowled deeply. It wasn’t clear whether he was offended by the jab or just unhappy at the thought of abandoning his General behind enemy lines.

As quartermaster, there was no doubt that Tristan was acutely aware of just how precarious their situation had become. Getting word back to Ralis wasn’t just a priority—it was their lifeline. And as much as he might hate it, he couldn’t deny the logic behind Luke’s plan.

“Fuck…” he muttered under his breath, casting a worried glance in Luke’s direction.

Luke couldn’t help the smile tugging at the corner of his lips. He’d be lying if he said he wasn’t uneasy about sending off his most reliable, system-enhanced subordinate. With no weapons, Tristan was still their best defense. Even a stick in his hand could become lethal in an instant.

“You can leave once we reach the river,” Luke said, patting Tristan’s shoulder again. “If you run, you should make it back to the capital within a week. Once you’ve secured the supplies, follow the Wu River south. By then, we should have a camp established along its banks.”

A long, begrudging sigh escaped Tristan’s lips.

“Did I ever tell you how reckless you are?” he grumbled, shaking his head.

“I prefer the term daring.”

The response drew a dark chuckle from Tristan. He placed a firm hand on Luke’s shoulder.

“If you die, I’ll find you in the afterlife and kick your ass.”

Luke smiled, choosing to ignore the quip. “Get some rest—you’ve got a long week ahead of you.”

Turning to the remaining officers, he gave a subtle nod of dismissal. The plan had been laid out clearly. Now it was up to them to relay it to the men and keep morale from collapsing.

They were in an incredibly vulnerable position. If the soldiers lost faith in him now, everything would fall apart.

Before long, Luke found himself alone in the tent. A single lantern flickered in the corner, casting dancing shadows on the canvas walls.

With a tired groan, he lowered himself onto the makeshift bed—a slab of stiff cloth over packed earth. It was barely better than the cold ground, but it would have to do.

He tugged off his boots with a wince, then summoned the system window with a flicker of anticipation. Navigating to the quest sub-menu, he held his breath.

Auxiliary Quest: Preserve your forces [Completed]

Details: Even when forced to retreat, the Sovereign does so with dignity.

Task: Successfully retreat from Xiandong Fortress with more than 70% of the initial forces remaining.

Progress: 74% of friendly forces remaining.

Reward: Skill Selection [Claim Rewards]

[Please choose your reward:]

Commanding Presence: Your presence inspires loyalty and respect. Subordinates within a certain range have enhanced combat effectiveness and resist fear.

Unyielding Authority: Your aura carries weight even in defeat. Troops under your command suffer 20% less morale loss during retreats, famine, or dire situations. Increases obedience to orders by low-ranking soldiers.

Voice of the Sovereign: Once per day, issue a decree to your followers that rallies morale or inspires sacrifice. Effects depend on the decree’s intent. Scales with Charisma.

Luke sucked in a sharp breath, eyes gleaming as he scanned the rewards listed in the translucent window before him. A quiet curse slipped through his lips as his gaze lingered on the last two options in particular.

Damn it… if only I could have more than one, he lamented inwardly.

Unyielding Authority and Voice of the Sovereign—both were game-changers. Quite literally.

The latter had the potential to turn the tide of battle with a single decree. Luke could already picture it: him issuing a command, and his army erupting into a frenzy, morale surging like wildfire.

It even had the word Sovereign in its name—the same as his system. Surely, that meant it was something destined for him.

And yet… the former was simply too valuable to ignore—especially given their current circumstances. With no weapons, no food, and morale as fickle as the wind, the risk of desertion was at an all-time high.

If even a handful of men fled and gave away their position to patrolling scouts, the consequences would be catastrophic.

I have to take that one, he thought with a reluctant sigh.

[Congratulations. You’ve received the skill: Unyielding Authority.]

The glowing message floated in front of him as Luke leaned back, trying to stay positive.

That Commanding Presence skill… it showed up when I took Sebastian as a subordinate. If the system cycles through unused rewards, maybe I’ll get another chance to secure Voice of the Sovereign...

It was a stretch—a desperate hope. But he clung to it all the same. After all, his morale was just as important as his soldiers’, wasn’t it?

[DING]

A sudden chime echoed in his mind, sharp and familiar. Luke stiffened.

Unlike the triumphant tone that had accompanied their escape from Xiandong, this time his expression darkened.

Don’t tell me… another quest? Damn it!

[Skill: Will of Conquest has activated.][Updating…][Skill: Unyielding Authority has received a blessing.][Congratulations. You have acquired the Sovereign-tier skill: Sovereign’s Bearing.]

Luke sat bolt upright, eyes wide with shock.

“No way…” he whispered, rubbing his eyes as if the words might vanish with a blink. But the notifications remained, glowing steadily before him.

He quickly navigated to his skill list, throat dry. There it was—Will of Conquest—the passive skill he’d received after recruiting Tristan just a few days prior.

Just to be sure, he read through it again:

Will of Conquest (Passive): For every Epic or higher-tier subordinate under the user’s command, all existing skills receive minor boosts in effectiveness. With enough high-tier subordinates, certain skills may evolve into Sovereign-tier variants.

Sovereign-tier variant…

Luke swallowed hard and scrolled to the next entry.

Unyielding Authority was gone.

In its place sat a new skill, marked with a glowing red exclamation—signifying its recent acquisition.

Sovereign’s Bearing: Your aura carries weight even in defeat. Troops under your command suffer 80% less morale loss during retreats, famine, or dire situations. Increases obedience to orders by all appointed officers.Additional effect: The presence of the Sovereign inflicts morale penalty upon enemy forces.

“Sweet mother of fortune…” Luke muttered, staring in awe.
