Every year in the inner sect, disciples can challenge others to improve their ranking, take the spot of the defeated disciple, and improve their treatment in the sect.

In a way, it was quite a barbaric event, as it was essentially just beating the shit out of someone and taking their home. But in this world, where the law of the jungle reigns supreme, such a thing was not rare.

Of course, this was just a milder form of raising disciples. It was not like one of those demonic sects, where young cultivators would have to kill each other, with only the last one standing being given resources.

So, that was a silver lining, he supposed. Ning pondered the intricacy of such an event being held yearly, nothing more than the strong getting stronger and the weak getting weaker.

That's why, needless to say, the arenas were packed with fights.

"I guess the improved sect subsidy is really enticing." Ning mused. As an inner sect disciple at his rank, he was given two cultivation pills as a subsidy along with the standard spiritual density in his home.

As for why he was here, it was simply to support his senior sister, Ling Yinran, in her placement challenge.

Ling Yinran, as a ninth-stage qi condensation cultivator, ranked twentieth at the Hidden Edge Peak.

Unlike the simply named Farming Peak, where Ning resided, which was wholly occupied by spiritual farmers, the Hidden Edge Peak was composed of a myriad of cultivators with various spiritual professions like puppet cultivators, spiritual brewers, spiritual paper crafters, painters, medicinal cultivators, and such, each rare and few in number.

Each peak had its own internal ranking, and disciples could challenge those above them to climb. Only after reaching the top ranks of their own peak would they qualify to compete in the wider inner sect rankings.

Ling Yinran's opponent was an insect cultivator named Shen Wei, nineteenth rank, one above Ling Yinran, and ninth stage of qi condensation as well. He stood at the far end of the arena with a calm expression and a sealed gourd hanging at his waist.

"Insect cultivators, probably a tough fight." Ning analyzed. It was simple logic, really. While his senior sister's metal puppets were physically immune to things like venom or bites, a swarm of bugs was usually small, agile, and numerous enough to clog up gears and disrupt a construct's pathing.

Besides, Ning had a blanket policy of being deeply terrified of anything that lived inside a sealed container. Xenomorph bugs in this world were a completely different breed compared to the harmless beetles back on Earth. Rumor had it there were things out there called Heaven-Sucking Mosquitoes that grew to the size of office buildings. That was straight, unfiltered nightmare fuel. He could only pray he'd never run into one.

The referee called the start, and the match began.

Ling Yinran deployed both of her puppets instantly. The two iron constructs slid into flanking positions with a seamless, fluid precision that only came from thousands of hours of muscle memory, which made sense, considering Ning rarely ever saw her without a wrench or a core-crystal in her hands.

Shen Wei didn't hesitate either, snapping the cork out of his gourd.

A suffocating, black-purple cloud of Ghost-Faced Locusts poured into the arena. These were trained spiritual beasts moving with a creepy, unified hive-mind intelligence.

"A swarm blitz. Predictable," Ning muttered from the sidelines.

As far as he knew, Shen Wei didn't own any iron-eating insects, the ultimate puppet counter, so he was defaulting to a classic glass-cannon strategy: eliminate the puppeteer, win the match.

It was a solid plan, especially since Ghost-Faced Locusts were notoriously resistant to standard five-element spells and could sense incoming magic early enough to dodge it entirely. Usually, a regular spellcaster couldn't wipe them out fast enough and would just get eaten alive.

But Ning wasn't worried about his senior sister. She wasn't a regular spellcaster, and she definitely hadn't gone into this blind.

Facing a wall of snapping mandibles and venomous wings, Ling Yinran didn't step back. She had spent the entire previous night coating her iron constructs and her own robes in a pungent grease refined from Stink-Root Weevils, the literal apex rival of the locusts.

The moment the vanguard of the swarm hit a five-foot perimeter around her, the invisible, foul stench took effect. The locusts violently recoiled, their sensory organs instantly fried as their hive-mind coordination dissolved into absolute chaos.

"Now," Ling Yinran hissed, her fingers snapping tight.

The two flanking puppets leaped into the mess, their specific modifications finally on display. These weren't her usual generic models; she had custom-built these things specifically to defeat Shen Wei.

A high-frequency sonic vibration rippled across the stage, perfectly tuned to shatter the internal equilibrium of flying pests. That was the nightmare of fighting a puppet master who had the resources, the brains, and the time to prep. Give a puppet master enough information about an opponent and they could spend months building the most annoying possible counter to that exact person. And this was not even her final form.

Shen Wei didn't have that luxury. His insect technique was just a stray branch of the grand Gu cultivation practiced down in the Southern Marshlands, and because he was thousands of miles away from the source, his inheritance was full of holes. He was fast, but Ning's spear was faster.

The cold metal tip stopped a mere fraction of an inch from Lao San's exposed throat, the biting wind of the strike cutting off his breath.

"Surrender?" Ning tilted his head, his expression utterly calm for someone one step away from ending a man.

Lao San gulped, feeling death hovering uncomfortably close. "I-I give up."

"That's it then." Ning put his spear away. "It was a good fight. Stand proud, you are strong."

Ning patted the still-frozen man and moved toward the referee, ignoring him as he simply collapsed to the ground panting.

He walked toward the referee, who nodded. "Disciple Ji Ning has defeated Disciple Lao San. You may either move into his courtyard, or the sect will adjust the spiritual gathering formation to improve the spiritual density of your courtyard to be equivalent to that of your defeated foe."

Not everybody wanted to move into another's courtyard after winning, especially spiritual farmers, since, due to the nature of their profession, they rarely changed courtyards.

"That's a no-brainer. Please increase the spiritual density of my courtyard." Ning answered with a smile.

"Hm. Okay." The referee nodded, then spoke. "Congratulations, Disciple Ji Ning. Your new courtyard number is now 66."

Ning paused. Among all things, he had forgotten that his courtyard number would also change.

"That means no number 69 courtyard. Damn, did I just fumble the perfect courtyard number?" Alas, Ning had won the battle but lost the war.
