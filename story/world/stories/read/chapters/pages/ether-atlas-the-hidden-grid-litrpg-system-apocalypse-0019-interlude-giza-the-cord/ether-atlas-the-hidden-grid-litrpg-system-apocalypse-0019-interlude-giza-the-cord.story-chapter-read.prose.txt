26th Century BC - The Giza Plateau, Egypt

Before the first stone, there was the cord.

The plateau lay bare under the stars, a shelf of levelled rock pegged out with acacia stakes. The whole reign had gathered to watch the king find the true north. Khufu stood in white linen, the double crown catching the low fires. Around him waited the priests of the House of Life, the hour-watchers who kept the star-clocks, and the overseers who would raise this bare ground into a mountain.

Nothing had been built. The most important night of the whole work was the one before the work began. A foundation set wrong by a finger at the base would open into a fault of whole cubits at the crown, and this crown had to meet something none of them would live to see.

Ipy was the youngest of the hour-watchers, and it was his hands that held the merkhet this day. He had trained since boyhood to find the pole as his masters found it, by looking at the Imperishable Ones. The stars that never drowned in the horizon. You sighted a northern star as it rose and again as it set and then you halved the angle between them. The line halfway between the two was true. He had done it a hundred times on temple ground. He knew the method in his wrists.

He found north now, checked it twice, then drove his stake.

Then the woman set hers.

She wore the panther hide and the seven-pointed star of Seshat, the goddess of measure. Ipy had served under three priestesses of Seshat, but none of them had sighted the sky as this one did. She lifted a curious rod and fixed it on a patch of empty northern sky where there was no star at all. She held it still while the heavens turned around it and then she drove her stake into the rock, a little apart from his.

Ipy looked at the two marks. Hers sat off true north by the width of two fingers. He knew true north. He had just found it twice, and her line missed it.

The king came to the cord when the priests proclaimed the foundation as true. He was a young god, and he wanted the reassurance of the old gods.

"Will it stand, mistress?" the king asked with a deference that he should not have paid anybody.

"It will stand until the stars change their houses." She did not bow to the king like the others. "Your name will go into the base, great one. The base will outlast the name and the language it is cut in. Men will cross deserts that do not yet exist to put their hands on this stone and ask who raised it. Be content that they will ask and be content that your name will survive in history."

That was enough for him. Reassurance from the mouth of Seshat was the measure itself telling him yes. The king went back to his fires, pleased.

She came to Ipy last. She had known all along that he had checked her line.

"You found it wrong," she said.

He should have lied to a priestess of the goddess and found that he could not. "North is not where you have set it."

"No, it is not. But North is not what we stand here to find tonight." She studied him for a long moment. "You align temples to the sky as it is now. I have aligned this one to how the sky will be. Four and a half thousand years after your grave, a light will touch the crown of this monument exactly where my line points and something that has slept the whole age between will wake. You will tell no one about the line. Even if you do, no one will believe you, but I will know that you did."

She held his eyes. "Pull your stake and set it by mine. Yours is honest. Mine is right."

Ipy pulled his stake and set it by hers.

For the next twenty years the mountain climbed the line she had drawn. Two million stones, raised true to a mark that sat a hair off the pole and dead centre on a moment that was yet to come. When it was finished it stood aligned to the heavens skewed by the width of a thumb. It held that alignment while dynasties fell into the sand around it. It held while the bright casing was stripped away downriver to build a later city. It held while surveyors came with instruments Ipy could never have dreamed of, measured its faces and went away shaking their heads. Men with cord and stars should not have managed it. They had.

Nobody thought to ask what it was aligned to. They assumed, as Ipy's own masters would have, that a thing so true must point at true north.
