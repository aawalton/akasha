The trip back home was uneventful. Callie felt tired after the previous day's events, so she actually slept most of the way back. When they finally returned to Greyfen, she felt like she could breathe again.

While Drymon had been an amazing place, the number of people there had been suffocating. Not to mention all the strangers. In the village, all the faces she saw were familiar. There went Auntie Mara. That's Aurelia's mother, and right over there was a group of older villager boys, Callen's friends.

One thing stood out among the villagers who had gathered to greet them on their return—mostly Mom and Callen. Nobody cared much for Callie, except for one man whose bright ginger hair could be seen from a long distance away.

"Dad!" Callie called and ran toward him. She hadn't even realized how much she had missed him.

"Callie!" Dad tossed Callie lightly in the air, then pulled her into a tight hug. "How did it go in the town? See lots of new things?"

"Yeah! It was so big! And the library had so many books. But there were way too many people. Dad, you won't believe what happened to us," Callie ranted.

Mom and Callen also came up to Dad and Callie, although they looked far more tired than she did. Dad instantly noticed that and narrowed his eyes at Callie. "What happened?"

Callie shrank back.

"It's a long story. Let's talk about it at home," Mom said, then turned to Silas. "Thank you so much for taking us, and for the library card."

"No problem! Callen helped me out a lot. That boy has the mettle to become a real merchant," Silas announced.

Mom and Dad shared a concerned look while Callen looked quite excited.

"You think so?" Callen asked.

"Yeah, you have excellent haggling skills. So long as you learn the true value of merchandise, you can easily make it," Silas praised.

Callie looked at the exchange curiously. Her brother wanted to become a merchant? That was something new. She had noticed that Callen really wanted to travel and see the world, but it always seemed like Mom and Dad would push him to become a hunter because of how strong he was.

This was great! Being a hunter didn't suit Callen at all. He was too kind for that. But if he became a merchant… wait, wouldn't that mean that he would leave? He had already been travelling with Silas on a few occasions, but if he truly became a merchant, he would be gone much more often.

"No, being a merchant doesn't suit my brother!" Callie announced in a panic.

"Why not?" Callen looked taken aback by her sudden declaration.

"B-Because… Because it doesn't! You can't be a merchant! It's a horrible profession," Callie said.

"There, there, Callie. Nothing has been decided yet. Let's head home. Let's talk about what happened," Dad said, carrying Callie all the way home.

She knew it wasn't quite nice what she had just said. But if Callen were to leave to become a merchant… What would Callie do? She couldn't imagine her life without her big brother. She didn't want him to leave.

Callen followed them, awkwardly scratching the back of his head. It seems her declaration had made him feel uncomfortable. That made Callie feel bad. Callen always looked so excited whenever he was travelling. She couldn't forbid him from leaving.

Dad, likely noticing how Callie was staring at her brother, asked. "So, tell me what did you learn in the library? Did you read any good books?"

Callie glanced around. There were too many people. She waited until they were well and truly home before telling them about her discoveries. Most importantly about the Myth of the Dragon's Gift.

"Y-You don't think I'm actually a dragonblood… am I?" Callie asked nervously once she finished her story.

Mom, Callie, and Callen were sitting around the dining table as Dad presented them with a large shepherd's pie. Her mouth salivated at the wonderful smell. When was the last time they had it? It must have been last autumn.

"Where did you get the meat?" Mom asked, looking at the pie in surprise.

"One of the neighbors recently butchered their pig in preparation for winter. In exchange for a leg, I fixed and reinforced their door," Dad announced proudly.

"You got a whole leg?" Callen exclaimed in surprise.

"Indeed. So eat up everyone!"

Callie dug in without hesitation. The pie melted in her mouth as she ate it. Before she even realized, she had finished her portion and asked for seconds. Callen likewise demanded seconds, and even thirds. That's how delicious it was.

"That was great!" Callie said, patting her overfilled belly.

"So what was it about that myth?" Dad asked. "It claims that those gifted with dragon magic are dragonbloods."

"I'm not sure how trustworthy it is. I mean… Callie… a dragonblood? That's not possible," Mom said, looking at Callie. "Is it?"

Callie shook her head. "I don't want to be a dragonblood! They hurt arcane beasts. No, I want to be a ranger!"

"What?" Dad blinked in surprise.

Mom sighed. "I'm not sure if you're going to like this…"

She then proceeded to tell everything that happened with the thunderfoxes and the rangers, not hiding a single detail. Dad's eyebrows furrowed more and more, until eventually he was glaring at Callie, who had sunk lower and lower into her chair.

"Approaching wild monsters, sneaking into a wagon, then exposing your magic to them. Callie, you…" Dad was pulling on his hair by the time Mom had finished the story.

"I'm sorry… Well, not about helping the foxes, but I am sorry about the magic part. I-It just came so suddenly," Callie said, then finally dared to take off her vambraces.

Mom, Dad, and Callen all looked at her hand and gasped.

"There's another petal there!" Callen said.

"That explains why you couldn't control it. But why is there another one?" Mom asked, tracing her fingers over the four petals. There was enough space for two more. What would happen to Callie once the flower was complete?

"When the thunderfox got reunited with her babies, she said thank you. I think that's when it happened," Callie said.

"It must be related to arcane beasts for certain," Callen said.

"What do we do now? How do we control this?" Dad asked, his forehead creased with worry.

"I… I think I got a clue," Callie said, and explained the old text the librarian had translated for her, and how it had helped her to control her magic to find the thunderfox mother.

"Well, it's now basically confirmed that our way of doing magic won't work for Callie," Mom said.

"Temper your heart…" Dad rubbed his beard in thought. "We need to focus on that. Controlling your emotions in a productive and safe way."

Callie nodded. "I can do it!"

"But your magic is stronger now that you have another petal. It will be harder to control it, no?" Callen pointed out.

There was a pause as everyone glanced at Callie. She nodded reluctantly. After all, the flowers had burst out so suddenly, she hadn't even had a chance to calm her magic.

"No more arcane beasts," Mom announced.

"WHAT?!" Callie gasped.

"Callie, your magic is out of control. We can't risk you growing another petal and for it to be even stronger. Your priority is learning control."

Callie wanted to object, but… Mom was right. Petals appeared whenever she got close to a new arcane beast. Wait…

"How about Wiggles and Snowglow? I already had a petal grow from them. Surely a new one wouldn't appear if I met them again, right?" Callie tried.

Mom and Dad shared a look. It appeared that they wanted to say no.

"Please… Wiggles must be missing me. I never get to play with him. He must be sad… Please…" Callie begged.

Mom rubbed the bridge of her nose, then finally nodded. "Okay, fine. But Wiggles and Snowglow only, and only if one of us is with you. Never approach a new arcane beast on your own, even if you are sure it is harmless."

"Okay!" Callie agreed, relieved at her permission. Meeting Snowglow would be difficult with others, since she was so shy, but perhaps Callie could convince the glimmerhare not to fear Callen.

"No lies, young lady. We're only trying to protect you. You understand that, right?" Dad asked firmly.

Callie nodded. Her family had become much more accepting towards arcane beasts, so if there indeed was a problem, she felt like she could tell them now. "I promise not to hide anything from you anymore! You're my family after all."

Mom and Dad smiled, and soon the whole family was pulled into Dad's strong embrace. "We're going to figure this out. So long as we stick together, we're going to make it."

They remained together for a little while until Dad released them.

"By the way, Callie, I got you something," Callen said and went to his bag, which he had left by the entrance.

He rummaged through for a while, then pulled out something that looked like a book. It had a simple brown paper cover and didn't look very thick, but it was undoudbtedly a book.

"What's that?" Callie asked curiously.

"Where did you get the money for it?" Mom asked at the same time.

"Silas paid me for helping him. You've been going through quite a lot lately, so I bought you this to cheer you up, Callie," Callen said and handed the book to her.

Callie looked at it in amazement. The pages inside were blank. This was clearly intended as a notebook. She couldn't believe her brother would get her something so precious.

Her eyes filled with tears of joy. "I-Is it really okay for me to use it?"

"You did want to make that arcane beast encyclopedia, didn't you?"

Callie nodded and hugged the notebook close. "Thank you, Brother! You're the best."

"How much is that merchant paying you that you are able to afford a whole notebook? Paper isn't cheap, you know," Mom noted as she inspected Callie's notebook from all sides.

Callen scratched the back of his head sheepishly. "This one was actually discounted. It has a tear on the back cover…"

Flipping the notebook, they saw that there indeed was a tear. But the rest of the notebook was completely fine.

"I still love it!" Callie announced. "In fact, I'm going to redraw all my notes into this notebook right now!"

"Not the dragon one," Mom warned.

Callie nodded. It was too dangerous to keep it with the others. She wouldn't be writing down the locations of the arcane beasts either. If by some chance, some mean hunter got their hands on it, then it was best if they didn't know where to search for the arcane beasts.

But everything else Callie would write with utmost care.  She thought a bit about how to structure her entries in a better way. She decided to add two more things to her entries: behaviour and warnings.

For example, she wrote that mirelings are not to be casually touched because of their acidic slime. She wrote that glimmerhares are very shy and skittish. She also decided to number her entries and add a new one.

Name: Thunderfox

Appearance:

Fox-like creature with silvery fur, a pale underbelly, and a large tail. Bright streaks run along its back and through its tail, especially when lightning gathers.

Behaviour:

Babies are very fearful and lash out without a parent. The mother thunderfox is very protective of her kits.

Abilities:

Lightning

Warnings:

Don't approach when frightened, as the thunderfoxes can discharge powerful lightning strikes. Kits can overuse this ability to the point of exhaustion or even death.

Notes:

Kits depend on the mother for survival. Do not separate!

Callie felt proud of her new entries. They felt much better than before. More informative, although it still felt like there were a lot of things she didn't know. For that purpose, she made sure to leave space on each page, in case she needed to add something to the notes.

With the thunderfox entry finished, Callie ran to her parents and Callen, showing them her work.

"I like this warning section. Very informative," Dad nodded approvingly.

Mom rubbed her chin in thought. "This could actually be very useful for hunters…"

"Hey!" Callie hugged her notebook close.

"I… I didn't mean to hunt them better," Mom shook her head. "You know we much rather not risk our lives fighting the arcane beasts if we can help it."

Callie relaxed. If hunters would use her notes to decide whether there was a way to avoid a fight, then it would be a step in the right direction. Callie was even more determined to add more to her notes. She would have gone to find Wiggles right away, but Mom said that it was time to rest.

Tomorrow, they would have to begin the big harvest and start their winter preparations, so they had to rest early. Nonetheless, Callie promised to find Wiggles first thing in the morning.

There was also another thought that crossed her mind. Her parents had forbidden her from meeting new arcane beasts, but what about the dragon? Surely, it could tell her how to properly control her magic.

After all, she could speak to arcane beasts, so she very likely could speak to the dragon, too. Finding it would be hard. Winter was coming, roads would close, and if the dragon didn't want to be found, Callie had no clue how to find it. But she had to try. After all, one more slip-up could be all it took for the dragonbloods to discover her.
