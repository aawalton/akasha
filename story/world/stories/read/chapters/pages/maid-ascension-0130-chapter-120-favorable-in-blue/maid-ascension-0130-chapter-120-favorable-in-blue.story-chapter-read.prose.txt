Lira stood next to one of the heating runes Emberborn and his apprentices had enchanted in her library, closer than any sane person would be in early summer. She kept her breathing slow anyway, in through the nose, hold, out through the mouth, the way the breathing manuals the court mage had donated all insisted upon.

She wasn't trying to feel the heat. She was trying to feel the mana flowing into the plate and then out again as disturbance.

As she had learned recently, she was technically blind when it came to mana sensing. She failed to spot the disturbance mass left from Aric’s Tier Two Healing Spell. She hadn't even been able to tell when her reserve had run out, back when she was trying to locate the fleeing women. She couldn't afford to be careless anymore, not with a series of new Tier One healing spells in her arsenal. If she wanted to go on a healing spree in the slums, collapsing halfway through was not the way to go about it. Gareth Velora hadn't raised his daughter to repeat the same mistake over and over again like an idiot.

So she stood by the rune and tried to feel the mana current pulling toward it, or at least sense the disturbance the way Samy had, that one time. Learning to see with her soul, not her eyes, whatever that meant.

She kept at it despite the heat for a good while, until she was interrupted by the sound of footsteps approaching the library door.

For half a second, she thought it was Hesta, coming to check whether she was slacking again, before she remembered Hesta was dead and pushed the errant thought away. She stepped back from the rune and noticed that her clothes were damp with sweat.

This won't do.

She crossed to the nearest obfuscation field hidden behind the history shelves and whispered.

"Yorad. Luneth. Velar."

She stepped back out into the open a moment later, dry, clean, and unremarkable.

An older maid was standing near the front desk, glancing between the shelves as if trying to guess which direction to search. She wore a dress slightly different from what most of the castle staff wore, dark grey trimmed in silver thread. When she spotted Lira, her face brightened, and she started walking over. That was when Lira noticed a noble crest she recognized.

"You must be Lira." The woman offered a small curtsy. "My name is Talia. I am Lady Valis's Abigail."

"Ah." Lira inclined her head. "So, you work for Lady Luxia."

"Technically, I work for Baroness Dalia Valis, Lady Luxia's mother. But I find myself serving both most days, since the young lady doesn't have an Abigail of her own yet." Talia smiled, the tired smile polite maids shared when they didn't want to complain about their lot in life. "It has been a busy month, you know, with the upcoming debut."

"I imagine it has," Lira nodded. "How can I help you?"

"Lady Luxia sent me to fetch you."

Lira found no good reason to refuse the summons, especially since the library was currently empty, so she followed Talia outside.

The guest quarters were louder than usual, doors open along the corridor, maids hurrying past with armfuls of ribbon and pressed linen, the whole wing humming with the chaos of a social season in full swing. During this time, balls became a daily occurrence, and half the noble houses in the capital were trying to dress their daughters up and have them compete over the few favorable matches.

Talia led Lira to a door further along the corridor before knocking once, then pushing it open.

Luxia stood in the middle of the room in a gown the color of a clear evening sky, deep enough to edge past sapphire without tipping fully into navy, the fabric catching the light every time she turned. Her mother sat nearby in simple morning clothes, a needle and a length of ribbon forgotten in her lap, watching her daughter with a proud look. Which was understandable; the girl looked stunning.

"Lira!" Luxia spun once, skirts flaring. "This is it. This is what I'm wearing to my debut. What do you think?"

"It's beautiful," Lira said honestly. "Truly, my lady."

Luxia's face fell immediately. "Are you sure? Wouldn't it look better in purple?"

"Not this again," her mother sighed, setting the ribbon aside.

"Every girl debuting this season is wearing purple," Luxia said with a pout.

"A purple gown would cost three times what this one did," Baroness Valis said, sounding as though she had already said the exact same thing several times that week alone. "We cannot afford it, Luxia. We have discussed this."

Lira kept her expression carefully neutral.

Oh. Of course.

She had handed Pallan the information about the season's fashion trend herself, over tea, months ago. As expected, he had already cornered the dye market by the time debut season actually arrived.

Sorry, Luxia.

"Well," Lira said instead, "blue suits you far better anyway."

Luxia looked unconvinced.

"I mean it," Lira continued. "It matches your eyes, which purple never would. And I have it on good authority that blue happens to be Prince Aric's favorite color."

That got her full attention. "You think so?"

Lira did more than think so. She had spent months watching Aric wander through her library with the help of her Enhanced Perception spell. His court doublets ran blue more often than any other color; when a group of people entered a room, his eyes often landed on whoever wore blue first. And when he had been given a choice between two near-identical travel journals, both supposedly about Almara, he had picked the one with the blue cover first without seeming to notice.

They were small, useless details, the kind Lira would never have caught without magical enhancement.

"I'm fairly confident," Lira said. "Blue is his favorite color."

Luxia let out a delighted little sound and spun again, skirts billowing out around her, laughing melodically at her good luck. Across the room, her mother caught Lira's eye and gave her a small, grateful nod.

When Luxia finally stopped spinning, she crossed the room and took both of Lira's hands in hers, suddenly serious.

"Lira. Would you come with me to my debut? Just for the day. Act as my Abigail, since I don't have one of my own yet, and Talia will be run off her feet keeping both me and my mother presentable." Her eyes were wide, hopeful, and entirely without artifice. "Please?"

Lira looked at her for a moment. At the earnestness in her face… the same earnestness that had nearly made her brother get himself killed in a duel over the sister's honor.

For a family of nobles, they never looked at her as a tool… she herself couldn't say the same, since she had used one as bait and the other as an executioner's sword.

She thought, briefly, of every reason to say no. She was supposed to remain in the shadows, and a ballroom full of nobility was the opposite of that.

"I would be glad to do it," Lira found herself saying instead.
