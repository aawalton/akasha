Seeing the cyan glow was already enough for me to know what was happening.

With all the chaos, and the inevitable war against the Night God drawing so close, I had completely forgotten the promise I had made to Pintre.

The deerlike deity was now hopping through Eriavel, making its way toward the Crimson Guardian where Tekla was finishing the ritual. She stood before the bowl filled with fruit, far more than there had been last year.

Wasn’t it supposed to arrive after the ritual ended and the velmoryns dispersed to their homes?

At least, that was what had happened the last time. But with Eriavel growing and the velmoryns becoming more closely united beneath my faith, the ritual itself had changed. There were far more palamris now, though not from every household.

Perhaps those changes had affected Pintre as well.

Apparently, the ceremony had never been common among all velmoryns. In the past, only the Crimson Tribe had performed it.

Now that the other tribes had settled in Eriavel as well, only a handful of them had chosen to embrace the tradition. They were still gathered near the Crimson Guardian, probably out of respect for Tekla and me, but they carried no palamris and had contributed no fruit.

As Pintre approached the divine sentinel, it suddenly leapt high into the air, soaring over the gathered crowd before landing on one of the Crimson Guardian’s enormous branches.

It observed the velmoryns below, its golden eyes gleaming.

“Velmoryn God, Pintre eats first. Then Pintre comes to Pintre’s friend,” it chirped, never taking its eyes off the bowl of fruit.

Pintre seemed to have realized that I could hear its words.

The ritual was nearing its end.

Tekla held a fruit in her hand just as she had the year before. She gently bit off a small piece, then returned what remained to the bowl and offered a small bow.

But this time, something was different.

The moment she completed the ritual, I felt a faint pull on my divinity.

A tiny portion of my power was taken by Tekla and channeled into the fruit as she placed it inside for Pintre.

[Warning: Passing the Divinity consumed 1 Divinity Point!]

I would have been furious if she had wasted my divinity like this before…

Now, however, a single Divinity Point barely mattered.

After absorbing the God of Beggars’ power, it was little more than a droplet in the vast crimson sea covering my realm. I did not particularly care about such an insignificant loss, especially since I was certain Tekla had not even realized what she had done.

What interested me far more was the implication.

If the fruit absorbed my divinity now that Tekla could channel my power instinctively, then what had she sacrificed last year?

She clearly had not used my divinity then, so what exactly had she offered?

While I tried to work out the answer, most of the crowd slowly began to disperse, following Tekla’s example. She headed toward the temple, where Aria was already bringing the rebels.

Once the area had mostly cleared, Pintre hopped down from the tree.

It looked around, meeting the eyes of the few velmoryns who had remained.

Or rather, it seemed to.

The velmoryns’ gazes passed straight through it. No mortal could see the half-halvyn deity.

That immediately made me wonder whether Lyle, Tekla, or Amon would be able to see Pintre.

Even if ordinary mortals could not perceive it, those three were different. All of them had been exposed to divinity, and Amon even possessed a fragment of his own. If anyone had a chance of seeing Pintre, it was probably him.

I’ll test it later, once Pintre accepts my mark…

The thought reminded me of another problem.

I still had no idea how I was supposed to grant Pintre my mark. I doubted I could simply turn it into one of my believers. Whatever status it gained after accepting my mark would most likely place it closer to Aurelion than to an ordinary believer.

Or perhaps Pintre would become something more similar to a divine sentinel, guarding Eriavel and my believers.

In the meantime, Pintre finished gobbling down the fruit and even performed its part of the ritual, bathing the settlement in its divine energy.

Guidance.

[A Blessing of a Guardian]

Certain deities with unique roles possess the ability to bestow minor blessings.

Pintre is one of the three guardians of the forest, created from the Purest divinity. Although its power is limited, it may bless areas within the forest and forbid its beasts from entering them.

I had already suspected as much, but the information Guidance provided confirmed that Pintre was the reason the Crimson Tribe had managed to survive the winters.

I had always wondered why those terrifying winter beasts never attacked the tiny settlement when they had been bold enough to charge straight into the much larger Yellow Tribe.

But the greatest revelation was the system’s mention of Purest divinity.

If that was true, then the Goddess’s Radiance must have been even greater than mine.

I thought her divinity was Pure too, like mine. But that’s not the most important part. If even a deity with the Purest divinity could lose and die… then I need to be very careful with this war. Every step I take has to be properly planned, and I need to consider what the Night God might do in response.

“Velmoryn God.”

Pintre’s call pulled me out of my thoughts.

The ritual had already ended. The half-halvyn deity was wandering through Eriavel, hiding in the shadows between buildings and observing the velmoryns. Though if any of them had actually been able to see it, the darkness would have done little to conceal it. No veil of night could hide those glowing golden eyes and luminous runes.

“Liraelwyn,” I called, using Pintre’s true name. “Come.”

I was almost certain I could pull Pintre into my realm without its consent, but I still chose to warn the creature as a gesture of goodwill. It was the least I could do, considering I still had no idea how to grant Pintre what it truly wanted.

Divine Kingdom.

Crimson energy began swirling like a vortex within my domain, not far from the throne. It rapidly expanded in both width and height as Pintre’s figure took shape within it.

It’s no longer afraid of me.

I chuckled to myself as Pintre stood tall, staring directly at the manifestation of the God of Velmoryn. Its golden eyes gleamed while its paws tugged excitedly at the fur along its sides.

“Pintre made a new friend,” it announced proudly.

“Yes, you did,” I said with a smile, assuming the creature was talking about me.

“Pintre’s new friend taught Pintre how to accept Velmoryn God’s mark,” it said with an unnatural grin, revealing rows of sharp teeth.

Huh?!

I was taken aback.

There was no way an ordinary mortal could see the creature and communicate with it, much less possess knowledge like that. I was a god, and even I had no idea how to grant Pintre my mark.

That so-called friend sounds dangerous. They might have lied to it or worse…

“Who is this friend?” I asked.

“Pintre cannot say friend’s name. She says Pintre must keep it secret. She says if Pintre says her name, she is no longer Pintre’s friend.” The half-halvyn shook its head vigorously. “Pintre is ready to accept Velmoryn God’s mark. Pintre is ready to die.”

“What?”

I frowned, unable to hide my shock.

“Pintre’s friend says Pintre must die and be born again.”

It lowered itself to the ground and began murmuring something I could not understand. The words came too quickly, sounding more like a mixture of growling and buzzing than actual speech.

“Wait!” I ordered, but Pintre either could no longer hear me or chose to ignore my words.

My realm trembled.

The mists began to swirl, and my divinity boiled. Even the Crimson Guardian’s projection shivered, the stars connected to it swaying in an even rhythm.

I have to stop this!

If Pintre had told me it had discovered some method on its own, I would already have been alarmed. But there was no chance I would trust some mysterious “friend” who had demanded that her identity remain secret.

I moved, and my entire realm answered my will.

Crimson surged toward Pintre as the gray clouds recoiled and my kingdom roared around us.

But before my divine energy could reach the half-halvyn, still muttering with its eyes closed, a blue window flashed before me.

[Becoming the Sovereign of a Lesser Deity.]
