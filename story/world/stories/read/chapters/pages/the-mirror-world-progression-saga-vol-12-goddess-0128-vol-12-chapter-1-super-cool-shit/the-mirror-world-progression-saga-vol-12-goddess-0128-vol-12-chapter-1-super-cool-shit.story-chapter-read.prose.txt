The sound of metal on ore rang out again and again through the winding caves, an effect like the beat of a madhatter’s musical attempt. Not for the first time, Sir Lucan turned his head to look in dismay and alarm at the visible fumes in the air — one of the primary reasons none had ever come for the valuable ores. Nonetheless, as he lifted his pick and dropped it down on another bit of semi-destructible rock, no spark was struck. The alchemically-enchanted picks were incapable of causing them. Otherwise, the whole place would detonate.

Damn that Palamedes.

On cue, Tristan’s voice rang out from somewhere. “Thanks again, old foreign friend! You really came up with a great solution for us.” Clang. “Just what I wanted to be doing with my life. And, oh, the ambiance! Oh, the smell!” Clank! “We’ll surely be taking it back with us, in our stony pores and whatnot!” Clank, clank, clank! “What a gift to go with these sorry bits of ore!”

“Easy, there, Sir Tristan,” came the pure and carrying, yet soothing voice of the newest member of their growing band, the one of the Gold, Sir Percival. “However humble our task is on the surface, our cause is noble, for whom do we dig up for? Whom also do we dig up?”

“Our queen and goddess is whom we serve here,” came the grinding voice of the Granite, Sir Bors, shortly followed by the loud sound of a crashing pick and breaking rock. He’d surely just cracked open a boulder somewhere.

“And we dig up a king, surely!” came the voice of the Clay, Sir Lamorak. Clang, clang, clang!

“These sorry bits,” Sir Bedwyr of the Wood added cantankerously, “will add up to enough in time, you soft, silver dandy.” Clang! “Cease your complaining, before I mine you as well!”

Scattered laughter answered this. But the picks never ceased to drop.

The rolling, rich voice of the Lapis Lazuli, Palamedes, finally answered, “You’re only sore because I discovered this gem, Sir Tristan. Look at the vastness of the luster we seize! See our glory fall strike by strike under our unending brawn, and know it will soon be brought into the world as a final reckoning!”

Sir Lucan huffed even as he struck and knocked a great nugget free. He picked it up and inspected it briefly — rough rock, with bits of mixed copper and red-gold coloring, yet glittering from within. It hinted at a special kind of splendor when purified.

And something only alchemy can do. There is no other way for orichalcum.

The entire mining area was dangerous and wholly abandoned for a long time, perhaps even forgotten. They’d needed sanction and allowance from the minotaurs just to come, as it was in their territory. Perhaps they could’ve mined it, had they fully understood what was below, but they knew nothing of such particular processes. Worse, orichalcum interfered with all magic, not just arcane, and the minotaurs relied on the divine. They likely considered this area utterly cursed.

As for golems such as they, well, it weakened them over time. They had to take breaks. It was far worse inside the mines as opposed to mere proximity to ore, so they used those breaks to haul up the pieces.

Hard work. Perhaps appropriate as one of our final acts.

Sir Tristan made a loud scoff. “Rich words, Sir Palamedes, when it was I who made sure you were put together! I’d say there was no more humble, sacrificial act than that — wouldn’t you?”

Palamedes grumbled something else before replying, “Irrelevant to our situation, I’m sure the rest would agree. Did we not all coordinate to find the last of the mythril? Our next brother may not even have to help, at this rate! Keep swinging, my friends and allies!”

A mix of agreements and sarcastic semi-agreements answered this. Sir Lucan just grunted and tossed the rock in his hand into a cart, as he eyed the rock in front of him for the best angle to get at the good stuff.

Palamedes is overly optimistic if he thinks we’ll finish before we need to return. The mythril body is almost ready.

He swung his pick down once more, in a cave no living thing had entered in a long time, and perhaps never would.
