The more Alexander examined the woods near the pool of water, the more discomforted he became. The Widow of the Wood circled Alexander with a sly grin, which wasn’t helping either.

“You are one specimen I would like to inspect before deciding if a contract is worthy of my consideration.”

Alexander didn’t like the way she carried herself as she looked his body up and down. He tightened his hold on his clipboard as she glided up to him. She reached out, stroked the brim of his hat with her fingers, and breathed deeply.

“May I see this contract you propose we write our sworn intent upon?” she asked, reaching out to take the clipboard.

He let it go as she pulled it from his hands to examine. Touching the paper, she smiled.

Alexander had had time to make one magical contract with his pedestal and felt this was the proper time to use it. As she examined both it and him, he felt as though he were laid bare before her, especially as she rubbed her hand over it.

“This is no mere mortal magic you propose to me—this is a bond. Do you so desire to make a bond with me? I don’t need such magic from you to do so…” She eyed him again.

“Legal documents in my world are necessary for… legal purposes,” he grasped for an excuse to avoid unforeseen entanglements.

“Come now. I could make this easy for all parties… bond with me, and I will bring all my powers to bear in releasing your friend and the young maiden you wish to reunite with her parents.”

Johnny cut in. “Are you asking him to marry you?”

“Marriage, lovers, partnership… you need not worry about titles. I feel he is a man of capability, so of course I should place my desires upon him.”

“I don’t think that a bond is necessary. I don’t even know you, so… plus, you are underaged.” Alexander floundered.

The Widow laughed. “Mortal, I am certain that my age is not under anyone on your plane.”

She approached Alexander again and reached out a hand toward his face. As she cupped his cheek, she hissed.

“What is this!?”

Alexander and Johnny stepped back and looked at each other.

“Are you bonded already?” she asked angrily.

“No… no,” Alexander exclaimed nervously, before thinking about Bru-haha. “At least I hope not. I have signed a contract with a familiar.”

The Widow’s eyes squinted. “Then you are ensorcelled… or possessed. Your aura is tainted and intertwined in a way that is unclean.”

“What!? How?” Alexander asked.

The Widow paced—or at least glided around on her roots as if pacing. She looked thoughtful before asking, “Tell me your desires for this contract… make plain your request.”

“Um… certainly. We want to bring Megan and Linda free of any connection to you and this pool of water, and return home safely with everyone who came here with us.”

The Widow, seeming far less patient, looked at Johnny quizzically and reached out to touch his face. She sighed and shook her head.

“Stay in my glade, and I shall think on what price I will require of you for this request. I shall give you guest right for a night and a day before I decide upon it.”

She waved her hand in irritation, dismissing them.

Johnny looked at Alexander and shrugged.“I guess we go back and let the group know what’s going on?”

Alexander nodded.“Bubba, let’s go. Linda, are you coming?”

“The greatest oil of them all…” Linda said, lying on her back near the pool, giggling.

Alexander and Johnny saw the light from a fire in the distance and made their way to it. As they approached, they made out their three remaining party members.

Brian jumped up while they were still a ways off, then, recognizing them, waved them over.“Well? How much trouble are we in?”

“A heap of it,” Johnny said, slumping down by the fire. “Linda is fully intoxicated and doesn’t seem to want to be anywhere except by some pool of water she drank from.”

Judah groaned.

Alexander continued, “For our immediate consideration, we met the Widow of the Wood, and she has possession of Megan. She has the appearance of an eight-year-old child, dressed in moss and gliding around on hair made of roots.”

Judah sucked in a breath, causing Brian to roll his eyes.

“Judah? You have some insight into this kind of being?”

“Just a guess. She sounds like a powerful dryad if she is in charge of this entire area. Normally they just pick a favorite tree, in legend. They are fiercely protective of their trees. I told you we shouldn’t have lit this fire,” he muttered at the end.

“We’ll open that can of worms in a minute. What else?” Brian asked, turning back to Alexander.

Johnny cut in, “She seems to fancy Alexander,” he said, chuckling. “She seems mighty impressed with his magical prowess.”

Cassidy scoffed. “So he has some minor magic tricks…”

Alexander ignored her. “She seems to want to make some form of bond with me.”

“DO NOT ACCEPT!” Judah said abruptly.

“I didn’t even consider it, but she seems to think I already have a bond, so she became disinterested. She asked what we wanted and insinuated she could help release Megan and Linda from their connection to her and the pool. So I requested that we receive that help and get home safely. She said she would think on what we must do to receive this kind of help.”

“How long is that going to take?” Brian asked.

“I’m not sure, but she said we have something called ‘guest right’ for a night and a day.”

“That’s good! At least she can’t attack us or chase us off. I don’t know if that means she is protecting us from outside attacks or not.”

“No blade shall be drawn while under roof, no curse shall they cast, no trickster’s spoof. Mind your own manners, be gracious and kind, for rudeness or theft breaks the magic you’ll find. You’ll find…”

The party was startled as the large wolf walked calmly out of nowhere and sat on its haunches by the firelight. The flickering flames reflected brightly in the wolf’s eyes as it peered at each of the party members. It inclined its head toward them before settling down and speaking.

“A fire is nice to chase shadows away; your stories are welcome—share with me this day! Of quests and of wonders, of heartbreak and glee, of ghosts in the night or the things you’ve set free. Set free.”

The wolf took on an animated voice as it spoke. Some of the ominous air it carried faded as it looked longingly at the group.

Johnny grinned at the wolf. “Fireside stories, is it? Not a bad idea.” He looked at the group and smiled. “Who here hasn’t had a good rumble recently?”

Alexander was hesitant to share, both because he didn’t want his current situation to be made overtly plain to everyone and because he wasn’t confident in his storytelling abilities.

Cassidy stood gracefully and proudly proclaimed, “I will share.”

She posed and dusted off her dress, then looked around to make sure she had everyone’s attention.

“Long before the colonizers encroached on the land, the Cherokee lived in the hills called Shaconage, or ‘the land of the blue smoke,’ now referred to as the Smoky Mountains. Aiyana was a young woman who nearly died from the icy fingers of winter. The power of fires made by the people had weakened and waned, their warmth failing to fight off the power of the winter wizard Potawatomi for a generation. She never wanted to fear the cruel, cold hands of Potawatomi again, so she decided to follow the old ways. At the beginning of the next winter, she hiked to a sacred place in these mountains, a point that overlooked the Cherokee nation.

“She shivered and prayed in Tsalagi, asking the ancestors for guidance, for warmth, for something to rekindle what they had lost. Thunder rolled without rain. Lightning struck a hollow sycamore across the gorge, igniting a living fire.

“Animals tried to claim it—raven scorched black, buzzard bald, possum singed—but all failed. Remembering her grandmother’s tales, Aiyana wove a tiny clay pot from reeds and hair and respectfully asked Grandfather Fire to share his light.

“The flames bowed to her in answer. She carried a single glowing coal back in the sealed pot. At the community fire pit, she placed it on tinder and blew gently. The spark caught, flames rose, and people gathered—elders, children, everyone—singing old songs around the Grandfather’s fire.

“But the gift went deeper. Aiyana felt fire alive within her: she could coax flames higher with a thought, shape them for gentle healing or fierce protection, light ceremonial fires from afar, or calm wildfires. The spirit of fire became her ally, guardian of the people.

“From then on, the community would gather around fires she kindled. The ancestral flame burns on—no longer through Aiyana, but through her descendant. Me.”

Cassidy finished her story with a gesture, and the small fire flared up with a burst.

Johnny clapped his hands happily. The rest of the group gave a hesitant round of soft applause.

“Very impressive, shaw! You have the gift of showmanship, I’ll give you that!”

Judah interjected, “Cassidy, I couldn’t help but notice that your fireballs, while strong, don’t seem quite up to defeating that giant serpent we saw in the scattered footage. How did you manage to blow it up so thoroughly?”

“Yes, well… I suppose I should be honest with my comrades-at-arms,” she said, regaining some humble composure. “That was a long, drawn-out fight of hide-and-seek. I did some hit-and-run before the serpent wound up near a large propane refueling station. I flew at an angle before peppering the serpent with firebolts. The stupid monster rolled right on top of the tank and… boom.”

Judah smiled. “I would have loved to have seen that.”

Cassidy sat down primly and looked back across at Johnny. “Are you taking a turn?”

“I suppose I owe a story in return. I hope you don’t fall asleep.”

“I was checking on my crawfish traps in the bayou in west Louisiana. It was a hot day, and the skeeters were biting pretty fierce. I was of a mind to get in and out quick, but then I heard a hollering I ain’t never heard before. So I moseyed over as quiet as I could to check what manner of animal was making this fierce cry. Creeping up to a clearing, I saw a massive cat struggling in a trap that had caught it by the wrist. It was hurting itself trying to escape. The beast was a cougar—they are as scarce a creature as you will ever find near the swamps. I had only heard tall tales about them myself.

“The cougar—or mountain lion, as some call it—was too majestic an animal to die a miserable death of starvation. They are protected in Louisiana as well. So I walked forward with a long, sturdy branch and approached it slowly. It thrashed all the more when it saw me. Broke my heart that it didn’t understand my intentions. I attempted to wedge the branch into the trap, trying to pry it open. It was obvious I wouldn’t be able to free it that way, and the cougar took a swipe at me. Those paws were massive and could have easily torn me up. Scars I would have worn proudly, but…

“So I did the only thing I could. I stepped back and sat down. I waited and watched the cougar for a few minutes. It stopped thrashing about and sat tense, looking at me, trying to figure me out. I looked right back. I sat there admiring the beast for a long time. It may have been an hour. Our eyes connected, and I… I made a connection. An understanding that I assumed was my imagination at the time was established between us.

“I felt it was right and did something profoundly stupid to my mind, but from the wisdom of my heart I walked up to the animal. Taking its paw in my hand, I wrenched the trap open. The cougar stepped backward, limping lightly on its paw. It stared me down rather than darting into the woods. My heart was pounding in my ears so loudly I was deaf to everything else.

“It moved away slowly, confident that I was no threat, and then fled into the woods. As my heart settled, a blue screen appeared before me, telling me I was now a druid—which I had to look up later to understand its meaning. I was honored to know that the land I loved so much loved me back.

“Some of you might have a negative disposition toward us hunting folk, but we love the land, we love animals,” Johnny said as he smiled down at Bubba and petted his back softly. “And of course there are the occasional assholes that get drunk and shoot everything they see.”

Johnny looked amused as he panned his gaze around the group. Alexander glanced at Cassidy; to his surprise, she was enthralled by Johnny’s rough story.

“My people, the Cajuns, have a history in these lands as well. Peaceful farmers, we are descended from those who fled the tyranny and starvation France produced in the late 1600s. We moved to Acadia in Canada. We were friends of the Wabanaki and all the peoples in our community. Some of us even married them. We were forced out, attacked, and enslaved by the British, and some of us escaped south. We are a mix of French, Native Americans, Africans, and Spanish. We settled in Louisiana but still carry the name of Acadia—the Cajuns. So… maybe we are cousins,” he finished, looking at Cassidy. “Though firepower doesn’t seem to come with the position.” He snickered.

The people around the fire took a breath. Alexander was only partially familiar with the history of the Cajun people. He reminded himself that snap judgments based on first impressions were disingenuous.

“That’s a good story,” Alexander commented. Cassidy nodded slightly as well.

“Why, thank you.” Johnny smiled. “Is it your turn to share?” he asked, turning to Alexander.

The voice of the Widow boomed through the air at that moment.

“Alexander. I have decided what cost will be paid to assist you.”
