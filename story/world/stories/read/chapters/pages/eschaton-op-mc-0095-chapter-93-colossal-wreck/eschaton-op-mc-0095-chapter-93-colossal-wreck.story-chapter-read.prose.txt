The announcer has a long spiel about our impending grudge match, but I don’t pay it any attention. My entire focus is on Vizier.

I watch him carefully as he joins me in the center of the mile wide sandy arena. He looks to be about a middle-aged man of indeterminate heritage. Not even Director Holden was able to tell me anything about him other than his role in the Empire.

He wears light robes in the Empire’s colors of green, black, and gold. Solid boots cover his feet, crunching through the sand. He drops the wooden quarter staff the Colosseum gave him.

A golden staff, tipped with a massive emerald, forms out of dust in his hands. The air whispers as it fades into view. It’s long enough him for to use a cane, though he doesn’t need it.

The entire thing burns with Potentia in my vision, overpowering the ambient energy of Khetmenet.

“I’ve been hoping to speak with you,” Vizier rasps. “I think we have a lot to speak about.”

“No, we don’t.”

“No?” he questions, starting to circle me. I summon the provided dulled greatsword and throw it away. It skitters across the sand. “We have much in common.”

“We have nothing in common!”

“Come now, Machina. We both want to instill order in a world full of chaos. We both intend to force the issue, do we not? People like us will decide the fate of the world.”

“You think wanting order makes us alike? Are you brain dead or just delusional!? You don’t get to pick the vaguest possible goal most people have and claim we’re the same! And besides,” I snarl. “If the Empire is what you believe order is, I’d rather have anarchy.”

His face scrunches like he took a long whiff of a dumpster.

“Really, now? Monsters are at the gates. The world ended, and we are left picking up the pieces. Sacrifices have to be made for the greater good.”

“What greater good!? Have you seen what you’ve done?!”

“You are simply too young to understand that sometimes hard choices have to be made.”

I roar and lunge forward, going for a grab.

“I’m gonna tear you apart!”

The world flows around Vizier and suddenly I’m lunging in the opposite direction. I stumble, and he snorts.

“Try not to hurt yourself.”

He tries to make it seem easy but I can see the sweat starting to bead on his brow. I don’t know what he did, but it took effort.

I explode forward again, ripping up parts of the arena and throwing them against the walls. Flashes of light flicker briefly as the magicks protecting the audience flare into existence. They’ll be alright.

His eyes widen and jumps to the side. The world twists again and he’s ten times the distance he should have been. I’ve felt that feeling before.

“Hm, it appears I was mistaken. Oh well. Perhaps a lesson on your proper place will suffice.”

He lunges toward, gleaming emerald first. The world twists and flows and spins around him. At first it seems like he’s coming straight at me, but flickers to the left and the right. I lurch forward as his strike hits me right in the spine.

I can feel something try to grab hold of me. It tries to force me to my knees. Gravity increases ten-fold, and then ten-fold again. The stone underneath my feet cracks and shatters as I refuse to yield.

I twist and crack Vizier in the side of his head with my elbow. He flies off, rolling in the sand. I leap towards him, cratering the arena again. More stone shrapnel blasts outwards, but the magick shields stop it from shredding the crowd of roaring onlookers.

Vizier waves a hand and diverts my course. I land again, missing him by a hair. His robes whip in the gale left by me flying by him faster than a bullet train. Finally, I get what it is. He’s warping the world, changing space. The twisting, falling feeling is similar to the Fractures.

He is reordering the world to suit his whims. Fire pours from me as he stands up.

“You are a child,” he says. “A child with too much power and not enough sense.”

“I don’t really care about what you have to say, monster.”

I lunge forward again, this time ready for him. He twists the world, and I twist it right back. I remember what my duplicate did, so long ago. It was able to tear apart space and time, changing the Fracture on the fly. If it could warp space, so can I. I wrench space and time mostly back into place. It’s like using a sledgehammer in place of a scalpel, and my fix is crude and barely any better than what Vizier had done.

His eyes widen and my fist slams into his gut with an earth shaking boom. The crowd roars. He bounces and skips off the ground and I rocket ahead of him, every step hammering deep into the arena’s sand cover stone floor.

Right before he can slam into the wall I spike him into the ground. His body pounds a crater into stone before bouncing. I grab him by the leg and slam him into the ground and again, hammering more craters.

Vizier clenches a fist and the world breaks, just for an instant. With the sound of shattering glass, the very world steals him away from me. He lies half a mile away, gasping for breath. He stumbles to his feet, leaning on his staff. He spits blood.

“You don’t seem to get it,” he growls, his calm facade finally broken. “There is an Order to things. And you are below me.”

He slams his staff on the ground and a ripping, tearing wave of force rockets towards me. It grabs chunks of the arena’s floor and throws it sky high. I vaguely hear the announcer trying to get us to stop. We’re causing massive damage to the arena, but the crowd is loving it.

I raise my arms and the wave of force and pulverized stone slams into me, hammering me into the wall of the arena behind me. The shield protecting the audience crackles and sparks, but holds. I took virtually all of the attack’s strength on my armor.

I leap towards him, desperate to get my hands on him and just to rip him apart.

He slides around my clumsy swipes, reordering the world just enough to pull himself out of harm’s way. My amateur attempts at undoing it merely forces him farther away. Brute force only gets me so far.

I almost get a hold of him, and he lunges backwards with wide eyes. A spatially enhanced dodge barely pulls him out of reach.

I snarl. “Stop running!”

He snorts.

“Perhaps a time-out will do you some good. If you’re going to act as a child, I shall treat you as one.”

He claps his hands and the single largest wave of power I have ever felt tears the world apart.

The sky becomes an illegible smear, and the arena disappears into a blurry haze. Instead of a singular guiding light, Great King Amun-Ra’s solar crown becomes a hazy streak all around me.

An echoing voice comes from nowhere and everywhere all at once.

“I believe you should learn what happens when there is only chaos. Maybe then you’ll come to your senses.”

I can feel the world twisting in on itself, becoming a maze of conflicting passageways and shifting gravity. There is no down or up or left or right. Even while standing still every sensor I have is screaming at me, telling me that I’m flying at the speed of sound, completely stationary, a hundred miles high, upside down, and rotating faster than a neutron star.

I try to force the world back into shape, but it’s like sand in my hands. I can’t revert what he’s done. It’s too complex, too alien to me. Maybe with time I can figure it out, but I don’t have the natural instinctive use of it like he does.

Just break him instead.

I sprint forward, focused on just finding Vizier and putting him down like the monster he is. I slam into a familiar form. For a nano-second I’m shocked. Vizier brought back my duplicate?

I bounce back and quickly realize that it’s not my duplicate. It’s me. I’m looking at myself. I fall backwards onto the floor only to fall through it. I briefly flail through the air before righting myself. Everything is the same shade of blurry tan. I can’t tell where the ground begins and the sky ends.

Instead of slamming boots first into the ground, down shifts and instead I hit the Colosseum’s stone floor face first. Vizier laughs as I pull myself out the crater I made. The mocking echo comes from every direction.

“*This is who the world considers as its greatest hero? It’s little surprise it’s in such dire straits. You are unqualified.”

“It’s in such dire straits because you make it worse!” I scream back at the voice all around me. “You broke it then claim that I am a failure for not immediately fixing it? You started the fire and blame me for the ashes! A continent lies in ruins because of you!”

Vizier wrenches everything in four directions at once and I go spinning. I can tell he’s trying to rip me apart with spatial distortions, but my armor resists even that.

More fire pours from my helmet.

I don’t need to be delicate to stop him from controlling space. I just need to break his construct.

Burn it down.

Blue fire wraps my arm and I blindly claw at the space in front of me. The labyrinthian structure Vizier made shudders and groans under my assault. It stands, but barely. A tangles of it breaks and unravels, but the majority of it continues to shift and warp the world.

“You claim to fight for order but look at what you’ve done! Entire nations are broken, millions of people have died and millions more are fleeing! Mogadishu burned for two weeks and you filled a thousand mass graves!”

“Bah! Ignorant child, don’t you know what I’m trying to teach you?! If it was true order it wouldn’t have broken! It takes strength to instill Order. Something Emperor Berhane understands well. I may not like or agree with all of his methods, but it is the end times. Sacrifices are necessary, whether they are willing or not.”

“You don’t get call atrocities a sacrifice!”

The golden staff hammers home into from empty space, appearing just long enough to knock me around before vanishing. Each strike hits me in a dozen places all at once and with the power to knock down a skyscraper. Shockwaves radiate from every hit. It’s only Vizier’s own spatial ability that holds me in place.

Between being completely disoriented and the hundreds of the rapid fire attacks, I’m driven to my knees. They continue to rain in, focusing on my joints and head.

He’s doing to me what he did to Isokrates.

I slap away one hit, guessing at where he’s going to attack next. Then another, and another. He’s clearly not a fighter. The battlefield he’s spent the most time at has been the bureaucratic one. He’s following some pattern he’s come up with rather than truly thinking on his feet.

I stand up, finally getting just a little room. Even without being able to tell up from down I know exactly where he’s going to strike from next. He tries to shift the gravity, and I follow along. He wants to force me to my knees again, get me on the ground. That requires striking me at specific angles and places. He clearly knows where, and so do I. It’s just math, and I’m a whole lot better at it than he is.

I summon my sword with a boom. I spin and slash at just the right time. I see part of his golden staff go flying off, spiraling away in a chaotic pattern.

I raise my sword, and more fire covers it. I know why I couldn’t break his little spatial labyrinth. It’s because I acknowledged it was his. That spinning ball of fury and power deep inside is just begging to be let loose. I tap into it and it roars.

It’s mine to destroy.

Gold flame floods out of me, joining the blue inferno. I carve through space and time, rending it apart. I cut the Gordian Knot of space Vizier tried to tie me up in, and it dissipates.

I find myself in the center of the Colosseum, and the arena itself is completely ruined. It looks like it was under heavy artillery bombardment for months. It’s more crater than solid stone, and the sand has been replaced by gravel. I see the Great King Amun-Ra high above in his royal seats. Light and power pours from him as he keeps the crowd safe from the collateral damage of our apocalyptic fight. The crowd doesn’t seem to notice just how hard their regent is working, and they roar with approval.

I see Thutmose in the crowd next to Tapera as they jump up at down. I give them a wave, and the crowd grows even louder.

Vizier lies on the ground, exhausted. His staff is carved in half and the gem is chipped away. Even as he pants righteous fury of a tyrant denied burns in his eyes.

“You don’t get it,” he wheezes out. “The world needs me. Needs us. It needs Order!”

He throws everything he has into one last attack. I can feel the monumental surge of energy as he thrusts one hand forward. I can tell this one is different from his spatial reordering ability.

Before I can do anything, the wave of power crashes into me, and the world grows heavy. The entire weight of the fate of Earth is placed on my shoulders. The burden is crushing, and it nearly forces me to my knees. I have to drop my sword just so I can hear the weight.

I know that if I kneel now, I’ll never rise again.

The burden of being responsible for billions of lives rests my shoulders. I’m one of few—maybe the only one—who is capable of leading humanity in the next age. Countless threats seek to destroy us. Monsters from Fractures. Monsters from home. The people who fight over the scraps remaining, and the others who see it all as an excuse to finally let loose.

The stone cracks beneath my feet and I’m forced deeper into the shattered arena. For the first time in what feels like forever I’m forced to lift something I actually find truly heavy. My normally perfect vision goes blurry as it takes everything I have to not crumble under the pressure.

There are so many people counting on me. They need to me bring back the sun crystals. They need me to clear Fractures humanity does not have the ability to do on their own. They need me stop men like Vizier from crushing their world in their grip.

I’ve met countless people in my travels across the world. If I fall here, they will die. Sergeant Callisto and Second Squad, the Typicals who face down monsters. The Cerulean Guard, valiant heroes doing what’s necessary to save the Bay Area. Alchemist Doxiadis, the grandfatherly chef-turned elixir maker, doing his best to make sure those at the front line live just one more day. The countless civilians huddled behind the massive concrete walls of the fortress cities, just trying to survive in a world gone mad. All of them will die if I fall here.

I’ve failed many times. I’m destined to fail again. Everyday is full of little failures, tiny mistakes.

But the one thing I’ve never done?

Given up.

The point has never been to be right all the time, to be perfect at every step. If I am a hero, it’s because I’ve gotten up when I’ve fallen. If I go down eight times, I get up nine. I keep going, even when I don’t know what to do. I might be an unstoppable machine but in that sense I’m no different from anyone else.

“Hm, stronger than I thought.”

Cracks in the stone floor radiate away from me. Vizier kicks a rock away as he limps towards me. The sand covering the arena has been long since blown away, lying in drifts against the walls. The world is warped and distorted as the weight of 8 billion people presses down on me.

He raises his staff, looking to hit me in the knees to force me to the ground. What little remains of the gem-tipped, golden staff comes crashing down.

I catch it. His eyes go wide.

“What—”

I rip it away from him, and in the same movement, summon my sword back to my hand. The blade of stars, looking like a hole in space, is familiar in my grip.

I skewer Vizier through the stomach, and he chokes with shock. The weight falls away I prove worthy of it.

“How—?”

I force him down and pin him to the stone like a bug on display. Something tries to keep me from killing him, but I bat it away. Vizier has to die.

He chokes and gasps and his hands grasp at the sword. His staff clatters to the ground as I toss it aside, the gem long since shattered.

“You can’t kill me,” he forces out.

Even with the sword through his stomach and spine his body keeps going anyway. As Ascended we very well may be immortal. This is the first time it’s ever been seriously put to the test.

He clenches a fist, and the world starts to twist again, though slower this time.

“I won’t let you get away this time,” I snarl.

Fire pours down my arms and through my sword. It crashes into Vizier’s body, pinned as he is the ground. He screams with agony, this one more visceral. It hurts.

The announcer is almost screaming at us, but I ignore him.

I pour every ounce of fury out into the world, and we’re both consumed by an azure wildfire. It hides the crowd from view, and for a moment it’s just the two of us, both hands my sword and a knee on his chest.

He scrabbles at me again, this time panic fueling his last moments. Blood pours from his mouth and chest before quickly boiling away by something more primal than mere heat.

He shudders, and his arm falls to the side, and I extinguish the fire. The spinning core deep inside me is still there, only barely satisfied.

His eyes focus on nothing, his body scorched and hollowed out by pure destruction only barely controlled.

I pull my sword out from Vizier’s corpse when the incarnation of the Sun lands behind me with surprising softness.

The crown around Amun-Ra’s bull head glows with the light of the sun, the singular yellow disk of something flowing in between two gold-wrapped horns. Black metal inlaid with all kinds of gems, some magical, some not wraps around his brow.

“You should not have killed him,” Amun-Ra says, glowering at me. “This is a place of worship, meant for honorable combat. It is not meant for petty squabbles.”

“If he did to your people what he’s done to mine, I doubt you’d call it petty,” I spit back at the Great King.

Amun-Ra nods slightly. “I suppose not. But this is my domain. So to me it is petty.”

There’s a pulse there, just barely, something so faint I think I’m the only one who would be able to notice. I could contest his claim. The little ball of power deep inside me is only barely satisfied and it revolts against the idea of being beholden to anyone else.

Unfortunately for it, it’s behold to me.

I pick up Vizier’s body and store it away with a flash of light. I’ll need proof his death for later.

“It had to be done. There was no other way this could have ended.”

Great King Amun-Ra studies me for a moment. I can tell he’s a man of great power, he has to be, considering he held back the worst of the collateral damage from Vizier’s reckless attacks.

“I shall offer you mercy this once,” he intones. “But there shall not be a second time if you break the laws of my land and Kingdom again.”

I nod. “Fine.”

He turns to leave. “With me,” he orders. “We have much to discuss.”

I give the crater where Vizier died, the stone looking like it was scooped by a giant spoon, one last look before I follow the Great King out of the arena.
