Zane thought she simply didn't want to show any weakness in that moment.

Then the silver crust bound her soul again, and she was silenced. Malzareth gazed down at her coldly.

Do you think this is the end—that you have nothing else to lose? That there is not yet more I can inflict on you? What did you have to gain, speaking like this? Your wits, such as they were, have deserted you. But you are but a human, ruled foremost by emotion.... Clearly I expected too much of you.

There was a silence as it surveyed the scenes—the elites held before it, and the rest of Dragonspire crushed or driven into total retreat—

It is not as I imagined, it said. This moment.

It seemed confused. This is the moment of my triumph. A triumph which was planned for the duration of this Cycle...which I so meticulously prepared... why, then?

It considered its question. Then it gazed down at them all again.

Your spirits, it decided. You have lost. Your armies are shattered—your best hopes crushed before you—and yet... you do not give it up. Such... senseless, inexplicable hope.

What is so difficult to understand? It seemed genuinely curious. Your kind is on the verge of extinction. There exist merely two in the history of your kind that had the potential to command my respect. One too young—in a thousand years he might have a chance, but he is nothing to me now, and he shall be nothing after this battle is finished. One, far past his prime. And even with this little resurgence he is but a pale shadow of his youth—a figure who can inspire nothing but amusement.

Malzareth was quite bare with its emotions, Zane realized—even as he was crushed in the midst of those Iron Golds. It was quite easy to track its curiosity, and then the feeling that came after.

It’s hardly fair. It turned away from them. A cycle of meticulous preparation, wasted by a single night...

It was still thinking about the Day 1 Massacre.

But then, what in my life has been fair? It continued. It was always those gifted with immense talents who were most conspired against. It is the treatment I have faced since birth... but that is the price of talent.

Even as Zane lay there, he was lucid enough to react to this.

It just seemed to him like it was whining.

It was a reaction that felt a little unbecoming of it, but there it was.

A part of him wished he could tell Reina he was fine and not to lose her composure. He couldn't make out her expression clearly with his soul senses; she was too distant from where he'd fallen.

He'd fought as well as he physically could've, and he knew the difference he'd made, holding back their army. At a personal level, he wasn't displeased with his performance.

There was a silence as Malzareth observed Dragonspire's army in full retreat. And then—

My Supreme shall conclude soon. As will your System Shields. It seems we have reached the finale.

It said all this dispassionately. Then it began re-summoning several of its Greater Endbringers, and both its Prime-level Summons too—refreshing them. Loading them with new Blessings and buffs.

Its Primes were actually swapped out. It brought out two extremely heavy bone-armored dragons, which had quite little visible essence. Tanks, to help it take whatever attacks it'd soon be receiving—attacks that it and Reina both seemed to understand would soon be coming. It summoned several large-scale barriers before it, shielding it some more.

And then a breath later its Supreme’s effects ended and Dragonspire's elites staggered, unfrozen.

Several were pale. Their shields winked out breaths later. They seemed ready to dodge, but Malzareth didn't bother trying to catch any of them with its dark spears. It seemed it'd decided to save them, keeping them hovering overhead.

You have a single chance. It said. One. Then your armies shall be vanquished, and this cage shall shatter...unless you succeed. It seemed amused by this thought. Let us see it, then.

Reina leveled one last glance at it.

Zane could sense her body with his soul senses, even if his head was crushed against Iron-Gold plate right now. Her body was trembling, but her voice was quite calm as she spoke.

"Lord-Commander," she said. "Ready?"

"Yes," said the man at her side.

Then, with a level of pure hatred Zane hadn't known she had in her, she cried—“Finish it!"

And the Lord-Commander drew his bow to full.

His universe expanded and lit up with gold. The avatar of the World Tree shimmered overhead, and dozens of golden spirits—Ancestral Spirits, spirits loaned from the World Tree itself—flooded out of his universe; hands upon hands, grasping at his bowstrings, nocking dozens of sun-gold arrows at once—

At the same time, Noughtfire loosed brilliant pillars of Destruction.

And then the rest started to come.

Silver exploded in the Astral Plane as souls went Supreme.

The Constellation King thrust up his staff, and a blurry starscape filled the space above his head, complete with a moon; at the same moment, a blurry sun did the same; sun and moon in the same sky, and they started coming into focus. Then hyper-concentrated starlight and sunlight cascaded out, gentle-looking but deceptively intense.

A Guild of Endless Shadows assassin let loose a single cursed sniper bullet, spiraling with immense curse energies, his eyes brimming Supreme silver.

Thalia of the Thundercrest let out a war cry as she unloaded her own offering—a spear that seemed to transform into a wedge of pure, incredibly blinding lightning, slicing through the lines, striking at Malzareth itself.

Thus far all these Supremes managed to do was dent the defenses Malzareth had prepared, but even that was a step up from what'd come before.

And Dragonspire’s elites weren't done.

"You don't know as much as you think," said Reina coldly, and Malzareth's eyes narrowed.

Don't I?

Supremes cascaded one after another. A dozen war cries sounded out, and you could hear the sheer exertion in the sounds—a dozen eyes shining silver, then more—

For most of these elites, this truly represented their final effort.

The Witch-King started to chant, and every word that left his mouth was the character of a curse; the characters made a chain that wrapped Malzareth's body in its entirety, spinning slowly as they did.

The Guild of Endless Shadows' #4 let loose a hailstorm of magic-piercing silver bullets.

The Huntress had her own spirits, which prayed in a circle around her, empowering her form. At the same time, immense lightning bolts struck her bow, one after another, charging a single arrow—an arrow she fired with conviction. It was one of the first attacks that managed to actually land on Malzareth—but there was a vast distance between landing and actually killing.

The dragon still seemed unconvinced.

Then the other half of the Supremes uncorked on it.

Several Supremes—like #4's and the Lord-Commander's—attacked in continuous streams, so that Malzareth had to address them constantly, even as more Supremes piled in. And several elites had taken certain elixirs which had granted them instantaneous boosts. These were extremely specialized elixirs, and each cost several hundred Credits just to produce.

They all knew they’d need every bit of extra strength they could get.

The Guild of Endless Shadows' #3 flicked his fingers and six pools of shadow enveloped Malzareth. Between them millions of knives streamed out, all at least Origin-grade.

These finally extracted substantial damage; for the first time lines of golden blood streaked down Malzareth's necks.

The Carved Saint sank his hands into reality, and it was like cold lightning ripped through the world—lines of pure freeze extended from each of his fingers, freezing everything in its path, including essence—they lanced through Malzareth's army and finding purchase on the dragon’s tattered wings.

The Stormweaver Saint rammed in a hyper-compressed storm that, in its full form, would've taken up half a Galaxy.

It was all a breathtaking sight. Any one attack might've been stunning on its own, but to take them all together, overlapping like this...

And it still wasn't done.

Noughtfire kept unleashing—not his Supreme, notably, but still extremely potent pillars of light that Malzareth was always forced to block with specially summoned shields—it seemed to make the dragon visibly irritated.

Striker unleashed his own Supreme, slashing seven consecutive times at seemingly thin-air, seemingly casually; Malzareth's eyes narrowed again, and it dodged. A breath later four giant slashes rent reality, slashing it thinly open—a feat that should've been impossible in locked space... Three slashes caught Malzareth's necks, provoking thick streams of golden blood, and it shrieked in fury.

The opening was enough for Noughtfire to land his own pillar.

And now it was properly bleeding. Bleeding a little more as the Infinite Bladeworld nicked it over and over, and the Lord-Commander's nearly machine-gun arrows thinned out its Summons army—forcing it to block more and more with its own summoned shields…

Still—

Twelve Supremes, for but a quarter of my Health, it said. Is this all?

It was true that it still seemed far from done.

But this wasn't all Dragonspire had, and Zane knew this entire attack might just rest on this one moment.

Then—

"Now!" came the command.

And the Barbarian Sage downed his own elixir.

It was an elixir that Zane had helped contribute to—one that'd utilized the Origin Legendary-grade Eras Timeflower he'd gathered from the Cult of Eternal Ice, along with several other ingredients he wasn't privy to. Ingredients gathered to make a single elixir, whose recipe Reina had purchased from the System Store.

The Sage finished it in a swig and grinned.

"Thought you'd never say it!"

Then certain esoteric Laws Zane couldn't quite make out started to swirl around his physique.

And the Sage's aura began to change.
