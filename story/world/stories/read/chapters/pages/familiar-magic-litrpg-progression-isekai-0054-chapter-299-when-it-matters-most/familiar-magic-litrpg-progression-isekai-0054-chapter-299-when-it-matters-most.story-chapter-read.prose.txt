Under the reddish sky of Potentia, a battle was being waged to decide the fate of three worlds. Titanic Champion rank demons fought with a ferocity that could level cities, while an army of Bonded soldiers from Earth worked alongside the last of the local Nator against a dwindling group of Followers.

Unfortunately, those Followers, few in number as they were, had final plans of their own. They had somehow merged with groups of Twilight demons, and the tide of the battle was shifting before Ethan’s eyes.

Things had only gone as well as they had up to this point, because the control the Followers seemed to possess was limited. The demons had moved and reacted slowly, and with no survival instincts. They had largely allowed themselves to be distracted, and gunned down by Bishop fire teams.

But that had all shifted. Now they moved with eerie coordination and fluidity, with even the groups of ten or more attacking and defending as if they were all one, singular being.

When the Bishops attacked, the demons with the greatest defenses would shift forward to absorb the damage. The moment there was weakness to exploit, the faster, attacking demons would rush forward, creating as much destruction as they could before leaping back–never over-extending themselves.

In every direction, Ethan was seeing Nator and Bishops being pushed back–or dying.

He did what he always did in moments like this, when everything seemed hopeless, and the odds were turning against them. He fought.

Lines of [Karmic Threads] pulled him across the battlefield, from one group of demons to the next. Explosive daggers fanned out, bolts of heat-lightning fell from the sky, and illusory copies of him turned enemy attacks back on them.

It wasn’t enough.

Even with Rothavaro and Banshee reacting the same way, and Michael reforming his squads into tighter formations, it just wasn’t enough.

The problem wasn’t that Ethan and the other Twilights couldn’t take out the demons. One to one, Bonded at their level could usually handle multiple demons, given the breadth of their power. But killing Twilights was never quick, and required focusing on a single target for some time.

Focusing on one Twilight demon, meant the others were free to target Bishops and Nator. That left Ethan bounding around the battle, making minimal impact, and even for all that effort, it wasn’t a stalemate. Slowly, but surely, they were losing. He was losing.

Ethan needed to think, despite the cost, and retreated to a spire of rock that gave him a good view of the battlefield. Deevees paid for that time by flying overhead, raining down dimensional destruction wherever they went. In the distance, Ethan could see Rothavaro’s massive gorilla shaman, and Banshee’s ghost doing something similar.

Still not enough.

“We need more, Tomo,” he whispered.

Tomo’s Astral form appeared next to him, looking out at the chaos of war. “We always seem to need more, Ethan Bishop. The only question is, do you have more to give?”

Ethan grinded his teeth. He should have more, he’d just been unable to find it. But Allyara had reminded him that it was these moments that seemed to propel him forward.

Taking a deep breath, Ethan did something that pained him. He closed his eyes, and ignored everything that was happening, in order to look inward.

His consciousness soared through the unreality that was his soul. He passed what he thought of as the habitats of each of his Familiars, letting his mind trace the remarkable journeys of each one.

First was Revan, stalking impatiently through the volcanic mountains. The Guardian Beast has lived and died a thousand times, waiting for the Chosen that would bring the Union. Ethan’s mind briefly flickered through the lives of some of the Chosen that had come before, feeling their confidence and connection to Flagras, and the strength they drew from his patience, determination, and hope.

Next, Ethan flew through the dimensional madness that brought comfort to Deevee. It was a shifting, confusing landscape of overlapping worlds, but always with the through-line of purpose. Deevee, like all the hydras, longed to be reconnected with Noctem, their great progenitor. That quest took them across countless dimensions, but their perseverance never diminished.

Finally, it was time to look at Tomo, his chosen environment seeming like an idealized Potentia. The once-Nator had been through so much, and been pushed so far. The demon that was a man, the shaman that had lived as a near-mindless animal. He’d died and lived again so many times, yet still managed to pull himself back from the brink.

When Ethan moved back out to observe them all, he realized for the first time how connected they really were. Each had lived multiple lives, known life and death, love and loss. Each had continued to push on, when victory seemed impossible. Each had fought for something greater than themself.

They were all joined through their suffering, and their hope. Their pain, and their triumphs. They were joined by–

“By you, Ethan Bishop.”

Ethan blinked, if such a thing were possible where he was. Somehow, Tomo was there, and Revan, and even Deevee, though the hydra was still fighting in the real world.

“We are joined by you, Ethan Bishop,” the demon repeated.

Ethan hesitated. That was too easy, he knew there was more.

“You’re joined by your experiences–” Ethan began.

“By you,” Tomo insisted.

Ethan shook his head. “By everything you’ve accomplished! You never give up, none of you, no matter what you’ve faced.”

We do as you do. It was no voice, just impressions, but Ethan knew it for Revan.

“It’s more than that,” Ethan insisted. “You’re all impossible! You’re remarkable, you’re unstoppable–”

Like you, brother. A dozen thoughts, overlapping and repeating as one. Deevee.

“You’re heroes,” Ethan whispered. “That’s your connection. You’re god damned heroes.”

Tomo smiled his draconic grin, and even the other Familiars seemed to laugh.

“Like you, Ethan Bishop,” the demon said.

Then he was back on Potentia, and he felt…lighter.

“I don’t see myself like that,” he said.

“You don’t need to,” Tomo said. “The connection is still there, and you understand what that means.”

“We’re united,” he said. “We share a soul, and a purpose... We’re one.”

And then: power. Power that had always existed, yet been out of reach. Like the sun in the sky, it had simply been. He could have lived a lifetime without looking up, yet still felt its rays.

Ethan smiled as he realized how fitting that analogy was.

He understood now why this had been so difficult, why it took so long for most to grasp it. Being Bonded was learning how and when to cycle, how to shift from one power to another. What he was about to do was like forcing himself to breathe underwater.

He reached out, just as he always did, to the power within. To the Familiars within. But he didn’t cycle, he reached for all of them at once.

The power resisted, of course. There was one road between his soul and his body, and only room for a single Familiar. He needed new roads. He needed the new connections he’d been building, slowly over years of shared purpose.

Deevee flashed away as the hydra felt his call. At the same time, Ethan felt that call answered by Tomo and Revan at the same moment. The power rushed up to meet that call, and it flooded through Ethan.

He knew something of Twilight’s Gift now, thanks to Ulric, thanks to Allyara. It was a joining, a combination, but it wasn’t the same for everyone. Not everyone had powers designed to mix perfectly. King Rothavar turned fire and sand into glass, a remarkable showing, but Ethan’s powers were more…synergistic.

Rifts began to tear open in the sky above him. Dozens of rifts. Some small, barely larger than his palm, and some big enough to drive a car through. Dimension was the vehicle for his power, but it was limited.

Mystic was there as well, it stabilized and shaped what was to come, creating pathways and shapes for powers that had none.

Flare and ruin were dangerous, after all, and they needed to be wielded with care.

All across the battlefield, people and demons looked up, eyes widening at the display. The rifts had all opened over the Follower generals, hiding inside their demon puppets, and they loomed dangerously, with only darkness to be seen through them.

The other side of those rifts had a long way to travel, after all.

There.

Each of the larger rifts suddenly lit up like beacons, searing light pouring through, followed by an avalanche of burning, churning liquid. Ethan had reached out to the greatest source of flare he knew…he’d connected his rifts to the sun.

Plasma shot forth, tubes of mystic power directing their flows like the barrel of a gun, until that terrible, super-heated star-matter crashed into the black-coated demons. It cut through them, melted them, bathed them in that overwhelming heat.

Then the rifts cut off. Ethan’s mana was already draining at an alarming rate, and flare had done its job well. Several of the Twilight demons appeared to have been burned badly enough that they wouldn’t survive, but that display of power could only have lasted for the briefest of moments.

Still, it had done its job, and the remaining demons were wounded, and in disarray. They were weakened, as Ethan wanted. Ruin preyed on weakness.

The smaller rifts were still open, and from them came blades of red-pink light…with something blacker than black hidden within. The mystic blades shot forward, stabbing into the injured demons pitilessly, then the red-pink light faded away, leaving behind the dark energy within.

Ruin erupted from the helpless demons, obliterating limbs, and annihilating enormous chunks of carapace and flesh. One Follower general fell from the remains of something that had been a Twilight demon only minutes early. The Follower’s body exploded into spikes of pure ruin, then went still.

Michael didn’t waste the time that Ethan had bought for them. The Bishops rushed forward, their fire teams unloading fury on any Demon that still remained. There’s been too many even for Ethan’s show of terrible, destructive power, but the odds had shifted once more in their favor.

Faced with certain defeat, Ethan had done what he’d always done, and kept pushing forward. Desperate for mana, and overcome with exhaustion, he began to release the three Familiars, suddenly shocked as he realized just how much of a strain it had placed on his body.

Then he was on the ground, some distance away, head spinning, and pain flaring through him.

“I did not know you could do that, little Chosen,” a strange, deep voice said.

Ethan forced his eyes open, and struggled to make his body stand up. He managed it, but wobbled on shaky legs. Then he looked upon the Emperor, just barely understanding that the Master rank being had struck him. Cracked ribs made it difficult to breathe as he tried to draw a weapon.

“Bek told me I shouldn’t engage you directly,” the Emperor said, advancing forward slowly. The Follower still wore a cloak, covering most of its body. “Bek has studied you, and was convinced you’d have some sort of plan. Something dangerous even to me.”

The Emperor gestured at the destruction around them. “Bek is seldom wrong.” The tiny, beady eyes in that pale face stayed locked on Ethan as he came closer. “But I think you are out of tricks now, yes? I think you are finally finished, and I can claim what is mine.”

Behind him, Ethan could still see the Champions fighting, both now pouring blood from a hundred wounds. No doubt the scorpiursi no longer needed encouragement, or guidance to fight.

Ethan felt the ground impact his knee as he collapsed, and tried to make himself think. There was still a way out of this, there had to be. His mana was already beginning to return, if he could get the Emperor talking, maybe he could form a rift–

“This ends now, little Chosen, tiny puppet of the three scourges. You end now.”

A clawed hand raised up, and Ethan saw his own death. He was simply too weakened by what he’d just done. He’d pushed himself too hard, and the Emperor’s strength seemed to emanate outward, with a terrible finality.

Against his will, Ethan felt his eyes closed. Sympathy and reassurance came up from his Familiars. Promises that he’d done his best, against impossible odds. Their love, trust, and warmth suffused his being, and a small voice told him that dying at this moment was far from the worst way this could have ended.

He felt the air stir as the Emperor’s hand came down. The force of it seemed to displace reality. It was like the very concept of inevitability was bearing down on him.

Then came the sound, like a bell, or a gong ringing out at such volume that Ethan felt himself pushed back, and his eyes opened by instinct.

In front of him was a man in gleaming black armor. A man Ethan hadn’t seen in years. A man who stood between good people, and bad things.

Valanor looked down on Ethan from behind an immense shield with a noticeable, fist-sized dent in it. He wore no helmet, and he looked like he’d been sleeping outside for a lifetime. But he was smiling.

“Get up, boy, this isn’t over yet.”
