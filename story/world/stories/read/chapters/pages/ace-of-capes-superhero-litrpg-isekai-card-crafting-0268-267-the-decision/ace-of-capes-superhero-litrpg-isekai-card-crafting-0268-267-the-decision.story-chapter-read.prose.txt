The air around Lexie stilled.

Her breath stopped in her chest, and a caustic whine began in her ears.

“What?” she asked, and it was like Stella could immediately hear the incoming threat, because she held up her hands placatingly.

“Now calm down. He's fine. The last communication from the team that left was that their spaceship had been caught in a whirlpool trap set by the Orcs. They managed to fight off the Orcs, but the ship had to do an emergency landing on one of the Orcan planets. They’re getting it together, but communications are going to be off for some time. We just wanted to let you guys know in case you try to reach him and can’t.”

Lexie swallowed down her instinctive panic. Waves of guilt and anger hit her.  She should have called him again yesterday after he didn't answer. Maybe she should never have let him leave.

And to think she’d felt something was wrong but hadn’t pursued it. To think that she’d called her dad, and when he hadn’t picked up, she’d simply moved on and assumed he was okay, when he could have already been dead.

“He’s alive,” Stella said as though she could read her mind.

“How do you know?”

“The Fae confirmed it with their last communication.”

Lexie shook her head. “I don’t believe it.”

“They sent a video. Visual confirmation.” She handed Lexie what looked to be a memory orb but it was more metallic than crystal. "We were able to download it for you."

Lexie took it reluctantly, but she still kept staring up at Stella in doubt.

“How did the Orcs manage to attack and disable their ship?” Lexie asked. “I thought the Fae had pretty much already defeated them.” The battle between the Orcs and the Fae had been going on for pretty much centuries at this point, and everyone knew that the Fae had the upper hand in just about every way. They had better technology, better magic. The Orcan population had dwindled, and several of their planets had been seized for Fae occupation.

“It's damn near impossible to completely wipe out the Orcs,” Stella said. “They reproduce too quickly, and they come back better each time. We don't know what kind of magic they used on the ship. But the Fae should be able get back on the road soon. All this means is that it will take a few more days for Aiden to return."

“Is that so?” Lexie narrowed her eyes. She was instantly suspicious because this whole thing stunk of conspiracy. Why would the Orcs have chosen to attack now, and why would they attack the ship that had her father within it? Was this some sort of scheme to buy time? For what?

What did the Fae plan to do with Aiden? The Ambassador certainly had a plan of some sort to get Lexie to submit to the Fae, but he’d left Lexie behind for a reason that she couldn't fathom entirely yet.

Maybe this was a trap. Maybe he wanted her to think her father was in trouble and break protocol to invade Fae planet, and that way they would capture her and keep her there and force her into an Oath with them.

But then why would they send the orb telling her Aiden was okay?

The Fae were confusing her, and she didn’t really know what direction they were going in. She knew a trap was coming, but she didn’t know if the trap lay in her staying or in her going to Aiden.

“Lexie?” Stella asked softly. “Are you okay?”

She nodded slowly. No matter what decision she picked, she didn’t want to alert her godmother first. Stella was often able to predict what Lexie would do before Lexie herself even knew it.

“Lexie, my telling you this is to calm you down, so that you don't do anything rash. That means no jetting off to the Orc planet to go rescue your dad and no going to the embassy to confront anyone. Understood?”

Wow. Stella really was a mindreader.

“Understood," Lexie answered. "I’m going to go watch the orb now.” She hesitated, cocking her head at Monty. “What’s he doing here?”

Monty raised an eyebrow.

“I came to visit,” he said. “Is that not allowed?”

“No, but...” Lexie shrugged. “You only really come to visit my dad.”

“He wanted my help with something,” Max said. “Wanted me to make him a gun.”

“There are plenty of gun makers in the Capital City,” Lexie pointed out.

“None that are as eccentric as Mad Eye Max, and none that can make the kind of gun that I want," Monty said.

“What kind of gun is that?”

"I’ll tell you after you tell me where you’ve been all night,” Max asked. Lexie was glad that only a single night had passed.  "Tate didn't tell you?"

"I have a feeling he didn't tell me the full story."

Stella looked alarmed, and Monty looked curious.

“I’ll tell you that later,” Lexie said, then she remembered to ask,” Have you killed Vulcan yet?”

Stella sighed. "No.”

“What’s the holdup?”

“Lexie…”

“Stella...” She said in the same voice she often used for her father. “This man is extremely dangerous, and if we let him escape, there's a chance we might never find him again. He’s committed multiple crimes that led to the deaths of multiple people. He almost killed Torin and me, and he's definitely going to try again."

"I understand that, but he's been neutralized for now, and we're still running an investigation."

"Investigation on what?" Lexie's frustration leaked out of her voice. "You know he did it. I know he did it. He killed Vacek."

"It's not that simple," she said. "We don't actually have hard evidence that he committed all those crimes."

"How? Vacek and my father have been investigating him for years."

"Yes, and a lot of what Vacek used to track him were intelligent hunches or methods that we can't really reveal in a public trial. Furthermore, the Alchemist is now claiming that he acted on his own and mind-controlled Vulcan.”

“That’s ridiculous. It's a ploy to buy time!"

“Yes, we know that."

"You know that, but you won't do anything about it?" Lexie was mad. "The Alchemist escaped containment before, and you're really going to wait for him to do it again."

Stella came closer, squatted in front of Lexie, and used her high charisma voice. "The Alchemist caused a lot more damage than what we see on the surface. He's hurt a lot of people, but there's also evidence that he's put things in place, destructive things that will activate upon his death."

"What if he's bluffing?"

"We have reason to believe he's not."

"Okay, so rip the information out of his brain and be done with it. Heck, if you don't want to do it, I will."

“No," Stella said firmly. "There’s protocol.”

“Who cares about protocol? He’ll escape sooner rather than later, especially now that Dad is stranded on some planet in System knows where. This might even be his doing. You guys need to kill him. Now.” Lexie’s blood was boiling. "Give him a truth potion to reveal all his crimes. Or like a memory thing, like what the Fae did. You said they were joining the investigation, right?”

"That also complicates things," Stella said.

Lexie glanced at Monty. “You can look into his memories, can’t you?”

“There’s a block," Monty said.

“A block?” she asked. “Well, undo it.”

“We can’t.”

“Of course you can't. Well, I can. Let me meet him, and I’ll make sure you have all the evidence you need to kill him.”

“Lexie, we’re not going to kill him.”

She narrowed her eyes at Stella. “You promised you would. You said you would kill him within a few days of my handing him over to you. So you lied about that, huh?"

“I know it looks that way. But there really are reasons for the things we do.”

“Yeah, I’m sure that’s what you tell yourself so that you never have to make the hard choices.”

Stella reared back as though Lexie had slapped her. Hur flashed in her eye, and Lexie felt a little bad for what she’d said, but she didn’t take it back. It was the truth, and Stella had to see that her commitment to maintaining the status quo, even in the defiance of common sense, wasn’t admirable. It was cowardly.

Lexie was also extremely frustrated. If only she’d just killed Vulcan when she had the chance. If only she hadn’t trusted the Association to actually do its job and keep people safe, then they wouldn’t be in this mess.

“Stella, you have to do something,” Lexie said. "I know you guys are following Jerry's lead here, but I don't think he knows what he's doing either. In Vacek's absence, he's probably just going by the book, and that's not what you need here. Vacek knew how dangerous Vulcan and the Alchemist were; that's why he risked everything to catch them.”

“We’re doing everything we can to rush the trial,” she responded. “But after years of chaos, it's eroded public trust in heroes. We need to earn it back by convincing the public that we got the bad guys and not just some patsy. That's why we're holding a public trial. The public isn't going to be convinced by our word alone."

"They don't need to be convinced," Lexie told Stella. "They just need to be safe."

"Our sponsors disagree."

Lexie nearly bit her tongue. So that was what it came down to. PR. Money. Sponsors.

How stupid.

"We need a clean and open trial, or at least a clean, closed one, like how your father had. We need the offenders to attempt to defend themselves in court, as your father did."

“Aiden didn't do half the shit that this Vulcan asshole did,” Max said heatedly, and Lexie nodded, glad that he was on her side.

“There are also his followers to worry about,” Monty pointed out. “We haven't captured all of them."

“Let me worry about that," Lexie said. "I can find them even without him, trust me. Just tell Jerry that we have to kill him. Or things will get worse."

“I’ll pass that along.” But her tone didn't sound like she would really stress on it the way Lexie wanted her to.

Lexie didn't get it. Was everyone at the Association stupid?

Lexie said, “Tell him this, too. If anything happens to Dewie, then that’s on you guys. And I will never forgive you."

Stella swallowed. Her expression was painfully flat, and that meant that she was probably hurt by Lexie’s words but was trying not to show it.

“Okay, I gotta go now,” Stella said, and kissed Lexie on the forehead, straightening. “We’re doing our best. Even if it might not seem like it.”

"I'm sure you think you are."

Stella heaved another sigh. Before she left, she ruffled Lexie's hair and gave Monty and Max a nod.

There was an awkward silence that followed her departure.

Lexie thought Monty would jump in to defend Stella, but he said, "For what it's worth, I don't disagree with what you said."

"You don't?"

He shrugged. "That's always been one of my problems with the Association. It's led by people who pull too far in either direction. Complete egomaniacs who want the world to bow at their feet, or those who just roll over to bureaucracy. It's hard to find the balance. Vacek was that balance."

"That's weird. Almost every hero I've met so far has seemed like a perfectly sensible person."

"And they are, individually. It's the establishment that's the problem. Right now, they're like a hydra with the middle head cut off. Vacek was in many ways a middle ground, and now that he's gone, the other two heads are fighting for dominance, and the rest of us get to just watch and see who takes control."

"Well, it's not going to be Stella's faction. Not with that attitude."

He cocked his head. “Do you have a friend upstairs?”

Lexie assumed he was talking about Tate. "Yeah."

“Mind if I meet him?”

“Why would you want to?”

He shrugged. "He feels familiar.”

“You're reading his aura."

"Something like that."

"That's rude," Lexie said.

He smiled a little. "Yeah, it is. Sorry."

Lexie shook her head and walked away.

She went to her room, still feeling heated about that argument. She played the orb, relief flooding her when she saw her father, unharmed.

"Lexie, honeybee, I’m fine. I know you’re probably worried right now, and I just wanted to tell you that everything is under control. The spaceship is docked temporarily, and we’re trying to figure out a way to bypass the whirlpool. But after that, we should be good to go. Stay put, alright? I mean it, if you try to come here and extract me, it will make things much worse. You might even get trapped here. Just stay home, and your family will protect you. Love you, honeybee."

The video ended there.

Family? The only family she had was Uncle Max and Luke, and they really couldn't do much to protect her physically.

Did he mean Naem? She thought he wasn’t talking to Naem anymore after they had the falling out.

The message was confusing. Was 'family' code for something? Did he say something off on purpose to alert her that he was in real trouble? Or was that just her overactive imagination seeing clues everywhere?

She got out of bed and teleported to her dungeon. It didn't take as much effort because the dungeon was almost on Earth, and Lexie instantly accessed Yasycht.

She used his essence to find Aiden, spotting him in the place with red sand and red skies, about to board a ship. Was it fixed already? Or were they simply sleeping there?

He was talking to the Fae ambassador about something. Neither man looked particularly antagonistic, but Lexie still felt on edge.

She could teleport there now, grab her dad, and bring him back.

Should she? She wanted to.

But if she went there right now, would it make things better or worse? Aiden said it would make it worse, but that could just mean he was worried about her.

She had to look at the bigger picture here? Was this a trap?

She didn’t know. She had to learn more about the Orcs.

For the rest of the day, in between checking on her father, Lexie searched all the sources she could for information about Orcs. She went through the library, Undernet, and Pvilycht, who did have some experience with the creatures.

It was a good thing she did, too, because the more she read, the more she understood why her father wanted her to stay away.

There was a reason the Orcs were so hard for the Fae and even Eldritch to get rid of.

Orcs were kind of natural Alchemists.

Fae magic was largely structural, and Orcs were able to break it by simply existing. It made sense. They were the closest things to chaos after the Eldritch, and unlike the Eldritch, they weren't linked by soul bonds and weren't restricted from deception, so they were much harder to nail down. They also didn't seem to have ambition beyond fighting everything and being stronger.

They also had more racial loyalty, even within the warring factions, and were extremely organized in the sense of pushing for the survival of the strongest.

Like Alchemy, Orcan magic was also extremely adaptable, defeat often making them better, pain making them stronger.

Fae magic depended on structure.

Orc magic broke that instinctively and corrupted the assumptions that Fae magic relied on. Orcs also mutated constantly, each generation better than the last, and worst of all, they thrived in battle.

While the Fae fought to conquer, the Orcs fought for sport.

So yeah, they were annoying creatures to defeat.

Lexie wondered if her soul card would work on them. Alternatively, there were a few other cards she could use to fight them off and save Aiden if it came to it.

At the same time, she wondered what to do about Vulcan.

She knew The Alchemist and Vulcan were buying time, planning something, and Lexie couldn't give them the chance to complete that plan. The duo had to have already known that the Association would delay killing them, which was probably why they weren't so bothered about being captured. They probably knew Aiden would get stranded on the planet, too, and maybe they were planning their escape now.

She needed to put a stop to it.

Lexie thought about all the reasons she shouldn't kill Vulcan, and the truth was that...she didn't have a good one.

The information he had about her mother still being alive was probably misdirection and was meant to make her not kill him.

But even if it were true, that would be asking for Lexie to choose between her mother, who she didn't really know, and all the other people who were alive, who Vulcan could hurt, like Dewie, Xena, and Aiden.

In that, there was no contest as to which side Lexie would pick.

Maybe human Lexie would have hesitated, but humano-Eldritch Lexie was more decisive.

She didn't need the world to believe that Vulcan was guilty because she knew that he was guilty.

She could probably access Aiden's pocket dimension through Yasycht if she offered him another favor.

And she could kill Vulcan and the Alchemist in a single second and sleep well knowing that the problem was at least solved.

So there was no point in her wasting time the same way the association did. It didn't matter what they said or what excuses the criminals gave.

If they couldn't kill Vulcan, then she would do it.

Right. Freaking. Now.
