Upon reaching the throne room’s ceiling, Phatagin stopped behind the almost invisible stream of solar power that was pouring down into the room and empowering the Sun Queen. Although the potency of the sunlight was greatly diminished due to the partial System lockout, his skin began to singe from mere proximity to the incredible flux of energy as he uncurled from his usual ball shape and raised his arms as far into the air as they would go.

 

“I suspected that the bizarre sensation I was experiencing had something to do with a natural progression of my Momentum Core,” he explained in a low voice that Elidi could barely hear over the roar of the colliding mana beams. “And I was correct. Observe.”

The pangolin’s beady eyes closed as he began to concentrate. After a moment, something shifted inside the torrent of energy pouring into the Sun Queen. The amount of mana that reached Elidi’s Stelar Core suddenly began to rise. She was currently operating on the barest reserves of internal mana, so her Core greedily drank every drop of extra power that arrived, like a succulent plant experiencing a downpour after months of dry weather.

 

She immediately put that energy to use, bolstering the power of her beam.

 

Incredibly, the influx of mana continued to grow more potent as Phatagin’s newfound ability showed its true worth.

 

“I suspect that my Core has evolved from a Momentum Core into something greater. It would have taken me much longer to make this leap while the System was aiding my natural comprehension of momentum, but, although I cannot deny that making the jump was quite advantageous in this moment, it was nonetheless dangerous: for a few moments, I almost lost control of the energy in my Core, which I imagine would have been fatal. But thankfully that did not happen, and I was able to comprehend a whole new ability. Now, instead of merely controlling a quantity of stored motion, I can redirect moving objects by altering their trajectory. Doing so barely utilizes any energy directly from my Core, which means that I can even redirect mana if I expend enough energy. But I can still refill my Core in the usual way, so this really is my ideal contribution to the battle, as the amount of mana you are receiving has increased by at least fifty percent since I began focusing the incoming energy.”

 

Elidi was grateful for the support, but she could not spare the attention to understand her new ally’s monologue. It was enough for her that, whatever the Squire was doing, it was working. Her beam attack continued to swell, pushing the invader’s insidious energy back.

 

“I guess your two hands are no match for little old me,” the Sun Queen boasted as the giant began to struggle for the first time. Veins popped out of his broad forehead as he grunted with exertion. Then, he laughed.

 

“Yes, yes, this struggle has ignited my very blood. I am very glad that I came in person, very glad indeed. Your death will undoubtedly fuel my progression to new heights. Perhaps I will even be strong enough to stand against the Symbol of the System.”

 

“As if!” Elidi scoffed as she pushed even more power into her incandescent beam. The crackling of ionized air and the scent of ozone hung heavy in the throne room as the ambient temperature rose in response to the incredible heat she was emitting. But even so, the four-armed giant soldiered on, and his only concession to his undoubtedly dwindling mana reserves was to drop the barrier shield surrounding him.

 

Teem, seeing that the giant was now undefended, sprang down from the balcony. The clang of metal on stone and a hiss of compressed air was almost entirely swallowed up by the roar coming from the colliding beams, but the invader’s keen senses immediately homed in on the Knight.

 

“Another ant has come to die,” the four-armed giant scoffed, shrugging off his purple robe. Under the outer garment, the invader wore black armor over his vitals that seemed to absorb all light. His limbs were entirely bare, revealing a rock-hard physique rippling with muscle and corded sinew. Teem’s figure suddenly blurred, moving around to flank the invader and hopefully catch him off guard.

 

“Too slow,” the giant chided as his massive foot slammed into Teem, sending the Knight rocketing across the throne room. There was a heavy thud and the scream of tortured metal as the cyborg-like Knight slammed into the wall before collapsing to the ground. A strange fluid began to pool underneath his body.

 

“Teem!” cried Teem as he too jumped down from the balcony. The Squire, however, stayed far away from the muscle-bound invader, and began performing some sort of first aid on the Knight.

 

“You’ve forgotten that I’m your opponent!” screamed the Sun Queen, roaring with renewed effort in a bid to completely overwhelm the giant.

 

“I have not forgotten you, O queen of slaves. I was merely waiting for something to happen,” the invader responded.

 

“And just what is that?” Elidi snarled.

 

“This.”

 

With that, the giant slammed the two orbs he was holding in his lower hands together. They shattered with an earsplitting crack. Reality convulsed for a moment, leaving the Sun Queen disoriented and nearly causing her to lose control over her beam attack. Phatagin, being less used to his powers, accidentally bent the solar energy pouring into the throne room the wrong way, striking Charlemagne a glancing blow before fixing his aim.

 

“Terribly sorry about the friendly fire, there, Charlemagne,” called the pangolin to the rooster, who was finally showing signs of life.

 

“What was that? What did you do?” the Sun Queen demanded after she had recovered.

 

“Oh, nothing much. All the energy we are throwing at each other has interfered with the System lockout, so I helped it along. It should be progressing…now.”

 

As if on cue, Elidi suddenly received a dire notification from the System.

 

“System Quest Updated: The Partial System lockout has progressed again, and a Full System lockout is even more imminent. Defeat the caster of the ritual immediately to end the threat and restore full System access. Rewards have been tripled.

Reward: Variable based on performance.”

 

In all her life, Elidi had never seen the System so desperate as to offer triple rewards. It must have tied up a lot of its power in this dimension, which made sense, since the Elite Systemic Games served as a mechanism for the System to recoup enormous amounts of energy through commercialization of the event. Still, she knew that the situation was dire. The next moment, she got to experience firsthand just how bad things were when her Stellar Core began to destabilize. Ropes of dense incandescent mana ravaged her insides, while extreme magnetic fields wreaked havoc on her nervous system.

 

Elidi screamed as she lost control not only of her own Core, but also of the energy that Phatagin was directing straight into her body. Without the Core to properly absorb and channel that power, it was lethal. But, before the internal damage could kill her, and before the damage from Phatagin could kill her, the giant’s energy beam was going to kill her. Probably painfully, if she had to guess.

But even in the throes of despair, the Sun Queen did not give up. She fought with every scrap of willpower that she could muster. Despite the growing damage to her Core, she continued to channel as much solar mana as she could. Her mana channels corroded as solar radiation ran rampant throughout her body, corrupting her flesh as huge swaths of her cellular structure were punctured over and over.

 

And still, she held on.

 

“I must admit that I have come to admire your tenacity. You would have made a potent ally. I shall cherish the memory of this battle,” the giant admitted, a hint of empathy creeping into his voice. “But your resistance is less than futile now. Enter unto death knowing that you struggled valiantly until the bitter end.”

 

Elidi let out a strangled cry as she attempted to increase her power output, biting back a sharp cry as something deep inside her cracked. In mere seconds, her Stellar Core would shatter. She couldn’t let that happen.

 

“If I’m going to die, then I’ll take everyone with me! Supernova!” the Vessarian screamed as she primed her Core to self-destruct. Immediately, blinding light surged from her body, hastening the destruction of her flesh, while beams of uncontrollable energy punched out in every direction from deep within her. Most of these were pointed in the right direction, but a few stray beams almost hit Phatagin, causing the pangolin to dodge wildly after they had already passed him by.

 

“Burn for eternity!” Elidi yelled. “Burn for what you did to my sister!”

 

“What power is this?” the four-armed giant exclaimed. “This...this is madness! Stop that immediately, and I will allow you to leave this place as my prisoner.”

 

“No one is leaving the place alive!” the Sun Queen screamed, half in vengeance, half in agony, as her body began to fail her. But just then, her Core stabilized, and the coursing energies that were building up to an inevitable explosion were stilled. Then she was roughly shoved out of the way by a clawed foot.

 

“Bawk,” Charlemagne chided as he stepped into Elidi’s former spot. The solar energy radiating from the ceiling and focused by Phatagin struck him dead on. His Ignition Core drank deeply of the potent energy, which was already so dense he had no need to compress it. In a flash, his own mana expelled the final remnants of the giant’s corrosive attack, and his left side regrew almost instantly.

 

As the rooster’s power swelled, the world around him seemed to slow to a crawl. The Sun Queen’s attack had been disrupted when he quelled her mana and forced her out of the literal spotlight. The giant’s mana beam would overpower it in mere moments, killing both him and Elidi unless he took action.

 

Wondering what emotion could be so strong that a being would willingly choose certain death over continued life, Charlemagne reached deep inside him and willed his strongest attack to come forth. It was a little more difficult than usual to form the mana ball attack, but whether that was because he was doing it almost entirely without the System’s assistance or because it was an order of magnitude stronger than anything he’d unleashed before was impossible to tell. All the rooster knew was that it was harder than usual.

Despite the added difficulty, mana that surpassed the heat of mere fire began to coalesce rapidly just in front of his open beak, creating a vortex so extreme that the negative pressure ripped the air from Charlemagne’s lungs and stung his eyes. In the distance, he could feel the four-armed giant’s attack closing in, but that didn’t matter. Nothing else mattered except the grape-sized ball of plasma and mana that struggled to break free of its confinement.

 

As if Charlemagne would allow that.

 

Summoning up all his willpower to contain the absolutely ridiculous amount of mana he had collected, the rooster unleashed a beam of pure devastation.

 

“Buck-kawk!”

 

To the rooster’s perspective, time resumed its normal flow as his superheated energy beam clashed against the giant’s corrosive power. As the attacks collided, Charlemagne couldn’t shake the greasy sensation that had accompanied his first brush with the strange mana type. But he didn’t have time to focus on the sensation.

 

“Behold, bold avian, the power of four hands!” cried the giant, placing the corresponding number of appendages onto its glowing stick. The stone walls and floor of the throne room began to crack under the sheer stress that all the ambient mana was placing on the room’s structural integrity.

 

Phatagin gave up on empowering Charlemagne and rode the shockwaves out of the throne room entirely, seeking the relative safety of the hallways, while Teem grabbed the unresponsive form of Teem and vaulted to the balcony. Elidi stayed behind, but whether she wanted to stay or was simply too exhausted to move was anyone’s guess.

 

For several long moments, the attacks were perfectly matched. But Charlemagne had mana to spare, while the four-armed giant had been fighting using his own reserves for some time. He began to flag, allowing Charlemagne’s attack to gain ground: slowly at first, but then more and more rapidly.

 

“Noooooo!!!!” cried the giant as Charlemagne’s attack finally reached him.
