With a rueful sigh, I internally concluded that I was returning with two potential liabilities, rather than a converted companion. Tetora sensed my unspoken decision, crossing his arms and tearing his gaze away.

“I’ll deal with Vernie,” he said finally. “That’s all I can handle.”

“Okay,” I agreed.

Bodhil had poisoned him in the past, so I can’t really blame him now.

Without another word, he grabbed Vernie and threw her over his shoulder. She was pretty subdued, most likely stuck in that middle ground before sobriety, where nothing seems right, but there is also no way to articulate the problem.

Nora looked almost longingly at the two of them for a moment before moving closer.

“You’re coming with us,” Nora declared to Bodhil, hands on her hips.

“Who’re you to tell me what to do, runt?”

Nora answered by setting her staff ablaze with electric blue lightning.

Bodhil flinched. “You—you did that to me?!”

“Yes,” Nora replied. “And there’s more of that where it came from!”

“Technically,” I started. “It was Beigaldi who—”

I paused, catching his instant look of terror.

“Er, it’s Beigaldi who’s going to keep you out of trouble,” I advised, leaving out the admission that he had stabbed her. “Unless you want me to see how Olethros is feeling? I’m sure he’d relish the idea of—”

“He won’t be in a position to fight for days,” Bodhil interjected almost smugly. “That jewel was meant to destroy him, not turn him into an Angel.”

That would explain why she’s almost back to her baseline bitchiness already while he's convalescing.

“You remember everything, then?” Nora asked, her eyes widening.

The demoness turned her head sharply, as if Nora no longer existed.

“Bodhil…” Beigaldi cajoled before hissing softly. His teeth clicked between sounds, and he ended whatever argument he was making with a low whistle.

Bodhil’s flames froze mid-flicker, then melted away. She turned to me, showcasing the most frightening eyes I’ve ever seen.

Eyeshadowed, exaggerated, and sparkling—the eyes of a tragic and desperate shoujo heroine, wildly out of place on a demon like her.

“Turn the flames back on,” I said flatly as Nora audibly gagged. “Then we’ll talk about whatever you want.”

“I will happily regale with you my most recent tragedy, Captain,” she agreed smoothly, her eye flames dancing once again. “Anything to help you kill the Holy Sage!”

Even Beigaldi looked a little disturbed by her antics. “I’ll ensure she doesn’t harm any of our compatriots here in the meantime, human oaf—oh, Captain.”

Nora picked up the blessed stick that had been used to paralyze her. “Take this to be safe yourself,” she muttered to Beigaldi.

Isn’t it a bit soon to be trusting him?

Nora gave me a knowing wink. “I can rescind it at any time.”

“Ah.”

As I reached out to retrieve my sword and ostensibly apologize for hurling him to the ground without warning, he flashed pink and flew back into my hand.

“What the—?!”

I did what anyone would do and tossed Will to the ground again—except he slowed mid-fall and returned to my hand before landing. Once back in my grip, he continued to burn merrily along his edges.

“Autonomous Will!” I gasped.

Will didn’t find my outburst funny, but he graciously gave me a playful glimmer anyway.

“Okay, healing makes sense, but returning properties? Rae, we’re gonna have to investigate your current build a little more!”

Healing? Me?

Oh.

“I didn’t heal anybody,” I murmured. “They did that on their own. I only—”

“Showed them how to get back to function?”

“Sounds right.”

“And also as efficiently as possible?”

I bit my lip. “That’s probably the extent of it, yes.”

Nora sighed, exasperated. “That’s healing! Just spiritual instead of physical. You’re a Paladin for sure now, just customized.”

“Paladin? A paladin! Yes! I’m finally a—”

I stopped, catching a glimpse of Bodhil’s irritated aura.

“You know what? You can be as sour as you want. I’m still going to be pleased with myself for healing you and saving your life.”

“You only did it for information!” Bodhil screamed. “You didn’t do it for any other reason!”

I let out a soft laugh. “Whatever helps you cope, dear.”

“I am not a dear!”

I glanced meaningfully at the antlers on her head.

She let out an inarticulate screech of pure outrage before exploding out of view.

Yes indeed, I really accomplished a lot that afternoon.

***

Back at Aziza’s Bastion, Tetora recommended we get a good night’s rest before holding council, promising he'd keep an eye on Vernie. Chester seconded the motion by soundlessly summoning portals to our respective bedrooms. I indulged a bit in my nightly ritual now that I had access to a properly plumbed full bathroom.

I’d give it all up, though, if it meant Ambrose could come back.

Eventually settling on the idea that he had chosen what he thought was best, I turned down the hot shower water a bit, thinking it would be best to conserve whatever resources were running this place.

Unless Anhinga was in charge of the heat.

Ultimately, I decided I didn’t really want to know.

Of course, this dithering was just a warm-up for what was really bothering me.

Olethros was still in my sword mark, and he had been unusually quiet. Tucking Will and Faith in the bed probably looked somewhat ridiculous, but I wanted them to be handy. Tonight, however, I pulled the mark off Will’s hilt, clasping it in my hand.

“Just for the night, alright?”

The mark glimmered faintly.

“I’d just feel better if you were close. Our newest additions aren’t entirely verified.”

It twitched, but for the life of me, I couldn’t figure out what that meant.

“If you object, now’s the time to say something.”

The mark warmed slightly in my hand.

“…Just don’t make this weird, alright?”

I curled up in bed on my left side, pulling the mark in close to my chest so I could keep it safe. “Night night.”

***

“… and Tetora says Vernie’s ready to speak,” Olethros was saying. “He believes she has information to share.”

“Ten more minutes,” was my drowsy counteroffer.

There was a long pause. “Even fifteen would be alright, I suppose.”

“Errm…” I yawned, finding the fake sunlight streaming in my window too bright. “Aren’t you supposed to negotiate down?”

“You have convinced me otherwise,” Olethros replied, his bright green eyes inches from my face.

“Ahh!” I screamed, now fully awake. “I specifically said not to make this weird!”

He pressed his forehead up into mine. “You’re the one holding me down.”

He was technically correct, but there's no way he couldn't have escaped my grapple.

“It was an accident!” I shouted, pushing him out of my bed. “I thought you were a body pillow!”

“Yet another inanimate object to be jealous about…” he murmured, poofing away.

***

Twenty minutes later, I'd thrown on armor and dragged myself to the tearoom turned council room, where everyone had already assembled around the long table.

“I only heard him speaking,” Vernie was saying quietly, a hand to her head. “He hasn’t shown his face in a long time.”

Bodhil was sitting on the opposite side of the long table, her back facing everyone. “All his orders came through the weird woman.”

“Aina?” Nora asked.

Bodhil shrugged, doing her best to ignore my existence. “Maybe that’s her name.”

Vernie continued. “He had been drinking, or so I thought. Poured one too many out, saying he kept trying to send Rae off, but couldn’t because demons still had her corpse. What was I supposed to do? If your companion drinks, you drink!”

No one agreed with her. In fact, Bodhil took offense at her argument the most. “As if choosing to poison yourselves together makes it noble.”

Vernie shot her a venomous glance, then looked at Tetora, then back down to the table. “He said Olethros was free and on his way to kill me. Then he said he would send me something to help me defend myself.”

“Instead of offering you shelter,” I added, folding my arms.

“Where could I possibly go to get away from the Demon King?” Vernie demanded, slamming her fists to the table.

“Same place he is? Paradise?” Nora answered. “Couldn’t he have sent the Angel to pick you up?”

Vernie groaned, slouching in her chair. “I didn’t think of that at the time, alright?”

Tetora cleared his throat. “As you can see, I explained everything to her…”

Vernie stared up at the ceiling. “I understand nothing,” she said hoarsely. “Even the things I thought I understood… don’t make sense anymore.”

Olethros reformed by my side, a bit more shadowy and translucent than usual. “Relias was content to see you destroy yourself and your companions,” he said. “If that truth is hard to see, I suggest you leave before your confusion endangers us again.”

“Everyone makes mistakes!” Vernie shouted as she stood up. “What do you want, an apology?”

I’d learned the hard way that most ‘mistakes’ behind risky behavior weren’t accidents at all… just one’s perception slipping away until the risk taken felt reasonable.

An apology would have been a good place to start, though.

All our heads turned expectantly toward her, but she folded her arms tightly, refusing to speak any further.

One of Olethros’s cold, reptilian eyes opened. “I want the factors that led to the error removed,” he said evenly, “so that the error does not happen again. You are one such factor.” His robes began to ripple, a sickly green mist swirling around him. “Another is your alcoholism, which you recognize, have been warned about numerous times, and continue to self-excuse.”

“Rae-Rae!” she cried, scrambling to her feet. “Don’t let him hurt me!”

A number of old warnings, just like this one, surfaced from my long-term memory, all of them ignored by Vernie.

“You could try that apology now,” I suggested. “But I’d follow it up with something even more tangible, like a promise never to drink again.”

Even as she feared his retribution, she failed to commit. “But don’t you think that’s too harsh? It’s not that my drinking was the only reason he—”

“From this moment on,” Olethros snapped, “alcohol will no longer grant you escape. It will taste as it truly does to you. Putrefaction. Rot. Death.”

Smoky streams of angry green lashed out, coiling around her.

“I will not harm you,” he said, folding his arms as the green smoke coalesced around her. “But inherent in my declaration is my curse, for never again will you mistake alcohol’s decaying embrace for comfort.”

Vernie, horrified, waved off the green smoke and fled into a mirror, screaming, “I’ll never forgive you!”

We remained silent after her flight, except for Bodhil, who laughed loudly and openly. I stared at the mirror, trying to decide who should follow up and when.

“I’ll check on her in a little bit,” Nora whispered.

Bodhil turned her head toward Olethros for the first time, slow and steady, her antlers catching the light as she moved. Her eyes gleamed sharply as she rose to her feet. "You arrive at last, Your Majesty. I have honored your bargain to the letter. Now cease your stalling and give us the eye so that we may find and free General Ragnerus."
