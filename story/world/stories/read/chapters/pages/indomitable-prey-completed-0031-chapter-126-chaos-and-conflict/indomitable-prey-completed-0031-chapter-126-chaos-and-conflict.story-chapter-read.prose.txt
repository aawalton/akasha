The door slammed into the wall, its iron bands clanging against stone. That clanging heralded the coming chaos, and as I stepped into the courtyard of Castle Ryonic, my senses were drowned beneath a symphony of madness.

The noise was first; wails of the injured mixing with the panicked yells of the still-hale as they did their best to add to that number, cut through by screaming metal and the hissing discharge of powerful skills.

Then came the smell, carried on the wind a moment afterwards. Acrid and harsh—piss and shit, as men and women died and lost control of their insides. A copper tang of blood and sweat and the now familiar hint of fear underneath everything. The smell was potent enough to almost leave a taste on the tongue. I spat to one side to clear the sour saliva that coated my teeth.

My enhanced perception was focused mostly on vision, human that I was, and so my mind spent longest trying to decode the overwhelming visuals on offer. Colours flashed in the dark night, smoke from a few small fires lit from within by strange skills and magic flying around. I saw the outline of soldiers as they disturbed the dark backdrop of the sky, and the glint of light against weapons and armour filled out the picture with detail.

Ryonic guardsmen still held the main gate, crammed along the high crenelated wall of the barbican like fish in a barrel, raining arrows and skills down at something below. I caught the outline of siege ladders and saw a man kicked full in the chest. He screamed as he fell to the inner courtyard in front of me, where the noise abruptly stopped.

He was armoured in overlapping plates of lamellar armour, bright silks of various colours beneath now stained with blood from a gaping wound in his side. His head was bound in a turban, with a silk veil twisted in a trail of blood beginning from beneath the conical helm where he lay. I didn’t recognise the uniform, if that was what it was, and it didn’t look all that effective given the circumstances. The helmet certainly hadn’t saved him.

A series of loud shouts drew my attention upwards again. A dozen men, similarly clothed and with curved swords flashing in the night forced their way onto the wall, forming a breach to either side as they turned the high wall into a charnel house of slaughter. Clearly in the 2nd tier by the speed they moved and the power of the skills they displayed, they cut through the guardsmen with ease. Another half dozen regular Sultanate soldiers soon joined them.

I saw Decker and another Ryonic veteran holding one side of the breach, while three red-cloaked warriors—Crimson Lions!?—stemmed the tide from the other side, but it was too late. Another half dozen regular Sultanate soldiers joined their more elite contemporaries, and the temporary breach was made suddenly more permanent. Only a small part of the wall needed to fall into enemy control for all hell to break loose, and I watched as the defenders rapidly lost their advantage, the siege turning into a more even battle as guardsman faced soldier on the barbican wall.

To my right was the north tower. There, across the courtyard and up the wooden scaffolding lining the inside of the castle wall—perhaps a hundred feet away—was a group of Ryonic guardsmen fighting off another group of attackers, though these were garbed in tight fitting black cloth, leaping from long poles onto the wall in waves. The guardsmen somehow managed to repel the invaders though, pushing them back from the wall and holding it far better than the guardsmen at the gate had managed.

Squinting, I recognised Nathlan’s lean frame atop the wall by the tower, the hulking form of Vera visible beside him. I frowned in confusion for a moment, but realised they too were dressed as Ryonic guardsmen, and when I looked closer still, I realised that no skills were being used. Nathlan’s flashing blade remained gleaming silver instead of gold or black, so I knew he wasn’t using either of his powerful blade-skills. Also, despite the leaping and striking being done by both sides, nobody seemed to fall.

Jorge had mentioned that the rebels and his companions were faking an attack, but it had taken a moment to recognise it amidst all the chaos. The likelihood of an unaware commander or guardsmen at the gate noticing was incredibly slim—even more so now, following the disastrous breach atop the barbican.

Looking back towards the gate, I grimaced at the chaotic melee. Things had taken an abrupt turn for the worse since Jorge had left. Hells, they’d gotten worse since I’d arrived in the courtyard, and that had only been a few seconds ago! There was precious little time to waste now. I needed to get to my companions, and soon.

Only moments after flinging open the door, I sprinted over the cobblestones of the courtyard. Skidding to a halt, I barely avoided the figure that landed before me. One of the soldiers I’d just seen breach the wall, his scimitar was clad in burning blue flames and already sweeping out towards me.

With heartbeats to act, I slipped to the ground, sliding beneath the weapon and stabbing out with Resolution as I passed. Clever footwork saved the soldier from a sliced ligament, but it gave me time to flip to my feet and turn to face the man. He was swiftly joined by two others though, and I now found myself facing three 2nd tier warriors, five swords between them and three confident smirks standing against their dark skin.

Wasting no time, I twirled my spear and threw, even as I hit the lead soldier with Axis-Shift. Balance shot, his crisp dodge turned to a drunken stumble and my spear took him through the shoulder, spinning him around with the strength of the throw. It had been a good cast, but one of his companions had managed to knock him aside at the last moment, transforming a fatal wound into just a very dangerous injury.

The third soldier leapt towards me, twin swords flashing in the night, and now I didn’t have a weapon in hand. A Frozen Pyrre was active the moment I’d released my spear though, and I was already punching out with my shield to deflect the strikes as my enemy advanced. Two scything cuts against my shield and then my hatchet was leaping into my fist on telekinetic strings, courtesy of my skill.

A furious exchange of twin blades against axe and shield followed, but while we might have been matched for attributes and skills, Jorge and Vera’s training was second to none. Despite little less than a year of on-and-off instruction, my skill with weapons surpassed the veteran soldier before me. Shatter Point detonated on impact as I brought the hatchet up and into the lamellar armour of the soldier’s chest in an unexpected cut, and he was thrown to one side, a hole blown through his torso from my skill-empowered strike.

I dove backwards as the second soldier—the one that had saved his friend from death-by-spear-point mere seconds ago­—swept his two-handed shamshir through the air where I’d just been. The weapon held a shallower curve than his companion’s shorter swords and a thinner blade besides, but it was near five feet long and deadly sharp. It left a keening in the air as it cut, and the man knew how to wield it; broad sweeps keeping me backpedalling to stay out of range of his looping strikes.

This warrior was more skilled that his companion, and I found myself unable to penetrate his constant assault with weapons alone. Two-handed weapons were notoriously good for holding positions and controlling the battlefield given their long reach, but only if one could keep them in motion. The moment their momentum was halted, the favour would swing towards the one with the shorter weapons, assuming they could get inside the effective range of the former.

I knew all of this, but that didn’t make it easy to find the right moment. The warrior cut high and ducked low, keeping a constant pressure with the graceful arcs of his deadly sword, coming worryingly close to my vitals a few times. Luckily, the savagery and quite frankly callous disregard for my own life that I’d once displayed came in handy. Noticing an opening, I moved to exploit it before even becoming consciously aware of the decision.

Seeing the man overbalancing on his next swing, I activated Break-Step and slipped aside from the oncoming strike, and then beneath the next, rising up inside of his guard with my hatchet in hand. I slammed it towards his gut, but he was an even better brawler than he was a swordsman, and he’d released his weapon the moment I ducked his swing. His free hands came up between my axe and his armour, and the weapon bit into a magical barrier rather than simple mundane armour.

The magical backlash released by the barrier as it shattered was akin to a kick in the chest by a particularly ornery donkey. I was thrown backwards by the force of the blast and when I finally stopped skidding across the ground, rolling to my feet, I was at the edge of the courtyard, back pressed against the inner wall beneath the north tower. I’d dropped my hatchet in the whirlwind of movement but called Resolution back to my hand even as I leapt back into the fight. The swordsman hadn’t bothered to retrieve his weapon, instead whipping his hands up above his head in a strange gesture. A moment later, a wall of pressure separated us, springing from the ground to tower over my head in a plane of rippling force.

I didn’t slow though, and jumped into the air, spear landing in my palm and raised above my head in a reverse grip. Whether magical or physical, a barrier was a barrier, and I trusted in Shatter Point to see me through. The tip of my spear met his magical barrier, and his barrier crumpled, shattering at the point of impact and dissipating the moment it was pierced.

I fell onto the soldier, dropping my spear to catch his wrists even as he pulled a rondel from his belt. We both hit the ground, his breath whooshing from his lungs at the impact. There was a frantic exchange as we grappled for wrist control, then blood spurted from his mouth as I pushed the dagger deep into his chest. He had been the one to draw it, but my positioning and strength were superior in the end, and so it was with his own dagger that I claimed his life.

A shout of rage had me rolling away from a scimitar that flashed through the space I’d inhabited, trailing blue flames so hot they cauterised the very air as they moved. I looked up to the man who knelt on the ground ahead of me—the first soldier that had tried to bar my path. One arm hung limply, armour bloody where my spear had taken him in the shoulder, his second blade useless and abandoned near his injured arm. The first one clattered against the wall behind me from his throw, and now he was completely unarmed.

He coughed wetly, the shout clearly having cost him, and I wondered at the degree of his injury before I saw the frost rimming the edge of the wound. Resolution was by no means a sentient weapon, but it seemed to have a life of its own sometimes, the Heart of Winter making up its head clearly having acted without my express intent. It was a potent weapon indeed, though I would need to keep an eye on it in future. Still, now was not the time for such thoughts, and I wasted no time in recalling my spear and then launching it once more at him.

He swayed to one side, falling over with a grunt but managing to dodge the weapon. A ball pf blue fire bloomed in his hand that he threw my way, but it was a feeble attempt, sputtering and dissipating before it even reached me. My fang dagger claimed his throat a few heartbeats later, then I wrenched the weapon free, turning in a quick circle to ensure no more enemies blocked my path.

Satisfied that the courtyard was empty—though for how much longer remained in question given the frantic battle taking place above—I recalled my weapons courtesy of A Frozen Pyrre, and accepted the notifications that were ringing in my mind.

I turned, determined to scale the scaffolding and make it onto the wall behind where Nathlan and Vera currently ‘fought’, but a sonic boom interrupted me. I activated Break-Step without conscious thought, spinning towards the source of the noise even as my hands came up to ward my face. I didn’t fully make the turn before I felt myself picked up by forces far beyond me and thrown across the courtyard. I    continued to spin while air bound, snatching glances at the chaos as I twirled through the air, my mind whirring to put together a fractious picture of events in the courtyard as I hurtled through it.

I saw beams of timber thick around as my chest burst inwards in a shower of man-sized splinters from the scaffold behind the barbican wall. A Ryonic guardswoman and silk-clad solider were battling atop that scaffolding, and the impact threw them bodily off the wooden walkway and into the wall behind. Their bodies left red smears behind as they impacted the wall.

Even as the gate crumpled inwards, the body of the duke came hurtling past to smash into the central keep. Unlike the unfortunate warriors before him, he didn’t crumple into mush when hitting the stone and was already pulling himself from the broken masonry and rubble left by his impact as I continued my flight. There was a trickle of blood wending its way down his forehead, but he looked otherwise unhurt, though his thunderous expression promised a world of pain for the one that had caused all of this.

Impact. I hit the wall with bone crunching force, but a quick activation of The Mountain’s Gate before I hit saved me from death. Letting the skill go, I stumbled to my feet and realised I was now on the opposite side of the courtyard to the north tower. Groggily, I made to cross once more before something caught my eye near the gate.

Duke Ryonic stood in the courtyard, staring my way. His gaze was utterly pitiless—icy blue eyes emanating a depth of cold that chilled my blood. I felt my gorge drop, the urge to retch suddenly overwhelming. Unconsciously, I took a step back, but my back hit the wall behind and I realised I had nowhere left to run. Like a hawk pinned beneath the gaze of a fucking sky leviathan, I was so out of my depth that even End of the Hunt struggled to activate, my mana sluggish within my soul as I tried to fill my veins with that now familiar fire.

The courtyard was empty save for the two of us. And the corpses. I suspected I would join that number soon. The duke took a shaky step forward, as if testing out the injuries he might have sustained from his trip through the gate. His expression firmed and he took another. This wasn;t good. This was really bad. I needed to move but my body was locked up, my pathbound skill activating slowly and with such a weakness that it made no dent in the paralysing ear that gripped me.

Eyes wide and frantic, I looked around for something to help. The great gate buckled inwards on its now broken hinges. The barbican above showed cracks in the stone, and the scaffolding lining most of the inner wall was practically obliterated.

And in the midst of all that chaos, in strolled the Sultan, colourful silks flapping in the wind.

“Your wards are now broken, your walls overrun,” the Sultan said in a voice bleeding with age. He sounded like the rustling wings of a thousand moths, and it matched his appearance perfectly.

His long grey beard was oiled and smooth, covered in gold rings every half-inch until it ended at his chest, capped by a shining red gem. He wore no armour, but his beautiful silks were unharmed, and his gnarled, ring-encrusted arms were raised to either side.

“Silence!” he then roared, and the voice that had felt so subtle and weak was suddenly booming off every surface, echoing around until its susurration was the only thing I could hear. Men and women on the wall stopped fighting, weapons raised but no longer moving. Even the injured no longer screamed, whimpering in quiet murmurs but otherwise complying with the Sultan’s decree.

“Bow to me,” he said to the duke. “I will accept your surrender, and you shall retain your rights to this land as my vassal. End this farce now.”

The duke walked forwards, shaking out his hands and summoning ruby-red blades of twisted glass into each hand. To look at them was to lose yourself, as I found my eyes drawn to the fractured space within each blade, like a kaleidoscope of branching realities—

“No,” the duke replied simply, and then the blades were moving, so fast that I couldn’t track them, and the spell was broken. I blinked and looked up to see battle resuming once more above, though the courtyard was now empty except for myself.

Gods, if that isn’t luck then I don’t know what is.

I looked once more to the north tower, glad to see a small section of thick scaffolding and walkway remaining that I could climb without issue. Nathlan and the rebels still ‘fought’ but Vera stood stock still, burning eyes boring at something beyond the gate, no doubt tracking the duke and Sultan’s battle.

I shook out my shoulders, cracked my neck, and made to cross the courtyard a final time. Then an arrow slammed into the stone in front of my nose.

I jerked back and looked sharply to the barbican, where I spotted an archer, war bow as tall as he was and dressed in a crimson cloak. He was swaying back from the frantic onslaught of a turban-clad warrior, but with a flicker-flash of movement, he buried a dagger to the hilt beneath the soldier’s chin.

He let the body drop limp off the wall and turned back to me, raising his bow once more as he pulled a barbed arrow from his quiver. Yellow hawk-eyes met my own, clashing with his dark skin, and I watched a cruel smile slip across his handsome face. He’d found his next target.
