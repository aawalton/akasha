“Siya?” Vir asked as he set out the plates on the small dining table. “Mind grabbing the bread from the oven?”

“Okay!” Siya replied, donning a heavy mitt to pull the warm loaf from the hearth.

Vir placed the cutlery beside the plates while she worked. Far from fancy as they were, it filled Vir with joy to get to use the woodenware Rudvik had crafted himself once again.

It was only proper, after all.

“I really don’t know why you had to bring it out to the forest,” Vir said. “Not like we had any way of eating it there.”

“Yeah, I only remembered that halfway there,” Maiya replied, sticking a tongue out. “But hey, this is better, isn’t it?”

“It certainly is,” Vir said with a chuckle, feeling the warm pranic thread that forever connected her soul to his. The last thread of its kind, now that he’d dissolved all the others.

“Alright, you two, as we discussed,” Maiya said, clapping her hands together. “Four equal portions, yes?”

“I get the fourth later, right?” Siya asked, eyeing the cake hungrily.

Maiya rolled her eyes. “Yes, yes, you little glutton. I feel bad for everyone, losing their share to you.”

“They won’t mind,” Siya replied, taking her seat at the table, knife and fork already in hand.

“You should do the honors,” Maiya said, handing Vir a kitchen knife.

He shook his head, smiling back at her. “We do this together.”

Maiya nodded, and together, the two portioned his birthday cake into four pieces. Something Vir hoped would become a yearly tradition in the family.

Carefully, Vir placed a piece into Maiya’s plate, followed by Siya, giving her the eye so she didn’t dig into it.

Then he portioned his, and the last went to the only empty seat at the table.

They each took their seats. The sun had just begun to set in the only window in their humble abode, casting its brilliant hues across the room.“Well, then,” Vir said, clearing his throat. “I’ve never done something like this, so I suppose I’ll get started then. For Rudvik,” he said, looking to his right.

“For Apramor,” Maiya muttered softly.

“For Janak,” Siya added.

“For Maion and Shari Garga,” Vir continued.

“For Aliscia,” Maiya said, sniffling.

Vir looked over at the empty seat and smiled. “And for Ashani.”

Maiya tilted her head. “Ashani?”

Vir stared at the empty seat, holding a hand to his chest. “A dear friend. Who gave more for the realm than anyone.”

“Strange that I don’t remember her.”

“Or me,” Siya chimed in, frowning in thought.

Vir smiled. “I have a feeling you would have gotten along well.”

“That’s too bad,” Siya replied. “Can we eat now?”

“Go ahead,” Maiya said, shaking her head.

And with that, they each took their first bite of the most delicious cake in the world.

The very same type of cake that Maiya had baked for Vir on his fourteenth birthday.

“It’s curfew now, isn’t it daddy?” Siya asked some hours later when they’d finished eating and trading stories by the candle light.

“Sure is,” Vir replied, opening the front door. “After you, princess.”

Siya waltzed through the door, tongue stuck out at him as she passed.

“Are you sure we have everything?” Maiya asked, surveying the tiny house, earning a chuckle from Vir.

“And what could we possibly need that we don’t already have?”

“Well, nothing, but… y’know? Just in case?” Maiya said, flushing as she walked through the door, leaving Vir to close it behind them.

The Brij night was cool and crisp. Not quite summer, but not quite winter yet. The perfect time of year, in Vir’s opinion, for a nighttime outing.

“The stars are bright tonight,” Maiya muttered, staring up at the sky.

“They are indeed,” Vir replied. “How convenient.”

Maiya gave him a knowing look that he dutifully ignored, instead walking around back to mount Bumpy, bringing him around.

Siya piled on in front of him with Maiya grasping his waist from behind.

Bumpy, despite his many protests, began ambling along the footpath, with Neel and all the Ash Wolves following in a small procession.

“Oh, come now,” Vir said, patting the old beast. “Not like we’re asking you to gallop to Saran in the dark or anything,” Vir said, earning him a snort from Maiya.

“That was a riot and a half,” she said. “Can’t believe Riyan ever sent two kids out on their own like that.”

“We thought it was normal at the time,” Vir agreed, having Bumpy set out from the village before turning north, heading uphill.

“That man sounds wrong in the head,” Siya said, shaking her head. “I’m shocked you two survived.”

The three of them exchanged laughs at that, though fell silent upon a protest from Bumpy, lest they anger their mount. The old Ash’va huffed his way to the base of a place that Vir had not been to in a very, very long time.

“C’mon’, Bumpy!” Siya said, hugging his neck. “I believe in you!”

The words seemed to give the aging beast a second wind, and he powered through the final paces, cresting the top of Rabbit Hill.

Dismounting, Maiya set out the picnic blanket she’d brought, placing it atop the short grass some ways away from the lone tree.

Neel immediately took his spot in the center. Siya kicked off her shoes and sidled up next to him, lying flat on her back.

“Wow,” Siya whispered, staring up at the stars. “You said you two came to this place when you were young?”

“Many times,” Maiya said, lying down beside her.

“And always without permission,” Vir added, doing the same on the opposite side, Neel and Siya between them.

The wolves, ever the dignified beasts, took up positions in the branches of the tree, watching over them.

“Sounds wonderful,” Siya replied softly. “I did something similar with Janak, once. If I had a place like this, I don’t know if I would ever have left.”

“We weren’t given much of a choice, you see,” Maiya replied with a small smile.

“That’s one way of putting it,” Vir said. “All those nights we’d look up at the stars, dreaming about all the adventures we’d have and the places we’d go.”

“Fantasizing,” Maiya added, “about other worlds.”

“How little we knew that each star in the sky was exactly that. And not just worlds, but universes unto themselves.”

“Still, a pretty impressive thought for a couple of village kids, don’t you think?” Maiya asked.

“Impressive indeed,” Siya added. “Ready, daddy?”

“In just a moment, Siya,” Vir replied, closing his eyes.

When he opened them again, they’d turned from their normal color to a brilliant white. Each glowing so fiercely that were a village of Brij to glimpse him in that moment, they would have lost their mind.

For in Vir’s eyes was the whole of the universe itself.

Not just one, but them all.

The power of a being who, thanks primarily to Siya and her forays into Janak's research, had ascended past the flesh. Who'd consumed the prana of an entire realm to become an equal presence against those who had, for all of every history, remained unchecked. The lone balancer of scales in all of creation.

Every possibility opened itself to him then. Every past that ever was. Every present that happened now, and every future yet to be written.

Vir saw them all, written out in detail in the tapestry that was the night’s sky, each star a destination yet to be visited.

“Tell me, Siya,” he said. “Which universe would you like to save next?”

“Hmm, let’s see…”

The girl from an Age long forgotten considered the decision for a moment before extending a hand, pointing straight up.

“How about… that one!”
