Percy’s Saint’s Eye was the only mutation that hadn’t merged with the rest. That didn’t necessarily mean that it hadn’t interacted with the others – in fact, Percy had already felt some changes to his sensory domain – but Phoebe’s Decree had probably decided that the scope of the two mutations was different enough to list them separately. Maybe it had to do with the fact that Percy’s clones inherited his enhanced vision but not his physique.

The next thing he noticed was that he’d made quite a mess.

Micky and Archibald had used their domains to contain the damage and filth to the interior of the vat. The person-sized tube was covered in cracks, though it had held, mainly because the Huehuan hadn’t stopped supplying it with ice mana.

The water had either been absorbed into Percy’s body along with the magical ingredients or evaporated away, leaving only layers of solid crust and viscous sludge coating the walls and base of the vat. The whole cavern reeked like a warehouse of spoiled fish. Thankfully, Percy had had the foresight to undergo the ritual in the hive, away from people. Or his furniture.

Climbing out of the vat, Percy wiped most of the grime away with a wave of willpower, before borrowing some liquid soul-freezing ice mana from Micky and scrubbing the rest as well as he could. He’d need a proper shower later – probably several, actually – but this would have to do for now.

“Micky, could you please seal the damn thing before the stench spreads any further?” he asked, wrinkling his nose. “Sorry for putting you both through this, but you can’t imagine how good it feels to have flushed all that trash out of my system.”

The Huehuan was already one step ahead. “I don’t think that putting a lid on it is going to be enough. Unless Nephthys’ wasps have developed an appetite for rotten goo, it might be best to toss the whole vat into outer space. I can do that, if you want.”

Percy nodded. “Make sure to aim at the sun. Not that anyone will ever find such a small object drifting out there, but I’d rather not create more unnecessary karmic connections that lead back to Remior.”

Searching through the mess in his spatial seal, he located his Phantomwoven Cloak. There were lots of empty vials and scattered tools that he’d have to organize later, but he ignored them for the time being. Focusing on his magical robes, he carefully summoned them directly around his body, wearing them with a single thought.

He didn’t like the idea of his clothes soaking up his stench, but he could always just phase the fabric through the physical residue after washing up properly. Besides, his attire felt a bit loose in a couple of spots that used to fit him perfectly, likely due to Percy’s body having grown leaner, so he would probably have to replace it at some point anyway.

‘It’s still useable, but it might not be a bad idea to redesign my equipment when we craft Micky’s…’

The adaptive self-repair enchantments inside the unit cells had automatically upgraded the material to Green after his promotion, yet his magiscript skill had also improved by leaps and bounds over the past decade, and the structure of the silk itself might also have room for improvement.

A project for later.

“Thanks for the help,” he told his grandfather. “I wouldn’t have survived this without you.”

“Yeah… remember that the next time you decide to do something crazy,” Archibald replied, patting Percy on the shoulder.

The patriarch’s expression was a cocktail of relief and irritation. He probably had some choice words for his reckless grandson, though he bit them back.

Percy’s friends and relatives had grown used to his daring experiments, though he had to admit that this had been a rather close one. Had it taken him even a second longer to figure out the correct heartbeat rhythm, his body would have failed to adapt to it in time. He would have had to keep his heart contained within a shell of willpower and would have been scrambling to find a way back to Thess’kala right now, to steal more Heartblood for another ritual.

He hadn’t expected things to get so dangerous, but he didn’t exactly regret his actions, as he could already tell that pushing his mutations to merge had been worth it.

‘I’m not sure I disagree with your grandfather,’ Micky said through the cord. ‘I know that we’re not new to risks, but don’t forget how many people are relying on you… all of Remior, Huehue, Amenthes, Melodia, the Vault, and Robari – just to name a few. Maybe we should stick to safer projects until we’ve resolved some of those responsibilities.’

Percy had no retort.

Fortunately, his friend didn’t wait for one. Borrowing a large chunk of their shared willpower, the Huehuan lifted the cylindrical box of filth off the ground and flew into the hole in the ceiling.

Percy didn’t follow Micky. Instead, he sat cross-legged on a flat metallic patch in a relatively clean corner of the cavern and focused on his cores. As much as he wanted to study his new mutation, restoring his Green grade took precedence.

The process of compressing his mana proved surprisingly smooth and quick, with each core only taking around ten minutes. It was a significant improvement over the first time he’d done this, which could partially be attributed to the experience he had accumulated after granting artificial promotions to his friends and relatives.

That said, restoring his grades still proved much easier than expected.

Playing with his mana reserves for a couple of minutes, Percy understood the reason: his new mutation.

His enhanced physique had greatly strengthened his body, allowing him to circulate more mana through his channels, expanding both his capacity and his ability to withstand mana of the next grade.

His reserves hadn’t increased that much – only by around ten percent – but his minimum threshold for maintaining his artificial promotions had decreased sharply. After a few tests, Percy estimated that he could probably drop to a fifth of his capacity without his mana coming undone.

The Cradle still required Circulation to remain active, and essentially cannibalized the benefits provided by the internal portion of the boosting art. Still, having more freedom to exert himself during a dangerous fight was nice.

The promotions weren’t completely permanent yet, but this was a huge step in the right direction. Percy had no idea whether the seals or his boosting art could be strengthened further, but the passive boosts provided by his third core might be enough to resolve the issue if he ever activated the Void Decree.

Once he was done, Percy leapt to his feet, noticing how much lighter his body felt. He itched to put it to the test, every trace of his previous fatigue having evaporated under the joint influence of his upgraded mutation and restored promotions.

One thing that bothered him slightly was that his domain hadn’t grown any stronger.

Obatala’s Approval had locked his Seventh Circle willpower in place despite his demotion, so he’d hoped that it would grow stronger again after reactivating the Cradle. Evidently, his ambitious plan of repeatedly lowering and restoring his grade to keep tempering his domain indefinitely had been little more than a pipedream.

‘Oh well… let’s not get too greedy.’

With his grade and cords restored, Percy turned his attention back to his grandfather who had waited patiently across from him. He spent a few seconds replacing the clone inside the patriarch’s body, instructing him to resume working on the second Cradle before bidding them both farewell.

‘Micky? Up for a quick sparring match?’ he asked his fellow aspect. ‘I want to see how much stronger I’ve grown.’

‘Come over here,’ the Huehuan replied, sending him an image of the Fungal Spire. ‘Better to fight atop the mushrooms than risk damaging the hive.’

While flying through the tunnel, Percy noticed that his Sage’s Pond could peer much deeper into the walls. His overall range hadn’t increased much, but physical surfaces inhibited his senses about three times less.

Evidently, the internal sight he had developed during the ritual wasn’t limited to just his own body. Perhaps, this was an ability of his distant ancestors that had been watered down into the goblins’ night vision, or maybe it was exclusive to Percy – made possible only due to the mutation’s interaction with his sensory domain.

Either way, he was tempted to renew the clone on Tenefyre. The enhanced sight would help him locate the droplets of divine mana hidden within the ocean of magma several times faster.

Percy and Micky had paused sending spiritual blood during the ritual, though their clone had probably been given enough to complete the task already. The cord was still in its empowered state, since maintaining Soul Resonance with a single clone was effortless, and the clones on Remior could communicate with the main body even without the spell.

‘Never mind. The clone’s almost done anyway,’ he remembered, shaking his head. ‘Micky, how confident are you in copying the effects of the ritual on your own body?’

‘Some of it should be easy enough, since you went through it slowly and methodically,’ the Huehuan replied. ‘But don’t expect me to replicate everything perfectly.’

Percy nodded.

Considering how long it had taken Micky to copy the effect of the ruby powder, it wasn’t surprising that he couldn’t reproduce a much more complicated transformation on his first try.

The Huehuan was already a beast with an extremely powerful mutation, so his body had less room for improvement. The Remiorian and Thess’kalan traits should also be less compatible with him, so Percy would be happy if his friend managed to borrow a few tricks from the ritual to strengthen his body slightly.

‘Don’t try anything until you get your new blessing,’ he suggested. ‘You might as well upgrade your materials before using them to build a new house.’

Reaching the surface, Percy briefly passed through his residence before changing course to the peak of the Fungal Spire. He was able to fly much faster above ground, as he didn’t have to worry about bumping into anything, so he reached the giant mushroom that Micky had picked for the fight in under a minute.

The Huehuan had transformed into a person-sized gladiator, manifesting both pairs of arms but no weapons, enchantments, magical attacks, or added bulk.

Percy landed on the other side of the glowing red cap, sending countless colourful motes swirling outwards in the process. He didn’t summon his scythes either, and even stored the top half of his Cloak inside his seal. He wanted to absorb his friend’s punches with his own body, to test its new limits.

“Ready when you are,” he taunted with a provocative smirk.
