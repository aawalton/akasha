Luke inhaled sharply, his vision swallowed by darkness. The sensation of falling had passed—replaced now by a familiar, weightless drift. He recognized it instantly.

It was just like when he used Eagle Eye.

But something was off.

The most obvious difference: he couldn’t see.

Steadying himself, he felt the subtle thrum of Dragon Heart working quietly within him. Despite the unknown, a strange sense of freedom settled over him—unexpected, but not unwelcome.

The exhaustion from the past week had vanished, as though he’d sloughed off his physical shell entirely.

Am I in soul form… like before?

That would explain the weightlessness. But if so, why now?

He hadn’t activated any skills. In truth, he didn’t even know how to enter this form voluntarily—hell, he hadn’t even known such a thing existed until a few days ago.

As he peered into the darkness, something began to materialize.

Two glowing orbs floated nearby, suspended in the void like distant stars. They were cold, blue, and beautiful—like sapphires buried in snow.

And yet, instead of awe, a creeping dread unfurled in his chest. The light emanating from them carried a quiet malice, a chill that spread from their center like frostbite.

The orbs swayed side to side in rhythm, slowly growing larger with each pass.

Something about their movement—slow, deliberate, hungry—tightened the knots in his stomach. Still, Luke stood firm.

They didn’t feel hostile.

Not yet.

Then the hissing began.

It slithered from every direction at once, layered and echoing, as though he’d stepped into a nest of serpents. The sound seeped into his skull, prickling the edges of his thoughts, filling his head until it was all he could hear.

When the orbs finally halted, hovering some twenty feet away, the truth revealed itself.

Eyes.

Twin, glacial eyes embedded in the massive, angular head of a serpent. Its gaze was unblinking—void of empathy, overflowing with quiet bloodlust.

It resembled the soul form of the Holy Knight he’d seen in the forest… but this one was far larger, its presence even more suffocating. Its body was shadow, endless and coiled, as if drawn from the void itself.

Luke blinked, breath caught in his throat. He couldn’t fully grasp what was happening, but one thing was clear:

He was in danger.

The blue eyes locked onto him. The hissing ceased in an instant, replaced by a silence so dense it pressed on his skull, fraying his nerves.

Then a voice cut through the void.

“We finally meet... Sovereign.”

Luke’s body went rigid.

Sovereign—the name of the system that brought him to this world. A name he had never spoken aloud. Not to Tristan. Not even in his own thoughts unless completely alone.

How could anyone know that name?

His mind spun, Scholarly Pursuit racing to parse every interaction, every lead, every hidden enemy who might know the truth.

The silence stretched, heavy with implication.

Finally, he spoke.

“You must be the Pope… or should I call you Bishop?”

The serpent’s form tensed. Then, its blood-red tongue flicked from its mouth.

It hissed again, but this time, the sound was softer—almost amused.

Like a chuckle made of venom.

“So, you do know who I am.” The voice coiled with amusement. “Though I imagine it wasn’t too difficult to deduce, considering I already hold dominion over Lhair.”

Luke said nothing, though his mind churned beneath the calm surface.

That he now stood before the Bishop of Lhair was disturbing enough—especially given he was still half a continent away—but that wasn’t what truly rattled him.

If he knows about my system… then he must have one too. Or at least something equivalent—some kind of Divine Blessing…

Either unaware of Luke’s racing thoughts—or simply unconcerned—the Bishop continued, his tongue flicking lazily through the air.

“You, on the other hand, were much harder to identify,” he said. “That is… until your little stunt in Clayton City.”

Luke stiffened. His instincts screamed.

“I still find it difficult to believe you managed to create gunpowder in such a short time,” the serpent continued, his tone light, almost conversational. “Regretfully, I lost a few promising Holy Knights in the exchange.”

A pause.

“Perhaps your biggest mistake was calling it gunpowder, rather than something more… ambiguous. Like black powder, for instance. No one in this world would call it that. Not unless they held knowledge of Earth.”

Earth!? It can’t be…

Another hiss followed—dry, amused, sharp.

“You led me right to you, Luke Drakon.”

The name echoed like a curse.

The darkness around him seemed to constrict, and Luke felt his thoughts scatter.

This man is dangerous…

He had been summoned here against his will. Dragged before a being who held not only immense power, but the strategic advantage. The Bishop had men in every direction, pushing deep into the Marxx Kingdom—perhaps even allies in Selis, ready to divide the spoils once the kingdom fell.

Luke was on the back foot.

But I can’t show weakness. If I falter now… it will all end.

The thought struck him with sudden clarity—not born of logic, but instinct. A pressure deep within him, as though the very world willed him to stand tall.

There was a reason he had been brought here.

He didn’t yet understand it… but he felt it.

“Why do you speak as if I’ve been trying to hide?” Luke said, his voice rising like a tide, sharp and resonant. The words rang through the void with surprising majesty.

The space itself trembled.

Cracks burst across the darkness, spilling forth radiant golden light. Blinding and divine, it spilled into the realm like a rising sun, revealing the serpent in full—its vast coils wrapped around itself, monstrous and unmoving.

Its cold, blue eyes still glowed—but the brilliance of the golden light made them seem dim by comparison.

“Do you think your victory is already written, Bishop?” Luke’s voice climbed higher, cutting and bold. “Do you truly believe you’ve won?”

He laughed.

It was a full, hearty laugh—not mocking, but real. Bitter and unshaken. It echoed across the space, daring the serpent to strike.

The Bishop remained still. His tongue flicked again, but his gaze did not waver.

Luke could feel it now—his confidence. No, his arrogance. The Bishop believed himself untouchable. He thought the game was already over.

And to be fair…

He wasn’t wrong.

The Marxx Kingdom teetered on the edge. The Bishop’s grip had already begun to squeeze. Victory was close.

But all was not lost.

Not yet.

In that moment, a calm confidence washed over Luke.

The lingering anxiety bled away, dissolving into the darkness like fog in the morning sun. As the space around him continued to fracture, the shadows retreated—drawn back by the brilliance pouring in.

He understood now.

He knew what he had to do.

“Keep your neck clean, Bishop. When I’ve beaten back your armies and claimed this continent as my own… I’ll personally take your head.”

[DING]

[Sovereign’s Blessing Activated]

The darkness shattered completely.

A flood of radiant gold consumed the realm, and from Luke’s back, immense golden wings unfurled—ancient, divine, and blinding. They stretched wide, as if they had long yearned to break free.

Power radiated from him in waves—an overwhelming pressure, far beyond what Domineering Air had ever achieved. It was regal. Celestial. Like a god descending upon a mortal world.

And yet…

The serpent did not flinch.

Its icy gaze flared with hatred as it glared up at Luke, unblinking.

It lifted its head high, coiling tighter, its tongue flickering like a flurry of crimson lightning.

Then, with lethal intent, it struck.

The serpent lunged like a javelin through space, maw wide open—revealing a blood-red throat and two monstrous fangs, gleaming with venom. Behind them stretched only void, as if its mouth led to the abyss itself.

Luke raised his hand instinctively—

And watched in awe as a golden-scaled claw erupted before him, intercepting the strike mid-air. It seized the serpent by its neck with brutal precision—like a hawk snatching prey mid-flight.

The snake shrieked, thrashing wildly within the grip.

Its jaws snapped open and sank into the claw, sinking its fangs deep.

For a heartbeat, Luke’s breath caught—

But then, a system notification flared to life.

[The Sovereign’s Blessing has negated the skill: Soul Poison]

This bastard can poison me? Luke thought, eyes narrowing.

He still didn’t know where they truly were—whether this was a soul realm, a divine plane, or something else entirely. But the fact that the Bishop could strike at him here, across vast distances, changed everything.

And worse—Luke had no active skills to use here. Not in soul form. No blade. No spell. Just instincts and this golden claw.

He squeezed tighter—

But no matter how hard he crushed the serpent, it refused to die.

Another notification appeared.

[DING]

A grin tugged at Luke’s lips.

A deep, rumbling laugh escaped him—rolling like thunder across the golden void. With a flick of his claw, he hurled the serpent away.

It flew end over end, crashing through the air like a limp rope, its dignity discarded like wet cloth.

The space trembled with the force of his laughter.

“You think your petty tricks can touch me, Bishop?”

He hovered higher, wings spread wide, his voice echoing like prophecy.

“Our war will be fought on the physical plane. Army against army. Men against men. My intelligence... against whatever backhanded schemes you manage to crawl together.”

He flared his wings once more, golden light bursting from his body.

“Either way, the result will be the same.”

Rising above the shattered space, Luke’s eyes narrowed.

“Remember what I said.”

A beat of silence.

Then:

“Keep your neck clean for me, worm.”

[Exiting the Divine Realm]

A furious hiss tore through the golden space—just before it collapsed.

Darkness fell like a curtain, and that too-familiar wave of fatigue crept over Luke like a cold tide.

When he opened his eyes, they stung—heavy, itchy, and slow to focus.

He was back.

The wooden ceiling of the church greeted him briefly—before being replaced by a face far too close for comfort.

Tristan’s.

The man’s goatee brushed Luke’s cheek, his expression twisted with concern.

“I’m okay,” Luke said quickly, pulling back a few inches. “How long was I out?”

“What?” Tristan blinked. “You just… fell. I’m not even sure you were unconscious.”

Huh?

I was there for at least ten minutes… does time pass differently in that place?

Luke cleared his throat, recovering quickly. “I was exhausted, that’s all.”

Then, shifting gears: “We need rest—and food. So do the men. After two days, we return to Ralis. But first…”

He glanced at the open doors of the church.

“We’re cleaning out the fish farm. I don’t want the enemy using it.”

Tristan regarded him silently, something unreadable behind his eyes.

Then he nodded.

Without a word, he extended a hand and pulled Luke to his feet, brushing dust from his shoulders.

“Let’s get out of here,” he muttered, giving the church one last glance. “This place gives me the creeps.”

“Mmm.”

As Luke turned, his gaze lingered on the statue near the altar.

A serpent, coiled around a sword—its head raised in regal defiance.

And just for a moment…

Its eyes glinted blue.

Faint. Almost imperceptible. But enough to make Luke’s skin crawl.

“Destroy this place,” he said quietly, not taking his eyes off the statue. “But don’t touch that thing.”

He nodded toward the idol. “Make sure it ends up buried beneath the rubble.”

He didn’t know what connection the Bishop had to these statues, but he was sure that the divine realm confrontation was related to it. If Tristan so much as touched that thing and got cursed or poisoned...

Luke didn’t want to think about it.

Tristan gave him a curious look, but didn’t argue.

“I’ll torch it after we’ve eaten. Should keep the lads warm.”

Together, they walked down the worn path leading back to the bridge—where ten thousand men still crossed in careful order.

Seeing the slow progression only confirmed what Tristan had said.

He’d barely been gone for more than a second.

Instead of easing his nerves, the thought made Luke even more uneasy.

I need to stop walking into churches, he thought grimly. I can't afford to get caught off guard again.

Things had gone his way this time—but only barely.

If he hadn’t unlocked the Sovereign’s Blessing back in Clayton City…

I’d have been dead ten times over.

He let out a tired breath, remembering the venomous presence of the Bishop.

Then his eyes flicked to the still-flashing notification window in front of him.

He opened it.

[New Quest received]

Luke sighed.
