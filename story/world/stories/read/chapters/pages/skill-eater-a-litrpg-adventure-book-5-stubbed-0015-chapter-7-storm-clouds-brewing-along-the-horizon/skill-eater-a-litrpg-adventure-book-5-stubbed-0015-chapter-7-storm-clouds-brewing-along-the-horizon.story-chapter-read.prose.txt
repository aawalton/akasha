Edge wound up sleeping straight through dinner.

He woke up in the middle of the night, staring at the ceiling while reflecting on the monumental events he’d experienced while exploring the Haunted Vale. He savored the fact that his friends were safe while pondering everything he had learned about Skill-Eater’s past, the tragic fate that had befallen the twenty-one Lords of Ord, and the Ancients’ plans for conquest.

While experiencing her memories had been incredibly intense, they had provided clarity into several mysteries that were plaguing him for months. In addition to shedding some light on the planet’s lost history, Edge finally understood why Skill-Eater had manifested as a benevolent eldritch being and an insatiable monster that wanted to take over his body and turn him into a killing machine capable of consuming every skill on the planet.

Although the Ancient’s mind was no longer entwined with her own and the monster was subdued by the chains of oblivion, devouring Boundless-Well and being tainted by the passenger had fundamentally changed Skill-Thief’s nature, transforming her into the ravenous lord. Even if we find a way to purge the monster from my core, she won’t go back to the person she used to be, and the same is true for me.

His dark passenger frightened him, and they hadn’t seen the last of the Ancient that had possessed Boundless-Well, Skill-Eater, and Edge himself. However, there was a silver lining. Skill-Eater hadn’t betrayed him after all. The lord intended to honor their pact and grower stronger alongside him, rather than use him as disposable vessel to fuel her advancement.

Furthermore, his assumption that the lord had been keeping him in the dark to manipulate him wasn’t true either. Skill-Eater hadn’t revealed the truth because her memories were fragmented, sealed away when she was reduced to a core and locked in a reliquary for centuries on end. He had a sneaking suspicion that she hadn’t told him everything, but now he knew her intentions were good.

In the end, for all their differences, they had a great deal in common too. Both of them had been shaped by tragic loss and a desire to escape painful recollections. They were learning to face the past, treasure the memories of the people they loved, and carry them into the future.

When slumber claimed him once more, Edge’s dreams were filled with alien wonders. Of traveling from planet to planet and scaling the peaks of power to heights beyond anything he had imagined possible.

It was a phenomenon that would persist for many restless nights as his subconscious integrated bits and pieces of memories that spanned millennia—the thoughts, experiences, and emotions of a magical creature whose mind worked so differently from his own.

***

Cassius watched in silence as Salandra wove a path between the fresh corpses littering the blood-soaked soil. The lieutenant was careful not to disturb his captain’s concentration as she picked out the perfect angle before planting her easel and placing the first brushstroke on her latest work.

Before her, a prisoner was trapped with a serpent’s coils, frozen in terror as the creature’s mouth opened wide to swallow him whole. While the body was reptilian, the head was human, an elderly woman with cruel eyes and a predatory smile.

The creature’s visage was horrifying, but it was neither monster nor beast. It was a nightmare dredged up from the depths of the man’s subconscious and manifested by the Artist’s will. It was the same fate that had befallen her other victims, but for some reason, this moment had captured Salandra’s interest long enough to place the man in stasis and render it in pigment.

The lethal young woman had descended upon the jailbird’s encampment earlier in the day, slaughtering the convicts who had the misfortune of residing too close to the staging grounds the Wardens were establishing in preparation of assaulting the Coalition’s stronghold. They still hadn’t located the faction’s base of operations in the Lakelands, but they were sweeping the region sub-biome by sub-biome, and it was only a matter of time before the smoldering conflict flared into the fires of war.

Once the Wardens cornered their prey and confirmed that King, Corpse, and Shield were in residence, the captains would launch a full-fledged assault and the blood staining this battle-scarred prairie would look like a puddle beside the ocean.

That being said, even with three captains, their senior staff, and the might of the third division at their disposal, Cassius knew victory would come at a significant cost. While the trio of scions and the elite unit known as the Problem-Resolution Squadron only represented a fraction of the Coalition’s strength, the powers granted by their Unique cores were far more troublesome than a conventional army. If any of them managed to evolve before the first blow was struck, the Wardens might incur losses that could threaten their control of the region.

There’s no chance that we can recruit the Wardens’ sworn enemies, which means we will slaughter the scions, extract their cores, and seal them until compatible bearers are located who are amiable to our cause.

Unfortunatly, King’s people had gone to ground mere hours before the Wardens arrived in World’s End, tipped off by one informant or another. Their subsequent show of force had failed to ferret out the Coalition, although they had seized a considerable pile of assets and decimated the faction’s operations within the free city.

Chaos threatened to consume the planet in the aftermath of the anomaly, but Cassius had to admit that he was looking forward to the battle to come. Salandra the Artist, Sand Fist Gin-Bei, and Karos the Walking Swarm were living legends for a reason. While he found some of their methods distasteful, he had never had a chance to witness what the captains were truly capable of, and it had been quite a while since he’d had an opportunity to spread his wings and unleash the full powers at his command.

I wouldn’t shed tears if Karos perished in the struggle. I don’t trust them and the Wardens would be better off without them.

Cassius wasn’t nearly as bloodthirsty as most of his colleagues, but he had sworn a life-debt to General Strieger and was looking forward to repaying the man’s kindness by laying his enemies low. This war might drag on for months or even years if Ord remained on lockdown, but the outcome was clear. By the time the last shot was fired, everyone would know why the Wardens were feared throughout the galaxy, and the fate of the Coalition would become a cautionary tale shared in fearful whispers for centuries to come.

Shield walked beside King as the men made their way through the bowels of the Coalition’s stronghold.

Each scion was lost in thoughts of their own. King was contemplating the trap he was laying for the Wardens. Shield was considering the man he’d been hoping to recruit ever since Edge bored a hole in the kaiju’s skull mere feet from Puppet Town’s gate.

After examining the warrior during a chance encounter on the silver ring, Shield was confident that Edge Vasher was a scion of the twenty-one, although he wouldn’t be certain until he sensed the man activate an ultimate ability. Regardless of whether Edge or the black-haired woman had discovered Corpse’s message, he was incredibly powerful for his stage, and the Coalition would be well-served by adding him to their ranks.

The negotiations would be tricky thanks to Corpse’s fascination with raising the dead, augmenting them, and pitting her experiments against anyone as unfortunate enough to catch her eye. At least the kaiju hadn’t destroyed their settlement, and the resources Puppet Town had collected from wiping out the squid and its stampede were worth a considerable fortune.

With any luck, Edge’s people would be willing to look past the little psychopath’s indiscretion and realize that allying with the Coalition was in everyone’s best interests. A former starter settlement of that size had no hope of surviving without assistance, and his faction could offer benefits they could only dream of otherwise—direct protection going forward, along with all the supplies, devices, and manpower they needed to endure being bordered by extreme-threat biomes.

Either way, it’s King’s headache now. He’s a master of manipulation, and I’m sure that some combination of bribery, flattery, misdirection, and threats will seal the deal. Puppet Town isn’t in a position to say no, particularly when considering the consequences of making an enemy of the Coalition when they are in desperately need of allies.

I hope the Wardens don’t find them first, or us, for that matter. We have plans for dealing with Salandra the Artist, but King didn’t expect to find Gin-Bei and Karos in the Lakelands too. If they discover our stronghold before our preparations are complete, we will have to disperse and head deep into the frontier, which will put a massive crimp in our operations over the short term.

I’m glad he convinced Corpse to stay put and play with her toys. She doesn’t have the temperament for subtly and it would be a disaster if she ran into the Wardens while hunting in World’s End. When this is over, I’m setting out on my own for the foreseeable future. I’ll keep looking for unaffiliated scions and unbound Unique cores—maybe try to pin down the reliquary we know the Natives have squirreled away in one of their hidden cities.

While Shield pondered the matter, he followed King past the vault where their secret weapon was stored. Once they found someone capable of wielding it, the Coalition could drive the Wardens out of the Lakelands for good.

One way or another, the war for the future of the Prison World entertainment empire was about to enter a deadly new stage, with fortunes beyond his wildest dreams hanging in the balance.
