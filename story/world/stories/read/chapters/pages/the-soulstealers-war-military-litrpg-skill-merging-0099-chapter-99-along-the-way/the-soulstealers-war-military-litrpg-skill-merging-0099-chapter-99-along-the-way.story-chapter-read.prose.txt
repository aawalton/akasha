I gathered up my squad and informed them of our mission. Brawl and Anchor scurried off immediately, rushing to get their equipment, but Sage grimaced. Morph didn’t move, even though he was in his humanoid form, holding her with one arm.

“Is something wrong?” I whispered.

“The Warlord sent a messenger? Personally?”

“That’s what Lord Ceonwin said.”

“That’s not a good sign.”

I nodded. “He seemed to think so, too. But he never explained.”

“My best guess? It means the Warlord heard about what happened to his Portreeve after the battle of Oschester. And it means that he isn’t pleased with what Lord Ceonwin did.”

“The Portreeve was a traitor,” I said.

“Yes. And he was affiliated with the Warlord. There will be aftershocks of some kind. Specifically, I cannot say. But do not underestimate the pettiness of nobility.”

I nodded. “Well, we can’t worry about that until it happens, then. Get yourself ready and meet us at the gate in fifteen minutes. About.”

People here didn’t have an innate grasp of how long ten minutes or fifteen was, I was realizing. They knew what a minute was, of course, and there were a few small sundials scattered around, but keeping time was a challenge, and they just weren’t as used to strict timing as I was. I figured my squad simply just decided to move as fast as they could when I mentioned minutes, so they wouldn’t be late.

I rushed back to the barracks myself. First, I took the riftrocks out of my kit bag. We didn’t need to burden the horses with them. Then, I checked my reading slate. My attributes now read:

Vitality: 15

Agility: 12(+4)

Strength: 16

Perception: 7(+1)

Focus: 21

Presence: 18

Nothing had changed with my information at the top of the slate, and I was still overall ranked third. Were Habit and Yesman more powerful? I still hadn’t beat Yesman in a one-on-one spar yet, and with Habit, I hadn’t had a chance.

At the very bottom of the slate, I checked my Skills:

Skills: Spearmanship (Apprentice), Archery (Apprentice), Art: Directional Gravity Manipulation (Journeyman), Commanding Presence (Apprentice), Horseback Riding (Novice), General System Knowledge (Novice), Shield Use (Apprentice), Shifting Weight (Apprentice), Light as a Feather (Novice), Skirmishing (Novice), Crossbow Charging (Novice), Swapping Weapons (Novice)

It was probably the longest I’d ever seen the list. After this mission, I had to start merging them to remove some of them. Or during—I just had to find somewhere safe and enough time to spare.

Once we had our equipment, we set off, trotting north on horseback. Although my knowledge of the surrounding area was getting better, I hadn’t been north nearly as far. But judging by the maps we had, it was about a two days’ ride if we pushed ourselves and our horses.

As we rode, I asked, “Any chance we’re able to make our horses better?”

“He doesn’t know?” Sage asked.

I sighed. “Alright then, apparently I missed something.”

“Could be a product of you being late to the entrance examination and all,” Anchor provided. “They told us about it before.”

“But what did I actually miss?” I asked, curiosity gnawing at me. “And why was there no repetition of it?”

“Burr was given to you by the sappers, right, brother?” Brawl asked.

“Yeah?”

“They are arcane beasts,” Sage answered. “At the moment, they’re very low-tier. Barely even Coppers. But all the sappers’ horses are from arcane beast bloodlines.”

“Huh,” I said. “So…how do we make our horses stronger?”

“It takes time,” Anchor said. “Which is likely why no one mentioned it. You must simply ride your mount for a year or two, growing accustomed to its company, and eventually, you will be able to communicate with it. Eventually, and given a touch of maegen, it will grow a stronger mind, and it will be able to advance similarly to other Path-walkers.”

I nodded slowly. “So talking with your horse is a good idea.”

“Not necessarily talking with it,” Sage said.

“It sounds like it would help,” I replied. “Does that mean they have to do…I dunno what you guys did at Copper and Iron, actually. But somehow a horse has to figure that out?”

“Thankfully not,” Sage replied. “Arcane beasts advance simply by accumulating maegen, slowly improving all of their attributes. Once they’re ready and have chosen their favoured rider, they’ll slowly start improving. You’ll see them running faster, and for longer distances. Their endurance will improve, and they won’t be so prone to overheating.”

“Huh,” I said. “So that means we should be spending more time with our horses, right? And you should get a horse of your own.”

Sage shrugged. “A horse of my own will be inconsequential. Morph advances along with me, and eventually, he will be stronger than a horse, and just as fast.”

“I’m hearing a lot of ‘eventually’ and maybe.”

“I can’t ride a horse right now, dustbrain. By the time that I can ride a horse, Morph will take that role.”

“And it is best to get started on a long road now, instead of in a few months, brother,” Brawl added.

For a few seconds, I wondered if that was actually Brawl who said that, but it made sense. But Brawl was more than capable of making forward-thinking conclusions—perhaps better than some others in our training program.

“Yeah. I’ll get working on it,” I said. I leaned forward and patted Burr’s mane. “Right, Burr?”

She tossed her head and whinnied.

“That’s what I thought.”

“You know you don’t have to talk to them,” Anchor said.

“I wanted to, though. It’s gotta help.”

“Atoning…” Anchor muttered.

“It’s just another upgrade to look forward to, then.”

Since we left halfway through the day, we had to stop halfway through the night. There was no town, so we camped on the side of the road. I kept the first watch, and when the others slept, I used my buglight to review the maps. Assuming the squads from the 294th were pinned down where they said they were, we were on the right track.

Then I let the light go dim and kept watch. As it turned out, Welkinmere had phases, just like the moon. Or…we were technically on its moon, weren’t we? I wasn’t sure if anyone else knew, but it made more sense to me, given its size. There was no way we were orbiting around it.

But even with my limited knowledge of astronomy, I had heard that the Earth would appear to go through phases from the moon, just like Welkinmere. Tonight, it was only a tiny sliver of purple storms in the night sky, and it was far darker out than I was used to. I had gotten used to the ‘moon’ effectively being five times brighter.

With Welkinmere going dark, though, it made it much easier to see the stars. I didn’t recognize any of them, and there were far more of them than I was used to seeing back at home, almost like the pictures of Earth’s southern hemisphere night sky.

When it was my turn to sleep, I instead took the opportunity to merge my Skills. It was the last chance I’d get, and I needed to be at my best when I went up against whatever had the 294th trapped.

So I drew myself into my nodespace, envisioned all the Skills as orbs, then sorted them around the space. I wasn’t sure what I could do today with what I had, but it was like an idea. I found I could develop them better if I just wrote them down and got started.

The Skills I wasn’t going to touch all went to one side. [Spearmanship] and my gravity Art.

Then, I sorted out the Skills necessary to upgrade [Archery]. Of course, the general archery Skill. Then I took [Crossbow Charging] and [Swapping Weapons]. Since the other skills that related to Archery, or that I could use as fodder, weren’t Apprentice tier yet, I considered my options.

To enhance my Art, I’d need more Journeyman tier skills. I could always merge the Skills that would work better with gravity manipulation, like [Shield Use], as fodder for upgrading my [Archery] skill to Journeyman tier. But that would mean that I had to later merge [Archery] with my main Art.

Sure, there were ways that [Archery] and gravity manipulation could synergize, but it seemed like a bit of a waste. I identified three core skills I wanted to work on: Spearmanship, my gravity Art, and archery. Perhaps three was too ambitious, given most sappers just focused on two, but I figured I could do it. And that meant the core skills should stay isolated from each other. The synergy could come from me choosing to use them differently in battle, not from how they interacted with each other.

So I instead decided to lay the groundwork for a gravity Art Skill merge. I sifted through what I had. [Commanding Presence], [Shield Use], and [Shifting Weight] were all Apprentice tier. [Light as a Feather] and [Horseback Riding] were great for that purpose as well.

I gathered them all up, then picked the main Skill. Shifting Weight and Shield Use formed the base—they were perfect for feeling my weight, adjusting my weight. Then I added in Commanding Presence, and finally, fed in the last two. Light as a Feather, again, for feeling my shifting weight. Then Horseback Riding—I had to make plenty of adjustments for how I sat in the saddle, for example, and it had yet again more knowledge of shifting weight and adjusting my stance.

They formed into a more concentrated knowledge skill, [The Weight of Things | Journeyman].

It wasn’t an Art, and it wouldn’t function as a resonance Skill, but it was a step in the right direction. I needed one more Journeyman-tier Skill (and one that I could afford to give up) before I could upgrade my Art, and the sooner, the better.
