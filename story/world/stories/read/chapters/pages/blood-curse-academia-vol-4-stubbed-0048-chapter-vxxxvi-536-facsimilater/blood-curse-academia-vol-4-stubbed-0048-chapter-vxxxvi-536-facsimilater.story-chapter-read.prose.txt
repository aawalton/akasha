Chapter V.XXXVI (5.36) - Facsimilater

“You honestly think this design will work better than the last one?” Kizu asked Basil. He held up the poster and examined it. It was the full-sized image of an academy student. Only, she wore a… modified uniform. Several narrow slits and a plunging neckline made it stand out compared to the standard issued uniform.

Basil reached across the couch to set a hand on his shoulder. “Trust me! I designed the clothing and Aoi took care of the image. It’s a masterpiece, executed exactly as I envisioned. This girl here, she’s the answer to all our problems. I promise she’ll help people take notice of the water fountains. Your current signs are trash when placed in a competition with this.”

“If you say so.” Kizu gingerly placed the poster back on the couch cushion next to him.

In the last Student Council meeting Sene had gone off on a tirade about how students were ignoring the current signs. Water fountain usage had only gone up a measly 27 percent in the last month. An unacceptable improvement. Sene saw this as a personal failure and was passionate about increasing that percentage. And so, Kizu had gone to Basil in hopes of coming up with a strategy.

Basil’s solution had been simple. Pretty girls. Unsurprising, as that seemed to be the answer for half the questions in the shapeshifter’s life.

As a result, they were lounging in their dorm common room with the poster cutout on the couch next to them. Kizu sat cross-legged on the couch, hunched over his scrying orb as he tried to locate the academy’s copying artifact. It hadn’t been in its usual place in the supply room, apparently another student had taken it out for a class project.

“I think I finally found it,” Kizu said, examining the information on his scrying orb. It had been a pain to get the orb to understand exactly what he wanted from it since he didn’t know the artifact’s actual name, but all the academy resources had a public record. “It’s called a facsimilater. And it looks like the last student to request it was… Cybelle Alder.” Kizu paused and frowned. He thought he recognized that name from somewhere. It took him a minute of consideration before it finally clicked. “I think… she might be one of Emilia’s friends. One of the ones that follow her everywhere.”

Basil smirked and raised an eyebrow. “Emilia? As in your ex?”

“She’s the only Emilia on campus.” Kizu sighed and rested his head on the sofa back. “Can I talk you into retrieving it from her? You know Cybelle, right? We’re you always flirting with her?”

“Nah, that was one of the other girls. Ichika.” Basil raised his poster up and admired it. “This is Student Council Business, nothing to do with me. However, I’ll offer my services as moral support backup while you ask Cybelle for the artifact.”

Kizu grumbled.

“Damn,” a student whispered.

Kizu rolled his head over and saw two other third-year boys staring. Not as him, as he was accustomed to when visiting the common room, but instead their eyes were locked on the poster in Basil’s hands. At least this proved that the image was eye-catching. However, Kizu also wasn’t keen on having this particular poster associated with him. Best to get rid of the thing as quickly as possible.

“Let’s go find Cybelle.” Kizu got to his feet. “Safe to assume you know where her dorm is at? We can go knock and see if she’s there.”

“Of course.” Basil blocked the line of sight of the gawking boys and winked at them. They blinked, finally broken from their stasis, then swiftly departed to their rooms.

Kizu followed Basil out of the dorm and down the hallway. With every step, his heart beat just a bit faster. He truly did not want to do this right now. Or ever. He’d done his best to avoid Emilia’s gang as much as possible these last months, ever since she’d decided to try asking him out again.

The third-year girls’ dormitory was a painting, like their own, though where the boys’ had an image of a common room within a high tower above the clouds, the girls’ showed an oasis camp in the center of a desert. There were large fabric barriers overhead, offering the plush cushioned seating shade next to the pool of water. Over to the side was a white stone wall with a doorway, likely leading to the individual dorm rooms. Throughout the painting, several tall palm trees reached up towards the brilliant blue sky. It was a nice painting and seemed like a great place to live.

Kizu felt along the painting’s frame. “How do we knock? Is there a button or—”

“Ahm,” Basil cut-in. “Are you forgetting who you’re here with?”

Kizu turned and realized his friend had silently gone through a transformation, a girl with short cropped hair and dimples now stood in his friend’s place. Basil flipped his hair and stretched his fingers out, admiring his own smooth skin and clean fingernails.

“This is giving me deja vu,” Kizu muttered. He stepped aside and gestured for Basil to continue into the dormitory. “Go ahead and fetch her.”

Basil strode past him. The shapeshifter had lost a bit of height in the transformation and his pant legs now draped over his shoes. A glance of irritation at his feet showed that Basil had certainly noticed and that it bothered him. Moments later though, Basil passed through the painting and disappeared from sight.

Left to wait in the hallway, Kizu pondered what he’d say to his ex’s friends. Should he skip the pleasantries and just get straight to requesting the artifact? That was probably the best option since it cut through any potential misunderstandings; however, it might be rude and upset the girl, causing further delays. He never thought Emilia as a particularly petty person, but his interactions with her friends had been very limited and he didn’t know them well at all. They might be extremely defensive of Emilia and ornery.

Basil stumbled out of the painting, breaking Kizu from his musings.

“That was fast. What did she—”

“Ah!” Basil grabbed Kizu and turned him around to face away from the painting. “Bad time, right now. We should try sending her a message and having her give us that artifact at the cafeteria. Oh! Or, better yet, why don’t we simply wait a few days? Fountains aren’t actually all that pressing, right?”

“We should… what?” Kizu stared, slackjawed at his addled friend. “What did you see in there?”

“Just, um, girl stuff!”

Kizu fumbled in his spatial ring, he made a face, then downed a gray liquid. As his body shifted, he made a face. He hated the taste of this stuff, but it should make his gender ambiguous for a few minutes. Good enough to bypass the dormitory protections. He only intended to stick his head in for a second.

“Don’t do tha—” Basil reached for him. His voice cut off as Kizu stepped through the painting.

The oasis dormitory appeared exactly as depicted on the exterior painting. Nothing was missing, the plush pillows, the shade, the pool. That was all there. Only, there were several additions to the layout.

Two girls his age were laughing and cackling as small origami golems stumbled about, bonking into things. Kizu’s brow scrunched up as he stared at the paper creations. Half of the golems looked humanoid and they were chasing random little animal origamis.

One of the paper animals bumped into his foot. It looked like a weird bear.

Kizu bent down and picked it up. His frown deepened as he noticed an image printed on its back. A familiar small painting of a two-headed bear. He immediately recognized Aoi’s art style. There were words and numbers scrawled on the paper.

“This is one of Ione’s bestiary cards,” he realized. His eyes widened and he looked again at the origami animals. One was in the shape of a kappa, another a big sea lion, another a tortoise. These were all her different summons. It was a menagerie of paper. For a moment, he thought maybe Ione had created these for some reason. After all, she was a third-year and this was the third-year dormitory. That thought was quickly dismissed as idiotic. She’d have to ask someone else to enchant paper golems and just getting her to agree to speak to Aoi about helping her with the card art had taken weeks.

Paralyzed by the mystery of what was going on, Kizu watched with horror as one of the little humanoid paper golems bumped into the tortoise origami. It pressed its paper body up against the paper reptile. Rhythmically. It was humping the other golem. The tortoise was struggling to get away from the paper person. As it squirmed free, the person’s face came into view.

Ione’s face.
