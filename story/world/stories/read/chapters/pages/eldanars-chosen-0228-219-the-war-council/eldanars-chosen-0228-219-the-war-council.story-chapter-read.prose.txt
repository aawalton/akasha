The suffocating silence that followed Caistina’s announcement lasted for only a single, terrifying heartbeat. Then, the Ashenfall Guildhall completely erupted.

The low, frantic murmur of the conscripted militia instantly boiled over into a deafening roar of sheer panic. Bakers dropped their rusted broadswords. Young merchants pushed against the heavy wooden tables, their eyes wide with the sudden, undeniable realisation that they were likely going to die before the sun began to set. The hoarse shouts of the city guards desperately trying to maintain order were entirely drowned out by the rising tide of absolute terror.

Caistina did not shout to be heard over the din. She simply slammed the base of her pristine white staff against the stone floor. A concussive, rippling shockwave of pure, authoritative magical energy washed over the massive room. It carried no physical force, but it struck the minds of everyone present with a cold, sobering clarity, instantly silencing the screaming crowd.

"Panic is a luxury we can no longer afford," Caistina announced, her melodic voice magically amplified to reach every corner of the grand hall. Her sapphire-blue robes practically vibrated with latent power. "If you surrender to fear now, you are already dead. We have hours to prepare a welcome for this horde, and we will use every single second."

She turned her amethyst eyes away from the crowd, focusing entirely on the small, hastily cleared circle around the administrative desks.

Josh, Brett, and Carcan stepped closer, forming a tight war council with the elven mage, Rochelle, and the highly stressed guard captain.

"Captain Aris," Caistina began, addressing the armoured human. "We need to finalise the defensive grid immediately. The original timeline is completely useless. What is the current status of the physical barricades?"

Captain Aris wiped a thick layer of cold sweat from his pale forehead, tracing a heavy, gauntleted finger across a large parchment map of the city rolled out on the desk.

"The stonemasons and the labourers have been working since midnight," Aris reported, his voice tight but professional. "We have already begun constructing a secondary, semi-circular stone wall directly behind the northern gate. It is crude, mostly rubble and packed earth, but it will create a brutal bottleneck if the main gates shatter."

"And the residential streets?" Josh asked, leaning his massive, steel-clad frame over the map.

"We are clogging them," Aris confirmed, nodding to the human Guardian. "Militia squads are currently dragging heavy wooden merchant carts, overturned furniture, and iron anvils into the main thoroughfares. We are building secondary and tertiary chokepoints. If the undead flood the streets, they will have to fight for every single inch of cobblestone, but those chokepoints will be manned primarily by the civilian militia and lower-level adventurers. They will not hold against anything big."

Caistina looked at Brett, her eyes dropping to the shimmering crimson Ember-silk cloak wrapped around the mage's shoulders. She had read the horrific reports of the sinkhole battle, and she knew exactly what the young scholar was capable of unleashing.

"Then our primary objective is to ensure the heavy assets never breach the walls," Caistina stated firmly. "Brett, I need you and the highest-tier elemental mages stationed directly on the upper wooden ramparts, specifically over the main gatehouse. You will be flanked by anyone who can throw magic, draw a bow or effectively throw a rock.”

Brett pushed his imaginary spectacles up his nose, his pale face hardening into a mask of pure concentration. "You want us to establish a kill zone."

"Precisely." Caistina nodded. "The city guard has already dug deep trenches in front of the timber walls and filled them with highly volatile alchemical pitch. You will ignite those trenches when the vanguard approaches. Your primary targets are the Crypt Weavers and the Bone Goliaths. If you see a siege ram, you melt it. We need your fire to break their momentum."

"But they will have hundreds, if not thousands of lesser infantry," Brett pointed out, tracing the defensive line on the map. "If they simply stack their own dead against the timber walls and create a ramp of rotting flesh, ranged superiority will not mean anything. They will just walk right over the parapets eventually."

"That is exactly why the ramparts will not be exclusively ranged," Captain Aris interjected grimly. "We are mixing fighters, veteran guardsmen, and spearmen directly onto the walls alongside the casters. When the undead reach the top of the timber, it will devolve into a brutal melee. The fighters on the wall will be tasked with repelling boarders and keeping the monsters away from the mages."

Josh studied the map intently. His mind, honed by months of brutal dungeon diving, analysed the choke points and the elevated positions. He understood the necessity of protecting the casters, but he also knew where his specific defensive skill set would be required the most.

"I will take the wall with Brett," Josh announced, his storm-grey eyes meeting the captain's gaze. "But I need to be stationed on the parapet directly above the main iron portcullis."

Aris frowned slightly, his scarred brow furrowing. "That is the absolute epicentre of the danger zone, Guardian. If a Bone Goliath hits the gate, the shockwave will likely shatter the wooden platform you are standing on."

"That is exactly why I need to be there," Josh countered, tapping a heavy steel gauntlet against the map. "If the main gate buckles, or if the secondary rubble wall looks like it is going to fail, I can jump directly down into the breach. I can trigger my defensive anchor in the archway and plug the gap to buy your militia time to fall back. If I am stationed on the far flanks, I will never reach the gate in time."

Caistina looked at the towering human, her amethyst eyes filled with profound, quiet respect. She remembered the sheer, immovable willpower he had displayed during the kobold invasion.

"The portcullis is yours, Josh," Caistina agreed softly.

"What about the civilians?" Rochelle asked suddenly, her sharp voice cutting through the tactical planning. The head clerk had her thick leather ledger open, her pen hovering over the page. "The city is currently overflowing with tens of thousands of refugees. We have women, children, and the elderly packed into the temple squares and the market districts. Are we initiating an evacuation order out of the southern gates?"

A heavy, fraught silence descended upon the small council.

It was the most horrific, desperate question of the morning, and everyone standing around the table already knew the terrible, uncompromising answer.

Captain Aris looked down at his mailed fists, his jaw clenched tightly. "An evacuation is a death sentence, Rochelle. You saw the young scout's condition. The dark corruption is spreading miles ahead of the main horde. The open plains to the south are completely exposed, freezing, and undoubtedly swarming with flanking packs of skeletal hounds and Crypt Weavers."

"He is right," Caistina added, closing her eyes for a brief, pained moment. "We cannot spare a single fighter to escort a civilian caravan. Every capable sword is needed on the walls. Furthermore, the nearest fortified city is a multi-day march away, even for someone fit and healthy. A slow-moving line of terrified refugees on the open road would be slaughtered before nightfall."

Rochelle slowly lowered her pen, her stern face tightening with severe sorrow. She looked out over the crowded, noisy hall, looking at the terrified faces of the bakers and blacksmiths who were currently strapping on boiled leather armour.

"Then they are trapped," Rochelle whispered, stating the grim reality aloud.

"They shelter in place," Aris commanded, forcing the emotion completely out of his voice. "The town criers will instruct all non-combatants to retreat into the deepest stone cellars of the city. They are to barricade the doors, remain entirely silent, and wait for the siege to end."

He looked around the table, meeting the eyes of the veteran adventurers. "If the walls fall, and the streets are overrun, you fight to the bitter end. You do not surrender a single inch of cobblestone unless you are entirely dead. We are the absolute final shield they have."

The raw, terrifying weight of the responsibility settled heavily onto Josh's broad shoulders. He looked at Brett and Carcan. They both looked incredibly pale, but their expressions were locked into masks of unyielding determination. They had run from the sinkhole, and they had retreated from the forward camp, but they were not going to run from Ashenfall.

"The tactical layout is set," Caistina announced, raising her staff once more. "Captain Aris, begin deploying your squads to the secondary barricades. Rochelle, inform the quartermasters to distribute the remaining health potions and arrows to the wall defenders."

The elven mage turned a brilliant, reassuring smile toward the three adventurers. "We have roughly three hours until dawn. The preparations on the wall are well underway. Go and prepare your minds, and ensure your gear is perfectly secured. You will hear the final horn blast when they start to cross the tree line."

The war council broke apart, the orders rippling quickly outward through the chaotic, crowded Guildhall.

Josh stepped away from the administrative desk, rolling his aching shoulders beneath his thick padded gambeson. He was carrying a massive amount of upgraded steel, and he needed to strap it on correctly.

"We have time," Josh muttered to Brett and Carcan, guiding them toward the heavy oak stairs leading to the upper dormitories. "I need to get my full plate armour on, and I need one of you to help me secure the leather buckles. A loose strap on the wall could get me killed."

"I will help you secure the plates," Brett agreed, pulling his crimson cloak tighter. "And frankly, I need to splash some freezing water on my face. The reality of fighting ten thousand zombies is making me slightly nauseous."

Carcan walked silently beside them, her amber-topped staff tapping rhythmically against the wooden floorboards. The usual battle persona she had displayed earlier had softened slightly, replaced by a quiet, focused contemplation. She was likely rationing her immense magical reserves, preparing her mind for the horrific triage she would soon have to perform in the temple square.

They reached the bottom of the winding staircase, the noise of the crowded militia muster point fading slightly as they prepared to ascend to the quiet of the upper corridors.

Just as Josh placed his steel boot on the first wooden step, a deep, incredibly gruff voice echoed loudly from the shadows near the stairwell.

"And exactly where do you three think you are going without me?"

Josh froze. He spun around, his storm-grey eyes widening in shock. Standing near the base of the stairs, leaning heavily against the stone wall of the Guildhall, was Bhel.

The dwarven fighter looked like a horrific casualty of war who had inexplicably escaped the morgue. He was heavily wrapped in thick, pristine white medical bandages from his waist to his neck, completely covering the terrible wound where the black sludge had nearly liquefied his chest. He was wearing his heavy, mud-stained iron boots and his thick leather trousers, but he lacked his upper armour completely.

Despite his pale, greyish skin and the clear, wincing pain evident in his dark eyes, the dwarf was standing upright, his thick arms crossed defiantly over his bandaged chest.

Carcan gasped, her serene composure shattering instantly. The terrifying, scolding healer persona violently re-emerged.

"Bheldur Giantguard!" Carcan shrieked, her melodic voice completely losing its usual calm cadence. She marched directly towards the dwarf, her silver eyes blazing with fury. "You are supposed to be heavily sedated in the temple infirmary! I specifically ordered the acolytes to keep you in bed! If you tear those internal stitches before the battle even begins, I will..."

Bhel simply waved a thick, calloused hand, entirely dismissing her furious medical rant.

"The acolytes were thoroughly unhelpful, so I politely requested they look the other way while I left," Bhel grunted, offering a massive, stubborn grin that crinkled the edges of his intricate, iron-clasped beard. "I am perfectly fine, Carcan. A little bit of dark magic is not enough to keep a mountain son on his back."

He pushed himself off the stone wall, wincing slightly as his damaged ribs protested the movement. He took a heavy step toward the stunned trio.

"I heard the warning bells ringing from the temple," Bhel explained, his booming voice dropping into a serious, heartfelt rumble. "I heard the town criers shouting about a horde of undead heading this way. You are marching into a slaughterhouse, and it sounds to me like every single available axe is going to be needed on those walls soon."

Josh looked at the stubborn dwarf, completely overwhelmed by a sudden, massive surge of profound affection for his friend. Bhel had nearly died holding the line for them just yesterday, yet here he was, heavily bandaged and barely able to stand, entirely ready to march right back into the teeth of the apocalypse beside them.

"You are an absolute idiot, Bhel," Josh laughed, the sound a mixture of deep relief and genuine joy, completely breaking the suffocating tension of the morning.

"I am a fighter," Bhel corrected proudly, pointing a thick finger at Josh. "Now, I distinctly remember requesting a new chest piece and some throwing axes from the artisan district before you left. Tell me you did not forget my gear."

"We got your axes, you stubborn rock," Brett grinned, stepping forward and clapping the dwarf gently on his uninjured shoulder. "And Helga managed to hammer your iron plates back into something resembling armour."

Carcan let out a long, defeated sigh, her furious posture completely melting away. She looked at the dwarf, a fond, exasperated smile touching her lips. She knew there was absolutely no force in Eldanar capable of keeping Bhel out of this fight.

"Just... do not swing too hard with your right arm," Carcan pleaded softly, shaking her head.

"I make no promises," Bhel chuckled warmly. "Now, lead the way upstairs. I need someone to help me strap this iron over these ridiculous bandages."

Laughing quietly, the four friends turned and began the slow climb up the heavy oak stairs. The horrific, looming threat of the undead horde was still marching relentlessly toward them, but for a brief, beautiful moment in the shadowed stairwell, they were simply a complete party again, ready to face the end of the world together.
