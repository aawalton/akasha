Xyphon’s. I am not making the same mistake again. A slur is a slur, thought Ciro, as he ran through the maze of trees.

It was far too easy. With each bow he took, he quickly gorged out an eye. He was stupid enough to take out a handful in one go. Yet Kaisu’s men were not looking at him. Their interest faded in him as soon as he dropped to the ground. Who needs things like pride and honor when you’re trying to survive?

Ciro smiled, elated at his success. The black glassy eyeballs felt like marbles in his hand, as they rolled against each in his sweaty palms. They popped out with surprising ease, no flow of blood or mess.

His sweat glistened under the filtered canopy light carried away by the force of his running. Ciro ignored the pain in his shoulder. If he dwindled for only a second, he’d be caught.

His foot snagged on a root, and for a sickening moment, the world flipped sideways. His shoulder hit the dirt first, pain shooting down his arm as he tumbled into a shallow ravine. Mud smeared his face, but he scrambled to his feet, his fingers clawing at the moss-slick rocks as he hauled himself forward.

The Xyphons were close — too close.

Their guttural, clicking calls echoed through the trees, bouncing off trunks and leaves, making it impossible to tell how many were hunting him. He could almost feel the heat of their eyes on his back, predatory and cold. The bushes rustled with their movements.

The air ahead was cooler.

The sound reached him first: rushing water, cutting through the chaotic drumbeat of his footsteps and his pulse. A river. His mind latched onto multiple half-formed plans, flickering between panic and desperation.

The Xyphons’ calls grew sharper behind him, closer now. He broke through the treeline, nearly stumbling as the ground gave way to a muddy slope, the river twisting below him. The water churned, flecks of foam swirling where rocks broke the surface.

Without thinking, without hesitating, Ciro dove.

He held his breath under the murky water. The Xyphons had emerged through the trees, Ciro could hear them. He floated. Still like a statue. He could hear the screams of their leader while her words were muffled. To his terror, he could still understand her words.

“You three! Fire your spears at the river, right where I’m pointing.”

Did she know he was there? Could she sense him? Ciro could barely see more than a few feet ahead, the riverbed a shifting mess of silt, stones, and drifting strands of alien vegetation.

“What did I say! Hurry and fire!”

He fought the instinct to kick, to claw his way to the surface for air, instead he submitted to the will of the river. The current took him. Rolling once, twice, until his back scraped against a smooth stone, sending up a thick cloud of mud that obscured the faint light above.

The spears sliced through the water; they struck based on the splashes, but they were blind, the algae and mud blurred their vision. Each spear drove down into the depths, but hit nothing but rocks.

Ciro allowed himself to be carried further away by the current. He was desperate to breathe, but he closed his eyes and focused on being still. He drifted further downstream, his body blending into the shifting glow of the algae and the debris carried by the current. A half-rotted log tumbled past, trailing a ghostly plume of green light, and Ciro curled himself low, using it as cover. The Xyphons wouldn’t see him, just another piece of alien flotsam swallowed by the river.

His lungs burned, begging for air, but he knew breaking the surface now meant death. So he clung to the log, letting it shield him, letting the current pull him deeper into the wild.

The river carried him to safety.

Another second, and he would have suffocated. He pulled himself out of the water, finding himself in a clearing. There were plenty of trees above, but the land was open and free. Ciro trudged out, his suit wet, covered in moss and algae. He still had the six eyes in his hands and most importantly, he had survived.

The worry of the Xyphon’s finding him still clawed in his mind. He stayed away from the opening. Climbing up, he returned to being surrounded by the trees. The raised ground he stood on seemed to incline upwards. Ciro took a deep breath, realizing he had no energy to climb a hill.

He looked around for the biggest tree he could find. He found one that seemed similar to a fig tree, found in his own world. Ciro clambered on to it, not climbing as high as the last time. The trauma of the Krovalith finding him still weighed on him heavily. He lay on a thick branch, taking deep breaths. His fingertips trailed the wound he had received on his shoulder.

The blood had dried, but the pain hadn’t. Ciro, unsure of the time remaining to complete the event, felt restless. He couldn’t afford to wait around. Yet the wound on his shoulder acted as a constant reminder that he was weak. A human with no discernible qualities.

Alya had been given the ability to create barriers. Carina was given some high-tech weapon that allowed her to fly. All he could do was run and see what people’s names were. Ciro sighed. He was out of his depth. Then again, what’s changed? He thought to himself.
