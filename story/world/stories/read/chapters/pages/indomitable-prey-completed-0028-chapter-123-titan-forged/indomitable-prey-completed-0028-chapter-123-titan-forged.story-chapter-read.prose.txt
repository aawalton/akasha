[Lamb POV]

When I came to, Varice was striding my way, her raven once more perched on her shoulder. My head pounded and I felt as if each muscle in my body was shredded, a red-raw soreness encompassing every inch of skin. My back was pressed against one of the walls of the diamond-shaped room, and given the way my head was ringing, I could assume I’d been flung against it with ample force by her spell.

“I almost feel I owe you for helping me kill Estan—he was a vile and pitiful little man. Still, the duke will take vengeance on you for that, I am sure of it. I’ll no doubt suffer reprimand for failing to stop you, but the brunt of the ire will be borne by you, thankfully.”

She pulled on a pair of gloves as she spoke, wriggling her fingers to make sure they fit snugly before turning back to me. “And trust me, Duke Ryonic is capable of a great deal of ire.”

I grunted. “Can’t kill me though. You still—” I subsided into a coughing fit, my lungs spasming with the after-effects of her skill shooting its way through my body. “…need me,” I finished.

“Yes, you’re quite right. We need your mind, your eyes, and your tongue. But I won’t risk a repeat of this,” she gestured down at her gruesome leg and the crossbow bolts scattered around the chamber.

She grabbed me by the back of the robe I wore and dragged me bodily towards the centre of the room. I could only hang limply as I was pulled along, my hands scraping along the ground beneath me and a string of bloody drool dangling from my lips. Blood in my mouth, blood on my face; sticky and coppery and only adding to the disorientation.

She dropped me unceremoniously and stalked off. I tried to roll over, to twitch or move do anything at all of consequence, but it was as if I inhabited somebody else’s body. My limbs would no more obey my commands than the sky would turn green because I willed it. I was left to pant in silence, listening out for the arhythmic sounds of her boots slapping against the symmetrical obsidian blocks that made up this entire ruin.

My faculties were slowly returning, and I desperately tried to think of some way out of my helpless state, but my traitorous mind kept flashing back to the memory. I had remembered something. And not something inconsequential—I’d recalled a piece of my past life, and it had been redolent with meaning.

Vanguard of the Lost had exceeded my wildest expectations. I’d hoped it would help me regain my memories in some distant future, once I’d levelled it well past the soft cap and perhaps only once I’d upgraded it to its 3rd or even 4th tier state. Jorge had implied that if it was the system that was locking away my memories, then I’d need to ascend to the legendary heights of the 4th tier before I could overcome such a working… But I’d done it now. As a mere 2nd tier warrior, in the midst of a battle.

Sure, the thoughts had been stolen again as soon as I’d lost that flow state of skill-guided movement, but what was lost could always be found again. Hope of reclaiming my past, not in some distant future but now; tomorrow, next month… soon. It bloomed within me, and I could hardly focus on anything else.

Varice returned before my thoughts could coalesce into anything useful. The air left my lungs in a whoosh as she booted me in the stomach, rolling me onto my back. She looked down at me wearily, as if she was resigning herself to an unpleasant task that she nevertheless intended to see through.

“I’m sorry about this, Lamb,” she said. “I’m not sure why, but you seem like a decent man.”

Her words worried me more than my own paralysis. I’d tried to kill her, had actually injured her, and unlike me, I doubted she’d had any great introspective breakthroughs during the battle. The sudden sympathy in her tone was unexpected, and I frowned as I tried to work out where it came from. My hatchet dangled loosely in her right hand. My skin grew cold.

“This might even help in the long run, though. As I said, I can’t risk a repeat of this ridiculous performance, and you don’t need both hands to get me out of here.”

She nudged my right arm out with her boot, moving it a little further from my body so that it rested flat on the floor, palm towards the ceiling. She then knelt beside me, small axe clutched in one delicate fist. “With any luck, this might even cause the duke to be lenient with you. He likes a warrior, and perhaps he’ll take more pity on a crippled one than a defiant one.”

My eyes widened with creeping horror as I realised what was coming, and I felt a shout bubbling up from my chest.

“I suppose we shall see soon enough,” she said, and then she swung.

Fire. Pure, blinding agony, starting in my wrist and wending its way up through my arm to the base of my neck. My skull soon flared with it; moments passing in a blur as I could feel nothing but the excruciating burn.

My scream echoed around the cavernous library, bouncing off the strangely uniform slabs of black glass surrounding us and returning twisted and changed. Like light scattering from a prism, the sounds were distorted and strange in tone and pitch. Somewhere nearby, a presence bloomed in response to the noise, but then Varice wrenched the blade free from the gaping wound and I screamed again, too wrapped up in the all-encompassing pain of my torture to take any notice.

Varice looked perplexed as she inspected the axe. Its misshapen head glinted in the harsh light of the library, my blood coating its surface with a sickening gleam. Her raven familiar cocked its head to one side and croaked something that sounded like “danger”, but she seemed too enraptured to notice.

She looked once more down to my arm and then back at the axe, before whistling softly. “Now that is a surprise.” I raised my head and tried to focus eyes rolling with delirium and pain on her, but the prickling of tears occluded my view. She seemed not to notice. “Look at that, you bent it.”

The only response I could make was a low moan of pain, drool hanging from my open mouth. My wrist was on fire, shooting pains threading their way up my arm still—building towards something. Every heartbeat caused the wound to throb and each second let the pain mount higher. A tidal wave of agony growing and growing, threatening to break over my mind with each moment. I whined at the feeling, desperate to look over and see what she’d done to me.

Had I lost my entire hand? Had my bone snapped into jagged pieces that even now pierced my muscles? Had she simply slashed through my veins and I was even now pumping my life’s blood across the dark floor? I didn’t know, and couldn’t move my head enough to see. All I could do was lock my quivering gaze on Varice’s face as she inspected my chipped and battered old hatchet with astonishment.

“It actually bent. And look at your arm! I don’t know what you’ve done to yourself, Lamb, but I have to say that I’m impressed.”

“Danger,” the raven croaked again, hopping to her other shoulder as she once more raised the handaxe.

“Bones like steel,” she mumbled to herself. “Well… if at first you don’t succeed,” she started, “simply try again.”

And then she swung once more.

I flinched and braced myself for another agonising impact—though there was precious little bracing would do—but none came. I opened my eyes again to see Varice staring behind her at something I couldn’t make out, an expression that might have been confusion swiftly giving way to trepidation. It was hard to be sure given I could only see one side of her face, but I caught the last expression clearly the moment it flickered across her startled face: fear.

I felt it then.

A presence. An aura, venerable and heavy with significance. Vera felt like a bonfire and the duke like a glacier, but this aura felt like a weary old soldier; ancient, tired, but with an iron-hard will that spoke of inevitability.

I’d never felt this exact aura before, and from the way Varice was stumbling to her feet, I gathered she had never felt its like either. But even so, my lips split into a ghastly grin, red teeth bared to the world.

“What’s the matter, spymaster?” I croaked between broken breaths. “Did you not realise I’d brought friends?”

“Who goes there?” Varice shouted into the gloom.

There was no answer save a quiet whistling that grew in volume with every moment. My hatchet lay on the floor an arms-length away from me, gently rocking back and forth on its bent blade in a small pool of my blood. I couldn’t take my eyes from it.

Varice had taken a few steps away and was busy muttering to herself, so I wasn’t sure she could even hear me any longer. It still felt important to speak though; to remain defiant till the end.

“I’ll tell you a secret,” I whispered as my eyelids drooped.

I felt the power of the unfamiliar aura roll over me. It eclipsed even Vera’s, unlike any I had ever felt before, but there was a hint of something within that I recognised. It brought to mind great trees, ancient and unfathomable, tinged with a sadness and regret I couldn’t quite comprehend.

As the aura of a 4th tier washed over the cavern and my consciousness threatened to fall away, I finished my last thought. “I’m one of the weakest.”
