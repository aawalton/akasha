Ace stared down at the man he had just murdered.

Victor's severed head had rolled several feet from his body, the merc’s eyes still open in his final moment of surprise. Blood leaked from both his body and his neck, dark and thick against the emerald grass, and it reflected the dim light like crimson glass. The killing blow had been clean. Efficient. A soldier's execution rather than a monster's frenzy.

Victor was dead, and Ace felt nothing.

No satisfaction. No remorse. Not the rush of victory, nor the weight of guilt.

Just... emptiness. A hollow void where some human reaction should have been.

He searched within himself for any flicker of emotion—any remnant of the man who once would have been haunted by taking another life—but he found only a cold, calculating stillness. This was revenge for Rachel, protection for his team, and elimination of a threat.

The blood on his hands might as well have been water for all it troubled his conscience.

A sharp, white-hot pain burned suddenly across his jawline. He flinched as the rush of agony shot through his skull. It almost felt as if an invisible blade had cut him.

The pain faded as quickly as it had come, and he tenderly pressed his fingertips against the area—only to find a thin scar that mirrored the scar on Victor’s face.

But the physical mark was just the beginning.

Thin shadows wriggled through his peripheral vision. He tilted his head toward them, but they always moved away at the last second, perpetually stuck in the corner of his eye. The shadows deepened, gaining texture and dimension where before they'd been merely absence of light. They whispered to him now, beckoning him closer with promises of power and new magic.

The Shadow Realm called to him yet again, louder than ever before. An extension of him, now, where before it had been nothing more than a place.

Ace reached his hands toward the shadows, driven by instinct rather than conscious thought. They responded immediately, coming closer, inching ever nearer, until they coiled around his fingers like loyal serpents.

Victor’s death had given him even better command of the Shadow Realm.

Incredible.

He slowly turned around, his movements fluid and silent in a way they hadn't been moments before. His body knew things his mind hadn't yet processed—optimal striking distances, how to distribute weight for maximum stealth, a thousand subtle calculations that transformed his military experience into something more lethal and refined.

A crimson pool of blood formed at Ace’s feet, and he studied his reflection. A fresh scar marred his jawline, but other than that, he looked the same. He wondered how much of himself disappeared with each kill and each level gained. His humanity quietly slipped away—not in dramatic moments of monstrous transformation, but in these small absences of feeling.

One of Victor’s fingers twitched, and Ace instinctively raised his sword to cut it off—but it didn’t move again. Instead, thin black lines spread across Victor's skin like a sickly spiderweb. His body shimmered and collapsed inward, dissolving into a pool of smoke that seeped into the ground. The corpse crumbled into ash and shadow, as if the world itself was erasing evidence of his existence.

In a theatrical poof of white smoke, the System appeared. She floated beside him with a wicked little smile on her face, her childlike features twisted in macabre delight. Her glowing eyes darted toward Ace, searching his expression for any reaction to the kill—any sign of horror or triumph she could exploit.

He didn’t show anything, namely because there was nothing there to show. Her eyes flashed with recognition, and her smile widened with something like approval.

“Oh, you naughty things!” she said with a chipper edge to her voice. “I can’t leave you alone for two minutes without you squabbling over something, now can I?”

Ace didn’t reply.

The demented little girl clapped her hands together in delight. "My favorite little soldier has killed his first rival! I’m proud of you, Sergeant. I wasn't sure you had it in you."

It clicked, then.

Ace closed his eyes and shook his head in disgust. “This was a setup.”

"Oh, of course!" She giggled. "I told you already, Sergeant—you’re my favorite."

“Goodie,” he said dryly.

"Don't look so glum." The System circled Victor's remains as it slowly dissolved to ash. "You just killed your first vampire, after all. That’s going to do wonders for your EXP Solutes—not to mention all the loot.”

Sure enough, red sparks danced along Victor's corpse like fireflies around his body, highlighting the grotesque angle of his neck and the vacant stare in his once-arrogant eyes.. The crimson light indicated Mythical tier loot, if Ace recalled correctly.

At least the bastard was good for something.

The System hovered beside him, laying on her back as she did the backstroke through the air, as though she were swimming instead of hovering four feet off the ground.

“There’s some good loot there, Sergeant,” the little girl said in a sing-song voice. “Don’t you want to check?”

Ace didn’t answer.

He stood over the corpse, his chest still heaving as adrenaline continued to pump through his body. He tilted his head and glanced at the others over his shoulder. To his surprise, they all watched him in stunned silence. Tara's hand covered her mouth, her eyes wide with shock. Marcus stood frozen and speechless, his face so pale he seemed as though he was about to pass out.

Olivia, however, watched him with a gruesome fascination, and her lips curled into a smile. Her back arched, and her eyes trailed down his body, as though seeing him for the first time.

Frankly, he didn’t know what to make of that, and he didn’t have the patience to figure it out right now.

"Come check if you got loot too," Ace ordered, his voice rougher than he had intended. "We're a Party. Everything gets split."

But no one moved.

They just stared at him with expressions that ranged from caution to outright terror, as if the monster they'd just watched him become might turn on them next. The silence stretched between them, thick and suffocating.

Something twisted in Ace's gut. A feeling he couldn't quite name. Disappointment, maybe, or betrayal. He'd been fighting for them, protecting them.

And yet, the way they looked at him now…

…it made him wonder if he was the monster.

Ace turned his back on them, and he stuffed the ache deep down. They didn’t have time for feelings—there was no telling what else would be drawn to the battleground.

"Fine,” he said quietly. “I'll check it myself."

Besides, he wasn’t ready to talk to them.

Not yet.

Ace knelt beside Victor's corpse, and his knee sank into the blood-soaked dirt. Cold liquid seeped into his pant leg as his knee pressed against the soil, but he ignored the discomfort and the thick tang of blood that coated the air above Victor’s lifeless body. Ace reached out and tapped his finger once against the dead man’s shoulder.

The moment his fingers made contact, a System's interface appeared before him, blinding in its brightness against the darkness. The loot list materialized, and to his surprise, there was a scroll bar from the sheer volume of what he had acquired.

—--

Loot List:

—--

Ace's mind reeled. This wasn't just good loot—it was a treasure trove.

“Mythical loot,” he said under his breath.

He wasn’t sure what that meant, exactly, but it sounded like a good thing.

“Oooh,” the System said in his ear. “Lucky ducky!”

He peered over one shoulder, but she wasn’t there. With a shake of his head, he returned his attention to his loot list.

“This kind of loot is different,” the System continued. “It’s not just about how common an item is. It’s about its potential. Mythical and Legendary loot can be upgraded and evolve, just like you.”

Well, now.

Ace sure liked the sound of that.

“How do I upgrade it?” he asked.

“By killing things,” the System said casually. “And by finding a good Blacksmith to reforge it for you.”

“Where do I find a blacksmith?”

“The cities, mainly,” the System answered. “Or a few of the temples scattered throughout the Floors. The temples are kind of culty, though. Not sure I’d recommend one of those zealots.”

Huh.

If the System didn’t want him to go there, then he definitely needed to find one of these temples.

He drew a deep breath and selected the first item.

—--

Name: Moonfall

BASE STATS:

Weight: 4.5 kg

Special Property: Lunar Absorption: The blade stores moonlight for later use. Each hour spent under moonlight charges the weapon, allowing for brilliant flashes that disorient shadow creatures for 5 seconds. (Cooldown: 60 seconds)

Unique Property: Shadow Severance: Attacks have a 15% chance to temporarily sever a shadow-touched creature's connection to the Shadow Realm for 10 seconds, preventing shadow abilities and teleportation. Critical hits increase this chance to 35%.

Requirements:

Description: Oh my, someone's found a shiny new toy! Do be careful where you point that thing, darling. Some cuts never quite heal the way you'd expect.

—--

The Forgeborn leader’s sword.

Ace tapped the [ ACCEPT ] button, and the blade materialized instantly into his hands. He drew it from its sheath and examined the stunning blade as it glistened in what little light stretched across the Highlands.

It radiated power. Majesty. Perfection.

The silver blade gleamed even in the light, its edge impossibly sharp. Despite its size, it felt light as a feather in his hand. A strange sensation washed over him, and the blade seemed to recoil slightly at his touch, as if recognizing that he was the very thing it was forged to destroy.

No way was this blade going into Abyssal Armory. If he’d learned anything from today, he needed a weapon on him at all times.

Might as well have a Mythical one.

Though he would have to attune it under moonlight, he went ahead and strapped the sheath around his waist and scanned his loot list once more.

There was something called a Bloodvine Core—an uncommon crafting material that could be used to enhance several different types of gear, including his Thornhide Armor.

“Wait,” he said under his breath, and he smiled before he could stop himself. “I can upgrade my armor?”

“Of course, silly goose,” the System said with a flick of her hand. “Where’s the fun in just having a boring old set of armor? Upgrade it how you want to, and you can get more and more powerful.”

It came at a cost, of course—this time, far more than he’d given to bond with the armor itself.

Great.

He hit the [ACCEPT] button, and the Bloodvine Core materialized in Ace's palms. Its weight caught him off guard, given its modest size. The object pulsed with an unsettling rhythm that simulated a heartbeat, and the fist-sized crimson crystal webbed with intricate black veins. The core radiated a subtle warmth against his palm, and when he rotated it, the veins shifted and realigned. The small orb refracted a reddish glow onto his face and arms. More disturbing, however, was its sense of sentience. Ace could feel it attempting to synchronize with him—probing, seeking connection—as though it retained a primitive intelligence and hunger to become one with its new host.

Given the 500 HP cost to attune it with his armor, this would have to wait.

He set it aside and moved on to the next item. The weight of his surviving teams stares settled on the back of his neck, and he did his best to ignore them.

They needed space to process what had happened.

—--

Name: Dominator's Gauntlets

BASE STATS:

Weight: 7.0 kg

Special Property: Will Crusher: Strikes delivered with these gauntlets have a 25% chance to temporarily weaken the target's defensive stance. Successive hits against the same target increase this effect by 5% (up to 45% total vulnerability).

Unique Property: Blood Resonance: When worn together with the Blood Tyrant's Signet, all melee abilities cost 20% less Soul Meter and crushing attacks gain a 35% increase in penetration power.

Requirements:

Description: What a perfectly understated pair of instruments you've found! So much more elegant than those gaudy spiked monstrosities most brutes prefer. The previous owner had such... specific tastes in physical domination.

These deceptively simple gauntlets of darkened steel bear microscopic runes etched into every plate and joint—each one designed to amplify the wearer's physical dominance. When worn, the gauntlets meld with your movements, making your opponents forget they're even there until your grip crushes their defenses.

—--

Damn.

He wasn’t leveled high enough to use them.

“What happens if I wear them anyway?” he asked the System.

Her smile widened, and those glowing eyes of hers glinted with mischief. “Try it and see.”

He frowned in annoyance and tilted his head. He wasn’t in the mood for games.

She giggled. “Your face! Oh, Sergeant, you really are too much fun.”

With a shake of his head, he hit [ ACCEPT ]. The gauntlets materialized in his open palms, and they fell like dumbbells into his hands. He grunted, surprised by the sheer weight of them—especially given that they only weighed about fifteen pounds.

He glanced briefly at the System, wondering what sort of trap she was setting for him, but these were too valuable. He had to at least try.

When he slipped the first one on his hands, it pulled on him with the weight of an elevator crashing to the ground. His closed fist broke open a hole in the bloodstained grass. He tugged at it with all his considerable strength, but the damn thing wouldn’t budge.

Behind him, the System’s giggles became full-on laughter.

“Noted,” Ace said as he slipped his hand out of the gauntlet and set it aside.

The next item in the list, however, he knew intimately.

Nightmare’s Spine—Victor’s whip.

Ace didn’t bother reading the info screen that popped up. Though he hit [ ACCEPT ], he almost hurled the damn thing into the forest. He wanted nothing to do with it. It reminded him of the way Victor had broken Olivia’s ribs, of the way he had used it to trip the Behemoth until it fell on Rachel.

At the thought of their fallen teammate, Ace winced.

He wanted nothing to do with this thing. Victor had wielded it with calculated savagery. Ace felt an unsettling hunger emanating from it, as if the weapon itself was alive and eager for a new master.

The remaining items were more straightforward—a new currency they hadn’t seen before. Thus far, they had no use for the currency, but he figured it would come in handy eventually.

Ace closed the interface, though his thoughts still raced at the wealth of loot he'd just acquired.

He reached for Nightmare's Spine first, feeling a strange pull toward the weapon. He could put this in Abyssal Armory, but using it would only remind him of the bastard who had killed Rachel.

No, fuck it.

He didn’t want this thing.

First chance he got, he’d pawn this off to someone else. Maybe he could get some money for it and use that to further upgrade his existing armor.

Ace stood, his new arsenal secured. With a deep breath, he looked over his shoulder at his team. They stared back at him with mixed expressions that made his skin crawl.

As Ace turned around, Marcus flinched. The man kept his distance, staring at Ace in horror. His hands trembled slightly at his sides as the color drained completely from his face, and he looked about ready to vomit again. His eyes darted between Ace and Victor’s severed head, as if trying to solve a particularly disturbing equation.

Tara, however, Ace couldn’t read. Her face remained a perfect mask—calm and composed. She met his gaze directly, her expression neither approving nor condemning. It was the clinical assessment of a paramedic who'd seen too much to be shocked anymore, but something lurked beneath that neutral exterior. Something Ace couldn't decipher.

Olivia, meanwhile, watched him with a primal hunger he didn’t fully understand. She watched him like he was something fascinating and dangerous. Her lips parted slightly, and her fingers toyed absently with a strand of her dark hair. The way she looked at him now—after he'd killed another summoned hero—sent an uncomfortable chill down his spine.

Like she wanted to rip of his shirt and take him, right there.

That girl really was crazy.

The silence stretched between them, heavy with unspoken questions. In just one day, they went from six to four. Two were gone—one lost to a monster that prowled this hellscape, and one to Ace himself.

“You did the right thing,” Tara said quietly.

A wave of relief crashed through him. At least she understood. The others would come around eventually.

At least, he hoped they would—but part of him wondered just how much camaraderie was left in their dwindling group of survivors.
