Asher stood there, staring at what had to be the creepiest undead he’d seen so far. And seeing as he’d spent nearly the entire day fighting against increasingly weird, mutated corpses, that was saying something. Her head and face seemed fairly normal, with long, scraggly black hair and large eyes that were locked onto his own, but the rest of her was a different story.

Most of Watcher’s long body was covered up by her red robe, but her arms dangled a good couple of feet out of her sleeves, her lengthy fingers dragging along the ground as she stood there. Far more unnerving than the weirdly long arms, however, were the eyes.

The dozens and dozens of eyes dotting the flesh of her arms and face.

“What is she doing?” he hissed, not taking his eyes off the undead as he directed the question toward Smiles. “And why does she have so many eyes?!”

“I just told you, she watches!” Smiles said. “And I don’t know, probably to better watch stuff. Makes sense to me.”

“So she never comes any closer than that?” Asher asked, desperately trying not to blink as he monitored the undead. He’d seen enough horror movies back on Earth to know what would happen the moment he blinked. “She just… stands there?”

“Watching, yes. I’m starting to think you weren’t actually listening to me,” Smiles frowned.

“So what, is she going to report my location to the King of Death? What does she do?”

“Seriously? She. Watches. You’re lucky I can’t actually feel anger, because I think I’d be feeling that right about now if I could!”

Realizing Smiles wasn’t going to be any help, Asher gave up. His eyes were starting to strain from not blinking, and he gritted his teeth as he tried to come up with a solution. Desperate to buy himself a few seconds, he triggered Comprehend’s second-tier effect.

Alright, super freaky undead lady watching me in the distance. What’s the worst that could happen here? She goes and tells the King of Death I’m on my way, and he gathers all of his minions and Servants and whatever the hell Weaver is and has them attack me at once. That’s probably the worst-case scenario.

Best case… She keeps being creepy as all hell and watches me from a distance. Or leaves and doesn’t do anything. I don’t understand why she would do nothing but watch, her purpose must be to report stuff to the King of Death, nothing else makes any sense. And if that’s the case, if I want to retain the element of surprise, I need to figure out a way to deal with her.

Of course, that was easier said than done. Asher didn’t have any good methods of traversing that distance quickly, and he had a pretty good feeling if he tried conducting plan B himself, marking an arrow, firing it, and Recalling to where it landed, she’d be gone when he reappeared. Soon enough, his time ran out, and his eyes began burning again as the world sped back up.

“Smiles… if you had to fight Watcher, what would you do?”

“Not a clue. I told you, I could never catch her. That’s why I didn’t tell you about her before, you only asked about dangerous stuff, and she’s not dangerous. She just watches.”

“Yeah, I got that,” Asher snapped, accidentally blinking. The moment he did, Watcher vanished, as if she’d never existed in the first place.

Jumping, Asher spun around, letting out a sigh of relief when he found her again. Still a good quarter mile or so away, still not moving, still staring directly at him with her many eyes.

“Some sort of teleportation power… And either a flair for the theatrics, or a way of knowing when I’m not looking at her. Smiles, what do you see when she teleports?”

“She just sort of vanishes. It’s actually pretty similar to your own weird trick you do while fighting.”

“Realm travel?” Asher muttered, focusing on the realm of ages. Just like the rest of the Deadlands, the realm of ages was fairly inert from the lack of movement. The astral realm would explain her vanishing, but she shouldn’t be able to move around that quickly within the astral. “Well, I guess there’s the obvious idea.”

Reaching into his Personal Rift, Asher pulled out his pair of protective goggles he’d snagged from Polly back in Dormaul. The lenses were dark to protect against bright flashes of light, which had the added benefit of preventing other people from seeing his eyes through them. Snapping them onto his head, one goggle at a time to ensure he never stopped watching Watcher, he nodded. She shouldn’t be able to actually see his eyes anymore.

Yet the moment he blinked, she was gone.

“Damn it,” he hissed, ripping the goggles off and quickly finding her standing in a new section of the Deadlands, still staring right at him. “Alright, enough complicated stuff!”

Shoving his goggles into his rift, Asher took off running in her direction. It wasn’t easy, but he focused on blinking only one eye at a time, ensuring he always had at least a single eye on her as he ran. Alternating blinking while running definitely wasn’t something he’d practiced before, and it felt incredibly unnatural. Still, Watcher didn’t vanish, and Asher grinned as he chased her down.

Only to realize after a couple of seconds that she wasn’t actually getting any closer.

Asher ran for a few more seconds, before gradually slowing to a stop. As far as he could tell, Watcher hadn’t moved a single decaying muscle. She still stood there, her robe completely still, her many eyes unblinking. Just as far away as she’d been before he took off.

“That doesn’t make any sense!” he argued to nobody in particular. “She didn’t move, but she’s not any closer!”

“I told you, I could never catch her,” Smiles repeated. “And I’m a lot faster than you are. Did you really think you could manage it if I couldn’t?”

“Not helping, Smiles!” Asher rubbed at his eyes, and then jumped and quickly found Watcher again when he realized what he’d done. The fact that she always remained about a quarter mile away was perhaps the weirdest magic Asher had seen in his entire time on this world. It sort of reminded him of…

“Spatial magic,” he said, reeling as he finally figured out the trick. “She’s using Spatial magic somehow, but not the realm of ages. It’s the only explanation. Her position must be locked on me. That's why I can’t get any closer to her. However, if that’s the case…”

Tossing Smiles to the side and ignoring his indignation, Asher pulled a bow and arrow out of his rift. He’d decided against plan B, seeing as she’d just move if he teleported, but there was a far simpler solution he could try. If she wasn’t planning to move so long as he wasn’t…

He could just shoot her.

Taking a few breaths to steady himself, Asher tried to remember the brief bits of training Brian had put him through. He’d watched the twelve-year-old fire arrows for hours back in Horntho village, and Brian had done his best to try and teach Asher the basics. Unfortunately, they’d quickly discovered Asher was particularly bad when it came to ranged weaponry, and he’d switched to practicing throwing his daggers instead. However, he definitely couldn’t throw one of his daggers that distance, which meant it was bow and arrow or nothing.

“Alright, a stationary target that’s around seven feet tall,” Asher muttered, drawing back his first arrow and taking careful aim. “How hard can it be?”

“Oh, that one was closer! Only missed her by thirty or so feet I think! Maybe fifty!”

“Shut up, Smiles,” Asher grumbled, shaking his head as he pulled yet another arrow from his rift. The last hour had been spent trying and failing to shoot Watcher with an arrow from their current position. As it turned out, accurately firing a bow a quarter of a mile away from one’s intended target was a bit more difficult than he’d first expected. Tack on the fact that despite her tall frame Watcher was extremely thin, far thinner than any sort of standard archery target, and Asher was having a tough time.

Granted, he wasn’t getting even close to her actual body, so how thin she was didn’t really seem to matter.

Asher had long since gotten over the creepy factor of Watcher vanishing any time he took his eyes off her. At first, he’d been afraid she’d teleport off and alert the King of Death about him. But for whatever reason, rather than alert her boss of the intruder surprisingly deep within the Deadlands, she seemed determined to just keep watching him. Regardless of how long he looked away, whenever he tried to find her again, there she was. Always a quarter of a mile away, always facing directly at him.

Always Watching.

“You know, I think I like you more than her,” Asher admitted, drawing back yet another arrow and letting it fly, cursing as it went wide by a good fifty feet. It was a good thing he’d purchased fifteen hundred arrows back in Dormaul for Samantha, because he was pretty certain he’d gone through at least a few hundred of them over the past hour. “This isn’t even unnerving anymore, it’s just annoying.”

“It’s boring is what it is. Did you know I can feel boredom? Because I can. I’m feeling it right now.”

“Thank you, Smiles, that’s incredibly helpful,” Asher said, pulling another arrow out of his rift. After the first twenty misses, he’d tried plan B all on his own just to be sure. And like he’d expected, the moment the world shifted around him from Recall, Watcher had already repositioned herself as quickly as she always did. “I really didn’t want to Recall away back to my team with her still watching me, but it doesn’t really matter what I think. I’ll need to get out of this Death Aura soon enough.”

Over the past hour, Asher had been growing steadily weaker as his body slowly succumbed to the King of Death’s power. Two-thirds of the way into the Deadlands meant that the Death Aura was incredibly strong, and he was already at the point where drawing the bow back was a struggle for his withered muscles, even with his Belt of Strength helping him.

“Why not teleport back to your friends?” Smiles asked, his head sitting on a nearby rock Asher had found. “Wouldn’t actually hitting Watcher be easier with help?”

“Obviously. But I’m worried rather than follow me, she’ll finally run off and tell the King of Death about me,” Asher explained, firing off another arrow and sighing as it didn’t even come close. His arm felt like decaying jelly at this point, so he couldn’t say he was all that surprised. “This is particularly frustrating, because I know Samantha could hit Watcher on her first try, even at this distance. Hell, I know a child that could probably do the same.”

“Oh, I don’t have any memories of children! I want one!”

“Yeah, no, that’s not happening. Damn it, if only I could get Spatial Lock around her…”

As a last-ditch effort, Asher pulled out one of his daggers and waved it in front of himself, hoping to cut through whatever weird skill was keeping her a constant distance away from him. But despite the fact that he could cut through tritessence, whatever she was doing, it didn’t seem to be anything like an invisible tether. Try as he might, he didn’t feel his blade hit anything.

“Welp, that’s about every idea I can think of,” he sighed, letting his hand fall and nearly losing his dagger in his weakened grip. “If I stay here much longer, I’ll just die, so I have to throw in the towel. Damn it, what an annoying enemy!”

“If it makes you feel any better, I don’t think she’ll tell the King of Death about you,” Smiles admitted. “He doesn’t like to be disturbed. At this point, you’re still in Servant territory. If anything, she’ll probably alert whoever is closest, after myself.”

“Still, if I don’t come up with a way to take her out, eventually she’ll run out of Servants, and then she’ll have to go up the chain of command,” Asher explained, running a hand through his hair and blinking as he realized a large clump of it had fallen out. “Okay, planning later, I need to go. I’ll be back in a bit.”

“What, you’re just going to leave me like this?” Smiles asked, frowning at him. “You said you’d kill me with a hammer!”

“I will! Later!” Asher said, shoving his bow back into his rift. Laying an Eternal Mark on Smiles’ head, he prepared to Recall.

“What am I supposed to do until you get back?” the undead monster huffed.

“I don’t know… here, watch the Lord of the Rings trilogy. Extended edition. That should keep you busy.”

“What’s the—”

Smiles’ eyes rolled back in his head as Asher transferred nearly twelve hours of memory into him in a few seconds, the act actually almost knocking Asher unconscious. It was far easier to share information than incorporate it in the first place, but even so, that was a lot of information. Staggering, he dropped to one knee, throwing one last wavering look at the distant Watcher.

“I’ll get you next time,” he managed, shaking a fist weakly before focusing on Samantha and Recalling away from the unnerving monster as it watched him leave.
