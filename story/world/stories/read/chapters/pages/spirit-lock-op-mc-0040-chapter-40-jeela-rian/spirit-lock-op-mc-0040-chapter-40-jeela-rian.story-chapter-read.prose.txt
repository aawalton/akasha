Leo knew Jeela was furious before she spoke—and she didn’t hold back in making it clear herself.

“Nebula just spent two hundred million credits on apology gifts for the patrons that came to watch your shameful display,” she said. “Your father is furious. Our patrons are furious. I am furious—and instead of showing remorse, you’re still berating tonight’s VIP?”

Leo stared at Hayden, baffled that he was referred to as the VIP. “Wait… Hayden’s…?”

“A VIP, yes,” Jeela said. ”It seems you still haven’t spoken to your father. I recommend you do that.”

Leo winced when he turned off the Do Not Disturb feature and saw calls from his mother, father, siblings, and friends. They had collectively called forty-seven times in the last hour.

Leo’s link suddenly rang—from his father. His eyes trembled as he accepted the request.

His father’s face popped up on the screen, showcasing eyebrows so sharp they looked like blades. “Do you even have the slightest clue what you’ve just done?”

While Leo was getting reamed by his father, Jora was cowering. Jeela Rian herself—a woman who could only be in one of her eight thousand auction houses—was somehow standing in front of him, speaking to Hayden Reskco, whom she had referred to as the VIP.

Jeela turned to Jora cordially. “Thank you for chaperoning my guest to the auction, Mr. Hedra. I will see that your family is compensated.”

“U-Uh, yeah. Totally. No problem.” Jora had never felt so socially incompetent in his life.

“Thank you.” Jeela turned to Hayden and motioned to the door. “Allow me to present you with your spirit in more suitable accommodations.” She led the duo to the grand lounge, where she sat Jora down on a couch, surrounded by people who could make his father sweat. Then, she took Hayden into a back room.

Jora sat in dead silence for about a minute before realizing something terrifying. I can’t get out of this…

Jora wanted to say Fine, I’ll accept a minor scandal, whatever—but he couldn’t. Not anymore. Hayden had galactic influence somehow; if he said anything—with the slightest proof—it would turn into a galactic scandal.

Jora released a faded chuckle. What the hell’d I get myself into…?

As Leo and Jora were reflecting on the gravity of their situations, Jeela Rian was meeting with Hayden for the first time. Despite her power and prestige, Hayden hadn’t changed his expression, something Loricks warned her about: Hayden only gives charming smiles or icy glares, he had said. Beyond that, he lacks the slightest hint of emotion. So don’t take offense. Those words seemed to be true.

“Once again, I want to apologize for the grievous inconveniences,” Jeela said. “While we met Belia Alicibria’s demands with a counter-threat, we don’t back patrons before understanding the extent of their value.” She drank from her wine glass.

“I completely understand,” Hayden said, running his finger around the rim of his glass. As he did, frost emanated from his finger, filling the glass with frothy white mist. Jeela’s eyes became transfixed. She reached for the cup. The second she touched the rim, she dropped it in shock. The wine spilled everywhere.

“Forgive me!” she said. “I was just startled. I’ll have someone—”

Suddenly, the wine drained from the tablecloth and levitated, suspended in the air above her. She stared at it unblinkingly, thoughts disjointed.

“There are things in this life that aren’t quantifiable, Ms. Rian,” Hayden said abruptly. “The value of youth. Immortality. Peace…” He spun his finger, and the wine gyrated in a hypnotizing circle. “I have insights into two of those—I seek the third. Unfortunately, it’s in short supply at the moment.”

“As it will continue to be,” Jeela said distantly, watching the spell-binding display. “No one can offer you peace in your position. You should know that.”

“I’m not seeking solitude—just neutrality. There isn’t much time before the rift opens; I want you to keep Belia Alicibria at bay, and provide me with the resources I need to accomplish my goal.”

“And in exchange?”

“Technology that allows ships to operate within rifts.” He pulled out a toy shuttle. He placed a cora stone inside it, and suddenly it lit up. It levitated and flew all around the room before hovering in the center of the floating wine.

Jeela watched it all from the lens of her Eyes of Appraisal spirit. Hayden hadn’t used a spirit once.

He stared her in the eye. “I assume that you would like such… universal… auctions to take place in your houses, yes?”

Jeela fought to restrain herself. It had been half a century since she was offered something that made her heart thrash about like a caged tiger. The auction value would be in the quintillions—maybe more. If the Big Four got involved, it could reach numbers that could buy her a galactic chain. Most importantly, it wasn’t insider trading. She would get blasted with lawsuits just for interacting with Hayden. She had to be careful—but not about this. This would be a defining moment that would put her at the very top.

Jeela had to have this opportunity.

“Give me specifics,” Jeela said. “As much as I yearn for this offer, I won’t commit suicide for an opportunity. That’s still the biggest factor here.”

“I would like a top-tier team of trusted rifters,” Hayden said. “Military transport, and protection. Beyond that, you would facilitate my moves, and communicate on my behalf.”

“That last request is remarkably vague.”

“Say I need to release technology, you will contact governments and businesses on my behalf.”

“Are you saying you can move governments?”

“This technology will do it, yes?” He made the tiny space shuttle glide around the ring of floating wine.

She swallowed. “Yes, that would do it. Though if you give it to them for free, you will lose my patronage.”

“You needn’t worry about that. I have plenty of technology to use for trades. I can strengthen bodies, reverse aging—to a degree—and cure illnesses. I am not a simple time-traveler, Ms. Rian, I’m an entity far beyond your wildest comprehension.”

He twisted his fingers, and the wine swirled into a ball. He lifted his wine glass and let it splash inside. Then, he lowered the shuttle into the wine, allowing it to float.

For reasons that Jeela couldn’t put into words, she felt the demonstration perfectly backed up his claim that he could influence Stella Insgley—the presider of the Golden Belt—one of the most powerful humans in the universe. The power to send shuttles into rifts wasn’t just lucrative—it was a matter of galactic security. That technology would indeed move galactic empires everywhere.

“If you aid me,” Hayden said. “I will give you wealth and power beyond your liberalist expectations—and crush your enemies with tyrannical force. I simply need time and resources to enter the Heline Rift and obtain what I desire.”

Jeela started to tuck a lock of hair behind her ear, a charming power tick that gave her confidence, but Hayden watched her with such a transparent gaze that she felt exposed—naked even. She dropped her hair and looked back. “I’ll consider it. Until then…”

Jeela pulled out four boxes.

“I’m presenting you with four offerings: your purchase, your complimentary item, your apology—and my personal gift. All of which are in your honor.”

“I’m charmed,” Hayden said.

Jeela smiled thinly. “Let’s start with your spirit.”

She opened a polished wooden box, exposing a small sliver of glowing quartz.

“This is your Clear Line spirit,” she said. “That’s your purchase. Now let’s talk about your gift—the complimentary item we gave everyone for technical purposes…”

She opened up another box, exposing a decorated bottle with swirling blue and green liquid.

“A dual expansion elixir. This elixir was made from the fruit of a life tree within the Scaria Rift. It will open up two extra spirit locks, allowing you to use the Clear Line spirit with your horix. This will give you flexibility until we sign our agreement.”

“It’s highly appreciated.”

Jeela nodded. “As for your apology…” She opened a box with another elixir. “In your honor, I gave all of my patrons the highest rank cleansing elixir I had on hand as their apology for Mr. Strister’s behavior. It’s of pristine quality. The elixir is made from water in a coran stream within a grand rift. It also has cleansing catalysts from Saik Grass, extending youth for at least fifty years before another elixir is necessary. It also has an appealing effect on your skin. I’m certain it could extend your popularity.”

She glanced at his clearing acne. It was almost gone, but his face was still red—as if inflamed.

“I cannot wait to use it,” Hayden said. “Though I’ll have to wait. There’s a mere week before I clear my acne naturally, so I plan to do so. It’s a matter of pride.”

Jeela lifted her hands in playful resignation.

“As I said, it’s a gift of apology. I think it will pair well with your superior core expansion elixir. As for my present…” She opened an elaborate mahogany box, and an immense pressure was released from within. Inside was a natural gray stone, like a rock one could pick up in any forest, but it was violently bending the air around it. “This is a Rank-4 wind gate catalyst. I’m sure it’ll help you obtain what you want from the rift.”

Hayden smiled. “Now that is how you’ll sway me.” He stared at the catalyst with a focused expression.

“Tell me, Mr. Rescko—do you know what that is?” she mused. “More than a type and number?”
