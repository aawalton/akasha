The officer in charge of the Ogre Gate, Nancho, wore a foul expression. His dark eyelids were heavy with thick eyebags—he hadn’t slept much these past few days and was feeling more than a little vexed.

Just twenty metres ahead, looming within reach, stood the stone fort the Ogres had built. They’d pushed dangerously close to the Ogre Gate. Now, the two sides were locked in a tense stalemate, each within the other’s Range Factor.

“Dammit!” Nancho cursed, spewing a wave of fire that turned the stone block in his line of sight red-hot. A ballista arrow slammed into it a second later, shattering it into pieces.

But almost immediately, another set of stone blocks slid into place, sealing the gap again.

“Fucking cowards.”

The Ogres never exposed themselves. Not even a glimpse. They moved like rats, hiding their large bodies behind layers of stone slabs. And like a serpent inching closer, their fortress crept toward the Ogre Gate, jaws widening—ready to snap it shut.

“They came prepared this time. Shows how desperate they are,” Rachad said from the side. He was seated next to a ballista, carefully uncorking a Healing Potion to take a small sip. Then, just as carefully, he sealed it and stored it inside a bag reinforced with multiple layers of protection. Once it was secure, he added, “Still, it gave us enough time to finish our preparations.”

“I want to rest once this is done,” another Level 2 Human muttered. A team of Level 2s had gathered at the Ogre Gate, summoned from their respective prisons in the Elven Prison. All of them were officers.

Just behind the Ogre Gate stood fifty trebuchets—half of them loaded with firewood, the rest packed with massive containers of oil.

“You’re right. This battle’s dragged on long enough,” Nancho grunted, Pronto’s face flashing in his mind. ‘All because of one guy’s actions.’

Pronto had stolen the Ogre Race’s Relic and fled to the Elven Prison. That’s why the Ogres were here—why they were targeting them. Nancho would’ve preferred if Pronto had just run straight to the Human Kingdom. ‘The soldiers there would’ve been happy to fight this war.’

‘Still, Sir Pronto followed orders to steal the Ogre Race’s Relic. And I’m just doing my job—protecting the Ogre Gate.’

Nancho raised his hand and shouted, “MY SOLDIERS!”

Then he swung his arm down with force. “Fire!”

The trebuchets launched. Firewood and barrels of oil arced through the air, crashing against the stone fort. Oil splashed across its surface, and flames piled up along the sides.

“Ready?” Nancho turned to his team of nine officers. Each of them had small containers of oil strapped to their bodies—ten centimetres long, two centimetres thick.

“Let’s finish them all,” Rachad said, tightening his boots and giving a quick kick to test the fit.

“Then,” Nancho raised his hand again and roared, “Shoot!”

All forty ballistae focused on a single point along the stone fort and fired. The wall cracked and crumbled, revealing a corridor behind it. Rachad whipped out his Fire Whip and lashed it at the nearest surface, igniting the stone in flames.

More barrels of oil rained down, spreading fire across the entire fort. The firewood piled along the sides formed a blazing barricade, sealing the Ogres inside.

The fumes were starting to suffocate the Ogres, forcing them to retreat deeper into the fort, drawing farther away from the Ogre Gate.

Nancho and his team of nine officers seized the moment. They slipped through the hole they’d blasted in the wall and rushed down the corridor.

“There’s a blockade up ahead,” Nancho said, pulling out one of the oil cylinders strapped to his body. He peeled off the cover at the bottom, revealing a sticky adhesive. Pressing it against the stone blockade, he backed off quickly with the others.

Once they were clear, Rachad’s Fire Whip snapped forward and touched the exposed end of the cylinder. The oil inside was a special mixture—designed to expand violently when ignited.

The cylinder detonated, driving the metal tip at the front—right beneath the adhesive—deep into the stone. Cracks split across the surface.

“It’s working,” Nancho said, pulling out a spherical container. Same substance inside, but this one was designed to apply force in all directions, unlike the cylinder, which focused it in a single spot.

He set the sphere into the hole formed by the first blast and triggered it. The resulting explosion blew apart the stone, leaving a wide gap the officers could pass through.

They kept up the pace, tearing through the corridor, blasting down any wall that stood in their way.

“They’re here!” one of the officers shouted, just as a wall came down—revealing four Ogres waiting on the other side.

The Ogres scrambled to morph a nearby wall into another blockade.

The officers burst through the wall and charged after them. “Kill them!”

Flames shot from their feet as all ten officers surged forward, racing down the corridor, closing in fast. They darted around the four fleeing Ogres like a swarm of houseflies, moving in perfect sync. Then the entire corridor erupted in flames.

—Graaah!

An Ogre screamed, bolting toward a wall. It opened a hole and leapt through—but just before the stone sealed shut, a cylinder flew into the gap. It exploded on impact, blasting the wall apart. The officers stormed through the opening.

“Take out the ceiling!” Nancho shouted.

Four officers sprang into action, planting cylinders on the ceiling. The blasts tore it apart, sending chunks of stone crashing down. Streams of oil dripped through the opening. Above them, trebuchets were still firing, hurling oil containers that shattered on impact, splashing the fort in flammable liquid.

“Get out!” Nancho ordered.

The team shot up through the hole in the ceiling and circled overhead. Then, raising their hands, they pointed down and released a torrent of flames.

[Spell: Torch]

The fire surged into the corridors below, flowing like water under their control. As it touched the scattered cylinders throughout the fort, a chain of explosions ripped through the stone halls.

“Good!” Nancho muttered as part of the stone fort crumbled. Several floors had collapsed from the blasts. Three Ogres were burned to cinders, while the fourth lay buried under rubble—half-charred, bleeding heavily.

“There,” one of the officers pointed to a section of the fort, “If we blow that up, we can reach another corridor—”

“No, retreat!” Nancho barked.

The officers instantly took flight. Spikes of rock shot out from the area near the collapsed section, aimed straight at them.

New walls began forming, replacing the ruined structure. Above them, a section of the ceiling slid open. Two Ogres emerged, stone spears in hand. They hurled them at the officers before sealing the opening again.

Seconds later, a barrel of oil slammed into the ceiling. A Flame Whip flicked up and ignited the surface.

“That was close,” Rachad said, clutching his side. A rock spike had grazed him. If Nancho had hesitated for even a second in alerting the group, he would’ve been skewered. “The Level 2 Ogres are getting sneaky.”

Just then, a huge slab of wall at the top of the fort tipped over and crashed to the ground. Its massive weight crushed the firewood barricade and snuffed out the flames. The force of the impact sent oil and splintered wood flying away from the fort.

One by one, more upper walls collapsed, clearing the firewood encirclement. Fresh walls rose in their place, holding the fort together.

“We only managed to kill four of them this time,” Nancho said, watching the fire fade from the fort. “Unless we take out the Level 2 Ogres, this battle’s never going to end.”

“We’ll just keep torching the fort, then.” Rachad cast Torch, blanketing a wide stretch of the fort in flames. The fire held steady as the trebuchets kept raining down barrels of oil. “We can cook them—brought enough oil this time.”

“That we can,” Nancho agreed. “Disperse and torch the fort. But stay alert for rock spikes. There are plenty of Ogres with a Range Factor in the triple digits.”

“Uhm…” One of the officers gulped, his spell faltering. He pointed toward the sky, his voice tense. “Something’s coming at us…”

“Hm?” Nancho quickly pulled out his telescope, aimed it at the spot—and dropped it a second later, eyes wide in shock. “A… flying island?”

The Stone Race of Ogres—Level 3 (Relic)!

He spun around and roared toward the soldiers manning the Ogre Gate. “Summon the WARDEN! NOW!”

“Yes, officer!” one soldier shouted, saluting before jumping onto a Sun Stone-powered bike and speeding across the bridge. He shot over the Lava Moat and disappeared into the forest on the other side. ‘I need to hurry! Only the Warden can face a Level 3 Ogre.’

‘The Warden’s so strong, no Level 3 existence can fight him alone. That means… there must be more than one Level 3 Ogre on that flying island!’

The soldier clenched his jaw and cranked the Sun Stone throttle higher.

Swoosh!

The soldier never saw it coming. Tense and focused on his mission, he didn’t even notice the wooden arrow—eighty centimetres long, a centimetre thick—until it punched clean through his chest.

He toppled off the bike, crashing hard into the ground and rolling a good distance from the momentum. “Gah!”

‘What… happened?’ Blood ran down his forehead as his vision blurred. He forced his eyes open and made out a hazy silhouette walking toward him, bow in hand.

‘An Elf?’

“H-How…”

Another arrow slammed through his skull, silencing him for good.

The figure strolled up to the body and placed a hand on it, fingers pressing gently into the cooling flesh. “Be one with the trees.”

The corpse withered, breaking down into compost, and was scattered across the roots of nearby trees along the ash road.

“I missed the mark with my first shot,” the figure murmured. “Still got a long way to go.”

It was Pinaka.

“Now, I’ll be claiming this for myself.” He reached for the bike and yanked out its Sun Stone. Then he wheeled it into the forest and hid it inside the trunk of a thick tree. “Unless they chop open every tree trunk in this forest, they’ll never find it.”

Back on the road, there were obvious crash marks. Pinaka crafted a large wooden cylinder and rolled it over the ash road, smoothing out the signs of impact until the surface looked untouched.

He broke the cylinder into pieces, carried them into the woods, and planted each chunk into the earth. Within moments, new saplings began to grow.

‘It’s easiest to hide a tree in a forest.’

Unless the Humans had counted and recorded every single tree in the forest, they’d never realize what he’d done. ‘And from what I read in their documents, they don’t keep that kind of record.’

Pinaka tucked the Sun Stone into a cloth bag he’d crafted and took off through the forest. ‘I hope the main body isn’t slacking off.’

The one who had killed the soldier was a clone, hidden inside a tree near the ash road. As expected, once the Ogres’ assault reached a certain level, the soldiers moved to summon the Warden.

If the messenger was eliminated, the Warden’s arrival would be delayed—just long enough for the Ogre Gate to fall.

And while the Warden was busy dealing with that mess, Pinaka would be free to move toward his real target.

Further along the ash road, heading toward the Human Kingdom, a metal carriage rolled steadily forward. Inside sat Pronto and his subordinates.

“Congratulations, Your Highness,” said a Level 2 old man who was handling the carriage’s movement. He gave a respectful salute. “You’ve accomplished what no other Prince or Princess has managed.”

“We’re still a long way from the Human Kingdom,” Pronto said sharply. “Don’t relax until we get there.”

“It’s a straight road to our home from here,” the old man said casually. “No enemies dare come near this stretch…”

Boom!

Out of nowhere, a massive wooden log—five metres long—slammed into the carriage with brutal force, knocking it over. A large dent caved in part of its side.

“Enemy!” Pronto shouted, just about to order his soldiers to disembark and retaliate—

Boom!

Another log struck, this one hitting even harder. The sound echoed through the trees like thunder.

‘How can someone produce this much force? No Ogre I’ve faced could throw like this... with that kind of power!’

“Hmm… one more.”

Two hundred metres away, Pinaka stood calmly, bow in hand. He loaded another arrow—this one containing a clone of his spine—and loosed it into the air. As it flew, it grew, rapidly generating wood until it transformed into another massive log that slammed into the metal carriage with a deafening crash.

Even before the impact faded, Pinaka had already drawn a third arrow.
