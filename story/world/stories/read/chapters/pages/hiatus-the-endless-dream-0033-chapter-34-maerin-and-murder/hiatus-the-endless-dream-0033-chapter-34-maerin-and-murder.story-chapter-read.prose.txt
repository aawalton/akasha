Dorian

The forest was vast. Larger than I thought it might be, and I found myself impressed—disbelieving almost—at the fact that Jeren had managed to collect all the Survivors he did. Of course, he had missed two and picked up a Visitor of unknown allegiance … but credit where credit was due.

From my vantage point at the top of the bell tower, I saw our surroundings clearer. The entire place was a basin surrounded by jagged mountains. The moon—now tinged with a slight pink color—hung overhead, bathing all in its harsh light.

Nothing moved. All was quiet. I found it peaceful. I wished I could have stayed there until the timer hit zero—just looking after the three Survivors here, but I couldn't. Jeren had already failed once, and I had yet to see him win.

I focused on those thoughts as I continued to scan the darkened horizon. There were few details to make out, with only the most notable landmarks visible. A massive lake at the center of the basin, a needle of stone that pointed towards the sky, and another clearing with what seemed to be the remnants of some wooden structure.

My eyes flicked between the three locations. Peeler had to be at one of them. The item's description mentioned it would be at a place of importance. Last time it had been in the Greenhouse with Nate and Maggie, who were in a lot of ways the crux of the previous Instance. There were more treacherous places the crowbar could've been, but something had deemed that location to be important. Peeler being there in the first place is likely the only reason I survived.

I didn't even know if I'd be able to use my crowbar right now. I had burned Identity from 47 all the way to 10. Even with all that Identity last time, I hadn't been able to see into the Instance's secrets proper, and now with less, I doubted I'd be able to see anything of real import. I looked at my Lucidity and smiled. At the very least, I wouldn't mistake myself for a box again.

Echoing footsteps traveling up the stairs broke me from my thoughts. The sounds were not quiet, which meant whoever was coming up was making themselves known or didn't care if I heard them. Fine by me. I took a seat on the shallow stone railing and waited for whoever it was to arrive.

It was the girl who had been waiting for us at the altar.

She was striking for a number of reasons, least of all her blazing red hair, which faded to a brittle white at the tips. For a moment, I thought large chunks of hair hung down the side of her cheek, but as she stepped into the moonlight, I realized the red there was not her hair, but rather burned skin.

I said nothing, fully prepared for the uncomfortable silence like Krevith had put forwards.

Instead, she immediately spoke. "I do not believe we've been properly acquainted. My name is Maerin. What is yours?" Her voice was measured and fluid. Certain.

I debated leaning into the Fiend persona, but I just couldn't think of anything that didn't sound stupid. "Dorian," I answered tersely. "Nice to meet you."

"I'm sure," she answered. She took a spot opposite me and stayed standing. "You are a Visitor, are you not?"

Who the hell- "What do you mean?" I asked, trying to keep the surprise from my tone.

She rolled her eyes, "The Dream may not be common knowledge to the masses, but it is not unknown to those higher on the pecking order."

Thoughts of Talin and our first meeting came to mind. She had known me as a Visitor.

I didn't hate that this Maerin knew, in fact, it made things easier. She came up here for a reason, and I lacked any practical knowledge of this world. "Let's do a trade, a question for a question."

"Agreed," she shot back instantly, "but I reserve the right to deny any question."

I shrugged, "So do I then."

"I'll go first," she started without any further discussion. "Krevith has told me you have a Fiend with you. Let me see them."

"That's not a question." I shook my head.

"You can treat it as one if you'd like." She raised her chin, which pissed me off for some reason.

I answered with a small amount of pettiness. "Then I'll ask my question first, you'll answer, then you can see it."

She said nothing, just waited expectantly.

"What do Fiends want?" I asked.

"To send our souls into oblivion. Nothing more or less." The answer came fast. "The Fiend, if you would." She held her hand out, and I sneered, producing the figurine, but keeping it in my hands.

"You said see. I'm sure your eyes work from there."

She didn't argue. If anything, her hand recoiled after seeing the grotesque creature. To her credit, she recovered quickly, her voice slightly forced. "Why is it obedient to you?"

"Because the Dream says it has to be." Another shrug.

"Wh-," I cut her off before she could continue. She scowled, but said nothing more. "My turn. Why would a Fiend help the Survivors here survive this Instance."

"Easy." I barely got my last word out before she started. "This Instance, the masses call them trials, are so the Survivors souls can be purged and returned to God. If Survivors are ejected into the Dream there are no Spirits to draw in their souls and the Fiends are free to use them as they see fit. How does the Fiend work?" She finished her lesson with a question.

"I'm done asking questions. That was 2 for 2. I believe we're done here." I said. I felt like I was at a disadvantage here, and I had already gotten what I needed to know. More than that, I wanted to gauge her reaction, to see what she'd do when things didn't go her way. She had been nothing but poised so far. I could handle that, but if she threw a tantrum, I'd have to reconsider how I went about this whole thing.

She took some time to respond. Her face would have been expressionless if not for the small uptick near her lip that betrayed a scowl. "No, we are not," she started. "The game was a courtesy. You revealed much of your hand. You are a Visitor, not a Fiend like you're pretending. I know not what you're planning, just that it is at my mercy."

So that's how she wanted to play it. Okay. I could work with that. "So I guess that means you'll be coming along. Fine. Two more questions."

She nodded, visibly satisfied through her annoyance, "There are three men in that group I want dead, but I do not wish to fail this trial. I believe your Fiend could be an answer to that. Can it?"

My eyes didn't leave the girl. I studied her far more intently than before. I hadn't considered that there could be interpersonal politics on top of whatever else was happening in this Instance. Even after hearing about the girl killing her husband, I had assumed the trouble ended there. It obviously hadn't.

I ran my finger over the smooth, yet far too bumpy, statue of the Fiend. This. This could be the answer to my problems. I hadn't wanted to use it until we were whittled down. I didn't know how the other would react, but if this was the cost to bring two capable Survivors into the fold. Well then, with one Survivor puppeted by the parasite, Maerin, The Huntress, and Krevith—who was apparently a Gladiator before this— that made four. Enough that I didn't have to rely on anyone else for the win. It would be all but guaranteed.

I thought through the plan, viewing it from different angles, thinking about all the things that could and likely would go wrong.

I didn't hate it. In fact, it seemed almost optimal. "Yes. It can." I finally answered.

"Are you willing to use it in such a way?" She pushed. At some point, she had looked out towards the forest, but now her full attention was squarely on me as she took a step forward.

I didn't answer right away. The girl seemed a little unhinged, but then again, I was no stranger to hate. Anger this intense did not come in a vacuum. It was anger enough to not only kill her husband, but to proudly display his body from a castle gate and be sent to these death trials. I knew better than to get in between anyone and the cause of their rage. That being said. The Fiend could only parasitise one person. I wouldn't just throw away two lives.

I settled on a compromise. I held up one finger, "One. You can pick one Survivor for the Fiend to take."

She scowled and took her time in turn. "Two." she settled on.

I shook my head. "You think you can bargain. You can not."

"I can, because you would not have offered any of them up if you did not think it was worth it." She practically spat the words out, and I could make out the shape of what made her a little better. She assumed I thought her dumb or naive, and resented that.

Her vermilion eyes bore into me, and I matched her gaze. "Three, but only in the last minute of the Instance, and only if it will not cause us to fail."

The eyes widened. In shock? In joy? In respect? I didn't know. "I agree to your terms."

Neither of us moved.

I extended my hand.

She stared at it.

In two strides, she took it in her own and shook, and then broke contact to return to her previous spot.

I shifted my gaze out towards the forest, hoping she'd take the cue that the conversation was over. I didn't hear her move.

Hmm. Awkward.

I felt like I was losing my mind again. How the hell did anyone actually socialize? Especially after planning murder. "Was there something else?" I asked.

"Huh? No. This is my tower. I do not know why you're still here," she said, and a wave of irritation washed over me. I felt entirely too petty for this.

"Well I'm here now."

"So you are."

Eventually, the silence grew somewhat comfortable. I didn't look at her, and I assume she didn't look at me. I paid attention to the countdown. Once it got a little lower, I'd gather everyone and we'd head out, but I just needed a minute to breathe and feel like the world wasn't going to collapse beneath my feet.

I looked up at the moon. It had gotten a little redder.

More because it blocked part of the moonlight rather than being illuminated by it, I saw some massive winged creature—something wriggling in its grasp—flying across the sky. I tensed, but quickly realized it was neither flying towards us nor circling for prey. Instead, it was getting smaller, flying straight towards the moon.

I did not understand why, but I took that as my cue to leave. The world kept moving and would not stop just because I was feeling pitiful. "Let's go. It's time we meet up with the others."

I left and headed down the stairs, where I found the Huntress and Krevith sitting on opposite pews discussing something. "It's time to go." I turned to the Huntress. "Do you have anything that can start a fire?"

She looked to the stairs, and didn't look away until she saw Maerin walk down a few seconds after me. "Yes. Krevith told me of the spirit you saw on the way here. It will take a little time to make a torch."

I frowned and gestured vaguely at all the wooden furniture around us. "Won't this do?"

She raised an amused eyebrow, "You do not know much about starting fires do you?"

"I leave it to the professionals," I shrugged. I did know how to start fires, actually. Just when I had gasoline. "How long?" I asked.

"Ten minutes." A simple reply. She looked past me and, after a second, nodded to who I assumed was Maerin before walking to some nearby chairs and smashing them on the wall. "If you want to be useful, gather some of the nearby cloths."

I let the others work on the torch while I scoured the area inside and outside the church for Peeler, just in case.

It only took eight minutes before everyone was ready to go, and by that time, the Huntress had a makeshift torch handle and wood shavings wrapped in the cloth on the head of it. For some reason, I thought she might have a flint and steel, but instead, she just produced a lighter, testing that the cloth caught before quickly putting it out.

I wasn't able to find Peeler.

I led our small group to the treeline before pulling out the stone, "Jeren. I have the Survivors. Where can I find you?"

"Shipwreck." He spoke quickly, and I could hear roaring and yelling in the background. "It's in a large clearing near the lake. Get over here fast if you can. We have an issue. The other Visitor isn't an ally."

I breathed in and closed my eyes, "Alright. It'll be a half hour. See you there."

"Stay safe." And there was no more noise on the other line.

We hadn't yet made it three steps into the forest when Paltra stopped. We all followed suit, and she looked from side to side, a deep scowl on her face.

"What is it?" Maerin asked, and I had to admit I was also more than a little curious.

"Listen," The Huntress hissed.

We all did.

"What do you hear?" she asked.

We all looked at each other, and Maerin cursed under her breath. "Nothing. I hear nothing."

Seeing our looks of confusion, Paltra turned to us. "When we first traveled. There were cicadas, crickets, and other manner of creature too."

And now there was nothing.

I paled and wondered: what exactly determined an enemy, and how much power would something gain from [No Free Kill] for killing an entire forest full of wildlife?
