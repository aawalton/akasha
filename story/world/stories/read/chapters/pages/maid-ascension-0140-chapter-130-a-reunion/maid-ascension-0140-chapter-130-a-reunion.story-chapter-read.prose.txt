The first thing Lira did the next morning was find Celene, who was standing in the servants' corridor watching a batch of new maids get familiar with their duties.

"Headmaid Celene. Do you have a few hours free this afternoon? There is someone I would like you to meet," Lira asked.

The headmaid frowned in confusion before her eyes went wide. She leaned over to whisper something. "M..."

Lira blocked the older woman's mouth with three fingers, looked left and right to make sure no one was watching them, then turned back to Celene and nodded.

Celene's eyebrows rose at Lira's reaction, but she said nothing more.

They left through one of the side gates in the early afternoon, and Lira led them on a longer route than was strictly necessary, down past the noble district, through a temple archway, and twice doubling back along market streets. She kept half an eye behind them the whole time, watching reflections in shop windows and listening for footsteps, making sure they weren't followed. By the time they reached a quiet stretch near the candlemaker's, Lira finally let herself relax.

She pulled Celene into an alley and removed her own cloak, handing it over to the older woman.

"Put this on over your dress." It was a summer cloak made of pale linen. "It is warm for the season, but it will hide what you are wearing underneath, and that is more important right now."

Celene's own gown was Fine cotton dyed a deep plum that had cost more than what most of the castle staff made in a month.

"You are more careful than I expected," she said, settling the cloak over her shoulders.

"That's how I managed to sneak the girl out of the palace." Lira shrugged. Free of her own cloak now, her oldest dress was revealed, the faded brown perfect for a commoner walking the streets of Halthar.

They walked another few minutes before the street opened onto the intersection of Crafting Street and Market Street, a few blocks from the Velora Scriptorium where she  grew up. Lira nodded toward a tea house that sat at one of the corners. She held the door for Celene and murmured a greeting to the woman behind the counter. The woman nodded once and waved them through toward the back.

Celene followed, though she was slightly uneasy heading into a strange place without any explanation from Lira. That worry vanished the moment she spotted Maren, who was wearing a grass-stained apron and grinding tea leaves.

"Maren..."

The former maid raised her head from her work at the sound of her name.

"Celene." Maren’s voice cracked.

Celene stopped in the doorway for half a breath, as though she needed the moment to be certain her eyes were not lying to her, and then she crossed the room and pulled the girl into her arms.

"Oh, Maren. Maren, you are safe. You are truly safe."

"Of course I am safe," Maren's voice came out dampened, pressed into Celene's chest. "I sent you a letter with Lira, didn't I?"

"I know," said Celene, and Lira noticed that the older woman had tears in her eyes. "But a letter isn't the same as seeing you."

Celene pulled back just far enough to hold the girl's face in both hands, studying her the way a mother studies a child returned from war.

They settled at the small table in the back room and started catching up. Celene asked whether Maren was eating and sleeping well, whether her new work suited her. Lira sat a little apart, watching them, feeling content.

"Your hair," Celene said, catching a strand between her fingers. "Blonde suits you more than I expected it to."

Maren made a face. "Lira forced me into it. Said the guards would recognize me otherwise."

"That's clever."

"I know..." Maren sighed, tucking the pale strand behind her ear with visible reluctance. "I still miss being a brunette."

"You should be safe by now regardless," Celene said. "The king decreed it so."

"She will never be safe while Kavard still lives," Lira said quietly.

Celene turned, and whatever she saw in Lira's eyes in that moment made her go still.

"I suppose that is true," the headmaid said at last.

Lira left Celene and Maren to their own reunion, slipping out past the curtain and murmuring a word of thanks to the woman behind the counter. The afternoon had turned so warm that Lira was glad to have shed her cloak.

It did not take long before she reached her destination.

Pallan was behind his desk when she found him, a ledger open in front of him and a cup of tea at his elbow.

"There she is." He did not look up right away, though his voice held the particular warmth he reserved only for her. "I was starting to think you had forgotten about your old uncle entirely."

"Never." Lira dropped into the chair across from him. "I know you would come knocking at the palace gate if I didn't show my face every now and then."

"You know it," Pallan said.

"So how is Maren settling into the new job?" Lira asked.

"Better than I expected. The other girls like her a lot. She has a gift for making people feel at ease, which would have been a great quality to have as a spy or a scammer. Sadly, she doesn't have the temperament for our line of work."

He finally set the quill down and looked at her properly, and whatever he saw in her face made the humor vanish. "But you did not come here to ask after Maren, didn't you?"

"No," Lira admitted. "I did not."

So she told him.

She told him about Taren's promotion, Harwin's knighthood, the marriage happening on the spot, and Cormenfield shifting hands. She told him about the little chat she had had in Celene's office, and the name Aric had dropped at the end of it, Kenth Kelroth, the new Master of Coin, how her face had gone pale enough for the prince to notice. She told him what she had been forced to admit afterward, Harwin's ledgers, Kelroth's acquisition of gambling dens, the nobles' debts, all of it true statements, but not enough to fully put the prince at ease. She told him about Aric's offer, the whole truth in exchange for a place at his side, and the month she had been given to decide.

Pallan listened without interrupting, his fingers steepled beneath his chin, considering every word she said.

But Lira wasn't done. She told him about Lord Taren's death. About watching it happen from her tower window as Estophon walked him out into the dark and pushed him over the edge. She left out the part where she had used magic to witness it happen, claiming only that she had been looking out a window and had accidentally seen what she had seen.

Finally, she told him about the investigation, the truth stone on the table. She detailed the questions she had been asked, and the list of names, with hers sitting fifth upon it.

When she finished, Pallan was quiet for a long moment.

"Lira, dear," he finally said. "I sent you to the palace so you would get the chance to experience a normal life for a while. Make friends who are not scammers or thieves. Fall in love with a footman or a guard. Experience a heartbreak like any girl your age should."

He let out a long sigh. "You were supposed to send me gossip about fashion trends, or maybe rumors of an affair or two, not get involved in high-level state intrigue. But I suppose I should have seen this coming. You always have been an overachiever."

"Sorry..." Lira lowered her head.

"Never apologize for being yourself," Pallan said, shaking his head.

He reached for his tea, found it cold, and set it aside without drinking it.

"So, you need help with your extraction?" he finally asked.

"What?"

"Your way out. You need new papers, coin, a destination far enough away that none of this would follow you there." He said it plainly, though Lira knew him well enough to see he was forcing himself to be practical.

"Are you not going to suggest I throw my lot in with Prince Aric instead?" asked Lira.

"I hope you are not sincerely considering it. You do understand that the first order he would give you, the very first, would be to help him dismantle my entire operation."

Lira blinked.

She had carried so many secrets, weighed so many risks against each other, that this particular one had never once crossed her mind. Of course Aric would use her against Pallan, well, not Pallan specifically, but whoever Lira owed her loyalty to.

"I had not considered that," she admitted.

"Of course you had not." Pallan's smile was faint, almost sad. "You might think that his healing of a few children made him a good man. Yet a better use of his time would have been doing surprise inspections of noble estates."

Of course Pallan would see himself in conflict with Aric specifically. Aric was a lawful man down to the bone, the kind of lawful man who left the women to work in Salvek's brothel because he had the right paperwork. Pallan, on the other hand, built his fortune by stepping over those same laws. It did not matter that Pallan's soup kitchens had put more food into hungry stomachs this year than the crown's ever had. Aric would not weigh it that way. He could not. The law was the law, and Pallan broke it daily, gladly, and unashamedly.

"So you think I should leave. Pack up my things and head to the Magari Republic."

Pallan shrugged.

"It's up to you to decide, niece. Whether you vanish or stay, I will always support you."
