It was only when the girls approached the town Elynara realized something must have happened.

The town looked normal at first. Familiar rooftops, the worn but sturdy wall extending to both sides, the business of the main path, and the low hum of market noise carrying faintly through the early afternoon haze.

But the gate stood open wider than usual.

And there were more guards.

Three of them stood in view. One leaned near the main arch, another patrolled just inside the walls, and the third – Elynara squinted – waved lazily from under the shade of a narrow overhang.

It was Josh.

Her step slowed.

That he had shown up at all was already strange. That he was flanked by two others – equally alert, with actual posture – sent a ripple of unease across her shoulders.

She glanced at Mina.

The girl hadn’t said anything, but her brow was furrowed. She didn’t seem upset or scared, but was clearly trying to work it out like Elynara was.

Maybe it wasn’t as bad as she thought?

The gates weren’t closed, so it wasn’t an attack the town of Oltenar was fearing.

What was the reason then?

Starpuff drifted closer to her shoulder, unusually quiet.

Mina finally turned her face to her and seeing her confusion only shook her head.

“I don’t know what happened either.” she said. “But what I do know is that the orphanage might have more kids to take care of soon…”

Elynara’s heart wrenched at that and she reached for Mina’s hand without thinking. The smaller fingers curled back around hers with a familiar firmness.

Maybe Josh will have the answers.

They continued ahead, now a little more hurried.

Josh spotted them when they were halfway across the clearing. His posture straightened immediately, and the lazy wave turned into a more sheepish kind of salute.

One of the other guards looked over, then away again with a faint nod. The third stayed by the gate, gaze sharp but not unfriendly.

She hesitated, then looked between the gate and the buildings ahead. The increased activity beyond it also became clear, and muted voices bled through with the wind.

But before Elynara could take it all in, Josh stepped forward with a grin far too pleased with itself.

“Well, would you look at that,” he said, planting his weight on one leg and motioning toward Mina with a loose wave. “Found yourself a little duckling, did you?”

Elynara blinked once. Her mouth opened, then closed. She deflated like a kicked wineskin, cheeks puffing up with the sharp breath she didn’t want to waste on a retort. Her grip shifted subtly on her staff.

Mina tilted her head, clearly confused.

Josh continued smiling.

Elynara tightened her jaw, raised her staff a finger’s width from the ground, then thought better of it. Her eyes narrowed as she let out a long, slow exhale through her nose.

“You’re lucky that I have other things to worry about now.”

“I live a blessed life.”

That grin of his didn’t budge, but Elynara couldn’t deny it had worked. Her chest wasn’t so tight anymore, and the rising thread of worry curling up her spine had been cut clean. She hated that he was effective.

Starpuff wobbled near her ear, visibly amused. Probably planning something worse for later.

Elynara gave the snarky guard a pointed look, then gestured between them.

“Josh, Mina. Mina, Josh.”

“I know him,” Mina said before he could offer a hand.

“You do?”

“He sneaks pastries out the back door when he thinks Sister Lyra isn’t watching.”

Josh winced.

“In my defense, I always say thank you.”

Elynara smiled sweetly.

“Apologize properly or I’ll have to consider setting your hair on fire right here.”

His eyes widened just a bit too dramatically.

“Forgive me, young miss. I am deeply, deeply sorry for implying you were poultry.”

Mina tilted her head.

“I don’t know what that means.”

“It means,” Elynara said, nudging him lightly with the staff, “that his kneecaps are safe for now.”

Josh stood upright again, tone relaxing.

“You probably want to hear about the noise, yeah?”

Elynara nodded, expression steady now, her curiosity hadn’t faded.

“So, what actually happened?”

“Not much,” he replied, voice turning just serious enough to match the air. “The town is fine, but there has been a mess in one of the supply villages. Mine collapsed. Two people dead, and a bunch more hurt.”

Mina’s shoulders tensed beside her, and hand squeezed a little tighter in hers.

“They broke through into deeper caverns. Old ones. No one knew they were there. Some deep-dwelling things got stirred up, but nothing got out. Monsters, maybe. Nobody’s clear on what, exactly.”

Elynara felt a chill flicker across her neck.

“Are they coming this way?”

“No signs of it, the mines are off-limits though. We’ve got more guards at the gates just in case, but it’s precaution. The problem is still local.”

She glanced around again.

“Then why does it feel like the town’s bracing for something?”

Josh scratched his neck, gaze drifting toward the far buildings.

“Families live out there. People here have brothers, sisters, cousins working those mines or living just around there. They’re scared. And now a bunch of them are here waiting for word. Or worse, waiting to hear what has already passed.”

Elynara swallowed.

“Right.”

“They’ve sent a team out to help with repairs and healing. Some adventurers went too. Gonna try and map the caverns while they’re at it.”

“Did anyone say what the monsters were?”

“No. Just that they weren’t seen before.”

Her fingers curled slightly at that. She didn’t like unknowns, but perhaps she wouldn’t have recognized the name even if she heard it. There was still more she didn’t know about the world and its inhabitants than she did, after all.

“Thanks,” she said.

Josh shrugged.

“You would have found out soon enough.”

Elynara let out a breath. It wasn’t good news. But it wasn’t the worst kind either.

Then, with a grin breaking back onto his face, Josh added:

“Still a worrywart, I see.”

Mina laughed once.

Josh shot her a victorious wink.

“She’s always like this, sticking her nose everywhere.”

Elynara sighed, one hand pressing to her forehead.

“I regret everything.”

“No refunds,” Josh said brightly, before waving them through.

They stepped past him, leaving the open gates behind. The air still buzzed with tension, but her steps didn’t feel heavy anymore. She glanced sideways as Mina bumped their shoulders lightly together.

“You okay?” she asked.

“Yeah,” Mina replied. “Are you?”

Elynara didn’t answer at first.

Then she smiled, faint but honest.

“Maybe I worried needlessly.”

Starpuff zipped down from above, pinged lightly off her hair, and vanished upward again with a satisfied flick.

Of course, it just had to disagree.

The familiar layout of Oltenar stretched ahead, but the streets felt busier. With more feet on the road, more carts resting on side paths, more small clusters of conversation that hushed when passed.

Elynara glanced sideways at Mina, who was adjusting her bag strap while Pufflet spun lazy circles under her feet.

“Didn’t expect to hear about the mines today,” she said.

“Neither did I,” Mina replied. “But it makes sense. Everyone’s been tense.”

They moved past a row of seated villagers, most of them holding small bundles or shallow cups of water. A few nodded in greeting. One smiled through Starpuff pulling off spirals in the air at them, without knowing what the weird little invisible green puff was doing in-between.

Elynara returned the smile.

Maybe she had worried too quickly. But worry was just care with a head start. She could live with that.

Elynara slowed her pace enough to take everything in. The threads of tension ran low but tight. People were holding it in.

She leaned down slightly toward Mina.

“Do you want to stop by the chapel first? Check in with Alaric?”

Mina shook her head, focused.

“We go to the guild together.”

“Alright then.”

They slipped through the crowd without much trouble. Most people were too busy to notice them beyond a glance. The voices were low, the kind used when talking about things too heavy for open words.

Still, no one cried and there was no crying or shouting. Only tired faces, sunburned brows, and quiet urgency.

The town looked like it had stayed whole.

Elynara exhaled softly, her fingers relaxing their grip on the strap of her satchel. Her heartbeat wasn’t racing anymore. The worst of the fear had passed with every careful step that didn't stumble into disaster.

They reached the guild door before long.

It creaked open with the same familiar wooden groan, and for a moment, that alone brought comfort.

Then she stepped inside and stopped, weirded out.

The guild was… empty.

She blinked.

The usual background hum of voices, boots, and banter was missing. It might not have been the most noisy of places, but there were always at least some people around whenever she popped by.

But now?

No clatter of chairs. No laughter bouncing off the walls. The mission board sat undisturbed, every slip in perfect Alicia-arranged order. The long table was cleared. No gear. No muddy cloaks. No half-dented mugs.

Only two figures remained.

Marcus leaned back against the far wall, arms crossed and eyes half-lidded, like he was trying to pretend the silence was by design. Alicia, on the other hand, stood behind the counter, pen still in hand but unmoving.

Her eyes had already locked onto them.

No. Onto Elynara.

She gulped at that.

Alicia’s gaze sparkled with precision, and something else that hovered between interest and expectation.

“Uh oh,” Elynara muttered.

Mina looked up. “What?”

“I’m being looked at.”

“You’re always being looked at.”

“Not like this.”

Alicia smiled faintly, a sparkle in her eyes. A knowing curve of the lips that suggested she had been waiting for something and now had it firmly within reach.

Elynara considered turning around.

Marcus didn’t move, but his eyes opened a bit more. Just enough to confirm he was awake and watching.

Elynara inhaled.

“Too late to pretend we’re lost?”

Mina smirked.

“Absolutely.”

Well, if she was going to walk into a trap, she might as well do it properly. She squared her shoulders and stepped forward.

Only Alicia’s calm professionalism and Marcus’s rough, unreadable stare kept Elynara from immediately blurting out the question she really wanted to ask.

So instead, they went through the motions.

Alicia welcomed them in with a nod. Marcus gave a quiet grunt that passed for acknowledgment. Elynara stepped forward and eased her satchel onto the front counter, giving it a pat like she was settling in for a proper debrief.

“You’re back, how would it go?” Alicia asked, voice mild.

Elynara walked them through the entire trip, showing her notes and explaining how she went from shrine to shrine, detailing the patterns she had noticed, the paths she had followed, and the adjustments she made on the fly.

Mina chimed in here and there about the parts she helped with, but also ones that involved her carving charms or Pufflet falling off things, which got her a smile from Alicia.

Elynara then explained how while some shrines still had the spirits hanging around, over half were empty of spirits and entirely hollow.

That earned a long silence from Alicia and a muttered curse from Marcus that sounded suspiciously like “dead gods above.”

Alicia’s expression remained measured, but the weariness behind her eyes eased, just slightly.

“And I promised Rina,” Elynara added, reaching back into her bag, “that I would ask the guild to distribute the updated map to nearby farms.” She pulled out a small scroll, tied with twine, and a sealed jar. “Also, these are her well-wishes.”

Alicia accepted both, lips twitching at the sight of the jar.

“She sent cookies?”

“She sends her regards,” Elynara said diplomatically.

Mina crossed her arms. “And her judgment.”

Marcus snorted, amused.

They didn’t linger long on the report’s gloom. Elynara laid out everything she had learned about mana interaction – how spirits responded only to live mana, not ambient flow. And which offerings mattered and why.

“It’s not all bad,” Elynara added quickly. “I found two unlisted sites where the spirits are still active. And I’ve been learning how to offer mana directly.”

Alicia looked up at that.

“Yes?”

“Mostly using unformed mana. Mist worked best. Scattered mana generally does, but they also like freshly charged items, like charms. Has to be live mana though.”

Marcus straightened slightly.

“Mist?”

“I laced a fine water mist with raw mana and scattered it. The effect was immediate. Spirits swarmed to it, and it made them visible even to Mina. At least briefly.”

Mina nodded once, more solemn now.

“It was beautiful.”

“It has to be live mana, though?” Alicia asked.

“Yeah. Ambient doesn’t do much. And stored in old items without intent, it fades too fast or turns into ‘not food’ territory. That’s the complication.”

“There always is some.”

Still, Alicia’s tone didn’t carry frustration. Only consideration. She glanced at the notes Elynara had already passed over, then tucked them aside.

“You’ve done more than we asked for.”

“It’s not enough yet.”

“It’s more than anyone else has done in years.”

Elynara didn’t argue.

A moment later, Alicia slid her pay across the table in a neat cloth pouch. It landed with a light clink.

Elynara palmed a smaller pouch from her side pocket, already prepared, and slipped it into Mina’s hand while she was distracted.

Mina blinked. “I didn’t–”

“You helped.”

“But–”

“No arguing,” Elynara said, already pivoting. “Anyway–” her gaze shifted sharply toward Alicia “–where is everyone? I heard about the mines from Josh, but the guild is practically empty.”

And there it was.

The spark in Alicia’s eyes didn’t fade.

It sharpened.

Marcus stretched one arm across his chest like he’d been waiting for this exact moment.

Of course they were.

It was Marcus who finally explained the rest.

He rolled his shoulders, let out a breath, and tilted his head toward the back wall like the words might weigh less if they bounced off the stone first.

“Nothing new, really,” he said. “Collapsed mine, opened caverns. Few monsters pushed out. Scouts went in. Couple dead, several wounded.”

Elynara nodded slowly. She had heard most of it already.

“Josh mentioned that much.”

“Right. What he probably didn’t say is how many people we sent.”

She blinked.

“How many?”

Marcus held up a single hand.

“All of them.”

“…All?”

“All the available adventurers. Every last one.”

Elynara straightened a little.

“Wait, all?”

Her eyebrows rose. “All of them?”

“Well,” Marcus said, rubbing his jaw, “one guy stayed. But he broke his leg trying to outdrink a merchant two nights ago. Doesn’t count.”

Mina snorted.

Elynara blinked, then looked slowly back toward Alicia.

Alicia didn’t move. She only offered the faintest smile, the kind that looked polite until you noticed the spark behind it.

Starpuff drifted lower from the rafters, circling behind Elynara’s head like a lazy thought with bad timing.

She swallowed.

Elynara didn’t answer right away.

The glint in Alicia’s eyes was still there.

Now she understood what it meant.

She gulped.

“Oh.”

“Yes,” Alicia said, her voice perfectly even. “Oh.”

“So… that makes me the only guilder left.”

Marcus raised a brow.

“You catch on quick.”

“For now,” Alicia said, tone calm. “A few may return in the next week. But the majority will be scouting the tunnels or assisting repairs for a while.”

“And we’ve got people coming into town. More requests piling up. Families asking for help. Sick, injured, lost deliveries, broken fences, lost dogs, badly documented inventories, half-missing parcels–”

Marcus ticked off each item with a finger.

“Okay,” Elynara said quickly, “I get the picture.”

“You’ll be compensated.”

“That’s not what I–wait, how many dogs?”

“Three. One might be a pig.”

Elynara stared at the desk like it might sprout fangs.

“And you’re assigning all that to me?”

Alicia gave the faintest smile.

“In time.”

Elynara narrowed her eyes.

“Please define ‘in time.’”

“You will return to the church. Drop off your things. Eat something warm. And then return to the guild.” Alicia’s smile grew a fraction. “Fresh and springly.”

Elynara looked like someone had just kicked her in the optimism.

“I do not feel fresh,” she said flatly. “And I definitely do not feel springly.”

Behind her, Starpuff zipped overhead, spun in a sharp arc, and zipped around her staff in tight spirals before speeding away, like it was shot from a catapult, pulsing with unmistakable delight.

Pufflet blinked once, then yipped at the rafters for reasons known only to him.

Elynara rubbed her forehead and sighed.

Marcus raised a brow.

“Springly?”

“It’s a word now.”

Mina was already covering her mouth with one hand.

Elynara opened her mouth to protest, paused, then sighed.

“Fine. I’ll be springly.”

“Fresh and springly,” Alicia corrected.

“I take it back.”

Elynara let out a long breath and straightened, the corner of her mouth twitching upward despite herself. She adjusted her satchel strap, looked toward Mina, then back to Alicia.

“I’ll head out now, then.”

Alicia gave her one last nod, firm but warm.

“Thank you, Elynara.”

“Don’t thank me yet,” she muttered. “Wait until I chase down the maybe-pig.”

Alicia nodded once, fully composed.

“Good. Report here before noon tomorrow then.”

Elynara turned, muttering under her breath.

“I’m going to be haunted by that word.”

“Springly?” Marcus called.

“Stop saying it!”

Starpuff looped once more, smug in every wobble of light, while Mina tried her best to hold in her laugh.

The days ahead would be busy.

Yet somehow, she didn’t mind.
