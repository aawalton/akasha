The corridor below the reef was made of frames.

They descended through tilted rectangles of gilded wood, each one hanging in dark water that behaved like stage glass. It parted around boots and armor, left leather dry, and closed behind them with slow ripples full of half-finished brushstrokes.

Valdrias went first because he had the tower shield, the canvas mark, and the survival instincts of a very sincere door.

His new shield glowed enough to turn the nearest frames into mirrors. Kage could see the whole party reflected in warped gold leaf. Ten players walking down through a drowned museum beneath a frozen storm, following paint stains on three wrists.

Normal Tuesday, apparently.

“Step only where Valdrias steps,” Kage said. “If a frame bends, stop.”

Jax followed behind Valdrias, sword out, plate polished to a hard shine. “I hate that we’re using ‘frame bends’ like a normal hazard callout.”

Zara moved two steps behind him, robes dark blue and silver, frost-threaded hems brushing against water that refused to cling. “You prefer ‘floor explodes’?”

“Floor explodes is honest.”

Finn’s hawk tried to fly ahead, then banked sharply and landed on his shoulder. The bird’s talons gripped his leather with visible annoyance.

Finn swallowed. “There’s no air current down here.”

Kage glanced back.

Finn had gone pale in the black water light. The open sky kid had entered a tunnel made from drowned paintings and sentences. Bad matchup. Still, his eyes kept moving. Edges. Shadows. Frame spacing. Tiny tremors in the hanging water.

Useful panic.

“What do you see?” Kage asked.

Finn blinked. “Uh. The frames aren’t random. They’re turning a little before we step on them.”

“Good.”

Finn nodded fast. His breathing steadied by half a notch.

Lily gave Kage a look from behind him. Warm. Annoyingly perceptive. The kind of look that said she noticed him doing something almost decent and planned to remember it for later.

Kage looked forward.

Absolutely tragic. One supportive sentence and suddenly I’m running a charity for anxious rangers.

The next frame sagged beneath Valdrias’s boot.

His chalk-white mark flared across his hand where it gripped the shield. Gray dust spilled from his knuckles, sank into the gold frame, and turned the sagging rectangle rigid.

Valdrias stepped fully onto it. “It holds.”

“Because you told it to,” Zara said.

Valdrias considered that with grave attention. “I did not speak.”

“She means the mark,” Lily said gently.

“Ahh.”

Zherdev’s cape drifted behind him in royal purple and orange, somehow catching a dramatic underwater breeze that affected nothing else in the dungeon. One shoulder burned with magma-veined plate. The other leaked shadow-smoke.

He peered over the edge of the frame path. “If I fall, who catches me?”

“Gravity,” Kage said.

“Gravity and I have a bad history.”

“Then make peace.”

Vami laughed from the rear, moving easily despite the darkness. His white armor had dimmed after the behemoth fight, but faint Lux lines still crawled under the leather seams. “If you fall, yell. I’ll watch.”

The corridor narrowed.

Gold frames became thinner. The dark water lost its painted reflections. The storm above faded into a gray smear, then vanished. The reef, the broken ship, the frozen rain, all of it peeled away behind them like a backdrop being pulled offstage.

Only the frames remained.

Then the frames began dissolving too.

First the carved corners unraveled into curved letters. Then the gilded sides broke into sentences, each one hanging in the dark with spaces where nouns should have been. The path beneath Valdrias became less wood with every step. Less object. More instruction.

A line formed beside Kage’s shoulder in pale script.

below the wet draft, a margin was left for the hunger that would keep the frame sealed

Jax stopped. “Please tell me everyone sees the creepy sentence.”

“Sadly,” Zara said.

Lily raised a hand. “I see it.”

Finn nodded too quickly. “Same.”

Kage watched the line twist.

The words existed as environmental description, a design note written into the room because the room had run out of walls.

So the dungeon is narrating its own bad idea.

More lines appeared as they descended.

it should wait where the light fails

it should coil around trespass and return it to silence

it should swallow the unfinished, for the unfinished must remain below

“Should,” Zara said.

Kage glanced at her.

She pointed at the nearest sentence. “It keeps saying should.”

Ren answered before Kage did. “Draft language.”

Zara’s eyes moved over him. “You read design notes now?”

“I read posture,” Ren said. “This place has the posture of something incomplete.”

Kage hated how much he liked that answer.

Vami leaned sideways to read a line upside down. “So we’re walking into a prototype boss?”

“Probably,” Kage said.

Jax sighed. “Prototype sounds weaker.”

“In games, prototype means nobody balanced it.”

The final frame under Valdrias’s boot collapsed into letters.

For one breath, the entire path vanished.

Valdrias dropped half an inch into blackness. His shield slammed down. Chalk burst from his marked hand and spread across nothing. The letters beneath him compressed into a solid white disk.

He froze there, knees bent, shield planted, breathing hard.

Kage stopped with one foot on the last surviving strip of frame. Behind him, the others halted in a messy line.

The darkness ahead widened.

No walls. No floor. No ceiling. No… anything, really. Just blackness filled with floating narrative lines, all orbiting a vast circular space. The lines formed a dome by implication. They curved in rings, layered over each other, sentence over sentence, until the empty air gained shape.

At the center floated the leviathan.

Kage’s first thought was scale.

The second was teeth.

The thing coiled through the middle of the word-dome, longer than a city street and broader than any creature had a right to be in a sensible dungeon. Its body had the unfinished look of a sketch abandoned after the artist decided the first draft had become expensive. Some sections held scales made of parchment plates. Other sections dissolved into black strokes. Fins stretched from its sides as long, ragged ribbons of ink, each edge trembling between cloth, shadow, and language.

Its head hung low, tucked toward the center of its own coil.

A closed eye the size of a cart wheel sat under a brow of layered script.

Around it, the narrative lines moved slowly.

it should be vast enough to make courage small

it should keep the final door beneath its tongue

it should punish the hand that edits the deep

Kage read that last one. His Author’s Ink stirred around his arm.

Thin black letters lifted from his skin in a slow orbit, eager in the way a knife was eager when set near rope.

Of course it has an anti-me clause.

It felt like standing in front of an opponent who might actually cut him if he blinked wrong. Kage clicked his tongue.

Valdrias stood alone on the chalk-white disk ahead of them. The floor existed only around him, and the edge faded into black letters that refused to carry weight.

Kage extended Mumyo and tapped the boundary with the flat of the blade.

The sword met resistance for three inches, then sank through drifting text.

“Floor is mark-generated,” Kage said. “Canvas marks make footing. Everyone with chalk, spread.”

Lily stepped from the frame path onto Valdrias’s disk. The floor brightened under her boot. She knelt and pressed her marked palm to the surface. Gray dust spilled outward.

Jax followed to the left, placing his hand down with a grimace. Zara took the right with the expression of someone filing a complaint against grammar. Finn moved slower, eyes flicking to the spaces between sentences, then planted his palm near the rear.

Four more chalk zones spread.

The blackness compressed into a circular platform under them, pale gray at the edges and darker toward the center. The floor looked like packed charcoal over glass. Words still moved beneath it, trapped like fish under winter ice.

Kage stepped onto the new floor.

It held.

“Five anchors,” Zara said. “Radius overlap gives stable platform.”

Kage crouched and touched the edge where Lily’s chalk zone met Finn’s. The texture changed under his fingers. Stable where marks overlapped. Soft where a single mark thinned. Past the edge, the sentence-dome waited with all the kindness of a falling elevator.

“Stay spread,” Kage said. “Overlap your circles. If one of you moves, call it first.”

Jax looked at the sleeping leviathan. “So the arena exists as long as we keep touching the floor.”

Kage pointed with Mumyo. “Valdrias center-front. Jax rear-left. Lily rear. Zara right. Finn left, mobile only when another mark covers your spot.”

Finn nodded. “So I can move, but the floor has to be handed off.”

“Exactly. Treat the platform like a group project where gravity grades everyone.”

“That’s horrifyingly clear,” Lily said.

“Good.”

Julie stepped forward next.

Her geometric black lines remained crisp around her wrists and hands, angular and exact. Where the Canvas marks made crude reality, Julie’s mark looked like it belonged to a drafter with a ruler.

She stared at the arena floor, then past it.

“Oh,” she said softly.

Kage looked at her. “What do you see?”

“Structure.” She lifted her hand. “Under the floor. Around the dome. There are support paths, I think. Spokes, rings, anchor ribs.”

“Show me a cheap test.”

Julie gave him a quick smile. “Cheap test. Love those.”

Vami opened his mouth.

Julie pointed at him without looking. “You are the reason I know what expensive tests look like.”

He closed his mouth.

Julie drew two fingers through the air between Lily’s chalk zone and Finn’s. Black ink-lines snapped into existence, precise as a drafting mark on vellum. The line touched both zones, then thickened into a narrow bridge of translucent gray.

Julie flexed her fingers. The black lines on her wrist dimmed by a small amount. Kage stepped onto the bridge. Solid. Narrow. Good enough for movement and bad for panic.

“Can you make cover?”

Julie turned her hand and drew a short vertical plane near Valdrias’s position. A low barricade appeared, angled toward the leviathan. It resembled clear glass with inked edges, unfinished and functional.

The barricade held for three seconds, flickered, then settled.

Julie frowned. “Cover drains faster unless it attaches to a support rib.”

“Use only on attacks we can’t dodge.”

“Sure.”

She glanced toward the leviathan.

Her eyes narrowed, healer awareness shifting into raid-mechanic attention. She raised her hand and sketched a small outline around a drifting fin near the creature’s flank. The black geometric lines leapt from her fingertips and wrapped around part of the fin like wireframe.

The fin gained definition.

Scales resolved. A faint targetable shimmer crawled over the outlined section, then faded.

The leviathan’s closed eye twitched.

Kage’s hand moved to Mumyo’s hilt.

“Stop there.”

Ren studied the outlined fin with sharp attention. “Its body lacks consistent range. During combat, some parts may exist only after she defines them.”

“Great,” Jax said. “A boss with conditional anatomy.”

Zara folded her arms. “That explains the large health pool we are absolutely going to see.”

“We haven’t seen its health,” Finn said.

Zara looked at the leviathan. “Finn.”

“Right. Stupid sentence. Sorry.”

Kage turned to the rare-marked players.

Vami’s wet paint mark had shifted from a downward pull to a sideways drag. The colors on his wrist coiled toward three huge gilded frames embedded in the word-dome around the arena. Ren’s mark did the same in calmer streams of deep blue and amber. Zherdev’s mark glowed with heroic overcommitment.

The three frames hung at different heights around the sleeping leviathan. Empty interiors. Massive carved borders. Each one sat on a faint circular rail made of gold dust and punctuation marks.

Kage pointed. “Rare marks. Touch those frames.”

Vami moved first, because telling him to interact with a dangerous unknown object was apparently an invitation to do it immediately.

Julie caught his sleeve.

He paused.

She smiled, and it had edges. “If it explodes, I’m telling everyone your favourite out-of-game activity.”

Vami considered his priorities as sweat formed on his face. “I’ll… I'll make sure it doesn't explode. Yeah. It won't.”

Julie continued smiling, unhurried.

Vami reached the nearest frame and touched the gold leaf with his marked hand. Wet white paint crawled from his wrist into the frame. The massive rectangle slid along its rail with a groan like old wood bending in a cold shrine.

Vami’s grin spread. “Oh. That’s nice.”

The frame rotated around the leviathan. Wherever its empty center passed over the sleeping creature, that section of body sharpened into finished detail. Scales gained color. A fin became targetable. Then the frame moved on, and the detail blurred again.

Kage watched the effect.

“Frame crops the boss into reality,” he said. “Probably target exposure or shield mechanic.”

Zara’s mouth tightened. “Probably.”

“You want certainty, ask the whale.”

“I would rather parse logs.”

Ren moved to the second frame with measured steps. He touched the gilded border. His paint flowed in deep blue and amber, less flashy than Vami’s but steadier. The frame obeyed with clean precision, sliding along its rail and stopping exactly where Ren placed it.

Ren’s gaze tracked the frame’s angle, then the body section it clarified. “The frame can hold a target zone. It resists drift if my hand stays on it.”

“Can you lock it?”

Ren shifted his wrist. Paint thickened over the frame corner. The rectangle clicked into place for a breath, then began sliding again.

“Briefly,” Ren said. “Costs pigment.”

Zherdev lumbered toward the third frame with his greatsword bumping against his back and his cape streaming like a patriotic accident.

“Allow me. Zherdev the Magnificent has extensive experience moving expensive art.”

Zara stared. “In games or after buying it IRL?”

“Yes.”

He slapped his glowing paint hand onto the third frame.

The frame shot ten feet along the rail, clipped a floating sentence, and sent a ripple through the dome. Jax’s floor edge softened under his boot.

“Zherdev,” Kage said.

“I have learned the frame has acceleration.”

“Learn slower.”

“Wisdom accepted.”

Zherdev braced both boots, grabbed the frame with both hands, and hauled it back with brute force. His wet paint mark flared in crimson, cobalt, and gold. The frame settled into place with the grace of a chandelier landing on a dining table.

A new set of objects unfolded from the word-dome.

Three shallow basins rose along the arena’s outer ring, each made from black stone and gilded rim. One filled with dark blue paint that moved like deep water. One held a pale, pearly white. The third held red so dense it seemed to tug at the eye.

[Pigment Wells revealed.]

[Pigment may be harvested by marked players.]

[Unmixed Pigment destabilizes after 30 seconds unless applied to a Frame or Palette Node.]

Vami dipped two fingers into the white basin.

Paint climbed his marked hand and hung from his knuckles in glowing drops. The drops stretched downward, then floated back up and dissolved into light.

[Pigment Harvested: Pale Wake.]

Ren touched the blue basin.

[Pigment Harvested: Cobalt Depth.]

Zherdev went for the red immediately.

Kage stared at him.

Zherdev froze with his hand one inch above the basin. “What?”

“Do not drink it.”

“I was not going to.”

Julie tilted her head. “You looked committed.”

“I appreciate fine liquids.”

“It says pigment,” Zara said.

“It is red and powerful.”

Kage rubbed the bridge of his nose. “Harvest it. Apply it to a frame. Try to avoid inventing a new medical category.”

Zherdev scooped the red. It surged up his arm, bright enough to clash with every other glowing disaster on his character model.

[Pigment Harvested: Vermilion Hunger.]

He stared at it with reverent horror. “This is extremely my brand.”

The three marked players tested the basins under Kage’s direction. Pigment could be carried along the outer ring. Applied to a frame, it dyed the border and changed the section of leviathan visible through it. Blue made the framed area clearer and slower, the strokes settling into distinct layers. White caused hidden lines to glow beneath scales. Red made the framed section pulse, aggressive and vivid, like a wound waiting for permission.

Zara took notes in the air with a gesture. “Blue reveals structure. White reveals hidden script. Red amplifies something violent.”

“Damage phase, interrupt phase, vulnerability phase,” Jax guessed.

“Maybe,” Kage said.

Vami rotated his frame with white pigment applied. The empty center passed over the leviathan’s closed eye. Under the frame, a sentence glowed beneath the eyelid.

the eye should open only when the arena remembers weight

Kage read it aloud.

Everyone stopped moving.

The words remained, bright and calm.

Jax looked down at the chalk floor. “Arena remembers weight. That means us.”

“Common marks made the floor,” Lily said.

Finn’s hawk ruffled its feathers. Finn stared at the outer dome. “The lines changed after the third frame moved. They’re circling faster.”

Kage tracked them.

Finn was right. The narrative rings had tightened around the central coil. Earlier, the lines drifted like lazy smoke. Now they moved with rhythm. Slow, then quick, then slow again. A breath pattern.

Sleeping animations for a sentence monster. Lovely.

Kage checked his own resources.

[Author’s Ink: 43%]

Awen full. Author’s Ink under half. Enough for a small rescue. A terrible choice for rewriting a boss apparently designed to punish edits.

The orbiting letters around his arm tugged toward the glowing line under the leviathan’s framed eye.

He stepped closer.

The script in the dome reacted. A blank margin opened beside the sentence, thin and inviting. The kind of empty space that made bad decisions feel elegant.

Kage felt the cost through his skin.

Heavy. More than the edit that redirected the behemoth’s bite. Less than deleting the Glasscoil. Still ugly.

He pulled his hand back.

Zara watched him. “You can change that?”

“Probably.”

“Probably wake condition. Could prevent activation.”

“Could also turn the whole arena into soup.”

Jax looked at the floor. “Please avoid soup.”

Kage pointed at the leviathan’s surrounding lines. “There are two kinds of text here. Stage directions, like attacks. I can edit those. Environmental definitions, like wake conditions or what the boss is. Expensive. Core purpose lines feel worse.”

Ren’s eyes shifted to the sentence: it should punish the hand that edits the deep.

“That line refers to you,” Ren said.

“Yes.”

Vami leaned over from his frame. “Can you edit that one?”

“At current ink, we probably get a funeral with better punctuation.” Kage looked over the arena.

The mechanics had shape now.

Canvas marks created and maintained footing. Without them, the floor returned to uncompiled text. Julie’s Sketch mark connected support ribs, made bridges and cover, and defined unfinished hitboxes. Rare Pigment marks controlled frames, harvested color, and applied pigment to change what parts of the leviathan became real or vulnerable. Kage’s Author mark could edit active lines and maybe cheat mechanics, with the dungeon waiting to punch him for arrogance.

A complete raid arena, written by someone who hated clean tooltips. Kage almost respected it. Almost.

“Positions,” he said.

The party shifted.

Valdrias took center-front, shield planted. His chalk mark spread into a wide stable disk. The glow from Zherdev’s gift shield washed over the charcoal floor in steady pulses.

“You hold the middle,” Kage said. “If the boss moves, you make the floor under it real enough for the rest of us to stand.”

Valdrias nodded. “I will hold.”

Kage waited for the tyranny speech. Valdrias simply adjusted his stance and checked his shield angle.

“Jax, rear-left anchor. Help Lily if adds spawn.”

Jax moved without complaint. That remained weird. “Got it.”

“Lily, rear anchor. Keep yourself inside two chalk overlaps. If the floor drops, you call first, heal second.”

Lily nodded. “I can do that.”

“Zara, right anchor. You get frame callouts too. Anything color-coded, you yell before Vami jumps into it.”

Zara’s lips twitched. “Gladly.”

“Finn, left anchor and motion watch. Your job is drift, seams, line speed, anything that changes before the obvious thing changes.”

Finn took his position, looked out into the dome, and inhaled slowly. “Okay. I can track that.”

His hawk launched from his shoulder and glided in a tight circle over the chalk platform. Its wings beat against air that technically existed only because the arena had agreed to host birds.

“Julie,” Kage said.

“Bridges and cover, hitboxes when called,” she answered.

“Conserve mark. Anchor your lines to Canvas zones.”

“Vami, Ren, Zherdev. Frames and pigment. Vami handles fast swaps. Ren holds precise locks. Zherdev moves anything that jams.”

Zherdev saluted with the hand covered in Vermilion Hunger. Red droplets floated off his fingers like tiny murder lanterns. “I am the emergency art mover.”

Vami spun his dagger once. “And when it targets me?”

“Try your rotation after we see a mechanic. One test.”

Julie folded her arms. “I support this plan.”

Vami looked betrayed. “Et tu, sunshine?”

“You stood in three overlapping cleaves last dungeon because you wanted to ‘feel the math.’”

“It was educational.”

“For my blood pressure.”

Ren stayed at his frame, one hand on the gilded border, the other near his sword. He watched Kage through the corner of his eye. Kage felt the question there again. Old footwork. Old ghost.

He turned away.
