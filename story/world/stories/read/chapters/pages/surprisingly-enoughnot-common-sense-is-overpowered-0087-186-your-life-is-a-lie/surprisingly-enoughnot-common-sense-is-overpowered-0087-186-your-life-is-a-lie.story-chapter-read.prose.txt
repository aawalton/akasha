Ning’s journey continued.

From sects to secret realms, from ancient ruins to forbidden mountains, from tournaments to battlefields, his footprints spread far and wide. Not because he wanted to wander, but because he was never allowed to stay in one place peacefully for long.

The longer he walked, the more he could no longer deny one terrifying truth.

He had rizz.

An astronomical amount, to be precise.

At first, Ning tried to ignore it. After all, cultivators were usually good-looking. Beauty pills, spiritual qi, body refinement, and even surgery existed in this world. Being admired here and there was not strange.

But Ning’s situation had long since passed the level of “admired.”

It had become a calamity.

During his travels, the number of people who pursued him was frankly unreasonable. Mature women, young geniuses, righteous fairies, demonic witches, cold sword maidens, cheerful alchemists, lonely princesses, mysterious masked cultivators, and even one beast-taming saintess whose spirit fox was just as taken with him.

Worse yet, his charm did not seem restricted by gender.

At one point, a male sword cultivator had stared at him beneath the moon and said, “Brother Ning, your back looks very lonely.”

Ning immediately moved three steps away.

Looking at his peach blossom luck, some people might wonder why he complained. Was being loved not a blessing? Was being surrounded by beauties not the dream of countless young men? Was this not the kind of fortune that made ordinary cultivators beat their chests and curse the unfairness of heaven?

Those people did not understand.

Excess was just as bad as deficiency.

The problem was not that Ning hated them. That would have made things easier. If they had all been unreasonable demonesses trying to harvest his yang qi, he could have simply run, fought, negotiated, or reported them to the nearest righteous elder.

But many of them were sincere.

That made it worse.

If he chose one, the others would be hurt. If he rejected all of them, they would look heartbroken. If he married them all, as certain shameless old cultivators enthusiastically suggested, then Ning was fairly certain he would not survive past the wedding night.

He was a body cultivator, yes, but even he had limits.

And that was not even mentioning the demonesses.

That was another can of worms he did not want to open.

Ning did not know how any of this had happened. He was not even trying to flirt. Shit just happened.

It also did not help that the heavens seemed determined to throw him into situations specifically designed to ruin his peace. Ancient inheritances, secret trials, collapsing caves, sealed chambers, yin-yang formations, suspicious pink mist, Ning had seen enough to recognize the pattern before the incense even finished burning.

Twice already, he had been trapped in a sealed inheritance chamber with a female cultivator while an aphrodisiac formation was activated.

Twice.

That was already one time too many.

Fortunately, Ning was a cultivator who packed everything that could be packed. His storage ring contained antidote pills, calming pills, qi-stabilizing pills, emergency purification pills, and even a pill meant for explosions if the situation became truly unsalvageable.

With great determination and proper dosage, Ning survived both incidents cleanly.

Unfortunately, this only made the women involved fall for him even harder.

Apparently, “restraining oneself under aphrodisiac influence through sheer willpower and medical preparation” counted as peak husband material.

Ning had no words.

Some people might say, It should have been me, not him fr.

Those people had never been chased across three provinces by a lovesick demoness riding a black phoenix while shouting that his self-control had conquered her heart.

They did not understand the suffering of excessive peach blossom luck.

And as for why Ning was lamenting this fate right now?

That was simple.

His warhammer had just transformed into a beautiful young woman.

It was supposed to be a glorious event. His war hammer had advanced further, reaching the realm of a magical treasure. The heavenly tribulation for its advancement had been rough, but his good old reliable hammer had pulled through.

It should have been a happy occasion.

His warhammer had awakened its spirit.

She looked around sixteen or seventeen, with long black hair streaked by faint lines of lightning. Her eyes were bright gold, her skin seemed carved from warm jade, and black battle robes clung to her slender figure. Tiny arcs of thunder danced around her wrists and ankles like ornaments. Her beauty was fierce and wild, like a thunderstorm given human form.

The problem was that she could not return to her previous state.

“What do you mean you can’t return to your previous form?” Ning asked aloud.

“Ehe.” The girl looked away meekly. “While I was going through tribulation, I might have chosen to permanently stay in this form?”

Ning stared at her.

“Why?”

“Master, it’s because of you, okay?” his war hammer said, suddenly sounding wronged. “Recently, you have been neglecting me and meeting with those hussies. As your beloved weapon, I must protect you from all threats, including those hussies.”

She raised her fist, lightning crackling dramatically around her.

“From today onward, I will stay by your side forever. Whether you fight gods, demons, immortals, or annoying pretty women, I will smash them all for you. We are one body and one heart. Wherever you go, I go. Whoever you hit, I hit. Whoever dares separate us, I’ll cave their skull in.”

The surrounding spiritual light surged.

It was a miracle. A partner born from countless battles, a bond that crossed the boundary between metal and soul.

Ning’s face slowly became blank.

“How am I supposed to fight with you now?” he asked. “If enemies come right now, how am I supposed to smash them?”

“That’s simple,” she said brightly. “I will fight beside you.”

“With what weapon?”

“My fists.”

“You are my weapon.”

“Exactly. Now the weapon can punch by itself.”

Ning closed his eyes.

For the first time, even his common sense had been defeated.

Inside the illusion, the sky was breaking apart.

Great wounds opened across the heavens, and beyond them was not darkness, but emptiness. The sun had dimmed, the moon had shattered, rivers flowed backward into the sky, and entire continents trembled beneath the pressure of the approaching end.

Ning stood above the ruins of the final battlefield, his hammer cracked in his hand, his robes torn, his body covered in wounds. Behind him lay the corpses of gods, demons, ancient emperors, immortal beasts, and whatever other absurdly high-level beings the world had thrown at him.

This was the war to end all wars.

And Ji Ning had fought them all.

The Demon Emperor, who wanted to devour the nine heavens, the Ancient Dragon Sovereign was corrupted by the Abyssal Dao. The Moonless Saintess, who had sealed away the sun. And finally, the world-ending threat itself.

The Calamity Origin.

A monster born from resentment, karma, heavenly punishment, and the essence of nothingness.

Ning had defeated it.

Somehow.

Through schemes, desperate battles, ridiculous opportunities, sudden breakthroughs, old friends appearing at the last moment, enemies turning good after hearing one heartfelt sentence, and finally, the Primordial Origin Treasure activating when all hope seemed lost.

The world should have been saved.

The final enemy had fallen. The battlefield had gone quiet. Far below, the surviving cultivators cheered his name, their voices rising like waves.

“Ji Ning!”

“Ji Ning!”

“Ji Ning!”

The hero who saved the world.

The savior of all realms.

Ning stood above it all and looked at the broken sky.

For some reason, he felt no joy.

Only exhaustion.

The endless escalation of threats, one after another, had drained him more than any single battle. Still, Ning did not say something foolish like, It’s finally over. In his long life, he had learned that a Daoist cultivator named Murphy had once established a very terrifying law.

And as always, this world did not forget to bend backward and screw him over until the very end.

A soft clap rang out.

Ning turned.

A figure stood before him.

At least, it had the outline of a person.

It wore no crown, robe, armor, or even clothes for that matter. Its body seemed to be made of pale mist and ink, constantly shifting, never fully settling into one shape. Its face was the worst part. There were no eyes, no mouth, no nose, no features at all. Only a smooth blankness where a face should have been.

And yet, Ning felt it smiling.

“Excellent,” the faceless figure said, though it had no mouth.

Its voice was gentle and warm.

“Truly excellent. You reached the end.”

The figure raised one hand, and the world stopped breathing.

The shattered heavens froze. Rivers pouring upward into the sky hung motionless like silver chains. Mountains remained half-collapsed, stones suspended in the air. Far below, countless cultivators stood frozen mid-cheer, their mouths still open as they chanted his name.

Ji Ning.

Ji Ning.

Ji Ning.

Ning tightened his grip around the hammer.

“Who are you?”

The figure chuckled.

“A reasonable question. Still asking those, even now.”

It turned its blank face toward the frozen world below.

“You defeated the calamity. You avenged the dead. You protected your companions. You shattered the schemes of ancient monsters and stood above every young master, elder, saint, demon, and heavenly genius who once looked down on you.”

Its voice carried a strange fondness.

“Was it satisfying?”

Ning did not answer.

The faceless figure spread its arms.

“The broken engagement. The old soul in the earring. The miraculous inheritance. The tournament. The tragic enemies. The arrogant young masters. The sects that collapsed around you. The treasures that appeared when you needed them. The enemies who arrived at the perfect moment. The companions who believed in you. The crowds that shouted your name.”

The world trembled with each word.

“When your sworn brother on a long and terrifying quest turned out to have been a hidden girl all along. When your war hammer transformed into a beautiful maiden and proclaimed she would stay with you forever.”

The faceless figure’s voice brightened.

“It was me all along."

Ning’s eyes narrowed.

The figure stepped closer.

“I arranged the road beneath your feet. I placed the stones that cut you. I opened the doors you thought you discovered. I sharpened the enemies you thought you defeated. I gave you suffering when you needed growth and fortune when you needed hope.”

Its tone remained warm, but that warmth had become unbearable.

“You thought you were clever because you noticed the patterns. You mocked the clichés. You avoided trouble when you could and prepared when you could not. You called it common sense.”

The faceless figure leaned down slightly.

“But even that was part of the design. It was all according to plan."

The cracked hammer creaked in Ning’s hand.

"I am the alpha and omega, the beginning and the end. In a sense, Ning, I am your father for you are my creation.

The figure spoke warmly, then just as abruptly it turned cold.

“What can you do, Ning?”

The question rolled through the frozen heavens.

“What can a pawn do after learning the board was never his? What can a character do after seeing the hand that wrote him? What meaning does struggle have when the pit was dug for you, the ladder was placed for you, and even your desire to climb was anticipated?”

It raised one finger.

“You were never free. Choice was the prettiest illusion I gave you."

For the first time, Ning’s breathing slowed.

The faceless figure watched him silently.

This man, before it was its masterpiece.

And now, what it wanted most was to see the expression Ning would make after hearing the truth.

Surely, it would be sweet.

His deepest anxiety was that his life lacked true ownership. That the path he walked, the choices he made, the people he met, the agony he endured, the strength he gained, and even the cynical personality he used to survive it all had been meticulously arranged by something beyond him.

A transmigrator feared many things. But the absolute deepest dread was always this: What if crossing worlds hadn't made him free? What if it had only moved him into a flashier cage?

“Tsk. Do you think I don’t know that?” Ning groaned, running a hand through his hair. “I know it better than anyone.”

He looked out into the space where the frozen world and the cheering crowds had just been. Why had he transmigrated? Why this specific world? Why a reality so absurdly familiar, as if someone had taken every webnovel trope he knew and shaped everything around it?

“So,” the being asked, watching him closely. “What are you gonna do?”

“Just what I’ve always done.”

Because Ning had lived with this anxiety for a long time, he had naturally given it a lot of thought. He didn't want his next inner demon to be this crisis, so he tried to understand it. But alas, what you fear would always come true; he was still confronted by this fear in this illusion.

At this point, Ning understood why the illusion had taken this form. To Ning, someone like Xiao Fan was the clearest example of a person wrapped in fate, heaven-blessed, constantly thrown into dangers beyond reason, somehow always surviving, always profiting, always dragging storms behind him. A protagonist was the closest image Ning had for a life shaped by unseen hands.

So the tower had turned Ning into one.

Chosen. Favored. Watched. Controlled.

He wasn't a rogue variable; he was a piece on a board. If his entire existence from his very first breath to his inevitable end was already a pre-written script, then every calculation, every bit of paranoia, and every cautious step he’d ever taken to survive was a farce.

Ning thought of his first life, that ordinary world where people lived, worked, complained, hoped, failed, and died within the safe, suffocating boundaries of a routine. Had that life been any more "free" than this one? He hadn’t chosen his birth there either. He hadn’t chosen his genetics, his era, his economic limitations, or the unglamorous way death had eventually come for him.

Then, this life.

He hadn’t chosen the sect. He hadn’t chosen his spiritual root. He hadn’t even chosen the face he saw in the mirror. He had simply been dropped into the middle of a chaotic, high-stakes fantasy world with zero instructions and a target on his back.

But who in the history of existence had ever chosen their beginning?

A farmer did not choose his soil. A servant did not choose the coarse fabric of his robe. A disciple did not choose whether heaven granted him a supreme-grade talent or a crippled meridian. Even the so-called heaven-blessed geniuses didn't choose to be born under a sky filled with jealous eyes and sharpened knives.

The universe had never been fair. Not on Earth. Not here.

Perhaps true freedom had never meant choosing the board you played on. Perhaps it was simply finding joy in the smaller things, in the grit of the game itself.

So what if an unseen hand had arranged his beginning?

So what if the world were merely the imagination of some cosmic entity?

So what if the stage had been meticulously built, the arrogant young masters placed, the ancient inheritances hidden, the disasters timed, and the path entirely prepared?

He had still been the one walking until his legs shook. He had still been the one forced to think when panic would have been the easier option. He had chosen caution over pride, raw effort over useless complaint, and survival over dignity when dignity was too expensive to afford.

If his pain had been arranged by a script, it still fucking hurt.

If his absolute effort had been expected, it had still exhausted him to his very core.

If his choices had been perfectly predicted, he had still made them from the depths of his own bleeding heart.

No matter who designed the maze, the climb belonged to him. It had still been his adventure.

In the grand scheme of things, Ning knew he was completely insignificant, nothing more than a speck of dust drifting through a vast, uncaring universe. But who was to say that even if his will were completely untouched by others, his life would naturally possess meaning?

Meaning wasn't something others could give. It was something one understood and decided on their own.

Ning found genuine joy in cultivating, in unravelling the bizarre mysteries of this magical world, and in slowly building himself into someone stronger. If his circumstances were being monitored by a higher power, then so be it. At the end of the day, he lived his life doing exactly what he deemed meaningful. If he died on this road, then at least he died on his own terms. That was enough.

The faceless being stared at him, the shifting ink of its form settling into a steady rhythm. When it spoke, the terrifying echo was gone, replaced by an intimate clarity.

"Your fear has not vanished," the being observed softly. "But you have accepted this fear. You have woven it into the shape of who you are."

Ning let out a dry, breathless chuckle, crossing his arms. "Cut me some slack, okay? It's kind of hard to completely cure a deep-seated existential crisis with just a single shonen realization."

"That is true, I suppose," the being nodded, its featureless face tilting with something resembling warmth. "Then let us move forward."

Fear was not the absence of courage. If anything, courage only existed because fear did. Anyone could act when nothing was at stake. What mattered was taking a step forward when every instinct screamed at you to run.

And for Ning, this was the only choice.

The entity slowly extended its pale, misty hand.

[I am you]

Ning's eyes shone as he reached out and slammed his palm directly into his own reflection.

"We only ever had one real choice here. Fuck it, we ball."

[You are me]

As their fingers met, the ink and mist flowed into his own skin, completing the circle of his own consciousness.

[Know Thyself]

And the illusion shattered into a thousand pieces of light.
