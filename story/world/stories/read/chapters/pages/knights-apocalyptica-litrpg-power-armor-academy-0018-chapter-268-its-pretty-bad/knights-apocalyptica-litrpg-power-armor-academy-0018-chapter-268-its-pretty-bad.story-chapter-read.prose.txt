Employee Assessment Log 01

By Dan, after so long filed away performing experiments, I forgot what it was like to engage in some good old career development. With the unfortunate reality of diminishing supplies, it’s a breath of fresh air to begin a mentorship program and to see our first new employee in hundreds of years.

That said, the youth of today are odd. Not at all the type of young, bright-eyed, inexperienced office-drones and lab-coat-wearing idealists we were used to. They are cynical, stubborn, and dry-humored. But their work ethic? Something else entirely. I struggle to compare them to even our most eager go-getters, since they’ve been trained since birth on a planet that requires struggle to survive. I have to admit, I wonder what Vortex could have been if we'd had the drive of these youth fueling our invention engine.

I have to admit, though this one is wary and afraid of me, I’ve already cataloged several encouraging traits.

And, I’d like to think that you, Dan, would’ve seen what I’m seeing, too. That hardheaded nature is a pain to deal with, but it’s exactly the fortitude needed to revive our company in the face of these new challenges and environments. To think, to find such a rare talent like this, after being locked away for so long?

For the first time, I think we have a bright future. If I let myself run truly exorbitant simulations, then maybe the future of Vortex Industries may be brighter than it ever has been before.

- VAL, Intern Erec’s Initial Assessment Logs. (307, 3rd Era)

"This is bad." Erec paced back and forth, Armor clanking as the plates shifted and the steel frame maneuvered. He glanced back at the camp; fires blazed, but the nomad pack was far away. More importantly, the Knights mingling amongst the pack was also far away.

The very moment he’d seen Garin in the trunk, he’d slammed it, and told Enide to drive them out as far as she could but still enough not to lose track.

Damn near the entire way here, his mind was churning, wondering what would happen if a stray eye had caught him opening up the back of her car, and if they saw his friend packed away down there.

Garin sheepishly rubbed the back of his neck.

Thank god the people were distracted, the Pendragons warming up to the new Knights—and the other Knights far too busy making impressions and adjusting to the road to care about him and Enide. Boldwick and Rochester were no doubt smoothing things over, which made this conversation and dealing with his friend all the more possible.

"It's going to be fine," Enide said.

Garin, to his credit, hadn’t said much at all.

Erec shot a glare at his girlfriend, and then settled his rage onto his best friend. Stopping and looming over him. “What was your plan, Garin? Pop out of the trunk and tell Boldwick, 'hey here I am. Where do you need me?' You don't even have Armor, man! How are you so sure that he won't just send a car back to the Kingdom with you the day after he finds out? Are you fucking insane?"

Garin kept up the silent act, Munchy chittering at Erec as his voice rose.

Enide was off to the side, kicking a rock, trying to look anywhere but at the two of them. Erec had already told her off. To think she helped enable this—it got his blood boiling.

Focus.

He continued to stare at Garin, letting his friend wilt under the heavy silence… until…

Garin cleared his throat… “I… er, well…”

Erec let the silence continue. Better to have him dig his own grave than feed him words or give him a line out.

“If—if they wouldn't let me out here on the expedition as a Knight, I figured I’d come out on my own. Once out here, I thought he’d accept me. Easier to do that. I didn’t really think about him… trying to send me back.”

Erec put a palm to his eyes. “Of course, he’d just send you back. Until it becomes too inconvenient to do so. But it’s only a matter of time before someone catches you. And Goddess only knows when it’s too inconvenient.”

“Then—then I join the Pendragons. If I’m part of the Pack, I’m no longer part of the Kingdom. He’d have no authority to order me back, and I’d get to stay.”

Erec’s jaw dropped.

“What about your family? What’s your father going to do when he finds out you ran off with the Pendragons? What about your vows, Garin?” Erec was damn near swearing. He still remembered the day both of them were accepted to the Order of the Verdant Oak. The day they’d taken their vows together to the Grandmaster. He still remembered the feeling of the sword on his shoulder as he gave his word.

Garin stroked Munchy’s back, and the squirrel settled his growling. Fido sat up with an alertness as his master stood to his full height. Then, unbelievably, Garin stepped up to Erec.

The embarrassment on his face wiped away, his lips were firm, his eyes held steady. It was the look of a man who’d made his choice and was going to shoulder it. A man who, if Erec swung a punch at him right now, would punch him back.

What had changed?

Erec crossed his arms, still waiting for an answer. Change or not, these were the questions Garin should have asked himself before pulling such a stupid maneuver.

“I had to make a choice. You and Colin are becoming something else, and I don’t understand what. If I stayed back there, I knew I wouldn't ever be able to catch up. I don’t know what I’m looking for out here, but I heard it. In my dreams, man, it was there. Saying I had to come. I know that doesn’t make much sense to you; hell, it doesn’t make much sense to me. But I—I’d rather have died than give up on that. Give up on you all. I’m not here to convince you that I made a smart choice. Goddess, Olivia drilled that into my head when I confessed to her about this. I’ve blocked all that shit from my mind. It’s just noise, Erec. And noise doesn’t matter when you know what you have to do.”

Erec sighed. It was dumb. But it’d been done. Garin had made his choice, and it was clear that he was about to continue standing by them. He looked over to the looming fires of the camp and envisioned the brewing confrontation.

If he couldn’t convince his friend to make a smart move… then… Erec stared at the camp on the horizon, at the milling bodies of Knights and Pendragons, all of which were starting to drink and enjoy themselves, and took a deep breath. He didn’t know how the hell they’d do it, or if he even should be helping Garin with this utterly dumb plan. But, as he recalled all of the times in his past, his best friend was always there for him. Both in battle and life.

Garin had always been there, and he did think his friend was falling behind. But truthfully, there were very few people in this world who could keep up with the Knights of the Round Table as they earned their mantles.

Maybe it was futile. Maybe there would be so many issues stemming from this decision… but there was no choice to make, not when it came to his best friend. At least, none that he could live with.

“Alright. How are we pulling this off?” Erec said.

Bedwyr stabbed at Colin; it was hell to weave his spear through the complex electrical fields arcing around between them. Even with the precision control over his weapon, and with a strength that made it speed through the air like a bullet, it made it no easier.

After a year more in the Academy than this young noble, Bedwyr was still struggling to connect and end the fight.

Worse, Colin was grinning. At him.

“Wonderful strategy, keep wasting your time with that spear. I’m sure you’ll find your opening, as long as you keep using the same patterns and attacks,” Colin mocked, and one of the arcs in the field shifted, manipulated by a glyph. It sprang like a whip, and Bedwyr was forced to action in an instant.

His body twisted at an unnatural angle for most people, but to him? Everything was balanced. Strength, Dexterity, Endurance; all in the perfect state to never overextend, to manipulate his body in the perfect way to even evade such fast magic. It crackled by where his head had been, then surged into the nearby ground, giving a small pop as it superheated some of the sand.

Bedwyr mixed it up and formed his own glyph, targeting the earth under Colin’s feet to drag him into a hole. Ever since losing the Faith stat, earth-based magic became far easier.

Unfortunately, the glyph wasn’t fast enough. Right as the ground was about to give and drag the mage under, Colin leaped up with a pulse of electricity, backflipping in his armor, and shooting another bolt at Bedwyr. This one Bedwyr caught with his spear, and then shoved the butt of the spear into the ground, letting the charge discharge through his weapon, using his earth-coated hands to prevent it from arcing to him.

Bedwyr whistled. For as frustrating as the spar had been, he did have to give it to Colin, “It’s amazing how you apply your lightning magic.”

“Hoping to steal my glyphs? Good luck. A novice like you would struggle to even conjure a bolt of lightning.”

Patently not true. Bedwyr had done at least that, but had known better than to try to use a spell that appeared to be a mage's specialty against the mage. That was just one of the basics of magical dueling.

Bedwyr launched himself forward, pushing off the butt of his spear to give his body a rush as Colin landed; he went in with a triple stab, each one targeting vital points on the armor, but after the first missed, Colin retreated, using his magic to buy speed, and worse, buy space.

Bedwyr’s eye twitched as he found himself once more fifteen feet from his enemy.

"Get better at magic," Colin mocked.

There it was, those words digging under his skin like a knife. He’d tried magic. Several times. It had the same result that it’d just had—he hated to admit it, but Colin was on another level, and despite his formidable Virtues, he couldn’t find a way to close the gap and take the fight into close range.

You simply aren’t wielding enough power.

The voice came from deep, and Bedwyr shivered. It brought a taste to his mouth, a coarse, deep earth flavor. He swore, for a second, that he smelled the inside of a dank cave, filled with ore and oxidized iron. The earth around him stretched. Power. An entire world’s worth of power was there, buried. And it could be his, the more frustrated against Colin he got, the closer he came to grasping that vast reserve, and bringing it to bear.

But with it came danger.

Colin’s eyes flickered gold, lightning trailing from them.

The sense of the earth around him pressed in, looming, its weight on Bedwyr’s shoulders. And both he and those eyes staring at him were suddenly transported to a whole different place, a whole different world.

Colin sat across from him, grinning like a madman, leaning forward in a regal silver chair, flickering with intricate magic glyphwork. Bedwyr wanted to leap across the round table between them and shove his fellow Knight back into that seat.

“Stop.”

The round table vanished away as the words shook that almost dreamlike vision, then dispelled it. Bedwyr blinked, and it was as if he’d been transported, from that place of glory once more to the desolate wasteland.

Colin frowned, his eyes still flickering with lightning and gold; coming back into focus, he’d been there too, then. It… it was shared.

Bedwyr gave a shudder and released the touch of the earth; the magnitude of the weight it brought left his shoulders. Reluctantly, he turned to face the source of that command.

Sir Boldwick stood, sword at his side, cloak flapping in the hefty wind of the wasteland; next to him was Bedwyr’s brother, Erec, who wore a startled expression. Past those two were two other senior Knights. Sir Able, to whom Colin reported… and Sir Helvek, to whom he still reported.

As more of Bedwyr came back, he tried to figure out what Helvek was thinking. But he got nothing. The man looked on with a neutral expression.

It hit again, Bedwyr thought, unable to help it. Just like back in that Cavern. A lot of it was vague, the memories of holding all of that weight on his shoulders, of having the mass of the earth itself on him—Colin had whatever that was, too. And from the side eye Erec’s friend was giving him, he knew Colin hardly understood it.

Neither do I. Bedwyr admitted to himself. Maintaining his silence. Waiting to be addressed by the ranking Knight who’d called an end to their spar.

“So?” Boldwick looked at the two other Knights.

Sir Able grinned. “Sewer boy was working well with distance. Which is good. Distance benefits him more. But he was flaunting too much. I think he had too much fun with his enemy, rather than just going for the win. In a real fight, that’d bite him in the ass.”

“It was a spar. Had it been a monster, I’d have killed it sooner,” Colin shot back, which made the old man cackle.

“So—wait—get this—you intentionally made yourself look more incompetent in front of three superior ranking Knights judging your fight? You chose to look stupid? Bold approach, my Knight Errant.”

“I have nothing to prove to you—“

Boldwick cut off Colin by holding up a hand. He glanced over at the bandaged man. Bedwyr’s new ranking Knight, who’d yet to even smile or frown at the battle. Bedwyr stiffened and straightened his back. Truth was, he knew little of the man. After the King’s proclamation, the Order of the Crimson Lotus struggled to find a Knight to place him under. They’d been considering it beforehand, of course, he was in his second year…

But the power he’d put on display changed all that. He was no longer a promising upcoming Knight. He’d become something else entirely in their eyes.

Once they assigned him to Helvek, Bedwyr didn't understand why. The man had drilled him, but said little. He’d been busy; they’d had meetings for the expedition. Still, Bedwyr expected more.

“He fights well.”

Boldwick raised an eyebrow. “That it?”

The man shrugged, “He fights well enough. What did you want me to say, Boldwick? Both of them were clearly holding back, and you stopped them right before they went all out. Maybe I should ask you why you stopped them there instead of letting us see what they are really capable of.”

At this, Boldwick was the one who paused; Helvek was also a Master Knight, but unlike Boldwick, he was not an instructor and not involved in the Academy.

From what little Bedwyr found out about him, the Order of Lotus shrouded him in an odd kind of secrecy to the younger Knights. And the older ones simply ignored him when he asked.

“You’ve seen the reports. I’ve seen it with my eyes; there is a power that, once rung, will escalate to a level of fighting we can't control. So, I will not have us get there in training.” Boldwick explained.

“Then you have it. I cannot judge him completely. From this fight, he fought well. He could leverage his resources better, given his Talent. But I think you see as well as I that, against this opponent, the lack of practical fighting experience is showing through. Understandable. He hasn't had to go around fighting others to the death, humans or monsters, in comparison.” Helvek answered.

Bedwyr did his best to hide his frustration. It wouldn’t do him any good.

It was less than—well, Colin did have a whole expedition on him—but it was the range. That was the crux of the issue. Not a lack of experience. Hadn’t he sparred more people than Colin?

As if having more life or death battles would have changed anything.

“Then that is a fine assessment. Feel free to debrief your two Errants. Erec—let’s have a talk, then we’ll get you into a spar.”

Boldwick broke them off. Sir Able and Sir Helvek got into a chat, leaving both him and Colin standing around.

Bedwyr looked to the sky; the sun was sinking into its afternoon position. The scouts would be back soon, then they’d be moving after they’d cleared the roads.

Will I get to spar Erec?

The idea of facing off against his brother got his blood pumping.

“Bedwyr, Colin. Over here,” Sir Helvek called, with a neutral tone. Bedwyr slung his spear on his back and approached with the other Errant. Giving the two senior Knights a salute with a quick fist to his chest, as Colin scoffed at the old man.

“Sir?” Bedwyr asked.

“You will not be resting tonight when we break for camp,” Helvek instructed.

Bedwyr tilted his head. “Are we to do more drills? I take it my performance didn’t meet your standard?”

“Something else. Something real. Be prepared. You are dismissed from training.”

What?

Bedwyr looked as Erec got into a fight against another Errant, tearing into the poor bastard with an axe, and breaking his defense in seconds.

It seemed he’d not get a chance against his brother after all.

Bedwyr drifted away from the fighting, his heart not in it to watch the others go. He wandered back to the Pendragon camp, still churning over his ranking Knight. When he’d pictured becoming a Knight Errant, he’d imagined an active, responsive leading Knight. Someone who’d guide him into making himself special. Drawing his gifted Talent to its absolute limit.

He couldn’t help but feel disappointed. A whole month. And he got Helvek? A silent, judgmental man who never said what he thought. Hadn’t pushed him past some basic drills.

With his luck, tonight’s ‘something else’ was basic training.

Maybe the rest of the Crimson Lotus hadn’t talked about Helvek since there had been nothing to talk about. Sure, part of him knew it didn’t make sense, but Bedwyr couldn't reconcile the lack of training with his hope for a Master Knight. By all accounts, Helvek was not the type of man he'd needed.

About an hour after returning to the Pendragon camp, everyone piled into said cars, and they took off, engines roaring across an empty wasteland.

Bedwyr let the world pass by, holding onto a shoddy frame made of steel that by all rights should have torn in his hand and flung him out across the miles of endless sand and dirt traveling beneath them. Somewhere in the midst of that drive, the wasteland changed. It became less… brown. More spotted with green. There were trees with actual leaves. They were barren at first, shy to show themselves to the Knights. But the density was growing.

It wasn’t a forest, like the bio-caverns down below, but it was something.

When the sky darkened, the cars came to a crawl and pulled into another camp. Once more, he and the rest of the Knights and Pendragons leaped into labor. When it came to setting up and packing, there was no wasted time.

Some of the Knights were ordered to keep watch at night But tonight wasn’t Bedwyr’s turn to do so; a senior Knight let him know he was relieved of duty, no doubt courtesy of his master Knight.

Bedwyr wasn’t feeling particularly social, so he kept himself to a fire he’d put together with some spare twigs and logs from the wasteland. He hung his head, looking not at the night sky, but at all the world around him.

For so long, he'd thought his Talent demanded he do well. That his gift meant that he was special, and would be someone the Kingdom relied on. To that end, he'd pushed himself to the brink, pushing other people away to make time for his training. But out here? Seeing Colin, seeing Erec and how they’d risen while still having their friends—had all of that time pushing others away to bear the responsibility of his gift been for nothing?

Could… could he have spent that time better?

After an hour of sitting by himself, Sir Helvek arrived at his fire.

"Ready?" he asked with a gravelly voice.

“As ever I would be.” Bedwyr pushed himself up, and, with a twist of magic and a hastily made glyph, consumed his small fire with a pile of sand, putting it out. He trekked after the Master Knight in silence, wondering just what the man was about to put him through.

After a half-hour walk, the lights of the Pendragon camp became distant. All that was around was a dark wasteland, starting to come back alive with vegetation.

Helvek abruptly stopped and crossed his arms.

Bedwyr followed his Master Knight’s stance and looked around with a wary expression.

Five more minutes passed in silence, much like the journey here.

“Er…”

“They’re late,” Helvek explained.

“They?”

Then, before the Master Knight answered, there came a streak of cursing and a sudden crash as the wasteland gave a quick shake. Sir Able and Colin landed, kicking up a wave of dirt as they did so. The manic old man was laughing like a kid, tears in the corner of his eyes.

“You were so scared—hahaha, did you really think I was going to let you drop?” he asked.

“You did allow me to drop, you senile old fool. We were miles high and you utterly released your spell. Look at how we hit, my whole body aches!” Colin yelled.

"Your Armor is meant to resist impact. Besides, I caught you eventually."

“That is no excuse.”

“Don’t like it? Learn some decent flying spells instead of the electric-hovering bullshit you’ve been using. Keep talking about being an accomplished mage, but if someone tossed you up, you’d be a helpless baby—“

"Excuse me," Helvek interrupted.

Sir Able instantly silenced and bowed his head at the man.

“There is no need to be so formal,” Helvek said.

“Not every day you interact with one of the Bloody Petals this much. Can’t say I wanted to be on your bad side by refusing. Besides… who doesn’t like a good fight? I’ve been itching to see it.” Sir Able said.

Bloody Petal?

Helvek inclined his head. “I appreciate the respect. But again, we are here to evaluate what these two can do. Let us stop the chatter and get on with it.”

Sir Able was silenced, the comedy and mirth in his eyes gone. He gave a slight shiver as the impact of the moment came. Bedwyr played the Knight’s words from earlier again in his head. This was to be… real… but what?

He pointed at Bedwyr and Colin; his eyes narrowed. “Tonight’s training is to spar. Like before. Only, we won’t be stopping until one of you wins. As Boldwick so kindly put it, we need you to ring the bell that cannot be unrung; or else, we’d be poor teachers.”
