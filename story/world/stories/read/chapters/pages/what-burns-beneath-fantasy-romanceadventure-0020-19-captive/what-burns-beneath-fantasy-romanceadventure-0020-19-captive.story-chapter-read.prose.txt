He wants the shard. And… maybe the runebook. But definitely the shard.

It’s not as though I have it. Not… here.

I examined myself thoughtfully. I’d gone to bed in my gray woolen nightgown, and that was what I wore now. The other night, in the cave, I’d slept in my tunic and trousers. And that was what I’d worn during the second dream.

It stood to reason, then, that what I fell asleep with… came with me. Somehow. Perhaps he was hoping I’d fall asleep with the sword fragment in one of my pockets.

And at the moment, it was an entire world away.

So surely he won’t kill me. Or… do anything too terrible. Not… yet.

I swallowed. “Do you have a name?”

The sharp divots of his beak curved upwards. “Most things do.”

“Alright…?”

There was a velvet rustle as the owl spread his wings and flapped twice, returning to his perch. “Humans. Your entire species walks around with their eyes closed.”

“What does that even mean?” The reply came out a bit more tartly than I’d intended. I winced, hoping he wasn’t offended.

He just chuffed another laugh. “Ah, little morsel. If I asked you for your name, would you give it freely?”

I hesitated. The response indicated that there was something important I was missing. Well, I supposed that went without saying; there was probably a salted mountain of things I didn’t understand about all this. But if simply giving one’s name in this place carried some kind of wicked magic behind it…

I remained silent.

The chime sang out beneath his claws. The Fae cocked his head to one side, listening to the music. When it stopped he murmured, “Horace.” He wore the shadow of a smile, as if enjoying a private joke

“Is that your name?”

“Mm. It will suffice.”

“Horace. Alright. And I don’t suppose you’ll tell me… er, the lord’s name?” The word tasted foul on my tongue. Lord. Ugh. As if he’s some venerable dignitary.

He gave a little coo. “Mm. I’m not in the habit of telling creatures what they already know.”

I furrowed my brow, casting a glance about as I puzzled through that statement. “You… what? What do you mean?”

Horace regarded me silently.

He’s toying with me. Teasing me, or… I don’t know. Perhaps he’s just mad. I sucked in clean, fragrant air. “Alright. Well, it’s been, er, nice to meet you, Horace.”

“It was, wasn’t it?”

“Thanks for… um, not eating me.”

He chuckled. “You’re very welcome.”

Rallying what shreds of courage remained, I walked towards the manor. The dais steps were cool beneath my feet. At the top, I glanced back towards Horace.

He watched bemusedly from his perch, eyes glowing in the moonlight.

I placed a hand on the enormous doors and pushed.

A foyer larger, and infinitely grander, than my entire house loomed before me. White marble walls stretched out, so polished that I could almost glimpse my reflection in them. A tiered chandelier glittering with sweeping silver and dangling diamonds hung high above. Plush midnight-blue carpet laced with pearly thread extended through the room and down hallways onto either side. Night-black sconces lined the walls, each set with fresh candles. Firelight, not red or orange but the pale silver of distant stars, flickered carelessly. The carpet swept ahead, adorning a grand alabaster staircase that curled upwards and disappeared into shadow.

I wrung my hands, struck still by fear and wonder. Alright. I’m in his… house. His fancy, terrifying house. Now what? My gaze drifted to the staircase, to its silent grandeur and waiting darkness at the top. An endless, depthless black. I shivered. I need to… salt, I don’t know! I need to wake up, but how?

Teela pounding on my bedroom door had woken me from the first dream. And in the second one… well, I still had no idea what had happened. It felt as though I’d been hurled back into the waking world. But it wasn’t something I had done. In both cases, someone or something else had woken me.

That wasn’t exactly reassuring. But that didn’t mean it was impossible to wake myself up, just that I hadn’t managed to do so yet.

Maybe I can find something tall to jump off of. That falling sensation always does it with normal dreams; snaps you right up in bed. I considered that more seriously, then amended, Er, but it probably shouldn’t be too tall.

The staircase loomed, silent and dark, as if it was waiting for me. I could go a short ways up, climb over the iron railing, and… well, jump. Not high enough to get really hurt, of course, but perhaps enough to jolt myself awake.

It’s not a terrible plan.

But I couldn’t seem to make my feet move. The shadows above were too deep, too malevolently silent. Too capable of hiding terrible things. I stared into the darkness with dread.

The anger came slowly. A spark deep in my belly, flaring meekly amidst this world of shadows and night. I was tired of being afraid. Sick of feeling helpless in the face of living terrors. My life had been violently transformed into a tapestry of fear, into sleeping in caves and running for my life.

I can’t just stand here, waiting for him to come find me.

I paced through the foyer. What could I do? Horace said I was prey. A helpless, confused mortal, entirely lost and out of my depth in a realm where probably just about everything could kill me.

My eyes fell to the floor, to the thick carpet beneath my feet. Plush and warm and… perhaps a way for me to fight back.

I growled and marched outside, down the carved dais and back to the treeline. Horace watched in bemused silence as I snatched up an armful of twigs and leaves. I stomped back inside, deposited my pile on the carpet and, before I could lose my nerve, held out one hand.

“Veth.” My fingers twisted and… nothing. My chest constricted and my skin tingled, as though my very body was reaching for magic, but no carved lines appeared in the air.

I stared, slack-jawed and horrified. That had never happened before. Nothing had never happened before; at least, not when trying a rune I’d already used.

“Veth,” I whispered again, heart pounding. Nothing.

I staggered back, my mind racing. Did magic not work here? No, that couldn’t be right; the Fae had healed my hands. Did divine magic not work? Was I too far from the gods, too displaced from where they could see or hear me?

Or it’s because I’m dreaming. Or some… version of dreaming. It’s not as though I’ve tried to use runes during normal dreams. At least, not that I can remember.

A dozen questions swelled in the back of my mind, but I had no way to find answers now. I shook my head, hands falling to my sides. Ghostly candlelight danced across the scattered leaves and sticks.

Well, it’s not as though I need to use my fire. I turned towards the hallway, ready to pry a candle from its sconce, and froze.

He was there. Watching me. His head was tilted to one side, night-black hair spilling down across one shoulder. The gray fabric of his vest shimmered like smoke, seeming to fade into haze at its edges.

I bit down on my lower lip as smoldering anger melted into cold fear. The reality of my situation sank in, turning my blood to ice. I was trapped in a dream, in which I could be hurt and had no way to defend myself, with a monster. More than that, I was in the monster’s house.

He seemed to be examining the mess on his carpet. Dark, angular brows drew together. “What are you doing?”

I swallowed, struggling in vain to salvage a few embers of brazen courage. Being angry and bold were much easier when faced with a dark staircase and not a bloodthirsty monster.

“I’m… trying to… s-set your house on fire.”

The Fae regarded my pile of kindling, hands clasped behind his back. If he was bothered by my declaration, he didn’t show it. After what seemed an eternity his gaze slid back up. Endless black and glittering silver held me, weighing me in the silence.

My pulse stuttered. “They’re stone. Your w-walls, I mean. You… you probably already know that. I didn’t think it would do much, just… ruin your carpet.”

“Ruin my… mm. You have a curious way of showing gratitude, mortal.”

I balked. “Gratitude? Gratitude for what?!” I didn’t give him a chance to answer. Anger clawed its way back through the shroud of fear. My fists clenched and my voice rose, echoing off the marble. “You’ve attacked my home, hurt people I love, and you’ve been… been… tormenting me in my sleep!”

From somewhere deep within I was vaguely aware that shouting at a Fae was terribly unwise, but… well, I was already at his mercy. If he wanted to kill me, surely nothing I did or did not do would have any hope of stopping him.

So I kept shouting. “What exactly am I supposed to be grateful for?!”

He seemed unfazed by my outburst. “Quite the list of grievances. I’m guilty of none.”

“You… well, you… yes, you are!”

Rose-red lips twitched with something between bemusement and disdain. “I haven’t harmed any humans. Well, not recently. And your dreams have been perfectly safe.”

“Safe?” I sucked in a sharp breath. “Well… saints, if that’s true, then let me leave!”

“I’d love nothing more, human.” He drew closer, lips curling with contempt. “But you have something I need, and I still have questions. Our conversation in your little hovel hardly sated my curiosity.”

His approach made all my brazen fire sputter and sent me backing away, glancing around for an escape.

Pointlessly. I couldn’t very well charge away through his house, and running outside… even if I made it out, he’d surely catch me.

“L-look,” I stammered, stumbling back, “I’m no one! Unless you want to know about growing tomatoes or mixing fever tonics, I don’t know anything about… anything!”

He paused midstep, tilting his head to one side. “What’s a tomato?”

I stared, pulse thrumming like a trapped bird. “What’s a… how can you not… it’s a red fruit that… hold on, I’m not telling you anything!” Not even something so utterly pointless. I’d give him nothing.

He snorted. “Stop being defiant, human. Your friend, or lover, or whatever he is, is probably asleep in his bed right now.” Those awful eyes narrowed, glinting like a drawn blade. “I wonder if anyone will reach him in time if you don’t uphold your bargain. I doubt he’ll live long.”

My jaw dropped.

There it was, then. Acknowledgment. Confirmation, from the monster’s own mouth, that our deal carried the weight of twisted, wicked magic.

“Is that how it works, then? If I break my… my pact,” the word fell through trembling lips, “then what you did to Royce gets undone?”

“I don’t think you want to find out.”

The statement sent ice crawling across my skin. Blood rushed through my ears as the awful reality of all this sent me sinking to my knees. Was this to be my life, now? Dragged to this cold prison every night? Until he was satisfied, or bored- and then what? Would I be slaughtered?

The Fae watched me, then rolled his eyes. “Don’t be dramatic. You’re perfectly safe, and your friend will be fine if you cooperate.”

“F-forever?” I choked out. Tears filled my vision.

He blinked. Then the carved, arrogant expression twisted into another sneer. “Dawn, I certainly hope not. You’re an ill-mannered, muddy little thing. ”

“I’ve had a long day.” A hysterical laugh followed the words.

“Some of my peers have taken a liking to your kind, you know,” he paced a slow circle around me, acting as if I hadn’t spoken. “They’ve been stealing mortals away in the night, keeping them as… well. Entertainment. Pets and playthings.”

Black boots stopped in front of me and the cold gaze trailed down. I got the chilling sense that I was being weighed, that every curve and facet was noticed, measured, and found wanting.

“I truly can’t see the appeal.” He scuffed at the pile of dirt. “Once you answer a few questions and return my weapon, you’ll be free to go.”

The claim only made my heart pound faster. I stared up at him, arms curled around my middle.

The Fae watched me for several breaths, as if expecting me to lunge upright and happily declare I’d cooperate. When I didn’t, he frowned. “I’ll remind you that I’ve been very kind, thus far. Humans typically aren’t welcome in my home, but I’m making an exception for you. I imagine this is the grandest place you’ve ever seen. It must be very exciting for you.”

Arrogant gravity soaked the statement and hung in its wake. Once again, he seemed to expect some sort of surrender on my part.

I just sat there. Speechless, mind racing, trying to find a shred of hope in the midst of my new reality.

His frown deepened. “If you can’t appreciate the majesty of your surroundings, perhaps you can at least appreciate my kindness there,” he gestured towards my hands.

I couldn’t find the words to answer.

The walls around us trembled as a familiar pounding echoed from very far away. The thick carpet shivered beneath me and silver light swallowed the room.

As the world slipped away, his frustrated growl echoed off the marble. “Fine, mortal. We’ll continue this tomorrow night.”
