Theramore gripped the railing of The Axiom, his knuckles white, his good eye locked on the horizon. The Cinderfall coastal fleet was a burning, splintered memory sliding beneath the waves. The Beastkin longships had already turned tail, their oars beating the water into a frantic froth as they fled the slaughter.

Only one obstacle remained. The Lumina Imperium.

Fifty magnificent galleons floated on the calm sea. Their hulls glowed with consecrated light, and their massive sails, woven from pure ambient mana, shimmered with a serene, pearlescent brilliance. Above their masts, faint outlines of lesser angels patrolled the airspace, trailing golden dust in their wake. They were the undisputed masters of the old world, the divine superpower that had never bowed to anyone.

Theramore felt the familiar, savage thrill pooling in his gut. His railguns were hot. The missile tubes were loaded. He desperately wanted the golden fleet to lower their lances and charge.

The Imperial flagship, a floating cathedral of white marble and gold, turned slowly in the water.

Theramore leaned forward, baring his teeth. "Come on, you feather-brained bastards. Make a move."

The golden ships ignored the bait. They completed their agonizingly slow turn, presenting their sterns to the grey wall of the 3rd Fleet. The wind shifted, catching their mana-sails, and they began to move away.

For the first time in recorded history, the navy of the Lumina Imperium was showing its back to an enemy.

"I'll be damned," Commander Trisa whispered from the console, her long ears twitching in disbelief. "They're retreating. They are abandoning Cinderfall."

Theramore slammed his fist against the railing, a curse dying on his lips. The disappointment was a bitter pill, but beneath the adrenaline, a sharp, cold paranoia began to set in. He watched the glowing ships shrink against the horizon.

"Damn, feather-brained bastards. They're always a tease like this," Theramore growled, his eyes narrowing. "Which side are they actually on?"

He turned to Trisa. "Did the sensors pick up anything before they turned? Any aggression?"

Trisa’s fingers danced across the glass readouts. "No hostile locks. Just high-frequency magical bursts. Encrypted communications between the Imperial flagship and the Cinderfall capital. Short, compressed packets of data."

Theramore stroked his grey beard, thinking deeply. On the surface, it seemed like common courtesy. A formal goodbye to their hosts before leaving a war zone.

But Lumina always played the long game. Were they letting Cinderfall bleed so they could step in later as holy saviors? Or were they just bored observers, watching the new kids fight?

He shook his head, pushing the politics aside. That was the Lord Commander's problem. "Well, this works in the young Lord's favor. We won't be forced to fight a battle with three nations on three fronts today." He pointed toward the smoking, debris-choked shoreline. "Deploy the Revenants. Secure that beachhead."

From the cavernous lower decks of the carriers, the boxy, heavily armored forms of the Revenant-class hover transports drifted out over the water. They moved like flying bricks, completely lacking the grace of the Wyverns, but making up for it in sheer, unadulterated armor. They hit the sandy shores, dropping their ramps to disgorge hundreds of Mark IV Infantry Automata and Mark III-B Engineers. The beachhead was established without a single casualty.

…

High above, in the sterile quiet of the CIC aboard The Aegis, I watched the golden icons of the Lumina fleet slowly move off the edge of the tactical map.

I kept my face impassive, but my mind was running a thousand calculations a second. The Imperium had always been an anomaly. Cinderfall and the Verdant Conclave were traditional enemies. They marched armies, claimed borders, and fought in the light of day.

Lumina operated differently.

I traced the edge of the holotable, my thoughts drifting back to a nursery in Wighthelm. I remembered the putrid smell of the assassins, the sudden, terrifying paralysis that had gripped the room. The isolation barrier they had used was an angelic artifact. Years later, during my flight from the academy, the tracking device that allowed the Phoenix Knights to ambush me over the Maelstrom Sea bore the same divine energy signature.

Lumina had a hand in both attempts on my life.

Were the angels actively pulling the strings, treating the other kingdoms like disposable puppets to test me? Or were they just being buttered up by Cinderfall, handing out holy artifacts for political favors without knowing—or caring—who they were being used against?

Information was scarce. My spy network, as vast as it had become under Nyx, hadn't reached deep enough into the Imperium's glowing heart. The holy wards surrounding their cities fried technological surveillance, and their zealotry made recruiting informants nearly impossible.

I glanced at a classified file blinking on a secondary monitor. A deep-cover infiltration mission was currently underway. Hopefully, I will have answers soon. Cinderfall and the Conclave faced you head-on. Lumina lurked in the shadows. I didn't know if they were a silent observer, amused by the situation, or an active participant holding a dagger ready to strike. I only knew they weren't an ally.

"Alarion?"

Nyxia’s voice broke through my brooding. She stepped up to the console, holding a steaming ceramic mug.

"You're grinding your teeth," she noted, her crimson eyes scanning my face with an unsettling level of perception. "What's wrong?"

I hesitated. I didn't want to worry her with the Lumina conspiracy just yet. We lacked concrete evidence, and she already had the civil war ravaging her home territory to manage.

"I was just running some calculations in my head," I lied smoothly. "The supply lines."

She accepted the explanation, or at least pretended to. She held out the mug. "You like it with no sugar, right?"

I was caught off guard again. She remembered. I took the mug with a polite, "Thank you." The coffee burned nicely on the way down, sharpening my senses.

I zoomed the holographic display in on the western coast of the Hegemony. The inland territories governed by House Black were already a chaotic mess of civil war. Morpheus's forces were bleeding the Cinderfall loyalists dry, pinning down the capital's reinforcements. King Theron was too busy trying to keep his own palace from burning down from the inside to focus entirely on the coast.

"Cinderfall is distracted," I said, tapping the map. "They are occupied with the carnage in the lands of House Black. They don't want to lose the capital so easily, so they're pulling troops inward. The coastal defenses are weakened."

I pointed to a specific, brutally straight line cutting through the coastal defenses on the holographic map. It was an unnatural formation, a direct, unyielding corridor I had ordered my forces to march through years ago when I first landed on these shores to rescue my family.

"Why fight through their reinforced checkpoints when we can tear open a wound that hasn't properly healed?" I asked. "The defenses along this specific corridor are still haphazardly rebuilt. A quick, decisive bombing run will blow that route right back open, giving us an express highway straight into the old territories of Aerthos."

Mirelle, who had been standing quietly by the communications array, snapped to attention. She had been observing our quiet exchange with the uncomfortable stiffness of someone trapped in a room with newlyweds, but the mention of tactics brought her instantly back to life.

"My Lord," Mirelle said, stepping forward. "Shall we finally do that?"

I set the coffee mug down. "Yes. Begin the carpet bombing rounds."

…

The Retribution hovered in the lower atmosphere, positioned directly above the Abyssal Fleet at the mouth of the old coastal wound. It served as the primary power projection platform for the assault, its hangars buzzing with lethal activity.

The order echoed through the flight decks. The time for targeted strikes was over.

Down in the coastal trenches, the surviving Cinderfall garrisons huddled in the mud. They were starving, exhausted, and terrified. The destruction of their navy had shattered their morale.

A low, rumbling drone began to vibrate the earth.

A young conscript, his armor hanging loose off his emaciated frame, looked up at the dawn sky. Dark shapes broke through the clouds.

"Look!" the boy shouted, his voice cracking with sudden, desperate hope. He pointed a trembling finger at the sky. "It's the metal birds! The ones from the capital!"

Word of the miraculous food drops had spread even to the front lines. The starving soldiers had heard the tales of silver packages raining from the heavens, filled with hot stew and fresh bread.

The veterans in the trench stood up, dropping their spears. They waved their hands at the sky, tears streaming down their soot-stained faces. They forgot the war. They forgot their King. They just wanted to eat.

High above them, the bay doors of a dozen Centurion C-52 Strategic Bombers slid open.

These massive, sweeping wingspans and eight-engine configurations belonged to the hammers of Alarion's fleet. The C-52s were built for one purpose: carrying an absurd amount of ordinance over limitless distances, powered by their internal Fission-Mana reactors.

The payloads dropped.

Instead of wooden crates and white parachutes, sleek, aerodynamic canisters of compressed plasma and highly volatile mana-gel plummeted toward the earth.

The conscripts realized their mistake about five seconds before the canisters hit the ground. The cheering turned into shrieks of absolute terror.

The plasma charges detonated in a synchronized, rolling wave of incandescent blue fire. The heat flash-vaporized the mud, the sandbags, and the soldiers cheering for bread. The entire defensive line was consumed in a carpet of overlapping explosions that turned a two-mile stretch of the coastal mountains into a sea of molten glass.

The C-52s flew on, uncaring, leaving a perfectly flat, glowing highway in their wake.

The bombardment was only the first phase. The Cinderfall commanders further inland tried to scramble their remaining anti-air ballistae, hauling the heavy wooden siege engines out of their bunkers to aim at the retreating bombers.

Orbiting the airspace above the newly created glass highway were the Centurion C-130 Gunships. Heavily armored transport frames bristling with retractable plasma cannons and auto-targeting mana-turrets on their port sides.

They flew in a slow, lazy circle—a pylon turn—banking hard to the left so their broadsides faced the ground.

The C-130s opened fire. The sound was a continuous, deafening roar that shook the air. Streams of red and blue tracer fire poured from the sky in a relentless, unbroken deluge. The gunships deleted entire grid squares. The heavy plasma rounds tore through the Cinderfall ballistae, shredding timber and detonating the magical siege runes before they could be launched. The C-130s orbited like angels of death, raining precise, devastating fire, ensuring nothing moved on the ground below.

Desperation set in. From a hidden keep further up the mountain pass, a wing of Phoenix Knights launched into the air. They were the elite, riding their fiery mounts, their lances crackling with desperate energy. If they could just reach the lumbering gunships, they could burn them out of the sky.

The moment they cleared the tree line, the true masters of the air descended.

The SF-21 and W-29 Wyvern strike fighters dove from the upper atmosphere. The dogfight was a massacre. The Phoenix Knights pushed their mythical birds to their absolute physical limits, diving and weaving with centuries of inherited martial skill.

A Phoenix Knight locked his sights on a Wyvern, his mount screeching as it prepared to unleash a torrent of golden fire.

The Wyvern pilot engaged the afterburners. Twin rings of blue-white plasma flared at the rear of the jet. The shockwave of breaking the sound barrier shattered the windows of the keep below. The Wyvern accelerated with such violent, impossible speed that the Phoenix Knight was left aiming at empty air, his mount choking on the superheated exhaust fumes of a machine moving faster than sound.

The Wyverns fought in perfect, algorithmic harmony. A lead fighter flushed a group of knights out of the cloud cover, and four wingmen instantly locked on with guided mana-missiles. The sky was filled with the tragic, beautiful explosions of dying phoenixes, their resurrection cycles overwhelmed by the sheer volume of kinetic and thermal damage.

Hidden amidst the chaos, unseen and unheard, the phantoms worked.

The original F/A-3s and the newer, impossibly sleek P-2 Phantoms cruised through the airspace completely undetected. Their stealth polymers absorbed the erratic magical pulses of the battle. They hunted the commanders.

A Cinderfall general stood on a balcony, screaming orders into a communication crystal, trying to rally his broken lines.

A P-2 Phantom glided silently three miles above his head. Its internal bay opened for a fraction of a second. A single, precision-guided smart bomb fell. It slipped through a thermal chimney in the general's bunker and detonated. The balcony collapsed, silencing the orders forever.

…

I watched the cinematic display of absolute, uncontested aerial dominance on the CIC monitors. The coastal sky belonged to me. The ground was melting under the fury of my bombers.

The corridor to the old lands of Aerthos was wide open, repaved in glass and ash.

"The corridor is clear, Master," Tes reported, her voice blending perfectly with the hum of the ship.

I looked at Nyxia. The shock of the overwhelming violence was evident in her eyes, but so was a grim understanding. This was the language Cinderfall spoke, and we were screaming it back at them.

"Tell General Goliath to advance the MECH divisions faster," I ordered, my voice cold.
