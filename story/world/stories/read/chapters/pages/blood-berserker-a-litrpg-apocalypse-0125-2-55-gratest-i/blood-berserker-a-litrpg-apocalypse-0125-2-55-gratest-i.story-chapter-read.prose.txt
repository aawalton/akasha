Nathan craned his head to the side—where he had heard the voice—to the sight of a man dressed in the weirdest robe he had ever seen. In fact, the man's appearance was so weird that the only other thing he could easily identify on him was a cheese grater hanging from a string around the stranger's neck, just like a necklace.

The berserker could see the smile on the corner of the stranger's face as he stared down at one of the priests, unconscious on the floor with a hand on his chin. “This one has a bright future ahead of him. It should be entertaining to keep watch of him." He mumbled, then turned to face Nathan.

In his eyes, where the pupils should have been, there was nothing but pure blue light being emitted from the empty sockets. Aside from that and the stranger's hair, every other thing looked normal.

To be honest, if the stranger had been wearing a hoodie and jeans with maybe the darkest shades ever, Nathan would have assumed that this was just a regular guy on the street. But the blue light and the stranger's glowing hair, which was glowing blue even under the crimson light of the sky, was enough to make Nathan know that this wasn't any other human, at least any other human he had met.

But the apocalypse was weird like that. And maybe this was one of those evolution things that had happened to some people on Earth, turning them into whatever the stranger was.

It seemed unlikely, but the berserker believed his theory was on point. Then again nathan had to factor in the fact that it hadn’t been so long ago that the apocalypse had begun on earth, so he couldn't help but wonder what kind of archetype the Stranger had secured to be able to stop time in its entirety.

“Nathan Orion,” The man fixed his eyes on the battered form of the berserker lying on the floor. "I don't think you are in the best condition for us to have this conversation."

Making sure to meet the stranger's glowing eyes, Nathan moved away from Oblivion, which the demon had been about to use to skewer him, clenching his teeth as he slowly and painfully pushed off the ground.

His insides screamed at him to drop to the floor, but he refused to listen, preferring to endure the pain until he was standing straight with King's Wrath in his right hand, as he locked eyes with the stranger.

He might have been beaten and bruised—maybe even overbeaten—but if the stranger had come for a fight framed as a conversation, Nathan wouldn't go down easily. “Who the hell are you?”

“Rude. I honestly expected much more cordiality from you, of all people. Still, I can understand how horrid the past few days have been for you, so I'll let that one go unpunished,” the stranger said, casually waving a hand towards Nathan, and watching with a smile as motes of blue light appeared from nowhere, surrounding the berserker, instantly repairing all his injuries and bringing him back to full health. “You can call me the Potato Peeler.”

The hell? The pain that had been wracking Nathan’s body a heartbeat ago had just vanished. Even the blood that stained his body had completely disappeared.

Nathan had no idea who this particular Potato Peeler of a person was, but one thing was certain: he was powerful. He was probably the most powerful being he had seen in his entire life, at least judging by the Demon Prince Kabash, who was still frozen in place under whatever time lock spell the stranger had cast.

The sight of everyone and everything except him and the Potato Peeler was enough to quash Nathan's budding hostility towards the man.

Besides, while the stranger healing him up and making the blood that soaked him vanish seemed like an act of goodwill on the surface, but Nathan knew the truth.

It was a thinly veiled threat.

It was just a subtle way for the Potato Peeler to let Nathan know that he wasn't scared of a fully healed and refreshed version of him. The stranger was definitely well-versed in the art of psychological tricks.

That aside, Nathan couldn't help but raise an eyebrow at the name the stranger had given him, Potato Peeler. “There’s no way that Potato Peeler is your name.”

“True. That is just my nickname. I'd tell you my real one, but your brain wouldn't be able to comprehend it.” The Potato Peeler raised a hand to stop Nathan from speaking. “When I say your brain will not get that, I don't mean that as an insult to you. I just mean between you, everybody in this world, and the rest of the multiverse, there are maybe ten or twenty beings who could hear my true name, understand it, and remain whole.”

Nathan's raised eyebrow only went higher. “Try me,” the berserker said, his voice unyielding.

The Potato Peeler let out an exasperated sigh. "Alright, my name is…"

[Hidden Mind (I) Triggered ]

Nathan seemed to immediately go deaf as soon as the Potato Peeler said the word ‘is’. And every other thing that followed, he didn't hear.

He could see the Potato Peeler's mouth moving, but his brain refused to register the words. In fact, his eyeballs felt like they were trying to hide inside his skull, the longer he stared at the moving lips of the Potato Peeler.

It was the most bizarre event that Nathan had ever experienced. The berserker hadn’t even known that he had stopped breathing until the Potato Peeler’s lips stopped moving and he involuntarily sucked in a deep breath.

Nathan realized that his body, his heart, and senses—except his sight— had stopped working, and his bones had been rattling the entire time, with blood dripping from his nose and down to the dirt, and it had only stopped once the Potato Peeler had finished speaking.

He put a finger to his nose, wiped under it, and brought it to his eyes. Blood.

Just the sight of the Potato Peeler speaking his name had been enough to destroy Nathan from the inside out nearly. Nathan. He couldn't even begin to comprehend what the strange man was capable of.

The Potato Peeler waved a hand at Nathan, dispelling all the negative effects the berserker was experiencing. Nathan stabbed King's Wrath into the dirt, sucking in a lungful of air as he dispelled the mental effects lingering from what he’d just experienced.

When Nathan finally regained his composure, his skepticism was replaced by wariness that had shot through the roof. And after witnessing what seemed like a fraction of the Potato Peeler powers, Nathan was very reluctant to say anything that would cause a fight.

“I keep warning people that they can’t handle it, but they never believe me. I probably should change the way I break the news to them.” The Potato Peeler said, with a distant look on his face. “But hey, you survived it, and your brain didn’t turn to mush and leak from your nose, so I guess you really are as strong as I believed.”

“What do you mean my brain would have turned to mush and leak through my nose even though I didn’t even hear your name?”

“Well, yes, that is a side effect some suffer after I speak my true name, and don't forget that I did try to warn you, but you didn’t listen.” The Potato Peeler said. “Plus, I was a bit curious about how mentally fortified you were.”

Nathan had to hold back a scathing remark on the tip of his tongue.

The Potato Peeler, or whatever his true name was, hadn't once been hostile towards him, and it was very much in Berserker’s best interest to keep it that way, and so Nathan held his tongue despite his growing frustration. Of course, Nathan had realized that he was partly to blame for not believing the Potato Peeler when he had warned that his name could harm him—he now knew better.

“I suppose you didn't do all this for show,” Nathan swiftly steered the conversation in a different direction. He spread his hand around, gesturing to the demon prince and the others frozen in time. “What do you want and what's with the cheese grater hanging from your neck?”

“Straight to the point, I like that.” The Potato Peeler said, with a smile that somehow reached his glowing eyes, and it was quite weird for the Berserker to see such an expression on a being that was that strong, so it took him a moment to register the Potato Peeler’s next words.

“I want you to fight in my upcoming games.” The Potato Peeler said, then leaned forward with mischievious look in his face. “As for the grater. It’s just a symbol for everyone to know that I am the greatest.”

Nathan's couldn’t help but chuckle at what he had heard but the bersker quickly refocused on the matter at hand. The Potato Peeler had done all of this for a game. “What do you mean by ‘games’?”

The Potato Peeler snapped his fingers, and two chairs made from blue motes of light appeared in front of him on opposite sides.

“Please sit.” The Potato Peeler pointed to the chair closest to the berserker as he sat on the other.

Nathan looked at the chair dubiously before taking a seat on it. It was surprisingly comfy, even though he could tell it was just a construct of some kind.

The blue motes of light felt velvety to the touch, and his joints—even healed up—seemed to appreciate the softness of the backrest, but Nathan couldn't dwell too much on that as the Potato Peeler began talking.

“To simplify things, the games are just a way for me and a couple of my friends to have fun without our farseer abilities ruining the surprise of how things unravel.” The Potato Peeler said. “ For example, you were going to die, but I intervened to have this conversation with you.”

Is this guy serious? “ Farseer… What does that mean?”

“A farseer is a being who can look into the future,” the Potato Peeler said. “ I’ll have you know that this is one of the very, very few times I have meddled in the affairs of those in the multiverse. And I've only done so because I am gathering a group of highly promising individuals to take part in the first iteration of my games.”

“Of course, you are free to decline and return to your fight against the demon.”

“Decline, huh?” Nathan said, “When you say return, I believe that you would allow me to cut off his neck before you unfreeze time?”

“Of course not, that would be an unfair thing for me to do for no gain. You would be returned to the very same spot and position I found you in when I froze time.”

Nathan pressed his lips into a thin line. This was just another show of power from the Potato Peeler. He was making it clear to Nathan that he could either accept the offer for the games or risk going a second round with the demon prince.

And even though he was fully healed, Nathan wasn't sure that the outcome was going to be any different from the last time he’d fought the demon.

The Potato Peeler was good, very good at psychological warfare, and so Nathan was left with no choice but to listen to the offer on the table. The berserker smiled humorlessly at the Potato Peeler. “Tell me more about this game of yours.”
