Feeling rested and refreshed, Henry opened his eyes and took in the quiet night for a few moments. Maurice was fast asleep, a few paces away at the other edge of the pool, clinging on to the head of a still awake Rocky who was fiddling away with a handful of items. Henry quickly saw a vial, a scale, a sundial, a literal pile of day-to-day tools, and a spyglass, which Rocky was now examining in detail. Recollecting the memories from his Octominds, Henry quickly learned that the objects had been acquired either as gifts after he’d shown interest or through making a request to Maurice, who’d then taken matters in his own claws and bargained for said items.

Unsurprisingly, Maurice overpaid. Willingly. And not because he had been duped. As to why? Henry had no clue. He was looking forward to hearing the logic there and as he eyed the items, he recognized a couple more from the pile. He saw a hammer, fishing hooks, a clothespin, knives. It seemed that once the people of Thalis had learned Maurice was buying mundane items, they just gave him everything they had on hand and made some quick, easy cores out of it.

But at least someone was having fun.

Rocky was gingerly studying the haul, one item at a time. Maurice must have been observing the curious kraken but at some point, the crab had clearly ended up dozing off. And understandably, Henry thought as he reviewed the sparring and meditation sessions Maurice had gone through. As much as carefree Maurice could seem at times, he was working hard as well.

Henry took a deep inhale as he stretched his arms, which Rocky noticed. Glancing up from his careful examination, Rocky waved at him, then turned his attention back to the scale he was fiddling with and chuckling, Henry turned his attention back to his work as well.

He still had a couple of fusions to get through.

Henry, feeling energized and awake, closed his eyes and sunk back within himself, and the soft sound of the water lapping at the edges of the pool faded away into the background. The plan for the next few days was that he would finish this fusion tonight, take a short break, level up to B-rank, then upgrade the Aspects over the next few days. Henry could potentially upgrade his Aspect sooner, but he just couldn’t shake the feeling that putting them through an upgrade right after an Aspect and a skill fusions and a cornerstone change might be stressful on them. Having shared his thought with Arisia, she said he had to trust his gut and that she agreed that it might potentially weaken the cornerstone skills. It just wasn’t worth rushing things. Henry just needed to be patient for a couple of days and then he could pull the trigger on the Aspect upgrades.

With his plan clearly outlined, Henry got started on the first item, immersing himself in a world of flitting shadows as he focused his attention on the Aspect of Dancing Shadows. Activating both Ink Clones and Blood Clones, he began by channeling both instances to the skill nexus hovering between his arms. Henry pictured them as two tiny krakens floating in his mind’s eye. One made of shadows and ink, and a second made of blue blood. Both krakens swirled about, each trying to hog the mana that was flooding the nexus as the skills tried to activate. With their nature being so similar, there wasn’t too much of a conflict between them, but there was some tension still, which Henry hoped wouldn’t be too difficult to smooth out. A moment later, a flow of slick, airy, black and purple smoke filled the space, bringing both clones to a dazed standstill as the conceptual essence of Dancing Shadows. It seeped into them, giving each of them a dark sheen that made the tiny clones seem realer. But Henry didn’t want the skill to output two real clones.

He just needed one that was better than both of those he currently had.

Pushing more mana into the nexus, Henry projected the vision he had for them, and it was almost surprising how easy the two tiny octopuses agreed, swirling around each other for a few moment before the blue one sucked in the ink clone. Its skin rippled with blues and blacks for a few moment, then there was a pulse that traveled out of the nexus and through Henry’s soul.

It wasn’t at all difficult to know where that resonating pulse had gone.

Taking a short beat to collect himself, Henry followed the pulse to the skill roots. This first fusion was one of the easiest he has had to do. Henry wrapped his will around both skill-roots, then he pulled them together. The two skills were pretty willing to merge, but Henry felt another will resist him, and he already knew where it was coming from.

Both skills were connected to the previous cornerstone of Misdirection, and Inkjet was resisting the fusion of its subordinates. But Henry wasn’t about to allow such an obstacle.

Gritting his beak, Henry shoved the resistance aside. It might have been a challenge if he hadn’t been sharpening his will for months by now. Between the life-and-death battles he’d been through ever-since he’d arrived in this world and after the impossibly demanding exercises Arisia had been putting him through, contending with the innate resilience of a skill was almost as hard as freeing his arm from a stubborn, knotted piece of algae.

It was annoying at most, and the spiritual twinge of pain was easy enough to ignore. Henry could have severed the connection away first, but he’d opted to do that after he fused both cloning skills. The connection would be weakened then, and the process would go easier. The cut would in turn weaken the hold Inkjet had on the Aspect itself, at which point he could implant the new skill that would result from the fusion of this duo and Blink.

Eventually, after Henry continuously held the pressure, the two skill-roots finally made contact and their fusion began, and all the way into the spell nexus, the glistening black clones pulsed with power. It took a dozen minutes or so, until there was a final pulse emanating out of the Aspect, and activating that, a new, black kraken with a pearlescent skin appeared in front of Henry. Its skin shone with reflected purple lights, and somewhere in his mind, he sensed the notification come through, but Henry couldn’t spare the attention, and he closed his eyes once more, dismissing that clone. While the iron was still hot, Henry activated the new clone skill again, trapping its output into a new skill nexus, then he brought Blink into the fold.

This fusion might be a little more challenge. But it could also be easily his strongest and to make things easier, Henry continued using visualization to keep his concentration sharp. For the clone, that was easy enough. He pictured the same thing he’d just summoned. For Blink, he visualized it as two orbs orbiting each other. If you touched one, you’d be teleported to the other. Simple enough, but it’d do. The goal was to give his mind a couple of extra “handholds” as he wrestled with the concepts and impressions, so he didn’t need the visuals to be overly complicated and so, with another infusion of shadowy concepts, Henry began the process of talking the two skill manifestations into working together, while he also made sure to chip at the grip Inkjet still had on everything.

As expected, the process took a while but bit by bit, the fusion progressed. It wasn’t as fast as the first one, and Henry had to constantly wheedle the two skills and had to restart multiple times as the skill nexus destabilized and dispelled, but eventually, the failures slowed until finally, when he almost was out of essence, the clone in his nexus managed to pop in and out of existence, then didn’t implode for nearly five seconds. The two skills pulsed, and that told him he was almost there.

Helped with the image and the essence he kept feeding them, Henry kept going, and with every attempt, more and more of the two orbs that represented Blink leeched onto the clone, making the purple shades of the latter shine brighter. He was getting close. But curiously, the orbs weren’t disappearing, no matter how much time he gave to the process. At some point, Henry got the impression that the clone couldn’t take anymore of the teleporting skill within it, but to him, it seemed like it had absorbed enough. Thinking it over for a second, Henry decided not to force thing and to keep going. The fusion might not be perfect. But it will be good enough. He could add to it in the future, once everything was stabilized.

By now, Henry could tell an equilibrium had been reached, and the skill roots were ready. So he nudged them together, and he observed, flooding the nexus and the area with mana to facilitate thing. With how much time he’d spent on this, the fusion took only moments. It wasn’t perfect, but it would do. Henry took a second to enjoy the win, then dove back in. It was the time to start cleaning up.

With a sharp blade of pure will, Henry focused his attention on the new skill, then he began cutting. First, he severed the connection that still lingered between the new skill and Inkjet, and that was… not enjoyable, to say the least. As soon as he began slicing the two apart, a sense of wrongness had stolen over him, reminding him of the time when the System had established the Aspects in his soul. Henry could even taste the tang of blood in his beak, but he didn’t let up until he felt the cord snap.

His immersion still didn’t break. It was unpleasant, but it it had to be done. Next, he felt at the remains of Blink that hadn’t been able to merge. He considered severing that as well, but then, the vision he had of the new cornerstone could still benefit from a connection to what used to be Blink, so he let it be, which led now to the final task. And potentially, the easiest: Push Inkjet and its roots away, and let the new skill take control of the Aspect of Dancing Shadows.

Once that was done, Henry would finally take a break.

***

When Henry opened his eyes once more, it was late afternoon. He was disoriented for a moment, but he quickly found his bearings and looking through his Octominds, he got a quick recap of what had gone on while he had been lost in his own inner world. Nothing was out of the ordinary. Maurice worked with Arisia on some of his skills before he sparred with Fabian and Ash, and Rocky was learning to read, just so he could get through some crafting manuals. As for Thalis, it was in a surprisingly frenzied state. Decorations were being hung, lanterns lit up, and food was being prepared for the celebration.

Even though the fatigue, Henry actually felt excited to join and see what they were up to, especially with everything he’d just accomplished. Sure, he was tired, but he was finally done, and he needed a well deserved break and a drink. It still felt surreal, to do all that he’d done without the system intervening. There was something about that. Like eating the bread that you kneaded and baked with your own hands.

With a flick, Henry pulled up the description of the new Aspect. Instinctively, he knew what the skill would be like, but he still took the time to read the updated description.

Dancing Shadows of the Trickster (C) - Level 10

Cornerstone: Trickster’s Shadow

Create a clone formed of ink and lifeblood that follows your commands and can be controlled remotely via yourself or through your Octominds. You may perceive through its senses, use skills through it, and every clone can be used as a relay to extend your range of influence. A connection to a clone can be weakened, but it cannot be fully severed so long as both parties are within the same plane of existence. You can teleport with any Trickster’s Shadow you can perceive, taking its place while it takes yours. The further the clone is, the longer and the costlier the teleportation will be.

Blink (C)

Teleport to a close location you can see.

Riptide Rush (D)

Summon a powerful current to propel yourself forward at immense speed.

Inkjet (C)

You can shoot powerful jets of ink to confuse, blind, and hide. Your ink can take on properties of recently ingested materials, with up to two properties remembered permanently. Your ink naturally disorients, and with an added mana cost, you can infuse the ink to create sensory deprivation effect for more effective concealment or misdirection.

Henry read through the updated cornerstone, and let out a sigh of relief, which quickly turned to a chuckle.

He’d gotten what he’d been going for. Sure, it seems like a couple of things had been weakened, and the possibility of turning an object into a Recall Anchor was gone, but now he could just teleport to a clone. He’d have to test things out to learn about the cost and timing, but he liked this a lot better. As the Aspect would grow in strength, this new cornerstone might allow him to extend his reach far, far more than any other skill. How many clones could he field? How far could he send them out?

As Henry created a human shell to go up and join the celebrations, he began planning. He had plenty to test and figure out, and he was glad that he didn’t need to re-create all of his clones and sentries, as he could sense the change spreading through them. Over the next few days, he’d have plenty to do. Leveling up, training, helping with the waves. “But for now, I’m done,” he mumbled, staring up at the sun and the second planet floating above. He raised both hands in the air, then took in a deep inhale of fresh air before he dropped both arms looked around.

“I need a drink. No… I need drinks.”
