Chapter 129

Familiar Faces, Part 1

I nearly drop my cold brew as it takes my brain a second to catch up.

A tall woman with impossibly pale skin, radiating a dangerous level of confidence. She shifts slightly, and the sunlight outside catches her amber eyes. Two orbs of molten gold staring through the glass like they can see every dumb decision I’ve ever made and are politely waiting for me to add another one to the pile. Her hair, a silvery white-blond is tied back in several braids in places, and hanging free in others, just enough to reveal the feature that confirms who I’m staring dumbly at: long, tapered ears ending in a point.

My fingers tighten around the plastic cup. Cold brew sloshes against the lid.

“Oh,” I whisper.

The café disappears around me. The hiss of the espresso machine fades. The chatter turns distant. The smell of roasted beans, sugar, and expensive croissants recedes beneath the sudden metallic taste of adrenaline flooding my mouth as I recall the last time I encountered this woman. She had just decapitated a fully-grown Storm Dragon as though it were nothing more than swatting away a pesky housefly.

I recognize her. No, I know her. I know her in the way you know a tornado after watching it tear a house straight off its foundation. My mind replays the System description I had received that first time I encountered her.

Sloth. Saint of the Cardinal Hand.

And now she is standing outside a coffee shop on Euclid Avenue in Cleveland, Ohio. The reality of this fact is more jarring by the contrast of what she’s currently wearing. Gone is the obsidian-like black armor. Now, she’s traded the armor for black yoga pants, a baggy oversized tee shirt, crocs, and a light blue denim jacket.

Her eyes glow, steady and alive with power.

Maybe I got hit too hard by [Unicorn Smash]? Maybe glitter entered my bloodstream and now I’m seeing demigods decked out in athleisure.

Sloth gives me an amused smile. Then she lifts one pale hand and gestures for me to come outside. A casual little curl of the fingers: Come here, dummy!

My heart hammers.

My [Perception] sharpens too hard, too fast as my brain scrambles catching up to the situation I now find myself in and trying to discern what’s the best move I can make next. I take a sip of cold brew.

Think. Don’t panic.

I lower the cup from my mouth, ever so slightly. I don’t break eye contact with Sloth, who seems content waiting for me to remember how my legs work.

I need Jelly.

Not because Jelly Boy can necessarily do anything against Sloth. I have no idea what he can do against her.  But he needs to know where I am.

I roll my shoulders, pretending I’m stretching. A totally normal stretch smack dab in the middle of the café. Sure. Someone behind me politely coughs as they try to politely tell me I’m blocking the path out of the shop.

I flex as subtly as I can. [Slimy Sending] slips out beneath the motion.

The connection opens with that familiar slick mental thread, a tug toward the general direction of my apartment building. Jelly Boy’s familiar mental presence brushes mine—sleepy, soft, lightly irritated, probably because he is either mid-nap or mid-TV. I’m assuming the former.

Jelly, I send. I’m near the café on Euclid Avenue. Couple blocks from the apartment. There’s a dangerous lady here. VERY dangerous! I include a mental image of my location.

There’s an immediate mental buzz. It slams into my mind with its alarm.

Things may take a violent turn, I add. I just want to let you know.

The buzz becomes frantic.

Stay ready. Don’t come charging in unless I call or you hear an explosion. Maybe wait on the roof?

Another buzz. A pause. Then an emotional impression that somehow translates as: what if I explode something first? Preemptive strike!

No.

Disappointment vibrates through my mind.

I cut the spell before we can negotiate property damage and avoiding unnecessary destruction. After all, we’re not that far removed from taking part in a battle that leveled most of Public Square.

Outside, Sloth’s smile widens by a fraction, like she noticed the spell I cast. Could she do that? I take another sip of coffee, muttering a curse under my breath. I then set my cold brew on the nearest empty table, then immediately pick it back up because walking out to meet a powerful murder elf without caffeine feels irresponsible.

The barista watches me. So do half the customers. One guy near the window seems to have recognized who I am, and has his phone halfway raised. I turn my head and give him a look. He lowers it.

Then, taking a steadying breath, I steel myself as I exit the coffee shop, cold brew in hand.

The bell above the café door jingles as I step outside. It is a very cheerful sound for a moment that might end with my spine being worn as a scarf. How strong am I now compared to a full-grown Storm Dragon? It’s a good question, but not one I’m eager to find the answer to.

Sloth stands on the sidewalk ten feet outside the café’s entrance, hands tucked into the pockets of her denim jacket. She looks at me and smiles.

“You’re finally ready,” she says.

“Er… ready?” I clear my throat. “What are you doing here?”

My eyes flick past her. Was there a Gate breach? That’s the first thought. It makes the most sense. It’s a scary thought. If Sloth is here—if someone as strong as her is just walking around my Realm in street clothes, then what other monstrosities may be out on the loose without any way for the general populace to detect the threat? I can’t imagine what kind of destruction Sloth can unleash on an unsuspecting city. And maybe worse things came through behind her. If so, that’s bad news. Very bad news.

I keep my guard up. [Perception] stretches the world thin, and details sharpen.

Sloth glances down the sidewalk.

“Should we take a stroll,” she asks, “or just stand here?”

I almost laugh at how casual the question is. I swallow instead. Think, Joe. Think. Walking might be better. Standing here puts everyone around us in danger if this goes badly. The café. The pedestrians. Random people going about their lives, which is already a risky activity in Cleveland lately.

If there’s an ambush, or if Sloth is looking for a fight, I need somewhere a little more secluded. Somewhere contained.

My mind clicks through downtown’s geography and a good-enough spot comes to mind. There’s an alleyway not far from my building in Playhouse Square, which leads to a loading area. Usually empty this time of day when I walk past it. Brick walls, dumpsters, little foot traffic. About as good as I can ask for, I think.

It quickly dons on me that it’s where I entered my first Gate. A Gate that led to Sloth’s Realm, actually. A place filled with gobblins, Storm Dragons, and blue slimes. How ironic.

“We should walk,” I say.

Sloth’s smile barely changes.

I turn and begin down the street toward Playhouse Square. She falls into step beside me like this is casual. Like we’re coworkers heading to lunch and not a magical gym idiot and a living magical weapon from another Realm engaged in what might be the world’s deadliest stroll.

For several seconds, neither of us speaks. I can feel her beside me. Her aura is still mostly contained, but even contained, it has a faint mental impression. Like a sleeping avalanche.

I repeat my question. “What are you doing here?”

Sloth looks straight ahead. “I have come to your Realm as a Participant in the remainder of the Divine Contest. My Realm is one of three sending delegates as Outworlder Participants.”

My foot catches slightly on a crack in the sidewalk. “Outworlder Participant?” I ask, catching myself.

She does not hide her satisfaction at my confusion. In fact, she obviously savors it. Her smile turns sharper, amber eyes glittering. “It seems we are still early in this Stage of the Contest,” she says, “and this news isn’t widely known yet. Then let me enlighten you.”

My mind is busy recalculating the odds of making it out of this next phase of the Game, trying to incorporate this new entirely unexpected factor. “Please do,” I say.

“There will be nine parties from three other Realms,” she says. “Each of those Realms previously hosted its own Contest. Certain of their survivors now enter this Stage. They will have the opportunity to steal the power of this Realm’s World Seed and the Wish it grants, and claim it for their own.”

The sidewalk seems to tilt under me. Nine parties. From three other Realms. Including past Participants. We’re competing against outsiders. People who already went through this on their own worlds and lived long enough to get a second bite at the apple.

Sloth keeps walking. “This is how the final stages of the Contest work.”

I grip my cold brew hard enough that the lid creaks. I take a sip. I wish I had asked Dr. Archewald more questions, I think.  The thought hits me with immediate, stupid regret. I had been with a being who understood the System, World Seeds, the God Game, all of it. I could have asked. I could have pressed and obtained information that could have given me an edge in this Game. Instead, I passed up the advantage because I was too short-sighted. You’re an idiot, Joe, I remind myself.

“That doesn’t quite answer my question,” I say. “Why are you here with me right now?”

We turn the corner toward the alley. The alley beyond is exactly as I remember it: a dumpster sits halfway down, a faded service door with peeling green paint is set into one wall. The sunlight doesn’t quite reach the back. I’m on edge, even if I don’t suspect this to instantly break out in a fight. Even if it’s a great place for Sloth’s party to be waiting to gang up on me, her motives are unclear but don’t scream violence. At least, not yet.

Sloth’s gaze flicks to me. “After our last encounter, I memorized your specific spiritual signature,” she says. “I was able to locate you upon arriving to your Realm.”

“That’s creepy.”

“I couldn’t pass up on the opportunity. There were others from your Realm that encountered my kin. I am sure they did the same.”

I pause for half a heartbeat. So, I’m likely not the only one being contacted by Outworlder Participants. “Why?... Why come find me? Isn’t your participation in the Game being a secret more advantageous than letting native Participants know about your existence?”

Sloth considers this. “I will need allies in this next phase of the Contest,” she says. “You will as well. We could help each other… No. We’ll need to help each other.”

I glance at her. “You want to ally with me.”

“I am considering it.”

“That’s not ominous at all.”

“Would you prefer I lie?”

“No,” I say. “I’d prefer this be happening to someone else.”

That gets a small laugh from her. “You should count yourself as lucky. It seems you’re one of the first to learn about this element of the Contest.”

I stop near the middle of the alley, giving myself space on both sides. Not enough space if she moves like she did against the dragon, but enough to pretend my positioning matters.

“You want to win,” I say.

“Yes.”

“And the others from your world?”

Her expression cools. “The others from my world are far more dangerous than I am. And they want the Claim the World Seed just as badly. Though you may find their motive far less… mutually beneficial.”

I stare at her. She says it all so plainly and calmly that it throws me off balance. I don’t know what to make of it.

“They cannot be trusted with the power granted to the victor,” she continues.

I snort. “Why should you be trusted?”

Sloth turns to face me fully. In the alley shadows, her amber eyes glow brighter. The denim jacket and yoga pants stop looking absurd for one second. The costume of normality falls away, and all I see is the thing beneath: an ancient violence and patient hunger. I know in that instant that she didn’t win her world’s version of the God Game, and whatever consequences resulted from the decision of the victor has honed her resolve into something so sharp it makes the air feel thin.

“Perhaps I can’t,” she says. “But you won’t have much of a choice. You’ll need to trust someone in this Contest. Eventually. And should choose wisely.”

“What do you want?” I ask. “The same rules apply to you, right? If you fail now, you’ll be culled by the System? You survived one of these Contests once before. Why choose to go through it all again, even if you came up short the last time.”

There’s a look of pain in her expression, just a glimpse before she masters herself. “You’re right. The same rules apply to me and the other Outworlder Participants. Including each consequence for failing to advance.”

“And you’re risking that for what? Power?”

Her mouth quirks. “I want to end the System.”

The words hit the alley and stay there. For a second, I don’t move, frozen by what I just heard. End the System?

“How would—” I start.

Footsteps sound behind us. Someone poking into the alleyway.

My body reacts before my brain does. I turn, staff appearing in my hands as I mentally withdraw it from my Inventory. My muscles tighten in anticipation.

At the mouth of the alley, a shadow passes. Just someone walking down the street.

I sigh.

Depositing my staff back into my Inventory, I turn back.

Sloth is gone.

The alley is empty.

“Hello?” I call. “You there?”

Nothing.

I step forward, scanning. [Aura Sense] reaches out, and I split my mind to also push on [Perception]. There’s nothing there. I am truly alone.

“Great,” I mutter. “Cool talk.”

My pulse is still high, and my skin is prickling. Her words keep turning in my head. I want to end the System.

After waiting a couple more moments, I finally decide to walk out of the alley. The daylight feels too bright. I start toward my apartment building, lost in thought.

The entrance to my apartment building comes into view. I’m still staring at nothing, replaying Sloth’s final words, when a voice pulls me out of my head.

“Joe?”

It’s familiar.  My stomach drops again, because apparently today is a greatest-hits album of people I am not emotionally prepared to see.

I look up.

Sarah Zorbas is standing in front of my apartment building.
