The city behind me had fallen silent, the streets deserted, as if the very air refused to follow me here. Gold had no luster in this place—just weight. I passed a collapsed statue at the base of the steps, half-buried in coins, its mouth open in a frozen scream, its hands reaching not in reverence, but in agony. A mural stretched across the outer wall, half-obscured by soot and time, depicting the Gilded God ascending a throne built from the backs of kneeling figures—other gods, I realized, their symbols cracked and fading. Greed had not conquered by force. It had simply offered more.

I paused. For just a breath.

Malice had once said, {Every temple is a graveyard pretending to be a palace.}At the time, I thought she was being dramatic. Now I wasn’t so sure.

The temple rose before me, towering and obscene, its spires twisting into the clouds like grasping fingers. The closer I got, the worse it became, every inch of the place radiating wealth. Gold filigree bled across the stone, veins of molten light pulsing in the cracks, and the stairs were carved from human bones, their surfaces polished smooth by countless footsteps, the marrow darkened over time, the edges worn from use.

I swallowed hard. The hunger twisted in my gut, biting deep, but I forced myself forward, boots scraping against the gilded floor, my breath ragged, my heart hammering. This place is wrong. My hands curled into fists. Every brick, every breath, built on someone else's ruin. The air buzzed here, thick and oppressive, pressing down on my chest like a weight. It smelled of burning coins, sharp and metallic, the kind of scent that sticks in the back of your throat.

I crossed the threshold into the throne room, and there he was: the Gilded God. He lounged across his throne like a bored serpent, robes pooling around him in molten waves, his antlers curling like molten branches. His skin gleamed with a burnished sheen, too bright, too perfect, as if dirt or blood could ever touch him. His eyes were half-lidded, his gaze indirect, a flicker of amusement in the corner of his mouth. He barely acknowledged me when I stepped into the room, remaining utterly still, merely raising a hand lazily, as if swatting a fly. A dozen constructs stirred to life around him—golems plated in gold and inscribed with Aurentum’s mark—turning toward me, hollow eyes burning like embers.

Malice’s voice crawled into my head, smooth as silk: {Oh, look… the master has sent his pets to play. How generous.} I clenched my fists. My skin buzzed with static, the Boon snarling behind my ribs, a wild, frantic thing screaming to be fed. My lips curled in a snarl, teeth bared.

The Gilded God took a long, slow sip from his chalice, his voice oozing across the room like honey poured over rust: “Take what you can carry, little thief. The vault is infinite.” His gaze slid past me, already dismissing me; he deemed me insignificant. He doesn't see me. My jaw tightened. Good. Let him stare at the shadows while I burn down the sun. The hunger roared in my veins. Oh, I was going to take. I was going to take everything.

Instinct took over. The Boon surged in my chest, screaming for release, and I lunged forward like a beast unchained. The constructs moved to intercept, their hollow eyes sparking as they raised their staves, but I was already inside their reach. My axe—golden, hungry, a shard of something alien to this world—swung low, cleaving through the nearest construct’s knee with a crack of shattering metal. It toppled, sparks spraying, and I pressed on. I tore the staff from its hands before it hit the ground, ripping the sphere free. The divine essence inside flared—a trapped soul, a flicker of life snuffed too soon.

I ate it. Action preceded thought; I pressed the sphere to my lips and breathed in, and it melted into me like warm light, searing down my throat and bursting behind my eyes. That was a life. The thought flickered—then vanished under the hunger’s roar.

It burned. Not just the light, but the feeling. Like swallowing a scream too large for my throat. That flicker of life—it had shape, texture, something almost human still clinging to it. I felt it try to hold on, to resist dissolution, to remember who it once was. But the hunger didn’t care. It flattened identity into flavor, reduced memory to marrow, left only power behind. I shuddered. Not from guilt. Not quite. But something inside me—something older, something smaller—quaked like a candle before wind.

Malice crooned in the background, smug and purring.{First one's always like that. Gets easier. Gets better. Or worse. Depends on how much you liked who you were before.}I didn’t answer. Couldn’t. The taste still coated my tongue—sweet, bitter, ruinous. The hunger roared, thrashing inside me, demanding more, more, more.

I whirled, slamming the staff into the next construct’s chest. The impact rattled through my arms, metal screeching against metal. The sphere cracked open, leaking golden light that twisted into jagged threads. I tore at them with my bare hands, shoving the threads into my mouth, devouring. Malice cackled in my mind, sharp and delighted: {Oh, darling, look at you. A little goddess of famine in the making. Continue now—there’s plenty more to go around.} Don’t call me that. But even I didn’t believe the protest.

The constructs kept coming, unthinking, relentless. I moved through them like a wildfire, the Boon guiding my hands, each blow fueled by hunger and rage. My breath came in ragged gasps, my muscles screaming, but I forged ahead. Every shattered core, every consumed soul, fed the gnawing pit in my chest. The mortals—the hollow, starving people of the city—watched from the shadows, too afraid to move, their eyes wide as coins, their mouths open in silent gasps. I knew what they saw. They saw a monster. I felt it too. And maybe I was. But monsters break chains. Gods just polish them. My skin hummed, my veins buzzing with stolen power. My hands dripped molten light, the taste of it sharp on my tongue—bitter, electric, addicting. I glanced up at the throne. The Gilded God remained still, watching me with that same lazy, half-lidded gaze, as if I were an insect, a curiosity to be observed, an object of mere observation. The hunger snarled. I was a force of nature. I was the storm.

His indifference was palpable. I tore his city apart, shredded his army, devoured the souls he hoarded like coins in a vault, and the Gilded God sat there like a king fattened on rot. “Look at me,” I hissed under my breath. He didn’t. Of course not. Sipping from his chalice with a bored flick of his fingers. “Take what you can carry,” he said, voice slow and dripping with condescension. “The vault is infinite.”

His words echoed: Infinite. The hunger twisted inside me, a gnashing, writhing knot of need that threatened to tear me apart from the inside out. I clenched my fists, breath ragged, the taste of molten power still burning my tongue. His gaze remained elsewhere. His power flowed through others. The constructs served as a delay, meant to hold the line—deflect, absorb the damage while he sat safe and untouchable. His domain was accumulation, a bloated parasite who let the world fight and bleed while he counted his spoils from the safety of his throne.

Malice’s voice oozed into my skull, thick and slow, coiled like smoke around a dying flame: {See it, darling? That’s greed. It wields no sword, engages in no fight; it simply possesses.} And it thinks it's untouchable. I clenched my fists, the hunger gnashing. My teeth ground together so hard I thought they’d shatter. My hands trembled at my sides. The hunger screamed.

I took a step forward, boots sinking into the soft dust of melted gold and ash. The floor pulsed beneath me, veins of light feeding into the throne like blood into a heart. I could feel it all: every stolen breath, every tithe ripped from the broken people outside, every ounce of divine energy hoarded in the walls, in the constructs, in the city itself—all of it flowing into him, a god truly built on theft.

The ground thrummed beneath my feet, a syncopated heartbeat that didn’t belong to me. For a breath, I couldn’t tell where my pulse ended and the temple’s began. My vision swam. The veins of gold in the floor pulsed like arteries, and every surface seemed to breathe, the light too alive, too aware. I heard whispers—not voices, but impressions—flashes of memory, desire, fear. The vault knew me. It offered illusions cloaked as rewards: warmth, touch, Maria’s voice, my father’s approval, my name spoken without scorn.

I blinked hard, banishing them.

No more promises. No more lies. My vision blurred, my skin buzzing with static, the Boon flaring so bright I could barely think. He still maintained his stillness, his silence, his utter disregard.

Malice whispered, a low, dangerous purr: {Well, darling… what are you waiting for? Ask permission?} The hunger surged. My lips peeled back into a snarl. My hands flexed, my muscles screaming for release. The Gilded God sat there, a monument to everything I hated, and I could feel it in my bones—he expected my departure, that I’d take my scraps and run.You don’t get to ignore me and live.I took another step.Not anymore.This time, however, would be different; this time, no.
