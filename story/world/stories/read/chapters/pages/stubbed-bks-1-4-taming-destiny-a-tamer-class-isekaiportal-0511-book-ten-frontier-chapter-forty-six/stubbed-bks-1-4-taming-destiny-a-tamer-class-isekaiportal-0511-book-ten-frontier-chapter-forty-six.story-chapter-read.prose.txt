Not in the mood to return to the settlement, I decide to make myself a temporary shelter here. Pulling up the earth, I harden it into a waterproof rock-like substance that covers me on three sides, its back to the wind. Then glancing at Tilathis and the still-sleeping Romeo, I make it bigger still.

“We’ll have to be a bit snug, but you’re welcome to shelter here,” I suggest to him, even as I lift Romeo and place him inside the shelter. Tilathis moves in wordlessly, pressing himself to one side of the shelter and lying down. As I suspected, with him in here we’re snug enough that we’re all pressed together unless we stay rigidly separate.

We were only out in the rain for a few minutes, but it’s heavy enough that we’re all drenched.

Using Water-Shaping, I pull the water from myself and Romeo. Then I look up at Tilathis whose coat also looks rather sodden.

“Would you like me to dry you?” I ask, not wanting to use magic on him without his knowledge or permission.

He looks at me, and I have a feeling he’s surprised.

That would be appreciated, he rumbles a moment later.

I nod and send my water-mana crawling over his fur, collecting all of the droplets of water like a lint roller collecting bits of fluff. It takes some time given his size and how he’s pressed against the wall of the shelter, but I don’t mind. In comparison to the last few hours of intense thought and constant trial and error – almost entirely error – doing something that I barely need to concentrate on is actually quite relaxing.

Thank you, Tilathis says quietly when I’m done and I’ve banished the large ball of swirling water into the sheets of rain pouring down from the sky.

I look at him, slightly taken aback that the prideful nunda actually acknowledged my efforts.

I can be polite, he tells me with a huff, obviously picking up on my thoughts. I’m briefly distracted by the idea that I should figure out a way of building a shield around them, or making them less easily audible somehow. Up until now, I didn’t mind that both Lathani and Kalanthia could read my thoughts – they’re both my Bonded and my emotional reaction is already largely available to them anyway. What is hearing my loudest thoughts in addition to that?

But Tilathis is not my companion, and I’m not keen on him having access to so much that he might use against me.

I will not, he sighs, and I tense as my point is proven. Perhaps I shouldn’t have thought about it so close to him.

“Won’t you?” I ask him skeptically. “What if I intended to do harm to Kalanthia or Lathani?”

Then I would act, he tells me with an expression that seems to question my intelligence. But I care not for anything else.

I sigh and relax backwards, not caring that it means I lean back against his hindleg. Given his lack of tension, he clearly doesn’t mind too much.

“I’d rather not rely entirely on whether I think my thoughts are something you don’t care about or not,” I murmur dryly.

Then you will have to learn to defend your mind, Tilathis rumbles.

Easier said than done.

“Any suggestions?” I ask.

Think less loudly, he replies immediately. Very helpful – not. At least he hasn't taken offense.

We don't speak for a time after that, instead gazing at the rain that continues to fall beyond the walls of our protective shelter. It’s remarkably peaceful, especially when I consider how Tilathis was like when we first met. Now, without Kalanthia’s state of health between us, the silence is almost…companiable.

There's no pressure to be a Beast King and settlement leader. We can just be. And Tilathis is surprisingly relaxing to just be around. Perhaps because he's a cat at heart, and their favorite thing is relaxing.

A flash of light splits the sky, followed by a crack of thunder. I lean forward and prop my chin on my hand, watching eagerly for the next strike.

Now that I know how to produce it myself, it seems even more beautiful and magnificent than before. I watch for a while, enjoying the calming sound of rain.

However, after a little time, the lure of the notification, the reason for creating this shelter and staying here rather than running back to the settlement, becomes too attractive, and I call up my notifications.

Congratulations!

You have earned a new Skill: Lightning-Shaping

I smile. I knew it, but it’s good to see the words written there. After so long attempting to learn it, I feel immense satisfaction for finally earning the Skill.

Lightning-Shaping

You have learned that Lightning-Shaping is not a solitary act, but always a duet of two parts acting together, a connection between earth and sky. Currently able to create lightning through magic alone, you will have to learn far more to be able to shape it to your desire.

That sounds surprisingly unenthusiastic for a Skill description, I muse. Basically, it seems to be telling me that all I’ve done is create a lightning bolt – and that if I want to do anything else with it, I’ll have to develop the Skill further.

I consider different ways I could develop it. Changing the shape of the lightning bolt is certainly an option – if I wanted it to hit many targets at once, for example. Or, I think with some enthusiasm, what if I was able to reduce its power? That might help Keshaka and Tiria learn how to get my electronic devices working again!

Obviously, there would need to be a lot of testing before they ever went anywhere near my actual devices – a lightning bolt would be sure to fry the electronics past redemption – but if we could build some basic circuits to teach them the principles….

It would give me an excuse to spend time with Tiria too, I consider, my enthusiasm dampening a bit at how unavailable she’s been. Partly it’s my fault – though the settlement’s needs have reduced a certain extent, and it’s becoming increasingly self-governing, I’m still busy. And she seems to be even busier – I don’t think she’s come out of her workshop in days.

Ultimately, we’ve only seen each other in passing since the Tier four died and I let her have one of the bits of Tier 4 Core to study, despite my invitations to share a meal together or go for a walk.

Feeling my mood lower, I decide to distract myself. I had something I wanted to talk to Tilathis about, and this seems a perfect moment to do so.

"Tilathis," I start, and the nunda indicates he’s listening with a grunt. "Did you happen to kill a group of humans sailing up the river? Not that long ago."

And if I did? he asks guardedly.

"I'm not angry about it – they weren't from our settlement. But it would be useful to know."

He eyes me suspiciously for a moment, then rumbles.

They were invading my territory, he says, with a defensive edge to his mental voice.

"I'm sure they were," I agree peaceably. "But they weren't there to take your territory. They wanted the ore in your mountain."

Tilathis grunts in complete disinterest.

"What if some of our people accompanied any future parties into your territory and made sure they didn't try to take it from you?"

Do you think I am concerned about weaklings such as them? he asks incredulously. They fell easily.

"Perhaps they did," I agree. "But they’re not going to stop coming. They found significant amounts of ore in the river that runs from your mountain to the shore – they know what's up there and they need it. The next group will include more powerful members. And they may send far more people. ”

Then I will kill them too, Tilathis says firmly, allowing his domain to expand as his ears pin back and his lips draw up. I will not give up my territory without a fight!

“Which is exactly what they will give you,” I point out. “How many fighters can you kill before you start losing ground? You’re powerful, but not invulnerable, and humans have numbers on our side.”

Although Rockfell itself might not have that many people to lose, all of the settlements need metal. If Tilathis’ mountain is the closest and easiest source of it – with only a Tier four guardian, rather than a Tier five or Tier six – then House Rockfell will be able to find allies.

Even our settlement alone has killed two Tier fours. I think loudly about the death of the roc – the way we brought it down from the sky and killed it by inches.

Perhaps Tilathis takes warning from my thoughts; perhaps he’s familiar enough with humans to know that I'm speaking truly.

Then what are you suggesting? he asks guardedly, but I acknowledge the victory that is getting this far with him.

"A deal with the other humans. You don't target the parties that enter your territory to retrieve ore as long as they travel along certain established routes. In return, they don't attack you or claim your territory as their own."

Will they accept the dominion of a Beast King? he asks skeptically.

"Probably not if it was only that. In the long term, they’d probably move to take control over the territory when they became strong enough," I admit. "But if I claim this territory as ours in front of them, they won't move against it without making enemies of us. And that's a risk most won't want to take, especially since we intend to get stronger just as much as they do.”

Tilathis looks at me suspiciously.

If you claim my territory, what is there to prevent you from taking it for yourself in truth?

I shrug.

“Well, what is there to stop us taking it now?” I ask pointedly.

He considers the question thoughtfully, then fixes me with a hard look.

You expect me to believe that you care enough about Kalanthia to not wish to destroy me in the search of wealth? How very…non-human.

“You can read my mind. Am I lying when I say that keeping Kalanthia happy is more important than taking over your territory by force?” I challenge him. “Especially if we can come to an agreement where we both get what we want?”

He eyes me closely for a long moment. I notice in the silence that the rain is easing off – the sound of it has become more of a gentle pitter-patter rather than the deluge of before.

You are not, he decides finally, his tone thoughtful. Kalanthia really does mean a good deal to you.

“She does,” I agree. “As do all of my close companions.”

It’s a risk, admitting such a weakness, but I sense that it’s what Tilathis needs to know right now.

Beautiful female too?

I jump – I hadn’t realised that Romeo had woken up.

“How are you feeling?” I ask him, turning to the larnatis and pressing mana into his system to check on him. Everything seems to be fine, physically speaking. A dip into his Core space reveals something, though – his internal matrix has just made a big step forward, especially the pattern around his white Core. The Energy channels around that have taken on a pattern distinctly reminiscent of lightning bolts. Interesting.

I am fine, he tells me as I pull out of his Core space. But you didn’t answer my question. Does the beautiful female mean that much to you? That you would spare her mate to keep her satisfied?

“I would do far more than that for Bastet,” I tell him honestly. “And she knows it. Which is partly why she’s being so choosy about her mate.”

Romeo’s side of the Bond takes on a thoughtful cast and he subsides into silence.

Very well, Tilathis rumbles. I will agree to entertaining a party of humans in my mountain to see what they wish to do – with the condition that I may choose to reject the proposed agreement with them if they are too bothersome.

“Fair enough,” I agree, happy that we have come to at least a tentative agreement.

At that moment, through the dripping that is all that remains of the rainstorm, I hear Elarion’s angry voice.

“Markus! By the gods, what happened here?!”

With a sinking sensation, I recall just how many trees we destroyed in the course of the night. I can only hope that I managed to keep the fields of wheat out of it – but I can’t say for certain that I did.

Pushing myself to my feet, I exchange a nervous glance with Romeo and, surprisingly, with Tilathis too. Then, taking a deep breath, I step out to face the angry Ice Druid.
