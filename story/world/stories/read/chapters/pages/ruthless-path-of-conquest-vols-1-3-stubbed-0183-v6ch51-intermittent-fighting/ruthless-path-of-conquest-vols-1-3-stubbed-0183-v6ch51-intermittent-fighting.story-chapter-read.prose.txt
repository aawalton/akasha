James felt it as the presence materialized within his range of perception.

One moment, the figure didn’t exist in that space, and then suddenly there was something there.

He sighed. It was his senses confirming, once again, that the System made the physically impossible into the routine. Matter could not be created or destroyed, only changed into energy. That was his conventional understanding of how physics worked.

I don’t know why it still bothers me when it happens.

He didn’t need to think much about who it was that had suddenly appeared. He didn’t sense the figure bleeding from multiple wounds and writhing in pain. So it must have been Zhang Chāo rather than his opponent.

James turned to face Zhang Chāo, and at the same moment, he sensed in his peripheral vision that the screen had shifted to follow a different fight.

“Congratulations on your win,” James said, smiling.

“You were watching?” Zhang Chāo asked.

“I have to keep an eye on my competition.”

James heard another sound of familiar footsteps, turned again, and saw his mother. She appeared to be using the back of her hand to wipe away a little blood from the corner of one lip. She waved as James looked at her.

He waved back but thought that anyone else seeing her at that moment would have probably been frightened.

Good old Mom. No one has more fight in them than you.

“You shouldn’t be looking at him just yet,” said Han Jianguo.

James turned back and saw the man giving him a meaningful look.

“I watched your bout too,” James replied after a moment. “I hope you have more than one trick to pull out, or our fight is going to get ugly.”

“Sometimes it has to be that way.”

“Ugly for you, to be clear.”

“You’re that confident?”

I won’t lose at talking trash, at least.

James just smiled back.

“Even if you manage to defeat me, a wall is coming that you will be unable to climb,” Jianguo said. He sounded a little bit rattled to James.

“Not that long before you and I will be fighting, the way things are going. Then you’ll be able to assess for yourself whether I’m qualified to tear down that wall.”

Han Jianguo lapsed into silence.

James looked over at Zhang Chāo. The Heavenly King just gave him an enigmatic smile in return.

No trash talk from him, then. He’s actually confident. James smiled. That’s the most rewarding sort of enemy to beat. I was dreading fighting him, because of the vision. Now I’m feeling a little differently. It’s rare enough to encounter someone who could make a credible claim to be your equal. To test yourself against them without making an enemy is a rarer opportunity still.

[The next round will begin in two minutes. Prepare yourselves accordingly!]

A timer appeared in the corner of James’s vision.

[00:01:59]

“Good,” Zora said. Her voice came from just behind James, beside Luna. “We won’t have time to get bored.”

“I’m glad I’m not in your bracket,” said Zhang Chāo, shaking his head.

“I’m not,” Zora said. “I’m not sure if there’s anyone strong in my bracket. I haven’t had the chance to truly test myself yet. There is a Holy King still in the competition, but the others have already dropped out. I think they were worried about facing me.” James turned and saw she was looking him directly in the eye.

It made sense the Holy Kings would fear her. They definitely still understood themselves and the Fisher Empire to be on a war footing, and Zora had Soul Magic just like James. A loss to her would mean more than just a bloody nose for one of them.

I’ll have to be ready to hunt them down back on Earth, then. It’s unavoidable. That could be a challenge when they’re in a place surrounded by their own subjects. I’d have to try and prepare an invasion, but I’ve heard they have millions of people. A population that dwarfs mine. Fanatics, ready to die for the cause.

As the timer ran down, James glanced at his next opponent and the possible enemies after that and shrugged. There was no one he felt intimidated by there.

No obvious threats until a few rounds to come, when he would encounter Han Jianguo and, assuming that all went well, Zhang Chāo afterward.

He stood and played out the fight he expected to have with Han Jianguo in his mind for the remaining seconds before he was transported, and he gave particular thought to which powers he wanted to make sure he concealed until then.

Then he blinked and found himself back in the ring.

This space was identical to the one he had fought in earlier, except that the ground was pristine rather than littered with signs of earlier fighting.

James guessed that the System had simply fully replaced the entire ground level of the arena. That might explain the two minute wait between rounds one and two.

He didn’t spend much time thinking about that, though.

He saw his opponent now, standing opposite him in the ring. It was one of those humans turned monsters, this one a sort of wetland style creature with a turtle-like shell on its back, green leathery reptilian skin, a beak, and webbed hands and feet.

He had fresh cuts all over his arms and legs, and a couple of them looked deep. James guessed that his opponent had barely made it through the first round.

As it looked at James, the man-thing’s posture changed. James felt it, how nervous the former human was.

“You’ve heard of me?” James asked. He realized their movements were not restricted this time. The System had already explained the rules before, so as far as it was concerned, the fight had already started.

“Yes,” the figure said in a voice that sounded more like a frog’s croak. “I had thought somehow that we were facing each other in a later round. I must have read the brackets wrong.”

“You can surrender if you like,” James said. “Or we can do this the other way.”

He smiled just dangerously enough to let the other man know which route James himself preferred.

“I… have come this far. I will not give in so easily!”

The aquatic creature opened up a Small Bag of Deceptive Dimensions and drew a long harpoon attached to a length of chain. Rather than immediately throwing it, he gathered Water Mana around himself next.

It seemed as if he was trying to open the fight strong, with some sort of combo attack using both water and spear.

But that was the wrong combination for him to use against James.

Lightning Strike.

James cleared the distance between himself and the opponent in a fraction of a second, his speed supercharged by Lightning Mana, and he threw a punch squarely at the enemy’s center of mass.

The reptile man triggered whatever Skill he’d been using to activate early, and his entire body was suddenly encased in a protective envelope of water—which James hit with Lightning Strike. His hand went all the way through the liquid before any further manipulation of it could happen. The punch struck the scaly flesh of the stomach, and the opponent’s entire body convulsed as electricity swirled through his body.

“Argh!” the aquatic man cried out.

He and James locked eyes for a long moment, the opponent’s beak frozen in a half-open position as he writhed with James’s power.

James gave him a little nod as if to say, There, you did your best.

He pulled back on the power that flowed through the other man’s body to allow him to speak properly.

“I—I surrender,” the reptile man murmured. He dropped to his knees in front of James.

Good choice.

“Better luck next time,” James said aloud. “You fought honorably.”

Then they disappeared from the ring. James reappeared in the waiting area.

As with the previous time, there were not many people around yet. Most fights were not as one-sided as James’s had been and therefore did not finish as quickly.

Or maybe other people aren’t trying to set a speed record. He looked around and didn’t see his opponent anywhere. I guess the people who are eliminated don’t come back to the waiting area.

He wondered how many of his own allies had been eliminated already.

Then he shook his head and dismissed the question. It was pointless to speculate. He would just have to make sure to actually look around properly when the matches drew near their end this time. For the moment, he walked around looking at screens to scope out future competitors.

It wasn’t long before he had company from people he knew, nor a particularly long time before the next round began.

The cycle that James had experienced before repeated three more times.

He fought an opponent who was not particularly threatening to him, he defeated them reasonably quickly, he returned to the waiting area, he watched other matches, and he waited to see which of his allies had made it through early rounds and remained active.

This continued until the competition whittled down the number of people James knew to Zhang Chāo, Han Jianguo, Luna, Samuel, and Zora.

“Well, it’s you and me now, isn’t it?” Han Jianguo said quietly.

James turned to face the man who had just approached him from the side. Han Jianguo was watching him searchingly, seemingly looking for any sign of weakness or perhaps just trying to gauge the Fisher Emperor’s emotional reaction to his presence.

“It is,” James said after a moment. “May the best man win.”

“To the strongest,” Jianguo agreed.

“Winner gets to face me,” said Zhang Chāo in a tone of faux smugness, deliberately breaking the tension.

“I’ll give Zhang Chāo your regards,” James said, smirking.

“If you escape in one piece,” Jianguo replied, rolling his eyes. “Everyone has a plan until they get knocked out. Isn’t that an American saying?”

“Something like that.”

“I’m not going to willingly surrender to you,” Jianguo said after a long moment. “Know that, Fisher Emperor. As much as Zhang Chāo is a friend of mine, my rematch with him is more important to me than I can easily say. I won’t stop fighting you until it’s impossible for me to continue.”

James’s eyes narrowed. It sounds like you’re saying this will be a death match. Be careful about that.

“As you wish,” was all he said in response.

The tension that James felt in the moments after that exchange was almost entirely from Zhang Chāo. Jianguo believed he would win. He had the utmost confidence in himself. Only the Heavenly King entertained doubts.

And, of course, James never willingly entered a fight with the thought that he would lose.

A quiet settled over the group as everyone around waited to see what the next round would bring.

The remaining time ticked down very quickly.

At the last moment, James turned and shot Han Jianguo one last cocky smile.

Then the two men vanished and reappeared in the ring. James acted instantly, as soon as his feet touched the tile.

He let loose a low growl as dozens of monsters suddenly burst forth from his body—all at almost exactly the same moment that he felt the power of Han Jianguo’s Skill Nullification hit him.

That didn’t matter, though.

All around James stood his monsters in a semicircle. Over a dozen of them. There was Doppelganger, of course—a nearly perfect copy of James himself, complete with virtually all of his Skills—but there were also a Skin Snake, his two wyverns, a Blood Slime, a Skin Balloon, a Flesh Golem, a Great Intestinal Worm, a Bone Spider, a Skin Dog, a Bone-Spike Turtle…

There was only one creature remaining inside James’s flesh. But it probably wouldn’t be needed.

“You were hiding all of that inside your frame?” Han Jianguo said. “So that was why you were confident. But I hope those monsters of yours aren’t reliant on Skills to be useful.”

James smiled. “Let’s find out.”
