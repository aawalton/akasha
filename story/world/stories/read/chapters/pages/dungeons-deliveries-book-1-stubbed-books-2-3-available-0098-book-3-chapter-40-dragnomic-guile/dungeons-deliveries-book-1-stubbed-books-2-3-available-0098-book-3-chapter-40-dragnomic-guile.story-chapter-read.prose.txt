After a life of isolating herself from her kind and waiting for something to happen, Boanne found everything quite exciting.

“Yeah?” The furry fellow in a precious little purple suit sneered at the fat human. “And what’s he doing here, then? Don’t trust him. Nope. Don’t trust him at all.”

The young, boisterous gnome woman sighed. Boanne liked her spirit, even if she was incredibly loud of speech.

“I told you all,” Dandelion Datura huffed. “He’s got some crazy skill that lets him see into the future. Plus, Emilio invited him. You going against the orders of Emilio?”

Boanne glanced at Emilio, and her new (very handsome) mate quickly caught her eye as he spoke into her soul. She didn’t quite catch the heated argument between Don Badger and Dandelion after.

No orders were given, Emilio said only to her. But it is good to let them argue. What else is a Council for, my Queen? To weigh our choices.

The dragon supressed her blush. System, she really liked his voice. Actually, there wasn’t a thing she didn’t like about him. What a body, too, all husky and hairy and sleek. He seemed capable of so much, and he’d literally moved a mountain for her. Well, he’d moved a mountain of earth, and technically that was the doing of the precious gnomes she’d so eagerly joined. Boanne hadn’t even needed to ask. When Marzipan had arrived battered and sombre, green glowing Eternity Seed core in hand, the gnomes had widened their underground tunnels in pace with her descent to accommodate her stature.

It wasn’t necessary. She could fly through it all. The gnomes hadn’t even asked. They just wanted to treat her like a queen. Of course, they’d said, she should have an easy time of reaching a respectable mudroom for Council.

“—don’t know crap from shite!” Petunia barked, snapping Boanne’s attention back to the argument. “You heard him. You all heard him. The Eternity Seed gets cultivated by her.”

Suddenly all eyes were back on Boanne. Dandelion nodded seriously, standing with Petal’s widowed wives Petunia and Marzipan. Don Badger stood with crossed arms. The silent gnome missing both his mouth and an arm and wielding a shield sat comfortably, assessing with his calculating eyes. Even the human, Peter, stared doe-eyed at her.

Right, it was her turn to speak.

“Is there even a disagreement?” Boanne asked. “Don Badger, Petunia, Marizpan, Dandelion, and…umm, I’m quite sorry, mouthless one, I don’t know your name. Are you even arguing different sides?”

Emilio’s chuckle rang through her head as they all looked at one another in surprise.

“That’s Acorn Turtleshell, my Queen.” Dandelion bustled over to the mouthless gnome, yanking him up to his feet. “He don’t speak much.”

“But he’s been invited to Council!” Marzipan yelled. “Give him the right of opinion!”

Acorn looked at Dandelion and made a hand gesture. Boanne didn’t know sign-language, but she was pretty sure a thumbs up was obvious.

All eyes went back to her. Boanne waited before continuing. It seemed most like what a Queen should do.

“Then most of you are in favour of me cultivating the Eternity Seed granted by Petal’s sacrifice.”

“Here here!” Petunia, Marzipan, and Dandelion cheered.

“Aye!” Don Badger called.

Even Acorn thumbed his shield on the ground.

“But,” Boanne continued. “You misconstrue what Petal said. Petunia, you remarked that Petal stated that I was to cultivate the Eternity Seed. That was not what he said.”

The gnomes looked at her confused.

“He said that I would choose who cultivated it. I believe his words were, ‘To be chosen for cultivation by only you’. Not cultivated by me. That I alone would select where this blessing shall best be nurtured.”

She held it up for all in attendance to see. Such a small thing. It was no larger than a marble and heavier than it appeared. Petal’s Eternity Seed core glowed brilliantly the colour of forest green, swirling lights and darks endlessly in its depths. Sparkling light shafted out and glittered the smooth mud walls of the room.

She closed her hand around it gently, shutting the presence of it away. Looking over them all, Boanne narrowed her vertically slit pupiled eyes. She’d been expecting something like this.

“I could have taken it for myself without full consideration. Perhaps that is what you expected of a dragon.” Boanne hissed at them. “Each of you knew what Petal stated. I may well cultivate his sacrifice, but only if that is the best choice. So please stop your ruse. And I ask you, do you think me selfish, or worse, do you think me a fool?”

The gnomes and Don Badger grinned back at her with devilish grins. Even Acorn arched an eyebrow her way.

“We had to be sure of your intentions.” Marzipan started.

“And check your guile.” Dandelion added.

“Can’t be having only a pretty face. Not these days.” Petunia continued.

“Seems like we made the right choice, then.” Don Badger chipped in.

And Acorn finished by banging his shield on the ground and nodding at Boanne.

“I have no doubt that you believe that.” Boanne raised her head. “Only, there is one more who needs to answer to the question of my intentions.”

She turned to level her gaze at Emilio, who sat nearby politely, blinking at her.

“Do you doubt my intentions and nature?” Boanne asked him.

A danger was there between them. She with the innate strength of her draconic nature. He with his seemingly bottomless depth of power and calm measure. Boanne felt him more strongly then through their bond, as if Emilio took up much more space than his physical size presented. She didn’t fear it, however, and leaned back with her own prowess.

Both predators shuddered and giggled in each other’s heads at the same time, and the dangerous feeling shattered away.

No, I did not doubt you for even a moment, Emilio said to her. But the gnomes must have their fun.

Boanne wanted to gnaw him, and she suspected he wished to do the same to her. Oh yes, she had picked her System mate bond perfectly well, it seemed. That was good. There was no need to break it. Breaking a mate bond ended in death for one member.

“Meow.” Emilio said to the rest of them, in a tone that confirmed that he did not doubt her either.

A collective breath was released by all the gnomes and the furry Don Badger.

“I appreciate your methods, and thank you for your Council,” Boanne said. “There are many who might benefit from the Eternity Seed, even if we do not know what it will garner.”

Dandelion sniffed. “If only we had a way to peak into what might happen.”

“If only.” Boanne agreed, and as one, everyone in the mud room looked at Pimple Pete.

“Um…” The young man blinked, licking his lips. “I…uh…I would be happy to assist you. It would be, um, the right thing to do after the uh…the uh…the Tax Guild Stone thing.”

His eyes searched frantically for a way out, and he wasn’t able to find one. When Council happened, the room remained sealed until a decision was made.

But Boanne did not want to start her rule with fear, and the gnomes looked at her again. This time, however, it did not seem there was an answer they were looking for.

“We will not force you.” Boanne dipped her head. “And my apologies if this is intimidating, Peter. Please relax.”

“Yeah, relax Peter.” Marzipan grunted.

“Ooh…ok.” Peter somehow managed to curl inward even standing up.

“You have my word, and Emilio’s too.” Boanne answered. “No harm will come to you. But we do ask the favour of the use of your skill. If you say no, then you shall be released without harm, and brought to your destination of choice.”

“Meow?” Emilio asked. He hadn’t signed up for that.

“Released without harm and brought to his destination of choice.” Boanne rumbled.

“Meow.” Emilio sighed, agreeing.

Peter unfurled out of himself slightly, and stood up straight. He took a deep breath and mulled over the decision. Truthfully, he felt horrible about what happened at the Falls. And the gnomes had treated him respectfully, bringing him water and plenty of delicious food. Even their tea was soothing.

“Alright, I’ll do it.” He decided with anxiety. “I’ll use [Got a Tip] to see the possible. But I have to be careful. The last time…well…you know what happened last time.”

The gnomes held their excitement, as did Boanne and Emilio, as Pete seemed to become a different version of himself before their eyes.

His irises rolled to the back of his head. He stood up even straighter, and seemed to lean out physically. His hair lengthened in a shimmering haze, and when he spoke, it sounded nothing like the unsure man previously standing before them.

“I see it. Goodness fucking gracious..” Pimple Pete saw and delivered as his body began to quake. His brow furrowed and his heartbeat hammered. Riding the twisting lines of possibility took an extreme amount of essence.

Suddenly, the slightly less chubby man collapsed in a heap, overwhelmed from all the twisting possibles.

He seemed to come back to himself, and his eyes rolled back to face the right way as terror etched his face.

“Whatever you do, it can’t be any of you.”

“Then who?” Boanne asked.

They all leaned in, eager for the answer to who should receive the Eternity Seed.

“That’s the thing. There’s only one good option. Everything else leads to disaster.” Pete continued. “It has to be Alex’s clone. It has to be Brody.”
