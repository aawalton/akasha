Once they’d been snapped out of their initial grieving and reminded that Mako could be brought back, Jameston and Cora quickly showed their professional nature as they collected themselves and led him over to where they’d first encountered the lich. Asher had been curious how the two of them had managed to find Ploryn in the first place, seeing as they didn’t have any tracking skills available to them.

Turns out spotting a giant, glowing ritual the size of a city block really wasn’t all that difficult.

Directly in the center of the mountaintop stood Ploryn, the lich hovering to and from as he drew with a glowing staff like he were working on a painting. Everywhere his staff touched a line of glowing tritessence was left behind, along with what Identify was telling Asher was a unique mixture of gemstone dust and ink. Unfortunately, they didn’t manage to get the drop on Ploryn, and the lich glanced up at them as they sprinted toward him. Ploryn wore a simple outfit that almost reminded Asher of some of the scribes he’d seen helping Donvath back in the palace with paperwork, but the dancing, yellow fires in his eyes were far from human.

“You bested the clones? I suppose I should have seen that coming. No matter how flawless my element reproduction, I just can’t seem to get the tritessence to match one’s true capabilities. Though I will—”

Ploryn’s words were silenced when Cora hurled a bolt of lightning at the undead abomination. But rather than explode or catch fire, Ploryn merely sighed while a series of runes lit up across the shirt on his chest. The jagged bolt streaking toward him fizzled out harmlessly right before connecting, as if melting away in the light of the runes.

“Please. I know you are all little more than infants when it comes to the workings of tritessence, but surely you have to be smart enough to realize trying to attack me with my own specialty is pointless, yes?”

“Skills are nothing more than tritessence given form, and he’s an expert on the stuff,” Asher muttered, holding out a hand to stop Cora from launching herself at the lich. He didn’t know the first thing about rituals, but it was obvious at a glance that whatever Ploryn was working on hadn’t been completed just yet. The ritual was strangely symmetrical, but there was one chunk that looked to still be missing.

Not to mention the mountain was quite noticeably not collapsed, which was another good indication the ritual hadn’t been finished.

“Impressive, Reaper,” Ploryn said, surprising him by actually nodding his head. “In another life, I would have offered to take you under my wing and teach you just for getting that far on your own. Other than wizards, so few people ever stop to wonder what elements truly are. They are content simply letting their skills speak for themselves. Waving their fingers about and making magic happen beyond their wildest dreams. It is painful to watch.”

“Is that why you’re about to kill a few hundred thousand people?” Cora demanded, all but snarling at the lich as Asher continued holding her back. “Because their existence hurts you?”

“Hardly. This is simple cause and effect,” Ploryn explained. His tone was oddly gentle, as though he were nothing more than a teacher instructing a few unruly students who were interrupting his class. “If we wish for nations to stay out of our business and allow us to further our studies uninterrupted, then we need to ensure they understand there are consequences to coming after us. You formed a coalition of countries and tried to wipe us out. We are just doing the same in turn.”

“If skills don’t work on him, how do we get close and take him out?” Jameston asked in Asher’s mind. “Can you just teleport over there and stab him?”

“He could certainly try, but it wouldn’t end very well for him,” Ploryn said, causing Jameston to recoil as his telepathic speech was overheard. “I already told you, your skills are not your own in my presence. It would do you well to listen.”

Frowning, Asher used Distortion to scoop a rock off the ground. Placing an Eternal Mark on it, he tossed it toward Ploryn. The lich stood almost a hundred feet away, but the moment the rock traveled even half that distance, his skill was peeled back and eaten away, almost like what happened any time he tried placing a mark on his void dagger, only far faster. Despite the fact that his Eternal Marks should be eternal, at the end of the day, like all skills, they were still just tritessence given form. And a tier-one skill at that. Ploryn seemed to have some sort of passive field radiating from him that unwove skills that got close to him.

And that’s just his defenses… I can’t even imagine what kind of offensive power he’s sitting on.

Despite the fact that wizards had any number of skills they were able to recreate with their tritessence, the majority of the liches making up the Council had seemed to prefer to rely on their own elements when it came to combat. Asher had thought it weird enough that he’d asked Richard about it at one point, but the wizard’s answer had made sense.

“Recreating skills that don’t belong to you isn’t really something one can do on the fly all that well. Let’s see how well you do solving a complex math problem while someone is trying to stab you.”

That said, Asher couldn’t shake the feeling that Ploryn could solve whatever problem was thrown his way should the need arise. He was a master of elements, and intelligent enough to come up with a way to do the impossible. Removing an element from another’s soul was supposed to be impossible, but Ploryn had found a way. Sure, it sounded as if it left the human soul in a horrific state, but it still worked. All that begged the question…

How did they go about taking down a genius who had potentially tens of thousands of skills tucked away inside their head?

“Enough of this!” Cora shouted, slapping away Asher’s hand as Ploryn went to start finishing his ritual again. Leaping off the ground in an explosion of fire, she turned into a living, flaming, bolt of lightning that blasted down toward Ploryn, intent on incinerating, detonating, and frying the lich all at the same time. But as the devastating attack shot toward the lich, Ploryn barely even spared her a glance. A wall of what looked like dense, dripping mud erupted out of the ground in front of him, stopping the realm wanderer in her tracks as she slammed into it. Her fire and electricity were equally smothered as she was sucked into the sudden quagmire, and Cora shouted and clawed before she was sucked completely into the lich’s skill, vanishing from view.

“Cora!” Jameston mentally shouted, sprinting forward to try and save his last remaining teammate. Cursing, Asher ran after him, his mind whirling as he tried to figure out a way to bypass Ploryn’s defenses and put the lich down for good. The only thing he could think of was hurling his void dagger at Ploryn and letting the blade erase the lich’s head, but he had a feeling it wasn’t going to be that easy. And if the lich somehow managed to catch his dagger by the hilt, he’d be down his most powerful weapon.

The only other alternative was coming up with a way to outsmart the lich or out maneuver him, but that seemed almost impossible.

“Would you believe the Mud element is one that many people look down upon?” Ploryn asked even as he went back to drawing out his ritual. “It has so many applicable skills, yet something about harnessing the power of mud seems to sit poorly with people. It is strange, the reasons humans will come up with to talk themselves out of harnessing power.”

By this point, Jameston had reached the wall of mud. As the realm wanderer desperately tried to claw the mud away and save his teammate, Asher sprinted past, his eyes locked on Ploryn and Ploryn alone. Not having come up with any better ideas, Asher grabbed a dagger and hurled it straight for Ploryn’s head, compressing space to give the lich less time to react. Just like when he’d tried throwing his daggers at Eupher back in the Hojon Empire, the blade froze in midair, before spinning around and shooting at him instead.

Flickering through the astral to dodge the blade, Asher gritted his teeth as he sliced off his left hand and hurled that forward instead. As if knowing the same telekinetic trick wouldn’t work twice, a long, tendril of grass shot out of the ground, wrapping around his hand and catching it before it could even get close to Ploryn.

“Such fascinating skills, your Eternity element,” Ploryn muttered, continuing to paint away at his ritual as Asher ran toward him. “I am looking forward to studying them in the near future.”

Lunging through compressed space, Asher blinked when his skill was undone, causing him to stumble and fall as he snapped back to his original position. Growling, he slipped into the astral, near enough that he could run the last twenty feet in the other realm. But to his shock, Ploryn merely shook his head before slipping into the astral himself, sighing as he turned and pointed a single finger toward him.

“Your companion’s Arcing Jolt is not bad when it comes to the Lightning element. But it is far from what the element is capable of.”

Asher didn’t even have time to respond before an actual bolt of lightning left Ploryn’s finger and struck him in the same instant. Asher was flung backwards and out of the astral realm as his entire body seized up and his muscles stopped working correctly. His heart fluttered weakly and threatened to stop beating for a moment before it seemed to find its rhythm again, and Asher let out a smoky cough as he stared down at the giant charred circle on his chest. He’d suffered a lot of different kinds of damage during his time on this world as The Reaper.

But being struck by an actual, full-powered bolt of lightning was a new one.

By the time he recovered his bearings and the world stopped ringing in his ears, it was too late. Asher watched as Jameston was sucked into that pillar of mud right after Cora, the man letting out a silent scream as his metallic body failed to keep him safe. Ploryn himself had put the finishing touches on his ritual and was now standing in the middle of it, right in the dead center of the mountain. The lich appeared to be double checking his work, as if wanting to ensure that there wasn’t a single speck of gemstone ink out of place.

“It is important to make sure large-scale rituals are correctly prepared, otherwise the creator might be the one that is harmed,” Ploryn explained to him even as Asher struggled to get back to his feet. His body was repairing itself quickly, but his muscles were weak and still not responding to him correctly. “Seeing as you are on top of the mountain, the collapse should not hinder you too badly. I look forward to examining your Eternity element once we have finished the eradication of this minor kingdom.”

“No… Stop!” Asher shouted weakly, his face paling at the realization that he’d failed. Ploryn had proven himself to be not just a step above the other liches they’d fought, but an entire staircase ahead in terms of knowledge and power. Rather than spare him a second glance, the fires burning in Ploryn’s eyes went out as he lowered his head and raised his staff. But just before he could press it down against the ritual and trigger the massive working, Asher received help from the last source he ever would have expected.

“Not this time!” Donvath shouted, the High Prince appearing directly behind the lich and grabbing him by the head. For some reason, there were bright blue wings of pure tritessence stretching out of Donvath’s back, but Asher barely even noticed those. Brilliant green light blazed from Donvath as the lich froze in place under his touch, even the flames that had momentarily burst back to life in its eye sockets going still as the lich was seemingly locked in time. Donvath’s face was pale and drenched in sweat, and it looked like he was barely standing. But even so, he pushed through whatever was afflicting him as he locked eyes with Asher.

“Finish him! I can’t hold this for long!”

Not needing to be told twice, Asher forced his battered body to its feet and stumbled forward. With a little help from Distortion, he closed the distance between him and the deadly lich. Even now, the powerful green light radiating from the lich’s body looked to be fluttering and dying down as Ploryn somehow fought back against the High Prince's powerful skill. In another few seconds, he’d be free once more. Free to kill all of them and destroy the Noala Kingdom.

Asher didn’t give him those few seconds.

Not taking any chances, Asher drove Crescent straight into Ploryn’s eye, slashing sideways and cleaving the lich’s head in two. Then he yanked his blade back out and slashed forward again, removing the undead’s head from his body for good measure even as Donvath’s skill puttered out. As Ploryn’s unmoving corpse dropped to the ground and the glowing ritual all around them slowly began to fade, Donvath let out a weak laugh and almost collapsed right next to him. Asher barely caught him in time, and Donvath gave him a strained smile as the two stood over Ploryn’s corpse.

“Alright, I take back what I’m going to say. Your plan wasn’t as bad as I’ll think it is!”
