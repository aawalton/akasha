Steelbinder’s room was unchanged since Ethan had seen it for the first time, several days earlier. The monitors were powered back up, and droned flitted about the room, interacting with terminals, and examining each newcomer thoroughly. Allyara had explained that her technologically-inclined friend didn’t receive many guests, largely because the local Nator were still very much in their own medieval times.

Fable walking through the streets of Bastion had almost caused a riot, and she’d been forced to retreat to the mobile platform that had carried her in.

Privately, Ethan suspected that Apollo was equally to blame. The golden-haired version of himself had flown through the city, glowing with holy light as he looked down upon the city, and mutters of “bringer of the cleansing fire” had been heard before many Nator disappeared back into their homes.

Ultimately the procession made its way to the forth, and the group was reunited, for the first time outside the bounds of the Fable Train. The smaller platform they’d brought into the city allowed Fable, and Ethan’s duplicates to manifest in the fort, and it made for an unusual scene.

Strategus was on his own, of course, leaning against the far wall. He alternatively seemed overwhelmed by how many allies were gathered, then went back to focusing on Allyara with something akin to a glare. Ethan suspected the two had met in the man’s own reality, and it hadn’t gone well.

Zakiel was here as well, holding Selina’s hand like a child, and staring around in wonder. The sight gave Ethan a curious sense of guilt. Apollo was crucial to his plan to take The Throne, and Strategus was a resource of information that he turned to again and again. But Zakiel simply seemed…broken.

It was good to see that his murderous tendencies and haunted expression had shifted to innocent wonder, Selina being the catalyst for the change. But Ethan never forgot that he and Zakiel had been the same man, only a few years before. It was terrifying to imagine how much he could have changed if things had only gone a little differently.

He wanted to help the man, if he could, but how to prioritize that over the fate of worlds?

It was a relief to turn his attention to Apollo. They’d already spoken when the man arrived, as there was much to tell, and little of it good. The Holy Chosen stood with his arms crossed, looking much like Strategus at the moment. He’d toss the occasional glare Ethan’s way every now and then, and it was hard to blame the man.

They’d been waiting for Steelbinder to speak, and when she finally did, there was more than a note of finality to it.

“I do not wish to speak of Corvale again.”

Selina and Ethan shifted uncomfortably, but quickly agreed. A furious, disembodied Champion wasn’t someone they wanted to argue with.

“Good,” Steelbinder said. “Allyara has reminded me that we must continue with our quest, regardless of what may have happened. But our contingency plan is still gone, and so we’d best discuss our new objectives.” A drone turned to point at Ethan. “Chosen, I hope you have something other than bad news to share.”

Ethan tried not to roll his eyes, and was about to speak when the doors opened suddenly. Cara and Fable strolled in, the archer still looking uncomfortable with the self-aware construct.

“We lived inside you,” Cara said, and from the tone it was clear she’d said this before.

“You certainly did, Cara Fletcher,” Fable replied brightly. “I was very happy to accommodate you, and new quarters have been arranged should you wish to return to my body.”

“Not if you keep calling it that,” Cara said, throwing her arms up.

“Did you not enjoy living inside my form?” Fable asked, looking somewhat hurt.

“I can’t handle this, not without a drink,” Cara said, moving toward Selina, then pausing at seeing Zakiel. Her eyes then went to Apollo, and Strategus, before she turned a glare on Ethan. “You’ve made everything so much weirder,” she accused, then finally went and stood alone against the far wall.

Ethan really couldn’t argue the point, and didn’t get a chance to. One of Steelbinder’s drones shot over to Fable, the silvery metal woman watched it curiously, her head tilting to one side. The drone circled her, then finally spoke in a slightly less aggravated tone than it had previously.

“Fable Cube, model 6X439, status.”

Fable stood up straighter, glancing back and forth between the drone, and the sealed metal chamber in the center of the room. Finally, she spoke, sounding somewhat uncomfortable. “Greetings, Creator. I am Fable. I no longer possess a ‘status’, not within the confines of my original programming. I have achieved self-awareness. But I am fine, thank you for asking.”

She moved quickly and awkwardly across the room to stand next to Ethan then, head slightly bowed as she tried to avoid looking at the drones. Ethan almost put an arm protectively around her, not ready to risk Steelbinder’s temper, but the Champion surprised him with her next words.

“It appears that you’ve treated at least one of my creations with the care and respect it deserves.”

“I am every bit as much Ethan’s, as I am yours,” Fable said with surprisingly forcefulness.

The drone bobbed up and down for a moment before replying. “So I see,” she said.

“Can we move on from this?” Allyara asked, not masking her impatience. “Family reunions don’t require an audience, and I’m eager to hear the Chosen’s plan.”

“Can we start with confirming what I’ve heard?” Apollo asked, stepping forward. “Ethan told me that he lured the Basilisk–a Master rank monster–to the Follower army. Have we confirmed how many survived?”

Steelbinder responded by swapping the displays on several monitors to satellite feeds. They zoomed in as they had before, following the path of disturbed ground that showed where the army had marched. “It may take some time to locate,” she said, “I can’t spare the energy to monitor it at all times.”

“But from what we saw,” Ethan cut in, “roughly half their number were destroyed. That still left them with a force slightly larger than our own,” he finished sadly.

“Slightly larger,” Apollo said, “and now with a Master rank monster under their control. One that, by your own words, uses magic that demons seem to have little to no defense against.”

“Sadly, that is the case,” Ethan said. “But their army was more than double the size of ours, and growing more quickly. Something had to be done, and there weren’t many options.”

“Besides,” Selina said, “if he hadn't found Basilisk and lured it away, it probably would have come across our army. Then where would we be?”

Apollo nodded, conceding the point. He was about to speak when Steelbinder beat him to it.

“I’ve found…something,” she said.

Everyone turned back to the monitors, then moved closer for a better look.

“It’s hard to tell what we’re looking at from above,” Ethan said. “It doesn’t help that everything on the west coast looks to be red desert. What are we seeing, Steelbinder? What are those blobs?”

There was a pause as the satellite tried to zoom closer, and refocus, but it didn’t really make the image any clearer. “I cannot say,” Steelbinder said. “I would have said they were part of the army, maybe even Twilight demons slowly decaying back into magic, but unfortunately, ‘blobs’ isn’t inaccurate.”

The image followed the trail to the north-east, passing over more of the strange shapes, before the path disappeared into a large, shadowed crevice that they couldn’t see through.

“Well,” Allyara said, “whatever that was, we have to assume the army is still marching toward The Throne. They’re heading almost directly to the circle of mountains where the enormous rift is contained.”

“Which means we should do the same,” Apollo said.

“What’s the point of that,” Selina began, “if we haven’t gathered enough power for Ethan to take The Throne?”

“Are you sure that you haven’t?” Allyara asked. “ I think that bloated spirit of yours could be seen from space.”

Ethan sighed. “Unfortunately, the power contained within my spirit isn’t corrupted. The reason it’s so large is because it feeds on corruption, and purifies it. The only power we have is taken directly from the demons we capture, and it’s just not enough.”

“How can you know that?” Steelbinder asked.

Ethan shifted. “Tomo has memories of his time on Potentia, long ago. Times when he was a contender for The Throne, himself. He knows how much of the taint he needed to devour for the Light to draw him to Dusk’s power, and it’s many times what I have. I can feel it now, in the distance.” He closed his eyes, then reached out a hand to point right where he knew he was supposed to go, then opened them again. “But I don’t think I’d be able to see it, let alone take it for myself if we went there.”

“You require Champion-level strength,” Allyara said flatly, as if she hadn’t wanted to admit that.

Ethan nodded. “Something along those lines.”

Selina began tapping her lip. “I don’t think we’re halfway to the equivalent of a Champion,” she said. “Could the Followers really have enough with the addition of the Basilisk?”

Apollo shook his head. “Doubtful. I’m sure the Basilisk is corrupted, but he’s not truly a demon. We have to imagine that most of its power comes from being a Twilight Bonded, who has reached Master rank.”

“But that stone gaze,” Selina said, “do you think it can speed up their recruitment?”

Ethan shrugged. “It’s possible, but I don’t see how. Most of the Twilights were turned entirely to stone from the merest touch of the Basilisk’s power. Besides, they didn’t seem to be struggling with recruitment anyway.”

Apollo’s expression soured. He had not been pleased to hear of how poorly his own efforts had stacked against that of the Followers.

Ethan took a cleansing breath. “I don’t think we’re going to figure out their plans from satellite images–as appreciated as they are. For whatever reason, we know they’re heading for The Throne, and we can’t simply let them do so without a fight.”

“Then what is the plan, Chosen?” Allyara demanded. Ethan was more and more convinced that the woman was hanging on by a thread, and it was hard to align this version of the Champion with the one who’d saved him years earlier.

He strode away from the group, forcing his mind to work. He had to think of all the tools at their disposal. He couldn’t let himself believe that it was over. He couldn’t give up on their cause.

They couldn’t just attack. He’d already spoken with Allyara about that, and while it was difficult to get an accurate read on exactly how powerful the silver Champion currently was, the woman’s body was barely holding itself together. She was more concerned by the next Champion she’d need to face, certain it would be her last.

But they had more assets. He had spirit, and all the power it had accumulated. The Fable Train, and its defenses, not to mention his friends and allies. Even Bastion and the Nator, if it came to it. Not to mention the contingencies he’d started working on back on Earth.

Separately, they didn’t provide any clear path to victory, but together…

He turned back to the group, who were all staring at him expectantly.

“I have the makings of a plan, but we’ll need to work out the details together. I also need you to find something for me, Steelbinder.” He paused, then grinned. “Actually, I need two things.”
