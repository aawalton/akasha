The core in my hand felt cold. I let it drop back into my pocket; it was useless to us right now.

The Crone wanted weapons? Weapons forged from portal metal? There was only one organization I knew of that had cracked that secret. The Traynor Corporation.

Or, more accurately, Rebecca Overholz. She and her company had figured out how to melt down portal metal and re-cast it into a useful shape—just before the Traynor Corporation’s hostile takeover. I didn’t know the exact physics and metallurgy behind it, but I didn’t have to. All I needed to know was that, in twenty years, human-forged weapons had done nothing to damage portal monsters, and delvers had been the first, last, and only line of defense for Earth. The Traynor-Overholz Cannons were the beginning of a shift away from that.

Ellen recovered first. “What if we say no?”

“Simple. If you say no, I end this negotiation, and you return to your world with nothing. No information, no leads. Nothing.” The Crone didn’t say it as a threat. It was a mere statement of fact, and I took it that way. “I don’t actually care whether the God of Thunder or the Placid King ascend, just like I don’t care whether you find the Soaring Sabers. In the long run, their ascensions past SS-Plus Rank don’t hurt me, and the vacuum of power they’ll inevitably leave behind through death or through leaving only benefits me. I have no stake in your conflict, Kade.”

Something about that statement—about the other two Paragons ascending—felt off. She was hiding something. Something important. She did have a stake in their conflict, but…but what?

“Then why offer us a deal at all? If you’re going to get something when they leave, why not speed that process up?” Jessie asked. She sat between Ellen and me, and her arms were crossed over her chest. I recognized the posture, and I didn’t have to imagine the glare she was shooting at the most powerful being in the universe.

“Because my only concern is with maintaining my place in this universe, dearie. I’ve been very up front about that from the get-go, haven’t I?”

“I guess so. It just doesn’t add up,” Jessie shot back. “Something’s missing, and it has to be something about you.”

The Crone stared at Jessie for a while. Then she nodded. “It’s a curse all our kind has. When you get to be as old as me, you get desperate for anything new to shake up the monotony. Those weapons are something new—and they’re something I can sell.”

Jessie went quiet. I closed my eyes and waited for her to react, or for Ellen to say something. This was their show, not mine. I couldn’t force the Crone to give us what we wanted.

“You want the current generation of Traynor-Olverholz Cannons? Bob’s the kind of guy who’s going to push innovation as fast as he can. Right now, they’re the kind of weapons you’d see on old wooden ships, but in a month or two, they’ll be rifled, explosive howitzers,” Ellen said. “The longer you wait, the better they’ll be.”

“Bah! People tell me that all the time. I don’t care about the quality. The novelty is all that matters to me, and it’s all that matters to half of my buyers. They’re the ones with wealth, so they’re the ones I care about.”

“Can we deliver them in two days?” Ellen asked.

Jessie looked back at her, then nodded. “It’ll take us time to get a shipment of them together and bring them through the portal. But Kade needs this information—and that key—now. Otherwise, he’s in serious trouble.”

“That’s fine. I can live with that. After all, Enolda’s on your side of the portal, and it’s not like I don’t know where you live. Now, girlies, the two of you need to get to work on getting my guns together. I have a few words to share with Kade. I’ll return him to your world when I’m finished with him.”

Once Ellen and Jessie left, I relaxed slightly. One less potential lever the Paragon before me could use to force my hand in her favor. She was powerful, but not directly hostile. Still, I didn’t trust her. Not quite.

The Crone waited until they stepped through the portal—I knew it because Ellen’s suppressed aura vanished from my senses—then cleared her throat. “Kade Allen Noelstra, I haven’t been completely honest with your sister or your partner. I’m sorry about that, truly I am, but they don’t need to know everything that you do. You’re up against the God of Thunder and the Placid King. They’re nothing. Not really. None of this universe is anything in the grand scheme of things.

“He probably told you about me. Told me I was the strongest of the SS-Plus Ranked Paragons. That I’d been here the longest. That instead of hoarding power for my own ascension, I’m hoarding it to stave off that moment.”

“Something like that,” I said.

“That is a lie. It’s a lie I’ve carefully constructed, but a lie nonetheless. The power I gather here goes to one project, and one project only.”

She paused. “I am the Eldest of this universe. The God of Thunder is only twenty millennia old. The Placid King is even younger than that. In comparison, I am eternal and ageless. I’ve seen the act of ascension before. The preparations for it. The violence of the act itself. And the aftershocks that shake the universe.”

“And you’re, what? Trying to keep the universe together?” I asked. “You can’t convince me that you’re some benevolent god.”

“I’m not trying to, and I’m far from purely benevolent—just better than my competition. Let me tell you a story about a young elemental known as the King of Thunder. He followed an elf, much like you followed him. Stormlord Nalthelas. The Stormlord attempted to ascend twenty thousand years ago. He prepared dozens of pupils to be sacrificed in the effort. Their energy would help to open the gate between universes. Their deaths would crack the seals. For ten thousand years, he prepared for that moment. Countless worlds—not portal worlds, but ones like yours—suffered to propel his attempt. But he failed.

“Another story. Deepqueen Vantessiala. Her attempt at ascension converted the oceans of a dozen worlds into dry expanses. Or Malackat of the Third Way. He attempted ascension fueled only by his own power. It took him nearly two million of your years. When he finally did it, the ripples broke entire galaxies into massive nebulae.”

Another pause, this one longer. The ghostly, bent woman regarded me with an eye that sparkled with reflected moonlight even under the tent’s cover. When I didn’t say anything, she continued. “I, too, feel the itch. It’s even worse than the whisper that compels me to kill people like you, Kade. It’s a pressure, and it becomes overwhelming the longer you ignore it. And I have ignored it for eons.”

“Why tell me this?” I asked. But I already knew the answer. It was so that I’d understand the God of Thunder and the Placid King. They were my enemies. I needed to understand why they did what they did, so I could figure out the best way to fight them. The Crone might claim neutrality, but she was more than willing to bend that. That was the question I wanted to ask. Why? Why was she helping me?

“Because you need to understand me. You’re playing the game just like the God of Thunder or the Placid King. Just like me, too. I haven’t given up on ascension, either. The smart move for me would be to kill you as a potential rival and add your power to my hoard. But that wouldn’t solve my problem. It’d only make it worse.

“Power is a blessing and a curse, Kade. We need so much of it to scratch that itch and move toward ascension. But the more we hoard, the worse it gets. It’s an addiction, and the only way to truly resist it is to use that power—here, in this universe. That’s the true purpose of the Ghostmarked. It doesn’t hold me here physically. The power it takes to maintain a portal world of this…unique…quality is enough to keep me sane.

“Your God of Thunder has no such outlet. He wants no such outlet.”

I tapped my fingers on the table. Not that what she was saying wasn’t interesting. It was even useful. But I had a mission. The sooner Ellen and I arrived at the Soaring Sabers’ world, the sooner I could—hopefully—come up with a solution for Ellen’s breaking core. The sooner I could get back to fortifying the Black Tower. “So?”

“So.” The Crone clapped her hands. “Don’t expect him to be insane. Not in the way you’re expecting, at least. But he’s an eccentric old bastard—they both are—and they’re both driven by nothing more than scratching the two itches that drive us. Killing and ascension. That’s all that matters to them. Expect the unexpected in both fights, and make sure you’re ready.”

I couldn’t put it off anymore. “Why help me? Why do you care?”

“Simple. Every time a Paragon ascends, the universe gets a little smaller—metaphorically, but also literally. There’s a little less power, and the wall’s a little thicker. If Eugene or the Placid King uses your power to fuel their ascension, the difficulty of leaving will increase. That’s why I’ve plotted to stop their ascensions from occurring, and why I sabotaged Deepqueen Vantessiala’s ascension. It’s why I attempted to free the King of Thunder and the other pupils before the Stormlord could use them. It’s why I watched Malackat’s self-powered ascension attempt with such interest.

“When I ascend, I’ll do it without leaving chaos and destruction in my wake. And to make that happen, I cannot allow the path to get any more difficult than it already is. All my energy—all the energy the Ghostmarket doesn’t take, at least—goes into keeping this universe’s barrier unbreached.”

Silence.

The implications were wild. Assuming she was being truthful, I had an ally. Not a friend. The Crone made it clear that she wasn’t my friend with every word—if I so much as attempted ascension myself, she’d be my enemy instantly. But she was willing to give me this information for free, because it benefited her for me to know it.

And more importantly, there were implications for me and how I chose to fight the two god-like Paragons gunning for me. My first thought had been to gain as much personal power as I could. If she was right, that would be a mistake. At some point, I’d start feeling that pressure, and I had no idea what it would do to me. But I also couldn’t beat either of them the way I was now. She was reinforcing two ideas while warning me to discount the third.

I needed allies. True allies—ones who’d stand with me.

And I needed an ace in the hole. A queen in the late-game. Something I could use that neither the Placid King nor the God of Thunder was ready for. The Black Tower itself, with all the power it harvested from the storm, wouldn’t be enough. These monsters had been hoarding it for twenty millennia. I needed something they couldn’t handle. Something that’d let me punch much higher than my weight class.

It was a good thing I was working on that even now.

“Well, that’s that,” The Crone said, interrupting my thoughts. “The keystone, then. To the Soaring Sabers’ portal world, right? You’re in luck. One of their representatives is here, right now, and he’s informed me that they’re allowing members into the inner sect very, very soon. You’ll want to hurry it up, though.”

She reached into her translucent robe, rummaged around in the space between cloth and skin, and eventually, produced a keystone.

It was a marble, much like the one I’d traded for in the aftermath of the fight against King Hendren. A tiny thing, the size of a pinky nail. But while that one’s color shifted to match its holder’s core—and only stopped doing that after I cleared the Solo Portal and claimed it as my own—this keystone core was locked to a single color. The color of molten portal metal, blazing a hot orange-gray, but still solid and sharp.

“Take it. And remember, your sister needs to get those guns to me as soon as possible. She’s a real dearie, but the last thing either of you needs is a conflict with a third SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon, and I don’t handle being cheated very well.”

The Black Tower was clear when I returned.

Dead monsters lay strewn around it, but the fourth floor was clear, and so was the stairway. Mostly, at least. I only stayed long enough to check that it was safe and turn off the traps before stepping back through its portal and confronting Jessie and Ellen. The tower would need every scrap of power it could gather if I wanted to use it as the late-game queen I knew it could be.

“You’re really not going to tell us what the Crone said, huh?” Jessie asked for the fifth time.

I shook my head. “It’s better if I don’t. Not yet. I don’t necessarily trust her.” That wasn’t true. There was no reason for her to lie to me, and every reason for her to tell the truth. She gained very little by moving me down a certain path. Just a chance that I could beat one of the two Paragons and take the pressure off of her in terms of stopping them herself. If I failed, it didn’t change much for her.”

“Fine. But I want to know eventually.”

I ruffled her hair as she pouted. She took a swing at my shoulder, and I let it connect. “Ow.”

“I know you’re faking it every time, Kade,” she said.

“Sure, but you still punch me, and I still say it. Now, Ellen and I should only be gone for twelve or so hours, max. You’re going to work on those weapons, right?” I pulled the S-Rank core out of my pocket and held it out to her.

She cupped her hands below it. “Yeah. I’ll get her guns to her. And I’ll work on the Guardians issue, too. I’ve got some ideas. How willing are you to work with the Portal Tyrants, Kade?”

I shrugged. “I can live with them. They’re not the worst guild out there.”

“Good, because to maintain the balance of power in Phoenix, we’ll need an ally with some power, and like I said earlier, the Roadrunners and Coyotes are both out. That leaves Terrel Young and his people—and us, when you get back. It’ll probably require a formal, public statement, but I think I can leverage the T-O Cannon transaction to help set up an announce—“

“We trust you, Jessie,” Ellen said.

“Yeah. When we’re back, we’ll work on that problem.” Time was ticking, and I pulled the keystone out and activated it. A portal appeared—one unlike any other I’d seen before. The golden glow was normal, at least, but the wreath of blades around its edges reminded me of fangs, ready to close around me at any moment.

I took Ellen’s hand. She tightened her grip around my palm. “Good luck, Jessie. You’ve got this.”

“You too, Kade,” she said, waving from her wheelchair.

And with that, Ellen and I stepped into the portal and entered the world of the Soaring Saber sect.
