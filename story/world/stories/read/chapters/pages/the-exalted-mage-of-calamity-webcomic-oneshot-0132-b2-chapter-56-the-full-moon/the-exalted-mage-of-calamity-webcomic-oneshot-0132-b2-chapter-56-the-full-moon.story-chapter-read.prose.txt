Night had fallen over Vitian.

Several kilometers from Maftem, the Mirabelle drifted aloft, high in the sky. It had been a particularly cloudy day, and tomorrow would bring the full moon. Everyone had done their part, spending most of the past two days preparing and settling any remaining obligations.

Orban and Theo still had leftover funds from selling the Steelhand Smithy. In exchange for providing them a room and workshop, they gave Veronica five thousand vix. It was too much for simple lodging, even compared to noble villas, but Orban made a point of saying the money should go toward strengthening their team. If Avalon turned out to be fake, he expected them to pay it back.

In the meantime, part of that money was then given to Gwen, who went on an errand to the Willow Mage Tower, the same place Veronica had bought the Blue Dryad’s potion from Rennia. With one thousand vix, Gwen bought two inferior healing potions, good for bruises and other minor injuries. Although Gwen was the healer of the party, she wasn’t infallible. Gwen was strong-willed and brave, but she was still inexperienced. Accidents could happen at any time, and having precautions to fall back on could very well save lives.

Their last expedition into the Valley of Silence had made it clear that they needed to be more prepared.

Likewise, Nasha was given two thousand vix. She went to a shop and bought a pack of thirty reinforced arrows, along with five specially enchanted ones. With her power of aura, she would be able to make those last quite a while. She also picked up a pack of basic adventuring supplies: rope, bandages, a fire-starter, and several other essentials.

Lastly, Veronica used the remaining two thousand vix to buy two mana elixirs. Unlike potions, elixirs helped boost a mage’s mana regeneration by stimulating their rings. This resulted in more total mana recovered over time, rather than an instant burst.

With all of their preparations complete, and the day finally over, everyone went to sleep.

Veronica was no exception. Unlike in her previous life, however, she now had someone to share the warmth of the night with.

“I… like this a lot, you know,” Veronica said.

She was pressed into Nasha’s side while Nasha gently caressed her head. They were in their bedroom, covers pulled up to their chests, snuggling as if nothing else mattered in the world.

For now, there were no more documents to read, no more materials to gather. Just the soft hum of Mirabelle’s engines.

“Being spoiled?” Nasha asked, looking down at her with a faint smile.

“Is that what this is?”

“Hm. Maybe. Though this is quite normal for some people.”

Veronica looked up at her. “You say that about everything.”

Nasha’s smile grew wider, carrying that teasing look she always had whenever they were alone together. “You can’t prove me wrong if you’ve never experienced a relationship before, right?”

Veronica rolled her eyes before closing them. Then she smiled and nestled her head into a more comfortable position. “I’m not exactly a slow learner, if my past life means anything. I’ll figure out what’s right or wrong sooner or later. Just you watch.”

Nasha closed her eyes. She leaned her head against Veronica’s, and the two of them remained like that for several long moments.

Then, she was the one to break the silence.

“You nervous about tomorrow?” Nasha asked. Her voice was softer now.

“No.”

“Not even a little?”

“I’ve long gone past the stage of worry, Nasha,” Veronica said. “Although I don’t know exactly what we’ll encounter within the Isles of Avalon, I’m certain things will work out just fine.”

“And why’s that?”

“Because I’ve handled tougher things before. I’ve seen all there is to see. I’m not worried about Avalon because I see this more as an opportunity than a risk.”

Veronica’s fingers found Nasha’s, and she gripped her lightly. “In this life… there’s a lot more for me to be optimistic about. There’s… more hope.”

A few seconds of silence passed before Veronica chuckled softly.

“What is it?” Nasha asked.

“It… feels strange. That you basically know everything about me. You and Gwen both. My story isn’t that unique. You could summarize my life in just a few sentences. And yet now, after coming back to the past… I don’t think that same summary would fit me anymore.” She let the silence hang for a moment before continuing. “I actually feel happy to be a normal person. Although I still have this mission hanging over my head, I’ve gotten to experience things I never would have before. I got to meet both you and Gwen. And I have to admit… it’s been fun.”

“You’re glad to have met us?”

“…Yeah. As uptight or arrogant as I may seem to be, that’s only one side of me. The side that’s been… controlled, like you said, by the kingdom. The truth is, there’s another side of me that just wants to have fun. To explore. To be a little wild. Being able to go around the city, buy things for the first time, even dresses, and having my own leisure… I’m grateful for that.”

Veronica’s hand tightened on Nasha’s. “That’s with both you and Gwen. But for you specifically… I can relax. I can feel a bit vulnerable, especially in moments like these. I can just… talk to you freely. And you’ll listen. I love that you can give me moments of respite like that.”

Nasha nuzzled her cheek against Veronica’s hair.

For once, she didn’t answer immediately.

Veronica expected at least some teasing remark. Something playful. Something flirty, the way Nasha usually was. Carefully chosen words that would continue to break her out of her shell. It was something no one else could ever say or do to an exalted mage.

Instead, Nasha just held her a little tighter.

“I’m glad I can be that special person for you.”

Veronica opened one eye. “That’s it?”

“Hm?”

“No teasing? You’re usually quick with stuff like this. Some smug comment about how hopelessly attached or clingy I am?”

Nasha chuckled.

Veronica could feel her laughter vibrating through her hair. It was ticklish.

“Well, you are a bit clingy. Almost like a lost puppy,” Nasha said.

“A lost puppy?”

“Mm. Like a stray that’s been living on the streets before finally finding its forever home with its owner.”

“Are you saying you own me now?”

Veronica could feel her smirk.

“Do you want me to?” Nasha said, her voice much more sultry than normal.

Veronica shivered but made no comment. Instead, she made herself more comfortable and relaxed.

For a while, neither of them spoke any further. Veronica could hear the soothing beat of Nasha’s heart near her ear. Outside, she could hear the ambient howl of wind brushing against Mirabelle’s exterior. She imagined the war she had fought against the demons. The restless days, the countless lonely nights, the monsters that had kept her awake.

And for this one moment… she let go. Her breathing evened out, and the tension in her body faded, easing away as sleep finally took hold of her.

Nasha remained pressed close. “Veronica?” she whispered.

There was no answer.

Nasha waited a few seconds before trying again. “You awake?”

Still, there was no response.

Veronica’s breathing had fallen into a steady rhythm, soft and even. She was completely asleep now.

Nasha didn’t move, but her expression shifted. Her lips parted as she spoke.

“When the time comes…”

Nasha’s words came out as a soft whisper. With Veronica asleep, she was confident that none of it would reach her. She was essentially speaking to herself, with no one else there to listen.

A couple of minutes passed. Eventually, Nasha closed her eyes and fell asleep, holding Veronica close. She had said what needed to be said.

“This everything ya need?” Orban asked. He stood in front of a small table that had been set up along the deck. On top were various ingredients and reagents, including lunar salt, voidglass shards, and a dryad seed.

Everyone was gathered on the deck, the night sky looming overhead as the stars illuminated Mirabelle. Most of the morning and afternoon had been spent on last-minute preparations. That included stocking food and other tools they might need, just in case.

They were over a hundred kilometers away from Maftem, safe above the wilderness with no one around. With the full moon nearing its zenith, the ritual to open Avalon’s portal was upon them.

Veronica stood in the center of the deck, holding an empty book where she had re-inscribed Morgan’s instructions on how to open the portal. Sage had already memorized it, but the book was there just in case someone else needed to perform the ritual.

“This should be everything,” Veronica said. She looked up at the night sky. The moon was directly above them. “According to Sage, there should be about two minutes left. I’ll go ahead and begin.”

Everyone took a step back and watched.

Veronica held the book open in one hand and gestured with the other. She levitated all the materials on the table, rotating them in the space in front of her. Then, she summoned forth a black flame made entirely of demonic energy.

Orban’s eyes widened as he stared at the flame. “What the hell? Is that what I think it is?”

Theo looked at him, then back at the flame, clearly unsure of what Veronica was doing or why the flame was special beyond its color.

“How are you using that?” Orban continued. He was flabbergasted. He knew what he saw. A bundle of demonic energy, condensed into a black flame, conjured by an elven mage? It was common knowledge that only demons could use demonic energy. Anyone from the races using it was unheard of.

“It’s a long story,” Veronica said. “I’ll explain after the trip.”

Orban looked as if he were about to argue, but the lunar salt floating in front of Veronica began to glow. The fine, silvery-white grains scattered through the air, each one shining like a tiny spark as moonlight bathed them. The voidglass shard rotated in the center, absorbing all that light like an endless abyss, making it seem as though the air itself were bending.

[Full moon alignment in ten seconds.]

Veronica turned and sent each material floating out in front of Mirabelle, spreading them wide. With a flick of her finger, the demonic black flame expanded, coating the voidglass shard. One by one, she pressed the other materials closer: the shining pearl, then the dryad seed.

The black fire stretched outward in a large circle, connecting to each scattered grain of lunar salt and forming a massive web. Finally, the moonlight above fully activated the grains, causing them to glow even brighter than before.

Theo took a step back, covering his eyes. “Master, is this… really alright?”

Orban was forced to shield his eyes as well, but he wouldn’t dare miss what was happening. He braved the bright light as much as he could.

The voidglass shard cracked.

A sharp sound rang through the air around them, like a hundred shattered windows.

The voidglass shard split into several fragments, but instead of falling, Veronica moved them outward, forming a large, rounded perimeter in front of Mirabelle. Next, the dryad seed burst open. A pure green light expanded from within, growing larger and larger until it formed an almost solid-looking wall.

The process didn’t take long, but each step was delicate, requiring Veronica to move every ingredient at the correct time.

Luckily, she didn’t fail.

Everything was firmly stabilized. The voidglass fragments formed the outer boundary of the portal, while the lunar salt and spiritual energy gave shape to the visible doorway. The other ingredients had their roles as well, but none were as striking as those.

Now, all that stood before Mirabelle was a massive silvery-green portal, large enough for an airship to pass through.

Veronica exhaled and closed the book.

When she turned around, everyone was staring at the portal with shocked expressions.

Not that she could blame them.

“Alright, everyone,” Veronica said. “To Avalon.”

For a moment, no one moved.

Then Orban let out a low breath. “By the deep fires… it’s actually real.”

Veronica allowed herself a small smile before turning toward the helm.

Mirabelle’s engines hummed louder. Slowly, the airship drifted forward, its bow cutting through the moonlit clouds as the silvery-green light swallowed the deck.

Veronica stood at the front, watching the portal draw closer.

Then Mirabelle passed through.

And the world vanished behind them.
