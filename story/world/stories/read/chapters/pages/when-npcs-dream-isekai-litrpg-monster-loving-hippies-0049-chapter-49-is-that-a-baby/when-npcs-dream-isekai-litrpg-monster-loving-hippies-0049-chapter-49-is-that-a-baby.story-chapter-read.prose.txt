Miriam’s first night without her mother was rough for her. She woke up twice, made a soft “mmm” sound, and then started crying — clearly wanting her mother. Both times, Lori came, held her, sang to her (badly), and after a while Miriam went back to sleep.

When morning came, I found Miriam awake, sitting quietly and just looking around the room with wide curiosity.

In daylight, I finally got a proper look at her. She was clearly half-goblin — her skin a pale green shade, ears pointy but not too much.

She had black hair, soft baby features, and surprisingly fierce eyes. Her gaze was intense.

I never had much of an opinion about babies before — hadn’t really seen many up close — but looking at Miriam, I thought she looked cool. For a baby, at least.

So, what now?

Maybe she was hungry.

Vel’nari didn’t leave me much advice on raising a baby — “she likes meat” was pretty much it.

So I brought her some salted strips of horned rat meat (which, despite what you might think, actually tastes good!).

Miriam looked at them with hungry eyes, snatched them up, and began eating them savagely with her sharp little goblin teeth. I guess she really does like meat!

After I grabbed a quick bite myself, I started thinking about my day. Honestly, I was insanely busy these days — the past few weeks had been absolute chaos.

Juliette wasn’t here. About a month ago she’d decided to go on some “epic king’s quest” far away with Santiago, Charlotte, Chantal, and three of the newer Stormcrows recruits.

Apparently, as our group grew — we hit 28 members now — Juliette realized that being leader mostly meant drowning in paperwork. She said she was going to lose her mind if she had to look at one more form.

She also admitted she missed the adventure — even if we’d come close to dying more than once.

Before leaving, she also decided that I, not Hyun-woo, would take her place as acting leader.

I didn’t like that one bit.

Hyun-woo was smarter and far better at bureaucratic bullshit than I’d ever be. Plus, I was already buried in work and barely had time to spend with Lori.

And if that wasn’t bad enough, about a week ago Hyun-woo went completely crazy!

He robbed the mansion of every single book and locked himself in a room, muttering things like “It’s the most important book” and “I just need to find the book.” Never really explained what he was doing.

So all of Hyun-woo’s duties magically fell on me.

Luckily, there was Yichen — the guy who joined us from Farhad’s old group at the keep. He’d been helping Hyun-woo for the better part of the year, and could take over some of his work. But Hyun-woo was our trade minister, and all trade decisions still needed my review and approval.

So, to summarize: with the two busiest people in our band abandoning me, my hands were full. And now, on top of all that — I got a baby.

A literal baby!

But honestly, Miriam was more important than anything else. I knew the first days or weeks without her mom would be really hard for her, and I wanted to make it as easy as possible.

Only problem: I had absolutely no idea what you were supposed to do with babies.

Luckily, I had a friend who did — Samar. She’d practically raised her little siblings by herself while her parents worked long hours. If anyone was a baby expert, it was her.

After we finished eating, I picked up Miriam and started walking toward the large clearing near town. Samar used it as a training ground for animals — she spent most of her time there with Kim Jong Un and his young brother - Stalin, training the beasts they controlled. We’d even built a big wooden shack there, stocked with food and equipment.

As I walked, holding Miriam, I felt my phone vibrating in my pocket. It never stopped these days.

“Geshem here,” I answered.

“Hi! It’s Miz’ethe,” squeaked a birdlike voice. “How… chu… are you doing? How is, err, your day?”

It was the bird player from relatively new group of about ten players based in Kuriolis. She was trying to imitate human small talk. It went poorly.

“I’m fine,” I said. “The books haven’t arrived yet.”

“Yes! The books!” she squeaked. “Bernard said 25 gold wasn’t enough! Too many magic books! And Janet. Janet said you are a thief!”

“Hey! Don’t tell him I said that!” I heard Janet yell in the background. “Give me the phone!”

There was some struggling, then Janet came on.

“Look,” she said, “25 gold for 20 magic books is a terrible deal for us.”

I rubbed my temple in frustration. “It doesn’t matter if you like it or not — it was the deal we already made! I’m waiting for those books! I have mages in training who need them! You can’t just… regret a deal after the fact!”

“Bernard said,” she continued unfazed, “make it 50 gold and we’ll ship today.”

“What!?” I shouted. “Those books aren’t even yours! And we’ve given you guys tons of stuff for free these past months!”

“Bernard said we’re training mages now too, so the books are valuable to us as well.”

“Fine — 30 gold!”

“40 gold!” Janet said. smugly.

“Fine! But ship today — or forget about the power rings deal!”

Bernard’s group was the third and last player group in Aldioli. There was another group of fifteen in the capital, Aldiolin.

Unlike Farhad’s group, we’d helped these guys a lot early on and had good relations — or so I thought. Somehow, they’d gotten hold of Farhad’s old collection of magic books without pissing off the authorities, and now they were trying to swindle me for extra gold.

“Now,” Janet added, “about those GPS devices—”

That’s when Miriam started crying loudly. My shouting must’ve scared her.

“Come on, Miriam,” I whispered, rocking her gently. “Shh, shh, Daddy didn’t mean to shout.”

“Holy shit!” Janet said. “Is that a baby? Why do you have a baby!?”

“Sorry, gotta go!” I hung up and kept soothing Miriam. “You’re a scary little goblin,” I whispered. “You don’t get scared by shouting…”

Somehow, it worked. She calmed down again, looking around curiously.

When we reached the clearing, I spotted Samar with Kim and Stalin. She was training Stalin’s second horned furred snake.

“HIII SAMAR!” I shout.

It was noisy here, as the snakes made these loud “moo”-like sounds every so often.

Samar turned, saw us, and her eyes went wide.

“WHERE DID YOU FIND THAT CUTE THING!?” she shouted back.

“It… sort of got delivered to me,” I said. “By Vel’nari.”

“Vel’nari…” she repeated, thinking for a long moment. “Oh…” and then— “Oh!”

“Is Vel’nari around?” she asked.

“This is permanent, I think,” I said. “She said it’d be bad for a goblin-human baby to grow up with the goblins — that they’d treat her poorly because of her human features. She thought it’d be better if she grew up with us. I guess she’ll visit sometimes.”

I hoped so. When Vel’nari brought me Miriam, I could see how much it tore her apart — she deeply loved her baby. Poor Vel’nari. It must’ve been horrible for her.

“She’s so tiny!” Samar said, reaching out her arms. “Can I?”

“Sure.” I handed Miriam over. She didn’t protest.

“You’re just the cutest thing!” Samar cooed. “What’s her name?”

“Miriam.”

“Miriam… Jesus’s mother! Great name!”

“No, she was Moses’s sister — a prophet,” I explained. “Anyway, she’s a six-month-old goblin baby now.”

“Well, she’s adorable either way.”

Miriam noticed one of the furry snakes crawling nearby, stretched out her little hand, and made a determined mmm noise.

“Yes, that’s Thubana,” Samar told her, walking with her closer to the giant beast.

“Wait— is that safe!?” I asked in panic. “Can’t the snake mistake her for food or something?”

“What? No!” Samar laughed. “Thubana’s the sweetest thing. She’s been with us for months. The new male — Thuban — yeah, keep her away from him. He’s a bit bitey. Maybe keep yourself away too. But Thubana? She wouldn’t hurt a fly.”

I was pretty sure I’d seen her fling an Armadillo Care Bear ten feet into the air, but fine — I trusted Samar’s judgment.

Miriam wriggled to get down, so Samar set her on the ground. Miriam stood, wobbled for a moment, then started toddling slowly — cutely — toward the huge furry snake.

“Holy shit!” Samar said. “You said she was six months old! How can she walk already?”

“It’s because she’s half goblin,” I said knowingly. “They develop faster than humans. Honestly, taking six months to learn to walk isn’t something to write about. Cats do it in like two days. Walking’s not that complicated. Sorry, but human babies are kind of retarded!”

“Na-ah!” Samar laughed. “They’ve got big heads and tiny feet — it’s hard for them! Oh look, Miriam’s petting her so gently!”

Miriam had reached the snake and was happily stroking its fuzzy fur. It actually looked fun — I’d never petted a furry horned snake before — so I joined in.

“Yes, that’s a nice deadly snake monster,” I told Miriam as we both petted it.

Thubana seemed quite pleased with the petting. Up close, she was stunning — nearly two feet thick, twenty feet long, her body covered in red and yellow fur stripes, with a long, sharp unicorn-like horn at her head.

After a while, I noticed something bothering me.

“Uh, Samar?” I asked. “Babies smile and laugh at six months, right?”

“Sure,” she said — then looked at Miriam and understood what I meant. “She will too. It just takes time to adjust to a new environment… and new parents. Honestly, it’s amazing she’s this calm already. She’s a strong-willed little girl!”

Eventually, Miriam finished petting the snake and looked up at me. I lifted her — and immediately felt something wet.

“Samar, my shirt’s suddenly wet,” I said.

“You want three guesses why?” she grinned. “Her diaper’s full! You need to change it.”

“What do I— how!?” I stammered, panic rising.

Samar sighed. “Let’s go to town. We’ll do some baby shopping.”

“Great idea,” I said quickly. “Thank you!”

She spoke with Kim and Stalin about the day’s training plans, and then we headed to town together.

Our first stop was Ysabel’s Clothier — my old workplace. We got stacks of simple cloth for diapers and a baby wrap — a long cloth you tie around yourself to hold the baby securely while keeping your hands free. Sounded useful, but wrapping it correctly looked complicated as hell. Samar promised to teach me later.

Next, we went to the apothecary for herbs and powders useful after cleaning a baby. There was a lot to learn.

On the way, we ran into Hyun-woo. He was walking down the street, holding three huge books in one hand while scribbling in a notebook with the other, muttering nonstop. Didn’t even notice the baby in my arms. Poor guy had gone completely coo-coo.

Running the trade business was hard. All the stress had probably taken its toll on him. And it was a ton of work — I knew, because now it was all mine!

Since Kamatouu was back under Aldioli’s control, and the Order of the Phoenix was technically a military body from an enemy kingdom, they could no longer handle trade directly. That’s where the Stormcrows came in — acting as intermediaries, and making a shit ton of gold in the process.

We bought caravans from the Order, sold their goods to Kamatouu’s merchants, and did the same in reverse. We took a healthy cut, and that accounted for about two-thirds of our total income.

The other third came from the industries we’d begun branching into.

I was teaching tailoring to our newer members. We used the high-quality pelts Lori and her helpers provided, and we crafted beautiful fur and pelt garments that sold for ten to twenty silver each.

Some went to Ysabel’s shop, but most were exported to the Order of the Phoenix, who resold them at outrageous prices across the continent.

We’d also started a candle-making business using bee-like local insects Nayeem discovered — though that was still new and not very profitable yet. Nayeem himself was busy running forest survival training, mandatory for all Stormcrows.

With our treasury in the hundreds of gold, we could afford high-value magic items, slowly building up real strength — in melee, archery, and yes, magic.

As the appointed Minister of Arcane Arts, I held magic training classes for the band members.

So yeah, I was a double minister and very, very busy!

By the time Samar and I finished our baby shopping, Miriam was peacefully asleep in my arms.

“There are two things left,” said Samar. “Baby clothes and a stroller.”

“Well, leave the clothes to me,” I said proudly. “I’ll tailor her the cutest baby outfits you’ve ever seen.”

And then, when my friend comments on how cute the little baby’s outfit is, I’ll casually say, “Yeah, I tailored it myself,” and they’ll all know what an amazing tailor I am!

“About the stroller,” said Samar, “talk with Nikolai. You know him—he recently started working as a carpenter’s apprentice. He’s pretty good, and he’d love to make you a stroller.”

“Cool!” A stroller could really come in handy in situations like this, when Miriam was asleep and we were on the go.

On our way back, we passed the mansion and heard loud cursing.

“Pissfunnel-hobknocker! Cruststool-shitwhapper! Insolent naked-Nula-NPC fool!”

Duchess was at it again.

She was our new mansion staff manager — self-appointed, really. She’d tricked Juliette into giving her the position by first refusing to work at all, then accusing Juliette of being lazy because her “manager job was too easy.” Juliette took the bait, told her to do it herself, and Duchess happily accepted.

Now Duchess could happily shout orders and boss around the servants and maids, imagining herself as the baroness of the place. She even hired a new stormcrow bird player for two silver a day to handle the real work whenever the NPC servants refused to do something.

And all the while, she earned twenty silver a day. Duchess knew how to get along in life — I’ll hand her that!

Then my phone vibrated again.

“Geshem here.”

“Hi Geshem! It’s Mustafa. Just arrived with two wagons full to the brim.”

Shit. Mustafa — a trader from the Order of the Phoenix. He was supposed to deliver goods today, and I was supposed to have everything ready for him. I’d totally forgotten.

“Hey,” I said quickly, “look, there were some delays. I need about an hour, okay? Really sorry.”

“No problem,” Mustafa said. “I’m not in a hurry.”

Thank god — he was a chill guy.

Samar had to go, so I waved her goodbye and hurried to the mansion. I needed Duchess to arrange NPC helpers for the cargo — which meant dealing with her. Joy.

“Geshem!” someone called. I turned to see Lakshmi, one of our newest Stormcrows.

“Holy shit!” she said, then lowered her voice. “Sorry, but holy shit — is that a baby?”

“Yes, it’s a baby,” I said flatly. “Did you need something?”

“Uh, just… the magic lesson?”

“SHIT!” I’d totally forgotten. “Sorry — it’s a mess of a day. Cancel it for today and tell the others I’m sorry.”

“Sure. Most of us have archery with Lori anyway. Maybe we’ll just ask her for some magic circle pointers.”

“Yeah, sure,” I said, already walking away. Then I thought — wait. Lori? Magic circles? She didn’t do magic. Lakshmi must’ve been confused.

I burst into the mansion.

“Hey, Duchess!” I called, finding her in the kitchen, viciously munching something. “I need Ergal and Moris — and a wagon — fast! The Phoenix shipment’s here!”

“Dickknob-breadslapper!” she squawked, pointing at Miriam. “What is that thing!?”

“It’s. A. Baby!” I said, exasperated.

“Why do you have it!?” she squawked,” Is it… Is it dangerous!?”

“What—? Why would you even— never mind! Please! I’m in a hurry!”

Then I heard Patricia from the textile division shout, “Geshem! Thank god, I’ve been looking for you! All the blue dye spilled on— is that… a baby?”

Then my phone buzzed again.

Then Miriam woke up.

She looked at me. I looked at her. Her expression changed — serious, focused, like she was concentrating hard on something. What… what could be the meaning behind such an expression on a baby’s face?

And then—

“SPLORRRRT!”“BLRRRRTTT!”“PRFFFFF!”

Ah! So that’s what that look meant.

Such was the glamorous life of a (temporary) band leader — juggling trade deals, leadership, and, apparently, diaper duty.

But honestly? Despite me mentally collapsing under all the pressure, life was peaceful.

The past year had been calm — no real dangers, no crises. Just building, growing, living. Sometimes I remembered John Smith’s old warning about calamity, but it felt distant now. We’d gotten so used to routine, to stability.

The idea of some terrifying “Controller Species Invasion” from far continent didn’t feel real.

Sure, rationally, I understood his logic… but emotionally? My mind was occupied with a thousand more pressing things.

Maybe those controller species decided it was impossible to cross the desert and chose to just live their lives peacefully. Or maybe they did invade another continent. Or perhaps, in a few decades, something will happen — and then we’ll deal with it.

But right now?

There was a diaper full of goblin baby poop demanding my immediate attention.
