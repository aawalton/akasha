The Sea hides much, but it is the Horizon you cannot trust.

It hides far more.

Above and below.

Harker wept, the salt of it running a marathon across cheek and chin. The world was dim. A veil of blue bled through the edges, honing his blurry vision into a tunnel. Somehow, the light was still too much.

Faded stars hung above him in crowded constellations, threaded with ribbons of gold that perched around the basin. Figures bent low over him, bodies no more than silhouettes against the night. A slantwise wind touseled them, rustling hair and robe and cloak, an aurum mist that concealed by fitful turns.

Empty eyes stared at him, heads tilted inward as if conversing in distant tones. Maelstrom hearts thundered in concert with one another. Too many to count, they shifted and split. Some were hard edged and unflinching, while others were malformed shadows, barely clinging to the edge of the basin with gnarled hands of tarnished gold.

"Who are you?" Harker asked. "What do you want?"

There was no answer. There was only the growing roar of the maelstrom and the flash of incandescent gold.

He blinked, and the light changed.

Gone now were the stars, aurora, and perched silhouettes of dissolute gold. The fungal florescence remained too bright, but now it hung further away, draped amid an emerald profusion.

Harker blinked again. Tears slid sideways across his temples, tickling skin that felt all too sensitive. The world sharpened. He laid in the bottom of the basin, and the moss-clad rafters bisected his oblong view of the ceiling.

He flexed his hands, touching his fingers against his palm and thumb. He could move—that was a considerable improvement alone. He stretched his jaw, running his dry tongue across his fuzzy teeth. Everything about him felt raw. An exposed nerve sat in cold muck at the bottom of a chill basin.

But the pain was gone.

He sat up, or tried to at least. His muscles felt loose, as if a series of unsecured strings filled his limbs and abdomen alike. With a grunt, he heaved himself upward, slapping a hand onto the basin’s rim. It squeaked beneath his grip, his hand slick with some foul substance, but he slowly hauled himself into a sitting position.

The movement alone drove a giddy, half-mad laugh from his lungs—but when his arm flashed, his hissing breath redoubled. Lines of gold bunched and split, releasing meaning in whispered light.

Breath caught in his throat, Harker stared at the progress bars. They had all jumped up immensely, with only the Trial of Truth refusing to move. The rest was so surprising, he couldn’t muster the will to be irritated.

Far better, however, was the notification that came next.

Three (3) Ordeals Completed!

Boons Earned!

Hand still gripped tight to the rim of the basin, Harker watched as the Chartermark changed. A new pattern emerged from the chaos of intersecting lines, one that started at his wrist before diving deep into his forearm beneath layers of inane gold. It was too complicated and far too swift for him to follow it, but he could see that three folded stars unfurled. Something was released to pulse through the gleaming lines.

He convulsed, nearly losing grip of the basin. There was no pain, not even a clenching tension, only the feeling of being buffeted by warm, turquoise waters.

Boon: Strengthened Locks

From foot to palm, soul to throat, that warmth spread, inundating his Locks. They shuddered, shedding pieces of themselves outward, like a hound shaking free the river. What was once cragged rock smoothed into polished stone. The perfect circles were already clean, but now they bore no burr or crack at all, and striations of gold were revealed across their surfaces, bright against the dark granite.

Harker choked, hacking up a mouthful of black, tar-like sand and salt. The foulness spattered onto the enamel, heavy and wet. His hands and feet did the same, the pores of his skin oozing the same gritty impurities. A foul smell followed—rotting vegetation, decaying fish, and the sharp turn of brine.

Boon: Strengthened Blood

Barely catching his breath, Harker’s heart hammered—but his blood slowed. His veins throbbed, pressing upward against his skin like blue webbing across his arms, neck, and legs. Potent, slow pumps drove his blood coursing through arteries and veins hard. The sound of his pulse was almost as loud as his labored breathing, and a momentary rush of disorientation made Harker’s head feel like it was floating.

Sovereign Sight dove deep, guided by Harker’s worried mind. The network of crimson vessels roared through him, vivid and bright despite the fleshy shadow within his body. The very color of his blood changed as he watched, its hue deepening as if it were saturated with everything essential to life.

Boon: Strengthened Organs

Where the blood rushed through his organs he could feel them shift and change. His lungs pulled air in great heavy mouthfuls, each exhale hard as a bellows. His digestive tract convulsed, compounding into an internal quiver as his spleen, liver, and kidneys joined in. It wasn’t Water suffusing them, but a brilliant, almost colorless sheen that made his dark organs shine. Sovereign Sight fed him details he couldn’t parse—from linings shedding ashen layers that dissolved into his blood, to fibers among his heart crawling across one another as they swelled to greater dimensions. For a moment, it was all too big, too thunderous—then it wasn’t. Whatever power drove the Boons, it rearranged Harker at a level he could not comprehend.

He was remade, there at the bottom of the basin.

Internal organs have been cleansed and strengthened. Blood is purified. And my Locks are as shiny as newly polished marble. Harker pressed his side against the cool enamel. There was comfort in its uncomplicated solidity. The effect was likely compounded by the Last Grace elixir and the medicinal bath. Combined with my refluxed Water… Huh. He couldn’t believe it. Three out of nine Ordeals complete.

That was a bit too momentous to handle. Instead, Harker cast his attention within, hunting for any trace that the Last Grace was still around. There was no trace of it, nor the blighted heat of the mudbath. Only dregs remained around his feet and hips, and even then, most of it was his own excised impurities.

Ancestors. The smell…

It was horrendous, stronger by far than any beached fish corpse. It clung to him, and Harker suddenly realized that he’d been stripped of his clothes. Moreover, his skin was pink and raw enough that even the faintest of breezes in the chamber felt like the harsh bristles of a washerwoman's scrub brush. The shifting of his feet and thighs squeaked against the enamel, and it was sharper and louder than he expected. Every sound felt amplified, as if his ears had never heard them before. He looked up and could pick out every leaflet in the moss that hung a dozen feet above him. The gills of the nearest mushroom colony were as detailed as the whorls of his own fingerprints.

Harker flexed his hand. His grip was diminished and weak. He could barely make a fist, let alone move his elbow. Even so, there was a tingling to his muscles that didn't feel like weakness. They were sore, certainly, but there was also a sort of energy coiled in him, just waiting to be released. Like a less harsh version of the kaffa sprout.

A lingering effect of my strengthened blood, perhaps. Blood carried nourishment to the rest of the body. It made sense that refining it would lead to qualitative improvements across everything else it touched.

But what else might have changed?

He delved deeper, hunting down any sign of his injuries. They had been cleansed from his bones, organs, and skin as if they had never existed. His skin, however, had not changed. A lifetime of scars remained, including the ones stained gold by his Chartermark. Harker supposed he was fine with that—scars were lessons learned the hard way.

What was left in him was brighter, stronger. Healthy flesh stretched across dark organs and vessels woven between muscle and fat. An unmistakable potency imbued so much of himself that Harker had a hard time pinpointing its source.

Had he refined a part of himself without knowing?

He tried to stand, but the muscles at his hips refused to budge. They trembled, knotting themselves for his efforts, but did nothing more than scoot him forward slightly. Harker frowned. Impurities still sat thick within himself, and even his improved blood, organs, and Locks all felt uncertain, as if they still had room to grow.

The Boon isn’t refinement. It’s an additional advantage. Harker poked around, feeling the pieces of himself bit by bit. I think I’ve only raised the ceiling on how much refined Water my body might handle.

Harker was eager to find out if he was right. Once I get out of his accursed tub.

"Why?" Harker's voice was rough. Unused.

Ironwine tilted his head, his antenna twitching. "Our Bargains have been completed."

As if waiting for that confirmation, a thrum began in Harker's chest, followed by a rapid creaking growth. The swell of Stature spread across his center like a cataract, but not just there. Stretched across his limbs and throat, the pale white supports thickened and multiplied. A moment of dizziness seized him—a fundamental shift heaved his center, ending an ache Harker had come to ignore. Harker gripped the basin tightly, his fingers almost slipping before he righted himself. His balance shifted and everything felt…steadier. Grounded.

The same as before, only more.

Harker reached inward toward that gossamer connection he'd once held with Stillwater. It was gone. There was only himself, and the profusion of Stature across his tributaries.

My tributaries…

The thought was suffused with sudden wonder. They stretched across his body like an overgrown garden, flush with a busy, complicated vitality. Bramble, wave, and wide spiral, each piece a memory that swam back to him across murky waters. Harker remembered cutting them, remembered the decisions he'd made in the moment, if not why. His primaries had widened by more than twice their previous size, and the simple spiral around his reservoir had become an undulating weave festooned with thorned branches. They spread up and outward, penetrating into the dark recesses of his body as well as the surface of muscle, skin, and bones.

His tributaries had become intensely complicated, fully realized, and finally complete.

“You have accomplished a great deal, human.” The Thrysalid nodded, body clicking in ways that rattled against Harker’s newborn ears. “Few have ever received three life debts from any Eidrhin, let alone the Scourge of Sails himself.”

Somehow, Harker had a feeling Ironwine wasn’t referring to himself. “Where’d Stillwater go?”

The Thrysalid shrugged. “Where he wills, I expect.”

Off to con more humans, no doubt. He tried to stand up again, but his legs still wouldn’t listen. Tendrils of cold dread crept through his stomach. He’d seen something like this before, always in patients that had been bedridden. “How long have I been here?”

Ironwine folded his small set of arms and his black eyes flicked upward as if checking something. “Seven weeks.”

Harker’s stomach dropped. He scrambled for his Chartermark, the golden lines swirling for a moment as the countdown brought itself forward.

“Blind Abyss.” He dropped his trembling arm. “I’ve only three weeks left.”
