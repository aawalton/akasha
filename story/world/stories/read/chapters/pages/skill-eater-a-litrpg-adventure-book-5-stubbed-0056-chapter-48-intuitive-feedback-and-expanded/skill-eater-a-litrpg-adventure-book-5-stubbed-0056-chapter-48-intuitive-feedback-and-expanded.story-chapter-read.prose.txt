Edge soon spotted a beast he wanted to hunt, a peak stage-two lizard hiding inside a hollow log.

He decided to let the chains tackle it without his assistance, gauging how much they’d grown after evolving to stage three. He summoned Foebinder and Magebane, moved their anchors outside his body, and let them come up with a strategy on their own, watching in silence without providing weapons or empowering them with additional mana.

They handled the creature easily. The living links were dead silent when they wanted to be and approached without alerting their prey. They were longer and thicker after his evolution, and their baseline attributes were about 30% higher, by his rough estimation.

They entered the log and struck in a flash, binding the lizard tightly while preventing the beast from casting spells. He praised their efforts while he walked over, knocked the beast out with his iceblade, and activated Extraction.

Edge advanced both Rare skills with a single theft each. He put the experience from the third power he stole into Enhance Senses, which would hit rank two in a few more days at this rate. Once he finished off his prey and butchered the carcass, he stepped into his inner world to select his upgrades.

The skill Kinetic Evasion has advanced to Rank 2.

Its mana efficiency has been enhanced.

Please choose between one of the following options:

Warning: If no option is selected, your skill will remain unchanged and gain a slight bonus to mana efficiency.

The first option sounded good at face value, but it would be a terrible pick for him. Reflexive dodging would be great for a crafter who didn’t see much combat and only needed to survive an occasional attack from wild beasts and the like. But for someone like Edge—who put his life on the line on a regular basis—anything that reduced his control over his body was going to get him killed.

Sometimes, he needed to turn incorporeal and let attacks phase through him or take the hit head-on, blocking with Harden and letting Rejuvenation repair the damage he received. Making his response involuntary would also prevent him from learning from the brand-new sense Kinetic Evasion provided, so he crossed it off his list and moved on to the other offerings.

The remaining upgrades were intriguing, and he considered each in turn before making his decision.

Increasing the range of sensations Kinetic Evasion provided would enhance his ability to perceive attacks, giving him a better sense of their unique dynamics and the precise moment they would land. It would take a while to sort everything out, but his experience with All-Seeing Gaze should make the process a lot smoother. Eventually, the upgrade should let him instinctively grasp the nature of any strike headed his way, especially when combined with his growing skill senses.

Moving on, being better at detecting attacks from concealed sources was a tempting choice, since getting taken out by a sniper or stealth specialist before he had time to activate his defenses was one of his biggest weaknesses. Edge would have chosen it without hesitation if it wasn’t for Wind Ward, his Organ-Guard, and Gaze.

After its last upgrade, the ocular skill projected motes of Disruption to supplement the particles of mana it emitted, which could counter a range of stealth powers outright and provided partial protection against even more. Since he kept the skill running most of the time these days and was exploring increasingly dangerous environments, he decided to go with the second option and sent his intentions into the System.

After congratulating Evasion’s avatar on its advancement, Edge called up his Guide and went over his options for Polearm Mastery.

The skill Polearm Mastery has advanced to Rank 2.

Its mana efficiency has been enhanced.

Please choose between one of the following options:

Warning: If no option is selected, your skill will remain unchanged and gain a slight bonus to mana efficiency.

Whoa. Edge read the list again as a warbling whistle escaped his lips. The upgrades for learning skills are unlike anything I’ve seen before. All three choices sounded awesome at face value, and when he pondered them at length, they still seemed just as good.

The first option would enable something comparable to a top-grade augmented reality training module. From the brief vision he’d experienced, he knew it would provide step-by-step instructions for learning a wide variety of new techniques, as well as a simulated sparring mode where he could test ideas in live combat—kind of like an ultimate version of shadow boxing.

Instead of delivering direct instruction, the second upgrade was geared toward helping him develop a custom combat style. The proprioceptive feedback would be invaluable in pointing out errors in his form, identifying weak points in his stance, and sussing out inefficiencies in his body mechanics. It would be useful now and remain a valuable tool, no matter how far down the road to mastery he strode.

However, the third listing intrigued him the most. Instead of teaching techniques or helping him train, it would let him apply observations and integrate insights as they came, improving his mechanics in real time and adjusting on the fly.

To narrow it down, Edge crossed the first option off the list. It would be invaluable if he were on his own, but in the free city, expert training and skilled sparring partners were easy to come by. With that out of the way, he thought long and hard about the role he wanted his polearm to play in his overall fighting style, both now and as time went by.

Eventually, that train of thought led him to go with his gut and select the third upgrade. If his ultimate goal was mastering martial arts or developing a skillset that was centered around polearms—or even melee combat in general—it would probably be his best bet, helping him reach a level of proficiency matched by few people on the planet.

But at the end of the day, he was a skill specialist first and a weapon fighter second. He didn’t use Opulence every battle—or a weapon at all, for that matter. But when he did, he bet his life on every slash, block, and parry. Anything that helped him make better decisions in the heat of the moment, when life and death were balanced on the edge of his blade, was worth its weight in gold. Mastery will still help me improve my technique over time, but this will give the skill a direct combat application.

“I’ll try the path of intuition. Give me offering number three, please.”

When the skill stopped shining, the glaive was bigger and sharper than before. Edge would have to wait until the avatar’s next training session to be sure, but he had the impression that Mastery’s movements were quicker and more precise. He spent some time congratulating it, then popped back into his body, poised to take his newest powers for a trial run as soon as the sun rose.

***

The crew woke up early the next morning. This was their last full day before the big meeting, and the team had a lot on their plate.

The hunters began their day by heading into the Lakelands, where they could discuss the situation and examine Edge’s skills away from prying eyes. They hiked across rugged terrain for a few hours, tossing ideas back and forth along the way. After making sure they were alone, he switched Kinetic Evasion on, closed his eyes, and asked his friends to send some weak attacks his way, leaving Gaze off for the duration of this experiment.

The results were extraordinary. He could tell right away that the skill’s sensitivity had been dramatically enhanced after its rank-two upgrade. He felt something hard and firm building between his shoulders a heartbeat before the rock Trapper threw bounced off his armor, leaving a bruise behind. Warmth engulfed his legs a bare second before Sasha’s Fire Breathing arrived, which was hot but couldn’t burn him too badly with Regulate running.

It soon became clear that there was a minimum threshold before Evasion kicked in. If an approaching strike couldn’t hurt him, he couldn’t feel it at all. It meant the skill wouldn’t warn him about attacks that inflicted indirect damage, like slow-acting venom, or strikes that changed form at the last second, like when he used Blast in grenade mode.

Even so, the skill was amazing. Edge had the team dial it up another notch, mixing in melee attacks and a range of elements while he did his best to get out of the way. He avoided a fair number of hits, although he messed up a few times by moving in the wrong direction or reacting too late.

The more they experimented, the easier it became to tell what was what. For the most part, the sensations correlated to what would happen if the attack landed. Cuts were sharp and long, revealing the angle of the slice, while punctures were concentrated, and the pain from blunt attacks radiated from the point of contact. Fire was hot, ice was cold, and acid stung.

Some aspects were trickier to incorporate. While each sensation’s location told him where a strike would land, they updated in real time, moving as he changed position. The intensity was based on the power of the attack as well as its distance, which made it difficult to tell the difference between a weak attack that was close and a strong strike coming from further out. However, there were subtle variations that would eventually make interpreting these feelings seem as natural as breathing.

Edge couldn’t dodge every blow with his eyes closed, but when they were open, he managed to avoid them all, effectively erasing his blind spots even with Gaze out of the picture. When the ocular skill was running, Evasion made it easy to gauge the strength of incoming attacks, since Gaze didn’t provide any information regarding the momentum or mana density of objects headed his way.

The takeaway was that the skills were complementary. Evasion only triggered against strikes that would damage him, whereas the ocular skill sensed everything within range. The information overload was distracting right now, but once he learned to use them together, he should be able to dodge almost anything below a certain speed.

I should invest in Reflex to improve my reaction time—maybe a bit of Perception to help my brain sort out the details.

When he was done testing Kinetic Evasion’s upgrades, he switched to Polearm Mastery. As advertised, the feedback it provided felt far more intuitive than before. It still provided a general sense of right and wrongness based on his body mechanics, but now the magic offered flashes of insight too—an instinctual sense for how effective his movements would be in the same moment he made them.

It helped him correct his stance in time to counter some tricky attacks the team sent his way, although he had the impression that it would really shine when fighting another weapon wielder one-on-one. The learning aspect was more profound too. Every time he performed particularly well or poorly, he obtained a general impression of why things had worked out that way. These micro-lessons stuck with him, and Edge knew they would yield valuable insights when he had a chance to integrate them.

Trapper spent the better part of an hour helping him measure the strength of each component of Enhance Senses. When they were finished, everyone wanted to watch him fly. It was a little tricky without a giant tree to use as a launch pad, but he eventually found a hilltop that offered sufficient elevation to get the job done.

He entered Shadow Form, primed Maelstrom, and activated Glide, circling the hill while his friends oohed and aahed. They broke in applause when he returned to the ground, sticking the landing with a flourish.

“Badass,” Jumo said. “Now you just need to pick some upgrades that let you carry the rest of us, and we’ll be able to travel in style.”

“I don’t think that’s going to happen anytime soon.” Edge laughed along with the crew. “I suppose it’s theoretically possible, though. If I take the skills involved to rank five, pick up enough Amplification, and create some kind of lightweight framework for people to grab onto…”

This marked the start of an animated discussion. The hunters decided to picnic on the hilltop part of the way through, enjoying the late-autumn weather before turning their attention to weightier matters.

When Edge stored the leftovers in his vault, they were ready to begin their final planning session, preparing for a conversation that was guaranteed to have grave repercussions for the crew and everyone who called Puppet Town home.
