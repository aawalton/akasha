Tears continued to trickle down my face, like streams flowing from my eyes, as we walked down the corridor. I felt numb. I was reeling, yet still in shock, and I could feel Seth walking beside me, but I couldn’t bring myself to speak. I had nothing I wanted to say, and my mind wasn’t working properly. It could only form one thought:

Katie’s gone.

She’d been injured and had to leave the Academy. I would most likely never see her again, never see one of my best friends again. I would never know that she was actually okay, and I didn’t know what to do with that. I didn’t know how to process it and understand what had happened, and I didn’t really want to.

If I processed it, if I came to terms with whatever had happened to her, it meant that I’d accept it. I’d have to accept that Katie was actually gone, and I didn’t want to do that. I couldn’t. I wanted to cling to the belief that one day, I would actually see her again, and I needed that.

“Grace,” Seth said, his voice breaking the silence that had settled over us.

I glanced at him. That was all I could do. It felt as though I should speak, as if I should reply to him, but I couldn’t. The words still felt trapped in my throat, and even if I were to open my mouth, I was certain that nothing would escape. I wouldn’t be able to make any sound.

But Seth seemed to be struggling just as much as I was. His eyes were sparkling with unshed tears, and he seemed to understand that I couldn’t talk because he continued.

“Can I—” he started before breaking off. His throat bobbed as he swallowed hard before trying again. “Do you want a hug?”

His voice was soft, and I wasn’t sure if he was offering because he thought I needed it or because he did, but I didn’t hesitate to nod. I stepped towards him, feeling his arms fold around me and hold me tight. His body hitched as a sob escaped him, and I felt something in me snap.

I had been crying before, but when I felt that, the dam that had been holding back my emotions shattered into tiny pieces. I broke. I couldn’t stop myself from clinging to the back of Seth’s shirt as my body was wracked by sobs.

Tears cascaded from my eyes, and I don’t know how long we stayed there, holding each other and crying in the corridor, but it felt like hours had passed. I couldn’t stop, though. I could barely even stand upright, and if it weren’t for Seth’s arms around me, I was pretty sure I would have collapsed into a heap on the floor.

After a while, I ran out of tears. I felt as though I’d cried them all out, and there was nothing left. A dull throbbing had started at the back of my head, but my eyes were dry. I took a deep breath, trying to pull myself together, so that I could step away from Seth.

I still felt fragile, though. It was as if I was being held together with glue that had yet to fully dry. If anything else happened, if I was knocked or jostled even a little, I knew I would fall apart again, and it would be irreparable. The pieces would be too small, too badly damaged.

But I felt strong enough to pull away from Seth. I was still heartbroken, still numb, but it was a different feeling than before. I could feel that I was still in shock, but it felt less suffocating and consuming. Rather than being on the brink of tears, I just felt empty and detached.

Seth hesitated, his arms tightening around me for a moment, before he let go. I stepped back, my eyes finding his shirt. A wet patch had formed on his chest, caused by my tears, and I had to fight the urge to grimace at the sight. I had already been struggling to meet his gaze; I felt ashamed about how I’d reacted to the news about Katie, that he’d had to hold me for so long as I fell apart, and the token of my weakness just made it even harder to meet his eye, but I forced myself to do it.

Immediately, the guilt that had been simmering within me soared. Seth’s cheeks were red. His eyes were shining and bloodshot from his own tears, and the sight made me feel even worse. I longed to take him into my arms again, to hold him and offer him support, but I couldn’t do it. Instead, I wrapped my arms around my own body, clinging to myself.

“Are you okay?” Seth asked softly, his voice thick with emotion.

I tried to answer, but all that came out was a laugh. It was closer to a sob, though. I had to fight to swallow down the tears that threatened to bubble over, knowing that if I let them, I would break down again.

A shaky laugh escaped Seth, and he lifted a hand to rub his neck.

“Silly question,” he said with a slight smile. “Sorry. I just meant—”

The intensity of the guilt coursing through my body forced me to speak.

“No, no,” I interrupted him. “It’s fine. I just…”

But I didn’t know how to finish that sentence. That was the best I could do, but Seth seemed to understand. The corner of his lips lifted in a sad smile, and he let out a soft laugh.

“I know,” he told me, saving me from having to think of a full response. “Me too.”

I returned his smile, the movement feeling mechanical and unnatural.

“Sorry,” I muttered, unsure what I was actually apologising for. I just felt the need to say it.

Seth shook his head.

“Don’t be,” he said firmly before looking around. “Do you want to go up to the roof, maybe? I want to be… not here.”

I knew how he felt. I didn’t want to be in the Academy either. I wanted to get away from it all for a bit and not be surrounded by the tutors and their rules. The urge to let myself float away into the dizziness waiting at the back of my mind pulled at me, but I couldn’t let it take me. I felt as though I needed to stay. I couldn’t leave Seth.

“Yeah. That would be good.”

Seth smiled at me again, the expression slightly less sad.

I glanced around as we started to walk, a little surprised to see that we only made it as far as the end of the corridor containing the tutors’ offices before coming to a stop. It felt as though we’d gotten further before, but I was glad. The hallway was still empty, and I hoped that meant no one had seen my breakdown.

Rodgers might have, I realised. If he’d heard my sobs or left his office for any reason, he definitely would have seen us, and that made me feel awful. What happened wasn’t his fault, I was pretty sure. I doubted he was the one to hurt Katie, and the image of his face as I left his office flashed behind my eyes, making me wince. He was new to being a tutor, and he seemed to be struggling with what happened too. I was worried that if he saw me, it would just make it harder for him, and I was almost tempted to turn back so I could knock on his door and apologise, but I stopped myself. We were already too far away, and I hoped he had not seen me.

Seth and I didn’t speak as we walked through the Academy again. Other trainees passed by, their voices loud and their laughter jarring. It felt wrong, as though they should be in the same state of shock and confusion as we were, but they weren’t. Their lives were continuing as normal, but ours had been changed forever.

It was a relief to get to the store room containing the ladder to the roof. As soon as the door shut behind us, the rest of the world was shut out. We were swallowed, alone in silence once more, and it made it a little easier to breathe. I hadn’t realised that I was struggling until then, but my chest was tight. My lungs felt as though they’d been straining.

“After you,” Seth said softly, gesturing towards the ladder.

But I hesitated. I was still wearing pyjamas, and the thought of climbing up the ladder in front of him in shorts felt weird. I knew that he’d probably already seen my legs, but it would be impossible for him not to see them if I climbed the ladder before him.

I doubted he’d be looking, though. He had other things on his mind, and I was being self-centred. Selfish. He was being sweet by letting me go first, and I just needed to hurry up and accept.

“Thanks,” I said, moving towards the ladder quickly.

Still, I couldn’t help but feel uncomfortable as I climbed. I felt very aware of my body, very aware of every move I was making. The urge to glance back at Seth and check whether he was looking pulled at me, but I forced myself not to. Somehow, that would have only made things more awkward.

As soon as I pulled myself through the hole to the roof, I turned. I’d expected to see Seth already on the ladder below me, but he was nowhere to be seen. Confusion washed over me, and I leaned forward, my hands braced on either side of the hole as I peered into the room below.

“Sorry,” Seth called, an apologetic smile on his face as he stepped forward, one hand in his pocket. “Just had to grab a tissue real quick.”

“Oh, that’s fine,” I replied.

I looked around the roof as Seth climbed. I’d been hoping that we’d be alone up there, that there would be no other trainees. Then, it would be easier. We could just sit in silence and not have to talk or deal with the world, but we were not. Another couple lay on their backs not far from me, their hands clasped as they watched the sky slowly growing darker.

Luckily, they seemed just as irritated by our presence as we were by theirs. Soft whispers drifted towards me, and by the time Seth had joined me on the roof, they’d already gotten up. I glanced back at them as they climbed through the hatch without saying another word, and a slight smile pulled at my lips.

We’d made our way across the gently sloped surface, moving as close to the edge as we dared, before sitting. The sun-baked tiles stung the backs of my bare legs as I sat, but it felt strangely good. It was kind of nice to have a break from the oppressive numb sadness that I’d been feeling ever since we’d knocked on Rodgers’ door, even if the thing I was feeling was pain.

I hugged my knees to my chest, my fingers gripping my legs tightly as I stared out at the road in the distance. Neither Seth nor I spoke for a while as the sun gradually set around us. We just sat there in silence. It was nice, though. At first, at least. Then, my thoughts strayed back to Katie.

A sigh escaped my lips. I didn’t know what to do. Katie had left the Academy, and I would never see her again, but I didn’t want that. I wished I could go back in time to when we were all together again. Things were so much easier then, and I was so much happier, but it was impossible. I couldn’t reverse time.

The worlds at the back of my mind edged closer, their familiar yet new sensations luring me in, but I refused to let them take hold. Even if I went to another world, one that felt similar, there was no guarantee it would have what I needed it to. I was pretty sure I was at the Academy in a few of the realities, but I didn’t know if Katie was still there in them, and even if she was, it didn’t mean that everything would be the same as it was before.

Perhaps my earlier worries would be proven correct, and they wouldn’t be my friend in whatever world I went to. They could all hate me. Or maybe Katie was still at the Academy, but someone else had been kicked out instead of her. Abbie could have been the one who’d gotten hurt. Or Scott.

Or maybe it was me. Perhaps in one of the other worlds, I was the one who’d gotten kicked out of the Academy. I might not have aligned with a specialism there either, and instead of deciding that I could leave the induction wing without being assigned one, they might have just decided that I could leave.

Would they have placed me with a foster family, like they did Katie? I doubted it. My parents weren’t physically abusive like Katie’s dad was, so maybe they decided that was fine. They could have just sent me home, and then I’d have to deal with my mom’s reaction.

She would be furious at me. She was always angry at me; that wasn’t anything new, but if she thought that I’d done something to annoy the government, that they’d given me a chance, and I’d thrown it back in their faces, she would never forgive me. She would become insufferable, and dealing with that, as well as the knowledge that I was not enough, would be too much for me to handle. I knew that.

And what if it meant that I couldn’t come back to the world I was already in? I’d already spent so much time in it, but what if the other version of the world felt too similar? They could get mixed up in my head, and maybe whenever I tried to go to one, I’d just find myself in the world where I’d been kicked out. Then, it wouldn’t just be Katie I’d never see again. I’d never see any of them again.

Everything would be ruined. I would have ruined everything, and I couldn’t do it. I didn’t want yet another world where I felt trapped and lonely. It was too similar to my real life, and although I didn’t feel as lonely there anymore, it was still too much.

I was too weak, too cowardly and afraid of losing everything. I didn’t have another world. If I made it so I couldn’t return to the Academy, there was nowhere else that I could go where I knew I was safe. It felt like every other place I’d visited, every other reality, was terrifying and full of dangers, so I couldn’t do it. I couldn’t explore the other worlds that were edging closer in my mind.

That meant it was my fault that I’d never see Katie again, and that made me hate myself.

“I’ve been thinking,” Seth blurted out, his voice snapping me out of my spiralling thoughts.

I blinked, surprised to see how dark it had grown without me realising, before looking at him.

“Oh yeah?”

“Yeah,” Seth said hesitantly. “Just about… Katie and Scott, and…”

He trailed off, and I felt myself wince. I hadn’t even thought about Scott and what he must have been going through until that moment, and I felt my heart break. Before I left the wing, it was clear that he was falling for Katie, had fallen for her, and she’d fallen for him. He must have been even more heartbroken by what had happened to her, and especially if he’d seen it happen.

That would have made everything worse, and I couldn’t help but imagine it. Despite not knowing what had actually happened to Katie, my brain filled in the gaps. I could almost see the fear on his face as the bullet smashed into her ribs, hear the cry that ripped itself from his throat as he threw himself towards her, trying to shield her from it, but it was too late. He would have held her, sobbing, as the tutor called for help. Her blood would have soaked into his clothes, into his skin, and he would feel it every time he washed his hands. I wasn’t sure whether he’d ever forget the sensation, ever forget what he’d seen.

“And?” I prompted Seth, trying to push those thoughts aside.

Seth glanced at me, looking away far too quickly.

“And…” he started before trailing off again and taking a deep breath. “And I don’t want to ruin anything. Like… I don’t know. I just can’t believe I’m here, you know?”

“Yeah, me either,” I said.

Seth nodded. It seemed like my agreement made him feel a little better, but he still looked nervous. He was fidgeting, picking at the skin around his nail, and he wouldn’t meet my gaze as he grappled with his words, trying to find the right thing to say.

He was starting to make me feel nervous too, though. I could feel my anxiety slowly starting to climb, and restlessness simmered beneath my skin, making me want to fiddle too.

“It just sometimes all feels like a dream. It’s a little too perfect,” Seth explained, his words spilling out quickly. “Like, I was taken from my crappy old life and just dropped into this one, and it’s so different from what I’m used to. It feels like I’m in a completely different world or something, and I keep waiting for something to happen and the bubble to burst. Or I’m just going to wake up back in my other life and realise that this is not real. Like it’s all nothing more than a dream or a fantasy or something. You know what I mean?”

I couldn’t help the soft laugh from escaping my mouth. His words hit a little too close to home, and I knew exactly what he meant, but it was different for me. Although it was real for one version of me, for the real me, it was basically just a fantasy. I was just visiting the world and going through the motions. If it didn’t feel quite so realistic, I would have still believed it was nothing more than a daydream.

“Yeah. I get it,” I told him.

Seth peeked up at me, a slight smile on his lips.

“Really?” he asked. “You don’t think I’m crazy for feeling like I’m in another world?”

The urge to tell him the truth about me bubbled up suddenly. I felt the words rising to my lips, but I stomped them down, refusing to let them escape. There was no way that I could ever tell anyone the truth. They wouldn’t believe me.

“No. Not at all,” was all I said.

Seth still looked nervous, though. His eyes flicked back and forth between mine before he looked away again, taking another deep breath.

“Good. Because that’s why I don’t want to ruin this. I can’t, but…” he trailed off, his courage seemingly abandoning him for a moment. “But after what happened, I just feel like I need to say something. Otherwise, I’m scared something will happen, and I’ll lose my chance forever, but I don’t want that to happen.”

I felt my eyebrows draw together as concern rose within me. Anxiety turned slowly in the pit of my stomach as I watched Seth turn towards me, trying to work out why he looked so scared.

“Okay,” I said carefully.

Seth swallowed before slowly lifting his eyes to meet mine.

“Grace, I… I really like you,” he told me, his voice a little uneven with anxiety. “And I don’t want to make things weird between us because you’re one of my best friends, and it’s fine if that’s all you want to be. I’m completely okay with that. Happy, even. But I just… needed to tell you in case you feel the same way about me.”

His words left him in a rush, and I stared at him, my jaw slack. Somehow, I didn’t see that coming. Of all of the things I thought he was going to say, that was not one of them, and I was completely at a loss for how to reply.

My mind was moving too slowly. It was still stuck on what had happened to Katie, still trying to process it, and I had to repeat Seth’s words in my head multiple times before they actually sank in, but I still didn’t know what to say. I had to come up with something, though. I could see the smile slowly dropping from his face.

“I really like you too,” I blurted out.

Embarrassment burned within me, and I had to look down as my cheeks began to heat up.

“You do?” Seth asked.

Reluctantly, I looked up at him, spotting the slightly surprised expression that had appeared on his face.

“Yeah,” I admitted, the word hard to say.

“Like… as more than a friend?”

I bit my lip, struggling to figure out how to respond. He sounded hopeful, though. That gave me some courage, and he’d been right. Something could happen to either one of us at any moment, and we may never see each other again. I didn’t want to miss my chance to tell him how I felt, especially if he felt the same way.

“Yeah,” I whispered. “As more than a friend.”

Anxiety thrummed within me as I met his gaze again, scared of how he’d react. Even though he’d confessed to me first, I was worried that I’d misunderstood him. I almost expected him to shuffle away, to tell me that he was kidding and didn’t actually have feelings for me, but he didn’t.

Seth’s smile slowly grew, and I felt my stomach swoop. I could barely breathe as I held his gaze, fighting the urge to look down again. Anxiety still churned in my stomach, and my chest felt tight, like I was being slowly crushed to death, but I didn’t mind it. In fact, it felt strangely nice.

My breathing hitched as I realised Seth was leaning towards me, and my body did the same automatically. I was so nervous yet excited that I couldn’t breathe as I let my eyes flutter shut, and my lips found his.

It was the perfect kiss. Tentative, soft and warm. I longed to kiss him again, to let my lips linger against his, but instead, I forced myself to lean back, knowing that there would be plenty more time to kiss him again.

Despite the sadness still burning in my heart, I was smiling as I pulled away from Seth. I could feel the goofy expression on my lips, and I opened my eyes, expecting to see him doing the same, but the look on his face sent ice down my spine.

A smirk pulled at Seth’s lips, and I felt my expression fall as I noticed how different he looked. It wasn’t just the smile. It was his eyes. They weren’t warm and happy, like I was used to. Instead, there was something cold, calculating, and triumphant about the way he stared down at me.

“Wow,” he said, his lips still close to mine. “You are so easy to manipulate.”
