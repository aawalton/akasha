King Xanders Valeris looked down at the stack of documents resting on his desk and leaned back into his chair. It was the most comfortable seat in all of Valeria, crafted from the hide and bones of a rare elder drake, shaped by master artisans and enchanted to ease both body and mind. Yet today, it did nothing for him.

All he felt was boredom.

It wasn’t as if the kingdom lacked activity. Reports flowed in endlessly—skirmishes with the wildermen, disputes, monster sightings, trade routes shifting, nobles bickering over influence. But none of it was new. None of it held any spark worth his attention.

In the century he had ruled, Xanders had seen everything there was to see.

He had been part of wars, fought and seen monsters capable of razing entire towns in hours, fought mages and spellswords in duels who had thought themselves above him and had even experienced more than a few betrayals.

Now, when he looked over the matters presented before him, they felt… small, almost petty.

The last time something had truly caught his interest had been the fall of the Frayser house.

A faint, almost amused smile touched his lips at the thought.

William Frayser. A man he had once called a friend. A man who had everything—wealth, influence, a growing family—and yet had chosen to reach for more.

Xanders still remembered the day clearly. The trial, the whispers across the court, and finally, the execution.

For a brief moment, he had almost felt something watching it happen, but that feeling had passed quickly.

A traitor was a traitor, and all traitors deserved death.

Since then, true entertainment has been rare. At times, he would watch the arena matches in the capital, observing lowlife warriors tear each other apart for glory and coin. Other times, he indulged in simpler distractions—fine wine, company of maids and concubines and even books.

But even those had begun to lose their appeal.

With a quiet exhale, Xanders reached forward and picked up the top parchment from the stack, flipping through it with little care. His eyes moved across the lines quickly, already expecting nothing of value.

He wasn’t disappointed.

Two barons named Thakar and Oz were arguing over a failed marriage alliance between their children, each blaming the other for slighted honor and broken agreements. A cluster of towns had reported repeated wyvern attacks, requesting more troops and supplies to handle the threat.

There were also other minor things happening like trade disputes and some merchant company trying to scam the uneducated commoners as usual. Nothing meaningful or interesting for him to give it attention.

But then, as he picked up another stack of parchment, this one thicker than the others, he paused.

Xanders glanced at the title written across the top of the parchment, and for the first time in a while, his posture shifted. He straightened slightly in his chair, the faintest hint of focus returning to his otherwise bored expression.

A few weeks ago, a report had reached him about a possible necromancer presence in the Pascar Plains during the dungeon extermination campaign. Back then, he had given a simple order and moved on. The Crown’s Hand had been deployed to deal with it. That alone should have been enough.

It had sounded minor. Nothing worth holding his attention. And so, like many other things, he had forgotten it.

But the report in his hand was written by Selene, and she was not someone who wasted words. If anything, she preferred to write as little as possible. If a situation was simple, her reports were not more than a few pages.

This, however, was thicker than expected. That meant only one thing.

Something had gone wrong. Or taking out the necromancer had been far more complicated than it should have been.

A faint flicker of curiosity finally surfaced within him as he opened the report and began to read.

His eyes moved across the lines quickly at first, but then they slowed.

The more he read, the more his expression shifted, the earlier boredom fading away completely. His gaze sharpened, attention locking onto every detail written before him. The mention of not one, but multiple necromancers was enough to raise concern. The addition of undead orcs only deepened it.

But none of that was what truly caught his attention.

It was a name. One singular name written within the report that made him pause entirely.

Xanders lowered the parchment slightly, his mind already moving ahead of what he had just read. Then, without hesitation, he set the report aside and reached toward the small bell placed at the edge of his desk.

A sharp chime echoed through the chamber.

It took less than ten seconds before the doors opened, and one of his advisors Johan stepped in with urgency, bowing deeply the moment he entered.

“You called for me, Your Majesty?”

Xanders didn’t look at him immediately. His gaze remained on the report resting on the table for another second before he finally spoke.

“Yes,” he said calmly. “I did.” He leaned back slightly, his fingers tapping once against the armrest before continuing. “Has Count Evans sent the full report of the Pascar Plains campaign yet? He's leading it, right?”

Johan straightened just enough to respond, though his head remained slightly bowed.

“Yes, and he hasn't sent it yet, Your Majesty. We expect it in two to three weeks. However, a preliminary report from the Crown’s Hand has already been delivered.”

“I’ve already read it,” he replied. “But I need more information. The Hand won't be back for a while either, if I'm right.”

Johan nodded. “Yes, but we did have earlier reports from the start of the campaign, if you want them, Your Majesty.”

“Does those reports have any mention of Rayne Frayser?”

As soon as he said that name, Johan visibly paled, but the man had worked for him for decades now and he soon schooled his expressions.

“Yes, the earlier reports do mention him.”

King Xanders finally lifted his gaze from the report and looked properly at Johan, his eyes narrowing slightly as the earlier boredom was replaced by something sharper.

“And yet,” he said slowly, “no one ever thought to tell me anything about him.”

Johan stiffened at once. The reaction was subtle, but it didn’t escape Xanders’ notice. The man straightened his back and lowered his head even further, choosing his words carefully.

“We did place the reports on your table, Your Majesty,” he replied. “Myself and the other advisors chose not to bring attention to it. At the time, we believed it unnecessary on top of other issues.”

He paused briefly before continuing, his tone turning more firm, as if trying to justify the decision.

“We expected him to die sooner or later. That is usually the fate of most forsakens, much less a traitor even the gods will condemn. And despite everything, he has no backing, no influence, no real support. A Frayser is worse than a commoner, you said it yourself on the eve of their execution, Your Majesty.”

Xanders leaned back slightly, his expression unreadable.

“And yet,” he said, tapping a finger lightly against the armrest, “this forsaken turned out to be a spellsword.”

Johan’s composure faltered for a fraction of a second. His eyes widened just slightly before he caught himself, though the change had already been seen.

“A spellsword?” he echoed, his voice quieter than before. “I… was not aware of that, Your Majesty.”

Xanders gestured toward the report on his desk.

“This one says otherwise,” he said. “Not only that, it states there were three necromancers present in the plains. Three. And this same man was involved in the deaths of two of them.” He paused, letting that settle before continuing. “Not just involved. A core reason behind their deaths, according to the Hand.”

Johan’s expression grew more strained with each word.

“The report also mentions that his growth has been… exceptional,” Xanders added, his tone carrying a faint hint of interest now. “For someone who has barely spent time in the army.”

The room grew quiet for a moment after that.

Johan lowered his gaze slightly, clearly unsettled. Xanders knew the reason well enough. It was the duty of his advisors to identify potential threats before they grew into something unmanageable. A forsaken would never qualify for one, but Rayne was different due to his background.

Xanders leaned forward slightly, resting his arm on the desk as he studied Johan.

“What do you think of him?” he asked finally. “Do you think such a man would serve the army and the kingdom well or do you think he will become another rot within it?”

Johan didn’t answer immediately.

For a few seconds, the room remained quiet as he stood there with his head slightly lowered, clearly thinking through his response. Xanders didn’t rush him. He simply watched, his fingers resting lightly against the armrest as he waited for the man to speak.

In the end, Johan let out a slow breath and raised his head just enough to answer.

“I don’t know him, Your Majesty,” he said carefully, choosing his words with precision. “So I cannot give a proper judgment on him. A spellsword can always be either a boon or a curse to the kingdom. That much is certain. But in this case…” He hesitated for a fraction of a second. “I don’t believe a single individual without the backing of a noble house can achieve much. Especially not someone like him.”

Johan’s gaze lowered again slightly.

“Being a Frayser, he will always be under scrutiny. If he makes even the smallest mistake, there will be more than enough people willing to remove his head from his body.”

A faint smile touched Xanders’ lips at that. “More than a few would be willing to do that even now,” he said lightly.

His gaze shifted back to the report lying on the table, fingers tapping against its edge.

“I’m simply intrigued,” he continued, his tone thoughtful now. “A farm boy growing this quickly. Not just surviving, but excelling. Gaining mana skills on his own. That isn’t something that happens often.”

His eyes narrowed slightly as he added, “It almost looks like the gods themselves are watching over him.”

Johan remained silent for a moment before speaking again, this time more directly.

“If you wish, Your Majesty,” he said, “I can arrange for his death. It would not be difficult. In the end, that is why we sent him to the army in the first place.”

The words hung in the air.

For a brief moment, Xanders considered it.

It would be the simplest solution. Clean and efficient. A potential problem removed before it had the chance to grow into something worse. He had done it countless times before, cutting down threats long before they could take root.

Even now, the thought lingered. Then a memory flickered through his mind.

Xanders exhaled slowly and shook his head.

“No,” he said. The decision was made just as quickly as the thought had appeared. “Just observe him.”

He leaned back into his chair again, his gaze drifting briefly toward the window before returning to Johan.

“There were interesting details in the Crown’s Hand report. Enough to make me curious,” he continued. “It seems likely he will be brought to the capital soon. Make sure that happens.”

Johan’s expression remained composed. Though there was a hint of confusion beneath it, he didn’t question the order.

“I will ensure he is brought to Valens, Your Majesty.”

Xanders gave a small nod. “That will be all.”

Johan bowed deeply before turning and leaving the chamber, the doors closing quietly behind him.

Once the room fell silent again, Xanders’ attention returned to the report on his desk.

He picked it up once more, but his thoughts had already moved elsewhere.

Back to that day of the Frayser’s execution.

He remembered it clearly, down to the smallest detail. The way William had stood before him, restrained but unbroken, his eyes still carrying that same stubborn fire even as the end approached.

Xanders had given him a final chance, as tradition demanded.

A last wish.

He had expected something simple. A good meal. A request for mercy. Perhaps even a plea to spare one of his legitimate sons.

William had asked that, but for a bastard child. One he had barely acknowledged before.

At the time, Xanders had thought little of it. Nothing more than a desperate attempt to keep his bloodline alive in some way. Perhaps guilt, finally surfacing at the very end.

Xanders had granted the wish. And then, just as easily, he had ensured that the same child was sent to the army. A place where he would die.

That had been the intention.

Now, sitting in his chamber, Xanders found himself wondering.

Had William known? Had it all been intentional from the very beginning? If not… then why? Why that child? Why make such a request at the very end, when everything else had already been lost?

Xanders leaned back slightly, his gaze unfocused for a brief moment as the question settled deeper into his mind.

Did William truly believe that boy would grow into something like this? Or had there been another reason entirely?

The answer eluded him.

And for the first time in a long while, Xanders found himself… interested in someone. Maybe he will finally have some reports to look forward to from now on.

As he thought that, another question emerged.

How much more would Rayne Frayser grow, and what had the gods intended for him?
