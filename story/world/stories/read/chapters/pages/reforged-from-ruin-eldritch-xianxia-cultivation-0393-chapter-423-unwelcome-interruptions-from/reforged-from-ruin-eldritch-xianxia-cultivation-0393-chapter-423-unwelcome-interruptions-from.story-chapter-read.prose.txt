The cost is immaterial. The targets have been assigned. Inform the savages that we will deal with their infestation. If their ingratitude becomes an issue, they shall be dealt with in turn.

-Orders given to several teams of very dangerous people.

The next few hours are pretty productive.

By this point she estimates that it’s been maybe two or three days since her new visitors arrived, which is interesting because time is interesting, but also because of how fast they’re growing. She wasn’t awake for the first day or so, but in the next two-ish, she’s taken a very active interest in all the goings-on, and it seems like maybe these are exceptionally slow cultivators.

Just a few hours each of cycling the energy inside her body and their flesh is all flush and healthy and changed, improved measurably with just a little bit of effort. Every time someone’s taken a bite of a part of her it’s either led to their horrific collapse or to a skyrocketing rate of advancement, pushing them through blockages and opening up whole new pathways through their organs. It’s like they’ve been held back somehow, or didn’t really have all that much talent, only just now finding proper resources.

She can’t really remember how She used to be about resources (there’re a lot of safeguards that far back in her self-reflection), but they all seem really eager, too. There’s more than one violent encounter around particularly Qi-rich pools of blood, plants, or other “natural” parts of the landscape.

Still, they seem like they’re having fun. Mostly. There’s a lot of loud emotions about the whole thing, anyways.

Most of her time and attention remains focused on the trial-takers, though. As fun as it is watching the little ones frolic about her outer surface, the ones who decided to risk themselves and commit to being bold are the really interesting ones. Even with their frankly not-very-impressive survival rates, they’re really doing their best, and it’s really interesting to watch them.

Most of the wandering cultivators tend to move much more cautiously, taking less risks but ironically willing to push past their limits more often. Those with life-saving artifacts often hesitate less, aware of their own mortality and their protection from it, but in the smart ones it’s clearly a conscious decision, or so minor as to be barely noticeable. Those associated with sects vary in their behaviors plenty, but most of them seem to have a sort of unspoken understanding of social norms with each other. Conflicts are resolved fairly quickly, and resources are at least partially shared amidst direct allies, resentment and gratitude both working hand in hand as those with higher ranks assume positions of leadership almost by default.

Teams remain the most common approach to the trial’s many challenges, and the conditions have led to some unconventional alliances. Some members of opposite Peaks have worked hand in hand, ironically betraying each other a bit less than their subservient sects betray each other. Meanwhile, wandering cultivators tend to stick together or try to ingratiate themselves with more stable groups, depending on their aggression towards more well-established sects.

It’s all very fun to watch. And to play with!

Forming creatures to populate the tunnels is perhaps her favorite part, though designing traps is pretty fun too. She’s made sure to vary things up, though whenever she has fancier rewards prepared or a group she’s particularly fond of, she tries her best to mix the two together. A series of thin platforms emerging from an abyss, for example, goes very well with waving tendrils from the ceiling, made to pull the slow and unlucky into mollusk-like crustaceans above. A series of tunnels with hidden seams works just as well with projectile spikes and bone-scythes as it does with wriggling worms and hidden spiders. She has particular fun making a large, semi-feline thing to populate one of her larger chambers, giving it enough intelligence to act as a proper hunter. The few who make it to that chamber, which acts as a central node to several other levels, tend to avoid the beast, ducking and scurrying between bits of cover and landmarks of giant intestines draped over and hanging around the space.

Which is smart, because if the felinoid thing catches them, it can call up the snakes. And the snakes can call up the spiders. And if you get the spiders? Oh man, the dogs are not far behind, and they’re really small, they can fit like ten of them inside somebody if they really squish in there.

They’re mostly knives and cartilage at that size, so they squish super well, too. She tested it.

It’s entertaining enough that she’s actually taken time out of producing unique rewards just to focus on creating more of the trial-spaces. She’s added in little rest areas, places for them to perform less sanitary business, hanging vines with fruits, tubers and meats hanging from them. Stuff to make the space survivable more long-term, if they’re interested. Or if they don’t find the exits. Which would make sense, as she’s not sure she made exits.

The rewards are nice too, though. Most reward chambers double as rest areas, offering an opportunity to solidify one’s gains and prepare for other opportunities. Alongside the Twin Bloods Pagoda, she’s also made the Profound Musculature Pavilion, the Beatific Bone Bathhouses, the Digestive Caves, and other such notable locations, each imbued with distinct resources to be absorbed and levels of potency to self-medicate with. Even then, amidst those spaces there are hidden chambers or side-rooms, places with Qi-infused biosteel, clusters of organs ready for harvest, even things like sword hilts and pieces of armor. She prefers to make those on request, but some, spaced around here and there, make for interesting thought experiments.

The longer they remain, the more of them she sees, the more inspired she becomes. She crafts a specialized set of astrological symbols on a board made of bone for a man whose scent reeks of stars and things yet to come. She builds a set of locks and keys for a woman who reeks of imprisonment and its breaking, and a set of barbed chains for a disciple whose smell is of rich earth and tilled soil. Each one that earns it becomes a new source of inspiration, helping her to grow and change from moment to moment.

And throughout it all, she gets to play with her new toys.

They keep dying or losing bits of themselves. For most of them, she just keeps them preserved, but for the spare bits that she has left over, she gets to play. Spiritual organs in particular are her focus, as always- the one organic tool she can’t quite replicate just yet.

It doesn’t help that she can’t really see or touch them.

The synesthesia helps, but that’s all it ever does. Scent, translated into sight, is always an improper tool, going through too many layers of filtration to be entirely accurate. On top of that, it doesn’t exist in quite the same way as their bodies. It’s like it’s on another layer, one deeper and higher than the others, where the scent originates from but cannot be touched.

Any way she has of seeing or feeling a meridian, or the pathways between them, is ultimately a cobbled-together mess of sensory organs and synesthetic interpretation. Inaccurate at the best of times.

But there is a direct connection between flesh and spirit.

How direct, she’s not sure- but there’s a reason that loss of a limb can be devastating or irrelevant to a cultivator in turn. If the cut severs the meridians, any limb that’s regrown will inevitably fall behind the rest of the tissue, incapable of being infused with Qi directly or controlled on that level. If it doesn’t, regeneration can be as simple medical procedure away, or born from an act of will. The pathways follow the contours of veins and nerves without ever fully overlapping, save where they intersect, and they grow as a body grows, changing from childhood to adulthood.

Several Raikas spend quite a bit of time cultivating flesh to convince the spiritual organs inside to grow along these new patterns. Others modify the internal architecture, hoping to convince more subtle shifts to occur. All of them have… mixed success.

It changes alright- but most of the time, the spiritual organs just come unmoored.

She doesn’t know what the patterns are yet. She’s not sure what things mean yet. But she has the resources and the time to start figuring it out. Probably.

She spends quite a bit of time in blissful enjoyment of all the lovely different things she can play with and try to figure out. She builds new chambers, expands the trial-spaces, watches and shapes the surface to make for more interesting stories from those left above. Only half of the cultivators that arrived are still in play, but that doesn’t reduce how interesting they are, not by a long shot.

She might have spent even longer in that time if she didn’t feel something poke, ever-so-slightly, at her main entrance.

Spatial shifting feels, to her, like a muscle. Her Comprehension of Flesh equates the two things, tying them together even as her relationship with both continues to grow. In turn, some spatial alterations feel like parts of her own body, and can be controlled as such.

Something is getting uncomfortable at the knot of space and “Flesh” that indicates where she touches the wider world.

It takes her by surprise, and on the fifth day of her existence and cohesive memory, she decides to take a look outside.

Dozens of other Raikas are busy inside of her / inside themselves, making for plenty of management. They’re not all the same person, not all one and the same with each other, but they do intermingle, fusing and melting out of each other as needs must. Several of them join back together to fuse their brain-matter into a single, stronger whole, capable of thinking more thoughts faster, stronger than the others would be. She takes some of her newfound understanding of what she is and what she can do, preparing herself, storing some extra goodies she’s designed that she didn’t feel quite unfair enough to unleash against Core Formation realm cultivators.

Manifesting out of a fold in her own Space, Raika extends a thin, slender eye through her folded tissues, slithering out into the fleshy grass that surrounds her exterior.

It smells a lot out here.

There are several encampments around her, all of them mostly empty save for a few specific and much stronger cultivators. They have their own little sections, festooned with magical arrays, draped in feathers and boats and cauldrons and other potent artifacts they seem to be using to distract themselves, while some simply sit, meditating actively. Five of them in particular have weight to them almost equivalent to her, and the smell, even through an edited eyeball, is enough to make parts of her recoil and sneeze into herself.

The two Peaks are on opposite sides to each other, surrounded by camps made from those who make their homes upon each Peak’s mountain ranges. On one side is a coalition of rich brown, steel, gold and metallic tones, while the other side holds esoteric blues and uniquely blue-ish greens. Each side warps the world around them simply by existing in it, flavoring reality differently by the sheer weight of magics and the power of the cultivators within each.

But they’re not what poked at her.

She feels several sets of attention turn to the slender little tendril of eye that she’s deployed, the pressure making its structure shiver and start to break apart.

None of them hold quite as much weight or exert quite as much pressure as the third encampment.

Three people stand at the far edge of the open plateau that she’s planted herself in. Each one of them is dressed in dark green robes with bright black pauldrons, bits of gold armor highlighting parts of their physique and glowing with the sort of weight and energy that make her brains hurt. Each of them wields a sword, a Jian, perfectly and utterly identical to the others being worn, each one dripping with a scent sharper than the ones that the three men carry. All three of them sit, with no protections and no encampment, staring directly at the portal that she’s sphinctered well shut.

And without any visible effort, just one of the three of them is pressuring her as much as any one of the Peak coalitions altogether.

She looks at these newcomers. Really looks at them. Hundreds of different forms of ocular nerves form, mutate, cluster and are discarded. Olfactory nerves blend into the jelly of the eye-snake, absorbing every drop of local energies to taste and interpret. A few new brains are grown and then absorbed by Raika just to make sure she doesn’t miss a single detail, tracking every possible chain of thought that she can think of.

All three of them have a sigil on their armor. Small, kept tight to their belts, but visible nonetheless.

A tree amongst trees. Greener than green.

Half of her brains shut down before she has a chance to recognize the safeguard even activating.

A safeguard. A big one. Old and entrenched. Something core to who she was, locked away on penalty of self-destruction.

Still she stares at them. She makes more of herself and consumes them, adding them to her mass, modifying each new body the slightest bit to try to adapt around the block.

The man in front, the one pressuring her the most, rises from a lotus position up to his feet, unhurried.

The encampments to either side stir, powerhouses able to rival the newcomers rising to keep them in their place. Still, there’s a nervous energy in the camp, the sort of tension that comes when an outcome is unknown or unpredictable.

More of herself is forcibly shut down. More of her brain matter goes inert.

She compensates, consuming what’s lost and recycling it to make something new.

This is important.

She thinks. She thinks hard.

Deep inside of her, a world of invented madness and divine flesh shivers, dozens of disciples left alive or kept alive all feeling the land around them react.

She remembers.

Not much. Not any details.

Just a name.

Feng.

The first of the three armored figures takes a step forward, eyes to either encampment as he approaches the fold in space that is Raika.

She doesn’t understand why she does it. But she does, in fact, open the fold just enough to launch a bomb at him.

Enough Qi to light up the mountainside flares, Qi-infused musculature flexing and folding in on itself to direct the blast, and the world goes white beneath the roar of it.
