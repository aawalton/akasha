Every time my eyes were off of you was a moment I couldn’t be sure you wouldn’t be taken from me. When the Mad King was finally killed, I felt such relief, believing my daughter was finally safe.”

Will I try to stop you from leaving?” She played with the question a moment, looking at the tea almost as if she expected it could answer for her. “No,” she finally said. “There is no way I can stop you. I never wanted you to feel like a caged bird. I wanted you to live a life of safety away from the horrors. I wanted you to feel safe. But in the process of doing so, I made you feel caged. Your father as well. We know how difficult it was with what you went through as a child. I know life has been hard, and I'm so grateful that you worked up the courage to speak with us the way you did. It helped to open my eyes to see I did exactly what I was trying not to do.

“You suffered as a child. Something you don't remember very much of, and I hope you never do. But watching what happened to you… I just wanted you safe. As did your father. So yes, we may have been a bit more strict than we should have been.”

She set down the tea and walked over to her daughter, reaching out and cupping the side of his face.

Tressa leaned into her mother's gentle touch. The soft warmth of her hands had always made her feel safe. Her scent of raspberry and Solstice Lily nectar was stronger than normal. She would miss her mother's warmth, her smell, her laughter and stories, her touch, and most of all, the love she always gave Tressa.

“Mother,” Tressa said as she hugged her. “I love you, I really do. I know you and Father wanted what's best for me, but I do need to leave.”

“I see that you are quite sure you can succeed at that.”

“I am, Mother. I…” She nodded to the window. “I have a flying spell. I’m going to leave that way.”

She blinked, then smiled. “You always were hard to keep somewhere if you didn’t want it.”

“I’m sorry, Mother.”

"Don't be. But you know, Aldmern is going to be in quite a lot of trouble when you are discovered missing in the morning.”

Tressa wrinkled her nose. “He deserves it. Believe me.”

“What did you fight about exactly?”

Tressa let out a breath. “I’m not sure if it is truly his fault or my own. I’m not sure how to think of it. But he oversteps, Mother… And he betrayed my trust.”

“I see…”

“Mother. When I leave, I might not be back for a very long time. Maybe not until my trials in five years.”

Lucyia paused at that but then nodded.

“I know, my child. And I won't stop you. I can't. If I tried, you would end up hating me. Just be safe. Send letters. I'll return them to you by way of eagle. And Tressa, most of all, please be careful. I wish not to lose you. I love you more than the stars and the heavens. You are my perfect gift that I was blessed with. The gods granted me the most glorious child I could have asked for. Your curiosity and brightness put even the greatest scholars and the sun to shame in your shadow. And I will protect your light the best I can, and by doing so, I need to let you shine in ways you cannot do here. So please be safe and return home with a new understanding.”

“I will, Mother,” Tressa said as they hugged one last time. “I'll return before my trials, I swear.”

She nodded, holding her daughter's face once more as they parted from each other's embrace. “How you've grown. I almost forgot how old you are. You'll do well.”

Tressa reached out her hand and called her staff to her. It flew from its place in the corner and landed firmly in her grip. She then walked over to the large window at the back of her room; she cracked it open, putting her foot up on the sill, but then just stood there for a moment. She felt the evening breeze blowing in and swaying her hair as she looked back.

“Mother. Apologize to Father for me. I do care for him, and I know… I know he means well. But I can’t be what he wants. Tell him I’m very sorry for that.”

“I will have word with him, but I won’t apologize for you. Nor do I think it is necessary. Your father has his views, but he too will see the incredible woman our daughter is becoming.”

Tressa blushed, and despite herself she smiled. She didn’t know if her father could ever truly see her that way. But she hoped it was true.

“Say goodbye for me! To Father, Leimia, Maikia, Narella, to everyone. Even Aldmern. Let them know I’ll miss them dearly.”

“Of course, Tressa.”

With that, Tressa threw herself out of the window. “Levant!”

Lucyia walked to the window, holding it open as she watched her daughter go, flying high above the nobel estates, looking back only once to wave goodbye a last time, and then disappearing behind the Falidorn wall.

“Go, my child. And may the goddess watch over you.”

Briadon chugged back a mug of ale and slammed it onto the table. “Barkeep! Another!” he called, raising a fist in the air. Lisha sat opposite him, with a map unrolled in front of her, a claw scratching under her leathery chin as she considered their route.
