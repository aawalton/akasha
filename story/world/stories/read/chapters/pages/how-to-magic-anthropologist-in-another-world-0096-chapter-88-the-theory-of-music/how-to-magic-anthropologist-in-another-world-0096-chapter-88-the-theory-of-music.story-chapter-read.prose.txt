The three of us settled onto the sofa while the impossible skyline of Earth stretched beyond the glass to our left.

I caught Riri stealing occasional glances toward the city before looking back at us as Nina and I discussed what to do next.

"So," Nina asked, sitting in the middle with her wings spread behind Riri and me, "what are you planning to show her? More of that music anime you showed me before, or maybe another one of those movies?"

Riri looked back and forth between us with growing curiosity.

"What exactly are those?"

"You'll see," Nina replied with an amused smile. "It's easier to show than explain."

I shook my head.

"I think we'll save those for another day. Before we get into modern entertainment, I want to start with something much older." I turned toward the screen. "Let's begin with what most people where I grew up would consider classical music. It's fundamental to a lot of modern music."

I looked toward the small speaker sitting beneath the television.

"Alexa, play Eine kleine Nachtmusik by Mozart."

The device chimed almost immediately.

"Playing Mozart: Eine kleine Nachtmusik, performed by the McGill Symphony Orchestra."

Riri's eyes widened.

"Did that thing just speak?"

Before either Nina or I could answer, the television screen came to life. The static image of a beach disappeared, replaced by a moving picture of dozens of musicians quietly preparing their instruments while others sat patiently with wooden flutes resting across their laps. Behind them stretched an audience hundreds of people strong, every seat occupied as they silently waited for the performance to begin.

"The painting is moving," Riri whispered under her breath. "That's just a Dreamer memory projection, right?"

"Nope," I said. "We have this in real life too."

Before she could say anything else, her attention returned to the screen as applause erupted to greet the conductor stepping onto the stage. He acknowledged the audience with a slight bow before raising a thin white baton.

A heartbeat later, the first violins began to play.

Riri didn't speak again.

Her bright orange eyes remained fixed on the screen as she watched every musician with complete concentration. Unlike Nina, who was watching the instruments themselves, Riri seemed fascinated by the performers. I caught her gaze drifting from one section of the orchestra to another, carefully observing how each instrument entered the composition before fading back into the background to allow another to take its place.

She watched and listened as the strings yielded to the woodwinds, then the horns entered. Finally, she nearly stood when the entire orchestra played together.

She remained completely silent for the entire twenty-four minutes and nineteen seconds.

When the final note faded and the audience erupted into applause, the television quietly returned to a recommendation screen of similar performances. Riri continued staring at it for several more seconds, as though she hadn't quite finished processing what she had just witnessed.

Finally, she leaned back against Nina's wing and slowly exhaled.

"Okay..." she said thoughtfully. "I completely believe you. You're from an Eighth Realm."

"Why do you say that?" I asked.

"The music evolved along a completely different path than ours."

"Really?" I asked. "I've already seen instruments here that look remarkably similar to the ones back home."

I was curious to hear the opinion of someone who had devoted her entire life to music.

"Every civilization will eventually invent strings, percussion, and wind instruments in one form or another," she said after a moment. "It happened independently across the Seven Realms."

"Yeah," said Nina. "We have a variety of similar-looking musical instruments, but Alice's world still seems to be a bit more advanced than ours."

Riri rested her chin on one hand, her fingers lightly tapping against her cheek as she stared at the frozen image of the orchestra. She remained silent for several seconds, her bright orange eyes drifting from one recommendation to another before she finally shook her head.

"The truly strange part is the cooperation," she said at last.

"Music evolved in the Dreaming Realm through inspired bards obsessed with perfecting their own art and earning fame and recognition in the Great Dream. It's an individualistic path." Her finger tapped slowly against one of the armrests. "Similarly, in the Mythic Realm, music evolved as an act of devotion to a god, an intimate conversation between a human and the divine."

"The Kindred have a more tribal tradition of singing together," she gestured toward the screen, "but it was little more than a way to pass the time during long winters or celebrate the harvest in summer. It was never a path to power for them, so it didn't evolve very far."

"The Soul Realm and the Bloodline Realm are even worse. They embraced music much later, mostly borrowing from Dreamers while pretending it had always been part of their own traditions. And if the telepaths had any musical tradition before their extinction, it was lost with them."

She pointed back to the screen.

"But this..." She paused, searching for the right words. "There were more than sixty musicians performing together, yet not a single one of them was trying to become the center of attention. Every performer willingly sacrificed their individuality so the composition itself could become greater than the sum of its parts."

She slowly shook her head.

"That would be almost unthinkable in the current musical landscape."

I smiled.

"That doesn't even begin to scratch the surface of human cooperation when it comes to music." I smiled. "Let me show you something a little more absurd."

I looked back toward the speaker.

"Alexa, play the Indian raga, the one with a Thousand Sitarists."

"Playing Grand Symphony of One Thousand Sitarists, Raag Hansadhwani."

The television shifted to a massive open-air stage.

Both Nina's and Riri's eyes widened.

Hundreds upon hundreds of musicians sat in carefully organized rows stretching farther than either of them could see at first glance. The sheer scale was difficult to comprehend, even for me, and I had already seen the video before. Rather than a single central orchestra, it was an entire sea of performers, each holding an instrument and waiting with perfect discipline.

Then the music began.

The first sitars rang out with their bright metallic resonance before hundreds more joined them, until the melody became an immense shimmering tapestry of strings. Wooden flutes entered one after another, weaving gentle melodies above the sitars, while tablas established a steady heartbeat beneath the performance. Finally, the vocalist entered, completing the ensemble. Every performer seemed to know exactly when to step forward and when to fade into the background, creating a harmony so vast that no individual musician could possibly stand out.

Neither of the Dreamers spoke. Their eyes remained fixed on the screen for the entire performance. When the final note faded away, Riri slowly let out the breath she had been holding.

"...One thousand," she muttered.

I nodded.

"Most musical performances on Earth never reach those kinds of numbers, but there are occasions when even that isn't considered enough."

I smiled again as I thought of something else to show them.

"Alexa, show the opening ceremony of the Beijing 2008 Olympic Games."

The television immediately switched to an enormous stadium where two thousand performers moved with impossible precision, every move perfectly synchronized with the music and with one another.

Riri stared in disbelief.

"They're all moving together like a single individual is controlling them."

Then the stadium lights went out.

The entire arena fell into darkness except for thousands of glowing batons, their lights flowing across the stadium like waves over the surface of the ocean.

Nina gasped.

"How..."

Riri couldn't even speak. She simply watched, completely absorbed as the performers transformed the stadium into a living work of art through nothing but movement, light, and flawless coordination.

"This is the opening ceremony for a global sporting festival," I explained after the performance ended. "It's meant to celebrate a special occasion, not something people would watch every day."

I thought for a second about what to show them next.

"A lot of the time, an orchestra doesn't exist to be the only performance. Instead, it's there to support a singer."

I played an opera performance next, Nessun Dorma from Turandot by Puccini, letting the soaring voice dominate the stage while the orchestra provided the emotional foundation beneath him. After that, I introduced them to Umm Kulthum, whose commanding voice effortlessly remained the center of attention even while surrounded by dozens of accompanying musicians.

"Although the genre has changed completely," said Riri in a contemplative voice, "the musical theory is the same. Dozens of musicians dedicated to supporting a single vocalist."

"I asked Alice to bring you here to see if you like one of the instruments," Nina said. "I could make it for you. But I guess what makes the music from Alice's world so amazing isn't the variety of instruments. It's the cooperation."

Riri nodded.

"You could make every instrument I saw on that screen, and it still wouldn't matter. I'd struggle to gather even five people willing to dedicate themselves solely to me and my music, let alone dozens."

After considering those words, I thought the next thing I should show them was something that sat somewhere between a solo artist and an orchestra.

"You've seen musicians sacrificing themselves for the composition," I said. "Now let me show you another solution we came up with."

I looked toward the speaker.

"Alexa, play I AM by IVE."

"Playing I AM by IVE."

The television switched to a brightly lit stage.

"Sorry for the abrupt shift in genre," I grinned as six young women stood in formation beneath a sea of colorful lights.

Unlike the opera, they weren't seated behind music stands. Every one of them stood proudly at the front, dressed in coordinated outfits that belonged together while still allowing each performer to retain her own identity.

The opening melody began.

One girl stepped confidently into the center while the others spread around her in perfect formation. As the verse continued, she naturally drifted backward, and another member took her place without interrupting either the singing or the choreography.

Riri frowned.

"Wait..." She leaned forward. "They're switching positions."

A third member moved into the spotlight.

Then another.

Each sang a different part before seamlessly giving center stage to someone else.

The chorus arrived.

All six voices blended together while every movement remained perfectly synchronized. Even though they were dancing, not a single gesture looked out of place. Even to someone who knew little about performing, I could tell that everything had been rehearsed hundreds of times.

When the chorus ended, the center shifted once again.

Riri's eyes followed the movement.

Nina tilted her head.

"I don't think there's a main singer."

"There is," I replied. "Well, there isn't."

"What does that mean?"

"It depends on the song."

The song continued.

One member delivered a powerful high note.

A different girl took over the dance break.

Another handled the rap section.

Yet somehow the whole performance remained cohesive.

When the video finally ended, Riri remained staring at the screen.

"I don't understand."

"What part?"

"They're all competing..." She frowned. “At the same time, they aren't."

I smiled.

"Exactly."

She looked toward me.

"Explain."

"This is what we call an idol group," I said. "It's a relatively new development compared to the music I've shown you before."

"It's like a carefully crafted framework for fame," I continued. "Before joining the group, those beautiful, talented girls start out as rivals. Thousands of hopeful performers audition for only a handful of places. Once they become members, however, they become a team, and like a freelancer team, they need to cooperate if they want to survive."

I paused for a moment.

"The success of the group becomes the success of every individual."

"If one becomes famous,” Riri slowly nodded, “they all become famous together."

"And if one messes up," I added with a grim face, "the entire group suffers."

"That's..." Nina blinked. "...harsh."

I nodded.

"Each member develops a different public image. One might be known for her singing, another for dancing, another for comedy during interviews, another for fashion. Different fans become attached to different members."

"But they're all selling the same group," Nina realized.

"Exactly."

Riri leaned back against the sofa, deep in thought.

"So this would really help with the networking aspect of the job," she finally said. "You get six people promoting the group rather than one."

I nodded.

"There is also a parasocial aspect to it. When fans start getting attached to a girl, they begin wondering how they interact in private. You already have that with freelancer teams, I think. An informant in Ectamal sold me gossip that Captain Osar slept with Sergeant Lanka, after all."

Nina laughed.

"Can you imagine? You, as a bard, becoming part of the gossip you usually spread."

"Oh, don't get me started," Riri groaned. "It happened to me once. After I slept with Lieutenant Hanakudo Agame, people started calling me the Dragon Slayer for months."

I laughed.

"Hey, most of the time, all publicity is good publicity."

"I noticed something else," Nina said, pointing toward the frozen image on the screen. "The visual aspect plays a big role in all this. The dancing and the pretty colors."

"This is why I changed my hair and my scale colors," Riri nodded. "The potion cost me thirty silver, but it was worth it."

"Still, the way they're doing it is fascinating," she continued. "It's far more subtle, as if no one wants to stand out more than the others."

"I've spent years trying to become the center of attention." She twirled a strand of her magenta hair around her finger. "While they've designed a system where you can seamlessly take it and relinquish it."

I nodded.

"It also helps every fan choose someone to connect with."

Riri chuckled.

"So people argue over which member they like best."

"They absolutely do. But it's part of the game, because those arguments serve as promotion too."

"I noticed something, though," said Nina. "The music comes from nowhere. These girls record the music beforehand so they can dance freely."

"No," I said. "Someone else makes the music. Sometimes someone else writes the lyrics too, and someone else even creates the dance choreography. Most of the time, the girls are only there to perform."

"And the people who make all that don't get the credit?"

"They do. They're part of the team. They share in the success."

"They aren't facing the audience," Riri frowned.

"This is even worse than the opera. At least you see the musicians on stage," said Nina.

"This is the idol formula," I said. "There's another way of doing things that may work better for Dreamer sensibilities."

"Alexa, play Bohemian Rhapsody by Queen."

"Playing Bohemian Rhapsody."

The television changed once more.

The opening a cappella echoed softly through the room as the four voices sang together.

Four men appeared on stage. Unlike the idol group, there was no dancing.

Then the piano intro began as Freddie Mercury worked his magic with both his vocals and the keyboard.

"This already feels more familiar." Riri relaxed immediately. "The musicians are also the singers."

"Hush and keep listening."

Seconds later, the song unfolded unlike anything they had heard before.

The singer dominated the first verses with remarkable control before the music suddenly shifted into layered harmonies.

Then came Brian May's iconic guitar solo. For nearly half a minute, the vocalist stepped aside completely while the guitarist became the undisputed focus of the performance.

Riri blinked before frowning as the operatic section began.

"It changed genres in the middle."

"Yes."

"This is insane." She shook her head.

The singer reclaimed center stage as naturally as if nothing had happened.

A little later, the music surged with energy once more, and the instruments became the center of attention while the vocals receded, carrying the song toward its conclusion.

After a moment of silence, Riri slowly looked from one member of the band to another on the frozen screen.

"They're equals."

"More or less," I said. "The singer is more recognizable, but nobody would say the others aren't important."

She nodded and pointed at the guitarist.

"He became the center for a while."

"And then he gave it back." I nodded.

She pointed toward the drummer and the bassist.

"Those two had their moments, but not as much as the other two."

"Yeah, a bit." I shrugged.

Finally, Riri smiled.

"I think something like this is more doable," she said. "Maybe with more dancing and more colors like the other performance."

"A style of music built on cooperation," she added, "where performers share fame rather than chase it."

"That's all well and good," Nina said, "but I'm actually more curious about the instruments in that last one."

"Which part?" I asked.

"Well..." She pointed toward the frozen image of Queen on the screen. "Those guitars weren't hollow, and the piano didn't sound like strings or pipes. Then there was that thing they kept singing into. Is it some kind of sound amplification artifact?"

I smiled.

"The guitars are actually two different instruments. One is an electric guitar, and the other is a bass guitar. They use electrical current to translate the strings' vibrations into sound."

Nina blinked.

"They used electricity?"

"What's electricity?" asked Riri.

"Harnessed lightning," Nina answered before I could.

"Yep."

I pointed toward the microphone stand.

"And that thing is called a microphone."

I pantomimed holding one near my mouth.

"It's actually surprisingly easy to make, especially since you already understand the basics of electricity from when you helped me build the taser spear."

Riri stared at me for a long moment.

"I'm sorry..." she said slowly. "Nothing on that stage looked easy to make... other than the drum set, maybe."

"I didn't even comment on how crisp those instruments sounded because they all looked impossible to reproduce." She gestured toward the city skyline. "I figured they were just another marvel of your world."

Her eyes shifted toward Nina.

"Are you seriously telling me she could make something like that?"

"Very easily," I said confidently.

Both Dreamers looked at me skeptically.

I shrugged.

"Alexa, play a video of someone making a speaker with a paper cup, copper wire, and a magnet."

The television immediately switched to a homemade tutorial.

A teenager wrapped insulated copper wire around a paper cup before placing a magnet beneath it. He used a lighter to burn away the insulation from the wire ends before connecting everything to an audio jack.

Neither Nina nor Riri blinked. They watched every step with complete concentration.

"You see?" I said. "This design can work as either a speaker or a microphone."

The video showed the finished device producing sound.

"The cone, in this case the cup," I explained, "moves when air vibrations hit it. The magnet and copper coil convert that movement into a tiny electrical current. That's how a microphone works."

"And if you send electricity through the coil instead, it moves the cone, which pushes the air and creates sound." I smiled. "Now it's a speaker."

Nina looked back at the television.

"...That's it?"

"Pretty much." I shrugged. "Honestly, once you have a weak electrical signal, the only difficult part is making it stronger. You could probably use a mana core for that."

Nina blinked.

"Seriously?" She looked back toward the screen, then toward me. "I... think I could actually make this."

"Insulating the copper wire is probably the hardest part," I said. "But you already understand the underlying principle."

Nina slowly nodded.

"I do." Her wings twitched excitedly behind us. "I really do."

Riri folded her arms.

"Hold on."

She pointed accusingly at the paper cup on the television.

"That still doesn't look anything like what the handsome man was holding."

"That's because it's only a proof of concept." I couldn't help grinning. "She can build you something a little more elegant."

I tapped one finger near my mouth. “She could make it look like a sleek, phallus-shaped object you can sing into."

Riri burst into laughter.

"Yes! I want it thick and rigid, just like the one the handsome man was carrying."

Nina covered her face with one hand.

"Alice, don't encourage her."

"Too late," grinned Riri.

"I walked right into that one." I sighed. "I didn't expect her to be this shameless."

After a few moments of laughter, Nina looked back toward the television.

"What about the guitars?" She frowned thoughtfully. "Do you just attach one of those microphones to the instrument?"

"Not quite." I shook my head. "A microphone listens to the air's vibrations."

I raised my hand, spread my fingers slightly, and held them horizontally to mimic strings.

"An electric guitar doesn't care about the air." I wiggled my fingers slightly. "The movement of the strings already creates the vibrations."

"You wrap copper wire around magnets placed directly beneath the strings." I placed my fist beneath my fingers. "The magnets detect the strings vibrating without needing the instrument itself to resonate."

Realization slowly spread across Nina's face.

"So..." She leaned forward. "That's why the body isn't hollow."

"Exactly," I said. "If it were hollow, you'd waste energy vibrating the air."

Her eyes lit up.

"So all the sound comes only from the electrical signal through the speaker."

"Exactly."

She stared into space for several seconds.

"I need to try this."

"I figured you would."

"I don't understand half of what you're talking about anymore," Riri laughed.

"I'm honestly surprised by how quickly you picked it up," I said to Nina.

"Oh, but you forget one thing," Nina said with a smile. "I've entered the Creation State for two different projects, one involving a string instrument and another involving electricity. This is right up my alley."

"I should be able to make a few prototypes." She looked almost ready to leave immediately despite being inside a dream.

I smiled.

"We should probably call it a night."

"Already?" Riri frowned as her eyes drifted back toward the television.

"We've been dreaming for quite a while, Nina and I."

"I want more," begged Riri. "Just one more, please."

I looked through the recommendation list, scrolling quietly along the side of the screen.

There were thousands of songs I could pull from my memory, and millions more if I were willing to spend Inspiration.

"Okay." I smiled. "One last song, then."

I paused, already knowing exactly what I wanted them to hear.

"This one's one of my personal favorites."
