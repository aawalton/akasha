“I will… see them immediately,” Rosslyn said, caught slightly off-guard by the Sergeant’s announcement. “Unless there is something else?” She turned back to look at King Janus, Adon, Frederick, Goldie, and the High Priestess.

Does Rosslyn sound… nervous? Adon wondered. He thought he could detect a little trace of it via Telepathy, too.

He hadn’t imagined that sort of thing could happen to her. Of course it could, though. She was just a human being.

“I have no business pressing enough to keep Her Majesty from the embrace of her subjects,” the King said lightly, smiling.

He seemed sincere. It was strange, from Adon’s perspective, that King Janus actually meant what he was saying. He had just a few minutes before been angling for his own advantage in this situation, trying to sink his claws into the new Queen of Claustria. Now he was just happily letting her go talk to her subjects, and seemingly wishing her the best inside of his own heart.

People are fickle, the butterfly thought. Or maybe he’s just the goes-with-the-flow type.

Adon, we should accompany her, Goldie sent to him only.

He replied with a simple Yes.

This would be Rosslyn’s first meeting with her subjects as their Queen. She should be surrounded by friends and supporters.

Without any further coordination, Adon and Goldie both activated Transformation at the same moment. For the first time, King Janus witnessed as the two mystic beasts he’d been speaking with turned from invertebrates into full-sized adult humans.

The golden-colored spider became a tall woman with black hair and dark, haunted eyes. The only hint of her true nature was an implausible fleck of gold in the irises. The butterfly went to his now go-to form, a humanoid with elf-like ears, though he gave himself an extra inch or two of height just because King Janus was a big bear of a man like King Alistair had been. He decided not to reproduce his wings in giant form. It would just make getting in and out of buildings a pain.

Goldie made her appearance more androgynous, Adon noticed instantly. Was that something Frederick liked, or is it just a change in her tastes?

It seemed as if, little by little, while he was focused on Rosslyn, his friend had been growing and changing without him really noticing. That would have felt like a good thing, if Adon didn’t have the feeling that Samson had been doing the same thing, leading up to the younger spider sacrificing himself and dying.

I have to pay more attention, the butterfly chastised himself. It’s not as if I ran around making a ton of friends and can afford to just throw them away. I’m definitely not going to use the one life where I managed to get, um, engaged—I guess?—and turn into one of those guys who forgets he used to have friends back when he was single.

“What are you two doing?” Rosslyn asked.

“We want to go with you,” Adon said. “For support.”

“Your people find the mystic beasts pretty symbolically significant, right?” Goldie said. “And one of them is now meant to become your consort. I imagine these forms will make it easier for normal humans to understand the situation.”

“Right, yes, of course,” Rosslyn said. A little smile broke over her face.

Adon sensed the warmth of her emotions like a little campfire from just a few feet away. That flicker of nervousness was completely drowned out in the buzz of good feelings. More important than their desire to support Rosslyn with their visible appearances beside her was the fact that she felt supported by Adon and Goldie.

“We should go, then,” the Queen added after a moment.

Sergeant Angus opened the door, and the trio walked outside, Rosslyn leading the way.

The day felt a bit different now that Frederick had been healed. William was still dead, Samson had still been left behind, Wayn was still lost, but it felt like some small progress had been made toward setting things right.

The glow of that surrounded them as the three strode out of the cottage and down the dirt path to where around two dozen peasants were standing in varying conditions. A few were injured and visibly bleeding, a handful had been injured and patched up, while the rest looked in more or less good condition.

But all were clearly tired.

“Your Majesty!” called out one of the women in the front excitedly, staring at the Queen.

The others took up the cry almost immediately.

“Your Majesty! Your Majesty!”

Rosslyn gave the small crowd a weary smile as she approached.

They greeted her with a medley of well wishes.

“Goddess save the Queen!”

“So glad you survived…”

“—knew there was nothing to the rumors you were taken captive…”

“—the only one who can save us!”

The joy began to break down near the end as reality set in again among the worn out people who had gathered to their new ruler. They began to ask fractured questions and report crimes by the Demon Empire’s ravaging army, talking over each other and speaking hesitantly.

“Your Majesty, what can we do next?”

“—my family, my village…”

“Our homes have been destroyed…”

“...your father?”

“...Wayn?”

A little girl spoke up last, but all that was audible of her words was, “my father… and your father.”

Adon felt Rosslyn try and fail to keep track of the different threads the people before her were addressing. He sensed that they weren’t even all from the same part of the country, which made it more difficult. These peasants had emerged from three or four different villages and simply met  each other because they were all in good enough shape to continue advancing.

They had all dragged themselves toward where a rumor said there might be safety, might be a human-friendly force, might be the Queen in one case—they weren’t all following the same rumor—but now they were winding down. Adrenaline was fading. Reality was catching up.

Their problems wouldn’t go away just because they had found some temporary safety or even their leader.

That wouldn’t stop them from trying to make that the case, though.

Rosslyn’s mouth twitched, and Adon sensed the Queen was about to say something. But he felt the uncertainty under the surface. She wasn’t nervous, but she didn’t have a clear plan. She was trusting in her own natural eloquence to carry the day.

That made Adon nervous.

The Queen was good at talking to him and her noble companions and presumably to soldiers under her command and household staff at her palace, but regular people?

It seemed like something yet to be tested to Adon, without fishing through her memories looking for examples. It wasn’t that he doubted Rosslyn, per se, just that he had no idea what he should actually expect. He knew how this situation would affect him, even now. How his knees would knock together, his tongue would seem to grow to twice its thickness and tie itself into a tight knot, his palms and armpits sweat…

Adon forced himself to stop thinking about how nervous that kind of situation would typically make him.

Instead, he focused up and sent Rosslyn a quick message: Rosslyn, they’re not looking for you to actually solve the problems right now. Just remember that. No one thinks you can actually do that, and it would be crazy to promise it. They just want hope. They want to know… They want to know that even if the city walls fell, you haven’t. That you won’t give up.

Thank you, fiance, she thought quietly, her inner voice almost bashful. Next time I address the peasants, you and I should work up a speech together. We will have to announce our engagement at some point, after all…

There was unmistakable affection in her tone. It hit Adon so hard that he had to focus carefully on his facial expression and not letting it shift to a shocked blush. The effect of the affection even temporarily overrode his fear of public speaking.

Right, he told himself. Real. It’s all completely real. Not a dream.

As he tried to keep an even keel, the Queen, apparently oblivious to the effects her word had on him, had begun to speak.

“My loving people, we… have all lost much over the recent days of treachery and battle,” Rosslyn said. “The Demon Empire has stolen land, burned homes, torn apart families, and committed crimes beyond counting. Their sudden aggression, a set of unfamiliar magic weapons, and unmatched numbers succeeded in scattering our military strength, and I feel certain many of you already know that we lost both the capital and my father recently…” She paused and choked back a quiet sob.

Did she have this prepared? Goldie sent to Adon only.

No, she didn’t, he shot back immediately. He couldn’t tell what effect the impromptu speech was having on the peasants emotionally yet. Most of them were surprised the Queen was speaking to them directly at such length and in such close proximity.

“I cannot promise to give you back all that was lost, as I myself cannot be assured that I will ever recover everything that was taken from my family in the last week. Some things can never be reversed. But we have powerful signs of our eventual victory to bring us hope. The High Priestess of the Goddess has been here, spreading blessings among those stricken by the Empire’s vile actions. Our ally, Parmonia, has brought their flying cavalry to our aid. The people of Claustria themselves are our greatest strength, a source of renewed vigor that the Empire cannot hope to match as unwanted occupiers. Even nature will turn against them. The mystic beasts sent by the Goddess have stood with us against the Empire, too.”

Adon and Goldie took that as a cue and used Transformation to return to their original forms, to the peasants’ astounded expressions and muttered exclamations of shock.

“I can only promise two things,” Rosslyn said. “First, I swear that I personally will never abandon the fight to reclaim what is ours from the clawing hands of the Empire. Someone must stand up to them if our people and our way of life is to survive. If they try to take what remains of our land, I prefer death to fleeing Claustrian soil. I am ready to live and die beside you in our homeland’s defense.”

Adon felt that promise as binding on himself, too. The Queen had not said as much, but he wasn’t going to abandon her any more than she was willing to abandon this war.

“Second, we will never cede to the Empire the land that they have so vilely stolen. Even if this fight must be continued by my descendants going forward for ten generations, we will not betray the men and women who have bled for this Kingdom by letting the enemy keep it.”

I apologize for not consulting you before I said that, Rosslyn thought in Adon’s general direction. If we marry… my promise would be considered a binding vow on all our shared descendants. If you wish me to walk it back, we can discuss how that might be done at a later—

I support you, Adon sent. We won’t let this be a ten generation war. Just focus on them for now.

A little upturn at the corner of Rosslyn’s lip almost spilled over into a smile that could have spoiled the effect of her speech, but she controlled herself as she continued.

“Please take care of yourselves and extend each other every compassion, all the kindness and decency that our people are known for. I have few resources to extend even to my staunchest allies at the present, but I will be wherever the soldiers are, for anyone who has need of me and what I can do with my two hands. May the Goddess watch over us all.”
