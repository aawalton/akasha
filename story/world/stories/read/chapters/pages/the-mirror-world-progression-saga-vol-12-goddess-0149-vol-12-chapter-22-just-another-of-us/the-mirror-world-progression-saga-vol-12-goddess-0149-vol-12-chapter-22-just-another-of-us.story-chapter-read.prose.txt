Behind the walls of the City of Heavenly Order, another hunt was underway. By necessity, it wasn’t composed of supernatural beings at all; by necessity, it was mostly made up of plain ole people amidst the teeming masses huddled behind the walls for solace from the conflicts.

At least, that was their initial reason. If they could have, most would have fled away from the conflict right outside the walls, too, but the final city, the final stand of the Dominion, was also their trap. Only a portion were allowed to leave, and whispers propagated in the shadows about where they were taken and to what ends.

The relative chaos of the flow of people had been ripe for the insertion of spies, and the alliance under Samantha had fully taken advantage of this. The intel for the war was a mixed bag considering how difficult it was to penetrate the inner places of that city, but they’d nonetheless obtained much with conventional eyes and ears.

Not just spies had slipped in, though.

Crow crouched patiently in the shadows of an alley, eyes closed, listening to the whispers continuously coming to him from a hundred different sources. Some were informants feeding bits of intel through the gestalt network, and this was yet another continuation of the mundane mechanisms with which they’d infiltrated the city.

But some of the whispers had less easy explanations. Some of the whispers in Crow’s head were from across a vast distance. They were garbled, so raw was this connection, like a wall was between them, but they wanted him to hear them.

They were victims, dead and gone, bubbled up back to a reality they left and shouldn’t have even been aware of. But Death had done something disruptive when he’d forged the connection that had allowed the talking skull of Nikesermos to summon their voices.

They had bled back into reality more and more, or Crow had begun to get more sensitive to them as a whole, even without knowing their names or their story.

In that moment, he knew the voices coming to him understood his target. They were stalking with him, encouraging him, pushing him forward, and with every step, they got closer to reality, closer to their desire and purpose.

The Dominion thinks their city can have no supernatural influences operating behind their precious walls. They must think their precious general is very safe, too.

Feeling out his direction suddenly by the grand assembly of a thousand points of data, Crow stood and opened his eyes. Hands in the pockets of his coat, he simply walked out into the street and proceeded down the path. People were everywhere — guards and refugees outside tents making shanty towns out of something that once had been a little greater than slums.

I lived not far from here. The building was condemned by officials and torn down years ago. In some alley, maybe even the one I just came from, I was beaten within an inch of death. Do I come for that vengeance, too? Maybe.

None so much as looked at him, even as suspicious as he must appear. But he walked right by guards that couldn’t care less at this point. Hell was churning up a new brand of fire beyond the walls, one made of war’s profound agony and terror.

Bizarre colors flashed from the sky as if rebounding off the clouds, and all brands of screams and warcries — animalistic and otherwise — traveled the distance. Vibrations seemed to presage the cracking of the world. What normal person could do much more than go through the motions, with the feel of the apocalypse in the air?

He was just another one of them walking on his way. Perhaps that was the most accurate description of Death, in the end.

These people are more dismal than I ever was. Pathetic. I pity them.

The information in his head wasn’t leading him to the wall fortress, certainly. That was hardly a surprise, even though some commander or three would be there. Why have your general, your mastermind, in the obvious place?

Especially when the sages had their own gestalt network to operate through. Depleted as it was, none were under any illusions that it was truly disbanded. Moreover, his quarry had demonstrated he would avoid conflict whenever it didn’t suit him.

Though he did not fully understand the physical way he was going, the whispers encouraged him, and he knew he was going the right way. He felt them pushing further, ready to break through the thinning divide between…

A reckoning is at hand for many. So be it, but know one thing: he is mine.

The whispers of the dead in his head leading him on by their uncanny means, [Death] continued his slow stalking, inexorable, undeniable, certain that no target he’d painted for justice could hide forever.

Samantha continued navigating the siege, tearing apart towers and monitoring all the while, feeding information to the Brain Trust continuously from her keen, unique senses.

The overall Dominion response to the spectral army continued to be nothing special. A hundred adjustments seemed to spawn behind the lines, most obviously from enchantments and spellcraft unseen, slowly propagating back out, thereby continually reinforcing the front lines.

Another obvious measure: Heaven Knights seemed to be distributed widely and systematically, to bolster the confidence of the masses and stifle the panic they’d been under. But there was more happening as a whole — intangible things — that showcased a general improvement in their tactics.

The common soldiers of the Dominion kept dying, their casualties severe. Sadly, there was always more where that came from, and meanwhile, they took out the spirits afflicting them steadily due to their uncontrolled frenzy leaving little room for defense.

They didn’t lose all of their witchhunter units, either — they’d lost some, but mainly had been used to stem the tide while greater adjustments were made and order was re-established in the ranks. They faded away at that point, their dogs unsummoned.

Signs showed they were being used like snipers hidden in the mix, using empowered specialty crossbow bolts, and pretty much executing specters at a distance with slow and inexorable impunity.

The towers fell again and again but seemed endless. Perhaps they’d broken a fifth of them, and a lesser fraction of soldiers in the meantime. Rather than get easier, the ranks only tightened up, the Dominion adjustments heavy on defensive fortification as they sacrificed offense.

The alliance had to conserve some of their buffing and spellcasting potential, so it steadily was returning back to a horrible slog to advance, even though they retained the advantage.

Mana. Mana is still an issue, and they have as endless a font of it collectively as I do personally. I should’ve expected no less of a legion of old wizards than to horde everything in their fortress.

Once more, Samantha debated using the crown, but resisted. It was something for the ‘last hump’ as a whole, and she was sure they just weren’t there yet. The enemy was bleeding and exhausting them, refusing to play their hand.

In the gestalt, Dax cursed. “Damn this general! He’s doing everything right here. They’ve figured out they can bleed us down through half of their forces, and they’re clearly holding their best for last. Not good.”

“What do you need me to do, Dax?” Samantha asked calmly.

“Time to drop one of our aces, Goddess. I’m not sure how wide-scale it’ll be, but we need them whittled down, and chaos would be good right now. Aside from that, I don’t think friendly fire will do much to the specters. Hmm. Could it even be beneficial to scatter them? Eh, fuck it! I’m gonna pull back the soldiers slightly, and you can go for it.”

Samantha, currently blowing up another tower as she dodged their pot shot magic response, struggled to figure out what she referred to. “Uhhh… I’m gonna need something more specific, Dax.”

Dax seemed annoyed. “Seriously? Fine, I get to steal your thunder and say it: Unleash the Kraken!”

“Hahahaha!” Six Moons Falling laughed gleefully into the gestalt. “Kraken! That’s hilarious! I hope I get to tease him with that before we all die.”

“Can we not assume death, please?” Estara begged. “Like fuck, some of us are hopeful for a happy ‘after,’ you know.”

“Oh, you poor little doll. Allow me to correct: I hope I get to tease him with that before reality as we know it implodes.”

Estara sighed. “It’s not any better, but thanks for fucking trying, I guess.”

Samantha felt put out as she realized Dax’s meaning. She had to resist slapping her forehead at forgetting about a ‘one-time’ promise made even before Rivermount was taken. “Aww. No fair. But really, I think he’d be offended if we said that, he’s not a k-”

Dax interrupted in annoyance. “Just get in position and do it, Samantha! I’m waiting on you. My foot is tapping as we speak!”

Suddenly brimming with excitement despite the promise of terrible destruction about to be unleashed, Samantha pulled back after collapsing her target tower, re-cloaking and reaching out through the deific mental spaces to touch base with her friend and ally from afar, the one known in Valuwei as Serpent in the Water.

“Avenging Leviathan,” Samantha intoned solemnly, “it’s time. I summon you!”
