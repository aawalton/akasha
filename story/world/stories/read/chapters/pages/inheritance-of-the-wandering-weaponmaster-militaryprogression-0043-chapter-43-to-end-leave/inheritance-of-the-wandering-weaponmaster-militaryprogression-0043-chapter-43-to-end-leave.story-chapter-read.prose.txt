“It’s damn hard to fight people instead of the goblins,” Ezra groaned as he settled back in the bath. “I kind of got used to fighting people through all the drills, but that was always… weak. You know? Pulling the hits, not trying to hurt anybody. I had experience hitting people a bit before, back when I was working for the Madam, but even then, it was always more of a need to show them that I meant business, not to knock somebody out or even kill them.”

Jormun nodded, though he didn’t say anything. Ezra took that as a sign to continue, saying, “The fight against the giant and the girl was good, but it really reminded me how much I’ve only practiced with a spear and, when I didn’t have one in hand, I wasn’t anywhere near as good as I should be. It was basically just me hoping for a lucky shot and then taking it.”

Part of the basis behind me pioneering the Weapon Master as a focus beyond achieving Knighthood and mastery with a singular weapon.

Ezra waited for Jormun to finish before he lost himself in thought. The grizzled ghost allowed the young man the quiet, and finally Ezra continued to say, “That Adept would have killed me if we’d fought, wouldn’t he?”

No doubt. Jormun’s answer was what Ezra expected, but it still hurt. His mentor elaborated. Putting aside the fact that he’s almost a Pseudo-Knight, you’d absolutely lose the fight. Going off of how he carries himself, he’s got years and years of experience fighting people. Given how inexperienced you are, you’d have to rely on your aura to bridge the gap in skill between you, but he’s got better reserves than you, as well as a natural element that is more well suited to lasting for a long time instead of being more explosive like wind tends to be.

So, basically, you’d exhaust yourself quickly, and he’d beat you to death. Probably without having to use his aura.

“Not going to spare my feelings, huh?” Ezra asked as he dipped his head under the water and ran his hands through his hair. The natural curls there’d tangled a little, but he teased them apart as he sat up and out of the tub.

If you could think of a good reason for me to coddle you and make you mediocre instead of learning, then maybe I’ll consider it. Doesn’t make much sense to me though.

Instead of answering, Ezra chuckled bitterly to himself and finished washing up. “Why don’t you help me during fights?”

What do you think I can do? Shout instructions to you? It’s better to use your eyes than to hope that someone else can take care of you when you’re in danger.

“I could still use some help,” Ezra protested.

Like what?

Ezra shrugged helplessly and focused on finishing washing up. As he dried himself, pulling a bit of the ambient aura into himself to keep from shivering, Jormun spoke up again.

You need to start training with fighting as an individual or, even better, dueling instead of just being a member of a shield wall.

Ezra couldn’t bring himself to disagree with that, and nodded as he pulled a spare tunic over his head. He twisted uncomfortably in it, the fabric under his arms pulling uncomfortably across his chest and pulling some of the hair in his armpit.

“Did Tilly give me a too-small tunic because she’s still pissed off?” he wondered to himself as he finished dressing himself and decided not to pull his new boots onto his feet. Instead, he finally gave them a chance to air out, leaving them in the corner of the room where their stink wouldn’t bother him.

“No,” Liam said as Ezra walked out of the bathroom. Ezra felt his chest clench at the thought of being discovered by someone, of being seen talking to himself, but the substitute barkeeper continued, “You’ve gained a lot of weight. You were so skinny before that what was a comfortably loose tunic now doesn’t fit across your chest. How much were they feeding you there?”

“Three meals a day, two with meat,” Ezra answered as he looked down at himself.

With Liam bringing his attention back to it, Ezra was forced to confront the reality of just how much his body had changed in those two months since he’d left with the army, and with the month of training under Jormun’s instruction before that. Before, he’d been wiry, even skinny, but as he ate more and more, his body was able to bulk up, his ribs no longer pressing firmly against his skin and his stomach no longer sunken.

“And they’re paying you on top of that?” Liam asked as they walked out towards the main hall of the new Dragon’s Toenail.

“Two large silver every two weeks,” Ezra confirmed.

Liam let out a long, low whistle before asking with a grin, “You think they’re still looking for any more to sign up?”

Ezra didn’t answer, just looked and cocked his eye at the man who’d avoided being conscripted as a levy. Even before Ezra thought to say anything, Liam threw his hands up and laughed.

They left off that conversation and instead followed the smell of a hearty stew. Knowing what was coming, Ezra jogged down the hall towards his destination, where a truly massive bowl lay on a table, waiting for him. As he drew close, the Madam stepped out from around the corner and said, “Your meal.”

He gave his thanks as he turned and sat at the table. The first bite just tasted like home, and Ezra felt a wide smile cross his face as he looked down at the meal. It was simple, fresh bread and a simple soup, the same meal that he’d almost invariably been fed for the past few years. He swallowed and forced out his thanks again as fast as he could before returning to gorging on the meal.

“Slow down,” Tilly snapped as she brought him a cup of cider. “No need to act like somebody’s gonna steal it from you.”

With a conscious effort, Ezra slowed himself and then explained, “Sorry. For the last month, I’ve been eating on the march. Breakfast needs to be eaten fast before we set out for the day, if you don’t wake up early. Then, you barely break for lunch, and you eat dinner fast to make sure you can get some sleep. I was doing all that alongside my training, so… yeah, I’m not used to allowing myself time to relax.”

“Why were you training while you were marching?” Tilly asked, walking around to the other side of the table and taking a seat.

“Um,” Ezra stuttered as he tried to think of what to say. Eventually, he said, “It’s complicated, but I’m basically in Knight training, and there’s a good chance that the Duke is going to relocate me to Brayton sometime pretty soon. So I’m practicing a lot to be able to do good when I’m there.”

“You what?” Tilly demanded. “You’d better not be telling lies.”

“With the Watcher as my witness,” Ezra swore, “I signed on as a member of Count Villy’s elite squad, and then managed to impress a Pseudo-Knight from the Duke’s named Chloe. She seems to like me and wants me to be a part of a better training regimen. So, chances are, the Duke is going to pay for the Count to pass me over to him.”

“If that is truly going to happen,” the Madam said, “then you ought to know that you would say that Chloe is from Duke Brayton’s duchy, or the Duchy of Brayton. I can’t imagine that the people who worked directly for the Duke would appreciate your inability to call their home by the right name.”

“It doesn’t matter if I don’t talk like a damn noble,” Ezra scoffed. “I’m a slum rat. If they sign me on, they’ll know that before I get there.”

“That seems to be a foolish stance to take. However, continue with your story.”

As the Madam finished giving her warning, Liam and Tilly, as well as several of the others, demanded that he tell them about his experience from the very beginning, and that was what he did.

By necessity, he’d needed to tell how he ended up getting recruited as one of the elites, and with nothing else to say, he attempted to explain how he’d drawn on his inner world and made an aura attack. There were plenty of gaps and confusion to be had, but the Madam confirmed that he had done something similar in The Dragon’s Toenail before it had been burned down. With that confirmed and his position as a probably truthful storyteller cemented, he continued his story.

Telling about the discrimination of the inner city recruits, especially William, was met with shouts of how to get back at them, and his story of how he led his small squad of recruits to victory over the others was met with cheers.

When he moved onto the marching, one of the youngest, a girl named Sariah who was the daughter of one of the more senior waitresses, asked, “How long are you going to talk about the walking? It’s no fun.”

“Well,” Ezra teased, scratching at his chin, “I suppose I could talk about it for as long as I did it. Two weeks. How does that sound?”

“Nooooo,” Sariah groaned as she shook his arm. “That’s boooring.”

“Then I’ll just say that I spent the time talking with friends and trying to get stronger. After all, I was going to fight monsters.”

Finally, he needed to take some time to decide just how much detail to go into with retelling the battle, and he settled on being sparse with detail, only mentioning the sacrifice of some lives and fighting hard, making sure to remind everybody that he’d seen Adam alive just a few days ago, long after the battle was over and won.

“After seeing what I did in the fight,” Ezra continued, feeling like his storytelling left much to be desired, “Chloe said that she was going to talk with the people from the duchy”—he shot a glance at the Madam as he said it—“and that’s about it. We marched back, and here I am.”

“So you really fought the monsters?” Sariah asked.

“Yup.”

“Wow. So you’re basically like a hero.”

“I don’t think so,” Ezra said, remembering Helen’s body, Artemis’s crying face, and the dozens and hundreds of bodies that’d fallen and were being brought back to their home. “I’d hope I did something good, but I’m not a hero.”

Sariah’s face screwed up at that, and she started to ask a question before the Madam clapped once. The crisp sound cut through any conversation as it began to brew, and everyone looked at the matron.

“Children, rest,” she commanded. “The night is drawing near. You adults, you may make the decisions you wish, but remember that tomorrow we will begin working in earnest, and if you wish to keep yourselves well rested, you must make decisions accordingly. Everyone has received their assignments, so you’ll know already how much time you need tomorrow to get your work done. Ezra, we are happy to have you back. In the morning, if you wish to stand by your offer, I’ll have a couple favors to ask of you.”

Ezra nodded in agreement, and most of the adults retreated immediately to their rooms, knowing how much they’d need their rest. Tilly stayed close and pestered him for a few more answers, and so he obliged. Otherwise, he was quickly ready to sleep, setting up in a room that was obviously prepared for storage. Even so, there was a mattress laid out for him, and it was warm, so Ezra settled into a sitting position and allowed himself to spend a relaxed time pulling various types of aura into his inner world.

***

The next day was a blur of motion, with the Madam having Ezra use his uniform and willingness to stand up to petty assholes at the wall between the inner city and the slums. As such, he spent the day walking escorting various different loads of materials for The Dragon’s Toenail’s reopening. Each time he led someone through the gate, the guards stopped him, and each time, he shut down their demands for bribes and humility with the same warning of what would happen if they tried to fight or kill him in the streets.

When night was close to falling, the sky burning with the sunset, Ezra had to give his goodbyes.

“Be careful,” the Madam said, her voice a command, and making him think of the last time she’d bidden him goodbye.

“Yes ma’am.”

“And don’t forget to write.”

“Yes ma’am.”

“Good.” Then she walked away, and Ezra was left giving his farewells to the rest, with Matilda giving him a much rougher command to remember.

“If you don’t write, I’m going to find you and kick you. Between the legs. Repeatedly.”

“I’ll write,” he laughed and pulled her into a hug. Her fingers tangled in the back of his uniform coat for a moment, but then she pulled away and they shared a little smile as he walked away.

Ezra rode the high of being able to help his friends the entire path back to the inner city, but that happiness wasn’t to be. At the gate, the same one that he’d been passing through all day, not two guards were waiting for him. But five. And by the smiles on their faces, he wasn’t in for a good time.
