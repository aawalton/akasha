Percy had mentally prepared himself for the demigods’ arrival, even though he couldn’t have predicted the exact timing. The authorities couldn’t have delayed their visit for much longer, as the villagers would have succumbed to the infestation without his interference.

It wouldn’t be the end of the world if the corruption spread to everyone in the settlement because the natives were quite experienced at managing their condition. However, their protocols only mattered during the early stages of the infection. Left unchecked, the black sludge would continue to accumulate beneath the natives’ skin until its negative influence grew too powerful to resist, causing the apes to lose control of their actions and start hurting one another.

Either way, Percy didn’t panic when the pair of powerful mages landed in the middle of the village.

To hide the evidence of his tampering, he willed the enchanted slabs comprising his formation to phase through the dirt and sink even deeper before shattering. The soft phantom mana dissipated entirely, while the particles of crystallized mana were scattered hundreds of metres below the surface.

Even if Percy had left the slabs alone, the demigods wouldn’t have detected them, but there was no point in preserving a bunch of tools that were about to be rendered obsolete. Once the residents were cleansed, it would be several months before the corruption accumulated to its previous level. Percy wasn’t going to waste his time waiting. As much as he wanted to continue training, he’d rather search for another settlement that was currently around the halfway point to its next purification.

‘Should we leave?’ Micky asked. ‘It’ll look strange if the fake merchants vanish right as the demigods are about to cleanse them, but nobody will guess that they were a bunch of alien puppets that had infiltrated the village to secretly purify everyone.’

Percy considered his friend’s suggestion before ultimately shaking his borrowed head. ‘I want to see if our sentries are convincing enough to trick them.’

One might argue that Percy was being reckless again, but he honestly felt that the exact opposite was true. He wanted to evaluate the fidelity of his constructs precisely to determine whether they were reliable enough to infiltrate a powerful faction.

In theory, the sentries could mimic the appearance, behaviour, mana networks, and even the souls of the natives, so they were very likely to survive the demigods’ scrutiny. And it made more sense to test the constructs on a lesser spring, where the repercussions for being discovered would be negligible.

Furthermore, Percy wanted to study how the locals dealt with the corruption. While the people of Mapel weren’t necessarily the best at pushing the infestation back, it wouldn’t hurt to cross-reference his methods with theirs.

Having made his mind up, Percy pretended to ignore the commotion. He had his sentries continue to sell elixirs until they got visited or summoned by the demigods, acting as if this was just another day. At the same time, he carefully scanned the powerful apes with his sensory domain, unwilling to miss even a single second.

Unlike the commoners, the Clear mages were quite well off. Every single one of their tails was adorned with golden rings, and the appendages themselves were wrapped around the demigods’ torsos in an elegant spiralling pattern. The cyan-furred life mage and the lime-furred light mage both sported the same symbol on their chest, indicating that it was the insignia of Mapel’s Divine Order.

“Alright everyone, you know the drill!” the life mage said while infusing his willpower into his voice. “Start gathering at the village square. We don’t need everyone to be here at the same time, but make sure that there are always at least thirty people queued up outside the formation. We have to wrap this up by nightfall, as there’s another place that needs cleansing tomorrow.”

His partner added, “You probably don’t need to be reminded, but evading purification without a good reason is punishable by up to fifty years in prison. If anyone is injured or ill, have someone carry them here and they’ll even get healed for free. If anyone is missing, they’ll have to travel to another settlement to get cleansed within forty days, and they must fill in a form explaining the reason for missing the original cleansing.”

While the second demigod was busy scaring the poor villagers stiff, the first had removed a dimensional marble ring from a tail wrapped around his neck and poured a wisp of mana to activate its enchantments. The ring didn’t seem to be a storage device exactly, but rather some kind of shrunken artifact.

Tossing the ring on the ground, the Clear mage watched with a bored expression as it expanded by hundreds of times to form a circular platform large enough to fit a few dozen people.

‘Interesting, if somewhat primitive,’ Percy thought.

He knew that dimensional marble was an excellent conduit for space mana that could be fashioned into a shrinking storage device like the amulets they had on Remior, but the people of Mapel seemed to have gone even further than that. Instead of using the material as a container for their purification formation, they had carved the second set of enchantments directly into the dimensional marble as well.

It made for a cool visual but was terribly inefficient. The material wasn’t as compatible with other mana types, and such extreme resizing had probably come at the cost of weakening the purification enchantment.

‘Well… they probably don’t want their cleansing component to be too powerful, for the same reason as us,’ Micky pointed out.

As soon as the first group of apes stepped into the ring, both demigods poured even more mana into the enchantments, causing the symbols to alternate between a vibrant green and a pale blue colour.

The natives winced as the light continuously singed their hair and made their skin blister, though the potent life mana kept mending their injuries as soon as they appeared.

The demigod’s healing didn’t regrow the burnt fur, nor was its minty scent potent enough to overpower the acrid stench permeating the village square. The balding apes would likely have to suffer their embarrassing appearance for a couple of weeks until their hair grew back, though they could take solace in the fact that their neighbours would be joining them soon, as well as the fact that their privates were still concealed by their tails.

Wisps of black smoke escaped from the pores and orifices of around a fifth of the villagers as the dark sludge inside their souls visibly evaporated. Judging by the awed expressions of the children in the crowd, everyone could perceive the scattered traces of corruption even without Percy’s ocular mutation. This was a consequence of removing the dark mass too quickly, though Percy’s formation had thankfully worked slower than that.

The Clear mages still didn’t seem very pleased with the rate by which their patients were being cleansed, however.

The light user removed a dimensional marble ring of his own from one of his tails and dangled it in front of the low-borns. “This is a storage device large enough to fit about as much stuff as an Orange adult can carry. If we finish up before the sun sets, I’ll consider gifting it to one of you at random.”

Percy was pretty sure that the demigod was lying, since it made no sense to hand out such a precious artifact at every village he visited. The apes probably understood that too, but it didn’t matter, because the man’s goal was different. The mere thought of receiving such a valuable device for free was enough to incite the villagers’ greed, causing their corruption to flare up and expose itself to the formation.

It only took about half an hour for the last person to be cleansed, after which the demigods unceremoniously kicked the first batch out to begin working on the second.

‘Colour me unimpressed,’ Micky said.

Percy nodded.

The demigods were obviously using a method very similar to his own. Their approach could even be considered inferior in some ways yet was arguably better in others. For example, the runework on the ring-shaped formation was a joke compared to the Vault’s magiscript, though Percy did spot a couple of interesting tricks that he wouldn’t mind stealing. While he wasn’t familiar with Mapel’s runecrafting language, he could tell that the locals had incorporated a variant of stability rune into their heating enchantments to keep the temperature constant, thus reducing the damage to the patients.

The Clear mages didn’t seem to have anything on par with Ningirima’s Mercy or Percy’s Cradles to augment their mana and willpower, but having someone with a life affinity to constantly heal the villagers gave them more leeway. Funnily enough, these people seemed a lot more open to suffering some physical pain than Percy had imagined, though he supposed that it made sense for them to make some concessions given the state of their planet.

The natives’ greatest advantage, however, was arguably the light user’s blessing. It was the same that Falanor – the Ollorian who had excelled in the special event of the tournament – possessed, and it was clearly potent enough to rival the artificial world’s superior runes and Ningirima’s Mercy combined.

Percy couldn’t help but feel a little envious of the demigod’s augmented mana. Unfortunately, he, Micky, and Nephthys wouldn’t be able to absorb a light blessing without the corresponding core, though the lightning affinity also possessed an auxiliary concept that was supposedly good at cleansing corruption according to Zoris.

It was probably the blessing that Metatron had previously mentioned, and Percy suspected that one of the Void Hand’s seven Rings of Sacrilege could grant it. After seeing firsthand how powerful these concepts were, he was tempted to get one for Nephthys.

‘I should keep that in mind the next time I infiltrate the alliance.’

In any case, there clearly wasn’t much he could learn from the demigods. Percy could tweak his enchantments slightly, and he could try combining his purification beam with a potent form of healing in the future, but that was about it. More developed factions had an easier time dealing with infestations than the people of Mapel, so there might be better techniques out there, though Percy was stuck with his current approach for the time being.

Satisfied by his analysis – if somewhat disappointed by the results – Percy patiently waited until the demigods had cleansed the majority of the villagers.

Strangely, the Clear mages didn’t have the ability to directly detect corruption, making Percy realize that his Saint’s Eye had provided him with yet another unique advantage. The demigods could only infer whether their patients were clean by watching whether any dark wisps escaped from their bodies while being subjected to the pale blue light of the formation.

Everyone seemed somewhat surprised that only twenty percent of the population had been infected after so many months. The people who had already been diagnosed with the corruption were the most baffled, though nobody could offer a good explanation for how they had been cured, so they could only chalk it up to luck.

About an hour before sunset, Percy finally moved his sentries to the back of the queue, eager to test his spells against the demigods’ senses. The Clear mages didn’t seem to have detected any foul play, yet Percy couldn’t help but tense up as his constructs stepped into the formation.

He and Micky had arguably done a decent job hiding the true nature of their minions for the last few months, but whether they could emulate the constant destruction and regeneration that was supposed to occur under the influence of the enchantment remained to be seen.
