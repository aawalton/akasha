After Gilbert left him alone in one of the library’s private reading rooms, Ferris immediately opened the primer on acid cores. He probably should have started with the mundane books, but he couldn’t help himself.

Many of the abilities in the book focused on either materializing acid in the real world, shooting it at an enemy, or both. The best example was an ability called Acid Bolt that shoots a ball of acid out of a hand. There was usually a type of ‘Bolt’ for every aspect. Ferris had seen delvers back home use Water Bolt, and the fire primer had strongly recommended Fire Bolt to new delvers once they had Fire Resistance.

Although they may be common, not all bolt abilities were equal. Water could be very dangerous at high speeds, but Water Bolt was slower than a thrown rock. Ferris felt that Acid Bolt would be a strong choice, although it probably wouldn’t work well underwater.

The best abilities apparently required more resources, support, and complexity in the soulspace, so there were many suggested alternatives. Sometimes it was better to accept a limitation that you could afford.

One such ability, called Acid Spout, caught Ferris’ eye. It materialized a flow of acid, but only at a trickle. The summary suggested collecting it in glass vials mid-battle to toss at enemies. Depending on the magical strength of the acid, it could eat through the glass, but slowly enough it could be thrown immediately. Jade was made of acid, could Ferris use the same idea without the ability?

Some abilities were more exotic, like one that turned the cultivator’s blood acidic. It stated that attempting this before acquiring Acid Resistance would be fatal.

Reading this, Ferris immediately skipped ahead to find Acid Resistance. He considered this ability as vital for him as Fire Resistance for a fire core cultivator. Not only would it let him swim in the slimespace, but it would also allow him to touch Jade’s core in the real world. Touching the core was a requirement to use aspected abilities like Acid Bolt.

The ability’s summary was brief, providing sparse details beyond that it would increase resistance to corrosion. At first the ability’s effect was limited, but as it was developed and strengthened it would resist stronger magical acids. This was a common theme in offensive and defensive abilities: there was no immunity, simply an escalation of comparative strength. If two abilities were equal in strength, the defense usually had the advantage. However, ‘equal strength’ rarely happened in the real world. Even a ‘weaker’ attack could overpower the resistance if the battle conditions were right.

Acid Resistance had a note that it required a human body. Ferris assumed this was mostly to inform those trying to develop abilities in their bonded weapon, but it also let him know what to avoid for Jade. All abilities that worked with or changed the cultivator’s body had a similar requirement, while those that projected something out like Acid Bolt didn’t. He wondered if there was a separate book for things like ‘Acid Resistance that could work on a sword.’

Reading through more entries, Ferris recognized another pattern: the advantage of specialization and restrictions. Generic abilities were either drastically more costly or less powerful than specialized ones. There was an Acid Resistance Gloves ability that only protected the hands; it had a note that it was a cheaper alternative to Acid Resistance.

The Acid Reconstruction healing ability… was not in the book. Clan Hollowfield knew it existed from the slime journal observing Janice and Gumly, but they didn’t have a method to recreate it. It was a good reminder that this book only listed abilities the clan knew how to acquire.

The book also neglected to give details on weapon pairings, or abilities specific to a weapon. The books Elder Harper had provided had an additional pamphlet near the end with those details, but that was missing here. The more detailed version may only be available to clan members, or perhaps restricted to cultivators that hadn’t yet chosen a bond.

Ferris diligently reviewed every ability in the book. A couple of them challenged his conception of what acid could do: like an ability that created a very slippery liquid, or one that created a corrosive gas—although the stranger ones were noted to be more difficult to learn.

Several were focused on making selectively corrosive acid. There was a spray attack that was more effective at softening armor, and a variation of Acid Bolt more effective at eating away at flesh.

Finished with the book, Ferris looked over at Jade as he processed the information. The slime was contently rolling around the floor, happy to repeat the same path over and over again.

Elder Harper had mentioned that Hollowfield had several martial traditions using acid cores, and the primer reflected that. It had many combat-focused attacks designed for both ranged and melee fighters, plus some utility and defensive abilities.

Unfortunately, he couldn’t have it all. The ‘at least a month’ time estimate Uncle Winton gave him to get a new ability functional was if he already had a seed. Everything in the book had additional requirements and magical resources that needed to be purchased in addition to the manual itself.

Ferris’ first priority was pretty clear. He wanted Acid Resistance as soon as possible. It would be worth the merit points, but he only had nine of the required twelve. And that was only for the manual, the book was clear he would need to buy additional resources to get it working.

Gilbert had recommended he purchase the two technical manuals first: Spiritual Harmony and Mental Materials. Saving up twelve additional merit for those two would take another four weeks of work. Ferris wasn’t sure about that, but would keep the man’s advice in mind.

More than anything, he wanted to focus on Acid Reconstruction. Ferris needed to wait for Elder Harper’s help to know how to proceed, but he couldn’t imagine the healing ability would be useable without Acid Resistance. How could acid heal him, if his flesh was melting away?

In fact, Ferris wondered if Elder Harper would provide the Acid Resistance manual as part of the research project. It was at least worth asking. She’d been generous so far with information related to her research.

After that, the most desirable ability was Acid Bolt. Ferris could imagine this being the basis for his delving strategy. Rather than risk Jade rolling up to attack directly, Ferris could have his familiar sit on his shoulder while he threw balls of acid at the monsters. Acid Bolt was an aspected ability, which meant Ferris needed to touch Jade’s core directly, but that would be fine with Acid Resistance.

This was the benefit Elder Harper had mentioned, where their library of acid aspected abilities would help him. Rather than sitting around waiting on random chance for months or years, here was directly what he needed in an attack ability. It wasn’t quite as nice as having a martial tradition, but it helped address some of the same problems.

This felt like a better potential strategy than Grow, although it had its own downsides. Acid Bolt was listed as requiring expensive materials. Worst, it required a meridian, which Ferris didn’t even know how to open. It also wouldn’t do well underwater, which was his long-term goal.

Although… why was Ferris considering it so much ‘better’ to keep Jade out of danger?

Closing his eyes, Ferris focused on his breathing. My mind is my own, he thought to himself, I refuse to be manipulated by this stupid bond. Jade is a slime. They can get up close to a fight.

The hard part was, he liked the idea of using superior range to win. It was objectively good to sit back and dump acid on your enemies from far away. He wasn’t afraid of a fight, but he didn’t want to rush in like an idiot for no reason.

But that wasn’t what had motivated his thoughts. That was just the logic he was hiding behind.

No, what he’d been truly thinking was: I don’t want to risk Jade. Exactly what the book had warned him about.

Forcefully, Ferris went back to review the book. Acid Bolt was clearly better at what it did than other similar abilities, but some provided valid alternatives. If they were strictly worse in all ways, Hollowfield wouldn’t have included them. No, each of the ‘worse’ abilities simply had a tradeoff, usually sacrificing range or flexibility for resources.

Unfortunately, the tradeoff Ferris really wanted didn’t exist. No matter which acid aspected attack ability he looked at, they all required an open meridian. It made sense. How else were they supposed to hurt the enemy, if they couldn’t manifest acid?

It also made him pessimistic about randomly finding a good attack ability seed from the Core Tree. It would only provide abilities that his soul could support. If Ferris wanted it to give him an ability like these, he needed an open meridian.

Well, Ferris thought, this is why I have a mentor. Uncle Winton should visit in about a week, I can ask him then.

For now, he would keep Acid Bolt and the other variations in mind. Before committing to anything, he would wait and see the next several fruit from the Core Tree. Best of all, once he figured out the slime’s Adaptation ability, maybe he could acquire some of these abilities just from the description.

Setting aside the primer on acid cores, Ferris picked up A Study of Acid. The book was hard to understand. It clearly expected the reader to have a foundation of knowledge that he lacked. What he was able to comprehend showed he had some basic misconceptions about how acid worked.

Fascinatingly, the book described what it called a chemical reaction. He’d assumed acid worked by pulling things apart, but instead it was this reaction that somehow changed solid matter into liquid and gas. Certain materials resisted acid not by being stronger or harder, but by failing to cause the chemical reaction.

While much was unknown or hard to comprehend, Ferris thought he better understood how acid worked and what influenced its strength. After watching Jade eat, Ferris had thought acid separated solids into liquids. Instead, it was all about bonding. For example, when dissolving a metal bar, the acidic solution would join together with tiny parts of it. After this bonding, the natural consequence would be that the pieces of metal were no longer part of their previous whole. Falling apart was more of a side effect than the actual reaction. As the acid joined with the metal, there would be less left to continue bonding. Thus, strong acids had more stuff able to bond with other things.

The book focused on mundane acid, but it often described concepts in comparison to magical acids. Magically enhanced acids worked by forcefully triggering this chemical reaction stronger than normal, and even with materials that normally did not react. Additionally, a magical ‘acid’ usually included other ways to forcefully dissolve solid matter, which the book claimed was a different mundane effect entirely. Some of the ‘acid aspected abilities’ Ferris had just read about wouldn’t be considered mundane acid at all, according to this book. For example, vibrations and heat could often aid the chemical reaction, which some acid abilities took advantage of.

The book claimed these so-called chemical reactions were a cornerstone of study for magical alchemy. Ferris would need to study some basic alchemy books to learn more, or find someone to teach him. He remembered walking through the fifth district, and the smell of brewing potions that permeated the area. Hollowfield definitely had a community of alchemists.

Speaking of smells, there were warnings about the gases released by the chemical reactions, claiming they could be hazardous to breathe. When Jade dissolved food, he’d never seen any gas released. He would have to watch out for this in the future.

Lastly, Ferris read through Material Tests Logs for Corrosivity and Corrosiveness. Rather than a typical book, this was a set of experimentation results. The author had collected as many different types of mundane acid as they could, gathered a wide variety of solid materials, and then recorded how long it took for the acids to break down each material type.

Ferris wasn’t sure what to do with the knowledge, but he was impressed with the methodical approach. He’d never seen anything like it before. Growing up he’d had the basic education provided to all clan members of Bluereef, but these two books showed a much different mindset. They focused on discovery, rationalization, and explorative testing rather than facts to memorize.

Grabbing a chalkboard, Ferris attempted to see what conclusions he could reach based on the book’s data.

The first thing he noticed was that there was no ‘best’ acid. Some were more or less effective than others at corroding specific materials. Some were faster or slower, and each stopped working after different amounts of time. Ferris thought about one of the abilities from the acid core primer. It had noted that it was specialized at melting flesh but wouldn’t work against armor. He’d assumed that was due to a magical focus, but maybe it was based on a particular type of acid that naturally reacted with flesh…

From the times listed in the chart, magical acid was staggeringly different than the mundane variety. Ferris had always heard people talk about ‘magical fire’ or ‘magical water’ as inherently different, but no one explained how. Magical acid appeared to be dramatically faster and more dangerous.

Ferris was excited to see that one of the compounds tested was ‘slime fertilizer.’ This was the product the slime ranch produced, used to quickly fertilize the farms for rapid magical growth inside the bastion. The book only tested what was considered non-magical acids, so he was slightly surprised to see the slime fertilizer included. Jade’s acidic body was clearly magical in strength, how could the fertilizer be mundane?

Apparently, the slime fertilizer was one of the weaker acids. It was stronger than the citrus or fermented vinegars, yet weaker than both stomach acid and most of the alchemical acids tested.

Ferris looked over the data for how various materials resisted corrosion. The book tested several different types of metals, rocks, glass, wood, and organic matter. He wasn’t sure what to do with it, but perhaps it would help once he learned more about the Acid Resistance ability.

Finished reading, Ferris collected Jade and left the library. He returned the books to an assistant waiting at a table near the room, but didn’t see Gilbert again.

Ferris and Jade ate lunch on a bench outside. Mother Lily had packed them a half dozen sandwiches, of which Ferris only needed one. Jade was thrilled and declared ‘lunch’ the greatest thing since ‘breakfast.’

The roasted bird meat was thinly sliced, then layered with lettuce and vegetables between a surprisingly fluffy bread. He enjoyed the sandwich, although it was a bit heavy on the garlic sauce. At least it wasn’t bland. None of Mother Lily’s cooking was. It was just… strange.

He took the opportunity to go over his safety commands with Jade, then practiced some basic communication. He wanted to err on the side of caution with the slime’s memory. He also tested if Jade could remember the first name from that morning, which they passed. No surprise there, as it had only been a few hours.

He was careful to slowly make sure Jade understood everything, but he couldn’t help being excited as they finished.

Slime in hand, Ferris headed off for the first meeting of the research project that would regrow his leg.
