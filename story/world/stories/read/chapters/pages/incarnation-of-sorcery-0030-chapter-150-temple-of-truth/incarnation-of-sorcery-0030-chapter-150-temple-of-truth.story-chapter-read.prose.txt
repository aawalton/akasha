Word was out. Whatever secrecy the institute had been keeping was now broken. Students were missing and those with power had been asked to join the hunt. To find the missing students and report back once they had been secured.

Ophelia wasn’t obligated to join in. She was here as part of the Arcoryx delegation, whose safety was the only thing she needed to worry about. None of the Arcoryx Academy students were missing so she didn’t need to concern herself with the current mess. At all. But Ophelia Brigmont wasn’t the sort of person to stand back in situations like these.

Students were missing. Potentially in danger. Just because they weren’t from her home world didn’t mean she could just sit back and watch.

Ophelia had power. She had trained for decades to become a Titan-ranked Heartshield Knight. It was now time to embody the ideals she had sworn to herself when joining her Conclave.

She just hadn’t expected things to bring her here. To a temple dedicated to Ryland.

To be fair, the students hadn’t been located yet. Even Ryland had failed to find them. But this temple presented a powerful clue, so it had to be investigated.

Even now, she couldn’t believe what she had read on the stelae. Ryland had created this whole world. From scratch. She was walking on something he had brought into being from nothing with the power of his impossible magic.

It wasn’t impossible. Ophelia was well aware that, even in that cataclysmic battle against the newborn Calamity, Ryland hadn’t revealed everything he was capable of. She had yet to see the pinnacle of his magic, had yet to witness the inconceivable heights that he could ascend to. Cursed Calamities, she might simply be incapable of witnessing them in her current state.

For if a man could create a whole world from scratch… then he wasn’t really a man.

Not anymore.

Of course, the stelae didn’t state Ryland’s name. It didn’t mention the description of him that she was familiar with.

All it talked about was the revered figure known as the Recreator. This near-mythical entity who had created a home for all those who had been displaced from the Realmbreaker Wars, for all the lost and fearful and distraught. It was from those early seeds that Rhel had grown into the mighty civilization that it was now.

But the signs were present from the very beginning that it was Ryland. Rifts were depicted on the stelae liberally. Phrases like “world-tearing magic”, “spear formed from the spine of a draconic god”, “fractured reality”, and the like ran amok in every other phrase extolling this Recreator’s virtues.

One could argue that there might have been others capable of such magic. Ryland had to have learned it from somewhere, right?

Yet the combination of very specific capabilities had evolved her suspicions to near-certainty. Unbreakable barriers, remoulding the world through the manipulation of energy, rewinding time itself. Just a small selection of powers the stelae talked about effusively.

A small selection of powers from Ryland that Ophelia herself had already witnessed.

The amulet with the Inscribed Glyphs of Questing and Communication shone as the Glyphs themselves lit up. “I have searched the eastern quarter. No sign of the students. Maintaining a lower presence and continuing search.”

Other voices echoed in, stating more or less the same thing. No one had yet found any sign of the missing students. Ophelia said the same when there was an opening.

They had been trying for almost an hour now. Magical questing wasn’t revealing much. Her Soul Glyph of Vanguard was great at protecting people but finding them was a different matter entirely. She hadn’t gotten around to discovering and using spells that could locate those she was seeking.

The problem was that no one else had. Surely, the institute had sent in those capable of seeking out others. So what was blocking them all?

Were the children not here to begin with? Had they received faulty information or were being tricked altogether? Ophelia was unable to say. Her job was to exhaustively search every fingerbreadth of space in and around this dilapidated temple, regardless of how successful the endeavour was.

A part of her wanted to ask where Ryland was. What was he doing at the moment? Why was he not here with the rest of them? Had he already determined that their search here was for naught?

Ophelia refused to believe that and doggedly continued her search.

They had to report their findings before long. Another round of the Glyphs lighting up and everyone exchanging messages passed. Except, there were fewer messages before Ophelia’s turn came up.

She frowned once she was done speaking. Maybe she was wrong. After all, she wasn’t exactly keeping count. With her current distractions, it was possible she was just overreacting.

But she wasn’t alone in suspecting that there had been fewer messages.

The High Academ from the Rhellian Institute leading their little search party came up with a solution in no time. “We’re all going to check in with our names and affiliations. I will begin. This is High Academ Zevo Volker from the Rhellian Institute.”

Others went afterwards. Ophelia had come to a stop, about thirty or so handspans away from the entrance to the old, broken-down temple. She stated her name and designation when it was her turn. What followed was a hard silence. She was right. There were indeed party members who were choosing against communicating.

Or were being prevented from doing so.

“I am initiating immediate cautionary procedures,” the High Academ said, his voice tight with barely-restrained alarm. “Proceed with utmost caution. Ensure all your magic is active. And issue an immediate warning if you see or sense anything.”

Ophelia’s manasoul started sizzling stronger and brighter. “[Saviour’s Bulwark]. [Relentless Sentinel].”

Golden mana burned out of her and clad her in defensive magic as her spell circles turned the entire area brighter than daylight. Now, Ophelia was ready to face anything. Whatever was going on here, she’d be ready to face it.

She approached the temple as fast as she could, but with the caution that the High Academ had suggested. Her shield and her broadsword were in her hands.

At the very first step that Ophelia took inside the temple, a Glyph went off.

An Inscription. A powerful one.

She cursed. Her whole body was frozen stiff, petrified like she had just faced off against a basilisk and had received an eyeful of its glare. It wasn’t freezing her vitals, of course. Her breaths were coming in much faster, and her heart was pitter-pattering in her chest with increasing frequency. What was going on? Why couldn’t she move?

Ophelia forced her training to reassert itself. This wasn’t anything unprecedented. She had handled this and worse before. Right now, she had to counter the spell that had caught her.

That’s when the drain started.

Ophelia gasped. All the mana keeping her spells active, all the golden, burning sorcery sizzling out of her manasoul, was suddenly heavy. The pressure was such that her very body was starting to bow down towards the earth.

All the magic was exiting her body like weight made liquid, sinking into the ground. The Inscription keeping her trapped grew even more powerful. What was going on?

Who was draining her strength and power like this?

Of course, with all her mana being redirected in that fashion, she was unable to send a request for help through the amulet with the Glyph of Communication. Something told her that had she tried to do it earlier, right when she had gotten caught, the drain would have simply activated earlier and prevented her from communicating.

After all, the others had been caught in a similar fashion, and there hadn’t been any warning messages or anything of the sort.

Distantly, she wondered if it could actually hold everyone? How strong was this Inscription supposed to be? It had successfully trapped a Titan-ranked Mage Knight like Ophelia. Would it lock down Primordial-ranked mages too? Even Empyreans?

She found that terribly difficult to believe. But then, she had seen the scale of the opposition that she might have faced in the Crypts. It had taken Ryland’s intervention to defeat the thing. Rumours had it that the enemy he had faced had been nearing the strength of a Calamity.

So, if they were facing similar opposition here, then of course they were all in terribly grave danger.

Ophelia’s senses weren’t impaired, which was why she was able to hear the arrival of loud, clanking steps. A Rhellian Academ hurried in, eyes widening at the sight of Ophelia being forced to kneel on the earth. She recognized him. That was High Academ Zevo leading the party.

She tried to speak up, to warn him that something was terribly wrong. Getting any closer could be a fatal mistake.

But her mouth wasn’t working. It was all she could do to continue breathing, to have her heart maintain its rhythm of beats no matter how discordant and panicked it was. Warning the High Academ was out of the question.

No surprise, then, that he got caught in the spell as well.

Even after the precautions he had taken. He hadn’t blindly charged at her, of course. This was a seasoned mage, one whose hackles had risen once he had seen Ophelia’s predicament. So, he had immediately started casting spells that might have potentially helped the situation.

It was at that exact moment that the trapping Incantation had activated, immediately hobbling him and pulling him to the earth as well.

“What… is… this…?” he ground out.

Well, at least he was capable of talking, which Ophelia couldn’t. So he was likely a bit stronger than her.

Laughter converged on their location after that. Riotous laughter. The sound was horrid. Ophelia’s eardrums felt like sails about to rip in a storm. Her heart was stuttering so hard in her chest, it felt like it was going to seize and stop beating any moment now.

“Well, well, well,” a man said. “It seems all the flies have been caught in the web.”

Ophelia’s face was being dragged to the ground just like the rest of her, but she still forced herself to look. To memorize the visage of the bastard responsible for all this.

He was a short, nondescript, and dirty fellow, garbed in a simple tunic smudged with dust. His hands were caked with dirt, as were his boots, like a gardener’s tended to be. He even had the audacity of continuing to carry a hoe, as though he really had been busy tending to the wild plants she had seen all over the grounds before they were interrupted.

“What… is….?”

High Academ Zevo couldn’t get his words out before he was forced into silence by the pressure of the spell.

The man tutted and stepped forward. “You all did so well to finally come here. Took you far too long, though. Perhaps I’m to blame for that, however. I’ve never been very good at planting seeds, you see. I’m only good for reaping them.”

He laughed again.

Ophelia was forced to shut him out for the time being. All the focus was now on just surviving her current situation. The drain was getting to be too much. Her manasoul was drying up, and she was a Titan-ranked Mage Knight. How much more power was this fiend getting from the High Academ?

The man was speaking again, gloating as far as Ophelia was concerned, but then a sliver of light landed amidst them.

With it came another man. One with intense mana steaming off his radiant form.

“Ah, the star of the show!” the strange gardener said. “I was starting to doubt you’d come, but you must have grown very concerned about the situation, haven’t you?”

“I didn’t come here to hear your babbling, mongrel,” the man said roughly. Somehow, he was still standing, despite the visible drain of mana drawing his power into the ground just like the rest of them. “You will fall to my blade. Just as you should have ages ago.”

“Ha! You really believe you can kill me, Guardian Vinum? When your superior himself is too afraid to drop in for a visit?”

Guardian. Ophelia’s panicking heart started thumping even harder.

She knew that term. In the context of Rhel, Guardians were the highest echelon of mages present on the planet, second only to the Demiurge himself. They weren’t necessarily the most politically powerful, for the High Adjutants held that claim. But in terms of pure magical prowess, they were entirely unmatched.

Ophelia wondered which exact Guardian this was. Vinum. The name wasn’t ringing any bells. But then, Ophelia didn’t really know any of the Guardians by name.

All she was aware of was that they had an internal hierarchy that determined their standing according to their power. The Guardians weren’t all at the same level of strength. It was just that their baseline was ridiculously strong, where none were below Primordial-ranked.

With every word the strange man spoke, his strength doubled, his voice resonating out farther and farther as his figure began glowing. He was turning inconceivably powerful. Just as Ophelia had feared, all this drain from the Inscription far underground was simply stealing their strength and gifting it to that dirty bastard.

“Fool,” the Guardian said. His voice was a bit more strained now, but Ophelia saw that he was still upright. Still shining with bright, brilliant, incandescent magic. How strong was he? “[Ever Bright Hail]!”

The spell rained down from the heavens. Spears of light crashed upon them, like sunlight turned into the attack of an irrepressible army. Ophelia couldn’t raise her head anywhere near high enough to spot what exactly was going on in the sky, but the way the spell began shattering around them, she was certain the sight would have been mesmerizing.

And yet, despite the awestriking power this Guardian was displaying, the strange man made short work of it.

“[Saviour’s Bulwark],” he incanted.

Ophelia’s heart threatened to burst out of her chest. Her lungs almost collapsed in shock. He was using her spell.

“How…?” she managed to grind out.

Despite the difficulty, she managed to tilt her head just enough to confirm it. That spell circle. Ophelia knew it. How could she not? It was as intimately familiar as any magic she had ever cast. A bulwark of golden magic rose over them all, stopping the shower of sunlit meteors with perfect ease.

Even the Guardian was shocked. He staggered back at just how easily his spell had been repelled. “Impossible. That was Tier [25]!”

The strange man laughed. “Anything more you’ve got to show me?” The Guardian was now trembling due to the strain of resisting the draining Inscription. Before he could utter another incantation, the man slashed his hand through thin air. “Actually, I think I’ve had enough. [Eternal Grave]!”

Around the shining armoured mage, the ground cracked and magic erupted out. Having already placed all his focus on resisting the unstoppable Inscription from beneath the ground, it seemed even someone as strong as a Guardian of Rhel couldn’t resist the man’s spell.

It crackled in with frightful power, caging him in, holding even a Primordial-ranked mage in check. Maybe even Empyrean-ranked.

The thought made Ophelia’s panic climb even higher. Why? Why did she keep landing herself in situations where she ended up so powerless? Why did her opponents always turn out to be so much stronger than her, where being subdued and defeated was only par for the course? Why, when she had taken pains to grow so much stronger, was she always so outclassed?

The man was still laughing, the noise pounding like the thunder of a storm, every boom of his voice shocking Ophelia from her train of thoughts. Or maybe it was the magic withering her manasoul, dragging out the vital essence of mana that kept her power flowing.

“We… mustn’t…”

The High Academ was still managing to speak somehow, but Ophelia could barely catch what he said. There was too much going on. Things were turning into a pandemonium she could barely withstand.

Which was when the rent decided to open up in the middle of it all.

Ryland stepped out.

With so much else that Ophelia was struggling with at that moment, there was only one thing that could rise above the flood of emotions stifling her mind. Relief. A surge of unassailable relief. For if there was one person who could withstand this madness, then it surely had to be the mage who had torn apart a newborn Calamity itself.

There was a powerful part of her decrying the sheer intensity of her relief. Wasn’t she supposed to be angry at herself for being so inadequate in the face of her opposition all the time?

But such feelings were muted utterly. For the strange man and Ryland were facing off.

The evildoer, the source of all this madness, had been stupefied into silence as Ryland had appeared. Maybe he was wondering just why in the world the Inscriptions weren’t dragging down Ryland as it was doing to everyone else.

Then he laughed as he had been doing all this while.

“Excellent,” he said with a mad light. “Excellent. This is a dream come true!”

Ophelia tried to get out a warning about the man’s power. That draining someone like Ryland could make this mad mage into a living monster. But her throat was too clogged up. Her mind was burning.

“Oh, where are my manners,” the man said. “Recreator! I beg that you leave. Otherwise, the Inscriptions will suck out your very soul. You wouldn’t want that, would you?”

Ryland remained entirely unimpressed. “My soul, is it? Alright, go on then. Have your fill. But know that no matter what you do, your fate is forfeit. For now, you face me.”
