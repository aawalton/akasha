Somehow, the hectic, pain-in-the-ass fight with the tiger ended up with Henry and Maurice in a dark, dank network of caverns. They had followed it in after it tried to run, and boy could it run. The tiger could chain Blink teleports like they cost it nothing, and it could shake off their attacks by turning intangible. After having failed in its ambush, Henry had felt it hesitate, but it still committed to attacking them, likely because of their levels. But when it realized it was losing, it ran.

Well. It tried to. But Henry wasn’t having any of that. He’d made his decision to push for A-rank himself, and he wasn’t letting the damn tiger slink away after it tried to stab its massive canines into the back of his neck. Which… wouldn’t have done much, considering his real body was a bit lower, hidden in the chest of the human-suit. But still, it had tried to kill him, and that made it fair game. So having already suspected the kind of crap the tiger could pull, he had one of his Octominds go tiny and invisible, then latched on to the feline right between its shoulder-blades.

With one of his Apostles stuck to it, there wasn’t much it could do to escape and luckily enough, the Octomind was brought along for the ride whenever it Blinked or phased away, and for Henry to follow, he just needed the latter to discreetely drop off one of the hundreds of stored clones he had stashed in his Maw. Then he’d switch with that one, wail on the cat while Maurice peppered it with bubbles, then do it all over again when it’d flee deeper into the cave network.

Now, the feline lay dead at their feet, its precious-looking fur pocked with cuts, discolored and oozing patches where Toxic Ray had landed, and torn bits where Maurice’s bubbles had hit it.

“It’s kinda small, isn’t it,” said Maurice after a second, still breathing hard from the effort. None of them looked away from the corpse, just in case it zipped away, though that should be impossible considering they’d gotten the death notification.

1x [Stalker of The Folded Wilds (A) - Lvl 28] greater core fragment was collected.

“Compared to the stuff we usually face, sure,” Henry said, eyeing his notification. “But it’s at least twice as large as any tiger I’ve ever seen,” he muttered. The two were quiet for a moment, not because this had been a particularly difficult fight—and it certainly had, as Henry was still flushing out the Aspect-infused claw marks out of his neck and back—but because of what this win meant.

Henry was close to A-rank. He’d mulled over pushing his Aspect first for a bit, but then with all the trouble they were dealing with, the levels would come in handy. Not that he needed them as much. Still, one question in particular hovered in the back of his mind, unanswered.

Was A-rank going to be a simple transition for him, considering the whole B+ rank he’d achieved by evolving with fused Aspects, or was this going to be a whole thing? Should he look into a safer spot, or could he just get it over with right now?

“So. You’re ready?” Maurice asked, gingerly poking the tiger with an arcane claw before he looked up. “Think this will take you over the edge?”

Henry wasn’t sure. But just in case it did, he had to make sure their spot was defensible. “Maybe. I’m level 95 now. This could be it. But let’s scout the area a little first.”

And so they did, stashing the tiger first. Henry hadn’t yet gotten the chance of getting a bite from it as he’d mostly used his arms to slam and cut along with Toxic Ray, but a nibble could wait until later. With the dark unable to hinder them, they followed the tunnels down, leaving an Avatar every so often, but soon, Henry found himself frowning more and more. Not because of how many Avatars he was dropping, as by now he could field upwards of sixteen-hundred clones, which was close to triple what he could field at the same level before Unfettered Echoes, but because he could feel how their range was progressively deteriorating faster and faster. His Arcane Avatars should have had perfect connection for up to six miles or so, but he could sense the deterioration starting to happen at only eight hundred meters or so, and while Henry couldn’t see into the rock and had no sense to divine its composition, he could sense the air change around them, growing more and more charged with mana while glowing blue lichen became more common.

As for the tunnels themselves, at first he thought it was the work of some big burrowing monster, but with every hour they walked down the maze-like underground expanse, it became clear that this wasn’t the work of just one being. Some of it was natural, which might have once been water reservoir or such. Some were carved in perfect circles. Some were a bit taller than they were wide, and some could have had two trucks drive in side by side. Though they all had one thing in common: The flora didn’t care who dug out what. It grew just the same, gorging itself on the heavy mana in the air. By now, it wasn’t just lichen that grew, but plenty of fungi and the occasional vine-like plant that’d climb the wider cave’s walls, filling the chamber with a sweet, fragrant smell that set all sorts of alarms in Henry’s mind. He could feel the urge to approach and smell their soft, white and purple-dotted flowers, but Henry knew better. He didn’t know if he was immune to plant-life as the vine didn’t give him anything when he Identified them, but he still cut off a few saplings to sample later.

If there was a potent toxin as he suspected, he could add it to his repertoire.

Eventually, after nearly six hours of spelunking and quiet chatting as they continued to work on their scrying skill, Henry and Maurice came to a stop in a calm, wide chamber with a lake. Maybe at first Henry had hoped for a dead-end they could block, but clearly enough, there was a whole world down here, and so he’d have to make do with what he had.

“Gonna take a look in there,” he said. “If it’s empty… We’ll take a break in there and I’ll consume the tiger’s core.”

Maurice gave him a thumbs up, hopping on a lichen-covered boulder, while Henry summoned a Lifeblood avatar out of his Maw, which he then directed toward the lake, where it slipped below its surface in silence.

Then, it was as if the whole cave had woken up.

Henry sensed the foreign awareness spread out long before he heard the low, guttural rumble that made the lake’s surface tremble, and immediately, he grabbed Maurice by the wrist and teleported them nearly five avatars back, and for a moment, he almost carried them all the way to the surface but already, he’d had an instant to process what he’d sensed and seen, so he knew that the all-encompassing aura had been merely a warning. A simple “go away and let me sleep”.

Henry hadn’t dared to fire-off an Identify, but he didn’t need to, and that was why he felt the need to look back. To not get too far from what he’d seen, no matter how terrifying it was.

That lake had not been too wide nor too deep. Only a few hundred feet, all in all. But in the midst of it, sleeping on a mountain of shining gems and the bones of everything that had challenged it, slept a legend. With scales glowing like silver in the moonlight, a giant serpent slept coiled around its treasures, resting on two forward limbs while its beard-like mane of white-gold floated around it, contrasting with the bright blue antlers that matched its neck spine. And when Henry’s avatar had slipped into the lake and seen it, it had cracked a white, blinding eye open, and Henry knew right then and there that the creature could have obliterated the clone just by the weight behind its gaze if it had desired to.

“What… what was that?” Maurice was still wide eyed, voice trembling. Henry slowly looked down, and he realized his own limbs were still trembling from the experience. He also still had Maurice’s hand in a vise. Letting go of the crab’s hand, a slow, nervous chuckle escaped his lips, which quickly turned to laughter that Maurice didn’t seem to appreciate.

But, Henry would explain. He just needed a second to gather himself. Not every day you waltzed in on a dragon and got to walk away unscathed. Still, a question slowly pushed itself forward from the panic and wonder he was experiencing.

How was that thing still here? Sure, the void mantis had been more terrifying and its aura more potent, but this creature was, by far, the strongest he’d ever seen in this world. He was almost sure it could beat Zerathstra senseless. True, he’d never been on Thalis’ guardian’s bad side, but the contrast in power was stark.

This being had felt on a whole other level. It felt like the first time he’d sensed an A-rank as a mere C-rank himself.

Why was it still around?
