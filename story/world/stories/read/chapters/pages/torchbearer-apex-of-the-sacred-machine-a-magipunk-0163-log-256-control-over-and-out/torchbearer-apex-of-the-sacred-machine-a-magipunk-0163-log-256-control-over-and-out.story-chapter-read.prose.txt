Scary as it looked, the chain-mesh fence proved no challenge to a couple of shots into the padlock that kept its gate shut. I’d suspected the barbed wire was about to wrap itself around me, or maybe the tower would grow feet and try to crush me, but neither of those things happened. It seemed Jacky had run out of tricks. Considering how much it had cost me to build a bathroom, maybe I should have been surprised she managed to pull off as much as she had.

I didn’t know why, but I leaned the gate closed before I started my hike up the winding staircase around the tower. There was an elevator, but despite my earlier change of mind, trusting this sudden peace would have been a dumb idea any day of the week. I risked a glance upward and regretted it immediately. The damn staircase wound itself around the steel struts in ways that weren’t physically possible, and never seemed to end.

But up I had to go, so up I went.

I was happy about my skepticism less than ten meters along the first stairway, when the entire thing grew crystals like an uncleaned dish of pasta grows mold.

“Here we go again,” I muttered, and pulled out Pharus.

As I made my way upward, the very architecture fought me to the bitter end. Doors appeared in front of me out of nowhere, crystal formations erupted from the struts to stab at me, a couple of times the stairs straight-up vanished under my feet. Still, I pushed on until I finally arrived at the underside of the platform at the top. There was no grand entrance, no orange jaw of death. Just an unassuming wooden door with a small, lit sign above it that said “On Air.”

The city was burning.

That much became obvious the moment I opened the door. The smells of coal and ash, of copper and molten plastic hit me before I’d even set a foot inside the studio. The entire room was encircled with windows, and behind them fires raged as far as the eye could see. Flashes of gunfire filled the streets, trying to stem the roiling, dark red tide of Ferals I’d unleashed. From east to west, north to south, the city was getting devoured in real time, and we had a three-hundred-and-sixty degree view of the destruction.

One of the windows was open, letting in wafts of smoke. Maybe it was an oversight, maybe another attempt at control. Either way, now that Arx knew what it was looking for, it didn’t even break a sweat.

“Admiring your handiwork?” Jacky asked, mistaking the reason behind my silent regard.

As far as terrain went, the studio wasn’t offering much help, either. The decor was a decade out of time, even in the 80s. Lots of oranges and browns, lots of big switchboards with red, green, and blue cables snaking their way this way and that. I was willing to bet a few hundred bytes that they would wrap around my ankles the moment I got close to them.

“I don’t think that’s what I’m doing,” I replied, as calmly as one could when there was a drawn gun involved.

“Oh yeah? Then, pray tell, what would you call that?” She pointed her pistol at the window and the destruction that churned beyond.

Salvation, I didn’t say, because that was certainly not what it looked like. Not to her.

“Exactly! I brought order to this place.”

It was probably supposed to come across like a challenge, but I heard it as the plea it was. So many answers went through my mind. That she believed people could be possessions, that we need to change to become palatable to those we love. Lots of different lines of thought, each worthy of an entire conversation. The smoke wafting through the window got denser.

“I’m not,” I said, gently unclasping Manus’ holster.

Jacky shifted, brought Darryl between us again.

“That must be so easy for you to say, Dagger. Ever since you set foot in my city, you’ve acted so high and mighty, so aloof. But if you really got it all figured out, then go ahead. If you’re so much wiser than me, tell me who I am.”

“His Shackle.”

She scoffed.

But I’d struck a nerve.

“So judgemental… Who are you, then, that you’re so much better than me? A Savior? A Dagger? Or just a cunt that goes around ruining people’s lives, and then insulting them?”

I shook my head.

“Retribution.”

Before Jacky could react, I exhaled, a bell rang, and all the windows shattered at once.
