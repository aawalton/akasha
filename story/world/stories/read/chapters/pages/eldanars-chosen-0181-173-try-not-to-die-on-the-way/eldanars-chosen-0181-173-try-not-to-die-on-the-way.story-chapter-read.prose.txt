The pre-dawn air was bitterly cold, the kind of damp, unforgiving chill that seeped through wool and boiled leather to settle deep within the marrow. Yet, as Josh and the rest of the party stepped out of their temporary lodgings and into the main thoroughfare of the battered frontier town, the sheer volume of human and animal activity created a palpable, stifling heat that defied the frost.

The caravan’s preparation was a symphony of organised chaos, a desperate scramble to flee a place that had become a graveyard. Wagon drivers shouted hoarse, frantic commands, their voices cracking from days of smoke inhalation, lack of sleep, and sheer stress. Massive, shaggy-coated draft beasts lowed in deep, resonant protest as heavy wooden yokes were strapped across their thick necks, whilst workhorses stamped their iron-shod hooves against the cracked cobblestones, sending up small showers of frost and sparks into the dim morning light.

Everywhere Josh looked, people were desperately tying down the last remnants of their lives. It was a sobering, heartbreaking sight. Tarpaulins were stretched taut over mismatched, splintering crates; bundles of soot-stained clothing were secured with frayed hemp rope; and wide-eyed children huddled together beneath heavy, patchwork blankets in the beds of wooden carts, watching the adults with silent apprehension.

The party had spent the previous evening in a comfortable bubble of warm camaraderie at the tavern, their bodies rested, their bellies full, and their immediate wounds healed by Carcan's diligent prayers. But the people bustling frantically around them bore the heavy, undeniable toll of the siege. Buildings surrounding the thoroughfare still bore the jagged, terrifying claw marks of the Kobold horde; half-collapsed roofs were shored up with hastily cut timber, and the pervasive, acrid smell of old ash, singed hair, and dried blood still clung stubbornly to the freezing air.

"Over here," Perberos murmured softly, his sharp elven eyes cutting through the thick morning gloom and the milling, panicking crowds. He raised a slender, leather-clad arm, gesturing towards the heavy, iron-reinforced gates at the northern edge of the town.

They wove their way through the throng, falling into a natural, loose formation as they moved. They had to dodge a panicked, red-faced merchant trying desperately to herd three stray, bleating goat-like beasts that were determined to eat a discarded cabbage, and then step swiftly aside for a pair of exhausted militiamen who were hauling a heavy barrel of salted meat towards a cart belonging to a frail old woman.

Near the gatehouse, standing beside a large, flickering iron brazier that cast long, dancing shadows against the stone walls, was the town’s Guard Captain. To Josh, the man looked at least ten years older than he had just a few weeks prior. His chainmail had been scrubbed clean of gore, but it was irreparably dented in half a dozen places; his official tabard was torn and hastily stitched, and dark, bruised bags hung so heavily beneath his eyes they looked like physical weights pulling his face downwards.

Gathered around the Captain in a loose, untidy semi-circle was a small, ragged assembly of about a dozen other adventurers. As Josh’s party approached, Brett slowed his pace, his foot tapping rhythmically against the cobbles. The mage's eyes narrowed sharply as he took in the state of their fellow sell-swords.

"Gods above," Brett whispered to Josh, his tone entirely devoid of its usual academic arrogance, replaced instead with a quiet, clinical horror. "Look at them. Really look at them."

Josh followed the mage's gaze, and his stomach tightened into a cold, hard knot. These were the men and women who had fought outside the portal alongside them, the ones who had held the front lines when the terrible Kobold wave had first broken against their defences. But they were mere shadows of the warriors they had been.

One man, a heavily armoured fighter who Josh vaguely remembered swinging a massive, blood-slicked halberd with terrifying ferocity, now stood with his right arm heavily bandaged and bound in a tight, restrictive sling against his chest. His posture was slumped, and his eyes were vacant, staring fixedly at the middle distance, seeing horrors that no one else could see. A young spellcaster, her once-elaborate robes singed and stiff with dried blood, was visibly trembling despite the heat of the brazier. She clutched a shattered, splintered wooden staff to her chest as if it were a lifeline, her lips moving in a silent, frantic mantra.

Another pair, a human and a half-elf rogue, stood incredibly close together in complete silence. They weren't holding hands, but they leaned into each other for physical support, their faces hollowed out by a grief so profound it seemed to radiate from them like cold air. They had lost people. Entire teams had been wiped out in the dirt outside those gates. They had lost limbs, friends, and, most crucially for an adventurer, they had lost their nerve.

They were leaving for Ashenfall not because they’d levelled up enough to warrant moving to deeper, more lucrative dungeons, but because they simply couldn't endure another single day on the frontier. The town had broken them.

The Guard Captain looked up as Josh, Bhel, Carcan, Brett, and Perberos joined the outer edge of the circle. The party of five stood out starkly; their armour was repaired, their weapons were gleaming and meticulously maintained, and they carried themselves with the quiet, alert confidence of veterans who had stared death in the face and won. The Captain offered them a weary, deeply respectful nod, acknowledging the vast gulf between them and the others.

"Right, that’s all of you, then," the Captain said, his voice raspy and thin, struggling to carry over the din of the preparing caravan. He ran a heavily calloused hand over his closely cropped, greying hair. "I won't keep you standing in the cold for long. You’ve got a gruelling, dangerous trek ahead of you, and the dawn waits for no man. I just wanted to get you all together before the wagons roll out to say... thank you."

The Captain paused, looking slowly around the circle. His eyes lingered heavily on the bandages, the missing weapons, the thousand-yard stares, and the haunted expressions.

"You held the line," he continued, his voice dropping to a gravelly murmur. "When the rift broke, when that endless horde poured out and the sky turned dark with them, you didn't run. You stood your ground in the mud and the blood so the people in this town could get to safety behind the walls. A lot of good folks—mothers, fathers, children—owe you their lives today. The Adventurers' Guild will compensate you in Ashenfall, of course, but I wanted to offer my personal, deepest gratitude. You did the impossible."

A heavy, incredibly uncomfortable silence fell over the group, punctuated only by the crackle of the brazier and the distant shouting of the merchants. The young spellcaster gripped her broken staff even tighter, her knuckles turning bone-white, a small, choked sob escaping her throat.

The Captain cleared his throat loudly, attempting a small, wry smile to break the suffocating tension. "And hey, if any of you ever get tired of the open road... if you ever fancy a quiet, predictable retirement after you've healed up, come back and see me. Being a portal guard is the easiest copper you'll ever earn in this life. Nice scenery, plenty of fresh air, nothing much to do but play cards, tell lies, and watch the grass grow."

It was a black, bitterly ironic joke. Historically, guarding a stable portal had been considered an easy, almost boring assignment—a prime job for ageing, injured veterans looking to put their feet up and collect a steady wage. Now, standing amidst the smoking ruins of a near-apocalyptic monster surge, the very concept was utterly laughable.

A few of the adventurers managed weak, breathless chuckles that sounded more like coughs. The halberd fighter didn't even blink, his dead eyes still locked on the horizon.

"Safe travels to you all," the Captain finished softly, his smile fading. He stood up a little straighter and offered them a crisp, formal military salute. "May the roads be clear, and may the Light guide your steps."

As the group broke apart, beginning the slow, shuffling trudge towards their assigned places in the massive caravan, Brett reached out and pulled Josh slightly aside, allowing a distressed-looking family to hurry past them with a handcart. Brett's face was pale and fiercely serious.

"Josh, if we get hit on the road by anything larger than a stray dog, we absolutely cannot rely on them," Brett muttered, casting a worried, sideways glance at the retreating backs of the other adventurers. "Half of them look like they’d shatter into pieces if a strong breeze hit them, let alone a serious raiding party or a pack of trolls. They are completely psychologically broken. Putting a sword in their hands right now would be more of a danger to us than the enemy."

"I know," Josh replied quietly, his jaw clenching. The weight of his swords suddenly felt a little heavier against his side, the burden of leadership pressing down on him. As the tank, the safety of his party—and by extension, the people around them—fell squarely on his shoulders. "We'll have to take the brunt of it. We'll stick to the very front, keep our eyes open, and set the pace. The town needs every available guard to stay behind and secure the walls, we're the only real defence this caravan has."

"It's a terrible tactical position," Brett sighed, rubbing his temples.

"We fight as a unit and look after each other first," Josh said firmly, meeting the mage's eyes. "We don't overextend to save a cart of cabbages, but we don't let these people get slaughtered either."

"Agreed," Bhel rumbled, stepping up beside them and hefting his heavy twin axes, the metal catching the dull morning light. "I prefer it that way, to be honest. I wouldn't trust a single one of those sad sacks to watch my back right now anyway. Better to rely on the steel you know."

"Let's find our spot," Carcan suggested gently, her hands clasped in front of her robes.

They made their way down the long line of wagons, navigating the mud and the frantic last-minute packing, searching for a place to stow their heavier, cumbersome travel packs. Because the other broken adventurers had clumped together in small, frightened groups and distributed themselves haphazardly along the flanks and rear of the convoy to hide in the numbers, Josh’s party had naturally gravitated to a roving position near the very front to act as the primary shield.

They found an ideal spot right behind the lead scouts: a massive, incredibly heavy-duty wagon pulled by a team of four immense, shaggy draft beasts that looked like a cross between a Clydesdale horse and a mountain bear. The wagon’s reinforced axles were visibly groaning under the sheer, unadulterated weight of its cargo. It was piled astonishingly high with solid steel anvils, racks of wrought-iron tools, enormous, brass-studded leather bellows, and heavy wooden crates overflowing with raw, dull-grey iron ingots. Nestled carefully in a small, hollowed-out alcove amongst the heavy machinery was a weary-looking woman and three young children, wrapped tightly in thick furs, their breath pluming in the cold air.

Sitting high up on the driver's bench, thick leather reins wrapped expertly around his massive hands, was a man the size of a small mountain. He had arms thicker than Josh’s thighs, a completely bald head that gleamed with sweat despite the cold, and a thick, incredibly soot-stained leather apron that he wore over his thick travelling clothes out of what appeared to be pure, comforting habit.

"Morning," Josh called out clearly, raising a gauntleted hand in greeting. "You mind if we stow a few heavy bags on the tailgate? We're assigned to the front as guards."

The large man looked down, his bushy eyebrows knitting together. His eyes scanned the heavily armed party, taking in Josh's sturdy plate armour, Bhel's axes, Perberos's longbow, Brett's walking staff, and Carcan's vestments. When he saw the pristine, well-maintained condition of their gear and the relaxed, inherently confident way they carried themselves, a look of profound, overwhelming relief washed over his wide, weathered face.

"Mind? Gods above, I’d be honoured," the driver boomed in a deep, gravelly voice that sounded like boulders grinding together. "I’m Geth. Used to be a town blacksmith, right up until a few days ago when the sky fell in. Now, I suppose I’m just a man with a very heavy cart and a long walk ahead. Throw your bags wherever there's an inch of room, friends."

He gestured with a massive thumb over his shoulder. "There’s a sturdy wooden bench right behind me here—easily enough room for two of you to sit and rest your legs at a time, if you don't mind the inescapable smell of coal dust, iron rust, and beast sweat."

"We've smelled far, far worse recently, Master Geth," Carcan said warmly, offering the blacksmith a gentle, reassuring smile as she stepped forward to secure her heavy pack against the base of an anvil. "Thank you for the generous space. We will make sure nothing gets close to your family."

Geth’s expression softened, and he offered a grateful nod to the cleric. "Much obliged, Sister. I've got my entire life's work in this thing. Every hammer my father owned, every ingot I bled for."

"Fine quality steel, too," Bhel noted approvingly, running a thick, calloused finger over the edge of a protruding anvil. "You kept a good forge, Master Geth. It's a shame to leave it."

"Aye, well, a forge can be rebuilt. A family cannot," Geth replied pragmatically, adjusting his grip on the reins.

"We'll rotate the seats," Josh instructed the party, breaking them out of the somber mood. "Two sitting, three walking alongside the wheels. We keep our perimeter tight, and we rotate every two hours to keep everyone fresh."

"I claim first sit!" Bhel announced instantly, not waiting for approval. He clambered up the side of the heavy wagon with surprising, almost comical agility for a heavily armoured dwarf, using the iron wheel spokes as footholds. He wedged himself comfortably onto the wooden bench behind Geth, letting out a long, satisfied grunt as he kicked his boots out. "My little legs have done enough running this week to last a lifetime. I intend to view the countryside in comfort."

Brett sighed heavily, muttered something under his breath about dwarven laziness, and elegantly floated up using a minor levitation spell to take the seat beside him, smoothing his robes meticulously. "And I require to conserve my mana reserves. Walking through mud is a peasant's endeavour anyway, and terrible for the hem of one's cloak." Everyone rolled their eyes, but no one argued with Brett taking the seat.

Just as Josh was checking the straps on his shield, a sudden, boisterous commotion broke out a few yards away, cutting through the general misery of the crowd. The throng of people naturally parted, and five incredibly familiar, highly energetic figures jogged towards them.

It was Bun’s team.

Bun, the fiercely competitive female bunny-kin, led the charge as always. Her long, golden-furred ears were twitching rapidly with excess energy, and her beautifully crafted armour was pristine. Josh noticed she had adorned it with several new, high-quality, magically imbued steel plates she had clearly acquired from recent, highly successful dungeon runs.

Behind her jogged Bean, the fiery dwarven rogue, who was casually and dangerously tossing a wicked-looking, jagged throwing knife from hand to hand. Bringing up the rear were Soul, the towering, broad-shouldered human mage who was completely blocking sight of Choco, the gnome artificer, and Butler, the small human healer. Butler looked completely out of breath, leaning on her staff and looking utterly exhausted by her teammates' relentless, unyielding pace.

"Well, well, well," Bun declared loudly, skidding to a dramatic halt in front of Josh and crossing her arms over her chest. She leaned back, looking him up and down with an expression of intense, highly exaggerated competitive scrutiny. "Look who’s running away to the big city while the real adventurers stay behind to do the heavy lifting. Found the frontier a bit too spicy for your delicate tastes, have we?"

Josh couldn't help but grin, the familiar banter a welcome reprieve from the morning's gloom. "We prefer the term 'strategic relocation to a more target-rich environment', Bun. And from what I hear through the grapevine, you only stayed behind because you absolutely couldn't stomach the horrifying thought of following behind us when we’re a higher level than you."

Bun’s nose twitched furiously, her competitive spirit instantly flaring. "I am not driven by petty jealousy, you overgrown plate-head! I am driven by the pure, unadulterated pursuit of martial perfection! And just for the official record, we cleared the third level of the rift three times yesterday. Three times! We are so close to level twenty-five I can practically taste it. I can feel the mana vibrating in my teeth!"

"Don't flatter yourselves," Bean chimed in, stepping forward and pointing her jagged dagger directly up at Bhel, who was grinning down at her from the comfortable vantage point of the wagon bench. "We’re going to hit twenty-five, get our class advancements, and by the time we finally see you soft-bellied, cart-riding cowards in Ashenfall, we'll be wiping the floor with you in the arena."

"You couldn't wipe the floor with me if I personally handed you a mop, a bucket, and an instruction manual, you over-grown garden gnome!" Bhel roared back, laughing heartily, the sound booming over the noise of the crowd. "But I look forward to watching you try! Keep your daggers sharp, Bean! You'll need every edge you can get!"

"We’ll catch up with you soon enough," Soul said, his voice surprisingly soft, deep, and melodic for a man of his immense, intimidating size. He looked past the banter and offered a slow, deeply respectful nod to Perberos. The elven ranger, usually aloof, held the mage's gaze and returned the nod in silent, mutual understanding.

"Just try not to get yourselves completely killed on the road," Butler wheezed, finally catching her breath and leaning heavily on her glowing healing staff. She looked imploringly up at Carcan, her eyes wide with shared, professional suffering. "Please, tell me you're taking the danger magnets away from here. I need a break."

"I am keeping a very, very close eye on them, Butler," Carcan smiled gracefully, stepping forward to gently clasp the smaller healer's shoulder. "I have enough mana potions to keep him upright. May the Light watch over your delving in the dark. Keep them safe."

"I try," Butler sighed dramatically. "But they make it so difficult."

Meanwhile, Choco had managed to extract a bizarre, whirring brass contraption from his bag and was holding it up to Brett. "Hey, mage! Look at this! It's an aether-compression valve I salvaged! I think if I wire it into a standard fire crystal, I can make a bomb that—"

"Please put that away before you vaporise half the town, you lunatic," Brett sneered, though he leaned precariously over the edge of the bench to get a better look at the craftsmanship.

"Alright, alright, that’s quite enough sentimentality for one morning!" Bun snapped, clapping her hands together, though she couldn't entirely hide the affectionate, warm smirk playing on her lips. She stepped right up into Josh's personal space and delivered a shockingly hard, incredibly fast punch to his bicep. Her knuckles connected with the thick steel plate with a loud, ringing clack.

"You watch your back out there, Josh," she said, her voice dropping its teasing edge for a brief, sincere moment. "The roads to Ashenfall aren't safe. Don't go dying on me before I have the chance to show you what a true, fully-advanced warrior looks like. When we hit the city, the first round of ale is on you."

"You have my absolute word," Josh laughed, rubbing his arm, genuinely impressed by the force of the blow. "Stay safe, Bun. Don't pull anything you can't kill."

"Always," she grinned fiercely, her long ears standing straight up in excitement. She spun sharply on her heel, her cloak flaring out behind her. "Right, team! Break time is over! Back to the portal! Those big kobolds aren't going to kill themselves!"

As Bun and her eccentric team jogged back down the thoroughfare towards the centre of the ruined town, a long, mournful, incredibly loud blast from a heavy brass horn echoed across the crisp morning air.

It was the signal.

"Here we go, then," Geth muttered from the driver's bench. He braced his massive legs against the footboard, tightened his grip, and cracked his heavy leather whip high over the heads of the draft beasts. "Hup! Walk on, you beauties! Walk on!"

With an almighty cacophony of creaking timber, grinding, ungreased iron axles, and the collective, desperate shouts of a hundred different wagon drivers, the massive caravan shuddered violently and finally began to move. The wagons rolled forward at a slow, agonizingly steady, inevitable pace, the heavy iron-rimmed wheels crunching relentlessly against the frost-covered stone.

Josh walked alongside Geth’s wagon, matching his stride to the slow, heavy rhythm of the draft beasts. His gauntleted hand rested comfortably, yet ever-ready, on the leather pommel of his long sword. He listened to the familiar, comforting, rhythmic thud of Perberos’s soft leather boots on his left, and the soft, reassuring rustle of Carcan’s silken robes on his right. The tension in the air was incredibly thick, a complex, swirling mixture of deep-seated fear for the unknown dangers of the long road ahead, and a profound, overwhelming relief to finally be leaving the haunted ruins of the frontier town behind them.

As they finally passed beneath the deep, oppressive shadow of the wooden gates and stepped out into the open, dew-covered plains that stretched infinitely beyond the town walls, Josh stopped walking for just a fraction of a second. He turned his head and looked back over his shoulder.

Past the receding stone walls, past the jagged, ruined rooftops and the thick, grey smoke stacks venting from the remaining forges, he could see the distant, towering, ominous structure of the portal frame standing stark against the pale sky. For a brief, heart-stopping second, a brilliant, familiar flash of ethereal, crackling blue light pulsed from the depths of the rift, illuminating the swirling morning mist.

Beneath that cold blue light, though they were far too far away to see the individual faces, Josh knew exactly who was there. There was a thin, fragile line of people standing watch in the mud. People like Bun, and Bean, and the stubbornly brave local town guard who had flatly refused to abandon their homes. They had dug their iron-capped heels deep into the dirt, vowing with their lives to hold the line and ensure that the horrific disaster of the Kobold wave would never happen again.

Josh faced forward again, staring out at the vast, empty road, letting the vivid sight of the blue light burn permanently into his memory. They were officially leaving the wild frontier behind, heading towards the towering, wealthy spires and the dangerous political machinations of Ashenfall.

Turning back to the road, he shouted, "Keep your eyes sharp on the eastern treeline, Perberos." His voice dropped all traces of warmth, shifting instantly into a cool, calculating, and professional tone.

"Always," the elf replied smoothly, a steel-tipped arrow already nocked loosely, instinctively, to his bowstring.

The journey to Ashenfall had begun.
