After months of practice, joint meditation had become second nature for Jia and Eui. It hardly took any effort compared to when they had first developed the techniques, and they had gained more nuanced control over the connection. For instance, as intense as the shared emotions of qi meditation were, they had learned how to calm their minds first to reduce the impact. For mana meditation, they could even somewhat control which thoughts and memories they shared.

Ki meditation was another story, however. Eui’s ki was notoriously volatile and difficult to control, which made establishing the connection a little bit tricky. Rather than both trying to connect their ki flows together at the same time, they had found it much easier for one of them to focus on suppressing their ki while the other established a connection. This one-sided connection would temporarily give one person control of both bodies while the other suffered total sensory deprivation.

It was always a bit frightening to have the connection to your own body severed, but both of them trusted the other to guide their combined ki flows to complete the circuit and enter the state of joint meditation. For Jia, she found it very difficult to suppress her ki—the lightning element seemed to loathe staying still. Conversely, Eui’s practice of the Six Arms of Asura had trained her to constantly suppress her ki, so they had fallen into the habit of letting Jia be the one to initiate the connection.

Still, despite all of their practice, Jia was struggling desperately to accomplish anything. Eui’s ki was rampaging indiscriminately through her body, and though she was barely conscious, Eui had noticed what Jia was trying to do and was making an effort to suppress her ki. Sadly, the reality was that if she had been able to suppress her ki, she wouldn’t be suffering in the first place.

Jia’s concentration was already being strained by all the cuts, burns, and broken bones she was suffering, and she could scarcely imagine how much worse it must be for Eui. Worse, Eui’s breathing was weak and ragged, there was no way she could properly practice the necessary breathing techniques for meditation. Jia began to lose hope as her ki was violently rejected again and again by Eui’s rampaging energy.

She wracked her brain, trying to think of a solution. What did she know about medicine? Or treating ki-related injuries? Had she not given Xin all her precious medicines precisely because she didn’t have the first clue how to use them? Jia’s mind desperately scrambled to recall every last scrap of knowledge she had. Every thought, memory, and lesson that could potentially pertain to medicine and ki.

There was one! One memory that might hold the key. Jia had nearly died once, when she had drained herself entirely of ki and mana. Without being able to move or control her body, she’d been unable to breathe. Without being able to breathe, she couldn’t take in more ki, even passively, but Ienaga had—

Jia acted immediately without thinking and placed her lips over Eui’s, forming a seal. She ignored the shocked gasp from behind her and focused on her memory. Ienaga had used some kind of crystal—probably a mana stone—to breathe energy back into Jia. In this case, Jia decided that using her own ki was preferable. She reversed the process of absorbing ki, calling it from her body into her heart, from her heart into her lungs, and then focused on keeping control of that ki as she breathed out.

As her ki entered Eui’s lungs, it was quickly absorbed and the effect was almost immediate. Jia’s entire world went white with agony and she screamed in pain. Unprepared for the experience, Jia recoiled from Eui and caused the connection to break. Though it had only been for a moment, the mere memory of what she had just experienced left her trembling, and her muscles twitched and cramped painfully from phantom twinges of pain.

“M-Miss Lee, are you alright?”

“I’m fine.”

Lee Jia brushed off Xin’s concerns, though she felt very far from fine. She tried and failed to hold back her tears as she cradled Eui’s head in her arms, leaning down until their foreheads touched.

“I’m so sorry, Eui.”

Jia’s voice was barely a whisper as she spoke through quiet sobs.

“I should never have asked you to do this. Nobody should have to bear pain like that. But I promise you, Eui, I will never let this happen to you again. And you won’t have to go through it alone.”

Their lips met, and once again Jia breathed her ki into Eui, bracing herself for the incoming wave of agony. Just like last time, it was nearly unbearable as she felt Eui’s body appear in her senses. The burns covering Eui’s skin were like bug bites compared to the all-encompassing agony of the destructive ki rampaging throughout her body. With great effort, Jia tried to focus through the pain and circulate their combined ki through Eui’s body.

It wasn’t as simple as she’d hoped. As their ki combined in the forge of Eui’s heart, it radiated outward through her blood vessels—and was promptly set upon by the destructive ki. No longer truly a part of Eui, the ki had deviated and taken on a sort of form of its own.

Jia recognized it as something that Elder Qin had called an internal demon, energy that a cultivator had lost control over, which then attacked them from within. Part of what made the destruction element so dangerous was that it was particularly prone to such deviations.

Jia redoubled her concentration and pushed against the deviant ki in an effort to flush it from Eui’s system, but her efforts were completely fruitless. If she pushed the deviant ki away from one place it would fill in another, and as soon as she shifted her focus to the new invasion her previous efforts would be reversed.

The problem, she realized, was that the destructive energy had nowhere to go. Ideally she would either neutralize the ki with its opposite element, or expel it through the breath. Since she didn’t have a way to make wood elemental energy, that option was out. Perhaps if Eui was able to use her spiritual arts—but she wasn’t in any condition to do so.

The other option was no good either—expelling the ki through Eui’s breath would just send it right back into Jia! Worse, it would need to be channeled through the heart first, and that was the one place the destructive energy could not be allowed to damage.

There was only one other thing Jia could think of, and she had no idea if it would work. She knew for certain that she wouldn’t be able to accomplish it on her own, though. So, with a heavy heart, she carefully circulated their combined ki and allowed it to flow back towards her own heart and spread throughout her body.

Jia and Eui’s bodies both spasmed briefly as Eui experienced the shock of sensation returning to her. Jia felt her own brows furrowing unbidden as Eui tried to make sense of what was happening. Without any means of proper communication, Jia tried to draw a character with her hand, hoping that Eui would understand.

“Isolate.”

Returning her attention to the ki in Eui’s body, Jia went back to pushing back the destructive energy. As the deviant ki began to push back elsewhere, Jia and Eui’s combined ki seemed to move on its own to stop it.

Jia smiled internally—it seemed that Eui had understood. Slowly, inch-by-inch, the destruction element began to lose ground in Eui’s veins. With both of them working together, they were slowly forcing all of the deviant energy together into one place.

An unfortunate side-effect of this strategy was that while the places they pushed the destruction away from were able to heal, the pain was intensifying greatly in the places where it was being concentrated. Their progress was also slowing down more and more as the energy compressed, growing denser under the pressure of their combined efforts to isolate it into a single place.

Jia lost herself in the effort, focusing all her attention on maintaining the push against the destruction. She knew that one small slip could cause massive damage, and she wasn’t willing to let Eui be hurt any more than she already had been. Much of Eui’s body had recovered—presumably under Xin’s ongoing efforts to heal her as they cleared away the destructive energy—but they had reached a limit.

No matter how much they pushed, they couldn’t seem to gain any more ground, yet they knew that if they ceased their efforts, they would soon end up right back where they started. Jia had no idea where to go from here. She had been operating entirely on instinct and intuition, and hadn’t really given much thought to what would happen once they had successfully concentrated the destructive energy into a single point.

Jia and Eui were both sweating from both the strain of their effort, and from the indescribable singularity of pain that they had managed to create within Eui’s body. Jia didn’t know how long they held it there before finally something gave way. All at once, the pain vanished and the destructive ki collapsed into a small point and crystallized as a tiny red orb that radiated with a strangely malicious energy.

The small orb had crystallized right in the center of Eui’s chest, next to her heart, and as their ki circulated through Eui’s body, the orb seemed to constantly absorb a small fraction of it. Jia had no idea what that orb was or what it meant, but Eui’s ki had finally stabilized and her body was recovering. Whatever the orb was, they would have time to worry about it later.

Having learned from past experiences, Jia tentatively broke off the connection before slowly separating from Eui and opening her eyes. Eui’s injuries had almost completely vanished, with the only remaining evidence of her previous condition being the ragged uniform—which even now was slowly reknitting itself—and the sickly pale pallor of her skin. Eui’s eyes had also lost their dark color, and her irises were now blood red.

As their eyes met, Jia belatedly realized that she had just spent that entire time locked in a kiss with Eui, and blushed so hard she felt like her face was about to catch on fire. As embarrassing as that was, Jia was too relieved to care and swept Eui up in a tight embrace.

“I’m so glad you’re ok!”

Despite herself, Jia started crying again. All the emotions she had been holding in since resolving herself to finishing the fight came pouring out at once as she held Eui tightly and wept into her shoulder. Eui returned the hug and patted Jia’s back awkwardly, her own blush creeping its way slowly up to her ears.

“T-thanks, Jia. Me too.”

“I—hic—I thought you—hic—you were going to die!”

Jia could barely manage to speak through her own sobbing as Eui tried in vain to console her.

“Hey, it’s ok! I’m ok, you saved me.”

They simply sat there, holding each other for a few minutes while Jia cried into Eui’s shoulder before the moment was interrupted by the sound of someone clearing their throat.

The rest of their team was standing around them, fully recovered and in various states of either awkwardly averting their gaze or staring straight at them. Xin Wei was the only one bold enough to actually interrupt them.

“Ahem, excuse me, but does somebody want to tell me what in the name of the Emperor just happened?”

After a small commotion the group agreed to give Jia and Eui some space to recover and get their bearings. While they were meditating, a small portion of the hill around them had been flattened to form a perimeter in which a small camp had been set up. Despite the frigid winter air, there was no campfire—they could all withstand the temperature comfortably. There was, however, a central area for them to sit around and all eyes were on them as Jia and Eui joined the group.

After an uncomfortable silence that seemed to drag on forever, Jia asked a question that had been bothering her.

“Um, how long were we—”

“Making out? Around six hours I think—ow!”

Takeda’s answer was interrupted by Eunae slapping her in the back of the head.

“Don’t be so crass, Rika!”

Lee Jia blushed again and stared down at her lap. That hadn’t been as long as she thought, considering.

“How have everyone’s injuries recovered so quickly? Especially Eui and Eunae.”

Xin pointed at the pile of medicines she had dumped from her ring earlier—now significantly smaller.

“I took the liberty of availing myself of your rather prodigious resources for our recovery. Some of them were quite valuable and potent, I hope you don’t mind.”

Lee Jia shook her head.

“No, I’m glad you did. I’m just happy to see that everybody is ok.”

Xin Wei nodded magnanimously.

“Yes, my skills are quite remarkable. Once I noticed the corruption receding from Miss An, I resumed her treatment as well. I had planned on treating your own wounds as well, Miss Lee, but somehow treating Miss An caused your wounds to heal as well. Quite mysterious, isn’t it?”

Lee Jia stared at Xin Wei in silence for a moment, her mind racing. Hadn’t Ienaga mentioned something about joint cultivation being taboo in Qin? Should she tell the truth? How else could she possibly explain what she had done?

“Please calm down, Miss Lee. I’m not trying to interrogate you, I just want to understand what I witnessed. It seemed to me that Miss An had suffered from some variant of qi deviation and was about to succumb to her internal demon when you used your—ahem—technique to aid her in fighting it off.”

Lee Jia pressed her index fingers together and looked anywhere except at Xin Wei.

“Y-yeah…”

“I saw Instructor Ienaga perform a similar technique on Miss Lee when she collapsed on the first day of training.”

Guan Yi came to her rescue, and Eunae nodded to corroborate his story.

“I remember that. Perhaps Jia learned it from her?”

“Yes, exactly! A recovery technique like the one that Master Ienaga used on me!”

Lee Jia pounced on the lifeline that Guan and Eunae had thrown her, but Xin Wei still seemed skeptical.

“I had heard about that...though I was busy either meditating or being beaten by that damned sav—”

Xin Wei cut himself off when Takeda shot him a withering glare. His smile didn’t reach his eyes as he revised what he was saying.

“Being encouraged by that delightful Lady Ienaga. So I didn’t witness it myself, though I was present at the time. Still, it sounded like a simple resuscitation to treat ki deprivation to me. While superficially similar, what you did was something else entirely. Now, are you going to explain or do I have to guess?”

“U-um...nnno?”

Lee Jia grimaced as Xin simply crossed his arms and stared at her. Stupid nosey, know-it-all cultivators! She looked around for another ally to bail her out. Eunae and Dae seemed just as curious as Xin—albeit less aggressive about it, and Takeda was glancing between her and Eui with a weird grin for some reason. Kim Yongsun seemed to be busy pretending they didn’t exist, and Guan Yi...was Guan Yi.

It shouldn’t have surprised her that the person to speak up in her defense was Eui.

“Hey, have you considered that maybe it’s none of your fucking business!? I know you can tell she doesn’t want to answer, and I know you think she’s too nice to tell you to fuck off. Luckily, I’m mean enough for both of us. So Fuck. Off. Keep your stupid nose out of our business or I’ll cut it off of your smug fucking face! Understood!?”

Xin Wei held his hands up and smiled apologetically at Eui.

“Loud and clear, Miss An. Forgive me if I poked my ‘stupid nose’ somewhere unwelcome. I simply let my curiosity get the better of me—I’ve never met dual cultivators before, after all.”

Eui’s already angry expression became furious and she reached for a knife before realizing she had lost them all. Jia put a hand on her shoulder to stop her before she could tackle Xin Wei and start pummeling him with her bare hands. She glared at Xin Wei, annoyed.

“If you knew the answer, why bother with the interrogation? And it’s not what you think it is. It’s not—like that. I’m done talking about it.”

“Very well. I sincerely apologize for pushing you, I just wanted to be certain. It is important to know one’s allies, you know. In any case, let’s change the subject—while you were occupied, we dealt with the corpses. Ash-furred Obsidian Wolves have a number of valuable parts including their claws, fangs, pelts, and—of course—their third rank magma cores.”

Lee Jia was grateful for the change of topic as she followed Xin’s indication to a pile of monster parts. The claws were no less intimidating after being removed from the beasts, retaining their razor edge, though they no longer glowed with preternatural heat.

Her blood turned to ice when Jia saw what sat on top of the furs, however. There, in a neat pile, sat five smooth round orbs, glowing orange with the power of the magma element. Tiny orbs of crystallized mana…

Just like the one that now resided in Eui’s chest.
