"This is so confusing. At first, it seemed like the best choice was to stay unnoticed, but now that feels impossible—or at least far more difficult," Pikar complained loudly as they walked up yet another tunnel.

"We could carry the seeds in our pockets instead of the spatial rings. That way, we won’t have a target on our backs," Ricarda suggested.

Luckily, that was exactly what Thalion had done the moment he read the message.

"Yes, but we’re just lucky the seeds are so small. What if the next treasure is larger? And if we run across the desert with them in our pockets, we’ll leave a trail those hunters can follow," Pikar continued, clearly agitated by the situation.

"We don’t even know how strong those hunters are. But so far, over a million have died, which suggests they’re at least D-grade. It could be that they simply overwhelm their targets with numbers, but that seems unlikely."

Thalion had reached a similar conclusion—but there was something Pikar hadn’t considered.

"Or they might be at the same level as us but equipped with weapons strong enough to kill in a single hit," Thalion added.

"Either way, they’re extremely capable. Otherwise, there wouldn’t be this many dead. And the more people die, the more likely they are to come after us. I just hope we get some kind of clue soon, or this event will become far more dangerous very quickly," Pikar muttered.

There was nothing more to add. The group continued their ascent, moving deeper into the structure. Pikar studied the map and the phrase on its back, determined not to miss anything. No one knew how much time they had left.

They also kept a careful eye on the ground, wary of triggering another trap like the stone slab that had sealed them in with the queen.

Finally, after what felt like an eternity, the corridor ended. They stepped into another massive chamber.

This one was filled with wall paintings, but unlike before, it also contained several stone consoles and five sealed gates. Each gate bore a single rune—none of which Thalion recognized.

"Oh great, another stupid riddle. The gods are really smiling on us," Pikar complained.

Thalion had a few choice insults of his own, but voicing them wouldn’t help. Instead of getting lost in the murals, he turned his attention to the consoles.

At their base were etched diagrams—plans of some kind.

After studying them for a while, realization slowly dawned on him.

"Hey… maybe we don’t need to travel across the desert at all. It looks like the underground is its own world—with multiple floors."

That caught the others’ attention immediately. They abandoned what they were doing and joined him at the central console.

"That’s right. And from the looks of it, we’re already on the second of five floors," Pikar said, a wide grin spreading across his face.

"This changes everything. Judging by this, reaching the deepest level should be extremely difficult. The fifth floor is likely the oldest—buried first—and should hold the most valuable treasures. The problem is, there are no direct connections leading out of it. Not every region even has a fifth floor, which means we’ll probably have to travel far to find access points. It’s a shame there are no clear directions… but luckily, I’ve already started piecing together the wall paintings."

Pikar’s grin widened as he continued.

"From what I can tell, everything once belonged to a single massive empire—far more powerful than anything that came after. That’s the fifth floor."

He pointed toward the murals.

"The fifth floor was where that empire ruled… until something happened. It shattered into multiple smaller kingdoms, each holding only a fraction of its former power. The queen we encountered was likely part of one of those successor states."

His eyes gleamed with excitement.

"So we need to pass through these newer kingdoms and look for clues that lead us down. And the best part? There’s no wrong direction. Four of these gates lead to remnants of kingdoms on the second floor, while the one on the right should take us to the third level. Honestly, it’s a good thing you spoke up—otherwise I might’ve burned out trying to make sense of those murals without the context from the consoles."

That was… reassuring.

And the fact that they wouldn’t need to solve another riddle anytime soon? Even better.

"By the way, how did you know this gate leads to the third floor? The rune looks… a bit dark," Thalion asked.

"Because that’s where the little girl with the black flower came from," Pikar replied with a grin.

He pressed the rune, and the massive stone gate rumbled as it rose, revealing the path beyond.

"Great. Another darkness-filled chamber. Perfect for my plants," Ricarda muttered, clearly disappointed.

Thalion, of course, had a very different opinion—especially after his recent gains in power.

"I think you’re being a bit too negative. Also… aren’t you a plant mage?"

"Of course I’m a plant mage. What are you getting at, human?" Ricarda snapped, a flicker of anger in her voice.

"Whoa, no need to pull the ‘human’ card. I just meant—"

"No need? Do you even know how hard this is for me?" Ricarda cut him off, her voice rising.

"I’m an elf. A Chosen. My entire worldview has been turned upside down, and now I’m forced to follow along… to follow the orders of my goddess…"

Pikar and Thalion exchanged a brief look. That frustration had clearly been building inside her for a while.

"Right… what I was trying to say," Thalion continued once her breathing steadied, "is that you can grow plants. If you could cultivate those dark seeds, we might end up with enough for everyone."

"...Then hand them over," Ricarda said, still visibly agitated.

Thalion passed her the seeds. Her hand began to glow with green energy as she examined them, then placed them back into her pocket, shaking her head.

"Something’s locked. I think it’s because they’re tied to the trial," she said, almost to herself.

Disappointing—but it could have been worse.

"Doesn’t matter. We’re following the trail of that girl, and hopefully she has more of those black plants," Thalion said with a wicked grin. "What do you think the gods in integrated space are thinking about us? Especially if the three of us end up at the top?"

"Considering you won the first System Event against a bunch of Chosen, I’d say they’re already bragging about you," Pikar replied.
