Jinyo reviewed the lessons from his fight with Esther while warming himself by the fire. His Sword Spirit proficiency had increased. Most impressively, Punishment Wave had leapt from Level 5 to Level 10 in the blink of an eye—likely because he’d brushed against the technique’s true essence. Among his stance skills, only Peach Blossom Dance was leading the pack.

On the magic side, Realm of Paradise had climbed to Level 15. Jinyo hadn’t even noticed how frequently he’d been using it. Though it placed a heavy burden on his mind, it also restored a tremendous amount of energy. Every time the Domain was deployed, it ran for hours on end, steadily raising the skill’s proficiency.

When there was no fighting, Jinyo tested a miniaturized deployment of the Domain—a ten-square-meter zone around the campfire. It was economical to maintain, and every minute the Domain was active, skill proficiency ticked upward. Inside the Domain, which replicated the Spirit Essence of the Garden of the Nameless God, all living things received enormous benefits.

Luciria sat meditating in silence, circulating her energy. Beside her, another large man whose origins were anyone’s guess had joined them—Esther, who wanted nothing to do with Jinyo, couldn’t resist the allure of the Spirit Essence. Cultivating within the Domain sped his progress several times over. Why would he sleep in a rathole when a five-star hotel was available?

The atmosphere around camp was stifling. No one had slept well—everyone was on edge, never knowing when the Cursed Villagers might launch an attack. Empty stomachs growled and complained. Many hadn’t eaten a thing since the day before. The Cursed Forest offered no food. The only thing readily available was the purple berry that Jinyo had declared lethal—and by now, patience was wearing thin.

“I’m going out to find berries. Anyone coming with me?” Peter spoke up amid the campfire circle of equally famished people. He was the shaved-head man who’d had a bellyful of Esther’s boot earlier.

“Is that a good idea? The Doctor said they’d shorten your life.”

“Shorten your life eating them, or die of starvation tomorrow—take your pick! You’d rather trust some stranger’s word and starve to death? Or take your chances and deal with the consequences later? You’re all adults, not idiots.” Peter scanned for allies. Many hesitated but raised their hands.

“Excellent! I figured you’d feel the same way. For all we know, that so-called ‘Doctor’ is just a con man who doesn’t know a thing but still tells us we can’t eat.” Peter’s group gathered weapons and manpower, grabbed torches, and marched out of camp of their own volition. If they couldn’t reclaim their fruit from Jinyo, they’d simply go pick more.

After Peter’s group left, others began banding together as well.

“Is this okay, Professor? They’re leaving.”

“The Addictive property is compromising their judgment. Even if Nephrin herself told them, they’d still refuse to listen. What they want to hear isn’t the truth.”

“What if we shared our rations with them?”

“I tried summoning produce, but it didn’t work.” Jinyo could do everything else within the Realm of Paradise—summon facilities from the Garden of the Nameless God—but agricultural products wouldn’t materialize. The Celestial Rice and Martyr Potatoes in the Gardener’s Bag were limited. They were critical resources Jinyo couldn’t afford to lose.

“Forget about them. If you want to survive a Hollow Gate, learn to spell ‘selfish.’ Be saints and do-gooders all you want—but don’t you dare give away my share of rice.” Everyone turned to look at Esther when he finished speaking. How food-obsessed could this man be? Then again, they understood—the Celestial Rice was indescribably delicious.

“Big Bro Yo.” Gretel looked up at his father with expectant eyes. While they ate well and comfortably, others wailed and moaned with hunger. The roasted potato in his mouth didn’t taste as good as usual.

“I can’t share the Celestial Rice. But I can’t just watch people starve to death either. I’ll try to think of another way. Worst case, I give everyone one Martyr Potato each—but that won’t actually fix anything.” Jinyo sighed. The weight of it was crushing—knowing he could theoretically save everyone from starvation but being unable to act on it. He didn’t want to look bad in others’ eyes, but the system’s agricultural products were the lifeline he couldn’t sacrifice.

As dusk crept closer, Peter’s group still hadn’t returned. Everyone watched as a young woman in black came sprinting into camp. Many rose to their feet—she was Nephrin, the pillar of faith for the survivors. The momentum of her run revealed more than her slender legs, but the panic on her face made it impossible for anyone to appreciate the sight.

“Someone’s injured! Does anyone have healing magic?” Nephrin shouted to the crowd. People glanced at each other, whispering, searching for anyone with restorative abilities. Medical magic was an immensely complex discipline. Out of a hundred people, only one might be clever enough to study it. Nephrin believed that among more than two hundred survivors, there had to be at least one who knew medical magic.

But Nephrin was wrong. She was from the capital, but the exam candidates here were rural folk who hadn’t even finished a bachelor’s degree. Out of two hundred, maybe two had the aptitude—but those two had almost certainly never studied medical arts.

“What happened?” Jinyo stood up as Nephrin called for a healer. People streamed in from outside the camp, every one of them bleeding badly enough to dye their clothes red. Luciria rushed over to look and saw a familiar face. A middle-aged man in butler’s attire was supporting a young man in his late twenties, ghostly pale, showing signs of severe blood loss.

“James!”

“Lady Luciria—I’m relieved you’re safe.”

“What happened to him…?”

“We fought one of those giant antler monsters. No matter what we did, it wouldn’t die—it became a war of attrition. Before we knew it, the Young Master was run through by one of those pumpkin-heads. It’s my fault for failing to protect him. If Lady Nephrin hadn’t happened to pass by, the Young Master would have…” James heaved a great sigh. Former special-forces agent or not, his skills had dulled with the years.

“It’s not your fault. But… he…” Luciria didn’t know what to do. To her, Sven was practically a stranger she’d only recently met. He was the only person in the mansion who genuinely cared about her—not as a tool for advancing status, but sincerely. Though they weren’t close in the way siblings who grew up together would be, Sven was the one person Luciria hoped to someday repay.

Sven’s breathing grew weaker by the moment. And it wasn’t just Sven—several other wounded had been carried in and were collapsing at camp. They’d dragged their battered bodies here praying for treatment. Some had stumbled and passed out along the way. Nephrin had done everything she could, but she was merely an experienced sorceress—not a god who could do anything. The pressure of having everyone’s hopes placed on her shoulders was so heavy she couldn’t pause for even a second.

“Isn’t there anyone with healing magic?! It doesn’t have to be advanced—even basic level would help!” Nephrin cried out again, hoping someone—anyone—would step forward. But it was a pipe dream. A thought flickered through her mind: if she could turn back time, she’d have taken a medical magic elective. The feeling of screaming into a void with no answer was absolutely wretched.

“Please help—save my brother. The Halfrin family will never forget this kindness. I’m begging you.” Luciria pleaded in a voice hoarse with desperation. She wasn’t good with words and didn’t understand how to communicate with most people. Every syllable she wrung out took immense courage. But no one could meet her eyes.

Two of the most prominent women in the exam were practically begging. If anyone had the ability, they wouldn’t have hesitated. Even Luciria’s brother—a young lord from a wealthy family—drew no volunteers. It was a golden opportunity to change someone’s life: earn a debt of gratitude from a noble house and a Hunter with a bright future.

But these were people too unskilled and penniless to do anything except become Hunters. The only thing they were good at was manual labor and risking their lives—not saving others from danger. Luciria exhausted herself after just a few sentences. The girl knew it was futile. She ran back to the campfire under everyone’s gaze, wondering what she would do next.

“Professor! My brother is going to die—please, save him.” Luciria rushed over and clutched Jinyo’s hands, imploring him. Jinyo was stunned that she had come to him for help.

“Luciria, I’m not a doctor.”

“You’ve worked miracles so many times. You saved my life again and again. Just this once—please save my brother.”

‘I’m not God. I’m just a botanist who happens to swing a sword.’ Those were the words lodged in his throat. But when he saw the face of a girl begging for her brother’s life, it reminded him of Naho. He swallowed every word and thought about what he could possibly do. He had no healing magic, no medical skills. His medical knowledge was on par with a first-year med student at best. But if he did nothing, innocent people would die.

Jinyo walked over and stopped in front of the gravely wounded Sven. Nephrin hurried to his side the moment she noticed.

“Doctor Jinyo, I’ll leave the camp in your hands.”

“I know what you’re thinking, but hold on. Even if you found a medical mage out there, you’d never make it back in time. It’s almost dark, and you know how much more dangerous it is at night.”

“I know. But we can’t give up. Giving up isn’t in my vocabulary. I just came to tell you—excuse me.”

“Wait.” Jinyo reached out and caught Nephrin’s shoulder before she could pull away. The instant his hand made contact, he nearly recoiled. This woman’s body was burning hot—even through her clothes, it was like touching an exhaust pipe. Nephrin hadn’t slept for two full days, continuously deploying fire-element Planar in combat. She looked like she could still stand, but she was nearing her breaking point.

“Sorry—I didn’t mean to.” Aside from Naho, he hardly ever touched a woman. He hadn’t been rude, had he?

“It’s fine. Don’t worry about it.”

“I have two reasons why you need to stay. First, everyone is panicking, and they listen to you. You’re the only anchor they have. Second, if you disappear, who’s going to protect them?”

“Fair points. But what do we do about them—just watch them die?”

“I’ll try to figure something out.” Jinyo used the Eye of the Sage on Sven. The system window displayed his condition. Jinyo tore open the man’s clothing to reveal the savage wound. The wound had begun to necrotize. If not for the fact that Sven was a trained practitioner, he’d already be dead from blood loss.

The internal organs were still intact. If the wound could be closed and the body left to heal itself, there was still a chance. The problem was that Sven probably wouldn’t last long enough for his body’s natural recovery to kick in. But Jinyo had a creative idea.

“I have an idea—but I’m not sure the patient would want it.”

“Doctor Jinyo, if you have a method, just say it. I’ll help you weigh the decision.”

“I told you the Cursed Fruit accelerates growth and recovery, right? If we feed the patient a large amount, there’s a chance the body will heal itself. We just need to remove all the necrotic tissue first.”

“That’s… you said yourself that anyone who eats the fruit loses years off their life.”

“I just heard someone say that a shorter life is better than death. Why don’t we take the gamble?” Jinyo laid bare every thought in his mind. He feared how others would look at him. Using the Cursed Fruit to save lives was utterly inhumane—it was like robbing someone of their future with his own hands. What kind of mind came up with something like this?

“Doctor Jinyo, you don’t need to be afraid. It’s up to the patient. Butler—could you draw up a document? A consent form stating the patient agrees to treatment and will not hold us liable for any future consequences, no matter what.” Nephrin turned to James.

“Understood, Lady Nephrin. Doctor Jinyo—whatever the outcome, the Halfrin family is already in your debt. Please take care of the Young Master.” James bowed to the two young people. The courage to make a decision and act was no small thing. Sven, clinging to the last threads of consciousness, gave a faint nod when Jinyo asked for his consent.

“Establish Domain.” Jinyo expanded the Realm of Paradise. In an instant, a large wooden cottage materialized in the middle of the survivors’ camp. Sven was carried inside and laid on a white bed that was rapidly being stained crimson.

“Lady Nephrin—I’ll debride the necrotic tissue. You cauterize the wound as fast as you can.”

“Cauterize? Is that really necessary?” Nephrin thought it was absurd. Remove the dead tissue, suture the wound, force-feed the patient Cursed Fruit, and pray the Boost curse did its work—that was the treatment plan. She was barely even needed.

“I can’t suture.”

“….” Nephrin.

“….” Sven was fortunate to have lost consciousness. Otherwise, he’d have risen from the bed and strangled Jinyo. You mean to tell me you can’t suture, so you’re using fire magic to seal the wound shut? You came up with a treatment plan involving Cursed Fruit but capped it off with a technique from the Stone Age?!

Jinyo coated a knife with Sword Spirit and carved every bit of necrotic tissue from the wound. The Boost status didn’t only accelerate the regeneration of healthy cells—it had never been written as a healing ability. It accelerated growth. If bones were misaligned or dead cells remained, those would grow too. That was why the Eltharok and the Cursed Villagers had such grotesquely warped physiques.

Once Jinyo was certain he’d removed all the dead tissue, he ordered Nephrin to cauterize the wound with fire magic. Sven’s eyes snapped open and he screamed. Jinyo stuffed a cloth into his mouth to keep him from biting through his own tongue. The young lord of a wealthy family had never endured agony like this in his life. He wanted to die at the very moment two people were fighting to save him.

First-degree burns managed to partially seal the smaller lacerations. Jinyo crushed a hundred Cursed Fruits into juice and poured it down the unconscious Sven’s throat. Sven had already blacked out and could perceive nothing. His body temperature spiked. The Boost curse began working, accelerating wound recovery.

It was nothing short of miraculous—Nephrin saw it with her own eyes. The tissue she had cauterized began regenerating new cells.

The treatment was working… at the cost of lifespan being consumed at a terrifying rate. No one could say how many years Sven had left. James took the liberty of entering the cottage with the signed consent forms. Jinyo didn’t ask where he’d found paper to draft them. With the formal request for treatment signed, not every patient had been willing to consent.

Jinyo summoned another structure—the granary—and had Sven moved there to rest. The next patient was carried in for Jinyo to debride, followed by Nephrin cauterizing the wound. Everything would have been far less brutal if Jinyo actually knew how to suture. Alas—no one was perfect.
