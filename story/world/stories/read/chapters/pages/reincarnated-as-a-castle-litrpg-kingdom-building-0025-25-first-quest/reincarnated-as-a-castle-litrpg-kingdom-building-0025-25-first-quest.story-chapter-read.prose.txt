Since I had some Points to spare, I exchanged 25 Points for 5 Powerstones.

Points Remaining: 66

I moved on to creating the quest by filling in the remaining lines of the form.

[Quest Creation Form]

Quest Name: Wood Gathering Quest

Prerequisite: None

Quest Info: Collect wood from the surrounding forest to help build and expand the castle.

Quest Type: Repeatable

Objective: Gather 100 Units of Wood from the forest, and drop them within the castle territory.

Reward: 1 Powerstone (F)

When I entered 100 Units of Wood as the objective, the System recommended a reward of 0 to 1 Powerstones. But when I increased the requirement to 200 Units, the recommendation shifted to 0 to 2.

(Wait, a 0 Powerstone reward? Is that even possible?)

But after thinking for a second, I understood why.

The goblins saw me as some kind of powerful being. I had not only saved their lives, but also helped them evolve. So even if I offered no wage at all, they would probably still accept the quest.

But... I wasn’t that heartless a ruler.

They were my citizens, not slaves. They deserved fair pay.

By my calculations, I could get back 20 Points if I sold all 100 units of wood back to the System. That was equivalent to 4 Powerstones, so paying 1 Powerstone for the labor was like giving them a 25% share of the return, which seemed pretty fair.

And if they performed the task well, I might even consider giving them performance bonuses later.

Not to mention, the profit would eventually be reinvested into the castle to improve their lives.

(Yeah, let’s go with it!)

Once everything was set, I hit the [Create Quest] button.

[Are you sure you want to create this quest?]

[Warning: Once a quest has been issued, it cannot be modified unless you take it down. Once it has been accepted, it can no longer be modified or canceled!]

I paused to read the warning.

So basically, no take-backs.

I needed to be careful. Imagine accidentally adding an extra zero and paying ten times the intended reward. On top of that, the rule also ensured that I could never scam my own citizens by canceling a quest right before they turned it in.

(Tch, I’m not that evil, you know!) I muttered to the System.

Finally, I double-checked the quest details to make sure everything was correct before hitting [Confirm].

The moment I did, a scroll icon appeared in my vision. The quest text swirled into it, and a second later, a physical scroll materialized inside my inventory.

Its description perfectly mirrored the quest details.

Now came the next question:

How exactly was I supposed to distribute it?

[To issue the quest, either give it to a citizen or post it on a flat surface.]

[If you give the scroll to a specific citizen, it will be assigned to them only.]

[If you post it on a surface, that surface will become a Quest Board. Anyone who sees the paper can accept the quest.]

(Alright!)

I did as instructed, focusing on the wall.

A sheet of paper suddenly materialized on the stone surface, displaying the quest details in neat writing. A sharp DING! echoed through the room, not just inside my head, but loud enough for the goblins to hear.

Gopuro and Gopuran perked up, their heads snapping toward the wall.

They scrambled to their feet and hurried over to the notice, spending a solid minute staring at it.

"Ooo! Wara wara!" Gopuro exclaimed, his eyes widening and sparkling with excitement.

Gopuran was no different. The moment she finished reading, she looked just as thrilled.

The two goblins placed their hands on the paper.

[Ding!]

[Gopuro has accepted your "Wood Gathering Quest" quest!]

[Gopuran has accepted your "Wood Gathering Quest" quest!]

(Nice!)

Excitement surged through me.

(But wait a minute!)

It only hit me then.

The text on that scroll was written in English. How the hell could the goblins understand English?

(Is this the System's doing?)

(If the System can make the goblins understand English, then it must also have some way for me to understand Goblish, right?)
