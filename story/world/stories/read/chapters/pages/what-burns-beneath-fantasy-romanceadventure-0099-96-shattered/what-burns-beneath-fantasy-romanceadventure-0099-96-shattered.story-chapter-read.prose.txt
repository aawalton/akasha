My world froze, narrowing to wild panic and the image of a monster holding, reading, intruding upon, the most private thing I’d ever written. A page of heart-wracked emotion poured out during the most raw moments of my life. All my visceral, wholly inadequate ways of saying ‘I love you,’ ‘please have a full life without me,’ and ‘goodbye.’

And he was looking at it.

“No!”

I lunged without a thought. Surprised eyes flicked to meet mine as one hip slammed against the pedestal, sending it crashing down. Crystal cracked and shattered and the silver platter rang out as its contents scattered to the floor. The wineglass was much quieter, but perhaps much worse; it sloshed out at my impact, sending ruby stains across Velidan’s luxurious shirt as he bolted upright.

I barely registered any of it through the roar of blood in my ears. Furious heat surged through every vein as frantic hands ripped the letter away.

“That’s not yours!” I clutched the parchment to my chest. My entire body was shaking. Tears flooded my vision.

“You gave it to me!” he snarled back. Every shred of the pleasant veneer vanished, and in its place loomed a face dark with fury. Winestains bloomed along creamy silk, looking for all the world like trails of blood.

“Not that part!” My shout was thick and choked as it echoed through the room. “When something says ‘dear Durst’ it’s obviously not addressed to you! It’s obviously private! It’s not for you!”

“You handed me…” He took a deep breath, fingers sweeping up to pinch the bridge of his nose as both eyes slammed shut. When he spoke again, every word dripped venom. “Why do you have that?”

“Because I wanted to show you the rune, and I didn’t have anything else I could write on!”

The silence was deafening.

After a long moment of his simmering, dissatisfied glare, I realized what he’d meant.

I wrapped both arms around my middle, staring up in disbelief. “Oh. Why… why do you think?”

He didn’t answer. The quiet somehow became even icier and, not quite knowing what else to do- and desperately needing to chase away the tide of emotion- I knelt down. Cold stone bit into my knees as I began to clean the mess.

The Fae towered over me, still as death, while I wiped my eyes and collected fallen bits of food from the mass of broken glass. My heart thrummed an unsteady beat as I braced for more anger, perhaps even fresh threats, now that the pleasant facade had shattered.

I didn’t think I’d be the one to shatter it, though. Saints, how much did he read? I almost wanted to voice the question aloud, but wasn’t sure I could bear the answer.

It would assuredly be a cruel one, too. Pleasant, cordial and, saints, even playful were about to be replaced by the monster who’d terrorized me a dozen times, who’d threatened unspeakable torments and called me his ‘property’ just a few moments ago.

“Get up.” The words came out clipped, as if he was biting each one in half.

My shoulders curled, but I ignored the command. I certainly couldn’t leave the mess for Slip or some other goblin to deal with.

He stepped closer, crushing crystals beneath one boot. “Leave it.”

I swallowed, not looking up, and reached for the hunk of bread. “I’m sorry about your dinn-”

“Leave. It.”

“I made the mess, I’ll- salt!” I hissed, jerking back from the sting of a too-sharp edge of glass. A few drops of blood glistened against my knuckles, then were swallowed by a much larger hand. The bruising grip brought a swell of panic and sent me lunging upright. “Don’t!”

The Fae held fast, shifting practically against me as my shriek echoed off the walls.

I tried in vain to twist away. “Let go!”

“You,” silver flickered within his grasp, and the pain in my hand faded, “are the most disobedient creature I’ve ever met.” A hard shove sent me tumbling backwards into the chaise. After a pause in which I stared up, heart pounding madly and every muscle rigid, he added dryly, “Also the most skittish.”

I sank into cushions with a sharp inhale. Then, flexing my hand and casting a cautious glance upwards to ensure he wasn’t coming any closer, muttered, “It’s a recent development.”

Velidan chuckled. It was a soft sound, quite at odds with all the splintering chaos of a few moments ago, and seemed to pull some tension from the air. “Noted. Is your hip hurt?”

“My… what?”

One black brow rose. “You just ran into a rock, Brin, and you’re nothing if not fragile. Are you injured?”

The area in question was throbbing with pain and assuredly bruised, but I wasn’t about to tell him that. I didn’t want his hands on me, and certainly not somewhere so intimate, regardless of the intention.

But I doubted a lie would convince him, so I opted for a frown and an indignant, “I’m not fragile.”

“You nearly died in your bed this morning.”

“Well, who’s fault is that?”

“The point still stands.”

I spent a long moment letting my racing pulse settle, then met his gaze and said softly, “I’m sorry for ruining your dinner.”

He scoffed.

“And your shirt.”

Starlit eyes fell, examining the stains as if he was only just now noticing them.

“And your… er, fancy table thing.”

“They’re trifles, mortal. They hardly matter.”

My heartbeat evened out. I offered a tentative, weak grin. “I don’t know, it was a really fancy table. It was probably worth more than my entire house.”

I’d hoped to encourage the re-emerging calm with that, but instead his expression darkened.

I pulled my knees up, curling against the soft fabric with a sigh. My gaze fell to the glittering mess along the marble. “Did you… n-need to see it again?” The parchment rustled as my fingers tightened around it. “The picture?”

He was quiet for some time. I began to dread that he would return the conversation to my letter. My stomach churned and twisted as I tried to brace myself for that line of questioning.

Fortunately, it didn’t come. Just when the stillness became oppressive he rumbled, “No, Brin. I recognize the rune. Or a variation of it, at least.”

“You do? Really?” I peered up, hardly daring to hope for any kind of good news. “Is it… helpful? Can you tell me what it does?”

Velidan tilted his head to one side. Dark hair gleamed with the tinge of moonlight as it spilled along one shoulder. “I’d rather show you.”

“Show me? What do you-”

“You still haven’t answered.” His voice was soft even as that endless, glittering gaze held me with a palpable weight.

One hand slowly tucked the parchment into my pocket as the other traced uneasy patterns along the cushions. “Oh. Y-yes. Yes. I’ll go with you.”

He was silent for a dozen heartbeats. Then, sounding almost wary, “Are you certain?”

“This Pit place sounds awful,” I offered a tremulous smile that probably looked much more like a grimace, “but… look, if people I love are in danger, and if doing this might help protect them…” I shrugged, then finished simply, “It’s not a difficult choice. Of course I’ll go.”

Those fathomless eyes searched mine. “Mm. I suppose I should expect nothing less from you.”

My lips parted, though I couldn’t quite find an answer. I wasn’t sure if the statement was meant as an insult or a compliment, though his tone certainly implied it was the former.

Perhaps it was simply a comment on my intelligence, or occasional lack thereof. I sighed.

Velidan continued, very casually, “Well then, mortal, our first order of business is to ensure you keep sleeping.”

My nose scrunched. “I’m sorry?”

“We aren’t going to the Pit until tomorrow, Brin, and based on what the human lackey told you it sounds like Embriel’s ritual isn’t until late at night.” He smirked. “You’ll need to be unconscious for quite some time.”

“Oh. Well… I brewed some sleeping tonics earlier. I could take them when I wake up and-”

“That would be too unpredictable. You couldn’t guarantee you’d appear here in time, and I doubt you’d remain sleeping for long enough. I have a solution, but we’ll need to leave soon.”

“Leave?” I blinked up, almost wondering if I’d misheard. “Why? Where are we going?”

“To procure an item. And, well…” He drew closer, casting me half in shadow, and the little smile sharpened. “Since I’m dragging you somewhere very unpleasant tomorrow, the least I can do is take you somewhere nice beforehand.”

“Somewhere nice? What, here? In the Night Court?” My brows knit together as I searched the Fae’s face and, more concerningly, the sly curve of his mouth. “I’m not sure you and I have the same definition of ‘nice.’”

“Given your description of a ‘nice evening’ earlier, you’re correct." He chuckled. The sound held a distinctly wicked edge. "But have some faith, Brin. I occasionally know how to have fun.”

“I don’t think we have the same definition of ‘fun,’ either.”

That earned a laugh. When the sound died out he stepped back, grinding more glass. “Oh, don’t be so sure. Wait here.” And without any further explanation, he turned and headed down the hallway, leaving me in bewildered silence.
