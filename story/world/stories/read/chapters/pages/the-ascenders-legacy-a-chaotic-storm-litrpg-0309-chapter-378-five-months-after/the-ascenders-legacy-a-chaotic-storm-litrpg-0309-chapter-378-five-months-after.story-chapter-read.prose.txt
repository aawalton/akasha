There is something about living on the knife-edge—about the blood-rushing chaos of constant battle. It hardens the body, sharpens the instincts, and teaches one to survive by urgency alone. I lived that way for a year, and it turns out Master ThunderWind was right. Peace is a gift I no longer know how to enjoy.

The Crown of Ruining Storms

Storm Sect, Gordon.

2277.

Five months had passed since the Balance War ended, and so much had happened in that time.

After their victory, the political reputation and power of the Storm Sect had grown tremendously. Allies had swarmed them—sects seeking nothing more than the prestige of being associated with victors.

Nearly a dozen had joined their alliance, making them the most powerful coalition of sects in all of Gordon. It was political influence beyond anything Gordon had seen in living memory, and beyond that, it was military power.

None of those sects had produced a fourth transmigrant yet, but Aodhán knew it was only a matter of time. And when they did, they would belong to an alliance capable of protecting them.

Their enemies, meanwhile, had grown correspondingly weaker as the Origin Plane of Storm expanded, swelling with the power and influence they continued to accumulate. The Serenity Sect had retreated deep into their shells, the shrinking of their Origin Plane already evident in the diminished potency of their cultivators. The Earth Sect had lost two transmigrants, and the Illusion Sect had seemingly dropped off the map entirely.

Sects like the Blood and Glass Sect had nearly drained their coffers paying a truly ludicrous sum—treasures, precious metals, artifacts, and soul seeds—to reclaim Butcher and Seraphine Ilex. Resources the Storm Sect had promptly reinvested into rebuilding and crafting more pendants, fueling even greater expansion.

They were daring. But they had earned the right to be.

A week after the grand speech, the Sect held a massive burial to honor all those who had fallen in the war. There had been tens of thousands of them. All Mythics save two—Calamity Shen Gu, and a Calamity whose name Aodhán couldn't even begin to pronounce.

Many had wept that day. Aodhán himself had shed a few tears, especially when he was called up to say a few words in honor of Meredith and Gyatso, who had given their lives for the Sect. He had barely managed the words—but somehow he had seen them through, heart heavy, mind supplying an endless stream of things worth saying about the man who had loved him like a son.

The deaths of so many changed the outlook of younger disciples who had watched the war from the comfort of their homes. When the ceremony ended, a clamor rose as disciples begged to learn the Path of the Undying Saint.

That was Sophran, by the way. It was his title now, and it couldn't have suited him more.

He had hesitated at first—after all, it was nearly impossible to pass on one's path through teaching alone. Experience, perception, and innate understanding played enormous roles, not to mention the willpower cost of walking such a high-tier path. But Sophran, being the saint that he was, agreed anyway, reasoning that more vitality-based paths in the world were a gain, not a loss.

Nymera had also gathered a fair share of disciples, but while Sophran's path could still be pursued with a normal cultivator's willpower pool, Nymera's was so willpower-intensive that a single skill would render most unconscious before they could fully manifest it. The same was true of his own path, and Ashscar's, and Seamus's—the latter of which was more broken and twisted than any of them.

But without the burden of teaching, Aodhán had been free to spend the last five months doing something he hadn't done in over a year.

Resting. Truly resting.

He meditated, coached Crixis daily, read, worked on his runes, deepened his understanding of his skills and aspects—but he never trained, never fought, never delved a rift.

He simply rested.

And oh, how his body hated it.

Ever since his arrival in ÆFLYM, he had rushed from one disaster to the next, one looming war to another, with barely enough time to breathe between them. He had needed peace. He had needed tranquility, and he had been determined to find it even when his mind stirred restlessly and his body itched for the adrenaline of near-death experiences.

He had stuck to the program, forcing himself to appreciate a life without the shadow of death hanging over it—to live the quiet, ordinary life his mother had always wanted for him.

He hadn't quite succeeded. His mind and body were too thoroughly conditioned for violence. But he had found a way to cope.

A way that carried him back to the days before bloodshed. A way his mother would have been proud of.

"Say lightning," he called out, a wide smile on his lips as he angled the camera.

The courtyard froze.

Nymera, halfway through sipping from a chilled crystal goblet, lowered it slowly and stared at him as though he had finally lost what little sense the war had left him.

Sophran blinked once, then twice. "Did he strike his head?"

"I have struck my head many times," Seamus said from the bench beside him. "And even I know this is disgraceful behavior."

"It is not disgraceful," Aodhán said, gesturing proudly to the amalgamation of polished glass, carved wood, and runic metal he called his camera. "It is art."

"It is extortion," Nymera replied coolly. "You summoned us here under the pretense of lunch, then ambushed us with... whatever this is."

"It's called a photoshoot." Darien chuckled, having assisted Aodhán and Elder Gumi throughout the crafting process. "At some point you have to admit it's a remarkable tool."

"It's nonsense."

"It's memory," Aodhán corrected. "Now stand closer together—all of you. I want to take a picture."

Nymera's gaze sharpened. "I would rather stand in fire."

"You have stood in fire."

"I would rather stand in more fire."

Sophran and Seamus burst out laughing. Aodhán sighed.

It had been two weeks since he, Darien, and Elder Gumi had recreated the camera from his home world, and Aodhán had been quietly ecstatic ever since. When he'd first brought the idea to Elder Gumi, he hadn't even been sure it would work. But Elder Gumi, being the genius she was, had filled in every gap he'd failed to account for.

Admittedly, the camera was heavier than it had any right to be. Louder, too—its shutter sound was closer to a gunshot than a click. And the flash rune had an occasional tendency to zap people.

But it worked. Mostly.

He had taken a thousand pictures of the Sect since, cataloguing its corners and its people with quiet, contented obsession. But every time he suggested a picture with his friends, they refused. Every single time.

It was just a zap.

"You are Storm cultivators," Aodhán said, sweeping a glare across all of them. "Surely you're not afraid of a little lightning."

"We are not," Ashscar replied easily, twirling a crimson-gold feather between his fingers. "We simply see no point in willingly placing ourselves in the path of what you so casually call a little lightning."

"I will fine-tune it," Aodhán groaned.

"You said that yesterday," Nymera said.

"And the day before," Seamus added. "What you need to do is return it to Elder Gumi and let her have another go at it."

Aodhán sighed, unable to argue with the logic and unwilling to surrender the camera. Not yet.

"Alright." He raised a hand. "I understand. I will return it. But first—one picture. Just one, with all of you, because I love you all and I want to keep this moment forever." He let his gaze move across each of them, and his expression softened genuinely. "Don't you want that? All of us together, happy, alive—don't you want to preserve that for all eternity?"

He had aimed the words squarely at Sophran's softness, and it worked precisely as intended.

"Oh, fine," Sophran groaned, dragging himself to his feet. "We'll humor him."

Nymera glanced sideways at him. "Who is 'we'? Surely you don't mean me. I'm not falling for that sentimental trap."

"Nor I," Seamus said firmly. "Time is to be stolen or borrowed, not frozen. I want no part of that."

"Come on," Sophran laughed. "Humor him. He's been unbearably peaceful these past few months. We should encourage the behavior."

"That was an insult," Aodhán said, slowly lowering the camera as his arms began to ache—which should have given everyone present a reasonable clue as to how ungodly heavy it was.

"If you won't humor me willingly," he continued, shaking out his arms, "I shall have to unleash my aura and see how well you like that instead."

The mention of his aura gave them all pause. A moment later, Seamus rose from his bench with the weary dignity of a man making a sacrifice. "You know what, I find I've always wondered what it looks like to immortalize a moment in time."

"As have I," Ashscar agreed flatly, scowl fixed firmly in place. "I shall learn it now and never learn it again."

Aodhán smiled. "I'm not sure that's how it works. A concept like this tends to require multiple lessons."

Ashscar's scowl deepened into something dangerous. He reached over and yanked Darien upright. "Since you're so enthusiastic about immortalizing time, you should be in front."

"I'll take the back," Nymera announced before anyone else could claim it, turning a withering look on Aodhán as she moved. "One of these days, that threat will lose its power."

Aodhán grinned. "Then I'd better take as many pictures as I can before it does."

The five Specials arranged themselves in front of Varéc, who lay stretched in a patch of sunlight, thoroughly unbothered. Not one of them was smiling, save Sophran, who was smiling rather too much.

Aodhán didn't mind. He was too excited to care.

He hefted the camera to his shoulder, adjusted the lens, and peered through the rune-glass with a proud, satisfied smile.

"Say lightning," he ordered.

"Lightning," Sophran and Darien replied obediently. The others scowled.

Nymera extended a hand. "Can we not redirect it toward that ghastly device instead of—"

Aodhán pressed the trigger.

The flash rune detonated.

White light exploded across the courtyard. Lightning cracked outward in six directions simultaneously.

Nymera shrieked.

Ashscar dropped into a crouch.

Sophran and Darien crashed into one another and went down in a tangle of limbs.

And Seamus—Seamus suffered the full brunt of it. His body locked rigid. His eyes rolled back. His hair stood straight up like a crown of offended needles.

Then he toppled sideways, smoke curling serenely from his robes.

Silence.

Then Aodhán frowned, lowering the camera. "It needs more grounding runes."

"You think?" Nymera shouted, sparks dancing through her hair. Beside her, Ashscar patted a smoldering sleeve with controlled displeasure. On the ground, Darien wheezed with laughter as he disentangled himself from Sophran.

Aodhán barely heard any of them. His attention was fixed on the camera, waiting for the familiar whir that meant the flash rune had done its job—that light and time had been channeled into the memory crystal within.

The whir came a moment later. Excitement surged through him as he leaned close to the printing slot.

A few seconds passed.

The camera clicked, and a thin square of printing film slid from the side.

"Ooh."

He snatched it immediately, grinning as he raised it toward the setting sun.

"Is it done?" Darien asked, already crossing the courtyard toward him.

"It's drying," Aodhán said.

Nymera muttered something venomous under her breath, but she stepped closer all the same—curious, despite herself. The others followed, and soon they were all gathered around him, watching in silence as color slowly bled across the surface of the film.

Shapes emerged. And then—

"What?" Nymera's eyes went wide with furious shock. "I look like a fish struck by thunder."

"And I look like a corpse," Seamus said flatly, staring at the image with undisguised horror. "Is this what we have immortalized?"

Aodhán burst into laughter.

He had to admit—Seamus truly did look dead. The picture had captured the exact moment of disaster with merciless precision: Nymera, eyes blown wide with outrage. Ashscar caught mid-crouch. Darien already laughing before the fall. Sophran radiant and startled all at once. Seamus upright and electrified, a sacred monument to regret. And Varéc in the background, lifting his head with the mildest possible curiosity.

It was a terrible photograph by any reasonable standard. And yet not one of them could stop staring at it, reluctant smiles tugging at their lips as they studied the expressions they had each been cursed with.

Silence stretched across the courtyard—and then Sophran laughed. Bright, warm, and helpless, eyes fixed on Seamus's electrified image. His laughter broke the dam, and before long they were all laughing, mocking each other mercilessly, competing over who looked the most pathetic.

Aodhán joined in, pointing out that had they simply cooperated from the start, the image would have been considerably better.

He realized too late that he should have said nothing at all.

Because the moment he did, Nymera's eyes narrowed.

"Then perhaps," she said pleasantly, "you should demonstrate."

Aodhán's smile died.

"No," he said immediately, taking a half-step back. "I'm the photographer. I don't stand before the lens."

"Oh, but you absolutely can," Seamus said, rising from the ground with renewed and alarming purpose. "We love you so very much, Aodhán. We wish to immortalize you forever." He turned to Darien. "You know how to operate the contraption, don't you?"

"It's a camera," Aodhán said automatically. "And no, Darien does not—"

"I do," Darien replied, already laughing. He caught the device cleanly as Aodhán instinctively tried to pull it behind his back. "Take your position."

"No." Aodhán took another step back.

His friends—his enemies, as of this moment—refused to hear it. They fanned out around him with the unhurried confidence of people who had cornered far more dangerous prey.

"Just relax," Sophran said cheerfully, advancing. "It's just a zap."

Ashscar cracked his knuckles. Seamus smiled in a way that sent a very specific kind of dread crawling down Aodhán's spine.

"Friends do not do this to one another," Aodhán said, pointing at each of them in turn.

"Friends absolutely do," Nymera replied, sounding genuinely delighted. "We love you, Aodhán. We want to immortalize you."

He lunged sideways. Sophran, Seamus, and Ashscar caught him before he'd taken two steps, and together they hauled him forward without mercy, positioning him before the camera—the monstrous contraption—like a sacrificial offering at the altar of justice.

Nymera smiled wide, every trace of earlier outrage replaced by pure, radiant satisfaction. "Say lightning."

"No—"

Darien angled the lens.

Aodhán's eyes went wide.

The rune brightened. And then—

A notification pinged through Aodhán's mind. Not only his—through all of their minds, simultaneously.

Everyone stilled. A familiar, instinctive wariness moved through the group as they checked the message.

[SPECIAL NOTICE]

You have been invited to the ASCENSION of Calamity Edrick Ghaltris. Make your way to the Elder Sect immediately to witness it.

Aodhán read it once. Then again, eyes widening.

He had never witnessed an ascension before, but he knew enough to understand that being present for one was a privilege—a direct blessing to his cultivation, granted simply by proximity to the act itself.

Every thought of the camera evaporated.

He turned his gaze toward the distant silhouette of the Elder Sect and wasn't entirely sure when his body moved—only that it did, tearing through space in a burst of instinct before conscious thought had fully caught up.

He wasn't sure either when he noticed the others were already ahead of him, streaking across the sky with the deeply shameful speed of people who had very clearly left the moment before he had.

But it didn't matter who arrived first.

Because there would be more than enough insights to go around. More than enough to bring his self-enforced rest to a very abrupt and very welcome end.
