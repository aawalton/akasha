Ike found a road and took it. Before long, he began to pass other people. He kept his eyes down and his hands to himself, and no one else caused trouble for him. Most of them were mages, and most kept their Rank suppressed, hiding their true power. After a moment or two of searching, Ike remembered how to suppress his and joined in. Although he remembered mages moving with confidence, as if they owned the world, these mages moved cautiously, their shoulders tense and their eyes darting. It was only fair, he supposed. Anyone could be powerful now. Some young mages had shot up in power immediately, while older mages used to dominating via buying powerful skills suddenly floundered. The world was still reeling from the redistribution of power. No one dared to strut their strength anymore, in case someone else took offense to it, or someone once weak recalled a grudge they’d had, found their old superior lacking, and decided to act on it. Better to hide their strength; that way, even those who had shot up in strength might hesitate to attack.

It was pathetic. They should simply accept the results of their poor decisions. Those who’d bullied their underlings, thinking their superior skills unassailable, should have to face their wrath.

Then again, he didn’t really have a leg to stand on here. He’d run off into the forest rather than deal with people, and his go-to reaction when things went sideways was extreme violence. He wasn’t exactly a model for dealing with other people. Compared to his people-handling skills, bullying one’s underlings was practically ideal behavior.

A small town rose up out of the scrubby, rocky landscape, and Ike wandered his way inside. Still on the hunt for a drink, and with a bunch of heads to potentially turn in, he made his way toward the tavern. At the door, he paused, eyeing the lizard adorning the signboard. This isn’t going to end in a massacre, is it?

“Outta my way.” A large man shouldered past him, deliberately knocking him to the side.

Ike stared at the man’s back, then closed his eyes for a beat. Be civil. We’re in society. He took a deep breath and followed the man inside.

The tavern was an airy, dark space, with a high, deep roof and many windows to let the breeze in. Between the shade and the breeze, it offered a bit of relief from the oppressive dry heat outside. Mages didn’t need to drink or eat, but with the System available to everyone and -thing, and mana so sparse, many had taken the habit back up again, so both food and drink flowed in the tavern. On Ike’s part, it was more because his mouth was dryer than the desert he’d just walked out of. He was thirsty, and he wanted a drink. He had a few coins from those unfriendly men in leather, so he was going to buy himself a drink. And then he was going to leave, and no one was going to lose their head. He’d already decided, and nothing was going to change his mind.

The large man who’d bumped him was at the bar, so he settled in a table by the corner to wait for him to leave. The bartender handed the large man his drink, and Ike stood, wandering toward the bar to coincidentally get there just as the man left, but instead of leaving, the man turned and addressed the bar at large. “Y’all seen this?”

“Seen what?” a tough-looking woman with weathered skin asked, giving him a skeptical look.

He drew out a crystal and tapped it down on the bar. Nothing happened.

“I seen it,” the woman said dryly, and the other weathered men and women in the tavern broke out into laughter.

“Give it a break. Hold on…” Grumbling expletives to himself, the man fumbled with the crystal.

Ike, who’d paused at the sight of the man choosing not to leave the bar, rolled his eyes at himself and approached the bar anyways, giving the man a wide berth. The bartender gave him a look for not approaching him, but Ike held his ground, pulling a coin out of his new storage ring and tapping it on the bar instead. The bartender sighed, but walked over. “What’ll it be?”

“Something sweet, lots of mana.” He was a simple guy. He liked sweet things, so he got something sweet. He wasn’t going to drink something gross to prove he was tough. He already knew he was tough; why did someone else’s opinion matter?

The bartender nodded and began to turn, and Ike remembered his other quest. He rapped his knuckles on the bar. “Do you have a bounty board?”

“Me? No. The town does, in town square,” the man replied, as if it was obvious, then turned, getting to work. A mage-bartender’s work was as much potion-brewing as cocktail-making, and a faint trail of colorful smoke emanated from the back of the bar as he hunched over a tiny cauldron. Ike leaned against the counter, watching the man with the crystal but pretending not to.

“There.” Something clicked into place, and the man stood back. The crystal began to play some sort of recording, grainy and unclear, the mana scrambled. The man cursed again and adjusted the crystal, fiddling with some fine control.

The bartender returned with Ike’s drink in a hefty tankard. Ike nodded and handed over the coin, but the man kept his palm up. “Two coins for that one.”

Not one to be stingy with other people’s money, Ike handed over another coin and retreated safely to his table, away from the large man with the crystal. From the back of the room, he had the perfect angle to see as the man’s crystal suddenly snapped into place and began playing back clearly.

It didn’t show much at first. Darkness. A large shape at a great distance, hunkered in the dark. The image snapped away, and another took its place. Instead of darkness, it showed a steady stream of ants, constantly crawling over fertile ground. It snapped again. A massive fire. Again. Something indistinct, moving through deep water. Again. A flash of feathers, high in the sky. Again. A sizzling stream of white light, bursting between craggy rocks.

“Hey, it’s that dream we’ve been having,” the woman commented.

“Hold on. The good part’s coming…” the man replied. He reached out, hovering his hand over the crystal.

Another snap. A man’s face. A startlingly familiar man’s face. Ike jolted, standing up from his seat. No way. His face. It was his face. The man in the recording, looking directly out of the crystal—that was him. A him he didn’t remember, in a place he couldn’t recall. He searched the scene, staring into the background for something, anything. He was in a dark space, but there were details. It was indoors, but stone. A castle, maybe? An intricate carving wandered off from just behind his ear. His head eclipsed a pillar.

Ike leaned in, trying to make out everything he could in the split second. The other him smiled, a big, knowing grin as if he’d caught someone peeking, and then the recording vanished.

“Ryker managed to capture his dream, and there you have it. The seven divine skills we’re all meant to be hunting for.”

Ike pressed his lips together, slowly sitting back down. All of us? Don’t like that. It wasn’t possible to extract a skill from a living person or beast. Or rather, it was, but only in rare cases like his River-Splitting Sword. Most people didn’t know how to extract skills from living people, in fact—most didn’t even know it was possible. He had a skill that its former user had extracted from their living body, and he still didn’t know how to extract skills from living beings. If they were hunting him for his skill—the skill that was apparently divine—that meant they were after his head. He liked his head where it was right now.

“Who cares? The System doesn’t control me. Giving out quests isn’t its job. It needs to get off its high horse and go back to quietly telling me how many skills I have,” the woman opined, and a few others in the room nodded along.

“Ah, come on. A good old-fashioned skill hunt. Might be the very last one, the way things’ve been going. Doesn’t that get you all fired up? Plus, look at this guy.” The man tapped the image of Ike, which he’d pulled back up. “He’s barely got his chin hairs in. Still an infant, practically. You don’t need to go hunt some legendary beast; you can just kill this chump.”

That chump, sitting in the corner, took a sip of his delicious drink and arced a silent brow. You can try.

“He’s got a divine skill, dipshit. What part of ‘has a divine skill’ makes him sound like a pushover to you, huh?” the woman pointed out. Sitting back in her chair, she waved her hand dismissively. “Go back to the mines and try striking gold. It’ll be easier than this.”

“You’ve just got no imagination. Who’s with me? Come on,” he added, as the bar failed to rally to his cry. “He’s just a man! He could be anywhere. He could even be among us right now.” He swept his eyes across the room for dramatic effect, and his gaze landed right on Ike.

Uh oh. Ike slurped deeply, not wanting to have to abandon his expensive drink.

“Hey there, stranger. Come to think of it, you’ve been hiding your face this whole time, haven’t you?” the man asked, peering over at Ike.

All eyes in the space turned to Ike. Ike went from slurping to chugging. He finished his drink, then slammed the tavern down. “What of it?”

“Nothing, it’s just… I happened to watch an interesting dream recording a moment ago. Got me wondering about your face, there. It’s not real friendly to hide your face, don’t you think? Come on. Let’s see those baby blues.”

“Nah.”

The man stepped forward. All around the tavern, men and women subtly adjusted their hands, reaching for their weapons. “Is that so?”

“Sure is.”

“You don’t think it’s a bit warm under that hood? We’re not in the sun anymore, are we? No need to keep it on.”

“Think I will.”

The man rested his hand on his pommel, a clear threat. “I don’t think you will.”

Ike sat back. He looked at the man, then at the tavern. He sighed aloud. I decided not to kill everyone. Stick to it. He reached down.

Swords shifted. The man’s hand slid around his pommel and gripped the hilt instead.

Ike lifted his hands, showing that they were empty. He reached toward his storage ring and drew out a man’s head, setting it on the table in front of him. The man stumbled back, startled, but Ike wasn’t done. He drew out another head, and another, and another, until the table was full of them, nothing but severed heads staring blankly the man’s direction.

Slowly, Ike looked up. He gestured. “I’m looking to turn in a bounty. Are you going to help me, or will you get out of my way?”

The man stared. Ike swung his arms over the table, sweeping the heads back into his storage ring, then stood. No one budged. Not a single man or woman in the tavern moved an inch. Casually, Ike walked to the bar, took the crystal, then turned and strode out into the sunny day. No one, not even the man, dared to impede him.

“What if he was the one?” Ike heard the man ask behind him.

“I don’t know, but I know I ain’t messing with the guy with a dozen heads in his pocket,” the woman replied, to a general grumble of agreement.

Ike snorted. Smart woman.

He took a deep breath. Everyone had the quest, huh? And his face was front and center in the advertisement. That 1/7 on quest completion wasn’t a head start; it was a death sentence.

His lips curled up into a grin, and he chuckled to himself. Fine! So be it. If the entire world was against him, then he’d fight all of them. It was a challenge he welcomed. Not just this quest, but the whole world could become fodder for his growth.

Let’s go find the rest of these skills. With determination in his heart, he set off, leaving the tavern and the hideous beast called civilization behind.
