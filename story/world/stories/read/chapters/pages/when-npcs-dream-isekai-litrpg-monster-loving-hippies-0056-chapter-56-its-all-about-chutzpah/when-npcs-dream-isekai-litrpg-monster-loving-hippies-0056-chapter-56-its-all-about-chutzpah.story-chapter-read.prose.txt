“Based on everything we’ve learned, the bamboo are such strong controllers that once they arrive here, they’ll easily spread control spores—through birds and animals. They’re using the kingdom’s resources to build the airship.  Our estimates say they could succeed in 7 to 11 weeks.

We must stop them.

One of our biggest advantages is that bug-people are immune to their control. Even better—bug-people can detect the spore signature emitted by controlled organisms. They know who’s compromised.

“Shooya, would you like to tell us about your experience with Uul?”

“You… mean… me?” she mumbled.

“Yes,” Juliette smiled. “You.”

“Oh. Okay,” she said.

Shooya rose slowly and walked to the raised floor.At first, she looked nervous, confused. Her antennae twitched, her eyes darted. She peeked at the crowd, and her eyes suddenly went wide.

Shooya was always a little weird—I knew that—but now she seemed especially weird. Was she embarrassed to speak in front of so many people? She didn’t really have to do this.

She stepped forward, clasping her trembling hands. Her voice shook—then rose, dramatic and bright.

“Oh, friends of the Stormcrow!” Her voice suddenly turn dramatic and bright. “You cannot imagine the torment I faced beside Uul—that pink, floating menace of fluff and pride!” She gestured skyward, as if the creature still hovered above. “For weeks he mocked me, scolded me, left me cold and small beneath the stars! I thought myself cursed to wander forever!”

She straightened, pacing in tiny, twitchy circles.

“He called me slow. Called me simple. Mocked my dreams and hopes! Oh, he’d float above me, smug as the moon, while I trudged through mud and storm! And when I dared speak from the heart, he would—oh, the audacity!—he would yawn!”

Her voice softened. Her eyes glistened.

“But then—you found me. You spoke kindly. You shared your fire. You made room for me among your wings.”

She bowed, her voice trembling with joy.

“Uul gave me nothing but wind and loneliness… but you, Stormcrow—you gave me a home.”

Shooya abruptly stopped, then immediately turned and walked back to the now very quiet—and thoroughly confused—crowd.

“…Yes,” Juliette said, hesitantly. “Thank you, Shooya, for… sharing.”

If they sail, we lose. Plain and simple.
