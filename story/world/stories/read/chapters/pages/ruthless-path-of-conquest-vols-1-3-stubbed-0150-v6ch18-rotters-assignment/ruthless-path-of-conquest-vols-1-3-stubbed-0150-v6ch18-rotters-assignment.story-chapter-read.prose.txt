As the Emperor gave his informal speech, Jeremiah Rotter began working on his assignments.

There was the task that James had given to all of his councillors and another task that he had assigned to the Council Secretary before this, which Jeremiah implicitly understood was frankly more important.

Jeremiah resumed the conversation that James had broken off with the Taiwanese delegation before.

“So, what are things like in your neck of the woods, honorable representatives of the people of Taiwan?” he asked. “Besides turf warfare with those filthy Communists.” He knew he was laying it on a little thick, but Jeremiah felt fairly confident that this was the right side to err on.

“The System’s descent was a disaster for Asia as it was for the rest of the world,” Zhang Chāo replied carefully. “The circumstances of our island have been nothing special in that regard.”

“Forgive me for prying, and tell me if it becomes burdensome to listen to me, but are there other regions where special things are happening?” Jeremiah pressed. “Or anything that would be of interest to the Fisher Emperor? Our information networks appear, admittedly, inadequate compared to yours, mainly due to my own deficiencies as a servant.”

“Is that really so?” Zhang Chāo asked bluntly, narrowing his eyes at Jeremiah. “It seemed to me as if, when I introduced myself, the Emperor himself took in that information for the first time… but you already knew who I was.”

Jeremiah felt beads of sweat suddenly appear on the back of his neck.

“Yours was one of the names I had heard before today’s Summit,” he admitted, deciding to be as honest as possible. “But the fact that I knew your name doesn’t mean I knew much else. Not enough to specifically direct the Emperor’s attention in your direction as a Ruler of interest. This is why I’m humbly asking you for news of the world.” He forced his lips into an awkward, embarrassed smile. “The truth is, I’m like a blind man, hoping I can persuade a sighted man to tell me what he sees.”

Zhang Chāo nodded thoughtfully, but he seemed to have taken the wrong thing from Jeremiah’s confession.

“So, I wasn’t important enough to be a matter for the Fisher Emperor’s attention,” he said, almost to himself. “Quite fair. We have not yet begun to live up to our potential as a nation.”

Jeremiah’s eyes went wide. He wondered if Zhang Chāo’s interpretation of what he had said would wreck relations between the two kingdoms for the foreseeable future.

Did I just cause an international incident?

Zhang Chāo locked eyes with him. “If James wants you to fish for information, that’s all very well. The truth is, I would prefer for us to eventually come to a place of transparency with each other, but we both have things we are hiding and reasons to hide things. That’s the way the game is played. Neither he nor I set the rules. It has been this way since time immemorial. However—” He gestured at James, who was still standing up on the stage—“your Emperor has already demonstrated good will towards me and mine. He went out on a limb. I can at least give you some basic news of the world, if you’ve truly had trouble gathering information.”

Jeremiah nodded and kept his mouth closed. Zhang Chāo had a way of reading into things he said that Jeremiah did not entirely like. Zhang Chāo at once absorbed too much accurate information that Jeremiah had not intended to convey, and also—more dangerously—speculated and came to firm conclusions from ambiguous statements even where there were leaps in logic required to get there.

Any words Jeremiah spoke from here on would have to be weighed more carefully, bearing in mind this tendency of the Ruler to read between the lines so thoroughly.

“Let me start by telling you about a rather disturbing incident that occurred in my neck of the woods, but over two thousand kilometers away,” Zhang Chāo said. “I will tell you everything I’ve gathered and all that has been done in the meantime. A little over a month ago, the main island of Japan sank…”

Zhang Chāo began an explanation that sounded so far-fetched that if Jeremiah wasn’t aware that he was in a room half filled with impossible life forms, he wouldn't have believed it.

The Ruler began by recounting the events of the day leading up to the island sinking, then explained how he had received word of the disaster. He was a good storyteller, but by the time he got to the point of sending boats out to investigate the aftermath, James had already finished speaking and was on his way back to the group.

I’ve made no progress in any of my assignments, Jeremiah thought.

“Wait, I think you may be burying the lead, Your Majesty,” he interjected. “What exactly is powerful enough to sink an island like the main island of Japan? And why isn’t this disaster the talk of the whole room?”

“We still don’t truly know,” Zhang Chāo replied, brow furrowed. “But we have clues. The crews that reported back to me found traces of an otherworldly creature floating in the waters. As if some monster had shed its skin after it finished growing into a new stage of development.” Zhang Chāo shuddered. “These pieces of the monster didn’t resemble any creature that existed on Earth before the System. I’d stake my life on it.”

“The poor Japanese civilians,” Mina said. “Were there any survivors?” She hadn’t been noticeably paying attention to their conversation while James was speaking, but now that he was off the stage, she seemed intensely interested.

“Lots of them,” Zhang Chāo said, smiling slightly. “The Japanese are a resourceful people. All those you see wearing armor that mixes magic and scientific advances? They’re all Japanese. Their equipment allows them to survive in the ocean. There are others who underwent Race Evolutions.” He pointed out two Mermen and a Kappa. “Most of the island sank slowly enough that a hundred different survival plans were spawned, though Tokyo was completely obliterated and full of strange life forms that seemed to have been mutated by the unknown monster. Even mountains were leveled. The one thing none of the survivors wanted to do was run off to mainland China.” He shook his head with a small, bitter smile, then became serious again. “However, we were happy to invite them onto our ships to stay in Taiwan indefinitely.”

“How generous of you,” Jeremiah said.

“Not at all,” Zhang Chāo replied. “Merely repaying a debt. We have a tangled history with the Japanese, but they fought alongside Taiwan and the United States in the War of Mainland Aggression, and we owed them for that.”

“You honor your history admirably,” James said, suddenly speaking from directly behind Jeremiah.

I really did fail at gathering any useful information while he was on stage, Jeremiah thought. Sorry, James…

“If we forgot all the debts we owed, who would trust us and back us after that?” Zhang Chāo replied evenly. “Best to behave with some semblance of honor as best we can and then tailor our approach to the response we receive.”

“Any intelligence on that monster that sank the island?” James asked. “Seems like it would be quite a menace to have lurking in the Pacific, but maybe if two guys like you and I put our heads together, we would find a good method to hunt it.”

You’re insane, Jeremiah thought. This thing sank a fucking island. The world is mad…

Strangely, it felt almost plausible that James could make a difference in a fight against such a creature, though. The Fisher Emperor’s madness must be contagious.

“The System doesn’t allow things that are too powerful to exist in the same space as a nascent universe,” Zhang Chāo said. “There have been no signs of continued activity. So our best guess is, immediately after shedding its skin and laying its egg, the life form was registered by the System as too powerful to occupy space on Earth. Then it was either erased from reality or transported to another dimension of some sort. Either solution seems to be well within the power of the System as we know it.”

“That’s a relief,” Jeremiah said. “Wait, what’s that about an egg?”

“You think there’s some version of the monster still on Earth, then?” James asked, sounding even more intrigued.

“You noticed it earlier yourself, James,” Zhang Chāo replied with a slightly teasing smile. He tilted his head to indicate a creature in the distance.

Jeremiah had observed it already but had consciously avoided looking too long. The monster that Zhang Chāo seemed to be indicating had a strange, many limbed appearance, and its body was difficult to comprehend with a direct glance. Just staring straight at it gave Jeremiah a headache, and after looking, he found he had no clear idea of what he’d seen, other than understanding that its flesh was of no color he had ever encountered before. Only by observing it through peripheral vision had Jeremiah gained a clue of what it looked like, and if he had to describe its appearance, it would be something like “beaked tentacle-monster with too many limbs and impossible asymmetries about its body.”

How long has Zhang Chāo been watching James? Jeremiah thought. Since the moment we arrived? And he seems to have taken an interest in the Emperor before then, even. Why? Jeremiah would have to check his recordings for clues as to Zhang Chāo’s intentions once he returned to the Fisher Empire. He had a digital tape recorder he’d been using to memorialize every interaction near him, since he could not use his other Skills to record.

“That thing’s a juvenile?” James whispered, the words tumbling out in one noticeably nervous breath.

Zhang Chāo gave him a slightly shaky smile in return. “The body parts match the traces I’ve seen,” he said. “To the extent that one can interpret them visually at all. If you wanted to hunt something particularly dangerous, it might be a good target. Before it reaches adulthood and tries to sink Australia.” The Ruler seemed to be aiming for humor, but his tone fell completely flat by the end. Zhang Chāo must have known that the joke he was making was far too close to the truth—and that his own island was at far more immediate risk than Australia.

“Why don’t we take a walk together?” James suggested. “We should meet more people—more Rulers, since you’ve just gotten me to make enemies with someone who, by all accounts, has something like a million followers?” He spoke the words in a friendly tone, but his gaze was sharp. Mildly accusatory.

Zhang Chāo seemed to let the words roll off his back. “Indeed. Why don’t I introduce you to my Japanese friends first? When you take in a few thousand refugees, those who remain behind often favor you a bit more than they might otherwise…”

The Fisher Emperor and the Heavenly King walked off together, leaving Jeremiah and James’s other followers to mingle with the members of the Taiwanese delegation. Jeremiah and Harry Luntz secured an agreement in principle that the Fisher Empire could supply the island of Taiwan with some of its surplus agricultural produce.

Then Jeremiah excused himself, leaving Harry to work out the finer details of what would hopefully become a key economic arrangement. If the Empire began to have a flow of commerce, with System Credits entering and leaving the economy, it would stimulate more development than they had experienced thus far. They had relied far too much on James’s powers up to the present, and if they could use the agriculture that his abilities nurtured as a foundation, hopefully they would be able to build an economy that would not lean so heavily on one man’s Skills.

Jeremiah, not being an economist or an agriculture expert, knew that he had nothing more to contribute to that, though. He had already greased the wheels a little. A bit of flattery here, a bad joke or two there… He was good at smoothing interactions. Inoffensive. Always ready to please.

Now I can spend a little time pursuing the Emperor’s other objective, Jeremiah thought. Time to try and figure out who’s nullifying everyone’s Skills.
