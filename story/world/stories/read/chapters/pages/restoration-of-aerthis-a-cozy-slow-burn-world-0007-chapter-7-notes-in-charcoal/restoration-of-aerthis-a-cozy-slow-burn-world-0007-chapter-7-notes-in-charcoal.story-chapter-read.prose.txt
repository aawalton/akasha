The pale morning light filtered gently through the dormitory window, soft and cool as it brushed across the wooden floorboards. Dust motes floated lazily in the air, caught in the sun’s gentle reach, and the world beyond the small panes of glass felt muted and motionless, as though it had not quite remembered how to wake up yet.

Elynara lay on her side for a while, letting the warmth of the blankets cocoon her just a little longer. Her limbs ached faintly, the ghost of yesterday’s tension still clinging to her muscles, but it was the good kind of ache. The kind that came after getting something done.

Eventually, she stirred, pulling herself upright and swinging her legs over the edge of the bed. Her fingers curled in the worn linen of the sheet, grounding herself in the morning quiet.

She reached for her notebook, tucked safely under her folded tunic on the small table beside the bed.

Opening to the first blank page, she took a slow breath. The paper had a slightly coarse but soft grain under her fingertips. Her pencil hovered for a heartbeat.

Then the words came.

Entry One – Reflections

I woke up in a new world, in a forest I don’t remember walking into, with a name I barely received and the past I knew I had, yet of which only scant few scattered memories remain.It should have been terrifying. Sometimes it still is. But more often, it feels like… possibility.I’ve been cold, confused, and embarrassingly reckless.

Afraid I will keep making mistakes I don’t see coming.

But it’s not all bad.I’ve also met many kind people here.

A patient and kind priest, who sheltered me with no trouble. Loud, curious orphanage children, always happy to drag me somewhere to play. And a green puffball that just won’t stop getting into trouble.

…It’s almost adorable, in its own silly way. But I would never tell it that, oh no!

There’s also Pufflet, a little fluff creature I only saw from afar. It looks like a living fur pillow with ears, waddling around acting like it owns the place. I can’t decide if I want to hug it or keep my breakfast away from it. My little floaty companion seems mighty suspicious of it.

Honestly, I am a little too – it’s just too cute!

So fluffy too!And somehow, in the middle of it all, I’m starting to feel like I belong.

I might not know exactly what tomorrow will bring...

…but I definitely want to see it.

Elynara paused, brushing her fingers against the edge of the page as she glanced at the window. The morning was still quiet outside, only just mildly interrupted by the faint sounds of life beginning to stir within the church. No rush to get anywhere yet.

She turned to the next page and let the pencil move again.

Entry Two – Mana

What I know so far: - My base generation seems to be 1.0 mana per hour. - Environments add to it. Near the altar and in the forest, it was stronger. In the town, much weaker (around 0.1/h). - Overfilling my core is possible. It’s uncomfortable. Useful for training? Probably. - Mana can be shaped into orbs, rings, strands. Holding it takes effort. Releasing it too fast burns a little. - Trying to ignite it works. Friction-like compression seems to do something. Will test more.

- Shaping is getting easier each time I try, and it’s fun seeing what I can do. - My sprite (Starpuff) likes playing with shaped mana. Maybe a sign of attunement? Is that even a thing? Or just mischief. Probably both.I look forward to having my staff made, it’s supposed to help me focus mana better.

Can’t wait to see how much easier handling the mana will be with it!

Starpuff buzzed softly near her ear, a soft hum like a question and a purr rolled into one.

Elynara smiled and shifted the notebook slightly, letting the green puff of mana settle on the upper corner of the page. It perched there like a scribe’s assistant, peering intently at her writing.

“I hope you’re not grading this.”

Starpuff bobbed in response, clearly delighted with its new role as a silent overseer.

She let her hand fall into her lap for a moment, watching the little spirit. So much had changed, and yet… the green glow beside her felt like the one constant. Strange, how something so small and wordless could ease the edge off solitude.

“Thanks for sticking around.”

With one last glance at the open notebook, she added a final line to the second page.

Need to remember: this world is vast, and I’m still small in it. But small isn’t helpless. And today, I take the next step.

She closed the notebook gently, smoothing her palm across the cover. The pages inside might have held little more than just notes still, but they were her anchor now. Her memory, if hers ever failed her again. Her proof that she was here, and learning, and growing.

With a small breath and a lighter heart, she rose from the bed, brushing her hair back from her face as Starpuff floated up and spun once in lazy encouragement.

Time to greet the day.

The warm scent of porridge and fresh bread met Elynara as she stepped into the dining hall, still holding the notebook close to her chest.

The morning bustle of the church had fully begun. Soft clinks of wooden bowls and muted laughter drifted across the space, though it retained the same gentle hush that seemed to blanket every part of the building.

A few children sat hunched at the end of the table, too sleepy to be rowdy just yet, their heads bobbing between bites. One of the older nuns moved quietly between them, refilling cups and setting down new loaves.

Father Alaric stood near the hearth, ladling out bowls with practiced ease. Without saying anything, he nodded slightly upon spotting her and handed one over.

She accepted it gratefully, the warmth from the bowl sinking into her fingers.

“Off to find your staff today?”

She nodded, spooning a bit of the thick porridge into her mouth before answering.

“A guard acquaintance offered to guide me. I’m meeting him soon.”

“That is reassuring to hear. I wish you a safe and fruitful search.”

After those words of encouragement, Alaric took a seat across from her with his own bowl in hand.

Starpuff, having found a perch on the back of her chair, bobbed once at Alaric like it was proud of her too. Elynara stifled a giggle at that.

By the time she finished her breakfast, the sun had crept a little higher across the windows. The rest of the town was stirring now.

She stepped outside into the cool morning air with a light heart and a slightly nervous stomach. The cobbled streets were just beginning to fill.

Shopkeepers tugged open shutters, stretching and muttering to themselves, while carts rumbled down the main roads in lazy procession. A few early risers haggled over baskets of vegetables, the low din of bartering blending into the rhythm of hoofbeats and bootsteps.

Elynara made her way back to the old well near the square, the guard’s promised meeting spot just ahead. She arrived a little early, pacing a slow circle around the stone well’s edge. The rising sun painted the rooftops gold and lit the market’s colorful awnings in soft pastels.

It wasn’t long before a familiar yawn cut through the air.

Josh emerged from around the bend, tugging on one glove with the half-hearted stubbornness of a man dragged out of bed a bit too soon. His cloak hung askew on one shoulder, and his hair looked like it had lost a brief battle with his pillow.

Seeing her his expression instantly shifted and he greeted her with a mock-grumbling tone.

“Goddess above. You’re even more punctual than Sister Elara. Should I be worried?”

Elynara folded her arms, playing along mock-serious.

“You’re the one who offered.”

Seeing his overly exaggerated wince almost made her laugh.

“I might have been sleep-drunk when I said that. You do know this isn’t a formal quest posting, right? No need to report in at sunrise with full gear and solemn expression.”

“I made a promise. And I keep my word!”

He laughed outright at that.

“Well, look at you. A lady of honor.”

He rubbed the back of his neck, then gestured to the street with a tilt of his head.

“Come on, then. First things first: you owe me breakfast.”

She rolled her eyes but followed with a grin of her own.

They didn’t go far, the food stalls surrounding the market's edge being only a short stroll away. Sweet fruit buns pulled straight from clay ovens, sizzling fish, and toasted grain were among the warm flood of aromas that greeted them there.

Elynara bought him a thick slice of flatbread with smoked fish and herself a lightly sweetened bun filled with dried apples. Starpuff snagged a crumb when she wasn’t looking and then darted skyward with a victorious hum.

As they walked, Josh munched happily and pointed out vendors as they passed.

“That one there, Kerral. Always overcharges for mushrooms, unless you compliment his beard. Don’t ask me why. This one – Marie – she’ll trade you pickled roots for any curious materials. Probably a hobbyist alchemist. Or mad. Bit of both, maybe.”

Elynara listened, half-smiling, her notebook still tucked safely beneath her arm.

He kept up the commentary as they walked deeper into the open square.

“See those crates? Those’re from the western coast. Salted fish and those weird crunchy sea cucumbers people pretend to enjoy.”

He leaned slightly toward her and added in a conspiratorial whisper.

“They taste like boiled regret.”

Elynara choked on a laugh.

“And down that road? Leads toward the quarry villages. Used to be they brought in raw stone faster than we could use it. These days, it’s mostly scrap hauling. They say one of the mines still goes deep enough to feel warm air rising through the rocks.”

Her eyes widened slightly at that, mind already conjuring half-formed questions about whether there is anything like geothermal mana or deep-dwelling spirits.

He noticed, and smirked.

“You’ve got the look of someone who’s already planning to sneak down there. Don’t.”

“I wasn’t…! Okay, maybe a little.”

He gave a theatrical sigh and pointed toward the southern road.

“That way leads to the farming villages. They feed this place. We send goods and guards in return. Small ones to the east are mostly herbs and orchard crops. Larger ones to the west, grains and root veg, mostly.”

As he spoke, Elynara felt the slow realization that the town wasn’t just a collection of houses and walls trickling in. That it was all living and interwoven with the world around it.

Well, duh, of course it was!

But one tends to forget the forest at the sight of the trees. It’s good to be reminded of it every now and then.

Done with her musings, she looked up at him as he finished a bite of bread and wiped his hands on a cloth.

“I didn’t know any of that.”

“Most folks don’t bother to ask. But if you’re planning on sticking around, better to learn what feeds the place you call home.”

The idea of it becoming her 'home' caught by surprise, just for a breath. She quite liked this town, and the people here were nice. But her own call for adventure, to learn all there is to learn about mana and more, would inevitably lead her away.

Yet in that particular moment the idea of settling down somewhere didn’t seem so bad. She nodded slowly.

“I think I would like that.”

Josh gave her a grin, less teasing this time, and far more honest.

“Come on, sprout. Let’s go find your stick.”

The town slowly gave way to open fields, the last few stone fences trailing off into tall grass and low brush. The forest waited beyond them, lush and sun-dappled, and far less ominous than it had appeared on her first day.

The road curved gently, and soon the trees surrounded them, filtering the morning light into shifting patterns of gold and green. Birdsong filled the air in a lazy rhythm, and the breeze carried the crisp scent of earth and moss.

Elynara walked just ahead, clutching her notebook and casting her gaze upward every few steps. The path beneath her boots was soft with fallen leaves, and Starpuff zipped ahead, already bouncing between low-hanging branches with barely-contained glee.

Josh strolled behind her at an easy pace, arms loosely folded behind his head. The notion of just calmly walking clearly didn’t quite appeal to him however. And after a while his light and amused voice broke the silence between them once more.

“Gotta admit. This is a first for me. Escorting a mage into the woods to look for a stick.”

Elynara turned her head just enough to give him a flat look.

“It’s not just any stick, and I hope to find several.”

“Oh, of course not. It’s a very special stick.”

She huffed, but there was no heat in it.

“It has to resonate with mana. The craftsman said I would know it when I felt it. Or… at least, I hope I will.”

“Solid plan,” he said, dry as sand. “Wander around until a tree whispers sweet nothings into your palm. Flawless strategy.”

She gave him a look of mock indignation.

“Well, if you’ve got a better idea…”

He laughed softly, brushing a low-hanging leaf from his shoulder.

“Nah, this is far more entertaining.”

Despite the teasing, his steps remained close, his eyes scanning the brush with the casual attentiveness of someone who had done this many times before.

“Let me know when you find one that hums with ancient tree wisdom.”

Elynara sighed at his lopsided smirk but his humor was infectious. Focusing, she stepped toward a fallen branch that looked promising. Kneeling, she ran her fingers along its length, then paused, closing her eyes.

A faint tingle brushed her skin. Not strong, but… something.

She reached inward, touching on the flow of mana in her core, trying to sync it with the texture beneath her fingers.

Nothing. Or rather, not enough.

She opened her eyes and gently placed the branch aside.

“No?” Josh asked, already crouched nearby with a hand on another piece of wood.

“Not quite. It’s hard to explain. It’s like trying to hear a sound that’s almost too quiet.”

“Try louder trees next time,” he said, deadpan.

Starpuff chirped and zipped in to poke at a nearby branch with unnecessary gusto. It jostled a few leaves loose, then darted to another one, clearly thrilled by the new game.

“You’re not helping.”

Elynara muttered to it, but the smile on her face betrayed her amusement.

Still, she took her time with each promising piece. She knelt beside half-buried branches, brushed the bark clean, and pressed her palm flat to the surface – listening. Feeling. Sometimes there was a flicker of something: a faint warmth, a slight pulse that mirrored her own. But more often, it was simply… just… wood.

Josh, meanwhile, offered his own kind of help.

“That one’s too warped. It’ll twist the moment any real pressure is applied. And that gnarled one? Snaps clean through if you breathe too hard.”

Elynara frowned as she straightened up, wiping her palms on her skirt.

“I never thought finding a stick would be this complicated.”

“Not just any stick,” he reminded her, echoing her earlier words. “Has to sing to your soul.”

“Oh, shut up,” she said with a laugh.

The tension in her chest had subsided despite the ribbing, though, and perhaps even because of it. The forest no longer felt like a looming threat, but a place to explore and learn. And that feeling alone was worth the mud on her boots and the ache building in her calves.

After nearly an hour of searching, she found the first one. A branch half-buried beneath moss, nearly a meter long, with a gentle curve and an odd smoothness to the bark.

The moment her fingers closed around it, it was like something clicked.

The mana within her stirred slightly and she knew she had the right thing.

“This one.”

Josh raised a brow.

“Looks like a stick.”

She gave him an incredulous look.

“That’s because it is a stick.”

“Well,” he said, scratching at his jaw, “don’t get sentimental. You’ve got at least two more to find, yeah?”

Elynara nodded, explaining her reasoning:

“One to choose from. One spare just in case. One backup for the spare.”

“Practical. I like it.”

Starpuff danced through a spray of golden leaves overhead, then dive-bombed a fallen branch with enough drama to make her snort.

“You’re going to break something doing that. And then I’ll make you carry the replacements.”

The sprite buzzed at her indignantly and zipped off again, clearly unfazed.

Her legs were sore and her shoulders ached from all of the crouching she had been doing by the time they located the second branch, a sturdy length with a slight ripple of mana she could feel through her fingertips.

The third came almost by accident, tucked between two rocks beneath a small overhang. She had to wiggle it loose, brushing away damp soil and old pine needles. But like with the others, she knew it was the right one straight away.

She stood, holding all three across her arms like prizes hard-won.

Josh gave her a slow nod, eyes scanning each one.

“You really going to make the craftsman choose?”

“No. But I’ll let him help me decide.”

He let out a satisfied grunt.

“Good answer.”

She turned towards him then, gratitude showing on her face.

“Thank you. Really. For coming with me so I didn’t have to do this alone.”

The teasing faded from his expression.

“You’ve got guts, sprout. Just don’t waste ‘em by going unprepared again.”

“I won’t.”

The forest around them rustled in the wind, the sun casting soft patches of light through the canopy. For a moment, it felt like the trees themselves were offering a quiet nod of approval.

And with her hands full and her chest a little lighter, Elynara turned toward the path home.

The walk back felt different somehow.

Although not much had changed in the forest, Elynara's steps were lighter now. Her arms were full, her hair damp with sweat, and her calves ached in protest, but she accomplished what she came here for.

The three chosen branches rested over one shoulder, bundled in cloth and tied loosely with twine the guard had offered from his belt pouch. Starpuff floated at her side, quieter now, though it occasionally flickered a brighter green whenever a sunbeam passed through the canopy.

Josh walked a few paces ahead, hands behind his head again, boots crunching through the underbrush in an easy rhythm. They had left the thicker forest behind some time ago, and the edges of town were slowly drawing closer with each bend of the road.

“You really think this kind of thing happens often?”

He glanced back at her.

“Hmm?”

“Bonefangs. Monsters like that. Is it… normal?”

He took a breath, eyes returning to the road.

“Rare? Sure. Unheard of? No. There’s always been mana-hungry beasts. Usually stay far off – closer to the cracks in the land, the wilds, or old dungeons. But sometimes, something pulls them closer.”

“Something like thinning mana?”

“Maybe. Or maybe just bad luck. Could’ve been hungry enough to wander too far.”

She looked down at her boots, the memory of those snarling jaws flashing in her mind.

“I was terrified. I didn’t freeze, but… I wasn’t brave, either.”

Josh chuckled, but not unkindly.

“You think being brave means not being scared?”

She glanced at him sideways.

“Isn’t it?”

“Nah, it’s doing the thing anyway. Even while your knees are shaking and your heart’s in your throat. Cowards just run. Brave folk tremble and still stand their ground.”

Elynara let the words sit for a moment. Then nodded.

“I think I understand now.”

“Good. Means you’re learning.”

They walked in silence for a while longer, the town’s rooftops beginning to peek above the tree line. A few birds scattered from a nearby bush as they passed, startled by Starpuff’s lazy spiraling.

As the trees thinned, the cobbled edge of the road welcomed them back to Oltenar. The town gate stood ahead, with a pair of guards standing in loose conversation nearby, offering lazy waves as they passed.

They slowed near the gate, and Josh turned toward her.

“Well. Guess this is where I hand you back over to the safety of town life,” he said, stretching once with a wince. “Wasn't half bad.”

Elynara smiled, exhaustion threading through her bones but not yet strong enough to weigh her down.

“Thank you again. For the company, the help… and the rope. Next time, I’ll buy you an actual meal.”

“Looking forward to it. Just no bugs. Or jerky. Or jerky made of bugs.”

She laughed, shaking her head.

“No promises.”

With that, she adjusted her bundle, offered him a grateful nod, and started toward the heart of town. Starpuff floated a lazy loop around her head before zipping ahead, no doubt eager for the next stage of their errand.

Josh watched her go, his hands sliding into his belt loops.

She was strange, that one.

Half the time she looked like she was going to float off into the clouds with her stars-in-her-eyes fascination with magic. The other half, she spoke with a kind of quiet certainty he didn’t often see in greenhorns. Listening and trying things out. And when she failed, she learned.

Josh thought back to her talking to herself, or her spirit thing? He always considered those village folks leaving offerings to some weird invisible things supposed to bring good luck and better harvest a mere superstition at best.

Though now he was having second thoughts. Only time will tell.

He scratched at his chin, watching until Elynara turned a corner out of view.

“Hope she’s got more luck than most. But if not… maybe stubbornness will carry her through.”

With a small shake of his head and a low chuckle about how he just went on an errand to find the perfect stick, he turned back toward the gate, his own duties waiting.

The craftsman’s workshop stood just where she had left it – nestled at the edge of the merchant quarter, half-wrapped in shade from the trees nearby, its stone and timber frame still exuding quiet solidity.

The scent of cut wood, faint smoke, and varnish greeted her even before she opened the door.

Elynara stepped inside, carefully adjusting the weight of the bundled branches resting against her shoulder. The soft creak of the floorboards and the scatter of wood shavings underfoot gave the space a warm, lived-in feel.

Behind the counter, the craftsman looked up from a piece of shaped timber clamped to his workbench. His wolf-like ears gave a faint twitch, then stilled as his gaze landed on her.

“You’re back.”

“I am. I think… I found a few good ones.”

He set his tools aside and came around the bench, wiping his hands on a cloth that had seen better days.

Elynara unwrapped the three branches with care and spread them out on the counter; they were all about staff length, different in hue and grain, but had that subtle hum that she had grown to recognize. Starpuff hovered behind her shoulder, watching the man’s movements with quiet interest.

The craftsman said nothing at first. Taking the first branch, which was smooth, pale and surprisingly light in weight, he bent it slowly to test its give. Then he let it roll against his palm and tapped it lightly with a small chisel handle, ears perked to listen.

He moved to the second, this one darker, denser, with a twisted grain that shimmered faintly in the light. Again, he ran his fingers along it, flexed it, tapped it.

The third followed, shorter than the others but clean and strong. This one he turned over a few times before raising an eyebrow.

“Well. You’ve got better instincts than most first-timers. All three of these would work.”

Elynara’s breath caught.

“Really?”

He gave a slight nod, the ghost of a smile tugging at the corner of his mouth.

“They would make different kinds of staves. But none of them are duds.” He tapped the third one again, the shortest of the three.

“This one, though… it will take shaping best. Grain is clean, weight is good, and it carries that mana hum all the way down.”

He paused, then looked at her.

“Do you want me to use it?”

She nodded quickly, trying not to beam.

“Yes. Please.”

“Good. Then I’ll shape it proper. Should take me a day or so to carve and bind it right.”

She watched as he gathered up the other two, laying them to one side of the bench.

“I can use the other two. Not for staves, but there’s always something to carve. I’ll take them off your hands, knock the cost down a bit.”

Elynara hesitated, then gave a firm nod. It’s not like she had anything else planned for them.

“Deal.”

Reaching for a small slate board as he grunted his approval, chalking something down – perhaps a rough design plan, measurements, or notes.

“You can come back the day after tomorrow. I’ll have it ready.”

“Thank you, I appreciate it.”

He just nodded again, already turning back to his workbench with a clear dismissal.

Elynara stepped out into the street once more, the warm sunlight a little brighter than before. The breeze caught her hair, and Starpuff zipped around her head in a lazy loop before settling down in her hair.

Like it was its personal nest or something!

With a little mock-huff of indignation, she ventured away, joining up the quiet rhythm of the town. The sun hung high but gentle, its heat softened by the breeze threading through narrow alleys and low rooftops. Wooden shutters clacked open as vendors set out baskets of late produce, and children’s voices rang from one of the nearby courtyards like scattered birdsong.

For once, she didn’t feel the need to rush anywhere. No teeth snapping at her heels, no looming tasks pressing in from all sides. Just a bit of quiet, and the gentle nudge of her own curiosity.

Elynara let herself drift from stall to stall with no real goal in mind, exchanging a few words with merchants and townsfolk alike. Some nodded politely. Others gave her small smiles, half-guarded but warming as soon as she spoke. A couple even recognized her as “the forest girl”.

Probably from the morning walk with Josh. The man really was well-liked around these parts!

Eventually, she made her way to the Adventurer’s Guild, ducking inside with a wave toward Alicia, who was organizing the front board with her usual brisk efficiency.

“Looking for another quest already?”

“Something simple. Just… to keep busy. Something in town, if possible.”

Alicia flicked through a few notices, then shook her head.

“You’re a little late. Everything light for today’s cycle is already taken.”

Elynara tried not to look too disappointed, but before she could turn to go, Alicia tapped a finger against the counter.

“…Though maybe there’s something else.”

She ducked behind the desk briefly and returned holding a small sphere of crystal, its surface somewhat translucent but now inert and dull, resting in a smooth metal frame.

“We’ve been testing a few storage solutions. This one holds about a hundred units of mana. I'll pay you from the guild's reserve if you can fully charge this battery, say… by this time tomorrow. Not much, but fair for the effort.”

Elynara leaned in curiously, gently picking up the crystal. It felt faintly cold and very empty.

“Just pour mana into it?”

“Quite. Slow and steady works best. Too fast, and it resists the flow so you waste mana. Think of it like steeping tea, not pouring wine.”

Elynara smiled at the comparison.

“I’ll give it a try.”

With a polite thanks, she slipped the crystal into her basket beside her notebook, then stepped out into the sunlight once more. Her path curved instinctively back toward the church, though she first stopped by the library the second she got there.

It was as quiet as before, and the nun at the desk greeted her with the same gentle nod.

Elynara returned to her little corner, slid out her notebook and pencil, and gently placed the mana battery beside her on the desk. With deliberate care, she channeled a trickle of energy into it, just enough to coax a faint glow to stir at its center. It shimmered a little but showed no other indication.

Starpuff bobbed around her hand as she began writing, occasionally landing right on the edge of the page with a weightless plop. She laughed softly and nudged it aside with the back of her knuckle.

“Not for eating.”

It hummed in amused protest but stayed nearby as she turned back to the map she had left unfinished yesterday. Line by line, she copied its winding trails, tiny settlements, and border markings.

The writing from her pencil smudged lightly under her fingers, but she didn’t mind. This made it feel more natural, as if she were creating something herself, a foundation no one else could lay for her.

By the time she added the final curve of the river and tucked the original map back between the pages of the borrowed book, her notebook had another page complete and she had no mana left to feed the now slightly brighter battery.

She allowed herself a quiet breath of satisfaction before packing up again and heading down the hall towards the orphanage wing.

The children were as always up to their usual antics, chasing each other between pews and hiding behind the wide wooden columns of the hall. One of the older girls was attempting to corral the younger ones into some kind of game, but their attention wandered the moment they spotted Elynara.

Within moments, she was surrounded.

“Big sis! Did you bring more stories?”

“No, play hide and seek with us!”

“Hey, hey, can you dance, big sis? I can!”

Laughter rang out as Elynara was busy fending off the overeager kids and their questions. Just letting the chaos and chatter swirl around her, her steps light and her shoulders relaxed as she followed the kids around.

Starpuff took that time to dive between them, zipping from hand to hand happily. Even being invisible to others didn’t stop it from having fun.

It took a little while, but after extricating herself from the kids, she turned to helping with a few remaining chores of the day: carrying in a small basket of folded cloth, untangling a length of twine, and helping a boy retrieve his shoe from behind the prayer bench.

All the while sneaking in playful nudges and teasing smiles.

Later, as the children settled into drawing or quietly playing with carved toys, Elynara found herself near one of the far walls, examining the crude etchings and faded lines she had noticed before. Flowers, stars, lopsided animals… and that odd, flower-headed figure holding a sword.

“You’re looking at the Flower Knight?” a voice said behind her, dry with amusement.

She turned to find one of the younger nuns standing nearby, a faint blush already creeping across her cheeks.

“You drew this?” Elynara asked, delighted.

“…When I was a child,” the sister muttered, pressing a hand to her forehead. “It’s supposed to be a hero. I think. Who defeated a bad mushroom king or something equally ridiculous.”

“It’s perfect,” Elynara said immediately. “I would be proud to have come up with a Flower Knight.”

The sister groaned and waved her away.

“You’re worse than the children.”

Elynara laughed and headed off before she could be drafted into more sweeping, the sister’s mock-glare trailing after her.

Evening arrived slow and gentle, with the smell of simmering stew wafting through the church’s halls. Elynara ate quietly, the wooden bowl warm in her hands, and shared a few words with the other residents.

Back in her room, she settled onto her bed, notebook and mana battery resting on the windowsill beside her. Starpuff nestled close, its tiny hum slow and content.

Elynara pulled up her status briefly, checking the values with a flicker of curiosity.

Mana: 4/23(+1.1/h)

The number comforted her, showing her small but steady growth. She focused on gathering the mana first, gently pulling her mana from surroundings, filling up her core, and pushing beyond and back again. She could feel the capacity growing by tiny degrees, the invisible threads inside her widening with each careful repetition.

By the time she let go, her reserves had grown to 25. Her breath was slow, her limbs heavy, but there was a smile on her face all the same.

Satisfied, she turned her focus towards just filling up the crystal with all the mana she could spare and gather. Up until the exhaustion of the day caught up with her.

Starpuff floated gently in the dark above her head, casting soft green swirls across the ceiling like lazy clouds caught in a midnight breeze.

“Puffy green clouds.” she whispered to herself, already halfway to sleep.

And then the room faded, soft and quiet, carrying her off with the promise of another day to grow.
