As it turned out, Lanaeus was not dead.

When the Cataclysm had hit, Lord Prince Gerald Halcyon and his strike force had been in the process of assaulting the location they had determined to be where central Grancrest operations had gone.

The resistance had been harsh, but Gerald had not been playing around. He’d approached it carefully, which was why it had taken so long. The disruption of the Cataclysm had affected the other side more than it had ours, probably because Gerald’s team was composed of a ton of high rankers, and they had managed to systematically dismantle the entirety of Grancrest’s leadership here over the course of the day without losing a single man.

I was impressed, quite frankly. I’d seen them in action during the events leading up to the Great Collapse and in the Federation incident that had also involved Grancrest, but those had both involved extenuating circumstances that hadn’t let them show off their full destructive power. When compared with our groups, who had struggled and taken loads of damage dealing with even their everyday forces, Gerald and his elite force had not only eliminated all resistance but also knocked out and taken Highmaster Lanaeus alive, alongside a Master-tier priestess who was apparently higher up in Grancrest’s leadership.

Their effectiveness made me reconsider just how many threats Liaren was up against, since they’d evidently had to be deployed elsewhere this entire time. Fortunately, their presence now meant that the plan could proceed mostly smoothly.

There was, however, one snag.

“What do you mean you can’t do it today?” Gerald asked severely. “You are aware of the situation, right?”

“Look, if you have a path into the Nightmare that isn’t fucked six ways to hell, be my guest,” I snapped back. “The Cataclysm dipped into it and messed with the whole thing.”

I’d tried soulwalking a couple of times today, each time hoping I would get a better result. It seemed like with time, the paths would open up properly again, but right now I couldn’t open a path more than about five feet from where my soul was, and even when I did successfully open one, my visibility was horrifically limited.

Astral Phantasm had also seemed like it would cost much more, so I hadn’t used it. All I’d done so far was confirm that Iryn was on her way to the elven kingdom, but she must have been stymied by the same thing annoying me.

I hadn’t realized just how impactful a single exterior force could be. While some change was to be expected, the Cataclysm had disrupted the Nightmare so fundamentally that it was nigh-unusable at the moment.

“So it was the Reaper, then,” Gerald mused. “I was wondering.”

Oh. Right. Other people wouldn’t necessarily be able to identify it immediately. I’d gotten a handy system notification telling me, but everyone else would have had to figure it out from news, which would be slow to arrive, or from intuition.

“Not a new one,” he continued. “That’s not a good sign.”

“How so?”

“The re-emergence of a Cataclysm does not count as a new one,” the Lord Prince explained, “which in turn means that the chance of the next new Cataclysm appearing is still climbing. We are due for one.”

That wasn’t very reassuring.

“In any case,” I said, bringing the topic back to the matter at hand, “I’ll keep checking, but I might need a few days.”

“Execute as soon as you are capable of it,” Gerald said. “I will prepare for the alternative otherwise. What do you want with these two?”

“Just leave them.” I opened the lids, observing both bodies.

The coffins were not magically reinforced by anything. Having them alive was more convenient for my purposes, since it meant that I would be able to harvest their souls at will rather than have to use Harvest Soul before their souls naturally dissipated on their own.

It did mean that I had to keep them from waking up, though. Both had been bound with seals that prevented them from using magic, but having been on the receiving end on one of those before, I knew better than most that the level of imprisonment they offered was imperfect.

As such, I spent my time holding an Anesthesia spells on each of them at all times, meditating and moving as little as possible to provide the best possible conditions for Soulwalk.

Each time I checked, the effects on the Nightmare had abated a little more. I still wasn’t confident in using Astral Phantasm to project myself, worried that it would do more harm than good, so I just kept overseeing Iryn as she quietly sped through wasteland.

Hours slipped by, and my body started notifying me that I hadn’t eaten or drank anything in a while. Gerald appeared to have accounted for that, leaving me tasteless rations that broke apart like dust in my mouth but nonetheless satiated me. It permitted me to continue both my watch over the two unconscious bodies and my experimental attempts to use Soulwalk to access Iryn.

One time, while I was checking in, she wasn’t moving quite as fast as the other times. It was almost entirely black out, but my Harmonic Awareness was functional within the area that I was capable of accessing.

Iryn wasn’t alone.

What the fuck? What was Locke doing here?

You know what, I thought to myself, I’m just not going to question it.

The Nightmare still wasn’t at a stable enough point for Astral Phantasm to feel like it was worth using, so I just watched as they spoke quietly about plans before continuing onwards, then returned to my body.

This cycle continued for three consecutive days.

Ren

More than seventy-two hours after the Cataclysm, the Nightmare abruptly returned to normal. At that stage, Iryn and Locke had already gotten well into the elven kingdom, staying stealthy all the while. They hadn’t finished whatever their plan was, so I assumed finishing it required the Nightmare.

The return to normalcy came all at once, as if a great pressure had borne down upon the divine plane and been abruptly released. Had the Reaper Cataclysm died? Or maybe just gone underground?

Whatever the case, Soulwalk’s paths all went back to normal and Iryn finished her part of the plan. During the cooldown period between two Soulwalks, the distance between the severed, preserved finger of mine and Iryn increased by nearly a mile. She’d planted it somewhere, though I wasn’t sure where yet.

From there, I rose from the position I’d been holding for over seventy-two hours, opening my eyes for the first time in days to see two powerful mages being held still by my Anesthesia.

I didn’t even feel anything in particular towards either of them. I didn’t know the one apparently named Ilena, and though Lanaeus had been an ass and captured me so I could be executed by Grancrest, so much had happened that I had little more than apathy to offer them.

That was helpful in a few ways, but most importantly, it meant that I felt nothing more than a touch of guilt when I stabbed both of them through the heart.

I will do good, I promised myself, but I will not hesitate in that process.

Growing too numb to killing was bad, I knew, but this was in service of good. There was no window for me to morally grandstand to myself, so I decided I would content myself with that promise.

I started my ritual.

“Harvest Soul!”

I wasn’t sure if it was because they weren’t monsters or because I had contributed less to killing them, but the surge of mana I got from harvesting Lanaeus’ soul was less than what the umbral wyrm had offered me.

Conversely, the priestess brought me way more power than I thought any Master-tier should have been able to give.

Buzzing off the overflow of extra mana, I slipped into a Soulwalk immediately, setting my range to a couple of miles. I knew I wasn’t going to be able to find Rivia right away, but I was banking on her soul being at least somewhat familiar to me because I’d seen it and even attacked it before.

The paths I had to access this time were significantly thicker than they’d been before. Something about the quality of the mana I’d imbibed had been different, and instead of just giving me more paths, I now just had more time in my Soulwalk.

I started checking paths. With the months I’d put into the skill, I was better at discerning what directions were favorable, so I sorted through a lot more than I had before. Bit by bit, I picked my way closer to my target.

Victory eventually came from an unexpected source. I couldn’t find Rivia through the Soulwalk on its own, but I did see her from another perspective. She was making some kind of public appearance, greeting a few dozen other elves that I guessed were royalty based on their dress and general aura of power. It was one of those other elves who I Soulwalked to, well within the range of Rivia.

I moved behind her and used Astral Phantasm for the shortest period of time I could manage, casting Mark Soul at the same time.

With the number of elves here in the courtyard of the palace, someone noticed me nearly immediately, naturally, and people launched attacks. Danger Sense flared in a dozen different directions, and my Astral Phantasm actually popped from the damage before I could take it back myself.

That wasn’t a problem for me. I still had a lot of mana, and now I had my target marked.

Watch out, Rivia.
