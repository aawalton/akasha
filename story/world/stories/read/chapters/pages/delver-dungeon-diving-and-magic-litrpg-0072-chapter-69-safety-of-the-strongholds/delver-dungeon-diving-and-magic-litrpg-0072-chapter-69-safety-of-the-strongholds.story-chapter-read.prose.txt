Hopefully by now, you’re well on your way as a Novice Mage. The Archmage is guiding you well, I’m sure, but the next step is by far one of the hardest.

Journeyman magic of three circles. Finally, you’ll have at your hands spells capable of piercing Rakatur Hide. Simplicity is a thing of the past. Here, spells gain names, and experimentation becomes even more dangerous. Do not push yourself to create your own spells without an extremely capable teacher nearby.

If that scares you, good. It should. Focus on your novice spells, let them become second nature. Don’t make the leap until you feel you’re truly ready for it.

Eridor’s Journal To His Sister

I stare over the open forest, sunlight filtering in through the leaves of the canopy. A familiar white stag looks back at me, easily as large as some of the trees. His herd walks behind him, munching on leaves as they lazily meander down their own path.

The Stag turns away from me, and continues into the distance.

I let out a breath. There were a few foals in the herd, and even prey animals can be extra aggressive when they have young with them.

I motion forward, and give a slight tap with my heels to the mare under me. Our group moves until a familiar house becomes visible.

An earthen wall covered in vines surrounds the house, and a gate that looks much better than the last time we visited sits closed with a simple latch.

I dismount the shadow-mare, giving her a soft pat on her side before making my way to the gate.

It opens for me, revealing the house and pristine garden. No one else is resting in the place that I can see, so I motion the others through. They ride through, dismounting in front of the garden as smoke starts to rise from the chimney.

“Commander Vakar,” I call to the Vishten as I close the gate, and lock it.

The tall man breaks away from a conversation with one of his men and walks over to me, “Yes, Leo?”

“Have you been in a Haven before?” I motion to the place around us.

He looks around the house, then shakes his head. “No, although I have heard of such places.”

“Make sure no fights break out, and that your people don’t harm the place.” I gently touch the earthen wall, feeling the faint presence of the spirit I’d noticed the last time we’d been here. “The Haven can and will throw people out.”

Vakar nods, “I will let Lady Kala and my people know.”

I give him a smile, then make my way over to Jasper, where Tanya is gently feeding him and several of the horses. A few owls escape from her tattoos, flying over to the branches of the fruit trees.

I scratch Jasper under his chin as he chews, then grab an enchanted arrow from the quiver I keep on him.

The Angel Statue looks better than when I last saw it, cracks in its stone having healed and almost making their form clear.

I kneel before it as I set the arrow at its feet. Then I bow my head, and place my hand against its feet, and send my mana into the earth.

The spirit takes the mana, and sends a pulse back that feels very much like gratitude.

I stand up, dusting off my knees, then make my way to the others. The mercenaries are chatting in a circle as Keve sits on the steps leading into the house, carving something with a small knife.

“Hey, Leo.” Daron calls out, breaking off from the others as I approach.

“Hey, Daron.”

“Do you think the Haven would mind if we added a few things?” A smile forms on his face.

I tilt my head, caught off-guard by the question. “What do you mean?”

“Benches.” Keve says, motioning to an open part of the ground where several of the Dralk are standing around.

“And maybe the start of a workstation. Figured with the four of us, we could leave them as thanks.” Daron motions to the group. “I saw some good logs outside, and they probably won’t be perfect with the time we have, but…”

“Go for it. Just take Shar with you to keep an eye out.” I look up at the sky, there’s about an hour of good daylight left, plenty for them to finish their project.

The mercenaries and Keve don’t need more than that as they head towards the gate. I whistle towards Shar, and motion towards them. She gets the message and flies out with them.

I head inside, where Eyla is sitting on the bed of the cabin, reading the notebook I’d given her.

Collapsing next to her, I stare up at the ceiling.

Her hand finds my hair, gently running through it.

“What are you thinking about?” She asks.

“The Strongholds.” I answer.

Her hand pauses, then continues moving through my hair softly. “What about them?”

“Growing up in that kind of safety, it must’ve been… different”

“It was. I didn’t see my first monster until I was twelve.”

I glance at her, “Really?”

She nods, and closes the notebook. “Do you remember Lorn’s story about the defense of the Stronghold?”

I nod. Most people had heard the story about one of the few incursions on Stronghold Deragun, when a corrupted beast had attempted to claim the mana of the place for itself. That was about twenty years ago, “You couldn’t have been twelve.”

She snorts, “No, I was barely a month old at the time. It turns out that we hadn’t repulsed the corrupted creature as completely as we thought.” Her hand stops moving as her eyes grow distant. “Some part of it lived in the mines with the protection of an undead witch it controlled. When it was ready, it pulled a Worm to it and tried to tunnel back to the surface.”

“I haven’t heard about this.” I say softly.

“The Worm was pushed away by the Stronghold’s enchantments. But it dug close enough to let the corrupted behemoth in. We were slow to react, and I saw the corrupted beast for the first time.” Her hand left my hair, as if reaching to something only she can see. “It had too many eyes, and too many mouths, each one looking to devour.” Her hand drops, and she looks to me from the corner of her eyes, “My Father killed it.”

“Your Father?”

She nods, “He’s no longer around, but he was supposed to take my Grandfather’s place before he died.”

I sit up, looking at her, “I’m sorry, I didn’t mean to push.”

She shakes her head, and looks down at her hands. Before anything else can be said, Lady Kala pushes the door open and steps inside.

I reach over, gently holding Eyla’s hand as the Dralk warriors enter the building, looking around curiously. She squeezes tight, her gaze going back to the notebook as she opens it with one hand.

I look at the two benches the mercenaries are working on. they’re simple things, carved from a large tree that had fallen and was carried over by Grildor.

Keve kneels next to one, a skill working as his knife moves effortlessly to carve into the wood. Grildor meanwhile happily wields an axe to turn what’s left of the tree into firewood.

A small firepit had been made in the grass out front, rocks placed in a circle around it, as Tanya cooks a large pot of food.

Keve finishes one of the benches, and I notice as a pulse of mana moves through it, and back into the Haven. The bottom of the bench sets into the ground as the wood smooths out, changing a rough, barely recognizable bench into something quite nice.

“Looks like the Haven likes it.” I say as I sit on the ground across from Tanya.

Daron lifts his chin and puffs out his chest. “What’d I tell ya?”

Grildor slaps him on the back, then immediately sits on the bench, “Yer a good ‘un, Daron.”

“See Libby? I think that means you should give me a raise.” Libby raises an eyebrow and looks at me.

“I’m already paying you guys too much.” I grumble as I pull a book from my bag.

“I too, wish for a raise.”

The group pauses as we all look at Keve, who looks back at me.

“You got it.” I smile.

“Hey!” Daron cries out.

The next morning, we gather fruit and herbs from the Haven’s garden, before leaving for the Stronghold.

Shar leads us through the dangers of the wilderness with increased confidence, and we are only forced to hide ourselves a few times before the walls become visible in the distance.

Lady Kala pulls up beside me as I pause the group to wait for Shar’s report. No need to rush things now that we’re so close.

“So that is it.” Lady Kala says quietly.

“Kolas’s Stronghold.” I glance at the sky, where clouds cover the sun. It’s not raining, but there might be some light mist soon.

“Are you certain they will welcome us?” The words carry a hint of worry that I haven’t ever heard from the woman.

“The Vishten are an honest people. And they know what it means to be hunted.” I glance over as Shar glides to us. “Give them your best, and they won’t turn you away.”

Shar lands on my outstretched arm, and a quick conversation with her has us leaving the path for an hour to avoid an aligned monster that has claimed the space.

Soon enough we’re back on track, the walls of the Stronghold moving ever closer.

I’m forced to hide us once more with the statuette, as a large bear comes looking for us, then we move forward.

The Vishten spot us as we approach, and I see more warriors gathering along the Stronghold’s edges as they take in the Dralk that accompany us.

I ride slightly ahead of everyone, taking point as we approach the gate.

I dismount the mare, giving her a small pat in thanks before walking towards the Vishten, Keve following me a moment later.

As we approach, I see bows drawn, hands tight on swords, and scarves raised to cover their faces.

Keve shouts out to them in their language, and their leader responds.

A quick exchange follows that I have no chance of following before bows lower, and hands move away from weapons. A single Vishten breaks from the group, running inside.

The Vishten warrior turns to me, and bows his head, “Explorer Leo, we are glad to see you.”

“It’s good to see you as well.”

He unwraps his scarf, then shouts something to his people in his language. “A group will come with you to guard the Dralk. Do not let them raise their weapons.”

I nod, then turn to the group who is waiting some distance away. I wave them over, and everyone approaches. It doesn’t take long for a large group of Vishten warriors to arrive. They fall into formation around us and escort us deeper into the stronghold.

Lady Kala stays riding her Elk, her head held eye as we are led to a large building.

The Stronghold has changed since I’ve last been inside. Although much of the inside is still forest, now tall buildings of stone and black wood stand in various levels of construction, many of them surprisingly far along for how short of time it’s been.

We’re led to the largest building, where a familiar Vishten Seer leans on her staff. Vishten warriors surround her, their scarves covering their faces.

Our entourage slows as we approach.

Lady Kala meets the eyes of Seer Varia and a staring contest lasts for several moments before the Foreign Dralk Lady bows ever so slightly.

Seer Varia smiles, then motions for her people to stand down. Scarves are lowered once more.

Lady Kala dismounts, Commander Vakar following the movement. The two of them approach the Seer.

Keve glances at me, then steps over as well to speak with his Seer.

I turn to leave, ready to finally take an actual bath.

“Explorer Leo,” A wisened voice calls out.

I freeze, then turn around to meet the eyes of the Seer.

“Please, introduce us to your new friends.”

My shoulders slump as I realize that I’m not getting out of this one. I take a deep breath, then walk over to the meeting of the two powerful women, leaders of their people.

I swear, I’m just a simple Delver.
