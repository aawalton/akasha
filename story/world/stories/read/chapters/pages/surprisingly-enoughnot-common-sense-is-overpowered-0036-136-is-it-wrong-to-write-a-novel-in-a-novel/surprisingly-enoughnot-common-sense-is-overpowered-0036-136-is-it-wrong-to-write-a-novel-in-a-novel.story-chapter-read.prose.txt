Spirit Gathering Formation.

Among all the formations used by cultivators, this was the bread-and-butter, the rice-and-lentils, the default option.

Every cultivation stage had its own variations, but the core principle remained the same: gather more spiritual energy into a fixed area so a cultivator could absorb it and further their cultivation.

Formations themselves were built from array patterns, the fundamental building blocks of the craft. The simplest entry-level formation might contain only one, but as more patterns were added, the formation became increasingly complex.

Basically, it leveled up like a Pokémon evolution. More patterns, more power, more effects... and more spirit stones consumed every day.

The Spirit Gathering Formation Ning had purchased contained four.

Ning placed the last carved stone into position. The patterns glowed faintly before connecting with one another, and the spiritual energy in the surroundings immediately began gathering toward him.

Compared to cultivating in the Black Boar Forest, where spiritual energy simply drifted through the surroundings, the difference was obvious.

Ning took a deep breath.

"Feels good."

After a full year in the outer sect, he had finally gotten the chance to use a proper formation. Feeling the dense spiritual energy gathering around him, he couldn't help but smile.

As far as Ning knew, cultivators could eventually survive on spiritual energy alone. He wasn't anywhere near that point yet, but breathing in such dense spiritual energy still felt strangely refreshing.

In truth, Ning had always been curious about formations.

Most of the sect's everyday conveniences depended on them. The lights, for example, used Bright Illumination Arrays connected throughout the sect in a way that reminded Ning suspiciously of a giant electrical circuit.

That alone was enough to interest him.

Spiritual energy was everywhere in this world, and formations were essentially tools for making it do something useful. Lighting, cultivation, protection, storage, transportation—Ning had barely scratched the surface of what they could accomplish.

Unfortunately, formations weren't something one could learn just by memorizing a few diagrams. From what Ning had heard, the craft depended heavily on talent and comprehension. Some people understood array patterns almost instinctively, while others could stare at the same pattern for years and still fail to grasp it.

Proper inheritances weren't exactly common either.

Still, Ning planned to study formations eventually.

That was one of the nice things about being a cultivator. If you lived long enough, kept raising your cultivation, and didn't die to some random nonsense along the way, there was plenty of time to learn.

Perhaps one day he really could become a jack-of-all-trades.

For now, though, cultivation came first.

Ning lit a stick of spiritual incense and let its gentle fragrance settle his mind. Once the last traces of restlessness faded, he sat down within the formation, closed his eyes, and began to cultivate.

...

“Elder, please take a look at this.”

Ning handed a book to Elder Shen, who was leisurely lounging in his chair.

“Hmm?”

Elder Shen glanced at the cover, where several bold characters immediately caught his attention.

Senior Sister, Please Behave Yourself!

“???”

The title alone was enough to pique his curiosity.

Back when Ning had been managing the pavilion, Elder Shen would occasionally ask him to bring over some of the more interesting books from the piles of confiscated literature.

Naturally, the elder insisted that it was purely out of curiosity.

He opened the book.

Swish!

The first page greeted him with an illustration that, while not quite R-rated, came dangerously close. The artwork was bold and suggestive, leaving just enough to the imagination.

Elder Shen coughed, studying the illustration with great “focus.”

“This illustration seems to have been drawn by a master,” he commented before turning to the next page.

[Senior Sister invited me to her Cave Mansion, using her status to pressure me into accepting. Helpless, I could only agree.

Who would have thought that upon entering her courtyard, I would find myself surrounded by a thick mist that obscured my vision?

Just as I was wondering what was happening, Senior Sister suddenly launched a sneak attack from the shadows.

Caught off guard, I fell straight into her trap.

Her methods were so treacherous that I had no choice but to fight back.]

While Elder Shen became increasingly absorbed in the book, Ning casually munched on the potato chips he'd brought along.

His purpose here was simple. He wanted another source of income.

As for why he'd chosen books, that was because most of his other ideas came with far too many problems.

As a transmigrator, Ning knew several ways to make money, from introducing clever inventions to proposing revolutionary ideas. Unfortunately, his common sense prevented him from pursuing most of them.

Take guns, for example.

Although Ning didn't know how to manufacture one himself, he understood enough of the basic principles to guide a weapon refiner in the right direction. With the methods available in this world, creating something similar shouldn't be too difficult.

But then what?

A mortal martial artist armed with such a weapon could potentially kill an early-stage Qi Condensation cultivator with ease. Even mid-stage cultivators would be at risk, while late-stage cultivators might have a chance of evading the attack using their spiritual sense.

Now imagine a weapon like that being mass-produced.

A cheap, easily manufactured weapon capable of killing the most common class of cultivators?

That was practically an invitation for trouble.

Call him paranoid, but Ning had absolutely no intention of taking that gamble, especially after witnessing just how petty cultivators could be.

Besides, most profitable industries were already controlled by established clans, sects, or powerful cultivators. Ning had managed to live peacefully so far precisely because he hadn't stepped on anyone's interests, and he intended to maintain that arrangement until he was strong enough to handle the consequences.

There was also the unfortunate fact that many things he knew from Earth already had rough equivalents here. With countless cultivation professions covering almost every aspect of daily life, there weren't nearly as many revolutionary ideas for him to introduce as one might expect.

Books, however, were another matter.

They were entertainment, and as long as he handled the business carefully, the risks were relatively small.

Which brought him to Elder Shen.

Ning glanced at the old man, who had already turned several pages without looking up.

As a cultivator whose strength had long since reached its limits, Elder Shen had developed quite an appreciation for mortal pleasures. However, he had no interest in visiting the red-light establishments outside the sect, preferring to satisfy his curiosity through literature instead.

And he was hardly alone in having such interests.

Cultivators might pursue immortality, but that didn't mean they were free from greed, anger, lust, passion, resentment, or desire. If anything, lower-level cultivators struggling with their daily lives had plenty of reasons to seek an occasional distraction.

Ning waited until Elder Shen finished another page before finally speaking.

“So, Elder, what do you think this book is worth?”

“Who wrote this?” Elder Shen asked without looking up. “It's quite different from the books I usually read—no, I mean, confiscate from other disciples.”

Ning's lips twitched, but he decided to spare the elder the embarrassment.

“I wrote it.”

“You?”

Although Elder Shen had his suspicions, hearing Ning admit it still caught him off guard.

“How do you know so much about these matters? Surely, only someone with considerable… experience could write something like this.”

He glanced between Ning's innocent face and the scandalous book in his hands, seemingly struggling to reconcile the two.

“Elder, art comes from imagination. With enough talent and creativity, one can write about anything,” Ning answered shamelessly.

Elder Shen considered this for a moment before nodding.

“That makes sense. So, you want my help selling these books?”

“Precisely. You see, the current market is dominated by obscure, flowery works like Flower Shadows Behind the Curtain and Spring in the Jade Tower,” Ning explained. “They're filled with metaphors and symbolism, leaving readers to imagine most of what actually happens.”

Elder Shen nodded knowingly, then coughed.

“Or so I've heard.”

Ning politely ignored that.

“The problem is that imagination takes effort. Senior cultivators with plenty of free time might enjoy deciphering such things, but what about ordinary disciples? After spending an entire day doing sect chores, they're already exhausted. By evening, all they want is to relax. Do you think they'd have the patience to interpret several pages of poetry just to understand what's happening?”

“So your books use straightforward language to satisfy the… spiritual needs of lower-level cultivators,” Elder Shen summarized.

“Exactly!”

Elder Shen stroked his beard thoughtfully.

“The illustrations are superb, and the writing is quite polished. You've certainly put considerable effort into this.”

Ning smiled. The elder was clearly interested.

Of course, selling the books was only part of his plan. What Ning really wanted was for Elder Shen to become the public face of the business.

The old man might be poor, but he was still an elder of the Pure Qi Sect, with all the status and connections that came with the position. Having him involved would make distribution much easier and, more importantly, discourage anyone from trying to cheat Ning.

Unfortunately, Elder Shen wasn't foolish enough to miss the implication.

“So, you want this old man to stand at the forefront of your business?” he asked, looking amused. “And sell books like this under my name?”

Ning had expected the question.

“Elder, have you ever considered why there's such a large market for this genre?” he asked instead. “Outside the sect, a visit to Fireworks Tower costs dozens, sometimes even hundreds, of spirit stones. A single night could wipe out months of savings for an ordinary disciple.”

He tapped the book lightly.

“And how many disciples have that kind of money to spare? They need spirit stones for cultivation resources, pills, techniques, weapons, and everything else. Most certainly can't afford to spend dozens of spirit stones whenever they want the company of a female cultivator.”

Elder Shen listened without interrupting.

“People have desires, and pressure needs an outlet. That's why this market exists in the first place.”

Ning gestured toward the book in Elder Shen's hands.

“And my book can satisfy those desires while giving readers the excitement they want. Simply put, it's pleasurable!”

“...”

“Lying drunk in a beauty's lap, awakening as an immortal,” Ning continued without the slightest embarrassment. “Fantasies that are unattainable in real life can still be fulfilled through fiction. Add in all the pressure cultivators already deal with every day, and…”

He looked at Elder Shen.

“Elder, do you think my book isn't competitive?”

Elder Shen's expression had become increasingly strange, but Ning wasn't finished.

“Especially since sect disciples are advised not to release their primordial yang or practice dual cultivation casually. Compared to the alternatives, reading a book is cheap, convenient, and doesn't affect cultivation.”

Ning nodded seriously, as though he had just reached an unavoidable conclusion.

“If you really think about it, my book is practically a humanitarian service.”

Elder Shen stared at him for several seconds.

“I've met people with thick skin before, but your shamelessness truly has no limit.”

Ning remained unmoved. Being called shameless was nothing new to him.

“Also, Elder…” Ning lowered his voice slightly. “Please don't mention my name.”

“Oh?” Elder Shen immediately smirked. “So you know it's not exactly something to show off?”

“How could that be?” Ning replied with a perfectly straight face. “I'm writing for the masses. Putting my name on it would make the whole thing seem too utilitarian.”

“…”

Elder Shen stared at him.

Ning looked back with an innocent expression. He even blinked a few times for good measure.

“Hahaha!” Elder Shen finally burst out laughing. “I really can't win against that thick skin of yours. Fine. I'll handle the entire process and keep your identity hidden. As a disciple, your main focus should still be cultivation.”

He thought for a moment before continuing.

“Of the revenue, I'll take twenty percent, another forty percent will cover publishing and distribution, and the remaining forty percent will be yours.”

Ning nodded. That was reasonable.

Printing enough copies, arranging distribution, promoting the books, and dealing with merchants would require connections he simply didn't have. With Elder Shen handling everything, Ning could collect his share without worrying about the business interfering with his cultivation.

Forty percent for doing practically nothing beyond writing the books?

He could hardly complain.

“To begin with,” Elder Shen added, suddenly adopting a perfectly serious expression, “you'll need to prepare the remaining chapters and give them to me…”

He paused.

“For quality control.”

Quality control. Right.

“As expected of Elder. You truly care about the well-being of the disciples,” Ning replied with equal seriousness. “I will do my best.”

With that, Ning took his leave.

Now everything depends on how many gooners exist in the sect.
