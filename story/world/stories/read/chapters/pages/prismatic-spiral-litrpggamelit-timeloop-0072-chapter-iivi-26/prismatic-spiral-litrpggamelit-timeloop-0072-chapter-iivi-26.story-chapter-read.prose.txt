Chapter II.VI (2.6)

With some spare time on hand before their departure, Mitsuko decided to spend it shopping. She wasn’t certain what exactly she’d need to fight the next guardian, but thought it prudent to familiarize herself with her options for future loops.

There was a single shop on Mauve Island that sold enchanted objects and one across the street that sold potions. Unlike the cities in Hon or Tross, Mauve had no World Dungeon or delvers to readily supply ancient artifacts. Instead, the shop sold weaker enchantments and imports from overseas.

The interior of Great Gadgets, the enchanted equipment shop, looked like many others she’d encountered. The cheaper stuff was out in the open while the more expensive pieces locked behind glass displays. Of course, even stealing the cheap knick-knacks would be a hassle. Mitsuko had no doubt the entire building had extensive wards embedded into it to deter thievery. And that was to say nothing of the glass boxes’ enchantments.

“Excuse me, ma’am,” a bitelas woman hustled over to her as Mitsuko rounded the corner into the nook that sold clothing. While Mitsuko still was far from an expert on reading the insectoid facial expressions, even she could tell the woman was nervous.

“Is there something you want from me?” Mitsuko asked bluntly. It might be a sales pitch for an object being sold, but Mitsuko doubted it. The vibe felt off for that. The way the woman held herself lacked the confidence of a shop owner plying their goods.

Then the bitelas woman’s eye flicked to Mitsuko’s hand, proving her instincts correct.

“I wanted to see, I mean, that is certainly a woner—”

“Not for sale,” Mitsuko cut the woman off.

“Of—of course!” The bitelas’s mandibles twitched in what might have been an attempt at a placating smile. “If you changed your mind though, my name is—”

“I won’t.”

The bitelas apologized for disturbing her and hustled out of the shop.

Mitsuko raised her hand and examined the ring there. The artifact that had saved her life hundreds of times over. If that woman bought it, she wouldn’t even be able to use it properly. It created a blade suited for a human’s hands. Not a bitelas. It would just be a trinket to brag about in her possession rather than serve any utility.

“Perhaps you should reconsider,” Juniper suggested from her shoulder. “You’ll have the artifact back on you at the start of each loop. While I personally disdain money as a concept, I cannot deny it has its uses in civilizations such as these.”

“Out of the question.” Mitsuko lowered her hand. “I have enough money and goodwill from saving The Selcouth Sable that I’m not hurting for cash. Getting rid of my primary weapon in exchange for some coins would be downright idiotic. I doubt I’ll encounter anything I want here that I can’t barter for or buy outright. Nothing will come close to my ring in utility.”

“As you will,” Juniper relented.

As much as she longed for her words to be proven false, and to find some truly brilliant artifacts in the shop, Mitsuko found herself looking at a range of enchanted objects from trash to mediocre. All were overpriced.

The best selling objects appeared to all be cooling related. There was an entire two shelves of objects and a rack of clothes designed to keep a body cold. And two thirds of it was currently sold out.

As for things Mitsuko personally thought might be useful, she debated over a few trinkets.

“I want to examine that necklace,” she told the human man behind the counter.

He jolted upright, as if electrocuted and snapped his book closed. “You want to…buy something?”

“Is this not a store? Do you not sell things here?”

“Well, of course.” He looked down at where she was pointing and unlocked the case to remove the piece of jewelry. “Do you want any information about it?”

“Do you have anything to add beyond the written description beside it?”

“Well…not really.”

Mitsuko held back a sigh. “Thanks then, but I’ll pass on the proposition of you reading the note out loud to me.” She held out a hand and the store clerk dropped the necklace into it.

It was a cheap enchanted piece of gear. A bit gaudy too with how the glass on it glittered. She wouldn’t be caught dead wearing it at a ball in Hon or Tross. But under her shirt it would be unnoticed and it was relatively light for its size. While it only would last half a dozen uses before it required an enchanter to recharge it, that would be enough to get her through the loop.

“Necklace of Mirrors,” Mitsuko read off the description. After a moment’s hesitation, she decided to buy it. Frugality was next to useless in the timeloops. She needed to spend her resources while she still had them on hand.

Her coin purse many doubloons lighter, Mitsuko crossed the street into the other shop of interest. A wave of smoke scented with incense poofed out as Mitsuko opened the shop’s door. Mitsuko rubbed her nose and breathed through her mouth. Every gulp of air tasted like swallowing an array of spices but that was preferable to repeatedly sneezing.

The potions shop was better stocked than Great Gadgets, but still lackluster in comparison to what Mitsuko was used to in Hon. While there were a variety of unique flora and fauna to brew with, the ingredients were significantly more limited. And a lot of the common bases used in potion making simply didn’t grow out here. They lacked an agricultural infrastructure robust enough to break out of basic produce and into herbology.

“Anything I can help you find?” a wistful voice asked. This shop clerk was a girl Mitsuko’s age with skin tinted a hint of green. Mitsuko recognized goblin blood from Verdant Island. She also recognized the signs of intoxication. Droopy eyes, dopey smile, lethargic movements. This girl was high as a kite.

“Just browsing,” Mitsuko replied.

She felt Juniper hop from her shoulder as Mitsuko skipped past the plants and ingredients and went straight to the prepared potions. Waterbreathing, fire resistance, an assortment of both plant and animal polymorph, healing, flight, gaseous form, body meld, explosive, acid, flames. While none looked particularly powerful, there were a plethora of interesting options to skim through. As well as a few for more mundane things like fertility prevention, boil balms, and teeth whitening.

“This leaf, where did it originate?” Juniper asked. “I don’t recognize it. Is it a newly developed species or simply foreign and beyond my knowledge?”

Mitsuko set down a hair growth tonic and peered over at Juniper’s plant. The bird stood on a small side table with a blue, six-pronged leaf in front of her.

“I don’t know,” Mitsuko replied honestly. “I’m not a herbalist. I just drink the potions. No talent for brewing.”

Rather than leave Juniper ignorant, Mitsuko repeated the sage’s question and lifted the leaf so the clerk could see it.

“It’s a sky fern,” the girl said. If she minded the fact a bird was pecking about her inventory, she didn’t voice any concern. Though Mitsuko came to the conclusion the clerk might not realize Juniper was even real. “They’re nice if you mash them up. Make your mouth and insides numb. Interested? They grow on the sky islands to the south. We got a supplier.”

“Sky islands,” Juniper repeated. “Hmm.”

“What are the sky islands?” Mitsuko asked.

“Like islands…but they float in the sky.” The clerk smiled wistfully.

“I mean, what’s their history?”

“Um. They’re like big clods of dirt and rocks stuck together…and up in the sky?”

This conversation was not productive. Mitsuko returned to studying the potions. She crouched down to get a better look at the concoctions on a lower shelf. She was debating with herself whether or not it was worth the risk to bring slightly unstable looking explosive potions on The Selcouth Sable when a chime announced the arrival of a new shopper.

“Oh. Hello, Mitsuko,” another light voice greeted her.

Mitsuko felt a shiver ripple down her spine. Slowly, as if not to startle the newcomer, Mitsuko straightened her back and turned.

Fuku stood there, her big eyes fixated on Mitsuko and long, white hair floating slightly around her shoulders.

“Fuku…” Mitsuko greeted cautiously. Her eyes flickered over to the exit. But as much as she might want to bolt for the door, she demanded that her feet remain in place.

“Your father and fiancé are seeking to reunite with you,” Fuku said. “Do you wish me to escort you to them?”

“No. Thank you,” Mitsuko struggled to string together more than two words in a sentence. She silently cast Mend on herself to reduce her heart rate. That didn’t work. And the sudden thought that Fuku might be able to detect the spell sent a new jolt of fear through her.

“Interesting bird you have there.” Fuku stepped closer, her big eyes now locked on Juniper. “Not native to the island. It must have migrated. Or did you bring it with you here? Stange…I can’t hear its thoughts. Usually animals have rather simple concepts, but this one is blank. Like a poorly crafted undead.”

“It’s…uh, magical,” Mitsuko half-lied. The bird itself wasn’t anything special as far as she knew. But the soul inside it was another matter altogether.

“Facinating.” Fuku continued to stare for a minute, her eyes unblinking.

“Oh, a new customer?” The clerk at the counter finally noticed Fuku and halted her humming to give the witch a relaxed smile.

“Yes,” Fuku replied. She turned away from Mitsuko to address the clerk. “I am a new customer. You have ingredients I wish to purchase.”

“Like what?”

“Originally, I intended to collect a stockpile of unique materials originating from the archipelago. Some interesting people also sent me an invitation to their island. But now I must devote my efforts to shattering the temporal prison.”

The clerk opened her mouth and mimed the words Fuku just spoke. Her eyes remained clouded and confused. “Prison break? Is there someone in prison here? Who?”

“Hmm. I suppose we are all prisoners within our own minds. Theologically, I’ve heard the argument that the mind is in essence its own place. A state of existence unique to the physical world. In it, you can craft heaven or hell.”

“Hell…that’s the place in the west, right?” The store clerk was struggling to follow the conversation and Mitsuko didn’t blame her. Listening to the two of them communicate made her head hurt. It sounded like Fuku spouted off an internal stream of consciousness without any real regard for the audience’s comprehension.

“The underworld,” Fuku corrected, her voice soft and wistful. “I long to go there one day and study the minds of the dead. Hell is a theological location. Some believe that the souls of the dead are segregated into two distinct groups, those who deserve the pleasures of heaven and those who deserve the punishments of hell. Though there’s no evidence to support that from the accounts of those few who’ve managed to venture into the underworld and successfully return.”

“What does that have to do with minds?”

“It was a comparison.”

“Oh. What plants do you want?”

“Monkroot, scarlet kelp, and succubus blood, if you have any.”

Mitsuko frowned. She didn’t know about those other ingredients, but succubus blood was notoriously used in love potions.

“Not for a love potion,” Fuku replied to Mitsuko’s thought. “I want to craft something to dissolve the barrier and monkroot turns succubus blood into an acid.”

Mitsuko felt a chill at Fuku’s casual reading of her mind. She immediately turned her attention back over to the potions in front of her and meticulously word for word read the written descriptions in her mind, one after another, to keep her mind occupied and not wandering to…other things.

“What’s a succubus?” the clerk asked. “We don’t grow that. None of our plants have blood. They have juice and sap sometimes though.”

Mitsuko continued to read about a tonic that promised to help lobito in men past their prime. The bottom listed a list of warnings, including possible mutations in offspring conceived while using it. Mutations like multiple arms or gills. From a certain perspective, those might actually be helpful additions to a normal human body. But Mitsuko clamped down on that tangential personal thought and continued to read the next bottle.

“I need the other two ingredients,” Fuku said. “Perhaps I’ll go hunt a succubus in the city? They like to slink about in the dark. Though the sun won’t set so it makes finding one more difficult. Perhaps there are basements and whorehouses I can visit?”

Mitsuko watched from the corner of her eye as the clerk paused and then meandered over to the botany section of the store and pointed at a stout plant with prickly leaves. Fuku swiftly sliced off a bit of the root then moved out of sight to collect the other herb.

“Interesting,” Juniper said from her shoulder. “How odd to find a Hon witch out in this region of the world. Only twice do I recall one visiting the archipelago in my lifetime. I assume from context that you know one another? A friend or an acquaintance?”

“An enemy,” Mitsuko whispered. Her mind flashed back to being imprisoned in the World Dungeon, kept as a disposable hostage. While Fuku had been ambivalent towards her during her imprisonment, the witch’s friends had been far less welcoming.

Without further thought of potions to bring on her trip, Mitsuko fled from the shop. The door dinged, announcing her exit.

If Fuku did read her mind at that moment, she would only find old memories of captivity and pain.
