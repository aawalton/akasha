The newlyweds stepped out of their suite the next morning with Clay on Trevor’s shoulder. It was a late morning and there were no signs of life in the hallways of the passenger floor. They had spent what they considered a worthy amount of time lazily watching the ocean go by from the bed and the couch before heading out to find breakfast.

They had also, unfortunately, missed the sunrise. Trevor vowed to do better tomorrow.

Horatial, who had been assigned to their cabin for the trip, had brought down an absolutely stellar steak dinner after they ordered in last night. The purple demon had been the epitome of classy service, even recommending some wine to Adabelle. While they certainly could have done the same for breakfast, they wanted to get out and see the ship.

Taking the elevator up to the lounge, which was a floor below the main deck, the doors opened to reveal a large room that had to have spread across half of the Red Dream. There were tables and chairs clearly meant for dining on one side, and plenty of small, semi-private nooks on the other. Shelves of games and books lined each one, and the areas next to them had space to play or read depending on what was available.

The smell of bacon was the first thing to hit them as they both took in a deep breath. On the other side of the lounge was a window for ordering food, and they naturally gravitated that way while checking out the crowd.

There weren’t many; only thirteen of the thirty-seven passengers were here. The majority were demons, but there were a couple of gnomes amongst them. What appeared to be a family of eight had pushed some dining tables together and were talking with each other while the others loitered about eating, reading, or checking out the games.

They put in an order for breakfast at the kitchen before taking their drinks to a seat next to one of the windows. They weren’t close to the group of eight, but neither did they sit as far away as possible. Trevor and Adabelle just wanted a view. Clay jumped down onto the table before resting against the glass, watching the endless ocean go by with interest.

“I’m glad you don’t have any problems being away from the ground like this, little buddy,” the courier said as he reached over and scratched behind his familiar’s smooth ears.

He received feedback similar to Mr. Tiggles’ purr through their bond. That was an interesting habit Clay had picked up after the Summoned cat let them cuddle with him. It wasn’t something they did physically, though, especially after they had managed to damage a stone countertop with vibrations alone once before. They were able to fix it but they had been careful since then.

Considering the earth elemental was currently pressed up against glass, no matter how strong it must have been to be on an airship, this was probably the right choice.

“You are rather remarkable, Clay,” Adabelle said as she watched them. They lifted their head to regard her. “Becoming Trevor’s familiar was pretty good for you both, wasn’t it?”

Clay immediately began nodding as they looked back and forth from their master to the demonkin. They left the window in order to crawl onto Trevor’s lap, where they curled up to rest.

“They are an excellent companion and protector,” he praised with a smile, and his familiar rolled belly-up in response.

“Your food, ma’am and sir,” a woman announced as she approached. She set down two domed plates in front of them. Grabbing the handles, she lifted the covers off of them. “Will there be anything else?”

“Not at this time, thank you,” Adabelle answered with a smile.

“Very well. Feel free to leave your dishes, we’ll clean up once you’re gone.” The server performed a small curtsy before returning to the kitchen.

“It’s nothing really too spectacular, but the presentation is nice,” Trevor said once the woman was back in the kitchen. “I guess that’s how they like to class things up here.”

His stomach rumbled as he looked upon the sandwiches he ordered. Hash brown, sausage, cheese, bacon, and eggs slammed between two halves of a biscuit. They had asked how he wanted his food and delivered it exactly as he described. Adabelle’s was more spread out like a traditional breakfast with the addition of fruit.

“So,” he started as he grabbed the large sandwich and peered at it. “What do you think we should do today?”

“I do want to visit the deck at some point,” Adabelle answered.

“Of course.”

“And we should check out some of those games over there,” she continued as she watched her husband try to take a bite out of his tall sandwich. He managed to succeed, though he had to stretch his jaw to do so, so he just nodded in agreement. “I also heard that there’s a swimming pool on our deck that has a glass bottom. That seems fun.”

Trevor made a questioning noise through his food before covering his mouth. He chewed, swallowed, and took a drink before getting out what he had meant to ask. “There is?”

“Yes, there is,” Adabelle replied as she handed him a cloth napkin. “I’m honestly interested in how Captain Braidley plans to race the Red Dream when it seems like there’s all this extra weight on board. There are quite a few rooms of couches, chairs, and beds that are weighing everything down, and all that water isn’t going to be doing him any favors.

“I can answer that!” one of the demons from the other table announced, raising his hand. They turned to look at him.

He was on the shorter side even with the twin horns sticking almost straight up in the air. His ears, which were animalistic like Adabelle’s, twitched as he excused himself from his family. He shimmied out of his chair, making sure his thick tail didn’t get stuck.

Trevor had been around enough nobility at this point to tell that his clothes were of rather fine make. Better than his, at least, which had been selected for comfort and practicality.

“Apologies for butting in there, but it’s a little hard not to overhear other conversations sometimes,” the man said, gesturing towards his ears. “And, also, I’m happy to back off if you don’t want the company. Not everyone’s up to meeting new people on cruises.”

Trevor, generally not one to mind meeting new friends, glanced at Adabelle. She nodded. “I’m no stranger to accidentally hearing every conversation in a room, either. Please, pull up a seat.”

“I’m Stammy, good to meetcha,” he introduced as he pulled up a seat. The demon stroked his fluffy black beard as he looked over the newlyweds.

“I’m Adabelle, and this is my husband, Trevor,” she replied in kind. The courier gave her a questioning glance when she didn’t introduce herself with her title, but let it go as she continued speaking. “Are you a member of the Red Dream’s crew?”

“Me? Nah, I’m just a racing enthusiast,” Stammy answered with a grin. “This here’s my new favorite ship, so when I heard it took trips over the Darel Ocean I decided me and the family ought to broaden our horizons by flying over it. That’s them over there, along with my wife, Dee.”

The oldest woman from the other table, another rather well-dressed demon, gave them a small smile and a proper wave before reaching out to help one of the younger ones.

“Big family,” Trevor said.

“Oh, yeah, but they’re a load of fun,” he laughed fondly before turning his attention back to the couple. “But, please, eat. Don’t let me distract you from your breakfast.”

“Thank you,” Adabelle replied as she started cutting into her food. “So, what do they do to make the Red Dream race-worthy? Captain Braidley mentioned something called a Cyclone-class airship, but we’re not sure what that means.”

“It’s a classification of size and weight, but one that’s actually a little smaller than the Red Dream normally is,” Stammy explained. From his tone alone, it was clear that he really enjoyed talking about this kind of stuff. “You see, the Red Dream was, at one point, the ship you see it as today.”

“But it’s not… now?” Trevor asked, confused, before biting into his sandwich.

“No sir, it’s not,” the demon answered, grinning. “You see, there’s actually a complex series of lifts, chains, clamps, and a bunch of other things that attach the passenger decks, including this one, to the airship proper.”

Adabelle paused. “I’m assuming there’s no risk of falling.”

“None whatsoever,” Stammy replied happily. “When the good captain had it split in two, he did so with magic and preserved the hull where it connected as well as could be. From there, Aletander’s best engineers worked alongside some foreign wizards to get the system rigged up so that they could configure it however they like. It can go from cruise airship to racer in, oh, about three hours. Which doesn’t sound like much, but it’s a downright stellar feat of craftsmanship.”

“It sounds like it’s probably a pain to disconnect and put back together, but well worth it if it means he gets to do as he pleases,” Trevor said once he was done chewing.

For sure,” he agreed with a nod. “Captain Braidley’s got a reputation for being very particular about his vessels. The Red Dream, for instance, was custom built. He had a big hand in designing it, too. Well, I say that, but it’s more that he had ideas and the engineers tried to make everything work. That’s why they didn’t have to go through so many renovations when he finally decided to split it.”

“That’s very interesting,” Adabelle said as she glanced at the ceiling. It didn’t look any different from the ones on the other decks, but he had told them that this was one of the floors that was left behind. “I would be inclined to believe he had racing in mind as well after hearing that. Airships, from what I understand, are already ludicrously expensive. There’s no reason to add a bunch of parts that wouldn’t do anything until you split it in two.”

“I’ve always said that, too,” Stammy remarked, beaming. “Another fun fact is that the envelope and propellers aren’t big enough to move nearly as fast as we are now while in this configuration.”

“But it works out because of the Power of Gales?” Trevor guessed.

“Precisely that,” the demon said. “If you stay up late enough, you’ll notice that we slow down by a fair clip for about four hours or so. Usually long after everyone’s gone to bed, except for those who prefer the night, but before sunrise.”

“That doesn’t sound like he gets a lot of sleep,” Adabelle stated. “It is a good indication of how powerful of a Summoned he is, though. I imagine he’s had a lot of success from his patron.”

Trevor smiled at that. Klar really did like to throw out as many quests as he could. He had helped the courier reach a few levels when Tosa had dried up in the past, too.

“I don’t know so much about that part,” Stammy admitted with a chuckle. “There’s a merchant prince from the Isle of Horns involved with all that, but I haven’t really dug that deep into things. Sounds too personal to me, you know? I’d rather keep to the racing side and, of course, the cruise.”

“I am curious about this Aletander Skybound Grand Prix,” the noblewoman said. “Will that be soon?”

“Oh, yes. It’s starting the day after we land and it’ll go on for about four whole days.”

“Wow, that’s a long race,” Trevor said.

“Aye, but Aletander is a very, very large country, taking up most of the continent,” Stammy explained. He paused, considering his words before shaking his head. “That’s actually a bit disingenuous. Aletander is huge—might even be the biggest country in the world—but that’s mostly because we claimed a lot of the mountain territories that are downright uninhabitable. There were big expeditions to set up villages and towns in the name of expansion, but only a few of them worked out.

“But that’s not important. The people who were really into racing airships, the die-hards, went about setting up flags in the mountain that can be seen for miles. This time of year we get some nice, clear weather from the cold fronts coming from up north, which makes it so the captains can actually see them from as far away as they’re supposed to. They’ve got maps, of course, but it’s better when there’s no cutting corners based on anyone’s individual perception.”

“Speaking of the individual, I imagine it can be tough for Captain Braidley given his Power,” Trevor said.

“Oh, yes,” Stammy confirmed. “There were a lot of fouls called against him before, but they’ve settled down since his early days. He’s had officials on board to observe his instruments and his opponents watch him like a hawk from their own airships. These are people who are the best in their field, too; if the Red Dream so much as lurches in a way that seems suspicious, like with a sudden updraft when there’s no call for it, then they’d jump on it in a heartbeat. He’s fairly respected now.”

“It would be hard to cheat in that scenario,” Adabelle stated as she finished up her food. “But it does seem like a very competitive sport. I wonder if they’ve planned for us to watch the start.”

“They might have. It could be pretty cool,” Trevor replied with a nod.

“They?” Stammy asked.

The noblewoman reached across the table and took her husband’s hand. “We’re on our honeymoon, and a friend of ours is footing the bill so long as Trevor here makes some deliveries for him.”

“Yup,” he said with a smile. His gaze traveled from his wife to his arm, where his Summoned tattoo was on full display. “I’m basically a walking, talking cargo deck, so I’m pretty good at courier quests.”

“Ain’t that a neat trick, then,” the demon laughed. “Well, congrats to you two on the honeymoon. It looks like you two are almost done eating, and my family’s gone silent which is usually a bad sign. I can feel the missus boring a hole in the side of my head with a look so I’ll be off. It was a pleasure meeting you both.”

“And you, Stammy,” Adabelle returned. “If we’re out and about, don’t be a stranger. Feel free to introduce your family as well.”

“Next time, definitely. We’ve booked out for parasailing after breakfast, and it’s probably about our time.”

She frowned thoughtfully. “I don’t believe I’ve heard of parasailing before,” she admitted.

“I’ll explain it to her,” Trevor said excitedly. “Do you know if there are any openings later? I’m sure she’ll love it.”

“You’ll have to check that one yourself, friend,” Stammy laughed as he stood up. “I do recommend trying it out at least once. I’m looking forward to the thrill of it, myself, but I don’t know if I could strap myself in a second time.”

“Strap yourself in?” Adabelle asked curiously.

“I hear you, gotta try it at least once. But, yeah, we won’t keep you and your wife really is staring,” Trevor said. “You have fun and be safe, yeah?”

Stammy looked back at Dee, who crossed her arms, and put a hand over his heart. “Yes, it is certainly time to go. We shall be, Trevor, and you do the same. Don’t be a stranger, and bye!”

They waved the demon off and he quickly returned to his wife. The courier picked up the last half of his sandwich as his wife leaned forward.

“Alright. So what’s parasailing?”
