Once again, Viktor found himself back in the meeting room of the Adventurer’s Guild.

Just like the last time, Isadora occupied the head of the table, with Guild employees, adventurers, and a few representatives from the caravan station seated along both sides. Viktor was among them, wedged between Alycia, whose arms remained crossed in silence, and Cedric, who kept glancing toward the far end of the board.

There, set apart from everyone else, sat the guest of honor.

Guildmaster Khalid.

A rich man. A powerful man. A man who was used to rooms rearranging themselves around him. But not this room. Here, he looked like a sheep that had wandered into the wrong pen and was now pretending not to notice the wolves taking an interest.

Nephra, despite being offered a seat next to her presumed superior, had chosen to stand instead. She drifted to a corner and leaned back against the wall with the unhurried ease of someone who had no stake in the outcome. From there, she watched the whole proceedings, the faint smile never leaving her face.

What’s her deal, exactly? Viktor thought. Why did she bother coming here at all, when she could have just dropped Khalid at the edge of town like she had done before? Did she just want to watch the man squirm?

“Guildmaster Khalid,” Isadora said. “Welcome to the Adventurer’s Guild of Daelin. Though I must confess I am rather puzzled by your visit. What exactly is it that you wish to parley about? I’m not aware that we are in any dispute.”

Well, yes. Officially, the secret hideout discovered within the Folded Chamber had no connection whatsoever to the adventurers of Iskora. So Viktor was genuinely curious how the guy even began the negotiation without implicating himself in the process.

“We do have a matter to discuss,” Khalid said. “It’s about one of my adventurers having injured one of your employees.”

Viktor clenched his teeth. Injured, you say?

“That’s quite an understatement for what happened, Guildmaster Khalid. The poor girl has been unconscious for weeks, with no sign of waking up. And I must say I find it rather strange that you are addressing this only now, after a prolonged period of denying any responsibility. The Adventurer’s Guild of Iskora has stated, on multiple occasions, that the incident was the act of an individual and not representative of your Guild. Now, suddenly, it becomes a dispute between us. Very peculiar, indeed. Still, I fail to see what there is for us to talk about. Just tell your man to surrender himself to the authorities, before the Conclave’s remaining patience runs out and a bounty is placed upon his head.”

“I came here hoping that we might find a way to reconcile this matter. It would be greatly appreciated if you could consider... withdrawing your petition before the Conclave.”

Isadora frowned. “You are asking me to drop the charge?”

“Yes.”

This was odd.

Viktor had expected a completely different conversation. He, and probably everyone else currently seated around this table, had assumed that Khalid’s visit was about the masked men’s secret lair being destroyed and subsequently exposed to the public. Instead, he was here for Xander. He was here to save that bastard’s hide.

Why?

Unlike other people, what Viktor had was not mere suspicion. He knew that the masked men reported directly to Zubayr, who in turn received instructions from Khalid himself. In fact, the order to hunt down the pyromancers had come from this very man sitting at the far end of the table. Hence, after his base of operations had been wiped out, he should have been thoroughly rattled, scrambling to contain the damage however he could.

Yet, he was here, doing this.

Did I underestimate how important Xander actually is? Viktor thought. Up until now, he had regarded the bastard as little more than a tool that Khalid wielded when convenient. A powerful tool, an expensive tool, but a tool nonetheless. The kind you replaced when it had outlived its usefulness.

Besides, if what Khalid wanted was Isadora’s withdrawal of her petition, surely there were better ways of accomplishing that goal than this. He was rich, influential, and backed by the Children of the Abyss, which meant he could easily apply pressure on her through the many mechanisms he undoubtedly had at his disposal. So why did he choose to personally step into a room full of people who despised him?

Viktor shifted his gaze toward Nephra, who remained exactly as she had been since the beginning of the meeting, watching the unfolding scene with that same faint smile fixed upon her face.

Unless... the whole thing was her idea. Khalid was forced to do this; he had no say in the matter.

But why? What was she playing at here? What could she possibly hope to gain from this charade?

“I’m sorry, but what you’re asking simply is not possible,” Isadora said. “That man must answer for the crime he has committed. There’s no other way around it.”

“I can offer compensation. To you, to your Guild, to that girl herself.”

Isadora frowned. “Money does not solve every problem, Guildmaster Khalid. As I’ve already told you, Claire remains in critical condition even now, weeks after the attack. You think your gold can undo what has happened to her?”

“I have a lot of friends, a lot of connections. I will reach out to the Emerald Order, to the Brotherhood of the Verdant Shade, to the Druidesses, to anybody who might possess the knowledge or the means to help. Surely someone among them should be able to do something for her.”

Isadora paused at that, and flicked a glance towards Viktor.

That was... a tempting offer, he had to admit. One more well-connected person searching for the cure would certainly increase the odds of Claire eventually recovering. Besides, his plan was to kill Xander himself anyway, so Isadora dropping the charge wasn’t really a big deal. Still, he was going to let her make this call. So he arranged a neutral expression on his face, showing the Guildmaster no reaction.

“A promise is not good enough,” Isadora said, turning back to Khalid. “I want you to begin this effort immediately. We can talk again once she wakes up.”

“But... there’s no time for that...”

“What do you mean?” Isadora asked, frowning. “It’s not like the Conclave will execute your man the moment he turns himself in. There will be a proper investigation, then a proper trial. Should Claire wake up before it concludes, surely such a development could be used to argue for a reduced sentence on his behalf.”

“You... you don’t understand...”

Understand what?

Clearly, something had happened behind the scenes, something very wrong, something ugly enough to drive Khalid to this level of desperation.

The rest of the room was starting to notice it too. They exchanged glances, as if asking each other whether they had any idea what was going on, only to be met with the same blank looks in return.

And that was when a voice rose from a corner of the room.

“May I speak?”

Isadora turned, fixing her gaze upon the woman with skin of bronze who had, at long last, broken her silence.

“You are?”

“A simple Riftwalker,” Nephra said, a smile playing across her lips as she began to walk toward the table. “I was merely hired to ferry Guildmaster Khalid back and forth between Iskora and Daelin. Nothing more, nothing less. Whatever discussion you two are having has nothing to do with me. Still, I can see that he finds himself in quite a predicament, so I’d like to offer a solution.” She stopped beside Khalid’s chair, looking down at him almost fondly. “May I speak on your behalf?”

“Yes... yes, please, go ahead...”

And there it is, Viktor thought, the main event of the day. The only reason why they had this so-called parley in the first place. Everything that came before was for this moment.

“My apologies,” Nephra began, “but I find this whole conversation, frankly, rather dull. Parley. Compensation. Investigation. Trial. All of it reeks of bureaucracy. Slow, suffocating stuff that exists only to make simple things complicated. We are adventurers, are we not? Surely we have better ways of settling a disagreement than all this tiresome back and forth.”

“And what might that be?” Isadora asked.

Nephra smiled. “A competition.”

The Guildmaster of Daelin arched a brow. “A competition?”

“Just so. Daelin sends a team, Iskora sends a team. They compete, and the loser does what the winner says. If Iskora wins, for example, you drop the charges against the man named Xander.”

“And if you lose?”

“Then every single adventurer from Iskora currently residing within Daelin must leave at once, without delay, and without exception. Moreover, from this day forward, none of them will be allowed to come within fifty miles of the town ever again, unless they have explicit permission from your Guild.”

“What!?”

Khalid shot to his feet, his eyes wide with disbelief as he stared at the woman.

Well, that reaction was understandable enough, all things considered. After all, such an outcome would effectively gut every ambition Iskora had ever harbored in Daelin.

Nephra, on the other hand, didn’t bother turning to meet his gaze. She simply said, her voice soft and gentle, “Sit.”

And he did. The richest man in the room swallowed, lowering himself back into his seat, his gaze fixed upon the table before him.

That, of course, drew everyone’s attention. Viktor could practically see the questions written plainly on their faces: Who the hell was this woman? How could she speak to a man like Khalid in that manner? And why did he actually obey? Was she really just some Riftwalker he hired?

“Interesting,” Isadora said. “I have to admit, you’ve got me curious. So, what kind of competition did you have in mind?”

“All we need is something suitably challenging for the two teams to compete over. Oh, I know...” Nephra’s eyes lit up, as though the idea had only just come to her. “We happen to have a dungeon nearby, right? I’ve heard that people have been stuck on the second floor for quite some time, ever since the old staircase leading down to the third collapsed. The only way to move forward now is to break through the great tomb in the middle of the desert, but no one has succeeded yet. So, here is my suggestion. Whoever reaches the third floor first will be declared the winner of the contest.”

...What?

What was she trying to pull here?

Also, did she just call Khenemhotep’s place the great tomb? No one outside the dungeon was supposed to know that particular detail. Then again, if she truly was Neferati after all, it meant she had literally come from the same world as the undead priest.

“That’s ridiculous,” Isadora said. “We all know that your side has much stronger adventurers than ours does. Xander is a Mithril-ranked adventurer, and so is Zubayr. With all due respect, this so-called competition of yours appears rigged heavily in your favor from the start. There’s simply no way for us to win. I cannot accept this proposal.”

Nephra remained unfazed by the rebuke, her smile never once wavering.

“Guildmaster Isadora, may I ask you a question?”

“What is it?”

“Why are you doing all of this? I mean, why submit a formal petition directly to the Conclave demanding Xander’s arrest? Are you doing it for that poor girl’s sake? Or are you merely using the incident to your own benefit, as a means to strike at the Iskora adventurers who have been a thorn in your side?”

“I do it to get Claire the justice she deserves. There’s no other motive.”

“How noble of you. But if that’s the case, then shouldn’t the one who has the final say in the matter...” Nephra said as her smile widened, “...be the victim’s family?”

“What—!?”

And, in that very instant, every single eye in the room turned, landing squarely on Viktor.

“You heard my proposal, Quinn. What do you say?”

“Is Xander going to enter the dungeon?” he asked.

“Of course,” Nephra said. “He will be the one leading Iskora’s team.”

What was this?

What was the meaning of all this?

A gift? A trap? A provocation? A declaration of war?

Well.

It mattered not.

“Then I agree,” Viktor said.
