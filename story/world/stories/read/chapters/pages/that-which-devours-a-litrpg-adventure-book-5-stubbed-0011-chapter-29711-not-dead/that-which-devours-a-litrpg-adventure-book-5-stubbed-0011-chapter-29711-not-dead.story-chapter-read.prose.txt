Lenna, at her Grandmother's home in the city…

“You really think everything is better now?” I asked, leaning back in the wooden chair.

My grandmother fluttered around the kitchen, and ladled a bunch of stew into bowls for the two of us.

Asceto had gone with the head crystal singer, ready to learn more. Our parting had been easy, easier than my parents would have wanted.

“You cut off the rot that infected our tree, now we need to give the tree time to heal and re-grow.” She sat across from me and placed a bowl in front of me. “Eat.”

I focused on my food and the muttering that lingered from the stars. Not being able to clearly understand the muttering usually meant I could ignore it, but right now it made my leg bounce.

We both concentrated on the food, eating in silence. It warmed me up, and gave me a moment to check in with Dengu.

He visited with the other bonded in the city. Their conversations were going much longer than the council meeting had.

“Tell me about this Alex,” said my grandmother.

“Alex?” I asked, after a bite. “She’s growing fast and pushing hard. The stars whisper her name often.”

“So, she is someone who is shaking the universe.” She eyed me a little. “She went after you, then blew up the portal.”

“Yes, Grandmother.” I didn’t like where this line of questioning was headed.

“That sounds like an intense bond between the two of you,” she said carefully, as she scraped up the last of her stew.

I finished my bowl and thought about it. “Without her, I wouldn’t be a Pathfinder. She challenges my view points, and what I thought I knew of the world.”

I shook my head, thinking about how I’d woken up next to her, and needed to rank up.

My grandmother listened, without saying a word.

“Just being near her made me want to reach even farther. I always wanted to wander the stars, now I have… and I want to continue.”

She patted my hand with a soft smile. “What do the stars say?”

“I need to join her soon,” I said with a frown. “But leaving here doesn’t feel like the right choice. Asceto is still getting settled, and the council…”

“Will be fine, as I said,” she patted my hand again. Her brown eyes stared into mine. “What does your heart say, not your head?”

I pressed my lips together, unwilling to voice my concerns.

“You know what you need to do,” she said, a twinkle in her old eyes.

Alex, outside Lakeside Landing…

I paused and glanced at Abby and John, eyeing the dark sky all around us. “I’ll catch up,” I said.

Abby gave me a nod, then motioned for John to keep going. He gave me a weird look, but continued on down the trail.

The buzzing slowly increased, then randomly cut off.

“That was one heck of a gift,” I said, thinking of the bit of Apochas.

“What did you do?” asked Noseen, though I couldn’t see him. The voice came from somewhere near my head, in front of me.

Pressure formed all around me, then vanished.

“This isn’t possible,” he grumbled, near my right ear. “You couldn’t have done this…”

“I played it smart,” I said, carefully thinking of the loot from the dungeon, and how I’d combined it with his gift.

A light buzzing picked up, then cut off again. “You are a smart one. Just don’t push the system. It can get grouchy.”

“So, you were worried about me?” I asked, in a joking manner.

“Hmph. The mark vanished. I thought you died.” This time, his voice came out flat. “But you are too smart for that, I see.”

“Do you want to put another one on me?” I asked, thinking about the help he’d provided over the months through the conversations in my dreams.

The air turned heavy for a moment.

“Are you sure?” Noseen asked.

“Isn’t it smart to mine you for information?” I answered with another question. The information he’d given me had been useful, even important. “Plus, what happened to it anyway?”

“When your new body formed, it was destroyed.” He landed on my shoulder, and I felt a light touch near my ear.

Yet, everything inside me focused on one word. Destroyed.

“What?” I asked. My voice cracked a little.

“Your new divine body. No more pesky problems like aging, or getting sick.” A small tap right behind my ear almost tickled, but I didn’t move until it stopped.

“You mean, I’m going to be this age forever?” I hadn’t connected immortality to becoming divine. Though, immortality might not be right. I’d killed things that were rank three.

Noseen lifted off my shoulder. “It's a benefit, to encourage you to keep progressing. At your rank, aging just slows down drastically. You need to reach my power for true…” His voice cut off. “I need to get back to the Sisters. That mark will give you some leeway on my planets.”

“You think I’m ready to go to the Sisters?” I asked. I had to have misunderstood him.

“No, but you tend to end up in places you shouldn’t go.” He chuckled. “And this world feels lacking now, doesn’t it?”

The absence of as much mana in the air as I was used to suddenly bothered me even more. I didn’t say anything.

“Just wait until you eat something.” He chuckled again. “Stay smart. You are not squishy to citizens anymore, but to other divines, you are back at the bottom.”

“I’ll be fine,” I said, thinking of Strange and going to rescue him. Well, less rescue and more fetch.

“I have prior arrangements I must return to. Good luck, Alex.” He suddenly started buzzing again, and flew off.

I turned back toward the path to Lakeside Landing. Part of me hesitated. He wasn’t wrong about this place. While seeing everyone was good, especially how happy John was, I wanted to head out already.

Shaking my head, I continued down the path, thinking of food, sleep, and how long I should stay before leaving again.

Music filled the air, along with chatter and laughter. The fence loomed, but no one manned the opening. A faint trail wound through the underbush following the fence. Then Holls appeared, padding down the path, not even stopping when he saw me, silent as can be.

That explained that.

Once inside the town, things appeared the same. No new buildings had gone up, and the gathering place still was the open plaza in front of the cavern opening. A fire burned bright in the center of the area, with people gathered around at different tables.

Abby waited near Doc’s serving station, and waved me over.

I headed in that direction.

A few folks noticed me at that point, and conversation lowered briefly, before picking up again. Multiple people tried to use perception skills on me. I bet it gave them almost nothing.

Doc grinned as I approached. “Alex, you're here!”

“As far as I can tell,” I said with a grin.

He shoved a wooden cup in my direction, only half filled. “Try this! I think you might like it.”

I sniffed the drink before I took a tiny sip. A sweet, fruity flavor filled my mouth, and I took another, bigger sip. Cherry. Actual cherry flavor.

“Is this cherry?” I asked. The buzz from the drink was so little it didn’t register as poison.

“Yeah! Benny got a few trees to fruit.” He motioned to my drink. “Yours is a bit heavy on the alcohol side. Abby won’t let me serve it to anyone lower level.”

“I can barely taste it, but poison doesn’t work on me,” I said, taking another sip before holding it back over to him. “Top me off.”

He gladly filled my drink bowl, making a pointed look at Abby, who rolled her eyes at him. “You know, showing up like this is gonna give everyone another kick in the pants to head to that fancy academy.”

I leaned back against the counter and looked out at everyone. “What do you mean?”

“Well, last time you showed up, everyone started going to the dungeon and leveling again.” He snorted, and took a sip of his own drink. “Now, we can’t even read you. Yeah, people are going to push themselves.”

“Growth is good.” Even with the lack of mana in the air here.

“We can’t be only growing all the time,” grumbled Abby.

Yet, when I studied her, she, too, had clearly spent time fighting and leveling while I was gone. She noticed by glance.

“I need to keep up with you,” she said, as she blushed. Then she tried to hide behind her cup.

“I think building a community is just as important as growing your class. Professions are important. Both are needed to advance.” Should I warn people what would happen when they reached rank three?

I shoved that thought away as fast as I could. Not a chance.

“Dad’s still at the academy?” I asked, searching through the crowd to figure out who was missing. Neither Benny nor Cass were nearby, the same for the commander. Hawk sat talking with Mary, so he was still here.

“Yeah, they left soon after you did.” Abby pointed to an empty table. “We should move, or no one will be able to get a drink from Doc.”

He chuckled, but as soon as we moved away from his serving station, a line formed.

Abby led me to an empty table. “How are you doing?”

“I’m fine…” I trailed off a little. I took another drink, and smiled at the bright, tart cherry flavor. “I have an errand I need to run, away from Lakeside.”

“Alex, we don’t expect you to stay here,” she said in a soft voice. “We miss you, but understand that you are so far beyond…”

“Everyone is making good progress.” I knew what she meant, but I didn’t want to really think about it. While I owned this territory, it made more sense to me now why those who owned their territories weren’t around. Once you started to rise, coming back didn’t work.

“We are, and we will continue,” she said, watching everyone. “There is a divide happening between those who are more like you, and those who want to raise a family in this new community.”

I swallowed hard, thinking of my conversation with myself. “That’s a path.”

Abby nodded, pressing her lips together.

A sudden thought hit me. “Do you want to settle down?”

She jerked to look at me, her eyes wide. “No, that’s not what I meant.”

“Then you’re asking the other question, who is going to take your place when you need to leave?” I added, getting to the meat of the problem. The one she was dancing around. “You’ll need to head to the academy as well, soon.”

“Yes, and I think some of us are going to grow beyond this place, even this world…” She clutched her cup in her hands, rolling it back and forth. “All I wanted was to help build a community in a new world.”

“You’ve done that,” I said quietly, finishing most of my drink. I really wanted another one.

“I know.” Abby let out a sigh, her eyes lingering on groups of various people. “Now that I’m here and we built this, everything is different.”

Music picked up in the background, a classic rock tune. Part of me still couldn’t believe my dad’s records had survived.

“I love this place and what we’ve accomplished,” she said, eventually. Abby frowned and glanced into her cup.

“Kabi could use help with his village, and nothing’s stopping me from claiming another area on a higher-tier world.” I hoped Kabi didn’t mind my suggestion. Abby would be good for him, especially to help deal with his family. Not that his parents were around much, I was sure.

Abby froze at my suggestion.

“Lakeside isn’t for everyone,” I said, trying to sound diplomatic about it. “We can find someone who wants to stay to take over, to give you space to grow.”

“You’d do that?” she asked, setting her mug down on the table.

“Abby, you made Lakeside into a lovely community where everyone is valued.” I stared at her across the table. “Of course I’d do that.”

Never mind the fact that if I wanted to claim a higher-tier territory, I’d need to kill whoever owned it first. And they’d need to be Rank Three for it to not make me feel bad. No pressure at all.
