Sally woke up, groaning and rubbing her eyes. She was having a weird dream about-

With a jolt, she snapped up to a sitting position, remembering where she currently was. The light filtering into the Personal Bubble made it clear it was daytime, but the intention was to wake up at daybreak - not sleep in.

Archie hopped up onto the small bed and sat. “Clearly you needed the rest.”

“That’s no excuse.” She tried to wave him away as she got her bearings.

“Humphrey is back on the Omen. He somehow fought for three more hours before succumbing to his wounds. Big brother had a spaceship land on him, so that’s quite the feat.”

Sally shot him a glum smile before opening up her STAR. It wasn’t quite that she had abandoned her adoptive father, but she still felt a little guilty. Impressive work for the Outsider’s best frontline tank, however.

Before she could send a message to the group, her eye caught her Mailbox. Several new entries.

With a widening grin, she withdrew all the goodies Bully had sent over. The attached note said that it would be the last of the potions he could send over. He’d not only run out of materials in the dwarven stronghold, but the king had decided to mount a counterattack against the Radochs - and the Alchemist would be joined them in battle.

“Looks like the skeletons have decided living forever isn’t all it’s cracked up to be.” She closed the Mail and shrugged at the cat.

“Difficult to be proud and superior over a barren rock.” Archie stretched out and yawned. “Did you get what you needed to move forward?”

The zombie pulled out a wide bottle that was practically vibrating. Green and brown swirls spiraled around within the glass container, shifting in direction every couple of seconds. “There are some downsides,” she said.

[Volatile Insect-Eater Potion][Allows consumption and digestion of Insectoid creatures][Increases metabolism (volatile)][Starvation may result in Consequences (volatile)]

Sally pulled a face as she put the bottle back in her Inventory. “I’m not sure I’ve ever seen consequences capatilized before. Best I drink this when I know I have a feast laid out before me.”

“Which might be sooner than you think. Give me the word and I’ll drop the Bubble.”

“Let me stretch out and I’ll be ready.”

“We’re not ready.” The purple-clad man shook his head. “I mean, even if we believe you, that’s not something we can just decide off the cuff.”

Theo was rather pleased the pair hadn’t killed him on sight. Then again, if they could read him anything like he could read them, he wouldn’t seem like a threat. He hadn’t been expecting a pair of magicians, but Max and Ren had heard him out, even if they were tense at the start.

“Naturally. You don’t have to agree to it straight away.” Theo put his hands in his pockets. “I’d be suspicious of anyone offering me immortality and safe haven as well.”

Ren had been practically scowling at him for the duration of the conversation, although that seemed to be the elf’s natural resting face. “In return for us fighting for your cause, though.”

The vampire grinned. “Unless you are truly content with a life of peace and do not wish to go against the odds.”

He watched as the magicians exchanged glances. There was always a chance that heroics were just a necessity, and a lifetime of rest was the perfect reward for a job well done. Theo knew as soon as he saw the pair that they wanted more. Not so different from Sally and himself.

“A universal traveling show,” Max murmured to himself. “Think of a stage that great in size.”

The elf rolled her piercing blue eyes, but didn’t seem to disagree. “It might be better than getting rusty. If this guy can come to our world, what’s stopping others? Ones who might threaten everyone here again.”

Max nodded before raising an eyebrow at the vampire. “We’re rather powerful on this planet. Although we don’t mind being the stars of the show, can you confirm at least that we wouldn’t just be carrying the weight of a new world on our shoulders alone?”

Theo extended his arms wide. “You’re welcome to test my strength.”

“A one-on-one duel?” The magician adjusted his jacket. “I suppose a private showing could be on the cards.”

The smile widened on Theo’s face. “Give me the full production. Really dazzle me.”

Max smiled as he lifted a bright purple playing card between his fingers. “Oh, you have no idea.”

“Yeah, beats me.” Sally stood and scratched the side of her head.

The closest Radoch ship was not only miles in the air but also at least ten miles away horizontally. With no method of travel, taking the vessels down seemed like an impossibility.

“Should we just go for your Maguffin, then? Maybe it’ll give you the power to do something about this.” She shrugged at the cat.

“That would probably be more productive.”

Overnight, the population of bugs had shifted. Even if the roaches could see decently in the dark, most searches for the power source had all but ground to a halt. With one of their ships falling from the sky, those in charge of the other two were being more conservative.

According to Jackie, most of the higher-ranking Radochs had returned to their quarters, along with their close retinue and some of the bigger monsters. Many initiate groups had camped out, their officers hoping to get a jump on the search come morning. Radoch presence on the planet was slowly increasing once more, but at a slower pace compared to the previous day.

“To think they’d use reasonable tactics.” Sally spun her dagger around as they moved away from the coast.

“Unfortunately, their greed will take precedence. It is unlikely they will flee if they believe they can still claim the item we seek.” Archie trotted along beside her.

“Hmm.” She tilted her head in thought. “So, if we wanted to kill as many as possible, we want them to believe it hasn’t been found.”

“Possibly difficult to do once I actually find it.”

Sally glanced down at the cat. He had still been rather tight-lipped about what this artefact was, but it seemed pretty important. While the mission to this world hadn’t produced as much information on the Radochs as she’d hoped, it would probably be worth it in the long run.

After all, she had a potion to eat the bastards now. Bully would be able to refine it before the invasion hit Sanctuary, to remove the volatile components of it. They knew more about the Initiates and the ranking system the bugs adhered to. They were immune to being raised from the dead. Edward knew how to pop their spaceship’s power generators.

“What do you think happened to the shadow entity?” she asked.

“I doubt they are dead.” Archie looked up at her. “Beyond that, no idea whatsoever.”

From the updated Map, it might be another two hours of walking before they got the whiff of combat.

Then again, after the meat grinder yesterday, she should be thankful for a rest from combat.

Theo was enjoying this fight far too much.

He stepped to the side as Max appeared behind him, narrowly dodging a knife. A purple card swirled through the air, slicing across his cheek. As a second darted down from above, the vampire swung his warhammer upward to block it.

At first, he thought Ren had declined the anything-goes invitation, but the elf had only backed away to the cottage for another reason. She needed to withdraw her weapon of choice - a sniper rifle.

Theo had nearly recoiled from the shock of seeing such a modern weapon. In fact, he had almost lost the fight from the outset when her first shot had blown open Max’s head. It was just a decoy dead body, but still, this was making him giddy.

He swung back around at the magician, and the purple-suited man exploded into a burst of confetti. Behind the veil of colorful paper, the extremely drab opening of a canon now stared the vampire down. Rather than put a heavy metal ball through his insides, the large weapon instead blew another bright wave of confetti around the area.

Theo rolled to the ground, avoiding the slash of three airborne cards. As soon as he righted himself, Max was there with a sword of obsidian. The warhammer twisted in his grip to block two slashes aimed his way. Sparks rang out as the sword transformed into a spear, before the magician hopped onto a summoned chair and brought down a hammer of his own.

Hand up, the vampire caught the stone head of the swung weapon. Pain ran down his forearm, but he wrenched the hammer away from the magician with his greater strength. Before he could adjust his grip and dual-wield, a sniper shot blasted through the clearing, knocking the weapon from his hand.

Cards spiraled around him as Max threw up a curtain and vanished behind it. As the fabric dropped to the ground, a demonic dog leaped out, snapping at the vampire. Novice strike came down quicker, a blaze of pink energy following his weapon. With a crunch, the animal dropped to the ground before fading away in a pulse of energy familiar to Theo.

He recovered and glanced over at Max. The magician was a distance away, one hand clasped to his wrist as he held a card glowing bright white. Over by the cottage, a spiral of light swirled down the sniper rifle as Ren readied a shot.

The pair were at a nearly perfect right angle to him, making it difficult to defend against both ranged attacks. He twirled the hammer around and placed the head against the soft grass, leaning against the shaft to grin at his opponents.

They attacked at the same time. Theo’s coffin expanded beside him, raising to form a barrier. With a clang, the sniper shot ricocheted away. Max’s empowered card also went as quick as a bullet, severing straight through Theo’s left arm.

Blood sprayed from the wound, but instead of splattering against the grass, the droplets held in the air. Crimson light flickered in his eyes as the hovering blood burst forward like a shotgun blast. The magician twisted away, but several sanguine projectiles scored hits, burning through his suit.

Max clicked his tongue. “Looks like we’ll have to cut the performance short, as you are not easily impressed.”

Theo grinned as his severed arm slid up into the air, a tether of twisting blood reeling it back into position. “On the contrary, this has been quite the buzz.”

As his grin exposed his fangs, the sky darkened. From behind the treeline, a moon of blood-red rose into the sky, painting the surroundings a ruddy hue.

“Near parlor trick. Here’s some real magic.” The expression on the magician’s face cooled, and his hand snapped forward. A rush of demonic energy flooded the area.

Gone was the small cottage and pleasant spring clearing. In front of Theo, the purple-suited man sparkled, standing atop a grand stage. He sat in a darkened row of hundreds of empty seats—rather comfortable ones, if he was honest—as the sole audience to a potentially great show. He couldn’t even get up if he wanted to.

Max’s presence couldn’t be denied. Power radiated from him as he stood with confidence upon the large stage. There was the slightest hint of hesitation, where he was probably trying to avoid killing Theo outright.

That was perfectly fine with the vampire. With some slight regret, he held his hands out, placing them together to form a shape.

[The Faithful Remain Free]

Max’s bright purple eyes twitched, and he glanced around at his showroom. Large cracks emerged around the unfathomly tall walls, breaking through the thick red curtains, and stage lights illuminating the warm laqured wood where the magician stood. All starting from where the vampire was sitting.

“Sorry.” Theo pushed his glasses up. “My best friend is a demon. I learned a Domain-shattering spell just to troll him.”

With a pop, the illusion shattered, bringing back bright sunlight, soft grass, and a rather perplexed magician to reality.

Theo put one hand in his pocket and held his arm out, offering to shake Max’s hand. “Not bad considering you were holding back. Wait until you see me with my full-”

His congratulations were cut short, as a grizzly bear burst out of nowhere beside him, tearing his arm off with a flick of its giant maw. A wide paw pinned him to the ground, as a second raised into the air to crush his skull like a melon.
