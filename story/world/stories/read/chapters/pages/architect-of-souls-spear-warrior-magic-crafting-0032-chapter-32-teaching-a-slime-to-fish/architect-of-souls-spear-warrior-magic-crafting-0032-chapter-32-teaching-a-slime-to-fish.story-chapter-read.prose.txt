Ferris turned his attention back to searching for a way to harvest qi from the Digest River.

He’d tried basic qi constructs and demon reinforced qi; both had melted in the river. The jade slip on crafting crossbows had listed many exotic materials, one it called ‘hardened glass qi.’ He had no idea how to create it. The manual Mental Materials Gilbert had recommended at the library would probably help, but he couldn’t afford it yet.

While his mental body was recovering, Ferris had spent some time considering what he could try. When they returned to the Heartfire, he decided it was worth using some liquid qi to experiment. If he saved qi, then he could make a new weapon in a day or two. But if his ideas worked, he could harvest the Digest River tonight.

When making the crossbow, he had learned about intent forming to make the string and limb flexible. Can I use the same technique to create an acid-resistant material? He wondered. Focus on the intent of being inert, like the books described?

Kneeling down at the Qi Pool, Ferris placed a hand in the liquid and imagined a small vial of glass, completely immune from the chemical reaction of the acid in the slimespace. Pulling his hand out of the liquid, he now held a sparkling pale blue vial.

Next, Ferris turned to the corpse of a chain demon. Before assaulting the fort, he had only been able to bring one of them back to his Heartfire. Lugging the thing back had been hard, but worth it. He’d already used several parts of it, grimly chopped off with his axe, but thankfully it didn’t decay like the real world. There was still plenty left.

Uncle Winton had taught Ferris to use demon corpses to form a stronger spear. The idea was simple, toss a demon in a pot with equal amounts liquid qi, heat until melted, and then use qi forming with the resulting liquid. Unfortunately, he hadn’t taught Ferris how to take the technique any further. The man claimed the demon reinforced spear was good enough to last Ferris a while.

Ferris wondered if Clan Bluereef had a manual similar to Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting, but for spears. They probably did, but it wouldn’t be that big of a priority for new Adepts. They would have bonded a spear, and it would show up for them inside their soulspace.

Maybe his uncle would let him see it, Ferris made a note to ask when he visited. There were only a few days left…

With a sigh, Ferris tried to refocus on how to collect qi from the Digest River.

The demon reinforced qi axe’s handle had melted in the acid, but Ferris had a couple ideas for how to take the idea further. First, he wanted to combine the demon reinforcement with intent forming, but he had a problem.

Ferris’ cooking pot was still full of rock-hard stone. It had taken more qi to make the pot than he had left, so making a new one wasn’t an option. Looking around, Ferris found the bucket he used to water the Core Tree and ability seeds. It only held about a third of what the pot could, but he didn’t want to make large items anyway—at least not for these tests. Both the pot and the bucket were standard qi constructs that held liquid, so he saw no reason it wouldn’t work for cooking.

He added a small amount of qi and a meaty chunk of the corpse to the bucket, heated it, and then formed it into another vial after it cooled, using the same intent. The resulting vial was tinted black.

Next, Ferris looked back to the corpse. The book at the library had shown that different materials dissolved in acid at different rates. He wondered, would all the various parts of the demon corpse corrode the same, or could he observe a difference? The book had specifically tested an animal’s flesh and bones, noting the flesh corroded much faster with certain acids. He expected it all to melt eventually, but he could test if different parts of the demon had different properties.

He collected a chunk of flesh, a bit of bone, and one of the chains the demon had wrapped around it. Separating a piece of bone gave him new respect for the Acid Dismantle ability. It was hard to get the bone clean, especially with just an axe. He was particularly excited about the chain. The demons had made it move like a snake, so maybe there was something special about it.

The extension of this idea would be to test mixing specific demon parts with qi forming. Until now, he’d always taken large pieces of the demon and melted it all together, but what if components mattered? To test this, Ferris repeated his demon reinforced qi intent forming three more times: one with just flesh, one with bones, and a final one with the chains.

Interestingly, all three materials melted in the pot the same. It was strange seeing flesh melt at all, much less at the same rate as metal, but the parts weren’t real. They were spiritual constructs.

This created three new vials: a red, a pale white, and a grey.

The different colors encouraged him to think that he was on to something. Hopefully it wasn’t just his intent subconsciously changing the color each time.

Overall, this had used about a fourth of his Qi Pool. Painful, but worth it even if the experiments all failed. He needed the knowledge.

Ferris took Jade, and his tests, to the slimespace. The Digest River was as peaceful as ever, clams lounging around the bottom of the river, displaying the qi chunks just out of reach. The river’s color was back to normal, matching the green of the ocean outside.

Sitting at the edge, Ferris dropped each item one by one into the acid. He carefully observed how long they took to dissolve, and then wrote it in the dirt next to him. He missed his new journal already, and couldn’t wait until he could find a way to get paper in the soulspace. It was a little silly dropping a vial meant for holding liquid into the river, but the shape was mostly to help him with the intent forming.

The chunk of plain demon flesh melted away the fastest. Ferris was excited to see that the bone did indeed last longer than the flesh. The chain lasted longer than both. The bad news was that all three unaltered items didn’t last very long, barely having a difference of four or five seconds.

Next, Ferris tested the vial made with the intent of being inert, compared to a failed trigger from the crossbow. Both were qi formed, without any demon corpse, but one had intent. It wasn’t a perfect comparison, as the trigger was larger than the vial, but Ferris didn’t want to spend the qi on a plain vial. Like before, the trigger disappeared quickly. Happily, the intent formed qi lasted much longer than the trigger, only melting away after about five minutes.

Ferris couldn’t help grinning. Now were getting somewhere.

The demon reinforced intent vials had mixed results. The one made from flesh was a failure, only lasting about a minute. Still better than demon flesh without qi, but worse than qi alone. The bone was an improvement, lasting around ten minutes. Best of all was the vial made using the chains. Ferris lost track of the specific time, but it was around half an hour.

Ferris was giddy, and he threw Jade up into the air a couple times to celebrate. None of the materials were immune, but if he did it right, he could gather more qi than he lost to melted items. More than that, it proved his theory that the parts of a demon used in qi forming mattered. Would a knife made from claws be sharper? Would a spear made of bone be stronger?

Returning to the soulspace, Ferris filled the bucket half full of qi, and half full of chains taken from the demon corpse. This took half of the qi he’d collected that day. He just hoped it would be enough.

After it melted and cooled, he placed a hand in and formed his intent. He wanted a long, thin chain with a small basket on the end. It should be a strong metal, and immune to any acidic chemical reaction.

Originally, he’d wanted a fishing line, but the tests made Ferris change his design to a chain instead. It more closely matched his material. He hoped this would form a stronger result, while providing the same function.

He knew the chain wouldn’t really be ‘immune’ to acid, but he wanted the intent to be as strong as possible.

Pulling his hand out of the bucket, he had made a length of chain. It looked similar to the silver chains wrapped around the demons, but shimmering blue with a basket at the end. It wasn’t very long, about four times the length of a spear, but it appeared sturdy enough. He wouldn’t trust it to hold his full weight, but he only needed it to lift small chunks of qi.

Calling Jade over, Ferris made up a new game to practice fishing for qi chunks. He threw his last failed crossbow trigger a short distance away. Then, he tossed the basket as close as he could to it while holding the other end of the chain.

Jade would then go push the basket. It was larger than the slime, although it weighed a lot less. Jade had trouble pushing most things around, but they were able to ‘get under’ the basket as they rolled. The slime pushed until it was directly behind the target, and Ferris could scoop it up as he reeled in. Jade had a lot of trouble at first, but like with most tasks the slime improved rapidly. The entire process was awkward… yet worked.

Once Ferris was happy with the practice, they returned to the Digest River in the slimespace to attempt the real thing. To reach from one side of the river to the other, he would need a chain four times the length. The clams, while spread out, tended to be closer together near the center, but there were plenty along the edges.

Oddly, the clams were fairly evenly distributed along the length of the river. He assumed that mundane clams would gather closer to the food source. Instead, these spread out from start to end, perhaps due to some quirk of the ability.

Ferris and Jade picked a clam well within range of the chain for their first target. Jade rolled into the river along the bottom, waiting next to the open shell. Ferris carefully swung the basket around a couple times, and then tossed it as close as he could to Jade’s position. His aim was badly off, but the slime started moving to compensate. The liquid distorted Ferris’ view of Jade and the chain basket. Interestingly, the slime had no problem adjusting to the distortion after Ferris warned them of it.

Despite his bad aim, the plan worked like a charm. Jade had to relearn how to push the basket now that it was underwater, and Ferris had to pull slower as the basket would sometimes bounce strangely, but they successfully hooked a qi chunk from the open shell, pulled it along the riverbed, and then out of the acid. The clam simply closed up afterwards, and then extended two fleshy tubes again.

Laughing with joy, Ferris had to shake the chain to get the basket to drop their prize. He didn’t want to touch the liquid still dripping from the qi chunk, but he also didn’t want to leave it behind to dry. Anything unattended could get stolen. He kicked some of the green dirt on top, then scooped it into his sack. Some dirt came with it, but at least it didn’t melt a hole in the sack.

The chain was still intact, although discolored. On top of the dark silver with blue shimmer, now it had a patchy white coating.

Quickly, Ferris directed Jade to the next clam’s qi chunk to repeat the process. They moved along the edge, able to reach up to a fourth of the way into the river, collecting qi and closing up clams along the way.

His smile somehow kept getting bigger as they worked. Jade didn’t really understand, but Ferris’ excitement was infectious over the bond. The slime started bouncing around, rippling in happiness after every successful catch.

Soon, they neared the edge of the dome protecting them from the ocean of acid outside. The river sloped down, flowing out to the darkness. Ferris stopped well back from the edge, wary of the dark waters. The fish that had killed Jade had entered from here.

Taking a break, Ferris and Jade sat and watched out into the ocean. He couldn’t see very far, as the light in the slimespace was emitted from the dome surrounding him and faded quickly beyond. The ants kept scurrying about, sometimes approaching close enough to touch. Ferris suspected more and more they were the so-called spirit animals.

They felt like part of some ability, but he couldn’t figure out how. The Consume Pool was his primary suspect, but then why do they travel all over? Could there be another ability that Janice hadn’t mentioned? How did the birds fit in?

While lost in contemplation, Ferris saw a massive dark shadow moving at the edge of his sight. In shock, he jumped to his feet, then backed away from the dome.

Chills ran down his spine as something larger than a whale swam by. He couldn’t see more than a silhouette in the murky darkness, but whatever it was could swallow him whole.

Retreating from the wall of the dome, Ferris and Jade moved inward. He suspected there were demons out in the acid ocean, but that thing was way out of his league. The edge was no longer a peaceful spot to relax.

It took a few minutes, and some breathing exercises, but eventually Ferris’ heart stopped beating so fast. When he was calmer, he told himself that whatever it was couldn’t get to him inside the dome. He even believed it, at least a little.

Then, the only thing to do was to get back to work. They returned to the Consume Pool and started collecting on the opposite side of the Digest River. When the sack was full, they had to make a trip back to his Qi Pool to empty it.

Back in the soulspace, Ferris spent some time wiping off the green dirt from each piece before tossing it into his Qi Pool. Thankfully, the dirt seemed to neutralize or dilute the acid. He wanted to experiment with the green soil, and he set it aside for later.

Then, it was back to collecting more qi. Unfortunately, soon after they resumed gathering, the chain broke. He was reeling it in when one of the links fell apart. There were still clams with qi chunks remaining along about a third of the back side of the river.

After dumping everything into the Qi Pool, Ferris considered the results. He estimated that he had gathered about five times as much qi as he had spent on the chain, which was fantastic. To collect from the river’s center—which had a denser concentration of qi chunks—he would need a longer chain. That would require more qi to make, but even at twice the cost he would still more than double the investment.

There was still more qi to harvest. He guessed there was twice as much in the center as along the edges, and he hadn’t even finished the edges. Unfortunately, he was tired. It was deep into the night, and the next day he would have to… go to work.

With a sigh, Ferris decided to stop for the night. He’d collected some qi, which he believed would let the river start accumulating again. All the stories claim daily qi collection is required to prevent waste, he thought. It was hard to separate fact from fiction in stories with the moral of ‘work hard,’ but he believed qi gathering abilities stopped accumulating if they got full.

The Digest River was no longer full. It was enough for now.

As a final task before bed, Ferris filled the bucket with liquid qi and watered the Core Tree. It hurt to see the pool shrink again, but the tree was his key to greater strength. Each fruit was a chance at an ability that solved all his problems, and watering would significantly speed up the production of fruit. Regretfully, he didn’t think there was enough to also water the two ability seeds.

Waking up in the real world, Ferris tried to stifle a yawn. Cultivation was no replacement for sleep, unfortunately. He passed out as soon as his head hit the pillow.
