[Time]: Summer Break, Day 67 — 10:00 AM[Location]: White City · Townhouse 107 · Estate Expansion: Lower Strata

The final day of the summer break arrived with the quiet, inevitable weight of a server maintenance countdown.

Hathaway stood at the edge of the tropical basin's upper terrace, her boots damp from the morning soil, the warm breeze shifting through the canopy below. The golden light of the perpetual afternoon glinted off the hot spring streams cutting their lazy paths through the undergrowth.

Weeks ago, she had slowed a touring cart near this exact coordinate, back when the whole expanse had still been an artificial tundra, and looked at a circular patch of snowfield glowing quietly from beneath.

The snow was gone. The tropical climate patch had long since reclaimed the terrain. But the subterranean glow remained: a pale, unearthly ring, pulsing quietly beneath the topsoil, faithful to its own physics. A buried heartbeat.

It's time to leave a save file, she thought, raising her wand.

[Move Earth].

The ground responded with a deep, bureaucratic groan. She forced the bedrock to fold, severing the soil's ancient anchors with the clinical precision of an eminent domain order and elevating the glowing ring into a sweeping, tiered terrace that jutted out cleanly above the surrounding canopy. The edges resolved into smooth, curved steps.

[Stone Shape].

She pressed both palms flat against the raw elevation and let the spell engage at the surface layer. Compared to the geological authority of [Move Earth], this was something quieter: a close edit rather than a construction order. The fracture lines along the step faces smoothed into clean, even planes. The platform rim, rough and accidental from the gross excavation, resolved into deliberate geometry.

[Fabricate].

The raw stone shuddered as she flooded it with transmutative mana. The heavy, dark granite bleached, compressed, and crystallized, the atomic lattice surrendering its opacity under sustained pressure. By the time the conversion completed, the platform floor was a seamless expanse of pale, frosted quartz: translucent, almost glassy at the center, grading to solid stone at the edges.

[Plant Growth].

She pushed her mana into the fertile root systems packed along the terraced steps, and the jungle answered immediately. Thick vines erupted from the loam with aggressive, time-lapsed velocity, threading through the quartz masonry with organic exactness. Broad-leafed tropical ferns uncoiled at the platform's corners, framing the sightlines without obscuring the sky. A cascade of glowing moss crept along the curved steps, pulling the architecture into the surrounding jungle like a seam locking two panels of fabric together.

She stepped to the estate's atmospheric control array and pinged the night cycle override: the golden light cut and the tropical canopy vanished.

The platform ignited.

The coral fifty feet below blazed to life, its cold radiance filtering upward through the frosted quartz until the entire terrace became a glowing stage suspended in the dark. The framing ferns cast sharp, crisp silhouettes against the glow. The moss on the steps pulsed gently.

Hathaway stood in the center of the light, arms crossed.

If a high-value environmental asset spawns underground, her developer brain compiled, running the post-mortem with the deep, settled satisfaction of a level designer confirming a successful playtest, you do not excavate it. You do not move the treasure. You build the scenic overlook and move the player to it.

A soft displacement of air settled beside her.

Victoria had descended to the platform silently, her silver hair catching the up-lit bioluminescence in a cold, striking halo. She didn't speak. She simply stopped shoulder-to-shoulder with Hathaway, her pale eyes making one quiet, comprehensive pass over the terraced steps, the framing flora, and the glowing floor, and then settled on the view.

After a while, Hathaway exhaled, reset the atmospheric array to its default golden afternoon, and turned for the sub-basement stairs. The developer had logged her creative session. The alchemist was already clocking in.

[Time]: Summer Break, Day 67 — 2:00 PM[Location]: White City · Townhouse 107 · Sub-Basement Alchemy Laboratory

The romantic triumph of the morning had been ruthlessly overwritten by the industrial drudgery of the afternoon.

Raw material identification was a hostile interrogation of physical matter.

The procedure required an encyclopedic mastery of alchemical baseline states and a stable enough mana output to sustain continuous diagnostic passes without drift: locate the closest baseline match in Common Mineral Traits, run a confirmatory test, then log the verified composition into the standardized table format prescribed by In-Depth Mineral Analysis, Alchemy Basics II.

She raised the Crystal Lens to her right eye. The device looked like a jeweler's loupe that had been subjected to aggressive magical engineering: six internal layers of enchanted glass, each calibrated to strip away a different stratum of physical reality. When she flooded it with mana, the jagged ore chunk in her other hand dissolved visually into a topographical map of glowing veins, particulate densities, and spiritual resonance gradients, color-coded by elemental composition, which she cross-referenced against the mental database.

"Seventeen percent gold," Hathaway murmured, her quill scratching across the ledger. "Twenty-nine percent red iron. Six percent cloud steel. Six percent weathered rock..."

She lowered the lens, right eye aching, peeled an adhesive label from the strip, wrote [G-0042] in neat block lettering, slapped it onto the ore, and tossed it into the processed bin. She picked up the forty-third rock.

These were gold companion minerals: the frustrating, heavily bonded impurities excavated alongside premium veins in the wild. Most apprentices rarely encountered them in unidentified form: market minerals arrived pre-certified, because no Witch holding a mining claim had any interest in undervaluing her own excavation. Raw samples required field access.

Wilhelmina had sent three industrial crates the day she heard Hathaway needed the practice.

She adjusted the lens and began the cross-reference again.

You do not start by brewing miracles, she thought, blinking away the strain. You start by becoming a human mass-spectrometer.

[Time]: Summer Break, Day 67 — 7:30 PM[Location]: White City · Townhouse 107 · Dining Room

The sheer volume of caloric output arranged across the dining table bordered on the obscene.

Hathaway took her seat, running a rapid situational audit. Central platter: pan-seared cod, golden-crusted and gleaming under a rich, dark reduction of El Dorado red wine and cracked black pepper, flanked by crisp, refreshing cubes of Desert Fairy-Cat Cactus fruit and delicate braised bamboo shoots. Side plates: Casendiara-style baked escargot, the traditional shells replaced by proprietary baked pouches of Casendiara potato whipped to silk with cultured butter and fresh cream, the savory, umami-dense snail meat sealed inside.

And at the center of the table: a monolithic, crusty sourdough boule roughly the size of an overgrown Lantern Cat. The interior had been surgically hollowed out and flooded with a boiling, wildly fragrant wild mushroom velouté. The opening had been sealed with a thick layer of buttery puff pastry, slow-baked until the crust fractured at the tap of a spoon and released a billowing cloud of savory, earthy steam.

We return to campus tomorrow, Hathaway's logic engine processed, unfolding her napkin with the calm of someone completing an obvious diagnostic. Margaret has activated the full culinary arsenal to preemptively buffer the institutional food supply chain.

She was halfway through her second pass at the mushroom soup when Rory, who had been vibrating in her high chair for the past several minutes with the localized kinetic energy of an unpinned grenade, finally crossed the threshold.

The toddler lurched to her feet on the upholstery, both hands clutching a rolled parchment behind her back, her wide eyes darting between the two mothers with an expression of desperate, barely-throttled conspiracy.

The containment window was approximately four seconds.

"Hattie! Hattie!" Rory shrieked, bouncing with full-body commitment. "Birthday!"

At the head of the table, Margaret and Anna froze.

A rapid, high-velocity tactical exchange occurred between them across the tablecloth: the synchronized, resigned look of two project managers realizing the NDA had been breached thirty seconds before the official embargo was scheduled to lift. The carefully choreographed reveal, whatever it had been, was administratively aborted in real time.

Anna cleared her throat with rapid, damage-control composure. "Well. Darling, today is your—"

"Happy Birthday!" Margaret interrupted, entirely failing to maintain the pacing, lunging across the table to shove a velvet box into Hathaway's hands before her wife could finish the sentence.

Hathaway set down her spoon.

Birthday. She ran a frantic query on her host body's chronological data. September 5th. The date had sailed past her internal calendar completely unacknowledged, buried under lunar-tide compensation coefficients and the impending weight of the A2 curriculum.

She opened Margaret's box. Resting on velvet was a heavy, flawlessly clear glass sphere set on a brass stand. It looked, at first inspection, precisely like a premium decorative snow globe.

She brought it close. Beneath the curved glass, a miniature weather system was actively circulating. Tiny clouds drifted over a landmass of considerable scale, threaded by branching river systems. Patches of green forest gave way to agricultural grids. And between those grids, barely visible at the limit of her perception: the warm, infinitesimally small flicker of campfires.

"It's a pocket sphere," Margaret explained, practically radiating parental pride. "A fully encapsulated micro-reality. The population is hardcoded under a million, with no magical resources, just standard grain agriculture. Very common among ordinary Witches who want the experience of total local sovereignty. Even a Witch with five hundred mana is functionally indistinguishable from an omnipotent deity to the people inside."

She smiled warmly. "It is basically a premium simulation sandbox. You can shape the rivers. You can alter the weather. You can—"

There are campfires in there, Hathaway's processor said, very quietly.

On my nineteenth birthday, my mother gifted me the absolute, unyielding dominion over one million sentient mortal souls as a desktop hobby item. I have officially become a slave-owning deity.

"Thank you, Mom," Hathaway managed, setting the universe down very carefully on the tablecloth.

Anna slid her own gift across the table with a warm, slightly knowing smile. A sleek, heavy wooden case.

"Witch Chess," Anna said.

Hathaway unlatched the lid. Inside rested rows of blank, unpainted figurines, intricately carved and ready to be bound.

"You paint the pieces yourself to bind them," Anna explained, her fingers tracing the case's edge. "It requires a Landlord to run the session and two or more Witches to play; the pieces represent mortal investigators, cultists, various servitor units, each run through a customized narrative world."

It was Call of Cthulhu, structurally identical, with one catastrophic, proprietary difference.

The pieces were alive.

"They possess simulated autonomous cognition," Anna continued, her eyes glinting with the specific amusement of a Witch who had done this many times. "You act as an unseen cosmic influence, projecting suggestions into their minds. If you push too hard against a piece's hardcoded character logic, say, forcing a righteous police officer to pick a lock illegally, there is a probability check. The piece achieves sudden lucidity. It thinks: Am I being controlled by a dark, eldritch force? It begins actively resisting."

Hathaway stared at the blank figurines.

I am now holding a simulator designed for gods to experience what it feels like to traumatize mortals for recreational purposes. The species comes pre-configured.

She had not yet fully processed her new status as a multi-planar cosmic horror when Rory scrambled out of her chair, sprinted the length of the table, and shoved a rolled parchment directly into Hathaway's hands.

It was a crayon drawing, executed with the chaotic, unapologetically aggressive pressure only a toddler could generate. At the center of the composition: a massive, brown, potato-shaped creature with an alarming, structurally improbable number of jagged teeth. A bright yellow crown perched on its head. Standing beside the beast was a stick figure rendered at a deeply questionable scale, labeled in enormous, triumphant, slightly wobbly letters:

HATTIE.

Hathaway dropped the pocket civilization, dropped the Eldritch horror simulator, fell to her knees, and pulled Rory into a fierce, crushing hug.

A gift! her soul wept, with a profound, cleansing relief that accessed emotional registers she had not visited in either of her lifetimes. A normal, wholesome, structurally safe gift! No free will subverted! No sentient beings were harmed in the making of this crayon drawing!

"I love it," Hathaway whispered into Rory's curls. "I am framing this. It is going directly above my primary alchemy workbench."

[Time]: Summer Break, Day 67 — 9:00 PM[Location]: White City · Townhouse 107 · The Observation Deck

Victoria had watched the gift exchange from the far end of the room with the quiet, composed attention of a scholar monitoring a controlled experiment. Once the dinner table chaos had settled and Rory had been carried upstairs, she rose, crossed the parlor, and took Hathaway's hand, and led her through the garden doors, down the shallow ramp into the tropical basin, and directly to the frosted quartz observation deck Hathaway had constructed that morning. The coral glow from underground pulsed softly beneath the translucent floor, casting the terrace in its familiar blue-white light.

But her level design had a new exit.

A spiraling staircase of the same frosted, light-catching stone had been carved into the platform's far edge, descending cleanly through the bedrock and piercing the ceiling of the subterranean hot spring grotto far below.

Hathaway walked to the opening and looked down: directly beneath her boots, the bioluminescent coral sea blazed in cold cyan and deep magenta, filtered through the translucent quartz and diffused into a spectral, luminous blue. But following the stairwell further down, the colors shifted and warmed: the cold light gave way to the rolling amber-orange glow of the geothermal springs, and below that, drifting slowly through the mineral steam, the deep crimson silhouettes of the floating sea-flowers. The entire vertical cross-section of the earth was alive, breathing, layered: a living geological exhibit built by two people who had never coordinated a single dimension of it.

Victoria reached into the pocket of her dark jacket. A choker: matte black leather, supple, completely unadorned except for a single dark metallic clasp.

"I made this," Victoria said, her voice quiet in the night. "The enchantment matrix stabilizes the spirit and maintains cognitive clarity under prolonged alchemical exposure." She paused. "Given your curriculum, I calculated you would need it before long."

Before Hathaway's processing threads could formulate a response, Victoria said: "Turn around."

Hathaway turned.

Victoria stepped close behind her. The cool, smooth leather pressed against the front of Hathaway's throat. The silver clasp was barely a sound: a single, deliberate click, but it was Victoria's gloved fingertips brushing the sensitive skin at the nape of Hathaway's neck as she secured the buckle that sent a sudden, sharp jolt through Hathaway's entire nervous system.

The frost-and-buried-warmth of her settled close.

Hathaway swallowed. The choker settled warmly against her collarbone, and the subtle enchantment immediately sent a clean, clarifying hum into her overclocked mind.

Victoria let her hands fall and stepped back half a pace to look.

In the pale blue irises, something surfaced: a flicker of something profoundly possessive, moving through the cold geometry of her expression like a light passing beneath ice. It lasted exactly one microsecond before being locked away with precise, unhurried composure.

She didn't step back far enough.

Her logic engine did not compile a decision tree. The anxious processing weight of the semester, the A2 curriculum, the T8 spell she didn't yet know how to ask about: all of it flatlined simultaneously.

Hathaway stepped forward and kissed her.

She felt the shock run through Victoria's frame immediately: the aristocratic programming catching a catastrophic null-pointer exception, the immaculate Wellington firmware failing entirely to predict or account for the aggressive preemptive strike. For a genuine, unscripted microsecond, Victoria was frozen solid.

Then Victoria's hand came up. Her index finger hooked smoothly under the front of the black leather choker and pulled Hathaway flush against her, eliminating the remaining space between them with quiet, unhurried authority. She tilted her head and kissed her back with a focused, sudden intensity that chased every coherent server process from Hathaway's cognitive architecture in approximately 0.4 seconds.

The blue-white light pulsed beneath their boots. The warm tropical air moved through the framing ferns.

This, Hathaway thought, her hands tangling in the silver hair, the server logs shutting down completely, the observation deck and the coral glow and the two layers of light below all dissolving into a single, steady warmth, is the best day of my life.
