Down in the chaotic, overflowing basin of the valley, life was incredibly cheap and brutally short.

The sheer density of the monster horde was forcing the creatures to turn on each other simply to carve out an inch of breathing room. Near the very edge of the horde, closest to the rocky defile where the adventurers waited in absolute silence, a pack of small, filthy goblins was milling about aimlessly. They were squabbling violently over the stripped carcass of a wild dog, their rusted daggers flashing in the weak morning sun.

A standard orc, easily seven feet of corded green muscle and foul temper, waded through the squabble. The heavy iron plates bolted to his chest clattered loudly as he walked. Annoyed by the shrieking creatures blocking his path, the orc let out a deafening, guttural roar. He raised a massive, jaggedly armoured fist high into the air, fully intending to crush the skull of the nearest goblin simply to clear the way.

The heavy fist began its downward arc.

A wet, incredibly final thwack echoed sharply over the guttural snarls.

The orc froze mid-swing. A beautiful, steel-tipped arrow had materialised perfectly through its left eye, burying itself so deeply into the beast's primitive brain that the bloody steel arrowhead protruded from the back of its thick skull. The massive creature swayed for a fraction of a second before collapsing forward like a felled timber, crushing the very goblin it had intended to hit.

For a single heartbeat, the surrounding goblins simply stared in dumb, uncomprehending shock.

Then, a second arrow whistled through the freezing air. It punched cleanly through the throat of a goblin, pinning the screaming creature to the frozen mud. A third arrow followed instantly, finding the unarmoured armpit of another orc.

High above the valley floor, crouched upon a shadowed stone ledge, Perberos activated a skill. His pale hands became a literal blur of motion. He was drawing, nocking, and loosing arrows with a speed that defied the physical limits of anatomy. The bowstring of his beautifully crafted longbow emitted a continuous, low-pitched, deadly hum. The arrows did not fall individually; they rained down upon the leading edge of the horde like a sudden, metallic hailstorm.

The other rogues and archers, taking their cue from the higher-levelled adventurer, leaned out from their high perches and released their own volleys.

A wave of absolute death swept over the front ranks of the monstrous army. Goblins were pinned to the earth in droves. Orcs roared in agony as shafts of wood and steel sprouted from their shoulders, chests, and thighs. The slaughter was incredibly efficient, systematic, and entirely one-sided.

But the horde was simply too vast.

Perberos’s arrows were landing so thickly that the ground looked like the back of a steel-quilled porcupine, but it was akin to trying to empty a bucket with a grain of sand. There were over twenty thousand screaming, hungry, violent beasts crammed into the rocky basin.

The sudden, concentrated barrage of arrows finally drew the collective attention of the horde. Thousands of monstrous heads snapped towards the narrow, rocky defile. They saw the shadows moving on the high ledges. They smelled the warm, fresh blood of the twelve heavily armoured humans and dwarves standing in an unbroken line across the narrow gap.

A sound like the earth itself tearing open rose from the valley. It was a singular, deafening roar composed of thousands of overlapping shrieks, bellows, and war cries.

The horde surged.

They did not employ tactics. They did not form ranks. It was a blind, frantic, completely mindless stampede of flesh and rusted iron, driven by a primal hunger and the insurmountable pressure of the thousands of bodies pushing from behind. The frozen mud of the valley floor turned into a slick, treacherous paste under the pounding of tens of thousands of feet.

As the leading edge of the green tide closed the distance, rapidly approaching the boulder barricades at the mouth of the defile, the archers were forced to step back from the ledges to avoid the incoming wave.

It was time for the heavy artillery.

Brett stepped right up behind the tightly packed line of twelve melee fighters. He did not need the hyper-concentrated, precision plasma he usually reserved for high-level elites. The creatures charging them were weak, low-level fodder. He needed raw, unadulterated volume.

The mage raised his hands, his fingers spread wide. The deep red, drake-scale gloves glowed with a blinding, incandescent heat. He slammed his palms together.

The tiny Cinder-Sprite familiar, hovering near his shoulder, darted forward, expanding rapidly as Brett channelled a massive, terrifying volume of mana through it.

"Burn," Brett commanded coldly.

A roaring, blinding tsunami of elemental flame cascaded down the rocky slope. It wasn't a fireball; it was a solid, moving wall of pure, crackling fire that stretched across the narrow entrance to the defile. The heat was so intense it instantly evaporated the morning frost on the rocks, turning the cold air into a suffocating, blistering furnace.

The wall of fire crashed headlong into the charging front ranks of the horde.

The results were utterly devastating. Hundreds of goblins simply vaporised, their screams cut short as the magical flames turned their fragile bodies to ash in a fraction of a second. The larger orcs fared little better, their iron armour superheating and cooking them alive from the inside out. The stench of burning hair, scorching meat, and vaporised blood filled the defile with a thick, cloying smoke.

Taking Brett's lead, the other lower-level spellcasters standing safely in the rear lines unleashed their own magic. Bolts of jagged ice, erratic spheres of kinetic energy, and searing streaks of arcane light plunged into the screaming masses.

The sheer, unprecedented volume of raw mana rushing into the narrow, enclosed space of the rocky defile had a profound physical effect on the environment. The air itself became supercharged, thick and heavy with chaotic magical pressure. Tiny, jagged arcs of purple static electricity began to snap and hiss violently between the sheer rock walls, creating a terrifying, miniature arcane storm hovering right over their heads.

But no matter the absolute, unmitigated force being directed down the slope, the mathematics of the battlefield were entirely against them.

For every ten monsters incinerated by Brett’s wall of fire, or frozen solid by an ice lance, or pinned to the earth by an elven arrow, one monstrous, burnt, screaming beast managed to push through. And with a horde numbering over twenty thousand, a ten percent survival rate meant thousands of monsters were still barrelling towards them.

The fire broke. The bodies piled too high, creating a gruesome, charred ramp that shielded the monsters behind them from the worst of the flames.

The remaining beasts hit the boulder barricades with the force of a crashing landslide.

"Brace!" Josh roared, his voice carrying clearly over the deafening cacophony of the charging horde.

Three massive, grey-skinned cave trolls, their thick hides covered in blistering burns and smoking arrow shafts, led the physical impact. They vaulted over the hastily constructed boulder walls, roaring in blind, agonised fury, and crashed directly into the twelve-man line.

Josh dug his heavy steel boots deep into the frozen dirt. He raised his heavy shield, tucking his shoulder tightly behind the metal, and activated his Living Bulwark skill.

A massive troll slammed into him. The sheer kinetic force of the impact was like being hit by a runaway carriage. Josh’s boots skidded backwards in the mud, carving deep, ugly trenches, but the line held.

As the troll’s massive, meaty fists pounded against Josh’s shield, the static electricity generated by his defensive skill violently discharged. Bright, blinding arcs of blue lightning snapped from the steel face of the shield, wrapping around the troll's arms and violently shocking its nervous system. The beast roared, its massive muscles spasming involuntarily.

Josh didn't waste the opening. He lunged forward from behind the safety of his shield, driving his flaming longsword upward in a brutal thrust. The enchanted, burning steel slid effortlessly under the troll’s thick jaw, punching straight through the roof of its mouth and directly into its primitive brain.

The beast went limp instantly, its massive, dead weight collapsing forward onto Josh’s shield.

Josh grunted, using all his enhanced strength to violently shove the heavy corpse to the side. There was no time to breathe. The moment the troll fell, three shrieking goblins and a furious, battle-scarred orc leapt over the carcass to take its place.

The defile devolved into an absolute, claustrophobic nightmare of blood, screaming steel, and raw, visceral slaughter.

Bhel was a terrifying whirlwind of iron and death to Josh's right. The dwarf had completely abandoned any pretence of defence. His twin axes, blazing with Brett’s magical fire, moved in continuous, lethal figures of eight. He waded into the incoming tide of green flesh, hacking off limbs, decapitating goblins, and cleaving straight through the crude iron armour of the charging orcs. He was soon painted entirely from head to toe in thick, dark, foul-smelling monster blood.

Thorne, the arrogant human fighter, was fighting desperately on Josh's left. The man was competent, his broadsword reaping a heavy toll on the encroaching goblins, but the sheer, unrelenting pressure of the horde was clearly terrifying him. He swung wildly, his eyes wide with panic as clawed hands constantly reached over the barricades, trying to drag him down into the mud.

The horde was not simply a melee force.

As the bodies piled higher and higher at the mouth of the defile, the creatures trapped further back in the valley began to retaliate. A continuous, deadly hail of crude, black-fletched bone arrows arced high over the charging lines, raining down upon the defenders.

Most shattered harmlessly against Josh's heavy plate armour or Bhel’s thick iron shoulder guards, but the volume ensured that some found their marks. Arrows clipped exposed arms, grazed thighs, and bit into the softer leather armour of the back line.

Worse still were the goblin shamans. Safe from the immediate melee, they began hurling highly unstable, erratic spheres of corrosive green magic.

The magic splashed wildly. Some hit the defenders' shields, hissing and popping harmlessly against Brett's thermal aura. But many missed the line entirely, impacting the sheer stone faces of the defile right above their heads.

The rocks shattered under the explosive, acidic force. Sharp, jagged fragments of stone shrapnel rained down continuously, stinging the eyes and slicing open the cheeks of the fighters and mages below.

Carcan was a beacon of desperate, unyielding light right behind the melee line. Her amber-topped staff glowed with a blinding, continuous brilliance. She was humming a complex, multi-layered prayer, her voice barely audible over the roaring battle. She cast minor, rapid mends to seal the shrapnel cuts on Brett’s face, threw quick, shimmering shields over the younger, struggling fighters on the flanks, and continuously pushed raw, revitalising energy into Josh and Bhel.

But even her vast magical reserves were beginning to strain under the continuous, chaotic demand.

The battle lost all concept of time. The sun climbed higher in the sky, completely hidden by the thick, choking canopy of black smoke and burning monster flesh that hung heavy over the valley.

It was no longer a battle of tactical skill. It was a terrifying, deeply gruesome mathematical equation of sheer physical stamina.

Josh swung his flaming sword in a wide, horizontal arc, perfectly severing the head of a leaping orc. He immediately pulled his shield up to violently bash a goblin backwards, then lunged forward to thrust his blade into the chest of another charging troll.

Swing. Block. Thrust. Shield bash.

The repetitive, brutal mechanics of the slaughter were tearing his body apart from the inside out. His arms burned with a deep, excruciating heat that rivalled Brett's elemental magic. It felt as though someone had injected molten lava directly into his biceps and his shoulders. Every time he raised his heavy steel shield to absorb an impact, his spine groaned in protest, and his lungs screamed for clean, smoke-free air.

He risked a split-second glance to his right.

Bhel had finally stopped laughing. The dwarf was still swinging his axes with lethal precision, but the movements were distinctly sluggish. His chest was heaving with huge, ragged gasps of air. The thick layer of monster blood coating his face and beard was now heavily streaked with pale lines of pure, exhaustive sweat.

Behind them, Brett was leaning heavily against the sheer stone wall of the defile. The mage’s drake-scale gloves were still glowing, but the light was noticeably dimmer. He was panting, his face incredibly pale, his eyes entirely bloodshot. The symptoms of severe mana overuse were creeping in. The younger spellcasters behind him had already collapsed to their knees, completely drained, unable to conjure even a minor spark.

They were hitting their absolute physical and magical limits.

Josh violently kicked the fresh corpse of an orc away from the barricade and used the fraction of a second of breathing room to look out over the top of his blood-slicked shield.

They had built a literal mountain of death. The charred, butchered remains of hundreds, no, thousands of goblins, orcs, and trolls were piled waist-high at the mouth of the defile. The ground was so slick with gore that maintaining a defensive footing was becoming nearly impossible.

But as Josh looked past the immediate slaughter, staring out into the wider valley beyond the smoke and the flying arrows, his stomach plummeted into a bottomless abyss of despair.

The valley was still completely full.

The writhing, shrieking ocean of dark green flesh stretched endlessly all the way back to the black, gaping mouth of the cave. The portal was still active, still vomiting new, furious monsters into the basin to replace the ones the party had slain.

They had fought like demons. They had rained hundreds of arrows and stood like a wall of unbreakable iron against an impossible tide.

But the terrible, crushing reality of the situation finally settled heavily into Josh’s exhausted mind.

They had not even made a dent. The horde was not even half dead.

The terrifying truth was that they were not going to die because a goblin got lucky with a rusted dagger, or because an orc outmanoeuvred them.

They were going to die because their muscles were simply going to stop working. They were going to die of exhaustion, buried beneath a never-ending avalanche of flesh and bone.

Another hulking, grey-skinned troll vaulted over the mountain of corpses, roaring its fury, its massive clubs raised high.

Josh tightened his grip on his screaming, burning muscles, raised his heavy shield, and prepared to meet the charge once more.
