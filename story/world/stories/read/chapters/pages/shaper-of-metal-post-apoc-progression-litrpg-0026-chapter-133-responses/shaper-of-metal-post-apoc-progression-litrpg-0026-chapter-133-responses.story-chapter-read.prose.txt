Chapter 133: Responses

Jack was quite simply instructed to head to his fake apartment and chill, which he promptly obeyed, taking another flying cab to get there. It was a quite affluent one in Herakles District — significantly bigger than his quarters in Everywhere Hall, but certainly not as impressive as the place he’d just come from. He was also told he could relax, because the place was being exceedingly, if clandestinely, monitored.

Yes, how comforting.

Jack ordered some food for the evening and settled in for some brain-drain TV time, as that could kill time like nothing else. He watched half of a throwball matchup, feeling very little for either side. Ball go down field, ball go down other field. Men chase ball; men fight for ball. Great stuff!

He inevitably fell asleep in the cush recliner of the living room. He awoke to a chiming alert from his phone.

Checking it, he saw, <You’ve received a text from Platform Latterbloom, a known independent proxy address source and generally considered suspicious. Do you accept or decline?>

Oh shit. Here we go…

He accepted and saw the text reading, <Son. I got word you were seeking me out. I’m glad. It’s been a while, hasn’t it? I admit, things are a bit weird. Big things are happening, though. I wouldn't mind meeting and discussing them with you. Sorry, but it can’t be in the normal rounds. I got a spot in the Latterbloom Complex, and I can meet in one hour. Hope you can get this message in time. Not sure when I can offer again, if ever. Been very, very busy.

It ain’t like old times. I used to sit on the back porch, smoking and watching you play in that big sand pile. You were so loud, I could hear the stories you made up. I was gonna use that sand until you got attached to it, you know. Priorities change, I guess. I remember you lost a toy, and we didn’t find it, even digging up and sifting the whole damn pile together. Your dirty ass moped and cried, and I had to buy another one. A robot, wasn’t it?>

Jack’s heart was pounding from the first of the text, mixed feelings of hitting paydirt and the fact that the ‘mark,’ so to speak, was probably his closest loved one.

It isn’t like he isn’t trying to play me, too, on some level.

The second part stunned him emotionally. For one, the whole thing was startlingly sober-minded. But moreover, his dad’s pockmarked memory summoned that specificity up? He quickly realized it was a presented proof… and a test.

Before he could start his reply, Memoria popped into his head. “Hold your horses, Jack! Get your head clear first. Aside from that, this bears thought and discussion. Take a minute to wake up.”

“Roger,” Jack said, dropping the phone on his lap and sitting back, taking a deep breath and rubbing his face as he stared up at the unfamiliar ceiling. He got up and went into the kitchen, finding a canned coffee energy drink in the fridge. He popped it and took a long pull. It was way too sugary. It also… didn’t really scratch the itch, because it wasn’t Infused.

Nothing like that in here. Doubt it would be something detectable, but who knows? Not worth the risk. It does leave distinctive residues and smells, after all.

He knew of Platform Latterbloom. An arcology consisting of one imposing construct and tons of farmland, primarily dedicated to independent agricultural engineering, financed by anonymous donors, though blatantly obvious to be of strong Farmer’s Alliance ilk. Technically like Eden, though — super private, independent, and ostensibly self-sufficient. In contrast to Eden, though, it had a decidedly old-world ‘corporate’ feel. Part of the project was making use of AI and drones outside of Memoria’s control, so it wasn’t exactly an ‘elbow grease and a kick to git ‘er started’ sort of operation, to say the least.

There’s big money injected in that fat fowl’s guts to make her juicy. She’s quite a sight and smell cooking in the oven; I’m about to feel the heat myself, it seems.

Memoria finally continued, “Alright, this is going to be a tricky one, Jack. It’s certainly dangerous, though using it as simply a neutral ground is feasible, even probable. I can’t just storm that compound — in fact, I suspect only a handful within are cult agents, possibly just extended allies. Suspicion isn’t enough. We have a couple of reliable informants inside, but by no means are they sleeper agents. I can’t even send you to this place; it’s just your decision, if that makes sense? Your invitation is the only reason this is possible. I’m contractually unable to send others without an emergency. So, you’re going into the belly of the beast completely alone.”

“It’s fine,” Jack replied. “I’m going in anyway.” After seeing what became of the man who was once Shreddy and seeing what happened to the likes of Screamer, there was no way in hell Jack wasn’t going to do everything he could to help. In truth, it went even beyond his dad, but here was the opportunity to salvage his mind in the process — to preserve what made his dad who he was. Almost literally, his soul was at stake here.

I imagine my birth mother would find that line ironic. She’d tried similar on me before.

“As you say, then, my son. On the plus side, any number of scenarios could work in our favor. In what is hopefully the worst-case scenario, if I rationally conclude you’ve been detained illegally, against your will, and so on, I can demand your release and return, and rather promptly go in after you if they don’t immediately start capitulating. If the tip-top elements there aren’t compromised, they will bow, because every independent platform fears the day of the goddess’s wrath, as it were. They’ve all seen the results in examples I’ve made.

“Meanwhile, if you are moved off of that platform, I’ll know it and immediately act as if an act of war was committed. They can’t hide you from me in that scenario — no one smuggles my children. Only in them not knowing who and what you are, would they even try it.”

Jack considered that thoughtfully as he swallowed another gulp of too-sweet coffee. “In that case, maybe you should let them.”

This actually seemed to stun Memoria. “Uh…”

Jack laughed. He wouldn’t pull that off terribly often, so he cherished it. “If he’s recruiting me, let me go undercover. They can’t easily tell that I’m an ANP, right?”

“I don’t know. It isn’t obvious. Outside of our order, no one would ever think you’re an ANP. It’s out of the question, even enough to disbelieve with the proof right in their noses. But it also isn’t ideal, and if they look hard eno- Jack. I don’t like this.”

He laughed again, even at the dreadful feeling of Memoria — someone who had scrambled horrors and terrors as her daily breakfast — admitting such a thing. “But you’ll do it?”

There was a long pause and then a sigh. “If you’re transported, in a reasonable amount of time after being on-site, and you remain mentally hale when you are transported… yes. But no promises after you cross another threshold. I don’t need much excuse to rain hellfire down on a community unfortunate enough to inadvertently pray for it. Expect cavalry at the first sign of danger, because they can’t block my sense of your status or location if they illegally detain you. I may act even sooner.”

“Why is that?”

“Because I’m a mother, to put it succinctly.”

“Ah. And what if it isn’t really illegal, because I go willingly?”

“In terms of the law, it’s irrelevant. You’re mine, and no independent is allowed to take you. I don’t do contracts that don’t have that stipulation. I’ll never allow my ‘chosen’ — as you know, actually my precious uncorrupted — to be tricked out of my hand. It’s also part of your contract, after all. Powers serve me — serve the greater war of the species’ survival to which I am the personification of — that, or no one. I’m quite bendable and flexible in general, but not at that particular junction.”

“Got it. Is there anything else, or can I text back?”

“I just want to make something clear, first: if you’re flown out, touching down on another platform will endanger your father more, or risk him slipping away.”

Jack grimaced and ran a hand through his hair. “I understand that, but we both know it’s about more than him. Can I signal you even if we’re in their territory? To come for us?”

“In a manner of speaking, you can. You have to send pain, panic, stress, to an extreme degree. It’s a little harder to do that deliberately. Punch really, really hard with that, and I’ll pick it up. Preferably, you have some trigger to give it more oomph. An actual sense of danger, in other words.” There was a pause and a feeling of sudden realization. “Or you could just route literally anything through Neex with tragic ease, because she has no contract restrictions! Ugh — my thought matrix is off today.”

“Ah! Good idea. That’s eureka-level stuff. Though come to think of it, I don’t cherish the thought of worrying her on this front…”

“What are you going to do, hide that you’re going on a dangerous mission from her?”

“Well…”

“Forget it. I forbid this as your mother and superior. I’m going to brief her — This is too critical of a resource! Mission success chances just increased! So just accept reality, son: it’s a positive thing.”

“You’re a stone-cold traitor, Mother. Anyway, fine. At least I know you won’t send her in with me now that you need her outside. I… think.”

“Now that’s seeing things positively, Jack! Good on you.”

“Just to verify, you won’t, right?”

There was an impression of Memoria rolling her eyes. “For Pete’s sake, Jack, of course not! She doesn’t have an invitation to begin with, and asking for a Plus One would be deeply suspicious, so it's moot.”

“Wonderful. Alright, is that everything? Can I text him?”

“Yes. The plan is for you to allow yourself to be persuaded to some degree. Your father appears likely to be at least somewhat compromised, but also sharp and shrewd. I shouldn’t need to tell you that your father is already that and very paranoid, by default. So don’t be too out of phase with who he knows you to be. I doubt you’d be an easy sell.”

The image of a random middle-aged man popped into Jack’s head. He wore a ratty leather cap and had a long face, with a sleepy, sad default countenance vaguely reminiscent of a basset hound. “This is Sammy The Slide. This is precautionary, as your father might already know, but you might need to drop a name to verify the feasibility of your new career, though you ultimately just started it. He’s your guy. Ostensibly a hardcore smuggler, but in our pocket completely and loyally. Former military and a tough hombre. Informed enough about you; you working with him should check out by multiple vectors.

“Use this phrase: ‘The Slide’s worked an angle or two for me.’ That’ll register a bit like a code and should be enough. Your father has had indirect dealings with him in the past, and, as far as I know, The Slide is not well-known in the circles the cult is thick into. If he was, I’d have been using that more.”

“I can’t think of a more appropriately shady handle than Sammy The Slide. It’s an honor to be among the classics, really. Do I get a special nickname, too?”

Memoria snorted. “Considering the magnitude of this operation, in which Jack Laker is surely involved, when it’s over, the ID will almost certainly be permanently shelved. That might go for Sammy, for that matter, but it is what it is, to cauterize this infection.”

“Aww.” Jack walked back into the living room, retrieved his phone, and sat down on the couch, the phone in one hand and his drink in the other. “What about if we nab the whole shebang and kick the Dreamer out of town? Would it matter, then?”

“It’s quite a stretch right now. The leader, Qingu, seems content to hide deeply out of my reach or perception and spread this infection through pawns. It’s a very chaotic, ‘throw it all against the wall and see what sticks’ sort of operation. If I weren’t merciful, I’d be burning so much down, Jack. By certain emergency declarations, I could. But like the ironic image of a homeowner nuking it thrice over to kill a problem mouse, I have to ask myself: what if it still gets away?

“Everything we got from Qingu’s leaders failed to lead us to her, though we crippled multiple side operations. The Dreamer’s plots are hard to track or even fathom as it experiments mutagenically. Your father is seemingly second or even third-hand affected, possibly through the drug. To put it plainly, the disease is lodged in our society like an old-world flu, and we’re scrambling to run containment. Your father is more likely to lead us to the eradication of ‘strain 18’ than to the coveted Patient Zero. More pieces of an alien intelligence’s puzzle, requiring continually evolving heuristics to unravel, both of us trying to think more like the other first.”

Jack took a sip of sugary cold coffee and sighed. “Damn. That at least makes me feel a little better about my dad, though. Not interfacing with the direct intensity that Shreddy did seems like a plus.”

“Let’s remain cautiously hopeful. Anyway, that’s enough briefing. Send the text and arrange the meeting.”

Jack began typing with one hand, balancing the art of careful strategy without getting too complex for text. <Dad! Thank the goddess you’re alright, man. Latterbloom. That’s weird, alright. Looks like we both made connections.

It’s the middle of the damned night, but I guess some things never change, huh? Sure, let’s meet. I look forward to it, despite the hour.

It wasn’t a robot, it was a cyborg soldier. The Silver Executor. I found the little rifle he held. When you got me the new figurine, I was happy because he could dual-wield the rifles, and thus had become doubly badass. Especially because they had sound effects that could layer.>

His dad was fairly prompt on the reply. <The Silver Executor. So it was. I remember the animated character doing ads for Cyber Gyros.

Sorry about the hour. Come in about 45 minutes to the Platform. You’ll need an authorized pilot for a drop-off. For access, give your pilot the code JUP0523398741. Looking forward to it too, son.>

Here we go. No option to land a return vehicle, either.

Knowing scheduling could be dicey, he used his phone and online interface to get a pilot in advance. The option of ‘Platform Latterbloom’ required the entering of a code to even schedule it, so that was already taken care of, along with the payment. He also had to do a virtual sign on a waiver document about dangers after a drop-off. How cute.

He took a shower, grabbed a bite, and otherwise prepared for his mission. He had no clue what to expect, but whatever he felt about meeting his father again was overshadowed by the severity of why they were meeting.

He doubted there was much chance of this ending up pleasant.

Platform Latterbloom was a fascinating place to fly over, at least for Jack. Farms were rigidly ordered into flat squares of endless precision, giving that at once enviable and disturbing sense of man’s presumed dominance over nature. With nothing but drones visible across the spectrum of the semi-artificial plain, one had to question whether man, in fact, was the one truly at the helm, or the mere figurehead.

Though the Latterbloom Complex looked small in comparison, at least from a distance, its grandeur became obvious as one got closer. It was a sleek, multi-time-renovated arcology. If there was any other way to describe an arcology, it was something like a fattened, giant connected mall of skyscrapers and other buildings, like a smushed-together city. This one had a significant hollow interior. It had everything except a soul and was a technologically-sophisticated, ostensibly self-sustainable facility with plentiful built-in plantlife and gardens plainly visible within its architecturally interesting, yet not quite describable, mutant shape. ‘Bubbly.’ That was the word he thought of.

It was as foreboding, alien, familiar, and fascinating as a sci-fi spaceship, as Jack saw it. A weird home that man made ‘somewhere else.’

Then again, maybe it feels foreboding because I feel like Dante descending into Hell, even if this one masquerades as Heaven.

Traffic by air was in pockets going to and fro, and more was evident as they crossed over the top to make the middle access visible. Small, very arcology-style (i.e., bubbly) transports flitted around, taking people here or there. His pilot dropped him off at one of a seemingly endless number of step-off platforms. Jack tipped him well and bid him adieu.

A man in a very arcology-style uniform was waiting for him right off the platform, wearing a polite, very arcology-style smile on his face. At the end of the platform, two very arcology-style helmeted guards, surely worth their weight in dirt, were watching proceedings. “Mr. Laker,” the uniformed gent said as he gestured with a hand. “I will see you to your contact, if you please.”

“Of course,” Jack said smoothly, sensing it was unwise to deliver any other answer. “Lead on.”

After they were past the suck-cheeked, prudish-eyed, constipated-looking guardsmen in their ridiculous, bubbly helmets, Jack asked, “Can I ask where we’re going?”

“Certainly, Mr. Laker.”

Jack waited a beat and tried not to roll his eyes or sigh. “Where are we going?”

“To the arcology, of course.”

“Right. At least you guys have a sense of humor.”

“What do you mean?”

“Are-... Nothing, never mind.”

“As you say, Mr. Laker.” Swish, swish, swish! The man led on, swift and straight-backed, no doubt the pride of his little empire. Other people he saw walked largely the same.

Oh heavens above, I hate it here already. Damn it, Dad. Why? Please take me elsewhere. If there’s trouble, I’d rather have it anywhere but here.
