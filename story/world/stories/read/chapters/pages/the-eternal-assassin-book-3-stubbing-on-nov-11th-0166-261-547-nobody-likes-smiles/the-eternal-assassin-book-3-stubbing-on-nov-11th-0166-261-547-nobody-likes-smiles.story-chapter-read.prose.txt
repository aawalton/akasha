The team made camp just outside the Deadlands once Asher returned and they decided to finally call it a night. It was strange actually camping for a change as opposed to simply Recalling back to the palace and sleeping there, but Asher didn’t want to risk putting too much distance between himself and Watcher. Despite the fact that Watcher hadn’t followed him back to the outer rim of the Deadlands or beyond, he could only hope that by remaining close, she wouldn’t feel the need to alert the King of Death as to his disappearing presence. Smiles seemed confident she’d go to the other Servants first, but he didn’t know how much faith he wanted to place in the severed head of one of their enemies.

“So she just stands there… watching?” Rulfar asked from the other side of the fire, still sounding confused despite Asher’s attempts at explaining.

“Yeah… she doesn’t move, doesn’t blink, doesn’t do anything but watch. And like I said, she’s Spatially tethered to you somehow as well. No matter how hard I ran at her, I never got any closer.”

“I still can’t believe you spent an hour trying to hit her with a bow and arrow from a quarter mile away,” Moxy snorted, stoking the flames aimlessly with a few light air blasts. “That’s a tough shot for someone who’s actually good with the bow, for the record.”

“It’s impressive you tried for so long though,” Samantha smiled, laying a hand on his thigh. “Want to bring me with you tomorrow? I should be able to hit her fairly easily.”

“The Death Aura is way too thick that far in,” Asher said, shaking his head. “I’d be worried about you just dying instantly. I could feel it overpowering even my skills. I’m going to have to take the actual center of the Deadlands in thirty-minute chunks. Maybe closer to fifteen, even.”

“An arrow probably wouldn’t work against that creature anyway,” Rulfar said, staring deep into the fire as the flames danced across his eyes. “It’s already exhibited Spatial abilities. I find it hard to believe it wouldn’t have a counter in place to prevent ranged attacks from hitting it. Similar to your Distortion.”

“Seriously?! Is that why I couldn’t even come close to hitting it?”

“Nah, that was because you suck at archery,” Moxy laughed. “But Rulfar’s probably right, that’s far too glaring of a weakness. I bet if one of your arrows had come close it would have veered off at the last second.”

“Well if shooting it doesn’t work, and you can’t catch it, how do you deal with something like that?” Samantha asked.

“Maybe you don’t,” Rulfar shrugged. “It sounds as though this lich has figured out how to create powerful undead minions. Perhaps this one was designed to be untouchable and unkillable.”

“My whole thing is taking down stuff that is supposedly unkillable,” Asher complained, poking at the fire with a stick. “I just need to get close.”

“What about our potion supply?” Samantha asked. “You and I each have a potion of Flare, right? If the thing is covered in eyes, maybe blinding her would work?”

“That’s not a bad idea… If her Spatial skill is connected to her vision, which might not be far off seeing as she’s always staring at me, that might actually let me get close enough to stab her. Of course, first I need to actually land a potion of Flare right in front of her, which is one hell of a throw.”

“You only need to get somewhat close. Those things are ridiculously bright,” Moxy said. “I had an opponent in the ring try to surprise me with one of those once. Cheap trick, not to mention illegal. Had to finish beating his head in completely blind after it went off. Idiot ended up blinding himself too.”

“Alright, I feel a bit better having something new to try next time she appears,” Asher said, wondering just how fast he could cover a quarter mile. He’d need to be able to close the distance before she recovered. And seeing as she had so many eyes, there was a good chance at least a few of them would recover rather quickly.

“So what happened to Smiles?” Moxy asked. “Little guy was starting to grow on me!”

“Left him in the Deadlands. He’d die if I took him out for too long. Though I don’t know how he’s growing on you. He’s a literal, emotionless monster.”

“I don’t know, he’s just sort of fun, you know,” Moxy shrugged. “Like a team mascot!”

“We are not bringing an undead, severed head with us as a mascot,” Rulfar said bluntly, cutting off Moxy before she could continue. “It will die with the Deadlands either way.”

“You guys are no fun,” Moxy sighed, getting up and brushing off her pants. “Welp, I’m gonna call it a night. I want to be fresh and ready for more zombie killing tomorrow.”

“I’ll take first watch,” Rulfar said, his eyes gleaming in the light of the fire as he watched the flames. If it were anyone else, Asher might be a tad concerned about their night vision, but seeing Rulfar’s chest rise and fall as the man took in deep breaths through his nose alleviated any concerns he might have. Rulfar could smell a zombie from literally a mile away, which meant they were as safe as they’d ever be.

“You don’t have to stand watch if you don’t want to,” Samantha said, pulling her amulet out of her neckline and tapping it. She wasn’t able to wear it while fighting, as her bow put her at her current limit of three artifacts, but she liked putting it on whenever it was time to sleep. “Amulet of Perception, remember? I can set up a Domain and feel when anything enters it.”

“I find it better not to rely on artifacts when in dangerous territory,” Rulfar said simply. “You two can turn in. I’ll wake Moxy first for the next watch.”

After cleaning up a bit with a little help from Samantha’s Fountain skill, the two of them hit their own tent for the night, set up a bit farther from the others to ensure they had a bit of privacy. Not that Moxy or Rulfar seemed to care about what the two of them got up to in their free time, but he and Samantha had agreed it was probably the polite thing to do.

Despite camping so close to the Deadlands, night proved to be uneventful, and the four of them suited up and prepared for another jam-packed day of killing zombies.

“Okay, I’m around twenty-five miles into the Deadlands. That means I’ve only got about ten-ish miles to go until I hit the center,” Asher explained as they finished eating and prepared themselves. “Remember, I’ll probably be Recalling back to you guys roughly every thirty minutes, as I don’t want to let my body get that worn out by the Death Aura if I can help it. Once I actually get the King of Death’s workshop in my sight, I’ll come back and let you guys know.”

With everyone in agreement, Rulfar, Samantha, and Moxy headed off to continue killing zombies and hopefully acting as a distraction. Now that Asher was avoiding killing zombies himself, they didn’t technically have to run around killing them, but they’d all agreed any attention they could draw away from him would probably be beneficial in the long run. At the very least, they might draw the attention of a few of the Servants, which would be a load off Asher’s mind. From what he gathered the Servants would be naturally weakened on the fringes of the Deadlands compared to the middle, and the team would probably be able to take them down easily enough even without his presence.

Focusing on his Eternal Mark's second-tier effect, Asher’s mind shot across the Deadlands until he found himself staring down at Smiles’ head lying right where he left it. The undead monstrosity was humming to himself, and it took Asher a moment to realize it was one of the songs from the Lord of the Rings he was copying.

Actually… Was that the first memory I gave him that contained music in it?

Shaking his head, Asher prepared himself for anything as he pulled out Crescent and his living wood dagger. His scrying could only see a couple of feet around Smiles in every direction, so for all he knew, there could be a thousand zombies all standing a dozen feet away, just waiting for him to pop in so they could eat him. Triggering Recall, the world warped around him, and he suddenly found himself standing directly over Smiles’ head.

“Oh, you’re back! It’s an ambush!”

Thankfully, Asher had already slipped into the astral the moment he appeared, just to be on the safe side. And a good thing too. At the same time Smiles warned him, a massive white spear of bone erupted out of the ground, stabbing straight through where he’d been a split second ago. The moment it missed, the rest of the ground erupted, revealing what looked like a giant scorpion made entirely out of shining ivory.

“Where did he go?” the scorpion chittered, the various bones grating and rattling off of one another to somehow mimic human speech that sounded like nails on a chalkboard.

“He just vanishes sometimes,” Smiles said, smiling at all the attention. “He’ll be back sooner or later!”

Not wanting to turn Smiles into a liar, Asher returned to the material realm behind the new monster, slashing out with Crescent and cutting clean through the thick bone tail of the scorpion at one of the many joints. A creature made entirely out of bones didn’t really have any vital points, after all, so he figured starting with dismantling its weapons was probably a good choice.

“Oh yeah, he likes to attack when he comes back!” Smiles added helpfully.

The new monster hissed and spun around, lashing out with a razor-sharp pincer in an attempt at cutting Asher in half. But the pincer caught nothing but air, as Asher had already slipped back into the astral. Appearing a few feet to the side, he slashed down, severing the pincer as well.

“What is this?!” the monster roared, scuttling backwards as it looked around. “He moves like Fade!”

“I thought the same thing!” Smiles said. “I did tell you he’d beat you, for the record. You’re a bad match up for both his fighting style and his weapon.”

While the two of them spoke, Asher ran forward in the astral, slashing with his dagger the same moment he dropped his skill. The bone construct lost another pincer to his blade, but rather than retreat, it leapt forward. The scorpion’s ribcage flexed open, attempting to snatch Asher up and pin him underneath the monster. Instead, Asher hopped back with Distortion, lashing out with a tunnel of compressed space and severing three of the bones the monster used as legs. Before the scorpion could recover, Asher ran to the side and struck again, finishing off the last few legs on the same side. Without any legs to support half its body, the monster crashed to the side, unable to move.

“Smiles! You didn’t warn me what he could do!” the monster chittered.

“You didn’t ask!” Smiles laughed.

“Glad to know you’re annoying to everyone,” Asher said, running around to the other side of the monster and cutting through the rest of its legs for good measure. With the scorpion completely beached and unable to do much more than twitch, Asher elongated Crescent with Distortion’s second-tier effect, using the mithral blade to cleave through the monster’s head and send it toppling to the ground. Not wanting a repeat of Smiles, Asher then used his elongated dagger to carve the head into pieces, continuing to cut them smaller and smaller until he eventually sensed whatever magic was keeping the thing ‘alive’ fade away. He'd briefly debated keeping the thing alive like he had Smiles, before deciding against the idea. Now that Watcher was onto him, there was every chance she would retrieve the severed head should he leave it behind. And if he tossed it into his rift, it would die from the lack of death energy anyway.

“Yep, that’s about how I expected that to go!” Smiles said, laughing as his fellow monster was killed right in front of him. “Poor, Marrow. He really enjoyed crushing people inside his ribcage and taking their bones. You should see the size of his collection, it’s crazy!”

“How did he even know I was coming?” Asher asked, kicking aside a few chunks of bones to ensure they were no longer sentient.

“Ah, that was probably Watcher. She was watching him when he showed up.”

“Where is she now?” Asher asked, looking around but unable to find her.

“I don’t know,” Smiles admitted. “Haven’t seen her since she brought over the other Servant.”

“Wait, what other—”

Before he could finish that question or tell Smiles how much he hated him, twin ghostly hands rose out of the ground under his feet, grabbing him around the ankles and yanking him down into the darkness beneath the earth.
