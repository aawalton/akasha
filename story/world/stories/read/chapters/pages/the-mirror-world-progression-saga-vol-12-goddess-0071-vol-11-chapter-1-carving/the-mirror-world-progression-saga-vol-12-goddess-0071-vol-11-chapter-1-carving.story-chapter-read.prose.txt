Below the Capital of the Dominion, which was the City of Heavenly Order, in the dark, twisted halls few not likewise ‘twisted against light’ ever frequented, The Sage of Truth, Inquisitor One, walked through the quiet interstices and felt the eyes of shadows.

He did not fear them, as he once did — as he feared death when bid to this place by the Master. Instead of punishment and judgment within, he’d found wisdom in the fullness of truth. He’d found enlightenment and freedom…

Clarity.

He made his way down and down the spiral staircase. He felt and knew each step was deeper into a different realm, and one that brought greater wisdom of the spirit in the secrets that it held. He’d been warned only to go so far, but the Master trusted him to roam and investigate the higher levels, as precious few in history ever could. There were unthinkable treasures within — many long unused, forgotten experiments, devices, spellcrafts, and creatures were left in these extensive halls. All of them drew power from the batteries of the gloriously departed, as harvested by the Master’s supreme arts.

As he walked, and his staff clicked and echoed with each descending stone, Inquisitor One found his dried lips cracking with a smile. He knew these were his final steps as a mere man in mere, weak flesh.

The time has finally come. The Ivory Caul is ready, and so am I.

He arrived at his target level and stepped out into the cloying entropic zone that would tear apart the unworthy and unready, mind, body, and soul. Instead, to Inquisitor One, it was like a glorious caress. He knew this reach to be one of purification and dissolution of the self. He was one with this place, for it had carved a section out of his soul and inhabited it.

And the fools wonder why I am aloof? They were nothing more than distractions tossed in the way. Fed to the foreign beast destined to test us to the brink of precious annihilation. None of those fools can fathom this. No one but the entropically anointed could understand this otherworldly force and power, resting and ever-gathering, forever too pure and delicate for the destructive bombardments of hard matter and light above. Fragile, but oh, what can be cultivated from it…

Through the hall of the giant stonecarvers, whose true name was obliterated, he walked on, seeing only the faintest outlines, but nonetheless walking true by familiarity. It was vast, like a cavern, and covered far more space than it should have, but that was how things went in such a realm.

In the distance, there was a hum, so deep it was like many giant drums in concert, with the undercurrent of something more discordant and disruptive. Beautiful; powerful.

He passed under an arch, and a red glow began to make for finer details, showing a chamber of many converging arches from four paths, acting like pillars to a tall ceiling fit for the giants who would walk below it.

None were walking just then, but kneeling with their hands pressed together, one giant corpse at each end of the arches. They were clad all in servant robes, and their bodies were desiccated and shriveled, like unwrapped mummies. None had eyes, but stitching was in the sunken sockets, making out symbols long dead of any meaning. No doubt they had meant much when they were laid to rest long ago. He’d read none had ever made an undead stonecarver lift so much as a hand to any menial, physical thing, including fighting. All they did, even in death, was carve and enchant.

They hummed in a symphony fit to rattle the bones, and wove slow, continual magic down the arches, feeding it into the center pit, from which the glow came, with rhythmic flickers as if from a flame.

Not quite the carving of stone for this rotation of the celestials.

Inquisitor One walked down the path between giants and underneath the great arches along one of the four paths. Mere seconds before he got to the edge of the pit, the humming suddenly stopped, the spellcraft completed. He reached the edge, and the red glow bathed and burned him, as he saw the finished glory below.

Behind an egg-like, partially translucent enclosure, a twisted network of bony growths writhed around an inner core, which pulsed like a heart and generated the ambient red glow of magic, continually scouring him with its direct energy. It suffused him and called him closer at the same time.

Incredible!

Inquisitor One hurried down the steps even as each pulse shot through him and weakened his body, beginning to rend and tear his flesh. Barely, he made it to the surface of the egg.

He didn’t need to touch it — it contorted itself and swallowed him inside in a blink, as the bony constructs within quickly pierced his flesh at a hundred points and carried him deeper. Incredible pain bloomed for one intense moment, as his flesh was stripped away under the unbearable intensity. The mass of bones contorted themselves into something more than mere twisted chaos...

Soon, nothing was left of him but bone and his staff, but he did not perish — he was preserved and transformed into something greater as all fibers became one.

Deep within the wilderness of the Everwood, amidst the smoldering ruins of a fortress, Crow discontentedly sifted through ashes — something he seemed to be doing far too much of, lately. In every direction, hordes of Servitors and warriors were either doing the same or hovering over as sentinels, ready to act on a dime if some trap was sprung. But Crow knew that was pointless, now. The whole fortress had already detonated, and at best, a few dozen witchhunters had died trying to bait them inside.

As soon as it was clear their Samanthian enemies were going to be cautious and start layers of powerful dispels from Antimage specialists, the witchhunters had triggered the whole place to blow with them still in it, presumably to protect their secrets housed within. Interestingly, no fires naturally spread to the forest. It went up, and it rather mystically stayed contained, even before Crow’s side had begun slinging out countermeasures to preserve what they could.

Cunning bastards, to figure out we’d discovered them in time enough to evacuate. Or maybe this is just how things are always prepared here, and the skeleton crew is normal? Impressive, either way.

“Might’ve found something, milord,” came the voice of Herne the Fairie Dragon. “Not a trap, either.”

Crow walked over the blackened wood to what at first appeared to be a giant lump of charcoal, much the same as everything else. But through a crack in the middle, clean stone could be seen.

Drawing his blade, Crow cut apart the black charcoal until it collapsed into two halves, and the stone within appeared.

It was a simple, miniature plaque of faint enchantment, with the light gray of the stone imprinted with black text that read, ‘For Hunters, the Hunt is Always On.’

Crow stared at it balefully. Damn these pricks. The whole lot of them is going to be annoying to the last, aren’t they? And one in particular…

After the complete destruction of Matrazere, word got out very quickly. All the will of the Dominion to hold border territories completely fell apart, and it was no more flagrantly evident in the actual Borderlands, which they all but completely fled out of. In large numbers, their soldiers deserted completely in the process, and locals frequently defected, sometimes even revolted. Against the combined might of the risen-up Borderlands and the Pantheon forces — including the catfolk foreigners — nothing could stand.

The one exception in the region had been taking the river north of Mot Mekess, the Asterway, and seizing Stokefield. Here, a disciplined order had to be confronted, and along the way, effective guerrilla tactics and terrorism were waged by what they increasingly knew to be the witchhunters. It hadn’t really stopped, truthfully — the Dominion army might’ve lost its will, and perhaps even the Inquisitors to a large degree, but the witchhunters just didn’t stop.

Through laborious efforts and investigations along the way, harvesting the secrets of the dead in addition to gains through what amounted to counter-terrorism, Crow finally uncovered the identity of the one he sought for so desperately: the killer truly responsible for Agatha’s death.

And it was the same bloody general who’d organized things through Stokefield.

Before the siege began in earnest, he’d staged an assassination attempt with a crack team, only to find a decoy and get denied. The bastard had slipped away yet again, dealing what damage he could from one base and then falling back to another. A losing game, but Crow cared more about his quarry losing his life than this or that battle, or even war.

For the lack of any other indications of where the bastard got off to, they’d hunted the hunters into the forest. Rather than attempting the absurdity of conquering the whole Everwood, a mostly untamed wilderness, they’d hunted down the fortress they knew was hidden away somewhere — mostly thanks to Crow’s sometimes uncanny information sources. Fortunately, some witchhunters weren’t as careful about their identities as others, and the war made for riskier enterprises…

He’d been rich with grudge-holding dead, essentially.

But the culprit had eluded him again, even to what he’d hoped was his enemy’s last haven. Instead, he’d simply found another decoy. Delivered something of a wound to a nerve center, perhaps, but hardly enough to kill a beast well-acquainted with injury and pain. One that expected and prepared for it.

They have no haven, do they? They simply hunt where the hunt takes them.

Crow sheathed his sword and turned away from the stone. He looked up to the sky through the trees and knew he had unconsciously turned precisely in the direction of the Capital. Somehow, he’d always known its direction, like a bird flown from a roost.

So it is. Wherever we go, we have to return to our origin eventually — even if we hate it.

“Home, sweet home,” Crow muttered.

“Pardon, milord?” Herne asked.

“Nothing. Let’s move out. Leave a crew enough to dig for anything worth a damn in this crap pile. It doesn’t hold anything I want terribly, unless maybe someone finds a skull.”

“Understood, Lord Crow.”

Ignoring everything around him, Crow closed his eyes, and he could feel the truth of Fate ahead of him, such was his attunement with the goddess.

Arthen Wybayer. You will not hide behind decoys again. I’ll see you on the final battlefield, one way or another. Pray you see me coming, or pray you don’t… either way, it’s your death’s arrival.
