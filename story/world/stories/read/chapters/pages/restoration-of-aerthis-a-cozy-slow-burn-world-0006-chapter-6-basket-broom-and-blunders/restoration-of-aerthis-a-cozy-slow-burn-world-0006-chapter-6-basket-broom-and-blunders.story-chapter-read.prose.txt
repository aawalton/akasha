The faint light of dawn seeped through the small window, painting gentle patterns across the wooden walls.

Elynara woke slowly, the comforting scent of old wood and faint herbs grounding her even before her eyes fully opened.

Her room was simple but felt safe. The thick quilt she had been given the night before was warm, and the low creak of floorboards beneath her feet when she stood was oddly soothing.

It was a small comfort, but after the terror of the wolf attack yesterday, she took it as a precious thing.

Inhaling deeply, she soon found herself drifting into the morning routine as if she had always been here. She helped Sister Elara with the morning fire, fetched water, and even managed to braid a few little girls’ hair before they ran off to their chores with shy giggles and bright smiles.

The church felt alive in the gentle light, soft voices and careful movements weaving together into something that felt almost like home.

Yet even in the warmth of it, Elynara caught herself pausing. Her eyes would find the wooden beams overhead and linger, her fingers stilling on a broom handle or a cup’s rim.

Father Alaric noticed, of course. He always seemed to notice.

When he approached, it was with a small, knowing smile.

He gestured toward the long table where a pot of tea steamed gently, the soft herbal scent inviting.

“Come, Elynara, join me for a moment. You look as though your thoughts are a world away.”

She hesitated but then nodded, settling into the seat across from him. The warmth of the cup in her hands was a small balm, the tea’s gentle steam brushing against her face.

For a while, they sat in silence. She watched the children’s laughter echo from the back of the hall, the way the morning light glowed across Father Alaric’s worn but kind features.

Finally, she found her voice, but her gaze still lingered on the cup.

“It’s… yesterday. That wolf-thing… It caught me off guard. I was so sure I would be fine, but – ”

She took a small breath.

“I wasn’t. I was reckless. I didn’t think things through.”

Father Alaric’s eyes softened, and he reached out, resting a warm hand over hers.

“You survived. That is to be celebrated, not agonized over.”

She nodded, but still refused to raise her head.

“And you learned something too, I think.”

Her fingers tightened around the cup, though she couldn’t help but smile when Starpuff decided it was a perfect time to dunk into her tea – only to immediately jump out of it with a cry she took for a ‘youch!’.

It was just the distraction she needed to finally look away from her cup and look at the person before her properly.

“Mana feels so… alive. I want to learn everything about it. But I know so little about this place, about what waits out there…”

A small huff of self-directed laughter escaped her.

“I didn’t even think about what kind of monsters might be out there. I was too caught up in… everything else.”

Alaric’s gaze held her steadily.

“Curiosity is a gift. But so is caution. This world is wide and old, and though mana is a wonder, it is not the only thing worth understanding.”

He paused, his thumb brushing the edge of her cup.

“You are new here, Elynara. Let yourself be new. Let yourself learn slowly. And if you wish to know more about the world, start with what’s closest.”

She blinked, tilting her head slightly.

“The library. It’s not large, but it holds much of what we have managed to gather about the land, the people, and the ways of things. As much as a small town reasonably could.”

He offered a small smile.

“Go there. Read. Take your time. Knowledge and wonder go hand in hand. But only if you take them in together.”

Elynara found herself exhaling slowly, the weight in her shoulders easing just a little. She nodded, a small, grateful smile of her own returning.

“I will. Thank you, Father.”

He squeezed her hand once before withdrawing it, the moment gentle but sure.

“Good. Now go, before Sister Elara decides to put you to work again.”

Elynara laughed at that, the sound light and warm. As she rose, she felt a flicker of purpose in her chest.

Father Alaric was right, she needed to learn more about this world. It was practically a must if she wanted to learn all there was to learn about mana. After all, you can’t learn if you are dead!

That decided, her first stop of the day became an obvious choice. She was going to the library.

Getting there proved to be quite easy. It was really just there!

She felt so silly when, after asking Father Alaric about where to find it, all he had to do was smile and point a finger at the inconspicuous door, set beneath a low wooden beam in a far corner of the church hall.

Elynara paused a moment outside. With her hand resting on the worn handle, the faint scent of old paper and polished wood drifted out to greet her.

She pushed the door open slowly, and a hush washed over her the instant she stepped inside.

It was a small room, really. The walls were lined with shelves built from the same dark wood as the church’s pews, each shelf bowing ever so slightly under the weight of books and scrolls. A single narrow window let in a sliver of light, illuminating motes of dust that danced like tiny stars in the sunbeam.

She moved carefully, eyes sparkling in admiration of how well-cared for they were despite their age, as her fingers grazed the spines of the books. Some were wrapped in cracked leather, others in worn cloth. A few had spines of raw hide or bindings that looked hand-stitched from scraps.

Pausing over a thick tome with an unfamiliar script along the edge, she felt a faint tingle of mana clinging to its cover. Like the weight of centuries pressed lightly against her skin.

The room was quiet but not cold; there was a warmth in the stillness, a gentle invitation in every shelf.

A soft voice broke the hush.

“Good morning,”

The nun at the small desk greeted, her tone polite and calm. She looked up from a stack of papers with mild surprise, her eyes glinting with curiosity behind the slight weariness of someone used to solitude.

Elynara returned the greeting with a soft smile.

“May I… look around?”

The nun nodded, her own small smile a gentle permission.

“Of course. Few come here often, but the shelves are always open to those who wish to learn.”

Elynara’s gaze flicked around, the weight of that quiet permission settling like a balm. She took a few slow steps deeper in, Starpuff drifting along at her shoulder, its green light muted but curious.

“You said few come here often, but why is that?”

“Most people are too busy with their daily labors. And those who have read everything of interest to them… well, they rarely come back unless something new is found.”

Curiosity sated, she thanked the nun before turning back to her search. She let her fingertips brush over the spines; each one a promise of knowledge, even if she didn’t yet know where to begin.

She didn’t open a book fully right away. She read a line here, a title there, her eyes catching on words like herbal lore or ancient wars or reflections on mana’s flow. Each hint of a story or a lesson made her breath catch just a little.

Starpuff seemed equally enthralled, darting from shelf to shelf like a bee flitting between flowers, its light brushing the dusty tomes with a quiet joy.

Elynara found herself smiling.

She didn’t stay long. Elynara knew she would only get lost in the words if she just let herself. But stepping back from the shelves, she felt the tightness in her chest ease. The world felt less unknowable somehow, the weight of her ignorance a little less daunting.

She gave the nun a small bow of thanks before slipping back out of the quiet haven.

Outside the library, the sun had shifted a little higher already. The day still stretched ahead of her, and she felt lighter for it.

For now, she had a place to return to. A place to learn.

And that felt like a very good start.

The guild hall bustled with life as Elynara stepped inside, the air filled with laughter and the clatter of mugs against wood. The warm scents of roasted meat and spiced tea drifted through the room, mingling with the faint tang of oiled leather and iron.

She hesitated just inside the doorway, watching as a party of adventurers - three men and a woman in well-worn cloaks – crowded around the counter. Their conversation was quick and low, a few clipped words exchanged with Alicia as she efficiently jotted down notes and sorted a sheaf of worn parchment.

Elynara waited, content to watch them for a moment.

The group’s laughter rang out, quick and bright. One of them slapped his friend on the shoulder, earning an exaggerated groan that only drew more laughter. They spoke in half-joking jabs and easy confidence, familiarity born of shared dangers and late-night fires.

It was a different sort of energy than the hush of the library. Here, everything felt immediate, grounded by the weight of coin and risk. She took it in, the contrast vivid in her mind.

The adventurers soon finished, giving Alicia a brief wave before slipping out the door in a swirl of travel-stained cloaks. Their absence left a brief hush in their wake.

Alicia glanced up then, her eyes finding Elynara almost immediately. A small smile tugged at her lips – part greeting, part relief at the sight of a familiar face.

“Back so soon?”

Elynara stepped forward, trying not to fidget as she spoke.

“I… I wanted to ask about a few things. The wolf-thing yesterday… It made me realize I need to be better prepared.”

Alicia’s smile softened a fraction, the faint lines at the corners of her eyes deepening.

“Good. Realizing you need to be ready is half the battle.”

She gestured for Elynara to come closer.

“What can I help you with?”

She took a breath, gathering her thoughts.

“That wolf-like thing that attacked me. Do you know what it was?”

Alicia’s face grew more serious, though not surprised.

“Likely a bonefang. They look like starved wolves, with mottled fur and a strange sheen in their eyes. Not much left in their heads once the hunger sets in. Mana starvation does that to quite a few monsters.”

“It didn’t feel like a normal animal.”

“It’s not. Bonefangs used to be rare this close to town, but things have been shifting. You did well getting away.”

“I don’t want to rely on luck next time. I want to learn more. About magic, about survival, just… how to start doing this right.”

Alicia studied her for a moment, then gave a thoughtful nod.

“You’ve got more sense than most. That’s already a step ahead. If you want to get your footing, start with the basics: gear, knowledge, routine. The library is a good first choice.”

Elynara perked up slightly at that.

“I went earlier, actually.”

“That’s good, keep it up. For the other things, I believe you might need a foci for your magic. Either a wand or a staff should do the trick. Though there is only one craftsman in town that could make those for you. You will find his workshop at the edge of the merchant quarter.”

Elynara nodded, feeling a flicker of determination stir in her chest.

“You also seem to be good on basic gear. That only leaves routine. As you now know, the first real fight is never easy, yes?”

“...Yes.”

“But if you intend to survive more, and even thrive in the wild, Marcus is whom you should ask.”

The name pulled Elynara’s attention to the side, where the burly man from the day before lounged in a corner chair, nursing a mug of frothy beer. He lifted his head at the mention of his name, his beard twitching with a half-smirk.

“Alicia here thinks I’m the answer to everything.”

As he spoke in a drawl, the mountain of muscle pushed himself upright with a casual roll of his shoulders. He took a few slow, confident steps across the room, greeting her with a half-grin as he approached.

“Forest sprout. You look better than yesterday, at least.”

She managed a sheepish smile.

“Still feeling pretty green.”

Marcus snorted.

“You’re alive. That’s a start. I’ll show you a few tricks and adventurer basics. So next time, you’re not just hoping the beast decides you’re not worth the effort.”

Elynara dipped her head in gratitude, feeling a flush of warmth in her cheeks.

“I would… like that. Thank you.”

Marcus shrugged one shoulder, already turning away to find his seat again.

“Don’t thank me yet. Save that for when you’re still breathing after your next fight.”

Alicia gave her a brief pat on the arm before turning back to her ledgers.

“Go on, then. And come back if you need more advice. The guild’s always open.”

Elynara let out a slow breath as she stepped back, her heart beating a little faster. She felt a little less lost, and a little more certain of what to do next.

After Elynara left, Alicia stayed behind the counter for a while longer, her gaze fixated on the front door of the guild as though it might open again.

It didn’t.

Just the usual sounds of the hall creeping back in. The clinking mugs, laughter too loud, boots scuffing across worn wood.

She exhaled through her nose and turned away, smoothing the hem of her sleeve with practiced indifference. Her fingers tapped the polished counter in a slow, even rhythm – more habit than thought – until she stopped, reached under the desk, and pulled out a thin folder from one of the side drawers.

A crude charcoal sketch on the top page depicted a gaunt beast with matted fur and overly bright eyes. The notes scribbled down the side were her own.

Bonefang sighted northwest. Local wildlife thinning. Avoid travel beyond the outer ridge.

She narrowed her eyes at the scrawl. That had been weeks ago. Too many requests went unanswered out there. Too many people stopped volunteering for the dangerous ones.

Behind her, the chair scraped as Marcus shifted with a low grunt, before commenting without even looking over.

“She’ll be fine. Little green sprout’s got some roots.”

Alicia snorted softly, not dignifying that with a reply.

“She’s just too green and unprepared. Bright-eyed and humming with mana, sure. But with no weapon, no sense of terrain, no habit of caution. And no backup.”

Her piece said, she tucked the folder back into place and reached for another.

Marcus turned his head just enough to raise a brow in her direction.

“You sound like you like her.”

Alicia didn’t respond right away. She flipped open the next folder and skimmed a line she had already read a dozen times before.

“She’s got potential. When I first saw her, I thought she was some junior mage from one of the border towers. She’s so full of mana it made my teeth itch. Anyone with even a hint of sense could feel it. Except…”

She trailed off, snapping the folder shut.

“I assumed she had some foundation, but… clearly, I judged too soon.”

Marcus sipped his drink, unbothered.

“Everyone starts somewhere.”

“Yes. And most of them start too late, or too alone.”

Her fingers brushed over a small stack of parchments. Quest requests that had sat ignored for weeks. A few even running back months and years. All with a common theme of being heavily mana-reliant.

There were just too few mages around now, the guild seeing fewer and fewer new faces everywhere. And the last one that passed by Oltenar… didn’t stay for long.

She gathered them up, tapping the edges into a neat stack.

“If she makes it through the first month, she might surprise us.”

Marcus tilted his mug toward her in lazy salute.

“You always were a soft touch.”

She shot him a flat look.

“Says the one who offered to train her.”

“Don’t need to like a sapling to help it grow.”

Alicia snorted again and tucked the stack away into her side drawer, out of sight.

“Let’s hope the weather stays kind.”

Then she turned back to the board, pen already in hand, back to work. Just another day at the guild.

The streets of Oltenar had a different rhythm in the late morning, more unhurried than what she was used to.

Sunlight filtered down between slanted rooftops, casting gentle warm lines across the stone and packed earth. The scent of fresh bread and roasting meat drifted through the air, mingling with the occasional waft of herbs and leather oil.

Elynara walked slowly through the market lane, her basket swinging lightly at her side. Starpuff floated just ahead of her, weaving lazily between awnings and crates like it was following some invisible trail of interesting smells.

Occasionally it darted off to peer into an open stall, or hovered above the head of a vendor before looping back to her, trailing little sparks of green light that quickly faded in the sun.

She still felt the weight of her talk with Alicia. Knowing what she didn’t know was its own kind of comfort, if a slightly irritating one.

One step at a time.

Her first stop was a small stall wedged between two larger ones, half-hidden by a drying rack of herbs. The merchant, a stooped old woman with a nose like a hooked beak and a voice like sandpaper, sold all sorts of odds and ends. Anything from waxed string, bottles with faded labels, thin bundles of charcoal, down to simple bound books.

“Looking for anything in particular, girl?”

The woman asked without glancing up from her seat, where she was slowly threading beads onto twine.

“A notebook. Something reliable and sturdy.”

The woman grunted and pointed with her chin.

“Second shelf. Left of the cork stoppers.”

Elynara crouched to browse, brushing her fingers along the spines. Most were rough cloth or leather, a few with mismatched bindings that looked repaired or repurposed. She picked one with thick, unlined pages and a sturdy cover.

After paying with a few of her remaining coins and tucking it carefully into her basket, she resumed her search.

Starpuff circled her head in a slow spiral, then landed lightly on the corner of the basket, peeking down at the notebook. As if it, too, was curious what she might write in it.

If nothing else, its antics brought a smile to her face.

The walk to the craftsman’s workshop took her toward the outskirts of the town’s busier streets, where stone gave way to packed dirt and the sounds of the market thinned into birdsong and breeze.

The shop sat just beside a small cluster of trees, its walls shaped from aged wood and reinforced with stone and copper bracing.

A carved sign hung above the door. Showing some half-faded runes surrounding the silhouette of a curved blade crossed with a staff.

She stepped inside.

The air was warmer here, carrying the scent of sap, smoke, and oiled wood. The interior was a narrow but tidy space, part workshop, part storefront. Finished pieces hung from wall racks: short spears, walking sticks, a few staffs with decorative inlays or light carvings, and one or two wooden bucklers with worn straps.

Some were clearly practical, others more ornate. She caught sight of a tiny carved fox nestled between two candlesticks on a shelf behind the counter.

The craftsman was a middle-aged man with greying wolf ears poking through his tousled hair. He looked up from a half-finished bow, the string still slack and the wood clamped in place.

“Morning. Need a walking stick, or are you here for something more specific?”

Elynara took a step closer, holding her basket a little tighter.

“Um, hello Mr…? I’m… looking for something I can use. I’m a mage. Or something like one. I don’t really have a proper way to defend myself yet.”

The man gave her a once-over, then tilted his head slightly.

“Name’s Tillian. Not many come in here asking for that. Most folk in this town either swing steel or stay behind the walls. You say you’re a mage; how’s your control?”

“I’ve been practicing. But I don’t have a focus or anything to shape mana with. I was hoping to find something that might work… or learn what I should be looking for.”

He nodded slowly at that, setting his tools aside.

“You’re asking the right way, at least. Most assume a stick’s a stick.”

Tillian reached behind the counter, pulled out two staves. One simple and straight, the other darker with a more twisted grain.

“Truth is, not every branch channels mana all that well. You want something that feels right in your grip. Flexible enough to carry the flow, sturdy enough not to splinter under stress. On top of that, some trees carry mana better than others. You’ll know it when you feel it. Kind of like a quiet hum in your hand.”

He handed her the twisted staff. She took it, testing the weight. It was smooth and cold to the touch, but didn’t spark anything particular in her.

The man watched her closely.

“Not that one, huh?”

She shook her head.

“I… think I would know if it was.”

“Then you’re halfway there. You’ve got the sense, just need the source.”

He leaned back against the workbench.

“I’ve got a few blanks lying around, but if you want something that really fits you, you’ll need to find it yourself. Something alive, or recently fallen. You bring me a branch with that right hum, I can shape it into something proper.”

Elynara blinked at that.

“So I… need to go branch hunting?”

Tillian gave a wry smile at that.

“In the simplest terms, yes.”

The idea of being part of the process of making her staff was oddly exciting. Finding one that would fit her well and be uniquely hers.

And it was a promise of a small adventure!

“I’ll do it.”

“Then keep your ears open and your eyes sharper. If you’re not sure what you’ve got, bring it by anyway.”

She nodded, already turning over possible plans in her mind. But she hesitated a moment before leaving.

“Thank you.”

He waved her off with a quiet grunt.

“Don’t thank me yet. Wait until I carve the damn thing.”

She stepped back out into the light, her notebook safely tucked against her side and her thoughts spinning in a dozen directions.

Elynara stepped out from the craftsman’s shop, the comforting scent of wood and varnish still lingering on her sleeves. Her mind buzzed with excitement, her fingers tapping lightly against the spine of her new notebook.

She had a clear goal now. A bit of a challenge, even.

To find a suitable branch. One able to carry her mana smoothly. She almost felt… proud. Like she was part of the process now, not just some clueless outsider bumbling through it all.

But as her boots clicked quietly down the path back toward the town square, that warm sense of accomplishment started to dim at the edges.

She needed to go back into the forest. Alone.

Where toothy, desperate monsters could lunge from the underbrush and try to take a bite out of her again.

“…Right. Brilliant plan, Elynara. Go find the perfect stick to defend yourself with – by going out defenseless.”

She came to a stop at the edge of the square, chewing her lip.

No matter how important the branch was, getting herself mauled trying to fetch it would be... less than ideal. She needed help. Or a better plan. Or both.

It was then that she spotted him.

Leaning casually against a post by the old well, the same guard from her first day scanned the square like he had seen it a thousand times and expected it to never change.

Elynara blinked, surprised – and felt a little relieved. Maybe… maybe luck hadn’t completely abandoned her today.

She crossed over before she could talk herself out of it.

“Um. Hello again!”

The guard turned his head at the sound of her voice, a grin already forming.

“Well, if it isn’t the forest sprout herself.”

She flushed a bit at the nickname but couldn’t quite hide her smile.

“I suppose that’s still better than ‘girl who nearly got eaten.’”

His chuckle was light but not unkind.

“Aye, I heard about that. But you managed to drive the beastie away, so it’s all good! Though I suppose not something you might wanna brag about.”

She flushed at his sudden enthusiastic retelling of her folly. Oh goddess, the embarrassment was unbearable!

“Soo… What brings you back to my corner of town?”

She hesitated. No point in beating around the bush.

“I, uh... need a branch.”

He blinked once.

“A branch.”

“For a staff. I want to have something that works with mana, and the craftsman said I would need to find one that resonates properly. From the forest.”

His eyes narrowed just slightly, the teasing fading a little.

“And I’m guessing you’re not keen on going alone again.”

She hugged her notebook a bit tighter.

“Not especially. Once was more than enough.”

The silence stretched for a moment before he nodded slowly.

“Lucky for you, I’ve got the night shift tomorrow. Morning’s free.”

He straightened off the post, rolling his shoulders.

“I’ll take you out myself. Help you find what you need, if you’re serious.”

Elynara blinked.

“You would do that for me?”

“Sure. But you owe me a proper meal for it. Preferably not something that bites back.”

She laughed – genuinely and brightly this time.

“Deal. Mr…?”

He gave her a lopsided grin.

“You can call me Josh. Meet me by my place. Just past the old well, up the left path. Early. Forest’s easier to navigate before the dew burns off.”

“I’ll be there, Mr. Josh! And thank you!”

As he turned to head off, Elynara watched him go, the tension in her shoulders easing. The excitement returned with the way ahead made clear.

She wouldn’t have to worry about being in danger anymore. And tomorrow, she would take another proper step forward.

By the time Elynara returned to the church, the afternoon was already in full swing, with the sky streaked with deepening blues and the warmth of the sun casting soft shadows. And given the cheerful voices coming from the orphanage area, she clearly wasn’t the only one enjoying the weather.

Moving towards the voices, she was quickly roped into helping wherever she could: carrying a few buckets of water, sharing gentle laughs with the children as they darted between her legs, and easing back into the gentle rhythm of the place.

After playing for a bit, and having nothing left to do here, she slipped away to the small library, her new notebook in hand.

The soft hush of the room greeted her like an old friend. The nun who tended the shelves glanced up and gave a quiet nod, then returned to her reading.

Elynara dipped her head in thanks before settling at a small wooden table.

The first book she pulled open was on the region’s history. It was a modest volume, its pages yellowed with age, but it felt alive under her fingers, like a window into a world she was only just beginning to see.

Nestled between its pages, she found a folded hand-drawn map, marked with careful symbols and faint notations.

Tracing the lines of every winding road sketched in precise ink with her fingertip, she moved her lips in sync with each area’s name, trying to see if she could glean what it was from names alone.

Her pencil tapped softly against the paper as she began to copy it into her own notebook, her letters small and careful.

Beside her, Starpuff drifted lazily from shelf to shelf, occasionally nudging her pencil or peeking at her careful notes. She smiled at it, finding comfort in the quiet presence that had become such a part of her life.

As the lamplight flickered and the shadows of the shelves grew long, she let her thoughts wander: about the forest, the staff she needed, the endless ways mana might weave through her hands.

She didn’t know yet what the next day would bring, or how long it would take to truly belong in this place, but she was no longer afraid to try.

By the time the nun gently reminded her it was late, she had only copied part of the map. But she closed her notebook with a sense of quiet pride. She would finish it tomorrow – or the day after.

There was no rush after all.

Back in her room after the evening meal, the simple warmth of the church walls wrapped around her like a blanket. She changed into her sleeping shift and curled up under the rough-woven covers, Starpuff settling on her pillow with a gentle hum.

For a while, she simply laid there in the dark, feeling the slow, steady pull of sleep.

Her thoughts drifted through everything she had done that day: the craftsman’s words, the guard’s offer of help, and her quiet refuge in the library.

She found herself tracing the faint pulse of mana within her chest, enjoying how its gentle rhythm soothed her tired mind.

Absently, she noted that the town’s mana still felt thin – a mere trickle of 0.1/h coming from the environment, barely enough to feel. Though like everything else here, it was another reminder that while things seemed harsh, they still endured in some small way.

As her eyelids finally grew too heavy to fight, she let a small smile curl at the edges of her lips. She was no great warrior or wise mage, just a girl with a name and a stubborn flicker of wonder in her chest. But with each step she took forward, it would change.

Tomorrow, she would rise again. She would gather, learn, and grow stronger. Slowly but surely.

The last thing she remembered was the image of Starpuff weaving through the air, a playful puff of green light in the darkness.

And then, with a soft exhale, she was off to sleep.
