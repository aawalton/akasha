A week and three Fractures after our surprise encounter with Aarana Chand, I find myself watching as Morgan paces in her gallery. Each step is silent, as she’s floating about two feet over the concrete floor. She’s currently seething over Palisade’s bold gamble on attempting to take the Sun Crystal. Not that I recovered one anyway.

“What the fuck was their goddamn plan? Just hope you managed to find the crystal we need? And then just… take it from you?”

“Their leader did not seem like the brightest sort,” I say wryly. “Given his status as 3-Star, he gets a lot of leeway when it comes to throwing Palisade’s weight around.”

“Oh, yes, the classic distancing,” Morgan spits. “If they succeed, it’s because of Palisade’s support. If they fuck up, it’s all on them. It’s a goddamn genius way to pull in so many Empowered. ‘Be your own boss’ has worked as a slogan for so long for a reason.”

“I doubt that’ll be the last time we’ll have to deal with Palisade sticking their nose where it doesn’t belong,” I mutter.

“They’re getting scary big,” Morgan agrees. “They’re even richer than the entire Conglomerate in Korea. Tell you what, I’m glad you tossed that guy around a little. Sounds like he needed it.”

“Yeah, that wasn’t my best moment,” I say wearily.

She scoffs.

“From what I saw from the videos, you were much gentler with him than he really deserved. If he was doing that near you, I’ve got to wonder what he was doing when no one was looking.”

“In any case, how’s the rest of the project coming along? I know you and your apprentices have been working overtime,” I say, changing the subject.

She huffs. “Pretty well, actually. The water crystals have been working like a charm. It really wasn’t too hard to come up with an Opus that generates water just from the ambient Potentia. The Fractures have been pouring it out. It’s insane just how much they leak. Once we get the crystal production underway—which is also going well—we can really start to crank them out.”

“How about the Hearts of Growth? Those aren’t easily replicable, if at all.”

“We’re pretty much done with those too,” Morgan says beaming. “It was a pretty snazzy piece of glyph work, but we came up with another Opus that accelerates local plant growth. It consumes a very small amount of Abyssal Kelp concentrate. It’s a small enough amount that feeding the hydroponic towers inland will not be a problem, even if we have to rely on aircraft for it.”

I whistle. “That’s… impressive.”

“Groundbreaking, more like,” Morgan says. “Too bad there’s no Nobel Prize for magic. I’d deserve five at this point.”

“Just one more component,” I say. Morgan’s good mood darkens at the mention of the missing sun crystal.

“We’ve got the whole world looking,” I say. “We’ll find it.”

“It better be soon,” she says darkly. “The last harvest is almost over. After that we’ve got a few more months with the coastal aquaculture farms. The world starts starving in, what, January? February?”

“We’ll need to ask Pythia,” I say with a sigh. “I was hoping we could avoid that. It really takes a lot out of her.”

“Defying time is not easy, even for us,” Morgan agrees. “I have no idea how she does it.”

We call the Council, and everyone agrees that another divination is required at this stage. We find ourselves at Pythia’s cliff-side home. This time the weather is much less kind.

The clouds are dark and thick with water. Lightning crackles high above, and a gale whips the sea into a frenzy. Instead of the calm and peaceful waves rolling against the cliff the last time we were here, the sea hammers away at the coast. Morgan looks at the sky distastefully, raising a brush. Likely to cast some kind of spell to either calm it or to give us a peaceful bubble.

Pythia holds up a hand.

“No, don’t do that dearie. You’ll interfere with it. The world and I must be one, hm?”

Morgan lowers her brush, and Pythia’s grandson grimaces. He’s next to her like last time, doing his best to weather the ocean’s fury. I stand next to him and try to shield him with my cloak as best I can, and he nods in gratitude. Pythia gives me a warm smile before leaning back in her rain soaked chair.

She breathes in, and the world roars with her. Rain lashes at my armor, at my cloak, at the house and its windows. The drumbeat of rainfall becomes a stampede as the wind moves from storm to typhoon. Morgan’s hair whips in the gale, joined by a trail of steam pouring from Estella’s body high above. Wind-driven rain slams in her solar form and flash boils away.

Zheng Fei’s half cape flows dramatically in the wind. He looks the part of a hero from a movie. The storm flows around Galahad, having found yet another mountain.

The screaming typhoon sings a heavy metal chorus to Pythia’s more moderate nature, and the torrential downpour is a rapid-fire drumbeat. Heat, magic, and light pours from Pythia as she writhes in her chair. She defies the one rule the rest of us must obey: the flow of time.

The world half dances, half fights her as she charges ahead of the Arrow of Now to many places of To Be. The storm strengthens again, ripping leaves off trees. Even a hundred feet above the violent waves we can feel the sea spray. Pythia’s house groans and the windows rattle. With a crescendo of crashing waves, beating on the cliff like a battering ram, the storm breaks.

Both the storm and Pythia are exhausted by her feat of magic. She starts to mumble, and I listen keenly for every word.

“Go where truckless legs lie,” she whispers, half lucid. Her grandson mops her brow, as she’s already feverish.

“Brightest Knight, to find a piece of the Sun, sundered from the heavens, go to the Ocean of Sand.”

She gasps the last words, each one seemingly painful for her. She shudders again. Despite being an Ascended, and theoretically beyond the ravages of time, she looks the part of an elderly woman at the end of her life. When her grandson carries her back inside her humble home, the rest of us retreat to discuss the prophecy. The breaking storm leaves the land quiet, at least for now.

“The Sahara,” I say immediately. “What else could ‘Ocean of Sand’ refer to?”

Guan Yu grimaces. “Half of the Sahara is within the Empire’s territory, and the other half of it is close enough for them to easily respond.”

“Definitely in Egypt,” Morgan says. “Near Luxor, probably.”

“How do you know that?” Galahad asks, arms crossed.

“Pythia probably referenced Ozymandias. It’s an old poem, a pretty famous one,” Morgan explains.

She recites the few related lines.

“I met a traveler from an antique land, Who said—’Two vast and trunkless legs of stone Stand in the desert…

And on the pedestal, these words appear: My name is Ozymandias, King of Kings; Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!’

“The name Ozymandias refers to Rameses II. He built a massive temple complex in Luxor, which was the original capital of the kingdom at the time.”

When I give her an odd look, she shrugs. “I took a couple history of art classes. It included all kinds of things.”

Guan Yu grimaces again. “That’s not good. The Empire took Luxor last month. Going there will attract a response from Berhane.” He spears me with an intense look.

“We will have to find another source,” he says. His tone brooks no argument, but I give him one anyway.

“No,” I say. “This is it. We are out of time to find it. We need time to research and manufacture more crystals. If we want to be able to build the arcologies in time to save the world from starvation, this is it.”

“If the Fracture is in Luxor itself, collateral damage is inevitable. More than a million people live there.”

“I’ll be quick,” I promise. “In and out. Just long enough to pick up the crystal, then I’ll get out.”

“You can’t promise that.” The General says coldly. “We don’t know how strong the Fracture will be. We don’t know what will be in it. We don’t know anything.”

“We know what will happen if we don’t do this!”

The rest of the Council looks unsure as Guan Yu and I continue to grow more heated. Galahad steps in between us, but before she can say anything, Aarana Chand fades into existence. She has a smile on her face and her hands are holding tight to her backpack’s straps. She looks around and whistles cheerfully.

“Woah! Looks like I came in at a bad time?”

“Ah, Miss Chand, I presume?” Guan Yu says slowly. She beams.

“Yep! That’s me! I found something!”

“The sun crystal?” I say hopefully?

She shakes her head.

“Nah! I did find a Cat-5 Fracture in Egypt though! Near Luxor, like Grandma Pythia over there said there would be!”

All of us—those who breathe, anyway—take in a deep breath at the thought of a Category 5 Fracture. A Cat-4 was able to replicate Ascended, even if they were poor, half-complete copies. What kinds of nightmares would come out from something even more powerful? Fractures don’t grow linearly, either, though trying to apply math to them is a losing battle, much to Dr. Patricks and DARPA’s increasing frustration.

“How did you hear that? She just said that.” Galahad asks suspiciously.

“I have my ways!” Aarana replies cheerfully. “Anyway. Egypt is really cool! Love it. You’ll find the Fracture in the Temple of Karnak.”

“Shit,” I curse. “Berhane has to know it’s there. They might already be inside!”

I turn to Morgan.

“Have you been there? To the Temple of Karnak?” She nods.

“Send me there. We have to go now.”

Morgan sighs, looking around at the Council. Most of them are looking to Guan Yu, though Singularity and Adrenaline are looking at me along with Galahad.

“Okay,” she says, “Just you. Maybe if you go alone they won’t escalate so badly. When you’re done, call me. I’ll pull you back. Be quick.”

“You’re making a mistake,” Guan Yu growls.

I don’t reply as I charge through the portal and into the Egyptian evening.

Aarana meets me on the other side without using the portal. It must be related to one of her abilities.

“This way,” she whispers. Her cheerful nature is diminished, and she’s far more uneasy.

The Temple itself is barren and quiet. I can hear the bustle of an overcrowded city outside of it. Gunshots, people crying, and more sounds of misery tell the tale of a city under authoritarian occupation. None of them are crying out for help. Why would they? The world said no. I said no.

I force that thought away. Thread terminated.

Morgan portaled me to the Avenue of Sphinxes. Arana sprints off down the path, under the careful eye of dozens of the truly ancient sculptures. I am far from stealthy, even with my cloak. Speed will have to be our way through rather than stealth.

I follow her as we charge through the ancient temple. Stone structures and carvings more than three thousand years old fill the grand temple complex, and I wish I could stop and stare.

Finally we come to the massive Hypostyle Hall, a literal forest of stone columns. All of them are massive, surpassing most trees in height. In the center of the great hall lies the Fracture itself. It’s surrounded by a dozen armed men. All of them are carrying a mismatched set of gear and weapons. Some of them are distinctly western, likely looted from foreign bases that were quickly abandoned early on.

Aarana yelps and dives to the ground when the dozen soldiers turn their weapons on us. She vanishes before she hits the ground.

“Ohshitseeyoulatergoodluck!” She half screams in a rush before disappearing from view.

I don’t stop, bullets bouncing off my armor. The ricochets take chunks out of the temple around me, sending puffs of dust and pulverized stone into the air. They only manage to get a few shots off before I flare my Aura, and send them scattering. They dive to the side and out of my way as I charge into the Fracture.
