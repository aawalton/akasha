The magic circle flashed, creating a stone pillar beneath my feet. This high up, the crystals had little effect, allowing the spell to function as normal.

Fourteen of Rings!

I strengthened the pillar, making it twice as wide. A fraction of a second later, I kicked off it, thrusting my body straight down.

Concentrating on the back, I looked beneath the glowing fur, seeing the segments of the spine. They were faint, but as long as one knew where to look, they were relatively easy to spot. That was the drawback of being created from moonlight.

Each vertebra was twice my size, connecting the giant skull to the rest of the skeleton. Most thought it strange that an aethereal bean would share anatomy with a mortal creature, and they would be right and wrong. The elk had no organs in the strict sense of the word. There weren’t any specific muscles, lungs, or intestines. What it did had—other than its base body—were eyes, bones, and a heart. Each of these was highly valuable, especially since, if extracted correctly, they remained after its death.

There was also one more thing that someone like me could take advantage of.

One of Swords!

Using a power thrust, I cut into the thick hide in the spot between vertebrae. The divine blades had the ability to cut through leather and flesh, but the moonelk was made of neither. Slicing through it was like slicing through rubber. None of my cards had the power to make the experience smoother. Thankfully, the momentum I had gathered along with the power of the trust itself was able to give the divine weapons just enough to sever the aether cluster along the spine.

In the blink of an eye, the back half of the monster vanished. Vertebrae and other bones spilled onto the ground as the energy necessary to maintain the light there suddenly vanished.

It actually worked. I pushed myself off the creature.

A veteran had told me this trick in the final year of my previous run. It sounded a bit far-fetched, but at the same time made a lot of sense. The method I had used before to defeat a monster was slow and torturous: inflict enough damage and let it evaporate out of existence. Once, I had seen a mage pierce its heard with a single strike, causing the entire thing—bones and all to burst in a bright display of light.

The elk plowed through the ground, missing its back half. No doubt this had significantly confused him. Light spilled into the air, pulled greedily by the crystals in the field. With the entity’s container breached, it was as defenseless as I was.

Twisting around, I prepared for a dash. The moment my feed touched the ground, I did just that, rushing forward straight towards the elk.

Just a little longer, I thought, as I put everything I had into reaching him.

For the moment the creature was confused, but that wouldn’t last. In a few seconds at most, it would recover from the shock, transforming its form into something whole, if a lot smaller. At that point, it would be prepared for the attack I had done just now.

Let me at him! Serp insisted, more eager than ever.

“No,” I whispered as I leaped into the elk’s body.

The moonlight felt soft, almost gelatinous. It didn’t restrict me nearly as much as I thought it would.

Taking no chances, I kept on slashing, using my cards to influence my strikes. Since none of them harmed the creature directly, it wasn’t immune, letting me cut through.

Leaping over the ribs, I continued in the direction of the heart. Unable to use my magic sight, I could only guess its exact location.

The moonelk violently shook its head, swinging what was left of its body in an attempt to throw me out. The moonlight around me thickened.

“Serp, you’ve got one chance!” I shouted.

The idea I had was insane, yet I had nothing left to lose. If I didn’t pierce the heart in the next second, I’d be cast out, and the fight would have to start from the beginning only with me at a disadvantage.

I threw the divine swords forward, then summoned the serpent blade.

“Use them!”

I felt Serp bite into my hand. Draining just enough blood, the blade changed into a massive serpent, extending forward. Yet, instead of trying to harm the elk directly, he coiled around the two divine blades.

A massive contradiction formed. The body of the elk did everything possible to stop the progress of the blades. Simultaneously, it remained intangible to the living weapon. The two forces clashed against one another for a moment, but there was no contest. The momentum Serp had obtained, boosted by my blood and flesh, let him push the weapons to victory.

There was a burst of bright blue light. The instant the divine blades pierced the heart, a flash filled the remaining body of the creature. A split second later it burst like a water balloon, releasing all the light into the open.

One moment I was within the moonelk itself, the next the only thing around me was half a polished skeleton. It was just like the time I had gone to a science museum as a child. Never one for rules, I had gone under the surrounding cordon, making my way directly beneath the reconstruction of the Tyrannosaurus rex. In this case, the difference was that there was nothing preventing the bones from falling on me.

Five of Rings!

I covered my head with both arms as I hardened my bones. A giant vertebra fell onto me. Thankfully, the pain was negligible. One good thing about bones of moonlight was that they weren’t particularly heavy.

Bleh! Serp said in my head. Moon sphere, he added in disgust.

Seriously? I glanced forward. That was more than a stroke of luck.

Strictly speaking, the sphere was as useful to me as it was to Serp: the only thing it boosted was the strength of one’s pack to their deity. Since I remained pactless, the sphere would have no effect whatsoever. Just having it was enough to almost make up for wasting the creature’s heart. If I had been able to obtain that, things would have been very different. From what was said, my aether reserves would vastly increase, allowing me to be a lot more lethal when using my Magician card.

Spotting the sphere among the bones and shattered crystal shards on the ground, I dashed in its direction.

Got you! I scooped it from the ground and instantly placed it in my inventory. Now, there was no danger that it would evaporate.

With that, only one thing remained.

The remaining skull was larger than the entire barracks the ex-participant had sent me to.

Making my way forward, I retrieved my divine blades, then unsummoned Serp.

“Breath of Moon,” I said loudly. “I have completed your request.”

Just come quickly, I thought.

Even at the edge of the field, the crystals were doing a good job draining my magical energy. For the moment, they were focused on the moonelk’s remains. Most of the aether dispersed from the initial blast was streamed directly into the shard pillars that were still standing. After that, the bones of hardened light would follow. Sometime between the two, I would also be drained of all my life.

The second passed, but I didn’t flinch. I knew the deities too well to let myself show weakness. Having me endure all this was their way of testing how far I was willing to go. Fortunately, I knew that I wouldn’t have to wait long. No deity would risk harming its prize.

A new ray of light fell from the sky, marking the grand entrance of the deity. A giant woman dressed in flowy garments of moon and starlight looked down at me. Her expression was completely blank, like that of a child staring at something it didn’t understand.

Bending down, she took the entire skull of the moonelk from the ground.

Always taking more than requested, I thought.

Nothing in the request mentioned the skull, and yet I was pretty sure I wouldn’t get an additional reward for it.

I watched as the massive antlers dissolved into a swarm of glowing dots, flying towards her like a giant swarm of cyan fireflies.

Patiently, I waited. There was no word of thanks, no gesture that I had surpassed her expectations, not even a faint smile. What annoyed me most of all was getting this shit from a minor deity. I had no idea what the goddess’ game was. She clearly wasn’t offering me a pact, though acted in such a way as if I she had rejected a request made by me.

Finally, she reached down again. A single drop of moonlight emerged from the tip of her finger, falling down until it splashed onto me.

Instead of dispersing, however, the light trickled up into my eyes.

I clenched my fists, enduring the pain. Nothing the deities gave came without a measure of discomfort. In this case, I felt as if I had syringes filling my eyeballs with more liquid that they could hold.

It seemed that minutes had passed. Each time I told myself that it was nearly over, more light was pushed into my eyes, blinding me completely. I gritted my teeth, refusing to give the deity the satisfaction of a yell. Then, everything suddenly stopped. When I blinked, everything was the same as before, only the deity was gone.

I took a deep breath to relieve part of the pain. The remaining elk bones were also gone, either taken by the deity or consumed by the crystals. That was my cue to get out of the field.

Sparing no effort, I dashed in the direction of the forest. Crystal shards crunched beneath my feet. Even separate from their core, they still attempted to pull as much energy from me as they could, creating a slightly prickling sensation in my feel and legs. When the sensation stopped, I knew that I had gone far enough.

Using the Two of Chalice to cast a few heals, I turned around.

A patch of destruction was visible on the edge of the crystal field. That was temporary, though. By morning, a new crop of crystals would have sprouted, starting their rise to the sky.

This was a bust, Serp said in my mind. All that work, and neither of us got anything.

“You’re wrong.” I rubbed my eyes.

I had gotten my reward, not to mention a bonus sphere.

Observer, I thought.

Messages appeared before my eyes. Each contained a snippet of information. Normally, I would have to return to the deity’s altar to be able to do that, but the Observer had its benefits. After all, it wasn’t a Grand Arcana card for nothing.

“What creatures are there in the area?” I asked.

Status boxes stacked one on top of the other. Each contained a different creature type. In addition to the expected stats, I also got to see a map with the general location of the creature. Despite everything, it couldn’t be denied that I had made a good choice. At night, the moon sees all, so I could rest assured that the monsters were where indicated.

The vast majority of creatures were familiar. All of them passed as strong, although I strongly doubted that most of them would drop a booster sphere. Rather, they would serve as offerings to the deities in exchange for booster spheres.

“Stop!” I ordered.

Something had grabbed my attention.

“Tell me more about the lynx,” I said.

An albino creature here? That was also rather lucky. Capturing the creature early on was certain to make things easier later on. Normally, I’d prefer to boost up a bit before charging after it, but time was scarce. I wasn’t the only participant sent here, and the others had access to their own deities.

According to the information blessing, the creature was in the forest on the opposite side of the city. If the guards were to be believed, that was the area from which the worm infection had spread. Going there was undoubtedly reckless, yet not doing so would be wasteful.

“Sorry to disappoint you, Serp, but there’s one more creature we must get,” I said. “This time, I need you—”

Stopping mid-sentence, I summoned my serpent blade and slashed the air. The tip stopped at head height, pointing into the distance. Fractions of a second later, a figure appeared.

“Hi, Greg,” Sparta said with a smile. The presence of a living weapon pointing at her head didn’t seem to faze her one bit. If anything, she was smiling. “I see you missed me.”
