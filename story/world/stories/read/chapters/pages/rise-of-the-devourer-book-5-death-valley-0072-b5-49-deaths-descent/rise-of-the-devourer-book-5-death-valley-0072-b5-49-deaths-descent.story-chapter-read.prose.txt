Unfortunately, they still had to deal with a very angry mother basilisk on their way out. Noah had killed enough of its children to incite its vengeful wrath, and its ability to paralyze him was significantly stronger than the average monster's.

He ended up sitting out the entire fight as a statue while Tony acted on his behalf and Zax fought the thing head to head. It was a truly spectacular battle, which Noah appreciated being able to observe, but would have appreciated participating in even more.

He had no particular interest in being a bystander in his own life or death, and the reminder that he'd sort of accidentally brute-forced his way into an Ascendant-level region left him irritated.

Freeing him from the paralysis required cooperation between Zax and Tony and nearly an hour longer than the battle itself, not including the two other groups of monsters eager to snack on the basilisk's corpse who needed to be fought off while he was indisposed.

"I think I hate basilisks," he said as soon as he could speak again, shaking off the stiffness from his limbs. "Who thought that was a good idea?" The only good part about the encounter was the hour of straining to reclaim movement gave him 'transformation resistance' which seemed to be an entirely new category he hadn't encountered until now.

"They were originally an artificially cross-bred combination of Nightmare Wyrm and Stone Drake, but evolved past their shared ancestry and became a race in their own right six hundred or so years later," Zax recited. "They are feared by most societies, but revered and raised domestically by the Stonebound clan of Deep Celestians. I have had a few friends with basilisks for companions, though it is still quite uncommon."

"Every time I start to think I'm getting a handle on what this place has to offer, something new shows up."

"Erandir is a world with countless peoples and societies. Even I do not know the half of it, even without taking into account the years of history I missed out on while bound and out of contact with it." Zax motioned to the basilisk's body. "You might want to store that unless you want to fight it again."

"Right, leaving something dead lying around in this much ambient death mana will make it a zombie real quick." Noah scooped the basilisk up into his storage and nodded. "How much farther? Should we rest before continuing, or press on?" Not that he felt tired. If anything he felt so overflowing with energy he didn't know what to do with himself.

"Not far." Zax gestured around at the basilisk cavern. "This was a side trip to collect that Shard of yours. The Reaper's Gate is very close. The very heart of the Valley."

They exited the basilisk's tunnels and emerged into a dim area filled with a thick grey mana that resembled gelatinous smog, slick and dense. It made Noah feel slimy just standing in it, let alone breathing it in. Thankfully, Zax's protective harness kept the mana pressure to a bearable level, equalizing it against his own internal power to prevent any damage. If anything, it made it even easier to traverse than some of the earlier on sections of Death Valley, despite being the thickest, darkest depths.

He gradually realized as they continued through chasms and ravines that the murkiness wasn't due to distorted sunlight, but rather a property of the mana around them. It created its own light and shadow, enforcing a perpetual twilight whether the sky above was day or night. If he needed to traverse it for any length of time, he could see it becoming incredibly disorienting.

As it was, Zax led the way through the twisting path, choosing a rift here or an abyss there, sometimes walking, sometimes flying, with Noah right behind him the whole way.

The passage they’d been moving through opened into a space so vast that Noah couldn’t see its boundaries, its walls lost in shadow, its ceiling invisible in the absolute black above.

“We are here.”

The death mana concentration was thick enough here that Noah felt like every step was forcing his way through taffy, and it took nearly ten minutes to cross half the cavern. His attempts to bypass the frustratingly slow movement by Arcane Step failed completely. The death mana was stronger than his spell could cut through.

At the center of that ocean of death, in the middle of this cavern, a cathedral rose from the cavern floor, a temple built by madmen. Black stone twisted into an incoherent structure; stairways and arches built upside down or sideways, or doors that led into open air, as if the building’s gravity had been perpetually shifting during construction and no one bothered ever fixing it when it finally settled down.

“The Gate of Endings. One of the few stable passages to the underworld. Built before ‘her’ death, it was meant to allow communication between the living and the dead. Now it’s a one-way door. Or nearly so.” Zax turned to face Noah. “Are you ready?”

Noah thought about it. Really thought, because this might be the last chance he had to say anything that mattered. “If this goes wrong, if I don’t come back, find Aurelia. Tell her…” Noah trailed off, unsure how to finish. How did you compress everything you felt into words that might be your last? “Tell her I wish she’d been here. That we’d been able to…” he swallowed. “That we’d been able to talk more, before the end.”

“I’ll deliver your message if needed, but I don’t believe it’ll be necessary. You’ve come back before, and your ritual is intended for this very purpose. This is just another threshold to cross. The question is whether you’ll learn what you need to while you’re away.”

Noah stepped into the mad cathedral.

His heart tried to beat and couldn’t. His lungs seized, muscles spasming around air they could no longer process.

He kept walking. The interior was empty, as if the outside shell was only a pretense of a building there to hide the glowing white archway that waited inside. Whether because of the contrast or because the death energy was even more concentrated here, he couldn’t see anything beyond the windows but blackness. Only the archway was visible, a ghostly outline more than anything tangible.

He reached the doorway, his body stiff and barely moving at his command, vision already dimming around the edges. He stepped through.

The world went dark, every perception he had access to cut off in an instant.

—

Vor Ashen was getting very bored of the afterlife. Having a divine quest was all well and good, but his enemy didn't have the courtesy of showing up on time.

Patience, his god whispered. Your own capabilities are your current problem.

He wanted to follow the instructions he'd been given, of course he did, but his new class was so strange. It didn't behave like anything he was used to, followed no rules of the world he knew, and overall felt more like a collection of mediocre utility powers compared to the grand multi-dimensional creation and destruction he was used to.

Vor Ashen didn't complain aloud, and he tried very hard not to complain in his heart either, but he still felt slighted and angered by the whole situation in Drakonias. After so long, he'd been so easily thrown out of his own groove. Then, instead of being allowed to directly confront the problem he was waiting around in the afterlife to ambush someone who may or may not even make it this far.

Worse still, his only option for amusing himself was to work on improving his collection of shiny new Lunar Consort abilities that were both far more powerful than they had any right to be and far too underwhelming for any of the usual Ascendant-level clashes he was used to.

He was bored of the grey endlessness of being in between existences and he was tired of waiting at the whims of a mere weakling who didn't have the courtesy to come die when he was supposed to. He wanted nothing more than to rampage through an unsuspecting planet for a few years until it was nothing but ashes and mana dust and some of this restless fury could be vented away.

Alas, his rage had no target and his purgatory no end in sight. All he could do was practice his Moonblade and Silent Step and Shadowlight and Starbreath and plan ever more intricate demises for the mere mortal who dared to disrupt his god's plans.

Then, finally, something changed.

—

[You have died.]

[Error. Astral script unavailable. Only that which is truly yours remains.]

The message faded and Noah opened his eyes to an infinite expanse of grey. It felt like the opposite of Death Valley somehow, light and unrestrictive, more like stepping inside a sunlit cloud than a choking smog. Except without the cloud or the sunlight.

Where the hell am I? Is this the underworld?

Noah instinctively tried to access his status menu, reaching for the familiar overlay despite the error message, but nothing happened. His health and mana trackers were gone, no comforting glow of the Astral Script’s presence, not even a repetition of the error message.

Nothing.

He tried activating his abilities, tracing their familiar paths without the need for outside assistance. If he could Astral Step in a direction, or create spectral weapons for himself…

Nothing.

"Great," Noah muttered, his voice sounding hollow and distant in the grey expanse. "I'd finally gotten used to having a magical HUD, and now I'm back to navigating life like some kind of caveman. How am I supposed to remember things without a quest log?" He laughed at himself, since it’d been a long time since he even looked at his quest log. He’d gotten very wrapped up in things that didn’t come with convenient quest markers.

The words echoed strangely, distorted by the nothing they bounced off before returning to him in a warped imitation of his own voice.

“Tony? You here?”

No response. Tony was part of his body and Path, left behind with the rest when he discarded the physical.

Noah looked down at himself and found a translucent form, the grey emptiness visible through his ghostly fingers.

Well, dying seems to have succeeded. Step one complete, I guess. Step two: figure out where the hell the Reaper actually is.

Noah tried to move and found that he could, sort of. There was no ground beneath him, no up or down to orient himself, but when he willed himself forward, he drifted in that direction.

Better than nothing.

He pushed forward, searching for anything that might indicate a direction. The grey stretched endlessly in every direction, featureless and unchanging. For all he knew, he could be moving in circles. Or not moving at all. Without landmarks to track his position, he had no way of knowing.

Time passed. He moved, or at least thought he probably moved, though the greyness never changed. He felt very lonely without Tony to reply to his thoughts, without Aurelia to talk to or Zax to debate with.

This is fine. Everything is fine. I'm just a disembodied soul floating through an infinite grey void with no abilities and no idea where I'm going. Nothing to worry about.

“If the Reaper is out there, I’d like to request a meeting.”

That’s when he felt the pull. Gentle at first, a current tugging at his ankles, then more insistent, dragging him downward through the formless grey. Noah tried to resist on instinct, his soul straining against the unknown force. But there was nothing to grab onto, nothing to push against, so he relaxed and allowed it to carry him where it wanted. Hopefully to the Reaper.

The grey began to darken around him, deepening from ash to charcoal. The current accelerated, and Noah felt like he was falling through layers of reality, each one colder and heavier than the last.

Memories flashed through his mind as he fell. His mother's face, pale and tired in the hospital bed. His boss screaming at him in the breakroom. A woman with silver hair and starlight in her eyes, saying words he couldn't quite remember.

With a sensation like breaking through the surface of dark water, Noah emerged from the grey and crashed back onto solid ground.

The impact drove the breath from his lungs, which was strange, because he was fairly certain souls didn't need to breathe. He lay gasping for air he didn't require, staring up at a sky that wasn't there.

Okay. Ground. That's new. Ground is good, I'm at least somewhere.

Slowly, Noah pushed himself up, his translucent hands pressing against stone that was blacker than black.

He stood and took stock of his surroundings.

The landscape stretched endlessly in every direction—a vast plain of obsidian-like stone beneath the starless void above. The surface reflected dull light from... somewhere. There was illumination here, a sourceless grey luminescence that let him see without providing any warmth or comfort. The surface was featureless, empty of landmarks, and definitely did not contain anyone resembling a god of death.

"Hello? Anyone here? Preferably someone who isn't trying to kill me?"

The distorted echoes sounded stranger and more alien each moment as the sound slowly faded away to nothing.

Worth a shot.

Noah looked down at himself again, taking proper stock of his form. His soul-body was more solid here than it had been in the grey void, more defined. He looked at his hands, translucent but functional, like he was made of cloudy glass now instead of fog.

Noah tried once more to access the system, focusing with everything he had on the familiar sensation of the Astral Script responding to his will. He'd done this thousands of times before. Opening his status. Checking his abilities. Assigning attribute points. The motions were as natural as breathing.

For a long, desperate moment, nothing happened.

Then he stopped trying and instead focused inward, searching for anything that might still respond.

His Paths were silent, the connection to the Astral Script severed completely. [Wyrmblood Guardian], [Runebody Arcanist], [Abyssal Predator], all of them were tied to the system, formatted and quantified by its structure. Without that framework to channel them, they were just... gone.

But as he searched deeper, pushing past the empty spaces where his abilities should have been, he found something else.

Peace. Knowledge. Resilience. Mastery. Binding.

Hunger.

There was no mana to gather here, but he could still remember how to meditate. There was no body to transform, but the draconic instincts persevered. The shadow in his heart and the cosmos in his blood. The devourer in his soul.

That's not something the system gave me, it's something I've always carried.

Other things weren’t so solid. The knowledge of how to summon void creatures felt hazy and distant, like it was slipping away from him.

Not that he’d ever actually used that knowledge. He didn’t even remember where it’d come from.

But the shards were a different matter. The Devourer. He reached for it tentatively. The hunger responded, sluggish and restricted but undeniably present.

I just have to figure out how to use any of it without the system showing me what to do. How hard could that be?

“Quite hard, you absolute idiot,” someone said from behind him.

Noah spun around.

His Void guide stood with two arms crossed and four tentacles trailing out behind him instead of legs and a frilled collar that looked like it belonged to some kind of deep sea creature. Dylan looked unhappier than Noah had ever seen him, and his form was flickering. “You’re making a terrible mistake, just so you know. This is your last chance to save yourself before it’s too late.”
