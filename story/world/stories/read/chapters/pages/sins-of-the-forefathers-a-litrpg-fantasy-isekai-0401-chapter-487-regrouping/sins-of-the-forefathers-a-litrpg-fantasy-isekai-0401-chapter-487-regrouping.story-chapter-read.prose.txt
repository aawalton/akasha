I turned the oddly shaped skull around in my hands a few times, glowering down at it. Thankfully, I wasn’t inside the flooded archive any longer, so even though the light within Karag Korvok itself was weak, it was at least something to see by.

Not that I enjoyed seeing this.

In my hands was the scorched skull of Serekh the Quiet.

With our entire objective having literally blown himself up in an act of spite, I had ordered our retreat from the archive. There was no more point in sticking around in there with Serekh being dead. Before we had fully pulled out, though, one of my soldiers had apparently ventured into the scorching hot blood mist left behind from the flames, and found this.

The blackened, bare skull of the Rendarch that had tried to kill all of them with his sacrifice.

I tilted my head to the left, considering the deformed skull. “Do you…” I started slowly. “Think this will be enough?”

At my hopeful tone, Meia merely gave me a doubtful look.

Renauld outright laughed at me.

I sighed. “Yeah, probably not.”

The Half-Orc Sorceress shook her head at our antics, but there wasn’t any real irritation in the gesture. “No, Hart. I suspect that if this mad dwarf was telling the truth about an entrance into the ‘Heartspire’, then we’ll need a living example of a Rendarch. Not…his Skull.”

“He was telling the truth,” Fade piped in, absolute confidence in his voice, with me relaying his words to the group around us. The Spirit Wolf was sitting on his haunches at my side, and for once, he wasn’t sitting in a pool of blood deep enough to nearly bury him. While we had been busy in the second spire, it seemed like a lot of the blood that had overtaken the banks of the lake had begun to drain back into it. Now there was only a small puddle beneath our boots and paws. “The Judgment makes it impossible to tell a lie with my fangs at a throat.”

Meia inclined her head to him. “As you say. But…that leaves us at a crossroads, doesn’t it? We need a living Rendarch if we hope to enter the Heartspire.”

I don’t think a single one of us expected what happened next.

A voice called out from beyond the huddled, exhausted forms of my battlegroup arrayed around us. It came from farther away than even the furthest resting warrior around me.

“Oh, is that all you need?!”

I recognized that voice. A smile immediately blossomed on my cheeks as I turned around, a name on my lips.

It immediately died when an absolutely massive hunk of meat flew through the air to splash into the blood at my feet. I didn’t blink as the blood sprayed me once more. Not just because I was getting extremely inured to copious amounts of blood, because that was true.

More because I was nearly short-circuiting at the sight of the bisected torso of the single largest Korvok I had seen since setting foot in this hold. Not only was it just a torso that looked to have been blown apart at the midline, but its arms looked to have been ripped off as well, and the stumps seared.

Oh, and it was still alive.

I couldn’t forget that part. How did I know that?

Well.

Because it was glaring up at me with crimson eyes brimming with hatred. I think if this…thing could speak, it would have been cursing me. Or, I don’t know.

Considering the Korvoks, probably calling me a hidebound heretic or something like that.

Of course, it couldn’t do that. Someone had melted the Korvok’s lips together, too. He was certainly still trying to speak, though. I could hear indignant, muffled grunts echoing out from those welded-together lips.

I tilted my head in dull confusion at the half of a Dwarf at my feet, as Renauld yelped behind me…and Meia laughed?

I turned an incredulous look at the Half-Orc, and she at least had the good graces to wince at my regard. “Pay me no mind,” She muttered, surprisingly sheepish. “It’s just…there’s this play about something like this back in the Dominion, and I liked it as a girl, and…” She trailed off.

I didn’t ask any more.

Instead, I shook my head, turned away, and fixed the approaching frame of the man who had thrown this thing at me with the dryest look I possibly could. “Is there a possible explanation, Bleddyn?”

The blood-soaked form of the Mynnyd man, accompanied by not only Bella and all their men, but Sylvia as well, smiled broadly at me. He nodded with visible satisfaction. “Yup,” He said smugly, crossing his arms over his bare, blood-soaked chest. “Brought you a gift.”

He nudged the mutilated torso in its ribs, and the thing’s muffled protests only increased.

I had to hide my laugh as a cough into my hand, at the mental image of a loyal dog bringing its kill to its master to appreciate. Bleddyn would not appreciate that comparison.

“I…suppose it is,” I eventually said, shaking my head. I exchanged a quick smile with Sylvia. “And who is your gift supposed to be?”

“Some Korvok big-shot,” Bleddyn shrugged his wide shoulders. “Tough bastard. Had to break out something new I’ve been working on to take him down. Called himself, uh…” He paused, scratching his chin.

Sylvia smoothly stepped in, literally and figuratively. “Draskul, Rendarch of the Cleaving Path. I arrived just in time for him to boast of his title, before Bleddyn defeated him.”

My eyes lit up at the news, and I considered the dismembered torso of our new Rendarch friend with greater appreciation. I noticed I wasn’t the only one to do so. Most of the people in my battlegroup had heard our discussion on just what we needed now.

“To our surprise, he wasn’t quite dead after his defeat,” Sylvia said, raising one Mithril eyebrow at our reaction. “Whatever method of regeneration he possesses, it seems to be enough to keep him alive even in this state. But to keep him…controllable…”

Almost unconsciously, over a dozen sets of eyes trailed down to the bloody, flayed torso sitting in the puddle of blood still washed up on the shores of the lake. The Rendarch merely glared up at us, and I heard some kind of muffled speech from behind his melted lips.

“We seared his wounds closed,” Sylvia continued, unperturbed. It took a lot to flap the Sculpted woman. “That seems to suffice.”

“Well-known tactic fer dealing with regenerators,” Bella smirked.

“And that’s that,” Bleddyn said, with a smirk of his own directed at the Rendarch he had apparently defeated. The de-limbed torso switched his glare over to the werewolf in response, but only caused Bleddyn to smirk harder. He eventually gave up his silent taunting to shrug a single shoulder my way. “Anywho. Apparently, you and the little ancestor can do something wit-is that a skull?”

I blinked at the abrupt question, following Bleddyn’s incredulous gaze down to my hands. The burned, bare skull of Serekh glared up at me with empty eye sockets. “Ah…yes. This,” I said, idly tossing the skull into the air and catching it. “Belonged to another Rendarch, who chose to kill himself over being captured. But he doesn’t matter anymore.”

This time, when I tossed the skull upwards, it was over my shoulder. I didn’t blink when it flew some distance to land with a splash in the crimson lake behind us. Instead, I crouched down, laying my forearms on my knees as I considered the helpless form of the captured Rendarch.

“You know,” I started abruptly. “Your buddy Serekh had some interesting things to tell us. Particularly about the Heartspire and how to get into it. He killed himself rather than all us to use him to enter it, but…you don’t exactly have the same luxury, do you?” I smiled at the visible fury I could see on his skinned features, standing up from my crouch. “Thank you, Bleddyn, for your gift. From what we learned, all we need to do is carry this sad-sack to the north shore of the lake, and a path will open for us into the central spire. That’s where these bastards are keeping both the Varlayan captives and Azarus.”

“And the Jarl,” Renauld pointed out smugly. “Can’t forget him.”

I breathed in slowly. “Yes, Renauld, thank you. And Jarl Jorvik.” I breathed out. “Now. That means we need to regroup for the final assault. Bleddyn, Bella, Sylvia, did you happen to find either Gustave or Rafael back where we left them?”

I’m not sure I liked the way their faces fell at my question.

“Not as such,” Bella said cautiously.

I definitely didn’t like that.

“Part of all this mess,” Bleddyn gestured to all of the many collapsed spires all around us. “Blocked off the entire damn entrance to the lake. Looked like the entire top half just fell over it. Which is good because it stopped all of the fookin’ Skarned from getting’ in, but none of the people we left behind were around. Includin’ the bag of bones and the turncoat-I mean Gustave.” He paused for a moment, but not because of the mild glare I shot him. Instead, he was considering the many destroyed towers across the lake. “Speakin’ of, do you know just what the hells happened with all of this?”

I let out a slow sigh, considering matters. “I’d been hoping you could tell me that, actually.”

Bleddyn grimaced, and I could see that he wasn’t the only one put out by the news. Because if it hadn’t been something either of our groups had done, then it had to have been Olag’s contingent.

And I couldn’t help but notice he or his people were here to give a report as well. Sena had even left us before we entered the archives to try and find the Florens Dwarf, and she was missing as well.

I didn’t like that, and I didn’t like what it might represent.

Olag was missing, which meant Maria was missing. Gustave was missing, which also meant that Rafael was missing.

Combined, across their groups, we were missing hundreds of soldiers as well.

Rafael worried me the most, as callous as that might sound. He was a powerhouse, one of the strongest people I had ever met. As a Lich, he no longer even had a Status or the subsequent Levels he had once fought so hard to obtain in life. But that didn’t mean any of his actual power had diminished. He had essentially been one of the central pillars of my planning, alternatively, our trump or get out of jail free card.

I refused to consider the possibility that he had been overpowered. Maybe he’d retreated, or possibly even moved elsewhere in the city.

I had to hope for that, because I couldn’t spare either the time or the men to go looking for him.

“There’s nothing we can do about it right now,” I said lowly, shaking my head. “We have to continue. Bleddyn, before you entered your tower, did you see where Olag went?”

To my displeasure, the werewolf shook his head. “Nope. He and Maria split at the spire I fought in. Left me at the door and then off he went.”

Sylvia let out a long, slow breath, a habit she’d picked up from us flesh creatures. “His spire was likely the originating point of the collapse, then.”

As one, we all looked across the lake at the ruins of said tower. Or at the very least, it’s base. The actual upper length of it had crashed into Rhogann’s spire, while only the destroyed base of the ground floor remained in a pile of rubble.

If he or his people were in there…

We had no way of digging him out. Not while we were still nominally in a conflict with the Korvoks.

“We continue forward,” I finally pronounced grimly. “We make for the north end of the lake with this sad sack in tow-”

I nudged the dismembered torso of ‘Draskul’ with my boot.

“-and accomplish what we set out to do. Then…maybe then we can look for Olag and Maria.”

I could tell that my orders didn’t sit totally well with everyone around me, but thankfully, nobody countermanded me. Even if a majority of my people were idealists…

They weren’t stupid.

Bleddyn picked up the limbless torso of the Rendarch, and the rest of my forces prepared themselves for the march around the lake. It wasn’t exactly a small installation, in the end, and there was the rubble of the collapse to consider. Once smooth paved surfaces were now littered with the remains of the fallen spires, some as large as houses. Not only that, but the tidal surge of blood from that chain reaction of destruction had rendered all of it slick. We were going to have to be careful as we navigated our way to the north shore.

But we had to do it.

Like always…

The fate of Vereden seemed to rest heavily on everyone’s shoulders, as we carefully picked our way through the rubble.

Mine, most of all.
