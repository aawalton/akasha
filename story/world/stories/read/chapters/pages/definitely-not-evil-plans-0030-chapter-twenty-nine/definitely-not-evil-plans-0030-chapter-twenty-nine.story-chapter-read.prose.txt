I ended up driving to the coffee shop and ordering something with three shots of espresso. I was halfway through my drink, watching the code as I stared off into space when my phone buzzed.

I looked at the caller ID and groaned. Mom.

I went ahead and silenced it. Unfortunately, for me I am a glutton for punishment if you haven’t noticed already. And so I decided to pick my phone back up and listen to the voicemail.

“Samantha. It’s your mother. I was truly hoping that you would have been able to get some forward momentum in your life. It’s been well over a month darling, it’s time to stop wallowing. Which leads me to my next point. We’ve been thinking about Thanksgiving. Your aunt Catrina and I talked with aunt Harriet and we’ve all decided that it would be in your best interest if you don’t come to Thanksgiving this year.”

She took a deep theatrical breath. “I wouldn’t want you to be embarrassed in front of everyone, dear. Without a job, or a boyfriend. You know how the family talks. Brittany will be there with Chad, and they’re doing so well. I’m sure the entire thing would just be difficult for you.”

I gripped my mug so tightly my knuckles began to ache.

“Use that extra time to get yourself sorted out,” she continued. “Polish up that resume. Maybe do some networking. That way, come Christmas you will be ready to make a proper impression. You know how important image is to me, Samantha. I expect you to get it together and to make us both proud. With a job. Preferably with a nice young man, but I know that is asking an awful lot given your… situation and lack of any movement in well over thirty days. Kisses. Ta.”

The voicemail ended. I ground my teeth so hard they were in danger of cracking.  I just kept replaying the conversation in my head over and over. She uninvited me to Thanksgiving. Where two of my cousins literally were greeters at Allmart, and one wanted so badly to not work she married Chad. Since I was single and unemployed apparently I was too pathetic to be seen in public.

I picked up my phone and without thinking I texted Kate.

Me: You will not believe what my mother just did.

The message sat there for a moment and its status never changed to ‘delivered’.

Right. Kate had me blocked. I’d forgotten in the moment of being overwhelmed by Helena. That’s right Helena. She was not a mother at that moment, and therefore I refused to give her that title. But without her, without Kate, without coworkers I was alone. Completely alone.

“Hey, I know you.” A voice called.

I stopped seething a moment and looked up. Brown hair, slightly shaggy. Plastic-rimmed glasses, warm amber eyes. Hands that couldn’t seem to hold onto coffee. It was him, the guy I stood up earlier this week. Scott.

“Oh hey, no need for the coffee shower today.” I’d meant it flirty, but it came out more annoyed than anything else.

He winced. “Yeah, I was hoping that’s not the part you would’ve remembered about me.” He paused studying my face. “Are you okay? You look like your dog just died.”

“I don’t have a dog.”

“Hopefully not because it just died?

The cheesy smile that accompanied it caused me to let out a short laugh. Then I sighed, “I’m fine. I just got uninvited to Thanksgiving by my own mother. Via voicemail.”

“Yowch,” Scott’s expression softened to genuine sympathy. “That’s brutal.”

“She doesn’t want ‘me’ to be embarrassed by showing up unemployed.” I made air quotes with my fingers around me. “Apparently my failure to have neither a job nor a boyfriend makes me unfit for family gatherings until I ‘get myself sorted out.’”

“That is incredibly terrible. Just…wow.”

“Oh, I’m sorry, I shouldn’t have dumped on you.”

“No no, you can dump on me any time.”

He realized what he said and he looked utterly embarrassed. He reached up to rub the back of his neck. “Forget I said that. What I mean is, I don’t mind. I’m a great listener, so vent away.”

“I’m fine. I’m fine.” In case it wasn’t apparent, I was not fine. My eyes burned and I definitely didn’t want to cry in front of the cute IT guy I cancelled plans on. “Sorry, I'm terrible company right now.”

“Mind if I sit?” Scott gestured to the empty chair across from me. “I promise to keep all coffee at a cafe distance either way.”

I hesitated. In a mood like this I could truly be bad company and make a total fool out of myself. Over sharing, crying and ranting. The triforce of ‘bad first date’. On the other hand, I did kind of ghost the guy so I figured I could endure.

Sure,” I said, “Live dangerously.”

Scott sat down and set his coffee down carefully in the center of the table theatrically gesturing to point.

The ridiculousness of it all did earn another quick laugh from me.

“So,” he asked cautiously. “Bad interview?”

“How did you know about the interview?”

He motioned at my blazer. “You’re dressed up, you look like there’s more than just your mom’s voicemail on your mind. Just a guess.”

“No, you’re right. Turns out it was a fake interview. They just wanted gossip about Harrison & Sons, not my design skills.”

“You worked there? That’s the company that’s been in the news right?”

I groaned.

“No, I get it. I’ve had a few of the ‘Tell us everything scandalous’ interviews.’”

“You have?”

“I did some contract work for a company that got caught up in a data breach a few years ago. For about six months afterward, every client interview felt like an interview for some gossiping blog.” He shrugged.”The news cycle moves on.”

“I hope so.”

We sat in comfortable silence for a moment. Then Scott said, “I should probably address the elephant in the room.”

“What elephant?”

“Well you ghosted me.” It didn’t come out accusational so much as a straight fact. “Remember. Lunch date. You cancelled? You said you’d reach back out when you were ready. And then… nothing.”

I was so embarrassed. I could feel my face heating up. “I’m sorry. I was going through some stuff. It wasn’t about you.”

“I was hoping.” He tilted his head. “Where’s your partner in crime?”

“We uh, had a falling out. She’s currently not speaking to me.”

“Oh man, I’m sorry to hear that.”

“It is definitely my fault. The guilt tumbled from my mouth before I could stop it. “I did something I shouldn’t have and she couldn’t forgive me. I thought I was doing the right thing but…” I shook my head. “It doesn’t matter. I can only hope she’ll forgive me.”

“What did you do that was so unforgivable?”

I averted my eyes as shame filled me. “I lied to her. She asked me to promise that I wouldn’t and I broke the one promise she asked of me.”

Scott was quiet for a moment. “It seems to me like you’ve been going through a lot and maybe you’ve been doing a little self-sabotagging by pushing people away.”

“Oh yeah? Didn’t realize you were a psychologist. Thought you were in IT.”

“Has nothing to do with work.” He said simply. “My dad died five years ago. It was a heart attack, very sudden and very unexpected. For about six months afterward I pushed everyone away. Friends, family, coworkers, everyone. I was convinced that I would bring other people down with my troubles. Then after so long, I was convinced that asking for help, or apologizing was weakness and I didn’t know how to let people back in.”

“What changed?”

“I realized that being alone wasn’t strength and it wasn’t protecting anyone from me, It was just loneliness dressed up as pride and self-sacrifice.” He met my eyes. “I’m not trying to say that your situation is the same. I’m just saying that sometimes when everything falls apart it’s easier to try to face it alone than to be vulnerable.”

I didn’t know what to say. It was far too close to the truth, too close to everyone and everything I’d ever gotten close to since my own dad passed away.

“Anyway.” Scott straightened, his tone lightening. “I have a radical proposal.”

“I’m listening.”

“You’ve had a terrible day and I’ve had an incredibly boring one. What if we got lunch? Somewhere with absolutely no connection to the design industry or corporate America? Somewhere we can ignore real life?”

“You still want to have lunch after I ghosted you?”

“I definitely still want to have lunch with you.” He smiled and it was such a warm cheerful smile that I felt something loosen in my chest.

He added, “Consider it a fresh start.”

“I should have said no. I was a mess. I was doing what I always did and was pushing everyone and everything away. But he was looking at me with those amber eyes, that adorable crooked smile and what was my alternative? Go home and sit alone in my beautiful apartment with no one but my dying plants to keep me company.

“Okay,” I said. “But I’m warning you, I’m terrible company right now.”

“Impossible.” He stood and offered his hand. “I have excellent taste in company. Now let’s go, I know the perfect place.”
