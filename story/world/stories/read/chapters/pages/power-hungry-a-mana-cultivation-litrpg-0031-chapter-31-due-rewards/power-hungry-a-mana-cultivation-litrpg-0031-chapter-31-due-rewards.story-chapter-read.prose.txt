It took three hours for the poison to wear off. After Cale and Aura dealt with the fever, he made rounds to push a detoxification pill in everyone’s mouths. Most of them were still pale, but some like Fiara, Ragnak and Galvon were starting to show some signs of a fever. That was good.

After Cale was sure his comrades would be fine, he sat down next to the convoy. He saw a bowl of spagon discarded on the ground. Cale looked towards the direction Cassidy had escaped to. The evening sun cast an orange glare around the red and black wasteland. Cale wondered how long it would take for her to reach a Sullied fortress.

So this is the way the game is played.

So far Cale had only experienced power games and clashing egos. The social machinations of cultivators were complicated even on an elementary level, but he hadn’t expected this. It was only dumb luck that Cale had been able to stop this sabotage. He wouldn’t let himself be fooled again.

How can anyone trust anyone in a world like this?

The guarded pride cultivators carried suddenly made a lot more sense. Cale found himself in a pang of pity. Memories of his old life came to his mind. Laughing while rocking on a swing. Rolling around in mud with a dog. Playing soccer.

Generic memories again… But they are a childhood. I doubt these cultivators ever had much of a one.

Cale had learned an important lesson here. This was a zero-sum game. If Cassidy had succeeded, she would have been rewarded. She had failed and she would be punished. Same worked this way round. Now that Cale had succeeded, he was expecting a reward. That gave him a thrill.

This is a game worth playing…

Maybe Darius wasn’t a half-bad mentor after all, Cale thought with grim humor. But it was time to focus. Rumination only got so much done.

Cale reached for his pouch and started cultivation. Only two Nevani mana crystals danced between his fingers as Cale fished for them in his pouch. That made Cale feel naked. Poverty did not fit a cultivator. But there was no time to lament. He had to work with what he had. And because Cale didn’t have much to work with, he focused on refining his attunement skills.

After three hours and two and a half Nevani crystals were spent, Cale had improved his control. The new insight he had gained in taming the murderbot mana was useful in refining his cultivation. He couldn’t quite solidify the mana yet, let alone crystallize it. But when Cale had started on his path, the mana he could work with had been mostly gaseous. Now he could create a thick sludge that he could condense into a tightly packed orb. When Cale sucked in the last dregs of that orb, he felt the energy flow into his body, strengthening it.

“How far along am I towards the next stage?”

“Didn’t you just advance?” Aura asked. “How greedy can you be?”

Cale smiled. “I’m just getting started.”

“Hmph,” Aura sniffed. “I suppose impatience has its place. You are 4% along the way.”

“Only four percent?” Cale asked, his brow furrowing.

“Impatient!” Aura admonished. “You are working with the purest mana my creators managed to create. You have an advanced personal, and might I add, scandalously majestic AI symbiote, specializing in cultivation instruction. And you are complaining about the speed at which you cultivate?! The nerve…”

“I just feel it was faster in the beginning…” Cale muttered.

“Of course it was faster,” Aura said. “The first levels are elementary. But cultivation gets exponentially more expensive and hard with each step on the path of advancement. I have a baseline for cultivation speed in my database, and you are surpassing it by a ridiculous margin. And you call this slow? I cannot believe the sheer arrogance.”

“I learn from the best,” Cale said and chuckled.

He exhaled slowly, feeling the last traces of the mana settle into his body. His strength had increased. It was subtle, but it was there. Progress. But not enough. Not yet.

His fingers drifted to the pouch at his side. Empty.

Cale had spent all his resources. And there were still trials ahead. It felt awful being poor. Powerless. Fortunately he still had one last card to play. Cale went up to his hoverboard and removed the mana crystal from the hoverboard’s engine. Cale looked at the crystal and sighed wistfully. The same soft purple glow that had fueled him since the start. Spinning it between his fingers, he felt a strange sense of finality.

I’ve come a long way.

But there was still further to go.

The biting red wind gusted and snapped him back. This wasn’t a great time for sentimentality. Cale put the crystal in his now empty pouch.

“The crystal is 92% of full capacity,” Aura said. “I suggest you don’t use it to cultivate. We might need the extra mana with your penchant for getting into trouble.”

“My thoughts exactly,” Cale said and pushed himself up, rolling his shoulders. His muscles ached, and his head was still foggy from the fever, but nothing he couldn’t push through. “Let’s go see if Fiara is lucid.”

Cale came up to the flushed blonde and lifted her head to give her some water. She murmured softly and grabbed the hand Cale was holding the bottle with and drank. Some of it spilled on her chin, but she gave him a tired smile. It was a tender moment, and Cale promised himself he would not use her vulnerability as leverage later.

She’s sure to be embarrassed about it later.

It took some time waking her up. She finally opened her eyes in Cale’s lap, blinked blearily and frowned. Cale offered her another drink and she took it greedily. She looked up and Cale smiled at her. She was already flushed, but she found a stronger shade of pink. She darted up to sit, turned and glared at Cale and then got up on wobbly legs. She immediately called Darius from her wrist device.

By the time Darius arrived, most of the recruits were good enough to stand. Fiara ordered them to secure the perimeter around the payload. Fortunately no attacks came their way.

Darius flew toward them in a streak of black, his umbrella open like a gliding specter. He barely seemed to register the wind tearing around him, as if the storm itself bent to his presence. His landing was effortless—one foot touching the ground before the other, smooth as if he’d simply stepped off a ledge instead of hurtling through the air. He dusted off specks of dust from his immaculate suit and looked around.

He ignored the other recruits and immediately stomped towards Cale and Fiara in long strides.

Cale explained what had happened. Darius pressed him for details Cale wasn’t sure he even knew. Color of Cassidy’s eyes. The tech she had used, what specific word choices she had used. A full debriefing. He was blunt and direct and there was nothing but hard professionalism in his harsh manner.

But after Cale had told everything, Darius clapped him on the shoulder. “Kid. You really pulled through this time. You were resourceful and you stayed loyal. And most importantly, you succeeded.”

Cale smiled at him. He felt a swell of pride in his chest. But pats on the head wouldn’t do. “You know, I had to expend resources to defeat her… Not to mention I passed on all her sweet promises.”

Darius gave him the side-eye, measuring him up. Then he burst out laughing. It was a boisterous thing that filled the great outdoors. Some of the recruits startled. He clapped Cale on the shoulder again. “You’re learning, kid. I was hoping you’d ask. This is not a world where things are just given to you. You have to be bold. You have to demand. You’re learning the game.”

“I have to admit, you do have a point sometimes,” Cale said. “Now that I’ve gotten a taste of this world, I see what you’re trying to teach me.”

“Finally the student listens,” Darius said and threw the umbrella on his shoulder. “We might make a cultivator of you yet, Cale. I’ll see that you’re rewarded in a moment. First I need to talk to the recruits.”

Darius jumped on top of the payload and pointed at each one of the recruits with his umbrella, demanding attention. “My temperance has not been tested like this in years.”

It was delivered in a quiet and dangerous tone. But Darius’s cultivation made sure the repressed rage of his whisper chilled every listener. “If the payload had been destroyed, I would have personally made sure to end each of your careers. Failures, that is what you are. Best and brightest the syndicate has to offer? You’re all trash. I told you all to keep a lookout for enemy interference. Did any of you even notice the Arcane Veil operative SERVING FOOD TO A SULLIED?! YOU SHOULD KNOW BETTER!”

BETTER… BETTER…. BETTER…

The echoes of Darius’s shout blared so loud that the jagged high structures around them cracked and pieces crumbled off.

Darius adjusted his tie and sighed.

“As far as I’m concerned, all of you failed this mission, apart from Cale. My action report to Madam Wren will be very clear on how our best operative recruits all got fooled by a pretty girl. Your careers aren’t over—but they’ll carry the stain of this failure. Mark my words.”

Darius let the heavy silence hang after those words. The recruits were visibly uncomfortable. Even Risto was serious, his lazy smile entirely gone.

But I am not entirely cruel,” Darius said. “You still have time to redeem yourselves. Show that you are worth the syndicate’s investment and PERFORM! Am I understood?”

There were nods and a few stray yessirs.

“And because Cale here actually managed to perform and single-handedly, without anyone’s help, stopped the sabotage, he will get rewarded. I am giving him a Storage device and Nevani crystals worth thirty thousand creds.

“What?” Madia asked under her breath. But everyone heard that.

“I’m sorry?” Darius asked in a dangerously sweet tone. “Are you questioning my judgement? Please do educate me on how I should reward and punish my underlings, as you’ve clearly shown great prowess in your judgement, trash.”

Madia lowered her gaze.

Darius took out the rewards. A silver and blue device that looked like a thick wristwatch, and a small pouch, similar to what Cale already had. “Let it be known that the syndicate is very generous to those that are valuable. Learn this: if you’re not useful, you’re not even pawns on the board. You’re just dead weight. You won’t even be in the game.”

Then Darius turned to Cale and with a decisive flick of a finger beckoned him closer.

Cale could feel the heat of the other recruits’ stares on his back. Resentment. Jealousy. Even admiration. Before Cale would have disregarded it, but now he found his mind catalogued it. The dynamics had shifted. He would need to factor that in. But right now it wasn’t important. Darius and his reward was the only thing that mattered now.

Cale stepped forward, meeting Darius’s gaze as he took the storage device and the pouch. The wrist device was heavier than he expected—smooth, metallic, with faint blue sigils carved into its surface.

“Seen these before?” Darius asked.

Cale shrugged. “Yours.”

Darius nodded and motioned for Cale to put it on. Cale snapped the device onto his wrist, and the metal adjusted slightly, tightening just enough to feel snug without cutting off circulation. A small panel flickered to life on its surface—a faint, pulsing blue screen with a single symbol floating in the center.

“Press that,” Darius instructed.

Cale tapped the panel, and a small black void shimmered into existence in front of him. It was about the size of a basketball, flickering faintly at the edges. He stepped closer, peering inside.

It wasn’t infinite, like some mystical pocket dimension. The space was about as big as a fridge—maybe a little taller, but just as narrow. A simple, bare-bones storage compartment in the grand scheme of things.

Cale shot Darius a look. “Yours is bigger.”

Darius smirked, tilting his head. “Don’t I know it.”

Cale chuckled.

Darius tapped his own wrist device. “These come in varying models. Yours is a standard-issue Type-1 vault, nothing fancy, but it gets the job done. You can upgrade it later, expand the space, or link it to a sub-dimension if you’re lucky enough to get your hands on one. These aren’t Nevani make and can’t be even really called Integra. They’re just gear. You can link modules to this one, like an RCI. You know what that is?”

“Yeah,” Cale said, completely engrossed with his new device. “Remote communication Integra.”

“Good,” Darius said. “And another thing. You’ll want to keep your notes there. Like on that Veil operative. Make a report. There’s a function over… here.”

Darius tapped the wrist device a few times and

“Hmph! Primitive!” Aura sniffed. “Next thing I know he is going to offer to teach you about the wheel or making fire! You do not need this. I remember everything.”

Cale smiled and coughed for attention. Darius cocked a questioning eyebrow.

Cale looked around, to see if the other recruits were paying attention. That was an understatement. They were all watching Cale like hungry hawks.

“Don’t need this one,” Cale said quietly.

Darius tilted his head, clearly confused. Then he got it. His face smoothened and he gave the slightest nod. “Got it. Good for you, kid.”

Cale nodded absently, already pulling his hoverboard off his back. It had been with him since the start, but now he had somewhere to store it without carrying it like a damn overgrown backpack. He held it out toward the shimmering black void and—

Fwump.

The hoverboard vanished inside.

Cale checked the wrist device. The blue panel now displayed a simple inventory readout. There was only one item listed. He tapped the screen again, and the void flickered shut.

Well, that’s useful.

He turned his attention to the pouch Darius had handed him. He loosened the drawstring and let the contents spill into his palm.

Soft purple glow. Smooth, translucent surface. Weighty.

Cale liked what he saw.

He recognized these.

These were the same Nevani crystals they’d found in the ruins. Darius must have made bank that day, if ten of these were enough to piss off Madia. There had been piles in the treasure room.

He rolled one between his fingers, feeling the familiar hum of energy beneath the surface. He was struck by that familiar rush of greed. It was a manic hunger that demanded that he sucked the energy inside, so that he could grow. Cale did not reign it back, he allowed it. He revelled in it.

He had earned it.

Cale sucked in a small stream of mana from the crystal. It entered his body effortlessly. No resistance, no violent surges—just a clean, steady flow of power.

He worked the energy, cycling it, refining it. The contrast was stark—the murderbot cores had been wild, aggressive. This was silk. He shaped the energy carefully, controlled it, and guided it toward Aura.

The response was immediate.

A soft thrumming filled his mind as Aura absorbed the energy into her core. “Ohhh, now that is more like it.”

Cale arched an eyebrow. Turned out his AI symbiote had a greedy streak as well.

Cale let the rest of the mana settle into his solar plexus. It was subtle, but he could already tell—his control had improved. His refinement process had improved. He started idly working the smooth mana into a more and more condense form, attuning it to Cale. The rest of the crystals he pocketed.

He grinned.

Yeah. This had been worth it.
