Despite spending time in the Silverwater territory during the early stages of my reincarnation, I remembered very little of the town itself. Back then, I’d had little reason or strength to leave the confines of the Silverwater mansion.

Now, standing at the threshold of what remained, I couldn’t compare memory to reality. But I'd always pictured it as being a fairly well-to-do, if quaint, city.

My cloak caught the wind sweeping in from the ashen hills surrounding the shattered town. I'd had to get all new clothes after experiencing my breakthrough into the gold realm and coming back to an adult body. At first, I'd believed that I had regained my original appearance from Ordite, and in the moment of Reformation, it had seemed so. But the months since had proven me wrong. I'd regained my height and frame from Ordite, but not quite my body. It was leaner, the bulk of decades of war pared away, as though it had settled somewhere between my original self and what I suspected Lilliana would have been as an adult. Her soft features remained. I'd retained the scar through my eye, the color of my eyes, the thickness and color of my hair, as well as the natural strength of my bones and muscles. My movements were more graceful and less brusque. I was faster than I'd been at this stage in my previous life, but not quite as strong.

I patted the cloak down and tucked my hands into the pockets of my black trousers, then raised my head to take in Lilliana's city. Officially, the city was Carlion. No one but cartographers ever called it anything but Silverwater. If it once had been a rather well-off town nestled at the heart of the wealthy barony, it was nothing like that anymore. Rather, it was little more than a graveyard steeped in ruin and regret.

Where homes and buildings had once stood, there was only flat, scorched land and skeletal stone or wood ruins whispering faint echoes of what had been. Even the earth bore wounds—raw, blistered, and festering with ancient heart energy that still carried the stench of destruction.

The wind shifted, and a new scent reached me. Not just smoke, though there was plenty of that, but something fouler beneath it. It wasn’t the clean, dry burn of wood, nor the acrid tang of mana-fire I’d come to know so well over these passed few months. No, it was the stench of charred flesh, long dead and baked into the stone. It clawed into my lungs with fingers stubborn and ghostly, as if the city itself was begging me to rescue it from its already cemented fate.

As I made my way through the ruins, trying to recall anything of this city, the streets offered little to guide me. Burnt-out husks of buildings lined every road, their foundations blackened, their collapsed arches resembling broken ribs. Some still smoldered in the dawn light, embers clinging stubbornly to existence. The fires that had consumed this place—that of the demon progenitor—had left nothing untouched.

From the remnant energy and mana still dancing across the city, I glimpsed the power and nature of the flames. Memories of my battles with progenitors back on Ordite came to mind, as did the hunger of their flames. Their flames were all-consuming, driven by a hunger as if jealous of the living. They sought out every flicker of life, ravenous and insatiable.

One of the knights I hadn’t bothered to learn the name of muttered some kind of prayer as we passed by the remains of a toppled tower. Its jagged remains pierced the boulevard we walked, bits of old energy still crackled along the fallen spire.

“By the Gods... was that a Scholar’s Tower?” the knight asked, voice hushed. “I thought those were protected by hundreds of defense magics.”

Dralos, walking beside him, laid a scaled hand on his shoulder. “I believe it was, Sir Alec. Once upon a time.”

The knight stepped forward as if to approach it. He immediately recoiled as a shard of remnant energy leapt from the rubble and slammed into his armor with a sharp crack. The breath rushed from his lungs as he twisted to absorb the blow and was sent crashing into a nearby ruin with enough power to kill a mortal.

I didn’t bother looking, attempting to ignore the knight's foolishness. Only when another knight edged closer to the fallen scholar's tower did I turn to scowl over my shoulder. “Enough,” I said, voice cold. “Just because something broke through the defenses doesn’t mean the defenses are gone. Keep moving, unless you'd rather I leave you behind to test your mettle against the beasts still lurking here.”

As if on cue, a beast howled in the distance, reminding everyone that the town of Silverwater bordered the Misty Veil Forest. The knights snapped out of their stupor. All two dozen straightened and threw up crisp salutes, their golden armor clinking as they hurried into formation.

Dralos moved ahead of the group to where I stood, the corner of his mouth twitching in the semblance of a smile at the sigh I couldn't help but breathe out. Again, I regretted agreeing to bring an escort. Despite being some of the Duchy’s finest Silver Realm cultivators, the power they wielded with such pride felt juvenile to me now. Since breaking into the Gold Realm of heart energy and the third tier of mana cultivation, I had felt... out of place in this world. My conflict with the Hydra had worked to temper my discomfort in Graedon, but there was nothing left to test me. Though I'd solidified both my cores over the passed six or so months, I'd failed to advance either of them in the slightest. It had not only taken me more than two months to fully cleanse my heart core of whatever tar that Desire System's attempt to corrupt my mind had left behind, but there was nothing left to push me in the world. Nothing threaten my life.

I hoped that, despite the low power level of this world, the Cultivation Doctrine possessed by the noble houses would be of some use to me. If not, then perhaps to those who followed me.

The realization that Nasq’s portal led to Ordite only intensified the pure anticipation thrumming through my veins. To advance further than I was currently at, I would likely need to return to Ordite once more. I kept remembering the feeling of the desert beast whose territory we had trespassed, how it had watched us from miles away. Temptation to return through the portal and face that beast evoked

Unfortunately, my work in Graedon was not yet complete. Since my death, Aedroninr had likely been absorbed by the Aexion Empire. I wouldn't be suprised to learn the entire continent was subsumed by the Empire.

To combat that, then, I would need my own empire.

That meant I couldn't afford to dedicate all my focus and attention to the Paragons, despite their insane speed of growth. The entire military under my command needed to grow and advance. The Paragons would grow with or without me. The Duchy’s knights needed handholding, especially in a world where power standards were... lacking.

We moved in silence past the ruined tower. Our boots crunched over scorched tiles and the shredded parchment of burned books. We passed another street of destroyed homes and shops before coming upon our destination.

“The Sanctum!” a woman cried, pushing past the knights to sprint ahead. Her voice rang out sharp against the still morning. She had long black hair and unnaturally blue eyes—so pale that in bright weather, her irises bled into the whites of her eyes. She wore a luxurious silk gown, luminous as molten sunlight, its hems and cuffs woven with gold and platinum in celestial patterns. The robe swirled around her ankles as she ran, and a silver circlet gleamed atop her head. At first glance, she'd appeared more princess than priestess.

The Sanctum itself had collapsed entirely. Its dome ceiling had caved in while its support columns were melted into twisted black spires. The air reeked with the scent of burnt parchment and the lingering rot of buried corpses.

“They must have been in prayer when it happened,” she whispered, falling to her knees at what remained of the entrance. Dralos knelt beside her, bowing his head. A few knights followed suit. Others stood at my back, silent.

I didn’t kneel. Instead, I dipped my chin in respect, not out of reverence, but because any Saintess worth her name would acknowledge the gods she represented. Even a false one.

Sanctums, I’d learned, were places of worship in Lysoria for Gods within the Pantheon of Light.

“It will be in here,” the woman said through soft sobs.

“Are you sure, Josephine?” Dralos asked.

She nodded.

Ah, so that's her name.

I hadn’t bothered learning it before. The king had assigned her as a representative of the Goddess of Light, but beyond that, I hadn’t cared.

“I wouldn’t step into a fallen Sanctum for anything less,” she said. “The Core Doctrine of a noble house is sacred. Nothing is more important.”

She moved around the fallen structure's front to the side of it before approaching a buried stairwell choked with rubble that appeared to descend below the Sanctum rather than leading into it. I followed her, and the moment I got close, the stench of death hit me like a wave. It was worse than most battlefields, like a suffocating miasma of rot and decay baked in heat for weeks. I suppressed an involuntary gag, but the priestess, apparently lacking any such discipline, collapsed to her knees and retched uncontrollably. She remained there, trembling, until I dragged her back to the knights.

The moment I released her, she slumped to the ground like a puppet with its strings cut. “Wait,” I commanded, jabbing a finger in her direction as she began to gather her senses.

Mortals.

I gestured for two knights to follow and advanced toward the buried stairwell leading into the ruined Sanctum. With our enhanced strength, it didn’t take long to clear the rubble, revealing a passage that descended into the darkness beneath the Sanctum.

I raised an eyebrow at Josephine. “Where does this lead?”

The woman ignored me, or perhaps was unable to hear me over the sound of her own dry heaving. I waited a few moments, then walked over and nudged her with the toe of my boot. “Priestess. Where does the stairwell lead? Will I find the Doctrine down there?”

“Perhaps,” she said, both cryptically and irritatingly. My glare made her falter, and she offered a shrug. “My lady, I’m not from this city. My knowledge of our religion’s Sanctums isn’t comprehensive enough to include every sanctum across the Kingdom. What I do know is that most Sanctums have catacombs beneath them—resting places for believers who joined the Light and escaped the Nothingness. That is where the Doctrines are held, for the catacombs are made to withstand what the rest of the world is not."

Useless woman, I thought.

I turned from her and made my way back to the stairwell, Dralos falling into step beside me.

“What’s the point of dragging her along if she knows nothing of value?” I asked the draconian, loud enough for the priestess to hear. A petty part of me hoped the jab would spark some effort, anything more than her sobbing and flailing.

“The Doctrine belongs to royalty,” Dralos replied with a shrug. “She’s here to retrieve it. Anything more is... incidental.”

I snorted. I didn’t buy that. There was something more to her inclusion—some deeper reason the Crown had assigned her to us beyond recovering a noble house’s lost scripture, no matter how sacred it was claimed to be.

The stairwell groaned beneath my feet as I descended into the darkness, the path spiraling down in tight, stone coils lit only by the faint pulse of long-faded, magic-laced hieroglyphs. We moved in silence, the only sound our footsteps echoing against the stone. My fingers traced the wall, skimming over the dimly glowing glyphs. Once, they must have shone with radiant brilliance. Now they flickered like dying embers, stubborn remnants of a forgotten art, clinging to life like a corpse refusing to rot.

“They say this writing predates even the Empire of Laxons,” Josephine said, her voice quiet yet oddly resonant in the oppressive silence. It bounced off the walls, disturbingly alive. “These glyphs tell the story of the Sun and Moon Gods…”

She kept talking, but I tuned her out. My attention shifted to the rising hum of magic and the increasing glow of the runes deeper in. Unlike those near the entrance, someone... or something... had imbued magic into these glyphs recently.

I lifted a hand and summoned a tendril of lunar energy, a white flame sparking to life on my fingertip. I held it aloft to illuminate the path. I half-expected a corpse or monster to appear, but the only thing that met me was more stone.

The air thickened the farther we went. Smoke clung to the tunnel like a second skin, soot and sulfur coating my tongue. The scents of fresh and ancient death saturated the corridor, undeniable and heavy despite there being no actual signs of battle or death.

At the base of the stairs, my boots splashed into shallow standing water. The tunnel leveled out, and I raised my makeshift torch, revealing a narrow corridor ribbed with support beams. Chains wrapped in prayer-script were strung between them. Some were broken, but most were intact, all softly humming with residual magic.

I reached out to one of the chains, curious. No ancient signature, no trace of the progenitor’s magic. Just something cold steel and a feeling of wrongness.

What are these?

A chill ran up my spine as I felt the chains suddenly begin to leech energy from my cores. I forcefully jerked my hand back, breaking whatever hold the anti-energy steel had on me.

“Careful,” Josephine said, far too late. “Sanctums use blended metals to suppress unwanted magic and energy usage.”

The tunnels twisted beneath the city like veins beneath skin—far wider and deeper than the Sanctum above. We passed blood-sealed crypts, long-abandoned meditation chambers, even a shrine to the Goddess of Light. Her statue sat pristine upon a pedestal, untouched by the ruin around it, as though time itself dared not defile her.

Still, we found no trace of death despite the oppressive smell of it.

Eventually, the passage opened into a long, low-ceilinged tunnel lined with statues—hooded figures clasping their hands in silent prayer. Despite cracks and scorch marks, their presence commanded reverence. One, in particular, drew my attention. It looked the same as the others, except for a single crystalized tear, melted down its cheek and pooled into glittering rivulets on the floor.

My steps slowed as I reached the sealed archway at the tunnel’s end.

The doorway blazed with glyphs far brighter than any we had seen, arranged in concentric rings and shimmering with increasing brilliance as I approached. The innermost ring activated first with a low, harmonic hum. Warmth radiated outward, wrapping around me like a sun’s embrace.

The other rings followed with agonizing slowness until the arch groaned open with the sound of a beast's dying wail.

A vast, circular chamber yawned before me, its ceiling lost in shadow. The walls were lined with collapsed or barren shelves, and what remained of its journals and texts spilled on the floor in chaotic order. Ash blanketed the floor and books like snow, obscuring the marble sigils beneath. And at the center of it all stood a lone plinth, untouched by time or fire. Resting atop it was a single, leather-bound tome.
