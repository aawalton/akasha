“Okay, so, here’s what I’m thinking: I summon one hundred Shades.”

Silence met Romulus’s words while he looked around at his gathered companions and Mortarius, and it was eventually the specter to whom all eyes turned expectantly. Mortarius regarded Romulus in silence for a long moment and then turned toward his companions.

“The idea is not lacking in merit,” the specter said after a moment. “The investment of Nexus Mana is substantial—it will cost him 150,000 of his current reserve, which has already been depleted by the summoning he used to bring the four of you here. Of the 1,000,000 Nexus Mana capacity, it currently contains a remaining amount of 940,000. After the Shades, it would reduce to—”

“790,000 Nexus Mana,” Corva interjected, her slender black elven brows furrowing as Mortarius nodded without umbrage at the interruption. “Sorry, Mortarius,” she said a moment later, “I’m just thinking. Are the Shades permanent or do they expire?”

“A wise concern,” Mortarius said with evident approval despite the interjection. “The Mana Nexus has varied unit durations. Golems, Lesser Revenants, and Reanimated Dead will persist. Shades will not.”

Romulus deflated slightly at the news, but Corva was undeterred.

“So animated bones and stuff persist, but Shades will fade,” the elf clarified. “Why is that?”

“Shades are not simply commanded constructs,” Mortarius answered immediately, looking from Corva to Romulus and then toward the attentive faces of Kate, Nate, and Maya. “They are willing souls tied to the authority of She of the End, summoned by the Autarch via the Mana Nexus and anchored through his connection to Her.”

“Is the Nexus the only way to attain them?” Maya asked next. “Could I pray to Her for one as a Celebrant?”

“Not as a Celebrant,” Mortarius said without reprimand, offering her a faint smile. “Death Knights and Sepulchral Hierophants could summon their service by Her leave, as can the Autarch—though Romulus remains too new to his power to sustain their summons permanently under his own strength.”

“What level do they come out as?” Kate asked, leaning forward with interest.

“Rank 10,” Mortarius said while turning to her. “Level 100.”

Nathanius let out a low whistle of appreciation, and Corva nodded again, pulling out her [Codex] and making notes with the small pen the System provided her. Her amber eyes traced the pages, then rose to look from Romulus to the throne, then back to Mortarius.

“Okay,” the warchanneler said thoughtfully. “So, in our world, I’m studying financial management. Forgive me if I start slamming you with questions, Mortarius.”

The specter, instead of looking aggrieved, appeared pleased by her noticeably more formal diction—clearly appreciating her effort to live up to his lessons on courtly comportment. “I welcome the inquisition, Corva.”

Cora grinned at him in appreciation, then made a note in her [Codex] as she glanced down.

“[Reanimate Dead], how does that one work?”

Romulus looked between them and felt momentarily managed, but Kate’s soothing hand touching the back of his neck at the exact moment he started to feel mildly aggrieved immediately relaxed him, and he settled back into her touch while the redhead flashed him a wry smile before refocusing on the others as Mortarius peered at Corva again.

“It depends on the complexity of the summoned dead. The Necropolis contains a considerable supply of usable material accrued from my reign and sealed in the mausoleums beneath its superstructure. The cost is a baseline for a single fully-assembled adult skeleton, not a complete consideration of possibility. Were Romulus of Initiate tier already rather than Novice, he could feasibly summon up to Specialist tier animated bodies. As it stands, he can only command the animated dead up to two tiers his superior before his control would be lost. In simple terms, level 74 is the maximum for an undead summon; the peak of Adept tier.”

“And a Shade comes in at level 100,” Corva observed, “but lasts temporarily instead of permanently.”

“Correct,” Mortarius affirmed. “The Nexus can support a summoning that lasts precisely 168 hours.”

“So, one week for a Shade, or a permanent skellybro at level 74...” Romulus murmured, drawing a mildly perplexed look from Mortarius at ‘skellybro’ and ignoring his mentor’s look of long-suffering unfamiliarity with gamer lingo. “So, maybe the hundred Shades isn’t as genius as I thought. The Crusade is a month away, the city is full of pissed-off Wardens and the Garrison is a thing, and I may need to keep regenerating the Shades long enough to not get invaded and killed, and I’ll probably need to keep enough of a reserve to be able to keep that going long-term, while Nate, Kate, Maya, Cora, and I do what we can to fill the Mana Nexus reserves without becoming useless.”

Mortarius nodded when he was done, and Romulus’s four friends looked at him thoughtfully before nodding among themselves as well.

“That is correct in strategic totality,” Mortarius said mildly. “You would also want to try to find new devotees in the time between now and the Crusade, as well as have enough reserve Nexus Mana to use [Dark Consecration] and convert Blackstone into a true Holy Bastion of the Dark Lady. That will grant you a far more potent defensive capability than merely praying the Necropolis will hold against anything the city sends at you.”

“The cost needs to be accommodated in regard to refreshing it, too, Dee,” Corva said while still writing in her [Codex]. “Every Shade you refresh is another 1,500 spent. You’ll want to balance that cost against the value. A bunch of Experts is going to make our lives easier, but it won’t do much against that Garrison we were talking about.”

“And I can hold a shield in front of you, brother, but I can’t fight an army at my level,” Nate added with a nod. “I’d certainly try, and you know that, but I can’t do much if someone can kick me into a wall shield or not.”

“Which an Expert certainly could,” Maya added, glancing at her boyfriend wryly.

“Oh god,” Kate said. “He has thinking face.”

Romulus gave the redhead a long-suffering glance at her words but couldn’t deny she was right. He was thinking. The Skeletons were tempting, but they were explicitly animated bones, not thinking soldiers, and he wasn’t about to pretend he had the same level of capability as Mortarius when it came to directing them. The Shades were thinking soldiers but were limited by time. It would make his life infinitely easier if he could—

His eyes snapped up to Mortarius a second later. “Can the Shades direct the Skeletons?”

Mortarius’s crimson eyes flickered with approval, and he nodded. “They can do so easily.”

Romulus’s lips peeled back enough to show his fangs with the grin, and he turned to Corva. “Fifty Shades and One Hundred Skeletons. How much?”

Corva glanced at him with a withering look and did the math near-instantly. “125,000 once-off, then 75,000 every Shade refresh, which there’s no way in hell we can replace easily.”

“I know,” Romulus accepted while his friends watched him, and he leaned forward with his forearms on his knees to look between them. “Worst case, I’ll reduce the Shade count for a renewal, but it at least gives us security after we’ve pissed off the entire city.”

“That leaves you with 815,000 Nexus Mana after the summons,” Corva replied after a moment. “If you need to push it, you could renew all fifty Shades for 75,000 at a time. How much can we recharge the Nexus per day?”

All eyes turned to Mortarius, and the former Autarch looked across them all calmly. “Your Dark Mana converts to Nexus Mana at a rate of one point per one hundred. One thousand mana refills ten points of Nexus mana, and so on. This is because of the density required for Nexus Mana to perform its function.”

“It recharges at one hundred per day, too,” Romulus noted.

“Okay, that’s a little helpful,” Corva murmured. “So, if we each put, say, three grand in three times per day, for seven days...” she trailed off while doing the calculation. “We would refill 3,850 Nexus Mana per week when including the regeneration.” Her eyes lifted to Mortarius and Romulus and then returned to her [Codex] as if it had betrayed her. “That is such a bullshit conversion rate.”

“The System does not balance for the minority population, Corva,” Mortarius replied calmly. “A Mana Nexus is usually sustained by a nation, not a bereft group of struggling Novices.”

“You really suck at pep talks, Morty,” Kate said with a snort, while Mayarella and Nate laughed.

“And only Dark Mana counts for the refill,” Romulus murmured. “That means that even recruiting a bunch of other Travelers may not necessarily do what we need.”

“Correct,” Mortarius said in an unruffled tone despite Kate’s words. “You will need a powerful following for it to matter.”

“Good to know,” Romulus muttered while sighing, earning a sympathetic look from Maya. “Alright, well, there’s my proposal: Fifty Shades, a cool one hundred Skeletons, and hopes and dreams until we can find a way to get more help. Thoughts?”

His friends looked at one another, considering the matter, and then glanced collectively at Corva, who gave them a shrug to show her concurrence. Nate nodded and then spoke, resting his hands on his new shield. “It’s a good start. We may have to reassess as the weeks go by, but it’s a solid security force, and I’m guessing Mortarius has some awareness of what happens in the Necropolis, so he can warn us of people approaching.”

“I do and can,” the specter agreed with a look of faint surprise. “How did you deduce that?”

“Dee—that is, Romulus, told us about you opening and closing the doors with your hand. I extrapolated that you probably have some control, given you built the place.”

Mortarius looked approving at the deduction. “A worthy observation. You are correct. I can warn you if someone crosses the outer bailey, and I can extend my senses across the city using the buried wards and enchantments that persist from my reign. Much of Blackstone is ruined, but my awareness persists.”

“Could I learn that?” Romulus asked immediately, realizing how useful it could be.

“No,” Mortarius said without bothering to sugarcoat it. “It is derived from my part in building Blackstone. You will gain an awareness of the Necropolis itself, in time, due to your attunement with the Mana Nexus—the city at large will not offer that certainty, unless the Lady makes a very dramatic alteration during the [Dark Consecration].”

“Good to know,” Romulus said, while Lightsbane pulsed in support. “That said, though, anyone else want to weigh in on the Shades?”

“I say go for it,” Kate supplied primly. “More eyes can’t hurt.”

“I agree with Nate,” Maya said afterward, nodding after Kate spoke. “We could use the security while we train. You and Kay already told us about the fact that we can gain experience with duels here under the Lady’s protection. I’d like to know we’re being protected while we do so.”

“Yeah, we’re gonna need the practice,” Nate agreed.

Romulus met each of their eyes afterward and then, after a final incline of the head from Mortarius, took a breath and brought up his [Nexus Interface] with a flex of will. His eyes scanned the options until he found [Reanimate Dead] and [Summon Shade], and he tapped both, specifying the numbers with another flex of will.

“Alright,” he murmured half to himself. “Here goes nothing.”

Romulus activated the expenditure, and a System screen appeared before him immediately.

SYSTEM MESSAGE

Mana Nexus command received.

50 x Shade will be Summoned for 75,000 Mana.

100 x Skeleton Warrior will be Reanimated for 50,000 Mana.

Do you wish to proceed?

YES

NO

Romulus confirmed it with a tap of his finger, and a new screen appeared immediately.

SYSTEM MESSAGE

Input confirmed.

Proceeding with Summons . . .

Proceeding with Reanimation . . .

Awareness flooded his mind almost as soon as the System screen vanished following its statement of action. It wasn’t like the Thrallbond with Bonnie; he knew that immediately—that was more of a vague understanding of where she was, which presently meant still sitting with Sara in her cell. It was also very different from the persistent warmth of Lightsbane’s own tether to his spirit. This felt more like a sudden expansion of awareness; an awakening of attention across one hundred distinct presences as they assembled themselves into existence deep within the bowels of the Necropolis.

He could feel bones rising, Nexus Mana suffusing them with Dark potency, and completed bodies rising to march to waiting, ancient armories and grip appropriate accouterment. Romulus let out a breath as the awareness settled into his mind, and a simple skeletal icon appeared above his Hemoforce in his HUD, displaying a clean “100” beside the icon.

“Okay,” he murmured, “that’s cool. I can feel the Skeletons.”

“Direct them with your will, not words,” Mortarius said with steadying certainty.

Romulus did so, ordering the Skeletons to assemble in the throne room and feeling the responsive affirmation from the soulless constructs. If he closed his eyes, he was sure he’d be able to picture them moving.

“I didn’t get a Sk—” Romulus cut off mid-complaint about the lack of [Necromancy] at the same moment as darkness abruptly began to gather ahead of the throne. His crimson eyes widened slightly and Kate gripped his arm while his friends turned, falling still as their eyes fell on the same sight. The darkness spread swiftly, separating into fifty distinct pools that rose upward into columns of black energy. Each column compressed rapidly, forming a vaguely humanoid shape faintly threaded through by blue fire.

The Lichfire torches within the throne room bent toward them as they started to solidify and settle into more distinctive shapes, forming arms, legs, helmets, armor, and weapons. One resolved into a powerfully broad warrior of significant height wielding a heavy axe, another settled into the shape of a woman with a longsword in each hand, and another came in the shape of a gnome with two wicked-looking translucent daggers. The armor was not universal; it differed in cut, ornament, ostentation, and material—and faces started appearing with distinct features including scars, unique haircuts, and even the suggestion of skin tone.

They were all faintly transparent with a dark cast to the energy comprising their body, but they remained distinct enough that each pair of faintly glowing eyes met his with intelligence and awareness both. Despite their lack of full opacity, their boots settled firmly onto the throne room floor as they finished forming, weapons coming to a disciplined rest or entering sheathes as they looked around at their environment.

Behind them, distantly, the first mechanical clatter of marching skeletons filled the Necropolis, and Romulus swallowed as the foremost among them stepped forward; his right hand settled on the hilt of an elegant broadsword, with a large kite shield slung over the back of his black-cloaked armor. He was tall, perhaps 6’2, attired in darkly ornate warplate reminiscent of Nathanius’s own, with a weathered face that looked to be around forty years of age. His right eyebrow held a pale scar that cut down over his eye, though the orb within the affected socket shone with the same light as the rest of the Shades’.

The Shade turned his attention first to Mortarius, bowing deeply in recognition, and then turned back to Romulus. He assessed the Revenant-King for a moment and then drew his blade, placing it tip-down before him and sinking to one knee. Around him, the remainder of the Shades did the same, planting their weapons or drawing them to do so, while the foremost Shade spoke in a deep, faintly resonant voice that carried within the Throne Room.

“Hail, Revenant-King,” he intoned formally. “You have called for service, and by the blessing of She of the End, we stand in answer to Her Autarch.”

Kate’s hand on his arm tightened, and Romulus reached up to touch it before rising from his throne, Lightsbane clutched in his right hand and vibrating with eagerness. He stepped forward from the throne, looked across the kneeling Shades, and forced himself not to laugh in wonder.

“I welcome you, honorable Darksworn,” he said after a moment, remembering the term supplied from one of Mortarius’s lessons. “My name is Romulus. With me are my closest companions. Your answer to Her call honors me. There is a great deal for us to do, a very large Necropolis to protect, and an entire city seeking our heads. I hope that won’t be, uh, a problem.”

He almost winced at his hesitation at the end, but the Shade who greeted him simply smiled warmly, looking up from where he knelt. Around him, the others similarly looked up, and Romulus saw a mix of approval, amusement, and faint anticipation cross over fifty translucent faces.

“Then we have been summoned at the right time,” the foremost Shade said steadily. “I am Decius Tyran Vallius. As in life, so in immortal death, I pledge to your aid my blade and the fealty of my soul, under the permission of She of the End. We shall serve you, Romulus, Revenant-King, Autarch of the Dark Lady. While She yet grants us form and purpose, we shall stand the watch at your gates, defy the incursion of your enemies, and uphold Her Dark Faith beneath your standard. We stand ready to hear your command, Revenant-King. Tell us for whom Death is owed.”
