I lie sleeplessly in my bed that night. I’m exhausted and really need the time to recuperate, but thoughts circle inside my head ceaselessly.

A shape moves near me and, trapped in my spiral of worry, I startle badly. A moment later, a flicker of reassurance brushes through our Bond and I recognise the shape as Bastet. She climbs up on the bed and lies down next to me.

For all that this cabin and bed are definitely the most luxurious on the ship, it’s still small by most standards – a queen-size rather than a king – and having a large winged panther lie next to me requires me to shuffle close to the bulkhead wall. And since she wants to cuddle, she lies so she’s pressing against me, even when I make room for her. I still appreciate her warmth, and not only the physical heat she emanates.

You’re thinking too loudly, she tells me with an exasperated fondness in her mental voice. Mama Bastet is clearly out to play. What’s wrong?

I just…I fear that we’re making a mistake here, I confess. The thoughts circling around in my head slow their spiral when I voice that. Ranad Murran indicated that the longer we wait, the harder it will be to avoid whatever is waiting for us. Yet we’re just…going with the flow. Literally.

Then let us leave, Bastet replies, as if it’s that simple.

We can’t, I sigh, thumping my head against the pillow and staring sightlessly at the dark ceiling. In this case, we’re the weakest link. Everyone who can has been helping – fetching, supporting, pulling ropes…but our steering is still going to take a couple of days to fix. And though the foremast and mizzenmast might be stabilised by tomorrow, that will only give us half our sailing capacity. As for the mainmast…that’s a major job, even with magic. Artio has been helping immensely, but growing dead wood isn’t his speciality, so he’ll have to grow a new mast from a seed, and that takes time. And time is what we don’t have.

Bastet looks at me patiently, a comforting rumble in her chest soothing my agitation.

Then it sounds like we cannot do anything other than what we are doing, she concludes.

I grimace.

Except Valence did have a suggestion – others could tow us out. But I know already that Josiah and Elarion aren’t keen on the idea – within the first forty-eight hours of the roc’s death, at least. The resources are just too valuable. And I can’t deny that I feel that way myself. It’s a Tier four. And we were lucky to survive it. Who knows when something like this will happen again?

You spoke with the magic humans all afternoon. Did they not offer any thoughts that might help? Bastet asks, not judging, but just gazing calmly at me. The touch of moonlight that enters the cabin through the bay windows at the back turns her eyes silver and reflective.

I give a derisive snort.

Them? They can’t stop arguing about what’s happening, let alone help me to make a decision.

Since all the ships are lashed to the carcass of the roc at relatively close distances, the longboats have been in very active use – either helping to move resources back and forth from the roc, or transporting people to and from different ships. My ship has been a revolving door of mages coming and going – and not only them.

That’s actually been another practical benefit to continuing to stay in one place: we’ve been able to share specialist craftspeople across the fleet making the repair process more efficient. I wish that we had a few more Classer Wood-Shapers – a single one over the whole of the fleet really isn’t enough. Then again, if Elarion hadn’t brought the Carpenter, we wouldn’t even have him, so small mercies, I suppose. Though, at least the non-Classer carpenters like Loren’s master are helping too. They’re just significantly slower.

The roc has definitely been sinking bit by bit, but the mages can’t decide whether that’s the current’s work, or the slow escape of magic from the carcass. Some of them say that there’s magic in the current, others disagree. Some of those who think there’s magic in it are determined that it’s wrapping more tightly around our ships, but even then there’s no consensus about how or why or what the effects might be. Definitely no ideas about what might happen if we allow it to continue or how to avoid being trapped if that’s what it’s doing.

I sigh angrily, feeling like pulling my hair out.

Perhaps Bastet feels my urge, as she shifts so she’s lying over my chest, pinning my arms down.

Hey, you’re heavy, I half-complain, more for appearances than anything else, but I stroke the soft feather-fur on her belly nonetheless. The rumbling in her chest increases in strength with her amusement and it soothes away my frustration.

Can you see the magic? she asks when I’ve relaxed a little.

No, I sigh. I’ve kept looking, but…it just looks normal to me.

And what did that grey-furred mage say? The one you spent most time with?

Oh, Vellus? I shake my head. All he can tell me is that the current definitely isn’t natural. But that doesn’t mean much. There are lots of reasons for why a current might not be natural – some dangerous to us, some not. And even some of those that are dangerous to us would be better met with more repaired ships, which happens faster if we’re not trying to sail anywhere….

Bastet is silent for a long moment, though the rumbling in her chest continues. It’s surprisingly soothing, actually. And I find it’s helped to actually voice my thoughts even if I haven’t come to any conclusions about what to do.

Is there anything you can do now that would be more useful than sleeping? she asks finally.

I hesitate before answering. Because there’s always more to be done. I could be using my Flesh-Shaping to help harvest more of the roc – since we’re under time pressure, teams are out there now, just as some of the crafters continue to work on the quieter repair tasks. Aingeal is happy to burn brightly as a false sun for those outside to easily work.

I could also be trying to learn Wood-Shaping – I’ve never succeeded before, but that doesn’t mean I won’t succeed this time. I might even be able to get some instruction from the Carpenter to help with that, though he’s asleep right now as far as I know.

Or I could help the healers get the injured back on their feet – though there are few of those left now – or a myriad of other tasks both pressing and not.

But…I need sleep. My higher Constitution might mean that I can endure periods of wakefulness for longer without losing my higher cognitive functions, but with everything that’s happened, I can feel the cognitive load. Sleep and dreams are still important for processing recent events.

No, I admit finally. I don’t have anything better to do.

Then sleep, Bastet tells me firmly. I am here with you. And true to her words, she doesn’t shift, but only settles her weight more firmly on me, rumbling harder in contentment.

Whether it’s the warm weight of her on my chest, or the soothing rumble, or even the calming presence she projects down the Bond, I feel my eyelids drooping.

Before I even realise it, I’m asleep.

“Lord Markus!”

The voice breaks my concentration and I open my eyes to look at the intruder in annoyance. It’s one of the younger slave children who has been put to work running messages and small items. He recoils from my expression and I quickly soften it – these children have been through enough without fearing the anger of the fleet leader.

“Yes, what is it Ronul?” He blinks, perhaps surprised that I know his name, but quickly regains his control.

“My lord, Mage Vellus is asking for you to come. As soon as you can, he says.”

“Where is he?”

“In the longboat, my lord. Down to the left of the ship. I mean, to port, sir.”

I nod at him.

“Please tell him I’ll be there shortly.” He quickly dips a deep bow, then hurries off again at a breakneck pace.

Glancing at the Carpenter with whom I’ve been working, I sent him an apologetic expression.

“It seems duty calls me elsewhere,” I sigh. “Would you mind if I repeat this when I find a moment? I’m finding it incredibly educational watching you work.”

“Of course, my lord,” the carpenter agrees easily, if gruffly. “I’ll continue working on this mast, and then move onto the mizzen. When your beast is done growing the main, I’ll come and finish that off too.”

“Excellent,” I agree, then nod to him and move towards where Ronul indicated Mage Vellus to be – the boy himself is nowhere to be seen. On the way, I pass by Artio. The owlbear is concentrating hard – growing a tree from a seed isn’t an easy task to accomplish. On my way, I brush my hand over him and feed him some magic. I haven’t learned Magic Share in the way I have Health Share, but experiments have proven that at least some of the magic I press into one of my companions is available for their use. I have faith that a bit more experimentation will earn me the Skill and a more efficient way of sharing my mana.

Joining Vellus in the longboat takes only a few seconds, most of which are spent growing my air-wings. I land lightly in the boat that’s almost bumping against the side of the main ship, it’s so close. The mage is the only occupant and looks up when my landing makes the boat rock.

“Lord Markus,” he greets, though I notice the lines of worry around his eyes.

“Mage Vellus,” I return, then get down to business. “Ronul told me you needed to speak with me urgently. What is it?”

Mage Vellus doesn’t waste any time either.

“The current has shifted,” he tells me bluntly.

I go cold.

“Shifted,” I repeat blankly. “How?”

In answer, he takes a wooden rod out of his Inventory. He places it on the water. I frown at it – nothing seems different about that.

Frowning at it, I observe how it’s bobbing with the waves, moving normally.

Then, a chill goes down my spine as I realise that first impressions are deceptive. Because it’s not normal for an object to stay equidistant from everything else in water. The breeze that’s blowing isn’t shifting its course; no cross currents are bringing it closer to the boat or further away. It’s just…bobbing.

Mage Vellus obviously notices that I have understood the implications and nods grimly.

“But that isn’t the worst of it,” he warns. Taking out a pair of tongs, he picks up the wooden rod.

My breath quickens as I see that what should have been a simple task, is anything but. The water clings to the wooden rod in ways that it really shouldn’t. It appears more like thick honey or syrup than water, for all that it looks identical on the surface.

But Vellus succeeds in getting the rod free of the water and the moment he does, the water left on the rod drips off it easily like normal water rather than honey. I still eye the rod cautiously.

“It’s safe,” Vellus tells me, running his own hand over the rod to prove his assertion. “It only seems to be an issue when something is in contact with the greater body of water.”

I close my eyes and activate my magic sight, looking more deeply than I ever have before. After what feels like an eternity, I open them a moment later, utter confusion filling me.

“There’s no magic in it,” I comment uncertainly.

“No, there isn’t,” Vellus replies grimly. “Which is more than a little worrying. Especially since the water wasn’t doing this yesterday.”

“Yes,” I agree. “Because then the question is if it isn’t magic causing this issue…what is?” A sinking feeling goes through me. And if the current holds on so tightly to a simple wooden rod…how tightly will it hold onto our fleet?
