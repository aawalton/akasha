Archie blinked.

The world around him disappeared, not unlike every other time when he’d met with the System Aspect.

The cabin, the warm spring breeze, the soft, distant chirps of birds, even the faint hum of the boundary traps Henry had laid down after keying him to the wards around his home, were gone.

It was just him, standing alone in a dark room, the very polar opposite of every other place where he’d met with the System Aspect.

He instinctively summoned a small tuft of mana in his palm, letting the warm blue glow hover just above the skin of his palm. But even that light didn’t reflect on anything else other than himself.

“Weird,” His voice came out hoarse when he spoke, as if even sound itself had been swallowed by the room. Double weird.

“…System Aspect?” he asked, already knowing the answer. No response.

Then a flicker.

A single light snapped on before him as a System Notification appeared right before him.

Tutorial Event II: Isekai Trials

Tutorial Event II will take place in a real world with a personalized Questline tailored to you to mimic the classic Earth genre “Isekai” from Universe 72. Each Participant has been assigned a custom Questline, built dynamically based on personality matrices, past Tutorial performance, and current Grade compatibility.

Participants will awaken in a new world; environmental conditions will vary per Participant and the quest type chosen. Quests, side-objectives, and world-state interactions will influence final ranking, rewards, and survival rates. Upon completing various stages of your Main Questline and the various Side Quests that appear along your Journey, you will gain Tutorial Points.

Said Tutorial Points can be used in the System Bazaar that appears after the conclusion of Tutorial Event II and your Second Tutorial Scoring.

Definition: "Isekai" – from Earth’s Japanese: 'Different World'. Typically involves an individual being transported or reincarnated into another world, often with specialized abilities or unique destinies.

Completion Criteria: Successfully complete your Personal Main Questline.

Time Limit: 2 years

WARNING: The world is not static. Choices and influence will alter both environment and consequences.

“FUCK!” Archie shouted, fists clenching. “Fuck! Fuck! We are so fucked! Not to mention I gotta be doing this for two years straight!”

He began pacing, though technically it felt like walking in place in every direction because the floor wasn’t a floor but more of a hard… concept.

“This is bad. This is really bad. Isekai?” he groaned, yanking his hair back as he mentally flipped through every anime, novel, and fever dream that fit the genre. “This could be anything, a slice-of-life bread-baking sim in a chill kingdom with mild tax drama? Sure. Or maybe a city-builder world. God, I suck at RTS games, I always end up nuking my own damn people by accident because I screw up the tech tree or end up making them all cyborg cannibals!”

He stared down at the flickering screen, now quietly pulsing with two more panels he hadn’t noticed before.

“Okay. Okay,” Archie said to himself while letting out a deep breath. “It’s not gonna be the dystopia hellhole where it's going to be an intergalactic warlike setting that involves a god emperor with a crap load of sons fighting against thousands of intergalactic nonhuman species. Don’t even think about that one.”

He waved his hand to dismiss the warning screen, forcing himself to focus as the next two notifications blinked into view:

System Event II: Personalization Protocol InitiatedPlease choose your Primary Focus for the Isekai Questline:

[Class] – Your Main Quest will be focused around your current Class.[Profession] – Your Main Quest will be focused around your current Profession.

WARNING: This choice cannot be changed later. However, you are able to browse through the options offered to you prior to locking in your choice.

Archie hesitated.

He reached out with a grimace and tapped [Profession] first.

The system obliged with a mechanical hum that he felt in his teeth. Then the list expanded. And expanded. And kept expanding.

“Oh man,” he muttered, scrolling through what had to be at least a thousand entries. Everything from LONE SMITH to SMITH OF THE PEOPLE to FLOWER SMITH. What the hell even was a Flower Smith? How was that related to what he did?

His eyes caught on three that didn’t immediately make him want to vomit.

LONE SMITH: A reclusive blacksmith in training residing in a dusty, half-forgotten border town in the <Unchosen> Kingdom. You have mostly kept to yourself after the death of your master and desire to spread your wings to the outside world.

BUDDING SMITH: A smith fresh off the wagon and countryside and just arrived in the capital of <Unchosen>. You are seeking to open up your own shop and reach the top, where the competition is fierce and unyielding. Most of the older smiths who’d been alive and have honed their forging for generations do not take kindly to newcomers and will use all they have at their disposal to get rid of you if you are perceived as a threat, even willing to resort to politics to try and get your business removed. Expect politics, forging, and a growing business in a place filled with business sharks who seek to take advantage of you.

REVOLUTIONIST SMITH: You’ve pledged your hammer and anvil to the revolutionary army after a noble of the Kingdom of <Unchosen> had killed your family after you refused to become their smith. You seek to arm and build said revolutionary army to take back control of the Kingdom of <Unchosen>. Expect violence, politics, difficult moral decisions, and constant pressure from both enemies and allies.

“Lone Smith reminds me of a generic start from one of the mods I used to play on a game that I haven’t touched in years,” Archie muttered, a bit lost in thought, lost in the nostalgia of playing said game and the various adventures he’d gone on and dragons he’d killed while playing it.

“Can’t go wrong with a generic start,” Archie shrugged. “Because it’s a classic, no goals in mind other than throwing myself out there with no guard rails. Which sounds like the exact opposite of Budding Smith and Revolutionist Smith.”

“Budding Smith sounds perfect for me to experience how it is to run a genuine business around smithing, which was something I’ve been thinking about ever since accepting my first commission…” Archie said thoughtfully. “I’d really like to get some experience in running a business so I can set one up out in the Multiverse, and this sounds like a great way to learn how to do it.”

Archie looked thoughtful for a bit before letting out a chuckle as a certain thought crossed his mind. “Is this what some of the companies I’ve seen back on Earth meant by requiring two to five years of work experience at a minimum to apply for an entry-level job?”

“… The only downside of choosing Budding Smith seems to be that it's just involved in a crapload of politics, judging by the last sentence, and I honestly can’t arsed to deal with politics. I’d rather just have a quaint shop in the middle of buttfuck nowhere and just post my wares and stuff on the internet… Aetherglass and take commissions online and have them pick it up when it's done.”

“And since I wouldn’t be tied down by anything other than completing commissions, I could head out on adventures whenever I wanted. Plus, I wouldn’t have to pay taxes to anyone," Archie said with a grin.

“Revolutionist Smith sounds pretty similar to Budding Smith in a sense where I’ll be forced to deal with politics, maybe even double the politics, considering that I’d probably have an above-ground business in the kingdom and an underground one with the Revolutionary army.” Archie considered before deciding to shelve both Budding Smith and Revolutionist Smith and moved to click on the [Class] option to see its options.

Once again, hundreds of choices began pouring in, and his immediate reaction was to lean back and mutter, “Oh, for fuck’s sake.”

He flicked his hand and let the options roll past him, and the ones he’d seen were even weirder than what he saw for his Profession options. Some were batshit weird, like Spirit Orphan, Brawler of the Corner, and Battlelusting Prizefighter.

“Like what the fuck are these options?” Archie said to himself out loud. “Like I get that there are thousands of options, but… my god, I’m gonna need a minute in order to get through all this batshit weird shit that I’m seeing right now.”

But like before, three options stuck out to him right away.

YOUNG ASPIRANT: You’ve just reached the age of maturity and wish to leave your border town, where you grew up in the middle of nowhere, behind you. With little more than a few coppers to your name, you aim to carve out a future and have legends told in your name.

ADVENTURER: You are a member of the Adventurers Guild on the planet of Kerak. You seek to explore the world and detail all of what you have seen on your various adventures back to the guild. The more you explore, the more renown you gain—and the more dangerous your missions become. Expect high travel, danger, occasional bounties, and very little downtime.

REVOLUTIONIST SOLDIER: You’ve grown disgusted with the kingdom you’ve been raised in. After the mass execution of your village when you were out hunting in the glades of another nearby village, you’ve joined the revolutionary forces fighting to reclaim the Kingdom of <Unchosen>. Revenge is what you strive for, and you won’t stop until everyone involved in the death of your family is dead. Expect violence, politics, difficult moral decisions, and constant pressure from both enemies and allies.

Archie let out a low whistle as he reread the Revolutionist Soldier prompt. “Right, so we’re not exactly dealing with happy-go-lucky ‘let’s go make friends in magic school’ vibes here.”

“… weird now that I think about it, I don’t even think I got an option about attending a magical school, those were normally all the rage in isekai communities over the past couple of years,” Archie mumbled to himself. “Small mercies, I guess, I’d probably drop out week one and go off and do my own thing if I were forced to choose that option.”

“Young Aspirant is just like Lone Smith in the regard that it reminds me of a game, but in this case, a fuck load of games in how it sounds,” Archie smirked. “A generic setup in which a kid wants to strike out on his own and go out into the world and explore it for the thrill of it all and have his Journey told in legends. Sounds pretty much up my alley, but I do wonder how the Questline for his prompt will be like, cause it looks like it’ll just be random and eventually build on itself once I do enough, or it’ll give generic prompts until something major happens.”

“Adventurer sounds too similar to what I’ve been already doing as a member of Omniversal Solutions, so I guess that could be an option I’d be down for as I can kinda rig the process and when I return back after the Tutorial Event ends I can just drop off the stuff I’ve discovered, documented, and recorded to an Omniversal Solutions higher up like Paleos so I can rake in rewards outside the Tutorial Event with the stuff I’ve done inside it.”

Not to mention the Adventurer’s prompt seems to have given me the name of the planet, Kerak, or at least one of the planets this whole Tutorial Event takes place on.

“And just like Revolutionist Smith, Revolutionist Soldier, sounds interesting and probably has a pretty intriguing story around it,” Archie muttered while rereading the prompt. “But I don’t like being ordered around so that’s also a no-go.”

With only Lone Smith and Adventurer left out of the six he'd been considering, he found himself stuck, unsure which path to take.

“Are there any other options I can look at?” he asked, scanning the list of options for both Class and Profession again.
