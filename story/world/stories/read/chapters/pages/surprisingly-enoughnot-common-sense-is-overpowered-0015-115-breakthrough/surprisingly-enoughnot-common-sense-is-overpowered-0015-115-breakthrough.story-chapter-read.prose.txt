Ning lit a stick of spiritual incense and placed it beside him.

During the Foundation Building senior's lecture, he'd heard an interesting explanation of meditation.

"Meditation is the process of locking the monkey mind; doing so leads to enlightenment. Tethering the will leads to transformation into a dragon."

Ning had interpreted it in simpler terms. Meditation was about restraining the restless mind and maintaining one's focus without distractions. Once the mind was firmly anchored, deeper cultivation could begin.

In short, it was all about concentration.

[Qi Condensation: 1st Stage (99/100)]

After more than three months of diligent cultivation, he had finally reached the threshold of the second stage.

A faint fragrance spread through the room as the incense burned, helping him settle his thoughts. It had been crafted by a Spiritual Incense Master, one of the more obscure professions in the cultivation world, specializing in incense with different spiritual properties.

This particular blend was said to improve meditation efficiency.

Ning had purchased two sticks, and since he was preparing for a breakthrough, he figured this was a good opportunity to use one.

Breaking through from the first stage of Qi Condensation to the second was fairly straightforward in theory. The Foundation Building senior had already explained the process in detail, so Ning knew what he needed to do.

He took out a Red Bud Pill, swallowed it, and settled into the lotus position before beginning to circulate the Pure Qi Sutra.

Unlike ordinary cultivation, advancing to the next stage required reversing the flow of spiritual qi in a controlled rhythm.

The process involved slowly drawing a portion of qi away from the dantian before rapidly forcing it back, repeating the movement until the accumulated pressure expanded the Qi Hai acupoint and loosened the bottleneck.

The Qi Hai acupoint was located about an inch and a half below the navel, along the midline of the abdomen.

Thanks to his daily control exercises, Ning was able to regulate the flow with steady precision. Each cycle brought a little more pressure to the bottleneck, and after roughly fifteen minutes, he felt a faint swelling sensation in his dantian.

It was working.

Ning gathered another portion of qi and reversed its flow, gradually increasing the pressure with each repetition. The spiritual energy surged through his meridians, and after several more cycles, a subtle tremor spread through his dantian as the bottleneck finally gave way.

[Qi Condensation: 1st Stage → 2nd Stage (Consolidating)]

Ning continued circulating the Pure Qi Sutra, allowing the newly expanded flow of spiritual energy to settle before slowly opening his eyes.

"Well... that was underwhelming."

He'd been expecting something more dramatic, like a sudden eruption of spiritual energy or a flash of light. At the very least, something with a little more oomph!

Instead, he'd simply sat there for a while, circulated his qi, and broken through.

Well, whatever. Being low-key suited him just fine.

Ning smiled and turned his attention to the changes within his body.

His spiritual energy reserves had nearly doubled, and although his physical strength hadn't increased dramatically, his body felt noticeably lighter.

The strengthening of the body during Qi Condensation was a gradual process, with spiritual energy slowly nourishing the flesh over time. It couldn't compare to the immediate physical improvements achieved through dedicated Body Cultivation.

At the early stages, cultivation was mostly a matter of accumulating spiritual energy anyway. With decent spiritual roots and enough resources, reaching the middle stages was generally achievable through steady cultivation.

Still, Ning was more than satisfied with his progress.

"Finally, I'm no longer a one-pump chump."

Previously, even with his precise control, he could only cast the Small Cloud Rain Technique at Minor Accomplishment once before exhausting his reserves. Now that his spiritual energy had nearly doubled, he could practice for much longer without constantly stopping to recover.

Ning glanced toward the green fields outside, a smile spreading across his face.

"Now then, it's harvesting time."

...

With the arrival of the spiritual grain harvesting season, the usually peaceful fields along the riverbanks became bustling with activity.

Spiritual farmers who ordinarily spent only part of their day tending their crops could now be seen working from morning until evening, carefully inspecting their fields and preparing for the harvest.

Unlike the sect's more valuable fields, which were situated directly above spiritual veins, these ordinary plots lacked a sufficient natural supply of spiritual energy to sustain their crops.

The farmers had to make up for that deficiency themselves, using techniques such as Small Cloud Rain to replenish spiritual energy and other farming spells to maintain the soil.

In the days leading up to the harvest, Ning barely dared to leave his field unattended.

Thousands of birds had gathered around the nearby lake, waiting for an opportunity to swoop down and feast on the ripening grain. Even a brief lapse in attention could result in a painful loss.

Wealthier spiritual farmers, such as Old Zhou, could afford protective puppets to guard their fields.

Ning, meanwhile, had constructed a scarecrow out of straw. The birds had figured out his ingenious defense after only a few attempts, leaving him with no choice but to guard the field himself.

By the time the harvest was finally over, Ning felt as though he'd spent more time chasing birds than cultivating.

"Finally, it's over."

Adjusting the straw hat on his head, he finished weighing the last batch of grain and checked his calculations.

The total harvest came to 120 kilograms.

"Not bad!"

Ning couldn't help smiling at the result.

Under ideal conditions, a skilled spiritual farmer could produce three to four hundred kilograms of grain per acre in a single season. Ning was still a beginner, but his careful management and growing proficiency with farming spells had allowed him to achieve a respectable yield from the land under his care.

The sect purchased first-grade spiritual grain at around one spirit stone per kilogram, with slight adjustments depending on quality. Ning's grain was of excellent quality, but after the sect took its seventy-five percent tax, he'd be left with only thirty kilograms to sell.

Still, thirty spirit stones were nothing to scoff at for a newcomer who'd spent the past three months working the fields.

Just as he was admiring the results of his hard work, Fang Zhu arrived.

"Junior Brother Ning, how's the harvest?"

Ning, who had been resting on a stool, quickly stood up to greet him.

"Senior Brother Fang, you're just in time. The harvest turned out quite well!"

He gestured toward the bags of grain stacked nearby.

"Take a look. I think you'll be pleasantly surprised."

Fang Zhu approached the bags and lifted one to judge its weight before inspecting the grain inside.

"About 120 kilograms in total? Junior Brother, that's impressive."

Ning was equally impressed by how casually Fang Zhu had lifted the heavy bag.

Damn, I didn't know Senior Brother Fang was built like that.

Of course, he kept that thought to himself.

"It's thanks to Senior Brother Zhou's guidance," Ning replied with a smile. "I also had some knowledge of farming before joining the sect."

He wasn't lying.

As a qualified keyboard warrior who'd spent plenty of time surfing the internet, Ning had picked up a surprising amount of random knowledge in his previous life. His textbooks had also covered some basic agricultural principles.

He understood concepts such as crop rotation and proper irrigation, even if he'd never had much practical experience applying them.

Old Zhou's guidance had helped him adapt that knowledge to spiritual farming, teaching him how different soils affected crop growth and how to manage the field more efficiently.

He was still far from being an expert, but those little advantages had made a difference.

It would've been even better if he could achieve perfect recall and recover more of the knowledge buried in his memories.

Alas, I'll have to wait until my cultivation improves.

"You did?" Fang Zhu looked surprised.

He still remembered Ning's pale complexion when they'd first met. The young man certainly hadn't looked like someone accustomed to working in the fields.

"Yes." Ning nodded before gesturing toward the grain. "By the way, Senior Brother, I'd like to sell you my remaining harvest after taxes."

Fang Zhu was currently his most reliable option. According to Old Zhou, the man had plenty of connections and could sell surplus spiritual grain without much difficulty.

"Of course." Fang Zhu examined the grain again before beginning his calculations. "The sect takes seventy-five percent as tax, leaving you with thirty kilograms. Since the quality is excellent, I can offer you one spirit stone per kilogram."

"That makes thirty spirit stones," Ning answered.

Fang Zhu nodded and retrieved the payment from his storage pouch.

"That's right. Here you go, Junior Brother."

Ning accepted the spirit stones and looked down at them with satisfaction.

Compared to the amount he'd previously borrowed, it wasn't much, but this time, every stone had been earned through his own efforts.

More importantly, spiritual grain was only a low-grade first-tier crop. Once his farming techniques improved, he could attempt to cultivate more valuable spiritual plants and earn far greater profits.

His recent breakthrough to the second stage of Qi Condensation would also allow him to manage his field more efficiently, opening up the possibility of cultivating a larger area.

For now, though, Ning was content with his first proper earnings.

It ain't much, but it's honest work.
