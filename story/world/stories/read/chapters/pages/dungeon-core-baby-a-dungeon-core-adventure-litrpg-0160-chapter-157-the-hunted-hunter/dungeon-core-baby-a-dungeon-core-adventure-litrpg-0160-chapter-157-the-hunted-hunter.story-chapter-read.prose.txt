Sir Calda

Shortly after Charlie and the others arrived at Fort Stonehaven

He ordered them to pick the pace up as they got closer to the forest edge. The beasts that Sir Thespin had driven off had been quick to retreat into the woods. But that didn’t mean they weren’t dangerous. It’s likely they’d abandoned their preferred territory in desperation even after their prey escaped the forest itself. But here within the woods, they would be more confident. More aggressive.

He silently pointed to four men, motioning for two of them to spread out to the right, and the other two to move to the left. They nodded and disappeared into the foliage. Behind him, seven others fell in line with him at the front. These men were his. Loyal to him. He’d picked them because they were capable, and they served him because they believed he was their path to the Twelve. One day he would take Brandt’s spot, and these men wanted to sit at that table beside him. None of them had a scarlet hex of searching technique, but that didn’t matter; they could track beasts on their own. Especially ones as large as Thespin and Obie had described. Something like that would leave a trail. He’d follow it all the way back to their nest and slaughter every one of them.

He surveyed the woods. The forest was quiet; whatever was lingering around here had scared off the local wildlife. The beast may be staring at them even now. He didn’t draw his weapon yet. He could wrench it out of its sheath fast enough to deal with whatever threat he faced, but he was wary of it on his waist. Muscle memory would make drawing the powerful blade easy. It would also make using it to butcher his prey as simple as chopping wood. Not that he hadn’t done his fair share of that for Aveo lately.

“Sir! I’ve got footprints over here,” one of his knights called out.

Calda nodded. Now it was only a matter of time.

One day later

They’d set out the day prior, following tracks that seemed promising enough, but now? It was as if the trail had disappeared into thin air. There were no signs they’d taken to the trees. Now he was wondering if they weren’t beasts at all, but hex beasts. A more dangerous variety of beast with the power to use magic and reason like humans. That would explain how they’d disappear into nothingness. But if they had magic, would they really have abandoned the fight with Thespin so easily? So many things weren’t sitting right with him.

A new thought was bubbling in his mind, and his attention shifted to it as they moved through the forest, trying to pick up the trail. Was this really his priority right now? That child, there’d been something off about it. Brandt had invested so much in locating this child, and yet, Brandt’s second in command couldn’t be bothered to check a baby for suspicious signs? It didn’t make any sense. But there might be an opportunity in this. The creatures wouldn’t leave the forest for now, and the fort was on lockdown. The storm would likely attract them to the fort, but it would attract hundreds of other creatures as well. Calda could deal with them then. In the meantime…

“Halt,” he called out.

“Find something, Captain?” One of his men asked.

“We’re going back. This is a waste of time. The beast will come to us when the storm arrives. Gregory may not be concerned with the good of Aysela, but I have a feeling about that child we let through the gates. There’s too much coincidence. They’re followed by creatures that should’ve easily overrun them, and then the creature’s escape at the first sign of resistance? The baby Brandt’s looking for has powers that allow it to consult with beasts. It’s possible this was a ruse from the start. We’re going back. We’re going to get answers.”

The knights nodded. This meant defying Gregory’s orders, but they’d long accepted Calda as their true leader. This was a means to an end. If Calda was the one to find the baby, Brand would reward him. It would be a matter of time before he ascended into the higher ranks of knighthood.

They turned to head back.

Richard

He’d stalked the knights for the better part of a day when Calda finally gave up the hunt. Richard ran his senses over the knights. None of them posed a threat to him. Calda was the strongest of them all, but he was unsure of himself. Coming all this way only to turn back was weakness. He lacked conviction, and no one without that could cross swords with Richard on equal footing. Still, he would be careful. His goal wasn’t to kill these men, not if he didn’t have to. He would have let them return to the fort in peace, but now he knew they were after Charlie. He wasn’t going to allow that.

“Shall I summon reinforcements? Bonehead and King Scalea would make quick work of these men,” Parent asked. He floated beside Richard, inhabiting the calodosian rings.

“No, stick to the plan. I don’t want any of Charlie’s friends getting hurt. He’s working to save Mary, when I cannot, I won’t let the ones he cares about get hurt in the meantime, regardless of how capable they are.”

He drew his sword and readied himself. With a breath, he stepped out to cut off Calda’s path.

Calda

Calda paused.

The man before them was rugged looking with flecks of white already beginning to form in his hair. More white than the last time Calda had seen him. He had a thick beard and was missing an arm. In the arm he did have was a sword. This was Sir Richard. Or at least, at one time, he had been that. Now he was no knight at all, but he was a threat. That much was obvious from the gaze in the man’s eyes.

But perhaps Calda was misreading the situation. Just to be safe, he tried words first. “Richard, here to report in?” Calda asked. His hand drifted towards his sword. This was no longer a situation he could afford to hold back. A fight against Richard was a life-or-death event. Some of his men certainly weren’t walking away from this if Richard didn’t hold back, and Calda would only get himself killed trying to protect them.

“No. Just here to fight,” Richard said.

Surely Richard wouldn’t make a move against the knights with Mary held hostage. Not unless…not unless Calda’s theory was correct. If the baby really had been the one they were looking for, then this was a rescue attempt. Richard had completed his assignment and found the child, but he hadn’t turned him over. They’d conspired like snakes.

He barked at his men. “Break past, get to the fort, find the child, whatever it takes.”

“Sir!” His men charged. Eleven in total. Richard incapacitated the first four to reach him in a flash. His sword moved so quickly even Calda couldn’t perceive what had been done. Two of his men skirted around the fight completely.

Richard turned, sword in hand, and flung it at the retreating men. It spun through the air, cutting through a tree trunk nearby. The tree fell directly on top of the knights who had got past him. In an instant, Richard used a foot to kick a blade off one of his unconscious opponents. He caught it, tested its weight in an instant and leapt back into action.

His men were losing this battle; already he’d lost six of eleven. It was time to join the fight himself. Calda rushed forward as two more of his men fell. The other three backed off to give him room. He exchanged a quick succession of blows with Richard. Richard held him off, and the other knights that had remained to fight as well, whenever they thought they had an opening.

Calda noticed movement, and though it was risky, he looked up to see one of the knights that had gotten past and been knocked over rise to his feet. He was limping and injured, but he was on his feet. He was on his feet and moving back toward the fort. Richard sensed it too. But Calda kept the pressure up to tie the infamous knight’s hand.

One man was enough. He had other subordinates waiting within the fort. His men had been tasked with guarding a portion of the wall. There were enough of them to deal with this, and by the time he finished with Richard and followed, victory would already be in his hands.
