The Accords are proof that even the powerful can be made to kneel before justice. Unfortunately, only the powerful were invited to write the law, and only the powerful were given the authority to enforce it.

—Councilor Mereth Vayne, On the Accords and the Mundane.

Cores, treasures, awakened plants, and awakened items had always shone to core sense like stars—blinding spots of light in the darkness of the world.

It wasn’t so dark now that Aodhán could see the energy clinging to every surface of the city below him. Still, despite the ambient glow, those blinding spots stood out in golden contrast against the world around them. Thousands of them, packed so tightly together that the entire city seemed to shine gold.

The sight shocked Aodhán.

From what he’d read, the human settlements of Gordon were home to a boastful people. People so proud of their mundanity that they refused the shelter of the great sects, choosing instead to glory in the achievements of their minds and technology.

It was like choosing to live on Earth where everyone was normal, rather than being normal in a city full of superheroes.

Aodhán understood it.

But for a people so boastful of the greatness of mundanity, they sure had a lot of magical possessions.

The thought brought a smile to his face, and he scoffed quietly. There was no way he could find Edrick Ghaltris’s legacy in this mess.

Fortunately, he didn’t have to.

Inhaling deeply, he stretched core sense to its absolute limit and whispered,

“Storm.”

Instantly, the world dimmed, thousands of lights vanishing as core sense filtered them out until only a few hundred spots remained.

All Storm affinity.

Aodhán smiled. “My ability works.”

A collective sigh escaped those around him, and Calamity Amoz whispered, “What’s your range?”

“Just a little under four miles,” Aodhán replied, his brows furrowing at the estimate. “I might have to do this a couple more times to cover the whole city.”

“Never mind that for now,” Calamity Amoz replied, though even with his eyes closed, Aodhán could hear the smile in the man’s voice. “Focus on your current range and tell us what you see.”

Aodhán obeyed, sharpening the clarity of his senses as much as he could.

The blinding spots grew sharper, and with them, their energy signatures. Most were items—weapons, shields, artifacts, or even mundane creations kissed by Storm essence.

But not all.

Scattered throughout the city were several spots of golden light Aodhán recognized as magical treasures and awakened plants. None were particularly strong, given the sparse density of energy in the city, and Aodhán dismissed them, focusing his attention on the more powerful signatures.

Wherever Edrick Ghaltris’s legacy was hidden, it couldn’t be weak.

Without hesitation, Aodhán filtered once more, scanning and dismissing signatures too weak to be a legacy, or signatures that were obviously items, until finally, only thirteen spots remained.

Thirteen spots of Storm energy powerful enough to be a legacy.

He opened his eyes and met Calamity Amoz’s piercing gaze, smiling proudly.

“I’ve narrowed it down significantly. In a five-mile radius, there are thirteen potential locations that could hold the legacy seed.”

“Then we have to start searching immediately,” Calamity Selia said. “We split up and search the areas the boy points us to.”

“Not yet.” Calamity Amoz shook his head. “We need permission from Vaymoz Alcatraz before we begin ransacking his city. He’ll discover our presence sooner or later. Better to go through due process.”

He glanced at Aodhán. “Of those thirteen spots, which is the brightest?”

Calamity Rezin frowned. “A legacy seed might not be the brightest, Amoz.”

“No,” Amoz agreed with a grimace. “But if there’s a treasure in this city brighter than a legacy seed, then I must know about it. Which is brightest, Aodhán?”

Aodhán closed his eyes once more, seeing all thirteen spots scattered around him. Four were to his left, the farthest of them all. Five were to his right. Three were behind him.

But the brightest of them all was in front of him, just slightly off-center.

With his eyes closed, he raised a hand and pointed straight at it.

“There.”

Whispers rose immediately, and when Aodhán opened his eyes, he found his finger aimed directly at a black spire near the center of the city.

The structure was covered from roof to foundation in massive weapons—cannons, lasers, and several other things Aodhán couldn’t name.

If the buildings of Alcatraz stood in defiance of magic and cultivation, this one was the antithesis of it. And when Calamity Amoz spoke a moment later, Aodhán realized why.

That was Vaymoz Alcatraz’s residence.

“What could something so magical be doing in his palace?” Calamity Selia asked with a deep frown. “I thought he detested magic.”

“Not quite.” Calamity Rezin scowled. “But he has been very vocal about not wanting anything to do with it himself. He teaches his people to defy it.”

“It doesn’t matter what he thinks of magic,” Amoz cut in firmly, his gaze fixed on the spire. “If there’s something that magical in that building, then either his disposition toward magic has changed, or he’s made an exception.”

He turned to Aodhán. “Do you think it could be the legacy?”

Aodhán shook his head. “I’m not sure. It’s simply the brightest Storm energy signature within a five-mile radius. It could just be a treasure.”

“Or an item,” Crixis murmured, his eyes narrowing at the distant spire.

Aodhán nodded. “It could be anything, really. But if it is the seed, do you think he’ll hand it over?”

Calamity Rezin and Amoz scowled immediately, their expressions hardening with unspoken understanding.

Amoz shook his head. “Vaymoz Alcatraz would rather die than be seen as aiding the sects in anything. Even this permission we seek, he’s sure to make difficult.”

“Then what shall we do?” Calamity Selia asked with a slight scowl. “Are we now to negotiate the possession of a legacy with a sleeper because of the Accords?”

Amoz glared at her. "Are sleepers not human beings too?" He shook his head. "We will go through due process. We simply have to be smart about it."

He turned to face the rest of them. “We had to visit the building to seek Vaymoz’s permission in the first place. We might as well kill two birds with one stone.”

Everyone nodded in agreement, and Calamity Amoz’s gaze hardened.

“I will do the talking, and you”—he glanced at Aodhán—“you and Selia will search for the source of that signature, whatever it is.”

They both nodded, and Calamity Amoz looked toward the others.

“The rest of you will come with me. Our numbers will not intimidate Vaymoz, but they should make him more amenable.”

“That’s assuming he even knows the legacy seed is there,” Sophran muttered, his voice quiet.

Yet his words caused everyone to pause.

“The boy is right,” Selia said a moment later. “There’s a real possibility Vaymoz isn’t even aware something that powerful is in his possession.”

Amoz pursed his lips in thought. Then shook his head.

“That still doesn’t change the plan. If it’s the legacy, we retrieve it. If not, we’ll leave like we knew nothing about it.”

Everyone nodded, and without further hesitation, Calamity Amoz extended a finger and snapped it, teleporting them straight toward the spire.

It was time to meet the ruler of Alcatraz and discover what magical thing he had secured within that building.

But as they arrived before the giant monstrosity of a structure, thunder booming unnaturally to announce their presence, Aodhán couldn’t help but wonder.

If the legacy seed was already in the possession of Vaymoz Alcatraz, why was the system still protecting it?

Was it still protecting it, or had they misunderstood its motives entirely?

Aodhán feared it was the latter.

***

Despite their unnaturally loud arrival, it still took the soldiers of Alcatraz a minute to rise to their positions.

It was a minute too long.

Had they been enemies—or cultivators with ill intent—they could have destroyed the city before its residents even realized what was happening.

And from the gazes of the soldiers as they aimed their barbaric weapons and engines at them, it was obvious they knew it too. Some looked grim. Others looked pale. A few gripped their weapons so tightly their knuckles whitened.

While most were still rubbing sleep from their eyes.

Calamity Amoz smirked. “Selia, Aodhán, this is your cue.”

Nodding, Aodhán activated Stealth while Selia activated an ability that hid her completely, even from his senses.

The disappearance was immediate.

One moment she was beside him, and the next, there was nothing.

No presence. No aura. No trace.

Even the pressure her power exerted on reality was gone. For all intents and purposes, Calamity Selia no longer existed.

Until her hands brushed against his, and then a quiet voice teased his mind.

I’ll be here. Even when you can’t sense me. Know that I’m by your side.

Aodhán barely stopped himself from jerking in fear as the words resounded through his mind, a whisper in the wind yet somehow loud inside his head.

He nodded uneasily, feeling a hint of fear curl in his gut before remembering that Selia couldn’t see him. He opened his mouth to speak, but before he could, Selia’s voice came again.

Don’t bother. Your Stealth is crafted to fool Mythics and Advanced cultivators. Not Calamities. A smile entered her voice. Proceed.

Aodhán swallowed and gave one last invisible glance toward Crixis and Sophran before flying toward the building, heading straight for the blinding spot even as soldiers rushed and shouted hurriedly beneath him, each carrying weapons that could barely scratch him, let alone a Calamity.

He shook his head and returned his focus to the glowing energy signature.

Even from this close, he still couldn’t tell what it was. This time, however, he was certain it wasn’t an item, Artifact, or treasure.

Whatever was within this building, it wasn't something core sense had ever encountered before.

He reached the reinforced walls of the spire an instant later and seamlessly began phasing through them. Just as he did so, a boom of thunder rumbled, cracking across the heavens like judgment itself.

“Morning, soldiers of Alcatraz,” Calamity Amoz said, his voice carrying across the city without needing to rise. “I am a delegate from the Storm Sect. I request a meeting with your ruler. Lead me to him.”

Aodhán smiled and let the building swallow him, amusement stirring within him as he imagined the fear the poor soldiers were feeling in that moment. A man with the voice of thunder had just appeared at their doorstep and politely asked to see their ruler.

There couldn’t have been a more terrifying request.

So terrifying, perhaps, that none of them had even noticed him and Selia slip away from the group.

Aodhán didn’t intend to waste the opportunity.

Whatever Vaymoz Alcatraz was hiding, he was about to find it. And if it turned out to be the seed itself, then this would be the second time he was stealing something from a noble.

Fortunately, he wouldn’t need to seduce anyone this time.

***

Five minutes earlier.

In another part of Alcatraz, Severian, Malverin, and Emily floated high above the sleeping city, looking down at its defiant infrastructure with undisguised distaste.

“Can you sense it now?” Malverin asked after a long moment of silence from Severian.

“No.” He grimaced. “It seems the system is yet to let up on its incessant meddling.”

“Pesky little thing,” Emily scowled. “What do we do now?”

“I should think it’s obvious, Emily.” Malverin glared at her sister. “We continue our search manually.”

She glanced at Severian. “What can you sense?”

“A lot, surprisingly,” Severian replied, his spiritual perception expanding to engulf the city in its entirety. “For a people so proud of their mundanity, the citizens of Alcatraz sure have a lot of soul-bound items.”

Malverin smirked. “Perhaps they think their weapons will save them.”

Severian snorted. “I doubt they are that stupid. But whatever the case may be, their hypocrisy has certainly made things more difficult for us.”

He glanced around, and his grimace deepened into a scowl.

“There are soul fragments everywhere. There’s no way I can pick out Edrick Ghaltris’s soul in this mess.”

Emily frowned. “Shouldn’t the legacy seed be brighter than the rest? It contains a fragment of the soul of an Ascendant, after all.”

Severian shook his head and was just about to respond when suddenly, he felt an undulation in the fabric of space several miles away. And then—a searing—as the bright, burning souls of several Calamities suddenly appeared in Alcatraz.

He jerked his perception back immediately, leashing it so tightly around himself and his sisters that they both choked. Energy poured from him in a stream so thin it was invisible, washing over the three of them and hiding them from sight just as the perception of the new arrivals swept across the area.

“Do not breathe,” he commanded.

Beside him, his sisters tensed, stilling so completely they almost seemed like statues.

For several seconds, the spiritual perceptions of four powerful Calamities scanned the entire city with unnerving precision.

Severian waited, not daring to move or even breathe as the searching perceptions swept over them. For several tense seconds, he remained perfectly still, until at last, they passed.

Only then did Severian allow himself to breathe again, irritation rising within him at the arrival of a new set of obstacles. The Storm Sect’s arrival meant that they too had been searching for the seed and had narrowed its location to this sleeping city filled with hypocritical humans.

He sighed. This was a problem that could make their already difficult mission nearly impossible if he didn’t find a way around it. Quickly.

“I take it those are our enemies?” Malverin whispered.

Severian sighed again, frustration mixing with the irritation already weighing on him. How was he supposed to find Edrick Ghaltris’s soul with the Calamities of the Storm Sect running amok throughout the city? How was he supposed to carry out his plans for infiltration?

He grimaced and whispered, “They must never find us. If they do, our plans are destroyed.”

My plans are destroyed.

His sisters nodded, and in a near-breathless whisper, Emily asked, “What do we do now? How do we find the core before the Storm Sect does?”

“We search manually,” Severian replied, resolve hardening in his gaze. “The system has suppressed the intensity of Edrick Ghaltris’s soul to make it harder to detect, but it has only delayed the inevitable.”

He scowled and turned to his sisters, raising his hands to their foreheads. “We are going to search this city from top to bottom until we narrow it down to the seed. I have placed a cloak over both your souls to hide you from the perception of Calamities. As long as you remain out of sight, you are hidden from their senses.”

Malverin’s eyes shone. “And soul sight? We want to help you.”

Severian grimaced, immensely pleased by the eagerness in her voice. He pressed his thumbs against their foreheads, then twisted them sharply, channeling soul energy straight into their minds.

Both sisters spasmed for a moment as the blessing surged through them like electricity.

They stilled a moment later, and when they opened their eyes, they glowed violet.

“Good.” Severian smiled.

But that smile withered as thunder rumbled unnaturally across the heavens, and the once-clear sky began to darken.

“They are moving already,” Malverin whispered, a deep scowl etched onto her face. She glanced at Severian. “We should begin as well.”

Severian nodded. “Take the far east, Malverin. Emily, take the west.”

His gaze settled on the spire at the center of the city, and he grimaced. Whatever was in that building was powerful. But it held no soul.

It wasn’t the legacy seed.

He turned toward the far north, where he could feel the densest concentration of souls, and whispered, “I will take the north.”

Malverin nodded. “Be careful, brother.”

“I will be,” Severian whispered, his gaze never leaving the north. “I’m not about to let fate steal this from me. I’ve come too far.” He glanced at his sisters. “We have come too far.”

They nodded, and Severian gave them a faint smile. “Go. Be quick.”

Malverin and Emily left immediately, streaking across the sky in blurs of blue and violet. Severian watched them disappear, his gaze slowly deepening into a scowl. He turned his gaze back toward the north and his eyes hardened.

He would find this legacy and consume the soul within, even if he had to kill for it and destroy their plans for infiltration in the process.

His advancement was most important to him.

And what could break this looming sense of a limit faster than the soul of an Ascendant?
