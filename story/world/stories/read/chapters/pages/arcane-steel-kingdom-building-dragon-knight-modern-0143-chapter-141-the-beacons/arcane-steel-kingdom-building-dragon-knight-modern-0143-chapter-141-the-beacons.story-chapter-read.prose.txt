I stood in my private study, staring at the blank wall. The silence was absolute, a heavy contrast to the churning anxiety in my gut. The war below was grinding on, a brutal stalemate in the mud, but my focus had shifted upward. Way upward.

I was preparing to launch the first three orbital beacons of the Aegis defense network.

This was my fail-safe. If I had to step onto the battlefield, if King Theron or his overgrown mama bird saw me and decided to end the war by eliminating the commander, I needed an insurance policy that didn't involve nuking my own position. I needed a Swiss Army knife in orbit.

The beacons were specialized, orbital stations that integrated directly into The Oracle's command network. They were powered by internal nuclear reactors, supplemented by massive solar arrays designed to drink in the ambient, high-altitude starlight. During my research, I had discovered a crucial piece of Aethelgardian physics: solar energy here was incredibly potent, a highly concentrated form of magical power. It made sense; all magic in this world originally stemmed from the Solarium, the star at the center of their system.

I finalized the launch sequence for the three stations on my datapad.

The first was a hybrid supply and support unit. Its primary directive was to shadow my Reaper armor. In the middle of a prolonged engagement, it could deploy specialized, high-velocity delivery drones. These drones carried spare power cores, fresh plasma katanas, ammunition packs, or even complete replacement limbs and chest plates for the Mark-VII. If my armor took a critical hit in the mud, the beacon could drop replacement parts in seconds, allowing me to hot-swap components on the fly.

It also held two complete, spare suits in its bays. Only the Reaper and Bob’s specialized Mark II-G possessed the architectural capacity to utilize standard power cores while simultaneously channeling draconic magic without blowing a circuit. They were irreplaceable on the ground, so I put the replacements in space.

The other two beacons were my answer to the Mother Phoenix.

They were Defense and Isolation hubs. If that giant, flaming bird decided to swoop down on my lines, these beacons would deploy specialized countermeasures. They housed drones equipped with localized isolation barriers—the same angelic tech the assassins had used in my nursery, reverse-engineered and weaponized. Other drones utilized localized, short-range wormholes to redirect incoming thermal attacks or high-tier fire magic.

And then there were the suppression units. Drones that deployed localized freezing arrays, dropping the ambient temperature to absolute zero in a matter of seconds. Some deployed swarms of fire-extinguisher spheres, packed with chemical retardants like Phos-Chek, stored in spatial pockets to maximize payload. Let’s see how the overgrown mama bird likes a face full of modern fire suppression.

I had also completely redesigned the launch vehicles. The old rockets I used were messy, burning huge amounts of fuel to escape the atmosphere and dropping discarded stages over the ocean.

The new system was a two-part, reusable design. The bottom half—the booster—utilized anti-gravity runes to push the rocket through the thickest part of the lower atmosphere, minimizing air resistance. Once it reached the upper atmosphere, the top half detached and ignited its own fuel payload to exit the atmosphere and achieve orbit.

The bottom half then engaged a series of directional thrusters and secondary anti-gravity runes, guiding itself back down to a soft, controlled landing at a designated recovery site on the Obsidian Dominion coast. In my old world, rocket recovery was a massive, terrifyingly precise burn of thrusters right before impact. Here, with gravity magic, the booster simply floated down like a feather, ready to be refueled and reused within hours.

But the beacons weren't the only things going up today.

I tapped my comms. "Tes, initiate transit to the CIC."

The air around me shimmered. Space folded. A heartbeat later, I was standing in the center of the Combat Information Center.

The atmosphere here was thick with a different kind of tension. General Mirelle stood at the secondary command console. Her posture was rigid, her hands gripped tightly behind her back.

I walked to the primary holotable. A different set of launch coordinates glowed a harsh, angry red.

Thirty Intercontinental Ballistic Missiles.

These weren't two-stage rockets. They were single-stage, solid-fuel nightmares. They would arc high into the upper orbit, cross the continent, and descend upon the target zone.

But they weren't carrying nuclear payloads.

As the missile broke apart during its descent, it would release over a hundred smaller, explosive sub-munitions. These smaller devices would deploy in a predetermined sequence, firing outward in expanding rings, creating concentric circles of devastation.

The objective was mass area denial. These munitions were incendiary. They were designed to set fire to a massive, sprawling area, burning everything within the rings with a terrifying, rapid intensity.

The target was Sylvanheim.

Specifically, the dense, ancient forests that formed the natural, impenetrable borders of the Elven kingdom.

The elves valued their forests above all else. The trees were their homes, their temples, their first line of defense. I was curious to see what they prioritized when the sky started dropping fire: fighting my army on the border, or saving their precious, burning timber.

The only thing that gave me pause was the environmental impact. The wildlife, the uncontrolled burn, the sheer amount of ash that would be pumped into the atmosphere. War is messy. The collateral damage is always a bitter pill.

But I wasn't the one who had planned this specific strike.

The Dark Elves had orchestrated this.

They had spearheaded the development of the sub-munitions, building upon my existing magitech framework. They had provided the precise targeting coordinates, utilizing old, guarded maps of the Sylvanheim borders.

And they would be the ones initiating the firing sequence.

For five millennia, the Dark Elves had been the hunted. Driven from the main continent, shoved into the ashen wastelands of the Obsidian Dominion, enslaved, and treated as lesser, evil variants of their sun-worshipping cousins. The Imperium and the Conclave had branded them as enemies of the light.

Today, after thousands of years of hiding in the dark, they were finally striking back.

I had been surprised when Mirelle brought the proposal to my desk. But after everything her people had done for me, how could I stand in the way of their retribution?

I finished my final check of the orbital beacons' launch sequence. The telemetry was green. I verified the ICBM targeting data. The strikes were concentrated heavily on strategic choke points and ancient, fortified villages that had, historically, belonged to the Dark Elves before the purge.

Everything was in order.

"Tes," I projected. "Initiate the launch sequence of the orbital beacons."

...

Deep within the hollowed-out caldera of the Obsidian Fang, three colossal silos groaned open.

The air pressure inside the mountain shifted as massive, localized anti-gravity fields engaged. The three sleek, black rockets lifted off their launch pads. There was no deafening roar of combustion, no blinding plume of smoke.

They simply rose. They accelerated smoothly, silently, punching up through the open crater of the mountain and into the bruised twilight sky.

As they hit the upper atmosphere, the true violence began.

The top halves of the rockets detached with a sharp, explosive sequence. Their internal plasma thrusters ignited, a brilliant, blinding white flare against the darkening sky. Kinetic shielding engaged, wrapping the ascending payloads in a frictionless bubble that sheared through the remaining air resistance.

They tore out of the atmosphere, piercing the veil of the world in under five minutes.

In the cold, silent void of orbit, the thrusters cut out. The payload fairings popped open, and the three Aegis beacons deployed, their massive solar sails unfolding like the wings of strange, mechanical insects, catching the raw, unfiltered light of the sun.

...

I watched the deployment on the tactical feed in the CIC. Three new, blue icons blinked to life, orbiting steadily above the digital globe.

"Tes, is everything running optimally?" I asked.

[Affirmative, Master. Rapid deployment of the orbital drones is possible. Shall I run a few test runs?]

"Yes."

[Beginning deployment of defensive unit.]

High above the planet, the Isolation Beacon engaged.

A small, sleek drone, shaped roughly like a stingray, shot out of the station's launch tube. It plummeted back toward the planet.

It didn't burn up on re-entry. The kinetic barriers held, slicing through the friction. The drone's internal gravitic runes engaged in reverse, amplifying its mass and accelerating its fall to staggering speeds.

Just a few hundred feet above the sea, the drone executed its braking maneuver.

The kinetic barriers flared to maximum output. The gravitic runes reversed instantly, snapping from heavy to light. The drone stopped dead in mid-air with a sound like a thunderclap.

It hovered there, absolutely motionless, and deployed a localized kinetic shield.

The entire process, from orbital launch to stationary deployment, took less than forty-five seconds.

That was rapid deployment.

The real question was durability. The logic behind my kinetic barriers was absolute: they dispersed kinetic energy evenly across the entire surface area of the shield. Mathematically, it should survive at least one or two direct, concentrated strikes from the Mother Phoenix before the power core overloaded.

I nodded, satisfied. I turned to my right, where General Mirelle stood rigid by the secondary command console.

"Since this was your proposal," I said, stepping back and gesturing to the launch controls for the ICBMs. "I'll let you do the honors."

Mirelle visibly gulped. Her hands, usually so steady when reviewing logistical manifests or Academy grades, trembled slightly.

She looked at the officers manning the surrounding consoles—young Dark Elves, the first graduates of her Academy.

"Run the final diagnostic checks," she ordered, her voice tight.

The officers’ fingers flew across the glass keypads. A chorus of crisp, professional affirmations echoed across the bridge.

"All systems green, General."

Mirelle looked at the glowing red activation rune on the console. She swallowed hard. Her voice, when she spoke, came out meek and hoarse, stripped of all its usual military cadence.

"Initiate firing sequence."

She pressed her palm to the glass.

A tears slipped from her eye, tracking a clean line down her cheek.

I watched her, a profound silence settling over my corner of the bridge. The woman I had found on the black sand of the Dominion, who had bartered with demon lords for scrap iron, had just ordered a strike that would burn the forests of her ancestors.

I looked around the CIC.

Some of the young officers were weeping openly at their stations, tears falling onto the glowing blue keys. They didn't stop working. They monitored the launch telemetry with ruthless efficiency, their faces masks of grief and fierce, overwhelming relief.

For generations, their people had hoped for this. They had prayed for revenge, to avenge the millions of lives lost, the endless, grinding suffering they had endured in the ash. They had clung to the hope of a prophecy, to the return of a Ghost. Most of them had stopped believing it would ever happen.

But the day had finally come. I had put the weapons in their hands, taught them the science of destruction, and now, the underdogs were shooting back.

The scene in the CIC was being broadcast on a secured frequency to the civilian habitation zones in the lower decks. I glanced at a secondary monitor showing a live feed from one of the massive mess halls.

Thousands of Dark Elves stood packed together, watching the massive screens. They were completely silent. As the confirmation of the launch flashed across the display, the silence broke. They wept. They held each other. The suffocating, inherited helplessness of five thousand years finally broke. They had the strength to fight back. They were standing tall.

On the tactical map, thirty red lines arced high into the stratosphere, heading straight for the emerald heart of Sylvanheim.

...

In the ancient, towering forests of Sylvanheim, the border patrols were already mobilized, waiting for the order to march west.

The sky above the canopy, usually a serene canvas of blue, suddenly streaked with thirty fiery contrails descending at impossible speeds.

The impact wasn't a singular explosion. The ICBMs shattered miles above the canopy, releasing their sub-munitions. It looked like a deadly, metallic rain.

Then, the rings ignited.

Concentric circles of unquenchable, chemical fire erupted across hundreds of miles of the border. Ancient Ironwood trees, magically warded against standard flame spells, were reduced to ash in seconds. The fire raged outwards, a coordinated, perfectly geometric inferno that consumed military camps, supply depots, and the sacred groves that had stood since the dawn of Sylvanheim.
