The party was two days away; the buzz was everywhere.

Even at breakfast, Alric couldn't escape it. The guards were debating whether the adventurers would actually manage eight barrels, as if this were a feat of great athleticism. A maid asked Seren if they'd be working the next morning, presumably hoping for a hangover exemption. Someone from the brewery arrived, asked if they could bring their wife, vanished, and then reappeared to see if children were allowed too.

People from the adventurers’ guild had already gone out to the muster yard, wagons carrying wood, tables, chairs, and whatever else Pavise had decided. Denner and Berrin had managed to get places to cook, along with six other taverns and inns. Many had been turned away; they had complained to both his offices and the adventurers.

I wanted to get rid of eight barrels of IPA. Somehow, Avengard decided this was a festival… Hang on, that’s not fair. If I offered two thousand, four liters of free beer at a college, I’d have a riot.

“It’s a big bunch of people getting drunk in a field.” Alric said to himself, then nodded, “Doesn’t make me less excited though.” He grinned.

He turned back to the ledger in front of him; they would soon be malting grain for the distillery. The warehouse had been sectioned off; grain would start moving around soon.

He pulled something out of his item box that was interesting him far more right now. A drawing with steel spikes and hooks. He couldn’t remember the exact specifics of the lever mechanism he wanted for the crossbows. He remembered it being called a goat's foot, but he would likely need to figure out those parts with Stromni. He tapped his finger on the page, trying to remember whether it was something permanently attached to the crossbow or not.

His thoughts were interrupted by a knock on the door. “Come in,” Alric said, glancing up.

Seren entered with a small smile. She was carrying a single folded letter.

Alric immediately recoiled, turning slightly away. He knew there was something strange about it; even from this distance, he could tell the paper was good, and if Seren was bringing it to him directly, that made him even more suspicious.

“Mister Alric,” Seren said, crossing the room and holding the letter out, “I have something that you should probably read yourself.” She waited, holding it out to him.

Alric swallowed; he took the letter, glancing briefly as he opened it. The seal at the bottom center took up the entire fold. It bore a motif: a hand emerging from a bed of leaves, holding a small round flask between thumb and forefinger; in the background stood a large stylized tree. Across the top, the lettering changed as it always did in this world to say ‘The Alchemist’s Guild’.

Alric felt his blood turn cold; he swallowed, feeling suddenly lightheaded. Taking a deep breath, he turned to read the text at the top. He read it three times before he dropped the letter and sat back, letting out a deep breath, staring at the ceiling.

“And?” Seren asked; she had one eyebrow up, having watched Alric through the whole exchange.

“They heard I bought potions from the adventurers' guild. This is an invitation to their shop in the noble’s quarter.” Alric gestured to it, but felt his shoulders loosen.

“Uh, I see. That was all?” Seren asked, still with an eyebrow up.

“Yes, that was all,” Alric said, sliding the letter over to her. Seren read through the letter while occasionally glancing at him.

Brannick bought those, and at the adventurers’ guild, no less. Somehow the alchemists had tracked the purchase and decided the cheap ones were beneath me. I can't decide whether to be insulted, flattered, or just worried that they found out so fast.

Seren folded the letter, still regarding Alric closely.

“Can you let Brannick know we're going for a small walk?” Alric said with a small smile. Seren pointed at the letter; Alric nodded.

“No problem, Mister Alric. I’ll let them know,” Seren said with a smile and stood.

Alric still liked walking through Avengard. There was a time when he’d have complained endlessly about the smell, but now it was just part of the ambiance, like the shouting or the risk of being trampled by a handcart carrying his own beer.

He glanced at his guards; this was a pain for them, he knew. A carriage was easier to protect and keep track of, but wow, it was uncomfortable. Brannick had assigned six guards to him, as well as himself. Behind him were the two new arbalists, both of whom were diligently scanning rooftops.

The city was busy despite the cold. Winter was next week according to the temple, and Avengard seemed determined to make as much money as possible while it still could. Firewood was stacked up to shutters that Alric could see; carts moved everywhere; hawkers were shouting about smoked meat.

The traffic cleared as they approached Merchants' Row. The nob bridge, as locals called it, was completely clear. As they approached the bridge, one of the guards snapped a salute, “Master Alric.” He said, looking ahead.

“Uh, thanks, good to see you,” Alric said, blinking rapidly at the man.

The second bridge guard even greeted Brannick. No one stopped them or questioned his need for guards as they had in the past.

When he’d arrived, he’d been overwhelmed, adopted by an inn family, and guided around the city by a seven-year-old with questionable navigation skills. Now the guards knew his name, even Brannick’s, and accepted the parade of armed men behind him as normal. He wasn’t sure when that had happened.

As they continued deeper into the quarter, he watched his guards' faces as much as the surroundings. For many of them, this was the first time they had seen how the nobles lived; they gawked but seldom commented.

Finally, they approached ‘The Alchemists Guild’. Alric stood outside for a while, just admiring the building. The building stood on its own patch of ground, at a crossroads, but there were no neighboring buildings.

What stood out most to Alric was the stone. Not quite marble, but certainly lighter than the usual noble’s preference for ‘ominous grey’. This was pale cream, almost white in places, and so smooth it looked like it had never seen a muddy boot or a tax collector.

Approaching the entrance, Alric studied the two statues that flanked the doors. One showed a robed woman holding a bowl; the other showed a man with plants climbing around his legs, holding what seemed to be a bottle against his chest.

“Hmph,” Alric said before heading to the doors.

If the outside had been impressive, Alric hadn’t prepared himself for the inside. Almost the whole floor was a large hall. The ceiling rose high with a gallery running on both sides. He glanced around, looking for any signs of work, but there were no shelves, bottles, work tables, or herbs anywhere to be seen.

Then his gaze saw the floor. It was beautiful.

A large map had been laid into the stone. Darker shades of stone had formed the oceans; different tiles made continents and islands. Thin strips of metal ran through the land, marking what Alric assumed were borders.

“That’s the world?” One of the guards asked behind him.

Alric glanced at them; several were nodding at the guard, the rest gawking at the map. He didn’t blame them. If he could, he’d spend half an hour copying it, but a wave from the far side interrupted his brief cartographic dream.

A long, polished counter stood on the far side of the hall; two people sat behind it, both women, and one waved at him with a smile. Alric approached the waving person.

Up close, she looked more like a clerk of a bank than someone who worked with dangerous chemicals. He dress was dark and fitted neatly with embroidery at the sleeves. Her hair was held back by a hair stick; it was the first time he had seen that.

“Good day, M’lord, welcome to the alchemists' guild.” She said smoothly with a small curtsy at the end.

“Uh… thanks, but I’m not a lord,” Alric said with a nod. He leaned on the counter slightly. “I received your letter. I wanted to speak to an alchemist?”

“The guild master is away today, I’m afraid. If you need something made, we can take your order for you. Depending on what you need, it can take many weeks, more so now with winter.” She said, gesturing vaguely to the wall.

Alric nodded slowly.

No chemical smells, this place smells more like wood polish for this counter than anything else. This isn’t a workshop. This is just a shop, an expensive one without a display.

“How do I know what you make?” Alric asked, gesturing to the hall around him.

She reached beneath the counter and pulled out a small book. “May I ask what you are looking for, sir?” She asked, opening the book and looking up at him.

“Healing Potions,” Alric said, watching the book carefully.

She nodded, turned a few pages, and slid the book to him. He leaned over it.

This is a damn catalog.

“Uh, which ones are the ones the adventurers sell?” Alric asked, glancing between her and the book.

She pointed to the ones at the top of the list. It was neat, an ornate ‘I’ letter, listed for thirty silver.

The potion we bought that Brannick treated like a firstborn? That’s the lowest quality they sell?

His eyes went down; his headache flared as he looked over the text. The bottom of the list was next to an VIII. “Four gold?” Alric said aloud before he realized he'd spoken.

The clerk nodded with a smile. “That is our best healing potion. It is best for severe wounds, deep cuts, shattered bones, severe burns, even significant blood loss.”

That’s sixty tons of grain. How much blood are you losing for sixty tons of grain?

“What other potions do you sell? Other kinds?” Alric asked; he glanced at the other clerk who hadn’t moved.

She turned the page back. The second page had five headings: Healing, Stamina, Mana, Antidote, Special.

“That’s it? What’s special?” Alric asked, his eyebrows lifting; he leaned over further.

“Those are what take a while, fire resistance potions, cold resistance, various venoms.” She said, turning to the page. There weren’t tiers on this page so much as just icons: a potion on fire, a potion with wind moving behind it, one with snake teeth.

Alric let out a low hum. Five categories were much more limited than he expected. He'd expected hundreds of potions. Strength potions, agility potions, hopefully hair restoration potions for when he hit middle age again.

Alric turned the pages back to the healing potion. He selected what was next to the III symbol. It cost seventy silver. “Can I get one of these?” Alric asked, glancing up at the clerk.

She smiled, curtsied, and went into a back room. Alric watched her go.

I suppose they just keep some stock here, but this level of calm isn’t confidence or even arrogance. It was indifference, possibly bordering on boredom. How do they enforce their monopoly? Do they even bother?

Brannick had moved closer while Alric turned back to the booklet. “Buying more healing potions, Mister Alric?” Brannick asked, glancing around.

“Hmm… Yes, just to keep something more potent at the headquarters if we ever need it.” Alric said with a quick shrug.

The clerk returned with a small wooden box; she placed it on the counter and opened the flap. Inside, cloth protected a lump of red wax.

Alric picked it up; it felt the same size as the previous potion. He turned it over and, knowing what he was looking for, found the spot where the wax was different. In an effort not to frown, he nodded and put it back into the box.

“I’ll take it.” He said, keeping his gaze down.

“Of course, sir, come this way.” She said, leading him to a different counter. Alric tried not to think about how much bread that was.

Seventy silver to rip apart another potion. I’ll at least rewrap this one.

Back in the headquarters, Alric frowned at the deconstructed potion. The potion was maybe ten percent larger than the mana potion, held about the same amount of mana, and was a vibrant red color.

I can understand why you set up a mana potion to expire. If nobles control most mages, then mana potions may as well be bombs… but this.

He tapped his finger on the desk, looking at the health potion.

At seventy silver, this isn’t a soldier's thing. Uniforms and armor for the guards came to forty silver a piece.

He hated that thought; he frowned but accepted it, as he stared at the potion.

You aren’t arming anyone with this. Are other potions expensive because this makes a loss? But then why set it up to expire? Is this just greed or something else?
