At dinner that night, Ferris was excited when a guest joined them at the table.

“Everyone,” Mother Lily led the stranger into the common hall just as everyone had gathered to eat, “this is my dear friend Adept Nyoka Sauda, she used to delve with my husband.”

Nyoka was a tall, thin woman with sharp eyes. She was completely bald, with a snake wrapped around her chest. The snake’s head stood up from her shoulder, examining the room with the same intensity as the woman.

Nyoka evaluated each of the Adepts in the room, one by one. When it was Ferris’ turn, her eyes darted down to Jade in Ferris’ lap for a brief second, but she quickly moved on. After she swept the room, her eyes moved to the common room’s decorations. The sharp look in her eyes softened a bit with each displayed item, but the snake’s head stayed locked on the Adepts at the table.

“And this lovely dear,” continued Mother Lily, holding out her hand to the snake, “is Nyofu.”

Nyofu unwrapped from Nyoka and slithered over onto Mother Lily. The snake playfully hissed, tongue extending to tickle the woman’s nose. Mother Lily gave the snake a hug, at least the section she could grab. Truly, the woman would hug anyone.

Nyoka joined Ferris and the others at the table, then took a seat. Mother Lily, with Nyofu still wrapped around her, left for the kitchen. Ferris watched in fascination as she left, calmly chatting with the large snake wrapped around her. Perhaps this was where the woman’s overfamiliarity with familiars came from?

“Greetings. As Mother Lily said, I am Adept Nyoka Sauda, retainer of Hollowfield. I’ll be joining you for the meal.”

“Well met, Adept Nyoka,” Karl replied, with a small bow of his head, “I am Adept Karl Goldhand, at your service.” He was acting more respectful than usual… Ferris wondered if he could tell Nyoka’s strength. Mother Lily had never said her husband’s tier, but he had died over a decade ago. For Nyoka to be a member of his group, she must have been delving for at least that long—likely far longer. Age was hard to determine for cultivators, as their bodies would be refined and reformed with each advancement. For example, Elder Harper appeared younger than Mother Lily, but was likely far older. She could be older than his grandmother, and Ferris wouldn’t be able to tell.

“I am Adept Ferris Bluereef, and this is Jade.” Ferris said, lifting Jade up.

“Adept Brixten Holder,” Brix pointed to herself, “and Adept Cyrus Wade,” and then to Cyrus, who rolled his eyes at her for interrupting his introduction. “Nice to meet you, what do you do for the clan?”

Nyoka smiled slightly at Brix’s eagerness, “I am part of one of Hollowfield’s full-time delver groups.”

“How do you even get that gig?” Brix asked, throwing her hands up in the air. “Everyone I talk to says not to worry about it, they won’t even tell me why there aren’t more full-time delver groups.”

“Anyone can delve as much as they would like,” Nyoka said, “but opening a rift takes reagents. More expensive materials open better rifts. Hollowfield sponsors a limited number of delver groups like mine to explore deeper and pricier rifts full-time.”

Mother Lily returned—Nyofu still wrapped around her—carrying a massive platter, which she set in the center of the table. It was filled with colorful stuffed peppers, arranged in a circle around heaping piles of potatoes, broccoli, carrots, asparagus, and more. Somehow, Nyofu was carrying three more bowls, held up within her coils. She placed two of the bowls on the table, but kept the last one for herself as she slithered over to Nyoka. The snake wrapped around the bowl, containing what appeared to be large brown balls, and began eating them whole.

Watching the snake eat was a bit unsettling, although in a different way than watching Jade melt food. Ferris focused on preparing a plate for the slime. Usually, Mother Lily would serve each person their own plate. Extra food was usually available on request, but this excessively arranged self-serve platter was a special treat.

Once everyone had a plate of food, Nyoka continued answering Brix’s questions.

“As for how I ‘got the gig,’ I did it the same way almost everyone does. I participated in clan delves on my days off until I got noticed by one of the sponsored groups.” Nyoka gave Brix a predatory smile. Ferris couldn’t decide if she was trying to be encouraging or not. “Just be better than the rest, and I assure you the clan has people who will notice.”

“That means,” Brix said, slowly, “they have people reporting on us in the clan delves.”

“Of course,” Nyoka said, “every Adept in the clan, retainer or otherwise, has a file. Expect group leaders to evaluate you afterwards, which affects who gets picked for future runs. If you make trouble, be prepared for fewer opportunities.”

At this, Brix just smiled back. Somehow, it mirrored Nyoka’s smile from earlier: sharp and promising danger.

“By the same coin,” Brix said, “I just have to show them my strength. I’ll make trouble all right. Trouble for anything in my way.”

Cyrus, who hadn’t been able to get a word in yet, couldn’t resist responding.

“Less trouble,” Cyrus said, “for both the delver groups and for me, is what you should be working on. It doesn’t help to kill everything in the room if you can only do it once. Not to mention risking everyone around you.”

“Well,” Nyoka said, “that isn’t the worst problem to have. Big and flashy can help you get noticed after all.”

“Please, Adept Nyoka, I beg you,” Cyrus exclaimed, overly dramatically, “don’t encourage her. You’ll be the death of me when she blows us both up.”

“Too late,” Brix said, laughing back at Cyrus, “turns out I just wasn’t going big enough yet.”

Karl just placed a hand over his face, sighing to himself.

“Adept Nyoka,” Ferris said, “it sounds like you have been with Clan Hollowfield a long time. I was told retainer contracts last ten years; did you renew it?”

“Yes,” the woman answered, “I originally joined to pursue an interest in alchemy.” She placed a hand on Nyofu. “My family has a strong tradition for familiar bonds, but little knowledge outside of delving. I only expected to stay for ten years, but I made friends here… and they sweetened the deal.”

“Then…” Ferris didn’t want to be insensitive, but it sounded like she had abandoned her family. “What about the restriction to only participate in Clan Hollowfield delving groups? Do you get to see your family?”

“I live a ten-minute walk from the house I grew up in. I can still see them anytime I want. There is also more freedom in the rules, after you have been with the clan for long enough. None of the rules forbid me from supporting or being a part of my family, as long as I protect Hollowfield’s secrets and interests.”

Ferris nodded, although inside he wasn’t convinced. There had to be more to it than that. Maybe… politics? Was she a bridge between her family and one of the powerful clans of Scalefort? Or was it simply the advantages of being part of a sponsored delving group? Perhaps her own family couldn’t offer the same.

Or… worse yet, perhaps her oath contract had dug deep into her soulspace. Had her loyalty been slowly and subtly converted away from her family by the binding oath?

“Do many retainers renew their contract?” Ferris asked.

“Yes, if they are given the chance. It’s a sweet deal if you can get your foot in the door. Of course, not everyone is offered a renewal.”

It was quick, but Ferris noticed that Karl frowned at that comment, before controlling himself. Karl was usually much more experienced and knowledgeable than anyone else at Lily House; was he worried about his retainer contract? Ferris knew the man had been delving for years, but not much else.

“You practice alchemy?” Brix rejoined the conversation, pulling her massive metal box up onto the table. “This is my bonded cauldron. With this, I can concoct and shoot out a highly flammable alchemical liquid in the middle of battle.”

“Interesting,” Nyoka stood up from the table, then walked over to inspect the cauldron closer, “and this battle concoction is the attack ability you mentioned before?”

“Yes, although everyone says it’s overkill for a starting ability. I’ve been working on a way to slow it down and keep the reaction stable between fights. That way I could fire short bursts, instead of having to empty the entire tank at once.”

“But, if you did that,” Nyoka looked from the cauldron to Brix, “then you would lose your powerful attack.”

Brix grimaced, looking as if she tasted something unpleasant.

“They won’t let me delve unless I have a reusable ability,” Brix said, although some of the fire had gone out of her eyes.

“How do any of the alchemists that bond a normal cauldron do it?” The woman asked, taking the tone of a teacher. “There are other ways to get an offensive ability, without weakening your strong point.”

Cyrus’ face shone with barely contained energy. The young man carefully looked away, meeting Ferris’ eyes briefly before pretending to be interested in something behind Karl. Of course, none of them believed Nyoka didn’t know the answer to her own question. She studied alchemy herself, after all.

From Cyrus’ reaction, he was on the verge of winning a long-standing argument. Or at least he thought he was.

“They make potions beforehand,” Brix answered, “I want to learn to do that too, but it would have taken time and resources away from my combat alchemy. I prioritized a plan to get one acceptable attack ability to start delving, and then use the additional resources from the rifts to support my growth.”

Ferris listened to her response and couldn’t help but guess there was more to the story. Maybe… she can’t do alchemy like normal? Or at least not yet. Brix’s non-standard cauldron had potential combat advantages… did it have trouble doing traditional alchemy? If she does have such a weakness, Ferris thought, she is crazy to talk about it. Even indirectly, like this.

Although maybe Brix just needed more time and experience with alchemy. Hemon had warned that it would take at least a year of study to meet the ‘basic alchemy training’ requirements for a clan job. That better matched what her mentor had advised: focus on alchemy and wait to delve.

Either guess could be true, or elements of both. Ferris wanted to help Brix, but looked at Nyoka. She was the one with experience, after all.

“Then,” Nyoka said, “now that you have the powerful attack, you can focus on making an ability to concoct a more traditional offensive potion. That will give you the best of both to use in the rifts.” The woman either didn’t have the same guess as Ferris—that standard alchemy wasn’t possible for Brix yet—or she chose to ignore it.

“Ugh,” Brix grunted, “that is just back to what Elder Tarr told me to do, spending months learning a new ability. I should be able to delve with the ability I have. It creates a lot of very flammable liquid quickly, but it’s only potent for about an hour. If I create it ahead of time, it degrades too much.”

Ferris was starting to better understand Brix’s problem. The ability she’d picked to develop first had been rejected. Her mentor told her to start a new one. She was ignoring that advice—or maybe couldn’t follow it—and trying to adjust her current attack to qualify and start delving.

In a sense, he understood her logic. She was starting a new path with abilities no one had seen. She hadn’t known the limitations of her current attack beforehand. Usually, the first ability took a long time to build in the soul, but it should’ve let her delve. That would’ve earned her resources to make the next one faster. Instead, she was stuck back at the beginning.

“Maybe, something in the middle?” Ferris said, hesitantly. Everyone looked at him. He wasn’t used to having real advice worth sharing in these discussions, so it took a moment to gather his nerve. “Instead of changing the ability, could you change how you use it? I’ve heard of an ability that stores magical acid in a vial mid-battle.”

The details of the ability were a secret, but everyone here was a retainer of Clan Hollowfield. It was just a description of an ability’s potential use from a list of options at the library. It felt fine for Ferris to share.

“Other alchemists take hours—or all day—to produce a single potion,” Ferris continued, “but it sounds like you can do it much quicker. Could you concoct the liquid inside the rift, and store it in vials for throwing over multiple fights? Then you could have the big attack ready for when you need it, while restocking the thrown vials between fights when you run out.”

“It… might work.” Brix considered. “I would have to figure out a way to fill the vials, instead of spraying it all out the hose. I’ve been throwing a torch afterwards to ignite the liquid. Maybe I could combine the vial with something to ignite it.” Brix looked back at her cauldron. “Yes, that could work.”

“We will dress you up with a bandolier of glass vials,” Cyrus said with a grin. “We can hang even more from your cauldron; your enemies will learn to fear the sound of glass clinking at your approach.”

“I’ll hang you from something,” Brix replied, but her heart wasn’t into it. She had a far-off look, getting lost in her own thoughts.

“I’d recommend scheduling a meeting to discuss it with the delving certification group,” Nyoka said, as she returned to her seat, “you at least have a chance.”

Dinner passed quickly after that. Karl asked Nyoka for some advice on optimal resource farming, but the names and locations they discussed went over Ferris’ head. Nyofu finished off her bowl of… brown balls, and proceeded to watch Jade. It was hard to view the snake’s gaze as anything but predatory, but Ferris tried not to be too intimidated. Jade was oblivious to the attention and happily asked for an additional helping or three.

Ferris enjoyed the food. The stuffed peppers were more of a sweet variety than spicy, but their filling really made the dish shine. It was a mixture of rice, roasted tomatoes, ground meat, onions, and a lot of garlic. The sides were less exciting, but plentiful.

One by one, the other residents of Lily House finished eating and departed. Once they had all left, it was just Nyoka, Ferris, and the two familiars.

Ferris could hardly contain himself. This was what he’d been waiting for all dinner. A chance to finally talk with another cultivator who had bonded a familiar.

“Adept Ferris,” Nyoka said, “Mother Lily asked me to visit tonight for your sake.”

Ferris nodded silently, feeling the weight of both Nyoka and Nyofu’s gazes settle upon him.

“I heard you have some questions for us.”
