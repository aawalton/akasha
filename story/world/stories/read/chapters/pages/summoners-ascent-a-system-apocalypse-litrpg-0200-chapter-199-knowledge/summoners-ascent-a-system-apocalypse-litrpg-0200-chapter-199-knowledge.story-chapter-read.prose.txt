"Oh, this? It's this pretty neat thing I bought from one of my summons. It's called a 'book'. Cool, right?"

Clementine grabbed one of the pillows from behind her and threw it at Ashton.

"Fuck off," she scoffed out.

Ashton dodged the pillow and smiled, flipping open the book. "It's written in another world's language, so you guys won't understand it. I can translate it for you if you want, though."

"Huh?" Sam leaned forward, surprised. "What, do you have a skill that translated languages?"

Ashton raised an eyebrow. "Of course not."

"Then how can you read it?"

"An acquaintance showed me the basics, and I figured out the rest from context clues after a while. Same as with these other ones. Except for Arcaean—this one here—I just flipped through a few dictionaries with that one."

Sam groaned loudly. "So what, you're just a language expert now?"

"Kinda."

"How many languages do you know?" Clementine asked curiously, leaning forward in anticipation.

Ashton thought about it for a moment. "Hm... I won't include monster languages, since those are kind of iffy and I only know a couple phrases from those. So without those, I want to say... 47? At a fluent reading level at least. Speaking is a whole other deal, that's just 19. A lot of languages require specific organs that I just don't have. Though I figure I could pick them up if I did."

"Go fuck yourself, what?" Sam let out, confused. "47 languages?"

"Lumia had different countries and languages too, you know? As I travelled, I just picked some stuff up," Ashton explained. "Learning languages is kind of shockingly easy when you can remember everything anyone has ever said to you."

He flipped the book in front of him open again and continued to read with the help of the looking glass.

Clementine leaned her head onto her hand. "Why are you so nonchalant about all this stuff? It's kind of awesome that you know like 50 languages."

Ashton's hand stuttered lightly as he moved his hand over the paper. "It would be if it didn't take me more than a century of suffering to learn those languages."

Startled, Clementine raised her head again. "Right, you're right, I'm sorry. I didn't–"

"It's okay," Ashton sighed. He flipped the page extra carefully, trying not to accidentally rip it. "It wasn't all bad, I guess. Especially..." He smiled sourly. "The Salesman had an item for me that some old friends of mine prepared for me. While I couldn't buy it yet, I was able to read the messages they left alongside it. For me, it's barely been a few weeks, or in some cases months, since I last saw many of them. For them, it's been thousands of years, and they still love me so much. The fact that my time in Lumia was enough to form those kinds of connections is fucking insane."

Sam laughed and leaned back, pushing his hands on the ground behind him. "Who'd have thought that the awkward kid who couldn't even talk to his childhood crushes without me would befriend gods."

"Well..." Ashton let out, not sure what expression to make. "It's not like they're really gods. I wasn't the one to make contact with any gods directly. Their 'aspects', the parts that were actually summoned, sure, which is technically like an extension of them, but I'm not sure if that translated to any bond with the actual gods in question."

Sam and Clementine stared at Ashton.

"I was... uhm... I was saying that as a joke. You've actually met gods?"

"Again, not directly or anything. But it's really not a big deal."

Clementine scoffed. "How is that not a big deal? Like, gods are real? Isn't that kind of crazy?"

"Did you not know that already? My friend Oliver—you met him, right?—summoned one of earth's primordial gods during his occult ritual. But honestly, I think you guys are sort of putting a bit too much weight onto the title of 'god'," Ashton pointed out, flipping the page again.

"Okay, wait, so, as the daughter of someone who is now a cult leader, I feel like I have to ask; what are gods, exactly? Like... if they really exist, then what are they?"

Sam nodded in agreement. "Right, are they just 'born' someday, or did they always exist?"

Ashton thought about it for a moment. "Hm, to be honest with you, I don't know the specifics, but it's that model of 'belief'. Some gods are fully brought into existence by the amalgamation of thought of conscious people, others were beings that were empowered by that same thought. They either die or lose their power if they lose that belief, though true 'death' for a god is sort of not really a thing. It's usually more like a type of sleep."

"So... if we all gathered together and tried to think really hard about a new god, we could just make one?"

Ashton scoffed at Clementine's question and shook his head. "No, not directly. It needs to be a deep system of faith and belief and trust that is imbued into your very soul. You can't just 'think' a god into existence. You need to believe in one so hard that you would offer a part of your soul for it to be true. But if that's really the case? It's shockingly simple for gods to become a thing. Not all of them are particularly powerful. There are gods that barely have more power than the elementals. The strongest of gods can be compared to the Elder Spirits, or to the Monarchs of Elementals."

Sam laughed nervously. "Wait, so, uh... what about gods like, you know... that are thought to be omnipotent? Are they really? Will they wake up again and fucking kill us all?"

Ashton shook his head, laughing. "No, of course not. Those wouldn't even be really omnipotent. For one, people as a whole are deeply unimaginative about that thing. If you can't grasp the concept of omnipotence, how can you believe in it? And two, gods' powers are almost completely contained to their own domains. Beyond that, they can only interact with the world through believers, or summons I guess, which automatically limits their powers."

"Okay..." Clementine slowly started. Ashton already knew what she was going to ask about. "So then what about the aforementioned cult leader? What happens when people think that my dad is a god, and they start to genuinely believe it?"

"He might gain some minor divinity. You know, some special power connected to what his believers think he can do. But it's not going to be anything insane. If he can hold onto that divinity for long enough, he will probably metamorphosise into a god," Ashton explained. "But overall, that's really not the biggest threat here. Him being a Marked is much more dangerous than a divinity gained through the faith of a few dozen people."

He faced his friend. Even if he didn't have eyes, he wanted her to know that he was taking this conversation seriously. "I do need to ask," he started. "Have you thought about what you want to do?"

Clementine clenched her fist and bit the inside of her cheek. "I don't know... I..." she forced out, slowly placing a hand in front of her mouth. "I've thought about it a lot. My father was a dangerous man even before this, but now? I know that he can't be left alone, but I..." Clementine looked at Ashton. Her mouth was open, but no more words were coming out. She just forced a quiet breath.

"I get it. I really do. But I promise, for the time being, if he doesn't do anything that I have to intervene with, I will do what you ask in regard to him," Ashton said. If it were up to him, Jonah's body would already be torn apart by scavengers. He knew in almost every way that just killing that monster born with a human's skin would be the right choice. And even if it might be scummy to leave that choice up to her, it was something that Clementine had to decide.

"That's exactly the issue, though," Clementine replied, her voice more quiet than before. "I know you will... I know that if I ask you to... to do it, you will. And that makes it even harder to actually make a choice, you know?"

"I know."

"That's why, I did want to ask you, but... can you come with me there? To see my mom and dad?"

"Why do you even ask? Of course I will. But preferably, it should be before the next quest can start. Camps will become a system-supported thing then, and I don't want to deal with any weird sub-quests popping up," Ashton explained. "Just tell me when, and we can go."

Sam sat back upright. "Wait, but what about Millie while you're gone?"

"Until she gets a new class, she's staying here. After that, I'll be taking her out with me to help her level up," Ashton said as he flipped to the book's last page.

"Seriously? But she's–"

"Just a kid? Yup," Ashton interrupted Sam, shrugging. "But I'm teaching her magic specifically so that she can stay relatively safe. She won't have to put her own body in harm's way. And I'd rather she learn how to deal with monsters now when they're this weak than have her be forced to deal with the ones that are coming out after this."

Sam and Clementine groaned. "Did you just call the monsters that are out there right now weak?"

"Well, sort of. There are exceptions. The draconid. The wyvern, obviously. But for the most part, yeah, they're pretty weak compared to what's coming." Ashton shut the book and placed it aside, quickly grabbing the next one.

"We can barely keep up with these ones..." Sam groaned, burying his face in his hands. "That weird flesh that took over like a whole fucking block of buildings and keeps growing, those weird robot-looking things, and not to mention all the new weird variations of monsters... Ashton, did you know that yesterday, someone saw a goblin trying to rap? Like genuinely, it was rapping. Like what the fuck?"

Ashton held back a laugh. "Sounds like a good time. But hold on, I heard about the 'flesh' thing or whatever, but robots?"

"Right, yes. I actually wanted to ask you about that, because they're really weird." Sam grabbed his bag from behind him and rummaged around in it. "They're strong and attack everyone around them, but they're not aggressive the way other monsters are. When you leave their area, they don't really chase after people. They're super sturdy as well, we've only managed to take out a handful of them. Most of the parts were basically rock and scrap metal, but this piece seemed interesting."

The moment the piece was held toward him, Ashton let go of the looking glass. He ripped the object from Sam's hand. Something about this object's mana must have distorted what he could see.

That's what he was hoping for, at least. But when he held it in his hand, and felt the grooves and engravings, and the bumps and pieces of magic stones that were embedded in different parts of it, Ashton felt his own heart give out as he clutched the 'Arcane Heart' tightly.

"Those fucking–" Ashton ground his teeth together.

"Wait, what? What is it?" Sam asked, nervously glancing at Clementine.

"Was it called an 'Ancient Construct'?"

"Uh... yeah. Yeah, it was. Why? Oh, does it have something to do with your summons? Is there another–"

Ashton smashed the Arcane Heart on the ground. Not on purpose, of course, but his anger clouded his mind.

He stood up from the ground and walked out of the tent, approaching the railing. Ashton really needed some fresh air right now.

"Ashton, what's happening?" Clementine asked, rushing after him. She reached out her hand, but pulled back. Something stopped her the same way you couldn't just make yourself touch a hot stove.

Ashton pushed his hands down on the railing. It was beginning to creak loudly.

"Sam, I told you about them before. About Oka."

"Oka? Was that the name of your..." Sam slowly glanced at Clementine. "... of your... partner?"

Ashton slowly nodded. There weren't really words to describe what they were. Friends, lovers, companions, partners... soulmates? Whatever it was, no word seemed to quite match up.

"I told you how they weren't from Lumia either. How they fell into that world from their own?"

"Right... what about them?" Sam seemed confused, at least for a few moments. The railing bent underneath Ashton's grasp. Sam remembered. "They're the things protecting those ruins, right? The ones that Oka investigated?"

Ashton nodded.

Karros, the world inhabited by Oka's people, had fallen victim to the council the same way that earth had. And since those beings showed up as monsters... "They failed to protect their world."
