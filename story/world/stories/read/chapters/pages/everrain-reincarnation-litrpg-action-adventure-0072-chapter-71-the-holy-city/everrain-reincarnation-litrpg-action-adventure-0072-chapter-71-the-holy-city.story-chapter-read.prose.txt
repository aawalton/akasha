Day 356, 2:10 PM

The capital is much larger than Tallrock and Deephorn, the central part of the city is the same fortress town, looming over a walled city which had sprang around the original citadel. The outer walls, however, are much smaller and humbler, built to give an illusion of security more than anything else. As for its defensive ability, it wouldn’t stop a lesser abomination, let alone a true threat.

“I would like to scout it, but anyone coming and going is probably subject to great scrutiny, especially with nearly one hundred and fifty inquisitors going missing.”

Edna says nothing, and the girls have firmly decided they want none of the decision-making responsibility for this excursion.

“I know a spell which would let me share eyesight with a chosen creature, but there are problems.” Those words pique Edna’s interest. “For one, I doubt they have bugs scuttling around town, and the second, even greater issue is that the inquisitors would almost certainly notice the aura of magic.”

I keep examining the settlement while talking. The huge lake fed by the fort’s deluge is on the north side of the city, overflowing into a slow, wide river, which snakes out of the city further to the north. There’s a hole in the wall in the middle of the river, and a plan forms.

“Edna, we can march your tree soldiers through the water for a while without drowning them, right?”

“I don’t know.” A look of panic flashes on her face as she stutters. Without neural alterations, she is a wreck of nerves most of the time. I wonder whether that’s because our power dynamic changed or because she was always like this behind the construct she had made.

“They will take a couple hours to walk upstream, some of the leaves sticking out of the water. They should be fine.” Edna shoots me a ‘why-did-you-ask’ look, but I keep talking. “Lucy and Gila, you two should stay here, safely hidden until the battle is over. We’ll either come for you, or you will have to flee.”

I pass a tome three fingers thick to Lucy. “These are the level requirements for all the mage classes, the spells one should learn to reach the peak of the mage class, and detailed instructions on how to become an archmage of two paths, matter and life.”

I don’t tell her that there are instructions on the last page with how one can open Hadriuse’s secret lab, in which I left the level up requirements for the archmage level up conditions, at least the ones I know about, as well as Hadriuse’s original grimoires, which should prove invaluable to the girls.

It’s quite likely we will die at least once, and I want the girls and my child to have the best conditions possible even in the timelines in which Edna and I fall.

“We should wait until nightfall before making our move, but whatever the outcome, the fate of the nation should be decided tonight.”

Their moods range from worried to afraid, and while I can’t blame them, I don’t share their sentiments. I am a conqueror. An anarchist. My blood boils at the thought of the imminent battle. There is a monster sleeping inside me, and I have been aware of it a long time, but it’s been even longer since I unleashed it in a proper tempest of violence and destruction.

My breathing quickens, but I force myself to calm down. We are hours away from the life and death battle, before the only thing that made me feel alive other than Manny.

“Let’s go, Edna.” The woods behind us rustle to life, branches and leaves shaking as hundreds of trees move not on legs, which seemed logical to me, but on tank treads made of roots. While odd, the means of locomotion has proved incredibly silent and surprisingly quick for something as large.

We take the long way around and reach the river without incident. Tree after tree creeps into the water as the light dims and rain intensifies. The train of tree soldiers heads for the citadel, and Edna and I rush to arrive before they do, so we could handle any surprises.

The evening downpour drowns the light of the covered fires atop the walls, the visibility so poor, I don’t see the point of stationing sentries, save to punish insubordinate guards and soldiers. We stand before the wall. I touch the timber, and with a slight exertion of my will and mana, it warps to let me and Edna through.

We wander the empty streets, both our scanner spells dismissed long ago. In fact, there’s not a bit of magic leaking from us.

“Your aura is even more subdued than mine,” Edna whispers. “And yet, you obviously knew nothing about magic when you first appeared here a year ago. How did you do it?”

“I told you, I’m an old monster. If you give me enough time and access to a library or two, I can do things which would make your mind implode. We can discuss those questions after we’re done here.”

She nods and keeps silent as we advance down the stone-covered streets between the uniform rows of stone buildings. I finally wonder whether the absence of plant-life within the borders of settlements is intentional or an accidental byproduct of combating the constant rainfall and the endless water.

Whatever the case, it denies archmages of my and Edna’s path their advantage in manipulating lifeforms. If we were truly deprived, we could transmute the slumbering humans into contagious zombies or something equally sinister and let them deal with the inquisition, but I would never do that. Even if it’s a fairly safe and cost-effective way of dealing with our enemy.

I entertain various wild thoughts, scenarios I could try in a redo loop, then restart, assuming I’m sufficiently criminally insane, but my morality is still strong enough I wouldn’t slaughter civilians for the sake of experimentation.

These aren’t my thoughts. Something is pushing me in the cruel direction for some reason. I don’t think it’s the thirst for power. In my previous life, I was an absolute monarch, declared a living god by some, and I held everyone’s life and death within the palm of my hand. Yet, I was benevolent.

I wished for my people to be happy and my family to be happy. Even with absolute power, I lived a relatively subdued but comfortable life. No, two things have changed. Either magic has warped my mind, or the Guide is doing it, and ever since I first interacted with it, I considered the Guide an aberrant concept.

If traits BSD has assigned to me can influence the way I and those around me think and act, the Guide should have the same ability. But there is a ‘we’ behind it. An intelligence, whether active or lingering, woven into the fabric of magic pervading the minds of those who have entered the dungeons.

We reach the inner wall while I’m busy, thinking about how to extricate myself from the false system this world’s denizens use to interact with the BSD. The tree soldiers aren’t here yet, so we are forced to wait in silence.

Forty-five minutes later, the first one emerges from the water, then another and another.

“Direct them to climb, Edna.” She does, and I watch as the tank treads unspool into roots, which wrap themselves around the trees and stab into the wall, seeking cracks between the massive blocks of stone. We hitch a ride to the top, where all is dark on the massive battlement.

The area is pitch black, wide enough for four cavalry to charge abreast, large enough for dozens of tree soldiers to gather. The city is dark beneath our feet, and I don’t dare augment my vision with magic to take in the sight.

Blood rushes through my veins so quickly my hands tremble with excitement. The battle is near, and I believe the Guide is enhancing my already existing thrill, turning it into bloodlust. I will need to fight conservatively—.

Edna touches my shoulder without warning. The gesture startles me, making me flare with murderous intent.

“A hundred have climbed the wall, we can start.” Edna’s voice is surprisingly calm. It’s as if the two of us have changed roles.

“The sooner the better.” A tiny surge of mana hits my eyes, changing not the organs themselves, but the light hitting them, amplifying it until the world seems as bright as it would on a clear day.

A massive white temple, topped with a polished dome, occupies the center of the citadel, easily covering a quarter of the district. The building has four entrances matching the cardinal directions, each with a hundred steps of white marbled with gold.

The rest of the area is populated with mansions, at complete odds with everything I have seen so far of Everrain’s stark, utilitarian architecture, which could be surmised as boxes with doors. For the first time, I have seen a garden inside the walls. About a score of them imitated the style of Hadriuse’s abode, with plants sporting multi-colored leaves to draw the eye.

Edna’s treants descend the wall, moving like a tidal wave of gargantuan octopuses, ready to swallow the opulent world below.
