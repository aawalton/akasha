Tuesday November, 1st

I’d stayed over at Kate’s Halloween night. I’d planned on ubering home but Kate’s headache was really concerning me. She’d dealt with headaches before but even though she was hiding it fairly the whole thing was concerning. On our way back home, she’d become pale and sweaty. I helped her into bed, got her a glass of water and a sports drink.

The next morning we sprawled across her couch, both of us a little worse for wear. There were coffee cups with various stale levels of coffee strewn across every surface where they were promptly forgotten the moment they were set down.

Curtains were drawn against the offending sunlight and a tangle of blankets and pillows were our solitude against being awake.

“Last night was fun,” Kate murmured into her pillow.

“It really was. Probably the most fun I’ve ever had.” Replaying the moments from the karaoke bar in my head I smiled. So many fun things. All of the costumes, the singing, the laughing. Honestly, just being there, present with Kate in that moment was phenomenal. I’d spent so much of my time worrying about the next project, and it had become painfully obvious that I’d spent far too little time enjoying my life.

The thoughts of all the people that Greg had hurt crossed my mind. If I didn’t do anything, would he get what was coming to him sooner or later?

“Kate,” I asked.

“Mmmm?” came a grumble from under a pillow.

“Do you ever think about karma?”

She lifted her head and the pillow she was hiding under fell to the ground. “Like whether people get what they deserve or not?”

Chaos took this as his moment to intervene. He jumped up onto Kate and began headbutting her hand.

“Yeah, I guess.”

“Sam, this feels like a trap.”

“It’s not a trap. I was just thinking about things. Like what happens to people who do terrible stuff. Do they get away with it forever?”

Kate absently pet Chaos. “Is this about work?”

“I don’t know, maybe. I was just thinking, does the universe balance it out if no one does anything.”

“I’m fairly certain there’s an investigation going on right now.” Kate yawned. “So I’m sure Daniel will get what’s coming to him.”

“What if there was real evidence of really terrible stuff? Like would anyone actually do something about it?”

“Other than the paycheck?”

I nodded.

“Hypothetically?”

I nodded again.

“Depends on the evidence, I guess.” Kate yawned. “And who had it and what they did with it. Like, even Martha Stewart had to go to jail for tax fraud.”

“Wasn’t it basically glorified house arrest?”

“Oh, I have no idea.”

“So what would you do if you were the only one with evidence of some really bad stuff against… Martha Stewart?”

“Hypothetically?”

“Yeah of course.”

She laid her head back on the couch and was quiet long enough that I thought maybe she’d dozed back off.

“I don’t know, Sam. I’d want to do the right thing but it’s hard to answer without the situation. What’s the right thing? What if blowing the whistle creates a butterfly effect that makes things worse for everyone?”

“Like what I did to Daniel?” The gloom and guilt in my voice wasn’t hidden very well.

“I didn’t say that.” She rubbed at her eyes. “Daniel’s situation is weird. Would I have handled it so publicly like that? No, probably not, but I wasn’t in the situation to know how I would have handled it. You didn’t make things worse for everyone. Daniel made things worse for everyone. His entire plan was rough at best, eventually it would have come out. You just sped up the process.”

“So you wouldn’t do anything? Let karma handle it?”

“Sam, what’s going on? Did you stumble onto something? Tell me there’s not more to this story. I don’t think I’m cut out for all this. I have had such a headache this month. Although last night did not help my headache at all.”

“I’m just thinking out loud.”

“Well, think a little quieter. I can barely hear myself in my own head as it is.” She threw a pillow at me, or at the chair five feet away. Either way, she missed.

“Can you turn on something mindless while we wait for our pizza? I don’t think my brain can handle any more philosophy at the moment.”

The latest season of the show we were watching was behind a paywall. After making sure Kate wasn’t watching, I dove into the app and unlocked the premium plan without commercials before I queued it up.

Kate fell asleep before the pizza arrived. I stayed awake and turned her words over in my head. Would it all come out eventually? Maybe it would. How many more people would get hurt before that happened though.

I looked at her sleeping face. Eyebrows furrowed as she moaned in pain. I wanted to tell her, but at this point involving her would probably be too much. The most painless way for everyone would be if he confessed himself. An idea began to brew–what if I could make him confess?

After pizza, I made my way back to my house. It was early afternoon, plenty of time. I cracked open my laptop and activated my code vision. Within minutes I’d made my way back into H&S systems and onto Greg’s computer.

He was at the computer looking over some proposal. I took control of his mouse and opened a text document and began typing.

Me: Greggory Harrison Jr. I hope I have your attention. If you are thereI would highly recommend responding before calling anyone. Type here in this document if you understand me.

Greg: What is this some kind of prank?

Me: This is no prank. This is very serious. I understand you had an issue with your home network the other day.

After a few minutes of no response I went ahead and continued. I could see his VOIP llit up and he was on hold with IT.

Me: During the time of your outing several very interesting things came into my possession. Does the email [email protected] mean anything to you?

Me: How about your insurance folder?

He responded immediately.

Greg: I’m listening.

Me: Good. You should hang up that phone.

The phone line cut off.

Greg: It’s off. What do you want

Me: I want you to confess.

Greg: When I find out who this is, I will ruin you and everyone you’ve ever known and loved.

Me: It seems as though our story begins with boarding school.

Greg: How much money do you want

Me: I don’t want money. I want you to step down from your position and I want you to confess.

Greg: one million

Me: Time is ticking.

Greg: What could you possibly do that would be worse than confessing.

Me: I could make it so that you have no chance of ever getting out.

Greg: What if I don’t believe you

I dimmed the lights in his office. I began playing Somebody’s Watching Me by Michael Jackson from his phone. Every line in his office began to ring. I streamed his house cameras to his computer screen.

His wife was sitting at the counter in his very large kitchen, monitored by a camera I doubted she even knew existed.

All at once I turned them all off.

Me: Do you believe me?

Greg: What am I confessing?

Me: You will admit to everything you’ve done to people at H&S, you’ll reveal your embezzlement, and blackmail. You will send a letter to every board member, and the following list of local news reporters. Containing proof of your confession.

I sent a list of the big names from both sides of the political field along with emails and phone numbers.

Me: You will make a public apology to all of those you’ve hurt. You will step down from your position

Greg: And then?

Me: And then you’ll have to deal with your consequences.

Greg: What about the rest of it?

Me: That’s your choice.  I hope you make the right one. You have until 6am.

Greg: That’s not enough time

I didn’t respond. Instead I erased everything in the chat except for the list of people from the press.

I sat back in my chair and cut my connection. I had pushed myself too hard. When I stood up I immediately threw up.
