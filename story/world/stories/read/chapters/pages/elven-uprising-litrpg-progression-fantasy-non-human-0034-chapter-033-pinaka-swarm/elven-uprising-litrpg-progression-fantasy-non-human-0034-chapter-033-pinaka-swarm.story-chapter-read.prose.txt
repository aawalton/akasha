39th Day of the Eighth Moon; just before dawn, Pinaka consumed his first World Tree Fruit in his new role as a potion factory. The effect was immediate—his stats jumped.

[Name: Pinaka][Race: High Elf][Authority: Lifeform][Control Factor: 2][Weight Factor: 34][Volume Factor: 2][Range Factor: 20][Speed Factor: 4][Creation Factor: 2][Spell: Status Epidermis, Root Glove]

On the 40th, 41st, and 42nd days of the Eighth Moon, and again on the 1st day of the Ninth Moon, Pinaka consumed four more World Tree Fruits. Each time, he digested just a tenth of the fruit—enough to push his stats higher—while concentrating on developing the spells he’d need to act next.

The result was a massive jump in power.

[Name: Pinaka][Race: High Elf][Authority: Lifeform][Control Factor: 3][Weight Factor: 68][Volume Factor: 8][Range Factor: 22][Speed Factor: 22][Creation Factor: 62][Spell: Status Epidermis, Root Glove, Wood Ski, Pinaka, Pinaka Arrow]

Using the Spell Root Glove on each foot activated both units of his Creation Factor. In that state, whenever Pinaka tried morphing the skin on his hand using his power over flesh, he gained experience rapidly—especially while digesting a World Tree Fruit.

That bump in activity pushed his Control Factor to 3, and Pinaka wasted no time putting it to use. With a Range Factor over 22, he could scale up and down walls effortlessly, letting him escape the prison grounds in no time.

[Spell: Wood Ski]

While traveling, he used the spell Wood Ski, tapping into all three units of his Control Factor. One was used on the moving foot. The other two were on the anchor foot—one to create a wooden base in front of it, and the other to break down the wood behind into leaves.

This way, as Pinaka glided across the forest floor, he left behind only a quiet trail of leaves, blending seamlessly into the already leaf-covered ground.

Deeper in the forest, Pinaka set up a camouflaged archery range. Dense foliage hid it from sight, and the area was fully sealed off to keep any sounds from escaping.

Inside the lava moat, each forest section stretched no more than a couple of kilometers wide. Their borders were sawed off daily and tossed into the lava, but the vitality of the soil—nourished by the World Tree—let the forest regrow overnight.

A few Elves were assigned to help maintain the growth, but most of their work happened during the day. Pinaka trained there at night, returning to the prison only when it was time to consume a World Tree Fruit in his role as a potion factory.

His clones trained nonstop, slowly pushing up his Creation Factor—the stat that was toughest to raise.

Scouting, training, eating World Tree Fruit—it all became routine, and through that rhythm, Pinaka built up his strength.

From the office, he picked up clues about Nancho’s summons for all the officers at Ogre Gate—things like the gathering date and other details. He pieced together that the situation was getting dangerous for the Humans, so he stationed a clone inside a tree near the ash road. The trunk had a small hole, covered by a loose piece of bark. The clone kept watch through it, waiting to act on D-Day—the 1st Day of the Ninth Moon.

It pulled it off—killed the messenger and stole the bike’s Sun Stone. That was Pinaka’s ticket across the lava moat.

Using one stat of his Control Factor, that clone began heading toward Pinaka.

Meanwhile, Pinaka used the remaining two stats himself.

[Spell: Pinaka Arrow]

He created an arrow with a spine running through its shaft—his own spine, crafted right after he’d formed the intent to expand the arrow mid-flight. That way, the spine retained the exact command even after it was fired.

Pinaka pulled the bowstring first, then activated the Pinaka Arrow spell, all while thinking about making the arrow grow. Without hesitation, he released it.

If his thoughts weren’t crystal clear at the moment the spell was cast, the spine wouldn’t carry the command. And even if the thought was flawless, the arrow had to be fired instantly.

Because the moment it formed, it started expanding. If he hesitated even a little, the arrow would’ve grown too large before launch, slowing it down due to the extra weight.

Pulling off a move like this took real skill. In this case, the only thing carrying remote authority was the spine—so this was the hard limit.

If Pinaka could cram his entire nervous system into an arrow, he would’ve done it already. That clone would’ve been able to follow every command freely, without the limits he was dealing with now.

‘Good, I’m doing some damage to the carriage,’ Pinaka thought. The arrow shot out at a speed of eighty meters per second. Fired from 200 meters away, it would hit in 2.5 seconds.

[Creation Factor: 62]

That meant he could generate 62 kilograms of wood every second. He kept one stat for himself, and another was with the clone rushing toward him. That left 60 full units of Creation Factor packed into the arrow’s spine.

By the end of its flight, the arrow weighed 150 kilograms and stretched five meters long. It smashed into the target with brutal force.

‘We’re about five kilometers from the Human Gate. These sounds won’t reach that far.’ It wasn’t just a guess—Pinaka had tested it the day before using a similarly loud blast.

The dense forest swallowed all sound, making it the perfect cover.

The Human Gate had never been attacked, so its security was the weakest. Pinaka had picked a section of the ash road wrapped in thick forest, exactly five kilometers out.

It was the ideal spot to hit Pronto. The logic was simple.

The Ogres were attacking the Ogre Gate with urgency—enough to make Officer Nancho call in every available Officer from the Elven Prison. ‘According to the documents, it all began after Pronto showed up.’

There were no written details about the cause of the conflict, but the clues were enough. ‘He’s a Prince, not tied to the Elven Prison. Rachad clearly wasn’t happy with him being here.’

‘For some reason, Pronto came to the prison. And even though he’s a Level 2 Human, he stayed quiet—kept his head down like he had no business interfering.’ That was Pinaka’s take.

Since the Ogres were launching a full-scale assault, it was obvious—Pronto had taken something from them. Either a prized Ogre... or a Relic.

A Level 2 Ogre was valuable, sure—but not valuable enough for the entire Ogre Race to go to war over. ‘Most of the Elven Prison is built with stone walls. That means there’s already a Level 2 Ogre enslaved here. So that alone wouldn’t set them off.’

A Level 3 Ogre? Pinaka shook his head. The power jump between levels was massive. Even if Pronto seemed unusually strong for a Level 2 Human, there’s no way he could handle a Level 3 Ogre—let alone capture one.

‘So, a Relic.’ That’s where the answer landed.

The Elven Prison had a Dwarf. Mahnaka had told him that on the first night after his birth.

Level 2 Humans could launch themselves forward using flame thrusts. And metal—being excellent at conducting heat and having high melting points—was perfect for that. Putting two and two together, Pinaka figured that Humans used metal vehicles powered by flame thrust.

On Day 40, he spotted a large metal carriage transporting grain through the Human Gate. It moved using Flame Thrust. ‘As Mahnaka said, the first generation of Elves reincarnated from Earth told the Humans here a lot about automobiles.’

That sealed the theory—Pronto’s automobile was damaged, and he’d come to the Elven Prison to get the Dwarf to repair it. The way Rachad and Pronto interacted gave Pinaka enough confidence to go after him. ‘The Ogre Race’s Relic must be with him.’

Boom!

Another log of wood slammed into the metal carriage, denting it deeper. Right after impact, Pinaka pulled back the Authority granted to the spine inside the log. He instantly redirected it into the next Pinaka Arrow he was crafting.

Seeing flames flicker out from the metal carriage, Pinaka brought his index and thumb to his mouth and whistled. He’d regenerated his thumb earlier that morning after digesting his piece of World Tree Fruit.

At the whistle, a line of figures stepped out from the thicket of trees. They were all built the same, matching Pinaka’s height exactly. Clones—each carrying his memories from a couple of hours ago.

[Spell: Pinaka]

The spell created a near-perfect clone of himself. If he didn’t have to divide his Authority between them, he could summon as many as he wanted.

With Pronto’s route known in advance, Pinaka had arrived early. His clones acted as scouts, racing through the forest at insane speeds using Wood Ski. The speed wrecked their bodies, but they were clones—it didn’t matter.

Each time a scout returned with info, Pinaka would make a new one, pass Authority to it, and send it out again. That’s how he stayed updated, planned, and moved in real time while tailing Pronto.

Puff!

One of the impacts had warped the metal carriage’s door so badly it couldn’t open. Heat blasted against it, melting the hinges. A soldier inside kicked it loose, sending the door flying.

He had just leaned out when the dull evening light lit up the crowd now surrounding the carriage.

“Im... Im... Impossible!”

Swoosh!

All of Pinaka’s clones were armed with bows and quivers. They stood ready, bows drawn, and the moment the soldier stuck his head out, five arrows were loosed.

“Shi—” The word never finished. An arrow pierced straight through his skull. His body was yanked back into the carriage in a panic.

“Move!” came a sharp command from inside—young voice, angry and urgent.

“I can’t!” an older voice snapped back, thick with frustration. “Our wheels are damaged!”

“Then go out and kill the enemy—” The voice cut off abruptly. Pinaka had just landed silently on a tree branch nearby and activated his power.

Beneath this part of the ash road ran a series of hollow spaces, shaped like rectangular boxes—each one eight cubic meters in volume, formed naturally by roots.

The tree he touched was the anchor. Its roots shaped the boxes right below the metal carriage. With a single command, the roots turned to sawdust. The weight of the carriage crushed through the weakened earth, collapsing the box underneath.

The oldest trick in the book—a pitfall.

“Argh!” Multiple screams erupted from inside the carriage as it dropped four meters into the hole—deep enough to trap it completely.

“Be ready to shoot,” Pinaka ordered his clones as he drew an arrow, aimed it at the carriage entrance, and let it fly. At such close range, the arrow hadn’t had enough time to expand before breaching the doorway.

“I’ll draw them out like rats now.”

And then, inside the carriage, the arrow began to grow.
