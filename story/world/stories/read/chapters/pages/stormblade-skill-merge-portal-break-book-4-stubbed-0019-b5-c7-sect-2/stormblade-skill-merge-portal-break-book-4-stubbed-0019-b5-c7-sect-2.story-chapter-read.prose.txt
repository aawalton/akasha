Ellen couldn’t help but shiver a little.

It wasn’t just her lack of power. She was pretty confident she could still do enough damage to beat any of the A-Rankers outside—and to give this Tanat woman a run for her money. Her core was damaged, but not as badly as Kade’s had been when they’d entered Queen Mother Yalerox’s portal. She’d been careful with it ever since the end of the Siege of Phoenix. That wasn’t enough time to recover, but it had stopped it from getting worse.

It was a general…ick. The entire sect felt…it didn’t feel right. She couldn’t put her finger on it, but it felt corrupt somehow. As Kade and Tanat discussed their deal, she tuned them out and tried to focus on why she felt the way she did.

And in the end, as it always did, it came back to Bob.

It was always Bob.

What Tanat had described sounded like so many black-tie and evening-gown events she’d accompanied her father to or helped him host. The auction. The celebration. Bob had always put on a mask for events like that, and so had Ellen. The gracious daughter, the perfect hostess, or even the observant pair of eyes—it didn’t matter what the role was, she’d played it. But so had everyone else. The black-tie gala with the silent auction was as much a time of political and economic deal-making as it was a time for flutes of champagne and string quartets, and everyone was playing the same game she was.

Ellen hadn’t been stupid when she was growing up. She’d seen it. She’d participated in it, at Bob’s behest. The perfect heir to the Traynor Corporation, right up until she wasn’t.

It was all the same—probably right down to the alcohol and formal wear—and Retan was at the center of it. He was the Bob here.

And then there was the Eye of the Storm.

That made Ellen uncomfortable on a whole different level. Queen Mother Yalerox had set it up as her grand weapon. She’d been setting a trap for Angelo Lawrence, and with the full power of the Eye of the Storm behind her, she’d have succeeded in killing both him and his team of A and S-Rank delvers. So, of course, Kade had it now.

Kade was fine to have it. He’d need it—and while she hadn’t been conscious for it, she knew exactly how he’d use it. It was fuel for an exceptionally powerful Stormbreak, or for his new spells.

If he was in the process of rebuilding the Eye of the Storm, it could only be because his core and the Queen Mother’s were one and the same now. And it would explain what she’d seen in her visit to the Black Tower. The skeletal metal frame. The lightning collectors. The feedback into the storm overhead. Kade was—somehow—reshaping the Black Tower to host the Eye of the Storm. Even so, it was a pale imitation of the storm over the sandstone castle.

He’d need a lot more firepower for it to be a viable weapon against the God of Thunder—or he’d have to accept a less powerful version. And inviting Tanat to share in its power felt like diluting it even further.

Ellen could only hope Kade knew what he was doing.

Tanat was silent for a while as she pondered my offer.

The cool breeze from the hallway and the covered garden beyond it ruffled Ellen’s hair, and I looped an arm around her for a quick hug.

“You’re sure you know what you’re doing?” she asked.

I shook my head. “I have no idea.” Around us, the simple, undecorated room seemed almost too quiet. The sounds of fighting outside were muted by the cavern and the garden walls, and if other people lived further down the cave in the other doors, they weren’t around right now. I tried not to tap my fingers on my knee as I sat, cross-legged, on the mat and waited for Tanat to come to a decision.

Eventually, she did. “That will be sufficient for now. I will share what I can of the sect so you can prepare yourselves for tonight’s celebration. But first, you’ll need to pay my friend separately for your partner’s surgery. The person I have in mind will want information.”

Right. Information. “Let me guess. They’re A-Rank.”

She nodded curtly. “And they’re stuck outside. Their Lonely Portal is too much for them as they stand right now, and the process of using Paragon cores to reach S-Rank is considered stunting. If you shared your method for reaching S-Rank with them, that would be sufficient.”

I swallowed. That method had been given to me by the God of Thunder. I didn’t know if it would work for the Path the Soaring Sabers followed. Then again, I’d be putting Ellen through it as soon as possible, so I was certainly willing to risk it. “Done—after Ellen’s core is repaired. So, the sect?”

“Yes, the sect. The Soaring Sabers have been here, according to Retan, for several thousand cycles. In that time, he has trained the teachers who instruct the lower-ranked students outside. Those students will be joining us for the auction—or at least, the most skilled and most wealthy will.

“Those students are after weapons and tools to allow them to beat their Lonely Gates. The first several items will be fiercely bid on, as the ones who still hope to reach the Inner School the traditional way look for edges against the monsters inside.” Tanat didn’t meet my eyes, but I knew exactly what she was thinking. She’d spent a fortune at auctions just like this one, and in the end, her best edge had been me. “Expect swords, armor, and the formulae for different Scripts, Bindings, and spells. We’ll be ignoring those items.”

“How often do students need help clearing their Lonely Gates?” I asked, suddenly suspicious about that, too.

“Frequently. It’s rare for even the most skilled of the outer school to reach S-Rank without purchasing at least three items. I bought two,” Tanat said.

I motioned for her to continue, but as she talked about the types of students who’d be bidding in the opening rounds—and about the fights that were almost inevitable when an item’s price got out of hand—I focused more on my suspicions. It was becoming more and more clear that not only was Retan playing the ascension game, he’d also found a way to increase the speed he could play it at.

“After the weapons—most of which can be bought for anywhere from a single B-Rank core to multiple A-Rank ones, or even a single S-Rank one—Renat will open his core vault. Each of those starts at an S-Rank core in price, but they typically increase rapidly from there. No single student ever has enough to guarantee themself the core they need, so politics and alliances come into play. That is how we’ll acquire your S-Rank core.”

“Oh?” I asked.

“I know a few students who require a Paragon of water’s core. We can trade the specific core for their assistance,” Tanat said.

“And how would that help us?” I asked. “We’d be down a core.”

Ellen nodded, though. “You’re offering the core they need for the one we need, but the real offer is in future cooperation between you and them, correct?”

I glanced at her as Tanat nodded.

“I figured. That’s how situations like this usually play out. Some of your allies have way more wealth than they’d normally need, but cores like this one are relatively rare compared to the number of students competing for them, right? So, a guarantee is worth more than my shadow core would be.”

“Correct. You clearly understand the game,” Tanat said.

“I’ve been playing it most of my life.”

“Great. You’ll come with me to the auction, then. Kade Noelstra, you will have to stay behind. The dueling grounds will be crowded.”

My head shook, and I stood before I even knew I’d done it. “I don’t think so, Tanat. Ellen’s under my protection.”

“I swear to you she will be safe there. Bringing a single honored guest would be acceptable, but two? That would only draw attention. And she understands the game.” Tanat said it like it was already settled, and as Ellen wrapped her hand around mine, I slowly nodded.

“I have concerns about the whole process here. You gave your core to Retan in exchange for S-Rank, but he’s going to charge at least a full Paragon Core for someone who needs it to buy it? And what do they need it for if the core method is so stunting?” I asked.

Tanat nodded. “It is stunting, but Lonely Gates are challenging, and many students don’t have my patience—or my luck. At some point, they take the easier way—coming up with enough money to bid. S-Rankers use the Paragon cores to craft their own weapons as well, or for other projects. They’re highly sought after.”

“How many times do you think Retan profits from the same core?”

For a moment, the room went silent.

“What I mean,” I said as the moment stretched, “is that there’s a reason Retan is doing what he’s doing, and it’s not for your benefit as students. He strikes me as—“

“As Bob,” Ellen said.

“Enough,” Tanat said at the same time. “I understand your concern, but the cost of keeping this portal world running is said to be extreme. Does your world’s authority keep cores for themselves?”

“They do, but—“

“Then you understand, on some level, the cost of leading an organization or a government. Just keeping things together costs more than I have ever owned, every month. I believe Retan is working for my benefit, and the benefit of the other sect students.”

I sighed. “Just…keep an open mind, okay?”

Tanat nodded and stood. She held a hand out to Ellen, then pulled my partner to her feet. “I will if you can, Kade Noelstra. This world is not the same as yours, and it would be a mistake to treat it as such. Please stay here. I will get your partner ready for tonight’s auction. Do not take this the wrong way, but neither of you smells correct for this world. Quite honestly, you both reek.”

Ellen stiffened for a second, and I snorted. But inside, I was seething. The evidence was starting to stack up. “One more question, Tanat. How many outer and inner school students are there?”

“There are currently several hundred in the outer school—at A-Rank—and fifteen from the inner one, plus the teachers. There are a few dozen of those. Then, higher up the cliffs, there are lower-ranking pupils living and learning in the cliffside gates.”

“Thank you,” I said, and as she took Ellen with her, I returned to the mat on the floor, crossed my legs, and did something I hated.

I meditated.

Meditation had always been Jessie’s go-to for managing pain—when she chose to manage it on the bad days instead of trying to push through it, ending up overwhelmed, and shutting down under a blanket. It had always been a little frustrating for me, even if I knew what its purpose was.

But the reality was that, until the auction started, I had nothing better to do. Ellen and Tanat were committed to ‘getting ready,’ whatever that entailed. I knew what it’d look like for me back on Earth. Ellen would pick out a suit from the collection she’d assembled for me, get me looking good, and hit me with the right cologne or whatever. It wasn’t a priority for me on Earth, not when Ellen knew the rules better than I ever could. Right now, Tanat was probably playing the same role for Ellen.

I also didn’t know how long it’d take for the two suns outside to set and the auction to start. If it were several hours, it’d make sense to slip out of here, rejoin the duelists in the sand circles, and try to level up a few skills. But if it were more, I’d need to find something else to occupy the time, and if it were less, I’d be there when the auction started.

I couldn’t risk that.

Not for what I was planning.

So, as Ellen and Tanat did whatever they were doing, I put my mind to the problem at hand.

Retan was obviously taking part in the same game that the God of Thunder and the Placid King were. Two days ago, I would have asked Eugene for advice. He probably would have intervened to remove a competitor from the board. But today, I was on my own. Even worse, any attempt to reach out to him would only alert him to my plans. No. It was better to be as invisible as possible.

So, I had several problems. First and foremost, I needed Ellen’s core fixed. That was the main objective today. Nothing else mattered until that mission was accomplished.

But second, and almost as important, was Tanat. She could be a viable ally. We’d fought alongside each other, and she’d saved my life at least once. I wanted her on my side, and I couldn’t do that as long as she didn’t understand what Retan was trying to do—or why there were so few inner school students compared to either teachers or outer school ones.

After thinking about it for a long time, I opened my eyes and waited for the two of them to leave. As soon as they did, I’d be doing a little work on my own, with a single goal.

I would find evidence of what Retan was doing, and it would be good enough to get Tanat to leave and join me at the Black Tower.
