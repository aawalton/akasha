More than one deity… I repeated the words internally. I was frustrated that finding Lilith would be far more difficult than I’d hoped, but I was also puzzled. I didn't understand how two deities had managed to share the same Authority.

It wasn't a question I could ask V without exposing my ignorance - I had already shown more of my vulnerabilities than I wanted to. So instead, I activated Guidance.

[Authority & Domain]

Two deities cannot hold the same Authority. However, an exception may occur if an Authority holder is inactive for a long time and a creature from their domain manages to ascend.

To ascend, a mortal must meet the requirements set by the Authority’s owner and receive a portion of that god’s divinity to accept their servant’s mark. However, if the god refuses or is unable to grant divinity, the ascended being is freed from the god’s influence and obtains a portion of the Authority instead.

Verde, be careful when letting your believers ascend. While strong, loyal subjects are a great help, if your mark on them isn't absolute, they may one day challenge you to steal your divinity. For a mortal to become a true deity, they must ultimately defeat their god.

My mind numbed. None of my believers were even close to ascension, but the reality that they could eventually become enemies was a bitter prospect. I was supposed to protect, nurture, and strengthen these creatures; the idea that they might one day turn against me was a fundamental contradiction.

I forced the thought aside. I couldn't dwell on it while V was still present in my realm.

I had at least confirmed that the second holder of the Sanguine Authority wasn't a true deity. They had secured some divinity, yes, but they weren't on my level.

This could work.

If I made it seem like I was interested in the Authority itself rather than its holder, the God of Merchants wouldn't be able to suspect anything.

“I want information on both,” I said. I kept my voice flat, bordering on the bored tone of a being discussing something beneath his notice. “The true owner of the Sanguine Authority is my primary interest, but since this demigod has claimed a portion of it, I’ll buy their information as well.”

“We are happy to sell the information about the… demigod,” V said, his voice low. “But anything concerning the true God of Sanguines can not be shared. Regardless of the price your Excellency is prepared to pay, that information is not for sale.”

“I expected more privileges, given our business relationship,” I tried.

“That is exactly why we refuse, your Lordship. My Liege values the trust of our clients above all else. Should another god attempt to buy information regarding you or your angel, they will be met with this same refusal. It is a core rule my Liege has established.”

Yeah… I’m not buying that.

Still, his tone and choice of words made it clear he wasn't trying to increase the price; he had simply drawn a line. The God of Merchants had indeed decided not to sell information about Lilith no matter how much I offered, and I wasn't going to waste my time on a futile negotiation.

“Should we begin gathering the intel on the demigod?” V asked, breaking my train of thought.

“Yes.”

I was a bit surprised by the delay. I had assumed V wouldn't offer the deal unless the information was already in his possession. If the intel existed, why did he need time to "gather" it?

Perhaps it was in another avatar’s possession and V was unable to communicate with them the way he communicated with the God of Merchants.

The theory was too flawed. Unless the transaction was too insignificant to warrant his direct intervention, the God of Merchants could have simply passed the information. I shook my head. It was pointless to dwell on the matter.

I didn't keep V in my realm any longer, and he seemed just as eager to depart. As the avatar vanished in a silver sheen, I turned my attention back to my angel.

When I told Aurelion I had a quest for him, I had intended to send him to deliver the new scrolls to the Velmoryns and warn Karla to feed the eggs her formless mana. But now, I hesitated. The brief chaos in my mind, while extremely dangerous, had revealed the extent of my untapped potential. I had scanned two thousand artifacts and checked on a velmoryn outside my forest in mere seconds.

That specific velmoryn was the source of my hesitation. He was a Gold Rank, and he possessed intelligence regarding the outside world that was too valuable to lose. Letting him die would be a massive waste of an asset.

But sending Aurelion was a risk. I had to consider how the other deities would react to my angel encroaching on their territory.

“My King,” Aurelion called. He stepped forward, the plates of his armor rattling. “You seem troubled.”

The wounds on his body had already vanished. The dark steel plates were pristine once more, and his scarlet wings trembled with the anticipation of a quest.

I looked at him. He had shielded me and endured significant damage without a word. And even after the pain he must have suffered, his only thought was that he wasn’t good enough for me. While I didn't care for this aspect of his personality, one thing was certain - Aurelion was loyal and reliable.

I decided. For the first time since becoming a god, I would be entirely honest with someone.

“I want to save a velmoryn who might be executed soon,” I said. “He is in human hands. My concern is that sending you may provoke the other gods into moving against us.”

Aurelion’s wings stilled as he considered the problem.

“What if I remain hidden?” he asked. “If I am seen, I will kill them and reap their souls. Their god will never know it was your servant - unless they are watching at that exact moment.”

That’s what I thought, but it’s too risky.

Asking Aurelion to perform a stealth mission was like trying to lead an elephant through a crowded street unnoticed. If he could control his divinity, I wouldn't be concerned. But Aurelion was a brute; he was built for combat, not concealment.

Wait. Why does it have to be Aurelion? Why not send someone unmatched in stealth and speed?

His suggestion had merit, and I used it as a foundation for a better plan - one that mitigated the risk of divine retaliation.

“Take Lucas with you,” I commanded, meeting his gaze. “He must rescue the prisoner alone. You are to reveal yourself only if Lucas is in grave danger and has no chance of survival.”

“As you command, my King,” he rejoiced, the feathers on his wings shifting. A moment later, he vanished in a swirl of crimson energy, teleporting directly to Lucas's side.

As I watched Aurelion, I realized that my most powerful subordinate, my angel, had been reduced to a mere messenger. While no one else possessed the strength and speed required to transport Lucas to the prisoner’s location in time, tasks like delivering magic tomes to Karla or an amulet to Mel were far beneath an angel’s station.

Previously, I had excused my clumsiness and lack of creativity as the growing pains of a fledgling deity. But I was on a different level now. It was time to act accordingly. While Lucas and Aurelion traveled toward their destination, I decided to resolve my lack of a proper messenger once and for all.

The first thing that came to mind was my favorite serpent. But using a creature as magnificent as a basilisk for deliveries made no sense. It clashed with the creature's nature and was inconvenient. Sending a towering beast to deliver a small trinket was simply stupid.

I needed something no larger than a velmoryn, with a humanoid physique to carry items and the ability to speak. However, I couldn't simply manufacture a random creature; it had to fit into my lore.

I took my time to recall the symbols I had connected myself to.

My earliest self-image, a cloaked figure holding a book, belonged to a time when I intended to be an unknowable god. A mysterious deity. But that was before I understood how this world worked.

My very first statue is a mistake.

That image no longer fit into my lore. My symbols had evolved into the crimson oak, the basilisk, and the angel. I had also brought the kelvarins into existence.

As I recalled the creatures that I believed were connected to my essence, I thought of a creature that would inevitably become my subordinate - Pintre. And then i remembered Kurraghal. The forest king I intended to claim as my own.

I’ll create a being similar to both. A dryad-like creature with the ability to enter my realm and roam the mortal world with equal ease.

I liked the idea, but I lacked the materials. While I could forge them from pure divinity, the results would be far more potent if I used actual matter. I was about to command Tekla to sacrifice some of Kurraghal’s remains to me, but at that moment, Lucas and Aurelion reached their destination.

I shifted my focus. This quest was too important to leave unmonitored. Beyond that, I wanted to see how my believers' powers fared against humans in a true trial of strength.
