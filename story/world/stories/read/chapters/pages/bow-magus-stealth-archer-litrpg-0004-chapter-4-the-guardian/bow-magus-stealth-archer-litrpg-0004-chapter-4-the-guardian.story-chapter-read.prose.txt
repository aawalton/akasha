The moment the Shrine’s countdown ended, Rex was pulled out of his world by magical tendrils and dragged into another. And it took him a dizzy moment to gather himself.

Blink and shaking away the daze, he found himself in a circular arena marked by glowing runes of the same color as the Shrine. And at the opposite end of the arena stood a monstrous mound of bulging gore. It stood nine feet tall on stumps of bleeding flesh, and two pinpoint red eyes glared out from oozing folds that formed its monstrous head.

“Are those things meant to be your limbs?”

The monstrosity took a step forward, and the stumps it walked on squeezed out blood with a sickening wet sound.

“That’s… that’s fucking gross.” Rex winced and shied away briefly.

Pulsating veins wrapped around the meat creature, and its chest visibly thudded. When the creature bent back its head, it gargled and spat blood from a mouth lined with several rows of razor-like teeth.

Rex forced himself to watch. As gross as it was, now wasn’t the time to get squeamish. He had a job to do.

“You look… friendly? Oh, whatever. At least I’m fairly confident nobody is going to miss you. Time to die, monster.”

He nocked an arrow and steadied his breathing. Rex aimed for its heart, or whatever it was making its fleshy chest beat, and fired.

He had many years of experience firing bows and hunting gerbils. But since then, he had gained 2 Strength, a significant boon for a rather skinny, malnourished sixteen-year-old. And so when he drew back on the bow, it felt effortless. So effortless that he drew it back in a second, and caught by surprise at his own strength, accidentally loosened the arrow. And it flew wildly and errantly off target.

“Well, it’s a good thing you're bloody slow," Rex murmured as he nocked another arrow.

Prepared for what was coming, the second landed true.

"Fuck!” He hissed as the arrow struck the meaty construct, piercing just deep enough to remain embedded in its chest. “I forgot to inscribe the arrow!”

“Third time's a charm!"

Activating his spell, Rex inscribed the arrow, mana-linked it, and released.

The arrow flew through the air, and once more, it only pierced a couple inched deep when it struck. But this time, it released a sickening hissing sound as the wound around it bubbled and melted away.

It worked. It actually worked. The “touch” spell worked exactly as he had hoped. And the implication of it sent his imagination spinning widely, and his lips bent into a shit-eating grin.

"How'd you like—wait, that's it?”

The corrosive damage had melted a hole a few inches deep and several wide, and completely dissolved the arrow. But then it had stopped. Sure, it had left behind a pretty gnarly wound, but he saw no organs or anything else likely to kill the thing quickly. Furthermore, the monstrosity didn’t seem to care, nor had it slowed more than it already was.

Luckily, it’s already slow.

Rex ran back a bit, creating more distance between them, and nocked another arrow. Swinging around, he picked his target and loosened. The result was much the same. He melted another hole in the monstrosity's flesh, but that was it.

“It’s gotta be doing something!” Rex muttered and drew back another arrow.

Once more, he focused on his Skill, and a rune appeared on the arrow shaft, and he felt the thread of mana linking it to him. But this time, it felt a little different. Like, there was less of it.

So, that’s what mana feels like! Great, just great. I'm obviously going to run out of mana long before this ugly bastard melts away.

He doubted blood loss was going to work either. The holes he’d melted away were pouring out less blood from the creature than what it naturally lost walking around. In fact, the acidic damage he had caused seemed to sear the bleeding monstrosity’s skin closed.

This wasn’t going to work. At this rate, he’d either run out of mana or arrows well before the guardian died. He couldn’t just keep firing arrows. He needed a plan.

Rex started running as he tried to think of something else. He needed to find a weak spot, but he hadn’t seen anything. And when he turned to look over his shoulder, his gut curdled.

The monstrosity leaned back, and the stumps it used as legs split in half with the cracking sound of breaking bones and tearing flesh.

“That is—” Rex swallowed down what had tried to come back up and shook the feeling away. “Is it trying to…”

Flesh tore apart, and the stumps were gone, leaving behind four thin, sinewy legs, wrapped with exposed muscle. The grotesque creature fell forward, testing out its new legs, pounding its meaty stumps into the ground.

“A meat centaur?”

The monstrosity reared up again and flailed about, causing blood to spurt from its snout. Then, it landed back on all fours, bent its head menacingly at Aaron, and galloped straight for him.

“Fuck. Big fucking fuck.”

The guardian might have been slow before, but it certainly wasn't now. And at that moment, Rex really regretted not picking [ Extra Step ].

So, this is why you pick a defensive spell, is it?!

He could have punched himself in that moment. But he had more important things to do.

Rex ran as fast as he could, but within a second or two, it was undeniable that the crashing and sputtering hooves behind him were getting closer.

Shit! It’s going to get me! I didn't go through all of this just to die here!

That wasn’t going to happen, he resolved. He had put up with the bullshit of Dorog all his life. And he finally had an opportunity to put it behind him.

Rex pushed himself to run faster, just enough to get a few more feet of space between them, and he jumped into a spin. Momentum carried him, and he swung around, drawing his bow.

Such a move would have been impossible before he gained his Class. But between his weak bow, his improved Strength, his enhanced Agility, and Perception, it all came together.

And in that moment, he knew why Classed were different.

It only took him a second to notice a flaw in the meat centaur’s design. Gone were its thick, stumpy legs, replaced by thin, muscular things, and they made the perfect target for [ Corrosive Touch ].

He marked his arrow and loosened all in an instant. The distance between them wasn’t great, and the arrow covered it in a split second, slamming into the monstrosity's knee.

The reaction was immediate. Unlike the thick flesh that covered it elsewhere, his arrow melted through the creature’s knee in an instant. When its hoof slammed down against the ground, it was completely compromised, and its entire leg collapsed in on itself, sending the monstrosity toppling forward and rolling into a skid that sent it barreling over itself several times.

Bones snapped, and flesh tore as the mishapen form contorted with each roll. It might have had cushioning against Rex’s arrows, but that cushioning did little to stop its heavy frame from crushing itself. If anything, it just added more weight.

“That did more damage than my damn arrows. Oh, whatevs. At least it worked,” Rex said, drawing another arrow as the sprawled and gargling monster tried to find footing on its broken legs.

He took a step forward, aiming, bowstring pulled taut as he looked for a good target. The creature slipped and fell back down. It looked pathetic, but he was still low on mana and didn’t have unlimited arrows.

“C’mon. Give me a good shot and let me end your misery.”

The monstrosity roared in response, spattering blood, and Rex spotted his next target. In an instant, he released, and his corrosive arrow flew down the creature’s throat.

The monstrosity shook, then went quiet for a second. It lurched forward, then lost its footing again, and fell sprawled across the ground as it began to violently cough.

Rex winced and stepped backward. Blood and gore were pouring out of the creature’s mouth, and with each cough, it seemed to get a little smaller. As if coughing itself up.

“You really are gross, aren’t you?"

The grotesque creature jerked back and forth as if dying. Rex relaxed. It was over, but then suddenly, it lunged for him.

Rex fell back over himself in startled surprise. He kicked his heels into the ground, and he shuffled back and away from the creature as it tried to crawl after him, its bloody maw chopping at empty air.

“Get away!”

In a show of defiance, it lunged for him again, grasping hold of his pants with its spiked teeth. Luckily, they were cheap miner clothes, and quickly started to rip as the creature pulled on them.

Within seconds, the pants were unravelling, like a ribbon pulled from around his legs. Then they were almost gone, and he could have gotten up and run away. Created some distance and went back to shooting arrows. But he got an idea.

Rex reached out and grabbed his torn pants just as the monstrosity was about to pull away the last inches of cloth, and marked them with a spellcircle, linking them to himself through a thread of mana.

Immediately, he jumped back to his feet and started running. As he turned back, the monster had just finished devouring his pants.

“Oh, you like that, do you?” Rex smiled and activated [ Harden ].

The monster's eyes widened for a second, and then it started to cough and splutter again. It got even smaller, coughing up bones and gore as it shrivelled up like a salt-covered leech.

“Shouldn’t go eating other people’s pants now, should you?”

The monstrosity tried to lunge forward again, as if responding to his mockery. But it had nothing left. It limped forward, but it was struggling to do anything, and when Rex fired another corrosive arrow into it, the creature hissed as its flesh melted away.

“Oh? So you can feel pain now, can you?”

His arrows were clearly doing more damage than before. But it was still big and bulky, and looked like it still had some life left in it as it tried to wiggle toward him.

Rex went to fire another arrow, but when he tried to inscribe a spell rune, nothing happened, and he felt a little weak in the knees.

“Outta mana. Shit.”

He didn’t want to waste unempowered arrows that were likely to do nothing. And then he remembered Damo’s rusty sword he had stuffed away in his cloth belt. He figured that the sword might just do the trick and help finish the monster off.

He drew the sword, waited a moment for the writhing creature to still for a bit, and stabbed into its pulsating flesh. And again, and again.

“Fuck. You’re not easy to kill, are you?”

Rex released his grip on the sword and wiped sweat from his brow. And the moment he did, the damn thing rolled over. He watched in horror as the sword snapped under its weight and was buried beneath it.

“Hey, give me that back!” Rex pushed the bloody mound, but it wouldn’t budge. It just croaked and trembled.

“Ah, dammit! Damo is going to kill me! You’ve really done it now!”

Rex was straight up pissed off now. He had promised to get the sword back to Damo by tomorrow, and that was impossible now. Enraged, he drew back on his bow—

—and it broke. Finally, his old, shitty makeshift bow snapped against his newfound strength.

“Seriously?!” Rex shouted and kicked the guardian. It just kind of groaned a little, but didn’t do anything. “What now?”

He looked at his broken bow and got an idea. It was already beyond repair, and he marked it with a spell, then smacked the guardian. Corrosive damage sizzled against its flesh, melting away more of it. But Rex had already been dangerously low on mana, and pushing himself to cast another spell was too much.

He felt his knees buckle, and he fell backward. He would have been toast if the guardian hadn’t already done for itself.

“And you’re still not dead? Really?”

The guardian was one hardy bastard. And it held on for another two hours before the death throws started.

“You and me both, buddy. You and me both,” Rex said as the creature shook, and he heard it croak out its last breath. By that time, he was just glad to be getting out of there.

Dancing light started to float up in motes from between the floor tiles, and a moment later, the monster’s corpse began to glow.

The Goddess Effyana has acknowledged you.

You have been blessed in the eyes of the gods with a Class, granting you the ability to pass freely through the Gates and access to the System.

You are now free to leave the Shrine.

Are you ready to continue your journey?

That’s it? No experience? I guess it was the guardian, and I got a Class out of it.

Magical energy was flowing all around, but before taking the goddess' offer, he kicked the corpse. It was really, really heavy. Even after it had coughed most of itself up, it was still the size of a bull. And even empowered with the strength of his Class, he doubted he was moving it.

“I think it’s broken anyway…”

He didn’t want to leave Damo’s sword behind, but was saving a broken sword worth covering himself in monster guts? Besides, he wasn’t even sure it was possible to move the corpse.

“Yeah, nah. Not happening. Sorry, Damo.”

Rex accepted the goddess’ message, and a second later, he realized that he was standing on the podium, back on Dorog.

“People?” He said, looking at the busy line of prospects waiting for their turn at the Shrine.

“I can see your balls!” Someone called out.

“Oh, shit!”

His hands dropped down to cover his manhood in an instant, but it was too late. He had gone and flashed everyone in attendance.

“Sorry.”

He held his head as low as it could get as he quickly rushed by. It was not how he imagined his post-Shrine celebration going. However, that thought reminded him that he had a Class.

“I’m fucking Classed!” Rex cheered, throwing fists into the air as he jumped on his way back into town.

“Look away, dear!” A man said, shielding his wife’s gaze. “Have some decency, and don’t go waving that thing about!”

Sure, everyone in town seeing his ass wasn’t ideal. But this was the first day of the rest of his life, and once he left Dorog, he wasn’t coming back.

But what about Damo’s sword…

The sword wasn't a reason to stick around on Dorog. But Damo had been decent to him…. Well, decent enough. And he wanted to repay him, regardless of whether or not the merchant had the ability to collect on debts once he left.

And pants… I definitely need pants.

Back at home, he dressed himself and packed a bag with whatever meagre belongings he had left. Which was very little.

Damo didn’t need to know about his sword just yet. Once he passed through a Gate, he could go anywhere. He could join an adventuring party and earn some real coin. The kind people on Dorog could only dream about. And then, paying off a rusty sword would be child’s play.

But where to first? One day, he hoped to build a big house and live on Elysia. Maybe even settle down with a nice elven girl. But right now he needed work, and there was no better place than the City of Adventurers.

Of course, Tartarus. I’ll go straight to Tartarus. From there, picking up a contract should be easy.

One day, he would get to Elysia, but for now, he had debts to repay and contracts to win. He would buy a new bow, some clothing, and then set his sights on grander goals.

Rex grinned as he stuffed the last of his belongings into his bag. It was time to leave this shit-hole.

“Hey, Rex!” A man slammed on the door. “Come join the celebrations! Greg filled his boot up with ale, and is about to chug it!”
