The disembarkation went smoothly, with Skippy and Tillienne being among the first to take their leave for treatment, while many of the expedition members headed to a rented-out bar in the Gray Conglomerate’s ward to celebrate.  Kyle didn’t blame them.  Despite knowing how dangerous the situation could be, it had exceeded their expectations.  Blowing off some steam with a party sounded like just the thing many of them would need.

“So, Tillienne, huh?”

Kyle felt Rochelle’s approach as he waited near the ship’s exit, watching the slow-moving group of D Grades step off to get scanned before being cleared.  He was quiet as she stepped next to him and continued.

“She’s really something else.  Powerful, confident, not to mention beautiful.  Quite the catch, Kyle.”

Kyle snorted.  “If she heard you say that I ‘caught’ her, I think we’d both end up with some bruises.”

“Oh, no doubt about it,” Rochelle teased.  “I just wanted to say that I’m happy for you, Kyle.  And also to not screw this up.  Not saying there won’t be complications, nothing at your level ever seems simple, after all.  But she’s a wonderful person.”

“Thanks, Rochelle.  She is.”

“So, are you coming to the after party?  Should be quite the event, from what I’ve heard.”

Kyle felt a smile tug at the corner of his mouth.  “Not for me, I’m afraid.  Go and have fun.  And thank you for everything you did.  If it wasn’t for your help, especially at the end, I’d have been stuck on that rock.”

“We would have come back for you.  Eventually.  Probably.”

The glitter in her eye betrayed the joke, and she turned to join the shrinking line departing the ship.  He thought back to when he first met Rochelle, and how stern she’d been.  Now, as a D Grade herself, it really felt like she’d taken the time to breathe and grow.  He had no idea what her path would look like if she got into one of the Guilds, but he hoped that she’d keep the free spirit she’d grown into.

Garth finally arrived, carrying a large pack of his own over his shoulder.  He nodded to Kyle, who fell into step beside him.

“Took you long enough,” Kyle admonished.

“Don’t pretend like you would have been on time without C.H.A.D.D.,” Garth shot back.

[SURPRISINGLY, DR. MAYHEW WAS ALREADY PACKED AND READY WHEN THE ANNOUNCEMENT CAME THROUGH.]

“What do you mean, ‘surprisingly,’ C.H.A.D.D.?  I’m not late that often.”

[MR. KULHAVEY WOULD TEND TO DISAGREE WITH YOU, DR. MAYHEW.]

Garth chuckled at the banter as they stepped off the ship, walking through a scanner that checked them for fungal remnants, then sending them on their way.  Originally, they’d all expected to be stuck in a quarantine center for treatment and decontamination, but Devourer’s Domain alongside Randolph’s more sensitive instruments allowed Kyle to neatly clear up the lingering spores.

“Have you heard from Stephanie?” Kyle asked as they made their way toward the teleportation station.  He and Garth had the same destination, after all.  Kertan Six.

“I have.  She and Gwen are doing well.  But both of them think I’ve been traveling too much.  And they’re probably right.”

A pained look flickered across Garth’s face, and Kyle understood.  Between the longer expeditions with Corthian Mining, jobs like these, and now having completed his Independent Licensure, Garth hadn’t had much time to settle in and make a home for himself.  Hopefully, that would change.

“On the bright side, there’s plenty of work to do on Kertan Six still.  Both Marcus and I will be away for a while, so a lot of that will fall to you.”

“That’s the bright side?  You two shunting off all your work to me?”

“Yes?” Kyle said, feeling his smile grow.

[IN FAIRNESS, A SIGNIFICANT AMOUNT OF THE WORK WILL HAVE TO WAIT UNTIL DR. MAYHEW RETURNS.  HOWEVER, THE PLANET HAS MORE THAN ENOUGH PREPARED FOR THE NEXT SEVERAL PHASES OF EXPANSION AND INDUSTRIALIZATION.]

They stepped onto the teleportation platform, and with a few quick jumps found themselves back on Kertan Six.  Kyle stood for a moment, really taking in the open skyline and green surroundings.  It was early evening, and people bustled this way and that as restaurants opened their doors and people wrapped up their day’s labor.  It was small, simple, and beautiful.  Eventually, that would change. The city would grow, industry would expand, and the skyline would be obscured by the new construction.  But for now it was a welcome break from the stony moon and cramped quarters on the Corthian Mining ship.

“When will you be taking off?” Garth asked, already moving toward his residence near the heart of the city.

“I’m not entirely sure, but it sounds like it will be soon.  I’ll at least send you a message when I have more details.”

“Please do.”  Garth paused for a moment, expression growing serious as he turned to Kyle.  “And thank you.  If it wasn’t for you, that bastard would still be out there, and it would have only been a matter of time until he cause more suffering.  Thomas and Amir can rest, now that he’s dead.”

Kyle nodded, letting the words sink in.  Garth was right, of course.  Eventually, DeRosa would have figured out how to expand his infestation.  And then they would have been right back to the same suffering he caused before.  And now, he was gone.

“You’re welcome.  And thank you.  I said it to Rochelle, but I’ll say it to you, too.  If it hadn’t been for your push at the end, we’d still be stuck on that rock.”

“We both did what needed to do, that’s all,” Garth said.  “Take care of yourself, Kyle.  I’ll see you around.”

Kyle watched the man go for a moment, before turning to walk toward the outskirts of town.  It would have been faster to take directly to the air, but he preferred the walk today, taking in the sights of the city a little longer.  He still got nods of recognition and some hushed whispers as he passed people by, but he didn’t mind.  At least, not anymore.  Even reining in his aura as much as possible, without a dedicated stealth skill he could only mask so much.  Even if it felt weaker than average, he was still a C Grade, after all.

Once he was out of the city proper, Kyle took to the skies with Aura Force, and flew at a leisurely pace toward his home.  The chaddling patrols were still following their orders, as were the razorpine trees planted near his residence.  Finally, he got to the front door and stepped inside, only to see his refrigerator open and a diminutive purple form loading a plate with fresh vegetables.

“Jarberry, good to see you,” Kyle said, not so much as reacting to the imp’s presence.

“What, no commentary about what a rude guest I am?” Jarberry replied with a toothy smile, popping a pogrit seed into his mouth.

“And give you the satisfaction?” Kyle shot back with a grin of his own.

“You really are learning,” the imp said as he floated over a nearby chair.  “Now, I didn’t come all the way out here for the food.  We’ve got business to discuss.”

“And what business would that be?” Kyle asked, filling a plate of his own before sitting opposite the imp.

“Pretty simple, you’ve got a trip coming up, and I’ve got a delivery that needs to be made that just so happens to coincide with your journey.”

“Wait, you’re talking about hiring me for a job?”

Jarberry’s smile turned downright wolfish.  “It’s not every day somebody gets an invitation into the Alabaster Court’s heartlands.  It would be downright irresponsible of me not to seize the opportunity.”

Kyle sighed.  Though the imp was a trusted advisor and friend, he was still an enigma to Kyle.  “What is it that you want delivered?”

“Three deliveries, if we’re being specific,” Jarberry clarified.  “There’s a letter I want taken to an old acquaintance, a puzzle box to be sent to a tinker I’ve worked with, and an information packet on a specific set of magical circles for one of the Lorekeepers.”

“And all of this is above board?”

“Of course it is,” Jarberry scoffed.  “No way I’m sending you in as a guest with contraband.”

“Then why don’t you handle it?”  Kyle asked.  “No offense to the Court, but it’s pretty clear you could make your way in without too much trouble.”

“Yes and no,” Jarberry said.  “I try to limit my engagements with the Velgians, at least on their turf.  Some of the jobs I’ve had over the years have involved getting in and out, and any time I make a visit, I’m giving their security more ideas on how to stop me.  The fewer chances they get to study me, the better.”

That explanation actually made a lot of sense.  It was well-known that the Alabaster Court was among the wealthiest of the member nations of the Collective, and was also the most isolationist.  If jobs involved getting in and out discreetly, Jarberry would need to keep his competitive edge.

“Well in that case, I’m interested.  But we’ll need to talk payment,” Kyle finally said.

“What, you wouldn’t consider this a favor for a dear old friend?”  Jarberry said in mock sadness.

“Absolutely not,” Kyle said, giving the imp a deadpan look.

Jarberry’s smile returned, even wider than before.  “Good.  That means some of my lessons are finally paying off.  The trip will earn you a million credits on completion, but I’ve also got a special advance prepared.”

With that, Jarberry pulled a small pouch off his belt, and tossed it to Kyle.  It was light, but Kyle immediately felt a metaphysical weight pressing on his mana.

“Is this…” Kyle began, and Jarberry nodded.

“Yep.  Your very own spatial ring.  You’ve been procrastinating at getting a spatial item ordered, so I took the liberties to get this done.  It doesn’t have the largest storage capacity, but it makes up for it with better concealment and durability.  I wouldn’t try to block any big attacks with it, mind you, but it should hold up against most anything you come up against.  It also has a connection to another spatial vault, which has an anchor that’s also in the pouch.  In case the ring gets lost or destroyed, you can access the items directly from the vault itself.”

“Isn’t that a security risk?”  Kyle asked.

“Not if you bind the ring with your mana,” Jarberry said.  “Once that’s done, you’ll be the only one getting in, unless a real formation master puts in some serious time and effort to rob you.  Most of the folks good enough to do that aren’t going to bother with somebody like you at all.”

“This is incredibly generous, Jarberry.  I don’t know what to say.”

“Start with ‘thank you’ and move on to ‘I gladly accept your job that this is an advance payment for’ and we’ll take it from there,” the imp replied.

“In that case, thank you,” Kyle said, opening the pouch and putting on the ring.  The anchor was a small gemstone that glowed with dull gray light, and as he pushed mana into the ring, he felt a bit of his own energy resonate through the gem.

“Excellent!  Now, one more question for you,” Jarberry said once Kyle was done.  “What exactly do you have going on in the basement?”
