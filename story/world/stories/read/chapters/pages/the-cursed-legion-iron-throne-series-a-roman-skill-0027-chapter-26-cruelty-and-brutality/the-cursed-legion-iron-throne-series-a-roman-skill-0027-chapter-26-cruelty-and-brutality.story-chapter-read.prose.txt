Max sat on the hard-packed earth and stared at his status page. He figured out a strange little quirk that wasn't quite important but gave the system some personality he hadn't known it had. Namely, that the system refused to add the stats and attribute boosts he gained unless he looked at the notifications provided.

Otherwise, he would be looking at the exact situation he was seeing.

A few notifications pinged him from his peripheral vision, but he refused to let them come up. At the start, he had been frustrated with the path he had been set on and the pace of growth he was seeing while being trained by Jonathan. It was completely different from Optio Ignacious's hurtling forward at an impossible pace to push them beyond their limited abilities into new frontiers of strength and power.

Frontiers and training he had figured out and found a perfect equilibrium to keep himself going endlessly.

And now, here he was, sitting on the ground two weeks since his arrival, working on his Mana Manipulation skill with no guidance and only his current understanding, and not enough working out to feel any soreness the day after. If anything, he could have gone for a dozen more laps and felt decent enough to walk back to bed.

It didn't help that Jonathan demanded to know all his skills without exception on the first day.

Max didn't try to hide anything other than the greater effects of the Ledger. They reacted quite strongly with just the information that he could improve and repair broken Shards for himself. There was no telling how they would react to finding out he could merge them into new, and much more powerful skills.

Add on to it how the Centurion nearly slammed Max face first into the ground because of his surname—

Yeah. That's not happening.

Max shook his head. He had more pressing things that concerned him.

The Centurion had turned and left with a single warning on the day he put him here. A serious expression, deeper than normal voice, low whisper, and a hand on his shoulder. All of which was burned into Max's memory, considering how lighthearted the conversation had been between Isador, the Centurion, and his friend Jonathan moments before the change happened.

"Be careful and never get comfortable. Few are as brutal and vicious trainers as Jonathan..."

And yet...

Everything he had done so far was far lighter than anything he had done with the Optio. Enough that he had even gotten a full night's rest, woke up, found out that he was an hour early, and then went back to sleep because Jonathan would have whipped his hide if he made any attempt to train without him present.

That had been made obvious on day one. The promises of cruelty Jonathan had made were not things Max wanted to test.

What it did do was make clear how blessed he had been as an orphan, even with all the horrible things that came with it. Sleeping long hours and getting a full night's rest were luxuries he could only imagine as a normal recruit in this Cursed Legion. A good eight hours of sleep, as ridiculous as it sounded, was a blessing he hadn't known he had until it was completely gone and then given back.

Of course, the training wasn't easy.

Max was still training four times a day, three physical and once with his Mana Manipulation skill. Running, push-ups, squats, ups and down tortures, sprints, and even a few new things he hadn't done with the Optio. The main one was carrying extremely heavy objects that he could barely hold and walking around the edges of the courtyard. Nothing special, just a simple back and forth.

He sighed and looked back at the Ledger screen before him.

It rested at seven percent already. The progress was lightning fast compared to when he first unlocked his Patron Inheritance. He would have needed months to get to this point with Mana Manipulation, making the gathering, manipulation, movement, and control of the wisps of mana, both external and internal, as easy as it was now.

Max frowned as he stared at the big issue he was facing now.

There was a blockage to reach the path that led to the eighth ball of light and the eighth percentage point. One that he could not break through with just Mana Manipulation and Mana Gathering level 1. Max had tried again and again to break through with as much mana as possible but could not dent the blockage that rested there.

He had two theories on why he wanted to test, but was delaying for the time being.

Either he didn't have the prerequisite control to gather enough mana to break through, or maybe there was another state of mana that he hadn't discovered yet. The hint and idea had come from the system notification when he unlocked the Ledger back then.

...

please break through blockages using pulses of charged mana

...

Max read it a few times more to comfort himself. It was either he was inadequate or there was some mystical charged mana he hadn't learned how to use yet. Of the two, he preferred the latter because it meant his potential hadn't been cut short at the very start of his journey.

The conundrum now was that he had no idea what charged mana was, how he was meant to use it, call for it, or if he was supposed to ask Jonathan about it. It crossed the threshold of risk he was not willing to pass for his own safety while surrounded with monsters that could crush him with the desire to see him dead, much less any action they would take. He couldn't imagine what a serious punch would look like.

Max cursed under his breath.

The wall to eight percent sat there and pestered him silently—

A knock sounded out in the courtyard.

Max looked toward Jonathan, who was currently laid out on a lounging chair with a wide-brimmed hat covering his head from the sun. He had been half asleep and shifted when the knock echoed in their otherwise quiet courtyard. Neither one of them spoke to the other much, nor did they have anything to talk about even if they did choose to have a conversation.

The person knocking slammed their fist on the metal gateway.

Jonathan cursed up a storm without getting up or removing the hat. "Go see who's at the door! Bastards won't let me take a beauty nap!"

You've been sleeping all day... was what Max wanted to say, but he kept his mouth shut.

His mentor did very little the whole day. In fact, cracking one eye open tended to be the most amount of movement Jonathan would commit to other than eating, drinking water, yawning, and sometimes stretching like a cat. Nothing else was worthy enough of him waking up or moving, not even the recruit he was meant to be training.

Max's stomach complained as he stood up.

He ignored it. He had gotten too used to eating seconds and now he only had a single meal. Yeah, it had all the recovery boost and mana within it... but it wasn't enough to satisfy him anymore. He was too used to asking for more from the Head Cook, who was all too happy to provide more without hesitation.

Jonathan got exactly what they needed from the cooks and kitchen and not a single grain of rice more.

The person knocked harder this time, threatening to bring the gateway down.

"Hurry up before they break my gate!" Jonathan shouted.

Max hastened his pace. He opened it and was greeted with Optio Ignacious. The giant man towered over him, but even he was not tall enough to breach the height of the courtyard's walls. Said giant had a deep frown and shadows looming over his face. Max stepped back out of sheer shock and fear as the gloomy look was fully leveled at him.

The Optio narrowed his eyes at Max for a fraction of a second before he looked up at the sleeping form of Jonathan. He stepped in without preamble, exposing a figure behind him. Max did not look at the second person as he stepped out of the way and kept an eye on the dark face and threatening demeanor of the Optio.

"The preparations have been completed," said the Optio. He stopped halfway to the napping man. "Everything done exactly as you asked, Lord Jonathan."

Jonathan sat up and removed his hat. He didn't have an ounce of sleep in his eyes as he subjected them to a full, blooming smile that made his eyes disappear. "Perfect. Just perfect. I can feel it from here. The emotions—all of it."

Max stared at the two back and forth during their short-lived conversation until they turned to look at the third figure who stepped over the gateway's entrance stone.

Max followed their lead.

His eyes widened as Joel walked into the courtyard proper.

What's wrong with him? What happened to his eyes? thought Max.

The larger boy's eyes burned blood red, skin pale and sickly, and his shoulders rose and fell as though he had just run a marathon. A raging bull that was let loose and prodded to make angrier and more dangerous. And his eyes were locked onto Max without shifting once to the foreign individual that had, by all rights, deserved more attention considering his sleeping nature and the way the Optio spoke to him like he was a superior.

"You," Joel hissed.

Max stepped back once more. Never had he been subjected to so much hatred and vitriol in a single word before. "Joel? What—"

"Perfect," Jonathan stood up and clapped. His giant smile had not diminished slightly. "He is perfect."

"W-what? What's going on? Why is he—what's wrong with him?" Max asked, ready to fight because Joel was barely containing himself, his muscles flexing and neck tendons showing as he struggled against whatever was holding him still. The larger boy's eyes flashed toward the Optio in askance, begging for permission to attack.

"Wait one moment." Jonathan ran back in with more excitement than he had shown since Max had met him for the first time. He nearly glided into the building and back with two wooden swords of average size, similar to the ones the legionaries carried. "I got them. Here, catch."

Jonathan threw one to Max.

Max caught it first attempt.

"Here, angry boy," he threw the second to Joel.

The flat of the blade thumped off his chest and clanked and clattered on the ground with zero reaction from the fuming, smoking boy. His eyes were locked onto Max. The Optio cleared his throat, causing Joel's eyes to flicker away from Max and give him enough sense to slowly reach down and fumble for the wooden weapon until he finally touched it. He paused for a second and then, with deliberate slowness, he rose with dull blade in hand. His eyes never broke from Max's.

Jonathan nodded, satisfied with what he was seeing. "Go on, Optio. I'll see you once this is over."

"No," Optio Ignacious stepped to the side and crossed his arms. "I will watch until the end."

Jonathan shrugged.

"Joel?" Max ventured an attempt to reach out to him and prevent whatever this was. "What happened? Why do you look—"

"You always thought you were better than us. Smarter than the rest of us! Superior to all of us! Never looking to help anyone but yourself! I lost almost all my friends because of you! My entire crew abandoned me after what you did!" Joel ground his teeth loud enough for Max to hear.

"What I did? The Optio made us sprint because you tried to bully—"

Jonathan clicked his tongue. "He can't hear you, Max—"

"Let me kill him! I swear he won't escape my blade!"

"—the only thing he can hear is the anger in his chest and the injustice you caused him."

Max was left stunned. Even Joel wasn't this crazy. No one that was part of the barracks acted like this. They were all orphans and had seen worse things than being punished for fighting or any of the hundred reasons that the Optio used to make them run sprints.

"He can't hear a single word you speak without it being tainted," continued Jonathan. "I sent over some Red Spirit Frankincense to the good Optio here—" he waved his hand at Ignacious. "—with this express purpose. Put a boy with the most negative interactions with you in a room full of the incense burning and remind him constantly of your little insignificant differences. His mental demons will do the rest of the work and drive him beyond any state of rage and hatred you can imagine... well, at least for a few hours before he finally calms down and the incense fades. Makes him a nightmare to fight considering he can't feel anything that isn't debilitating."

Joel began circling around him, his eyes and body begging for the sign to start.

"What's the point in this?" Max got into a defensive stance as best as he could.

They hadn't been taught to use a blade yet. He had carried a knife before and even swung it at wooden posts, but that was the extent of what he was capable of. The fight with the goblin to get their first level and unlock their status pages was proof enough.

Jonathan laughed. "The point? The point is that you do what you are told, however I tell you to do it. Don't question me or my methods unless you wish to die, because you will die. Right now, actually, if you don't put this boy out of his misery. Kill him, Max."

"I won't kill him for some bullshit—"

"But he will kill you. Do with that what you wish, just don't die on me. Isador will not be happy to have his new interest ceasing its horizons so soon."

The Optio closed his eyes and groaned like he had taken a physical hit, a disapproving sound.

"In three," said Jonathan. "One. Two—"

Joel charged, screaming a battle cry for all to hear.

Max barely got his wooden sword up before the first swing slammed into him. The impact rattled up through his wrists, forearm, and into his shoulder.The older boy was much stronger than him, an advantage he never had against any foe he'd faced, except if they were much smaller or younger than him.

What he did have was better movement and agility.

Max used a backward retreat to soften the blows every time Joel struck with all his muscles tensed.

Blow after blow came without pause, driven by the red rage that went through him now. He swung again and again—overhead, diagonal, horizontal, a wide looping haymaker that would have caved Max's skull in if he had connected, stabs and everything else he could think of during the fight.

Joel screamed again as the latest blow landed.

Max could barely feel his wrist by this point.

The bigger boy paused and stared at him for a few long seconds. "You think you're better than us, don't you?" he said.

"What?" Max blurted out. "Joel, listen to me, I'm not—"

"You think you're superior?" Joel charged again. He swung, attempting to take Max's head off clean. "You walk around like you own this place, like we're beneath you, like you're a... noble or something."

"What are you talking about? I've been too busy trying to survive and nothing else—"

Max remembered Caleb's statement before he had been taken away by senior Cassius. He knew that the other boys were looking at him from a certain perspective now, due to all the interactions he's had with their trainers and superiors, even if he had nothing to do with it himself.

He had no control over who wanted to speak to him or why. If he was being honest, he didn't exactly know for what reason they were doing all this. None of them had known his surname until the Centurion had figured it out, not even himself.

Max backpedaled as Joel tried to stab through where his face had been just a second ago.

"You could keep your lies to yourself!" Joel screamed, voice cracking, and spit flying from his mouth. His bloodshot eyes burned with fury as he pressed forward, swinging harder and harder as though he was made out of unlimited stamina. "We all see it: you never hang out with anyone but your two pals, Calum and whatever his face is. The chubby one. You haven't even introduced yourself to anyone else in our entire barracks. The only reason we know your name is because the Optio, the Captains, the Recordkeeper, the head Cook, the Old Legionaries—they all somehow know your name, but none of us knew it!"

Max parried a thrust of the blade, aimed at his ribs. The shock traveled through the wooden blade and numbed his fingers that had just started recovering slightly.

"You always run off to get special treatment from everyone. That includes the people I just mentioned, especially the record keeper. The Optio too; he looks at you differently. Captain Hector looks at you differently. The head cook always gives you seconds. Everyone bends over backward for you. Just you. Not me. Not Olir. No one else but you. You aren't special, Max. You're nothing but an orphan. Just like us, like me. Nothing special, just like us."

Max grunted as he barely blocked a heavy blow that would have landed on his shoulder.

Joel grunted as he pulled back and swung again. "Any one of us would do better than you if we got the same opportunities, the same chances."

The older boy stepped back and sucked in a deep, long breath.His muscles visibly swelled beneath his shirt; veins crawled up his neck and through his face until they vanished into his hair. "Iron Bull!"

The air around Joel's entire body shimmered with a faint red energy. It vanished a second later, but the effects on his body had not faded away.

Max's eyes widened as the next swing came faster than anything before it.

Max raised his wooden sword on instinct as Joel nearly blurred toward him. He was thrown off his feet sideways before he slammed into the hard-packed earth on a shoulder. Max rolled as best he could as pain lanced through his collarbone and shoulder bone. His wooden sword had clattered away to the side, spinning across the packed earth.He scrambled on all fours to get back up, knowing what was about to happen next.

Joel was already closing the distance in a straight line, each step pushing him further than he should normally have been capable of. His patron inheritance pulsed through his veins, his bulging veins in visible waves that made him infinitely stronger than before.

Max dove to the side towards the sword, picking it up as he rolled with it. He noticed Joel trying to change direction after him, but was forced to come to a dead halt.

Wait, he thought, he can't charge at an angle, only straight? That couldn't be... No. There's only one way to find out.

Optio Ignacious rumbled in dissatisfaction and stepped forward. "That's enough. We can't allow him to use his Patron Inheritance when Max doesn't have a physical—"

"No." Jonathan had taken a seat in his lounging chair by this point. His smile never diminished either. "Let it play out."

The Optio gritted his teeth, visibly fighting the urge to disregard Jonathan's words and charge in to stop the fight. Yet, after a few seconds of internal struggle, his shoulders sagged and he stepped back into his position, arms folded across his chest and eyes narrowed.

Joel charged again.

This time, Max didn't attempt to block the blade, but rather dove again to the side. He watched as the larger boy reacted the same way to the first dodge, stopping in his tracks after attempting to veer to the side, only to come to a dead halt and turn around to face Max directly.

Max's eyes widened as he stayed on his knees from the latest dodge, but he quickly hid it. He had discovered Joel's weakness, and that was his victory made manifest.

Joel circled around him, his breathing coming in ragged, wet snarls. Foam and spit dripped from the sides of his lips, red eyes bulging in wrath. His singular focus was locked onto Max.Max doubted that the bigger boy had even heard the conversation between Joel and the Optio.

"Joel, I don't want to fight you," Max said.

"Then die standing there!" Joel shouted as he lunged without using whatever special skill was given to him by his patron inheritance for a straight-line boost of speed. Two slashes, one left then right, and he dropped his shoulder. The second Max raised his sword in a feint, Joel bullrushed straight through Max's guard.

Max tried to sidestep it but was too slow. He hadn't expected Joel to suddenly change his tactics.

Joel's shoulder caught him in the chest. The air in his lungs evacuated in a violent burst that left him breathless and scrambling as he was launched onto the ground. The collarbone and shoulder winced once more as more pain lanced through them. The wooden sword this time didn't fly out of his reach and stayed beside him.

Joel ran towards him as Max attempted to gather his bearings.

Max's eyes were blurred with tears as he saw the older boy's silhouette reach back with his leg and attempt to kick him in the head. He threw himself sideways. Joel's boot barely missed his temple.Max rolled a few more times, sword in hand, as the sound of large stomps and a wooden sword slamming onto the ground echoed behind him. He kept moving without looking back, changing directions and zigzagging as best as he could to lose the older boy.

A roar of frustration sounded behind him.

He ignored it as he kept moving, his chest burning and lungs feeling like he had been inhaling flames instead of air.

Only when he was 100% positive that Joel wasn't charging after him did he venture an attempt to turn around and look back. The bigger boy stood a few paces away, chest heaving and shoulders rising from growing exhaustion rather than just breath.

Max's jaw tightened.

Joel thinks I have special treatment? thought Max. He think this is special treatment? Being separated from all my friends without warning? Being stuck in this courtyard with a man who sleeps twenty hours a day and trains me half of what the Optio trains us is special treatment? Nearly dying from the Centurion's anger? Not knowing if I am being set up because of my surname?

He took a deep breath. The last thing he needed was to turn into a raging bull like Joel.

No. His advantage was his calmness in the heat of battle, patience for the perfect opening, and better agility in this situation. Against the goblin, it had been his bigger size, lack of starvation, and strength. Now was different, but the rules were all the same. Find his advantages and exploit his opponent's disadvantages.

"I'm done trying to reach out to you, Joel."

Joel's face turned a shade of red that bordered on purple. He gritted his teeth visibly. "Good, now fight me like you mean it, because one of us is dying today. And it won't be me."

Max exhaled through his nose and got into a lower, tighter stance.

Joel charged.

The first swing came in high. Max ducked under it and jabbed the tip of his sword through Joel's stomach. The bigger boy grunted, barely, and swung a backhand with his opposite arm. Max leaned away and felt the air pass in front of him in the form of a backhanded fist. Max stepped in once more and cracked Joel across the bridge of his nose with the back of his blade.

Blood sprayed as the older boy stumbled backward.

Max watched with wide eyes as Joel didn't even flinch. His eyes locked onto him.

The bigger boy charged again.

Max cursed under his breath. Right, he can't feel pain, Jonathan said that.

This time, Max began to circle Joel, who followed slower now. He had been burning through his stamina the whole time, especially with the increase of power from his patron inheritance. Every strike was enhanced beyond what his body could handle easily. Not to mention, the red spirit frankincense that masked his pain and exhaustion didn't make it disappear. All of it was still there, hidden by the frankincense. Slowly, it would build up to a boiling point, and that was when Max would finally strike.

Joel charged again, swinging his blade wide. Max parried high and dove, and drove his knee into Joel's midsection. The bigger boy doubled over for half a second, but channeled it into a punch, into a wild haymaker that missed Max's face but struck his already hurting collarbone shoulder area.

Max stumbled back in pain, but his eyes were focused on the battle before him.

Joel swung his blade towards his head, attempting to finish the fight in one blow.

Max ducked under it, struggling to keep his focus through the blurring of his eyes from the pain. He didn't have Joel's frankincense-raging effect that prevented him from feeling pain. His head was oddly clear to his detriment, which meant he felt every bit of the agony that pulsed through his collarbone and shoulder.

They kept going back and forth, swing after swing, hit after hit, exchanging blows where Max would get two to Joel's one.

Every strike of the older boy was a haymaker that crashed into Max's guard like a battering ram. Some he was able to parry, and others he had to block straight with his blade or his arms and shoulders, both of which had deep purple bruises every time he moved his arms or shifted his body.

Max made sure to bite down the screams that threatened to escape his lips with every strike that Joel landed.

He doubted he would have still been standing without the boost from his patron inheritance and the skill shard within it.

I can't keep taking hits anymore. I need to bring him down.

Max waited for the next overhead swing, perfectly timing it as he stepped into the strike instead of away, letting the blade glance off his already screaming shoulder. Agony erupted down his entire arm, and he drove the butt of his wooden sword into Joel's solar plexus with everything he had, trying to drop the boy and finish the fight with a kick to the head, very similar to what Joel had tried to do to him in the beginning.

Joel stumbled back. Max tried to follow, except he stopped in his tracks.

The bigger boy wheezed and struggled to breathe, but he stood there, eyes bulging and locked onto him.

Max hesitated for a second, then followed through anyway with a jab, a feint with his sword that made Joel react sluggishly. There was a jab to the nose, already crunched, then another to the stomach, and then a kick to the inside of Joel's knee that buckled his stance. Precious seconds, he swung another strike trying to hit the older boy's head but barely missed as Joel spun sideways and stumbled back a few steps back to him.

He tried to follow.

Joel spun, attempting to catch Max on an unexpected spin.

Max ducked under it and slammed his wooden sword across the older boy's ribs and stepped back, jabbing him in the chin. Joel's head snapped sideways, a line of blood and spit arced through the air, and yet he didn't go down. The older boy stumbled back and forth sideways, left and right, as his body trembled from exhaustion and muscles twitched and spasmed, but his feet remained planted on the ground and his sword raised.

Max stared in awe.

What the—How much punishment can he take?

Max knew from Jonathan's little monologue that the red spirit frankincense blocked most pain and enraged him, yet he had not been expecting this. This was on a completely different level than what Jonathan had suggested. This was insanity.

He kept pushing. He kept pressing forward. Anyway, even when Joel wouldn't fall down, jabs, crosses, kicks, swings of his sword into the ribs, stomach, and liver area, hitting him in the head as many times as he could. He pounded Joel again and again and again till his own arms felt like they were filled with wet sand.

The oldest boy's face was bruised and bleeding from a few deep cuts. His body, tortured by the constant attacks Max had punished him with. His skill shard kept him standing, recovering his stamina little by little after every blow and buying him more time.

And yet, Joel stood there, unwilling to fall down.

Max charged again, intent on putting the larger boy out of his misery—

He froze mid-step. Joel's bloodshot eyes, still red from the beating he had taken and the blood that seeped into them, lost the knitted anger and hatred that had been in them. Joel stood there, owner of the Iron Bull Patron Inheritance, a normal kid and orphan no longer turned insane from wrath.

Max felt guilt rush through his soul.

This wasn't Joel's doing.

The bigger boy had been used for this purpose.

How can I blame him for this when he is in the same situation as me? Max shouted in his own mind. Forced into whatever situation these older, stronger legionaries forced him into without being able to control his fate. Just. Like. Me! These bastards! No! Jonathan! It's his doing!

Max dropped his sword and looked back up at Joel.

The older boy tried to raise his sword, but his hand trembled and shook, fear filling his eyes instead of hate.

Max walked toward him.

Joel tried to step back but found no strength to pick up his legs. "S-Stay b-back," he whispered and swung his sword in a lazy, ineffective attempt.

Max let the blade hit his arm, pain bolting through his entire body from the dozens of bruises there, as he stepped into his guard.

He grabbed Joel's shoulders and looked him in the eyes. "It's over."

Tears welled up in the larger boy's eyes. His wooden sword clattered on the ground as he dropped it.

Max pulled him into a hug.

He heard a stream of notifications flooding his system, but he ignored them, like he ignored the attribute boosts recently. There were more important things in life than his status page, titles, achievements, levels, and all the other bullshit that caused this whole thing.

Joel was an orphan just like him, even if he had bad tendencies.

They were all around thirteen years old after all.
