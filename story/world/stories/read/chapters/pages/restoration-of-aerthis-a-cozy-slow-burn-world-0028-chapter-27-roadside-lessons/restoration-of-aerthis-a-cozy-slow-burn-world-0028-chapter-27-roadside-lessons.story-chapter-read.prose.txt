The next five days weren’t exactly a flurry of activity, but they didn’t leave much room for boredom either. Between the long walks, daily routines, and growing lists of things to train and track, Elynara settled into a rhythm that almost felt comfortable.

Almost.

The mornings, for one, had fully stopped being hers. She no longer expected to wake up naturally, Starpuff had made sure of that.

The spirit had apparently taken it as a personal mission to rouse her before sunrise, and with increasingly creative methods at that.

At first it had been a simple nudge, then a bop, and now it was escalating into coordinated aerial dive-bonks. Last time, it shot down from the ceiling, ricocheted off her pillow, and vanished before she managed to sit up.

She groaned into her blanket, hair a mess and dignity nonexistent.

“Fine,” she muttered one morning, shoving her arms out of her blanket cocoon. “If it wants to play like that, I’ll just counter-bonk it! Or at least fail trying!”

That didn’t sound all too convincing. But hey, what was she to do?!

Trying to catch that mischief incarnate with her hands was a lost cause. Starpuff was too quick for that, and far too delighted to show off about it. It didn’t fly in arcs so much as it looped smugly, twisting and swerving with just enough flair to make it obvious it knew she was trying.

It was mocking her. She could feel it.

So!

Next best option: mana.

The first morning she tried, it worked. Technically. A quick mana ring had snapped out, and the sprite flew right into it.

Which turned out to be the wrong call.

It didn’t panic, or struggle. It didn’t even get annoyed.

No.

It sparkled with delight, flopped around mid-air in a victory twirl, and then started zipping through the loop again. And again. And then decided to challenge the shape entirely by shooting through it backwards.

Her mornings had turned into a full-on game.

Every sunrise since, she would spend the first minutes of the day shaping mana tools in an effort to catch – or at least contain – the little rascal. Rings didn’t last. Lassoes had too much slack. A net almost worked until Starpuff figured out it could just eat through the threads.

It was eating them. That rascal!

At one point she managed to create a mana funnel that gently guided the sprite in a circle. And instead of fleeing or fighting back, it settled into the movement and made a loop of it, weaving around and through, having the time of its life.

Which gave her an idea.

The game shifted. She started creating simple mana hoops in the air, just wide enough for it to fly through if it chose to. She didn’t even bait it. Just made the shape, stepped back, and waited.

It worked better than expected.

Starpuff zipped through the first one immediately, spun, and aimed for the second before it even fully formed.

She began shaping them with variation. Different diameters. Heights. Spacing. If it was going to be a morning ritual, it might as well help her practice. The loops trained her shaping speed, the flow of mana, and even the control of density if she tightened them just right.

At least it was helping her get better at handling mana.

Probably.

Maybe.

Well, she was having fun at least.

And that counted for something.

From the hallway, Mina wandered past once, eyes half-open and hair stuck to one side of her face. She paused mid-step and stared at Elynara spinning her hands in circles, whispering numbers under her breath while shaping invisible loops in the air.

The girl blinked. Looked again.

Then kept walking.

That happened more than once.

To be fair, Mina usually wasn’t at her most perceptive during those early hours. Most days she mumbled something about “too much morning” and shuffled off in search of something warm and edible.

Still, a few of those glances lingered. Elynara could tell.

But it wasn’t like she could explain the daily sprite duel to her yet. Not until Mina managed to see mana properly.

Yes. Definitely.

Until then, she would just accept the puzzled looks and keep practicing.

Because counter-bonking was now a permanent fixture of her mornings. A personal mission, a training method!

And occasionally, a very bright and smug puffball victory lap.

She grinned as Starpuff spiraled past her again, leaving just the faintest tingle in the mana trail behind.

Game on.

Starpuff’s loop finished with a flick that scattered tiny sparks of mana across her vision, and Elynara took that as her cue to move. Morning games were done, for now.

She rubbed a hand over her arm to ease the hum of lingering mana, then flicked it off like water. Time to get to work.

Training hadn’t slowed, but her gains definitely had. The boost to her mana pool had noticeably plateaued, now just nudging past 125 after those few days of steady effort. She kept pushing anyway. It still grew, just slower.

It was a problem for the future Elynara to overcome.

Present Elynara had a full day lined up!

After the last two rounds of spirit mapping, the area was starting to come together clearly in her mind. She already had the habits down—check in with the main shrines, note anomalies, top off wherever needed, and keep a loose eye out for any new or unknown spirit clusters around the farms.

Easy enough with Starpuff acting as her occasional radar blip. When it wasn’t attempting ear dives.

She nearly toppled forward the first time it zipped into her ear during one of her surveys. Not in, just near it. Close enough for the sound to ping straight into her skull like a mana chime misfiring.

Now, she gave it a narrowed look whenever it got close.

“That was not funny,” she had told it.

It sparkled back, utterly unconvinced.

Spirit search patrols took most of her walking hours. The rest was filled with helping Mina.

The girl still had bursts of stubbornness but was visibly pushing herself now, even if the results hadn’t fully caught up yet. Rina stepped in where needed, always with that calm, steady hand and a quietly placed tray of snacks nearby. The woman seemed to think food solved most things. Elynara hadn’t yet found a reason to argue.

Especially not when cookie feasts were the result.

It happened on the third day. Mina had been practicing her sensing for a while already, crouched under the oak tree with her brows scrunched and fingers curled just slightly inward, trying to reach for something that wouldn’t quite settle.

Elynara didn’t interrupt. She just watched from a distance, tracking the tension in her shoulders, the way her breathing had slowed.

And then it happened.

A blink, a gasp, and then wide eyes and a sudden shout that startled two nearby chickens.

“I got it!”

Mina’s whole face lit up in that moment, brighter than any notification. She squealed something incoherent, spun on the spot, and then tackled the nearest thing in reach – which happened to be Pufflet.

The poor animal let out a confused squeak but didn’t protest the tight squeeze.

“It’s like a whole new world just opened up!” Mina said, still clutching her baffled companion. “Before it was just, like, fog! And now I can see threads! I can see them!”

Elynara grinned and stepped forward to confirm it for herself. A quick pulse of mana rising from her fingers had Mina tracking it with her eyes properly. She grinned at that, congratulating her on that grand achievement.

One step closer.

Rina used it as an excuse for a feast, of course. Brought out more dishes than seemed strictly necessary and only offered a vague smile when asked why so much food had been prepared on such short notice.

Elynara had suspicions.

Not that she would ever voice them aloud.

Not to the woman who made those soft flaky rolls with the fruity glaze.

It didn’t stop there. The next morning, Rina handed them aprons and directed them to the kitchen.

“You eat it, you help make it,” she said, already handing out trays.

Which led to Elynara learning how to bake cookies.

Or, at least, learning how not to burn the bottoms. That counted too.

Mina and Finnie made a mess of the flour by the second batch and were exiled to shaping the dough instead, which only created a new problem when the two decided the weirdest shapes were the tastiest.

The “failed” batch came out lopsided, cracked, and vaguely mushroom-shaped in one case.

They were also the best ones.

Mina and Finnie were given permission to eat them as they were ‘not fit for the pantry’, and the gleam in their eyes could have lit a furnace.

Elynara bit into one herself. It was slightly overbaked at the edge, but warm, soft in the center, and sweet enough to make her pause.

She chewed slowly, then smiled.

It was a good time.

And good progress, too.

The cookie crumbled a little more than she expected when she took the next bite, so Elynara held her palm under it just in time to catch the falling edge. Still warm. Not bad, actually.

She flicked the crumb up toward Starpuff, who intercepted it mid-air with the urgency of a tiny gremlin on patrol.

He didn’t eat it. Just batted it back onto her head.

“Rude,” she whispered, but the grin was still stuck to her face.

It had been that kind of day.

And that kind of week, really. Between helping Rina around the farm, guiding Mina through the fine-tuning of her newly awakened sense, and dodging Theren’s latest attempts to teach her how to wield a hoe like a weapon (or was it the other way around?), she had still carved out time for practice.

Her water spells were still annoyingly mana-hungry.

She didn’t understand why they taxed her more than fire ones did, but the problem wasn't going away by itself, so she worked with what she had.

Smaller orbs, less water, and tighter compression.

The early versions were more splash than spell, but she kept refining them, adjusting the flow, minimizing waste. She could push out a dozen now before running dry. That was progress.

On the evening of day four, the tiniest cup-shaped spell held long enough for her to nudge its edge and twist it, curling the top. It flopped a bit, then reformed. Kind of like clay.

It looked more like a blob than a flower, but it was still a shape.

“Basic ones,” she said aloud, turning it in her palm. “I’ll get there.”

Starpuff flew right through it.

The mana construct burst like a soap bubble, and the little mischief bounced away with a spin that looked very intentional.

“Excuse you. That took effort.”

The sprite whirled in a circle like it hadn’t heard her, then zipped over to perch on her shoulder.

Back in the field that morning, she had shown Mina the smaller orbs, just as a demonstration. The girl had perked up immediately.

“Can I drink it?”

Elynara blinked. “You want to drink the spell?”

“Well, it’s water, isn’t it?”

She held it out, letting the orb float just within reach. Mina leaned in and took a careful sip.

The face she made deserved its own painting.

“Pff, look at you. Just what were you expecting?”

“I don’t know!” Mina wiped her mouth, then made another face. “Something cooler. But this is just fresh water, isn’t it?”

She snorted at that, Mina’s reactions really were like her own.

“Yep.”

They both broke into giggles that echoed down the orchard lane, bouncing off the row of fruit trees like little bells.

Breaks like that had become something of a routine now.

At first, it had just been a way to catch their breath after walking or working. But somewhere along the way, it had turned into something gentler. They would sit and share small thoughts, laugh at Pufflet’s antics, or just stay quiet under the shifting shadows of late morning light.

They totally weren’t slacking off!

And even Finnie had stopped asking why they always came back with bits of grass in their hair.

Despite her work on surveying the land, neither Theren nor Rina had any problems assigning her more work around their farm.

Elynara didn’t mind the chores. Helping carry tools or hauling baskets gave her time to listen to her thoughts without them crowding her. She liked the way sunlight played off the tall grasses, liked the smell of earth and sap and wind, liked how Mina seemed to be catching on to more things without being told.

It made everything feel like it had a rhythm now. Neither slow nor fast, just steady. And hers.

She still had questions about water magic, and the mana cost was annoying enough to poke at the edge of her mind every time she pushed out more than a few orbs. But that could wait a bit longer.

For now, she had proof that progress was possible.

And that sometimes, the best feedback came from a scrunched-up face and a playful insult at her spell's taste.

So she cast another one and floated it over, watching it wobble midair as Starpuff began orbiting it like a tiny moon. Without any disruptions this time. Just curiosity. It was learning too.

When Mina looked up from shaping a piece of wood with a stubby carving knife, Elynara caught her eye and smiled.

“You want another drink?”

“Only if it’s cold this time!”

“Learn to do cooling magic then, and we’ll talk.”

Mina groaned dramatically and flopped onto the grass, arms spread wide in defeat.

It was peaceful. Bright. A good day layered on top of a string of other good days.

Elynara leaned back on her hands, stared up at the clouds drifting past, and let her breath settle.

You didn’t need to be cast into the void for what-felt-like-forever to appreciate this sort of thing.

But it probably helped.

The next time Starpuff zigzagged through her water spell mid-cast, Elynara didn’t sigh or scold. Simply paused and let the threads in her vision settle.

Then she cast again. Once with fire, once with water. Slowly. Precisely. Just to watch.

Something had been bothering her for days now, ever since she managed to shrink her water spells enough to avoid draining her entire pool in a handful of casts. But the discrepancy between elements never stopped needling her.

Why was it still so much more expensive?

She thought she was imagining things at first. But then she paid attention to what her Mana Sense was actually showing, not just what she thought it should be. That was when something started to itch behind her eyes.

So she went back to the basics. One cast at a time. A flame, held and compressed. Then a water orb, shaped slow enough to see every curve form. It didn’t click at first. But when Starpuff decided to gleefully interrupt a chain of castings by swirling into her vision and bouncing straight off the water orb again, she saw it.

The way those spells reacted… or rather didn’t react.

It didn’t break like before, but more importantly…

Her gaze narrowed. She dispelled the spells, reformed them, and cast again.

Then again.

And again.

Until the issue took shape in her mind.

Fire didn’t just burn. It had help.

The air around her spells ignited too – faintly, but enough to notice. Combustion fed on everything it could touch, not just her mana. It was a greedy little thing, and she had trained it well.

Water, though? That wasn’t how it worked at all.

Her spell wasn’t pulling from the air. It wasn’t using anything around her. It was just converting mana. Raw, unassisted conversion.

She stared at the next water orb in her hand.

So that’s where the cost came from.

It wasn’t inefficient casting.

It was… creation.

The flame burned the mana, but also borrowed from what already existed. The water was made.

That was kind of amazing.

And incredibly wasteful.

She blinked at the orb until it popped.

Then did what she usually did when things didn’t line up – poked at her memory.

That... didn’t help much.

It was still all blurry edges and half-glimpses. But this time something tugged just hard enough to be useful. Water existed in the air. In the ground. All around.

So why wasn’t she just pulling from that?

It made sense. Maybe not perfectly, but enough for her to shift her spell and try.

She reached with the same spell, the same pattern, but she changed what she asked of it. Instead of forming water directly from mana, she guided it to draw the moisture already present. Not to create, but to gather.

The orb took a little longer to form.

But when it did, her system chimed with the faintest blink of approval.

And she only felt a third of the cost.

Her hand curled slightly. She cast again. Slower this time, but lighter.

It worked.

Starpuff floated above the newest orb and bumped it with his head. It wobbled. Stabilized. Then bobbed up and down like it was testing him back.

“Well,” she murmured, “that solves that.”

The sprite zipped a tiny circle before darting back to her shoulder. He always knew when she was pleased with herself. Possibly too much.

She launched another series of orbs, testing slight variations – direction, shapes, cohesion. Her mana didn’t dip nearly as fast. The drain was still there, but manageable now. Tactical.

And with that... she had options again.

She dropped to a crouch, brushing grass aside with her hand while the last of her orbs dissolved over her shoulder, water falling down like a tiny localized rain.

The mechanics weren’t just abstract theory anymore. She could feel them. Shape them. Choose them.

Which meant she could start designing spells… any way she needed.

No more just throwing water around and hoping it did something helpful. Now she had a way to build on it, tailor it. Control how much she spent, and when.

Even if it took practice to get each effect down, it was a repertoire that would only keep on growing.

That was another part of her Plan started on! Well, it didn’t have that point until now, but she made it, and so it now did!

At least it would mean she wouldn’t run out of mana mid-cast now. Or mid-problem. Or mid-experiment. This unlocked an entire new selection of options!

And maybe even nudged open a door or two she didn’t have sketched out yet.

Elynara stretched her arms overhead and let the tension slide out of her shoulders. The sun felt warm on her sleeves. The air held a bite of a colder front coming in it.

Behind her, Starpuff started spinning in lazy circles over the path.

Ahead of her was more practice, more walking, and probably more chores.

But her magic had just taken a leap forward, and her Plan had one less mystery to untangle. Many more were left however, and she was not nearly done practicing either.

The next few trials with fire spells didn’t go as planned.

Or rather, they went exactly as planned… just not in a way she could replicate right away.

Elynara crouched behind a thick patch of brush at the edge of a low hill, flicking her fingers at a twisted, dried stump half-buried in the soil. Flames surged up, snapped, and vanished. Too hot. Too fast. She frowned.

That one burned way stronger than it should have.

Another try. She shaped the mana again, compressed it more than usual, and this time, added a small breath of wind just as the cast finished. A puff, light and barely focused.

It worked. Sort of.

The fire flared higher for a blink before settling. Not as wild, nor as wasteful. But something in the pattern clicked into place. The flicker of understanding followed behind it.

“Okay,” she murmured, eyes narrowing. “So it’s not just about compression.”

Starpuff hovered next to her elbow, wobbling like a sleepy bubble. It hadn’t even moved during the last few casts, only nosing toward the stump with passive curiosity.

“Lazy today?” she asked, nudging the sprite with her fingertip. “Or secretly judging my technique?”

The puff rotated slowly in the air and made a half-hearted spin. That was probably a yes.

Still, the air shift idea had legs. Adding air before ignition seemed to act like an accelerant. She wasn’t sure what it was specifically encouraging combustion, but either way, the result was clear.

“Less mana to get hotter flames. Or… same mana, bigger effect.”

A tradeoff she could work with.

And that meant yet another tweak she could build into her spells. It made her wonder what else she was missing. If wind helped fire, what would water get easier with? Could she use heat to direct flow?

The possibilities started stacking fast. It sent a small buzz up her spine.

Elynara straightened, brushing off her knees. The wildflower fringe rustled underfoot, and a few curious bugs took flight as she stepped through.

It wasn’t that long ago that she thought of magic as one-size-fits-all. Pick a spell, cast it, and done. But now… now it was more than that. Magic bent when you understood it. It grew with you. It changed with how you asked it to respond.

Knowledge was good.

But understanding – that was what mattered.

And she liked earning that.

The hill gave way to a gentler slope of farmland below, where the afternoon haze turned the landscape into soft greens and golds. Rows of late-season vegetables stretched across the beds in tidy lines, interspersed with tools resting against wooden stakes and the occasional slow-moving figure tending weeds. She raised a hand in greeting toward a distant pair, but wasn’t sure if they saw her.

There was a smaller creek past the far end of the property, part of the survey she had been avoiding until the very end. It didn’t connect to any of the known spirit sites, but was still part of the farms.

So of course it made her curious.

She followed the narrow path down, brushing aside reeds and watching her footing near the muddy bend. Starpuff zipped ahead, then immediately reversed course when it discovered a patch of brambles.

“No shortcuts,” she told it, hopping the trickle of water and pushing a branch aside.

The creek itself wasn’t much. It was shallow, slow, and barely audible above the hum of insects—but the moss-covered stones clustered near one side gave her pause.

She could feel a faint amount of higher than average mana from them.

Present and clustered.

She knelt beside one of the larger rocks, letting her hand rest against the grass as she focused her Mana Sense. Threads lit up in her mind’s eye, slow curls of warmth nestled into the shade.

Two spirits. Small, shy things. They hadn’t noticed her yet.

Or… were pretending not to.

“Hello you two, hiding down here.”

She reached into her reserves, pulling out a handful of mana and letting it hover near their hiding place.

A little offering of food.

The spirits stirred as they sensed the mana, before deciding to come out. Slowly and shyly at first, soon they were gobbling up the mana with gusto.

She smiled at that.

Starpuff floated beside her again, unusually still. It stared down at the rocks as if contemplating whether it should interrupt, then gave a tiny spin and drifted back to her shoulder instead.

She smiled.

“I’ll count that as progress.”

The sprite made a soft pop sound against her hair in reply.

She lingered a little longer, watching the light shift in the grass and letting the quiet settle.

Then her gaze lifted to the horizon, where the last farm markers glinted under the late sun. Not far now.

Almost done.

“Alright,” she said aloud, tucking her notes back into the satchel and rolling her shoulders. “Last leg. Let’s go.”

Starpuff bounced once and zipped forward in a lazy loop.

The breeze picked up again, brushing at her robe and tugging her hair across her cheek. She didn’t mind, the motion quite pleasant.

There was still more to map.
