“We’re too far south!” Valanor bellowed. He seemed to be screaming more and more each day, as if he’d forgotten how to do anything else. The corruption inside him was like a constant buzzing through his mind, like a headache that never relented.

“You blame me?” Savilar demanded. “I warn you, again and again, that the Light is unreliable! Maybe it takes us to The Throne, maybe to some terrible demon. I have no way of knowing!”

Valanor grumbled. The Light. The Throne. He still remembered Ethan raving about them long ago, shortly after he’d pulled out of the Deep Cell, in the bowels of Corvale. At the time, he’d thought them to be some expression of madness, never expecting he’d be relying on them some day.

He looked up into the sky, hoping to see the sun behind the thick, red clouds, but the mountains blocked most of the horizon. He couldn’t remember when he’d last seen anything other than mountains surrounding him.

“We’re too far south!” he repeated, not even realizing he’d opened his mouth. Savilar growled.

“Then you lead, shield knight! I tire of repeating myself. The Light takes me where it will! I have no control over it! Maybe I can sense The Throne, maybe! But it’s a fleeting, distant thing! I doubt I’ve the strength to sit on it, yet, the Light guides me elsewhere.”

“Too far south,” Valanor grumbled again, storming off in a direction he hoped was north.

“Yes, you guide us then, little brother,” the white demon muttered from behind. “Maybe the Light will take you where it refused to take me.”

Valanor missed a step at that. He knew it was nothing but an offhand comment from Savilar, but the truth assaulted him.

Sometimes–only sometimes–he thought he could see the light.

It would be there, just out of the corner of his eye, like a dim glow in the distance. Tantalizing, beckoning. Burning proof that the corruption of Potentia was slowly taking him.

It didn’t help that he knew what the Light was supposed to be, thanks to the Church’s hidden records. The scholars who studied this world were convinced The Throne was what remained of Dusk’s power, hidden somewhere, and waiting to be claimed. The Light, on the other hand, was thought to be the last vestiges of the god’s shattered will, guiding potential successors on a path that would prepare them to take his place.

Of course, the great joke was that it wasn’t possible. For most, at least.

“What do you hope to do, if you actually find The Throne?” Savilar asked, as if reading Valanor’s mind. Of course, he’d asked the question many times since Valanor had revealed something of his plans. “You claimed you hunt a god, but we both know that Dusk is every bit as lost as Dawn or Twilight. Perhaps moreso.”

Valanor growled, hating that he was starting to sound like the demon. He quicked his pace as he felt the ground rising beneath him. The mountains were thick with trees, and he often couldn’t see through the canopy above to tell where they were going. But up meant they were scaling another mountain, and that would at least provide a view. That would hopefully give them some kind of guidance.

“I have no intention of trying to take The Throne,” Valanor muttered.

“So there are limits to your foolishness,” Savilar snapped. “At least I won’t have to talk you out of that particular death. So tell me, if you’re not planning on taking Dusk’s power for yourself, what are you hoping to do with it?”

Valanor had to ignore the sarcasm. He knew that the not-so-subtle undertones were speaking louder than the words themselves. No one had been able to claim The Throne in at least a millennia, and those who tried must have included Champions of all types. He wouldn’t be surprised if even the Church had tried at some point.

“It’s not meant for me, or you,” Valanor said. “Only a Chosen has the connection required to make any meaningful connection to one of the gods.”

“And do you have a Chosen hiding somewhere in your pockets?” Savilar said with a sneer.

Valanor’s hand automatically went to his inventory rune, and he smiled slightly. “Perhaps I do,” he said, enjoying the glare it earned him from the white demon.

“This world has left you mad,” his brother growled.

“Quiet,” Valanor said in an angry whisper. They were nearing a crest in the mountain, and he thought he saw some movement between the trees. Savilar didn’t argue–they might bicker, but they both respected the dangers of this world.

The brothers got low, creeping through the trees and up the rise, leading to a steep cliff. They looked out at the view while staying as hidden as possible, and Valanor was relieved to find a large valley before them. There were still mountains everywhere, but they’d at least be able to see where they were going for a time.

“What is that?” Savilar asked, and Valanor’s relief faded away as he looked where his brother pointed.

One side of the valley had most of its trees ripped away, and it was clear that some kind of battle had taken place. There was also a deep, dark hole burrowing away in the depths of the world. It was enormous, so large Valanor struggled to get a sense of its scale. But even stranger was the sinewy, ruined body just outside of it.

“Some kind of serpent?” he said, trying to make out the distant corpse.

“If so, it’s enormous. I’ve never seen a Twilight so large. I don’t know if I’ve ever actually encountered a Master rank in my time here, but I’d wager we’re looking at one now.”

Valanor found that he couldn’t disagree. Even from a distance, it was clear that the creature had been enormous. Its magical body was slowly wasting away, but even still, what remained of the serpent would have been large enough to drive a wagon through.

“What are those things around it?” Savilar asked. “They’re like…stone pillars? Ruins?”

Valanor couldn’t see as well as his brother’s demonic eyes, but he did make out distant shapes. They did appear to be odd shapes of stone, but he couldn’t imagine what kind of structure they might have belonged to. “Statues, perhaps?” he said absently.

Savilar shrugged. “Perhaps we have gone too far south,” he muttered. “Since my mind began to return to me, I’ve wondered why there are so few Masters and Champions on this world. I would have thought to find Potentia overrun with them. I don’t think I’d like to stay and find out what’s been killing them.”

For once, Valanor completely agreed with his brother.

Ethan ignored the clawing hands and snapping fangs until they grew too irritating, then finally caught both of the man’s wrists in one hand. His Twilight strength made his grip a vice, yet the rage-filled Nator struggled wildly to escape, snarling all the while.

“Sorry friend, this kind of treatment would go better with haloperidol, but I doubt there’s any sedatives strong enough to calm you down at the moment.”

He was in a wide building deep in the city, one that had lent itself well to a triage center. Beds now lined the walls in neat rows, nearly a hundred, and most still holding a recovering Nator. It was taking time for Steelbinder to find the exact combination of factors that would make his latest mad plan even remotely possible, but Ethan was happy to spend that time helping these people.

His right hand locked onto the man’s chest, feeling the thick muscles straining there as his patient struggled to get away. Never mind that the man had been utterly calm when he walked in an hour ago, desperate to be helped. The rages took them without warning, and Talnor was completely lost to the episode now.

Unbound, he was just barely strong enough to be considered Dusk rank, and offered no real threat to Ethan, and so Doctor Bishop just continued to work, ignoring the attacks.

With the arrival of the Fable Train, Ethan once again had access to his Spirit, and its incredible ability to cleanse corruption. Doing so on living things, however, was far more complicated than simply pulling the corruption out of the air. Doing so was possible–his Spirit has grown more than powerful enough to rip the taint away–but such methods were dangerous, and traumatizing for the mind.

These people hadn’t become corrupted, after all. They were born to it. It wasn’t like restoring someone who had visited this world, it was more akin to pulling a fish out of the ocean, and hoping it could breathe the air. Without the power of Ethan’s rune, most wouldn’t survive the process.

Magic rushed into Talnor as Ethan channeled the corruption into his Spirit. The power of three worlds–and three gods–worked in harmony to restore balance to the man’s mind and body. The Nator’s eyes fluttered back into his head, then he gratefully lost consciousness.

“It’s for the best,” Selina said, easily lifting up the man, despite his frame weighing easily four-hundred pounds or more. She carried him to a nearby bed, whispering her own healing spell as she did so. They’d found that the combination of their efforts seemed to have a more lasting effect on the patients, and over the last two weeks, nearly the whole population had been treated. “They always recover faster with sleep,” she said.

Ethan wished she would smile with her usual joy at those words, but he understood why she didn’t. Removing corruption from her people had greatly calmed those who’d seemed most demonic in their transformations, and provided relief to the others, who merely appeared twisted by Potentia’s terrible power.

Yet clear minds had led to the entire population of Bastion falling into something of a depression. They could think and act with more freedom than ever before, but that seemed to highlight how dire their situation truly was.

“I hate that we’re healing them, just to send them into war,” Ethan said. He kept his voice low, but he could sense that all the patients were in comfortable sleep.

Selina did smile then, and came to put a hand on his shoulder. “They deserve to fight for their world, Ethan. You’ve given them a chance to have real purpose, don’t underestimate that gift. These are the first Nator in more than a thousand years who will live to do anything more than just survive.”

Ethan nodded, though he couldn’t match her smile. “I just wish we weren’t trapped in this damn cycle. Every time it seems like we’ve brought some sort of calm, it turns out that the storm is right behind.” He looked at the long rows of beds. “There’s a reason I never wanted to be a battlefield medic,” he said sadly.

Selina was clearly readying to say more–reassuring him again, as always, but the door opened suddenly, and Allyara stood there. She looked good, compared to when she’d rescued him. She had a complete torso again, and even the makings of a shoulder.

“Steelbinder sent word,” she said, then shook her head as if she couldn’t believe her own news. “They’re about a week away.”

Ethan let out a long breath, then forced himself to smile. “Well then, it’s almost time to go to the sea.”
