Chapter V.LIV (5.54) - Ground Rules

The following fights throughout the day returned to their earlier form and, as such, the audience returned to its normal state again soon after Evie disappeared from sight. People were still excited by the battles, but not to the same degree as they had been during both Finn and Evie’s fight. The only other one that came close was a duel between Tip and another fifth-year. Even that, though, was over too quickly for any proper tension or hype.

Kizu’s father returned to his seat, accompanied by a very smug Finn. As they spoke, Kizu didn’t think he’d ever heard his father say so many kind words to Finn… well, ever. Of course, he simultaneously added additional weight on his son to continue to match his earlier performance for the rest of the tournament. Finn tried to insert a snide remark about Kizu not having won a fight yet, but Kizu simply rolled his eyes at his brother’s attempt to provoke him. These fights were a means in which Kizu planned to ascend the rankings of the academy leaderboard. He couldn’t care less about what their father thought of his performance.

The only other fight of note for the rest of the day was Harvey against that snotty boy who worked in the library. Kizu smiled to himself as the boy got the daylights beat out of him. It was over far too soon. Unlike in their first month at the academy when Harvey publicly offended the boy, Gob Lucas, the boy’s half-brother, had no reason to retaliate from this clobbering. Fast, efficient, done. Harvey didn’t even give the whimpering boy a second glance as he turned his back at the match’s conclusion.

After the fights concluded, Kizu’s father declared they were going out to eat for a family dinner, to celebrate Finn’s triumphant victory.

“I really need to get ready for tomorrow’s fight,” Kizu said in a desperate bid to escape to Owl’s Respite. “Plus, I need to check in on Anata tonight. She will need to do make-up schoolwork tonight in preparation for her missing class tomorrow to spectate.”

“Bring the girl along,” his father said cheerily, surprising both Kizu and Finn. “She’s clearly taken the mantle of your protege, regardless of her origins, so it’s best I evaluate her to better advise you.”

They set up a meeting point and time for dinner then Kizu jumped back to his ship. As he paced the deck, he wondered if letting his father interact with Anata was actually a safe idea. It would probably be good for her to meet her grandfather and get to know the human side of her lineage. But… Kizu just wished the humans in question were a bit kinder and a better representative of their species as a whole.

While mulling it over, Kizu started a new brew. A simple burn salve that was good to have stocked in bulk but didn’t require a lot of thought to create. He needed to stock up on a variety of potions, not only for the up-coming battles, but also for his planned delve into the World Dungeon.

He decided to set some ground rules with his father. No negative discussion of Anna. No talk of taking Anata away. No belittling of illegitimate children in general. Before picking up Anata at school, he stopped by his family villa to drop off his demands. His father noted and agreed to them, though his patronizing smile and tone implied he was simply placating Kizu like a bratty child.

Kizu’s eye twitched, but since his father committed to the rules, he didn’t say press the matter further. He wished he could place unwavering faith in his father’s words and relax, but in the past his father had bragged about occasions when he tactfully backstabbed trade partners and reneged agreements. If he was willing to do that in his business, Kizu had zero faith that his father intended to keep his promises to his children.

When he told Anata about their dinner plans, she seemed disinterested. She gestured at Mae several times and pleaded with her eyes.

“We aren’t bringing Mae and Kon to our family dinner,” Kizu replied to the nonverbal question. The mere thought of the two sides of Anata’s family colliding sent shivers down his spine. He had no desire to repeat the field trip debacle with new players.

Anata crossed her arms and stuck out her bottom lip.

“No amount of pouting will change my mind on this.”

That was the wrong thing to say. Anata stomped her foot in the dirt path and refused to walk home. Kizu debated his options. He decided to first attempt reasoning. Then negotiation. And, if that all failed, he could jump them back to Owl’s Respite and leave Ione and Mort on babysitting duty.

“Mae needs to go study with Stina tonight, anyway,” he told his angry niece. “She won’t be available.”

“Rui,” Anata muttered.

Kizu blinked. Anata angrily muttering was a new development. He quickly searched for a reason why Rui shouldn’t be allowed to join them. In some ways, he wasn’t as dangerous a dinner companion as Mae, in others, he was far worse. Kizu pulled from his mental storage in an attempt to better recall information about the boy.

“Rui… doesn’t like fish!” Kizu exclaimed, relieved to have recovered that little fact about the boy. “We’re eating at a fish restaurant and Rui hates eating fish.”

Anata’s response wasn’t quite what he expected from his niece. He expected her to protest or get further upset. Instead, her brows crinkled in frustration. An expression he was familiar with on her. He clearly hadn’t understood whatever she was trying to express.

“Do you need to speak to Rui?” he tried.

She shook her.

“Did he do something to upset you?”

This time Anata paused and seemed to consider. Then she raised a hand, palm down, and tilted it back and forth in a ‘close enough’ gesture.

“Do you want me to talk to him?”

Her eyes went wide and she shook her head vehemently. Then she trudged forward, not looking back.

Confused, Kizu decided to let her drop the subject. Despite the strange note it ended on, he considered this a success, as he hadn’t even been forced to bargain with the girl.

Once Anata was safely escorted to Taroe’s doorstep, Kizu jumped back to Owl’s Respite. Since Taroe’s confrontation with him, he hadn’t seen the ex-Elite and didn’t particularly want that to change today.

Kizu forced himself to stop thinking about Taroe. He’d found that when he started, his mind nearly always wandered over to the cause of their fight. Blood.

He reached into his spatial ring and drained one of the potions meant to repress his addictive tendencies. A wave of relief washed over his body as he drained the small bottle. He’d replicated the recipe Roba had shown him and had a small stockpile of the potions stored away. Still, they only kept the ticks at bay. The potions didn’t do what Anata’s blood could do. A constant weight of weariness followed him everywhere and he had to slow his spellcraft studies considerably. It was one of the big reasons he’d been dedicating so much of his time recently to brewing, a field that required no blood for the majority of its scope.

Once Anata arrived at Owl’s Respite through the gate, they sorted through her schoolwork and got everything organized for her day-off. As Kizu read through her assignments, he was struck how basic so many of them seemed. It was no wonder Anata acted out during class if she was being expected to complete basic arithmetic and readings well below her level. He suddenly realized why Anata asked him to look over her homework with her today. And it wasn’t because she required help.

“Is this really your assignment?” Kizu asked, lifting up a paper with a list of double-digit addition and subtraction questions.

Anata gave him a long-suffering look that effectively communicated how she felt about her schoolwork.

Originally, Kizu planned to separate the assignments into chunks for Anata to complete over the next day. Instead, she knocked everything out in half an hour and had everything complete an hour before they left for dinner.

“Anata,” Kizu said, kneeling down next to his niece. “I want you to know, no matter what your grandfather says, you are my priority. You and your mother are the only family I need, and if they threaten you in any way, then I have no qualms with cutting all the others off for good.”

His niece stared, her uncovered human eye boring into him. He knew without a physical tell that her father’s inheritance likewise met his gaze, hidden behind her enchanted eyepatch. That was fine. He loved every bit of her, no matter the origin.

And clearly Anata read that in his eyes, for she simply nodded once and hugged him.
