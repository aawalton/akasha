In the year 785, Kis of Summer, fifteen days before Blindlight, Remallon, the capital of Grenstel region of the Ember Republic smelled of hot sweat and not much else. The city was the peak of civilization, a haven for commerce and religion, for law and tightly controlled crimehouses. Its foundations were built upon twin-fractures, the two rivers dividing the city into three parts. Eastside, the largest part housed most of the population, poor to middle-class Clenn and Scarrd living in tightly packed shelters ranging from dirty sleeping corners to respectable accommodations. They formed the local underworld. Westside, considerably smaller, was a pristine stretch of land with manors surrounded by gardens, grand squares with high-reaching sculptures looking down on the rest of the city. Parts of the Civil House, made out from the strongest and wealthiest of the Clenn, as well as the rest of the overworld resided there. As for the last part, Midlane, it was nothing more than a three-feet wide stone wall separating the two, built between the pair of narrow fractures.

Kalsin had barely taken a handful of steps inside, and he already wished to be gone.

He wore a nondescript cloak as he made his way through the streets, heading for a darker neighborhood that suited his purpose. Jack had gone to reserve a balcony spot for the meeting with their intel, a rot, the Scarrd equivalent of a root; their mind-enhancing surge made them suitable as spies and informants of the underworld. Only after years of tracking, bribing, and scouring all the different underworlds of the Republic did they manage to set up a rendezvous between someone who might know more. Someone who might know something. Anything. Since that fateful day, he'd been chasing a fool's phantasm, a drawn curtain. But he was so close to finally stepping ahead and taking a peak behind it.

Less and less people spotted the streets as he advanced into a rougher side of the city. When the last patrol of guards disappeared around the corner, Kalsin drew his hood deep into his face, suppressing his distaste at the thought of committing the grave crime of covering his scar. Old habits. It did grant him anonymity, however.

It was a short, but no less nervous walk until he found his first target, a kneeling beggar with a scar marring his bare chest. To a regular civilian, he might've appeared just a simple beggar, but the muscle on his body told Kalsin a different tale. A dirt who wouldn't be afraid to put his strength to use, laws and morals omitted. Assuming the price was right.

Kalsin approached, bowing deep so his face was obstructed entirely. He slipped a few copper pieces in the dirty cup. "I need men for a job," he said, rasping his voice. He couldn't gauge a response, his face downturned as it was, but he spilled out the same amount of coins again. "Meet me at the Eastern gate. Tomorrow. Not a word to anyone." He let one last copper slip from his fingers. "If you're able, bring something that burns."

"Who should I look for?" asked the pretend-beggar, and Kalsin gave the name.

He found the next one two streets down, a woman, her nose broken and badly healed, sitting with one bare leg extended to display the scar on her foot. He bent down again, disposing of another stack of coins. "I've a job for you." Coins fell. "Three weeks. Here, at Dawnbreak. Prepare for some bloody business." She too asked about his person, and he answered.

That's how Kalsin spent his morning, going through the shadier streets of Remallon, dodging into an alley and onto a different lane when crossing the occasional guard patrols, searching for the next Scarrd outlaw to make richer and his pouch lighter with every encounter. By noon, when Warmlight crested the sky, his coppers had nearly run out, but he felt more content for it.

He had the comfort to eat when and where he pleased, but not everyone was so lucky. An unjust world is all the more reason for humanity to try and balance it. Not with raving about some higher purpose like priests, creating an ideal to lure the naive, but with doing. He knew that one person could never change the world, but aiming that large was a fool’s plan.

Far as Kalsin was concerned, idealists could go and bite glass. The reason people engaged in the act of charity was to make themselves feel better. The act in itself, admirable. Do it privately, inhale the high it gives, and leave without a word. Shouting it to the world, preaching hope for the masses? The underlying construction of a pedestal for the world to view them as being better? Fuck right off.

Jaded, maybe. But most who are, they tend to have their reasons.

Kalsin still had time before the meeting with the rot, and even more so if he wanted to be fashionably late. Cinder, as per her usual routine, had split off from their group miles before entering the city only to cross the gate by herself. She would search for the local hunter's respite where Clenn hunters gathered for anything and everything regarding their profession, be it sharing jobs and rumors with those who want to hear it, or fetch a price for the magical trophies their battles netted them. All in likeminded company and drinks to go around.

Naturally, the respite was a safehouse only of the Clenn. Hunters of the Scarrd gathered in a scoundrel's pitfall, equal in terms of function, but the flow of drinks less and the filth more defined. Being a Scarrd himself, Kalsin only needed the hideous ticket plastered on his face to grant entry.

The pitfall in Remallon was less a building and more a walled-off square with dusty benches arranged to form lanes crowded by people looking to do business. Kalsin wasn't intent on shouting his way through the rabble in the faint hopes of squeezing out whiffs of mythos footprints. He only had eyes for the benches stacked with curios and marked for sale.

He passed a basket of gelatine eggs, green-tinged leathers with scales the size of his head. A row of bottled liquid sat in a display case, looking too much like bubbling blood for comfort. A wrinkled hunter proclaimed to everyone in a five-foot vicinity that a vile mythos had turned his partner into a figurine as he poked the doll with a finger. A pair of rat-like creatures clawed at one another in a dirty aquarium filled with murky water. Carnivorous pets, two-or-nothing sale.

Kalsin looked through the attractions, stopping when something caught his eye, though he never asked a question. He watched the wondrous, sometimes monstrous things he'd never seen before. But above all, he watched the people.

He drank in their fascination, their disgust flash as they filtered the sensations each their own way. Their attuned perception clashing with the new and previously unknown to form blank expressions into a mirror of their thoughts. He smiled when they did. Wrinkled his nose on command. And he was invisible, blissfully invisible.

Some time later Kalsin left the pitfall with fewer coins on him, but his pocket weighed down by a red-capped vial of colorless liquid. Who knows. It may come in handy if things get bloody.

Their meeting with the informant was to take place in Jon's Hostelry, a place with a reputation respectable enough as far as Scarrd establishments were concerned. People milled on the outside without a single Clenn in sight, the comings and goings of Scarrd mildly paced among their own. A line started forming before a pastry stall, luring the street with a sweet smell. Nearby, a priest of Hadúr, god of conflict, life and death, chanted their religious teachings for those who were willing to listen.

"Remember not to look to the past, nor the uncertain future for guidance. There's only one with power to reach into the middling of your lives, into the currents of now, and allow you past the heavenly gates of Éden. Devote your earthly possessions to Hadúr, so that war and decreation may follow in his fall!"

Kalsin joined the gathering with the inn's balcony in view and scanned through the guests until he found the telltale, half-shaved mane of Jack. She was seated with her back to him, but he'd recognise her broad shoulders and statuesque posture from anywhere.

Kalsin got his pen and notebook out, his fingers deftly running across the paper and glancing up occasionally. The man facing Jack was the opposite of his bodyguard; a scrawny, jumpy fellow who had his eyes constantly shift from one point to another. His scar split his upper lip, stretching above, as if trying to crawl into his nose.

Kalsin shivered. Some people always have it worse.

He slammed his notebook closed, detached himself from the priest’s listeners, and entered the inn to make his arrival to the single link standing between them and their goal.
