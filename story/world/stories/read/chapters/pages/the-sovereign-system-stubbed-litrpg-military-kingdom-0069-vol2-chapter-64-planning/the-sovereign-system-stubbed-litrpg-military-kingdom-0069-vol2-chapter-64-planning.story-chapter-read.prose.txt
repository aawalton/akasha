“Fuck me… how much did I drink last night?” the red-headed man muttered, rubbing his eyes vigorously. The motion seemed to hurt—he groaned in response.

A moment later, he lowered his hands and fixed his bleary gaze on Luke, blinking several times. Confusion turned swiftly to disbelief as he stumbled to his feet, unsteady.

“No… You’re dead,” he breathed, retreating a step.

“Quiet down,” Luke hissed, rising to his feet and grabbing the man’s arm. His hand barely wrapped around the barrel-like bicep. “Sit down, Brian. I’ll explain everything.”

Only after Luke spoke again did Brian’s dazed mind seem to catch up. Recognition lit his features—and then he lunged forward.

Luke was crushed in a bear hug that lifted him clean off the floor. His ribs groaned under the pressure, the air forcibly expelled from his lungs.

“You bastard! I thought I lost you…” Brian’s voice cracked as he gently lowered Luke back down, leaning his heavy frame against him for a moment.

“I already lost Arthur…”

A pang of guilt twisted in Luke’s chest. He patted Brian’s back, awkwardly but sincerely. “Sorry, man… It’s a long story. What are you doing in the capital? Weren’t you supposed to be back at the academy in Clayton City?”

From what he recalled, Brian still aspired to become a general—or at least an officer. So why had he left?

Brian sniffled and wiped his nose on the sleeve of his robe before settling his large frame back into the chair. His eyes lingered on Luke before he let out a long sigh.

“With Master Gale dead and Master Boyd gone, the academy’s a joke. Hayden took over for a while, but once he got called up for the latest campaign… I left.”

Luke shuddered involuntarily at the mention of Hayden. The idea of the Blood General’s ruthless descendant teaching the next generation sent a chill down his spine. What kind of twisted ideologies had the man tried to instill?

Hopefully, it had only been a temporary assignment—one the academy would rectify with a better replacement.

“I don’t blame you,” Luke said, “but that still doesn’t explain why you came to the capital. Don’t you have family—”

He cut himself off. A memory surfaced: the Nautilus family hailed from the northern provinces, near the coast.

They had a proud legacy—shipbuilders and naval captains who’d long served the Marxx Kingdom.

But with Lhair’s invasion, everything north of Clayton City had been lost. There was no telling if Brian’s family had survived.

Despite the hangover still etched on his face, Brian gave him a thin smile and patted his shoulder. “I came to see my old friends, of course. Kayson told me you'd been sent to the Empire on a campaign under the king’s orders. I only found out yesterday that you were—well, supposed to be—dead.”

He let out a small, hollow laugh. “That’s why I got drunk and spent all my coin.”

At the mention of Kayson, Luke’s eyes lit up. “You saw Kayson? Is he okay?”

“Wait—you haven’t seen him yet?” Brian stared at him, mouth agape. “Are you keeping this secret from him too?”

His voice dripped with judgment, and Luke could almost see his friend's opinion of him nosediving. He quickly waved his hands in protest, trying to dispel the misunderstanding. But when he noticed some of the other inn patrons beginning to stir, he realized this wasn’t the place to explain.

“Let’s have breakfast first. I’ll take you back to my place afterward and explain everything.”

Brian still looked suspicious, but he didn’t press further.

Over the next hour, the two of them caught up like old friends. It had only been a few months since the siege of Clayton City, yet it felt like a lifetime had passed.

They were both changed—older, perhaps not in years but in weight. A quiet heaviness clung to Brian, hiding just beneath the surface of his usual larger-than-life demeanor. Luke didn’t need his Scholarly Pursuit skill to guess the cause.

Arthur’s death had left a mark—an ugly, unhealed wound in the heart of the big guy.

Eventually, the kitchen opened, and Luke was finally able to order something warm to break his fast. The food was simple, but he savored every bite. No seafood. No dry rations. No battlefield.

Brian declined the offer to eat, turning pale at the sight of food and shaking his head. “I’m in no state to stomach anything,” he muttered.

Once Luke finished his meal, he led the towering redhead out of the inn and back into the winding alleys. The rain had stopped, though the streets still glistened with puddles and mud.

They drew glances as they walked—not surprising, considering Brian’s sheer size and striking red hair. He stood out like a bear walking beside a fox.

“It’s through here,” Luke said, navigating the alleys with practiced ease. He moved like someone who’d taken these paths a hundred times. Soon, a small run-down shack came into view—his temporary home. Still as bleak and uninviting as ever.

He held back a sigh and stepped inside.

“You asshole!” a voice screamed.

Luke barely had time to register what was happening before a blur of motion crashed into him, sending him stumbling backward—right into Brian’s solid frame.

He braced for a blow, but instead, he found himself trapped in yet another bone-crushing hug. For the second time that day, he was squeezed like a stuffed toy, lungs struggling for air.

“I-I’m sorry… Don’t kill me,” he wheezed out.

The grip loosened.

Luke looked up to see a familiar face—blond tousled hair, piercing blue eyes filled with emotion.

Kayson.

His brother. His friend.

Just seeing him again made Luke’s heart feel lighter.

“Kayson, I—”

“Luke, how could you fake your own death? If Hilda hadn’t warned us in time, Sebastian would’ve marched straight into the palace and killed the king…”

Kayson’s words came fast—too firm to be mistaken for a joke.

Luke blinked, caught off guard. He turned slowly toward one of the nearby figures. The gray hair, the newly grown goatee—there was no mistaking the man who was his first friend in this world.

Sebastian.

The concern in the butler’s eyes said everything. No words were needed. Under different circumstances, this might have been a touching reunion—but Kayson’s revelation hung heavy in the air.

“I’m sorry,” Luke said slowly, brow furrowed. “Did you just say he was going to… march into the palace and…”

“Kill the king,” Sebastian confirmed, voice calm and unapologetic.

Luke blinked again. Was this some kind of twisted joke?

He opened his mouth to laugh—but then stopped cold.

Shit. Prince Lyon.

His eyes darted toward the far corner of the room.

“Maybe we steer clear of regicide talk in front of the second prince?” Luke muttered, patting Kayson on the shoulder as he passed.

He approached Sebastian, looking him up and down. A thousand snide remarks danced on the tip of his tongue, but he swallowed them. Instead, he opened his arms and stepped into a hug.

The older man’s frame was wiry and firm, still powerful even now. Yet, there was a gentleness in the embrace—something that Kayson’s bear hugs lacked. Luke felt the faintest tremble in the butler’s arms.

“Glad to see you’re okay, Grandpa,” Luke whispered warmly.

Sebastian flinched.

Luke stepped back, startled. The older man stood frozen—eyes wide, shimmering like pools on the verge of spilling over. Tears welled up, unshed, clinging stubbornly to his lashes.

Before Luke could speak, a heavy hand landed on his shoulder.

“Sorry it took so long to organize this meeting,” came a familiar husky voice.

He turned to see Hilda, her towering frame filling his vision. She looked tired—hair messier than usual, eyes slightly puffy from lack of sleep—but there was a warmth in her expression when she looked at him.

“I’ve been dealing with the aftermath at the Pavilion. Ming Hu’s little rampage left quite the mess.” She shook her head, sighing. “The guy’s scrambling—trying to latch onto the Crown Prince’s faction. If he weren’t hosting so many nobles, I wouldn’t have had the chance to slip away.”

At the mention of the Crown Prince, Luke’s gaze drifted to Lyon, who now sat quietly with his head bowed.

He’s lost his brother… and likely his father too.

A pang of sympathy struck Luke, but there was no time to dwell on emotion. Not when the stakes were this high.

“We need to talk,” he said, voice lowering into something more serious. “There are things you all need to know.”

He gestured for everyone to gather closer.

What followed was a condensed briefing—a rundown of the campaign, the harrowing retreat, the rescue of the second prince, and the chain of events that led to his return to the capital.

Luke didn’t hold anything back—except the true identity of his Sovereign System.

Everyone here already knew he was different. Whether they understood the specifics of his power or not, it made little difference. Still, it was safer to err on the side of caution.

Who knew what the system might do if he ever revealed it outright?

For now, he stuck to the same explanation he always used: a blessing from the gods.

When he revealed the truth about the traitors within the kingdom, only Brian reacted with visible distress. The big man groaned and cradled his head like he’d just been hit with a hammer.

“This is not what I want to hear while hungover…” he muttered bitterly.

Luke ignored the complaint and pressed on.

“An informant told me the king has retreated into the Inner Palace to participate in a mourning ceremony… alongside the noble consort.”

His gaze shifted toward Lyon, who sat slouched in the corner, his face blank with quiet despair.

“That’s where they’ll do it, right?” Lyon asked softly, voice flat and emotionless.

Luke hesitated—then nodded.

“The traitors will want to make it look like an act of mercy. A show of compassion.”

“Overwhelmed by grief after losing his son, the king took his own life,” Lyon muttered, mocking the line like it was a bad play. “What utter bullshit.”

He clenched his fists in his lap.

“My father never loved me. Not like he loved Leo…”

A silence settled over the room, thick and uncomfortable.

“Anyone in the palace who hears that story will know it’s a lie,” Lyon continued bitterly. “It’ll spark civil war the moment it’s announced.”

Luke studied the prince for a moment, caught between surprise at his insight and a familiar, gnawing guilt.

“You’re right,” he said quietly. “Which is why we don’t have much time. At most, we have a few days before the news spreads—and once it does, the capital will descend into chaos.”

He began pacing, his words growing more urgent.

“The Loyalist Faction will rise to oppose the traitors. Their most likely move will be to install the Right Minister as a temporary regent—with the understanding that the throne will pass to your youngest brother once he comes of age.”

Luke’s voice sharpened.

“As for the traitors… whoever they put forward doesn’t matter. It’ll be a puppet king, nothing more. With Ralis fallen, Lhair will have effectively seized control of the Marxx Kingdom. One by one, the other cities will follow.”

The weight of his words settled into the damp air of the shack, thickening the tension.

“So…” he said, voice barely above a whisper, “if we want to save the kingdom—we fight.”

“Huh!?” Brian jolted upright, eyes wide. “Fight? Luke, you don’t have an army. How the hell do you expect to take part in a civil war without any forces?”

Luke let out a slow sigh, feeling the room’s attention narrowing on him like a vice.

“Ideally… we won’t need to fight,” he said gently. “We have the one thing they don’t.”

He glanced toward Lyon.

“The true heir to the throne. The next in line.”

But it was Kayson who cut in next, brows furrowing.

“Wait—Lyon isn’t the Crown Prince. He’s not next in line.”

Luke opened his mouth to respond—but Lyon beat him to it.

“My brother is dead.”

The words fell like a stone into still water. Silence followed, deep and complete.

Even if the Loyalist Faction managed to win and eliminate the traitors, they might still refuse to crown Lyon, believing that the Crown Prince was still alive. Without a clear line of succession, the entire kingdom could remain fractured.

This was a flaw in his plan—a significant one.

“Well… I haven’t finalized all the details yet,” Luke admitted, clearing his throat awkwardly. “That’s why I gathered you all here. I need to know what resources we have before I can form a solid plan for the operation.”

The tension in the room thickened as grim expressions settled onto each face. Luke could feel the anxiety pressing down on them like a storm cloud.

But they had no choice. Not really.

A large Lhairan force was marching toward the capital even as they spoke.

Then, like he’d been clubbed over the head with a hammer, Luke froze mid-thought.

“Ah… I forgot to mention something,” he said sheepishly.

Silence.

Every eye in the room locked onto him. The air grew colder by the second.

A few moments later…

“How the fuck do you forget a detail like that?” Kayson groaned, rubbing his temples like he was trying to stave off a migraine. “I don’t remember you being this crazy, Luke. Seriously…”
