Don started snuffing torches.

One at a time. Quiet, focused, methodical.

He stayed low, used Camouflage when needed, and practiced the little compression trick with his Telekinesis. It still wasn’t easy. Some of the flames took longer. Some refused entirely until he adjusted the mental grip. But every time he got it right, a torch died. One less glow. One more patch of darkness he owned.

It was great practice for his Telekinesis. Don really had to strain himself and use mental energy each time. But it got easier and easier. After some twenty torches, he got that sweet, sweet notification.

[Racial Skill Increased: Telekinesis]

[Telekinesis Level 5]

After the level up the snuffing out torches got significantly easier, and Don didn’t have to waste so much time. He had a lot of lights to take out.

He moved like a shadow through the shanty castle. Corridors, storage rooms, balconies, stairs. Wherever a torch burned, Don followed. And when he found a brazier, he got creative. He’d pull the coals out one by one and roll them across the floor until they cooled. Sometimes he dropped them into cracks in the boards.

Every few extinguished lights, he paused to listen.

The drums in the throne room still beat. Muffled now, less intense. No shouting. No search party. The Grimoids hadn’t noticed yet. Or if they had, they didn’t think anything of it. Maybe they just assumed a gust of wind came through, or a servant knocked a torch down. Probably happened all the time in this ramshackle maze of creaking wood and half-burnt meat smoke.

Or not. Maybe it was a feature of the dungeon. Some manner of System bullshit. On one hand the Grimoids seemed intelligent. They had weapons, military tactics and architecture. But on the other hand they were mindless.

Don shrugged and didn’t question his luck. He just kept moving.

After snuffing out a complete corridor, Don ran into a pair of guards. The Grimoid hobgoblins gibbered to each other in quick nervous tones. They didn’t see Don, he was in a shadowed corner, his Camouflage activated.

But they leveled their spears and started poking at the air and jumping at shadows. It was all very amusing. Like half-blind kittens stumbling around trying to look important.

When they got close enough to pounce, Don killed them with a few deft moves. He was getting good at the assassination tactics.

Eventually, the upper levels of the castle were pitch black. Only a few stubborn torches near the king’s hall remained, casting long orange lines through slats in the floor. Don stayed in the dark, crouched behind a broken railing, watching shadows flicker across the lower platforms.

He felt calm.

The plan was all but ready. Only the king’s chamber was lit. Don could snuff out a torch from ten feet away within a few seconds. He could maybe take out a few in the boss room before the enemy would notice. But that was fine if they did. They would chase him and he would use their own home against them.

It’s time I set this in motion.

Don moved back to the kitchen and carefully pushed the door to the boss room slightly ajar. The king still sat on his throne, leaning against a fist. The hobs were at the tables, still drinking and laughing.

Don put a torch by the door. Then he held his breath.

Nothing happened. The hobs didn’t notice. They were too occupied with their tankards and meat. But the king’s eyes moved.

Slowly they turned towards the door. It couldn’t see Don because he had angled himself by the wall. Surely not. But when Don peeked in the shadows with his Stealth and Camouflage activated, a slow smirk spread across the king’s brutal face. It slightly tilted its head and reached for the giant knobbly club.

In the next moment the kitchen door shattered, as the massive weapon shot through it like a missile.

The king barked a few harsh words and immediately the laughter and gibbering talk stopped. Chairs were pushed, weapons gathered.

Good.

The hunt was on. Don was already running. He looked back and saw the Grimoids enter the kitchen, pushing each other. They stopped and exchanged short words and grunts. Don threw a spearhead at the closest one. It roared in anger when the blade pierced its hand, making it drop its shield.

Immediately the Grimoids united in a warcry and started charging towards Don’s direction. Don ran deeper into the castle.

The Grimoids followed, spears leveled. But only two of them could move side-by-side in the narrow corridors. That would be Don’s advantage. Darkness and lack of ability to use their superior numbers.

Don ran around in the castle, throwing his spearheads and blowpipe darts. Sometimes he would nick a Grimoid with an attack, then run into a corner and leverage his Stealth and Camouflage and let a few of them follow him. The Grimoids would run past Don, and he would go lure another group into another direction of the labyrinthine castle.

Soon enough Don had the enemy scattered.

Alright. It’s time I start picking them off.

Don moved like smoke through the dark. He jumped up, ten, twelve feet. Then he jammed his claws into the wood and hung mid-air, like a spider in wait.

The first group passed. Four hobgoblins, separated just enough to be vulnerable. Don watched from above completely still. He waited. Let them pass. The rear one was lagging behind, clearly not used to fighting blind.

Don dropped on it like a guillotine.

The claws went in clean. He clamped the thing’s mouth shut with one hand, yanked it back into the shadows, and finished it fast. No scream. Just a wet crunch.

The others didn’t notice.

They turned a corner and Don followed. He let them reach the edge of the corridor where two broken crates had fallen in a pile. Then he picked up a fist-sized brick of charcoal with his mind and launched it. It struck the leader right in the side of the head. Not a kill. But enough to stagger.

They turned around, snarling, weapons up.

Don was already in their faces.

He slashed the staggered one across the throat. Turned and slammed his palm into another’s face, sending it stumbling back with a psychic shove. The third tried to jab him with a spear but hit the wall. Don kicked the shaft out of its hand, grabbed it, and broke the creature’s leg with the butt end.

They died messily.

Don barely glanced at the notifications. He was already moving again.

The next pack was trickier. They’d stuck together. Five of them, two with shields, three with spears. Don led them into a loop of corridors, making noise just enough to keep them chasing him. When he was sure they were following, he zipped through a tight squeeze between two support beams and stopped at a torch he’d left burning earlier.

He doused it with his mind.

Now it was pitch black.

The Grimoids rounded the corner. Spear tips scraping the floor, heavy feet stomping. Don didn’t attack head-on. He stepped into the shadows behind a rotted pillar and waited. When the rear one paused to squint, he grabbed it and dragged it backward, claws first.

The others didn’t even turn. They kept moving forward.

Don grinned.

He trailed them, picked them off one at a time. A dart to the knee here. A coal to the face. A silent stab. By the time the last one realized it was alone, it panicked and ran straight into a dead end.

Don didn’t even rush it. He walked forward. Calm. It jabbed its spear, wide and desperate. Don caught it mid-thrust, pulled the creature into his elbow, and jammed a claw through its throat.

Don dragged his victim into a corner and waited for his comrades to stumble around in the darkness, and start chasing shadows.

Then Don used Devour.

[Health at 100%][Energy at 100%]

He exhaled slowly, crouched again, and listened. The drums had stopped.

Silence.

He had won the first fight. But the king hadn’t moved from its throne. It would require Don to fight in its lair.

Fine. Bring it.

He glanced at the kill count through scrolling through the System notifications. he’d dropped twenty-seven of the hobgoblins by now. That was most of them. All from the shadows. All without giving them a fair fight.

He liked it that way.

Nine left. Then it’s time for a little regicide.
