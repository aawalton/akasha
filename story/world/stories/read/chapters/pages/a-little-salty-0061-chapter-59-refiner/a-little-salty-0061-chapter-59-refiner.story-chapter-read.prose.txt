Despite my eagerness to play with this so-called remnant, I am not completely immune to Shirasil’s warnings; I can’t use it to kill Widengra if it kills me, first. I’ll need to handle it as carefully as my poisons—more carefully, probably, given the poison resistance I’ve built up.

There’s one issue to deal with first, however; I’m supposed to turn this in to the Guild tomorrow morning.

Or, night.

Whatever.

After Shirasil leaves, my mind is spinning with too many ideas to fall back asleep. With an annoyed sigh, I get up and remove my Apothecary from my Inventory.

The spread covers almost the entire floor of the room, and looks as if the contents of an occultist warehouse got tangled up in a fishing net.

Which isn’t far from the truth. The backing is made of huge woven strips of leather, cross-hatched so it’s easier for me to fix things to it. I’ve got everything from alchemic equipment, to bottles of potions, vials of poison, raw ingredients, and even some emergency food rations in case I need to do some meal prep on the fly. One nice thing about the Inventory is that nothing within it seems to age, so as long as I don’t keep the Apothecary outside of my Inventory for too long, I can carry pieces of raw meat around without worrying about it spoiling.

The only real drawback is that it’s all or nothing; if something isn’t tightly secured to the Apothecary, it gets left behind. This can be really annoying if, say, you add a pouch of coins to the Inventory without closing the pouch all the way. In that case, the Inventory might label them as two separate things, adding the sack to the Inventory while spilling all the coins across the ground.

It’s exactly this “problem” that I intend to exploit now.

I remove the gourd from my Apothecary, where I had secured it the previous night. I can hear the remnant rolling around inside the gourd when I tip it. Doesn’t sound much bigger than a marble—though it’s at least big enough to not fall out the opening. How did they even get it in there in the first place?

Not that it really makes a difference for me. I pop the corked end open and hold the flask over a folded cloth. “Echo, add this gourd to my Inventory.”

[Gourd added to Inventory.]

The flask vanishes, and something falls onto the cloth. I lean forward to get a better look.

At first glance it looks identical to the decoy Shirasil gave me: a black marble, not quite an inch across. But as I lean in to get a better look, I can make out something moving over its surface—or maybe, within it. The movement is slow, like the rotation of a storm, and made up of hundreds of tiny markings. Runes, I think. I reach for it, and the runes spin faster. I move my hand away, and they slow once more.

“Huh.” Echo, Check.

[Check: a refiner. Occupied.]

“What’s a refiner?” I ask.

[A refiner is a device which contains a remnant.]

I guess that’s why it’s “occupied.” And why Echo labeled the decoy as “empty.” I again try to ask Echo for clarification on what a remnant is, but I still only get [A powerful relic that <REDACTED.>]

Why is this thing dangerous to the gods? How? I’m itching to get to the bottom of it.

Right now, however, I’ve got work to do.

“Echo, remove gourd from Inventory.” The gourd reappears before me, and I catch it. Leaving the remnant—or refiner, or whatever—alone for now, I set about grabbing a handful of different tools and ingredients from my Apothecary.

I haven’t made a Shrinking potion before, but I have the recipe for one. Annoyingly, it comes from one of the alchemy books Lisari gave me. At one point not long after I learned her true identity, I considered burning it, just out of spite. But the book is just so stupidly useful, and in the end I couldn’t bring myself to destroy a book—even one that was given to me by a god. Besides, after comparing it to some spell books I’ve since borrowed from the Guild, all the spells I checked against each other had the same recipe, so there’s no funny business going on.

The cauldron starts to simmer as I chop up a handful of dried silverhock and begin to weigh it out on a balance scale. A sharp, earthy scent rises into the air as I work through more herbs, and I let out a content sigh, enjoying the simple process of simply cutting, weighing, scraping ingredients into bowls, and grinding bones into a fine powder. This is the best part of the potion making process, because there’s no hurry. Once the first ingredient goes in the pot, however, timing becomes everything; over or under cook the brew, heat it too hot or too cold, and the spell can completely unravel—or, better yet, backfire.

[Daily Role Requirement complete,] Echo says as I finish preparing the ingredients. And that’s another perk of the prepwork part of the process.

As soon as everything is ready, I kick off the process by hovering my hands over the steaming water. My Heat Resistance I got from my upgraded Class comes in handy here, but I’ve also gotten a lot more efficient about adding my mana to potions; it only takes a couple seconds of focus to get the green light to wisp down from my palms into the cauldron. There’s no sound as the magic makes contact with the water, but the entire pot lights up green and stays that way, even when I remove my hands.

Next comes the silverhock. Then the ground mouse bone. Then a touch more magic. My back starts to ache as I crane over the pot, keeping a close eye on its color, consistency, and even smell.

After a grueling few hours of brewing, cooling, adding more ingredients, and brewing again, the potion finally settles into its finished state: One bottle of a murky, orange liquid. I again check the spellbook for the right ratio to use; I don’t want the spell to last too long. Looks like two drops should achieve what I want.

I drip a couple drops onto Shirasil’s duplicate refiner. Being careful not to touch any of the liquid myself, I hold a finger over the marble and leak some of my magic into it.

The liquid glows when my magic touches it, activating the spell. Slowly, so, so slowly, the marble begins to shrink. I wait for it to stop; the last thing I’d want is to pick it up before the spell is complete and have those few drops of potion affect me. Although, since I’m so much larger than the refiner, it probably wouldn’t affect me for long. Still, though. Best not to subject myself to any potions accidentally. I do learn!

Once it’s stopped shrinking, ending up the size of a pea, I roll it inside the gourd and stick the cork back on. Now all I have to do is wait until the spell wears off and it’s grown back to its original size, and no one will be the wiser.

Looking around the mess I created from making the potion, I grimace. At least I’ve got something to occupy my time.

First, though, I turn back to the real refiner—the one that has a remnant in it. I wonder what it’s a remnant of. And what does a remnant do? Time to find out.

I cautiously poke it a few times with a ladle, and nothing happens. I even drop a bead of my mana onto it, and the marble doesn’t react. Using a cloth to cover my hand, I gingerly pick it up.

Nothing. Seems like a normal old marble to me. Well, the kind of marble with tiny runes swirling all over its surface. I roll the refiner out onto my bare palm.

Just as before, the moment it touches my skin, I feel something. It’s a faint tingle, like static electricity, but there’s something more. Like a word that’s on the tip of your tongue. A thought barely out of reach. I roll the marble around in my palm, and the sensation spikes, then fades. I frown, pinching the marble between my fingers and holding it up to inspect.

I slowly rotate the refiner and again feel another strange jolt of sensation. Something to do with its orientation? Like a compass? I continue to roll it between my fingers before I finally catch sight of it.

There’s a tiny, hairline crack, no more than a few millimeters long. When the crack touches one of my fingers, that’s when I feel the spike in… well, whatever that is. It’s not responding to orientation, then—something’s leaking out.

I gently pinch the marble. When I don’t feel it give, I squeeze a little harder. Still feels like a marble. I crush it in my fist as tight as I can manage, but the refiner is as unyielding as stone.

I take out my knife next and try to score the surface, but my blade won’t even leave a mark. I have a feeling that no matter how much pressure I apply, it won’t be enough to crack this egg open. So if conventional forces can’t do anything…

Then magic has to be the answer.

What proceeds is a series of unilluminating and increasingly violent experiments.

I try dripping some of my poisons onto it with no apparent effect. Heat and cold don’t seem to do anything. Acid rolls right off. Even my invisibility potion doesn’t turn it invisible, and it seems impervious to even my newly brewed shrinking potion. Frost seeds, mana bombs, decay potions—nothing. After an hour of throwing everything I have at the refiner, I remain stumped.

But far from deterred. In fact, I’m almost a little glad it wasn’t easy; the harder it is to crack open a chest, the better the loot it holds within.

I jump when a knock comes at my door. Who’d come looking for me at this hour of the day? The thought is answered only a moment later.

“Sal?” Cyros’s voice comes muted through the door. “You home?”

I grimace, looking around the messy room. If it looks like a bomb has gone off in here, that’s because one had.

More than one, actually.

Well, no time to clean it all up, now. I pocket the refiner and pick my way over to the door, tiptoing around cauldrons, cutting boards, and dozens of potion bottles.

I open the door with a squint. “Cyros? What are you doing here?”

He raises a skeptical brow. “I was going to ask you the same thing. You weren’t at the Guild so I came looking for you.”

I stifle a yawn, pushing the door open to let him in as I turn back to my room and start the laborious process of cleaning everything up. “In the middle of the day?”

“No?” Cyros stops in the doorway, mostly because it’s impossible to step further into the room without utilizing some form of acrobatics. “It’s past dusk. I saw on the job board that you hadn’t turned in your—”

“It’s what?” I turn back to him, eyes going wide. “What time?”

“Three hands past sunset,” he says, glancing around my room. “What in the Heavens have you been up to in here?”

Crap! I’m late. I begin haphazardly stuffing corks back onto bottles and hurriedly strapping things down to the Apothecary. No time to clean up cauldrons and mortars; I leave most of my potion making supplies in a corner of the room and add the Apothecary back to my Inventory. A handful of ingredient bottles clatter to the floor from where I failed to strap them down securely enough. I glare at the offending herbs but leave them; they all can wait. What can’t wait is the job board.

“Did today’s posts already go up?” I ask as I tug on my boots and clasp my potion bandolier around my chest. I throw a cloak over my rumpled clothes and claw my hair back into a ponytail.

“Er, not when I was there,” Cyros says, watching all this in bafflement. “Did you get any sleep since last night? What have you been working on?”

“Stuff.” I snatch up the gourd with the replacement refiner in it; it’s been long enough the potion’s almost certainly worn off. I clip the horn to my belt and tuck it back within the folds of my cloak, then grab my chef’s knife, hastily wiping it off before sheathing it at my hip.

“Okay.” I pat myself down, spinning around once to make sure I haven’t left anything important in the room. “I think I’m good. Ready?”

“Yeah,” Cyros says, a bemused smile creeping over his face. “Are you sure you’re ready?”

“Totally.” I step out of my room and Cyros waits as I reset the magic locks on my door. The spell circles glow as I push magic into them, then fade from sight. “Right. Let’s go! I don’t want to miss any new job postings—have to grab the best ones right when they go up, you know?”

Cyros snorts. “Yeah, I know. Well, I know you do that. Can’t say I feel I’ve ever missed out on any good jobs by being an hour late, though.”

I strike out into the street at a brisk pace. “Well that just shows you don’t have any taste for what jobs are good.”

“Sure,” he says, drawing the word out in amusement. “Because I’m so inexperienced.”

“You’re not inexperienced,” I grumble, knowing he’s about to rub his seniority in my face. “But you’re nowhere near as driven as me.”

He snorts. “I won’t fight you on that.”

I glance at him as we hurry toward the Guild. Now that I’m thinking about it, he’s always been pretty nonchalant about this whole Assassins Guild thing. And after running into more of the members—all of them quiet, solitary, and shifty—Cyros doesn’t exactly fit the standard Blackcloak bill.

“Why’d you want to get into this business, in the first place anyway?” I ask him.

Cyros blinks, turning to me in surprise. “Where’d that come from?”

I shrug. “Never asked.”

He gives a noncommittal hum, turning his attention back to the streets. “I needed something I couldn’t get anywhere else.”

“That’s a dodgy answer,” I remark.

He snorts at me with a wry smile. “Yeah, because you’re so well known for your honesty and forthcomingness.”

“Ouch. But touché.” I don’t press him on it. If he doesn’t want to tell me, it’s not my place to go digging.

But dammit if I’m not curious now.

As we make it to the Guild, I swing by the job board to see Zaquedyil in the process of adding more notes. I swear, hurrying over to snatch my completed jobs from the “pending” area of the board and then rush to the verification counter. I’m already maxed out on jobs, so I can’t grab any of the new ones Zee is adding until I’ve resolved my current batch. I shift impatiently from foot to foot as I hand over the items, intel, and job papers, waiting for the dhampyr to process them all. They tap each page in turn, spells illuminating on the page to check if I’ve met the requirements for satisfying their terms. I try not to stare at the gourd as I hand that over as well; no one should be able to tell it was tampered with. Shirasil said so. He wouldn’t screw me over on this, right?

No, he totally would.

A knot is tying itself in my stomach when the dhampyr nods, clearing the jobs and items off the counter. “The board will be updated appropriately.”

The building anxiety leaves me in a rush, and I grin, already spinning back toward the job board. “Thanks!”

I rush over to Cyros’s side, who is already checking out the new postings. “Anything good?” I ask him, heart beating fast and nearly out of breath. I chalk that up to the rushed pace we set to get over here rather than the little bit of subterfuge I just pulled off.

In the Blackcloak Guild.

While surrounded by professional assassins.

It’s definitely just the walk over here that’s got me all jittery.

But Cyros is staring at the wall with a frown, and doesn’t even react to my question.

“Hey.” I wave my hand at him. “Cyros?”

I glance up in time to see my token raise from Rank C to Rank B jobs. “Hey!”I grab his arm, shaking it in excitement. “I just went up! Did you see?”

Cyros finally looks at me, but his expression is grave. I falter, my hand slipping from his elbow. I’ve never seen him with a look like that before. I’ve seen him take things seriously, sure. But never a look like this. Like someone’s life is at stake.

“Sal.” His voice is low but intense, his eyes hard. “I need to ask you for a favor.”
