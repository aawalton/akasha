Now that he had Guide right there, though, Luke wanted more answers. A multitude of half-formed questions still rattled around in his head, but when he turned, raising a finger as if to point at something, the damn man, if he could be called a man, was gone.

"Figures," Luke grumbled. Looking up at the sky again, he shook his fist. "I know you're out there watching! I've got more questions!"

No answer. Meh. Dungeons were still important. That much was clear. Vital, even if you didn't count the level and experience gain. If everyone just stopped doing the dungeons, The Greater System would succumb to the void. Grim, that. That meant he couldn't spend every waking moment working on enchanting whenever he wasn't off dealing with the void in a more direct manner. Before entering, Luke sent a long message to Milla and the others, detailing what he'd just learned. Guide said he could share information with Luke, but nothing about Luke keeping it to himself, so that was sort of on him.

Stepping through the portal, Luke did his utmost to sense the weave. If it was just a thin coat of paint over the void, he should be able to tell now that he knew, he figured. Not so. The fresh air and sunshine, the gentle breeze through the leaves and the chirping of birds overhead felt as real as the clothes he wore. The weave in there was limited and simple, but there was no hint of the void lurking around its end seam. Strange. It wasn't a simulation. All that he saw, felt, and smelled was real, even if it was not. He should have pressed for more details from Guide because there was just something about this that didn't add up, but he couldn't say what, and now time was up.

The sound of a door slamming open drew Luke's attention to a small cluster of buildings farther down the path. A skeleton leapt out, wielding a scimitar over its head, evil-red light gleaming in its empty sockets. Perhaps its clicking and clacking charge would have been scary at some point, but it was the middle of the day, and Luke had seen far worse.

Skeleton. Level 32.

The level was pretty high, but c'mon... It ran at Luke, who narrowed his eyes and tried to discern if this thing was a creature of the void, as Guide had explained. No matter how hard he tried, all he saw was a skeleton. When the undead monster drew close, Luke withdrew his staff and held it out in front of him so the butt pointed right at his charging opponent. The skeleton was clever enough to sidestep the length of wood, but hadn't anticipated threads of mana emerging from within it. A moment later, it was on the ground, wrapped up in threads, its weapon out of reach on the ground.

Luke walked over and hunched down. "Can you speak?"

It craned its neck, but rather than say something, it bit the air, struggling against the restraints. Delving into its narrow mana channels revealed nothing, and its withered little monster core wasn't even worth feeding to Smudge, who'd loped off when Guide appeared. Disappointed, Luke killed the monster by retrieving the core. If Earth were going to run on these things, they would need a lot of them going forward.

Picking up the scimitar, Luke enchanted it and then tossed it into his inventory. Maybe it would be worth a few credits.

"Please help!"

The female scream, full of fear, originated from the largest building up ahead. Luke set off running and checked his interface while he closed the gap.

New Quest: Free Willow's Rest from The Cult of Undead.

Reward: Lessen the strain on The Greater System.

Way to make it obvious what was going on...

"Couldn't throw in a few credits?" Luke shouted at the still forest while he ran between the buildings. Sounds arose from almost all four buildings, but he kept going to where he thought he'd heard the cry for help. Barging through the front door, he saw a woman pressed up against the wall with three more skeletons standing right in front of her. There was fresh blood on the floor, and a dead man slumped over a round table in Luke's path. His tunic was also covered in blood, but the woman had none on her gray dress or white bonnet. As if those weren't clues enough, a subtle spellweave formed over her head even as she cried out for help again.

Luke weavestepped forward, pushed the skeletons aside, and slammed a fist into her face even as he sliced through the spellweave. Acting almost on reflex, he didn't have time to surmise the exact nature of the spell, but he'd seen some of those shapes before in poison magic. Nasty business. The skeletons fell to pieces the moment Luke finished the job, taking the woman's monster core. A core and not a meta-heart. If there were no other clues, that would be enough. Holding the core up in the palm of his hand, it was about half the size of a tennis ball but almost a perfect sphere. In his experience, those with more mana control, spellcasters and the like, would often have rounder cores with fewer imperfections. He wondered what that meant. Also, what the hell were monster cores? If these things were void creatures, what were the cores?

"Should've asked Guide that," Luke said while he took all corpses into his inventory. They wouldn't fetch many credits, but it had become a habit at that point. He looked up at the ceiling. "Don't want to weigh in? Monster cores? Hello?"

Just for fun, he attempted to message Guide through the gem. It didn't work. Of course, it didn't.

The sounds from the other buildings had been growing stronger, and feet slapped on the short stone steps leading up to the front door as Luke turned around. A man appeared, one inspect didn't work on.

"You're not one of the cultists, I hope?" Luke asked.

The older man threw himself over the dead man, tears streaming down his cheeks. He spoke in a language Luke didn't recognize, but after the first few words, they turned to English, as if by magic. System magic.

"No, no, no. I thought they wouldn't come back. Not after all we've sacrificed! Oh, Lupe!"

Luke was about to speak again when a group of four skeletons charged through the door. They got stuck for a moment when all of them tried to go through at the same time, but soon untangled themselves and charged in. They wielded hatchets, knives, and a hammer, tools you'd find on a farm. Despite their levels, all over thirty, Luke didn't have a problem dispatching them. By the time he was done, he saw the old man watching him from behind an overturned table, where he'd hidden.

"Who are you?" the man asked.

"Luke. Is this Willow's Rest?"

The old man blinked. "No, this is my home. The village is a few hours' ride down the path." Tears formed in his eyes again. "They took everything from me! My son!"

Luke swallowed hard and looked away from the older man's mourning. "Who did? The cult?"

"You know of them, then? Yes! They came recruiting last year, taking many of the listless young from the village, barely men and women yet. They returned six months ago to recruit again. When no one wanted to join them, they threatened to take our children by force!"

"Assholes," Luke said.

The older man kept going, spewing backstory and adding color to the quest. "We sent a few who were willing to sacrifice themselves for the greater good. They wanted to investigate the cult and bring others back with them in turn. None returned. Now they're back again, and it's like they don't recognize us!"

"And they're in the village?"

"Oh dear lords of the forest, I hope not."

"I'll go check in a minute," Luke said. "Since there were more skeletons here, I'm guessing there are another cultist or two around your farm."

The older man stood over his son again, a gentle hand on his blood-stained shoulder. "Thank you, stranger."

As Luke stepped outside to see at least twelve skeletons arrayed before him, some holding bows and proper weapons like swords and spears, others with nothing more than their bony fists, he remembered something.

Frowning, he fingered the front of his robe. "Wasn't this thing supposed to help against you guys?"

The bow-wielding skeletons drew, and the others charged without a sound.

"Maybe this is it?" Luke asked, raising the hood over his head. The runes glowed, and the skeletons stopped dead in their tracks. When he took a step forward, they stepped back. If he didn't know better, he'd say they were trembling. "Run!" he shouted, and the skeletons all broke, threw down their weapons, and fled.

"It was the hood," Luke said with a grin.

"Interesting," a voice called out as the fleeing skeletons all fell to pieces. "Most interesting."

Luke wondered if the scenarios in the dungeons were something that depended on The Greater System or if it was some random thing, or maybe things that had happened in this system at some point, before it fell to the void? Perhaps he should start writing questions down for the next time he met Guide.

A thin, pale man with long white hair, stained brown with dirt and leaves, wearing nothing but short, torn trousers, walked out into the small, empty space between buildings. "To make my sweetings run in spite of my direct influence? Interesting indeed."

Adept Karlsson. Level 41.

Luke turned to him. "Are you the boss? Because that would be super convenient."
