Ethan looked down on the ruins of the Nator city from a tall cliff, Selina beside him, already in full scholar-mode. They’d left the army behind, not wanting the thousands of demons to ravage what remained of one of the few pieces of Nator civilization. The city–more of a village, really, though perhaps a thousand souls could have lived here comfortably–was built into a kind of bowl in the earth. For protection, it would seem, something of a theme based on what Ethan could see.

Most of the old construction had been lost to time, though virtually everything was made from stone, so the skeletons of buildings were still visible. Yet the interior of the city was practically barren when compared to the surprisingly large wall.

“Looks like they were trying to keep something out,” Ethan mused.

“Many somethings, from the look of it,” Selina said.

“See the way the wall is built tilted slightly outward at the top? Impressive construction, but more importantly, you’d have to be Dusk rank just to climb the thing.”

Tomo appeared next to them. “This place appears to have been constructed after my time. We were still growing used to the increasing danger from the wilds when I perished.”

“Your people must have lasted some time,” Ethan said. “Everything about this place suggests it was made specifically to keep enemies out. That wall is forty feet tall, and fifteen thick. How’d they even build something like that?”

“You’re not thinking about our Soul Runes,” Selina said. “We’re naturally stronger than humans, but anyone who lived past childhood would have gained powers of some kind. They likely carried those stones by hand, and thought little of it.”

“Hard to imagine,” Ethan said. “Magical power used for fighting, and big walking cities has started to feel normal to me, but even on Viridus the vast majority of the population was Unbound. It’s hard to picture a world where everyone is basically a superhero.”

“Not quite so extreme as that,” Tomo said. “Do not forget that Soul Runes are an extension of one’s nature. Our world may have grown more dangerous, but that doesn’t mean that every Nator was a warrior. Many produced food, or made things with their hands.”

“I wonder if that changed as the corruption grew,” Selina mused.

“We should enter while we can,” Ethan suggested. “It looks relatively peaceful now, but our presence could draw demons.”

“Agreed,” Selina said excitedly. “I can’t wait to explore!” She leapt into the air, landing on a disc of light, then sailed the hundred or more feet down to the city.

Ethan looked at Tomo. “How are you handling all this?” he asked.

“I look upon the ruins of my own civilization, Ethan Bishop. There are no words for what I feel.” He paused, then looked down at Ethan. “But if I must face this, it is better to do so with a friend.”

Ethan smiled, then jumped after Selina, landing just outside the wall that any castle on Earth would have envied. Still, it hadn’t been enough, as Selina was inspecting a thirty foot gap, likely the work of whatever had ultimately seen the end of the village.

He put a hand on her shoulder, then they walked in together.

They found themselves not far from what appeared to be the main street, based on how the remaining buildings were oriented. They could distantly make out the ruins of some kind of tower in the center of the city, and began walking slowly in that direction.

After several minutes of passing empty stone houses, Selina asked Tomo if he could try to play tour guide.

“I will do my best, kinswoman,” he said, then gestured as they walked. “Homes were large, as our people were all long-lived, and many generations remained under a single roof. I myself had many children…I cannot remember how many, but I have impressions of a busy home. I suspect I had enough grandchildren to start a small village of my own.”

He paused, seeming surprised by how quickly he’d become lost in his own memories. Finally he cleared his throat. “The home was not meant for much more than sleeping, and quiet evenings. Community was very important to us, and we worked, played, and ate our meals outside.”

He gestured to the wide roads, and large gaps between ruined structures. “Great fires burned for most of the day, for cooking, and warmth. The manthis trees provided more wood than we could ever need.” He looked up at the empty sky. “Their forests blocked out the horizon in many places.”

“Ahead is the market. We did not have much commerce or trade–the roads were always dangerous–but we had many craftspeople. Even those of us with other vocations enjoyed making things with our hands. I…I weaved. The market was largely for trading, and an excuse to talk and visit amongst the clans.”

“Clans?” Selina asked.

Tomo nodded. “An ancient tradition, and one often forgotten in the face of other needs, but most villages had several primary lineages, often with specific skills. My own clan had many shamans. We provided leadership, and kept our peoples’ lore.”

“What are those for?” Ethan asked, as they passed a third stone plate, this one cracked and broken.

“Tunnels,” Tomo said. “Nator cities almost always had large tunnel systems dug beneath them. We used them for storage, defense, and shelter from the storms.”

“Storms?” Ethan said in surprise. “I’ve only ever seen the same bleak, cloudy skies. I was beginning to wonder if it ever even rained here. Let alone stormed.”

Tomo smiled. “The weather does seem to have calcified, for reasons I cannot guess. But I smell a storm coming even now. Likely in the next ten days you’ll see what Potentia considers a light sprinkling.”

Ethan decided he didn’t like the twinkle in the demon’s eyes.

They eventually came to the broken tower in the center of the city, and found it surrounded by a large carved out bowl, not unlike the city itself. Within were rows of fallen stones, half buried in red dirt. Just below the tower stood a raised platform, and Tomo looked at it longingly.

“Here is where my people took council, told stories, or hosted debates. In more difficult times, it was where we planned for battle.”

Selina looked up. “Was there much fighting between Nator?”

Tomo shrugged two sets of shoulders. “A difficult question to answer. Potentia provided enough hardship that few found reason to look beyond their own borders. But there is always someone. When war came, it would either be struck down quickly, or spread across the lands.”

The demon gestured to where the massive stones of the broken tower had fallen on the opposite side of the bowl. “If we move those, we should find the history of this village.”

“Amazing!” Selina said. Then she pointed at the blocks, the ones that weren’t broken were easily as large as a car. “Ethan, get to work.”

Ethan grumbled, but there was a smile on his face. He was enjoying imagining his friends’ people before the tragedies that had befallen them. But something about being Twilight rank felt a lot like being the guy in the friend group with a truck. He may have powers beyond the imaginations of most humans, but somehow he kept getting stuck moving heavy things.

Still, there was something pleasant about picking up several tons of rock, and tossing them like they were weightless. After less than a quarter hour, Ethan had revealed a twenty foot wall of stone, mostly intact. It was entirely covered in writing, and Selina moved forward eagerly. Then she froze, aghast.

“Who would do this?” she asked

“Apparently Potentia has a graffiti problem,” Ethan said, similarly surprised.

Tomo walked forward and rested a hand against the deep, messy scratches that overlaid the small careful print beneath. “This village saw some of the final days of my people,” he whispered.

Ethan raised an eyebrow, but saw that his friend was lost in emotion. He leaned toward Selina. “What is this?”

She frowned. “I can’t quite read the proper writing yet. Hopefully this sample gives me what I need.,” she began. “But the scribbles are simple, they say: ‘Run. They are coming. We go north.’”

“This was happening even in my day,” Tomo whispered. “There was always talk, rumors of safe places in one direction or another. These words would be left when the village was abandoned, so any who passed through could follow.”

Tomo traced the writing with a hand, then paused. “This…this is unexpected.” Selina and Ethan looked over, trying to see what he was referring to. “The village history…it tells of changes in our people.” He stepped back, eyes widening. “Come,” he said, marching away with purpose.

“I guess he’s in charge now,” Ethan said, hurrying after.

Tomo moved unerringly toward a stone plate on the nearby street, lifting it with one hand, then tossing it aside with a crash. He moved down into the darkness below, red-pink energy rising from his body to illuminate the way. Ethan and Selina hurried after, both hesitating slightly before venturing into the unknown depths.

At first, it wasn’t much more than Tomo had described. They found storage areas for tools and supplies, and more than a few that likely held food at some point. But as they followed Tomo into lower tunnels, a different purpose became clear.

“This is a tomb,” Ethan whispered, and Selina nodded beside him.

The walls were lined with remains. It looked as if they’d been wrapped in some kind of cloth, but it had almost all rotted away by now. Skeletons remained, some nearly dust, but as they ventured deeper, Ethan saw more recent dead.

Then he noticed a trend, and thought he understood Tomo’s concern. Above, the walls had been carefully carved, and decorated in writing, and scripts. The further they went, the more hastily he found the tunnels had been dug. Bodies went from carefully wrapped and interred, to deposited hastily, sometimes with more than one crushed into a single alcove.

Eventually, the tunnel opened wider, and the true tragedy of the city was revealed. Skeletons were heaped in piles, many still wearing rusted and ruined armor. Their bones were often broken, and many were missing limbs. They’d clearly been slaughtered in large groups, too quickly for proper burials. But that wasn’t what was truly disturbing.

As they moved even further, finding chamber after chamber, the skeletons changed.

They were subtle things at first. Some appeared too large, too broad of shoulder, or with odd proportions, but the changes became more pronounced as they ventured further. Ethan saw skulls with more than one pair of horns. Skeletal structures with superfluous bones, often around the back and shoulder.

By the sixth chamber, Ethan had begun to see extra limbs, and even wings. But those might be the lucky ones. Some were hunched, with extra joints, as if they were meant to walk on all fours. Others likely couldn’t have walked at all, but based on the armor, they’d still died fighting.

“This is what became of our people,” Tomo said, stopping in the final chamber. The group was surrounded by the most recently dead, and if they hadn’t just come through the previous tunnels, Ethan wouldn't have known he was even looking at Nator. This seemed like a pit of demons.

Selina looked pale, and kept turning in place, looking as if she might be sick. “I need to leave this place,” she said.

Tomo rushed to her side. “I will take you, kinswoman,” he said, then scooped her up, and ran from the room.

Ethan watched them go before moving closer to the nearest bones, and looking at them with the eyes of a doctor. He didn’t think the others had truly understood what they’d discovered. Yes, this was the terrible descent into demonism of the Nator, but that wasn’t new, merely horrific to witness.

As Ethan crouched and took a bone in his hand, testing its strength, he saw what his friends had not. Tomo had regained enough memory to know that he’d first lost his life and been reborn millennia ago, and assumed his people had fallen shortly after.

These bones were only a few centuries old.
