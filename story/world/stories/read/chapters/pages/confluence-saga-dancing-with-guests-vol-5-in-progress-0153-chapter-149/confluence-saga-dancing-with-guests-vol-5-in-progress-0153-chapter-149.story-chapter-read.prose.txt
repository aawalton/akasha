Annual Training Hall Competitions.

While the graduation rankings are ultimately the most important, the annual competitions between students feature as a fond memory for many of the students. At least for those who perform well.

Individual performances, both in the actual individual sense and the group sense, matter greatly. Winners of the solo, pair, and group dueling tournaments are seen as the likely front-runners for graduation honors, while overall placement of one dorm compared to another is treated as reason for bragging rights and camaraderie between the residents.

Tournaments are treated similarly to a typical class, restrictions applied on behaviour to ensure no risks to the participants and close oversight by the instructors. Full use of one's skill-set and strengths is only permitted during the graduation year tournament, or when explicitly authorized by the Dean to ensure a fair contest.

Fay stared at the results of her latest EDN research in disgust. There had to be something more out there. She couldn't even find the information she'd found years ago, she knew it was there, she knew that it existed, and yet apparently someone had managed to scrub it even more completely.

It was possible that she simply didn't have the access required to find it, that somehow it was in a partitioned area that a Holding Seer wouldn't even know was there. But there should have been at least some hint of reference to how the Doorkeepers had been created, how they worked.

A notification from her feed caught her attention. She didn't have any queries trawling for information so why was new data coming in, and from where? Her first glance told her nothing useful, it was just another set of metadata that matched her typical style but without the markers she used to keep track of their purpose.

A second look had her nearly sticking her face into the feed, not that it helped her see any better but it did leave her feeling confident she wasn't imagining anything. The results she had been expecting, the information about Doorkeepers she knew existed, was scrolling past her eyes.

The search had completed, the Technique had terminated, hadn't it? She'd sent it out, set it the task of finding the information and it had come back empty. But here was a second set of data, a new result that included the search, as though it was still working.

A Technique that kept working, one that persisted. A Construct? But then…

"I don't think you need to be that close."

Fay jerked back, toppling her chair over and falling to the floor as she heard her girlfriend laugh.

"Bri?"

"You're late for dinner."

"I'm..? It…" she said as she pulled herself to her feet.

"Come on," Briana said. "Your feed will still be there for you to stick your head in in the morning."

"But it answered twice."

"I don't know what that means," Briana said before holding up her hand. "You can tell me over dinner, not standing here in the office."

Fay took a step towards the doorway, pausing to glance over her shoulder at the feed before letting Briana guide her the rest of the way out of her office and shut the door behind them. There was something there, something that would explain what happened. She'd figure out what it was, figure out how to make it happen again on purpose.

Cara forced herself not to fidget as she stared at their opponents. They hadn't actually faced each other in months, not in any way that counted. Anya and Seb had been too exhausted from their training to do more than go through the motions.

This was different, they'd had days to rest, to recover, to get back to their old selves. And Cara knew everything was going to go badly. Gwen hadn't ordered her to fight without restraint, hadn't told her she had to stop pulling her blows. But Gwen had to, Bern had made sure of that. Which meant Gwen was going to be taking risks and pushing herself, that Cara would need to be right there beside her to keep her safe.

Be careful, she sent, the private band between them still feeling odd even after almost a year of paired dueling.

End things quickly, Gwen sent back, stress and pain underlining her thoughts.

Quickly. That she could do, at least she hoped she could. The shorter the fight the less chance someone made a mistake. The light reflecting off her sword caught her eye before she refocused on their friends.

"Fight," Sarna said.

Six months ago she'd have known how things would go. Anya would charge at her, they'd engage while Seb tried to reach Gwen, both taking advantage of any opportunity to help end the main engagement. Seb would look for gaps, for weaknesses, Gwen would look for chances to relieve pressure on her.

Today started out almost the same, but within moments Cara knew this was not going to be the same fight. Anya hit her shield dead center, stopping her in her tracks before forcing her to hunker behind it as the blows kept coming. She struck back blindly, trusting the drills she'd been taught to guide her blade.

Over and over all she found was empty air. Even her Techniques finding nothing to ground them, her Energy going to waste as Anya managed to slip past every blow. She hissed as a crystallized blade bounced off her shoulder, cutting past her guard from an angle she was certain she'd had covered.

Gwen?

He- Where?

She pushed forward, Energy blasting off her shield to blow her opponent away. Anya swayed, adjusting her stance as the effect wrapped around her. A countering kick set her shield ringing from the force even as she felt Gwen panic.

She had to- Gwen was- She couldn't get away, the scratch in her shoulder pulsing as she tried to turn away from Anya, her attention caught by the memory of the kick she'd just blocked. She froze, her desire to help Gwen compounding on her need to protect her even as the challenge, the demand that Anya had somehow inflicted on her kept her from her charge.

"No!" she shouted as she threw herself at Gwen.

She lost track of her sword, she knew something would be said about that later, but her shield caught Seb's blow. She crashed into Gwen, tackling her to the ground as the follow up strike left her knee numb and crystals peppered her face and side.

She could feel Gwen struggling, physically demanding to be let up. She could feel the emotional strain through the bond, the demand that she fight, that she throw herself at their foes and come out victorious. The urge to resist was winning, the fight had escalated too quickly.

"Winners, Holding Syl," Sarna called out.

No! Gwen sent before sagging beneath her, the demand on her broken.

Bern failed, she continued almost gleefully. He made me and it didn't matter.

Gwen?

I'm fine Cara.

Gwen was fine, was safe. She had moved in time, had blocked in time. She knew nothing would have actually happened, that Seb's blow wouldn't have caused true injury. It didn't matter though, her role was to protect, to keep Gwen safe.

She stood and pulled her sister up beside her. She winced as the shallow cuts all over her Husk flared in protest. She'd be better by morning, but that didn't mean-

"I'm sorry," Seb said. "I didn't think you'd-"

"No," Gwen said. "You fought fairly. Both of you."

"But Cara…"

"I'll be fine. Ended up worse fighting goblins more than once."

"We all have," Anya said as she stepped over.

Cara looked at the pair. She'd seen them every day, she'd eaten with them, sat around talking with them, woken them up before they made fools of themselves in class, but she'd never taken the time to really look at them, not since they'd started training seriously.

They still smiled, they were still half lost in whatever conversation they had going on, whatever shared observation of the world. But something had changed, something small, something she couldn't quite name. They'd won the fight in moments, they'd taken Gwen and her apart the way all of them had taken apart the Blehmans time after time.

"Bern expected us to not pull our blows," Gwen said. "He wanted-"

"He pushed her," Cara said, surprising herself by the need to explain. "He named her so she had to do it."

"Cara's right. He pushed, he demanded it. But it didn't matter. He risked our getting in trouble for his pride."

"Next duel," Anya answered and Cara fought not to shiver at the tone. "He tried to hurt our family."

She almost said something, almost warned them to be careful. Then she took another look at their expressions. They knew, they understood. Her Gwen was safe, Bern on the other hand-

Your Gwen?!

I- she sent back as she spun in place, trying to find a direction that would let her hide.

She hadn't sent that, had she? But Gwen knew, she'd reacted to the unspoken words.

"Cara?" Anya said. "Is your hair turning pink?"
