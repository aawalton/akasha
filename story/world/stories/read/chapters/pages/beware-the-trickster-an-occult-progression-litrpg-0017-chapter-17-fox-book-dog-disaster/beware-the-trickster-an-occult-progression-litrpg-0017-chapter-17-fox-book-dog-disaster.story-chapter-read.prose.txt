“Hey… I think I’m seeing movement near the target’s house,” murmured the man in the black cloak.

Caleb exhaled through his nose, sharp and tired. For fuck’s sake.

First, it had been some random hooded burglar with no name, slipping in and out of houses like a damn ghost. Every lock in this neighborhood might as well have been made of butter for that guy. And now, just when the thief decided to crawl through the window of their target’s place… silence. Not a peep from him since. And suddenly there were strange flickers of motion outside the house.

Their target was a woman named Julia. Caleb still couldn’t believe his luck when he’d stumbled across her so soon after getting the commission from the Order. Fortune, or maybe just whatever cosmic prankster looked after desperate men, had decided to cut him a break. His assignment was simple: find the girl. The details of who wanted her or why were well above his pay grade. At the ‘Manor,’ information flowed up, not down. Grunts like him weren’t supposed to ask questions.

So, Caleb didn’t. As long as he found this Julia woman, he didn’t care who the hell was throwing the coin. Besides, if he handled this job right, maybe, just maybe, he’d earn more than a few coins. The whisper going around was that exceptional service at Manor could get you something better… a taste of real power.

He’d seen enough over the years to know the supernatural wasn’t just drunken rumor. Working as a detective, he’d encountered too many cases that defied natural explanation. It was one thing to read the score of the unnatural in the files of his work. It was another to see the music performed live.

He had seen his contact’s reflexes, the way he simply knew what was about to occur—which cutpurse in the square would make a attempt, which fist would fly. Once or twice could be written off as a gambler's luck, but he had watched the man predict an entire incident that ended with a family ritually slaughtering one another.

There was no doubt in Caleb.

And that was the man who served as his only tether to this ‘Manor.’ In a way, he was his agent. He provided the work and paid handsomely for the service. But Caleb had been a spectator in this shadow-world for too long. He knew something else lurked in the margins, something that could grant potent strength. And he wanted it.

It was the agent who’d steered him here, to this quiet street, to the new woman who’d just moved in earlier today. A beautiful stranger whose looks had already sparked gossip among the locals. And, just as the intel described, she wasn’t alone. She kept a pet, an impossibly fluffy little fox.

Exactly as reported.

Caleb straightened. Tired as he was, this wasn’t the time to slack off. Every detail mattered. His contact had warned him to stay sharp on this mission— don’t let her notice you, no matter what.

But just as he followed the line of Miles’s pointing finger, Caleb saw a flash of white fur… the distinct shape of a fox kit. It had to be her pet. But what was it doing outside at such an hour?

Caleb narrowed his eyes, trying to pierce through the thick darkness, but the shapes beyond the yard refused to make sense.

Maybe the little thing had slipped out… escaped somehow. But for a creature that small, “escape” could just as easily mean death sentence. One swoop from a hungry owl, or a stray dog with nothing better to do, and that baby fox would be gone before anyone even noticed.

Still, the longer he watched, the more off everything seemed. Faint flashes of light— irregular, colorless— flickered in the gloom. His fatigue vanished in an instant, adrenaline scrubbing away the drowsiness like cold water on his nerves.

“I’m going to take a closer look,” he whispered, barely moving his lips as he spoke from the rooftop’s edge.

Miles, crouched beside him, shot him a disapproving glare. “Hold on. Didn’t Sir Gideon tell us to wait before doing anything? Silas already went back to the Manor with the report, Gideon’s probably on his way here right now.”

Caleb knew that. Of course he did. The fox wasn’t his problem; none of this was. His orders were clear: observe, report, wait. But those flashes, those damned flickers of light, gnawed at him. Something inside him was screaming yes, that’s it, that’s supernatural! His pulse quickened at the thought. Magic. Finally, something real, something tangible.

“Just gathering a bit more intel,” he said, keeping his voice low but firm. “I’m not going far. You’ll still have eyes on me the moment Sir Gideon shows up.”

Miles opened his mouth to argue, but Caleb was already moving. He grasped a jutting brick, planted his foot into a shallow crack in the wall, and began climbing down, careful not to scrape or kick loose debris. He landed in a heap of garbage bags below with a muted thud and let out a quiet breath of relief. “Phew.”

Without glancing back, he slipped down the alley, sticking to the shadows until he reached its end. He pressed himself behind a wall and peered around the corner. Closer now, close enough to make out details that had been a blur from the roof.

And what he saw made his brain stall.

A book.

There was a book sprinting across the cobblestones, like it had sprouted legs made of its own pages and covers, flapping and stomping in chaotic rhythm. Caleb blinked hard, rubbed his eyes, and blinked again. No change. It was still there. Still running.

And chasing after it, of all things, was the white-furred baby fox.

His jaw slackened. For a man who’d spent years chasing rumors of the unnatural, seeing it with his own eyes left him momentarily speechless. The grin that slowly stretched across his face was half awe, half vindication.

So this was it… the real thing. If everything he’d witnessed under Sir Gideon could be brushed off as coincidence or trickery, this couldn’t. How in the name of the Goddesses did anyone explain a bloody book fleeing from a fox kit like it had a will of its own?

Now he understood exactly why Sir Gideon’s orders were so firm: do not engage, no matter what you see. Once Julia’s location was confirmed, their first move had to be to report it, and that’s exactly what they’d done. One of his apprentices had already gone to deliver the message. Enough time had passed that Sir Gideon was probably en route now.

Still, even standing there, quietly observing, Caleb couldn’t shake the feeling that he was bending some invisible rule. That just watching this scene counted as crossing a line.

But he wasn’t interfering. Just watching. Just collecting proof with his own eyes.

So he stayed where he was, breath shallow, gaze locked on the bizarre chase playing out in the dark.

Just observing.

And soon, what Caleb witnessed made his jaw drop further with every second.

The book was the source of the random, flashing colours. Symbols bloomed across its pages, each one invoking a fresh headache in Caleb’s skull just to look upon them. And these symbols wrought weird magic. Sometimes the spell seemed to speed the thing up, but other times, its own sorcery appeared to backfire spectacularly. Caleb knew nothing of how magic worked—this was, literally, his first glimpse—but he could somehow tell the book was tripping over its own power. The fox, meanwhile, seized every misstep, ferally tearing at its pages the moment it messed up.

And then he realized… they were making their way toward his alley. It was a slow, chaotic procession, but the book was already in the middle of the road, crossing toward his side, with the fox chasing it with a ferocity that bordered on unnatural. Something no one could expect from a stature so small and fluffy.

Caleb tensed, ready to retreat, when a low bark broke the tension. He frowned and looked down. Just a few meters away, half-hidden under a tree, a big stray dog was lying in the shadows. No wonder he hadn’t noticed it earlier, its fur was pitch black, blending perfectly into the night. And judging by its size, it wasn’t the kind of mutt anyone wanted to piss off.

The book noticed the dog too. But the dog’s gaze wasn’t on the book, it was fixed on the small white fox, eyes narrowing, teeth bared. In the next heartbeat, the dog lunged.

What Caleb didn’t expect was for the fox to lunge back.

It shot forward like a white missile, slamming straight into the dog’s stomach. The impact made a dull, gut-punching sound, and the bigger animal yelped in shock, stumbling backward. Before it could recover, the fox was already on its back, claws digging in deep. Then came the blows. Sharp, fast, vicious little slaps. One. Then another. Then another.

“Holy shit…” Caleb muttered under his breath, watching in disbelief. The dog’s head jerked side to side as if being pummeled by invisible hands. How the hell could something that small hit hard enough to knock teeth out? But sure enough, he saw something white scatter across the pavement.

“EE EEEE EEE EEE!” the fox muttered something intelligible as it slapped the life out of the poor dog.

He barely had time to process it. The book had finally stopped running, as if catching its breath, fluttering its pages like lungs. For a second it just stared at the scene, at the brutal one-sided brawl, before it began glowing again. This time, the light was red.

A faint circle of symbols appeared above the dog’s head, pulsing like a heartbeat. Caleb felt the headache return full force. But he couldn’t tear his gaze away.

The fox, too, paused for a second. It stopped its assault on the poor guy, then turned towards the book with a vicious grin.

The book gave a visible flinch. And right before his eyes, Caleb watched as the dog’s injuries sealed themselves shut and its teeth grew back, far sharper than before. Its eyes swam with a bloodshot rage while its muscles bulged, tearing its own skin. For a moment, it glared down at the puny fox who had dared to grab its neck and pull it down.

Even Caleb nearly pissed his pants. If before this dog was something you wouldn't want chasing you, now it was something you wouldn't even want to see in your nightmares. You were simply dead meat if it caught you.

An actual, honest-to-goodness monster. It had to be the diary's magic that had forged it into this new, terrible form.

The monster-dog now stood twice its original height, glaring down at the small fox, who merely looked back with a deadpan expression.

Then the fox lifted a paw and, with dexterous grace, pointed directly at the book before making a swift, unmistakable execution gesture across its own throat. The dog snarled... and then lunged… not in an attack, but into a prostrate, bowing gesture.

What in the seven hells was this turn of events?

Before Caleb could process the inverted obedience, the dog turned its deadly stare toward the book instead. The book flinched in a palpable recoil, and immediately started to run away.

Wait… not away. It was running right toward him. OH NO. NO, NO, NO! FUCK! FUCKING FUCK!

Caleb cursed, backing up before breaking into a full-blown sprint. He might not know the rules of the magical world, but Sir Gideon had warned him time and again: do not engage with any unnatural thing he might witness. Any. At all. The man never said "supernatural" or "magic," but the implication was chillingly clear.

And those words, coming from a man Caleb was 100% certain could see the future, and was actively watching it unfold, carried weight. He’d treated that advice like gospel. Just this once, curiosity had pecked a hole in his common sense. By the Goddesses, if that dog caught him, he’d be mincemeat before he even learned magic. That was not the epitaph he had in mind.

Fuck!

Cursing his own idiocy, he scrambled back to the trash heap beneath their perch. He didn't bother looking back. His hands found the familiar dent in the wall, his shoe jammed into its partner, and he hauled himself up. Adrenaline was a hell of a performance enhancer; even he was shocked by his own speed.

Miles watched him collapse onto the rooftop. "What happened to you?" he asked, worried.

Oh, a great many terminal things would have happened if he hadn't run like a man possessed… which, to be fair, he was.

"My view was blocked by the building after that, so I couldn't see much more," Miles said.

Caleb looked up, wrestling his breathing under control as he choked out the answer. "Well, why don't you get a front-row seat?" He pointed a trembling finger toward the alley he’d just vacated.
