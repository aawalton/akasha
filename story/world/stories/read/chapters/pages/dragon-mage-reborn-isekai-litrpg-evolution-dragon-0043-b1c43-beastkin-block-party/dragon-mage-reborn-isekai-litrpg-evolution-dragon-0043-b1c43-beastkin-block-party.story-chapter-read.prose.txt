Nolan scolded me for not sticking to the original plan, but I was glad I didn’t. If that demon were to leave the cave, there was no telling how all of that would have ended, especially with Kazak defending it like he had. Admittedly, I was giddy inside that he was so worried about me. Sure, I was the fate of the world and stuff, but his concern seemed more personal than that. He’d never admit it, but the badass gunslinger cared for me.

“Are you even listening to me?” Nolan snapped his fingers in front of my face.

I blinked. “Yes. Sorry. No more deviating from plans. Got it.”

“This isn’t a joke, Freya. That thing could have killed you.”

“But it didn’t,” I sang with a cheesy smile on my face.

He sighed defeatedly. “You’re going to be the death of me, you know that?”

After the village set burial plans for their fallen elder, the shaman and his daughter reconvened with us.

“Today is a day of much reflection,” the shaman said heavily. “We must make plans for the future.”

“It’s a lot to take in,” I said. “I’m sorry about Elder Kazak.”

“Oh, yes, thank you. But, it’s much more than that. Our village worries that without our offerings, we will reach famine.”

“I’ve studied farming techniques from western Nemet,” Nolan replied. “With proper irrigation and crop rotation, your harvests could be more abundant than ever before.”

The shaman smiled and bowed. “Any assistance would be much appreciated. We will make preparations here. Meanwhile, Kauli will take you back to the village.”

“You can share some of that knowledge with me, Nolan,” she added delightfully.

“Ahh, but before you go, I must return you to your natural state, infant dragon!” the shaman replied.

Aww, good bye, opposable thumbs….

After the shaman worked his magic with his crystal, I was back to being a small scaly baby. The transformation never got any less bizarre—bones reshaping, skin stretching into scales, and that weird moment when my consciousness shifted perspective. One second I was five-foot-seven, the next I was barely over Nolan’s ankle.

‘There’s our little harbinger of destiny,’ Nolan said with a smirk.

‘Oh shut it.’ Being unable to talk was the worst part. All my clever comebacks were trapped inside my scaly little head.

Kauli giggled, reaching down to scoop me up. “I’ve come to know that you are wiser than your years, but you are absolutely cute this way.” She tucked me into a pouch at her waist, my head poking out so I could still see.

‘Cute?’ I mentally huffed. ‘I’m supposed to be terrifying. I’m a dragon, for crying out loud!’

It didn’t matter that I saved a village as an intelligent, well-mannered, clone of the young beastkin. Through her eyes, I was still as cute and cuddly as a plush toy. It was something I had to accept, and even though I missed my evo stones, I was getting used to all of the attention my stage one body was giving me.

The journey back to the village took half the time without having to navigate around the ritual sites. I bounced against Kauli’s hip, watching as she walked suspiciously close to Nolan as they talked, their shoulders occasionally brushing. I tucked my head lower as I playfully squinted my eyes, making fair observations between the two, but keeping my comments to myself.

Kauli led us to her hut, a pretty spacious place with warm glowing lanterns set on every flat surface and tribal beads dangling on walls for decoration.

“We are all shaken up about the truth behind The Dark One and our elder,” Kauli said disappointedly. “A lot of us looked up to him.”

“He was looking after his village, but his methods weren’t pure,” Nolan commented.

“Yes, which is why this rebirth of our village is truly embracing,” she replied, trying to crack a smile. “While I am saddened by his passing, I am excited for our future. I always felt this heaviness over our heads. Like there was a presence of malice lingering around us.”

“And now?”

“Now, liberation.” She nodded wholesomely. “I cannot thank you enough.”

“Don’t sweat it.”

“My father should be back soon with your payment.”

“Wait a minute, what’s this?” Nolan asked, referring to a gold ladybug looking piece of jewelry sitting on a workbench in a clear-top, velvet bottom case.

“Oh, that’s my father’s legendary Ulmah, a desire seeker,” Kauli said. “It was an item my great grandmother passed down.”

“What does it do?”

“It searches for your deepest desire and leads you to it. The artifact works like a compass. Its value is priceless.”

Nolan picked it up and inspected it, and then Kauli walked over to him with a bright smile on her face. “It is yours, if you wish.”

“Why haven’t you or anyone in this village used it already?” Nolan asked a valid question. The item had the ability to basically grant you a wish!

“He was saving it for me,” she admitted, laying her hand over Nolan’s as he held the item in the middle of his palm. “It had my imprint, which meant no one could use it besides me. Until now,” she said, before a faint gold glow emitted underneath her palm. “It can be activated every ten years. In a decade’s time, its power is restored, and someone else may use it. I could have used it a week ago, but… I... couldn't bring myself to do it.”

Nolan watched the golden glow intensify between their hands. “Why not? If it could lead you to your deepest desire….”

Kauli withdrew her hand, the light fading as she did.

“Because I already know what it would show me,” she whispered. “And sometimes knowing your heart's true desire only brings pain when you cannot have it.”

She turned away, busying herself with arranging tools on the workbench. “Some desires are not objects or places, Nolan. Sometimes they are choices we must make ourselves.”

Nolan felt the small golden ladybug grow warm in his palm. “Are you sure you want to give this to me?”

“Yes.” Kauli nodded firmly and then turned to Nolan, trying to fake a smile. “My father would approve. He always said treasures are meant to find worthy keepers.”

Nolan closed his fingers gently around the golden ladybug and bowed his head respectfully. “Thanks for this, really.”

“I am back!” the shaman cried out, walking into his home with a small herd of sheep. He had a proud smile on his face, while Nolan looked at him confused. “Your payment! I told you, I’d pay you handsomely.”

“Egh, sheep?”

“Not just any sheep! These are Mordayandor sheep! The best of all the eastern territories. Their wool is worth triple that of common stock, and they produce it all year-round.” The shaman patted the nearest animal's fluffy head with evident pride.

Nolan stared at the small herd now crowding the shaman's modest home. One of the sheep nudged his leg and bleated softly.

‘Mmmm, super fluffeh!’ I purred as I brushed my head against one’s cushiony leg.

“Father,” Kauli said with a sigh. “Nolan is a traveling man. He can’t go herding sheep across the continent. Perhaps a more portable payment would suit him better?"

The shaman’s smile faltered slightly. “Well, I….” He stroked his beard thoughtfully. “I suppose I didn’t consider the logistics.”

Nolan looked from the sheep to the golden ladybug in his palm and back to the shaman. “I appreciate the offer, truly, but we’re good. This payment is more than enough.” Nolan extended his hand and the shaman leaned in, almost in disbelief.

His jaw dropped when he saw what was in Nolan's palm. “The Ulmah?” he whispered, eyes widening. “Kauli, you gave him—”

“Yes, Father,” Kauli said firmly. “It was my choice to make.”

The shaman looked like he was about to protest, then closed his mouth and nodded slowly. “Indeed it was. And perhaps a wise one.” He studied Nolan and smiled. “You must pardon my initial reaction. For so long, I had intended for my daughter to use it. But… if it wasn’t for you and your dragon, my daughter wouldn’t be here today. I can’t think of a worthier person to receive it.” The shaman placed a hand on Nolan’s shoulder. “Do you know how to use it?”

“I think so,” Nolan said, examining the golden ladybug. “Kauli mentioned it works like a compass, leading to one's deepest desire.”

“Not quite so simple.” The shaman chuckled. “The Ulmah requires intention. When you’re ready to seek your heart’s true desire, hold it in your palm under moonlight and speak the phrase ‘Nekutare vos mayum.’ The wings will open, and it will guide you. Take its path, and its reward.”

Nolan nodded with a smile, and then carefully tucked the artifact into his inventory. “I’ll remember that. Again, thank you for this.”

The shaman chuckled and turned around to his small cluster of soft friends. “Now,” the shaman said, clapping his hands together, “about these sheep….”

‘Can we keep just one?’ I asked Nolan internally, rolling onto my back beside the fluffiest of the bunch. ‘I promise not to eat em!’

‘No,’ Nolan said firmly. ‘We're not taking a sheep.’

‘Awww, come on!’

“I’ll take them back to the farm,” Kauli volunteered, grabbing the rope that held the cluster of sheep together. “Nolan, would you mind helping me? They can be a bit stubborn sometimes.”

“Sure,” Nolan agreed, following her outside. I tailed Nolan, the two having a conversation among themselves. They talked about the village, the surrounding landscape, and where Nolan might be heading next. As they walked, the sheep bleated and trudged along the dirt path toward the farm at the edge of the settlement.

“So,” Kauli said after a comfortable silence, “where will you go now? With the Ulmah, I mean.”

“I’m still thinking about it.”

“I am curious, what could a man like you desire?” Kauli said, almost in a flirty tone? I giggled internally. Was she hitting on Nolan? Nolan glanced at her sideways, a small smile tugging at his lips. "Honestly? I'm not sure myself."

‘Oh, she’s definitely flirting with you,’ I chimed in mentally. ‘Tell her you desire her company on our next adventure!’

‘Quiet, you,’ Nolan shot back instantly.

‘I mean, I was the one who saved the village, technically speaking! Why are you scoring all the points?’

“The Ulmah reveals a deep desire that has been lingering in your heart,” Kauli continued, leading a particularly stubborn sheep around a puddle. “Not what you think you want, but what you actually need.”

“Is that right?”

“Yes,” Kauli replied, her voice softening. “That’s what makes it so precious. My father once used it thinking he desired wealth, only to be led to a humble orphanage where he found his long-lost sister. The Ulmah knows us better than we know ourselves.”

“I am curious, how many times did your father use it?”

“Not as many as you think. It was stolen a few times, and recovering a legendary item like that isn’t always easy.”

Nolan scoffed. “I could imagine.”

“We are having a festival tonight,” she disclosed sweetly. “And I’d love for you to come. You and Freya,” she said, before looking down to me sweetly.

I wagged my tail happy to be invited.

“Egh, I wish we could, but—”

‘Oh, don’t do that!’ I hissed. ‘That’s rude! Accept the invite!’

‘We still need to—’

‘Ep, ep. I don’t want to hear any excuses, sir,’ I retorted. ‘Kauli gave you an amazing item. It’s the least you can do.’

Nolan hesitated, reconsidering his answer. “Actually, you know what? We’d love to join the festival.”

Kauli's face lit up. “Wonderful! It’s to celebrate new beginnings for our people. After you and Freya saved us from the Dark One, we believe our village has been given a second chance.” She smiled warmly. “There will be plenty of food, music, dancing….” She trailed off, her cheeks flushing slightly.

‘Dancing!’ I chirped mentally. ‘Oh, I love to dance! Tell her I can teach her to do the robot!’

‘Seriously, no one knows what the hell you’re talking about.’

“Everyone will be there.”

“Sounds nice,” Nolan admitted as he helped her lead the last sheep into the pen and secured the gate.

She giggled. “It’s best if you use the next thirty minutes before the festival to wash up. The both of you. Follow me.”

Kauli led us to a nice hot spring nestled against the hillside. Steam rose from the crystalline waters, creating a misty veil around the natural stone basin.

“This is where our people have bathed for generations,” Kauli explained, gesturing to the springs. “Its waters heal small wounds and soothe tired muscles. I thought you both might appreciate it after your journey.”

‘Oh, sweet merciful gods, YES!’ I bounded toward the water, barely restraining myself from diving in headfirst.

Nolan looked over to Kauli and thanked her. The poor girl looked a little heart broken. Maybe because Nolan didn’t invite her? I blushed at the thought!

She left us fresh towels on a flat bed of stone, and a bottle of replenishing glitter in a bottle. Supposedly, the bottle was some type of magical Tide with the power of cleaning clothes without the washing machine. I dove in first, Nolan hesitant to jump in after, even when he was on the other side of the large boulder splitting two sections of the hot springs. So he waited until I was done before he went for a swim. Meanwhile, I had fun with the glitter bottle, accidentally pouring the entire contents on his clothes after Kauli mentioned only needing a few drops.

Hmm, I wonder why this stuff didn’t work on people….

After a quick refresher, Nolan and I took the directions to the festival. It was hard to lose our way to the marketplace plaza considering the area was lit with colorful lanterns. The sound of music drifting through the air led us there. The plaza had been transformed into a block party, with tables filled with food, musicians playing traditional instruments, and villagers dancing in circles around a central bonfire.

I couldn’t keep my perky dragon smile off of my face, or that hop out of my step. My first in-world party experience, in an actual beastkin village. How awesome!

While I was excited, I couldn’t tell if Nolan was, too. The expression on his face said that he was deep in thought, and I couldn’t help but notice how he kept casting glances my way. He was trying to be subtle about it, but I caught every look. ‘You’re staring,’ I commented.

‘Just wondering what you’re plotting,’ he replied with a half-smile. ‘You’ve got that look.’

‘What look? This is just my face!’ I protested, swishing my tail innocently.

‘Exactly. That’s the look that usually precedes chaos.’

I rolled my eyes. ‘So, you haven’t told me what you’re planning on using that ladybug on.’

‘It doesn’t work like that, remember?’

‘Yeah, yeah, but you should have some idea on what your heart needs.’

‘Hm. It’s a process….’

‘Like, I don’t know, Scarlet?’

He chuckled. ‘So you think you have me all figured out, huh, short fry?’

‘If it’s anything else, I’d lose all of my money.’

‘How about we just focus on having a good time, all right?’

‘Fine by me!’ I took the lead as we entered the plaza, immediately drawn to a table piled high with roasted meats. The aromas wafting through the air made my stomach rumble.

The villagers greeted us warmly, many bowing respectfully or offering grateful smiles. Children pointed excitedly at me, some brave enough to approach and ask if they could touch my scales. I obliged, of course, stretching out like a contented cat while tiny fingers gently stroked my back.

“The heroes arrive!” The shaman’s voice boomed across the plaza as he made his way through the crowd, now dressed in ceremonial robes adorned with colorful beads and feathers. “Come, come! You must have the place of honor!”

The shaman guided us to a raised platform near the bonfire, where cushions had been arranged around a low table filled with the most enticing dishes I’d ever seen. Platters of honey-glazed meats, exotic fruits, and steaming bowls of fragrant stew covered every inch of the surface.

“Our finest food for our finest guests,” the shaman proclaimed. “Eat! Drink! Tonight we celebrate life!”

I didn’t need to be told twice. I dove into the feast with enthusiasm, sampling everything within reach while Nolan accepted a goblet of sweet-smelling amber liquid from a smiling village elder.

The festivities around us swirled with wild energy. Choreographed dancers moved around the bonfire, musicians played oversized percussion and flutes with passion, and villagers laughed and sang as the night deepened. The celebration was intoxicating, a whirlwind of color and sound that swept everyone into its joyful chaos.

I noticed Kauli approaching through the crowd, wearing a flowing dress embroidered with golden threads that caught the firelight. Flowers had been woven into her hair, and she moved with a grace that drew admiring glances from everyone she passed.

‘Incoming,’ I mentally nudged Nolan, who was deep in conversation with an elderly villager about some local legend.

Nolan looked over, the look on Kauli’s eyes already inviting the gunslinger to dance.

‘You know, all of this encouragement from you, and the maiden is only a few years shy from being your age,’ Nolan pointed out, his comment almost making me hurl!

‘As if! Old timer!’

“Nolan,” Kauli sang, offering him her hand as she approached. “Would you honor me with a dance?”

‘Go on, old man,’ I teased. ‘Show her those creaky moves.’

Nolan shot me a withering glance before standing and taking Kauli’s outstretched hand. “I should warn you, I’m not much of a dancer.”

“That’s alright,” she replied, her eyes sparkling in the firelight. “Just follow my lead.”

I watched as Kauli guided Nolan into the circle of dancers around the bonfire. Despite his protests, he was pretty darn decent! After giving them a few moments for themselves, I broke into the dance floor and graced everyone with my own moves.

I was bobbing my little butt up and down and swinging my tiny head side to side until I noticed someone along the corner of my eyes. She looked familiar in that deep red cloak, but I couldn’t tell for sure. She almost looked like a speck given how far she was from the crowd, her movement shifty and sus with that bag over her shoulder. With her head low as she glanced over her shoulder, I could only imagine that she was up to no good.

She dipped out between two buildings before turning a corner to the next street. Something inside me told me to follow her. I promised myself I’d be quick, and by the time I reached her block, I realized who she was….
