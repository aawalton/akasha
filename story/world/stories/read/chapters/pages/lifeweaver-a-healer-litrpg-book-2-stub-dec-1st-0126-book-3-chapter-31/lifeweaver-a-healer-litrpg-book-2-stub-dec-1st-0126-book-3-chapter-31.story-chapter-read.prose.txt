In the end, Luke couldn't very well let Lemira go outside like that. She was liable to drop the shorts at any time. Instead, he used the trade function for only the second time since becoming Integrated, the first being when he sold a couple of corpses on that strange trading site, and gave her credits to buy clothes. Like with everything else an Integrated might need, the system provided most things you might want to wear at a more than reasonable cost. He wondered if this was some ruse to make the system drop everything other than fighting, but theorizing along those paths would give him no answers, so he dropped it.

In the end, Lemira chose loose-fitting, but not to the point of falling down, comfortable pants, and a long-sleeved T-shirt, both in black, matching ankle socks, and a pair of sneakers. Everything looked like it could've been sourced from any number of regular stores on Earth, which meant she wouldn't stand out too badly. Like before, her locks of hair covered most of her small horns, but she, on Luke's suggestion, also donned a thin black cloth cap. Once she'd laid out the options, Luke took the opportunity to enchant the different garments. It didn't help much with ranking up the profession, but every little bit helped.

"Great," Luke said, nodding in approval when she walked back in to show him her picks. "You'll pass for a human like that."

"That is well," Lemira said, looking around the small Shelter.

Luke pointed to the new room he'd added before falling asleep. "You'll sleep in there from now on. If you want to stay here with me, that is."

"Please," she said.

He got up and headed over to the door. "Let's head out."

She followed without a word, and he returned the Shelter to his inventory. Noticing Hannah's place of residence was gone, Luke figured she'd headed to Seattle already. The sense of time was a little weird coming and going from dungeons and, in his case, a different system, but at least he hadn't slept through the entire day, and they had plenty of time for a stroll over to ISG headquarters.

As they walked, he figured it was a good time to get to know Lemira a little better, seeing as she'd be a frequent face from then on. If she were going to show true colors as some evil mastermind, it would be a good idea to know beforehand.

"So, Lemira... What do you want?"

She turned from having watched the surrounding tall buildings as they walked to facing him, a look of consternation on her face. "What do you mean, Luke?"

"You're here. On Earth, I mean. In our system. And you're alive. So far so good, but now what?"

"Well, dungeons, of course," she said, stopping. "I am Integrated, like you. We share a common purpose, do we not?"

"Right."

"We must grow stronger to face what is coming."

He nodded. "The void."

Lemira frowned. "Well, yes, but there are more dangers not so far off on the horizon."

"Ezezal said something about that before I... He... Well, you know. So did Leminal. Something about a joining. Do you know what they meant?"

In the distance, a small group of people ran along the street at full tilt. Seeing as some of them carried weapons, Luke pegged them for Integrated, who were either running to or from some threat. The streets were quiet otherwise, with very few people daring to leave their apartments. Or perhaps most had already left.

Lemira watched them go as well, while mulling over his question. "There are many dangers, but the most common one is a joining. My system, or father's system to be precise, is old, much older than yours, judging by the state of this metropolis and your levels, so information has trickled in over the years and years of our preparation."

"I don't follow."

"We have heard of joinings, but have not experienced one ourselves. In our case, it was the monster spawns that upset our society."

"Monster spawns?" Luke asked, gesturing to the street. "Out here? We have that. At least I think we do. Some escape through portals, probably, but I doubt all of them have."

Lemira looked doubtful. "Perhaps, but it must have only just begun in that case. If you're lucky, you might not see a joining then. Not for a long while, at least."

"You're going to have to explain what a joining is at some point," Luke noted.

"Sorry," she said, her cheeks turning a soft red. "It is when two or more systems come together, either through permanent portals or the transportation of one system closer to the other."

"New neighbors? Sounds like a good thing. If we're going to fight together against the void, it makes sense we learn to coordinate and help each other first."

That prompted her to stop again and give him a long look, as if searching his expression for clues whether he was serious or not.

"What?" he asked.

"How have your previous encounters with other Integrated turned out?"

He was quiet for a moment before answering. "...Not great."

"Consider that on a system-wide scale."

"That's bad," Luke said, mulling over what that might look like. "Really bad."

"From what my father had been able to learn, the competition is fierce. Not all systems are deemed worthy of moving past that stage."

"What happens to those who don't?" Luke asked, waving for Lemira to continue walking. While they had plenty of time, standing around didn't feel right when talking about the possible annihilation of Earth. "Death?"

Lemira was about to answer when a thought struck Luke. "Wait. Dungeons?"

"That is a theory, yes," Lemira said, pursing her lips. "But many thinkers find the idea unlikely."

"Why?"

"The portals take us to places with still functioning societies on occasion, and far too little desolation for having survived a joining."

"I don't know what to think about this."

She shrugged her narrow shoulders. "They are theories, not facts. As I said, our system has not experienced a joining. Most of it is pieced together from information found in dungeons and from what little my father and the other first-circle demons learned from our System Being, as well as his studies into my mother's people and their history."

"You're making my head spin, dropping this stuff on me like this. System Being? You mean Guide?"

"Guide?"

"He's the one who speaks to us."

"There is one such being for every system."

"Right, of course."

Thinking about it, that made sense. Guide couldn't be everywhere all at once. That was impossible even for him.

"Your father thought the Lilineese 'lost' their joining?"

"He did," she confirmed.

"And? Did they?"

Lemira reached up with her hands in a gesture of helplessness. "I don't know. Their recorded history only went back so far, and the constant undead threat didn't help."

"Any idea why your system didn't get a joining? What is it called? Your system, I mean."

"Infernal Flux. As far as we know, it just hasn't happened yet, but it will at some point, when we are deemed worthy. Please remember that all my information comes from my father. I have never been to our system myself."

Before Luke could overwhelm her with more questions, she continued on her own with a look on her face that he read as troubled.

"Also, you previously referred to your system as Earth, but am I to understand that is the name of your system, or this particular celestial body?"

"Er," he began, not sure what she was getting at. "It's this planet."

She nodded. "I see. System and planet are not interchangeable."

"Huh?"

"I would be remiss to mention that bringing me here might also have been a poor choice."

"Why not?"

"An Integrated becoming a permanent resident of your system might perhaps trigger a joining event."

Luke stopped again. "Your father didn't mention that tidbit. You're sure?"

"Another theory," she said, shaking her head.

Just to be safe, Luke fired off a couple of messages to his contacts at The Department of Integrated Affairs, most of them going to Johanson, telling them to prepare for invasion. If Singers started dropping in through portals, Earth would be in trouble.

"You have a lot of theories."

"Not mine," she said. "My fathers. Infernal Flux is a new system, much like your own."

Getting them back into a walk, Luke wasn't sure how to piece all this new information together. "You said you've been around for a long time, the system, I mean, but you're still counting yourselves as 'young'? Your father's level was pretty high."

"It is not a matter of age or even levels. You should consider it in terms of stages. Young means not having taken part in a joining. While we have been Integrated for years and years, and have gathered much information-"

"And theories," Luke added.

She nodded in recognition. "And many theories, it has been a slow process. Your own level is not much lower than my father's, and your integration was not long ago, correct? A few years?"

"Months," Luke corrected.

Her eyes widened at that. "That makes your level all the more impressive."

"I've had some good fortune," Luke said, not wanting to get into it.

"Even so," Lemira continued. "Your companions' levels are quite high as well. While Infernal Flux has the advantage of years and experience, it seems to me you grow stronger at a quicker rate than us. Now, my father was trapped for many years and didn't gain much in terms of experience during that time, but he was considered one of the most powerful demons in our system before leaving."

"You've given me a lot to think about," Luke said as they approached ISG headquarters. "Never thought to check your level and class. Want to share?"

"Being aware of your allies' abilities is a vital part of forming a cohesive fighting unit," Lemira said, almost making it sound like a question.

"Yeah."

She raised a hand to her cap and scratched at her left horn through the fabric. It looked a lot like a nervous habit of sorts. When she caught on to what she was doing, she pulled her hand back so fast you might have thought she'd burned herself.

"We are allies, aren't we?" Luke asked.

"That is how I would prefer it," Lemira said, her shoulders dropping a little. Had the question of their friendship, or whatever you wanted to call it, been a point of stress to her? It seemed that way, and who could blame her? Alone in a new system, there was much she didn't know, and her safety wasn't guaranteed by anyone other than Luke himself.

"Same. I'm starting a guild with my sister. I'd like you to join it."

He'd already cleared the idea with Milla, of course, or she would've given him a beating he wouldn't forget in the years to come. His sister was gathering everyone and getting ready to head to New York. Their arrival was imminent. Once she got going, there was no stopping her.

The idea of Lemira joining made her radiate happiness, her anxious smile turning wide enough to show teeth, and her eyes glittered as they flashed red for a moment.

"I would like that very much," she said.

"It's settled then. You already know my class and level. I'm a healer of sorts. What about you?"

"Healer?" she asked as they pushed through the doors to the ISG building, past the large contingent of guards, and continued to the reception desk, where Luke was instructed to wait for someone to come get him. The large number of people around seemed to make Lemira a little uncomfortable, because she kept jumping to the side to make way, and her gaze shifted back and forth, trying to judge if they were threats.

"Healer, yeah," Luke said, picking up the conversation again in an attempt to calm the half-demon. "But I have some tools that let me join in fighting too. What about you?"

She didn't answer. Instead, she just kept looking this way and that, so Luke waved a hand in front of her face. "Hey. You're okay, Lemira. This is a guild, and we were invited to talk to one of them. No one will hurt you."

"It is a little strange being around so many people. The Lilineese were gone before my birth, so I've never been around so many who look like me." Again, she raised her hand as if to touch one of her horns. "Well, mostly like me."

"Must be overwhelming."

"It is."

He nodded. "Focus on me if you can. You still haven't told me your level and what you do."

Lemira forced a weak smile but stopped turning this way and that, at least. "I am Level 20. My class is called 'Core Caller'. It is a sort of companion class where I use cores to change and create monsters. It is from my mother's side. She was one and thought I might feel closer to her if I chose the same class."

"Did it work?"

She gave a weak shrug. "Perhaps to an extent." Looking down, she continued, her voice low, "But I am not Lilineese, nor am I demonic." Then she sighed. "I am nothing."

He gave her a look. "Well, you're here now. You'll be one of our guild members if you want. That's not nothing."

"Thank you," she said, peering up from under her locks as she looked like she was searching for more words, but before she got a chance to speak again, Peter interrupted them.

"Welcome," Peter said, holding out his hand.

Luke took it and shook. "Hey. I've got a friend with me. Hope that's okay." He nodded to her. "This is Lemira."

"Of course, of course. Welcome, Lemira. So glad you could make it. We have a lot to discuss. Not all of it good, I'm afraid."
