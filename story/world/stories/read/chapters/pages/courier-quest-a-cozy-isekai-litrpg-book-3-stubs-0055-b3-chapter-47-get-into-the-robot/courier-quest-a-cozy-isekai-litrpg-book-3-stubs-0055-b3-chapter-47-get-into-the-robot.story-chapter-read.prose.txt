Adabelle, unfortunately, did not have any of the dormant muscle memory that Trevor did that came from a lifetime of gaming. Her high stats meant that she processed what was going on with the fighters on stage easily enough, but that same benefit was given to her opponent thanks to the Equalizer and she fumbled more often than not.

That wasn’t to say she didn’t have a good time. It was another novel experience, and playing against everyone except for Jedfry and Hedry was fun. The two boys were, unfortunately, set on trying a little too hard. Trevor stepped in when they started being too smug, though Lilsian also had the same effect.

Stammy had said they hadn’t fought another Summoned before, and it showed. It was interesting watching the kids get better in real time as they adapted to the wide catalog of strategies the courier had brought with him. From mind games to trash talk, it was clear he had the advantage once he had gotten his bearings.

The platforms could disappear into the wall to change the stage’s configuration, which allowed things to stay fresh. They played for hours, getting out the spare controllers and fighters so they could do doubles and free for alls. Trevor did have a harder time when he teamed up with his wife, but it was also when he had the most fun.

Lunch was a delectable beef stew made by Dee, who bowed out early to check on it and prepare the rest of the meal. Stammy and the children left early to make sure everything would be ready for the next game.

Once they were done eating, they were taken to a locker room. Dee took Clay, citing that they wouldn’t be able to participate in this event, but gave the earth elemental enough belly rubs that they didn’t seem to mind at all. Especially when they were promised a great view.

The newlyweds changed into their very own flight suits. They were different sizes made to fit the general public, but were comfortable enough and smelled freshly cleaned. Once they were done, they stepped through the next doorway as instructed and into a short hallway.

“… Of fun teleporting around, and it’s always good to be back in Aletander,” they heard Lora say from up ahead.

“I’m glad you like my home so much. You’re always welcome here, you know, even if you just want to visit without work,” came a smooth male voice.

Trevor and Adabelle gave each other a look before entering the room. Four blue pods lined the opposite wall, and both inhabitants wore similar flight suits. Lora’s looked a little big for her, as the woman was very slender, but the other was clearly custom made.

The demon’s eyes lit up as he saw them, and a grin crossed his face. It was clear at a glance that this was Stammy’s eldest son. He had the same horns and smile.

“Welcome, friends!” he greeted loudly. He stepped away from Lora to shake their hands, towering over both of them. “I’m Boel. It’s really good to meet you, Trevor and Adabelle. Little pixie here has told me a lot about you two.”

“Hey!” the teleporter complained, crossing her arms as she pouted. “I’ve been stringing them along for days and you go and out me just like that?”

“Huh?” Boel asked, turning towards the woman. There was a look of confusion and concern on his face. “What did I do?”

“You called me little pixie!”

“Yeah, but I always call you that! What did I do?” he asked, a little bit of panic setting in.

Adabelle stifled a giggle at the look on Boel’s face. “We didn’t know she was a pixie before she was summoned here as a human,” she explained. “It was a big secret that we were trying to work out.”

“Honestly, we probably wouldn’t have even thought much of it if you hadn’t reacted that way, Lora,” Trevor added with a comforting smile. “Neither of us had fae on our list.”

“Ah, well. Dang. I’m sorry, Lora,” the demon said, scratching his arm.

“Well, cat’s out of the bag now, I guess,” she said, shrugging. “Doesn’t really matter. Just means that I’m going to be really mad at you.”

“How can it not really matter when you're still mad at me?” Boel protested weakly.

“Tell you what, if you score higher than me in this game, then I’ll consider forgiving you.”

A glint of challenge sparked in his eyes. “Okay, you’re on. I’ll make sure to carry this team to victory,” he announced, throwing a fist into the air.

“While I do love a good carry, what game are we playing?” Trevor asked.

“Robot Laser Tag,” Boel answered as he walked to the side of the room.

“Oh, I am all sorts of ready for this,” the courier said enthusiastically.

The pods opened with a press of a button, revealing a large robot in each one. None of them had a head, and they only had one arm with what appeared to be a barrel of a gun instead of a hand. They were blue with four panes of glass in the front to create a circular viewing section.

Their interior was a space big enough for a fully grown demon as well as an opening for horns at the top. It was clear to Trevor that someone was supposed to sit inside and operate it. The thought of it made him giddy.

“These are our robots. We’re the blue team, while the other side is red,” Boel explained, patting one affectionately. “They’ve got three hit points, one from each of the outside panes of glass. When they’ve been hit, they darken, making it slightly harder to see out of. The one in the center will have your reticle. If all three are hit, then the robot automatically returns to base where it regains health.”

He pressed a button on the side of the pod and the robot turned, revealing an open back. There were plenty of handholds that someone could use to climb into its torso, and it was tall enough that there were ladder rungs on the legs. The pilot seat also had a pane of glass on the back, which he pointed to.

“This is your weak point. If anyone from the opposing team strikes it, then you’re out,” Boel said.

“Even if you haven’t been hit in the front?” Adabelle asked.

“Right, you could go from full health to zero in one shot,” he confirmed. Then, he hesitated, looking back at the newlyweds. “Little pix—I mean, Lora mentioned you two are familiar with guns and how they work?”

“Yes. One of our friends back home has the Power of Guns, so we’re very familiar,” she answered with a smile.

“He has his very own range that he rigged up with targets that move back and forth, and he likes to drag us along to it every so often,” Trevor added, thinking back to the first time he had been taken there. Wayne had given him a talk about Adabelle, but it hadn’t gone at all how the Texan had expected.

“Okay, good. Then I don’t need to worry about explaining that. Sometimes we have to boil it down to point and click, which is ignoring a whole world of nuance,” Boel said, nodding. “If you already know that, then we can skip ahead a bit. Family rule, no spawn camping; there is a red line three quarters of the way across the arena, and we can’t pass it. They can’t pass the blue line. Don’t unbuckle from your seat when the robot is powered on, don’t ram into anyone, try not to hit any walls. You control it with two joysticks, one for moving, one for turning and shooting.

“We can talk to each other, but only when your robot is active. As in, you have control. If it’s automatically returning to base, then you can’t communicate. We have different settings such as capture the flag and base destruction, but we’re starting with a simple four versus four with a goal of twenty deactivations. The arena is set to random so my family doesn’t have such a big advantage. This little pixie has played a few times and has taken to it quite naturally, and I’m the best the Sammersons have to offer, so it won’t be a curb stomp even if you lag behind.”

“Don’t you worry about us. I’m excited to get into the robot,” Trevor replied.

“I must admit that I’m curious as well. This should be easier for me to grasp than Ultimate Fighter Rushdown,” Adabelle said.

“Oh yeah, how’d you guys do?” Boel asked as he began prepping the robots one by one.

“Trevor won enough times that he could be your new champion,” the noblewoman bragged, pulling him closer with her tail.

That got a whistle out of the demon. “That good, huh?”

“I had a lot of experience that translated well,” Trevor said humbly.

“Well, maybe we’ll get a chance to play before you go. I’ve been wanting a challenge,” Boel said before gesturing for one of them to come over. “Let's get you all inside.”

Lora was already starting to get into one of the robots and Trevor allowed his wife to go first. She climbed inside, guided by Boel, before strapping in. They took safety very seriously here. Once the two women were situated, they disengaged from the pod to start walking around with loud, crashing footsteps.

“So,” Trevor started as he and Boel walked to one of the remaining robots. “You and Lora, huh?”

The demon’s face immediately flushed as he turned back to see if she was out of earshot or not, and calmed when he realized she was. “I’d like that, but it probably won’t work out. I’m okay with just being friends, though,” he admitted before he cleared his throat and gestured towards the pod. “Alright, in you go.”

Nodding and letting the topic drop, Trevor climbed into the robot’s cockpit. It was surprisingly spacious. There was no hatch, leaving it open in the back, but he didn’t worry since this was a no-contact game. He settled into the chair and buckled in with the three different straps. Afterwards, he adjusted the steering rig so that it was in the perfect place.

Boel instructed him to press a green button, and the robot came to life once he did. It reacted easily to his touch, and the twin joystick design was very intuitive. Trevor pushed forward, leaving the pod he had been docked at.

The footsteps were somewhat jarring, but the seat seemed to come with shocks to help dampen that. Their top speed seemed to be a slow jog, which was fine by him. It seemed as though the only advantage the Summoned were going to get was in reaction time since the robots themselves could only move so fast.

They spent a couple of minutes getting used to the controls by walking around and aiming at spots on the wall. The reticle was in the center pane of glass as promised, but the gun arm didn’t move. If they had to point upwards, then the entire robot would have to shift with it. There was a height limit, too, so the machine wouldn’t fall backwards if it looked too high.

“Ready check, Blue One,” they heard Boel say over a speaker. “I’ll go through the roster, so speak aloud when I ask if you’re ready. Blue Two, Lora.”

“Ready,” she responded.

“Blue Three, Adabelle.”

“Ready.”

“Blue Four, Trevor.”

“Ready, Blue One.”

“That’s what I like to hear. We are good to go,” Boel said, an obvious grin to his voice. “I have heard back from Commander Mom, who’s in the control center, and she says we’ll have an easy, level arena with the same cover on both sides. Once these doors open, it will be time to teach those other Sammersons a lesson. Let them have it, Blue Team!”

The two doors leading to the arena opened almost right away. Boel and Lora were the first to leave, followed by Trevor and Adabelle. They entered the arena to see columns growing out of the floor along with walls and boxes. Some of them were taller than the robot, while others were just high enough that the reticle was an inch or two above it.

That was half-cover, Trevor decided. The two upper panes were still visible from the other side, but the lower one was completely obscured. He called it out to Adabelle since she wouldn’t know to look out for it right away.

The courier followed after Boel just as his wife shadowed Lora, and he couldn’t help the smile on his face. Every step his robot took felt powerful even with the chair’s shocks dampening every jolt. It wasn’t going to be something he would forget any time soon.

Two members of the red team emerged from around a corner, one after another, too far away for Trevor to see who was in them. Boel reacted quickly, the torso of his robot shifting in a tight circular motion. Two shots struck the panes of glass of the leading enemy, darkening them, before they returned fire.

Trevor’s robot was immediately struck once, and the upper left quadrant of his vision went black. He could still see through the glass, but it was as though he was looking through a haze. Biting the inside of his cheek, he took aim at the second foe and fired.

The arm gun shot one invisible laser at a time, but it fired as quickly as the pilot could pull the trigger. There was a display showing five shots to his right before he would have to reload. He went through them all quickly, striking two of the panels on the enemy’s mech.

“Lilsian down,” Boel announced over the radio.

“Adabelle returning,” Lora said.

Above him, a blue one and a red one popped up almost simultaneously as his demonic ally and a member of the other team managed to score. One of the two enemies immediately turned tail and fled, which left the other one.

Trevor’s view screen went dark as he reloaded, and he sighed as his robot returned to base. Boel announced his departure, which was just good communication. His sacrifice wasn’t in vain, though. The demon took out the other one, and the score changed to show both teams had two.

Adabelle was leaving the gate as he entered it, and she gave him a wave and a grin, which he returned. All of his controls came online after he entered the practice room. He turned around and marched right back out.

Instead of following Boel, he stuck to the outskirts of the arena. There was no way anyone would attack him from the right side, since that was the edge, and he half-walked, half-strafed forward. His vision was just enough to see ahead of him while giving him a chance to turn quickly if need be.

A member of the red team barreled around the corner. Trevor flicked towards them, firing deliberate shots this time. He could see Jedfry inside the cockpit, eyes narrowed in intense concentration, before he ducked to the side and reloaded even though he didn’t need to yet.

The kid had been hit once, and so had he. Trevor ran back into the hallway as his reloading animation finished and fired three times. His lower screen darkened so that he could only see through the upper right side of the cockpit, but he watched as Jedfry’s robot turned around and started heading back.

“Jedfry’s down,” he announced to the praise of the others.

Moving ahead cautiously, he turned slightly and was immediately struck down by Stammy. Snapping his fingers in disappointment, he sat back as the robot returned to base.

It was going by quick, and the blue team was up five to four. Despite being shot down twice, Trevor grinned. He was enjoying this game, too.
