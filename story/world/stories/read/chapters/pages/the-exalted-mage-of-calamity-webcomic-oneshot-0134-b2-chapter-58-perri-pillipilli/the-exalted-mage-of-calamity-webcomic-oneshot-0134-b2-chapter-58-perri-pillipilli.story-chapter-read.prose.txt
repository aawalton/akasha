“Are you sure we’re going the right way?” Gwen asked.

They had been walking for nearly fifteen minutes, and yet the forest didn’t seem to end. Based on how Faewood had looked from above, they should have already reached Merrywind Grove.

The small fairy flew several paces in front of them, occasionally weaving between low branches. “Yes. We’re almost there.”

“It doesn’t feel like we’re almost there,” Gwen said, her voice flat. “Are you sure you aren’t tricking us?”

Perri glanced back at her. “I already told you. You won’t reach the grove without me.”

“That wasn’t her question,” Nasha said from the back. “You could be telling the truth and still leading us into a trap.”

The fairy went silent for a moment. “…I’m not tricking you.”

Veronica glanced around the forest.

Even after what Perri had explained earlier, it was difficult to understand exactly what Faewood was doing. They had continued moving in roughly the same direction, and there were no obvious illusions covering their surroundings. The trees weren’t shifting or moving either.

Yet somehow, Merrywind Grove never seemed to get any closer. Even Sage wasn’t able to analyze their surroundings properly.

“Are we being moved somehow?” Gwen asked. “Like we’re walking forward but actually getting sent somewhere else?”

“No,” Nasha said.

Gwen turned to look at her. “What do you mean, no?”

In Nasha’s hands were small stones painted red. She held one up. “I’ve been placing these as markers for us, in case we get lost. I’ve already placed four of them, and we haven’t seen another appear in front of us. So no, we aren’t in some kind of loop. Unless it’s a very sophisticated one.”

“Wow, that’s really clever,” Theo added, looking at her with respect. “I didn’t even think of something like that.”

“You don’t think at all, boy,” Orban added.

Theo chuckled nervously, rubbing the back of his head.

“Our master-student relationship is a lot different than theirs, huh?” Gwen whispered to Veronica.

“I already mentioned it before,” the fairy said, cutting in. “Faewood normally traps visitors inside so they can’t escape. Not unless you have a fairy, like me, to guide you. So you stinky humans should be grateful.”

Gwen groaned. “I’m starting to understand why Morgan hated dealing with fairies in her book.”

“Morgan didn’t hate us,” Perri said.

“You knew Morgan?” Veronica asked.

Perri shook her head. “No.”

“Then how the hell do you know she doesn’t hate you bugs?” Orban asked.

Perri stared at him for a few moments, then ignored him and flew several feet farther ahead.

There were several more fairies throughout the forest, all darting between the trees and watching them. None approached, however. Whenever one came too close, Perri would say something in a strange language Veronica couldn’t understand, causing them to quickly disappear again.

Whether she had any real authority over the others was still up for debate.

For the time being, Veronica was simply glad they were keeping their distance.

After fifteen more minutes of walking, Veronica finally noticed a break in the trees ahead.

Sunlight.

“Hey! I think I see it!” Gwen shouted.

It turned out that Perri hadn’t been lying.

They stepped beyond the edge of Faewood and entered Merrywind Grove.

The change in atmosphere was almost immediate.

Instead of a dense forest filled with tall trees and spying fairies, they entered an open grove covered in pale white.

Drooping trees stretched across the landscape, their bark almost completely white. Their branches were covered in similarly pale leaves, along with dozens of colorful fruits.

Golden yellow ones were the most common, but Veronica could also see red, black, white, and even several that appeared almost transparent.

The ground itself wasn’t covered in regular grass either. A layer of pale moss spread beneath the trees, carrying a faint white sheen across its surface.

Then Veronica noticed the falling leaves.

One drifted down from a nearby branch. A current of wind caught it, carrying it several feet through the air before it began to descend toward the ground.

Just before touching the moss, it disappeared. More accurately, the leaf broke apart into tiny motes of light, scattering across the ground like dying embers.

Veronica looked back toward the branch. Another leaf had already begun growing in its place.

Perri flew ahead of them and turned around. “Welcome to Merrywind Grove.” There was a hint of pride in her voice.

Theo approached one of the nearest trees. “What kind of trees are these?”

“Pillipilli trees.”

“And the fruit?”

Perri looked at him. “Guess.”

Theo paused. “…Pillipilli fruit?”

“Yes.”

Gwen walked up beside him. “Can we eat them?”

“Some of them.”

That immediately caused Theo to lower the hand he had been reaching toward one with.

Veronica turned toward Perri. “Do you know what effects they have?”

“Of course.”

The fairy flew toward one of the lower branches and landed beside a black fruit. She plucked it from its stem, offering it to Gwen.

“The black ones make your mana more active.”

Gwen looked at the fairy suspiciously. “You’re lying, aren’t you?”

Before Perri could respond, Veronica cut in.

“Don’t eat that,” she said. Beside Veronica, Sage had already compiled the information from Morgan’s records and displayed it across her status screen.

Perri slowly lowered the fruit.

Veronica looked at her. “The black ones are poisonous. They destabilize your mana, making it turn wild and go out of control.”

Gwen stared at the fairy. “You were seriously suggesting I eat that?”

Perri frowned. “I told you what it does.”

“She did, technically,” Theo chimed in.

“No more misleading suggestions,” Veronica said, pointing a finger at her. “Or else we’re having fried fairy for lunch.”

Perri crossed her arms and looked to the side. “Fine.”

Theo reached up and plucked one of the golden yellow fruits. “What about these?”

“The yellow ones are safe,” Perri said.

Veronica checked Morgan’s notes anyway. “According to Morgan, they strengthen your body and make you more resistant to diseases and sickness.”

“Like… forever?” Gwen asked.

Veronica shrugged. “Don’t know. It didn’t say.”

“Wouldn’t these be extremely expensive, then?” Theo asked, looking at the fruit in his hand. “That sounds like a treasure.”

“Possibly. I’m not sure how strong the effect is or how long it lasts.”

Orban tapped his foot against the ground, testing the soil beneath the moss. He raised one hand, and a thin pillar of stone slowly rose beneath him until he reached one of the higher branches. He plucked a golden fruit before allowing the stone to sink back into the ground.

Veronica used a simple wind spell, precisely cutting the stem of a much farther yellow fruit with her augmented vision. She caught it, barely.

She took a bite of the round, yellow fruit. It was juicy on the inside, with a mild fruity flavor. A mix between an apple and a pear, though there was a bitter aftertaste.

[Analyzing nutrients…]

While Sage examined her body and how exactly the fruit affected her, she finished the rest of it. Everyone else also ate one of their own.

“What about the others?” Theo asked. “Are the rest of them poisonous, too?”

Veronica looked at the rest of the information Sage had logged. “The white ones cause hallucinations. The red ones increase vitality.”

“Vitality… as in lifespan?” Nasha asked carefully. “Or do you mean aura capabilities?”

Once again, Veronica shrugged. “Didn’t say. Morgan barely described these in the books.”

“And the hollow-looking ones?” Theo asked.

“Those… absorb the life force of whoever eats them so they can grow and mature into one of the other colored fruits.”

Theo stepped away from the hollow fruit he had been looking at.

Perri casually tossed the black fruit she had been holding into the air and caught it. “Merrywind Grove has to eat somehow, you know.” Then she proceeded to take a bite of the black fruit.

“Ain’t that poison? Why are you eatin’ that?” Orban asked.

Perri chewed and spoke at the same time. “Only for people who use mana. We fairies use spiritual energy, which can neutralize the poison. Otherwise, they taste very sweet.”

“Does that mean Nasha can eat those? She uses aura, not mana,” Gwen said.

“I wouldn’t risk it,” Veronica said. “While she does use aura, everyone has dormant mana inside their bodies.”

Maybe she could eat it using Selene’s Tear, but why risk it on a fruit that gave no benefit?

“There’s plenty of these fruits around, so should we collect as many as we can?” Theo asked, plucking another yellow fruit.

Veronica nodded. “Doesn’t hurt. Let’s take a few with us as well, but don’t empty the grove out. I’m not sure if we’ll anger some guardian or accidentally kill the grove if we do.”

Everyone nodded and began collecting the red and yellow fruits.

The red fruit tasted much more tangy and sour. It wasn’t exactly pleasant, but it wasn’t as if they had come here for good food.

After spending a few minutes collecting fruit from the trees, they ended up with about fifteen extra yellow pillipilli fruits and ten red pillipilli fruits. They even grabbed five of the black, white, and hollow fruits, just in case. They weren’t planning on eating them, but perhaps they could be worth something on the market.

“What do we do with the bug?” Orban asked. Perri was hovering above them, feasting on some of the farther black pillipilli fruits. “We already made it out of the forest. Maybe we ditch her?”

“I second that,” Gwen added, clearly still upset that she had been offered a poisonous fruit.

“I’ll follow whatever Master agrees with,” Theo said.

Nasha simply shrugged with open palms.

“I’m actually against that idea,” Veronica said. “At least, for now. I say we bring her with us temporarily. She probably knows more about this island than any of us. I’ll verify whatever she says with Morgan’s books. It’ll be useful to have a guide, as untrustworthy as she is.”

Orban scratched his beard with his other hand. He contemplated it for a few hard seconds before finally agreeing. “Fine. It’s not a bad idea. We can just fry her if she keeps lyin’ to us.”

After a few moments, Perri flew back down, and they negotiated with her.

“I’ll be your guide, but only if you can do me a favor once we get inside the castle,” she said.

“The castle?” Veronica repeated. “Why there?”

“None of your business,” Perri said, crossing her arms haughtily. “That’s my only condition.”

Everyone looked at each other.

“Fine,” Veronica said. “But you have to be truthful with us and not deceive us. No wordplay or anything like that, either.”

“Deal.”

“Then for starters, how about you explain this place to us? As you probably noticed, we aren’t native here.”

Perri lifted a single finger. “You’re on an island called Avalon. This place is a garden, a grave, and most importantly, a prison.”

Everyone’s expression tightened upon hearing prison.

“What do you mean, ‘prison’?” Gwen asked.

“It is a prison for the one in gold.”

Veronica’s eyes narrowed. Morgan had mentioned the golden warriors in the book. “Are you talking about the Asvarians?”

Perri tilted her head. “Is that what you call them?”

“So they really did come here,” Veronica murmured. Then she realized something. “Wait. You said for the ‘one’ in gold. Morgan mentioned in her book that several Asvarians came.”

Perri shook her head. “In the past, there were many. A group of them came. But now there is only one.”

“So the rest of them died? How long ago was this?” Veronica asked.

“They didn’t just die,” Perri said slowly. “They were all killed.”
