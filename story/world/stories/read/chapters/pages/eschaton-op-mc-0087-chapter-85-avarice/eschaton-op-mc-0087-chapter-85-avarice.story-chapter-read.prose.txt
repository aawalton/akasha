The squad of Palisade Security marked helicopters race across the desert, and Thompson idly taps at his phone, bored. The rest of his team, made up of four 2-Stars, excitedly chatter amongst themselves.

“Oh, we’re gonna make so much goddamn money!” Brenda laughs.

“General Cooper was very agreeable. I’m glad we were able to find someone who knows how it is.” Tim says, joining in. Cooper was willing to hand over the salvage rights to a Fracture deep in the Mojave Desert—for a price, of course. Machina himself is there looking for the Sun Crystal. If he finds it, Thompson and his team is going to make so much money selling it right back.

“Billions! Each!” Greg laughs. “You can’t put a price on saving the world!”

Of course, even if Machina doesn’t find the crystal the entire world is on the hunt for, it’s still a Cat-3 Fracture. The Fractal Shards alone are worth hundreds of millions of dollars. Even after giving Palisade and Cooper their respective cuts, that’s still enough to set up a man for life if he wants to retire.

The trio of helicopters scream over the desert, racing towards where Machina’s platoon of worthless hangers-on are waiting for their betters to complete the task they could never do themselves.

“Camp is in sight,” the pilots say. Thompson never bothered to learn their names, being the Typicals they are.

“Land a hundred meters away,” Thompson orders. The transport helicopter begins to land, and the two cargo helicopters follow suit.

His squad has shark-like expressions on their faces.

“Let’s make some goddamn money.” Thompson says with an equally cruel smile.

All five of them saunter into the camp, ignoring the Typical soldiers and their toys. The four Bradley IFVs might knock him around a little, but he’d be able to outrace their gun’s traversal no problem. If he and his boys wanted them dead, they’d be dead. Everyone knows it too, and they’re waved in without complaint.

The commanding officer in charge is a Captain, a tall man, though still a head shorter than Thompson.

“I’m Captain Rains,” he says, “How can I help you, Captain…?”

Thompson ignores his question, and shakes Rains’ hand. He squeezes just shy of bone crushing.

That’ll leave a mark, Thompson thinks.

“We’re here to take possession of whatever salvage Machina may pull out,” Thompson says.

“The National Guard has already claimed this Fracture,” Captain Rains says apologetically, trying to sneakily shake out his aching hand.

Thompson hands over the order granting him and his team the rights with a smug expression on his face. All four of his squad slowly spread out around the camp, making sure none of the Typicals try anything stupid. Captain Rains takes a long time to read it. Unsurprising given his nature as a Typical. His brain simply moves so much slower than Thompson’s. Yet another reason important tasks should be left to the Empowered.

Rains passes it to a pretty blonde Lieutenant who reads the order with disdain. She wrinkles her nose at it before smoothing her expression into a mask of professionalism.

“Do you know who you’re messing with?” Captain Rains asks mildly.

Thompson snorts. “Look, buddy, Machina might be strong but he’s also a goody-two shoes. We’ve got the legal rights. He’ll be a good little boy. Just don’t get in the way,” he says menacingly.

Thompson flexes his Aura. It’s been a useful tool to get people to get out of his way. Violence free, which is nice. Helps keep the bribery costs down. When none of the officers in front of him react, he internally frowns and presses harder. They still don’t react, despite the assuredly overwhelming feeling of something greater than themselves looming overhead.

“Your funeral,” Captain Rains says dismissively.

“Sit down and shut up,” Thompson growls. “I’m in charge and I don’t want you getting in the way. Everyone will go home just fine if you just do what I tell you to do.”

With the camp and its inhabitants put in their proper place, Thompson and his team settle in to wait. Brenda pulls out her phone to loudly watch some trashy TV show. Greg jokes swaps jokes about Typicals with Tim. Dan yawns, leaning against a cargo truck.

The National Guard look nervous and uncomfortable, as they should. They aren’t in charge anymore. People like Thompson and his boys are the future, and they aren’t about to let some bleeding-hearts get in their way.

The pretty blonde Lieutenant wanders around the camp, checking in on the squads posted up around the Fracture.

“What are you doing?” Thompson growls.

“My job,” she says coldly. “I am in charge of the management of the camp’s day-to-day activities and logistics.”

Thompson snorts.

“Whatever,” he says. He ogles her a little before returning to his phone.

Hours pass as Thompson idly taps away his own phone, scrolling through mansions, supercars, and private jets. He daydreams about all the things that a few million dollars could buy—maybe even billions if Machina finally gets lucky.

He licks his lips in anticipation. Thompson was always a gambler. A bet like this is too good to pass up. He can’t lose. No matter what Machina pulls out will be worth enough money to get him anything he could ever want.

He’s lost in a haze of fantasizing about luxury when the Fracture pulses and three armored vehicles exit the Fracture. Machina follows close behind, splattered with mud made from sand, blood, and guts. It slowly drips off him with a disgusting squlech. The APCs open up and Machina’s lackeys start to file out. The Fracture closes with a hum and vanishes from the world.

“What’s going on here?” Machina asks warily. He pierces Thompson with a blue, incendiary gaze. He feels a chill go down his spine as the sheer weight behind that look just looms over him.

Thompson brushes off the ominous feeling and stands up, sauntering over to the big robot. His squad slowly surrounds him, and Machina’s sorry excuse for soldiers scramble back into their APCs.

Like that’ll save them, he thinks to himself. One day they’ll get the message that they can’t hold a candle to us, no matter what they do.

“We’re here to take possession of what you’ve salvaged from the Fracture,” he says smugly. He snaps and points at the pretty blonde Lieutenant—Ah, McKinely, by her nametag—and she dutifully hands Machina the order signed by General Cooper. The sheet of paper looks like a post-it note in his massive gauntlet.

Machina tilts his head up after reading it, staring at the setting sun casting burnt oranges and golds across the sky.

“So this is how it’s going to be?” He asks the heavens. Thompson scoffs.

“Look, pal, no need to get all melodramatic over it. Just hand over what you’ve got and we’ll get out of your hair.”

With his sword still on his shoulder, Machina looks down at Thompson. The weight of the gaze nearly crushes him.

“Captain Rains,” he says, not looking away. “Did they do anything particularly heinous?”

“No,” Rains says. “Nothing more than we usually get from people like him. Just general dickishness.”

“Go home,” Machina says to Thompson, and he nearly falls to his knees, almost crushed by Machina’s titanic Aura. Thompson thought his was strong. As a 3-Star, and a powerful one at that, there’s almost no singular person who could stand up to him.

Machina plants his sword in the ground. The sword-shaped hole in space is taller than even Thompson himself despite being driven four feet deep into dry, cracked soil.

“Look,” Thompson says, licking his lips again, this time nervously. “We’re just here for what we’re owed. We have the salvage rights.” The daydreams of luxury, hedonism, and his pride are the only things propping him up. He will not back down, not with all of these Typicals around to watch.

“I don’t particularly care what you think you’re owed,” Machina growls. He sounds like an avalanche roaring down a mountainside, and his Aura is no different. Thompson’s heart races and sweat pours down his brow. Every instinct is telling him to run.

Fuck it. All the way or not at all, he thinks to himself.

His team is slowly backing away from the angry demi-god. What else could he be, with power like that?

“Halt”, Machina commands, and the world freezes. Thompson can barely breathe much less move, and it’s clear that his team is no different. Out of the corner of his eye, he can see Brenda twitch, trying to break free of the command.

Machina looks down at Thompson again.

“W-we’ll go,” Thompson barely manages to force out through a half-paralyzed face.

Machina leans in, close enough to feel something from the fire in his helmet. The blue flame is ominous, and Thompson can see small sparks of golden glass mixed in. It’s not cold or heat or wind or lightning or rain. It’s everything and nothing all once. It burns with the potential to be anything, everything. Creation and destruction balanced on a point, just a tap away from one side or the other.

Right now it speaks of destruction. It whispers of breaking Thompson down into his constituent components. Of grinding him into nothingness.

“That’s it?” Machina growls. “As soon as someone bigger than you shows up to tell you no you fold like a house of cards?”

He grabs Thompson by his metal breastplate, the enchanted metal giving way with a screech. It was strong enough to bounce 30mm AP rounds. It cost him literally millions of dollars, and it bent like cardboard in Machina’s grip.

“Oh, I know men like you,” he spits. “Cowards who take and take and take. Who view this world as nothing more than a race to eat everything they can. People like you are a fucking cancer.”

Machina draws Thompson up to eye level, and Thompson scrabbles at the metal gauntlet to no avail. He’d have better luck trying to deadlift Mount Everest.

“I wonder, have you learned your lesson? Most likely not, I think.” Machina wonders out loud. Thompson shakes his head.

“N-no! I’ve learned plenty, just put me down. We’ll go and you’ll never see us again!”

Whatever dreams of wealth Thompson had earlier have now burned away under the harsh light of reality.

“No, I need to make sure this lesson sticks. It’s a good thing you’re a 3-Star, you’re just so damned durable.” Machina says. Thompson’s stomach turns and he goes pale in fear.

Machina lets go, and Thompson flails to land on his feet. Before he can, Machina balls a fist.

“Don’t bother people again,” Machina says before slugging him in the gut.

The metal gauntlet drives all the air out of Thompson’s lungs with a gasping, choking wheeze. He feels a rib give way as his breastplate screams again. He fires out of the camp like a cannonball, trailing blood and saliva. He slams into one of the helicopters, a tad over two hundred meters away.

He lies in the twisted wreckage of one of the cargo helicopters. The rear rotor is bent to the side, having collapsed when he slammed into the rear hull of the Chinook. The pilots scramble out, looking at him with wide eyes.

The smell of leaking aviation fuel, hydraulic fluid, and who knows what else fills his nose. He tries to pull himself out of the now good-for-scrap helicopter but slumps back down into the massive dent his body left in the hull. His head spins and he can barely breathe. He has several broken ribs and he’s pretty sure one of his shoulders is dislocated. Between the concussion, caustic fumes, and everything hurting it’s hard to tell what exactly is broken.

A minute later Brenda and Greg pull him out and drag him into their transport helicopter. The pilots of the now destroyed helicopter join their compatriots in the other Chinook.

“Shit, shit, shit!” Tim yells as they lay Thompson down on the helicopter’s floor. Thompson’s only reply is a choking, gurgling groan. He probably punctured a lung. For a Typical, he’d be critically injured. As a 3-Star, it’s not close to mortal, but deeply unpleasant. Like he was on the wrong side of an MMA fight. He might be down, but certainly not dead or dying.

Tim puts his hands on Thompson and a golden circle with two concentric rings forms above his chest. The air hums with magic and Potentia. A pulse of light bursts from the circle as it reaches a crescendo and vanishes. Thompson can feel the sickening feeling of bones resetting themselves and flesh weaving itself back together under the guidance of one of the most powerful healers on the west coast. A few pieces of metal scrape his bones as they’re pulled out by the powerful healing magic.

Thompson staggers to his feet and leans on the door with a groan. He jerks away from the window as he sees Machina’s burning gaze following him.

That was him being gentle, Thompson realizes with a full-body shudder. He could have snapped me like a twig.

The rest of the helicopter ride back to Los Angeles is silent.
