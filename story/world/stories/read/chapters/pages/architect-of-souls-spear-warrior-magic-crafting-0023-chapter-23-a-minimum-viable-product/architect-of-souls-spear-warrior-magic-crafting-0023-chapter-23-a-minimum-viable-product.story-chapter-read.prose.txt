Entering the gate, Ferris found himself in a space much like his soulspace and the slimespace, but also completely different.

He was in his mental body, with the familiar feeling of existing as a construct. Yet… the space surrounding him was not Ferris’ soul. This place was missing the inherent connection Ferris felt in both the soulspace and slimespace. Ferris had never consciously known it was there until now he felt it gone.

Ferris had the intrinsic feeling that he could only leave—at least with his mental body intact—back through the gate. It was similar to how he felt directly around his Heartfire, like there was a hole he could move through if he just shifted his weight. He could forcefully leave from anywhere, but it would be the same as the death of his mental body. That would cause the same backlash Jade currently had, and then require time to form a new body. Thankfully, that would be the worst of the consequences.

This place is a lot nicer than my soulspace, Ferris thought with a bit of embarrassment. Directly in front of him were a series of ascending platforms. Each platform had an intricately carved statue and a book.

Looking at each platform, Ferris could see that it would take a single step to climb up from each one to the next. First Steps of the Soul, indeed.

Despite the pun, the overall aesthetic was striking. Each statue was carved with bold details and dramatically lit. Strangely, Ferris’ first thought was regret that Jade wasn’t here to explore a new, interesting place. It had only been a few days, but the slime was definitely growing on him.

At least his familiar was with him in mind, if not mental body. As he looked around, a constant stream of everything he saw was being transferred to Jade over the bond.

Ferris stepped up to the first platform. It had a statue of a faceless person sitting in front of a fire. There was a book on a pedestal, which Ferris started reading.

It was a basic introduction to the Heartfire and soulspace. There wasn’t anything new or surprising to Ferris, but the information was presented clearly and directly.

Stepping up to the next platform, its statue depicted the same faceless person sitting in a common meditation pose. The book described several breathing techniques, and then provided instructions on how to find the Air Passage in the soulspace. It outlined several variations in how the qi gathering ability could present itself, and recommendations on solving common problems. Reading about problems Ferris could’ve had was interesting, but not directly helpful to his current issues.

Worried about spending too much time, Ferris continued up through the platforms, quickly reading through the books along the way. They covered creating a Qi Pool, qi forming simple equipment, how to create a lightstone, and several other quality of life improvements that could be made around the Heartfire. Then, the focus shifted to how to bond a core, and what steps to take afterwards.

The only place it really deviated from Uncle Winton’s advice was what to do with the core in the soulspace after bonding. His uncle had told him to make a pedestal, but the advice here was to make a heavy lockbox to protect it. Considering Ferris had just had something stolen from him, he saw the appeal. It would, however, make it harder to move between soulspaces if he had to unlock the box each time.

The books expected the reader to have a traditional weapon or item—not a familiar. The primary instruction after bonding was to begin nurturing a Core Tree to grow.

After that, there was a platform explaining more about abilities in the soul, which Ferris thought was the one Elder Harper had referred to. It discussed natural abilities, but only to contrast them against abilities grown from a Core Tree seed.

Apparently, the subtle vibration he often felt was a telltale sign of a nearby ability. That would be useful when Ferris was searching the slimespace for Adaptation and Acid Reconstruction. It didn’t explain why he felt it, or what it was, but at least he knew to look for it.

Whatever the vibration was, it was important. The book cautioned against confusing ‘techniques’ for ‘abilities.’ A technique was simply something done in the soulspace, like qi forming. Sometimes it could look like an ability, but wouldn’t have that vibration.

The book cautioned against relying too much on the ‘name’ of an ability. When a cultivator ate the Core Tree’s fruit, they would learn information on its seed’s potential ability. That information usually contained a name, which made things surprisingly standard across different groups of people.

However, two abilities with the same name could be completely different, both in construction and function. Ferris chuckled a bit at this, thinking about Jade’s Jump ability. He bet it was different than a Jump ability meant for a human. Then, once a cultivator started developing an ability, it often worked completely differently. Many people decided to arbitrarily change their ability’s name on a whim.

Then there were abilities not granted by the Core Tree. The Air Passage, Heartfire, and Qi Pool were all considered ‘natural abilities.’ Many were common enough to have widespread names, but when someone developed their own natural ability, they usually just named it whatever they wanted. This was how Ferris had named Jade’s Consume Pool and Digest River. Janice and Gumly likely had completely different names for them.

The book didn’t give any details for how people developed new natural abilities, but he knew a little. All living creatures were born with a Heartfire, and Ferris had created his Air Passage through years of meditative breathing. His Qi Pool, on the other hand, was created when Ferris first gathered qi into one location with purpose.

Mostly, the book recommended studying how an ability worked, and focusing on the concepts it presented in the soulspace. This was probably the part Elder Harper wanted Ferris to know.

It was interesting, but he didn’t allow himself to get bogged down thinking about the research project. He read the information, looking for anything to help with his current crisis, then moved on.

At the last platform, there was general advice on how to progress ‘to the second steps.’ Not surprisingly, it recommended purchasing Spiritual Harmony and Mental Materials from the library. Other than that, it told him to work with a mentor to find a cultivation path to the second tier.

There were a couple of higher platforms far above Ferris' head, but he guessed those were more a metaphor for future aspirations. He would need to be able to fly to reach them, although he wouldn’t put it past the author to hide some secret on top of them. It would be fun to come back and check once he had the ability to reach them.

Overall, Ferris could appreciate the value in having all this information written out, but he much preferred the personal nature of the instruction he’d received from his uncle. It appeared that Ferris would need to get used to this. He would be learning a lot from jade slips in the future, rather than one-on-one lessons. The thought made him a bit sad.

There were a few interesting techniques that Ferris hadn’t known, as well as alternatives if the first method didn’t work. Unfortunately, there wasn’t anything here that could help his current crisis, so Ferris quickly returned to the gate and woke up.

Jade was still patrolling the tiny area around the table they could reach. Ferris repeated the safety instructions and swapped out the jade slip to Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting.

After falling back into meditation, Ferris was once again able to enter the gate device. Instead of the large open space from before, this time he found himself inside a large room. It was constructed much like the interior of Hollowfield’s library, with clear aesthetic inspirations. He had entered through a stone archway built into the wall behind him. All around him were pedestals, each containing a book.

Looking at the closest pedestal, the book had the title Basic Crossbow Construct Crafting, an Introduction. Opening the book, Ferris began skimming through.

Unlike before, this time the author’s name was listed. Perhaps First Steps of the Soul was a collaboration or deemed not worth crediting the author? The introduction was written by one Elder Webb of Clan Hollowfield. He had compiled the jade slip of basic tier one crossbow designs as part of a research project for the clan, with the intention of providing a variety of different techniques and options to build while learning, growing in complexity along with the crafter’s skill.

Ferris began browsing the titles on the pedestals. One was a description of the proper form when shooting, but the rest contained instructions on how to build crossbows, increasing in complexity.

Near the back, Ferris found a hallway full of doors. Each one had a plaque above it, specifying a specific component. Exploring the rooms beyond, he was unsurprised to find they each contained many books all focused on that room’s component. For example, one had dozens of different trigger mechanisms, progressing from the simplest lever to an intricate magical contraption.

Glancing at one of the designs, Gilbert’s warning about foundational knowledge came to fruition. It required several tools and terms Ferris had never seen, like ‘compressed qi,’ ‘aspected qi,’ and ‘intent forming,’ but then gave no explanation for how to create or work with them. Thankfully, Elder Webb had provided a book in the first room cross-referencing to other technical manuals Ferris would need for each technique. Mental Materials was the first one listed, but many more technical manuals were included.

Eventually, the designs started to use magical materials. Ferris still had three merit points left, the minimum cost of a tier one crafting resource. He’d hoped that there would be something he could use to make a magical weapon, but soon gave up on the idea. The most basic plans using magical resources required skill and knowledge he lacked.

Perhaps it would have been better to purchase a mundane crossbow and then seek out a way to pay for it to be transferred to his soulspace. Ferris felt his understanding of how complex it would be to create a magical qi crossbow had been lacking… but at this point he would simply have to live with his choices. He’d made the best decision he could at the time, and most likely the other path had pitfalls he wouldn’t know about until he tried.

Thankfully, the order of the pedestals was designed to give a progression of complexity growing a crafters skill. Ferris had been able to craft a spear with just qi forming, and the first design started only slightly more advanced.

While Ferris had only created qi constructs that were one solid piece, the first crossbow design showed separate components, assembled afterwards. The weapon’s stock, trigger, and bolts were nothing more than solidified qi given form, but the string and limb specified using ‘intent forming’ to add flexibility.

Ferris hadn’t heard this term before, but had just read First Steps of the Soul. It had emphasized adding ‘intent’ in its instructions on qi forming a lightstone. It was worth trying the same technique here. Ferris had also created a bag that was somewhat flexible, perhaps he had used intent forming… unintentionally.

In retrospect, Uncle Winton’s instructions on how to make the core pedestal also used the technique. His mentor had told him to ‘focus on his intention.’

The plans had very specific measurements for both the physical size of each component, and the amount of liquid qi that should be used to craft them. Ferris had always just visualized what felt right, and he had no way to measure how much qi he was using. The exact size had never mattered before, but for a crossbow the components needed to fit together, and the bolts would need to slide in the track.

Ferris spent some time studying the blueprint. Overall, it wasn’t very complicated. Ferris felt he probably could have figured it out on his own, but that is often the case after an answer is shown. The stock had a ring jutting out from its front. Cocking the crossbow involved placing a foot through the hole and drawing the string up onto a notch—not something Ferris could easily do with one leg, but in the soulspace he would be fine. The bolts had a groove they sat in, and the trigger was a simple lever that pushed the string out of the notch. Later designs had mechanical cranks, magical bolts, and other wonders; but this was as simple as it could be.

Confident in his understanding of the parts, Ferris took Jade and left the library. He had made all the preparations outside of the soulspace he could think of. The only thing left would be to wait for Jade’s mental body to recover. In the meantime, he could craft himself a new spear and crossbow.

Back at Lily House, Ferris locked himself and Jade into their room. He tied the slime’s chain to the dresser, wondering how long they would need. ‘About two days’ to reform a mental body was a large time window.

Really, the only way to know would be to check. Ferris laid on the floor, with just his unburned hand extended to touch Jade. After a final repeat of the safety instructions, he began to meditate.

As he tried to quiet his thoughts, they would persistently bubble up. What would happen to him if the chain demons destroyed the statue? Most of his ideas hadn’t worked today, but the library had been worth it. The jade slip on crossbows showed how much further he had to go; how much work a cultivator had to do.

After a while, Ferris opened his eyes to his soulspace. He quickly checked that nothing else was missing—it wasn’t—and then stood in front of the core pedestal. Like yesterday, it was untouched by the chain demons. Touching the core, he entered the slimespace.

The slimespace was the same, with its green waterfall and strange swarms of ants crawling everywhere. The grey birds flew around within the limits of the dome, still showing no interest in him. Ferris was pleasantly surprised to see Jade hopping around their Heartfire.

When Ferris had died in the soulspace, he had needed two days to recover. It had only really been one and a half since the fish killed Jade, yet the slime had already reformed. Perhaps it was something to do with the slime’s simpler body, or their magical nature. Or they just got lucky this time. Whatever the reason, it was a good thing.

After calling Jade over and up into his arms, Ferris returned to his soulspace. He left the slime to explore around his Heartfire while he approached the Qi Pool.

The design from the library had specified that the crossbow should require 60 qi of liquid. In his Qi Pool… he had no idea how much he had. The concept of measuring it precisely had never even occurred to him before reading the books in the jade slip.

The plan had also specified exact measurements of each part. He had no way to measure the parts he was making, so Ferris decided to make the largest component first, and then reform the others until they fit. It wasn’t perfect, but it would hopefully work… presuming he didn’t run out of qi.

Ferris considered hunting a demon to use the reinforcement method but decided against it. The book had mentioned ‘demon mixed qi’ in several early designs, but not the first one. Until he had time to experiment, he decided to wait. Better to have a working basic crossbow than a more advanced failure. He eventually did want a demon corpse to make a new spear, though, since that was a proven design.

The largest piece was the stock of the crossbow. Ferris closed his eyes, placed one hand in the Qi Pool, and pictured it. It should be a solid piece of wood that he could brace against his shoulder, with a straight track along the top for the bolt. There was a ring at the tip to brace his foot, a groove cut for the limb, a notch to hold the string taut, and finally a hole for the trigger. Pulling his hand out, he now held the newly formed qi construct of the stock.

The Qi Pool was smaller, but only by about a sixth. Ferris guessed that he had used as much liquid qi for the stock as he had on an entire spear.

Next, he pictured the trigger. It was much smaller than the stock, but Ferris had to be more precise. It would be a lever that hung on the bottom of the stock, pushing an arm up through a hole to release the string when pressed against the stock. The trigger had to slot into the hole, while fitting into another set of holes that it used as a pivot.

His first trigger fit perfectly into the pivot holes, but its arm was nowhere close to the correct length. The second try was better, but was too long. The third try fit in all the correct holes, but wouldn’t swing freely—it would stick tightly when pressed down, never releasing. The fourth try finally worked well enough, although it was a bit rough in places. The Qi Pool shrunk with each attempt, although the total spent on all four triggers together was less than the amount used just for the crossbow’s stock.

The bow limb only needed to fit into one slot horizontally across the stock, but it needed ‘intent forming’ to be stiff yet flexible when pulled. Ferris’ first attempt was too small and flopped around before falling out of the stock. It was flexible, but it felt wrong for a bow limb. When he bent it, it gave too easily and didn’t spring back into place. The second attempt, refining his intent, was much better. It was a bit too big, but Ferris was able to wedge it into the slot with an excessive amount of force. He almost constructed a hammer to help, but then just used the side of his demon reinforced qi axe instead.

The string was easiest of all, and only took one try. He now had practice at ‘intent forming.’ He even made the string longer than necessary on purpose. The extra length was wrapped around the limb, which could come in handy.

Finished with the crossbow components, all that was left was to craft some ammo. Ferris made a quiver and fifteen bolts before he stopped due to worry about how low the Qi Pool was getting.

Rather than having metal tips and fletching, the instructions from the blueprint had him make the entire bolt as one piece. The tip, shaft, and stabilizing fins were all formed together as one continuous material.

The bolts were nowhere close to identical, but they were mostly straight and fit in the groove of the crossbow. It was a bit frustrating, as qi forming used his mental image. Why did they all have differences? He’d tried to imagine the same picture in his head each time.

When it was all finished, Ferris excitedly practiced cocking the crossbow. It took some strength, but he felt he could easily break the limb if he pulled too hard. Simple liquid qi was probably not the best material, but it was the only design from the book he could use.

Feeling the crossbow in his hands, he once again wondered if he should’ve used demon mixed qi. It wasn’t quite as solid as his latest spear had been. He just didn’t know if it would’ve worked with the intent forming. It could’ve changed the behavior of the materials and made the crossbow unable to fire. He didn’t have a lot of qi to experiment with. He thought he had left enough to create a spear, but just barely.

He placed a bolt in the track of the cocked crossbow, took aim at the ground about ten meters away, and pressed the trigger up against the bottom of the stock. The trigger arm moved up through the stock, and cleanly pushed the string out of its notch. The limb sprung forward, propelling the bolt along the track, and shot it forward.

With a thwump, the bolt stuck into the ground pretty darn close to where Ferris had aimed. The jade slip’s book on proper form had helped, although it was brief. Ferris reloaded and fired four more times. It took him about twenty seconds between shots, but he thought he could get faster with practice.

So, Ferris practiced.

Draw back the string, load a bolt, take his stance, aim, and shoot. Repeat, over and over again.

He broke three bolts in the process, but it was probably worth it. He saw a lot of improvement in how fast he moved, at least at first. After about an hour, he hit a wall where he stopped seeing any noticeable progress.

Overall, he was happy with the crossbow. The only thing left would be to test it in battle.
