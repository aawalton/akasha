Ellen and I ran straight from the room we’d meditated and ranked up in to where I’d set up our portal world’s entrance. Now that we’d finished ranking up, and we’d gotten the unexpected power-up to our auras, we had no time to waste.

But there was one person who didn’t see it that way.

“Hey, Kade, come here!” Jessie said.

“What?” I turned, stopping my dash for the portal. She sat in an armchair, her wheelchair nearby and her laptop open on her…well, lap.

“Just…just come here, okay?”

I sighed, nodded at Ellen, and walked the half-dozen steps to sit down next to Jessie.

As I did, I couldn’t help but be proud of her. My sister was having one of her bad days, and she had been for a while. The pressure of it all had stacked up on her, and she’d…we’d…neglected some of her stretches. Her knees and elbows had swollen up, and she looked seconds from whimpering like a hurt puppy. But she was still powering through, still working hard.

A long time ago—so long ago it felt like ages—she’d told me she wanted to do her best, too.

“A long time ago, I told you I wanted to do my best, just like you,” Jessie said. I did a double take. How had she known? “I was just thinking, Kade…about Dad, and about…uh, Mom.”

She stiffened as she said that last name. Alessa Noelstra had been our mother. But Jessie had never called her Mom. Not since she left. “What about Dad?” I asked.

“I have no idea.” Jessie went quiet. I resisted the urge to look over my shoulder. Ellen was back there. She’d be watching—or, more likely, she’d be turned around, looking at the hallway. “I know you have that avatar of him, but it’s not him. Not really. There’s so much I wanted to say to him. When I marry Stephen—“

I snorted. I couldn’t help myself. “Jessie, you’re sixteen.”

“I know that, Kade. But he texted me like five minutes ago. He knows something’s up, because I haven’t bugged him since this mess with the God of Thunder kicked off, but he’s still trying to be engaged as much as he can. He’s a good guy. If it works out, I’d be happy. So, anyway, when I get married, he’s not going to walk me down the aisle.”

“You know I’ll do it.”

“Yes, I know that too.” Jessie’s eyes narrowed. “Let me finish.”

“Sorry.”

“It’s not fair. You have that avatar, but it can’t do any of the stuff Dad could. It’s just here to fight like him, and it can’t…” she trailed off.

“It can’t replace him,” I finished for her. “It never did. The closest it got was just a few minutes ago. The Stormkin Avatar was almost Dad. But now it’s not.”

“What do you mean?”

I sighed. “I let him go.”

“Good. He deserved that.” A long pause. The silence stretched, and a tear moved down Jessie’s cheek. I couldn’t tell if it was from pain or grief. Then she slowly nodded. “The thing that’s the last fair about it wasn’t the avatar, Kade. It’s that, at any moment, Alessa could try to slide right back into our lives. She’s…she’s still out there. And I just can’t…I don’t think I could handle that.”

I gritted my teeth under my lips, careful not to let Jessie see. Not that she couldn’t read my body language or feel my anger. Of all the people I knew, Jessie was the one most in tune with me. She and I had spent our whole lives together, after all. I shook my head as she cried. “I won’t let her. Like you say, she’s not a mom. She’s not our mom.”

“A real mom wouldn’t have abandoned me. I know I’m a…hard person to take care of. But Dad did it. You did it. I’m not impossible. Just difficult. And I try. I’ve been trying ever since Dad taught me meditation and the therapists figured out the right therapies. I just want to do my best. And I have to be brave every day, because sometimes, it’s so much, and my best is so little.

“But Mom didn’t want to do her best. If she’d been any kind of brave, she’d have stayed. I wasn’t that bad. You made it work.”

“No, I didn’t,” I said quietly.

“You did.”

“No, Jessie.” I leaned over and very carefully hugged her. Her face left a wet spot on my shoulder, but I didn’t mind. “You did. You did your best, and it’d been so much more than I expected.”

Jessica Gerald, leader of the Desert Wind Guild, member of the GC’s Inner Council, and Earth’s unofficial ambassador to the most powerful entity in this universe, was being torn in two.

Part of her—the vast majority of her, in fact—needed this. To have a few minutes to not be any of those things. Jessie liked being in charge of the guild. She liked being a member of the Inner Council. She even liked visiting the Crone and playing her own version of chess with the SS-Plus-Ranked Paragon, with the fate of Earth potentially in the balance. She wouldn’t trade any of her life for that of a normal sixteen-year-old. No girl about to be a junior in high school had ever had this kind of power. Maybe someone whose system had awakened, but…no. No, she had more influence than any delver.

But with all of that responsibility, sometimes it was hard to remember that she was a junior in high school, and that it was okay for her to be sixteen sometimes. There was too much to do. Too many things could go wrong. So, she had to bottle up the stupid, useless, childish angst and uncertainty and be J-Dawg, or Representative Gerald, or Guildmaster Gerald.

Sometimes, though, she just needed her brother’s shoulder to cry on for a few minutes. She needed to be a teenager, and let her emotions overwhelm her. To feel her body hurting, and more pressingly, her soul hurting. So, yeah, this was nice. She was safe. Nothing could hurt her here, and she could let herself have a nice cry.

On the other hand…as great as it felt emotionally to just let go for a while, it was physically…starting to get uncomfortable.

“Kade, you’re pinching my shoulder back.”

“Sorry.”

Kade slowly, carefully lowered her back into the armchair. She relaxed into its soft, plush cushion, letting her arms and legs settle into a neutral position that hurt less than anything else.

Hard days were the worst. Sometimes, she couldn’t even let Stephen touch her arms, and it was all she could do not to bawl like a baby when her therapists carefully bent her into shapes her body just didn’t want to bend into. Today was absolutely one of those days—and worse, she couldn’t just find a bed, lie flat on her back with her arms and legs spread out, and whine quietly in the dark until she fell asleep. There was too much to do. Too much only she could do.

“Thanks. For all of that.”

“No problem.”

Silence. It didn’t feel awkward to Jessie. She’d spent hours like this, on her laptop while Kade read through books of build options. She was used to the quiet.

But at the same time…

It wasn’t fair to Kade, but she wasn’t quite ready to face her body’s limitations and try to push past them yet. She needed just a tiny bit more whining, and a tiny bit more sympathy, from her brother.

“I’ve been doing so much, Kade,” she found herself whining. “The guild, the GC, everything. Keeping the Guardians from counterattacking every time you trample their crops on the way to and from the Wickenberg Portal.”

Kade grinned sheepishly. “Sorry.”

“When this is over, I’m taking a vacation. Maybe I’ll buy a convoy and punch over to the West Coast cities. Sit on a beach, in the sand—“

“You can sit in the sand any time, Jessie.”

“—and feel the waves on my toes, Kade. I can’t do that here. Take a few months off from school and being in charge of everything.” Jessie forced herself to stop. She’d done enough of her pity party, enough fantasizing about what’d come after the battle that was about to happen. It had been nice to be a normal sixteen-year-old girl, or as normal as she ever got, but now wasn’t the time for that. She needed to be Guildmaster Gerald—and J-Dawg—for a few more hours.

The beach would have to wait.

She sat up straight, her eyes narrowing both from the pain and from the wave of determination rolling over her. Her laptop sat on the table nearby; she reached for it, and Kade handed it to her. Instead of opening it, she slotted it into the holder on her wheelchair and looked up at her brother. He looked…sad.

“You have a fight to get to, Kade.”

“I do.”

“I trust you. You’ll come back from it. But…well, it’s going to be an ugly one, and it won’t be over when you get Tanat back. If I were Eugene…”

Kade nodded. “You’d bring the fight to us as soon as you could. Especially if we’d just attacked your power reserves. I’m counting on it.”

“And when he attacks Earth instead?” Jessie asked.

This silence felt awkward. It hung over their heads like the sword on the string she’d learned about in Mythology class. And, after a minute, Kade nodded. “You have a plan for that, don’t you?”

Jessie grinned. “I do.”

“Then make it happen. I’m counting on you, Jessie.”

As Kade left, Jessie groaned and shifted herself slowly over to her wheelchair. She could easily have asked Kade for help. He’d have done it a lot faster. But she hadn’t. That was…kinda stupid. Sometimes, her pride really got in the way.

She started typing.

J-Dawg: Hi, guys. Been a few days. Who’s online?

User295: What’s been eating your time?

Yreanne: I’m always online, J-Dawg.

J-Dawg: Great. I need your help. It’s urgent.

As Jessie typed, her plan slowly took form. Kade would be a solid half-hour, and after that, they’d have an unknown amount of time before the God of Thunder made his move. She finished chatting with User295 and Yreanne, then went to her official GC emails.

Guild leaders, please put your guild members on alert. Assemble your teams, and prepare to defend your regions of the city.

Governing Council, the Desert Wind Guild recommends the following actions:

The message went out. She wrote another one.

To: Desert Wind Guild Members

RE: Incoming Portal Surge (URGENT)

All members of the Desert Wind Guild, barring delvers Jeff Carlton, Kade Noelstra, and Eleanor Traynor, are ordered to report to the Portal Tyrants’ castle immediately. Assemble by teams if applicable, and by rank if you are currently teamless. Bring any and all necessary equipment to fight either in Phoenix’s streets, on the 303 Wall, or in an unknown portal.

We are the tip of the spear—us, and the Portal Tyrants.

Jessie counted to ten, then started slowly wheeling herself toward the garage. She needed the head start, because any minute now—

Her phone started ringing. She picked it up. “Guildmaster Gerald. Hello, Councilwoman. Yes, things are in motion. The stuff we talked about earlier is happening right now. Yeah, it’s faster than I figured it’d be. I delayed him as long as I could. It’s going to be an ugly one, but I think we can win. Yes, my idiot brother’s responsible for it all.”

She went quiet for a while as Councilwoman Myers went off. The woman had only left the Portal Tyrants’ building a handful of minutes ago, and now, things were in motion. She sympathized. It was really hard to stay on top of events when some people just kept making them happen.

The team was ready.

Ellen Traynor. The Shadow Witch. Even if I hadn’t wanted her here, trying to keep my partner out of this raid would have been impossible. She wasn’t fully recovered from the God of Thunder’s assault on her, but she was close. More importantly, she’d kill me if I didn’t let her come. So, she was in. She wore her battle robes, and her new aura pushed against her control, eager to surge out and rip into the shadows that welled around her.

Sarah Cullman. The Spark of Life. We’d need her to keep the team alive against the God of Thunder himself. But more importantly, there was a chance Tanat was alive. I wasn’t ready to risk the alien woman’s life, so we needed a medic, and Sarah was the best—though Sophia would be standing by just outside the portal. Sarah was quiet, waiting at the entrance to my portal world.

Terrel Young. The Portal Tyrant. I’d fought alongside him and Sarah before, in the Carlsbad portal. I trusted him, and he was a competent, powerful S-Ranker. He’d be able to hold his own. But for all that he was strong, he wasn’t the Light of Dawn, and we’d need more firepower than even Angelo Lawrence could muster. He’d brought his warhammer, his chained axes, and a double bandolier of daggers—every one of them an S-Rank weapon. The sheer cost of it all would have bankrupted a lesser guild.

Me. Obviously.

And, standing in the corner, trying—and failing—to look small next to the four of us S-Rankers, was Jeff.

Jeff Carlton. C-Rank.

He was fully armored in the best gear we could get him. It out-ranked him, and he could hardly move in it, much less fight. It wasn’t just his rank, though. The rest of us knew exactly what we were getting into. We’d fought against an SS-Plus-Ranked enemy before, whether it had been the Placid King’s tentacles, the squid-like Paragon himself, or the God of Thunder. We’d prepared ourselves.

Jeff couldn’t know. And that was killing him.

But, under his helmet, his eyes blazed. He wasn’t as strong as the rest of us. But he was still all-in.

And the core clutched in his portal metal gauntlets was ready for him. Frost spread all the way up his armored hands to cross his vambraces, the snowflake patterns growing together and covering the shining metal in a white crust.

I nodded. “Thank you all. I owe every one of you for this.”

Then I rested my hand on Jeff’s shoulder and looked him in the eye. “Are you ready?”
