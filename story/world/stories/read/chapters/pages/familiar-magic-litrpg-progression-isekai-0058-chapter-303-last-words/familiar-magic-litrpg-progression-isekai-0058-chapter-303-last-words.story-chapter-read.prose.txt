Emperor Gallow refused to let it end like this. Lifetimes had passed in his pursuit of the three scourges, thousands of years of silence among the stars, searching, waiting. Now, his peoples’ story would finally end the way it was meant to.

He would defeat these Twilights. Twilights! The shame of being pressed by beings of such lesser power infuriated Gallow. But the wound, the cursed wound just would not heal!

Another Master had died to do this to him, and it clearly hadn’t been an idle attack. They’d planned to destroy him, and nearly succeeded. Gallow held to life, but the gaping cavity in his chest felt as if it had just been injured. There was an absence there, like that part of him had been erased from existence.

Gallow had been prepared to live with the injury, knowing it was one of countless wrongs he could set right when Dusk’s power was his. But now…

His clawed hands moved in a blur, deflecting one attack after another. A hammer crashed into his forearm but the bulky, armored one. Two clubs crashed into his back at almost the same moment. Then the blades came. Those damnable blades.

Conquest had turned on the Followers, as Bek warned he might. The hold had been too weak, and weaker still as Gallow was forced to fight in this insulting duel. Now his own tool had been turned on him, and how it stung.

The swords could barely pierce his flesh, of course. The hammer, little more than a slap. The clubs, practically a massage from one of the lessers. His wound had done nothing to weaken his flesh, though it slowed him, and sapped his strength.

That left him in this miserable impasse. Another orb of gravity pulled him to one side, opening his flank for those swords once more. Gallow reacted in silent fury, catching one blade, and hurling Conquest over his head.

Hot magma spilled over Gallow for his trouble, though he had the momentary joy of seeing his former tool nearly fall into the pit where the Champions warred. They’d gotten dangerously close to what was now a perilously steep cliff, and each combatant had to fight with greater caution to avoid the drop.

Gallow, himself, had to continually send commands to the scorpiursi to keep it from coming any closer. Those two colossal creatures were simply too ancient, too powerful, and far too imprecise to be brought into this battle.

Still, that left Gallow with four opponents, and increasing frustration at his inability to kill any of them. If he could just lock his hands around one’s throat, he was certain he could even the odds slightly.

His eyes searched for the right moment as his body continued to dance and shift with the flow of the battle. The knight was too-well protected, that heavy armor would be difficult to get through.

As he dodged two more sword-thrusts, he considered targeting conquest. But that one was nearly as fast a Master, with his wind affinity. The demon–why did a demon help them?–was no better a target. Their physiology was famously unusual, and he could never be sure he was using his time for a killing blow.

And a killing blow was what he needed. The time it would take to truly end a Twilight would leave him open to considerable wounds, even from those as weak as these. Only the close quarters and constant movement kept them from unleashing their true powers, and their Familiar.

When he stopped to kill one, the others would make him pay, and he had to be sure it was worth it.

A spear of pink like lanced into him once more, and Gallow’s target was set. This one was a stranger to him, smelling of another world, but he’d kept his distance for the entire fight. He manipulated gravity and hurled bolts of energy, but always he stayed back.

A mage, then. Powerful, but likely the slowest and least capable of surviving a direct assault. Gallow held no special powers of his own, beyond his darkborne Familiar, and he needed his companion to maintain the link to the Champion.

Gallow needed to take the man’s head, or put a hand through his chest–a mirror to his own wound would be fatal at Twilight.

He bided his time for a few more moments, lulling his opponents into a rhythm. Then he struck.

As Conquest darted in from the side, Gallow leaped over him, and shot directly for the mage. He closed the distance in an instant, and took grim pleasure at the look of shock and terror in the man’s eyes.

It was the look of one who knew he was about to die.

Gallow’s hand was already forward, claws extended, and he’d put all of his strength into this dash. He could already see in his mind’s eye what the aftermath would look like.

Yet, nearly a pace from impact, something appeared in front of Gallow. It was another man–the hated Chosen.

Gallow had only the barest instant to hope that the Chosen was sacrificing himself, before his hand disappeared through a tiny, blue-white rift shifting across the man’s body. Gallow’s eyes widened in surprise, then he was being hurled back as a sword blurred through air, leaving a slash across the Emperor’s chest.

Gallow screamed inside his mind as he was set-upon by five different warriors at once. He’d wasted his moment!

He fell into a defensive stance, putting the cliff behind him to keep his opponents on one side, then went about trying to come up with a new plan.

Unfortunately, the addition of the Chosen was no linear advantage. The man was infuriating!

He would disappear from sight, then appear from an unexpected angle, slashing out with that blade of his. Then he’d seem to retreat, only for his hands to appear through small rifts, sword poking through with no time to dodge.

Several times, Gallow had been sure he’d caught the man, exulting in the feel of impending victory as his claws passed through the man’s grinning face–only to be revealed as a duplicate. An exploding duplicate.

But Emperor Gallow, one of the last true-born of the People, was more than a hundred thousand years old. He’d fought on a hundred different worlds, against a thousand different races, and faced powers these five couldn’t dream of.

He may be wounded, and frustrated, but he was a Master, in rank, and in technique. Gallow worked to calm his mind, and fell more firmly into a fighting form that he’d learned before his world had been crushed by the scourges.

He. Would. Not. Fall.

“This bastard just won’t die!” Ethan muttered.

All five of them were pushing as hard as they could, but this Follower was like nothing they’d faced before. He wasn’t overwhelmingly powerful like the Champions fighting alarmingly close-by, but he may as well have been Emperor of the cockroaches for how hard he was to kill.

Not only were they landing fewer and fewer good hits on the Master, but Ethan could see the ones they had landed visibly healing. The Emperor was drenched in blood, and covered in at least a hundred small injuries, but they were little more than scratched.

The only consolation was that the Follower seemed unable to end this decisively either. But every moment that passed was another chance for the Emperor to try something desperate. He’d seen the man eyeing the scorpiursi more than once, and a stinger the size of two buses loomed over them.

“We have to end this,” he said to Valanor through their communication runes.

The knight was panting, and clearly low on mana. It was clear he’d needed to fight his way to Ethan in the first place, and every one of the five was using fewer and fewer of their powers. Twilight or not, they’d been hurling everything they had at this beast for nearly a half hour.

Ethan himself was in agony. He’d been pushing himself despite his mana channels being recently fried by the use of his Twilight’s Gift.

Valanor managed to respond only after blocking several more strikes, barely keeping a clawed hand from Calevaro’s face after the prince over-extended.

“We both know what needs to be done,” the knight said, “unless one of you has some card you haven’t played?”

“Just more of the same,” Dean said.

“We cannot seem to deal any lasting damage,” Calevaro added. “I’ve tried everything I have.”

“And our Familiars are just liabilities,” Valanor added.

“What about Michael?” Dean suggested.

“One more recently ranked up Twilight,” Ethan said while hurling exploding daggers through a tiny rift. “It just won’t be enough.”

“We know what we have to do,” Valanor said again.

And they did. Of course they did. Every one of them was painfully aware of the sacrifice play. Ethan just refused to acknowledge it, because he was the only one who couldn’t do it.

“You’re just delaying this,” Valanor said, as if reading Ethan’s thoughts. “We all know it’ll be me.”

“We can find a way to win this!” Ethan insisted, not believing his own words.

“This is how we win this,” the knight countered. “We’re losing this battle of attrition. Soon enough, one of us will slip, then another, and by then it’ll be over.”

“It… It…” Ethan struggled for words. “It…doesn’t have to be you,” he said.

“It does,” the knight said flatly. “I won’t let it be the prince. He’s still my charge to protect, whether he sees himself as such or not. And it can’t be you, boy, you have work to be done.”

“Then let me do it,” Dean said. “I’ve lived a long life knowing I was willing to die for a cause.”

“Dean, no!” Ethan said reflexively. But he knew what he’d done as soon as he said it.

“And that seals it,” Valanor said, voice a mix of victory and sadness. “Ethan needs his brother. Let me do my duty.”

The knight made a wild charge without another word. He began to grow in size, using his most powerful ability, and the swings of his ever-larger hammer pushed the Emperor back. Back toward the pit, where the Champions fought.

The Follower would get around the massive figure, though, they all knew it. He’d prove just how fast, and agile he could be. Heavy swings wouldn’t be enough, and Valanor was prepared for that.

As the Emperor glanced back, beady black eyes taking in his proximity to the cliff, they could all see his shift in stance, preparing to dart around the shield knight.

The rest of them reacted with instinctual speed.

Ethan’s blade came through a rift, pushing the Follower back. Calevaro sent a blast of wind that must have nearly bankrupted his mana supply. Dean created a massive orb of gravity hovering out over the pit, and pulling the Emperor toward it.

Valanor didn’t waste the opportunity. As his hammer crashed down, the determined Emperor still managing to knock it aside, two enormous hands reached for the lithe Follower. He would grab the Master rank monster, and send them both tumbling over the edge, right into two Champions of immeasurable power.

“Valanor.” A deep, oddly calm and eerily familiar voice said. Ethan’s eyes widened–he hadn’t realized Savilar’s communication rune still worked.

The Church-knight turned demon was capitalizing on the moment too, and he proved far faster than Valanor.

The red and white figure crashed into the Emperor’s middle, just an instant before the knight’s hands reached him. Calevaro’s wind and Dean’s gravity orb pulled the pair out into the air beyond the cliff and a hundred paces farther in nearly an instant.

Ethan could only watch, mouth agape as the pair soared directly over the Champions, then began to plummet downward.

“Savilar!” Valanor bellowed.

And through their runes, all heard the man’s final, defiant words.

“I…was…RIGHT!”
