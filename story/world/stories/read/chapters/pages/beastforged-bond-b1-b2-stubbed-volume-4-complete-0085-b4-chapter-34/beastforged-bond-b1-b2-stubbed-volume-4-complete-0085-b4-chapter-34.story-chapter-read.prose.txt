****It felt like an eternity passed before the last of the Mark’s fragments sank into the core. The nectar accumulated since unlocking Pond of Life had been fully used to mend the core’s cracks, yet it felt like the process wasn’t done just yet.

My Gates… I cursed inwardly, sensing only now that most of the ether within me had been consumed, including the ether within the Gates.

Only a fraction of my Gates prevailed, and even they were rapidly sucked dry. All that happened within a few seconds, because it was unlikely that more than that much time elapsed, even if it felt like an eternity. I was still alive, after all.

Anyway, my core was no longer a blank sphere. Intricate lines, forming a complex design, were now etched on the core. A Mark. Not Volca’s, but… mine? It was white, dazzling even, and outlined in filigree gold. It stirred my entire being, flooding it with a kind of power that seemed to encompass everything.

My eyes snapped open to a world in which time had come to a crawl. Volix was still engaged in battle, Scott was trying to best the pinned Spawn, and the others stood there, ready to leap back into battle once the phoenix fire faded.

Looking down at myself, the corner of my lip curled upward. Volca’s Mark was no more. Instead, a new Mark came to be. A white flame, shrouded in a galaxy of golden stars.

“That’s better,” I muttered to myself. As if waiting for this, the world resumed its work. Time sped up, and chaos unfurled all around me.

A new sword appeared in my hand. It was shorter than the other sword, yet it weighed more heavily in my hand. I felt weak, slow, and no more durable than a Journeyman. That sensation only worsened when I consumed most of the ether left at my disposal.

Nullblade materialized around the sword, but it was no longer the same. Its grayish-silver hue was brighter, as if it had gotten rid of its impurities, and it wreathed the blade more intimately.

The divination beast appeared beside me, its head lowered to touch the flat side of my sword with its horn. It neighed quietly and lowered its front further until it knelt before me.

Yet another shift occurred as the beast’s horn came in contact with my blade. Nullblade grew thicker, emanating a presence I could only describe as dangerous. Then came the flames, pristine yet raw. They erupted from the sword’s handle, coated the blade, and burned hot enough to melt the enchanted weapon.

My chest tightened as the Mark flared up like a supernova. It was hot but not uncomfortable. If anything, the searing heat spread comfortably throughout my entire chest, offering power and warmth that felt more intimate than anything.

Nullblade’s coat merged with the pristine-white flames. A moment later, I used Blink and appeared amid the battlefield, sword cleaving through the first of many Spawns.

For the first time in centuries, the Tehorian Ocean stirred. Magma Wyrms, Orikon Dwellers, as well as the inhabitants of the Dragon Nest from the nearby mountain range awoke in the middle of the night. They moved frantically as the presence of their host and master moved.

The Tehorian Ocean, an ocean made of superheated lava and the purest of ether, split apart as a gargantuan figure emerged. It towered high above the nearby mountaintop as it scrambled to its feet for the first time in a long time.

Volca moved. Not his consciousness in a vessel made of compressed ether, nor by possessing one of his many creations. No. Volca, the Spirit of Fire, moved his main body. For he was awake.

Surprise reverberated through his age-old body, even though he may be shouldn’t have been surprised.

His Mark left on the Primal’s heir was no more.

Is he not the one? was Volca’s first thought. He supposed he had made a mistake. But his creation, his offspring, was still alive, their connection severed. No, his offspring was still bound to a World. To the World of the Primal’s heir. But the Mark was no more. Volca felt it shatter.

And that was not all. Even the fragments of his Mark vanished like they never existed.

“What are you doing, Allfather?” Volca muttered, yet his voice rumbled through the land of fire with a force only the greatest of beasts could endure.

Primal Spirit, Allfather to Spirits, God King of Fae, Greatest of the Thirteen Realms, Conqueror of Death, was dead. Killed by the Devourer. The Spirits felt it. The Realms fell, and the Spirits, defeated, were told to flee. That they did, even though they wanted nothing more than to join the Primal Spirit in the last stand against their greatest nemesis.

They sensed their father’s death. He was no longer with them, yet not all of him was dead. The Primal Spirit left behind an inheritance.

And the young human was supposed to have a World containing a trace of said inheritance. He was heir to the Primal Spirit’s legacy. Or so Volca hoped.

He shook his head. No, he did not hope. He knew.

Adam’s World gave birth to the Voice of Earth even though the Earthen Spirit was no longer among them. His World managed to bind the Elemental Phoenix, showing that it did not only have the highest compatibility with the earthen sacred beast but other sacred beasts as well.

If that was not enough evidence to prove that the young human’s World had been tampered with, Thyria’s sacrifice certainly was. Not only did the World Tree spare Adam, she even sacrificed her power to present him with her offspring. Her intentions were self-centered, but that did not change the meaning behind Adam’s bond and survival.

Three sacred beasts and many other beasts that had been growing stronger when their racial potential should have long since been used up. That was not a coincidence. It was proof.

But Volca wanted more. He wanted to watch Adam to see if he had what it took before making a move that would surely attract the Devourer’s attention. That was where the Mark came into play. The Spirit of Fire wanted to observe Adam from far away. That was taken from him, ripped out of his grasp, and he didn’t even know how. In a way, that was the worst.

How?

Volca straightened his back before sending off a message to his other creations.

To watch Adam and keep him updated.

Volca was curious. He was interested in Adam’s growth, but even more so in learning what happened to his Mark.

Maybe… He shook his head. No, the young human would not do that. He could not. He was not as weak as he had been when he last checked on the Mark. And if he did… in that case, Adam was as good as dead either way.

Or he would surpass the Fire Spirit’s expectations and make it out alive. Volca remembered the Ruler of Fire and his obsession with the Elemental Phoenix.

“If he really did it, they would clash soon. How curious.” The Fire Spirit murmured to himself, his molten face distorting into a smile.

Best the Ruler, and you will have my support, little heir.

The white flames were terrifying. While they didn’t burn me, I could feel the power within them.

The first Spawn split in half from the first cleave, and it failed to recover. If anything, the Spawn’s fate was sealed as soon as the pristine flames clung on to the Devourer’s Spawn. They became leeches, the flickering flames burning brighter as they spread like a wildfire, devouring the Spawn rather than being devoured.

The Spawn thrashed, but it was already split in half and unable to transform.

My attention shifted to the second Spawn. Volix burst into action and restricted the creature temporarily. A moment later, I cleaved it in half as well.

The third was still pinned to the ground. It resisted and tried to move around, but no matter how much it moved, the Elemental Phoenix was by my side.

My leg caved in as more and more strength oozed out of me. My vision blurred shortly after, my arms grew heavier, and the dazzling light of the pristine flames was soon replaced by darkness. Yet I did not collapse before thrusting my melting sword into the third Spawn’s chest, piercing its core in one go.

What did you do? How did you find the Spawn’s core? Volix called out, but his voice sounded distant.

Hmm?

Those flames. They’re not Volca’s creation. Your Mark… you did not accept it? Why would you not do– You created your own Insignia! How did you do that? The Elemental Phoenix sounded befuddled. That was new. It was also something I quite enjoyed. Or would have if I wasn’t totally drained.

My body resisted my mental commands, then my brain failed me as well. The pristine flames died down. As they did, I collapsed to the ground with one thought on my mind.

We killed the Spawns. I smiled through the pain and weakness.

With only one Wild Gate left, which was not even full at this point, I was lucky to be alive.

Alas, the Fiend was still alive. The pristine flames hadn’t injured it. If anything, the Fiend appeared attracted to the flames and their power. Or… my World?

That was something my friends and Soulkins had to resolve. Or so I hoped as the world around me turned dark.

Daniel had no idea what was happening, but he shuddered at the chaos before him. Adam was a man-made catastrophe… to his enemies only, fortunately. The Spawns they’d barely managed to fight and stall were no longer moving. They weren’t healing either.

Adam had killed the Spawns. Alone, in a way.

Adam was a monstrosity, and Daniel could not help but be proud of his friend. And a little jealous, maybe. But only a little. It was only human to feel like that. That didn’t mean Daniel disliked Adam for the power his hard work earned him. Sure, fortune had been on his friend’s side, but Adam deserved every bit of it.

Still… since when does he have so many powerful Soulkins? Daniel swallowed hard as Adam’s Soulkins emerged from his World.

He knew Aureus was powerful, but how long had it been since the Earthheart became a Guardian beast? Truthfully, Aureus was not even a Guardian beast at this point, yet the Earthheart increased the Fiend’s gravity by more than ten times. Daniel knew that because he’d been too close to the Fiend when Aureus modified the Fiend’s gravity.

He had also been too close when Aureus hurled a mountain of earth and soil at the Fiend. After that, he kept his distance and watched Aureus compress yet another mountain of earth and stone using… the sacred beast was probably using gravity for that as well.

The gravitational pull increased once more as the compressed mountain took shape. It transformed into a massive spear, which Adam’s mantis leaped at to wreath it in a silvery hue. Then it was hurled at the Fiend, while the Ferronox Mantis was still clinging to it.

The spear struck the Fiend square in the chest. It twisted aside, or tried to, but the spear hit its mark anyway. In the meantime, the mantis leaped at the Fiend and actually injured the humanoid beast with his nasty scythes. In return, the Fiend grasped its legs, punched the mantis several times in quick succession, and hurled it away like dead weight.

It didn’t die, but Daniel didn’t think the Ferronox Mantis had much life left in its disfigured body when it was carried back to Adam. Roots emerged from Adam’s body, which was yet another thing Daniel couldn’t quite grasp about his friend, and they pulled Nox back into Adam’s World. Or so Daniel hoped. If not that–

Anyway, when the time to attack the Fiend came, Scott and the divination beast followed up first. Daniel and Lea were next, all while the Fiend ignored them in a desperate attempt to reach Adam. It failed miserably at that.

They blocked the Fiend’s path, which only resulted in more and more injuries on the humanoid beast’s side. It took the hits–voluntarily, one had to add–to close in on Adam.

Why? It could have attacked the others, probably even killed them, because the phoenix and other Soulkins were focused on protecting Adam rather than joining the battle. But the Fiend no longer cared about reason. It was like it was no longer sentient as it attacked over and over again. Until it looked worse for wear, and it realized that it would die long before reaching Adam.

Then, it fled rather than engaging in battle with the others.

As weird as it was, Daniel was thankful. Fighting several Spawns had already been dangerous enough. And, if not for Adam and the phoenix, they would have died. Neither would have lasted more than a few minutes.

They would have become the Spawns’ meals.
