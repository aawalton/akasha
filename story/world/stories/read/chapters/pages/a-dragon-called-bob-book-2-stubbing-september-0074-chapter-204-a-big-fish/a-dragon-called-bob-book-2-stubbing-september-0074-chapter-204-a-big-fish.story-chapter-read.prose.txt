An orca, when it dies violently, is not a pretty sight.  If you can imagine what it looks like in a washing machine when you leave a pinky sock in with a white load… that is a very mild image versus the reality that bubbled up to the surface next to me.

Something had turned Orcinus inside out.  His stubby arms were gone, and jagged gashes ran down the sides of his body.  Shudders and twitches made the flippers and tail flick about randomly.

“Bob… There’s something else down there.”

“Thanks, Ali, I would never have guessed. Hold the otter while I take a look.”

I passed the king of the cuties over to Ali.  She grabbed his arms, and I could see that her claws were a hair's breadth from biting into his hide. I stuck my head back underwater and tried to get an idea of what the hell was going on.

I’d assumed the otters would break once we took out their king, but they were still fighting.  The little shits outnumbered their attackers and were still exploiting that advantage.  Groups of Merkin were being swarmed now that the orca and myself were out of the fight to serve as targets.

The walrus dudes were taking a lot of damage, but they just couldn’t stand up to the punishment.  The three-pronged claws of the lutra left clean cuts in their thick hide.  Often it didn’t cut through the blubber to the muscles below, but even then, having their skin split must have been agony.

I yanked my head out and found Ali was holding the otter king off my back as he thrashed back and forth.

“I have to help them!”

“You can’t return to the fight,” Ali muttered.

“I don’t want to fight!  For the love of clams!  I need to tell them to withdraw.  He’s here.  We’re all dead.”

“Your guys are nasty in the water.  What the hell killed the orca?” I demanded.

“He did.”

“Emphasising a neutral word does fucking help, dude.”

“He knows you.  He knew you were coming,” babbled the otter.

“I was always going to return.  How could you abandon the codex?” Ali demanded, shaking him back and forth.

“Not you.  Him.”

“Me?  I guess I’m famous.”  Vanity preened in my mind.

“Not in a good way.  We found him. What was left of him.  And we rebuilt him.  Fusing mammal and crustacean into a superior being. A killer and a clam.”

I stuck back under water and saw that the Merkin had been routed.  They were in full flight.  Bodies drifted upwards, rising up towards me, surrounded by clouds of red and pink.  Small fish were darting among the bodies, snatching chunks from the all-you-can-eat buffet that had been created.

Water sprayed as I pulled my head back.  “You need to call your furry torpedoes off,” I snapped.

“I need to be under the water.  It won't stop him, though.”

“I’ll stop him, whoever he is.”  I grabbed him with a pair of tails and shoved him back under the water.  A series of clicks and squeals went out from the king before I yanked him out.

With my head beneath the waves, I could see the lutra break and scatter, disappearing into the depths or bolting for the surface as far away from me as possible.  The Merkin began to form up, forming into depleted units and staring around in fear.

“The otters fucked off, but the fish folk are still freaking out.”

“The murder king is down there. Hartkleplep had me summon him before you caught me.  Our clams are cooked.”  The otter king shivered within the embrace of my tails.

“The Crow Lord wouldn't deign to set foot on a testiland, the Murder is constantly engaged on the Tip,”  Ali muttered.

“Are you speaking code?  What Murder?”

“The collective noun for crows is a Murder, Bob.  Who the hell is the Murder King?” Ali demanded from the otter.  I squeezed slightly.  Not enough to turn him into an old tube of toothpaste, but enough to make him aware that it was still an option.

We found him floating on the surface over a month ago.  Too powerful to die, despite what some mighty blade had done to him.  Hartkleplep took him to Remy, and then the rat king took him to the fungest.  The noblins experimented on him.  Turned him into something… obscene.”

“You  guys made a monster?”

“Mistress, he came to us as a monster.  Powerful, dangerous, implacable.  Even half and dead, he slaughtered a hundred lutra.”  The otter was shaking now. “I had control over him.  I could feel him in my mind, all the time.  A bundle of rage and hate that lay next to the slimy presence of the worm.   Now… I've got no sense of him.  He’s been unleashed.”

I stuck my head back under the water as Ali opened her snout to interrogate the king some more.  Second-hand accounts are always coloured by the person giving them, and a randy otter who’d just had a brain worm ripped out of his skull didn’t strike me as a solid witness.

The merkin were scattering, swimming away as fast as possible.  One group suddenly bolted towards the surface, splitting apart like they were being hunted.  A blue blur passed through them, and a pair of them exploded into clouds of red.

I couldn’t keep track of the perpetrator.  It disappeared into the background. It must be cold-blooded.  Another pair of Merkin died, turning to chum in an instant.

Hints of legs and arms, that was all I’d seen as it shot through the water.  They were jagged on the right-hand side but bulky and muscular on the left.  Some kind of hybrid?

“It’s fast.”  I glared at Ali as the water dripped off my nose.  “I can’t even see it.”

“I can’t fight in the water. I’m a doggy paddle kind of girl.”

“You’ve been alive for a couple of millennia and never learned to swim?”

“I’ve always been on land.” She looked left and right at the endless ocean all around.

“Land is about fifty miles that way.”  I pointed with a tail.

“Actually, it’s over there,” said the otter king helpfully, pointing in the opposite direction.  I squeezed a little harder until his eyes bulged.

I paddled over to Orcinus’s corpse and nodded at it.  “Hop on.  I can’t fight with passengers.  Otter, you’re going to behave?” I fixed him with a level nine glare that made him shiver.  His nod was jerky, but Ali nodded slightly as well.

He stumbled as my tails let go, and he fell down onto my back, going to all fours.  “I’ve been a prisoner in my own skin for so long…  Finally free.  If you can kill the Murder King, then my people will be forever loyal.”

“Yeah, yeah.  Kill the big bad, earn the undying loyalty of people whose idea of war preparation is an orgy. I can see you being so useful next time I need to deal with pervy cultists or a fucking water level.”

As soon as they were off my shoulders, I dove, wing flicks sending me spiralling into the deep.  I’d taken a deep breath, inflating my massive lungs as much as possible. The oxygen timer I was working under shot up my list of worries.

Whatever nightmare the noblins and the demons had cobbled together was a real threat.  God forged scales were rather overpowered, after all. However, I still needed to breathe.

Resisting the rage to snack on some of the larger chunks of lutra and merkin, I saw a vicious melee going on in the dark.  I could feel the flashing shades that still showed some evidence of being warm-blooded.  My zombie otters were doing their best, but they were dropping like flies.

The thing was always moving.  It never stopped, and whenever it passed close to anything living or dead, it died.  Again, in the case of the latter.

I swam after it, but it was too fast.  It flashed past me, battering my scales in passing.  I flipped over to avoid it slamming into my wings, and I kept moving.  A couple of times, one of my tails managed to swat it in passing, but the thing didn’t even slow down.

Reversing direction, I began to lead it back towards the surface. Glancing blows kept throwing me off course, pain echoing through me as it tried to batter through my armour.

I emerged from the water like a sub-launched missile, half a mile away from the orca carcass, now acting as a sanctuary for Ali and the captive otter king.  And I kept flying.  My wings beat hard as I sought altitude.  I couldn’t fight that thing in the water.

Heat vision was a lot less effective when it had to transition from air to water.  Below the surface, everything was a murky swirl of blues and greens.  The waves passed, the ripples of my eruption interfering with the slow roll towards the distant shore.

A blast of fire cut down from my nostrils as something came up after me.  A cloud of steam exploded as the flame impacted the thing.  You-Only-Catch-Flak-When-You’re-Over-The-Target sent me rolling to the side as the enemy shot passed me.

I pivoted and glared at the blue-green blur cutting up into the sky.  It was colder than the air around it, so at last I was able to track it properly.  It reached the zenith of its climb and surprised me.  It hung in the sky above me.

A lumpen thing.  I was hesitant to call it a human, but it was human-shaped, at least half of it was.  The craggy side flashed red, and it shot towards me.  I opened my mouth and then thought better of self-delivering food.  I’d used that trick myself.

It could move fast in a straight line, but couldn’t turn for shit.  With my wings and natural instinct for flight, I was able to outmanoeuvre it in the air.  I couldn’t land a blow, though.

My flames either missed or were ignored, and my claw swipes caught nothing but air.  We jousted in the skies for several minutes before it slammed to a stop twenty metres away and stood on nothing to glare at me.

The left side was human, the right was an amalgamation of barnacles, mussels, and weird tube-shell things that I couldn’t name.  They were melded together to form the semblance of human limbs and body parts, grotesque and jagged.

The human side was the problem.  I knew that empty eyesocket and massive frame.

“Big Kenny.  Nice to see you again.  Pretty sure I killed you already?”
