After the fall of Heralin, the lich with the flaming blue eyes who’d tried and failed to hide away within their bunker, the rapid pace that the coalition was moving at slowed down a good bit. Despite managing to take out two more liches in no time at all, they hadn’t gotten away completely unscathed.

While Asher had sustained minor soul damage and the team of mercenaries from the Lambit Republic had been brutally crushed to death while going up against the undead abomination, the real setback had been Lanra almost killing herself saving the rest of them from that magical explosion. She and Richard were in charge of helping everyone move around the continent at such a rapid pace, and with her practically comatose and Richard devoting all his time and tritessence to caring for her, that meant the rest of the coalition wasn’t going anywhere anytime soon. Luckily, after leaving behind an Eternal Mark so that they could return and finish off the hundreds of undead that were still moving around, Asher was able to use Recall to at least get everyone back to the Noala Kingdom.

Much to the surprise of a certain representative from the Lambit Republic.

Asher stared at Hounth’s startled expression as the man walked into the meeting room the following day for the mission debriefing. While Lanra was absent, Groln, Amanda, Donvath, and Asher were already sitting around the table waiting for him. Hounth managed to smooth over his expression into his usual passive mask soon enough, but it seemed Asher wasn’t the only one who had noticed.

“Why, Hounth, what’s the matter?” Donvath asked, the High Prince giving the representative a look absolutely dripping with concern. “Did you sleep alright? Was the bed comfy enough? Did your assassin manage to get word to you that she’d succeeded in her hit on Asher here?”

To Hounth’s credit, the man didn’t so much as pale or even flinch at the accusation. Narrowing his eyes, Hounth crossed his arms, standing firm against the four heavy stares levied against him. “It sounds like you are trying to accuse me of something. By all means, I would love to hear it.”

“And I would love to hear why you idiots thought you could possibly get away with any of your usual corrupt plans while in the heart of my kingdom,” Donvath said, sighing as he shook his head and looked toward Asher. “Seriously, seeing as Lanra is not here, I suppose I’ll have to play her role for her as the resident Lambit Republic hater. Asher, care to tell us about what happened to you last night?”

“An assassin tried to kill me in the street while I was out walking with Samantha,” Asher admitted, getting a surprised look from Amanda and a curious one from Groln. “They ended up getting away before we got anything from them, but I have a feeling Donvath didn’t have any issues tracking them down.”

“Unlike those fancy Shadow Squads, regular assassins leave so many trails for my truthseekers to follow it’s not even funny,” Donvath grinned. “And nobody is better at pulling the truth from people who refuse to talk than they are.”

“You are claiming that this supposed assassin framed me during your interrogation?” Hounth asked, still looking entirely unbothered by their story. “How convenient for you that your own personal guard would say whatever it is you wanted to hear. If that is all the evidence you have, I am looking forward to how the Lambit Republic will react when you attempt to imprison me.”

“See, that’s the thing,” Donvath said, his grin vanishing as he stared hard at Hounth. “My people tracked her down, only to discover she’d already been murdered in kind, and her body incinerated to the point she couldn’t be resurrected even if we wanted to spare the funds to do so.”

“How unfortunate,” Hounth said coldly. “In that case, what is the point in continuing to humor this hypothetical? Last I checked we had liches we were attempting to hunt down.”

“Do we? You flaked on accompanying us at the last minute yesterday,” Groln snorted, the demonic man raising an eyebrow as if challenging Hounth to disagree. “And your ‘elite mercenaries’ died almost instantly.”

“As I told you yesterday, there was a logistical issue with our soldiers that needed to be addressed,” Hounth countered. “As to the mercenaries, it is a shame, but you know how they are. They should not have oversold themselves. They have no one to blame but themselves. We spent good shards on their assistance.”

Throughout all this back and forth, Asher watched Hounth like a hawk, trusting in Secret Seeker to help pick out any sort of tell as the man spoke. Honest Ears wasn’t giving him anything at all, but that was to be expected. Seeing as Identify and Judgement were both being countered, it was clear the man either had resistances, artifacts, or skills at play keeping such skills from working on him. Asher was pretty confident in assuming the man was guilty, but he preferred to be one-hundred-percent certain before he killed anyone.

It was small, but as accusations continued to be levied and argued against Hounth, Asher began to pick up on the slightest of wrinkles around the representative’s eyes every so often, as if he were starting to frown before stopping himself before the motion could even begin.

It was far from any sort of confession, but it was certainly a start. Not to mention his efforts didn’t really matter in the end.

Not when Donvath finally revealed he’d already held the smoking gun all this time.

“This is absolute absurdity, and I will not stand for such hypotheticals or besmirching of my name any longer,” Hounth declared, surprising Asher by taking his seat rather than storming out of the room. “There are more important matters at play here than what was clearly a failed assassination. Can we start the meeting?”

“You see that everyone?” Donvath asked, gesturing to Hounth as he looked around the table. “That right there is the pristine image of work ethic. The man was just accused to his face of plotting an assassination in another kingdom, and he’s able to shrug all that off and get back to work. His attitude is downright commendable I’d say, wouldn’t you?”

“I certainly would have left by now if I were in his shoes,” Groln grunted. “Or more likely started throwing punches.”

“Then I suppose it is a good thing we are not all tainted by demonkind, isn’t it?” Hounth said, glaring at the grinning slayer. “Now, regarding the missions from yesterday. How—”

“Apologies, hold that thought for just a moment,” Donvath said, holding up a glowing sending stone to his ear. Asher couldn’t make out the voice on the other end, but he had no problem hearing Donvath as the High Prince began nodding and raised his voice. “What’s that Christian? An assassin infiltrated the palace?! And tried to break into Richard’s suite where Lanra is being treated?! Just now, right when I told you they would, while Hounth conveniently has the perfect alibi of meeting with none other than myself?! My word, what an astonishing development. Yes, excellent job dispatching them without killing them. I’ve got a number of questions I intend to ask them.”

For the first time, Hounth’s face paled slightly, and a bead of sweat began dripping down his face as his eyes darted to the door. As he did so, Donvath just sighed and tucked his sending stone away.

“I wouldn’t recommend it. Your skill to transform into some sort of misty cloud is impressive, and it does prevent Groln or Amanda from touching you, but Asher still cuts you down before you make it halfway to the door. He’s not called The Reaper for nothing. I don’t really care about you dying for the record, but I sort of gave him this whole speech about not killing leaders of countries, and it would be a little embarrassing if he was forced to do so anyway right in front of me after I wasted all that time. Even if you are only like one-sixteenth of the Lambit Republic’s leader, I suppose that still counts.”

Hounth took a deep breath, collecting himself as he dabbed at the sweat on his brow. “...Regardless of what you think you know or chose to claim, this will still cause an international incident if you pursue it further. The Lambit Republic volunteered to join up with the coalition in order to wipe out the Council of Death when the vast majority of the continent was too cowardly to do so. This will look like the Noala Kingdom attempting to make a play for power.”

“Ahh… So that is why your people voted for you specifically to come here, despite you not wanting any part of the coalition," Donvath said, noting the confused look on Asher’s face as he did so. “Hounth here is the representative for the portion of the Lambit Republic that is directly adjacent to the Noala Kingdom. Not the entire border, but about sixty-five percent of it. If I were to accuse him of foul play, the Lambit Republic could potentially spin it as me making an excuse to invade their land and take some of its territory for ourselves. Despite the fact that I would never do such a thing.”

“Bah. Politics,” Groln grunted, looking thoroughly done with the conversation already. “I came here to help kill liches. I did not sign up for any of this crap.”

“We’ll get back to the important stuff in a moment, I promise,” Donvath said, shaking his head as he gave Hounth one last look of disbelief. “What I don’t understand is why you ever imagined you’d be able to pull one over on me, in my own kingdom? I don’t exactly go around shining my own shards all that often, but I’m the realms-blasted Immortal. I can control time! Even if you were some sort of genius, which you’re clearly not, I could just undo whatever it is you managed to accomplish! It doesn’t make any sense!”

By this point, Hounth seemed to have finished collecting himself and was back to his impassive mask free of emotion as he returned Donvath’s stare with one of his own.

“If you are planning to toss me in a dungeon or make any formal accusations, I would recommend you get on with it. The Lambit Republic will of course move forward with denouncing you should you decide to do so, but I suppose that is your choice.”

“I think your masterwork plan has a single, teeny-tiny hole in it,” Donvath countered. “I can’t toss you in prison or make any formal accusations, but that’s fine. I wouldn’t want to do so anyway. You may have been a conniving snake, but I would never besmirch the name of a fallen hero. It’s a shame you fell so quickly after insisting we bring you to the battlefield so you could personally try to find the remains of those mercenaries who fought and died for you. Though I suppose that’s just the type of man you are.”

As Donvath spoke, Hounth’s mask was shattered a second time, the man actually recoiling in his chair when he realized the High Prince wasn’t joking. Before Hounth could do so much as move a muscle, Donvath nodded toward Asher, who tossed up a Spatial Lock to trap all five of them together. Immediately, Hounth’s body exploded into a powerful wave of mist which pressed up against Spatial Lock’s near-invisible barrier but failed to escape. As he desperately tried to flee, Asher focused on his distant mark and triggered Recall, bringing all five of them directly into the center of the fallen abomination as they appeared atop the solid bunker.

“Well, that’s gross,” Donvath said, grimacing at the thousands of corpses piled up all around them. “Reminds me why I rarely choose to leave the palace. Asher, if he doesn’t turn back into himself, I’d recommend stabbing the densest part of the cloud. With your ability, it’ll probably kill him quickly enough.”

“Please,” Hounth said, his misty form coalescing back into flesh and blood as he dropped to his knees and stared at them in desperation. “None of this was my idea! I was just carrying out the plan!”

“You could have stepped back from your position and warned the Noala Kingdom rather than try to help the Council of Death,” Amanda said, looking at the pleading man in disgust. “I’d kill you myself if I didn’t want to sully my blade.”

“The Hojon Empire will be saddened to hear that you fell in battle,” Groln said, still having yet to even uncross his arms. “Better to have the death of a hero, than the life of a traitor."

“There’s a chance he might escape the undead using his mist form," Donvath pointed out, tapping at his midsection and raising an eyebrow toward Asher. “If you would?”

Asher stared at the man who had tried to pioneer not only his death and Lanra’s, but hinder their attempts at taking down the Council of Death so as to make the Noala Kingdom suffer. Beyond the same liches he was currently hunting down, he was startled to realize such an act was probably the most evil thing he’d ever personally witnessed. Yet despite all that, the man somehow had the gall to look pleadingly at him, his hands clasped together as he stared hopefully up at Asher. As if hundreds of thousands of people, if not millions, wouldn’t have potentially suffered had he been successful.

Shaking his head, Asher pulled the same bolt that had recently been shot through his head out of his rift and, with a quick tunnel of compressed space, shoved it straight into the man’s abdomen. It wouldn’t have been a fatal wound inflicted by anyone else, but with Sever the Thread at play, it wouldn’t be long before the man bled out.

“Here,” Asher said, feeling nothing but cold anger as he envisioned what might have been had the man succeeded. “I figured you’d want this back.”

Gasping in pain, the man exploded into mist once more, the small cloud zipping away and over the fields of stitched-together undead as they raised their hands and dragged fingers through the mist to no avail. But even if they couldn’t touch him, the damage was already done.

The representative from the Lambit Republic would be dead within the hour.
