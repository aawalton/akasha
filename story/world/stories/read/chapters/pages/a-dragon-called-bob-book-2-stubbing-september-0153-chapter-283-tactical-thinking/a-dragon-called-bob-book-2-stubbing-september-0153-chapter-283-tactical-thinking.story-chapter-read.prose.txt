This pocket dimension was massive.  Acres of what I assumed were literal bales of reefer, fat stacks of shinies, I wasn’t sure what to do.

Even allowing for the size of their pipes and lung capacity, there was enough bush in here to fund a mid-sized country for a year if it went on the open market.  It would take a long time to burn, plenty of time for them to put out most of the fires and save the majority of their… crop.  A better solution was required.

Setting aside the canuggeting, I like the giants.  The hippy vibe was annoying as all hell, but the nudity kind of balanced that out for me personally.  The endless drum circles were definitely a negative.  So what to do?

My plan to piss them off and have them chasing after me, leading them across half a continent to where the fighting was going on had seemed sound.  Everyone knows if you anger a giant, they’ll chase you down.  But if I didn’t run off with the gear, would they even chase me?  Hippy pacifist douchegiants might be too chill to bother.  I needed more motivation.

Fee fi fo gimme back my ganja.  It made perfect sense.  The question was whether or not my Possum Pouch evolution would let me hold everything.  The gold and jewels were non-negotiable.  I was a dragon; if I was going to burn this bridge, I was going to get paid.  But rather than destroying the other stuff, I could take it with me and use it as bait.  That way, I wouldn’t have to stay floating in the Changer’s back lines, fighting for my life in order to keep the angry giants interested.

A clever dragon I am, a clever dragon Bob is.  Slipping into third person and present tense to describe myself caused a brief moment of panic; it was never a good sign for one's mental health, but I shoved it aside and started loading up on pot.  Lots and lots of pot.

Took me a few minutes, maybe fifteen?  But I could fly, and the place was big enough to zoom above the stock and trail a tail low enough to snatch everything into my pocket dimension.  Turns out possums have really big pouches.  I managed to fit everything in.

Thieving felt dirty, but some part of me was running interference in my head to justify it, most likely my Greed.  I needed the giants to save the bloody world.  Annoying them into doing it wasn’t my first choice, but it looked like the only thing that would get them off their arses and engaged in the world below.

This storage didn’t have any alarms, as evidenced by the lack of enormous hands currently reaching in to try and catch me, so some way to present my “crime” to the big people was necessary.  Something flashy would likely do the trick, but not getting stomped on like a scaly insect was going to be the tricky part.

After having filled my seemingly endless storage space with loot and enough green to get a major city wasted, I snuck back out and turned to quietly close the door.  They’d know soon enough, but I wasn’t born in a barn, and a civilised dragon should always close doors behind him.

“And what do we have here, then?”  rumbled Gnarly Wave, both heads booming at once.  My first thought was cheese it.

“I like you guys.  But I need your help, and you won’t do it.  So I’m being a canugget.”

“A canugget you say?”  He drew heavily on both pipes.  “You remember the story of Jack?”

“I remember how he chopped your stalk and off’ed one of you.”  Moving to the side, I got clear of the secret door and prepared to make a run for it.  “That isn’t going to happen.  I need you to fight, not die.”

“Fighting means dying, for someone.  Usually not us,” Gnarly said.

“Very rarely us,” Wave added.

“But some kid with zero business acumen managed to do it.  I won’t hurt the stalk, I guarantee you’ll reach the ground, but if you want your dope back, you’ll have to come get it,” I snarled, inching back towards the cloud-whirlpool that would let me escape this place.

“Or we could just stomp you like a bug, little dragon.”

“I’m not little!  I’m proportionate!  Kill me and you’ll never get access to my pocket dimension.  Your shit will be gone.  Got you by the canugget hairs, don’t I?”  A very clever dragon I am.

“We can extract the loot from your corpse,” Wave said.

“Hey, what’s going on man?  You said you’d get more canuggeting dope and we could step the party up.  The lobsters are… what the fuck is going on here?” Chef-bro asked, appearing next to Gnarly Wave and looming over myself, the hill, and pretty much everything that wasn’t the size of a modest skyscraper.  “Bob?”

“Sorry dude.  A dragon’s gotta do what a dragon’s gotta do.  We need you.  Everyone down below needs you.”

“But it’s all muddy down there, lizard-buddy,” Chef-bro complained.  “We’ve got booze and reefer and big old naked boobies bouncing around up here.  Hear that bongo solo?  That’s Blossom.  Your dwarf friend is living the dream on a trampoline!”

“I’m sorry.  I can’t let you sit this one out.  If you want everything in that lock box back, you’re going to have to come and get it.”

Vertical take-offs aren’t as easy as you might think.  Despite my sinuous form and vast wing span, I was heavy.  All those scales and claws really tilted the scales in the wrong direction for my BMI.  This time I managed it, sort of.

A spin and a lunge away from my now antagonistic giants, combined with a powerful downbeat of my wings, had me mostly airborne before they could do anything.

“Non Volantes!” Gnarly called, and I slammed back into the ground.  Dammit.  I still had four legs, momentum and terror on my side, so my acceleration wasn’t far off what I could manage in the air.

A shadow descended from above, five long finger-shaped occlusions robbing the world of light ahead of me.  I sent a blast of flame back over my shoulder, and Chef-bro hissed at the heat.

“Canugget!  We were mates!” he complained.

“We still are, dude!  I want to try some of that seafood gumbo next time I go fishing for you!”  A clod of earth the size of a house landed just off to my right, and I began to serpentine.  I was built for it, and took advantage of my snakey body to dodge and weave as lumps of dirt and stones the size of my body thudded down around me.

“He’s nicked the ganj!” Wave bellowed.

For the first time since I came to this place.  The bongos stopped.  No drum circle, no incessant, pounding rhythm in the back of my mind.  It had become like engine noise.  You’re aware of it on some level, but consciously you just phase it out.  Only when it stopped did it become truly noticeable again.

A new type of thudding replaced it.  The sound of multiton footsteps slamming into the ground, making the grass shake and me bounce around like a pea on Pantera’s snare drum.  I had physics on my side.  Despite not being able to fly, my wings and tails made balancing a cinch.  With four feet, I had a low centre of gravity and plenty of contact points with the suddenly quaking ground.

The swirl of clouds around the stalk appeared in the distance, and hope joined terror in giving me a profound sense of urgency.

As a general rule, I was the largest being about.  In this instance, I was the equivalent of a chihuahua with a herd of pissed-off normal-sized humans after it.  Role reversal, outside of very specific celebratory evenings spent with Esme, was not a major part of my new life.  This made me feel even more out of sorts, and all of my mental menagerie were in agreement that getting the canugget out of here was the best course of action.

My claws bit into the dirt, throwing much smaller clods back behind me as I powered towards what I hoped would be safety.  Zig-zagging hard, I managed to avoid the various projectiles

“Catch me if you can!” I crowed as I dove down through the doughnut of clouds.  Bits of rock fell past me, but none of them was too close.  It was at this point that I realised I’d made a terrible mistake.  My wings beat furiously but generated bugger all lift.

Fucking mages and fucking magic.  As great as it was when I used it, when other people had access to reality-breaking bullshit, it was annoying at best and “plunging to your death even though you can normally fly” at worst.

Gravity is a dick.  Every woman of a certain age learns to curse it as things sag, but as a flying creature, I’d come to think of it as almost a friend.  It kept the ant-like enemies all the way down on the ground while I cavorted through the skies, raining fire and dodging their puny arrows.

Now I was a giant scaly rock plummeting to my doom, useless wings flailing but catching no air, and cursing the crettin, both heads and all.  The option to dump all the dope out of my stash and let it create a comfy bed for me to land on ran through my mind, but the wind speeds at this altitude would spread it all over the place.  The devil goats down below would have a great week as they nommed their way through the giant's stash, but in terms of splat prevention, it would be ineffective.

Unlike Winnie the Pooh, I had no thinking cap, and my forelimbs were currently flailing around as they tried to figure out how to stand on a gas.  This would require a new level of Bob-ish craftiness.

“Me Fuga!” I bellowed against the wind.  Nothing, nada, zip.  My magic to fly didn’t cancel out the crettin’s magic to stop me flying.  This seemed somewhat unfair as the ground headed towards me, intending a very terminal and messy hug.

I pulled a portal stone from storage at the same moment as I returned to my mammal suit.  Naturally, I dropped it as my paw changed size and clawiness.  As soon as I was skydiving as a human without a parachute, I pulled out another stone and decided to experiment with timing.

What goes up must come down; this is a well-established rule.  Gravity is a cruel mistress, as we have established.  But I’d once played a trick on that asshole Kenny.  His terminal velocity had been transferred into horizontal motion when I shifted one of the portals.  I did only get half of him, which was my bad, but the half that came out of the new location went whizzing away into the sky despite having been travelling down rather quickly.

A blue oval opened beneath me, and I vanished into it, only to find myself rising upwards as I emerged from the output portal next to it.  As my momentum bled away, gravity going from foe to friend in this instance, I reached a pinnacle, floating in the air as angry naked giants began to swarm down the Bube Stengel.

I reset the portal, the input directly below me and the output safely on ground level.  The stone and snow hit my bare feet, and I flipped the bird at the now distant giants.  Time to Forrest Gump this shit and run across a continent.
