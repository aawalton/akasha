Lira returned to the Academy with Lyth the next morning, the carriage ride spent in a discussion about Lord Taren's death that turned into a discussion about the dangers of drink.

"You see, Your Highness," smiled Lira, "ask any noble youth and they will tell you that drinking is fun, that it's a social lubricant, and that any party needs an open bar. Otherwise, would it even be called a party?"

"So you are saying that drinking is an unavoidable social convention."

Lira blinked at the complex words he was using, though she shouldn't have been surprised. Lyth was turning out to be quite the little scholar.

"More or less," Lira nodded, "though I wouldn't say unavoidable. The most important thing to understand when handling alcohol is moderation. Drinking a glass of wine or two at a social event is one thing, but drinking alone until you collapse or black out is a completely different matter."

"I don't get it," said Lyth, frowning. "Why would you want to do something that makes you lose control to the point of urinating yourself, and then do it again the next day until it gets you killed?"

"That's the danger of addiction," Lira sighed. "Some people are more susceptible to addiction than others, whether it's drinking or gambling. It's like a sickness of the mind."

"They can't help themselves?" Lyth asked with a frown.

"It's more complicated," Lira grimaced. "It doesn't completely absolve them of wrongdoing; it just explains it. When it comes to their weakness, those people are unable to moderate, so for them, complete abstinence is required. They should avoid their weakness the same way they would avoid poison."

"Does that mean I shouldn't drink when I grow older?" asked Lyth.

"If drinking is a weakness for you, then yes. If not, you can engage in moderation. Gambling, on the other hand, I would avoid on principle."

"Why is that?"

"For that, you need to understand probabilities."

It was around then that they reached the Royal Academy. They were greeted once again by Vice-Dean Mura, who bowed them through the doors and led them to the library before leaving them to their own devices, with a promise to check in later. Grandpa Waxy didn't show up to meet them this time, which was a shame. Lira would have loved to talk with the old man for a bit.

Lira left Lyth to familiarize himself with the concept of probability from books, while she busied herself with book divination, looking for interesting topics, strange theories, and obscure knowledge that could be turned into interesting spells. She also figured she might as well find more contemporary authors that might be worth adding to the castle library's collection. Judging by her current predicament, she might not be able to keep working there any longer, but that didn't mean she wouldn't want to help whoever took over after her to keep the library running. If anything, she was at least doing it for Prince Lyth's sake.

This time, she didn't bother with the Mana Obfuscation and Mana Stabilization enchantments. The library itself was magical enough that absolute cleanliness would be more conspicuous than short flashes of disturbance, especially if she cast inside the thin layer of disturbance already caused by the sound-dampening field.

"Ladar. Linasara. Oshkan. Cognar. Turat."

Her divination would point her toward interesting books; she would use the reference in the catalogue to get its name and ask one of the aides to bring it to her. Then she would head back to the sound-dampening field and absorb it, or skim-read it, depending on how recently she had cast the taxing Book absorption spell.

After two hours or so of this, Vice-Dean Mura returned, this time with a man at his side Lira hadn't met before. He was middle-aged, dressed in the same blue and silver as the rest of the Academy staff, though his skin carried something she had seen only a handful of times in the capital, bands of darker pigment running in strip-like patterns against a lighter base, like something you would see on a tiger.

Magari blood, she assumed. She had seen it only a handful of times, on merchants from the eastern trade routes, and on some of the dockside urchins, children of prostitutes with foreign fathers.

"Your Highness, Miss Lira." Mura gestured to the man beside him. "This is Master Yevi. He teaches Magari to our incoming students."

Yevi bowed, a small, precise motion. "Vice-Dean Mura tells me you've been teaching the prince yourself, Miss Lira."

"A little," Lira said, and then, testing the water, switched into Magari. "Only the basics, Master Yevi. I am better at reading it than speaking it, I'm afraid."

Her accent came out rougher than she wanted; she was fairly sure she mangled the [ɯ] vowel, pronouncing it closer to an eu sound, her mouth badly unpracticed with the language.

Yevi's eyebrows rose, pleased to hear a foreigner speaking his language more or less fluently. He answered her in the same tongue. "Your grammar is impeccable, Miss. Where did you learn Magari?"

"Books, mostly," Lira admitted, still in Magari.

"You got this fluent using only books?" he said, impressed.

Lira just shrugged. Her father had helped with the foundation, but it was true that she had learned the rest from books alone.

Master Yevi then turned his attention to Lyth. "And you, Your Highness? Has your teacher taught you anything yet?"

Lyth straightened at once, visibly pleased to be asked. He rattled off a careful string of words, a greeting, his own name, a clumsy but earnest compliment about the library, each one pronounced carefully, still needing to sound out every word individually in his mind before using it.

Yevi clapped once, delighted. "Very good. Very good indeed. Your accent will need work, but everyone does at first."

They spent the next hour pleasantly chatting about the Magari language, about the difference between living here and in the Magari Republic, about some of the food and customs, all of it switching back and forth between Haltarian and Magari so Lyth could keep up. The young prince had his usual endless well of curiosity, and thankfully, Master Yevi was amiable enough to indulge him.

Lira couldn't help but feel slightly relieved. After a stressful few days, she was glad to be able to kick back and relax with books and good company for once. It didn't solve her Aric ultimatum, nor did it help her deal with Kelroth's imminent arrival, but she needed it anyway.

I should visit Maren and Pallan next.
