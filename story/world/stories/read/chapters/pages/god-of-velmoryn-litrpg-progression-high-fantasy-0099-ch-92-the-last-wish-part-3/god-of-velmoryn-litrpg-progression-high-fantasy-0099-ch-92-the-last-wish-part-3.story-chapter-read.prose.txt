I ignored the notifications that kept appearing and focused on the sphere instead.

I searched for a connection, even the faintest one, but found nothing. Unlike the stars in my realm, which I could instinctively feel anytime I wished, the white sphere felt alien. Foreign.

Guidance.

[Core of the God of Beggars!]

When a deity possessing a single Authority perishes, the god who claims their essence may assimilate that Authority, bestow it upon another divine being, or destroy it.

You may assimilate the Authority: God of Beggars.

None of your avatars currently possess sufficient divine presence to ascend as a god.

If the core of the fallen deity remains within your realm, it may gradually recover by drawing upon your divinity and eventually awaken.

You may establish communication with the God of Beggars by channeling divinity into the core. Caution is recommended. If excessive divinity is provided, the deity may recover enough strength to escape, should they prove powerful enough.

Despite what Guidance had told me, I could feel that the core was not feeding on my divinity. If anything, the faint green shimmer still surrounding it was acting as a barrier, preventing my divinity from touching it.

Still, I was not sure I wanted to risk removing that barrier and channeling my power into the core by my own will.

Even if the strength the God of Beggars had demonstrated felt disappointing, almost weak, the amount of Divinity Points I had obtained after his death proved that this deity had been at least one Rank higher than me. What if this had been the plan from the beginning? What if the goal was to let the God of Beggars enter my realm and then try to seize control from within?

If I had a unique power that allowed me to split myself and recover, who knew what kind of abilities other gods possessed? Perhaps they had hidden their unique powers and prevented them from reflecting in their Authorities, just as my God of Velmoryn domain was completely detached from Astral Rebirth.

Looking at the white sphere, I found myself stuck on one question - what kind of unique power had he possessed?

Was it smart to risk awakening him?

Yet letting go of the God of Beggars’ core and wasting this chance to extract information from a deity far more experienced than myself felt even more foolish than taking that risk.

So although I reminded myself to stay alert and prioritize safety above all else, I still decided to communicate with him.

A wisp of crimson energy formed beside the sphere and brushed against the weak green light surrounding it. During the fight with Aurelion, when I had felt this divine energy, I had thought its Radiance was lower than mine. But I couldn’t be sure. Perhaps the divinity he had used then had simply been of lower quality. Perhaps it had been part of his plan…

But now that he had lost and fallen into such a sorry state, I could make sure.

As my crimson touched the green, I felt the two forces collide, and a smile spread across my face.

Once again, my divinity was stronger.

Far stronger.

Even more overwhelming than when I had subdued the Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility.

It should be safe, but let’s make absolutely sure.

A wave of crimson surged toward the sphere, surrounding it like a cocoon. I made certain none of it touched the core itself, letting it act only as a barrier that sealed the God of Beggars, or whatever remained of him, inside.

Only then did I create another wisp and channel it into the sphere.

“You are awfully cautious for someone in your position,” I heard his voice.

Unlike when he had spoken in the mortal realm, his voice now sounded pleasant. Well, compared to before.

“Are you not going to say anything?” he asked. “Worry not. I have no intention of recovering and becoming a pawn again. Death is more merciful than spending decades in shackles that seal away free will.”

“Pawn?” I asked. “What do you mean?”

“Did you truly believe I would lose to an angel who cannot channel divinity? That I am so weak I could unleash no more than…” He stopped. “It does not matter. Nothing matters. I am a victim of my own mistakes, and I accept that.”

He paused.

I felt some of my divine energy being drawn into the core, but almost immediately, the sphere shimmered and expelled green divinity.

[Warning: You’ve gained 0.1 Divinity Points!]

Is he really trying to die? But why? What can be worse than death?

I was genuinely confused.

To me, as long as I was alive, any situation could be turned around. I saw no glory, honor, or freedom in death. Death was the end, a dot at the end of a sentence that left no room for continuation.

Well, in my case, I had truly obtained continuation and even greater freedom after death, but would the same happen if I perished as a god?

“Are you saying you lost because you wanted to die?”

“No. I lost because I was not allowed to use my power. Because my death benefited your enemy,” the God of Beggars said. “That is of no importance to you. I chose to speak with you only because I need something from you. Something you will be delighted to grant me.”

He paused again, likely expecting me to ask.

“What do you want?”

“Revenge,” he hissed. “Swear to me that you will kill the God of Justice, and I will tell you what his plan is.”

[Divine Contract]

The God of Beggars wishes to form a divine contract.

If either party violates the contract, they shall die.

Note: Because this contract compels a deity into action and serves primarily as an offensive measure, the Pantheon will be alerted.

“I cannot make such an oath,” I said without even a second thought. “Not until I know whether I can even kill him. I cannot promise something without knowing if it is possible. Besides, you already know he is my enemy. I do not need extra motivation to win this war.”

“It is not your victory I seek,” the God of Beggars hissed. “I want his death. His demise. I want him to suffer the way I did…”

His voice weakened and cut off.

For a moment, I thought he had died, but I could still feel his presence.

“I do not have much time,” he continued, his voice faint. “I will tell you what I know. At least this way, my death will not be for nothing.”

I waited as he paused, probably to gather either his thoughts or enough strength to continue speaking.

I struggled to believe him. His desire to help me, and more than that, his desire to die, went against everything I believed in. Even if he claimed he was doing it only to take revenge on Noah, I still could not bring myself to trust his words.

But information was always welcome.

So I listened.

“Only decades after awakening this cursed domain did I realize it was more of a hindrance than a boon to my power. Regardless of how much divinity I gained, and I gained plenty, no one begs for help and salvation more than beggars, after all…” he snorted. “But the weak have weak souls, and I failed to improve my Radiance. Without it, I could never ascend. I could never gain greater strength. I was the weakest god of humanity, and in time, I became desperate.”

He paused again as his voice grew weaker. This time, even the white sphere dimmed, looking less like pure snow and more like something made from dirty slush.

It took the God of Beggars a moment to regain enough strength to continue.

“It was already too late when I learned that once you assume an Authority, you cannot give it up willingly. Your domain, the very thing meant to become your source of power, becomes your cage. And to escape that prison, I chose a terrible path, but the only one left to me. I made a divine contract with the God of Justice.”

He paused, and the green shimmer around the sphere trembled faintly.

“He never revealed how he intended to help me, but he definitely has the means. Of that, I am certain. Otherwise, the divine contract would have been null. In exchange, I was supposed to help him one time as the God of Beggars. But he never specified when he would help me, and he managed to turn that one time of aid into eternal slavery.”

What the fuck?!

I became even more suspicious.

Even if the God of Beggars was only giving me a summary, the way he phrased that “one time help” was already enough for me to see how it could be twisted.

Was he lying? Or had he truly fallen into such an obvious trap?

“Today,” the God of Beggars continued. “He gave me his final command. He ordered me to descend and die. I was supposed to fight you without using most of my powers. I was supposed to feed you my divinity.”

“But why? That would only make me stronger. Why would…”

“Because of your Rank,” he said.

Although it did not come through his tone, I could feel the snort behind his words.

“Now that you have fulfilled the conditions, you will ascend. And once that happens, you will fall into slumber. How long was it last time? A decade? Two?”

“Six months,” I replied, dread settling over me.

“Six months…” he muttered, venom seeping into his weakening voice. “High Radiance… birthright of chosen ones…”

The sphere dimmed further.

“When you wake up, your believers will be gone. Your angel will be dead. But you will be stronger. Angrier. Use that power. Use that hatred…”

His voice trailed off, and the sphere turned completely dim.

A new notification flashed before me, but I did not even look at it.

I had bigger problems.

Only after the God of Beggars told me I would ascend did I notice the golden window already hovering beside me.

[You may raise your Rank…]
