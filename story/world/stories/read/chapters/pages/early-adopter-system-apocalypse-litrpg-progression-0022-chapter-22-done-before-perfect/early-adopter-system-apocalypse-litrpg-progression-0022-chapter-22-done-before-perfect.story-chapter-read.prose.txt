Level 5. No wonder she’d jumped a Level, she thought. She remembered fighting the roidcoons.

That first level-up had come after she’d killed the second roidcoon. The first had been Lvl. 2, and the second Lvl. 1. Then once she was Lvl. 2, not even killing two Lvl. 2s had yielded her another level. Only after she’d killed the level 3 had she seen her own Level increase.

This pattern suggested diminishing returns when killing things at or below one’s own Level. That, or increased returns when killing something above it. She’d also remembered Levelling up when she’d solved the shadow bat mystery, suggesting combat wasn’t the only way to gain experience.

For now, she would proceed under the theory that facing tougher opponents, and more difficult challenges, yielded more recognition – EXP – from the System.

More than her Level though, was her Mana. Eight. She had eight Mana. Something told her that had as much to do with Mana Sense as it did Meditation. She made a note to meditate before bed, and during moments of leisure. As well as to constantly exercise and test her mana and psionic senses.

Before any of that, though, Scarlet needed to allocate her free Stats. She didn’t want what had happened to Willpower to happen again if it wasn’t entirely unavoidable.

She had 21 FS, which was just enough to bring most of her Stats up to fifteen. However, she didn’t feel that was the most optimal distribution. While some of her Stats had a lot more room for growth others had a faster growth rate.

Her Stats did not exist in a vacuum. Much as she'd often wished it were so, her mind was not independent of the rest of her, and she could not simply divorce herself from her emotions either. If she was going to be strategic about getting stronger, becoming more powerful, she needed to think of every step of her progression as pieces of a connected whole; the various parts of her working in tandem to make up who she was.

A higher WIL would help her mental recovery more than something like INT. That recovery might then boost her VIT. Likewise, her INT could inform her perception when she encountered something new.

She was aware she had been leaning towards a higher 'Perception' and 'Intelligence' build to help her leverage her strengths. However, she realised she needed to be strong overall. It was the only way to truly maximise her talents.

Scarlet would not advance half-heartedly. While her body may never be on par with her mind, she would make well sure it was strong enough to keep up.

There must be an optimal way to allocate these. Only she didn’t have access to it. Did she? She tried querying the Guide.

[Optimal Statistic allocation is different for each individual and their unique paths.]

Okay, but considering there were other ‘worlds’ in this ‘System-Network’, there had to be common or even uncommon wisdom about individual optimisation.

The Guide did not respond.

She supposed that meant she was on her own until she had a better source of information.

For now, instead of going for a flat distribution, she figured it would be best to see if she couldn’t give all the Stats the same room to grow. Her lowest Stats, Vitality and Strength were both at five. However, because of their lower starting point they had comparatively more overhead before they hit their ceiling. STR had eight more Stat points while VIT had six. MNA on the other hand had only two while WIL had reached its cap.

Her goal then, was to make sure all her Stats had at least three points to grow into before she began thinking about where she should focus the rest of the points.

Strength, Vitality, and Perception were at or above three, so she went in and added one point to Mana, three to Willpower, one to Intelligence, and one to Dexterity, leaving her with 15 FS left to allocate.

Something told her that gaining Stats was only going to get more difficult the higher they got, however while they were relatively low, and until she was able to access a better source of information, she was going to drop her remaining FS into the Stats that maximised her advantages.

Twelve hours. It took roughly twelve hours for the average person to regain consciousness. That meant that most people had only woken up around the time she was heading to sleep.
