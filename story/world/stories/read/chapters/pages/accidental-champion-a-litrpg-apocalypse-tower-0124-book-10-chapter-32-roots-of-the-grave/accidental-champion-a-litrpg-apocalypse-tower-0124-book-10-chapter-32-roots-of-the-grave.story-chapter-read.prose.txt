Xavier raised an eyebrow. The description was not quite what he’d expected. Grown from the seed of a dying planet? He looked at the beast anew. Something told him that if this beast were out in the open, it would have grown far larger.

It’s only this “small” because of the constraints of the tunnel.

He’d noticed the Multiversal Standard as well. Class C7. The Gorgotlin had been Class C10, which meant this thing was significantly stronger than they’d been. It hadn’t attacked him yet—and he was sure it wouldn’t until he took another step.

He tilted his head to the side and asked the Personal System to delve deeper into the spells. He wasn’t about to go into this fight unprepared. As the Personal System described each of the spells, Xavier slowly nodded his head, getting a feeling for how the fight would go. Lava Death sounded like a ridiculous name for a spell—then he heard the description, which gave him a healthy amount of wariness toward it.

The other two spells were also rather powerful, but he didn’t imagine having much trouble with them. This thing was stronger than the last enemies he’d faced here, but it wasn’t as powerful as him, and he was eager to test his new abilities in combat. The instinctive way he was able to shift and change how he cycled, and how he’d been able to change that Heavy Telekinesis spell pattern so easily…

He was ready for this fight.

Xavier stepped forward, walking at a leisurely pace. As he did, a thousand possible spell patterns entered his mind, so many ideas forming all at once. Though the spell patterns didn’t feel like spell patterns—not exactly. With his new instinct for how energy worked and flowed, he understood, on a deeper level, how all those runes manipulated energy. These spell patterns didn’t merely come from the vast array of knowledge he’d gathered over the years, they came from pure instinct. They gave him the ability to shape reality to his will, something he’d been striving for all this time. Of course, he’d been able to shape reality to his will since the very beginning—but this was more.

And the more he developed his ability, the better it would become.

A bone spear grew from his arm. The spell pattern he usually used for this was already something he’d altered in the past, yet as he’d manifested it this time it had changed in subtle ways. And the pattern hadn’t merely changed, the way the energy flowed through that pattern changed as well. He pushed it this way and that, made it fill the runes not all at once but in a specific pattern, until every single one lit up and the spell was ready to cast.

And this all happened in… an instant.

It wasn’t something he had to think about, wasn’t something he had to agonise over. He didn’t need a separate part of his mind to work on the problem. He could just do it.

The bone spear was infused with energy as it became fully formed, and it glowed a fierce red. His fingers wrapped around it. He could feel that energy like electricity on his palm, and he knew it was the most powerful weapon he’d ever wielded.

Holding that spear, he couldn’t help but think of Bones, his soul bound weapon. The weapon was still with him—his soul was tethered to it, after all, and that was a bond he wasn’t about to break—but he’d discarded it because it couldn’t keep up with him.

Now that he was learning so much more about how energy worked, about how to manipulate reality, it made him wonder if perhaps he’d set aside the soul bound weapon too hastily. His bone weapons had come in handy, in a thousand different ways, and he might not still be alive, standing here, if not for them.

But there could be a way for him to empower the soul bound weapon in a way he couldn’t before.

Perhaps I can bring it to my level of strength. Perhaps one day, I could wield Bones again…

The prospect made him smile.

Xavier halted, pausing at the threshold he imagined was the border between when the Ragatni would start attacking him. As he’d walked a few steps forward it hadn’t shifted its gaze from him. This whole time it had simply stared, but it was still a good distance away. Its hammerhead-shark head didn’t shift. It simply stood there.

Xavier could feel much from the beast with his expanded senses. The smell of energy wafting from its giant maw every time it released a breath—sharp, strong, but also with a hint of something rotten. Rotten like the strange runes on the wall were rotten.

He couldn’t pierce its veil with his Immortal Soul lens-enhanced spiritual sense, however. That hadn’t grown much more powerful with his expanded senses, and the things veil, its cloak of concealment, was stronger than any he’d encountered in his own universe.

There were other things he could discern, however, things that were more clear to him now than before, instinctual things—he somehow knew how this thing would move, or else he had a very good idea of it, even though it hadn’t moved yet.

And if I play my cards right, it won’t get the chance.

A spell pattern manifested inside him, cast immediately. Ice began to form on the stone ground beneath his feet, spreading outward to cover the walls of the Dark Tunnel, then heading toward the Ragatni. The instant the spell reached the Ragatni it was like the Void Dungeon-beast became activated. It reminded him of a mob from an MMO, just standing there until it “noticed” he was there, even though it already knew.

Must be a quirk of the dungeon.

The spell acted quickly, engulfing the massive creature before it could take a single step forward with any one of its six thick legs.

Xavier grinned. Then he began to run. One, two, three steps before his wings flapped and he took to the air. A twinge of energy came from below, and he hastily avoided something that sprang straight out of the solid ground grasping for his ankles.

A hand. A giant hand the size of a man, covered in dirt—grave dirt—the tips of its fingers bloody and its nails jagged and worn, as though it had plunged itself right out of a grave.

Roots of the Grave.

Xavier avoided the grasping hand with ease, but he sensed more sprouting up from the ground, and they sprouted fast, one after the other. They cracked the stone with ease—stone his blade hadn’t scratched—and the ice that covered it along with it.

He dodged most of them with ease, his confidence steadily growing, until the last one moved faster than he’d thought possibly and wrapped its fingers around his leg.

The grip was unyielding.

Xavier was flung straight into the stone wall so hard it cracked the ice that now covered it, and many things inside his body cracked along with it. He tried to teleport away—while there was a lock on portalling in this place, there wasn’t a lock on teleportation, assuming he could see where he was teleporting to, as something stopped him from teleporting straight out of the dungeon—but teleports and phasing were blocked by the vicelike fingers gripping him.

Something he’d known from the description of Roots of the Grave, he just hadn’t imagined these damned hands would be fast enough to grab him.

Spikes grew out of Xavier’s ankle, bone spikes that shot into the flesh of the fingers, weakening that grip until he was able to prise himself free. He fell to the stone—that damned intense gravity slamming him down hard.

There were ten of the damned hands now, and they weren’t just reaching out from the stone ground, they’d freed themselves from it and were crawling along the Dark Tunnel like human-sized spiders, each step of their fingers like a spike chipping ice, their jagged nails finding easy purchase.

Maybe this will be harder than I thought.

Xavier grinned. He flashed forward, moving with a fluid grace even under the oppressive gravity, spell patterns instinctively forming inside his mind and manifesting inside his body. He touched the closest human-sized hand, and it froze in place. It didn’t freeze like the Ragatni was frozen—he’d stuck it inside a temporal cage similar to the spell Time Prison, but far more robust.

Killing the hands was out of the question. Not because he couldn’t kill them, but because it would take too long. The description of Roots of the Grave that his Personal System had given to him said these things were undead creatures, and it took a lot to kill them. Xavier didn’t quite understand how undead creatures could be conjured in such a way, but he didn’t need to understand that to know it was best to simply cage them.

He teleported from one to the other, touching each on the back of the hand, freezing them in the temporal cages until he’d gotten the last one.

Then he turned and faced the Ragatni. As he’d been fighting against the hands he’d noticed the shift in the tunnel. The steam wafting off the beast, the slight wave of warm air in the otherwise cold environment. The entire body of the Ragatni glowed red from inside the ice, a puddle forming around its six feet. The fight with the hands had taken less than a second or two, and yet it had taken too long.

The Class C7 Void Dungeon-created beast started shifting its legs, making the ice crack and slide down its tough hide until it slammed into the ground.

Xavier moved. He needed momentum for this, so he didn’t teleport straight to the thing. Instead, he sprinted forward, wings expanding, then he flapped them at the same time as he leapt, the bone spear still safely within his grip, and slammed it straight into the beast’s thick skull.

It lodged into the beast’s skull, sliding a few inches before abruptly stopping.

Xavier was about to blink in slight confusion when the Ragatni’s shifted its massive head sharply to the left. Still gripping the spear, Xavier flew straight for the wall. He didn’t slam into it this time, however, as he was able to teleport away at the last instant. Fifty feet away, he stood on the ground and stared at the large beast. Steam wafted from its nose and open maw as it pawed the ground with two of its left-side hooves.

The spear remained lodged inside the beast’s head.

It should have been powerful enough to pierce the beast’s brain. It had been the most powerful weapon he’d ever wielded, and he imagined he sent it into that beast’s head with enough force to put a hole through the stone walls of the Void Dungeon, yet the damned thing was still standing.

And now it was angry.

The spear, however, remained lodged in its head, moving wherever the Ragatni’s head moved.

Maybe I can use that to my advantage…

Xavier grew something else from his body. Not a spear this time—but a hammer. A massive, heavy hammer that would take a great deal of his strength to wield in a place with such intense gravity.

He would make that spear pierce the beast’s skull.

For a second time, ice started spreading from where he stood. It flowed down the Dark Tunnel, closing the fifty feet between him and the Ragatni in almost an instant, and started to engulf the beast once more. He should have cast the spell before slamming that spear in its head, but honestly he hadn’t thought he would need to.

My arrogance strikes again.

He was about to sprint forward—ready to teleport when he was satisfied he’d generated enough momentum—when a sweet sound rang in the Dark Tunnel. A sound coming from that rotten-smelling beast’s maw. But it wasn’t merely coming from its maw, it was also sounding straight into his mind, in the same way as when spirits were talking to him.

The sound was beautiful. Music like he’d never heard before. It spoke to him in tones he couldn’t ignore. His whole body relaxed in a way he could never remember it doing. The grip around the bone hammer loosened, and the hammer’s haft fell from his fingers, slamming into the stone and falling to one side.

Then his knees followed, his legs too relaxed to hold him upright any longer.

What a beautiful sound…
