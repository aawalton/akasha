“Morgan! I have something for you!” I call out as I step into her gallery.

There’s a flurry of activity, her half-dozen apprentices scurrying around at a break-neck pace. She floats in the middle of the chaos, dreamily painting a picture in the air. She lazily pulls from a couple of different brushes and pens orbiting her.

It appears to be a ritual circle of some kind, done in miniature. It’s four concentric circles, each one made of dozens of glyphs. Some I recognize, but most I don’t. They were likely created by Morgan herself just for this project. As far as I know, no one else has managed to either create—or maybe discover?—new glyphs. This is something only Morgan can do.

“How long has she been like this?” I ask the nearest apprentice, a young woman by the name of Tasha.

She brushes a lock of brown hair out of her face, clearly exhausted. Probably both magically and physically.

“A few hours,” she pants. “We’ve been working to prepare the various devices she’s designed. I don’t know how she does it,” she says with no small amount of awe.

I walk over to her and shake her by the shoulder.

“Morgan? You in there?”

She jolts and looks around with bleary eyes.

“Wuh? Huh? Oh, Mach. Hello.”

“Hello. I brought something for you.”

She lights up. “Oh, great. Lemme see!”

I drop the five buds I took from the Carnivine on a nearby table. Everyone in the room stops and stares in shock.

“Oh wow, that’s strong,” Tasha coughs, eyes watering.

“Where did you get that from?” Morgan asks. “Those have got to be some of the most magical objects I’ve seen, minus the Fractal Shards.”

She places a hand on one and hums.

“This will be perfect. This is a great start for my research. Where did you get them from?”

“A Carnivine. A giant man eating plant. Very big, very angry, very hungry. I would not recommend trying to grow more of these.”

She holds the basket-ball sized bud in her hands and hums.

“Unfortunate, but sensible. I tried working with healers on this, but however they heal doesn’t really apply to making plants grow. I can taste the magic in these, and it’s different from the kind healers use. I’ll get to work right away.”

“What about whatever you were working on before?”

“Oh, the crystal growing ritual?” She waves a hand dismissively. “Almost done, and not really necessary right now. This,” she holds up the almost-pulsating bud. “Is far more important.”

“I need to go find the next component. I have no idea where to go, though. Pythia never told us.”

“I’ll leave that to you. I have a lot of work to do.”

“See you later, Morgan.”

“In a while, crocodile.”

I offload the rest of what I recovered from the Fracture in the adjacent warehouse. All of it is far less interesting than the Hearts of Growth, though I do make a note on the manifest and let an apprentice know what I’ve turned into the stockpile. After that, I call up Warden, who’s currently working as Pythia’s bodyguard.

“Machina, how can I help?” He says after picking up immediately.

“Is Pythia available? I retrieved the Heart of Growth—Hearts, actually. Five of them—but I’m not sure where to go next.”

Warden sucks in an audible breath.

“I’m afraid not. She’s still in poor condition. Feverish, incoherent. She keeps babbling about the Brightest Light. Her grandson said she’s never been like this before.”

“I suppose defying time—the one thing all of us obey—has a great cost.”

“I suppose so,” Warden agrees. “I wonder why we make it worse.”

“Who knows. In any case, I guess I’m on my own. Let me know when she regains consciousness, but if divining the future is so costly…”

“We should only ask her when it’s absolutely necessary.”

After saying goodbye, I start scanning the public Fracture spotting network. Generally used to allow civilians to avoid Fractures or alert officials to the existence of one, it’s also been useful for Empowered teams looking to make some money. Or Typicals with more courage than sense.

Unfortunately, there’s nothing that particularly stands out to me right now.

I run off towards Denver. There’s nothing stopping me from helping out anyway, even if nothing sparks my interest.

It takes several days for a Fracture that might actually have something relevant for the hydroponic arcology project form. Which is surprising, given how many form each day, both in and around the city. Still, the vast majority of them are Category-1, and I don’t have high hopes for anything particularly valuable in them. At least for my purposes, anyway.

I let most of those go, instead only fighting when requested. I don’t want to step on any toes that I don’t have to. My recent experiences with the Council has taught me just how important making friends can be. Ultimate power means a lot, but sometimes you just need a second pair of hands. Or a different set of abilities.

The Fracture is a Cat-2 that formed in the Denver Botanical Gardens, and I ask the National Guard to lay claim to it for me. Given where it formed, it doesn’t seem likely that it’d form a sun or water crystal. Still, it might produce something useful for further research. Grow beds, fertilizer, magical sprinkler systems, it’s impossible to know for sure.

Hopefully this one isn’t as horrible to traverse as the last one. Time to gather some friends.

Sergeant Callisto is training with her squad on various scientific instruments when a familiar presence nears. The scientist—not Dr. Patricks, he returned to DARPA’s lab sometime ago—instructing them stiffens.

The familiar, slow, plodding thunk…thunk…thunk of Machina shakes the ground. But the more recognizable part of his arrival is his Aura. It feels like a mountain on the move. A friendly one, thankfully.

“Sergeant Callisto, it’s good to see you again. Do you know where Captain Rains is? I could use your help with something again,” he says.

“He’s with Lieutenant McKinely in the Pineapple Express. I’ll come with you, sir.*”

He follows her back to the Pineapple Express’s hangar bay where the command staff is currently hard at work doing… something important, probably.

“So while we’ve made good progress on the arcology project, I’m not sure where the remaining components are. But there is a Fracture in Denver that might have something useful, even if it’s not exactly what we’re looking for,” Machina explains to them all.

“It’s a Category-2. It formed in the Denver Botanical Gardens. I’m hoping either for some plant samples for crops—unlikely, if I’m being honest—or maybe something for general magical botany research. At the very least whatever we pull out might be usable for fertilizer or compost.”

“We’re far more likely to be able to bring the heavy armor in a Fracture like that,” Lieutenant Matterson muses. “A Cat-2 would be a whole lot easier on us, too.”

“Should give us a chance to conduct a little more research than the last one,” Machina agrees.

“Welp, I guess that settles it,” Sergeant Callisto says. “I hope you boys and girls are ready for some real action. Personally, I was getting a little bored myself.”

The day of the Fracture dice, Second Squad pulls themselves out of bed hours before the sun is set to rise.

Eric grumbles like he always does, but he gets up nonetheless, and Callisto is saved from yelling at him. Despite his constant bitching, he’s diligent when it comes down to it. They all trudge into the armory, though they’re far from the only people there. The active defense of Denver and its outlying towns is an around-the-clock job.

Lieutenant Matterson meets them at the vehicle pool. Second Squad piles into their respective armored vehicles, with Sergeant Callisto taking Stryker 1.

“Second Squad, you are cleared to move out,” Captain Rains tells them over the radio. “Good luck, and Godspeed.”

Callisto rubs her aching back as the Stryker finally comes to a stop, having arrived at the Fracture they’re meant to dive today. The Stryker can drive over anything, but that doesn’t mean it’s pleasant ride. Most of the Greater Denver Fortress’ roads are in extremely poor condition. Heavy military vehicles are taking their toll on them. All the construction equipment that could repair them is busy building the wall.

It’s planned to be over a hundred miles in total length, and while it’s extending by a few miles every day, there’s still months before it’s slated to be complete, and roads have a low priority except where absolutely necessary. With most civilian cars banned except for emergencies, most of the active vehicles can handle the torn up roads.

Other National Guard troops are overlooking the Fracture and keeping civilians with more guts than sense away. Despite the extreme danger, even Typicals have been attempting to dive Fractures. Even a few scraps of magical stuff can be worth huge sums of money.

A few pounds of dirt can be worth a few thousand dollars. The ultra rich mix anything and everything they can get their hands on in food or drinks. Usually in a so-far vain attempt to become Empowered themselves.

As for the more official Empowered groups, clearing a Fracture can set up a team for a few months just on the closure bounty itself. That doesn’t even count the value of the Fractal Shard or whatever else they manage to recover. With millions of dollars on the line, arguments over the rights to the dive and salvage can become vicious.

Already two other teams are there, and both of them are in the middle of a heated argument over the Category-2 Fracture. Both teams are a handful of nervous looking 1-Stars with one or two 2-Stars mixed in.

“We got here first, which means it’s ours. Back off!” The leader of one jabs the other in the chest, and she rocks back. An ugly look crosses her face. The Captain in command of the National Guard squad tasked with holding the Fracture is nervously trying to calm both of them down.

“Captains Illya and Tiller, please, there’s no need for this,” she says placatingly. “While I understand that you got here first, Captain Illya, the National Guard is already taking care of this Fracture. Machina himself has requested it.”

“Oh? Machina wants something in there?” Captain Illya licks her lips greedily. “Must be reeeeeeeeal valuable, then.”

The air grows even more tense, and it looks like a fight might break out. All the Typicals are slowly backing away and looking for cover. When Empowered fight, Typicals tend to get chewed up as collateral damage. 1-Stars might not have the durability to stand up to heavy machine guns, but they’ve got quick reaction times and destructive abilities. There’s a reason they say a great defense is a good offense.

“Ah, shit. Lieutenant, we’ve got a problem here,” Sergeant Callisto says, hopping on the squad’s radio channel.

“I can see that, Sergeant. I’m open to ideas. We can’t exactly fire on them. Can’t afford to lose Empowered, even ones like these.”

“We should call Machina, tell him to hurry the fuck up!” Eric says. “If anyone could put these dickwads in their place, it’d be him.”

“Good idea.” Matterson switches to the channel dedicated to calling Machina for assistance. “Machina, Matterson. We’ve got a situation on the Fracture and need your assistance. Come quickly.”

“On my way.”

Captain Illya turns around and hits Captain Tiller, the leader of the other Empowered team right in the gut. He goes flying off into a Humvee, slamming into it with a boom. The heavily armored vehicle rocks with the impact and the chassis folds like a tin can. The soldier manning the mounted gun drags himself out of the Humvee, and Tiller pries himself out of the wrecked vehicle’s twisted armored door. He cracks his knuckles.

“So that’s how it’s gonna be then, huh? Alright, you asked for it!” He charges Illya, fist raised. The other team members look uncertain and eye each other warily, unsure if they should get involved.

Sergeant Callisto has her head poked just barely out of one of the top hatches of Stryker 1, watching all of it unfold when she hears something zip by. A crack follows a millisecond after, and she reflexively slams back down into the APC.

“Oh shit!”

The brawl comes to a harsh and sudden stop as a twelve foot long sword made of stars spears into the ground all the way to the hilt. A huge cloud of dirt blooms outward, and all the Typicals are left coughing for a moment.

An Aura washed over the entire area, one filled with both righteous indignation and disappointment.

“What are you doing?!” Machina booms, stomping into the gardens. “What are you fighting over?!“

Captains Illya and Tiller are frozen like deer in the headlights.

“Oh, shit, they’re in trouble now,” Eric laughs.

“Well, Captains,” Machina asks acidically. “I asked you a question.”

Illya gulps as the rest of her team looks close to making a break for it, clearly terrified by the weight of Machina’s Aura. The mountain that Callisto felt yesterday is back, and this time it is not friendly.

“We—we were here first, Machina. It’s our right to dive the Fracture!”

“The National Guard had already put a claim on it. It’s not going to be dived for mere greed.” He spits the last word.

Illya scoffs, pride, indignation, or maybe avarice giving her courage. “They’re just Typicals, what they want doesn’t matter—”

All of a sudden there’s a hum in the air. Callisto can taste blood on her tongue, and the smell of a thunderstorm about to break fills her lungs. It’s so strong she coughs. Machina’s Aura hammers down on Illya, and she crumples to a knee, nearly gagging.

“Just Typicals?” he says dangerously. “You look down on them because they weren’t lucky enough to gain powers?”

He looks down at her. “They are a million times the hero you think yourself to be. I look around at these men and women and see legends in the making. Do you know why? Of course you don’t. You’re too blind to fucking see it.

“They have no magic powers, no spells. They are normal people, wielding weapons that barely annoy even the least of monsters. They are not blessed with power. They cannot make fire with a thought or shatter rock with their fists. Yet I still see them here, standing firm. They have nothing but what we have been able to make and they defend the city every minute of every hour of every day!”

He jabs a finger at Callisto and Second Squad.

“They have waded through hell and come back not because of raw power but through courage, trust in each other, and an unshakeable conviction that what they do is right!“

He deflates and sighs, looking up at the dawn’s early light. The taste of blood and a storm on the horizon fades away.

“Aren’t you tired of this?” He says exhaustedly, gesturing at Illya and Tiller, both of them now laying on the ground and looking up. “Aren’t you tired of living in a world that’s constantly screwing you over?

“Aren’t you so tired of being so afraid that all you do is attack first? Aren’t you tired of not being able to trust someone else? I know I am. This is what I mean when we can do this together. When we’re not endlessly focused on ourselves and nothing else, look at the great things we can accomplish! Normal people, without powers or magic can hold back monsters! We can move mountains and fight against the end of the world!”

Both of the clashing Empowered captains stagger to their feet, and they’re both clearly ashamed.

“I…I just don’t know what else to do.” Illya says. “This is how it is. I don’t have another choice, right?”

“There’s always a choice. It just depends on what you’re willing to do. I make a choice every day to help where I can, to do what I can, when I can do it. There’s so much tragedy and evil in the world that it can seem overwhelming. That it’s like an endless flood, and there’s no point in trying. But if we all pick up a bucket, and work even a little, it adds up. It really does. I can’t save the world. A single person saving the world was never possible. That’s a task for us.”

Illya and Tiller look at each other before looking at the damage their short spat caused. An armed Humvee lies wrecked with half its chassis caved in. A medic looks over the gunner who scrambled out, now lying on the ground wincing in pain. Callisto guesses that he might have cracked a rib or three. He got off lucky. When Empowered fight, it’s the Typicals around them that tend to get the worst of it.

“I think you both have some choices you need to make. About who you are, and who you want to be. About what you are doing to the world, and what you want to do.”

“I’ve got a family to feed,” Tiller mumbles. “This is the only thing I can do.”

Machina places a gentle gauntlet on his shoulder.

“We can make sure everyone is provided for. We’re already doing it. We don’t leave anyone behind. Life is not a zero-sum game, everyone can win if we want to. Now, I think it’d be best if you take your teams and leave. You all have some thinking to do.”
