The next morning, Tressa stood with Lisha outside in the cool morning air, her breath slightly fogging. The sun hadn't even risen yet, and the street lamps flickered, lighting the space around them. She stared up at the stars far above them, twinkling in the vast sky. She could almost feel the energy they gave off and the soft, waning moon, which was slowly disappearing to make way for the rising sun.

“Did you make it to the mail?”

Tressa looked over. “Yes. He was a little surprised to see me there this early. But I paid him nonetheless. He said he would get an eagle within the next day or so and send my letter home.”

“That's good. I'm glad you were able to do so. I was a little surprised when you said you needed to make a quick stop before we left.”

Tressa gave a slight nod. “Well, I did promise my mother before I left to keep in touch as much as I could. And I haven't sent one in some time, so I figured today would be a good time before we left for two months.”

“Mmm, I see your point.”

Briadon emerged from the inn clutching his club and yawned, cutting the two off from their conversation.

“You okay?” Lisha asked.

“Yeah. Just don't really like the dark.”

“You're the one who wanted to be up this early.”

“Well, to help us get a head start,” he said, though his tail flicked, his ears twitched, and his voice gave a slight shake.

“Okay, we can go if you're fine.”

“Yes, let's go.” He pulled a torch from his bag and walked over to one of the massive braziers outside the inn on the street, where several farmers, craftsmen, and even hunters stood around chatting. They fell silent as the minotaur towered over them. After getting it lit, he gave a slight nod, then walked back to Lisha and Tressa.

Lisha grabbed one herself and lit it. Then they looked at Tressa.

“Um I have a light spell, remember?”

“Right, I forgot about that. It's kind of a force of habit to use torches.”

She smiled. “I'll use the spell once we get out of town.”

They turned, heading west out of town, and the sky began to turn light blue and yellow as it brightened, dimming the twinkling stars. That wouldn't help them for long, though, as they headed into the dense forest, traveling on the long, worn road made by travelers and merchants.

“Were you able to send your letter?” Briadon asked.

“Yes. I woke up earlier than normal. The postman wasn't too pleased to see me so soon, but he relented when I offered fifty bronze.”

“You know sending a letter only costs twenty-five, right?” Briadon said, annoyed at her careless spending.

“Yes, but if you notice, I sent it before business hours.”

“That's true, Briadon,” Lisha agreed.

“That’s still not worth double,” he huffed. “He ripped you off.”

“I… didn’t want to argue,” Tressa said with a wince.

Briadon opened his mouth to complain, but then Lisha hit his stomach and gave him a look.

In truth, Tressa knew it was a ripoff, but she didn’t want to risk the letter getting thrown away and not actually sent. It was a person she’d not seen before at the desk when she went in so early. She could tell instantly upon him seeing a High Elf that he wasn’t happy. It was a reaction she’d become very used to since leaving Falidorn.

Briadon cleared his throat. “Ah, well, it doesn’t matter, I guess.” He clenched his torch as they entered the black woods, the darkness wrapping around them like a blanket, blocking out the light from the rising sun and plunging them into darkness.

Tressa summoned her staff and held it out in front of her. Then she muttered her light spell, and a ball of light as big as a walnut floated in the air around her.

“Handy,” Briadon said, holding his torch and looking a bit envious.

Lisha sighed. “I wish I could use magic. I bet that is a lot better than just lighting a torch, especially during the rainy season.”

“Aye.” Briadon agreed.

Tressa smiled. “I still have to use mana, and that can make me tired, which is why I'm not making it glow brighter or bigger.”

They nodded, not really understanding but agreeing with her. The trio walked deeper into the darkened forest. The group fell silent as they walked, their feet crunching over dead leaves and bits of gravel kicked up by horses and wagons. The road was well kept, as many merchants, hunters, and other travelers used it while heading southwest.

Sometime later, Tressa asked, “I’m wondering about something. Why does Briadon speak Common so well? Nobody ever says ‘what’ when he speaks. Do minotaurs not have their own language?” Lisha was very good in Common as well, but Tressa had picked up that on occasion people don’t understand her so easily. That is never the case with Briadon.

He huffed. “Well, we have our own language, but it’s complicated.”

Tressa hoped she hadn’t stumbled on some dark history as he thought it over. Luckily, that didn’t seem to be the case.

“So we minotaur have always been tribal but even more so in the distant past. All the tribes generally spoke the same language, but it differed from tribe to tribe. We could still communicate but it wasn’t the same. Then, centuries ago, when the merchant’s guild began expanding more and more through the continent and down to the south, the minotaur tribes began to learn the Common tongue to trade with them. We would call it the trade language, and it became every bit as important to learn as our own tongue.

“Anyway, supposedly, after several generations the different tribes began just talking to each other in Common more and more as it came more naturally, and before you knew it we were all pretty much just speaking Common.” He let out a bellow of laughter. “At least that is what I’ve heard!”

“Fascinating, so language can change for a people over time…” Tressa muttered.

“Given the right circumstances, this makes sense,” Lisha admitted. “On my islands, not everyone can speak Common. Of my brothers and sisters, only a few of us can. I know my parents cannot.”

Tressa pulled out a notebook and conjured a mana quill, also casting the spell to keep her book stable while walking. She simply wanted to jot down a few notes so she wouldn’t forget all of that.

Frankly, she had learned so much about minotaurs and statiki just by traveling with Briadon and Lisha these last six months. She imagined if their adventures together continued for long, she could write an entire book on just those two species alone.

She was sure her people’s library’s had no information of statiki whatsoever, and what they had recorded of minotaurs was so limited it might as well have been nonexistant.

Whether it was always that way, or a result of the Mad King burning part of the library, she did not know.

Then, she lost focus on her mana quill, and it vanished as a howl split the silence around their group, causing each one to jump and sending their nerves into overdrive.

Briadon let out a shudder as another howl echoed in the far distance. “I really hope that wasn't a merawolf. I don't feel like messing with them this early.”

Lisha and Tressa both nodded in agreement, Tressa returning the notebook to her satchel.

As they moved on, eyes peeled and ears straining to listen, Tressa held her staff tightly and focused on the howls and cries. Each was at a different pitch. One lasted longer. Then, after several seconds, a far deeper and louder howl split the silence in response to the much higher howl.

They remained on the road as the howls and cries grew more distant.

“It seems they might be fighting,” Tressa said as she listened more closely to the sounds as they moved farther away.

“How can you tell?” Lisha asked, though she had felt the same way based on the types of cries. In truth, whatever the wolves were fighting, she didn't want to meet it.

“The pitches of the cries. They sound like they are in pain. And then the howls—I'm guessing those are their attacks. Mera wolves are so cool.”

“Not when you're facing them down,” Briadon said.

“I agree,” Lisha added.

“I've never seen one.”

“Don't get your hopes up,” said Briadon. “They are dangerous even for seasoned hunters like ourselves.”

Tressa made a slight pouting face but also agreed that she didn't want to meet one. She had heard they were relentless and would do anything to take down their prey, and that their packs could range from five to twenty. They would be led by the Merega, an evolved version of the Mera wolf that stood as tall as a Gailain horse, which itself would stand eye level with Briadon.

“Tressa, your light.”

“Oh, sorry. I got lost in thought,” she said as she cast her light spell.

“What were you thinking about?” Lisha asked, falling into step with Tressa.

Some time had passed, and the sun was now rising and casting a gray light into the trees. But it was still dark enough that they needed the light from the torches and Tressa's spell.

“Oh, I was thinking about the Mera wolves and how big they can get. I have heard they can grow to the size of Gailain horses.”

“Gailion?”

“No, it's pronounced gale-ay-in.”

“Gailain?”

“Yes. They are the high elves' prized horses. We breed them to be swift, sturdy, and powerful. They are bigger and faster and can outlast a human horse.”

“So just how tall is the horse?”

“Hmmm. It would look Briadon eye to eye.”

Lisha looked up at Briadon, who towered over both Tressa and Lisha.

“Hmph. Don't compare me to a horse,” Briadon said.

“I'm not! I'm talking about their height.”

“So what are they bred for?” he asked.

Tressa paused. “Well, we use them mainly for hunting. But they can travel long distances and can pull the weight of a wagon on their own. Their smaller counterparts, the Galtion horses, are meant more for speed and endurance, and overall, just riding. I think the fastest one belongs to Lord Altmen,” she said, not wanting to mention the history that Gailain and Galtion horses were also used in the Mad King’s wars, and a symbol of High Elven superiority over other races.

“How fast is it?”

“I'm sorry, what?”

“How fast are the horses?”

“Hmmm. I'd say it could complete a trip that a human-owned horse could make in half the time and have greater endurance. So if a horse can make a trip in a day, I'd say his horse could do it in half that time, or thereabouts. Our messengers breed them. I was promised one after my Trials.”

“Doesn't that make them extremely valuable?”

After some thought Tressa nodded. “I guess so.”

Briadon sighed. “I bet kings would be obsessed with them and wage war on the elves if they ever found out.”

Tressa paled. “Well, then I'll not talk of it. I don't want to see another war come about.”

Lisha and Briadon gave each other a look, noticing Tressa took that just a little too seriously.

“I wouldn’t mind owning a horse,” Lisha said. “Travel could be so much faster.”

“Wouldn’t do me any good,” Braidon laughed. “Say Tressa, could one of this Gailion horses carry me?”

“Gailain,” she corrected then thought about it, looking him over. She grinned. “Maybe two could pull a cart?”

He let out a bellow of laughter at that.

Lisha’s tongue flicked out as she listened. “Stay alert, everyone. The merawolves seemed to have moved on, but don’t forget where we are.”

“Yeah, good call,” Briadon agreed.

The three fell quiet again as they continued down the road, the distant howls fading somewhere deep within the forest.
