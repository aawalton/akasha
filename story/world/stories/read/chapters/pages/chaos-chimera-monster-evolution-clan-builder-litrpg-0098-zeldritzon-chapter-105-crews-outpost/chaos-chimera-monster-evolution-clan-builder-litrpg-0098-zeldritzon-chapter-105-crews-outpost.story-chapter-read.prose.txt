Zeldritzon [Cycle 22] - Evening

Current Location:

[Twistop Zone]

[Tilz's Twistop Outpost]

[DreaGoth Canyon of the DreaGoth Realm]

"Tsk. This is ridiculous," Helz grumbled, his voice rising in frustration. The kolkobalt lieutenant was tightly bound by roots and vines, yet he still found energy to complain. "You just waltz into our territory and think you can capture all of us? Who do you think you are?"

"Me?" I replied, watching as my companions expertly secured the other kolkobalts. "I'm just your friendly Merecritt. We'll be on our way soon enough."

"Good luck with that," he said with an edge of menace in his tone. "I doubt you'll get very far before we kill you."

"Oh, I will," I said, letting a smile creep across my face. My gaze shifted from him to his fellow captives, then landed on his leader, Tilz, who was tied a little farther away. Tilz leaned against a building, exuding a dry chuckle at Helz's bravado.

"Don't let her rattle you, Helz," Tilz said.

Helz scowled at Tilz. "Are you kidding me? Do you remember how many monsters we've faced together? She took you out like it was nothing!"

"Yeah, well, I wasn't expecting her to be that strong," Tilz said.

I lidded my eyes, tuning in to their banter. This was getting interesting.

Helz shook his head in disbelief. "You're pathetic. I can't believe you let a girl take you down like that."

"Shut up! It was just a fluke!" Tilz snapped back. "I'll get her next time."

Helz laughed mockingly. "Sure you will."

Tilz growled, his expression darkening. "Great. Losing this outpost is going to earn us Lord Jalkra's wrath," he lamented, casting a worried glance around.

Helz winced at the thought. "You think? He'll probably execute us for losing such an important location."

"Well, that's not going to happen. Not if I can help it," Tilz said, a newfound confidence sparking in his eyes. "We'll just capture her and bring her to Lord Jalkra ourselves—"

Scarbol suddenly grumbled, interrupting their plotting as he whacked the two tied-up kolkobalts across the head.

"Hey!" they whined in unison, surprise flashing across their faces.

Scarbol's expression hardened. "Don't even think about it, you two. You're not capturing anyone anytime soon."

"Oh, really? And what makes you so sure, old friend?" Helz shot back, glaring at Scarbol.

Scarbol crossed his arms. "Because you're both going to be locked up until we decide what to do with you."

Mina snickered as she knelt in front of Tilz. "Hah, bad luck for you guys. Looks like you got the short end of the stick."

"You…" Tilz glared at Mina.

"Me." Mina smirked. "Surprise!"

Tilz snarled, "Mina, untie us. Maybe Lord Jalkra will be in a forgiving mood. He's a reasonable man, after all."

Mina shook her head. "I don't think so. I think I'll pass on that offer. Besides, if I let you two go, KiAera would have my head. And I rather like it attached to my shoulders, thank you very much."

Tilz let out a laugh, but it quickly turned into a grimace. "Fine. If you want to die so badly, I won't stop you. Just make sure to say hi to the afterlife for me."

"Oh really? How are you planning to do that? You're tied up." Mina poked his head with a grin. "Besides, I doubt you'll be doing much once we claim this outpost for ourselves."

Tilz hissed, "You traitor!"

Mina's grin widened. "Exactly! We're taking over this place and all its resources, including its prisoners—"

I reached out and tugged sharply on Mina's ear. "Quit it. We're not pirates." Honestly, we just wanted to be on our way after freeing Willow from these guys. And even though I had learned that Tilz was Tailza's father, it still seemed wise to keep them bound to prevent any pursuit once we departed. But for Mina herself…

"Ow! Ouch! Stop it! It's just an old habit!" Mina protested, rubbing her ear with a pout.

"Ahem."

Loa stood a few paces behind us, her sleeves swaying in the evening wind. She'd remained unnervingly quiet during the scuffle, but now she approached. Her geta made a distinct, almost ceremonial tap with each step across the stone floor of the outpost.

"I believe that's enough gloating," she said with a tap on Mina's shoulder. Her eyes moved from Scarbol to Tilz, then rested on me. "May I speak?"

I studied her a moment, not answering immediately. The wind pulled at her sleeves like it was trying to read her too.

"You may," I said at last, cautious.

Loa gave a shallow nod and turned her attention to the captured kolkobalts.

"Your efforts were... ineffective. Tilz. Helz. You've failed to maintain security in the very zone Lord Jalkra trusted you with. And now, your threats are airless things. Words spoken by men with no more ground beneath them than smoke."

Tilz squinted. "You are… Loa…? The tactician?"

"Former tactician," Loa corrected, her tone clipped but formal. "I serve another now."

"You what?!" Helz barked, struggling against his restraints. "You were Jalkra's!"

Mina laughed from behind me. "Not anymore. She's ours now. Well... KiAera's, technically."

I raised a brow at that and shot Mina a glare. "Don't go handing out loyalty like it's candy."

Loa, to her credit, didn't flinch. "I came not to pledge. Only to advise. You wanted infiltration. I gave that." Her voice drifted like smoke, but it hit like cold iron. "Now you have a foothold—this foothold—but Jalkra will notice soon. He always does. And retaliation will not be slow."

I folded my arms. "I figured as much."

She stepped closer. "Which is why I propose we don't just escape. We use this outpost."

Helz let out a cackling laugh. "You're insane. You think you can hold this place? Lord Jalkra'll flatten it with a flick of his wrist!"

Loa ignored him. "This position straddles three canyon veins. It's a choke point for travel and an access node for subterranean storage. If we hold it, we'll have access to his flow routes. His couriers. His mineral runs. And if we fake its destruction…" She finally turned to me, "We can make him think it's gone, and that he's safe. And in that time, we slip under."

Rox spoke up, wings still unfurled. "That's a lot of assumptions."

Viz frowned. "Even more risk."

"Strategy is always risk," Loa said. "I'm not offering guarantees; I'm offering leverage."

Amber looked to me. "Do we trust her? She's kind of... spooky."

I didn't answer immediately. My gaze drifted to Tilz, who was now silently brooding, and then to Loa, whose sleeves whispered like wings against the wind.

"I don't trust her," I said finally. "Not yet. But I'm listening."

Loa inclined her head, not offended. "Wise."

Mellow, who had perched on a crate, raised a small hand. "Um... I like the idea of pretending we exploded something."

Everyone turned to her.

She blinked. "It's... dramatic."

Kyrawl let out a low, wind-whistling trill that I suspected translated to: I agree with the caterpillar.

Scarbol turned to me, folding his arms. "We'll need guards and scouts if we hold this place. And a fallback in case it does blow up."

"We'll need more than that," I said, rubbing my forehead.

Mina stepped beside me. "We'll need a plan."

Loa smiled faintly. "Then it's time to draw one."

Zeldritzon [Cycle 22] - Evening

Location: Twistop Valley Ridge | Berylgem's Perspective

Far above the fractured basin of Tilz's outpost, a ridge of warped stone curved like a gnarled claw against the bleeding dusk. Crags jutted skyward, catching the light like shattered teeth. Here, perched in the shadows of half-burnt trees and fungal growths, Berylgem the Gremgem licked his cracked lips and hunched forward on his long, crooked toes, peering through a crooked monocle stitched into his eye socket.

The lens clicked. The basin came into focus.

Below, he could make out the flickering of campfires, the movement of unfamiliar silhouettes. He saw the bodies—bound kolkobalts and scattered patrols being rounded up. And at the center, that floppy-eared little beast—KiAera, the one his spies whispered about. The one who'd humiliated Tilz.

"Ghehehehe…" Berylgem's laugh crept out like steam through broken pipes. "Look at her… frolicking about like she's won. But she's only swept the yard for me."

A twisted banner unfurled behind him, etched with the sigil of his liege: a skeletal maw brimming with warsteel fangs. The Grimgore Warlord—his true master. A being who craved footholds in the canyon like marrow from bone.

Grush, the towering bone-clad juggernaut beside him, gave a grunt and shifted his massive arms, vertebrae along his shoulders clicking with every flex. "They've wasted their strength on each other," he said with a hollow voice. "Tilz's command crumbles. That rabbit's crew is tired, injured, distracted. A few more blows and they'll fall like rotted fruit."

"They did all the hard work for us," Berylgem sneered, his grin showing rows of pitted green teeth. "That Mezmeic brat and that Dorvus runt I left behind? Pfft. Decoys. Tilz wanted them interrogated. I wanted them to get caught. Either he beat her, or she beat him. Doesn't matter. Now they're both limping."

He tapped the side of his head with a gnarled claw. "That's how you conquer with class, Grush. Let your enemies rip each other's guts out, then waltz in and claim their corpses!"

Grush rolled his neck. "Why don't we attack now? We crush the outpost, claim the zone, and by the time Jalkra returns, it's too late."

Berylgem tilted his head in thought and slurped back a glob of acidic drool that had pooled in his cheek. "Tempting," he mused. "But let's wait a bit longer. We should let them settle and relax."

His monocle whirred, zooming in even further. "They're still reinforcing. We can't have them fluttering about—the winged lizard, the bats, and the feathered dog-woman. I want to understand their habits before I scatter their bones."

Grush bared his jagged teeth. "They'll shatter like the rest."

"Oh, they will." Berylgem's voice lowered to a hiss. "We'll strike when they start fortifying. Once they think the danger has passed. Right when they claim the outpost as their own."

He turned, and a chorus of growls and snarls greeted him. Dozens of monsters—skinless fiends, horned goblinoids, armored chompers, and cloaked archers—waited on command. This was no ragged raiding party. It was a strike division, an elite detachment from the Grimgore's southern flank.

Berylgem raised one bony finger to the sky.

"Prepare the siegeworms. Ready the veilsmoggs. We'll draw them out first, but flush them into the open. Then we smash what's left."

He paused, glancing one last time toward the flickering lights below.

"And remember," Berylgem said as he turned, "leave the girl alive: KiAera. Lord Grimgore may want a... chat."

Grush grunted. "Why?"

Berylgem smiled, a green glint in his monocle. "Because anything that can beat Tilz that cleanly… might be worth keeping."

The ridge darkened as the wind howled, and the first of the siegeworms rumbled beneath the cracked canyon soil, digging toward the heart of the basin.

Zeldritzon [Cycle 22] – Dusk

Location: Tilz's Twistop Outpost | DreaGoth Canyon

Perspective: KiAera

The sky above the basin had cooled to a bruised violet, where dusk folded across the jagged stones like the end of a long sigh. The air buzzed faintly with tension, even after the fighting had stopped. My crew was settling in, Mina and Loa pacing in and out of the half-burnt armory, Scarbol securing captives, and the Nexperbax triplets on lookout from the crooked watchtower.

I lingered near the base's perimeter, standing just beyond the vine-strapped body of Tilz.

He wasn't what I expected. Bigger, yes. But quieter, too, now that the fire was out of him. The kolkobalt sat slumped against a broken support beam, his arms bound and his gaze sunk into the dusty ground. A warrior disgraced, but not without his pride.

I crouched beside him, ears low, eyes steady. "So," I began, "you said something earlier about bringing me to Jalkra. What changed your mind?"

Tilz didn't look at me. Instead, he let out a breath through his nose, like he was trying to push the weight of the canyon off his chest. "Wasn't my idea. That whole mess with the Dorvus and the Mezmeic kid? That wasn't mine either."

I tilted my head. "Go on." "The Dorvus and Willow. Why were they even here?"

He hesitated. That surprised me.

"They were brought here… by my colleague." He said the word like it tasted foul. "Name's Berylgem. Nasty piece of work. Gremlin-type monster with too many ideas and no loyalty. Said the Mezmeic brat and her deer monster were important. Claimed they were dangerous—alive—but valuable. I didn't ask questions."

I frowned. "You let him drop off prisoners at your base without asking questions?"

He scoffed. "You think I had a choice? This is Jalkra's territory, not mine. Berylgem reports directly to him, same as I do. We don't always see eye to eye, but he outranks me in everything but guts. Keeps his true loyalties slippery."

My stomach twisted. "So he planted them here… knowing we'd come."

Tilz nodded grimly. "Probably. The Dorvus… we'll call him Scorch, was guarding that little bug. Willow, right? Cute thing, but sick. Fevered. We tried to reach her, give her water, food, even a healer…"

"And?" I asked.

Tilz's shoulders slumped a little. "That Dorvus wouldn't let us near her. Every time we approached, he lit up like a thunderstorm. Crimson force fields, shocks so strong they nearly fried my medics. You think we didn't try to help? We did. But there was no getting past him."

I stared at Tilz for a long, quiet moment.

"I assumed you were holding them captive," I said. "Torturing them."

He barked a rough, bitter laugh. "I'm a lot of things, but I don't make sport of hurting the helpless. That Dorvus had blood in his eyes, sure, but it wasn't for us. It was for anyone who got near the caterpillar."

Willow.

The thought of her shivering and pale, with the Dorvus constantly sparking around her like a living barrier… it hurt. She had been terrified when I found her.

And Berylgem meant for Tilz to keep them locked up. Knowing the Dorvus would make it impossible to interact with them safely.

"And what did Berylgem do after dropping them off?" I asked.

Tilz spat to the side. "Disappeared. Just said, 'Handle them until the command comes.' But I knew something was wrong. That imp wasn't sent by Jalkra. He used this base like a box to keep the mess in while he cooked up something worse."

"To make us fight each other," I said flatly.

Tilz looked at me again. "Yeah. And now here we are. My outpost in shambles. You pissed. Berylgem gone."

I crossed my arms and exhaled, feeling the day's weariness sinking into my bones. "He played both of us."

"And now, he'll come back to claim what's left."

I met his gaze. This time, no anger; just understanding.

"He's not going to get the chance." I stood and turned back toward the main camp. "Not if we reinforce and push him back."

Tilz let out a slow grunt. "I doubt you want my help."

"I don't." I looked over my shoulder. "But I might take your knowledge. Depends on how much you feel like making up for it."

He gave a short nod. "If it means that little bug lives? I'll tell you everything I know."

I didn't smile. But my tail gave a flick as I walked off. There was planning to do. Walls to reinforce. Monsters to prepare.

And somewhere, not far from here, a gremlin with a grudge was coming.
