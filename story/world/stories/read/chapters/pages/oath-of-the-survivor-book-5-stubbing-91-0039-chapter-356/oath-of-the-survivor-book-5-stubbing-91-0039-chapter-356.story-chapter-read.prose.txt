It didn’t take long for Kyle and C.H.A.D.D. to get a meeting scheduled with Marcus, the duo quickly finding themselves waiting for one of the high-speed trains to take them to the familiar saloon.  Marcus was waiting at the bar, two glasses of amber liquid waiting for them.  The older man put his tablet aside, the drones flitting behind him turning to face them.

“And you even made it fourteen seconds ahead of schedule.  You’re learning, kid.”

“It takes me a while, but I get there eventually,” Kyle said with a smile, walking up to the bar.

Marcus grabbed a glass and gestured for Kyle to do the same.  “Cheers, Kyle.”

“Cheers,” Kyle said, gently tapping the older man’s glass with his own, then taking a long pull from the whiskey.

“I take it you saw the news?” Marcus asked as he set his glass down.

“What news?” Kyle asked.  “Other than scheduling this meeting, I haven’t been keeping up on many current events since the Tribunal ended yesterday.”

“Sounds like the Voorth Capital bastard who financed this whole thing got what was coming to him.  Assistant attacked and killed him when he got fired.”

“That sounds awfully convenient,” Kyle said, doing a quick search on his nav bracelet.

“Everybody worth their salt knows it’s a cover-up job,” Marcus said, pouring himself another splash.  “The real question is whether or not everything got covered they wanted to.  In any event, keep your wits about you.  There are some dangerous folks out there with no problems killing to keep their secrets.  And you’re still in the middle of the mess, whether you want to be or not.”

“I appreciate it, Marcus.  We’ll be careful,” Kyle assured him.

Marcus snorted.  “The hell you will.  Kyle, I’ve seen your definition of careful.  You’re going to run headlong into trouble whether I warn you to or not.  I’m just asking you to keep your eyes open when you do.”

[WE WILL ENDEAVOR TO AVOID BEING TAKEN BY SURPRISE AGAIN.  FORTUNATELY, WE ARE BETTER EQUIPPED THAN EVER TO DEAL WITH THE CHALLENGES AHEAD.]

“I hope for your sake you’re right,” Marcus said as he took another long drink.  “I doubt they’ll try to move against you now, in any case, but remember that you’ve got a target on your back.  And of course, that goes for your associates, too.”

“Which begs the question, are you sure you want to be involved with the auction?  You’re probably on their list already, but you’ll be a lot higher on it if you decide to help me with it.”

Kyle was genuinely concerned for the older man, despite Marcus’ recent breakthrough to high-tier C Grade.  For all of his personal power, he was a craftsman through and through.  And with at least one B Grade, Ger’Sinh’s backer, loosely connected to Voorth Capital, there were risks involved for everybody in Kyle’s circle.  Even though there were balances in place with the Administrators, B Grades weren’t existences to take lightly.

“Of course I’m going to help,” Marcus said.  “We saw what happened when I left you to your own devices; you went and got yourself killed.”

“In my defense, I did get better,” Kyle replied.

“No, C.H.A.D.D. saved your sorry behind.  In no small part because of the upgrades you paid for.”

[THE UPGRADED COMPONENTS WERE MOST HELPFUL, DR. MAYHEW.]

“Whose side are you on, C.H.A.D.D.?” Kyle complained.

[THE SIDE THAT INVOLVES YOU NOT GETTING KILLED,] the drone responded immediately.

“I always knew that C.H.A.D.D. had the most sense,” Marcus said with a chuckle.  “In any event, you should be more worried about yourself than me.  I’m not the adventuring type, and I think you’d find that even most B Grades would think twice about trying to attack me on my home turf.”

Now that was something Kyle believed wholeheartedly.  Marcus had a virtual army of drones and robots, varying from E Grade to C Grade.  On top of that, he had a litany of powerful defense and security systems, the most powerful of which Kyle still hadn’t had a chance to see and experience.  That’s not to say that Marcus would necessarily win, particularly against an opponent like Perhades who had mass-scale destructive abilities, but he could certainly put up quite a fight.  Enough to draw attention to his attacker, at least.  The very last thing that these shadowy organizations wanted.

“I do think that the situation is less dire than you’re expecting, Kyle,” Marcus continued.  “If what your liaison sent from the Tribunal was accurate, Voorth Capital was more of a broker than anything else.  That means that the real dangerous individuals involved are going to more concerned with covering their tracks than trying to put the cat back into the bag.  Sure, a few groups might have a specific reason to target you, but now that the investigation is over I doubt that they’ll want to put themselves right back in the spotlight.  Doubly so when you’ve still got a lot of attention on you.  Unless they have a specific reason to target you, of course.”

“So you’re saying they’re likely to wait, at least a little bit, to take a shot at me when things have settled a bit?”  Kyle asked, not sure if he should feel reassured.

“It’s the smarter play,” Marcus said with a shrug.  “Besides, by waiting, they can see how this issue resolves itself.  Do you keep taking jobs?  Do you start an advocacy group?  Your own business?  Do you affiliate with another larger organization?  Or maybe you retire to a dusty old rock, and stay out of the way.  How they choose to handle you looks very different depending on what you end up doing, if they still find it worth it to try.”

Kyle nodded slowly, seeing Marcus’ logic.  He had to remember as well that virtually all of the companies implicated in the Voorth Capital scandal were headed by C Grades.  While Ger’Sinh’s group certainly had some more powerful individuals in it, the vast majority were headed by individuals in mid-tier or late-tier C Grade.  Even if they had a bone to pick with Kyle, they’d need to have the personal power to do something about it.  Not everybody out there is at Zierlan’s level, Kyle reminded himself, thinking back to Iberious’ advice form their conversation.

“Then what have you learned about the auction?” Kyle asked, turning his attention back to the upcoming decisions.

“That’s where things get a little trickier,” Marcus replied.  “First, it looks like the auction timeline is being pushed up considerably.  Now that the Tribunal is done, I expect they’ll announce a date within the next month to get the issue of the planet resolved.  That said, with all the mess related to Voorth Capital, interest has dwindled.”

“Isn’t that a good thing?” Kyle asked.  “Less demand should help to drive the price down, right?”

“You’d think so,” Marcus agreed.  “However, there are still Collective-established floor prices for planets.  Unless somebody is willing to pay at least that minimum threshold, the planet will be seized by the Collective and gifted as an asset in-kind to one of the member civilizations.  From there, it would be up to them to figure out what to do with it.”

Kyle thought to the several million credits in his account, and quickly pulled up a screen to check the minimum purchase price.  He blanched when he saw the figure.  Two hundred and fifty million credits, he thought.  Not an unreachable number for a C Grade, given the type fo work they were often asked to do, but not a figure he could reach anytime soon.  Certainly not within the span of a month.

“How much would I need to subsidize for somebody else’s purchase to make it an attractive offer?” Kyle asked, still looking at the number on his screen.

Marcus sighed.  “That’s the second major issue.  In the planet’s current state, it’s not terribly useful.  And with its current controversy, there are other purchases available that come with a lot less baggage.  I’m thinking for most folks, the terms for your negotiation would be extremely unfavorable to you, not to mention your overly-idealistic goal of relocating the Earthling refugees.”

“I see,” Kyle said, feeling a pang of guilt.  He knew it would have been a long shot, but hearing that there was nothing he could do still struck him harder than he thought it would.

C.H.A.D.D. sent reassurance through their bond, while Phaegos sent a note of annoyance, followed by a note of hunger.  Kyle got the message, and sent a quick thought back.  We’ll be back out and hunting soon.

To Kyle’s surprise, Phaegos actually took the effort to form a full reply.  Good.  I want to fight C Grades again.

“Don’t look so gloomy,” Marcus said, snapping Kyle’s attention back to the older man, who had a rare twinkle in his eye.  “Now, as all of this nonsense had been going on, I did some research on Kertan Six.  It sounds like there are some valuable resources there, but they’re nearly impossible to mine with traditional equipment due to the prevalence of sinkholes and other groundwater flooding the mines.  A problem for most major corporate enterprises.  Less so for a smaller-scale boutique operation.”

Kyle was confused for a moment, until the pieces started to come together.  “Are you saying that you’re interested in purchasing the planet.”

“I think I am,” Marcus said.  “It’ll be a loss leader for a while.  And you will owe me both credits and labor to help get things re-established.  But I think that, with the right partners, a viable venture could come out of this.”

“What about the refugees?” Kyle asked, thinking back to Marcus’ comments when they first met.

“I’m still not in the humanitarian aid business,” he said firmly.  “If they want to come and work, they can.  There will be opportunity, and fair wages for work.  But if they don’t, they can stay right where they are, or go looking for something different across the Collective.”

Kyle nodded slowly.  That was fair, and all he’d hoped for them, after all.  “How long will it take to get the specifics figured out?”

“Already working on it,” Marcus said.  “We’ll get a formal contract over for review, but I’m asking for you to help fund the startup costs for an initial settlement near the forest you made, asking for expansion of the plant life to help stabilize the soils, and for you to establish a permanent residence.  Working on a residency clause that will involve you spending some time on the planet between jobs as well.  More to come, of course.”

That sounded extremely fair.  He’d have to see the specifics, naturally, but overall it felt like it could be a great deal.  “Thank you, Marcus.”

“You’re welcome.  But remember, kid, this isn’t charity.  I’m going to keep expanding my manufacturing operations, and do intend for this to turn a profit.  I’m not going to get involved in the leadership or governance aspects beyond being a landlord and employer.”

“That’s still generous,” Kyle said.  “Giving them an opportunity was all I hoped to ask for.”

“Then you’d better hope I win the auction,” Marcus said with a chuckle, pouring a third drink.  “I expect things are going to get pretty busy for a while, at least on my end.  Before that though, I do have one question for you.”

“What is it?” Kyle asked.

Marcus pointed to the tied-up sleeve on Kyle’s left side.  “When are you planning to do something about your missing arm?”
