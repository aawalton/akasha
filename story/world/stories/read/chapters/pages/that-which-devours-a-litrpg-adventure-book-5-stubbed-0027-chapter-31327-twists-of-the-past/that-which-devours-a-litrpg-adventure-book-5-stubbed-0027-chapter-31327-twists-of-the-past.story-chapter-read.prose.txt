Alex, in the jungle…

Nothing moved in the trees, but my thoughts kept going back to Snapper. If he entered the cavern under the waterfall while we were gone, he’d easily kill those we’d left behind. Then again, the fight for the planet was over.

Hopefully, it wouldn’t be a bloodbath with us leaving.

Still, traveling through the jungle for the next couple of days didn’t sound like fun.

“Do you think we could travel faster?” I asked Lenna. On the way here, we had moved quickly to save Strange. Not that he needed it.

“The mana has evened out in this world, but it isn’t as excess to me as before,” she said with a concerned look. “I might be able to keep up.”

Flying her would cost me, but it’d be the quickest way. I’d only need to hunt a bit on our way.

“What about flying?” I asked.

My shadow inched across the ground, and I felt it, each tiny pebble or leaf it covered. I resisted shivering.

“Sure. I want to return home,” she said, while staring at the sky. “I miss Dengu.”

“He’ll be happy to see you,” I said, as my form shifted. My wings flared out behind me, then I leaped into the air. I circled overhead but watched my radar. When nothing moved, I dove down and scooped Lenna up.

Then I shot straight back the way we’d come, with my map guiding me.

We flew through the air, faster than I thought we would. My arms didn’t bother me, and my wings didn’t drain me as much. I also didn’t bother flying too high. I stayed in that sweet spot just above the trees, where the air seemed to give me a bit of a boost.

Time passed by rather quickly, along with the distance, and we only needed to stop once for me to eat. A quick pause, when I spotted a group of small creatures fighting. Stealing the kill left some lingering guilt. Especially after consuming the carcass, and the even smaller core. The scaly bird wasn’t even that tasty.

The downside was that I didn’t get anything for it besides energy. But that was all I needed to get us back in the air. The sun moved overhead, beating down on my black armor, but I didn’t heat up much.

By the time we reached the edges of the mountain range, sunset had started. I landed on the same massive boulder that others must have used before me. It rested lower than the frost line.

Strange leaped out of my shadow and chased after a few bugs, while both of us stretched.

“That saved us so much time,” said Lenna, with a pained smile as she massaged her calves.

My wings vanished as I dropped the form. An idea came to mind, and I went for it. I chucked the remains of the carcass from earlier into my shadow. My shadow ate it up faster than I could have, leaving nothing behind.

“I didn’t need to see that,” said Lenna, turning away.

I blushed and whinced. “I didn’t think about that part…”

“Don’t let others see that. They might freak,” she added, shaking her head.

“But not you?” I asked with a chuckle.

Strange chirped like that was funny.

“I know you, and that you wouldn’t hurt an innocent.” Her voice came out smaller than before. “Others don’t know that. Be prepared for their reactions.”

“You know know this, or…?” I let my voice trail off and glanced at the darkening sky.

“It’s common sense. The stars don’t need to chime in.” Yet, I caught a glimpse of her eyes flicking upward.

The crunch of several bugs came from Strange. Then, he joined up with my shadow again. He curled up somehow and went to sleep. I slowly walked around the boulder and my shadow dripped over the sides, even though it shouldn’t.

It swallowed a few small bugs that didn’t flee fast enough. The small bursts of energy didn’t last but seconds.. Thankfully, I didn’t get any taste of them. While Strange loved to chomp down on flying bugs, it wasn’t my thing.

“Let's get moving,” I said, as I turned to the trail up the mountain.

Lenna hesitated and shook her head, then continued up after me.

She stepped into my shadow and nothing happened. Not that I thought anything would.

I hadn’t been worried. No. Not at all.

This time the trail wasn’t bad, and we both made good time as the sun set. As soon as we crossed the frost line, I tossed Lenna my cloak, which she took thankfully.

Finally, the portal with the round small wall surrounding it came into view. The barrier that had been up was gone, along with any trace of it via second sight.

“It feels better here,” said Lenna, as she paused before looking up. She pressed her lips together and just nodded.

“Let’s go get noodles,” I said with a grin.

“Don’t you have some stored away?” she asked, trying not to chuckle.

“Yeah, but that’s not the point. Those are emergency noodles.” Those treats would stay in my inventory until a very important moment, then I would eat them. Probably.

I started to the portal, but Lenna snagged my hand. I slowed down as she intertwined her fingers with mine.

“You okay?” I asked, just before entering the glowing light.

“Just want us to stay together…” she added.

Then I touched the light.

Something yanked me through much quicker than before. Lenna, thankfully, went with me, and I found myself in a place I shouldn’t be.

We should have appeared in a small courtyard in the massive city. Not a small cavern with a familiar campfire.

Two bright yellow eyes stared at me from the darkness.

Lenna's hand tightly clenched mine, almost cutting off the feeling in my fingers.

“There you are,” whispered the deep voice. He stepped into the light and shifted into the form closer to our height. The face armor remained, along with the six legs and sharp stinger.

“Hai. This was unexpected,” I said, trying to think of why we were here.

“Alex, you have crossed the line into the beginning of divinity. Congratulations. That is a major step on your journey.” He paused and stared at me for a moment. “I apologize for my interruption, but I fear my children have gotten themselves into trouble. Along with some of your lineage.”

I paused and tried to figure out what he meant. “Wait, what did Kabi do?”

Hai chuckled, and the sound echoed around the cavern.

Lenna's hand tightened again. I squeezed it twice in return, and she relaxed a little.

“Oh, not that young one. Unfortunately, his father is a bit rash.” He shook his head, and gazed into the fire.

“Havi?” I asked, stepping close. “He left with Manee to find the…”

I let out a sigh as my voice trailed off. I saw where this was heading.

“Yes, the Forgers, and he recruited your father to help.” His statement hung in the air as my mind struggled to catch up. That shouldn’t have been possible.

“You said they are in trouble,” said Lenna, into the silence.

“Yes. They have gotten themselves caught in, what was that phrase, a tight spot. It isn’t my place to interfere, but Alex here is much closer with her lineage." Hai turned to look at Lenna, leaning a little closer. “Ah, a pathfinder. It’s good to see that path has opened up again. Too many need guidance. Like Manee.”

At her name, Lenna pressed her lips together. “She needs to heal her pain, not try to destroy those that caused it.”

Hai’s tail flickered behind him. “She walks a path I do not follow.”

“I’ve seen it,” said Lenna, looking away from the First of Kabi’s people like she saw something in the distance. “It leads to more pain if she isn’t careful.”

Hai bowed his head a little.

“Back to my Dad. He’s with Havi and Manee… where?” I asked, trying to figure out how the heck we would find them.

“Yes, he is with the two of them, and others, but I cannot tell you where. Kabi can lead you there,” he said with a frown. “He is in Lakeside.”

I nodded. “Thank you for this information. My family is important to me.”

“The portal will take you to Lakeside, that's the best I can do.” He nodded at me and stepped back into the shadows. His eyes grew larger. “May your Hungry Shadows hunt well.”

Then his eyes blinked out.

Lenna yanked me toward the portal before I really thought about it. Then, we were standing in the bright sunlight in front of the portal in Lakeside that led to Kabi’s world.

The sunlight made me blink, then Strange woke up and darted away from my very small shadow. He sprinted to the trees and vanished, heading away from the settlement and toward a small cluster of smaller dinosaurs in the distance.

No one waited for us, though my senses spread quickly across my territory. Abby and John were in the workshop. Others were all over the place, including Benny, who worked with others in the orchard.

I led the way to town and waved at Hammy, who guarded the entrance. He stood inside his mech suit, but carried a gun in his hands. He waved back with a metal hand. It caused me to slow down.

“Hey, Hammy,” I said, trying to sound normal.

“Alex, it's so good to see you! You're not going to believe the strides I’ve made on my mech suit. I cannot wait for my turn with the dungeon,” he said with a grin. He lifted up the gun. “I have so much more firepower now, and John and I are working on potentially making it fly!”

“In that?” I asked, glancing at his suit from head to toe. The solid metal had to weigh a ton.

“There is a lighter version that is more like armor. Kinda like superheroes from before…” his voice trailed off, before his face brightened again. “But you! You are so far beyond me.”

I scratched the back of my neck. “Just following my path where it leads me.”

“Shouldn’t you be leading it?” he asked, before smiling at Lenna. “Look at you, holy smokes! You’re higher leveled than your parents now, right?”

“I might be,” she said with a frown. “I was pretty close before I left, and, well…”

“You got into a mess with Alex,” he said with a chuckle. “That’s always how it happens. I hope you guys stick around this time. It’s been too quiet.”

“Quiet is good,” I said, still distracted by his metal hand. It should heal when he ranked up. The goal was for people to be safe here. “I need to catch up with Abby.”

The portal flared to life on my senses, and out stepped a friend, another who had gotten injured, but right in front of me.

Lenna tilted her head at my sudden freeze.

“Kabi’s here,” I explained.

Strange darted out of the trees and in his direction. “Strange is grabbing him, we can wait here.”

It didn’t take long for him to join us, though Hammy’s eyes went wide at the giant man. Even with the extra height from the mech suit, Kabi stood taller.

It dawned on me he wasn’t hunching over anymore, and his lost limb didn’t have any bandages on it.

“Damn, man, you are massive,” said Hammy, staring up at him.

To my surprise, Kabi started laughing, though it sounded a little strained. “To some of my people, I am small.”

Hammy just shook his head as Kabi’s laughter faded. He grew serious as he glanced at me.

“We can chat in the workshop,” I said, then motioned for him to follow me. “This way.”

The hike through town only took a few moments, and we didn’t run into anyone else. That was intentional, as I wound through the back streets instead of the main road to the plaza.

The door to the workshop sat open, and I walked inside.

Abby glanced up sharply as I entered. “Alex, we have a problem…” her voice trailed off when she spotted Kabi. “What trouble are you bringing?”
