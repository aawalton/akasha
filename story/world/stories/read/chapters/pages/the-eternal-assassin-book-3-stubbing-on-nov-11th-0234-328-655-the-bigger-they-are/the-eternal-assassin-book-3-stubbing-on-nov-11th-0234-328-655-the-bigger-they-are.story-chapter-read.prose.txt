The team finished their preparations the next morning quickly enough. Fresh layers of Polly’s magical bug repellant were rubbed into their skin and clothing. Glass helmets with their own air supply were donned on heads. Moxy begrudgingly put on Asher’s Ring of False Death yet again, just in case she ended up contracting the lich’s terrifying poison-disease hybrid. And Kroult cast his immune-system-boosting spell on each of them to top everything off. In no time at all the five of them were decked out and ready to go lich hunting, and Asher took a deep breath of artificial air as he looked around between them all.

“Alright. As always, the main objective is to find and kill Jormon. The lich has some sort of giant, most likely horrifying monster hidden away within that fort of his. Ideally, we’ll be able to slip past it and enter the fort without having to fight it. However, I think it’s safe to assume Jormon will have made it so that’s not possible. Otherwise, what’s even the point in having a giant guard monster in the first place?”

“So we kill the monster, continue onward, and kill the lich,” Moxy said, punching her knuckles together eagerly. “Simple enough.”

“I hate that we have no idea what we’re going up against,” Rulfar muttered, gripping his battle axe as he sighed. “A lack of information is the worst.”

“The walls are too thick for me to slip through via the astral realm, and while it looks like we could fly up above the wall, I’d bet every shard in my soul he’s got some sort of defensive measure in place against fliers,” Asher explained. “We’re going to go in straight through the front door and roll with the punches as they come.”

“My kind of fighting!” Moxy laughed. “What are we waiting for, let’s go!”

Everyone seemed to be on the same page, and Asher saw no point in waiting any longer. Jormon had already received an additional three-weeks of unlife than he should have gotten, even if the lich wasn’t aware of it himself. Forming a Spatial Lock around the group, Asher triggered Recall, pulling them all halfway across the continent as they appeared barely a dozen feet away from the lich’s wooden fort in the heart of the forest.

“I don’t hear or feel the monster,” Asher whispered, motioning for everyone to head for the giant door. “Maybe it’s sleeping!”

“That would be a nice break for a change,” Samantha said. Her wings were already manifested and ready to move should the need arise, and she had an arrow nocked and prepared to fly.

Without needing to be told, Rulfar shifted into his battle form, his already large frame stretching and growing as he turned into a ten-foot-tall hybrid between man and bear. Thankfully, his elongated face was still able to fit within the glass helmet, but it was definitely a close thing. Planting his feet in front of the door, the bloodhound sucked in a large breath of air before slamming his hands into the door, pushing with all his might and forcing it to gradually slide open one foot at a time.

“Unlocked door,” Asher muttered as Rulfar strained. He let go of his void dagger that he’d been preparing to use as a backup plan to eat through the door’s hinges if they found it to be locked. “You guys think that’s good or bad?”

“I think it means the lich isn’t too concerned about anyone breaking into his incredibly well-hidden base located in the heart of a deadly forest,” Moxy snorted, cracking her knuckles as she bounced on the balls of her feet. “Don’t read too much into it.”

Deciding Moxy was probably right, Asher focused on the task before them. Crescent and Nail were held at the ready, and he waited as patiently as he could for Rulfar to finish up. Eventually, the bloodhound let out a powerful grunt as he forced the doors open to the point where even his large form could squeeze between them, and he panted as he stumbled back, grabbing his battle axe that he’d set down.

“By the realms, those things weigh a ton,” Rulfar grumbled. He tried to wipe the sweat from his brow on his own fur-covered arm, but only managed to knock his head back as he bonked into his own helmet. “For the love of… Just don’t ask me to close them.”

“Everyone stay back, let me scout,” Asher instructed, ignoring the complaints from Moxy as he slipped into the astral and walked through the open door. As soon as he was through, he paused, making sure not to move anything beyond his head as he looked around.

The distance between the outer wall and inner fort was pretty significant, with a good few hundred feet stretching between the two. There was a dense layer of bugs buzzing around the place, and many of them were already starting to drift over to the opening in the door as they investigated the new smells. Deciding to trust in Polly’s magical bug repellant, Asher ignored them, looking for any sign of the giant monster.

It wasn’t hard to find.

His eyes widened and he took a reflexive step back as he spotted the largest living thing he’d ever seen. The creature looked like a four-story-tall lizard, complete with six legs, each one of which was thicker than Rulfar in his battle form. Its tail was long and muscular, ending with a jagged stinger that looked like the world’s largest needle. The monster was almost three times as long as it was tall from head to tail, and it sat only a little ways off to the side, turning its head as it stared at the opening that Rulfar had just created. Yet despite its terrifying size and barbed tail that looked like it could pierce through a foot of solid steel, neither of those two things were what really gave Asher pause.

No, that would be the obvious sign of intelligence in its eyes as it stared at the open door, its skin rapidly shifting color to match the surrounding landscape as it all but vanished from sight.

Running back out of the door, Asher returned to the material realm, shuddering as he shook his head.

“Okay! Good news, I saw the monster.”

The team waited for a moment, until they realized that was the full extent of the good news.

“…What’s the bad news?” Rulfar asked.

“It’s four stories tall, looks like it could eat Rulfar in a single bite, can turn invisible, and I’m pretty certain has some semblance of intelligence,” Asher said, giving them all a nervous smile. “Oh, and it has a tail with a huge stinger on the end of it bigger than any sword I’ve ever seen. I’d say it’s probably poisoned, but if anyone gets hit with that thing, poison is going to be the least of your worries.”

“What do you know, that actually sounds like something of a challenge,” Moxy grinned, raising an eyebrow at the couple of bugs that flew out of the door and tried landing on her. Moments before their feet could grab her clothing, Polly’s repellant kicked in, and they died in midflight, falling to the ground. “Damn, Polly’s stuff is high quality.”

“I’m willing to bet the thousands of bugs I saw in there are the real layer of defense,” Asher said, peering between the open doors at the distant fort. “Samantha, I think I know how this is going to play out, but just in case... Can you try firing an arrow at the central fort? If we could use plan B to Recall straight past the monster and go after the lich, that would be ideal.”

“Sure,” Samantha shrugged. She handed him an arrow to imbue with an Eternal Mark before taking careful aim and letting it fly, even going so far as to trigger her bow’s piercing enchantment. The arrow whistled across the ground, but before it could make it even halfway, dozens of hard-shelled bugs swarmed up out of nowhere with astonishing speed, intercepting the arrow and allowing themselves to be blasted to bits in order to stop it dead in its tracks. As the arrow fell to the ground, nowhere near close enough that Asher could make it to the fort even if he Recalled to its position, he sighed.

“Figures it wouldn’t be that easy. Alright, we do this the old-fashioned way. It’s probably for the best we take the monster out regardless. If this thing is the lich’s guard dog, I wouldn’t want to see what sort of destruction it would cause once the lich is dead and it manages to escape. There are villages bordering the forest just like how Horntho Village did, it wouldn’t sit well with me if we skipped past the monster and let it destroy innocent lives.”

“Agreed,” Samantha said, giving him a warm smile as she nocked another arrow. “Monster first, then Jormon. Everyone ready?”

“Let’s do it!” Moxy said, surprising Asher by holding out her hand. “One potion of Sensitivity, if you would, Runt. You know what, better yet, just pull me into your rift for a second. Can’t exactly drink a potion out here with this helmet on.”

“You’re actually relying on a potion for something?” Asher asked, taking her hand and doing just that. After counting to ten, he pulled her back out, shaking his head as he gave her a hard look. “What the hell happened while I was dead these last few weeks?”

“If we’re fighting an invisible opponent, this is an excellent chance to train my ability to sense fluctuations in the air,” Moxy explained, tossing him the empty vial. “No sense letting a good training opportunity go to waste.”

“Maybe focus on taking down the monster first, and training second,” Asher said, before he slipped into the astral and left the safety of the outer walls. Walking ten feet inside, he looked around, frowning as he failed to find any sign of the monster whatsoever. The fact that something multiple times larger than the house he’d grown up in could simply vanish from sight was absolutely horrifying. While he had Identify and Secret Seeker to rely on, neither of those skills worked on things in the material realm while he was in the astral. Same went for his Shard Detection, but he had a strong hunch the monster’s strange camouflage would make the skill worthless as well.

“Alright,” he muttered, holding up his daggers as he prepared for anything. “Let’s see where you’re hiding, shall we?”

Letting himself shift back into the material, Asher nearly had a heart attack as the space barely a dozen feet to his left shimmered in the air, revealing two giant, narrowed eyes as the monster tried to sweep its tail through him. He had plenty of time to slip into the astral and dodge the attack, only to make a rather unpleasant discovery at the last second.

The monster was so ridiculously large that its tail had too much mass for him to phase through.

The monster’s powerful tail smashed into Asher’s side with all the force of a runaway truck, shattering most of the bones on that half of his body before sending him flying like a ragdoll. Asher snapped out of the astral realm as he soared, the sudden movement draining far too much of his energy as he traveled unintentionally through the astral.

Ow, he thought, catching his wounded body from crashing into the ground with a quick trigger of his Boots of Levitation. Carefully pulling a potion of Lesser Restoration out from his rift inside his helmet, he downed the liquid as fast as he could, his eyes widening as he watched the monster chase after him.

While he was currently hovering about ten feet off the ground, that wasn’t even at eye level of the hulkish monster. As his bones and flesh knit themselves back together from the combined efforts of Eternal Regeneration, his amulet of Improved Healing, and his recently downed potion, he had just enough time to whip his void dagger out of its sheath with his right arm.

The monster’s tail stabbed forward this time, clearly intending to finish him off by running him through with its impressive stinger. Timing his defense perfectly, Asher ignored the sting of his void dagger eating away at his tritessence as he turned the flat side of the blade toward the oncoming attack.

Rather than run him through, the long, jagged stringer struck the flat of the void dagger held in front of Asher’s chest, and was completely consumed by the neverending hunger of the void.

The monster yanked its tail back in shock, but the damage was already done. Almost the entire spear-like stinger on the tip of its tail had been eaten away by the void dagger, and the monster let out another earth-quaking roar as Asher’s body finished repairing itself.

Before he could shout to his team to get in there, an explosion of air from the doorway signaled reinforcements as a ten-foot-tall wall of muscle wielding a gleaming battle axe shot through the gap in the doors. Rulfar let loose his own cry as he combined the momentum from Moxy’s Air Blast and his own immense strength to cleave his battleaxe straight through one of the monster’s thick legs, severing it and causing blood to literally explode out of the wound as his ravage enchantment got to work.

“Don’t forget about us!” Moxy laughed, blasting herself after Rulfar as she began throwing out a wild barrage of Air Blasts, each one ripping a fist-sized chunk of flesh from the monster’s massive hide. “Damn, we might be here a while!”

“We’ll spend as long as it takes!” Samantha said, shooting through the doors with her wings before beginning to rain piercing arrows down into the monster’s hide as it continued roaring.

“I think I will sit this one out,” Kroult called out from just outside the doors, the lich giving them a calm thumbs up from his safe position. “Just know I believe in you all. Go team.”
