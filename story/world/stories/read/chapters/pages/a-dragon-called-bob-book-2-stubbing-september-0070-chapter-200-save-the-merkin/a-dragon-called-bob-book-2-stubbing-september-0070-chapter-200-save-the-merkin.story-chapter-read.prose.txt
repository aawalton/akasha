Bighorn was willing to sign on as a minion, and that dude was heavy. Lutratown was on the southeast side of the island, far away from where the Armada had anchored. I had a werewolf and an inappropriately named minotaur perched on my shoulders as I began my descent.

“That’s the Water Palace,” Bighorn yelled. I assumed he was pointing at something, but coming in for a landing while toting a ton or so of roast beef on my neck didn’t give me the option to check.

Still, I could make a decent guess where he was referring to. Otters really love clams. The clamshell motif dominated their architecture. The buildings were two halves of scalloped wood, with sea-smoothed pebbles somehow plastered into the cracks, that met in the middle to offer protection from the elements.

The sun hadn’t quite yet risen, so the town was lit with an electric glow. It was different to the effect caused by lightorbs. The magitech balls cast a solid glow, whereas what the otters were using flickered like old-fashioned lightbulbs from back home.

The bay sparkled in the light from the dying moon and the glimmers of the sun on the horizon, and at the point where water met land, a large complex had been built. It looked like a damn water park. Slides and chutes led down from the upper levels of the shell-like buildings, ending in the water dozens of metres offshore.

Some of them even had upturned lips that would launch anyone who went down them into the air. Otters liked to have fun, it seemed.

We landed in the central square set just inland of the Water Palace. The handful of sleek-looking critters out at this ungodly hour scurried away as I descended, squeaking loudly in fear.

Once my passengers had disembarked, I shifted back into a human body and pulled on some clothes. There was a chill to the air. Being further south, I would have thought this place would be warmer, but it felt colder than Ratville.

“In the name of the Shell, stay where you are!”

The guard captain led a detachment of fifteen otters. None of them wore armour beyond what looked like bone skullcaps, like… fingerbones glued together into a cone. They all carried shiny-looking halberds that they levelled on us as they formed a cordon.

“They’re kind of cute.”

“Tricky fighters,” Bighorn rumbled in response.

“Lutrakin. I am Moon Shiver returned. I demand an audience with the King of Clams.” Ali stepped forward, and the sleek-furred guys stepped back. They were short, beady black eyes set in fur that was various shades of brown and grey.

“She who gushes in pleasure is gone and dead,” the captain replied, stepping forward. They might have been maybe nipple-high on me if they stood up straight, but the halberds they wielded were normal size.

“She who… Ali, I’m really starting to worry about your history. Look, let’s cut out the bullshit. I’m Bob, dragon, imperial ambassador, baron, entrepreneur and all-around nice guy. Take me to your leader.”

“A human noble, a hornhead, and someone claiming to be the Uniter returned. It’s like the start of a bad joke. I’ll escort you out of town now. If you don’t comply, you’ll end up in the chum-pools.” His soldiers drew in closer, but none of us moved in response.

“There’s always one bloody guard…” I muttered.

“Wait, Bob.”

“I’m not going to kill him, Ali. I still need to get a level before I can kill anyone.”

“You know the trial, captain. You’re welcome to kill me.” Ali took a few more steps forward until the tip of the halberd was nestled between her middle pair of breasts.

All these anthropomorphised critters were hard to read. Dragon's facial expressions were straightforward to read, and humans were well known to me. But five-foot-tall otters who seemed to communicate through whisker twitches and flashes of sharp front teeth.

The weapon was withdrawn slightly, the tip dropping a little to hover over her navel, then it plunged forward. I winced. Gut stabbed was a cruel way to go. Bighorn slammed a fist against his chest and mooed loudly, making the windows shake in the nearby houses.

“Jesus, just finish her quickly, you vicious little bastard.” The captain looked up at me before twisting the spike that topped the weapon back and forth slowly in Ali’s guts. He spun backwards, and the axe blade flashed through Ali’s neck.

Her head fell and rolled to my feet as her body dropped to the ground. I nudged the head back to the now vacant neck and crouched down to watch. Tiny filaments of flesh reached out and began to drag the head back into position, drawing it closer to the neck. The otter stepped forward and kicked the head away again, swinging the butt of his weapon into my head in the process.

He looked down at me in surprise when I didn’t budge. God forged scales for the win once again. I rose slowly to my feet and smiled a toothier grin than usual for my mammal suit.

“Mooder and mayhem!”

My peace mission was rapidly turning to shit. A decapitated werewolf, how long that would take to heal, I couldn’t guess, and a rampaging minotaur.

The otters moved fast, swarming around us. The long spike that crowned their weapons flickered out to plunge into Bighorn as he passed through them, eliciting bellows of pain. The captain tried his decapitation move on me, but I stepped forward, using Ali’s corpse as a springboard to launch myself towards him.

“Augendae Vitae! Abnegant Mortem! Sanara Vulnera! Systema Tarda!”

Green light enveloped the bull, and he hissed in pleasure as he caught a halberd and yanked it out of the otter's hands, spinning it around in one hoof to clear some space from his attackers. His size and reach gave him a decisive advantage.

I barrelled into the captain, sending his weapon spinning away. Claws raked at my arms and ribs as I brought back my fist to punch his cute little face into semi-sentient mush.

The point of another halberd slipped past my wrist and twisted, deftly locking my arm between the shaft and the curved axe head. Another one stabbed me in the chest, knocking me backwards just enough for the slippery little bastard underneath me to squirm away and bounce back to his feet.

“I’m starting to get annoyed now. Big guy? Kill.”

Bighorn now had a halberd in each hand and was spinning about like a tornado, trying to smash the otters away. The ones that he had disarmed had pulled long triple-clawed knife-gloves. They dropped to all fours and scmapered in inder the halberds and leapt on the minotaur, one on his front, one on his back. The claws sank into his muscles.

He dropped the weapons and grabbed the otter off his chest, throwing it down and stamping down with a gleaming hoof that pulverised the otter's chest.

“Augendae Vitae! Abnegant Mortem! Sanara Vulnera! Systema Tarda!”

Another round of healing bathed him in a green glow, and he leapt up, coming down on his back to double up his otter-pancake. Then a metal spike tried to slide through my eye and into my brain. I ducked backwards and shifted back into my real body.

A second later, I’d bowled over the otters nearest me, and my tails snaked out to snatch them up and hold them off the ground. The captain had dodged back, but I beat my wings to lunge forward and pin him down with one paw. I leaned down, my face less than a foot from his now terrified eyes and treated him to a real grin.

“Moistclaw! Stop!” the captain squeaked. I glanced across and found that of the half dozen otters that had been harrying my bull, only two were left, and at the captain’s words, both of them darted backwards and kept their distance. Four otters had been pounded into a large furry pizza on the street.

“Bighorn, leave them.” Steam didn’t, in fact, come out of his ears as his breathing started to slow, but I felt like it ought to have done. The veins that had bulged out on his neck, chest and biceps began to fade back into the oversized steaks he called muscles.

“There’s always one annoying little shit who gets in the way. Is it some kind of secret brotherhood? Like the Spankers of Bulb, but for suicidal guards.”

“Kill me then. But spare my warriors.” He spat up at my snout.

“Bob.” Ali croaked from behind me. I looked over, and my jaw dropped open.

“Tiny.”

“It will be back to normal in an hour.” Her snout was perhaps a third of the correct size, and her skull looked like someone had stitched a child version of her head onto her neck. It swelled slightly as I stared.

I stepped back, releasing the captain who leapt to his feet and stared at Ali as my claw and arm withdrew.

“She who walks the streets… It is you.”

“Yes. Well done on your very thorough application of the trial.” She rubbed at her chest. “Did someone stand on me?”

“Erm, it was that guy.” I pointed a free tail at the guard captain and hit him with Hunter’s Gaze as his mouth opened. I was shedding gold sparks like a fountain.

“Release him, Bob.”

“You’re not the boss of me.”

“Bob?” She raised a miniature eyebrow as she drew next to me.

“I can’t. It’ll wear off. I only gave him a weak blast.”

“Mistress. I avenged you many times.” Bighorn came over and went to one knee. “You have a puppy head.”

She reached out a hand and patted him between the horns, earning a happy moo. “You can’t avenge what cannot die. But thanks anyway. Captain, your name?”

“Manyclams. Mistress, can you get your dragon to release my lutras?”

“Told you it wouldn’t last long.”

“It was the beast that trod on your mammaries, Mistress.” I shot him a regular glare, but lowered his otters to the ground gently and shifted back to my mammal suit.

“Red,” gorlwed Bighorn.

“Dammit, give me a second to get some trousers on.”

“Manyclams, your people are at war with the merkin?”

“The king says they’ve been raiding and killing our fishers. We’ve only sought justice and secured our clambeds for the future of the Lutra people.”

“The merkin—” I sniggered, earning an angry look from the pair of them, “—have always been fair about the ocean. They aren’t crammed in like the rest of us. The rats are still at peace with them, and the Odobenus and Pinnipedia Alliance are allied with the merkin.”

The otter spat to one side. Now they weren’t attacking; they were back to looking adorable. “They have been fighting against us. Forgive me, I’m just a soldier. If you want to speak to someone who can answer these questions, you need to talk to the Master of the Romp.”

“Romp?” I fell in beside Ali as Manyclams led us towards the Wet and Wild that they called a palace.

“Romp on land, raft on water,” she said. The wall around the palace was tall and fluted at the top, and the gates opened as the captain approached. Beyond the palace were shell-like buildings stacked on top of one another.

“Is raft some kind of euphemism?” I asked as we passed into the secluded compound.

“No, Bob. Next time, why don’t you just put the guards to sleep?”
