Luis, Naomi, and the others were ready and waiting when the invisible airship arrived, appearing as a faint shimmer in the twilight sky as it descended to meet them. The valley rendezvous was in a strange quadrant dominated by towering pine trees bristling with metal needles that scraped against the hull as it descended into the small clearing they'd designated as their meeting point. The location wasn't far from the quadrant node, where Tara had asked for her companions to be dropped off before their act of larceny, and Jade was worried that it would be the first place any pursuers would look for them.

"Are they in, yet?" she asked, craning her neck to look into the rear compartment. The small hatch afforded a poor view, but she could hear the thumps of footsteps against the rear ramp.

"We're all good, amiga!" Luis's head appeared around the side of the door frame. "Next time, ask us to wait somewhere that doesn't try to turn people into pincushions."

"There'd better not be a next time." Jade said, gesturing for him to strap into one of the jump seats. "Piracy doesn't agree with my stomach."

"Are you sure that isn't just Seris's flying?" Tara called from somewhere out of sight.

"Tara…" Seris flicked a switch, and Jade saw the rear hatch indicator change from "open" to "closed". "Do you want a pilot for your crazy mission or not?"

"It's a joke!" She replied, still out of sight. "Humor builds bonds of camaraderie. Strong social bonds increase group cohesion, that's why we spend years in the academy together prior to forming teams!"

"Says the person who left the academy to find her team."

There was a moment of silence.

"…You may have a point." Tara conceded. "Naomi, do I need to work on my social skills?"

"We like you how you are, Tara. If we can put up with Jade, you have nothing to worry about."

"I heard that." Jade rolled her eyes as the others laughed. "Seris, let's get out of here before anyone comes looking."

Their first stop, if you didn't count a detour to avoid a wing of tek'kalla airships patrolling the skies in search of their stolen vessel, was a Labyrinth Sanctuary. They had promised Seris a chance to talk to the Labyrinth attendants about their task, and Jade still had to show him the divine relic Elaria had entrusted them with. Since they didn't know how detectable its magic was, she'd decided to keep it in her dimensional pouch until they were inside, just in case. After a little searching, they located a Sanctuary entrance on an island in a quadrant dominated by deep gorges and rushing rivers. Seris impressed Jade by neatly setting the large craft down on what turned out to be less of an island and more like an overgrown sandbar, accomplishing what she would have sworn was a perilous landing with apparent ease. Either the ship handled much better than it looked like it should, or he was simply that good at flying it. Possibly both.

They opted to enter the Sanctuary together, having flown far enough from the tek'kalla city that no search party would find their reappropriated airship anytime soon, and Luis took charge of going over the plan a second time. The large man's usual good cheer was absent as he laid out their strategy for confronting the demon lord and how they planned to reach him. Damos made an appearance as well, staying just long enough to confirm their story was accurate. The Labyrinth Spirit had an unusually haggard appearance, and Jade could only imagine the chaos that was befalling whatever divine mechanisms drove this strange world.

"So," Seris mused, once they had finished. He looked between Jade and Luis. "it's not quite as simple as you made it sound."

"Nothing in the Labyrinth is simple." Jade said, crossing her arms. "But it's the best plan we have."

"It's way better than the goddess's first plan of letting you go in there by yourself." Naomi said. "There are so many ways that could go wrong."

"And this plan is safe?"

"It's safer." She insisted, emphasizing the last syllable. "And now that we have a fancy airship, we have a good chance of sneaking out before anybody realizes what's happened."

Jade had a feeling that things wouldn't be that easy, but she had given up on trying to dissuade her friends from joining her. The safety of their homes and loved ones was at stake too, and it would be selfish to deny them the right to see this through. If for no other reason than because Naomi was right, they had a much better chance of pulling this off together than Jade did alone.

That… and because Jade needed them. She could admit that to herself, now. She'd faced danger before, but nothing like this. The idea of confronting the strongest demon in the cosmos alone was a terrifying prospect, even if she possessed a weapon specifically tailored to defeat him. No matter how weak his current circumstances left him, even though he was secluded in isolation, separated from his many subjects, Jade knew she couldn't do it by herself. She remembered the vision that had manifested upon first touching the death fragment on the second floor, an imposing figure shrouded in shadow, burning eyes boring into her soul, and suppressed a shudder.

"No, I actually like it." Seris said. He was holding the Destruction splinter out for her to take, and Jade realized she must have missed part of the conversation. "It's just… hard to come to terms with this. It's a lot to take in at once."

"Join the club." Jade said.

"But I understand why secrecy is so important, now. The more people you bring in on this, the less likely it is to work."

"See?" Tara preened. "I told you he'd be onboard."

"What made you so sure?" Seris asked, raising an eyebrow at his friend as Jade accepted the relic, tucking it away in her pouch.

"Well, you're a test pilot." she shrugged. "Test pilots are all about doing crazy stunts, right?"

"Wait, what does that make the rest of us?" Naomi asked.

"…Just as crazy?" Luis offered.

"Sounds right to me." Fenrin snorted.

"What would be crazy is having the chance to stop this war before it explodes and not taking it." Jade cut in. "Yeah, it's dangerous. But we have a good plan and the element of surprise." She nodded at Seris. "And now we have a fast way to get home. Most people would do the same, in our place."

Her friends looked at her with varying degrees of skepticism.

"No, Jade… they wouldn't." Naomi spoke after a short pause. "I'm not sure I would, if you weren't so set on this."

"You've always been one to throw yourself into the fire, so other people don't have to." Siora said. "You've been doing it since the day you agreed to take me home, even though I was a complete stranger."

"You are pretty crazy, Jade." Luis added, throwing an arm around her shoulders. "Hell, you walked into that goblin camp on the first floor just because you heard there might be people trapped there that needed help."

"That didn't work out so well for me." Jade reminded him, feeling a little embarrassed at the sudden praise.

"Sure it did! We're all still here, yeah? What more could you ask for?"

They were exaggerating. What her friends mistook for valor and selflessness was nothing but a mixture of overconfidence and poorly managed frustration at being thrown adrift in a world of monsters and danger. But it did feel good to know how much they trusted her. More than their words, the emotions she tasted behind them touched her.

"That's all true. But-" Tara said, raising a finger. "You're still a reckless idiot sometimes. I still can't believe you were really thinking about doing this all on your own."

"Yeah, think of all the trouble she'd get in without us around."

"Hey!" Jade protested. "I liked it better when everyone was praising me…"

"Don't look at me." Seris shrugged. "I just met you, but your first impression didn't exactly say cautious and conservative.""You did get him to steal an airship."

"That was your idea!"

Going over the revised plan took another hour, and by the time they were finished they were ready to turn in. The group collectively decided to spend the night in the Sanctuary, taking advantage of what might well be the last chance they'd have to rest in pleasant accommodations until this was over. If all went according to plan, they wouldn't need to find somewhere to sleep while outside the Labyrinth proper, but Jade knew better than to count on that. Sleep eluded her, so Jade lay awake on the roof of the stone tower that formed the centerpiece of this Sanctuary, gazing up at the star speckled sky and trying to still the tremor in her hands.

On the whole, she had been relieved to discover that the goddess's focus on her was simply due to chance. She just happened to be the one to accept a demon race at the right time, in the right place. There was nothing preordained about her lot, and any number of people could have been in her place. And yet, a selfish part of her wished that there really was something special about her. Maybe then, she wouldn't be so afraid.

Jade sighed, staring up at the tranquil sky. It really was beautiful, exactly the sort of thing Siora had told her to look for in the Labyrinth, all those months ago. She'd been so set on hating it, then, even promising to find a way to tear it all down. Now it really was coming apart, and she was fighting to prevent it. Who could've guessed that this world's existence was the lesser evil, protecting them from a ravenous and powerful enemy.

"By the time we're back, it won't matter anymore." She whispered to herself. "The demons will be gone, and the Labyrinth won't need to keep growing. Everything… can go back to normal."

It was a naïve wish. Earth's connection to the greater Universe wasn't going away, and Jade herself was a changed woman. Even putting aside her inhuman nature and the powerful magic now at her disposal, she had changed. She'd grown accustomed to spending her days battling monsters, could kill without hesitation, and regularly shrugged off wounds that would have horrified her past self.

How did someone like that just go back to living a normal life?

Is that even what I want?

Maybe she and Alan could move into the Labyrinth somewhere, if they found somewhere safe. The thought made her smile. He'd like that, especially if it came with the opportunity to learn magic. Could she convince Tara to teach him?

But that was getting ahead of herself. No, she had to remain focused on the task at hand. Tomorrow, they were diving into a world that had been lost to the rest of the Universe for thousands of years to confront a force the very gods had sealed away. Her friends were counting on her, and she wasn't going to let them down.

She couldn't.

Jade drew in a deep breath, reflecting on the bonds of camaraderie that compelled her companions to accompany her down this perilous path. She could do this. For the people that believed in her, she could do this.

When she exhaled at last, her hands were still.
