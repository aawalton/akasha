“That very much wasn’t the speech I had prepared for you, Mach,” Morgan says glibly.

We’re both lounging in her gallery, and she’s once again wearing her iconic greatcoat and heavy boots. The cloak means I don’t need to use furniture that’s closer to construction equipment.

“I know, but it seemed very stuffy. Sorry if I messed it up.”

“Oh, no, don’t worry about it. Like I said before, you’re powerful enough that anything strange you do is seen as eccentric. That’s the privilege of wealth and power. You’re never crazy. Besides, I don’t think anyone was that surprised, really.”

“There’s no way they didn’t notice how nervous I was. I swear, it felt like I was going to stumble over my words.”

“Can you even do that? I bet you figured out what you wanted to say in milliseconds.”

“Microseconds, if you really want to know. I might have gotten stuck overthinking it. Still, if I could sweat, I would have been sweating bullets.”

“Mach, you don’t sweat, you don’t shift your weight, and you don’t stutter. No one would know you were nervous unless you told them. I’m telling you, everyone else was a whole lot more nervous than you were. You scared the poor Cerulean Guard out of their minds.”

“I feel so bad about that. John actually stumbled down a few stairs. It’s my damn Aura. I need to get better at controlling it.”

“I don’t know if it’s your Aura, Mach. I think it’s you. You just… warp the world. You are the center of it, whether you want to be or not.”

I internally shiver at that. Memories of my duplicate in France surface again.

“That’s not what I want to be.”

“It’s not who you want to be,” Morgan corrects. “I think you’ll always be that, just a little bit. That’s the nature of life. We’re often things we don’t want to be. We have to keep fighting against parts of ourselves. But just as they always exist, they don’t always define us. We get to define ourselves with the choices we make. That’s the power of a soul. The ability to fight against what we are.

“But I’m curious, what did you think of the party? I know it’s not something you’re used to.”

“It was… alright. I still feel so out of place. I have to be so careful around everyone, it’s exhausting.”

“What do you mean?”

“Morgan, I weigh two tons. Even moving slowly, I could seriously hurt someone even just by tapping them. I really don’t like crowds.”

“And that’s why you snuck up to the roof.”

“That, and to get away from the constant barrage of people trying to get me to do things. Political hopefuls wanting endorsements, of all things, got tiring after the fourth one. Corporations offering contracts got annoying even faster. At least Palisade didn’t try to bother me again.”

Morgan grimaces at the name of the mercenary megacorporation.

“Yeah, I’m sorry about that. They’re too big to exclude from an event like that, and I figured they might try to toss a few bucks at the Academy as a peace offering.”

“Did it work?”

“We won’t know for a few days. I’ll ask Benny about it later. I’m feeling pretty good about it, though.”

We rest in companionable silence for a while. Morgan drifts off in her chair, the non-stop work of the last few days finally catching up to her. Even the prodigious endurance of an Ascended can only go so far. Even I have my limits, though I do wonder if I could ignore them. I’m capable of modifying my own thoughts to a degree. I could just carve away the parts of me that are tired. I could tear away the things that hold me back from just… going. I could just keep fighting.

It’d be nice to stop hurting, for just a moment.

I don’t, though. I barely managed to hold back, one metaphorical finger over the button. If I stop caring, who will I become? I think I know the answer. I’ve seen it. I think the pain is necessary. It tells me I’m still alive, in some fashion.

The night passes with us both in our chairs. Morgan’s soft breathing is the only sound in the gallery. She stirs in the morning, just after dawn breaks.

“Hrm? What?” She smacks her lips and groans. Still half-asleep, she fumbles with her coat. She throws back the energy drink she pulls out of a pocket, and finally cracks her eyes open.

“Oh, that’s the stuff.” She pulls a second one out and slugs that one back too. “Mach, have you been here all night?”

“Yes. I didn’t want to disturb you.”

She stands up with a spine-cracking stretch.

“What’s the planly, Stanely?”

“I was just going to clear some more Fractures around Denver, it’s time for me to get back to work. Time is of the essence.”

She hums, tapping a finger on her chin. “Why not try to clear some in the Bay Area? The Deep has got some pretty powerful Fractures, could be something interesting in them. Not to mention how often they keep rupturing. One of these days something they can’t hold back is going to come out of one. They’re barely standing as it is.”

“Second Squad won’t be able to help me out with the science stuff. The Fracture is going to be underwater.”

“Mach, buddy, you just made friends with a team of 2-Stars that can help you out underwater. Go ask the Cerulean Guard. I guarantee they’ll help you for a multitude of reasons.”

“Alright, fine. But I do want to know more about the Hydroponic Arcology, how’s that coming?”

“Mach,” Morgan says dryly. “We’ve been working on it for less than two weeks. We need more time. Go do some science shit. Once we know a bit more about them and the design, I’ll be able to give you some more concrete information on how it’s going.”

“Alright. Send me to the Bay, please?”

A mid-air painting made of light later, and a portal appears. I step through onto the Academy’s grounds.

Guess I should find the Cerulean Guard.

The Adventurer’s Guild—wouldn’t Empowered’s Guild be more apt?—seems to be the rapidly growing hub for Empowered. It’s mostly an informal society rather than a company or an official governing body. It’s mostly been used as a place for Empowered to find others to team up with, and as a place to sell what they pull from Fractures.

They even have a physical location in San Francisco, though it’s their only one. Probably one of the reasons they never reached out to me. They started here in the Bay Area, and haven’t spread out yet. Travel to other fortress cities is difficult and costly. That doesn’t even count how expensive purchasing property is these days.

The Cerulean Guard doesn’t have any public contact information, and I’m not about to tear my way into a couple of data bases out of convenience. I call the Guild, hoping they might be able to put me in contact.

“Hello, this is the Adventurer’s Guild, the premier society for humanity’s greatest defenders. How can I help?”

“I’m looking for the Cerulean Guard, I’d like to discuss something with them.”

“I can forward a message for you, of course. They aren’t taking public calls at this time, unfortunately. I hope you understand. Who should I tell them is calling, and what about?”

“*Machina. I want their help with something.”

“…Excuse me? Who did you say was calling?”

“I’m Machina. Looking for the Cerulean Guard.”

“The Machina? The one who saved New York?”

“I didn’t know there was another.”

“…I’ll go see if they’re here. Can I place you on hold?”

“Sure, no problem.”

Faint, barely intelligible music plays over the phone line. I start to jog down the crowded streets. The Guild’s headquarters are located in the Ferry Building on the other side of the city. I take my place in the traffic flow, much to my amusement. The bus ahead of me is packed, and a young girl with her parents looks out the back window with wide eyes. She hesitantly waves, and I wave back. Her mother turns around and stares with equally wide eyes. She stares at me in shock until the bus pulls away with a jolt, and I take a turn the bus doesn’t.

Most of the civilian cars have been replaced with either buses or military trucks hauling goods. There’s too many people here, and not enough space for all their cars. The city ground to a halt under the massive influx of cars for a while before they banned nearly all personal vehicles. Most of the fortress cities have done the same thing.

That didn’t go over too well, but what other option was there?

My cloak billows behind me as I run through the city. Blue light crackles under my feet as the magic takes hold. It prevents my weight and pace from cracking the already battered road. The surge of heavy military vehicles has not been kind, and the asphalt has seen better days.

“Mister Machina, are you still there?”

“Just Machina, and yes.”

“Ah, yes, my apologies. Machina. Well, unfortunately the Cerulean Guard aren’t here at the moment, they’re currently in negotiations with someone. They aren’t available, unless this is an emergency…?” The receptionist sounds nervous.

“No, not at all. I was on my way over to meet them in person, in case they weren’t available over the phone. I could wait for them there. Would that be alright?”

“Uh, absolutely, please, feel free to come on by. Someone can be made available to offer a tour, if you’d like.”

“I hate to be a bother, I’m sure you’re very busy. I could wander around on my own, no problem.”

There’s some indistinct muttering on the other end of the line, and a new voice comes on.

“Hello, Machina, I’m Guildmaster Carrigan of the Adventurer’s Guild. I’d be happy to be your direct contact going forward. I’m more than willing to give you a personal tour of our headquarters.”

“I don’t want to impose.”

“Nonsense! It’s quite alright, and really, it’s well within my duties as Guildmaster anyway. When will you arrive?”

“Twenty minutes.” I tell her after querying my pathing algorithms.

“Perfect, perfect. I’ll meet you at the front gate! Can I assist you with anything else?”

“No, no. Thank you.”

“Of course, Machina. It’s my pleasure to assist.”

Kalliopi Carrigan, Guildmaster of the Adventurer’s Guild, meets me at the front gate of the Guildhall. The tall, wrought iron fence is clearly brand new. Nearly ten feet tall, it’s been put in place to make sure only a select few are allowed inside.

The first thing I notice about the Guildmaster herself is her 4-Star Aura. She floats in the air, hovering just above the ground. She’s no Morgan, though. She fights against the world’s rules. Morgan rewrites them.

I do too.

“Ah, Machina, it’s so good to finally meet you. I’m afraid I was unable to attend the gala yourself and Morgan hosted. I had other prior commitments that simply could not be postponed, I’m sure you understand. Shall we begin the tour?”

“Yes, I’d love that.”
