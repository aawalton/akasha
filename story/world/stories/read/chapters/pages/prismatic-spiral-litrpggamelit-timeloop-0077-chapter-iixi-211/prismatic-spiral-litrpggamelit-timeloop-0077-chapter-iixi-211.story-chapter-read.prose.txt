Chapter II.XI (2.11)

“Toll!” the troll roared as it used both hands to fling a boulder half the size of Mitsuko at her.

Rather than Mend it back in his direction, a technique she was well versed in at this point, Mitsuko danced to the side and let the stone projectile barrel past her. It slammed into a cliff face behind her, shattering into chunks.

She stood on muddy ground at the bottom of the gorge, having jumped down after announcing her presence to the troll. Unlike every other encounter with the mad creature, she held no sword in hand. After years of practicing with the blade, she decided relying on the weapon in this fight would only serve as a crutch if she wished to improve her magic. In a real fight, she’d obviously utilize every tool at her disposal to achieve victory. But in her mind this was a simple training spar. A deadly one, to be sure. But she’d experienced death enough times to not let that threat intimidate her. It would act as a whetstone to sharpen her less used talents and increase her options in future confrontations.

Madness danced in the old troll’s eyes. It stood nearly twice her height with its loose skin a pale shade of gray. She wasn’t well educated about trolls as a species and their varieties beyond knowing how to kill them, but that color didn’t look healthy. She knew from past experiences that his healing factor didn’t work nearly as quickly or effectively as younger members of his species. Though even if he was sickly, he still managed to chuck boulders at her with ease.

Not taking her eyes off her target, Mitsuko plucked a palm-sized stone out of the mud. She reeled her hand back, took aim, and pitched it forward with all her might. As it left her hand, Mitsuko accelerated the stone’s speed with a cast of Deteriorate.

It shot forward towards its fated mark, far swifter than any natural toss, and smacked into the troll’s torso. The impact sent belly fat rippling and made the troll stumble.

Mitsuko t’sked. Her attack hadn’t broken the skin.

Howling, the troll advanced. “My toll! Pay my toll!” He threw himself at her with the clear intent of smushing her under his considerable girth. Sick or not, the troll’s size was still huge.

Uninterested in becoming further acquainted with a naked troll belly and the muddy ground, Mitsuko cast Mend on the troll’s ragged shorts and dragged him backwards. She winced as the cloth ripped and threatened to tear off his body as he physically fought against the spell. Thankfully, the shorts were made of sturdier material than they appeared and they held together long enough for Mitsuko to reposition herself.

Releasing the spell before she was exposed to a display of the troll’s reproductive organs, Mitsuko pitched another stone at the troll, once again accelerated with magic. This time she aimed for his head.

Her aim was off. The stone clipped his ear, taking a chunk of cartilage, but otherwise only serving to anger the ancient creature.

“My toll! Give me toll! Bridge not free!”

“Yeah, yeah,” Mitsuko muttered. “Here, take your toll.”

Instead of throwing another stone, Mitsuko reached into her purse and pulled out a handful of doubloons. She tossed them into the air. The gold glittered as it passed through the beams of sunlight that broke the canopy above. Then they landed, scattered across the mud.

The troll stopped mid charge and gazed down at the shining bits of metal. For a moment, he seemed to forget about Mitsuko, which suited her just fine. He bent down and moved to scoop up the mud and gold.

Mitsuko cast Deteriorate on the coins. They shot out of the muck and pressed up against the Troll’s skin before he picked them up. She had hoped that the spell would move the coins with enough speed to puncture his flesh, but she lacked that power. The only reason the stone throwing had worked was because it was already propelling at speed and the spell multiplied that speed. The troll would have lifted the coins up far slower, so multiplying that speed had lackluster results.

Throwing more stones would probably work now that her enemy was temporarily pinned in place by the coins. The troll’s body healed far slower than one in its prime and she could probably force down with a hundred minor wounds. Death by a thousand cuts. But that strategy felt lazy. She wanted to push her spell in interesting ways.

The troll was currently confused as the coins continued to press up against his skin. They lacked the force to push him back, but he struggled to pick them off himself. His frustration clearly grew as he dug the coins out of his body and forced them out alongside chunks of skin, fat, and sinew.

As Mitsuko watched the troll tear himself apart to free himself from his prison, she reflected that she wasn’t actually all that different from the monster in some ways. She, too, would rip herself apart if it meant freedom. The timeloops had numbed her to permanent consequences. Each week likely brought her closer to the troll’s madness.

But that newfound strange sense of kinship between herself and the mad troll wouldn’t stop her from seizing victory here. It only took a glance to the side to spot snapped bones and old bits of humanoid clothing half buried in the muck. A reminder that this troll was a threat to all the creatures that lived on this island. Madness did not excuse the death of innocents.

“Fire and drowning,” she muttered to herself. Those were the two tried and true methods of killing a troll. Even beheaded was but a minor inconvenience for them. Granted, at this troll’s age and healing factor’s speed, it might be possible to slay it with more mundane methods. But nothing besides those two options was a guarantee.

In her last fights with it, she’d made it drown on its own blood. The only method available to a swordsman like her. But without her sword she lacked a valuable tool needed for that strategy.

The troll, now free of the gold coins' pressure, stepped over to the side of the gorge and gripped a stump sticking out of the mud.

His muscles flexed under the strain, but he pulled back, ripping the tree out of the muck with a loud squelch. He swung the dead tree and sent blobs of mud scattering across the gorge.

Mitsuko cast Mend on a blob of blackish mud inbound for her, returning it to the troll and keeping herself clean. Well, relatively clean. She had still ducked and rolled through the mud earlier at the start of the battle’s engagement.

The troll obviously thought that his new weapon would grant it an advantage on her. And, against a normal foe, it obviously would be correct in that assessment. A fulcrum for damage and a longer reach. But Mitsuko was far from a  normal foe.

The troll charged forward with its weapon raised. Mitsuko cast Deteriorate on the weapon the same moment as she darted to the side. The tree slammed into the mud where she’d stood moments earlier. The troll, his grip tight on his weapon, had been carried through alongside the attack and now stood in a hunched position with his neck exposed.

Mitsuko’s hand twitched. She resisted the urge to flick her wrist and create a sword of ice. This whole battle was meant to be a test of her magical might alone.

What else did she have at her disposal though? She glanced around at the gorge. A crumbled stone bridge overhead. Mud and the remains of the troll’s victims. The troll’s weapon.

“Pay the toll!” the troll roared. He swung the tree in her direction.

This time Mitsuko didn’t bother to step aside. She raised a hand to the side and cast Mend on the weapon. Immediately, the tree blasted backwards, as if ricocheting off of a barrier.

The troll stumbled and fell.

So far the only offensive use of Deterioration she’d found was throwing stones. That seemed wrong to her. She should be capable of more. But she couldn’t cast the spell on other living creatures like she could Mend. What options did that leave her? Maybe she should have kept Juniper around after all. The sage doubtlessly knew of more options.

“Well, I guess it’s back to throwing stones,” she muttered. She plucked a stone protruding from the mud and tossed it in the air. Catching it, she took aim at the troll’s neck. She just needed to pierce the juggler and hopefully it would choke on its blood from there.

But, just as Mitsuko was about to throw the stone, something caught her eye and she redirected her aim. Empowered by a Deterioration spell, the stone slipped out of her fingers and cracked the air as it darted forward at an untraceable speed.

The ground beneath her feet shook as the projectile made contact with its target.

The troll looked up just in time to see the crumbling stone bridge collapse over his head.

“Toll!” he cried out one last time before buckling under the sudden weight. At first, he struggled against the weight of the rubble, but then Mitsuko launched one more stone and a landslide buried him.

Once everything fell still, she still waited for several minutes before letting out a sigh of relief.

“That seemed a tad excessive,” Sterling commented from above her. “You know, I very nearly was standing on that shelf of dirt when it all fell down. You could have killed me!”

“You’re fine.” Mitsuko tossed a non-magically empowered stone straight up into the air and then caught it in her hand.

“You should be more mindful of collateral damage.”

Mitsuko cast Mend on the stone and let it carry her weight up the cliff. She swung her legs over the cliff’s lip and let go of the rock, both physically and magically.

With one last glance down at the unmoving rubble left in her wake, Mitsuko couldn’t help but feel just a twinge of guilt for killing the troll. But further surveying the area reminded her that this monster had been killing random people who walked near it for who knows how long. It wasn’t like she had any other options to end his rampage.
