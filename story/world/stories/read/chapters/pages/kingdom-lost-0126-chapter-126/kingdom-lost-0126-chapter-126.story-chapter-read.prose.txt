Chapter 126

Riley, Valrik, and Thorne stood inside the resource building at the heart of the Clawborn depot. They looked at one another while Riley thought. She stared down for a long moment, then finally raised her head.

"What do you mean we are burning daylight, it's still morning?" Valrik questioned.

Thorne shot him a sneer. "I mean let's get things moving. We don't have a ton of time."

"Agreed. Here's what we're doing. We've got equal parts of everything but gold. Food, wood, ore, stone. The Clawborn have massive mines, so stone and ore are less valuable to them, but food and wood are. So we move the most valuable first. Gold, then ore. And if we run out of time before the Clawborn arrive, we burn the wood. The fire takes the food with it, and the stone won't matter."

Valrik looked up. "What about the fallen? What about the men who've died?"

Thorne met his eye. "We do need to send them back to the community, get them into the hospital, get them resurrected. But the clock's ticking. Resources first."

Valrik said, "The resources won't spoil; they can wait for the fallen to be transported back."

Thorne replied, "The fallen will resurrect regardless of when we take them to the hospital. Today, tomorrow."

Valrik said, "If we take them first, then we have more hands to move the resources."

Riley looked down in deep contemplation, then stepped away while they went back and forth, each staking out his reasons, arguing their positions.

Riley muttered, "Relay."

Both Valrik and Thorne stopped arguing and looked at Riley.

"Re, what?" Thorne said.

"Relay. We have a road running from the depot to the Hoshin village which is a good chunk of the journey. The Clawborn resource wagon is in the yard. We can load it up with gold and the fallen. Move them to just before the Hoshin village, where the treeline hugs the road. Move the lot into the treeline out of sight. The Clawborn may go to Hoshin, so we can't leave them there."

Riley paused and looked up as she opened the HUD and summoned most of the army. She split them into two groups. The larger one would march to the rally point where they would dump the resources and place the fallen. The rest would come directly to the depot to help with loading the wagon and moving resources. All the cavalry she had in the group headed to the rally point; the second group would be soldiers on foot.

Riley tuned back into the conversation with Valrik and Thorne, both waiting.

"I have summoned the troops. I will create a waypoint for you to follow to where the fallen will be placed and the resources dumped."

Riley was conscious of the words she used when referring to the fallen. She was watching Valrik's face and was given little to go on.

Thorne was completely engaged and chomping at the bit to get things moving, and apparently oblivious to how his words were affecting others on this sensitive subject.

Thorne and Valrik listened as she gave her orders. She didn't make eye contact with either of them. She could see Valrik's shoulders had rolled forward, his eyes on the ground. The weight of the moment seemed to be coming down on him in a profound way.

When she finished, she said, "Alright. Are we ready?"

Valrik snapped to attention, his shoulders back. "At once." He walked out and began gathering the men, getting the Clawborn wagon ready.

Thorne looked at Riley. "These are the tough decisions that have to be made. You are right not to hesitate."

Riley didn't make eye contact with Thorne and started for the door. Her head was down, heavy with the burden and consequence of the choices she was making and faced with. She didn't turn back, but she paused at the threshold. "I don't think you realize how much these fallen have already gone through, or what is in front of them. The Clawborn will be coming for us, and the price they paid will save many in the battle to come. They give so much. How much more can I ask of them?"

She walked out.

***

Riley watched the men work, running the logic over in her mind. The Clawborn would send another wagon at least, maybe more. The smoke from the burning towers was rising, and it might well be visible from the citadel.

She pulled Thorne aside as he came out of the building. "Put out scouts. Three of them. One far ahead, two closer in. Make sure they're well hidden and that they can signal to each other. I want long advance notice if anything comes this way. Add one in the unburnt guard tower as a lookout."

Thorne nodded and went to carry it out.

Riley turned and scanned the rest of the depot. Paperwork, she thought. There has to be paperwork here. Information. She'd have Valrik or Thorne sweep the buildings and take every scrap. Any of it might be worth something once they had time to decode it. Sounded like a job for Nickola. None of the Clawborn were alive. There was nobody left to interrogate. Even the fourth tower, the guard tower, stood empty, the only one not burning. Its guards had come down for a final stand when the main gate went in.

She knew hard decisions were coming, and she knew she probably wouldn't get most of the resources back. She'd already run the alternatives. She couldn't build a silo this close to the depot and empty the haul straight into the system account. Too far from her tower. Moving it meant the woods, where there wasn't even a clear path. Even loading the saddlebags and having the soldiers carry what they could, it would be slow. Cutting a road through the woods would take longer still. At the very least she would deny the Clawborn their prize.

The collective grunt of the soldiers trying to move the wagon brought Riley out of her thoughts. She watched them work for a moment and her heart felt heavy.

***

Riley walked the field outside the depot among the Clawborn dead. Her troops had been collected and loaded onto the wagon; the gold was next, and then it would move. She looked up at the now just smoking guard's tower. The fires had burned off. These Clawborn would be some of the same that would be coming for her community, maybe all of them. She looked at their blister-burnt faces. The Clawborn would be coming for their fallen and would probably take these level four soldiers back to be revived. The ground was littered with them.

She looked down. "Don't hesitate."

These were the hard decisions she would have to make for hers. To protect her soldiers and the Hoshin, that was her duty, and the ugly things would be part of that. She pulled up the HUD and called for one of the soldiers to meet her in the field. Moments later he arrived.

"Ma'am?"

"Where is Thorne?"

"He is in the depot, making the final arrangements for the wagon to leave."

"Splendid. Tell him I want these Clawborn collected, stacked, and burned."

"Ma'am?"

"Burned with wildfire, burned until there is nothing left for the Clawborn to collect."

The soldier paused, his brow furrowed and his face hardened. Then he stood to attention and gave a single nod, turned, and marched back to the depot's gate.

Even the ugly things were now on the table to consider.

Clawborn Citadel wore the mountain like armor. From base to summit, its walls rose in slabs of dark stone cut from the peaks themselves and never softened, never smoothed. The fortress did not welcome. It watched.

Above it, the sky had turned the color of an old bruise. Clouds heavy with unspent rain pressed low against the towers, dragging their bellies along the higher battlements. Wind moved through the switchbacks in long, uneven gusts, carrying the metallic taste of a storm still gathering. The air smelled of iron and cold pine, and beneath that a colder scent, the one that always leaked out of the mountain when the weather turned.

Inside the outer walls, the citadel had woken with a sharpness that had nothing to do with dawn. Bells had not rung. Horns had not sounded. Word had come from the Great Meeting Room in the small hours and moved the way the citadel preferred, quietly, from mouth to ear, from ear to hand, until every hand had a task. The Empress had spoken. That was enough.

Torches guttered in their iron claws along the courtyard walls, flames pulled sideways by the wind. Ravens on the parapets shifted and resettled but did not fly. The stones underfoot were slick where the first thin sheets of rain had spit down, then held back, then spit again, as if the storm itself had been made to wait.

The main gates were shut, iron bars set into stone, the citadel closed against the coming weather. Six wagons had been drawn up in a rough line, loads lashed down beneath heavy tarps, ready for a dawn departure. The teams were still in the lower stables, waiting to be brought up at first light. A group of Clawborn had gathered around the wagons. Drivers moved between them with heads down and hoods up. Quartermasters walked the line counting crates, marking tallies on wax boards, comparing notes in low voices. No one spoke more than the work required.

Above the gate, the claw sigil bit into the lintel deep enough to cast its own shadow even under the flat storm light. Somewhere higher, behind the narrow window slits, the Empress was waiting for results. Everyone gathered around the wagons could feel the weight of that waiting settle across their shoulders like wet cloth. The storm was still coming. So was whatever answer she had already decided on.

“Ready the teams for first light.” One Clawborn barked at the group.

“Why so many wagons? We still haven’t received the wagon that left for the depot.”

“The orders came do from the Empress herself. We need all the resources brought back and processed immediately. No time to wait for a single wagon team to return.”
