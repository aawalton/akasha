Chapter V.IV (5.4) - Healing

“Now that you all have at least a surface level understanding of this discipline, we will begin to delve deeper into the subject and begin practicing spellcraft in earnest,” Taroe announced to the Rejuvenation and Restoration F class.

“We’ll be able to start healing?” one Kizu’s classmates asked excitedly. He noticed the student was a first-year. Someone without any instruction or natural talent in the subject. Unlike the small faction of older students who were using the class as a dump class to focus efforts elsewhere in the academy. Kizu spotted one such student next to him doodling images of lightning based elemental attacks.

As much as Kizu wanted to ask the boy beside him about his doodles, Kizu decided to focus. Despite his older age, Kizu was not using this class as a dump class. He needed to rank up fast in this subject to reach the heights in the rankings required to pull information from the headmaster about his missing sister.

“You will begin learning the basics of healing shortly,” Taroe confirmed. His eyes went hard as he examined each student in the room. The disheveled ex-Elite was clearly in a no-nonsense mood today. “First. I need to emphasize appropriate methods of training our spellcraft. The simplest body to heal will always be your own. It’s an easy starting point for many budding medics. Everyone innately understands their body better than all others. Assuming you reach the A or S classes, you will get to the point in your studies where you can mend your broken self with little thought and minimum magic. And time after time that simple realization leads to disastrous consequences. So I will be clear. If I ever discover any student in this class begins enacting self-harm as a means of advancing your skill, I will not only remove you from my class, I will also appeal to Headmaster Ballarfulur advocating for your expulsion from the academy. Do I make myself clear?”

There were some nervous glances at each other and nodding across the room.

“Verbal confirmation. Do. I. Make. Myself. Clear?” Taroe punctuated the end of the question by slamming his hands against his desk, sharp eyes drilling into all of them.

“Yes, Professor Taroe,” the class said in slightly broken unison.

“Good.” Taroe’s posture relaxed slightly. “Now. I’m going to tell you why you should not engage in self harm, no matter the short term benefits you might experience.

With a quill, he scratched a summoning circle into his desk then conjured a wicked looking blade that vaguely reminded Kizu of Sojan. Though this was a standard reflective metal surface rather than Sojan’s deep black. Then Taroe dangled the blade over his open palm.

“Hurting yourself is easy. A mere moment of pain to make significant progress on improving your efficiency and cast time. Every wound healed, the more efficient you become.”

Taroe dropped the blade over his exposed flesh and one of Kizu’s classmates screamed. But Kizu watched in fascination as the blade bounced off of Taroe’s skin. He wondered if it had been an enhancement spell or some means of controlling his skin density or thickness through a rejuvenation and restoration technique.

“Why did you scream?” Taroe asked Kizu’s classmate. “You know I’m one of Hon Empire’s most experienced field medics.”

The girl sat there stunned for a few seconds at getting called out. Finally, she stumbled through a response. “I…I just was scared. I thought you’d hurt yourself.” Tears trickled down her reddened cheeks. “I’m sorry. I thought, I mean, I should have better control over my emotions.”

“No.” Taroe crossed his arms. “Absolutely not. You are not allowed to be sorry for feeling empathy for another. For fearing for the safety of another. This is one of the most important reasons why you are not permitted to engage in self harm.

“Everyone starts believing the same thing. ‘I’m only harming myself. Nothing bad will come of it. And I’m making so much progress.’” Taroe spoke in a mocking tone, a snarl of contempt scrawled across his face. “And then it’s not enough. Those leaps and bounds of improvements? They dry up. The stagnation is like an itch that refuses to be scratched. The self harm becomes more and more extreme for less and less gains. A peeled of face, severed fingers, self removed livers. Even those injuries become not enough to satisfy the craving for advancement. And then they go out and try it on others. Often they start with animals. That will slow them down for a bit, since their anatomy is so different from our own. They watch as flayed cats and limbless dogs gasp their final breaths. Sometimes they even manage to prevent them from passing, reapply the skin and fur. The good ones might even be able to pop those limbs back into the dogs’ joints and seal them back into one piece.

“And then that’s not enough. So they hunt those like themselves. Humans. Gnomes. Tainted. Kemon. It happens every single time without fail. And we have to put down yet another serial killer. Another person tortures the elderly in their basement, attempting to discover the cure of aging. Or children, trying to better understand the bone density of a youth’s skeleton. Then I get called to kill yet another genius expert of health, too callous to understand pain. Ambition and curiosity is only ever the start. The end is numbness to the world. And then death.”

There was silence. Taroe stood at the head of the class, watching them.

“That seems…a bit extreme,” Kizu said, despite his better judgement. Beside him, he felt Ione perk up. “I know people who’ve needed to hurt themselves to escape bad situations. They didn’t turn into serial killers.”

Taroe nodded. He didn’t glance at Kizu’s leg, but he still felt a hot pressure there as the ex-Elite met his eyes.

“Correct. Sometimes circumstances require sacrifice. Let me ask you a question, did this person want to hurt themself? No? That is the difference. Those who self harm to improve their restoration magic are addicted to the pain. It’s tied into their minds with the sensation of progress and advancement. That is their first solution to every problem. Let’s say you got imprisoned by an enemy species deep within the World Dungeon. Normal delvers break their chains. Those I speak of break their bones. Slice off their fingers. They’d rather cut out their eyes than close their eyelids. That is the path I warn you against.”

“Is it mental magic?” a student in the class’s second row asked. “That compels mages to self harm? A magical disease?”

Taroe snorted. “No. Maybe some who start down the path are susceptible to temptation due to a diseased mind, but the addiction comes from a different place altogether. It’s a mental, not physical disruption. It would be far simpler if only a witch’s curse. Some of the greatest medical mages in the world are sealed deep beneath Keimusho Prison. All of them mad."

A silence settled on the classroom. Even the dump class students looked rattled and uncomfortable by Taroe’s lecture. In fact, the only person unperturbed by the grisly lesson slouched next to Kizu.

Ione picked at her fingernails, after Kizu’s question, she’d slumped down and appeared to have lost all interest in the subject. Which in a way made sense. They were discussing cautionary tales for something she never needed to worry about. She couldn’t get addicted to healing herself even if she wanted to.

“Good,” Taroe said. “I believe my point has been made. Now we can begin lessons to assist your lack of education on my subject.” He waved a hand and an image of a magnified patch of skin stretched out behind him. The hair follicles stuck up like dead trees in a desert. “This is an example of what we will be using for practice in this class. It’s a bit of skin grown in a laboratory. We will be practicing slicing it open and stitching it back together.”

“That sounds like necromancy,” someone said.

“Does it?” Taroe scratched his beard and unhoused a dozen fleas. “Interesting connection to make on the first day. I suppose it’s on your mind after hearing about the Drama Club debacle during Parent’s Day. Unfortunately, I have little comfort to offer you in regards to your observation. It looks like necromancy because our fields of study collide frequently.”

“You’re going to teach us necromancy?” That question got some nervous tittering in the classroom. Every sane student at the academy knew better than to mess with the natural order. Even Kizu considered necromancy and soul magic a dangerous subject to juggle. Of course, unlike the rest of his peers, he didn’t fear the magic as much as differ respect to it. But the rest of them clearly were unsettled by the idea they might be toeing a line into the field of study.

“No,” Taroe said bluntly. “I do not practice necromancy. I know the theories and have even lifted techniques and learned to apply them to the living. But the dead stay dead under my hand. In this class we will not be studying any of the crossover aspects between the two subjects. That remains a subject solely for my S ranked class, which you are all far from. Are these laboratory stretches of skin akin to necromancy in some ways? Correct. Will you be learning how to recreate these skin patches? Absolutely not.” Taroe reached under his desk and pulled out a sack piled high with patches of skin. They were small, only the size of Kizu’s palm. But he must have had hundreds inside that sack. “Centuries ago, before we developed the means in which to replicate skin, medics were forced to learn the craft on the battlefield. Consider yourselves fortunate you have such a cushioned training regime.” He held out the sack. “If you’re all going to squirm about it, then come up here and grab your skin patch first. And one of these scalpels too. You need one of those to cut it up.”

With a bit of reluctance in their steps, the class lined up and slowly approached Taroe one by one.

“Grab me one while you’re up there?” Ione asked.

“Sure.” Kizu followed the stream of students before claiming his patch of flesh and a spare for Ione. It was surprisingly warm to the touch. But, then again, if it was actually alive he supposed that it only made sense for it to produce heat like normal skin.

“We often use skin patches like those you hold when grafting particularly bad burns or when a monster or spell rips off someone’s skin,” Taroe explained after they all reclaimed their seats. “These are a common utility product nearly every medic has on hand along with emergency healing potions. Both of which have a lifecycle and require replacement after some time. A truly talented medic won’t need to rely on these tools on most occasions. But even the most powerful mages run out of blood. And once that happens, these sorts of crutches are invaluable in saving lives.”

Kizu prodded the bit of skin with a finger. It didn’t move.

“Make an incision no longer than a couple centimeters,” Taroe instructed. He waited for his students to all complete the assignment before continuing. “Now, place your hand on the wound. The type of magic you’re looking for is similar to that of enhancement spells. You need to feel the skin and allow it access into your senses for a moment. Not the sensation of pain, just the awareness of it.”

Kizu closed his eyes as he followed Taroe’s instructions. The skin radiated a warmth against his fingertips. He pushed the magic forward and felt his fingers enhance with strength. Not quite what he wanted. Pulling the magic back, he refocused his efforts. External. Like casting an elemental spell.

That effort earned him a soaking bit of flesh. Then he felt his grafted monster leg twitch.  The leg reminded him that he already had commanded foreign flesh in the past. He felt like on the verge of a breakthrough. Closing his eyes again, he revitalized his efforts.

This time he felt something channel out. It indeed felt very similar to an external version of an enhancement spell.

“Nice work,” Ione said. “Fast and effective. First one to complete the assignment.”

Kizu opened his eyes and beamed down at the patch of skin. Only the slightest white line of a  blemish remained of the earlier gaping wound.

He glanced around at his classmates. Sure enough, they were all still staring intently at their little patches of skin, willing them to seal closed. Some physically pinched the skin sides together. Nobody else looked to have made any progress.

“My leg helped me,” he explained to Ione.

“As in, it gave you a natural affinity with healing?” she asked, sitting up a bit. “You mentioned it did something similar with fire. Your elemental spells using fire have been more potent.”

“No…I mean, yes the leg helped me with fire. But this feels different. It’s more like it gave me a unique mindset to understand a puzzle. Not like the puzzle itself is simpler innately.”

“Hm.” Ione picked up the skin and flapped it. “Well, your special insight seems to have paid off this time.”

Her flapping the skin got Taroe’s attention and the ex-Elite made a follow-up announcement that anyone who’d completed the task was to repeat it while expanding on the cut.

At his words, several students started looking around to figure out who’d already finished, only to look dejected when they realized it was Kizu.

Ione grinned at the glares and sighs of frustration, then elbowed him. “You need to stop being so good at everything. You’re making everyone in the class jealous.”

“They only need to show up at Conjuring F to put that jealousy to rest,” he replied shortly. “A headstart with the knowledge of how to heal a scratch isn’t quite as remarkable when you realize I can’t summon stable creations.”

Ione shook her with an amused smile. “Greedy. You’re sounding like my sister. Careful or you’ll collapse under the weight you’ve upon your shoulders. Most normal people have one or two subjects they are decent at. If they’re lucky, a couple A classes by their fifth year.”

“I’m not most normal people,” he said.

“I’ve noticed.”
