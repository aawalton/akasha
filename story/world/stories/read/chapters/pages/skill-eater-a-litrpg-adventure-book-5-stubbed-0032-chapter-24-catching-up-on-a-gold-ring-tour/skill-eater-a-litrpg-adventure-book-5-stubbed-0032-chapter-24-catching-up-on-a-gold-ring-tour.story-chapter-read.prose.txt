“We haven’t had many chances to enjoy each other’s company away from the rest of the crew.” Trapper smiled at Sakura as they climbed the spiraling staircase embedded in the wall of the hollow butte.

“I want you to know how much I appreciate everything you’ve done. None of us would still be breathing without your help. While hunting might not be the profession you would have chosen under other circumstances, you are a valued member of the team and I’m glad you came along for the ride. You’re a good person no matter what those tattoos on your arms say, and I think of you as a friend in addition to an esteemed college.”

“Thank you,” the scion replied as the women passed the silver ring while heading toward the level above. “The feeling is mutual. Before I arrived in Puppet Town, Rita, Sam, and I spent every waking moment struggling to survive. Things were better after Dialla agreed to let us stay, but we were leading double lives, trying to keep our jailbird status a secret while I protected the settlement from the shadows.

“Meeting Edge changed everything. He was willing to trust me with his life, which led to meeting all of you. Being accepted for who I really am, without any lies, misdirection, or masks, means more than I can put into words.”

Their conversation continued as they arrived at the landing. Now that both women had evolved to stage three, they could enter the gold ring whenever they pleased. They were cleared for admittance by a ridiculously cute core sniffer and waved through the Magisters’ checkpoint.

This terrace was home to the most exclusive services and expensive merchandise World’s End had to offer. Trapper was even more excited about gaining access to the auction house, where items owned by the settlement’s wealthiest individuals changed hands, including devices and gems that couldn’t be acquired anywhere else.

The gold ring was a work of art. The streets were etched with intricate engravings, and the buildings were decorated with murals, including works by some of the greatest painters in the planet’s history. Even the light posts and restrooms were luxurious enough to fit right in with an emperor’s palace.

Trapper grinned as they took in the sights, memorizing the swank establishments and world-class eateries they passed along the way. In addition to the scattered storefronts, there were plenty of places where people could meet, both publicly and privately. This level featured the free city’s best restaurants and bars, along with recreational and professional services provided by people with Rare and high-rank skills. They cost a staggering sum, which made her wonder what was available on the terrace above.

It was common knowledge that the platinum ring was frequented by the handful of stage-four powerhouses working out of World’s End. It was a place where they could interact with one another away from prying eyes and negotiate private contracts with the settlement’s premier organizations. Rumors were rife regarding the exotic pleasures and decadent luxuries the exclusive terrace provided, each more implausible than the last.

Whatever is up there, we’re not going to find out anytime soon. As the women drew near their destination, Trapper returned her attention to the matter at hand. Auctions were held monthly. She wanted find out what was being sold so she could determine if there was anything the crew wanted to bid on and suss out opportunities to strengthen Puppet Town.

Only the lobby was open right now. The duo stepped inside to check out the venue and peruse and the list, which was only shown to people who could reach the gold ring. Her eyes widened when she took in the sheer volume of top-grade materials and gear, along with the minimum bids. While some of the resources were reasonable considering their quality, the price tags were staggering, revealing the gap in wealth between the free city’s powerhouses and the unfortunate souls living in Lowtown.

Trapper knew the economy was still in flux as everyone adjusted their new lives as settlers instead of the tourists trying to get rich from royalites from the feed. Even still, the naked self-interest on display made her eyes narrow in disdain. Distributing a single auction’s worth of goods would let the city’s low-stage hunters have a decent shot of cycling up in the Lakelands, as long as they were accompanied by veteran teams.

While it seemed like a simple solution to implement, the Magisters were only nominally in charge. They didn’t have the strength to dictate terms to the settlement’s elites, or any of the major factions, crews, guilds, gangs, or mercenary groups for that matter.

She smiled when she saw the items her team had brought in from the Haunted Value, which would net the crew a bonanza of Credits if everything sold during the auction. Trapper jotted down anything interesting she saw on the list while Sakura did the same. In addition to potential upgrades for her friends, she recorded any resources that would benefit Puppet Town, which she would hand over to Dialla’s delegation when the Wave-Bound Warrior returned.

“All right,” she said when she reached the last item. “That’s everything I needed to take care of. Why don’t we stop by that restaurant we passed on the way, and I’ll treat you to lunch.”

Sakura licked her lips. “Those meat pies smelled heavenly. I’ll gladly take you up on that offer.” Arm in arm, the women left the auction hall and made their way toward the eatery in question, looking forward to the tasty treat.

Edge’s first low-rung brawl was only an hour away.

He was excited to get some hands-on experience in an official match and determined to qualify for the Duos’ Clash before the cutoff. After reporting Rue’s success to the beast hunters’ society, he stopped by the Lost Traveler to pick up Melenia and Jumo, who wanted to tag along.

While he was waiting for the guardswoman to change into her armor, he popped into his inner world for his daily skill-bonding session. When his mind slipped into his central chamber, he had a surprise waiting for him. Skill-Eater was sitting in his father’s armchair, surrounded by the diverse collection of avatars living in his village.

This was only the second time the ravenous lord had appeared in person since they overcame the dark passenger. Until he evolved, her time here was strictly limited, so he knew the matter must be important.

“Edge, it’s good to see you. I must commend you on the state of your inner world. Your skills are quite happy here and they speak well of you, which bodes well for our myriad ambitions.”

“It’s nice to see you too. Is something wrong? I know it takes a lot of energy for you to show up like this.”

Skill-Eater rose from the chair and walked over to his side. They took a lap around his central chamber, letting her inspect each component in turn while she explained.

“No, today’s visit serves a different purpose. Now that the dark passenger has been suppressed for the time being, I can contemplate everything I have experienced since you unsealed my reliquary with a clear mind. Before I can offer council, I need to understand what I’ve seen, and I can only learn so much by drawing on our link.

“The world has changed greatly since my feet last touched her soil. You humans have developed a novel approach to magic, since your civilization flourished long before discovering its existence. The System, Puppet Bodies, the Prison World broadcasts. None of it resembles anything I’ve encountered before, and I have lived for a long, long time and traveled to places beyond your wildest imagination.”

“That makes sense,” he replied. “I’m sure I wouldn’t be able to understand much if I suddenly woke up on your homeworld. Ask me anything. I’ll do my best to shed some light on aspects that aren’t obvious unless you’re steeped in my culture.”

This marked the beginning of a rapid-fire question-and-answer session. Edge hadn’t had an opportunity to speak to Skill-Eater at length before. Even though part of her mind was still sealed away, it was obvious that the Lords of Ord were incredibly intelligent and perceived the world in a way that was far removed from his human perspective.

They began with the comet that brought magic to Earth, then moved on to the ascent of spell-casting AIs, the discovery of the portal network, and the System’s journey to Ord. Skill-Eater was fascinated by the existence of self-aware digital entities, which were fundamentally different from anything she’d seen in her old life.

He shared the story of how the Prison World broadcasts had grown to become the most widely-viewed media in human history, detailed the invention of simucast technology, and summarized the origin of puppet tourism. He finished by outlining everything he knew about the Prison World Administration, the Wardens, and Ord’s other factions, along with how everything had worked prior to the anomaly.

The ravenous lord had been present for the events that followed, although she was asleep much of the time. Edge filled in the gaps by providing his perspective on several matters that had taken place in recent days.

“Fascinating. Your people, their technology, and these living machines are marvelous. I would study them for centuries if we didn’t have other pressing matters to attend to. It makes me wonder what the future will hold if we can defeat the Ancients, free my kin, and shield this planet from outside interference.”

They spoke for a few more minutes before Skill-Eater’s energy began to wane. She thanked him for the information and bid him farewell, stepping through the black door and into the space beyond the boundary of his inner world. He was curious what changes had taken place to that storm-swept coastline now that the passenger was chained and the lord was free, but that would have to wait until after he evolved, and she was awake more often.

Edge spent the rest of his allotted time playing with his avatars. After returning to his body, he walked beside Jumo and Mel as they strolled down the ramp connecting this terrace to the arena rising from the middle of the city.
