The tek'kalla transport craft looked more like something that had flown out of a science fiction movie than a magical airship. Jade's mental image of the flying vehicles had included open decks and multiple sails, or possibly something more akin to an ornithopter with flapping wings. Instead, the craft Jade and her friends now sat together inside was sleek and modern in appearance, all sweeping curves and polished metal. Jade was immediately reminded of Earth helicopters, except in place of rotors the vehicle had large glowing gems protruding from its roof and belly. Its front was bulbous and faceted, like the compound eye of an insect. Two pilots sat within the glass dome, skillfully flying the craft over the shifting landscape splayed out beneath them.

"This is awesome." Luis said, craning his neck to peer through one of the small windows in the passenger compartment. "Why doesn't everyone use these?"

"Because the gems that keep it flying are usually only found on the sixth floor." Tara replied, a hint of smugness in her voice. "Not just anyone can harvest skystone. Most Wayfarers don't go past the fifth level."

"What level are the monsters up there?" Naomi asked. She sat opposite Jade in the somewhat cramped passenger cabin, holding her guitar between her knees as she adjusted one of the strings.

"They hover around the low forties, but I've never been past the fourth floor, myself. You've finally caught up to me."

"Most people aren't crazy enough to live on the fourth floor, Tara." Fenrin said. "Even on the third, roaming monsters are a major problem for smaller communities. Up here, they're an existential threat."

"That's why we live in flying cities. They make keeping the population safe a lot simpler."

"I can't wait to see one." Luis said, not taking his eyes from the view outside. "That academy you graduated from is there too, right? They must be huge."

"I hope you like it." Tara said. She shifted in her seat, giving Jade the sense that Luis's enthusiasm was making her a little self-concious. "It can get a little crowded.""Crowded? Ha!" Luis laughed, leaning into her and patting her on the shoulder. "You should've seen the place I grew up. Couldn't walk five feet without tripping over a relative. That's how it should be, yeah? Nothing better than having a good community around you."

Jade wasn't sure she completely agreed with that, she liked having her own space. Then again, she'd had her fill of isolation and loneliness in the aftermath of her gymnastics injury. And now she was here in the Labyrinth, where she'd barely spent more than a handful of moments separated from her party since they'd first teamed up. A party that was willing to walk alongside her into the gates of hell simply because they wouldn't let her do it alone.

Maybe Luis has a point.

"So." Jade said softly, casting a surreptitious glance towards the front of the aircraft. They'd been in the air for almost an hour now, it was time to get to business. "Let's go over the plan."

They were accompanied by three other tek'kalla, two pilots and the ambassador from the Illysport embassy that Tara had arranged their transportation with. All three were currently in seats at the front of the aircraft, a sufficient distance from them that Jade was confident they wouldn't be overheard. While the vehicle wasn't as oppressively noisy as an Earth helicopter was, the rhythmic pulsing of the skystone gems filled the compartment with more than a little background noise.

"Right." Tara said, regaining her composure. She spoke softly, and the rest of them leaned in to listen. "Once we reach the Intrepid, that's the name of the city ship, the first thing we need to do is find an old friend of mine. His place is near the academy, it won't take us long to get there."

"Will we need his help?" Fenrin asked.

Tara gave the front of the ship another glance. Jade resisted the urge to do the same, not wanting to appear too suspicious. She settled for monitoring the pilots' background emotions, finding nothing alarming in their surface feelings.

"He's in the pilot corps. I'll explain more when we get there, but he'll be able to help us get what we need."

"Won't the ambassador want to take us straight to the city leadership?" Jade asked. "You told them we have critical information about the demon invasion."

"Maybe, but I think they'll want to review the data crystal we pulled together before questioning us. And it is critical information." Tara huffed. "It's not like we're lying."

No, just getting ourselves transported to the city under false pretenses so we can steal a prototype stealth airship. Jade thought to herself.

Still, Tara had a point. The previous evening, they'd helped the tek'kalla mage compile all the information they'd learned to date regarding the demons. Everything from the ritual they'd stopped on the third floor to what the goddess had explained to them about their origin and intentions. Now that demon armies were forcing their way into the Labyrinth, Jade wasn't sure how much of that information was actionable, but at the very least it provided crucial context to what had to be a chaotic and confusing situation. Hopefully, delivering that data crystal would help. If everything went according to plan and they successfully shut down the planar gates, it might not matter much. If for some reason they couldn’t enter the demon world or failed once they did… well, that wasn't something Jade wanted to dwell on.

"After that." Tara continued, still speaking softly. "We'll have to track down one of the dimensional rifts and have Jade find us a way through."

Wonderful. No pressure there.

"Do you think it will be hard to locate one?" Siora asked. "The fourth floor is supposed to be huge. Your city ship won't be near the fighting, will it? That would be dangerous."

Tara grinned at her.

"That's exactly why it'll be nearby. My people are always looking for a good fight."

"You know, that attitude is why most people on the lower floors don't think well of tek'kalla." Fenrin pointed out.

"Well, some do take it too far." The mage grimaced, sitting back in her wall-mounted seat. "But that's hardly my clan's fault. Does that stop anyone from being suspicious of us too, though? Of course not. You should've seen how people reacted to me down in the city I met you in…"

"Some things are the same everywhere." Luis said, giving her shoulder a squeeze. "But hey, if your people fight alongside the Wayfarers and other people from the third-floor cities against the demons, maybe that'll start to change."

"Nothing brings people together like a common enemy." Fenrin agreed.

The conversation continued, but Jade found herself unable to focus on it. The immense burden of her task was weighing on her mind. Now that she'd had time to process the enormity of her goal the whole thing seemed even more absurd. She was responsible for saving the Universe? The task had been given to her by a literal goddess? A year ago, Jade hadn't known magic was real. Now, she was preparing to confront a demon lord from another dimension with little more than sweet lies and clever schemes. All because circumstances had forced her to take a demon race when she'd first entered the Labyrinth.

It was almost enough to make her crack. But her friends were here, and they kept her anchored.

At least their deal with the Ebon Eye had born fruit. Before they'd left Illysport, Jade ran across more than a couple of groups of well-equipped Earth Wayfarers. Apparently, the city shops had followed through with giving Earth-born humans preferential discounts, even amidst the ongoing chaos. Jade prayed that would help keep them safe in the coming days, and that none of them were foolish enough to take on the Labyrinth's global quest to confront the demon invaders themselves. None of them, including herself, were ready for that kind of fighting. Fortunately, the whole point of their current plan was to avoid any significant conflicts. Jade couldn't help but chuckle softly at that thought. As if things ever went that smoothly for them.

"Alright, everyone strap in." The co-pilot turned in his seat, waving to get their attention. "We're starting our approach to the city."

"Do I have to put my seat in the upright and locked position?" Luis asked.

The tek'kalla gave him a confused look.

"…What?"

"Just ignore him." Naomi said, rolling her eyes as she pulled the seat harness tight over her shoulders. "That's what we do."

"Hey…"

"I've forwarded the data file to your father, Tara'lynn." The tek'kalla ambassador said, leading the way down the transport's ramp and stepping onto the landing pad. "I expect he'll wish to speak with you and your team as soon as possible."

"Is he deploying with the taskforce to combat the invaders?"

"No, not as far as I know." The ambassador tapped on a crystal tablet a few times, scanning its display. "I believe captain Kasta is helping to organize the city's defenses. I'll speak with his staff and let you know."

"Thank you." Tara gave the older tek'kalla a shallow bow. "We'll wait in my quarters."

The pair exchanged a few more words but Jade was too distracted to catch them. They were standing on a flying city. A flying city. Not even the enormous pressure of their task could suppress the sheer sense of wonder the sight filled her with. Crystal spires dominated the central portion of the broad disk that made up the top surface of the vessel, towering buildings that stretched dozens of stories into the sky. The outer region was filled with smaller buildings spaced farther apart, reminding Jade of dense suburbs clustered around the city center. Just outside the airfield they'd arrived in were homes, parks, and even a flowing stream winding its way across the landscape. If Jade hadn't just flown here herself, she'd have had a hard time believing they were truly in the sky.

"This is incredible."

She wasn't the only one impressed. Her companions were also looking around in wonder, taking in the strange and beautiful sight.

"How many people live here?"

"Somewhere between nine and ten thousand." Tara said, returning to the group as the ambassador walked off. Nearby, ground crew were already inspecting the newly arrived airship and speaking with the pilots. "About two thirds of that number are full-time residents, with the rest here for business, the academy, or are using the city as a base of operations for Labyrinth activities."

"That's way more than I was imagining." She admitted, looking up at the towering skyscrapers. "How do you feed everyone? I don't see any farms."

"We import most of our consumables, either from the home world or other Labyrinth cities. That, plus raiding Sanctuaries for supplies as often as we can, is enough to sustain us indefinitely."

"Isn't it dangerous to rely so heavily on imports for food?" Naomi asked. "I mean, the Labyrinth is a dangerous place. What if monsters attack the couriers?"

"That's why one of the most common assignments for taskforce members is convoy escort duty." Tara explained, leading them away from the ship and towards a squat, white and gray building. Judging by the number of people moving between similar aircraft and the doors, Jade guessed it was a transportation terminal. "If I'd joined a team straight out of the academy, that's probably what I'd be doing right now."

"Instead, you found us." Luis said with a grin. "And got tangled up in some wild adventures."

"Didn't Tara get us involved in this?" Naomi asked, cocking her head. "She's the one who discovered the demons were stealing dimension effusion in that second-floor city."

"Details." Luis waved his hand dismissively. "What matters is that she ended up with the better deal."

Considering what they were about to face together, Jade wasn't so sure about that. Tara, however, simply smiled, a hint of shyness coloring her face. She fell into step beside Luis, her slender frame appearing especially slight compared to his broad build. Emotions, warm and tender, wafted off the two of them like steam from a hot spring. Jade did her best to close her mind to the sensation, feeling an odd sense of guilt for intruding on her friends' private thoughts. She couldn't help but smile, though, at how good those positive feelings now felt to her. She thought back to her most recent encounter with Ashar, and the realization that had hit her then.

When this is over, after I make sure Alan is taken care of, I'll go and see him.

Something to look forward to would be nice. At first, Jade had thought the goddess's task was a suicidal one. It had certainly sounded so when she'd explained their objective. But the longer they'd discussed the mission, the more Jade's confidence grew. Their plan was sneaky, underhanded, and relied on misdirection. The perfect ploy for a succubus. As long as the information the goddess had given them was correct, they could do this. And if Elaria was wrong about what obstacles they'd face… well, that would be apparent from the start. Jade wouldn't commit to anything unless she was confident they could make it work. Jade ran a hand down the length of her arm, shuddering softly as she recalled a memory. As far as the end goal of the plan went, at least, she had a very good reason to believe it would work.

A line of people, mostly tek'kalla but with a few other species sprinkled throughout, were waiting to speak to what looked like some sort of customs inspectors. They slotted themselves in at the rear of the queue as Tara handed out guest IDs she'd received from the ambassador.

"These identify you as one of my team members." She said, holding up one of the crystal cards. It was the size and shape of a credit card and was covered in blue runes. "Foreign team members are allowed aboard as long as they're accompanied by a local."

They passed through the customs checkpoint without issue, and a few minutes later, were let into the city proper. Jade found herself standing on one side of a narrow street flanked by what appeared to be hotels and small shops. The street was busy with foot traffic, although she didn't see any vehicles.

"So, what now?" Siora asked. The elf was clinging to Jade's arm, as if worried she would be swept away with the bustling crowd. "You said we're going to your home?"

"Is there something you need, there?" Fenrin asked. He glanced back at the airfield, which was dotted with several large hangars. "This is where the airships are. That's what we came here for, right?"

"These don't look like military ships." Luis said, casting a critical gaze over the sleek, rounded hulls of the transport ships lined up on the tarmac. "I'm guessing they keep those somewhere else.""Maybe we should talk about this somewhere, you know, private?" Naomi hissed, looking around nervously. The area in front of the transit terminal was quite busy, although Jade didn't sense anyone paying special attention to them.

"Definitely." Tara said with a nod. She pointed down the street. "My place is near the academy, we'll talk there."

Tara led the way through the neat, clean streets of the city ship. Apparently, tek'kalla architects were enamored with crystals, as many of their buildings were sculpted from a substance that both looked and felt like quartz. It made the streets sparkle and gleam in the mid-afternoon sunlight, giving the city an ethereal feel that was enhanced by the low hanging clouds drifting past overhead. Their diverse group caught more than a few strange looks on their walk through the city, and Jade got the sense that groups of outsiders weren't very common, but the trip passed without incident. Soon enough, they were grouped together in Tara's quarters, a rather spartan studio apartment on a middle floor of one of the residential towers closer to the middle of the city.

"Okay, we can talk here." Tara said, sliding the door shut behind her and flipping a switch beside it. "The room is warded against divination, and I set up soundproofing enchantments in the walls when I moved in.""Nothing worse than a noisy neighbor." Jade muttered. She had more than one bad memory of college dorm life.

"Exactly. And just another item on the long list of reasons my people shouldn't look down on magic the way they do."

"Aren't we here to steal a fancy stealth air ship from them?" Luis asked. "Your people have some cool tech, Tara. It's not all bad."

"Relying on it too much is." She insisted, crossing her arms. "And after we stop this invasion for them, maybe they'll finally start to see that."

"So, how do we get this ship?" Fenrin asked. He was looking around Tara's room with interest, clearly unaccustomed with modern aesthetics. Other than the glowing mana crystals embedded in the various household appliances, the place could have been mistaken for an apartment on Earth. "We should get started now, before your father calls us in to question us about how we got all that information on the demon plans."

"The work being done on stealth enchantments and technology are overseen by the taskforce academy, so the airships are kept in the campus hangars. I don't have full access, but one of my friends does. We were in the same class, but he went through the pilot track instead of the Wayfarer one."

"He'll be able to get us in?" Jade asked. "Will he help us?"

"I'm sure I can convince him, we go way back." Tara said, waving one hand in an airy gesture. "Even better, I know he's trained on these models before. He used to tell me all about it when we were in school together. So, he'll definitely be able to fly it, too."

"Wait." Jade said, feeling her heart skip a beat. "You won't be flying it?"

"Huh?" Tara blinked, looking around at the confused expressions of her friends. "I'm a mage, not a pilot. I don't know how to fly an airship."

"So, the plan is to talk your friend into stealing a ship with us?" Naomi asked incredulously.

"Requisitioning." Tara insisted. "We're requisitioning the ship."

"And then have him fly us through an unstable portal into a world full of demons?" Naomi continued, ignoring the interruption.

"Over the demon armies that are battling it out with everyone else." Luis added. "Don't forget that part."

"Right, that too."

"Um, well when you put it like that..." Tara began, her cheeks flushed. Then she drew herself up to her full height. "But unless anyone else knows a better way the rest of us can sneak past those armies, then that's the only way we're going to get in there with Jade."

"And no." Naomi added, shooting a pointed look at Jade. "You still aren't doing this on your own."

There was a long moment of silence as they all considered her words. Finally, Luis broke it.

"This is going to be a tough sell…"
