Archie swept the rest of the tavern with a critical eye, boots thudding softly across old and moldy wood as he peered behind the busted piano and found nothing of value, unfortunately. Similarly, under the bar counter, he found dust, some glass shards, and what he really hoped wasn't a rat's foot, and behind a collapsed velvet curtain that looked like it had once been red before decades of smoke and mold made it the color of dried blood.

All in all?

“Empty, empty, and more empty,” he muttered, flicking a small rusted spoon off a table like it had personally offended him. “No weapon rack, no starter chest, not even a hint of anything that would be able to help tell me who I am – what kind of Isekai starter zone is this?”

He spun in place on his heel, already rerunning through the mental map he made of the building while exploring, when something by the foot of the boarded-up front door caught his eye.

Two small, clean flasks sat nestled just beneath one of the cross-beams used to board up the entrance, if not for a suspicious glint of brass around their necks that looked incredibly gaudy and eye-catching – he would’ve dismissed them as just as some of the many bottles of varying sizes that littered the damaged wooden floor of the tavern.

He crouched down and plucked one up, raising it to his eye as he focused on trying to see any identifiable qualities or quirks of the glass flask holding dark yellowish-brown liquid. Identify.

Flask of Oil (Common):A glass flask that is filled with highly flammable linseed oil, extracted by Bale, a peak E-Grade Oil Miller. Status: Good. Requirements: Lv 100+ & Humanoid Race.

Archie raised his eyebrows and gave a low whistle. “You know, I was just thinking today needed more dangerous combustibles.” He tossed both flasks into his Spatial Storage without a second thought. “Good for traps, distraction, or just good ol’ arson.”

Rising, he turned back to the door and stepped up to it, brushing some cobwebs off the frame with one hand while the other hovered over his chest. With a flick of thought, he activated Vital Sight.

Immediately, the world bled colors.

Every visible surface dulled into gray, faded wood, rotting cloth, all of it stripped of hue, except for the bright red, writhing, pulsing outlines of vital energy beyond the tavern walls. Dozens of them. Maybe more. All humanoid and all clustered together like sardines in a can, their vibrant outlines blurring and overlapping.

“Holy hell, Vital Sight changed,” he muttered, eyes slightly widened in shock. “This is like a thousand times better than before, with how I don’t need to immediately lower the opacity of the skill in order to see the area around me and not just the vital energy outlines and their Vital Sparks… Holy shit, this feels like some sort of Detective Mode or some sort of “spy vision” in some games.”

He let Vital Sight deactivate and decided that he’d spent enough time trying to explore the inside of the tavern. “Alright, since Camilla isn’t showing up on the compass and enough time has passed, I guess I need to figure out how to leave where I am and ride off to see where I can get a signal on her.”

With a sharp crack of his boot against the crossbeam, he kicked the door open… well, more like blew it open.

The sound of splintering wood echoed like a cannon fire through the narrow alleys beyond.

He blinked against the sudden rays of daylight glaring at him from above; it was not quite morning, nor was it noon, rather it was that weird limbo hour between the two – honestly, in his opinion, it was the worst time of day to be outside.

Adjusting his eyes to the bright light, he then took notice of the rows upon rows of rundown shacks and patchwork buildings stretched in every direction. Shouting echoed down the cramped streets as many rushed into their houses and away from the screaming echoing from the alleys.

People were everywhere, some wearing tattered clothes, others wearing armor cobbled together from random bits, not to mention that everyone he looked at had that same twitchy, hungry edge to them. Like if you so much as looked too long at someone’s boots, they'd try to sell them to you – or stab you for looking at them wrong.

And every single one of them had turned to stare at the sudden intrusion that was Archie, framed dramatically in the ruined doorway, sunlight catching off his armor like some budget version of a hero arriving five years too late.

“Well,” he muttered under his breath, “at least I’m not the only one who looks confused as hell.”

Quickly Identifying those rushing into their houses, he saw that they were all E-Grades, ranging between levels 50 to 78.

Why would they live like this? He wondered to himself. They’re plenty strong enough, stat-wise, to go and live off on their own and not subjugate themselves to this type of living… Are the creatures outside of this place too strong for them to handle? What’s going on here?

Seeing that they weren’t a threat to his life, Archie turned around and stared at the building he’d just come out of. Upon doing so, he confirmed his previous assumption that he was teleported into an abandoned tavern called Mugtopia. He snorted. Nice name.

He turned to leave.

However, he stopped himself as he heard a shout telling him to stop.

A group of six approached, their stride slow and heavy with casual menace. They wore ragged armor and patched cloaks – one had a butcher’s cleaver slung across his back like a greatsword, another twirled a butterfly knife between scarred fingers, while the others had wands or normal-looking swords.

Archie barely gave them a glance of acknowledgement and was just about to leap away and ignore them, but upon his eyes landing on their tattoos, his interest was piqued: a blue wolf head inked into their skin. He spotted it on a neck, a shoulder, the side of a shaven scalp; they all had at least one blue wolf head visible on their body.

Gang markings, clear as day.

The biggest of the group of six that approached him, a hulking half-orc who looked like someone had force-fed a battering ram steroids, stepped up to Archie with a sneer carved deep into his face. “Oi, shiny,” he rumbled, the stink of rotgut and something worse clinging to his breath, “this here’s Bluefang turf. You make a big noise. You get deaded.”

Then the half-orc reached out and grabbed Archie’s shoulder and tried to pull him in like someone manhandling a drunk out of a bar.

But Archie didn’t move.

Not an inch.

The half-orc’s brow furrowed. “...Huh?” he said aloud, half to himself, turning slightly so he could see the faces of his group, and in doing so, he saw his human leader nod at him to follow through with the shakedown.

The half-orc’s sneer twisted into a snarl, and with zero wind-up, he swung a heavy punch using all of his strength straight at Archie’s face.

Archie blinked.

And with a slight tilt of his head, the punch slid past with all the grace of a drunk trying to hit a shadow. His own right arm came up, smooth and without any rush at all, and snaked over the massive outstretched arm.

Crack!

His tight fist connected with the half-orc’s jaw; however, in doing so, for a split second, just before the punch connected, the half-orc felt an overwhelming rush of fear. And in that moment, to it, Archie’s punch was no longer just a punch, but the charge of a D-Grade Buffalo.

The half-orc was launched backwards like someone had just fired him out of a cannon. He hit the opposite building with a thunderous boom, smashing into the wall hard enough to crater the old brick wall, dust billowing out in thick clouds as cracks spider-webbed through the side of the building.

The entire street went dead silent.

Archie stood there, arm still half-raised, blinking in surprise at his fist.

He looked at the rest of the group, who’d stopped in their tracks mid-step, looking at Archie in deep shock.

“The hell’s wrong with that guy?” Archie muttered, eyes narrowing slightly in confusion. “I didn’t even hit him that hard.”

Oh, that’s why, Archie thought to himself. I keep on thinking that since everyone looks my age or older wouldn’t be the same level or higher than myself considering that–

“YOU BASTARD!” the knife twirler yelled, his voice raw with disbelief and fury.

All at once, they drew out their weapons, and the cleaver guy let out a guttural roar just before he charged at Archie, activating a booster skill of sorts that made him slightly increase in height. All five of them lunged toward Archie in a chaotic storm of movement and shouting, spells already flying before they'd even crossed half the distance.

The first bolt of fire crackled past his ear.

Archie didn’t flinch; instead, he was debating whether it would be worth it to immediately kill someone in a place where laws were in place and potentially put his starter zone at risk, as it most definitely had to have some sort of connection to his origin story, which pertained to his first quest.

…Yeah, I guess I’ll just knock them out. It's just too much of a risk to kill them when I could be barred from this city for potentially breaking a law.

The next spell he was faced with was a jagged lance of violet mana that surprisingly didn’t feel like dark mana, but at the same time, it felt like it – he sidestepped it with the barest tilt of his body and let it crash into the tavern, Mugtopia, behind him.

Almost immediately, he then leaned backwards as the cleaver swung wide above his head. Yeah, killing people who look like they belong to a gang not long after being isekaied wouldn't be the brightest idea.

Archie rose before the cleaver sweeper could recover. Archie shot forward, snapping his left fist into the side of the guy’s face before quickly kicking in the guy’s right kneecap – Pop! – and letting the scream of pain guide his spinning elbow strike straight into the guy’s jaw, sending him to both Morpheus and the ground.

One down, Archie casually thought to himself as he caught the glint of the butterfly knife in his peripheral vision and pivoted, catching the wielder’s wrist mid-slash, yanking him forward into a brutal knee to the gut, then by grabbing the front of his tunic he raised him into the air before throwing him down into the cobbled road. Two down.

An arrow of wind slammed into his chestpiece, striking dead-on – only to splash harmlessly against the armor Archie had crafted just a few hours earlier. Finally, he thought, eyes locking onto the caster who’d launched the spell. Now my armor won’t get turned into Swiss cheese every time I get hit with magic.

During his time in his D-Grade Profession Trial, Bralmir’s lesson echoed in his mind as he thought back on why the armor he’d forged before had always been vulnerable to spells. He’d been forging poorly, failing to properly imbue his mana into the strikes of his hammer, which would refine the metal itself and reinforce its overall durability.

The caster’s eyes widened right before Archie appeared in front of him and slammed his head into the guy’s face, knocking him out with it judging by how the guy’s eyes rolled to the back of his head. Three.

A sword stab was aimed at his liver –  Archie ducked, grabbed the stabber’s wrist, twisted, and disarmed her with a twist of his own wrist. Her wand hand came up in a panic.

Ooh, a wand and sword combo, I haven’t seen anyone with this type of fighting style before, Archie thought to himself as he caught that too, then spun behind her and gently chopped the back of her neck, causing her to stumble and trip over her own unconscious ally, and hit the ground with a groan. Four.

The last guy, whom he presumed to be the leader, since he'd watched everything in silence, tried to backpedal and throw some kind of binding spell at him. It fizzled harmlessly as it came in contact with Archie’s chestpiece, and by the time he made it three feet away from where he previously was, ten or so vines shot out from the ground and held him in place, and before he could do anything, a swift uppercut sent the guy into unconsciousness.

He glanced around and found the previously chaotic street filled with people was now empty with the excpetion being the six people that attacked him.

“Well,” he muttered, brushing some dust off his armor. “…What now?”

Glancing back down at the presumed leader, he grabbed the guy by the back of his tunic and placed him over his shoulder before leaping onto the rooftop of Mugtopia.
