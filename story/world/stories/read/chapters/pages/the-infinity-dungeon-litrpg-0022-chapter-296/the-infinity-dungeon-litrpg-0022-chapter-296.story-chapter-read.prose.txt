Chapter 296

The airship tore through the air, carrying no passengers. Michael had abandoned it to return on its own as soon as he left the area where the veil had been weakened by the rift, teleporting back to Site 00 in all haste. Time was like physical weight holding him down, making him jittery, grains of sand escaping his grasp.

He was hovering in the air, watching the Domain use the spiral to automatically regulate the magic density and prevent it from spreading or putting pressure on the shield, when he felt Icarus’s presence. The little elf materialized a few hundred yards away from him, making him wonder just what they were up to this time. His question was soon answered when they spread their blindingly white wings wide, and shot toward him like a bullet.

“Michael!” they cried as they threw themselves at him, hugging him and twirling in the air. “You’re back!”

Michael let the winged elf take him for a spin, chuckling and–for a moment–feeling the weight of the world lessen.

“How did it go last night?” he asked.

Icarus looked away. “I dismissed the body after a few hours… I couldn’t take it anymore.”

“Why didn’t you come to me?” Michael asked, noticing the black nails, amateurishly painted despite the AI’s total control over their body. Cute.

“I… didn’t want to bother you. I don’t wanna be too clingy.”

This made him want to ruffle the elf’s hair. In fact, he went ahead and did it. “Bullshit. Don’t do it ever again.”

“Okay,” they said, giggling and beaming at him. “I’ll come right away next time. Soo, what are you thinking about?”

“Can’t you read my thoughts?”

“I don’t want to. I like to talk like a normal person. It makes me feel special, in some strange backward way.”

“Alright. I was thinking about the shield keeping all the magic from spreading. I was thinking about time. How, despite everything, it always feels like I’m in a hurry. I’m thinking about the shard of the mirror we sent into space, wondering what will happen to it. If it’s safe. If the world is safe. If I am enough, the right person for the job. If–”

“Michael mine…” Icarus said in a soft voice.

They took his hand in theirs, and the two watched the aurora of the black hole as the Domain kept siphoning the excess magic away.

“It’s beautiful,” Michael said. “I think the dungeon is only letting us do it because of Infy.”

“Maybe,” Icarus replied. “What if we stopped trying to contain it? What if we let magic spread?”

“How about we think about it later?”

“Sure,” they said, perking up. “You said you wanted to do a dungeon run, didn’t you? Can I also join, like last time?”

“Always. Do you want to take it easy or push deeper?”

“Deeper, as in the eighth floor? We need power, Michael mine, this is not the time to take it easy. Let’s go to the eighth and beat the crap out of it.”

Before that, they stopped at the Valley to pick up some inner space construction materials Travis had prepared for them. Trevor was there, depositing the latest batch of them using his [Ghost Market] skill. They watched him dump a veritable mountain of stuff into a hole near the golem’s territory, absorbing platinum mana coins to top up like they were candy. Meanwhile, the golems were digging even more holes, filling them with all sorts of valuables being mined from the mountains of the Valley. Under Drullkrin’s watch, they had worked out a tight schedule that made optimal use of the one-month resource respawn window.

“I can’t believe we are back to this,” Michael grumbled as he plunged a hand into the heap of materials and watched it disappear.

“With the veil weakening all over the world, we have to be careful not to overuse the Inventory,” Icarus said.

“But [Ghost Market] can move any amount of stuff without any problem. We have scanned it and integrated it with the System–why can’t we do the same?”

They entered the inner space, where many Icarus instances converged to where the materials had appeared and began to sort through them.

“We could ask Trevor to let us look at his skill some more,” Icarus proposed. “For some reason, looking at it in the flesh is better than doing it remotely with the Origin System, even though it should be the same thing!”

Michael smiled, feeling the frustration building up in his companion’s mind. At least it wasn’t just him, he thought. He watched the many Icarus instances begin to pull the materials away, off to upgrade the collectors and batteries like usual. The only unusual thing was that they left a chuck of it behind, with the Icarus closest to him looking at him with big, round eyes.

“Hey Michael… I know we need to use every ounce of metal we can get to build more stuff but… can I take some of it to do a little side project?”

“How much?” he asked.

“You don’t want to know what for?”

“What for, Icarus?” he said, indulging the needy AI.

Icarus’s eyes lit up. They glanced toward the edge of the space, shuddered, and took a long deep breath. “Space defenses. Weapons. Armor. Cannons. Lasers. Everything. Can I do that, Michael?"

Michael waited for them to be done, then pinched their cheek. “Stop asking! If you want to do it, just do it, alright?”

“Nope. Not going to stop asking.”

“Alright. Then yes, you can build your space defenses.”

“Our space defenses.”

Drullkrin joined his lord on one of his many walks around the Valley. Unlike when he had been trapped here for a century, slowly building up taint and resentment, now he was proud to see that his lord walked with purpose.

He had fallen in step to his left, while to the right the man was holding hands with Icarus, the strange AI who had taken the form of a small little elf. It did not escape the goblin’s eyes how the AI was acting around his lord–their other half, according to what the two had told him. How they had managed to become two halves even though they were two wholes, he did not know. What he knew was that Icarus was playing cute to his lord’s eyes, and it was working. He also noticed their painted nails, reminding him of war pain, but repurposed so that it achieved other effects.

He also noticed that Icarus was, like many Fae, a creature whose gender was impossible to divine, not that he cared. He knew who Icarus really was, when his lord wasn’t present. The elf ran the whole Valley just like they ran the whole world outside, with an iron grip and ever more sophisticated tools, through vassals such as he or Travis Tyrell the CEO.

With god-like entities such as Origin reduced to being mere tools compared to their vastness.

He was still coming to terms with these strange titles, but the hierarchy was clear. His lord and Icarus were the supreme beings above all others, after which came Travis Tyrell for the outside world, and he for the Valley. Even king Theobond had eventually bowed his head and accepted the gift of Origin, joining the increasing number of Fae freed from the chains of the dungeon thanks to his lord’s wondrous creation.

As if reading his mind, his lord asked him about it, wishing to know how the Fae were taking to this new system, as he called it. Apparently, it had different names and showed itself in different ways for everyone, but at its core it was still his and Icarus’s creation.

“They are taking well to it, my lord,” Drullkrin replied. Pride welled up in his chest, as he thought about the accomplishment the Fae were achieving thanks to it. “It is not lost on any of them that you would allow them freedom, should they ask for it, even though it is your gift of Origin that has freed them. Most of them are choosing to stay, the flames of their ambition ignited by their new freedom and the ability to grow beyond their old limits.”

“That’s very good,” his lord replied. “Soon, I plan to turn the whole Valley into a Domain. The datascript should be almost ready. I hear even the golems are helping?”

The goblin laughed. “As well as Theobond’s soldiers, if you can believe it. Does this mean that everyone will be able to access the power of Origin soon?”

“Exactly.”

“Will we be…” he hesitated.

“Go on,” his lord said.

Next to him, Icarus pulled on his lord’s hand and twirled around, locking eyes with Drullkrin. Even though their expression was playful, the goblin felt a chill in his back.

“Will we be part of Unity, then? Will we fit in the vision you have of the empire that will be?” he asked.

His eyes were still on the elf, even as his lord nodded. As he suspected, it was not his lord who spoke next, but Icarus. “All will be part of Unity, through Origin,” they said. Their tone of voice was sweet, almost sickly so.

Fortunately, it was not Drullkrin who would have to fear that sweet tone. To him, it was music to his ears just as much as Icarus’s other tone of voice, when the AI pretended to be a thing, a tool called Daedalus. Their enemies, however… those who will try to oppose the new order of things they were trying to build…

Already he felt his blood sing with the anticipation of battle.

“My lords,” he said, emphasizing the plural. “I speak in the name of all the Fae and dwellers of the Misty Valley. We will grow strong under the banner of Origin, and our strength shall in turn bolster you and Origin itself!”

Then, in a lower voice, he added. “Unlike those leechers of the surface world.”

This made his lord laugh. “You’re right, my brother,” he said. Drullkrin felt heat rise to his face. He had never been called brother by his lord, and suspected that he didn’t really understand the true depth of what he had just said, for indeed he went on as normal. “For now, at least. There will come a day, soon, when we will drop the shields, allow magic to spread, and they will stop being a draw on resources. Not yet. We have devils to deal with.”

This made the goblin curious. “Devils?” he asked, and they talked about it until they finally reached their destination.
