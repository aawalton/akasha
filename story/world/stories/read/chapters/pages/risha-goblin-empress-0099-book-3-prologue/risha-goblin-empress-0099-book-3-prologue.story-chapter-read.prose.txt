The Elf kept their hood up, trying not to meet the gaze of the passing group of monsters. Despite their seeming placidity, he had no desire to garner their attention. Goblins chatted with Kobolds and Orcs, a terrifying combination if it ever came to a fight.

The band of hunters chatted and laughed loudly as they passed him by. Several of them looked at him curiously, after all, lone travelers were rare, and his hood blocked most of his features. Still, they didn’t bother him. Whether that was because of the sword at his waist, or because they were more focused on their own worries, Alalio didn’t know.

They passed him by and Alalio finally turned his attention to the path ahead of him. The city was just becoming visible in the early morning light.

It was not the most beautiful of cities, but it was still impressive for such a new civilization. Walls stood tall, with buildings made of stone, and cobble. Outside the city walls, efforts to expand were clear, as buildings in all states were erected. It was there, that Alalio saw the beginning of a building of marble. It was the largest of the buildings being constructed, taking an entire hill to itself. Magic settled over the building, and Alalio was tempted to see what enchantments could be so visible in the morning light.

Still, that was not his objective here. He had a mission. Perhaps it was a fool’s task, but the Ancient Mother had given him this task directly.

At first, he had wondered if she wanted him dead, but he didn’t know what he could’ve done to anger her so.

He made his way towards the city, and did feel some reassurance in the fact that the trees were so happy here. They were healthy, hearty creatures, much like the forests of his home, and their strength flowed into him as he walked, promising safety despite the danger that waited for him here.

The monsters on the path became more frequent as he walked, and he couldn’t stop the fear that filled him as he spotted the first warbeast. He’d thought they would all be asleep by now, but this one had white fur, and seemed fine despite the cooling weather. The energy of the eldritch stuck to it like honey, but its spirit contained that energy with powerful bonds.

He stepped off the path to let it pass, his hand going to his sword despite himself.

The spider noticed his fear, and looked at him as it passed by. The orc walking beside it followed its gaze, and simply snorted in disappointment.

This orc’s equipment was different from the others he’d seen. It wore chitinous armor, and its sword practically glowed with death magic. This looked to him like one of the legendary Deathbringers, fearsome warriors that had taken far too many elven lives.

Alalio had killed more than a few himself, but this one felt different, stronger.

He watched them go for a minute before continuing his descent, and entering the outskirts of the city.

He was surprised to see humans joining the menagerie of monsters. He’d heard of their defecting, of course, but he hadn’t realized how many of them had left their upstart kingdom. The kingdom’s emissary had made it seem like it was a few Drake Riders and their families, not enough to fill their own camp.

The dirt path turned into a cobblestone road quickly, several other paths meeting it and leading into the gates that stood wide open.

Wagons rolled past, taking precious cargo to and from the city. He noticed several of the wagons heading into one of the nearby hills, and spotted the telltale ears and skin of a Dark Elf driving one of the carts.

His eyes narrowed.

It was to be expected, but it seemed that their enemies had already made contact with the Monstrous Empire. He did not hold many of the prejudices others of his kind did, but he’d still seen their poisons take out entire battalions of his kin, and knew that they were not to be underestimated, and far too often, not to be trusted.

He took a breath and focused himself as he made his way to one of the kobold guards that stood at the gate.

The gray wolf’s sharp eyes noticed him immediately, and Alalio noticed the tension that spread across its body.

So many alphas walked these streets, but he hadn’t seen any of their lesser brethren. How many unevolved kobolds must there be to support this number of their evolved brothers?

Alalio stopped, then bowed his head to the kobold. He spoke in the local human tongue, Alveronian, hoping that the wolf would understand him. “I am Alalio, Tender of the Ataro forest. I have come as an emissary, may I speak with one of your leaders?”

The wolf looked him up and down, then growled something to another kobold nearby. The wolf turned to him. Its voice came out like gravel, a rumble that filled Alalio’s body, “Human come.”

Alalio was surprised to hear the human language come from the creature. He’d never known a kobold to speak outside of their tongue before.

Moments later, a harried looking woman came running down the path, following the Gray Lupus that had been sent to fetch her. Something was different about her, but Alalio couldn’t place his finger on it. The mana acted strange around her, flowing in and around her in an unrecognizable pattern.

She froze as she met the Elf’s eyes.

Alalio bowed his head again, “I am Alalio, Tender of the Ataro forest. Who do I speak with?”

“Oh. Uh, I’m Elizabeth, Councilor Ryan’s secretary.” She turned to the wolf, then growled something and spoke softly to him.

“You speak the monster language?” Alalio asked.

She turned to him, and shook her head, and motioned for him to follow her.

He stepped behind her as she calmly walked past the kobold. She was tiny compared to the large wolf, but didn’t even flinch as she made her way deeper into the city. His eyes caught on the sheer variety of creatures that walked the streets. Scaled walked with Orcs, Goblins, Kobolds, Humans, Demons, and even the occasional Dark Elf, or Dwarf. Alalio swore he saw a Shade in the distance before it turned a corner.

“Everyone here speaks an orcish variant of Alveronian. It’s close enough that it doesn’t take too long to pick up.” Elizabeth spoke, not even noticing as several orcs moved out of her way with respectful nods.

The small interaction told Alalio that she was more important than she first appeared. It was rare for an orc to treat any creature that wasn’t one of their own with such respect.

“I’m surprised they stay with the language of the orcs.”

“It’s our Mother’s native tongue.” Elizabeth answered.

Alalio raised an eyebrow. “Mother Shyanne?” A connection to the human mother would explain the strange actions of the mana around the girl.

Elizabeth laughed, then quickly covered it. “Of course not, The Mother Of Monsters, Risha.”

Alalio nearly tripped over himself. For a human to call another mother as their own…. It was unheard of.

His eyes caught on a large Sapling stepping through the streets. What was a monster of nature doing in the city?

Elizabeth stopped in front of one of the larger buildings on the block. “We’re here.”

He looked up at the building of orcish design, all edges and overhangs. It was clearly a leader’s home of some kind. “Where is here?”

“This is the council hall. The council is not in session until tonight. Sun-Chaser, the Mother’s advisor, will meet you inside.” Elizabeth smiled, then turned around, ready to walk away.

“Ah, should you not stay to introduce me and translate?” Alalio called out.

She stopped, and turned to look at him. “I’ll join you in a minute. Councilor Ryan has need of me elsewhere, but Sun-Chaser speaks Human Alveronian perfectly well.”

He bowed his head, “Thank you for guiding me, Secretary Elizabeth.”

She gave him a small wave before hurrying off.

Alalio pushed the doors open into a large hall, several ornate chairs arranged around the room.

Several creatures made their way around the room, speaking in soft voices. Towards the back, a beautiful fox-like kobold sat on one of the higher chairs, nodding along as an evolved Undergoblin spoke to her. He’d expected a goblin to be the Goblin Mother’s close advisor, but seeing the way everyone paid some sort of attention to the fox, and the way her fur glowed with a beautiful array of orange and purples, much like a sunset, he could surmise that she was Sun-Chaser.

She turned towards him at his entrance, and he spotted five tails arranged behind her, a mystical symbol that reminded him of writings about the first kobolds, back when Sparks walked the land.

He walked towards her, drawing the eyes of the few monsters in the room. Once he arrived before her, he kneeled, removed his hood, and bowed his head with his hand on his sword. “I am Alalio, Tender of the Ataro Forest. Ancient Mother Lalaila has sent me as an emissary to greet and establish relations with The Mother Of Monsters, Risha.”

Sun-Chaser turned to the Elf, and studied him with eyes that saw far deeper than he would’ve liked.

Finally, she smiled. “We welcome you, and I will inform Mother Risha of your presence. You can join the human delegation over there.”

Alalio looked up, then followed her gaze to a group of humans wearing the clothing so common to human nobles. Among them, a single Saint wearing a bone mask and bone earrings snoozed, clearly having fallen asleep.

He nodded his head to the Fox kobold, then made his way over to them. He hid a smile. Perhaps, the Ancient Mother’s mission had more hope in it than he realized.
