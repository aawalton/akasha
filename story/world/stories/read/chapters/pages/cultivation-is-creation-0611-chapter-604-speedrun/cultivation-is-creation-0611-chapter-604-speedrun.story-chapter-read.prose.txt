There was silence as everyone stared at Wu Kangming. Whatever technique he had used to casually bisect a Peak Lucid Lawbearer, who was able to reset himself from all injuries, was clearly on a different level to anything I’d seen before, and that didn’t come as a surprise since I wasn’t as familiar with the dao as I wanted to be.

“A dao strike,” I murmured. “Which dao was that?”

“We all have our secrets,” Wu Kangming replied, a whisper of an amused smile on his face. “I doubt you would tell me how you have access to your main body's energies in this realm, so it is only fair that I don’t tell you about the dao I'm comprehending."

The guy did have a point. As typical of me, I’d kept my abilities a secret throughout the expedition, especially qi and the two sun energies, so that Wu Kangming didn’t figure out my true identity. And even though, we both knew the truth at the moment, it didn’t mean I’d feel comfortable telling my opponent exactly how I accomplished something that everyone would say is impossible.

"Fair enough," I said since he deserved the same courtesy to protect his own secrets.

Honestly, up till now, I had some confidence in defeating Wu Kangming in the Dream World because unlike him, I had access to my full abilities. I had doubted he would be able to overwhelm me with only dream qi and his understanding of the sword, but Wu Kangming had proved me wrong and reminded me that these protagonist-types just couldn’t be underestimated.

I never would have guessed he had already touched the dao and was now able to manipulate in a way that let him transcend normal energy systems entirely. I sighed inwardly. It seemed that whenever I felt like I was becoming overpowered because of my various advantages, the cultivation world would humble me.

What other reason was there for my opponent to have a genius-level sword comprehension, a mysterious master sealed in an artifact, a tragic backstory, and dao comprehension before even breaking past the Qi Condensation Realm?

I didn’t get a chance to feel sorry for myself, not that I wanted to, because a spiritual pressure began to gather at the center of step ninety-two, and an elderly figure manifested. The old man had white hair and a long beard that reached his waist. His robes were dark blue and covered in silver embroidery that showed scenes of death and decay. But it was the heavy and oppressive spiritual pressure radiating from him that made me frown.

"I am Zhong Ji," the manifestation said with a voice that suggested he had lived for far too long and just wanted everything to end. "I walked the Dao of Ending for three thousand years before I was consumed when attempting to end a curse that plagued my sect."

With eyes that seemed to be able to see into our souls, his gaze swept over us.

"It is without a doubt that everything comes to an end," Zhong Ji continued. “Every life, every technique, every dao. None can escape fate. But my question is simple: can you accept that ending when it arrives?”

"I don't need to accept philosophical questions, old man," Li Na said, stepping forward as her hands moved in complex patterns. "I just need you to die."

For a second, the ancient cultivator stood there like he couldn’t believe what he was hearing.

“Now listen here, young girl,” he began, “back in my day, we—”

Before he could finish, golden threads spread across the ground and wrapped around the manifestation's legs, sweeping him off his feet into the air. Even though his spiritual pressure surged in an attempt to break free, it was too late, the sacrificial technique had activated, and in less than a fraction of a second, massive gashes opened across Zhong Ji’s chest and arms.

The old man’s leg bent at an impossible angle as the bone shattered, and blood poured from his mouth and nose as internal organs ruptured from the damage Li Na had inflicted by sacrificing her own life.

With a look of shock in his eyes, the old man collapsed to his knees as the injuries overwhelmed his cultivation base, and a moment later, there was only blood on the black stone as evidence he'd ever existed, before even that dissolved into nothingness.

The number in the sky changed from 9 to 8 as Li Na turned toward me with blood running from her mouth and her left arm hanging at a wrong angle. I braced myself knowing that Nine Deaths Transfer technique had activated.

Just like last time, pain exploded through my body as my ribs caved inward, my left arm snapped, my liver ruptured, and my kidneys failed. The sensation was so overwhelming that it caused everything to turn black. When I gained awareness again, I found myself standing exactly where I'd been a second before but with no injuries. I let out a sigh of relief that the Dream World's resurrection mechanism had worked without any issues.

"Thank you," Li Na smiled, her own form free of any injuries.

I didn’t get the chance to respond because the next manifestation was already forming on step ninety-three.

This cultivator appeared middle-aged with brown hair and ordinary features, and he wore simple traveling clothes without any sect insignia. But that didn’t make me underestimate him. His spiritual pressure was genuinely at the Peak Lucid Lawbearer level. And for a reason I didn’t still understand, like the two cultivators before him, he introduced himself.

"I am Zhou Xuan," he said. "I comprehended the Dao of Repetition by observing how patterns recur throughout existence. Birth, death, rebirth. Day, night, day. Beginning, middle, end, beginning. If you ponder well, you will see that everything cycles and that nothing is truly new."

With a wave of his hand, a circular, complex formation appeared in the air above us. What I found interesting was how the symbols repeated themselves in smaller and smaller iterations as they spiraled inward toward the center.

Li Na once again took a step forward, but Zhou Xuan spoke up before she could make a move.

"Let me show you what repetition means," he said, activating the formation.

Suddenly, we were back at the beginning of step ninety-three, watching him manifest. The temporal loop was forcing us to repeat the last ten seconds, but I had no idea what he sought to gain from that because it worked against his favour.

This time Li Na activated her technique immediately as Zhou Xuan's body finished manifesting.

The middle-aged looking cultivator erupted with injuries and dissolved just like Zhong Ji had.

As the number changed from 8 to 7, Li Na turned toward Wu Kangming this time, who nodded once, accepting what was coming. The Nine Deaths Transfer activated, causing the sword prodigy to collapse as the fatal injuries transferred to him.

When he reformed to find himself whole and healthy, his hand moved to his sword hilt as if checking that the weapon had survived the death and resurrection process.

"Interesting experience," Wu Kangming whispered despite the tension in his shoulders that suggested death wasn't as easy to handle as he was pretending it was.

Step ninety-four's manifestation appeared before we could discuss the temporal loop technique. This one looked wrong because his form seemed slightly translucent and his features kept shifting so I wasn’t able to focus on him properly.

"Xu Kong," the manifestation said. "I cultivate the Dao of Nothing, a dao that is about absence. What isn't rather than what is. The space between things holds more truth than the things themselves."

He gestured and sections of the step simply stopped existing.

Even though Li Na tried to form her technique and end this battle just as fast as the other ones, Xu Kong manipulated absence itself to remove the spiritual energy the girl needed to activate her ability.

It was time for me to act. Channeling qi through my meridians, I launched a Phantom Strike toward Xu Kong's chest, but instead of connecting, it passed through empty space where his body had been. Dammit, he'd made himself absent from that location.

Wu Kangming attacked from a different angle with that same dao-infused strike he'd used against Yuan Shi.

"Clever," Xu Kong murmured as the attack found its mark, forcing his form to flicker. "A dao can affect another dao. That is true. But my absence is more fundamental than your cutting principle, boy."

With that, he began to fade entirely, and it looked like at any moment he was going to escape death.
