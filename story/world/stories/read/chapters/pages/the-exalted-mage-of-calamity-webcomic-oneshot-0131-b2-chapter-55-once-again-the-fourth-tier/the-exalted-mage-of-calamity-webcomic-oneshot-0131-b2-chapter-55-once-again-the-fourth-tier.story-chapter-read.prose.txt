Two weeks passed in a blur.

The Mirabelle drifted high above the wilderness, far from Maftem’s walls. Tucked away in the clouds, the ship was covered in a thin veil, hidden from sight.

Aboard the ship were Veronica, Gwen, and the two temporary members of their party: Orban and Theo. The two of them were inside one of the ship’s workshops. After spending some of the money Orban received from selling the Steelhand Smithy, they had bought a small forge and other blacksmithing tools, including anvils and casts. Gwen was on the upper deck, practicing her phoenix spells while focusing on her mana control for long-range casting.

She was surprisingly diligent, something any master would appreciate.

Nasha, meanwhile, was in Maftem. Her body was already used to using Selene’s Tear. As far as Sage’s calculations went, the energy the tear provided was limitless. However, it was hard to pinpoint how limitless. Was it something that needed to recharge? Maybe the pool of available power was deep, but could still be damaged or emptied. It was wiser not to be reckless with it, so restraint mattered more than excess.

Instead, Nasha spent her time singing and working her job in Maftem. She also explained that she had several friends in the city she wanted to catch up with.

As for Veronica, she had an important task to complete.

Inside Mirabelle’s shower room, Veronica sat naked and cross-legged inside the large bathtub. She had learned her lesson from last time, on her journey to Ronswick. Since she had dual mana cores, the process of ascending was slightly more involved for her. This also meant it was more strenuous: perspiration, sweat, extracting impurities, each one a byproduct of focused ascension.

The water was set low, covering the lower half of her stomach. As she concentrated, small ripples emanated outward in steady, concentric circles. Each pulse represented her breaking the wall to the fourth-tier, slowly but surely. Each came with a faint ache, not enough to hurt, but sharp enough that she couldn’t be careless.

[Mana Core Density has reached 96%. Third-tier mana core has reached 11% durability.]

Veronica’s eye twitched as she maintained her concentration.

Ascension was usually unique to a mage’s pathways. For a calamitous mage like her, she had to form an outer shell around her mana cores, one that would act as a new core wall. Then, she would have to shatter her inner mana core wall so the mana inside could expand and fill the new vessel.

Half of the drakebone ash had been brewed into tea using the emberstone she had bought, and was now circulating within her system. That power was currently being used to form the shell needed for a fourth-tier core.

This wasn’t a literal physical expansion, but spiritually, it was the same. If she entered her mental space, her mana cores would appear nearly fifty percent larger than they had been when Hugh tested her inside the mana visualizer.

Veronica drew in a slow breath.

[Mana Core Density has reached 100%. Third-tier mana core wall integrity has reached 3%.]

She tightened her focus. The pressure within her mana cores pressed tight against their shell. It built slowly, like a glass of water about to spill, its surface trembling and wobbling just before the fall.

A snap echoed through her body, something only she could hear. The pressure gathered within her chest exploded, cracking the inner shell and filling the outer wall of mana she had compressed. Her body twitched as both cores finally broke through.

Veronica’s eyes opened. A pale purple light flickered through her pupils as her mana coursed through her pathways once more.

[Congratulations. You have ascended to the fourth-tier.]

[You have completed a quest: Achieve Tier-4.]

[Reward: Spell Overload.]

[New quest assigned: Achieve Tier-5.]

She looked down at her palms and flexed her fingers. They were still stiff, since her body had been locked in place for so long. As she moved them, she could feel her mana channels being filled. Her power was still barely a fraction of what it had been when she was a tenth-tier mage, but it was much closer now.

Her gaze moved to the rest of her body. As expected, she was covered in sweat from head to toe.

She raised her hand and pointed toward a lever at the end of the bathtub, which controlled the faucet. She stopped it once the water filled the tub all the way up to her chest. Next, she dispensed some soap from another faucet directly into the water. Finally, she made a circular motion with her finger.

The water around her began to swirl, creating a smooth vortex with her at the center. She took a deep breath, closed her eyes, and submerged herself. The pressure of the water pressed against her body, washing away the sweat. After a few seconds, she surfaced with a large gasp. She pointed at the drain near the bottom of the tub, and the water slowly disappeared before she filled it again with fresh water.

Her mana control had certainly improved. Now she could manipulate things from a distance a little more easily.

She sat back, relaxing in the tub. It was a good time to check her improvements.

“Status.”

Name: Veronica Everwells

Current Tier: Tier-4 Mage

Primary Path: Path of Ruin

Secondary Path: Path of Tempests

Mana: 1500/1500 MU

Mana Regeneration: 700 MU/hour

Perks: Ascended Body, Sensory Boost, Augmented Visualization Analyzer, Passive Mana Shield, Excess Mana Conversion, Mana Ring Rotational Burst, Flow State, Demon Slayer System, Spell Overload

The values for her mana and mana regeneration were still quite… arbitrary. The numbers themselves didn’t mean much to her. She could feel exactly how full or exhausted she was. Not to mention, the mana regeneration was only her passive recovery speed. With two mana cores, she could accelerate them to exponentially increase that number, entering her exalted form.

“Sage. What’s the perk this time for reaching the fourth-tier?”

[Spells you cast can now be filled with as much mana as you can generate. I will take care of expanding the equation to hold the necessary mana. This will allow you to cast spells that are much more powerful than their current tier.]

Veronica raised a brow, curious.

To a certain extent, she could pour in more mana than a spell required to make it stronger. However, spells were made up of equations. Once mana exceeded a specific limit, the spell would collapse, and all a mage would be left with was a burst of mana backlash.

“So you’re saying I can make a second-tier spell as strong as a third or fourth?”

[In terms of spell output, that is correct. However, it does not mean the spell will necessarily be as strong as a third or fourth-tier spell.]

Veronica hummed. She could understand the philosophy behind it. For instance, if she cast a regular-sized fireball, she could pour in nearly all her mana to make it larger and more destructive. Even if it used more mana than a fourth-tier spell, that didn’t necessarily mean it would be stronger than one.

Even at the tenth-tier, there were a few lower-tier spells she still liked, but they were no longer powerful enough to meet her usual standards. Reinventing each spell at a higher tier was also time-consuming.

After fully cleaning herself, Veronica put on her clothes and left the washroom. She walked past the workshop and noticed Orban and Theo in the middle of forging something. They looked busy, so she left them alone for now. Climbing the steps of Mirabelle, she came up to the top deck, where Gwen was practicing her magic.

A small fiery bird, if it could even be called that, was flying in a circle in front of Gwen. She held her staff out, waving it in a careful circular motion as she tried to guide it. The baby phoenix was constantly dissipating and reforming, as if it were on its last breath but being forced to live.

Veronica stepped closer. “I see you’re up here working hard.”

Gwen jerked her head back to look at her, but quickly faced forward again, trying to hold her concentration. “This… is a lot harder than it looks…”

“I’m sure,” Veronica said. “I’m not good with complicated magic like that. Most ruin mages aren’t.”

The small phoenix gradually slowed until Gwen let out a long breath and lowered her staff. It dissipated into fleeting embers. “So? How’d the breakthrough go?”

Veronica placed a hand on her chest. “I succeeded. I’ve reached the fourth-tier now. I just need to finish forming my fourth set of mana rings, which shouldn’t take long since I’ve been working on them ever since Sage finished repairing my mana cores after the fight in the Valley of Silence.”

“That’s good,” Gwen said, turning around. She tapped her staff against the wooden deck. “Does that mean all of our preparations are ready?”

Veronica nodded. “Since we’ve gotten the lunar salt, we should be good to go for now. The next full moon should be in two days. That’s when we’ll open the portal.”

The past two weeks hadn’t been spent only on training, but also on clearing up any logistical obstacles still in their way. The main one involved the lunar salt. Since the harvest this year was particularly poor, the price had increased, settling around twenty-five thousand vix. After the auction, they didn’t have nearly enough money for that.

Galland was the first person Veronica contacted. Unfortunately, it seemed she had stretched Zenith a bit too thin. He had already given her all the funds he could manage; everything else in their treasury had been reserved for emergencies.

However, they did find one particular noble willing to give them the loan: Lord Vaun. They traveled to his mansion and asked if he would lend them the vix. At first, he refused her. Although he owed her a favor, twenty-five thousand vix was a steep amount to loan away, especially to someone he had known for less than an hour.

However, he changed his mind once he saw the airship docked above his mansion. He had left the auction early and had no idea who had purchased the two airships that much of the city had begun talking about.

When he realized Veronica had the backing of an archmage, he agreed to the loan. But only under the condition that she set up an audience with Ballamus whenever possible, along with paying back the loan interest-free.

Footsteps came up the stairs behind them.

They both turned and noticed Orban climbing the steps.

“Yer finally out?” he said. His apron was dirty, and he was still carrying his hammer.

“Yeah. Reached the fourth-tier.”

“Hmph. After that little sparrin’ match we had, I’m surprised you were actually just a third-tier. Swore you were higher than that.”

She shrugged. “There’s a reason for everything.”

“Reason you ain’t willing ta share just yet?”

“No. Not yet,” she said. “Maybe after you forge me a staff.”

Orban crossed his arms. “Speakin’ of which, you haven’t told me what crystal you want installed in the staff. Do you even have one?”

“No. Not on me. But I do know there will be several available within Avalon.”

According to what she remembered, the expedition teams that went there had seen several treasures on the island, including some of a more magical nature. Mana crystals would be the least of the valuable artifacts.

He grunted. “This Avalon place… I know you told me ’bout it, but do you really think it exists?”

Within the past two weeks, they had also told Orban and Theo about Avalon. Mainly because Morgan’s book said the place contained a forge, one capable of making mythical weapons. Of course, the dwarf didn’t believe it. But it wasn’t as if he had anything to lose if he was wrong.

“Well, if it doesn’t, then we’ll just have to travel to Annesheim for you to make me a staff,” Veronica said.

“I guess that’s true. Should be the only place with a good enough forge if this fairytale turns out ta be jus’ a myth.”

“This place… won’t be dangerous, right?” Gwen asked.

“I don’t know,” Veronica said plainly. “I only know that most of the conflict happened between the people who raided the place. I did hear about traps, but it shouldn’t be anything we can’t handle if we’re careful.”

The dwarven smith raised a brow. “What’s this ’bout a raid? Someone already went?”

Both Veronica and Gwen looked down at the old dwarf.

Although they had mentioned Avalon to him, they hadn’t told him that she was from the future. Nor that the world would end in fifteen years.

Veronica faked a cough. “I’ll tell you about it after the trip.”
