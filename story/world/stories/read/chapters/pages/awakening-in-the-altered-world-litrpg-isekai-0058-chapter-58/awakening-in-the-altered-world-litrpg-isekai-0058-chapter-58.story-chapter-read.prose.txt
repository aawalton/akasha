Adrian’s eyes lingered on Marius, but it seemed he wasn’t curious enough to ask.

“You almost seem happy Caide’s performed like this,” said Marius, grinning. “That’s cold shit. Really living up to your reputation, are we?”

“Caide’s hardly more than a child,” said Adrian, ignoring him. A slight pause. “A child that… for worse, more than better, idolizes me, and his sister, and father. He was frozen in there. He’s aware of his own name, it makes him unable to think. How is a man—a kid, really—supposed to fight like this?”

Adrian gave a slight shrug. “A blemish might be precisely what he needs. He no longer believes he has to be perfect, and so perhaps he’ll try to be himself. Perfection’s an illusion you show to others; you’re never supposed to believe it yourself.”

“He was fighting very silly,” Marius agreed. “Like it all went out the window—were you ever like that?”

Adrian smiled a bit. “I was a wreck. Which is why I think he might very well be fine. That was… entirely preventable.”

“If he’d decided to conserve even a little energy,” said Marius. “Time his tries, pick his chances, yes. That crack on his gem’s a waste. S’pose some credit has to be given to this arcs kid. It’s hard to tell what he is, we’ve only gotten a glimpse.”

“He seems a decent talent,” said Adrian idly. “I’ve yet to see anything that truly excites me.”

“I have, but it isn’t entirely about his performance—we’ll see. I might just keep an eye on that one. Regardless—seems like the talent rankings are coming online?”

The displays over half the folks’ heads were starting to change; a new figure was added.

Marius paused.

“Well, I’m not sure I’d agree with that.”

V-dueling dulled the pain slightly, compared to real life, but it still sucked dying, after having your body shredded and shattered.

The sensations were still very much fresh on Alex’s mind as he was revived, gasping; he was still a little in shock as he stared across the station, at Caide.

The realization was sinking in, of what’d happened.

He’d lost; not only that, he’d failed to even draw blood.

He was starting to realize the difference between him, and someone like Caide, with all his gear; he felt a little embarrassed at how badly he’d misjudged his chances.

He felt a little ludicrous.

He smiled weakly. Fuck that, actually, I’m not going to apologize for trying.

I really kind of thought I’d be able to weather that last…

Well. Whatever.

Which was when he saw Caide walking over. He’d raised his hand, saying good fight.

Caide went right by him, clearly deliberately, and Alex froze.

For a few seconds his face burned.

You just humiliated me in a fight, and you can’t even show a little fucking sportsmanship? Still too good for me?

Well, I guess I don’t have a cracked instance item, like you, or some fancy family backing me.

Prick.

Except it was really a split-second reaction, borne of his embarrassment; in the seconds after, he was able to think a little.

Caide clearly felt quite humiliated as well. And it did make sense why; Alex could see his perspective fairly easily, actually; he recalled clearly Caide’s face as they’d dueled, his expressions—at times flickers of fear.

Alex still felt kind of angry, despite also kind of getting it. You can have reasons for acting like a prick; it doesn’t excuse the fact that you are acting like one.

Whatever.

Alex went back to the walkways, clearing the station; it seemed like section Q’s official was prepping the station for a second duel, between the other two talents—talents who seemed a little shocked still.

Alex was aware of a few eyes on him in the walkways, which he tried to ignore; the second round of fights was starting up, and a few of the folks were clearing out, but Alex also couldn’t help but hear a few snippets of conversation—people weren’t really trying to hide their words. A few O2 Guilders were walking by.

“Wait, so like—was tyrion just, weirdly good?” said an O2 girl, uncertainly. “Or was Caide a little overhyped?”

“Sometimes first fights are weird, fighters aren’t warm yet, especially if it’s against a new face,” said a tall O2 guy, shrugging.

“It’s all style, this St. Clair. Substance isn’t there, he isn’t Adrian, obviously. Adrian at F-rank would’ve crushed this arcs in like five seconds.”

Caide was standing about fifteen feet away, ignoring Alex kind of awkwardly.

“But this guy wasn’t bad? I think? He did shield pretty well, didn’t he?”

It was growing quite clear to Alex, as he listened, that plenty of spectators didn’t have a clue what was actually happening.

Maybe it’s because it was a lot of really fast moving parts? Or, like—it was largely flashes, it kind of sounds like a lot of people didn’t see things clearly.

He was kind of surprised folks were evaluating Caide this harshly. He was hearing a few folks—well, the same few folks who’d just walked by, saying, ‘obviously that sword thing wasn’t that strong, he couldn’t even break this guy’s shields?’

Which Alex didn’t really get. Except—there wasn’t any frame of reference, he supposed? Like, I guess if you see a sword hit a shield, and only barely break through, either they’re both quite strong, or—they’re both not as strong as they might’ve seemed.

Especially since I am seeded 49th, hardly all that high.

If Alex had a choice he wouldn’t have listened in to all this, because it was kind of ragebait, as well as simply uncomfortable, realizing he was being evaluated like this.

Around this point, a few minutes before the next fight was set to begin, Alex realized the display above his head was changing. As was the display above Caide’s head, a few feet away; as were dozens of other displays in his field of view, actually.

Alex’s read,

_1arcstyrion_ (49) (41)

Lv. 5, elo 747?

(41) was added to his display.

Caide’s display featured a (9); and Alex quickly realized what these were, after listening in a bit more and gathering context clues; folks all around them were reacting to it.

Talents rankings?

Subjective talents rankings—he followed the gestures of several passerby, pointing at a second, very long pane of tinted glass, adjacent to the observers’ deck; there was another across the hall as well.

Alex hadn’t realized these were a thing. He was kind of surprised.

But as he considered it, it kind of made sense; the Rising Stars Cup was a talents ranking. And factors such as your Level, or your items, would obviously make a massive difference, regardless of your ‘talent;’ which was why the Cup also employed evaluators, from US Leagues as well as the Guilds, to rank and re-rank talents after every fight.

Alex’s stock had somehow risen, to 41, from what he assumed was his starting position of 49.

And Caide’s had fallen all the way to 9. Which seemed to say to Alex, these ‘evaluators,’ whoever they were, also weren’t quite sure to what extent Caide wasn’t as talented as he seemed, and to what extent Alex was more talented than his elo gave him credit for.

Alex wasn’t at all sure how to feel about being ranked 41; a part of him was quite certain he wasn’t the 41st most talented talent here.

I guess, what else can I ask for, from a fight where I lost without landing a single blow? He mused. Maybe I should be glad I rose at all?

Because I absolutely didn’t get to show what I was capable of in there. Why didn’t I take a few more chances?

This ranking felt a little ridiculous to Alex; it felt insanely subjective, for one. But if a large reason for the Rising Stars Cup was to identify, well, Rising Stars—I guess it makes sense you’d want a rank-ordered list of talents?

It’s probably not even for our benefit, it’s for the Guilds.

Alex was quite ready to simply set it aside, because why care at all what these faceless evaluators’ opinions of him were? He wasn’t here for that. And it was likely influenced by optics, so it’d incentivize showing off a little; he wasn’t inherently built for that, he felt like.

Except—wait a second.

He paused. Didn’t I hear something about talents prizes earlier? I swear folks were talking about it. With Ivy Liu, was it?

Wait.

I swear—

In the description of the Rising Stars Cup, it did mention ‘talents prizes’ for recognized exceptional talents, as a secondary thing, right?

It didn’t specify exact criteria, simply ‘recognized exceptional talents,’ but Alex had a weird feeling, a feeling he went to the section Q official to verify.

“Hey, sorry about this—what are the talents prizes, again?”

“Identify the square, and it’ll tell you,” said the official brightly. “Same as the standard prizes, except without the cash prizes.”

Alex did identify this floating square, high above, and a description of the Rising Stars popped up, along with a secondary blue box detailing the prizes.

First received 35 Credits, as well as a selection of 3 treasures from a prize pool. Second received 25 Credits, and 2 treasures. Third received 20 Credits, 1 treasure. It was as he recalled. And the treasures, potentially more than the Credits, were what were really valuable; since they were drawn from US Leagues Vaults, and specifically tailored to be useful for talents at this Level range.

All three, of course, also qualified you for the massive Duelist’s Cup Stark City, which was a while away.

“And are the prizes exclusive?” At the official’s strange look—“Can you win a talents prize, and a podium prize?”

“Of course!” she said. “It happens all the time, actually.”

“I see, thanks.”

With that, Alex returned to the side of the station, and waited, and watched as the second section Q duel was underway.

He focused on the next highest-ranked person in the section, the Rank 29 O2 Guilder, Karnation_, who seemed to be a Fire archer, as Alex contemplated.

I guess—that changes everything, doesn’t it?

I feel like I don’t have a choice.

He smiled a little reluctantly, and glanced at the second panes of glass.

The duel between the two finished in about 30 seconds; it was Rank 29 against Rank 93.

Alex raised his head, thinking—Clarissa was right. It really is all about optics.

“Next,” said the official. “Karnation_, against arcstyrion! You will have 5 minutes of rest. Timer begins, now.”

Alex exchanged a glance with Karnation_ as he walked toward the station, and lilly_xd walked to the waiting area.

Sorry, dude.

I don’t like it, but—his eyes flickered up to his display, and he smiled wanly. It seems like I have to redeem myself, after that display.

Fine. It’s the way of the world. Whatever.

I’m going to have to show off. Just a little.
