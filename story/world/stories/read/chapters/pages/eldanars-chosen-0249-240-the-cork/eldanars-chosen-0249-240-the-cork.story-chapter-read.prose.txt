The idea of a fighting retreat sounded clean and methodical in the quiet safety of a war room. In the freezing, blood-slicked mud outside the gates of Ashenfall, it was an exercise in pure, suffocating agony.

Josh stood at the front of the jagged, moving circle of veterans. His heavy steel boots carved deep, dragging trenches through the churned earth as he slowly stepped backward. Every single inch of ground surrendered was paid for in burning muscle and ringing steel. The mindless, shrieking horde of zombies and Crypt Ghouls did not care about tactics or formations. They simply poured forward like a rotting, infinite ocean, using the sheer, impossible weight of their numbers to try and crush the tiny island of living fighters.

"Keep your footing tight!" Torin roared, his gravelly voice cutting through the deafening noise of clashing iron and snapping jaws.

The scarred veteran was fighting directly to Josh’s right. Torin swung his heavy iron mace in brutal, crushing arcs, caving in the skulls of leaping ghouls with mechanical efficiency. He did not waste energy on wide flourishes. Every strike was perfectly calculated to shatter bone and halt forward momentum.

To Josh’s left, Korg the beastkin was a blur of spinning axes. The dual-wielding mercenary used his lower centre of gravity to weave between the heavier strikes, cleanly severing the hamstrings and knee joints of the taller skeletal warriors.

They formed a tight, impenetrable ring of seasoned muscle and dark steel, acting as a human breakwater against the apocalypse.

But the physical pressure was astronomical. Josh felt it entirely in his left shoulder. He held the massive, rune-inlaid heater shield rigidly in front of him, absorbing a continuous, unrelenting barrage of impacts. Rusted broadswords, heavy wooden clubs, and jagged, rotting claws battered against the dark metal every single second. The silver runes flared brightly, passively eating the kinetic shock, but the sheer bodily mass of the horde pressing inward threatened to physically bowl him over.

Josh gritted his teeth, tasting copper and ash. He swung his crackling longsword in short, defensive sweeps, entirely focused on maintaining the integrity of their moving perimeter.

The ground beneath their boots was an absolute nightmare. The frozen plains had been trampled by ten thousand marching monsters, turning the frost-covered grass into a deep, freezing quagmire of black sludge, shattered bone, and coagulated blood. It was incredibly slick, making every backward step a desperate fight for balance.

That treacherous footing almost cost them everything.

A heavy-set mercenary named Vane, anchoring the rear-left flank of the veteran circle, took a step backward and planted his iron-plated boot directly onto the slick, severed ribcage of a fallen forest troll.

Vane’s foot shot out from beneath him. The large man let out a sharp, breathless grunt as his balance completely vanished, his heavy chainmail dragging him heavily down onto his back in the freezing mud.

The horde reacted to the momentary gap in the defensive line with terrifying, predatory speed.

It was not a single ghoul that exploited the opening. A massive, densely packed cluster of six purple-skinned zombies, driven by the dark magic surging through their rotting veins, launched themselves forward simultaneously. They did not swing weapons. They operated on pure, brutal animal instinct, throwing their entire bodily weight directly at the fallen mercenary in a horrific, coordinated rugby tackle.

Vane disappeared instantly beneath a writhing, shrieking mountain of rotting flesh.

"Vane is down!" Korg bellowed, pivoting wildly to hack at the encroaching mass.

Josh heard the muffled, panicked screams of the mercenary coming from beneath the pile of bodies. He did not hesitate. He abandoned his forward-facing guard, trusting Torin to temporarily plug the gap, and spun around.

The sight was sickening. The zombies were frantically tearing at Vane’s heavy chainmail, their jagged, rotting fingernails seeking the vulnerable leather padding at his throat and joints. The sheer, dead weight of six adult bodies was pinning the massive man flat against the mud, suffocating him.

Josh triggered Juggernaut's Tread, a brief, blinding flash of blue lightning propelling him the short distance across the interior of the circle.

He did not use his sword. He brought his dark steel heater shield down like a heavy iron anvil. He slammed the bottom rim of the shield directly into the spine of the topmost zombie, shattering the vertebrae with a sickening crunch and physically hooking the creature. He hauled his arm upward, forcefully peeling the heavy, thrashing monster off the pile and hurling it backward into the mud.

Korg joined him a fraction of a second later. The beastkin roared, driving his axes into the ribcages of two more zombies, using the leverage to violently drag them away from the fallen mercenary.

"Get up!" Josh roared, reaching down with his free right hand to grab the thick iron collar of Vane’s armour.

Josh hauled backward with a massive surge of adrenaline, his Adamantite-infused muscles straining. He physically ripped the heavy mercenary out from beneath the remaining three zombies, dragging him roughly across the slick mud and throwing him back into the centre of the defensive ring.

Vane gasped for air, his face pale and smeared with black sludge, but his chainmail had held. He scrambled back to his feet, grabbing his dropped halberd with shaking hands.

"Hold the line!" Torin shouted, his iron mace crushing the skull of a skeletal hound that tried to leap into the momentary chaos. "If we stop moving, they will bury us all!"

They quickly re-established the perimeter, stepping backward once more. The brief rescue had cost them precious stamina, and the horde was pressing in closer than ever.

As they slowly fought their way back towards the towering timber walls of Ashenfall, Josh risked a quick glance over his shoulder.

Through the swirling, foul-smelling smoke and the falling snow, he could see the massive, thirty-foot-wide archway of the main city gates looming just fifty yards away. The staging area was a scene of frantic chaos. The last remnants of the civilian culling squads and the terrified refugees were desperately trying to squeeze through the bottleneck.

But the retreating veterans were no longer the only target.

The sheer volume of the undead horde meant that the tight, defensive breakwater Josh and the veterans had created was simply not wide enough to contain the entire tide. Hundreds of shrieking zombies and rotting villagers were spilling around the edges of their veteran circle. The monsters were peeling off, ignoring the heavily armoured fighters entirely, their milky eyes locking onto the soft, vulnerable civilians still bottlenecked at the gates.

"They are slipping past us!" Korg yelled, severing the reaching arm of a ghoul. "They are going for the stragglers!"

Josh looked at the terrified civilians and the exhausted militiamen trying to push their way into the city. He saw a young woman stumble in the mud, weeping in sheer terror as a pack of corrupted skeletal hounds bounded through the mist towards her.

If those monsters reached the crowded gateway, it would be a slaughter. The civilians were packed too tightly to draw weapons or defend themselves.

Josh felt the heavy, iron anchor of his class settle deeply into his chest. He could not let the horde reach the gates.

He stepped entirely out of the tight, protective veteran circle, exposing his flanks completely to the crushing tide.

He called upon his severely depleted mana core, ignoring the sharp, warning ache in his temples. He needed to be the brightest, most undeniable threat on the entire battlefield.

Josh triggered Lodestone Taunt.

He did not just pulse the skill once. He flared the magic repeatedly, pouring raw, unadulterated willpower into the magical broadcast. The sensation was physically taxing, feeling exactly like a heavy, iron bell tolling violently inside his own ribcage. The invisible, magnetic waves of aggressive magic washed outward in massive, overlapping ripples.

The effect on the horde was instantaneous and terrifying.

Every single undead creature within a fifty-yard radius stopped dead in its tracks. The skeletal hounds bounding towards the fallen woman snapped their jaws shut. The zombies attempting to bypass the veterans froze.

Their heads turned with mechanical, unnatural synchronicity. Hundreds of milky, lifeless eyes and glowing green sockets locked directly onto the towering human standing alone in the mud. The magical taunt completely overrode their primal hunting instincts, painting Josh as the single, supreme threat in their entire existence.

A unified, hollow shriek erupted from the horde, and the entire ocean of rotting meat violently converged on his position.

The physical punishment was immediate and utterly devastating.

Josh raised his dark shield, bracing his boots deep into the sludge. A massive wave of zombies crashed into him simultaneously. The kinetic force pushed him backward a full foot, his boots carving deep furrows in the earth. Rusted weapons, jagged claws, and snapping teeth rained down upon his heavy steel plate armour from three sides at once.

"Josh, fall back!" Torin roared, rushing forward with the veterans to desperately cover the Guardian's exposed flanks.

Josh did not retreat. He held his ground, acting as a solid, immovable anchor. He swung his electrified longsword in wide, desperate arcs. The crackling blue lightning flared wildly, vaporising rotting flesh and shattering bone, but the magic was visibly sputtering. The high-voltage static flickered and dimmed as his mana core scraped the absolute, hollow bottom of its reserves.

He took a glancing blow from a rusted spear that scraped harshly against his steel shoulder guard, showering sparks into his visor. A Crypt Ghoul managed to slip beneath his shield, its claws tearing uselessly against the thick steel plating of his thigh. He bashed the creature away, instantly bringing his sword around to block a crushing overhead strike from a skeletal warrior.

He fought blindly, mechanically, entirely consumed by the brutal, suffocating grind. He flared the Taunt again, ensuring not a single monster looked away from him. He was buying the civilians the seconds they needed with his own blood and bruising muscle.

Finally, the frantic, screaming bottleneck at the city gates began to thin.

Garrik, the tavern landlord, was standing within the shadow of the massive iron portcullis. The broad-shouldered man grabbed the weeping young woman by her tunic, physically hauling her out of the freezing mud and dragging her roughly over the threshold into the safety of the staging area.

"They are inside!" Torin yelled, smashing his mace into the skull of a towering orc zombie. "The civilians are clear!"

Josh bashed a skeletal hound away with his heavy shield, taking a rapid, desperate step backward to rejoin the tight veteran circle. They had successfully covered the retreat. The only living souls left outside the walls were the heavily armoured fighters forming the rearguard.

They slowly ground their way backwards, fighting for every single inch, until their steel boots finally crossed the threshold of the massive, thirty-foot-wide archway of the main gates.

They were standing beneath the towering stone walls of Ashenfall. The staging area behind them was relatively clear, the panicked civilians having fled deeper into the residential streets.

But the victory offered absolutely no relief.

Josh risked a quick glance over his shoulder at the massive, yawning opening of the gateway, and a cold, sharp knot of absolute dread formed in his stomach.

The main entrance to the city consisted of two colossal, solid oak doors, heavily banded with thick black iron, designed to stop a charging army. But right now, those doors were pinned completely wide open to the freezing winter wind.

Josh looked back at the endless, shrieking sea of the undead pressing violently against their veteran shield wall. There were thousands of them, right on their heels.

The brutal, tactical reality of the situation crashed over him.

"Torin!" Josh bellowed over the deafening clatter of clashing steel, his voice raw and frantic. He deflected a rusted halberd, his arm burning with sheer agony. "How the hell are we supposed to get through the gates without bringing the entire bloody army inside with us?"

It was a fatal, terrifying flaw in their retreat. A thirty-foot-wide archway was entirely too broad to serve as a defensive bottleneck for a small squad of tired fighters. If the veterans turned their backs to run into the city, the zombie horde would simply wash right through the open gates behind them like a tidal wave of rot, completely flooding the staging area and slaughtering everyone inside.

Torin smashed his iron mace into the chest of a Crypt Ghoul, throwing the creature backward to buy a fraction of a second. The scarred veteran glanced quickly over his shoulder, his dark eyes rapidly assessing the massive, pinned iron doors.

Torin’s tactical mind, honed by years of brutal warfare, instantly formulated a desperate, highly dangerous solution.

"Jax!" Torin roared, turning his head to single out a lithe, dual-wielding rogue fighting on the rear flank. "Get off the line! Get into the staging area right now!"

The rogue did not question the order. He ducked beneath a swinging club, slipping smoothly backward through the veteran formation and sprinting into the shadow of the gatehouse.

"Listen to me!" Torin bellowed, his voice echoing loudly in the stone archway. "Run to the left door! Unpin the heavy timber braces holding it open! Swing the left door completely shut, and wedge the dropping bar into the stone floor! Lock it down solid!"

Jax sprinted towards the massive left-hand door, throwing his weight against the heavy wooden braces locking it to the stone wall.

"Then get to the right door!" Torin continued to shout, deflecting a leaping skeletal hound. "Unpin it, but do not close it all the way! Push it until it meets the left door, but leave a gap! We need a gap wide enough for two men to squeeze through! No wider!"

Josh’s storm-grey eyes widened behind his visor as he understood the brilliance, and the terrifying risk, of the veteran's plan.

Torin was not attempting to close the gates on the horde. He was attempting to artificially narrow the massive thirty-foot archway into a claustrophobic, six-foot-wide chokepoint. If they could funnel the infinite numbers of the undead into a gap only wide enough for a single zombie to step through at a time, the heavy fighters could hold the line indefinitely.

"Hold the perimeter!" Josh ordered, surging forward with renewed, desperate energy. "Do not let them push us inside until those doors move!"

The veteran squad tightened their circle, pressing their boots firmly against the freezing cobblestones directly outside the archway. They fought with a frantic, burning intensity, knowing their survival depended entirely on the rogue working behind them.

Behind them, the mechanical, deep groaning of ancient iron hinges echoed loudly.

Jax, assisted by two terrified city guardsmen who had remained in the staging area, threw their entire combined weight against the massive, thirty-foot-tall oak door on the left. The heavy timber shrieked in protest against the stone floor. Slowly, agonisingly, the colossal door began to swing outward on its iron hinges, moving to block half of the massive gateway.

CLANG.

The heavy iron dropping bar on the left door slammed securely into the deep groove carved into the cobblestones, locking the massive timber firmly in place.

"Left is secure!" Jax yelled, sprinting across the archway towards the right-hand door.

"Push them back!" Torin roared.

The veteran squad surged forward as a single, unified wall of steel, pushing the undead horde back five crucial yards to give the right-hand door the clearance it needed to swing.

The right door groaned loudly, beginning its slow, heavy arc across the cobblestones. Josh watched the massive shadow of the timber sweeping towards the centre of the gateway, narrowing the wide, terrifying opening second by second.

"Hold it!" Torin bellowed, looking over his shoulder at the closing gap.

Jax and the guardsmen stopped pushing.

The right-hand door came to a grinding halt. Between the heavy, iron-studded edge of the right door and the solid, locked timber of the left door, there was now a narrow, vertical gap of open air. It was barely six feet wide.

"The chokepoint is set!" Jax confirmed, drawing his short swords and taking a defensive stance directly behind the narrow opening.

"Fall back!" Josh commanded, his voice cracking with sheer exhaustion.

The veteran squad immediately broke their wide, circular formation. They moved with practised, fluid discipline. The fighters on the far left and right flanks peeled away first, sprinting backward through the narrow, three-foot gap into the safety of the staging area.

The undead horde surged forward instantly, shrieking in mindless fury as the prey attempted to escape.

Josh and Torin remained on the outside, acting as the final, desperate rearguard. They stood shoulder to shoulder, directly in front of the narrow gap, their heavy shields raised to block the surging tide of rotting meat.

They were the cork in the bottle, and the entire weight of the apocalyptic ocean was about to crash directly down upon them.
