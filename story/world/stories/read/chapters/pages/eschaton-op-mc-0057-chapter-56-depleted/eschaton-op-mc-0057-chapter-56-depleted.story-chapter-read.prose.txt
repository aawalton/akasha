The black scaled dragon stands up, towering over me. It’s nearly two hundred feet from tip to tail. A series of jagged black spikes, all at different angles and lengths, run in two rows from its nose to its tail, bracketing its spine. A faint green glow surrounds it. Its body is so radioactive its causing its own localized Cherenkov radiation. As it wakes and stands to its full height, my internal Geiger counter screams. A Typical would die in an instant, their nervous system fried by the intense gamma radiation.

It roars, and corium spittle nearly splatters over me. Only a quick backpedal saves me from being covered in radioactive goop. It lunges forward with a claw as long as my sword. I give up ground again, leaving another crater behind.

I rocket in the air, intending to land on it. Its tail whips out and slams into me. I crash through the wall of the cooling tower, and through a second. I finally crash into the open field between the main facility and the rows of cooling towers.

The dragon charges through the cooling towers, ignoring the collapsing concrete megastructure. Steel and stone bounce and shatter on its scales like rain. I watch it approach and feel… nothing.

I haven’t been scared of a monster in so long. Maybe the Nightmare back in DC. Not even my duplicate really scared me. Looking back, I was probably too busy at the time. The ground shakes and jumps as the warship-sized dragon stomps towards me.

I’m tired. I wish I could sleep.

I should be concerned. I should feel something, anything. Rage would be easy, but even that slips from my grasp. It’s hard to be angry at forces of nature. I can’t say I’ve gotten mad at winter storms. They’re just something you deal with as best you can.

At this point, killing monsters is just a thing I do. It’s not combat, especially when inside of a Fracture. Without the risk of collateral damage, it’s easy, as fighting against monsters goes.

The dragon swipes at me with a paw twice my size. I lazily duck under it, and my sword takes half of the paw in retaliation. Molten salt pours from the severed limb. The dragon’s bones glow brightly, made out of nuclear fuel rods.

“I’m fine. Really, I am.” I tell the dragon. It roars back at me. “I have to be. I can’t not be fine.”

It spews radioactive corium at me. I dance out of the way, having long since calculated and predicted it based on how it tenses its muscles. I’ve gotten enough scans of it and seen it move enough to give me a highly accurate model of its musculoskeletal structure.

It rages and stomps and swipes at me. It carves up the ground with depleted saturnium claws and nightmarish strength. It melts and burns away concrete rubble with the breath of a nuclear meltdown. I dance around every strike and attack.

A step here, a jump there. A duck just low enough for sparks to fly off my armor as a claw the same size as my sword skitters off. I roll with the hit, using the attack to dodge a follow up I saw coming half a second in advance.

It’s easier here. In the Fracture. I’m disconnected from the internet. I don’t have to field a thousand emails an hour, a hundred phone calls from sycophantic politicians or executives. I don’t have to hear ten thousand people begging for help that will never come. I don’t have to be the witness of a dying world.

Because Earth is dying. Both at the claws of monsters and by the hands of those who are scrambling to hoard what little is left. It’s sickening. What am I supposed to do? I’ve tried to be a hero. One that everyone needs. But being a hero didn’t come with a manual. It didn’t give me the ability to know the right answer to everything.

So I fight. And fight and fight and fight and fight. I said I’d slow down, but if I slow down I have to look at the rest of the world. It’s cowardice, I think. Everyone praises me for diving into Fracture after Fracture, but they shouldn’t be.

The dragon tries to slap me with its tail again. A slash of my sword sends it flying away, and even more molten salt blood pours onto the increasingly destroyed terrain. Craters dug by me and by the dragon dot the landscape. Some of them are filled with boiling radioactive sludge.

The screams of rage turn into roars of agony. It stumbles back, though a few missing or mutilated limbs make it clumsy. It falls to the ground with a boom in its haste.

Great wings beat, sending toxic waste and molten metal splashing over the devastated terrain. A half-hearted slash severs a wing made of asbestos laced fabric. I wade through the muck towards the head. Panicked eyes roll around, looking for a way out. Normally monsters are suicidally angry, and willing to push through almost anything to attack people.

Everything and everyone has a breaking point.

My blade comes up, and goes down. The struggling monster twitches once, twice.

The reward orb and exit form, signaling the end of the Fracture. Time for me to go. To step back on Earth, and to return to being a witness to every horrible thing in the world.

I turn back to the dragon, the exposed bones glowing with a sickly green light. The bones are made of nuclear fuel, and harvesting it might take a while.

I heft my blade, and get to work.

The moment I step out of the Fracture, alarms in the nuclear plant start screaming. Several nuclear plant workers and CBRN trained soldiers wrapped in layers of heavy hazmat gear motion me into a decontamination shower.

The howling alarms are not the first thing I notice. Instead it is the Aura that permeates the entire facility. Through my esoteric senses, I can see what I can only describe as a star outside. The world shudders under the weight of our presences. Everyone around me stutters, and a few even fall to their knees. The world is unsteady. Our Auras clash, though not aggressively. Simply in the way two binary stars warp the fabric of space-time. It is in our nature, our existence.

I haul back on my Aura, balling it up as best I can. The nuclear plant workers all take a deep breath and shakily return to their duties. Even Typicals, with their nearly non-existent Aura senses sensed the clash of Titans.

A peer is here. General Guan Yu, China’s premier Ascended, has arrived.

After I’m cleared from the radioactive isotopes and heavy metal covering my armor, I exit the facility. General Guan Yu is outside of it waiting to greet me. He’s an imposing man, easily the tallest of everyone in the entire base. He wears extremely ornate Chinese lamellar armor. Tiny plates made of steel laid over each other not unlike scale-mail make up the armor over his chest and stomach. His legs and arms are covered by stiffened leather with silver chainmail overtop. Held in a one handed grip with the butt planted on the ground is a massive, long bladed spear. It has a heavy silver blade, something that would belong on a falchion rather than a polearm. The entire thing comes up to my shoulders.

“I am General Guan Yu, of the People’s Republic of China. I am the leader of our great nation’s military, and our most powerful Ascended. I greet you as a peer, Machina,” he intones.

“It is good to meet you, General. I hope we have a productive relationship in this dark times.”

He strokes a long, thin bear, as black as the night. He raises his weapon and slashes to the side of him. A gash in reality appears, and he pulls it open. On the other side is a howling, snowy blizzard.

“Machina, I would like to talk privately, if that would be acceptable?”

I nod and step through Guan Yu’s rip in the universe. As soon as I’m through, I’m being battered by a vicious snowstorm. Chunks of ice slam into my armor before shattering. The tear closes behind the General as he too steps through. He grunts in annoyance as the snowstorm ruffles his hair. With a flash of will, his glaive shines. The storm lessens, flowing around us rather than trying to throw us down the mountain side.

“Where are we?”

“The eastern face of K2. It’s never been climbed by Typicals, we will be alone here. Now that we aren’t at risk of being overheard, is there much I believe we have to say. But firstly I would like to thank you for assisting us. Your tireless efforts are an inspiration to us all.”

“No need for thanks. I’m just doing what has to be done.”

“Well, your words on unity and cooperation have not fallen on deaf ears. While you were in the Fracture, I made some calls to a few choice people in our government. If you’re willing to hand over a majority of the nuclear material to us, we will release what we learn about it. And, of course, anything else you choose to hand over as well, not just nuclear material.”

“Sure, that works for me. I found a lot of saturnium—what I started calling the magically infused uranium—throughout the Fracture, among other things. Strange metals, most of them were converted into something truly magical rather than simply infused with Potentia. I don’t recognize them.”

General Guan Yu raises an eyebrow. “That’s it?”

“Yes?” I ask confusedly.

“You aren’t going to ask for anything else? I’m sure you’ll be receiving truly jaw-dropping offers for whatever you do decide to keep.”

“Are you trying to negotiate on my behalf?” I ask half-jokingly.

“Merely that I wish us to stay on good terms. I would hate for you to feel that you were taken advantage of. You are a man who enjoys honesty, and I shall be honest with you now.

“We Ascended are weapons of mass destruction. The capabilities that we wield are unmatched except maybe by each other. Even that is an uncertain guess given how…varied we are. The world is too fragile, too easily bent by accident. Surely you felt it earlier.”

“The Empire of the Sun.” I reply with no small amount of distaste

“Yes, you see it as well. A single one of them can—and has—slaughtered an army, razed a city, conquered a country. How do you suppose anyone stops them? Who could?”

“Us? The Ascended?”

“Do you really want a fight between people strong enough to rip apart cities? Do you really want to see what will happen to all the people caught in the middle of that? War never stays confined to those who start them. Usually it’s the opposite. We don’t even know if we Ascended are even mortal. The fact we must ask even these basic, fundamental questions means a physical confrontation between any of us, not just the Empire, is too great a risk. This brings me to my final request.”

He reaches into a pouch at his waist and hands me a thick letter. Something inside glows with Potentia.

“Something for you and Morgan. I hope you’ll consider it.”

“What is it?”

“A council of peers. I think the time has come for others like us to engage in more official discussion, don’t you think? Given what we’re capable of doing—what some of us are doing already—it’s important we can keep a line of communication open. And to offer us a way of coordinating our responses in case of extreme emergency.”

“If you’re planning on trying to convince me to help you rule the world, we’re going to have problems, General.”

He chuckles.

“No, no. Nothing like that. Let’s call it the United Nations of Ascended, hm? I assure you, I have no desire to rule the world. I fully understand that sentiment is not shared by all of us. Consider this a chance to put a halt to such aspirations. A diplomatic one.”

“I’m not sure the Empire of the Sun deserves diplomacy after all they’ve done. What they continue to do.” I mutter darkly.

“It’s not about what they deserve,” the General says grimly. “It’s about what’s necessary.”

He places a hand on my arm.

“I understand what you mean. But this is the way of the world. Monsters rarely get their just dues. Only in recent times has that even partially changed. Please share the missive with Morgan. Both of you are invited, of course. You’ll need her help anyway. We can talk more then.”

I exit the Chinese Embassy in DC, only to be met with a crowd of reporters. Many of them are crying out questions. I ignore all of them, instead focusing on the one person given a wide berth. Morgan floats just above the ground, coat slowly waving back and forth. Her arms are crossed, and her fingers tap on her arms.

Uh oh.

I follow her out to a park with enough empty space. Still silent, she paints a portal in the open air. It opens with a crack. We both walk through it, and arrive in Morgan’s gallery. As soon as it closes, she rounds on me.

“What were you thinking?!” she yells.

I jerk back in surprise.

“What’s going on? What’s wrong?” I ask.

She sighs.

“Oh Mach. You’re so… you. You did nothing wrong, or made a terrible mistake. It depends on how you look at it. Unfortunately most people aren’t happy about you running off to China.”

“They asked for help. What was I supposed to do?”

“Say no. They can handle themselves. They have six Ascended for crying out loud. They didn’t need you!”

“They asked for help. I wasn’t going to turn them down. What’s the huge issue anyway? I thought you were all for community and working together.”

She sighs again.

“Mach, I am about working together. But it’s about the optics of the situation. You’ve told the US government to stuff it, and then not long after you run off to China on their dime? It doesn’t look good.”

“I don’t care about the goddamn optics Morgan!”

It’s her turn to jump back in surprise, and she tries to respond, but I run right over her words.

“I really couldn’t possibly give a flying fuck about them in the goddamn slightest. I’m so goddamn tired of being able to do nothing. I will help where and when I can. I could help there, so I did. If the US wants help, all they have to do is ask. I don’t care if China is our ‘rival’ or whatever nonsense the politicians want to cook up. This is no longer about nation against nation. This is about people versus monsters. This is about the survival of all humanity! How could optics ever possibly compare to that?!”

I turn away and rub my faceplate.

“Ah, hell. I’m sorry Morgan.”

“No, Mach, I’m sorry. I shouldn’t have blown up at you over that. I know who you are, Mach. Everyone does. It wasn’t fair of me to get mad at you over something no one ever told you about. It’s just…” she clenches her fists.

“It’s just that optics are important. Relationships and how people think of you are important. It doesn’t matter how critical or necessary the work is. If people really don’t like or trust you, they’ll be more than willing to sink their own boat if you’re on it. Trust me. I’ve seen it more often than I’d like.”

She gestures to the gallery.

“Do you think getting this compound built so quickly was by throwing money at it? At a certain point, money can’t solve a problem. It took leaning on friends and allies to get this squared away. And yes,” she admits. “It did take money, and lots of it. But without the friends I’ve made, even ten times what I paid wouldn’t have been enough. I just don’t want to see you suffer for being you, you know?”

“Yeah. I get it, I think. It’s just so stupid.”

“It is. I think people are greedy, short-sighted, and maybe even a little bit naturally cruel. It’s just how it is.”

“No, you’re wrong,” I say, shaking my head. “I think people are good. Just that those who are in power happen to be those who are willing to do whatever it takes to get there. The average person is good. The average person cares about their neighbors, their community. They want to help, they want to work together. Just because those in power are greedy and callous, that those on top are cruel doesn’t mean everyone is.”

“I… never saw it like that,” she says after a lengthy pause. “I grew up around industry leaders and powerful politicians, and since I could talk I was taught to live that life of wealth and power. Maybe you’re right. I hope you are.”

“People will surprise you. You just have to trust them.”

“Okay. I guess we’ll trust them. I hope you’re right. On another note, I have something for you. I had some time while you were gone to make you something. I think you’ll love it!”
