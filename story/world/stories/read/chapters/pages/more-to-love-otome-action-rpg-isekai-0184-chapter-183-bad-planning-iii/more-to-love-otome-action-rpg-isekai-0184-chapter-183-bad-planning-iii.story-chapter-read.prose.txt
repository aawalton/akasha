Sue sheepishly poked her head over the edge of the wall on the far side of the building.

“Hi… I chose a really good time to come over, huh?” She heaved herself over the wall. “Are you okay?”

Based on how nervous Sue seemed, Aida could only presume she had some idea of the conflict in the Loreh family. And why wouldn’t she? Levi’s summary stated that Aida had essentially been adopted into the Heslia family, and that the two girls grew up as sisters. It wouldn’t have been surprising for a young Aida to complain to Sue about what was going on in the home.

“Yeah, I’m fine. You came back pretty quickly.”

“Right! Yeah, Dev left pretty soon after you and Lily did so that he can go talk to his team about their first-ever assignment.” Sue sighed as she leaned against the wall beside Aida. “I wish I could have been part of the mini patrol.”

“Why can’t you? Do you have something else you need to do?”

Sue shuffled beside her. “Well, Arryn said that me coming along would just be taking up a slot from a Talonwing member. He wants them to learn to work together, because after their apprenticeships to the Gullbeaks, they’ll have to split off on their own.”

She sighed again before she brightened. “Anyway, I didn’t get a chance to ask—how did your meeting at the new HQ go?”

Aida grunted. “I don’t know. The new employees don’t seem like they care about what happened with Agnes. They didn’t even take down any details about Agnes and Ernie!”

Struck by this sudden realization, Aida straightened indignantly. They clearly weren’t even trying!

“Sue—the next time you go back the HQ, can you at least ask them to speak with those two?” Aida asked urgently. “They live near Buddington Market, and Ernie says he has a tailoring stall in there—”

“Oh, sure—”

“Ugh, I wish I had drawn a map or something. But Agnes also volunteers at Charity Temple, so I guess they could also track her down there—“

Aida kept rambling about ways for the Gullbeaks to find the residents she had met two days ago until she realized she wasn’t getting any responses from Sue. When she turned to her captive audience, she found the lilac-eyed beauty watching her with large, sympathetic eyes.

“What?” she asked, unnerved. Sue blinked.

“You really are worried about Ezra, aren’t you?”

“Of course, but this isn’t only about Ezra—it’s linked to all the weird stuff we’ve been seeing. The weird monster attack patterns, what happened to you in the Western Woods, that evil presence that was in the school lake…”

As Sue tilted her head at the vehemence in her voice, Aida realized she didn’t have a good explanation prepared for why she thought that way—and she couldn’t just tell her they were all in a game where the plot existed to force Sue to do certain things.

“Okay,” Sue said soothingly, alarm in her eyes. “I get it…”

This is not how one should be talking to the main character who’s supposed to save us all, Aida chastised herself. She took a deep breath to stabilize herself.

Objectively, she had no reason to be this worked up. Agnes was just the first case. If she really wasn’t a fluke, then other such cases would start springing up around town, and the Gullbeaks would be certain to hear about it—and since Yoon and Tyda were already aware of the mechanism, they would be able to react accordingly, no matter what their newly hired employees thought.

If she was being honest with herself… she couldn’t accept the premise that she was only a side character. Theoretically, it was an easy enough concept to understand: she was just the side character in everyone else’s life, just as everyone else was a side character in her life. But considering herself as a side character in her own life?

That was much harder to swallow.

“Aida…” Sue pressed her down onto an overturned bucket so that she sat. She crouched in front of her, blinking in concern. “I can see that visit with Agnes affected you a lot. And I don’t know how to best help you…”

She hesitated, chewing her lip worriedly as she looked into Aida’s eyes. “Look. You’re leaving in two days, so you can’t follow up with them anyway. But you have me. I’ll help you get to the bottom of this, okay? You… you work hard on your meditation training while you’re away, and I’ll take on investigating any reports of endless sleeping.”

Sue squeezed her hands tightly, her heat spreading through her reassuringly. “The instant I figure something out, I’ll rush over to Burston or Moonglade, wherever you happen to be at the moment. And the instant you make a breakthrough with your meditation…”

She paused, considering. “Your job doesn’t have as much flexibility as mine, does it?”

Aida gave her a wry smile. “Just because an Adventurer doesn’t have a set schedule doesn’t mean it has more flexibility. You’ll probably be too busy fighting and rescuing all of us to worry about me.”

“Don’t say that,” Sue said, exasperated. “That’s a lot of pressure.”

She poked Aida’s forehead and gave her a cheeky smile. “But don’t you worry, I’ll definitely come to your rescue if you ever need it.”

Aida laughed. “I appreciate it.”

“At least until Ezra comes back,” Sue added. She smiled wistfully. “Then I’ll let him take care of you.”

Aida sobered at Sue’s words. Theoretically, with Sue now being out of school and no longer restricted by academic obligations, things would start happening faster now… which meant Ezra would come back sooner.

Or his soul would be trapped in the spirit realm permanently, in order to increase the stakes.

The unpredictable nature of this world was straining Aida’s patience. There had to be something she could do to figure out what was happening. She couldn’t just wait for things to happen. Being reactive meant being behind.

There had to be a plot-relevant reason behind Ezra going into the spirit realm and refusing to wake up, and if she could only figure out what the connection between that, and the monster attack in the Lake, and Sue’s unknown adventure in the Western Woods…

“Sorry,” Sue said, chagrined. “I didn’t mean to bring the mood down… I just wanted to skip to the happier part of your life.”

“It’s not your fault,” Aida said quietly. She sighed. ”Thank you for worrying so much about me. You’re a good friend.”

“It’s the least I could do!” Sue puffed herself up, standing with her hands on her hips so that Aida had to look up at her from her bucket. She wilted a bit when she looked back at Aida, looking… guilty.

“What’s wrong?”

“Nothing!” Sue perked up immediately again, though Aida could sense that her mana was still swirling with unsteady emotion. But as she narrowed her eyes at her, Sue quickly diverted the conversation. “What are you planning on doing tomorrow? It’ll be your last day in town.”

Aida considered. Since she and Lily had their ride to Burston sorted, she didn’t have to go back to the Buddington InterTravel Terminal to scout the process, and she didn’t have any other plans lined up with anyone else.

“I think… I might spend the day with my parents,” Aida said slowly. “They’re obviously still feeling bad about what happened before, so… I think it might be worth it to spend some time with them. Show them that I don’t harbor any ill will towards them, you know?”

Forgive and forget, although for me… it’s more like ‘forget.’

Sue nodded vigorously, though she still looked a little uncomfortable. Despite that, Aida could tell she was trying to hide her discomfort. “Yeah, yeah… that would be really kind to them.”

Maybe she’s weird about it because she thinks I’m too forgiving… but I can’t hold a grudge when I don’t know what the back story is…

Aida frowned thoughtfully. She couldn’t force Sue to spill the beans on what her concerns were, especially since she was trying so hard to be respectful towards Aida’s relationship with her parents, even though she clearly disapproved in one way or another. But she might be able to glean a little more information if she forced more interaction between the two parties… or at the very least, take care of one worry that kept lingering in the back of her mind.

“Hey, Sue? Can you do me a favor?”

“Yeah, of course! What do you need?” Just like that, Sue rebounded into the helpful, energetic best friend who was willing to drop whatever she was doing to support her friend.

“While I’m gone, will you watch over my parents?” Sue’s earnest expression closed a little bit at the request, though she still maintained an eager smile.

“I mean, just check in on them whenever you’re at home base,” Aida added hastily. “My dad says they shouldn’t have any more issues with the family they engaged me to, but… you know how it can be, something might happen later.”

“Yeah, that makes sense,” Sue said, nodding slowly. She gave Aida an encouraging smile. “I’ll take care of them. You can count on me.”
