“Perri gave us a rough idea of what happened when the Asvarians came to Avalon,” Veronica said. “We wanted to know more about them.”

“Asvarians?” the Great Stag repeated.

“The ones in gold.”

Phillus hummed. “I see.”

“She told us that the one you call the Last Gold is trapped inside the castle. That he killed the others and somehow became younger each time?”

He nodded. “That is correct. For a time, the gold ones worked together in search of an escape from Avalon. But as the years passed and age began to take them, they turned against one another.”

His gaze shifted toward the castle in the distance.

“One among them was vastly stronger than the rest. With every kill, he became younger. Stronger. After the first few fell, the others could no longer stop him. The Last Gold slaughtered them one after another.”

“And that’s when you sealed him inside?” Veronica asked.

“Morgan left us instructions to seal the gold ones within the castle,” Phillus said. “But there were too many, and they rarely gathered in one place. There was never an opportunity to trap them all.”

His expression hardened. “Once only one remained, we acted.”

“Matches what the bug told us,” Orban said.

Phillus slowly turned toward him. “What?”

Theo immediately leaned forward, hands spread. “Um, what my Master means is, was there anything else important we should know? From how Perri described it, the Last Gold sounds almost like a vampire.”

“A vampire?” Phillus repeated. “You outsiders have a name for creatures that feed upon the lives of others?”

“We’re not exactly all the same race,” Gwen chimed in. “Orban’s a dwarf, and Veronica’s an elf.”

“Part elf,” Veronica corrected. She turned back toward Phillus. “Perri also told us you kept one of the Asvarian bodies.”

“I do.”

“We’d like to examine it. If the Last Gold was draining something from the others, there might still be traces left behind. Anything that tells us what he was doing, or what his race actually is, would help before we enter the castle.”

Phillus studied her. “Before I grant you access to anything, I would hear your true purpose for coming to Avalon.”

Veronica paused.

There wasn’t much point pretending they had come purely out of concern for the island.

“We came for what was left behind here,” she said. “The castle, mostly. Its vaults, and the forge.”

Phillus’ eyes narrowed. “Treasure. That’s all you outsiders ever want.”

“Morgan wrote about Avalon. She mentioned the Asvarians, the castle, and the treasures inside. She also wrote that if someone eventually reached this place and confirmed the gold ones were dead, they were free to take what they needed.”

Phillus said nothing, simply studying her.

“We aren’t here to raze the island to the ground. If the Last Gold is alive, we’ll kill him. If your friends are still alive inside, we’ll rescue them.” Veronica glanced toward the castle. “If we solve a problem that’s been sitting here for four hundred years, I think it’s fair that we take something in return.”

“How exactly do you plan on defeating the Last Gold if he still lives?” Phillus asked. “We hear echoes and heavy impacts from within the castle every so often, so we assume he does. You could not even defeat me. How will you defeat him?”

Veronica smiled faintly. “You’re assuming I was actually trying to kill you.”

Phillus scoffed. “Of course not. But from what you displayed, I can estimate the extent of your power.”

Veronica chuckled quietly.

“I know you were holding back,” he continued. “But so was I. I could not risk destroying the Lightless Spring. Even with that considered, I do not believe you are strong enough to defeat him.”

“How strong is the Last Gold, exactly?” Nasha asked.

“Strong enough to defeat several guardians with ease.”

“How many of you are left on the island that can fight?” Orban asked. “What if we all work together to defeat the old fogey? Four hundred years ain’t nothin’ to scoff at. Even if he absorbed the life force of the others inside the castle, he can’t be immortal now, can he?”

“He could be immortal, if he’s actually a vampire,” Gwen said.

“That remains to be seen, girlie. So far that’s just you all speculatin’.”

Phillus closed his eyes. “Aside from me, there are two other guardians still on Avalon. However, one of them is too old to fight any longer. The other… has no interest in freeing those within the castle. They believe the seal should remain untouched until every soul inside has turned to dust. She’s very… eccentric. Never one to be involved in things.”

Phillus opened his eyes. “You assume time has weakened him. That is possible. But I have no proof of it. Many of the island’s guardians were trapped inside that castle. If he did find a way to absorb the vitality of a guardian, he could live for thousands of years.”

“Then that’s even more reason to examine the body,” Veronica said, leaning forward slightly. “We need to know whether he’s actually a vampire, or if some artifact or strange power is responsible. If we can figure out what he is, we may be able to exploit a weakness.”

A few moments passed in silence as the Great Stag considered her words.

“Very well,” Phillus said at last. “I’ll bring you the body.”

It took Phillus around twenty minutes to return.

When he did, everyone immediately stood.

Behind him, several thick roots moved across the ground, carrying a large slab of stone. On top rested a single body. Beside it was a smaller slab carrying gold armor and several other items.

Phillus stopped before them and lowered both slabs onto the ground.

Veronica narrowed her eyes. At first glance, she would have thought the man was sleeping. He was completely naked, with no armor or jewelry on his body. His skin was pale, but not in the way she would expect from a lifeless corpse. There was still some color to it. His muscles hadn’t atrophied, nor had his flesh sunken down to the bone.

He looked healthy. Alive, even.

But everyone could tell that wasn’t the case.

He was around five-foot-eight, with an otherwise average build, perhaps slightly more toned than most. Nothing about him immediately distinguished him from a human. No pointed ears to mark him as an elf, nor any features resembling beastkin or another humanoid race.

When Veronica looked closer at his face, she noticed his eyes.

Completely white. No pupils or irises.

Nasha leaned forward. “He certainly seems quite… average.” Her eyes then traced lower across his body. “Mostly.”

Veronica glanced over. “Come on, be serious.”

She shrugged.

Gwen coughed and looked away once it fully registered that the corpse was bare. Theo, on the other hand, stood a little straighter and made a deliberate effort not to stare.

“So this is one of them Asvarians? Doesn’t look drained to me,” Orban said.

“I submerged his body in the Lightless Spring,” Phillus said. “I was hoping I could restore his life and interrogate him, keeping him prisoner. Unfortunately, he had already passed.”

Veronica stepped forward and placed a finger against his arm.

Sage. What do you have?

[Preliminary results conclude he is human. There is nothing I can find that suggests he belongs to another race.]

“So?” Gwen asked, glancing back at Veronica. “Got anything?”

Veronica hummed. “Well… it seems like he’s human. Nothing stands out that proves otherwise.”

“If he was bitten by a vampire, wouldn’t he have fang marks on his neck or something?” Theo asked. “If Phillus healed him, wouldn’t that mean the marks are gone?”

“That’s… a good point.” Veronica turned toward the Great Stag. “Do you remember if he had any injuries on him?”

Phillus shook his head. “No. His body was too shriveled for me to tell.”

“So we got nothin’, then,” Orban grunted. “He’s human, and we got no idea how the Last Gold killed him.”

Theo walked over to the pile of armor and other artifacts. He picked up the pieces one by one, inspecting them for damage.

There was none.

“Master, I don’t see any dents or scratches on these,” Theo said.

Orban walked over and took a look of his own. He held up the golden chestplate. “Hm… yer right. Nothin’ here. Don’t see any ’chantments on it either, so it can’t be self-repairin’.”

They spent the next several minutes examining the man’s body and searching through his equipment. As they had already confirmed, the Lightless Spring had restored the corpse to near-perfect condition. There were no visible injuries left behind and nothing that hinted at how he had died.

Among the items he carried, however, Veronica found something quite valuable.

“A storage ring,” she said.

Storage rings contained small pocket dimensions that could be used to store and retrieve items. Since very few mages specialized in time-space research, they were exceedingly rare.

Even in her past life, Veronica had only been gifted three.

It didn’t surprise her that an Asvarian would possess one.

She slipped the ring onto her finger and held out her fist, releasing everything stored inside. Disappointingly, not much came out. It seemed the Last Gold had already looted most of the valuable items.

Still, there was one thing among the contents that immediately caught her attention.

A few sealed glass vials rolled across the stone slab, each containing small blue pills. There were also several sheets of thin metallic paper covered in faint spell equations, three empty mana elixirs, a bundle of dried herbs, and a handful of small trinkets Veronica couldn’t immediately identify.

Theo picked up one of the metallic sheets. “Transcription paper?”

Veronica nodded. “Looks like it.”

Orban sifted through the rest. “Couple o’ mana potions. Nothin’ special. This one looks like some fancy compass.” He held up a small golden disk with a narrow needle set into its center. No matter which way he turned it, however, the needle didn’t move.

Veronica spotted the eleven-pointed star among the items, made from alternating black and gold metal.

The insignia of an Asvarian.

They set the useless items aside until Veronica noticed one last peculiar object. A small leather pad. It wasn’t exactly a notebook, but something similar. There were no loose pages, and it opened through a pair of metallic latches along the corners.

Inside, lines of unfamiliar writing covered the pale surface.

Veronica studied the text closer. She recognized the script, even if she couldn’t read it herself.

It was the language of the Asvarians.

Sage immediately began deciphering and translating the text, rearranging the symbols within Veronica’s view.

“Expedition order, field unit seven,” Veronica read aloud.

Everyone straightened and looked toward her. Theo and Orban seemed particularly surprised that she could read Asvarian.

“What is it?” Nasha asked.

“Mission orders, from the looks of it.”

Veronica continued reading. “Primary objective: head to the indicated coordinates and capture the Fatebearer. Do not kill.”

Below that was a list of eleven names, with one marked by a red star. Presumably, it belonged either to the unit’s leader or the corpse lying before them.

There were two additional sheets she could flip through, each containing another objective.

“Secondary objective: head to the indicated coordinates in Veilfallen and acquire twelve healthy specimens of Hollow Mourning Bloom. Third objective: head to the indicated coordinates in Ichmentar and capture as many demons displaying exorbitant amounts of demonic energy as possible.”

“Demonic energy?” Gwen repeated.

“Are Veilfallen and Ichmentar different worlds? Like Avalon?” Theo asked.

Orban crossed his arms. “Wouldn’t surprise me. Think I’ve heard of Veilfallen before, but I can’t remember where. Almost wanna say it was another fairytale, same as this place.”

“Seems plausible, especially if these Asvarians are involved,” Nasha added.

It was valuable information, but none of it helped with their current problem. They still couldn’t determine how the Asvarian had died, nor whether the Last Gold was some kind of vampire or possessed another means of draining vitality.

“Do you remember how the Last Gold drained the others?” Veronica asked Phillus. “Did he bite them? Use an artifact? Maybe some kind of spell?”

The Great Stag shook his head. “I do not know the exact method, but I believe it required physical contact. Whether it was a bite, as some of you suspect, or something else, I cannot say.”

“So that doesn’t rule out vampires,” Gwen said.

“Doesn’t confirm it either,” Nasha replied.

“What about the guardians or anyone else living on Avalon?” Veronica asked. “Did the Last Gold ever drain one of them?”

“No. I have never heard of him attempting such a thing. He only drained the other gold ones.”

“Maybe he can only drain other Asvarians?” Theo suggested.

“Odd restriction,” Orban said. “Unless they’ve all got somethin’ in common.”

Gwen’s eyes widened slightly. “If that’s true, then maybe he can’t absorb the guardians inside the castle either. If he’s only able to drain Asvarians, he could be extremely weak after four hundred years.”

“Not a bad thought, outsider,” Phillus said. “But it is still only an assumption. If he simply chose not to drain the guardians before, and eventually did so inside the castle, then he may be far more dangerous than expected.”

“You said the Last Gold only became younger, right?” Gwen continued. “He regained his strength because he was already old and frail. That doesn’t necessarily mean every kill made him stronger than he used to be. Maybe it only restored him to his physical peak.”

Phillus tilted his head, considering it. “That may be true. He might have grown somewhat stronger than when he first arrived, but perhaps not by much. Even so, the gold ones were all extraordinarily powerful.”

“How many guardians are there?” Veronica asked.

“Nine in total. Six remain trapped inside the castle, including Perri’s father. Then there is me and the two others still roaming Avalon.”

“And the Last Gold is stronger even if all nine of you fought together?”

Phillus nodded. “Of the nine, only seven possess enough strength to challenge him at all. I am the second strongest.”

“Is there any other way to defeat him?” Gwen asked. “Some kind of trap, special artifact, or instruction Morgan might have left behind?”

“I recall no such method,” Phillus said. “If there were one, I would have already tried it.”

“Um…” Theo said.

Everyone turned toward him.

He hesitated for a moment.

“What if… we don’t fight the Last Gold?”
