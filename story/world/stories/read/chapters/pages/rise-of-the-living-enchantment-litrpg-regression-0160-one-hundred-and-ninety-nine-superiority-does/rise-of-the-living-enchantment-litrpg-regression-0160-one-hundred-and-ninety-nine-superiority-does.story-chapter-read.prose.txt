With a sigh, Aiden dismissed this portion of his interface. Out of all the four skills he had gained, he could only say for certain that from the information flooding his brain only three were truly useful.

[Enchanted Awareness] was a skill almost every [Enchanter] who got strong enough had. It allowed an [Enchanter] to gain knowledge of every enchanted tool within a certain vicinity around him. He had possessed it in his previous life, and knew the advantage it gave him. It was a very useful skill.

[Disenchantment] was on the tricky side. As long as he interpreted the enchantment into hand signs, he could weave an enchantment into existence that would terminate the effects of the enchantment in question. The conditions were that he had to be present upon its activation and act at almost the same moment as the enchantment’s activation. It was a good skill to use against [Enchanter]s and those who relied on enchanted items. Still, he would need a lot of practice with it if he was to go up against enchantments of true worth. Speed and knowledge was needed. Luckily for him, he had the knowledge.

[Disrupt Engraving] was of little to no use to him. If a person wanted to make an engraving null and void, all they had to do was as simple as striking a mark across the engraving. Ruining the engraving in any way ruined the effect. The only use for the skill was in regards to engravings that were fashioned to simply release catastrophic effects when tampered with before or upon activation. Booby trapped engravings, to be more precise.

The last skill, [First Support] was the odd one out. He’d never seen it before, neither had he ever heard of it. But his mind told him its purpose. Picking an enchantment of his choice, he would bind it to his mana permanently. It would never change. It would activate upon impact, then go through a cool down process. Choosing the enchantment to bind was a no brainer. But that was something to be handled soon enough.

Moving on from his gained skills, he moved on to check on his stats.

[Dexterity 85 > 121], [Agility 56 > 89], [Mana 92 >129], [Speed 66 > 89], [Perception 80 > 101], [Strength 58 > 79].

Aiden stared at the final state of his stats. The interface had been nice enough to give him the final update. He studied it. [Dexterity] had increased by thirty-six points. That was the highest increment. It sounded like a lot until it was put into perspective. Killing the Immortal—Aido Barragon—had given him over forty levels.

He was fairly certain that if not for the threshold of fifty levels, he would’ve gone far beyond it. It gave a new meaning to the threshold. If a person decided to make someone overpowered by allowing them to kill something they had no right killing, they would still be locked to the fifty levels threshold. The question became this, was this a design made by the interface itself or a design of the body?

Still, there were a few things on the title that bothered him. The first was the immunity to aura effect.

Three hundred and fifty-one.

It was a very specific number and a very suspicious level of specificity. But while it bugged him, it did not worry him. What worried him was the second thing, the one in the title description.

Perhaps the gods should keep an eye on you.

Aiden nodded slowly. That was an acceptable argument for him. The problem was that the interfaces believed in the gods, and it believed that they just might not like him anymore.

Well, you refused to believe in them until an interface told you otherwise.

Personally, Aiden liked to think that it was not his fault. The very foundation of his religious belief was in one true God. And being teleported to another world had not been enough to make him stop believing. And even when he began to doubt the existence of his own God, it was not enough to make him believe in the existence of another.

Until an almighty interface told you otherwise.
