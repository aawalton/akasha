Chapter LXI (61)

The children never slept. Whether fueled by the ever present melody echoing through the ruins, or simply by the freedom of no schedule, they displayed limitless energy. Which put Mitsuko in a difficult position. Ideally, she would sneak away and investigate the pyramid while the bulk of the children napped. But that didn’t seem to be an option for her.

“I need a way into the temple,” she reflected. She stared up at the pyramid from where she sat on a fallen tree at the edge of the ruins.

“A keen observation,” Sterling replied, rubbing his face with a paw.

“I might be able to shadow that acolyte, Hallie, and get inside. But I’ve yet to encounter her yet. And one mistake could result in my mind being invaded. Then I’m back to where I was at the start of this loop.”

“True.”

Mitsuko sighed. “Do you know of a way in?”

“I do.”

Mitsuko weighed her options. “If I do lose these memories, will my questions be reset?”

“Of course. That would only be fair.”

“Then I’ll use another question.” She hesitated, frantically thinking about whether or not she had a better question. She could use it to gain knowledge on the guardian. Sterling had implied he knew who this ‘Mother’ was. Knowing her capabilities could prove invaluable in their upcoming confrontation. But it mattered very little if Mitsuko had no means in which to face that guardian. “How do I access the temple?”

“Follow me.” The cat arched his back in a big stretch then trotted off.

He brought her around the back of the pyramid. A few of the children called out to her as she passed, but she pretended not to hear them as she chased after Sterling.

“It should be…ah, yes. Here we are. Exactly as I recall. Now, I do wish to warn you that the moment you enter, there will immediately be a pendulum that will attempt to remove your head from your shoulders.”

Mitsuko crouched and pushed aside the thick green foliage. At a glance, the pyramid’s brick looked like all the others. But as she felt at its side, she found a divot. Her small hand fit inside perfectly and her fingers slid in, groping around until they found a latch.

She heaved, pulling back, and the wall shuddered. After a moment of nothing happening, Mitsuko was about to cast Mend on it to trigger the secret door into opening that way. But then it clicked and the brick swung inward.

Mitsuko, ready for the pendulum, took one step in, then immediately two steps back. A blade swung down from the side, swiping past her and nearly removing her nose. Mitsuko glanced at the floor where she’d stepped and spotted a pressure plate.

“Mia!” someone yelled behind her. “Mia! Where’d you go? Is your kitty safe? Why was he running?”

“Damn.” They’d followed her, likely thinking she was trying to catch Sterling. Of course it would have looked like that to them.

Sterling strolled forward, his weight too light to trigger the trap mechanisms.

“Can I use Mend to freeze everything in time?” Mitsuko asked the sage. “Reverse it so slowly as to not move at all?” If that was possible, then maybe she could stroll through the temple without concern.

Sterling scoffed, not bothering to look back as he continued on his way. “No. That’s not my spell.”

Not his spell…there were implications there but Mitsuko didn’t have time to dig for more information. She dove into the temple.

The bladed pendulum attempted to swing down at her again but she cast Mend, blasting it backwards.

Only, she pushed it too far back and then suddenly it was swinging back at her again. She dodged to the side and felt her foot crunch down on a different mechanism’s pressure plate.

Mitsuko glanced down at her foot and saw a green oozing slime slowly spreading over her leg. She cast Mend on it. But the slime resisted her spell. She attempted to tug free, but it held onto her.

“Mia? Is that you over there?”

There was a shuffling of the foliage near the secret entrance. Mitsuko cast Mend again, this time on the wall. It reassembled itself, sealing her inside and plunging her into darkness. More muffled voices outside but Mitsuko ignored them. The slime was up to her knee now.

She flicked her wrist and jabbed her sword down into the ooze. It gripped the blade, now grappling it as well. So Mitsuko created another. And another.

After the fourth sword, Mitsuko realized that the slime had stopped ascending up her leg. It was receding.

Then it occurred to her, the slime was attempting to consume the ice swords and crawling up that rather than her flesh. Excellent. Maybe it liked the freshwater taste of the blade as it melted?

A minute later she tugged her leg free and stumbled off to the side.

“I think she went this way,” a muffled voice said. One of the children was standing on the other side of the temple’s wall.

“Do you think maybe she knows elemental magic? Maybe she moved the temple’s stone.”

Sterling scoffed. “As if this building didn’t have ancient wards in place to prevent exactly that. Damaging this temple with anything other than powerful temporal spells is next to impossible. Even spatial spellcraft would require a master to rip it open. Of a quality I doubt your era can provide.”

“Interesting,” Mitsuko muttered.

Sterling turned, his glowing green eyes the only light source. “I’m including that information as part of the package deal that came with you asking for an entrance into the temple. Happy to comply with any other architectural related questions.”

“What other traps are in this temple?”

“You think I know every trap? Please. Why would I bother to take note of that? There are thousands of traps across the several temples in the archipelago. I only knew of the pendulum because it once splattered a companion of mine and I had to reattach their pieces before death took a hold. A very stressful outing.”

“So what architectural wisdom can you supply?” Mitsuko asked.

“Well I could launch into the historical construction of this building. It is actually quite fascinating how the stone masons recovered the stones this temple is constructed out of. The quarry caused quite a number of casualties. And the constructors of this temple had never before built something out of stone before so they required an outside source of help. Note how the temple takes influence from western architecture?”

She tentatively reached out and touched the stone wall. Nothing happened. Good, that at least granted her a bit of security.

“I can also supply you with the knowledge of the central chamber. However, it is quite tricky to access without a map. Let alone a light. But perhaps I can guide you in the general direction of your objective.”

“That would be great. Thanks, Sterling. Can you help me avoid future traps while you’re at it? Give me some sort of general layout of where to expect danger? You must know something from your journey here.”

“Alas, all traps were deactivated swiftly upon my previous arrival. I did lecture the temple’s servants at length after the debacle where their little tricks split open my companion. The verbal reprimand was enough for them to rescind and deactivate the other dangers.”

Mitsuko followed the cat’s glowing eyes and slowly moved forward, one hand gripping a newly formed sword while the other traced the wall.

“I need to start carrying a lantern with me everywhere,” Mitsuko said to herself as they walked.

Sterling ignored her comment and continued rambling about the method in which the architects had the stone transported. They used complex mechanisms to get it through the jungle. When she questioned why they didn’t just use magic, Sterling let slip that the island’s previous inhabitants couldn’t cast earth elemental spells. They had no synergy with that branch of magic and historically abstained from stone structures before the construction of this temple.

That was interesting. Mitsuko knew that gnomes like Holly had an innate talent for illusions, but hadn’t ever researched other races’ specialties.

“Are there any races that are particularly talented at temporal magic?” she asked Sterling, cutting him off mid-sentence.

He paused. “No. There are no races naturally gifted with our art.”

The bluntness of his response contrasted with his normally long-winded replies. She didn’t think he was lying to her, but there was something else there.

She only had one question remaining at the moment and she liked keeping something to fall back on in an emergency, otherwise she might have pursued that line of thought further.

With the weight of her next step, Mitsuko heard a soft clunk.

“Ah, that’s bad,” Sterling said.

“What’s—”

“Run!” Sterling declared. Then he sprinted forward.

Mitsuko chased after the sage’s glowing green eyes, currently unconcerned about triggering further traps. If Sterling deemed them in danger, Mitsuko was inclined to trust the cat.

Behind them, something smashed into the ground. Mitsuko slowed her run, believing the danger to have passed and thought to return to a more careful speed to avoid other triggers. But then realized Sterling had done no such thing and his green eyes continued to shrink in the darkness. And a grinding noise behind them revealed enough of the puzzle for Mitsuko to understand why. One hand still brushing against the wall let her feel the vibrations as whatever had hit the floor now pursued them. The rough noise of stone grinding against stone grew louder as it chased her, gaining on her shortened stride.

Something gave way under her foot and Mitsuko cast Mend instinctually, hoping to have undone the damage. Nothing notable appeared in her path so she counted that as a success and thought no more on it. She barreled through another two traps, one that shot darts into her neck and another that sliced at her and nearly removed her arm. Both times she cast Mend on the wounds. Her body rejuvenated as it expelled the poison and her arm’s pain immediately faded.

Mitsuko rapidly attempted to cast Mend on the object behind her, but lacking sight made distinguishing the object near impossible. She didn’t know what exactly she was trying to send back in time. So her spell miffed, accomplishing nothing.

“Veer to the left!” Sterling called to her. The light of his eyes dipped in that direction and disappeared.

Mitsuko felt the wall disappear and her hand extended out into open air. She leaped to the side.

Before she even hit the floor, she felt the whoosh of air as a large object passed her by.

“What—” Mitsuko struggled for breath. Her tiny lungs struggled to take in enough air. She hadn’t been very athletic as a pre-teen. Then she realized she could just cast Mend and her body’s fatigue disappeared. “What was that?”

“A very large, round stone,” Sterling said. “Remarkable work avoiding the danger. I considered throwing myself between you and it in a noble effort to slow the danger, but correctly deemed you more than capable.”

“Horse shit,” Mitsuko said. “That’s the fastest I’ve ever seen you run.”

“I was simply performing my role as your guide to the best of my capability. I guided you to safety, did I not?”

Mitsuko rolled her eyes. While the darkness obscured her vision, she hoped the gesture was still seen by the sage.

“What’s next?” she asked Sterling. “How much further until we reach the chamber with Verdant Island’s sage?”

“Oh. We have barely even begun. We’re still on the first floor after all.”

“The first of how many?”

“Six,” Sterling replied. “The sage will be in the central chamber at the top.”

Mitsuko barely resisted the urge to kick those glowing green eyes as they twinkled in the darkness.
