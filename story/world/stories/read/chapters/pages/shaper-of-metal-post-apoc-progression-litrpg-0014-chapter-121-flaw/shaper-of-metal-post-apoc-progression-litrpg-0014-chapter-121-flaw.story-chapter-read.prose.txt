Chapter 121: Flaw

To Chime’s credit, his reaction in the snap timeframe needed was exactly what it should’ve been. Ignoring the suddenly visible and convenient targets showing up — so long denied him — he immediately spun around and lifted his hand to direct sound back at the reformed metal wall, to make a way to his other two teammates, knowing it was critical.

Bo was ready, though. She’d left Chime untouched all this time, just to hit him ‘raw’ on exactly this sort of occasion. She didn’t disrupt his powers because she didn’t need to — she disrupted his hands. They went spastic, and he dropped his cowbell and his offhand clap-stick, or whatever it was. He couldn’t interfere with the wall, in any case.

Meanwhile, Highfive exploded out of the soundproofed room, his sheath fully formed and surrounding him, calling out, “Lemmie give you a hand!” He became a giant fist rocketing across the space, aiming to smash Chime dead on-

Naturally, Defiant was there, interceding and interposing himself in the way, taking the charge head-on with his hands out, blocking and being pushed back in a slide like so many times rehearsed in sim training. Highfive was ready for this, too, pulling off a last-second juke in a physical feat he’d only been doing his whole life as a true-blue sportsy boy. He spun and pushed off of Defiant, carrying past to avoid the full force of his enemy’s rebounding force, even utilizing it in a way to make space. Highfive had a hard head, yes, but he’d learned an even harder way that Defiant simply could not be overpowered.

Highfive moved to position himself between them and the metal wall, finally spinning around to face Chime and Defiant, his maniacal laugh audible even from a distance as he rose from the floor and filled space. His sheath-hand wriggled its fingers in anticipation.

This was all picture-perfect. It wasn’t only Chime that could get through that metal wall, as Defiant, something of a juggernaut with such things, could force his way through manufactured barriers and utterly shrugged off any active reinforcement.

On the other side of the wall, Hiccup kicked the panel-wall, denting it. Jack wasn’t going to play around with that — he immediately repaired it and reinforced it. This didn’t take his full focus, however. From the ceiling, he dropped metal cords, directing them at Chime. Unsurprisingly, Defiant intercepted and fought off the main branches doggedly. This was fine, as it kept the bastard occupied.

A sweeping cord managed to trip up Chime, though, sending him to the floor, as he still struggled with himself, his arms flailing and the fingers of his hands twisted around. On top of dealing with this, he had to throw himself into a roll and dodge the cords ready to follow up and punish him.

“Diminishing returns are coming up heavy,” Bo reported, referring to her disruption on Chime. “Switching to hitting him with a Sandy. Plenty of energy to exempt all of you right now.”

The telltale edge of the distortion could be seen, but that was all. Chime would be suffering far worse. It was so damning to just now be dropping that, even if they had training against it. On the other hand, Chime had AoE, and it wouldn’t do anything at all to Defiant.

On the other side of the wall, Hiccup kept up a barrage of focused, empowered kicks on the door, forcing Jack to spend time keeping it repaired and reinforced. Jack walked a fine line between that and keeping offensive pressure up on Chime, unable to focus on one thing.

Highfive moved in, making a strong effort to stretch past the intercepting Defiant to finger-slam the fallen Chime, utilizing his superior reach. Chime was deeply harried at this point, with both Jack and Highfive striking at the same time, and his perception of space was completely warped.

On top of this, someone triggered the quick-changing, blaring alarm that had been waiting, creating another barrier to anything Chime tried to do. Perhaps only seconds of difficulty, but in this situation, pure gold.

Délight’s pulse went off, and the barrier of the fortress did nothing to stop it. It hit Chime and Defiant and enhanced them for a second. Defiant managed to curl his body in and throw off Highfive’s finger-slam maneuver, causing it to whiff. Chime, meanwhile, used his enhanced movement to find an angle to the metal wall filling the hole. Even with Highfive still blocking the way, he couldn’t cover all the space.

Chime raked a small hammer over a xylophone on his hip, sending a cone blast of sound as he ignored his flank, by necessity. He missed initially, and the intensity seemed disrupted, but the blast had a lingering AoE as he ‘played’ — he adjusted and swept the cone over the perfect spot quickly, and just as quickly, the tone was modified, gaining back strong intensity.

Jack recognized what was happening. Defiant had good eyes, and as soon as Chime blasted, he gave him a heading correction.

Damn. Great teamwork right there.

Jack was already moving to punish Chime, a cord wrapping around his ankle and jerking him down…

The sound hitting the metal wall didn’t blow through it, instead seeming to twist all inside, within, and through it, as Jack felt something like a vast spiderweb of damage in the tiniest capacity. He’d weakened it; made it fragile. Repair was neither quick or simply.

Jack knew what was coming. He tried to brace and reinforce the wall in response — just as Hiccup was drop-kicking herself through it in a feat of supreme speed and force, shattering the wall’s fragile metal everywhere as she burst through. Immediately, she displaced herself back into a charging position for Highfive’s back.

The coordination, the sheer beauty of the teamwork, was almost mentally staggering to Jack.

Nonetheless, Jack grinned behind his mask. But, once again… a fatal flaw exists in their tactics.

Chime cried out in terrible pain, as a shimmering chalky figure suddenly materialized, leaning over his prone form, a dagger plunged about where Chime’s kidney would be, slipping just under the lower protection of the vest in that perfect benefit of angles to having your enemy flopped down and exposed. Obvious sim chamber protections blocked it from true penetration, and subsequently paralyzed Chime. Pain had been simulated, that was for sure.

The chaos and confusion had been an absolute heavenly romp for the likes of Inkblot, and he’d bided his time while moving in, lurking until the perfect moment arose to strike.

Almost instantly, Defiant was there, bodyblocking Inkblot away, who took it in stride, falling back, rolling, and retreating, already fading away and going invisible again.

But Highfive had no less killer instinct. He was already moving, and the nearby Hiccup was still between displacements, as well as the whole group being on the downflow of Délight’s aura. Highfive did the quickest thing he could in the time allotted: he formed a hammer fist and smashed Chime into the floor. It was something of a rush job, as he had little choice but to turn around and face the enraged, oncoming Hiccup.

Chime was extremely bad off. Jack added to it by tightening his cord and snapping the sound-user’s ankle, surely breaking his foot. He wasn’t ‘out’ exactly, as the brace of the vest had kept his insides from being turned into goo from the slam. But he was likely on a track to being taken out shortly, and the internal bleeding simulation likely made him a goner. Naturally, if he could be finished off, they should.

Defiant reacted to this by rushing to rip Jack’s cord free of Chime. Mostly irrelevant, as Jack couldn’t deal much more damage to the broken ankle, but it did keep Jack from pulling him further away. A dubious desire, as Bo had her Sandy locked on Chime. She probably should’ve just done a basic radius instead of the lock, but hindsight was 20/20.

“Délight thinks she’s doing something,” Bo reported. “Disrupting it! I’d like to disrupt her aura too, but full interference will cost me heavily.”

“Yeah, do it,” Highfive replied. “I can handle Hiccup.”

Jack recognized this as a bit of a decision point. It was tempting to blot out that pesky aura, but it was entirely workaroundable if they coordinated timing. They weren’t quite in position to capitalize fully on hosting Délight.

“Just the offense disruption,” Jack said. “All the aura is doing is stalling. Half the time, it can’t help.”

Hiccup’s charge came in much less effectively than she would’ve hoped, thanks to the Sandy all but tripping her up. Highfive almost swatted her in punishment for her failed offense, instead just grazing her, and she displaced to readjust. There was no helping it — she would have to take her time inside that field, so she couldn’t yet come to her beau’s ‘rescue.’

Délight indeed had come limp-running in and was trying something, but stumbled slightly as if woozy, from Bo’s disruption. On the other hand, she was positioned right beyond the edge of Bo’s Sandy radius.

Jack was primed to go full offense here. From the ‘branch’ of a metal-framed ceiling, he unleashed cords on Délight and Chime at once, while also throwing out a feint at Hiccup to help out Highfive, who was effectively checking Hiccup from helping Chime. Meanwhile, Defiant was lurking and waiting, ready to burst in whatever direction he was needed.

Thankfully, he can’t help more than one right now.

Cords went for Chime’s throat and Délight’s ‘anything.’ Defiant made the choice Jack found quite predictable: he prevented Chime from getting a coup de grace, grabbing the cord and defending the writhing Blaster.

It was predictable because, as Délight began dodging and doing her best — ordinarily not enough — she triggered her aura’s pulse as soon as it came up, annoyingly improving all of her team’s speed of perception once again, including herself. That it helped her seemed downright cheaty. Délight dodged and weaved and evaded the snaking cords with fluidity, even with an injured ankle, which had to be plenty painful by now. Meanwhile, Hiccup moved to punish Highfive for keeping her away from Chime, attacking him in a blinding fury; only his reflexes and long-time instincts fighting her allowed him to counter. He still took a kick in the knee that looked like a solid crack.

Jack waited it all out, coordinating his team for their next move in this chess match. He did not want to lose their advantage. They had to pick their target and assess whether their enemy had enough tools to match them if they both went in the same direction. Would Délight assume she’d be targeted, or Chime? They were pushing hard to preserve the last gasp of their injured teammate. Perhaps it was admirable; perhaps it was just what they wanted Jack to think.

Chime was a man of tremendous grit. Right as the duration of the pulse died, he managed to summon something from deep within himself, even half delirious. Jack had watched how, as he writhed, his empty hands had tried to grab for his instruments, but just couldn’t get there. So, instead, he just clapped his hands together in a shockwave, sending it directly at Jack and Bo.

It was a desperate attack, and neither of the targets reacted to it. They successfully identified its desperation and diminished intensity — its strategy of attempted distraction. They let it wash over them and focused on their existing plan, even as they felt the wave of concussion and the nasty ringing in their ears. Jack took a step back; Bo braced with a hand on Jack’s shoulder to keep steady.

It’s not enough, buddy. Nice try, though.

The truth was, Jack knew they were about to hardcore protect Délight, and he didn’t care. They were going for her anyway because they could. Because it was the right play.

Highfive somehow juked out Hiccup by reading her ass and leveraging the Sandy's confusion, and turned for Délight, unleashing a charge right for her. Hiccup, lacking the speed bonus, tried to trip him up again, but Highfive had the edge thanks to his shifted commitment and feint, not to mention his sudden, intense speed when charging. She whiffed.

Lest Délight get smushed like a pancake, Defiant had to interpose himself to stop the charging rhino, body blocking him and unable to do anything more than commit to stopping him in his tracks.

Jack focused on nothing more than entanglement. As such, he used a preposterous mass of branching cords and wires. To this end, he unveiled that he had wires hidden in a recess of the corner nearby — as he had in every corner, with not really much setup needed on his part. Bo had simply dumped them there.

Though Délight put up quite an effort dodging the deadly cords, the wires still got her and entangled her. A flash lit up the whole area, momentarily stunning Jack. Not long enough, thanks to mitigation from his faceplate.

His vision was oddly warped, one side all but blind and distorted, the other clear but still confused by the other eye. He closed it, which helped, but it did mess with his depth perception. Jack kept hold of her and focused on pulling up her arm, specifically.

Bo did not disrupt her stun deliberately, because it was right then that she disrupted Délight’s body itself, paralyzing and sending her into seizures. Jack literally held her up…

In came their lurking executioner, once again unchecked, with Chime on the ground. Inkblot’s blade flashed, going right for the flagrantly exposed armpit — one of the best targets of minimal armor, not at all protected by the vest, and if struck true, could hit a major artery and/or nerve cluster.

And Inkblot pretty much always struck true.

However, the flash had delayed them all just slightly more than they wanted. As such, even as the blade was coming in, the annoying tank, the bastard defensive expert, Defiant, spun and moved quicker than he had any right to, leaving Highfive to stumble forward at the ass-end of what momentum he had left. Defiant grabbed Inkblot’s arm around with both of his, arresting the strike at an unclear stage.

It was quickly clear that the stage was late by a narrow margin. Délight screamed in a sound unlike anything Jack had heard since he’d been haunted by Screamer’s terrorized peel. As the depth of that sound hit, it became clearer it wasn’t just pain, but something interlaced with frustration.

Délight’s cry was ultimately cut off as the sim chamber lit her up with a paralyzing field simulating incapacitation. Jack made the metal holding her go limp, so she collapsed to the floor, making her less accessible to Defiant. He didn’t let up this time, though. He drove sharpened wires into her armpit, digging in and aggravating the wound. Hypothetically, that is, as it was simply simulated in the field around her intensifying, essentially hastening her demise.

Jack was aided somewhat in this coup de grace by Defiant grappling with Inkblot and taking him to the ground, injuring his arm.

“I think he’s pissed,” Inkblot said calmly, if his mental voice was a bit strained by the current efforts. “Or tired of not causing damage. Let him waste time on me, and go for the others.”

Amazingly, perhaps admirably, Hiccup neglected to go for Chime, instead making like a bandit for Highfive’s back, via speed and a sudden burst from her displacement. Narrowly, partly thanks to a warning from Jack who could see it all from his vantage, Highfive twisted his body and avoided a kidney kick, taking another crack in the body slipping through his sheath, but still protected by the body armor vest.

Hiccup was totally over-committed here, and without the convenient speed bursts provided by Délight’s aura — and still inhibited by Bo’s Sandy — she couldn’t avoid Highfive’s quick counterattack sweep. He swatted her with a sweep of his sheath’s fingers and sent her flying to the floor. It would’ve been far worse if he had fully avoided the kick. As it stood, he had to be grimacing behind his mask.

As Defiant was somewhat worryingly occupied with Inkblot, Jack and Bo nonetheless trusted their teammate and cooperated against the fading Chime. Bo disrupted his body, effectively paralyzing him, as Jack wormed a thin cord with a blade on the end of it underneath his neckguard, ripping the thinner fabric and gaining access to the bare skin. He focused all of his overcharged output and Control into cutting, slicing, and digging in with as much speed and force as possible, aiming to trigger protections quickly while Bo’s effect persisted.

Chime was incapacitated once more by the injury, and Jack kept at it, soon sorrying and worrying the ‘wounds’ enough to trigger a full-on ‘death lock’ simulation as Jack finally sliced a virtual femoral.

Ironically, this was right before Délight was simulated for the same death lock, from bleeding out, so she’d just outlasted the tough bastard.

Jack found it hard to contain his glee at the relief he felt suddenly surge up. He hadn’t even realized how much tension he’d been in over it, until it finally released — or tried to, because he couldn’t yet. He assessed the situation and sent out commands for the final ‘clean-up.’

Bo switched her Sandy to lock onto Hiccup, and Jack stepped out from the soundproofed alcove, closing it up behind him to eliminate access to Bo. She didn’t really need line of sight anymore, at least not to a sufficient degree that it was worth risking her to a sudden attack run from Hiccup.

Inkblot had finally gotten away from Defiant, apparently by ‘ink-sliming’ his body and particularly his opponent’s faceplate. Defiant had then ripped it off and tossed it aside, his scowling face now fully on display inside the frame of his helmet.

However, Inkblot had a limp arm, obvious just before he went invisible once more. His dominant arm, too. And Defiant had his foot on Inkblot’s dagger. Hiccup was nearby, waiting, obviously aiming to stay close to the tank now that there was no one else to protect.

“Sorry, guys,” Inkblot said, annoyance in his voice. “Underestimated his grappling. And levels. I fragged up.”

“It’s fine,” Jack said. “Four-on-two is the result, so we’ll take it. You aren’t useless, are you?”

“I’ll make sure I’m not.”

Jack moved out slowly, as if he was helping to circle the two in the wide area of the new battlefield. In reality, he hoped to bait them to a certain area. Especially Defiant. Meanwhile, Highfive circled so he was between them and the wall Bo was behind. Just in case.

“Let’s get this shit over with,” Hiccup said, spitting out every word. “We’re taking one or two of you assholes with us. Count on it.”

Jack cast his hands out. “Here I am, bitch.” He left a pause. “Oh, I didn’t mean you, Hiccup, sorry about that.” He turned his head to Defiant. “I was talking to him.”

Defiant sniffed and sneered. “Cute. How about you back it up, big man? One-on-one wrestling match, right here, right now.”

“That’s even cuter, little man. Too bad it doesn’t fit the scenario.”

“We’ll make it fit!” He began running for Jack hard.

Jack backed up toward the door, brandishing a metal shield and a three-headed metal ‘whip’ levitated out in front of him. Yes! Come on, idiot! Just a little further!

Meanwhile, Hiccup burst out toward Highfive, calling, “I’m gonna finish what I started, numbnuts!”

Hiccup arrived first, doing some sort of complex feint. Highfive ate it up, going for a thumb-smash.

Wait-

Defiant suddenly abandoned his charge, zipping across space to block Highfive, though it was seemingly a false effort, as he abandoned it quickly, throwing Highfive off-balance and moving aside, letting Hiccup move in for an offensive follow-up attack.

Bo wisely disrupted this, throwing off the careful timing to save Highfive from injury. He was the damn leader, after all.

Meanwhile, though, Defiant slammed himself into the wall that Bo was behind. He didn’t exactly dent it, but as he kept moving, he began to bend the metal inward, forcing it to give way from his undeniable advance.

Hrm.
