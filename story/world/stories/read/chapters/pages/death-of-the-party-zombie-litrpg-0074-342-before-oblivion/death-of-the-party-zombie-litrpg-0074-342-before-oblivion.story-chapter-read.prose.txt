The war raged on across Sanctuary. Smaller, unprepared sections of the Radoch army fell to the sudden arrival of new zombies. Others, who were more entrenched in their positions, managed to push the undead back. The slow-rolling blob of the Aspect of Life along with her massive retinue picked up wandering groups of zombies as they marched through the Wastelands toward the Tropical Isles.

Meanwhile, the rest of the world’s population waited impatiently down in Hell, separate from the invasion. Some were hesitant about their return to the surface, but others were gnashing their teeth at being held back.

Dent swung his sword in a small training area, striking a target dummy three times in quick succession. He withdrew his blade to inspect it and ran his metal fingers along the edge. Moderately satisfied, he glanced over at Jackie, who was leaning against the wall, hat tipped low and an unlit cigarette in her mouth.

“Not actually going to smoke that?” he asked.

“Eh. As soon as I spark up, Fran will appear and catch me.” She pushed herself from the wall and lifted her hat to sit back on her purple hair. “Don’t wanna go dying with that guilt, ya know?”

“Shouldn’t count your chickens yet. Things aren’t… all that bad.” The swordsman frowned as his sword vanished to be replaced by another.

“Well, you know Chuck best. How down the river are we?”

Dent exhaled and changed into a fighting stance opposite the dummy. “I’ve seen you running recon on what’s going on up there more than anyone else. As to whether we have a paddle or not… Chuck doesn’t have a way to wave away the problem.”

The mobster grunted and closed her eyes as she moved the cigarette from one side of her mouth to the other.

With a flash, Dent snapped forward and split the dummy’s head in half. After taking a step back and relaxing, he rolled out his shoulder. “You that worried about death?”

“Worried I won’t take enough roaches into the grave with me.” She grinned. “After the bastards clear an area, they seem to split up and spread out. Like they’re claimin’ land or somethin’. Each bug has an allegiance to a Commander. Makes some areas more dangerous than others, right? But if we pick off the weaker groups, they’ll just swarm back into one army in response.”

“So, are we talking… assassinating the Commanders to leave the riff-raff confused?”

“Either way, there ain’t no avoiding them knowing we’re back. Might pique their interest more, or they could just blast us to pieces again.” Jackie shook her head. “I’m gonna go knock some heads together in the war room and see if we can’t come up with a plan.”

He nodded in response. “I’ll be there in a minute.”

The war room was currently the hub for the Outsiders, Guardians, and important individuals. While strategy was the main talking point, no consensus could be reached on how best to approach their return to Sanctuary. A holographic version of the world floated in the middle of the table, showing the location of Sally’s undead versus the Radoch forces.

Archie stared at the slowly turning planet, some exhaustion on his face. Eventually, he snapped his attention away to look over at the vampire. “I accept your proposal, but now is not the right time.”

Theo grinned. “You’re waiting for the remaining Awakened to show his face, correct?”

“Although it is obvious who it is, and you know full well, there’s no telling when Chuck will finish whatever he is working on.”

Max frowned as he looked up at the ceiling. “A way for Sally to bring the Radochs back as zombies might turn the tide. Failing that, a nuclear bomb?”

A small bubble containing a sweat drop appeared beside the head of the hooded Lucius. “I’m sure nothing so drastic is necessary.”

“If we hit the collector ship with everything we have, that’s no different in terms of severity,” Humphrey mused.

Theo continued smiling to himself. He watched the small dots on the map move into each other. Some marching on, some disappearing. There was a lot of death out there.

And plenty more still to come.

Hunter rolled across the ground, backing up against the stone wall as an explosion rocked through the barricades the Radoch had set up. As the first roach stumbled out of the carnage, the shadowed man put a beam of darkness through the distracted opponent.

He stood back up and turned the corner. The chamber of his silver revolver clicked around as he fired another four shots into the defenders. Three Radochs were eviscerated as the last bullet cored through a structural support of a wooden platform. It collapsed, sending a handful of bugs sprawling into the carnage.

A burst of movement brushed past Hunter as over twenty zombies surged into the small fortifications, immediately setting upon any injured bug they found.

Hunter pulled back the hammer on his gun and swept the area, but it looked as if the undead had the situation under control. Tagging along with Sally felt a little awkward, but he’d been able to weaken any camps of resistance to lower the loss of zombies. Not long after the faster group had overrun the Radoch forces, hundreds more surrounded every side of the location.

Flanked on one side by Big Joe and the other by her dwarven squad, Sally in her Aspect form slowly crawled into the area.

Hunter pushed his hat up and watched as several long vines reached out, picking up the dead and dying Radochs. They drew the bodies in close, feeding them to the large plant mouths. One of the smaller ones lowered toward Hunter, bobbing in the air. It regarded him quietly, but intentionally.

“Feeding keeps your form active longer, huh?” He continued to look at the impassive mouth. “Yeah, I can imagine it is draining to maintain an army across the world. Oh... is that so?”

He shook his head. Sally would die as soon as she ran out of energy. While powerful, this Ultimate granted by eating Theo's heart had locked her into a do-or-die scenario.

“Well, let's press forward.” He briefly tipped his hat to her. “We're coming up to the edge of the Wastelands, which you said starts with a swamp? Might be slow trekking before we get into the Forest proper.”

Unheard by anyone but the shadowy man, Sally grumbled her displeasure before her countless roots pulled her forward across the ground. Like a pack of dogs, her zombies turned and marched forward again, bumping over each other as they broke through what remained of the outpost.

Hunter spun the revolver's cylinder before snapping it shut. He continued along with them.

Theo spun the moon key around on his finger before gripping it tight. He continued watching the holographic representation of Sanctuary.

The efforts of the resurrected dead were mostly hit or miss. Zombies easily overran the smaller Radoch groups, while anything more substantial pushed back. The only fighting front making serious progress was Sally’s flock, but the Forest was about to become a bloodbath. From the Tropical isles, several large groups of elite roaches and their collected monsters were moving to meet the undead force.

Not that he didn’t have full faith in her, but he’d seen with his own eyes how strong some of the invaders could be. Now that the STAR+ System had done away with granular levels, Relative Threat Gradient determined the difficulty of encounters. A neutral or zero on this scale meant a fair fight for your power and abilities. +1 Threat meant the enemy was a step above you in strength, and likewise -1 Threat was the opposite. The range went to five in each direction, but six occasionally showed up for things that were at the complete extreme ends.

Most Radochs were -4 Threat for him. A couple of the elites had been +2 Threat until he had transformed into his Aspect. Archie wouldn’t tell him how powerful the Hive King was relative to his power, but Theo had a feeling the worst of the worst were still on the Isles around the collector ship.

His eyes drifted away from the globe. Only the Outsiders remained in the meeting room now, as the rest prepared outside in Hell to prepare the rest of Sanctuary’s populace for their plan. Theo’s brow furrowed slightly as he looked at the empty space at the end of the room.

After a couple of seconds, blue light danced across the walls. Three versions of Chuck flickered into being, with a fourth joining briefly before vanishing. The trio wavered in and out and then also vanished - leaving just one left.

The Architect rubbed at his temples. “I am… yes, this is the right me. Good.”

Archie sighed. “I pray your resurfacing is not a bad omen.”

“No, well…” Chuck continued to frown as he looked around the room. “I don’t think I could make things any worse at this point. Sally is currently taxing some resources I am reliant on, so I’ve come bearing gifts instead.”

“A nice last-minute plan to turn the tide in our favor easily?” Max asked.

“If only.” Chuck gave them a tired smile. “Weaponry. Consider them parting gifts for all that you’ve done for the world.”

Theo made a face. “Not a fan of the way you’re wording that. Almost makes it sound like us escaping Sanctuary on the Omen and leaving the world to it’s fate is an option on the board.”

“You’re up first.” Chuck ignored the insinuation and gestured for the vampire to stand with him. Once joined, he handed over the weapon.

“A bo staff?” Theo held the metal pole in his hands. “Made of the same metal as…” He adjusted the grip, and a box in his STAR+ popped up.

[Attachment not found]

With a raised eyebrow, he reached into his jacket pocket and brought out a small cube of metal. His coffin. As he threw it gently into the air, it expanded out to the full size - and immediately snapped into place against the metal rod, the new handle clipping halfway through the dark coffin.

“Looks more like a great-mace,” Chuck admitted, “but counts as a hammer for your passives.”

Theo held it aloft, one hand just below the bottom of the coffin, and the other nearer the end of the handle. He swung it in a few short motions, displacing air through the room.

“Exceptionally light. That’s how you know it’s good,” Theo said, giving the Architect a nod. With a gesture, his coffin popped off and shrank down to the small cube once again, and the rod vanished into his Inventory. He then went back to his seat.

Ren’s gift was a metal bullet. Examining it produced a System window with four slots for spell scrolls. The inserted scrolls changed the payload of the projectile when fired, detonating on impact - anything from stacked fireballs, an elemental mix to combine with each other, or even healing and defensive spells.

For Max, a belt which could hold several additional card decks. Rather than requiring Inventory manipulation, it acted as an extension to his usual magical deck. With a sweep of his hands, hundreds of cards spun out around him before becoming a wall separating the meeting room in half. A clap of his hands turned each one into glittering confetti, which vanished upon landing on the floor.

“The potential applications are staggering.” He gave a brief bow before taking his seat.

“Sally is preoccupied, so I will delivery hers at a better time.” Chuck flickered slightly as he smiled. “If I could speak with Archie alone for his, please?”

The others gave some brief surprised resistance before leaving. Theo closed the door and stepped over to the other two. While the blinds mostly obscured the wizard and Architect, there was some visible movement within the meeting room, even if no sound was audible.

Max raised an eyebrow. “Anything, my dear?”

After frowning, Ren shook her head. “I can’t make out any words, but it sounds like they are arguing.”
