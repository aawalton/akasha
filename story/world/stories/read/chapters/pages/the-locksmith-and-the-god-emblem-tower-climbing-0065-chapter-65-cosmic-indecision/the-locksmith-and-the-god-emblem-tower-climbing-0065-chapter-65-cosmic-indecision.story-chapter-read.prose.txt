"First, I must tell you that this is not my real body." Raven began, oblivious to the Astronomer's scowl.

"Where is it, then?" Harry asked.

The floating figure twitched for a second, which unnerved the rest of the group. As they all stepped back in unison, Raven raised the palm of his hand and... coughed?

What just happened? The Locksmith thought to himself as he struggled to understand Raven's actions. I've never seen him like that...

"...It is on the moon." He said, his voice so clear he could've been a step away from the group. "Unfinished and yearning."

Like a nimble acrobat, Raven slid away from the lenses of the telescope. The moon, hiding its dangerous nature, looked like a drawing stamped onto the sky. Unlike the original, its presence and volume were laughable, quickly revealing that, like all the other floors, this one was nothing but a poor imitation. Despite this, Harry felt drawn to the satellite like a wayfarer in search of purpose.

The Astronomer, as if anticipating the direction of the conversation, covered his ears and walked toward a pile of books. Before Harry could inquire about Cardley Logos's exotic reaction, he once again felt the presence of the mysterious figure, who seemed anxious.

Realizing it was his turn to continue the conversation, Harry raised his face to look at Raven. "So where do this body and this mask come from?"

Even without being able to confirm it, the Locksmith was almost certain that Raven was giving him a malicious smile, as if he had been waiting for this question for a long time. The masked figure then began to float around the telescope as if orbiting it. Deviating from his set trajectory, he seemed to approach the Astronomer as he spoke.

"This all began when Cardley here," Raven said, "found something curious on the moon, didn't he? If he hadn't been so arrogant and naive, I wouldn't be here. Well, at least he still lives within me, if that's what you wanted."

"Enough!" The man shouted, turning to face the figure. "You are not him! Raviel has been gone for a long time! If any part of him still lives inside you, you suppress it. You are a parasite, a crawling creature that deserves nothing but contempt."

Raviel!? Harry remembered the moment he tried to use his first Emblem, using the Lock Symbol.

"Come now..." Raven seemed to be enjoying himself. "There's no need for that, Logos. That's the name you gave it, isn't it? Curious how that word seems to have different versions and meanings depending on the context..."

"You use that pretentious mask and pretend you have the same authority as the real one!"

"...On you it seems wasted." He continued, despite his opponent's complaints. "It seems it was always meant for me. Logos is reason, is principle, is it not? I feel it suits me. I only need freedom, however."

"And yet you won't find it!" Cardley shouted, but Harry could sense a melancholic knot still caught in his throat, as if he were swallowing a sob. "I will keep you here until I find a way to separate the two of you!"

"...Are you Raviel? The Astronomer's son?" Harry asked, addressing the masked figure. "But how can that be..."

"You finally remembered!" The figure celebrated in its own exotic way. After finishing what appeared to be a burst of laughter, it tossed a small book to Harry. "You can keep that, by the way; I no longer need it. I'm now certain it's really you."

"Certain of what?" Madeline chimed in now that the focus had shifted to the Locksmith.

"That he will be the future user of the God Emblem, of course." The entity's words seemed to shake the thoughts of everyone present. Goldman and Vela turned to the young thief, bewildered by his supposed divine inheritance.

Even Matthews, who was confused by all these recent revelations, understood the power behind the God Emblem. It was the reason so many travelers had lost their lives to reach this floor. Not just thieves and princes; nearly everyone who heard what lay at the top of the tower was tainted with the mark of greed. For Matthews, of course, this had only ever been a story passed between drunk nomads and pretentious bards. Fortunately, or unfortunately, Harry's brother was wrong.

It was surreal to hear that name and the weight behind it. Harry had suspected its existence when Conrad, still on the second floor, had spoken of it, even in his broken state.

The Locksmith then took back his journal and opened it. Beyond poorly made sketches and important notes, he saw entire pages dedicated to a single shape; the Lock Symbol. Just seeing it brought back his moment with Devon and how the poor man had been caught up in all of it. That memory, however much it had been suppressed over the years, had never truly left the young thief's mind. The screams of despair from the man he now recognized as the Astronomer seemed even louder.

Harry reddened, realizing the intimate moment he had witnessed. "I-I don't understand... What does my journal have to do with any of this?"

"...You still haven't seen it? It is the union of the three primal formats!" Raven seemed irritated. "The God Emblem is what you drew on your first attempt. It was there that your soul was marked and our destinies intertwined. You witnessed my birth and will be the cause of my death, or rather, my resurrection. My out-of-jail ticket, to put it more plainly."

"...What is he talking about, Harry?" Matthews asked, stepping closer to the Locksmith.

"I didn't know..." Harry kept staring at the shape of the lock and soon saw what Raven meant; a circle held at the top, followed by a square below, and a triangle piercing the other two. It was the three formats that gave structure to power. The circle manipulated space, the triangle granted potency, and the square manipulated biology.

"Unfortunately," Raven continued, his voice growing sharper. "The so-feared Astronomer had the same idea the moment I was conceived and decided to trap us here until he found a solution, didn't he? But little did he know that the power of Logos is too strong, and so the tower was created; a physical representation of a child's drawings... Curious, isn't it? It's a good thing the power quickly slipped from his hands, otherwise..."

Still not fully understanding how it all fit together, Harry could nonetheless deduce a few things, and noticing Raven's irritable tone in contrast with the Astronomer's weeping, he felt his blood run hot.

"...You are not Logos." Harry stated. "Whatever that thing is, it is still trapped on the moon, isn't it? That's why so many nobles came to the tower seeking shelter; the presence of that entity is tearing the moon apart. That's why Moon Rock is used for Emblem creation, because it is marked with the presence of Logos."

"What do you mean—" Raven began, but was quickly cut off by the Locksmith.

"Conrad could not bond with the God Emblem because it already had an owner. In attempting to do so, probably under your guidance, his mind was shattered."

"...That was not my wisest idea, I'll admit." Raven seemed to regain control of his imposing tone. "But don't act as though you know everything. Yes, a tiny fragment of the Emblem still resides within you, and that is why you are so important. That said, I realized with that brute that you would come to the tower regardless, seeing as you were still connected to the Emblem. Had you died, even of old age, I would have been long gone from here."

"...That scene," Harry said, taking a step forward. "The Astronomer's son, Raviel, being transformed into that green jelly — was that you? Was Raviel consumed by staring too long at Logos?"

"On one thing you are correct, boy." The Astronomer said, now with more confidence. "He is not Logos; he is a petulant, restless little fragment that gained consciousness. He is a virus that stole my boy from me. He holds a portion of the power but not all of it, so he will never reach his full potential. That is why he wants to leave the tower; he wants to return to his source. He—"

"I have no source!" The autonomous fragment of Logos, Raven, screamed, shaking the entire room. "I was simply stolen from my continuous and perfect existence!"

The Astronomer took a step forward. "You know, I don't think it's entirely your fault. It makes sense that a cosmic entity placed inside the body of an eight-year-old boy would behave this way. Perhaps it was my fault after all. He was the best thing that ever happened to me, and yet I let him go."

As Cardley ruminated on his thoughts and mistakes, Harry observed him and soon felt a deep sadness. He then understood the power in his hands and how he could resolve all of this. He could prevent his mother from dying, and in doing so prevent his brother from taking this troubled path. Goldman could live on the water without becoming a pirate and Daniel could live his life with Brun. Manes's father would never die and Madeline wouldn't need to resort to thievery. Prism could get help and Petrikov would not have suffered for the sins of others. Harry would be happy.

"How do I use this power?" Harry said, determined. "What do I have to do?"

Sighing deeply for the first time, Raven composed himself and answered calmly. "You only need to channel your power into me and we can begin."

"Couldn't we have done this on the first floor? Why did you wait all this time?"

"You, Harry, must be in tune with the Emblem, and that is only possible on the last floor. It is where the Emblem was first used, so your connection is strongest there."

So that's why my heart has been beating so fast... Harry remembered his abnormal heartbeat.

The Locksmith then turned to face the group, who looked frightened. "Don't worry. I'm going to fix everything. You'll all have the lives you always wanted."

And despite their anxiety, Harry embraced them collectively while suppressing tears. Confused at first, they embraced him back. This is the trust Harry always wanted. This is the love he always sought.

He walked toward Raven, who now floated before the telescope. He caught Cardley Logos's face and sensed something was wrong. Before touching the bandaged hands of the entity, whose presence was so potent that even gravity around Harry seemed heavier, he once again felt a malicious smile behind the mask.

"...How do I know I'll get what I want?" Harry asked.

"...The power will come from you. I am the one who should be worried. In the end, you will have your mother and I my freedom." The figure said, its voice sounding far more intimate and small when close to Harry. "Isn't that what you wanted?"

"But what about them?" The Locksmith frowned.

"What about them?"

"Will they be alright?" Harry asked. "And how can I be sure that when it's over, the moon won't fall to Earth and kill us all?"

"Why do you care about them?" The figure asked, its tone more irritated. "You want your mother back, don't you? That is what matters. That is what you climbed all the way up here for, Harry Bockart. To hold her again. You only get one wish, Harry. Use it to free us."

As if his heart had been pierced, Harry almost took a step back as he realized what Raven was doing. He had almost let himself be swept away by the emotional manipulation of an entity that clearly did not have his best interests at heart. Even so, Raven was right. Harry would see his mother again. He could eat her pastries and smell her perfume from across the room. He would live with her all the things he never got to live in 25 years. That was what he had climbed the tower for, wasn't it? That was his wish.

"But what about the rest?" Harry asked again. "If I do nothing, they'll carry on with their lives just the same, and I won't be able to stop it."

"...They don't matter, Harry." The figure said, bringing its mask close. Its voice was tiny now, almost like a child's. "What matters is my freedom and your life. Now do what needs to be done."

Harry closed his eyes as he took the entity's hands. Around him, he could sense Cardley Logos gnawing at his nails with anxiety over what the future held. He could get Raviel back for himself, or his son would be lost to him forever.

He imagined a future with his mother and his brother, a future that was almost perfect. That future, however, would never be reached. Somehow, even if he lost the memories of the Logos Tower, he would live a perfect life while the others carried on in their broken paths.

I love you, son. He remembered her words and her unconditional love. That was what he would get back. That was all he ever wanted. Did he love her enough to ruin the lives of others? Was that what she was worth? Was that what his mother would have wanted?

Harry then opened his eyes and smiled calmly. He adjusted his glasses and straightened his tie one last time. What kind of man would he be if he left them behind? Even Harry, who could be irritating, was a thief with principles.

"Turns out I've changed my mind." Harry said. "Funny how indecisive we all are, isn't it?"

In a smooth movement, he clenched his fist and, with all the strength he could muster, punched Raven square in the face.

Seeing this, Matthews smiled.
