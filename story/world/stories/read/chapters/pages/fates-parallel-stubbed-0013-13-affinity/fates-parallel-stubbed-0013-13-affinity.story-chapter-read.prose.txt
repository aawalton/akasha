In the end, Eunae and Takeda couldn’t offer Lee Jia much. Takeda agreed to join the sparring sessions and offer martial arts tutoring, and while Eunae was unable to provide any talismans, she claimed that she might have something else if Lee Jia would wait a few days. Lee Jia was feeling a little downcast as she returned home. She had exhausted her only resource and the few connections she had, and it hadn’t amounted to as much as she’d hoped for.

When Lee Jia returned home she was surprised to find Eui in the living area, sitting with her knees drawn up to her chest, and staring into the fire. Eui glanced up at Jia as she entered, and Jia couldn’t help but feel as if she looked even more tired and sullen than usual.

“Welcome home, Jia.”

“Thanks, Eui. Um, are you ok?”

Eui drew her knees closer and shook her head as Jia took a seat on the couch opposite her.

“Not really. I wanted to apologize for earlier. I shouldn’t have blown up at you like that—it wasn’t your fault. You just wanted to include me and I was a total bitch about it.”

“I should apologize, too. You were right, I’ve been putting you on a pedestal. I keep telling myself you’re actually a good person—and I still believe that—but it isn’t fair of me to reduce you to just that. I can’t be friends with just the good parts of you, and I don’t want to be. A real friend would accept the bad with the good, and I haven’t really done that. So I’m sorry for being a bad friend.”

Eui chuckled and looked askance as Jia.

“Jia we’ve known each other for a week. We’ve been friends for half of that. I feel like you might be setting the bar for good friendship pretty high.”

Jia blushed and puffed out her cheeks.

“I-it feels like it’s been longer, ok? Maybe it’s because we shared memories or something…”

Eui put a hand up to her mouth to cover a giggle.

“I get it, I was just teasing. Anyway, I’m sorry for being a bad friend too—I guess we both need to do better. I’ll start by supporting you however I can with your duel. Whether it’s giving you pointers on how to stab people, or anything else. Just ask, ok?”

“I will, Eui, thank you. Same to you. If you need my help with anything, you can just let me know!”

Eui nodded and smiled.

“Thanks, Jia.”

The next morning, Jia and Eui went straight to the training field to meet with Master Ienaga and the much smaller advanced martial arts class. Lee Jia felt out of place among the group of entirely Yamato disciples. Lee Jia realized that aside from Ienaga and Takeda, she hadn’t really interacted with anyone from Yamato. The only person she recognized other than Takeda was that red-headed girl with the air of grace and superiority she’d noticed before. Takeda ran over to the pair with a wave as soon as she noticed them.

“Good morning! Are you guys excited for your first day of real martial arts practice? Ienaga’s a fantastic teacher! I bet she’ll have you running circles around that Yan jerk in no time!”

Lee Jia couldn’t believe how enthusiastic Takeda was so early in the morning. She didn’t need as much sleep anymore, but Lee Jia still hated waking up.

“I hope so. Oh, I never properly introduced you before. Takeda, this is my roommate, An Eui. Eui, this is Takeda Rika. We met on the first day after she had a duel with Yan Zhihao.”

“Ahaha, that sure was embarrassing. I got beat so bad Lee Jia had to help me get home. Nice to meet you, An Eui.”

Takeda extended a hand. Eui regarded it coldy and crossed her arms.

“Sure.”

“Haha, right, I forgot you were a shy one. Come on, I’ll introduce you guys to the others.”

Takeda took Eui’s rejection in stride and led them over to the rest of the group. Jia gave Eui a questioning glance, but only got a shrug in response. The others seemed to appraise them as they approached, and Lee Jia found herself doing the same. Aside from Takeda, there were two girls and two boys.

“Lee Jia, An Eui. Allow me to introduce you to the advanced martial arts group. First up, Kasai Hanako.”

Kasai had a doll-like appearance, with pale skin and long brown hair tied into a pair of braids. She smiled and bowed as she was introduced.

“Nice to meet you! You can just call me Hana!”

“Next, the angry looking one here is Satou Ryuuji!”

Satou wore a sharp expression as he regarded the pair coldly with crossed arms. Lee Jia was reminded of a male version of Eui. He offered little more than a grunt of acknowledgement.

“Hm.”

“Ok, Satou, you can talk the new girls to death later. And this is our top student Hayakawa Kaede!”

The red haired girl glanced over and nodded before gazing elsewhere disinterestedly.

“And the last one is Toshi.”

Takeda pointed at the last boy and spoke with significantly less enthusiasm. The remarkably unremarkable looking boy scowled.

“My name is Miyata Toshiharu! I know you’re just using that nickname to annoy me, Takeda. Why are we even entertaining these youkai anyway? I heard that they cheated somehow.”

Lee Jia frowned. Damn Yan Zhihao and that stupid rumor.

“Quiet, Miyata, you’re embarrassing yourself. Do you really doubt Master Ienaga’s judgement?”

Hayakawa rebuked the boy with a bored tone and he immediately bowed deferentially to her.

“Of course not! My apologies for speaking out of turn.”

Lee Jia immediately understood the kind of person this Miyata was. The kind of weak person who would suck up to someone stronger and then act as if that person’s strength was their own. Baselessly arrogant and lightning fast to back down when faced with someone stronger or more important than them—she’d seen dozens just like him in the street gangs back home. Once again it was almost refreshing how little things seemed to change in higher society compared to the world she was accustomed to. Her thoughts were cut short by Ienaga’s arrival.

“Lee, An, Takeda, with me. The rest of you pair off and warm up with some light sparring.”

Ienaga wasted no time on pleasantries, and for their part the disciples quickly snapped into action at her words. Ienaga led Lee Jia’s group aside and brought out a large engraved rod of white jade that resembled an oversized jade slip.

“Now that you have awakened your ki, your bodies are akin to unworked steel. It is here that the process of forging yourselves into a keen blade can begin. Now, any blacksmith can take an ingot and form it into whatever shape they desire. However, a true artisan will tell you that each ingot has its own destined shape. A master smith coaxes this form out of the ingot and embellishes upon it until they achieve perfection. The same is true of martial arts.”

Ienaga handed the jade rod to Takeda as she continued.

“This charm will help us ascertain that form and better guide you on your path to martial perfection. Takeda, please demonstrate.”

Takeda flipped the charm in her hand and winked at Lee Jia and An Eui.

“It’s a tool for checking your natural elemental affinity.”

“Takeda!”

“Sorry, master!”

Ienaga sighed as Takeda shrunk under her reprimand.

“She’s not wrong, though it irks me to hear it described in Goryeo’s terms. Still, you two have already demonstrated the value of magical study, so I will try to keep an open mind. Now Takeda, a demonstration?”

“Right!”

Takeda sat down and began meditating, holding the rod out in front of her with one hand on each end. Lee Jia tried to focus on what was happening with her mana sense, but it was difficult to perceive the ki inside someone’s body and the formation inscribed on the charm was hopelessly complex. After a few moments, the charm began to vibrate with a low hum and Takeda let go of it, moving her hands slowly away as it hovered in place.

“As you can see, Takeda has a strong affinity with the element of force. Her family’s signature technique uses telekinetic force to project their attacks and harden the air. The patriarch of their clan can stand on thin air as if it were solid ground and attack freely from any angle.”

“Haha, grandpa’s always showing off that airwalking trick.”

Lee Jia stared with fascination at the hovering charm. It fell into Takeda’s lap as she stopped focusing and she passed it back to Ienaga.

“If you focus, you should find that you can redirect the flow of your ki. It’s easier to do while meditating so that you can ignore outside influences. Directing the flow of ki outside of your body is difficult, but can be made easier through the use of a conduit such as this charm or—for certain techniques—another person’s body.”

Ienaga handed the charm to Lee Jia before continuing.

“You’ll want one hand to project your ki, and the other to receive it back to complete the circuit. The charm will amplify and manifest the natural inclination of your ki. Give it a try now.”

Lee Jia nodded and sat down to meditate with the charm held out in front of her. Using the breathing patterns taught to her by Master Ienaga, everything outside of her body faded away and became distant. She focused on the flow of ki through her body, willing the energy in her left hand outward and the energy in her right hand inward. After a few failed starts, the ki began to flow in a circuit and the rod felt like it had become part of her body.

Lee Jia’s hair and fur began to stand on end, and she heard a strange crackling sound. Opening her eyes, she saw that the rod was now glowing a pale blue color, and tiny arcs of blue lightning flashed across its surface and throughout her clothes and hair.

“Ack!”

She leaned in for a closer look and yelped as a particularly powerful arc zapped her in the nose and caused her to drop the charm. Ienaga calmly scooped the charm up off the ground.

“Lightning. That’s a relatively uncommon affinity, but since it’s believed to be the element of the heavens there are a lot of techniques that utilize it. That’s an auspicious result, congratulations. An, you’re next.”

Eui caught the charm that Ienaga tossed towards her and quickly sat down to repeat the process. After nearly a minute of silence, Eui furrowed her brow.

“Um, I don’t think it’s working. I’m pretty sure my ki is channeling through it bu—aaagh!”

Eui was cut off by her own pained scream as she suddenly clutched the charm tightly and doubled over. Lee Jia saw a spider-web pattern of black veins begin to spread out across Eui’s hands and cracks form on the charm. In a panic, Lee Jia moved to try to help Eui, but was held back by Ienaga.

“Don’t touch her!”

“Nnnngh!”

Sweat was forming on Eui’s brow as she struggled to suppress her cries of pain through clenched teeth. The black veins had spread up just past her wrists when the charm suddenly shattered, and the individual pieces disintegrated. Eui was breathing heavily and her hands were trembling as she spoke.

“W-what the fuck just happened? Did I mess up? I...I didn’t mean to break the thing.”

Ienaga knelt down next to Eui and placed a hand on her shoulder. By now the other disciples had been drawn over by the commotion, but Ienaga paid them no mind. Lee Jia’s ears twitched as she heard Ienaga whispering to Eui.

“Shh, it’s fine. What’s important is that you’re ok. You didn’t do anything wrong. Destruction is an extremely rare element and it’s manifestations tend to be—well, destructive. It might be best to give up on your natural affinity. There are a few techniques I know of, but they are either incomplete or theoretical. Those who try to harness the power of death usually end up being consumed by it sooner or later.”

“But I—”

Ienaga stood up and shook her head as Eui protested.

“You don’t have to decide now, give it some thought. The rest of you can stop gawking. Back into pairs, we’re going to review the first forms. Lee and An, you can trade off practicing with Takeda. Let An take a break first.”

The students quickly scrambled to comply, and Lee Jia found herself across from Takeda. Ienaga began to describe and demonstrate movements for them to practice. The forms they were practicing were something that Ienaga called Bujutsu, a basic hand-to-hand combat technique that utilized the ki within the body. The basic forms were a series of steps and stances that were meant to be worked into one’s every movement until they became second nature. Moving with the flow of one’s ki, Ienaga explained, should become as natural as breathing.

As they were instructed, Jia and Eui took turns practicing the movements with Takeda and meditated to internalize the lessons during their breaks. Lee Jia found the practice to be something like an elegant dance—when she performed the steps properly, her movements felt lighter and more graceful. It was the difference between a charging bull and a prowling cat.

By the end of the lesson, Lee Jia felt like she’d been stumbling around clumsily all her life and she’d just learned how to truly walk. She was a little bit disappointed that they hadn’t yet learned any combat techniques, but Takeda corrected her as they finished the training with some stretches.

“Actually, you did. That footwork will become the core of your movements in a fight, and dodging is the first line of defense. Perfecting those forms will improve your speed, balance, and flexibility, all of which will go a long way towards avoiding attacks and controlling your positioning in a fight.”

“Oh. I don’t really get it, but if you say so.”

“Haha, you’ll see. You said that Guan Yi guy was going to be meeting us here for some sparring, right?”

Lee Jia nodded. They were expecting him any minute now.

“Yeah, once you’ve had a few bouts you’ll understand, I think.”

“I’ll take your word for it.”

Lee Jia shrugged and left Takeda to her stretches, joining Eui who had been sitting off to the side and hugging her knees in what Jia had come to recognize as her brooding pose.

“How are you feeling?”

Eui glanced at Jia and chuckled mirthlessly in response.

“I suppose it makes sense that I would have an affinity with an element that’s dangerous and self-destructive. Anything else would have been a surprise at this point.”

Lee Jia pursed her lips as she carefully chose her words.

“Hm, maybe. You do tend to be pretty hard on yourself. It’s not like you’re forced to train in techniques that match your affinity though, right? Maybe you could do lightning and we could match! Or something more balanced, like Hwang talked about. Actually, I meant to ask Ienaga about that but I got distracted…”

Eui smirked as Jia trailed off, but then she shook her head with a sigh.

“I could, but that feels wrong somehow—like I’d be running away from who I am. Like you said last night about accepting the bad parts along with the good. I have to do that too, except with myself. I don’t think you’ve really got any bad parts, anyway.”

Eui blushed and looked away as she mumbled the last part. Jia looked askance at her friend and nudged her with an elbow.

“Now who’s being delusional? You know I was a thief, right? I stole from innocent people. Sometimes I took things they needed for their own survival, just because I needed them more—or at least I felt like I did.”

“Yeah, but I—”

“I know! I get it, but your crimes being worse doesn’t absolve me of mine. We’re both criminals doing what we can to be better.”

Eui paused for a moment to consider Jia’s words before turning to her with a curious expression.

“Better people, or better criminals?”

Lee Jia giggled, then considered the treatment she’d received from the likes of Xin Wei, Yan Zhihao, and Miyata. Then again, there were the friendships she’d made with Dae, Takeda, and even Eunae. She stroked her chin thoughtfully as she responded.

“Maybe both?”

When Guan Yi finally arrived, he was bemused at the sight of Lee Jia and An Eui lying in the grass and giggling uncontrollably.
