Climbing back up from the cliffs was a careful exercise in stabbing bones at strategic spots and praying that they wouldn’t completely shatter the rest of the cliff face. It was rather reminiscent of Fred’s mound, Pyre thought amusedly, except arguably even harder now since she was quite a bit heavier. The woes of high constitution.

Llyr lingered below the whole while, tail idly swishing behind him as he watched the demon’s movements closely. Pyre used the opportunity to apply the [Aquatic Adaptation] subtrait and pulled up its description.

Subtrait: Aquatic AdaptationUser is able to move more freely in the water and is more buoyant. The effectiveness of this subtrait increases with the main trait’s level.Main Trait: [Amorphous]

It was a little vague, especially that “move more freely” part, but it was the buoyancy that she was really after. She had no plans of becoming an expert swimmer and was in no way keen on getting into the water, but she assumed that with this subtrait, she at least wouldn’t sink like a rock the moment she hit the waves. It was good backup if shit went wrong, at least.

Pyre shot another bone into the cliff face above and tested its weight. Once she was sure it was secure, she swung herself over and caught herself with another bone hold. A few stray rocks fell down, but the rest of the cliff stayed strong.

The demon hadn’t expected to get a full level from the shark-octopus lusca or whatever, but she certainly wasn’t mad about it. She silently distributed her points, putting 7 in magic resistance so it equalled her constitution again, 7 in strength to round the number out, then the last point in agility for shits and giggles. And to slightly aid her current monkey bars mimicry.

Waves of warmth flowed through her body just as she swung herself up onto the top of the cliff. The demon landed on solid ground with a heavy thud. No drowning tonight.

She straightened and waved casually down at Llyr, who shook his head amusedly before diving back below the waters. Under the surface, the siren’s shadow swam south at an impressive speed. Based on the sky, Pyre suspected there was about another hour before sunrise, so the timing was perfect.

Turning, she pulled up her updated stat sheet and gave it a quick once-over. She then closed it and continued down the shore.

Llyr was already waiting on the beach by the time Pyre returned, which wasn’t really a surprise considering he’d swam and she’d strolled casually.

The siren had shifted back into his human form and was in the process of extracting the water from his clothes and hair as she approached. A ball of water floated over his palm, the sphere growing in size the more droplets he added to it.

“Did you gather all the subtrait shards you needed?” he asked curiously when he saw the demon.

“Yep.” Pyre warped her arm into a human shape again and rolled her coat sleeve back down. There was a single tear by the elbow and some holes along her back where she’d grown those bone spikes, but for the most part, the coat had stayed mostly intact. She was kind of impressed. Callan and Daphne would be proud.

The demon ran a finger along the coat rune and let the magic mend the holes. Meanwhile, Llyr used the opportunity to toss the ball of water back into the sea, now fully dry and perfectly human-looking. There were zero signs of that silvery tail from before.

“That was a lot of water you moved,” Pyre commented idly. While it had only been in the area around the lusca, that had looked like pretty advanced magic to her. It was a bigger scale than anything he’d pulled during their fight, that was for sure. About on par with Daphne’s sanctum-destroying explosion.

“I can manipulate water more easily in my mer form,” Llyr explained. He cocked his head, blue earrings swaying with the movement. “My true form is even more potent.”

“Ah yes, your big fish form,” Pyre drawled. The siren chuckled.

“I still have a humanoid torso, it’s really only the tail that changes. But yes, it’s quite large and rather inconvenient to move around in. That’s why I don’t use it much.”

“Too large for the ocean? The fuck? You a blue whale or something?”

Llyr raised an eyebrow. “I’m not sure how the color of the whale is relevant,” he said dryly. His eyes briefly scanned the demon, considering. “I also have yet to see your true form, so we seem to be equal in that regard.”

Pyre waved dismissively. “Eh, you basically saw it during the fight. Not much to say about a giant mountain of flesh.”

“So your demon form has no defined shape at all?” He looked curious. “Interesting.”

The words suggested that other demons did have more of a shape beyond “fleshy mass,” but that wasn’t all that surprising. There were probably variations between the different sin subcategories, and Pyre vaguely wondered if her “Empyrean” status also meant things worked differently for her. She’d been born directly from Fred as a flesh lump, after all. Later generation fiends weren’t.

“We should head back before Daphne wakes up,” she remarked, eyeing the horizon. A line of golden light now divided the ocean and sky, and she suspected the noble would be awake earlier rather than later.

Llyr nodded, albeit somewhat slowly, Pyre noted. There was still some tension between him and the other mage, it seemed.

“Yes, let’s.”

Pyre turned out to be correct, because when she shoved open the mansion door and strode into the dining room a few minutes later, Daphne was already awake.

The noble was fully dressed and hunched over the map from the previous night with Callan, the two muttering and pointing at various locations. They seemed to be trying to plan out a route for the ghost to take during his search. Personally, Pyre thought it was kind of a pointless effort when the “map” was only one step up from literal chicken scratch.

Both heads immediately snapped to the door when Pyre and Llyr stepped inside. “Mornin’,” the demon said.

“Where were you two?” Daphne asked, glancing between the two reincarnators.

“Fishing.” Pyre strode over to the table and peered down at the map, her height easily allowing her to look over Callan and Daphne’s hunched forms. “You know, I get what you two’re trying to do here, but I think you should wait for a better map,” she pointed out, amused.

Callan sighed. “I know,” he muttered. One hand rubbed at his forehead. “It’s just, it doesn’t feel right to be sitting around waiting. This is the best lead we’ve gotten!”

Daphne hummed in agreement, her own eyes still scanning the page. “I’m still surprised Lord Alardice agreed to help us,” she said. Her eyes narrowed. “That ambush was still an asshole thing to do, though.”

“Do you know when mail is usually delivered here?” Callan asked Llyr. The healer hummed in thought.

“Noon at the earliest, sometimes the afternoon.”

“So we wait.” Daphne sighed and leaned back against her chair. “We can’t really do much until we have the invitation. Then we’ll need to buy clothes for the ball, though I guess we could do that now.” Her eyes swept over the other three people in the room. The corners of her lips quirked up in a smile. “I’ll need to give you all a rundown on what to expect, too. Maybe teach you a few steps.”

Callan’s translucent face further paled. “We won’t have to dance, right? Pyre and I’re going as servants. We really shouldn’t need to.”

“Technically no, but you should still know the basic structure of the ball, expected etiquette, that sort of thing.” Before Callan’s shoulders could relax too much, a grin spread across Daphne’s face. Her eyes twinkled. “But if I’m going to have to teach Llyr anyway, I don’t mind giving the two of you some pointers. You never know, you could catch some Galen noble’s eye!”

Callan looked like he’d rather die a second time. Pyre gave the ghost an amused look. It was pretty clear the noble was only teasing, but now that the seed had been planted, Callan would undoubtedly be paranoid about this for the rest of the week.

She herself was fully intending on lounging around the perimeter of the room with the guards the whole night. Attempting to dance seemed like a particularly bad idea for her, considering if she accidentally stepped on someone’s foot, she’d probably crush all the bones in it. Which wouldn’t be very good for the purpose of blending in.

Daphne turned to Llyr, studying the healer critically. “…Have you been to a ball before?” she asked slowly.

“In a way.” He didn’t elaborate further. Pyre thought back to what he’d said about his past life and hummed. She could see how that answer could be complicated.

Daphne gave him a long look, clearly contemplating something, and the demon vaguely wondered how much she and Callan had put together about Llyr’s past at this point. They might not have heard the full details like she had, but the information they already knew was probably enough to make some educated guesses. Especially since they already knew about Ithren’s history. They weren’t stupid.

“I guess the balls in Ithren were probably different, anyway,” Daphne finally said, subtly ending the line of questioning. Llyr’s shoulders relaxed slightly. “I’m not sure how things are done west of the Spine.”

The noble nodded to herself, clearly coming to some sort of conclusion. “Okay, I can teach you some basic steps. Unless…” Her voice trailed, and that mischievous spark returned to her eyes. “…unless you want Lord Alardice to teach you instead. I bet he’d be over the moon.”

Callan choked, and Llyr let out a genuine laugh at that. “I do believe he ended that courtship with the ambush,” he said.

“No no, he’s definitely still interested. He just won’t admit it. He’s probably traumatized from all those other attempts.”

“Six,” Callan muttered again. The ghost really wasn’t letting that go. Understandably so, in Pyre’s opinion. She wondered if the noble was aware of just how much ammunition he’d given them all with that whole revelation. The scales had tipped dramatically in Daphne’s favor in the two nobles’ silent pissing competition.

“We’ll see,” Llyr said, amused. He peered out the window, where the sun had now fully risen and was shining rather blindingly through the glass. “Have the two of you eaten yet? I’ll make us some breakfast.”

Cute of the healer to still include Callan in that even though he knew he was a ghost now, Pyre thought with a snort. Llyr disappeared down the hall into the kitchen, dark hair swaying behind him like a wave. The sound of his footsteps soon faded.

Pyre pulled out a chair and sat down at the table, idly peering out at the shining waters. The ocean really did look quite different during the day compared to the night. She wondered how long it would take all those sea creatures they’d scared off to return to the area.

“So, when you said you were fishing, did you actually mean it?”

The demon glanced over at Daphne. “Yeah, I was. I was trying to get some subtrait shards, and since I can’t swim, Llyr herded the fish over. Was pretty effective, actually.” She raised an eyebrow. “What’d you think was going on?”

Daphne frowned at that. “I don’t know,” she admitted. “I just… I still can’t bring myself to trust him,” she muttered quietly. “I know he’s not part of that group, but…” She sighed and shook her head. “I don’t know. It’s just hard to go back to how things were. Maybe I’m overthinking it.”

“Nothing wrong with some healthy suspicion,” Pyre pointed out. “A little wariness doesn’t hurt. And I doubt he totally trusts us, either.”

Daphne’s lips thinned. “Maybe,” she said, but it was clear that this was still bothering her.

Before the noble could say anything else, however, a muffled knock suddenly sounded from down the hall. They all glanced at each other. The noise had originated from the front door.

Callan shifted. “It’s too early for the mail, right?” he said nervously. “Maybe it’s a knight, or Lord Alardice sent someone?”

In response, Pyre rose from her seat. “I’ll go see,” she said simply. Before either of the mages could argue, she turned and strode out of the room.

Along the way to the front door, the demon paid close attention to the vibrations in the area, but it seemed like whoever had knocked had remained in place.

Her eyes narrowed when the entrance came into view. The person was standing so that they couldn’t be seen fully from any of the windows, but they were clearly still there. And yet, the demon’s [Thermal Vision] didn’t detect any differences in temperature on the other side of the door.

Pyre strode forward, already having an inkling of who the arrival was. The stillness combined with the lack of scent and neutral temperature all pointed in one particular direction. When she swung the door open, her suspicions were confirmed.

On the other side of the entryway, Pax’s monochrome form greeted her.
