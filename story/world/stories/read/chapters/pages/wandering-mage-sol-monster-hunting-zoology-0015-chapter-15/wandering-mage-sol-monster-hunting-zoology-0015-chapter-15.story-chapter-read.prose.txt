They arrived in the city with Tressa feeling like a prisoner being marched to her public execution. Every head seemed to turn her way, followed by whispers and pointing fingers. She was just glad it was so late so not too many people were out. Apparently, it had become a pretty big deal that she had gone missing, especially on the heels of being called early for her trials.

Now returning after three days of sleeping outside, she was sure she looked like a mess on top of everything else. She could only imagine the rumors spreading from each random person who saw the disheveled daughter of House Windshade being ridden in by one of the private guards.

Once they arrived at her home, the guards stationed by the front door came forward.

“The master wishes to see his daughter immediately.”

“Can’t I get washed up first?” Tressa asked as Aldmern helped her off the grey stallion.

“I’m sorry, but no,” the guard said.

“Aldmern, help me out here.”

“Apologies, my lady. But I’ve been given the same orders,” Aldmern said.

Tressa groaned. “Just tell Father I’m here, but I’m taking a bath.” Whatever he had to say to her, she knew it wouldn’t be good, and being as unkempt as she currently was would just make him even worse.

“Apologies, Lady Tressa,” the house guard said, “but your father’s orders were explicit. You are to be brought to his study the second you arrive, with no exception.”

“Where’s my mother?”

“Please come with us, my lady,” he said again, ignoring the questions as he turned and opened the door to escort her straight to their master.

“Or what, you’ll drag me? I’m sorry, but I’m going for a bath. I feel gross and sticky, and no offense, but I want the leaves out of my hair and I’m tired. It’s almost morning!” She looked at Aldmern for help, as he could be protective at times.

He shrugged and said, “I’d help if I could, but your father was quite serious about things.” Then he said in a lower voice, “Just go to him. I imagine he’ll storm right to your bedroom or wherever you are otherwise.”

“I see…” She muttered back. She supposed he was right and she should have expected as much.

“Indeed, our orders have come explicitly. You will come with us now.” The guard gave her a look that said he was done negotiating.

Tressa gave one last defiant glare, then limped after him as Aldmern walked behind her.

“Are you sure you don’t want me to carry you?” he whispered.

“No.”

“Then let me take your—” he reached for her hand but the look she gave stopped him.

“No. It’s like I’m a prisoner in my own home. The least I can do is have some form of dignity left.” She also hadn’t quite gotten over how he had treated her friends. He hadn’t apologized properly yet, not to mention their argument from when she first left. She would have to come to an understanding with him before she let him touch her so familiarly again. She was grateful he apparently hadn’t sold her out, but she still wasn’t sure how she felt about Aldmern at this moment.

They arrived at her father’s study, the guard reaching out and opening the door, standing aside so she could go in. She limped in, seeing her father behind his desk, and this time he put everything he was working on to the side as he looked up to take in the sight of his disheveled daughter.

Here it comes, she thought. She could only imagine the verbal beating he was about to lay on her. At least she wouldn’t have to imagine it for long, as she’d be getting the real version in just a moment.

“Tressa!” he said, walking around his desk. “Welcome home. I’ve been worried for you.”

She blinked. “Oh. Sorry.” Then, as he stepped forward, she was taken aback as he put his arms around her, giving her a hug and pulling her in close. It was something he hardly ever did. “Uh… Father…?”

After he let her go, he looked her up and down and wrinkled his nose as he took note of how bad she smelled, but shook it off and said, “Please, have a seat. There is something important I wish to talk to you about.”

Okay, he is unusually upbeat. This has to be a trap.

She limped over to the chair and sat.

“Are you injured?” he asked.

“Just a mild sprain, Father,” she said. Though, it was clearly more than that. Her leg was bandaged, as well as another bandage around her head, several lumps from bee stings, not to mention all the bruises and scratches.

“What exactly happened to you?” he asked, concern in his voice.

“I was saved by two hunters. My foot got stuck between some branches, and then I got this,” she touched her head, “by saving them back.”

“What about the red lumps?”

“Oh, um, mosquitoes.”

“Mosquitos?”

“Yes,” she lied. “I think it’s a variety I hadn’t encountered before that I must be allergic to.” There was no way she was going to admit to him she purposely got so close to Torrent Bees.

“I… see…” he said. “I’ll have someone take a look, then.”

“It’s not urgent, Father. They actually feel fine and are improving.”

He looked like he wanted to complain about that, but instead cleared his throat. “Well, it should be looked at anyway. In any case, I have some things to discuss with you first.”

“Father?” she asked, bowing her head.

“Yes?” He seemed confused by her sudden form of respect. It was unlike her.

“I wish not to cut you off, as I know you have something important to discuss. But I’m very sorry for being gone so long, and I ask you to please allow me to take a bath and get some rest before we continue. I am utterly exhausted and would like nothing more than to rest. So when you do speak with me, I can do so in a manner much more presentable than my current state.”

He paused, seeing the bags under her eyes and the dirt and dried blood on her clothes. Opening and closing his mouth, he knew she was right. And frankly, he could smell her, and it wasn't at all pleasant.

“Yes. Very well, Tressa. Let us continue in the morning. But I’ll at least send Terrin to see to your wounds and get them healed.”

“Thank you, Father,” she said, bowing her head. She stood slowly, then limped back to her room. Inside her bedroom, she glanced to her bookshelf to confirm it hadn’t been moved.

I suppose he’s just relieved I’m back at all. I hope Mother didn’t worry too much either. I’m sure once he gets over it, he’ll be laying into me again in no time.

She then heard voices as she realized some maids were in her bathroom, drawing a bath for her.

“Ah—Lady Tressa, please, right this way,” one invited. “We have a bath ready for you.”

“Actually, please leave my room. You can lay out a change of clothes on my bed. I wish to take care of things myself.”

“Are you sure, my lady?”

“Yes, please,” she answered.

Thankfully, Maikia wasn’t here, as it was quite late and not her shift. There is no way Maikia would have taken no for an answer, as she’d give Tressa a deep scrubdown and fuss at her the entire time as if she were a child.

Once the maids were gone, Tressa undressed, gave herself a quick rinse with the wash basin, then sank into the tub. The warm water instantly seeped deep into her aching muscles.

Tressa woke early, before even her servant Lemia could make it to her. By the time she entered, Tressa was already dressed and putting on her shoes.

“My Lady?” Leimia looked at her, absolutely taken aback by the fact that Tressa was awake and ready for the day. Though her hair was clean, it was still a bit of a mess.

“Sorry. Father seemed very serious last night.”

Lemia looked at the cuts on Tressa’s leg and her head, just above the eyebrow and going back under the hairline. Terrin had healed her the best he could with his magic, but all that remained were some scabs that were a little deep but not too bad.

“Don’t worry, I’m fine,” Tressa said with a smile. “I just got hurt a bit while I was gone.”

“I’m glad you’re okay, Tressa. We were all so worried when you didn’t return. The masters were distraught over your absence.”

Tressa winced at that. “I know. I never meant to stay away for so long. And… well… I’m sorry.”

“I’m just glad you’re safe. Rumor has it that you were held hostage by a crazed minotaur and some strange scaled woman.”

“What? No! They saved my life. And she’s called a statiki. Their names were Lisha and Briadon.”

Leimia cleared her throat, looking around as if not sure she should say something. “Well… word has spread amongst the servants. The guards were talking about how they found you with them. They said you had been beaten up and tortured by them, and they were probably going to enslave you. I was so scared when I heard all of that.”

“Goddess! How do rumors get out of hand so quickly?” Tressa groaned. “I’d only been back for a single night, and this happens already? But no. They were monster hunters. Official monster hunters from the Hunter’s Guild. Respectable people. They saved me, and I repaid them by helping them get their last bounty.”

“I see, I’m sorry that I had assumed things so easily,” Lemia answered.

Tressa shook her head. “No, it’s alright. I know it wasn’t your fault. I just wonder who started that rumor… Must have been one of the search party exaggerating.” She smirked. “Briadon is pretty scary, I guess.”

“Well. To be honest, it is more strange that a minotaur and… did you call her a stagiti?”

“Statiki. Sta-tee-key.”

“Statiki.” Lemia repeated. “But it is more strange they were kind to you. The high elven race isn’t exactly looked upon in a good light or with respect like we used to be. Not since the Mad King.”

“Yes I know. And… well, to be fair, I got the impression Briadon didn’t much like elves generally. But he is a good person, I know he is, and I think he looked past that for me.”

“So the minotaur… was nice?”

“I don’t know if ‘nice’ is the word.” Tressa chuckled. “But I think of him as a good man. He is not at all like I had heard minotaurs to be. I have heard they are wild savages, but that is not Briadon at all.”

“What was the Stagiti like?”

“Statiki.”

“Statiki,” Lemia repeated.

“She was quite interesting,” Tressa said. “I think she does a good job reining in Briadon’s temper.” Tressa laughed. “They were truly a lot of fun. I…” she trailed off.

“Tressa?” Lemia asked, turning her head.

“I um… i-it’s nothing. I shouldn’t keep Father waiting.” In truth, Tressa hoped that she could actually catch up with Lisha and Briadon again. But at the same time, what was so sure in her head last night felt a little more daunting as something she might try to manifest into a reality. She knew little of the world outside of Falidorn. She knew High Elves were not liked by most other races, especially after the Mad King. Yet, despite all of that, she felt this was what she wanted to do now. No matter if it was scary. She just hoped that her mother and father would respect her choice.

She grabbed her satchel off the hook next to her door and found herself heading to her father’s study as thoughts swirled through her mind.

Why was Father so compassionate last night? Is he trying to put on a face to convince me to join the temple? I’m sure he’s thinking about how to lock me up for the rest of my life after this last stunt. It’s unlike him to behave as he did last night.

Her heart beat with anxiety as she found herself standing outside of her Father’s study. Taking a breath, she knocked on the door.

“Come in,” she heard him call.

Pushing the door open, she found her mother in the cushioned chair to the side, sipping her favorite herbal tea. Her father sat at his desk, going through books—

Tressa gasped. They were hers! They were her notebooks!

Had Aldmern sold her out after all? She looked at her father, her heart sinking, and she was sure her face was going white as she tried to remind herself the bookcase hadn’t been moved. Could they be other of her notebooks? She wasn’t sure. But perhaps if Aldmern had privately sold her out to her father, he simply decided to keep it to himself for now before letting it get out of hand by reporting her to the council.

She tried to hold down her worry as she bowed and asked, “You wished to see me… Father?”

He closed the sketchbook and set it down. “Tressa, please. Come in.”
