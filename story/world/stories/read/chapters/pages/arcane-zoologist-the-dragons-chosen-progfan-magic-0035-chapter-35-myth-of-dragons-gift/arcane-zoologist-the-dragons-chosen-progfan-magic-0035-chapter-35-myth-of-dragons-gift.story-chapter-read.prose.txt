"H-How can one place have so many books?!" Callie exclaimed.

"Shhh! You must be quiet in the library. It's the one most important rule," the librarian warned.

Callie put her hands over her mouth and whispered. "Sorry!"

"What would you be interested in reading? Admittedly, we don't have anything meant specifically for children," the librarian said apologetically.

Callie thought about it for a moment. She wanted to learn about magic. How to control it more specifically. But she couldn't just outright ask that. It would be way too suspicious. Maybe she could ask about magic in general and about some other topics, too.

A brilliant idea just crossed Callie's mind. Could there possibly be a book about arcane beasts? Surely, surely, there should be something on them. Someone must have tried to catalogue them similarly to how Granny Ethel has catalogued herbs.

"Is there perhaps something about magic and arcane beasts, oh, and perhaps herbs?" Callie asked nervously. She hoped that she hadn't appeared suspicious to the librarian.

But the woman smiled at her. "Yes, those do sound like the topics that would be interesting for a young girl such as yourself. How about you sit down somewhere, and I'll bring you a few books that would be easy to read?"

"Y-Yes, please!" Callie inclined her head. She wouldn't even know which shelf to look at with all the overwhelming amount of books available.

Nervously, she sat down in a seat located deeper inside the library and admired the shelves closest to her. On the sides, the general topic of books was written—history, language, poems, and so on. She couldn't believe that there could be so many books written about a single topic, yet the shelves were undeniably full.

After a few minutes, the librarian returned with a small stack of books in her hands. "I think these should be interesting and easy enough for you to read. Once you're done, just leave them on the table. If you want more, just come find me."

"Thank you very much," Callie once again inclined her head gratefully and looked at the books the woman had brought.

A few of them were about herbology and were similar to the encyclopedia Granny Ethel had made. There were some interesting herbs she hadn't known about before, but their general content was already familiar to Callie. She had read through Granny Ethel's book multiple times after all.

After that, Callie looked at two books, which in their essence should be exciting as they were introductory books to magic. But reading just the first few pages of those books made it clear that they would not be useful to Callie. They described the same method for using magic that Mom and Dad used.

Namely, the one where one's body was the guiding force behind magic. The book described basic breathing techniques and exercises one could practice to use specific types of magic. It seemed these exercises were different depending on what kind of magic you wanted to use.

This made sense. When Dad used his magic, his arms seemed to flex, and he performed a few quick, sharp movements. Mom, however, used fluid and elegant motions to use water magic, which was in line with what the book described. But this didn't help Callie in the slightest.

Her magic clearly didn't work that way. That was clear both to her and her parents. So she took the next book in the pile. This one excited her at first glance. Basic Beastiary. The title was barely legible as the book was extremely worn and old.

Opening the weathered pages, Callie just felt angry at whoever wrote the book.

Swamp mireling. Mindless creature that lives near swamps. Its slime is highly acidic. Do not touch.

Forest glimmerhare. A dangerous creature that leads travelers to their death by shining its horns in the dark.

Snow glauxis. Apex predator of the north. Its silent flights and white fur make it a perfect ambush predator. Frightened by loud noises.

Thunderfoxes. Small, fast, and cunning. This creature uses its powerful lightning to take down larger opponents. Do not approach without proper protection.

Most of what this person had written about arcane beasts was completely wrong. Callie was tempted to throw the stupid book away, but that would obviously be wrong. A few bits of information had piqued her interest.

Were the glauxis truly frightened of loud noises? And what counted as proper protection for approaching thunderfoxes. Surely the author couldn't mean something so crude as just a sharp weapon to stab the poor creature with. Whatever the case, Callie pushed the book away from herself in distaste.

The last book was about myths. They seemed like nothing more than silly children's stories. Some of them, Callie had already heard a hundred times from either her parents or Granny Ethel. But there was one that caught her eye.

In the first days, when dragons still roamed beneath the endless skies, their vast magic kept the world in balance. Yet when darkness rose and that balance trembled, the dragons looked upon humankind and chose those with brave hearts, strong minds, and unyielding wills.

To these first champions, the dragons gave a spark of their own magic. With it, they fought to restore peace and guard the world from ruin. Though dragons have long since vanished, their gift lives on through the blood of those champions and their descendants, who continue to uphold the world’s balance. We call them dragonbloods.

Callie was amazed to find that a book described people who had received magic from a dragon just like she had. So she wasn't the only one. There were more people like her out there.

But this story also suggested that these people were dragonbloods. That couldn't be right, could it? Dragonbloods were scary people. Sure, they could use magic, but they also hunted arcane beasts. The fact that dragons were declared extinct, when Callie knew for a fact that they were not, showed that something wasn't quite right with the story.

However, the part about dragons bestowing magic onto people was true. That Callie knew too. It was also likely true that the magic of dragons was different from the magic that Mom and Dad were using. However, the book sadly didn't describe anything about controlling the dragon magic.

Maybe I should look through more old myths. One of them might have something.

Callie took the book of myths and approached the librarian who had been sorting through a pile of books at the front desk. "Ex-Excuse me, are there perhaps more books like this one?"

The librarian took the book, examining it briefly. "Is there any myth in particular you're interested in?"

Callie opened the book and showed her the myth about dragons bestowing magic to people. Surely, it wouldn't be suspicious if she were interested in that myth in particular.

The librarian's expression turned a bit somber. "Yes, that is quite a fascinating one. It's the dragons that caught your interest, right?"

Callie went stiff, her hands instantly turning clammy. Did the librarian suspect something?

"It's only natural for kids to be interested in magic and dragons. Unfortunately… This library is quite small, and its main purpose is to keep the documentation necessary for the governor. There's barely anything available on the topic of dragons. Give a minute and I'll bring you the little bit that we do have."

Callie nodded and sat down to wait. After some five minutes, the librarian returned to two rather old-looking books. "Do be careful with these. They are very old. The language in them is quite archaic too, so you might struggle to read this."

"I would still like to try," Callie informed the librarian and opened the first of the books.

The woman hadn't been kidding when she said that the books were old. The paper had gone completely yellow and in places it felt so thin that it felt like it would crumble underneath Callie's fingers. She turned the pages as carefully as she could, trying to read through the text. However, it quickly became clear that she wouldn't be able to read much of it.

One line did catch her eye, even if she couldn't make any sense of it.

Dracan cræft nis manna cræfte gelīc. Ne cymþ hē of līchaman, ne of ǣþme, ac of heortan deopnesse. Getempera þīne heortan, swā man īsen on fȳre tempriaþ, and se dracan cræft þē gehȳreþ.

"This must be written in another language entirely," Callie muttered.

"No, it is merely a very archaic form," the librarian noted.

Callie nearly jumped out of her chair from fright. Had she been here the whole time?

"My apologies for startling you. I got a bit curious about myself. Languages have always been a passion of mine," the librarian chuckled.

"Can you read it?" Callie asked hopefully.

The librarian sat down in a chair next to Callie and carefully studied the paragraph.

"This is truly archaic… The grammar is so unpleasant and hard to understand…" the woman muttered, studying the text for a while longer. "Roughly, it says that dragon-craft is not like the craft of men. Perhaps not born as men's craft is born. The next line mentions body and breath… no, it says that the dragon craft does not come from those. And the final line…"

The librarian narrowed her eyes, glaring at the book. "The final line says Temper your heart, and the dragon-craft will hear thee. Or listen to thee or something like that."

"I don't get it…" Callie muttered. What even was dragon craft? It doesn't come from the body and breath? Temper your heart? It seemed like a whole bunch of nonsense to Callie.

"I'm sorry, dear. This is often the case with old texts. They seemed to favor riddles in the old times," the librarian said apologetically.

"No, it's alright. Thank you for helping," Callie forced a smile.

Soon after, Mom returned to the library, and Callie had to bid the library goodbye.

"Do come visit again. I'm sure there are still many books you would find interesting," the librarian suggested.

"I wish I could, but I'll have to wait until spring…" Callie sighed.

"Then I shall eagerly await your return in spring, Callie. It is truly a pleasure to see a child so entranced in reading. You surely will have a bright future ahead of you," the librarian said.

Callie thought that the woman was surely exaggerating. What could books have to do with her future? Nonetheless, she thanked her and bid her goodbye.

"So did you find something interesting?" Mom asked.

"I think so," Callie said and went on to describe what she had read, including the bit about dragonbloods being gifted magic by dragons and the frustrating riddle.

Mom pondered her findings for a moment, then said, "Dragon craft must refer to dragon magic? It not coming from body and breath is what we already know—this magic is different from the one Dad and I use."

"Temper your heart, and dragon craft will listen to you…" Callie repeated the librarian's translation. "How does one temper one's heart?"

They both stared at each other for a moment, and it was quite clear that none of them had an answer to that.

"Perhaps we can ask Granny Ethel when we get back home," Mom suggested.

Callie perked up. "If somebody understands what it means, it must be Granny Ethel!"

While the library hadn't exactly provided Callie with clear-cut answers, it had given her much to think about. About dragon magic, dragonbloods and even arcane beasts. The line about thunderfoxes appeared in Callie's mind, and she couldn't help but sigh. If only there were a way to help them.

Scared… Help.

Callie stopped in her tracks. This made the crowd press against her, pushing her to the side as they gave no heed for the little child in the middle of the market. But she ignored them all. She had heard it again. The voices were faint and some distance away, but those were the thunderfox kits.

Mom… Help.

Callie felt intense relief. The kits were still alive, and they were nearby. She had to help them. She turned to speak to Mom, wondering how she could convince her to save the little kits.

But looking around, she saw that she was surrounded by a sea of strangers. Mom was nowhere to be seen. Did they get separated because Callie had stopped so abruptly?

Help… Scared.

It was terrifying to be in this crowd of strange people all alone, but the cries of the poor kits forced Callie to push away any fears she had. She would find Mom later. For now, she gently pushed past the busy market goers, following the voices of the little kits.

Then she saw it. The wagon of the rangers was standing right at the edge of the marketplace. Callie clenched her fist. These evildoers undoubtedly intended to skin the poor little creatures and sell their pelts.

While she knew that she couldn't just rush into danger, that kits would likely lash out at her purely out of fright, that Mom would be absolutely furious once she found out… Callie was the only chance these kits had at survival.

She would set them free!
