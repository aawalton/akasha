Samantha was getting real tired of having sand in her underclothes. Granted, the pristinely white grains on the beach were finer than the reddish-brown sands in the Shattered Lands, but that did little to improve her comfort as they wormed their way into seemingly every crack and crevice. She shook out one leg, then the other, to try and empty her pants of the gritty granules, but it was an exercise in futility. Accepting she’d only be free of it after stripping down and washing it away later, she turned her attention outward.

The wind against her face was cool and refreshing, and nicely blunted the warmth from the bright, shining sun. She basked in it for a breath, letting the breeze and rays kiss her lightly tanned skin and rustle the black, shoulder length hair which peeked out from under her leather helm. The sunshine caught itself on the flattened arch of her brows and on her short, dark lashes, before lighting up the earthy brown tones of her eyes.

Looking across the boundless, undulating waters of the sea left her with a feeling of insignificance similar to when she found herself in the desert, but somehow, it was kinder. More welcoming. Here, she was not an abnormality amid a barren and unforgiving landscape, but one of many living creatures which called the skies, islands, or ocean home. She could tell as much already from the birds coasting on unseen air currents, the critters scuttling around near her feet, and the fish darting through clear waters just beyond the shore. High pitched, rhythmic cries from the distant birds mixed with the low rumble of crashing waves to create a uniquely peaceful atmosphere. Only the abundance of intrusive sand and the smell—which was reminiscent of a fishmonger’s shop—left something to be desired. Still, compared to where she’d just come from, this place was like paradise… which, upon reflection, had her guard up. Because, when had standing out in the wilderness ever been so peaceful?

She fetched her weapon from her personal spatial storage and trickled qi into the spearshaft’s inscription. As the energy fueled the enchantment, its usual length of six feet was restored from the compact, three foot version she’d used to conserve her storage’s limited volume. She could feel several System notifications vying for her attention, but the arrival of an unfamiliar creature had her pushing them to the back of her mind for later. One of those birds with the high pitched calls had just swooped down and landed a short distance away. She lifted her left arm and activated the shape altering script on her thick Vilaris-leather bracer, causing its folded layers to spring open into the circular shape of a buckler. The leather was still malleable for now, but the enchantment would hold its shape until it was struck—at which point the Rank 15 spirit beast material would instantaneously harden to resist any incoming blow.

She eyed the beast warily as it cocked its head and waddled nearer. It was only two feet tall at the most and didn’t look to be glowing with any spectacular power in her Qi Sight, but that didn’t mean much. Silas was also deceptively small, and could mask his qi when it suited him. The way it was approaching her so fearlessly made her nervous about what kind of predator she might be dealing with. It walked on two bright yellow, stick-like legs, and had wide, webbed feet tipped with small black claws. The animal had mostly white feathers. Only its wings and tail feathers were colored with additional areas of gray and black. Its hooked, narrow beak matched the yellow of its legs and feet except for a splotch of red near the tip. Focusing on it, Samantha fed 10 qi into [Inspect] just to be sure the art punched through its natural qi defenses.
