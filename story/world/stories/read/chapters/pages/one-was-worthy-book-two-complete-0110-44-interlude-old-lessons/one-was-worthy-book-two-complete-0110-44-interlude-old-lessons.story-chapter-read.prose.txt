“Here you are, Breecen! Special for you! Drink up. Gonna need it today,” Lupkep said as he dropped a drink in front of the tanner.

“I need it every day,” the man groaned. He took a sip, and his eyes widened as his limbs were flooded with Vitality. “Gods be good. Thank you all the same.”

A new shop had been going up for him. It wasn’t completed, but it had been close enough to finished for him to start working days ago.

Troll leather shoes and jackets had become all the rage recently, and there was good work for a tanner in a booming town like Pemolar in the month of Frost.

“Aye. It’ll keep you up and running. Wears off a few hours after dusk, so make sure you’re abed when it does. It’ll kick your ass if you’re still working.”

“Blah blah. You don’t really develop new lines, do you?” the man said. It was a bit rude, but well… he was being talked to before he’d had his brew! Couldn’t expect a man to be cheerful without sommat to wake him up.

“Oh, I do, yes. I used to just give the gift without the warning. Every time I did, you know what happened? I’d get some jackrabbit in here complaining that he passed the fuck out in his fields, yeah? Infested with bug bites, or chewed on by the woods dogs. Once, Travis woke up with a snake! So’s now I warn folks. Every time,” he said cheerfully.

The man paused to consider that. Looked down at the drink, then looked back up at the bartender.

“You’re a good sort, Lupkep,” he said. “Sorry.”

Lupkep beamed and patted the man on his back before turning to the next table. He delivered two more drinks to weary customers, enjoying the similar reactions.

He was a Brewer. He couldn’t make his concoctions without a little kick. The bigger the kick, the bigger the effect. The trick was making sure the kick wasn’t so big it knocked them on their ass. Most people didn’t really enjoy alcohol in the morning, no matter how some of them might protest come the night.

With juice running low, spiked milk tea was the best he could do. Most folks enjoyed a bit of a boost to Vitality in the mornings, but some, like the soldiers, or especially the woodworkers, preferred Dexterity or Strength.

He turned, and his eyes widened in delight as the town's new success story strolled in.

“Burnom! Still strange to see you with the sun on that side,” he said.

“Aye, strange for me, too.”

“What’ll it be?”

“Strength. Whatever you’ve got’s fine,” he said before putting a few coppers on his tray. “Be mixin’ mortar all day.”

“Strength coming right up. Don’t have much real fruit but I know you Rechin folk prefer your sweets. Got a tea that might sit nice in the stomach. Good for if you’re trying to stay off the hard stuff.”

Burnom grinned. “I’d like to say you don’t have to worry about that anymore… but well. Thanks.”

Lupkep nodded and made to turn, but Burnom stopped him.

“Seems… so odd to be building at a time like this. The hospital… feels like good work. Old man Moron is chomping at the bit for it. Plans to move in, I think. Mera is paying for about a thousand ovens on top of that though, and people have been coming to me non-stop trying to get proper chimneys ever since they saw my house and Travis’s new one. You should hear the complaints when I tell people I’ll need to take out the old one entirely!”

“I might not turn down another one of those myself,” Lupkep said.

“Add it to the list,” Burnom replied. “I wish I could say I’d get to you soon, but winter might be over by the time I get through all this. I’ve got more money than sense, and now that I finally do, there’s nothing to buy. Cept a drink in the morning, and some food.”

“Could buy a crossbow.”

The man barked a laugh, and Lupkep grinned.

“I’ll get ye sorted out right. Not much food to offer, but if you want some eggs, Edra’s got some hot.”

The man nodded and took a seat at the bar.

Lupkep turned and took in the place. It was nearly full. Quiet, as it only got in the mornings, but full. He’d been making more money than he knew what to do with, and honestly, he and Edra had been quietly putting their own money toward town improvements that they’d always talked about but never implemented. The second outhouse over the river helped.

Edra was training water mages and now had a small crop of assistants for that unfortunate task. The young Akkiwa was proving quite adept, and Edra was learning the Rechin method of healing in turn.

Gods, this town had more secrets than mice. A Rechin Princess, and the drunkard mason who found her, only to be shocked when she wanted nothing to do with her home country. The merchant daughter of a former duke — he’d been proud of himself for figuring that one out. The home of an elite-talented Weaver. A healer from a forgotten Duchy no one had ever heard of. Normuran Preschott, hah.

And of course…

He turned as the door opened and saw the object of his thoughts enter the bar.

Elmerina Farmer looked miserable. She should be in bed resting, but it seemed she had more iron in her bones than she had sense in her brain. You didn’t fight sickness.

“Mera! What are you doing here!?”

The girl spilled into a chair at the bar, where Edra immediately began to tut over the girl.

“Wanted to see if anyone needed…”

“You’re an eighteen-year-old girl! The only one who should be needing you is that boy of yours!” Edra barked. “Get out of here!”

“Can’t…” the girl moaned, her head slumping onto the wooden table. “Need to pay the… the Burnom.”

Edra snorted at that, and Burnom laughed, too.

“Somehow I think he’ll be alright,” Burnom chimed in about himself. “I know you’re good for it, girl. Go rest! Get healthy so when the Eschal fuckers do decide to come die on our wall, you’ll be able to give ‘em hell.”

Mera gave a weak smile out of the corner of her cheek but didn’t lift her head.

“Not just you though. Need to make sure Neogon knows… I had a thought. What if Eschal decides to attack by water?”

“Eschal? Use boats? They’re landlocked. I doubt they even think of it, but I suppose it's possible. It’s not like they're hurting for timber.”

“I’ll bring it to him myself. He usually makes his way here within the hour. You really should go,” Edra said.

Burnom shook his head. “Don’t matter anyway. The man knows. He’s got watches patrolling the beach, with mages ready to send up signal flares. An attack by sea would be Eschal suicide.”

“Well. I… there’s got to be something I can do!” Mera said, lifting her head.

Edra raised her hands. “I know you and those eyes. If you’re gonna stay, at least let Lup make you a drink.”

Lupkep eyed his wife. What a strange thing to say. Of course he was going to make her a damn drink, even if she did leave! The Vitality would certainly help. The poor thing looked pale as a ghost.

“A drink would be fantastic,” she said, lowering her head back down.

Poor girl.

Now that Mera’s money was flowing around the town like water, she was surprisingly bereft of tasks. She’d created a monster, and now it was operating without her. Villagers and newcomers alike were taking the initiative to build new buildings, fix things that had gone unrepaired, and generally grow as a town. While Mera had the coin to keep pushing that, with her help, everyone else did too.

A massive dock was being built out at the Ophician. The town really had needed one for years. The hunters' lodge was more of a hunters' compound now, and would continue to grow until Eschal finished closing the siege.

Last he’d heard, the army was still fording the river.

Burnom’s hospital was also a rather huge relief to the bar. A massive building meant for housing wounded would certainly go a long way towards clearing all of the sickly and injured out of his bar at the very least.

Somehow, despite the army gathering around the outskirts of town, and the pervasive fear lingering behind every word, people were… getting on with their business. Like tomorrow everything would be okay, even though all signs pointed to the contrary.

Lupkep sighed, and headed back into the backroom, to ladle out a few drinks. Vitality for Mera. Strength for Burnom. A few more vitalitys for some other patrons he’d only just met.

He was in the process of grabbing the last cup when Edra came in and grabbed Mera’s glass.

He was a fat, jolly man, and he knew it. However, something about his job perhaps, or maybe just his own devotion to situational awareness had gifted him with an unnaturally high dexterity. So high that it almost felt like a second sight at times. He’d gained at least three points in the attribute with every level. It was essential to a barkeeper! A man needed to know when his patrons were rubbernecking after all.

That was why he could tell that the woman had added something to the drink.

“What is that?” he asked, without turning.

Edra jumped.

“Gods be damned, man! I’ve told you a thousand times not to do that!” she said.

He wouldn’t be deterred. He turned away from the cups and looked at his wife.

“Edra, love?” he insisted.

“M-medicine. Mix of willow bark. I went and got some.”

“No, you didn’t. The woods ain’t safe, and the nearest willow trees are all the way up near Clairmont. Where the Eschal Army is. Wife. What are you doin’?”

His brow was furrowed.

No one else was sick. No one else was really getting sick. It was a very strange coincidence that Mera alone seemed to be affected. He’d been afraid they’d have a load of colds and sick customers any day now. Everyone knew sickness traveled in waves and affected half the town before running off. But it hadn’t.

Edra dropped her hands to her side.

“She’s not an Elite talent, Lup.”

“So?”

“It’s not Masterful, either.”

“I don’t see your point. What does that have to do with you putting sommat in her drink?”

“Suffer no legends. I… Lup, she’s going to get us all killed!” Edra said. “Something must be done!”

“Some–! What is this nonsense! Sure, sure, suffer no legends! But this is Mera we’re talking about. Little Elmerina! We held her as a babe! What have you put in the drink!?”

Edra sighed. “Poison. A small dose. Very small. The girl has some sort of sickness-fighting skill, else she’d already be long dead.”

“What… gods! What!? Why!? Edra, she’s… that girl is like a daughter to me! To us! I thought!? What!? Poison?”

Edra’s eyes were wet. “Lup, she has to die. Her talent is Legendary. At least.”

“Everyone with one working eye knows that! But she’s our gods-damned legend, and you’re… you’re…!”

“Don’t pretend you’re ignorant. Legendary Talents always lead to the same story. Suffering, and death. She’s probably why that army is out there!”

“She’s going to bloody well save us all!” Lupkep shouted.

Edra’s cheeks had small trails now.

“She’ll lead to untold suffering. They always do.”

“And what proof do you have of that, huh? Stories? Legends!? Give her a fucking chance, eh!?”

“I…”

“No. I… Edra,” he paused, and spotted the drink, still her hand. Water mage or not, he was still much stronger and faster than her. He snatched the glass out of her hand and smashed it against the floor. “My pub… my gods damned… what the hells? She’s like a daughter.”

“Do you think I don’t know that!?” Edra shouted beneath her breath, just low enough that none of the patrons outside would hear. “Why did you have to notice! Now you’ll have to help, for the good of us all!”

“I’ll do no such thing, and neither will you,” he said.

“Lup… Lup, it has to happen! Do you think we get to pick and choose who the stories apply to? Don’t you think all the past legends who’ve caused wars and upheaval… Don’t you think they all had loved ones who weren’t strong enough to do what we need to do now? We’ve all heard it since we were babes! Suffer No Legends. I’m sure the girl herself has! It’s not just a… a talisman! Some stupid ward against evil! It’s a directive! We have the chance right now to prevent deaths on the scale of the Summer or worse.”

“You think we’ll survive the Eschal army without her?” Lupkep said, trying to use practicality and logic where the heart had failed. His wife — of decades! — was plotting to murder someone. No. Not plotting! Actively committing it!

“To the hells with the sayings! The old wisdom! They don’t know our Mera!”

“Don’t they, though?”

All of that was just… well. No. It was more than folk tales and superstition. There was certainly truth to her words, but this was Mera! The idea of applying that to her felt like stabbing his own child in the back.

They had no children of their own.

“I don’t know,” Edra said. “I know what my ma taught me.”

“Wait then! Just… wait it out. We’ll address this after the Eschal threat is over.”

He stepped up to the woman. Grabbed her waist. It felt… colder now, somehow. She fell in.

She didn’t want to do this any more than he did. The difference was that she was. She had. And now… gods, what was he supposed to do?

“I… okay. But after the war…”

He hugged her, so he didn’t have to respond.
