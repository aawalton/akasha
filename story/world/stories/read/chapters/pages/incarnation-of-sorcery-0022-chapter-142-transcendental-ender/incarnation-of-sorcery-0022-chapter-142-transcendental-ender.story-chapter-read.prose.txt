Listen closely, students, for what I am about to say will reveal to you your goal. Your final desire. Your ultimate happiness. It will gift you guidance beyond anything you could ever hope to achieve. And yet, it is a simple thing. A small matter, in truth.

For all I am about to say is that the peak of the Grand Frame, the pinnacle of mortal strength recognized by the system that governs us all, is Transcendent.

Once you reach that pinnacle, that peak, you are now immortalized in not just history, but in legend! It isn’t mere dusty textbooks withering away with the ages that will hold your deeds. Oh, no. Now, songs will be written in your honour, tales will be shared of your exploits, mortalkind will see you as an icon lasting infinity.

And it isn’t impossible. There already exists beings of such a nature, of such incalculable and seemingly unattainable might. Rare though they may be, impossible though they may be to reach, they exist. And therefore, they are living proof that such a feat is accomplishable.

What is that? When will I become one? Please, jest not. While such a distant goal does drive me towards excellence in all that I do, it will be centuries yet before I ever come close…

Lecture On Transcendents

Vistima F.

Viren was doing his best to not hold anyone back. It wasn’t that he considered himself weak or anything of the sort, though he might be one of the weakest present in High Academ Zevo’s group.

The problem was that he was distracted by Ryland. Or rather, by what his mentor was about to do.

There was a good reason Viren had childishly wanted to accompany him.

Thankfully, he reined in those impulses and did his best to follow along as fast as he could. Zevo was good, taking them through the Crypts without a single moment to pause and consider whether they were heading in the right direction or not, which suggested he was quite familiar with the area.

“I can’t believe we got… possessed,” said the Rhellian student who had dared to attack Ryland. Suthor. That was his name. “How could this happen? We couldn’t even fight back.”

Viren’s sympathy was limited. The kind of idiots who thought assaulting Marionys Ryland would go well were the kind of idiots who got possessed by dumb ghosts in ancient crypts. His lack of sympathy slowly crumbled, though, as he felt listened to the rest of the possessed morosely corroborate the first Rhellian boy.

What in the world would Viren have done if he had gotten possessed? How did it even feel? What was it like? He couldn’t wrap his head around it, and he knew that he was only thinking of it so casually because he hadn’t experienced it himself yet.

Viren wanted to keep it that way, though, so he stuck a little closer than normal to the others.

“Don’t worry,” Acrypha said in a low voice. “If a ghost possesses you, I’ll make sure to beat you within an inch of your life. That way, the ghost will be forced to leave you alone.”

Viren had so many different responses and questions for her crazy statement that his brain momentarily short-circuited. “What?”

“Think about it, Viren. If your body is broken, if you physically can’t move or do anything else, then what is the point of possessing you? They’d be stupid to do so, right? So clearly, the best method of exorcising these spirits is by turning you into an unrecognizable bag of meat and bones.” She paused for a second. “And I suppose some skin and hair too, for decency’s sake.”

Viren blinked several times at her very rapidly, before just turning around and focusing on their trek. He wanted to think Acrypha was joking, but she had sounded absolutely serious.

Best to ignore her completely.

High Academ Zevo remained silent throughout the whole little trip, especially once the tremors from far, far below started shaking everything like the discordant heartbeats of a gargantuan beast.

“Is that…?” Saera asked, looking meaningfully at Viren and Acrypha.

He nodded, as did Acrypha. Wherever Ryland had gone, whatever he was doing, he was more or less showing just why he was unmatched by anything Viren could even conceive of.

The man had destroyed a Calamity-cursed, well, Calamity, for crying out loud. Viren had witnessed that in person. The shock from that day, even almost two months later, still refused to settle properly in his mind. Like he had suffered a branding wound that he’d need to contend with for the rest of his life.

This Dreadlich? Viren wasn’t sure what it actually was, but it couldn’t be anything that Ryland couldn’t simply wipe out from existence itself,

They arrived at their destination before long. Zevo’s guidance was immaculate.

“What’s going on?” Tyra asked. “We evacuated the Crypts as soon as we received the notice to do so.”

She had rushed over to the Arcoryx contingent with Zevo, looking over them all with concern. Bulgon and the rest of the students from Vyrd were all with her. They looked worried but unharmed, which Viren was thankful for.

Zevo talked shortly with High Academ Tyra and the professors from the rest of the academies. They were all equally concerned, although, like with Arcoryx, none of their students looked harmed in any way. It was only Zevo’s Rhellian students who had been affected by the possession.

Something Viren found very odd. He wondered what in the world was going on with them all.

“Are we sure you have all of our students?” the Jure High Academ was asking.

“I believe so,” Zevo said quickly. He stared at his group for a moment. “The Rhellians are all here. Could you kindly corroborate if that’s the case with you students as well?”

They did so. What followed was a bit of haphazard chaos as everyone tried to ignore the constant tremors from underground and tried to focus on making sure all the students were present. They did account for everyone, except—

“Where is Ryland?” High Academ Tyra asked. She was staring at Viren and Acrypha almost accusingly, like Ryland was a forbidden candy none of them should have been able to get their hands on.

“Uh…” Viren said, unsure if he should be answering. Would it be betraying his mentor’s trust? Was the whole thing with this Dreadlich supposed to be a secret?

“He went to stop the ghosts from possessing people,” Acrypha said.

Well, that was very politic of her. Viren supposed that came a lot more naturally to nobles.

They had just about finished their counts to reassure themselves that everything was fine when the worst tremor yet coursed through the land. It was immediately followed up by a terrific blast, a detonation that had nearly everyone clasping their ears and hunkering down against the rush of air that scoured over everything with a vengeance.

Against the wind ripping through his hair and trying to slice across his eyes, Viren managed to peer into the sky enough to see what exactly was going on.

An enormous eruption had torn open the mountains in the distance. Nearer at hand, a dust cloud bigger than any portent of storm Viren had ever seen clouded the range. But that was fine. The real magic was happening far higher in the air.

Where an actual storm cloud flickering with lightning was fighting something.

Or someone.

The battle was tremendous. Every clash felt like more lightning was splitting the sky in half. Every collision of tremendous powers was sending out blasts that had everyone else panicking terribly.

The Academs were busy casting barriers over everyone, even when their own manasoul started growing tired. At the same time, they were all being moved farther and farther away from the site of the furious clash. The Academ from Eviliyen School was constructing a complicated barrier array in a bubble of atmosphere around them that almost intrigued Viren. Almost.

For most of his attention was still locked onto that distant clash. On magic that was almost apocalyptic in its fury.

On pieces of broken land big enough to flatten Arcoryx Academy flying around with deadly velocity, on a mountain-sized cyclone that was frighteningly familiar, on a maw so enormous in scale, Viren started feeling vertigo even on the ground.

And then came that moment when the sky was blanketed in darkness, which started slamming down earthwards. For just a moment, they had all frozen. No one, not even High Academ Zevo, who had courageously tackled even Ryland himself, had thought they’d survive magic of that catastrophic calibre.

But the sky didn’t land on them with all its black fury. Instead, it staggered to a stop still a few leagues over them all.

At which point, it chose to explode.

Viren certainly wasn’t alone with his scream. It felt like the world was ending, like the sun had been called forth to peek through the upper atmosphere, shining so brightly that they’d be permanently blinded. Where even closing one’s eyes wasn’t enough.

When things calmed down enough, when all was silent and still, Viren somehow managed to look up skywards again. The state of his vision was absolutely horrendous, yet he persisted anyway. That sort of magic… Who in the world had cast it? One of the combatants had to have been Ryland. Viren had recognized at least one of the spells. But who was the other one?

It was hard to tell. Even that dark storm cloud was dissipating to nothing. Was that good? Did it mean Ryland was victorious?

Viren had been soundly ignoring the rest of the students and the Academs in his effort to find answers. They were all busy checking in on each other and ensuring that everyone was alright. Well, all but Acrypha. She clearly had the same intention as him, going by the direction of her stare.

“You think he won?” Acrypha asked.

Viren stared at her next. “Are you seriously doubting sir?”

“Not exactly. But you’ll recall that I didn’t ask if he was alive or not.”

Viren grunted, conceding the point. Winning wasn’t necessarily the same thing as beating one’s opponent. For all Viren knew, Ryland had been seeking something specific in that altercation, something he may or may not have found. Viren wouldn’t know till he met his mentor.

Said meeting occurred just a moment later, when a golden fracture in spacetime had Ryland emerging from one of his classic dark rifts.

“Ah,” he said, smiling as soon as he saw them all. “I’m glad you’re all alright.”

Viren just shook his head. Looking at Ryland, it was impossible to think he had just gone through a clash that would have wiped out everyone else present in the area in the blink of an eye, including the High Academs. There were no wounds on him, no tears in his clothes or signs of exhaustion, not even a single speck of dirt. He looked absolutely the same as he always did.

“Sir,” Viren said, trying not to let all his stupid emotions show on his face or his words. None of his awe, his exhilaration at the impossible magic, his sheer fear for his own safety at one point. He tried to let none of it appear, but he wasn’t quite successful. “What in the world did you just do?”

Before Ryland could answer, others were hurrying over.

“Ryland!” Tyra hurried over. “What in the absolute, gods-forsaken, Calamity-cursed, monstrous madness just happened?”

Ryland sighed, then gave a savvy reply that clearly impressed Acrypha.

Right. In this chaos, with so many others present, Viren wasn’t going to get a straight answer. He’d just have to be patient and listen in. And not be distracted by what he had seen a while ago. Because if he thought about it for too long, Viren was liable to get lost in the awe he couldn’t leave behind.

Ryland was tempted to take care of the piece of himself first and foremost before anything. He had come to Rhel with the intention of finding more pieces of himself, and now, he had done so. If in a roundabout way without a great deal of effort from himself personally.

Unless defeating Pious counted as effort, which Ryland wasn’t sure it did. Not for him, at least.

Yet the Grand Frame had decided to count it.

You have prevented the rise of a new Transcendent-ranked mage despite being only Exalted-ranked.

Rank up from Exalted [51] to Exalted [94].

Reward: Glyph of Piety [Tier 25]. ]

Ryland couldn’t help but blink at the reward. A Glyph of… Piety. He had never been a very faithful person, unless the subject of faith was in his own capabilities to achieve whatever he set out to accomplish. As such, receiving a Glyph of that sort seemed extra strange.

He wasn’t even sure what the use case for something of that nature would be. Maybe the priests and priestesses of the Crypts would appreciate it a lot more.

Especially after that battle between him and Pious had wrecked a wide area of the Crypts.

Ryland decided to head down. Quick uses of [Omniscience] and [Translocate] had him right back where he had left the students with High Academ Zevo. It was a good thing he found no one there, especially since everything was broken and ruined. Ryland refused to breathe when there was all that dust in the air.

A repeat cast of his last two spells got him to where nearly everyone had gathered. He was glad to see everyone was fine.

He was then busy talking with everyone and explaining what was going on. Ryland stuck to the barest minimums specifics about the situation. He didn’t want to alarm anyone, especially the Rhellians, about everything he had seen and experienced, or with everything he had since discovered about what was going on.

Their shock was expected. So much so, that Ryland already had a countermeasure for it.

“You destroyed the Dreadlich?” High Academ Tyra asked, staring at him with wide eyes.

“There was a member of the Flourinus Order present too?” Sint asked, eyes widening.

“Indeed,” Ryland said. “Which is why I need to depart so I can report to the actual authorities.”

High Academ Zevo stepped up. “If there is any reporting to be done, then it falls on Rhellian authorities to do so. We must not force our dear guests to toil any further than we already have. It would bring great dishonour on us.”

“I should think that allowing a Calamity to potentially rise would bring great dishonour too.”

“It…” Zevo quickly martialled himself after that blunt point. “There should never have been a Calamity down there to begin with! The Dreadlich was contained. In fact, its containment was why the Crypts were constructed the way they have been.”

“They are no longer in that constructed state, now are they?” Ryland pointed out.

“I suppose not…”

That admission was enough for Ryland to gently but firmly nudge them all away from the fact that he needed to handle the issue himself. They had other priorities. Somewhat more mundane ones, but Ryland wasn’t about to point that out so callously. Everything had a certain level of importance.

Once he had bid his farewells and ensured nothing was wrong, nor was he really needed, Ryland was about to head out.

But Viren and Acrypha came over to him for a final goodbye.

“Don’t worry about us, sir,” Acrypha said. “I’ll make sure Viren stays out of trouble.”

Viren glared at the girl. “You are trouble.” Before she could contradict that supposition, he quickly turned to Ryland, though he was hesitating for some reason. “Sir, do you know if… the exchange semester will keep going? I mean, with everything that happened…”

Ryland smiled and ruffled his hair. “Don’t worry, Viren. I believe you’ll still have loads of opportunities for fun.”

“Sir,” Acrypha said. “It’s true that academy student is one of the most hazardous occupations in any civilization, right?”

Ryland really wanted to know where in the world she had learned that little factoid, but he had to admit there was a certain nugget of truth to that. “I believe there might be some statistics supporting that conclusion, yes. Although, you’d be hard-pressed to find anyone making that an actual claim, I should think.”

Viren sighed. “Where exactly are you going to go, sir?”

Ryland straightened and let his magic emerge. “[Translocate].” A rift formed, and he stepped halfway through. “I’m heading straight for the Demiurge’s palace. Straight to Kindly Mavar.” The revelation brought wide-eyed looks from both children. “See you two later!”

He did exactly as he had stated. Stepping through the rift brought him to the grand palace that Mavar Val Arona, the ruler of Rhel, called home. Right into its very centre. Where the Demiurge sat on his throne.
