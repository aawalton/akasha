Eric walked on. He moved slowly, picking his route as deliberately as he could, and kept his ears open for the dry rustling of scorpions. A distant cry made him freeze, the sound somewhere between roar and something he couldn’t quite identify, but when nothing immediately attacked him, he pressed on. Occasionally, he came across some scorpions, but it was nothing his boots couldn’t handle, except once, where he turned the corner to find an entire knot of scorpions all tangled up in the center of the path. A quick Explosion had taken care of them, and he’d ran on. He took turn after turn, wandering the dungeon at random, his choices reduced whenever the walls felt like moving.

“You know, it occurs to me that picking a maze for my dungeon speed run might not have been the wisest choice.” Eric snapped and pointed at the air.

“What did you do that for?” Cy asked.

“So they’d know to add in the studio audience fake laughter.”

“They who? Joe?”

“Well, Joe, the clippers who reupload this to Youtube, someone.”

“What’s a you tube?”

“It’s…” Eric paused, then sighed and preempted Cy’s interjection. “A primitive video uploading service.”

“Oh, of course.” Cy sat up and scratched behind his ear with his back leg. “What’s a video uploading service?”

“You didn’t have video servers in your world?” Eric asked, shocked.

Cy scoffed. “Of course not. Why would we need such clunky technology when we had elegant magic instead?”

“Uh huh.” Eric shook his head at Cy and walked on, going deeper into the dungeon—then stopped dead.

“What is it?” Cy asked, craning his neck in something akin to interest.

“There.” Eric pointed. A few dents in the ground and some scraps of white laid on the floor. He kneeled and brushed them off, revealing fragments of vertebrae. “That’s where I found the skull. We’re close to the entrance of the dungeon.

“Congratulations. So what?”

“So…” He pointed ahead of them. “That means this is the passage the scorpions poured out of earlier. The direction the maze didn’t want me going.”

Cy slapped his shoulder. “Less talk, more moving!”

Eric shot a look at the rabbit, chuckling a little under his breath. How fast he went from so what to go go go! “Yes, Your Majesty.”

“No, no, too formal. For one of my nobility, it would be Your Lordship.”

“I am not calling you “Your Lordship.””

“But you would call me “Your Majesty,” the phrase of address reserved for the king alone?”

Eric shrugged. “I don’t have a king. Sure.”

Cy jolted. He drew back, leaning as far as he could from Eric’s face to get a better look at him. “You have no kings?”

“Well, not none, but fewer. It’s mostly democracies nowadays. At least, it is where I live.” Kinda. Not that he needed to get into that with a talking rabbit.

“De mo…”

“The common people vote for a ruler,” Eric explained, walking on.

Cy gasped. “How crass! How would the common man know who to choose? The peasants aren’t equipped for such higher thinking!”

“Women can vote, too,” Eric pointed out. It wasn’t just the common man.

“Yes? Why wouldn’t they be able to? If they’re just letting anyone do it, aren’t they part of the commonfolk?”

Eric could almost feel the air quotes around “commonfolk.” He wasn’t sure what they meant, but he was sure that Cy didn’t mean anything pleasant by them. He gestured vaguely. “You said man… never mind.”

“Hmph.” Cy settled back on his shoulder, then shook his head. “Ruled by the masses! Devolving to mob rule! This world is certainly primitive. When you win the Game, be sure to install a proper world ruler. Anything else would be preposterous.”

Eric coughed. “There isn’t such a thing as a world ruler here.”

“There… no? But how do you handle inter-world communications and politics?”

“Not really a thing. You had inter-world communications?” It was Eric’s turn to stare at the bunny in shock. Maybe Cy’s culture really was more advanced… at some things, at least.

“Well, of course. Like any complex civilization, we communicated with other worlds, piercing the veil between them with our spells. Until the Game began sweeping through. First one, then another… We watched the worlds wink out, one at a time, powerless to stop them.” Cy went quiet, and for perhaps the first time, Eric noticed real pain in his eyes.

“Oh, yeah?”

“Yes.” Cy went silent. Eric waited, but he said no more on the subject.

“Well, that’s not going to happen to Earth.”

Cy harrumphed, but he didn’t directly contradict Eric. “I will do everything in my power to stop the Game. No matter what it takes.”

The path widened ahead of them. The roar Eric had heard earlier sounded again, but much closer than it was before. Eric swallowed. “I think we’ve found the boss chamber.”

“Are you prepared?”

Eric scanned his mana repositories. He was still over half-full; the dungeon so far hadn’t been particularly dangerous. He’d mostly stomped scorpions and walked around a maze. “As I’ll ever be.”

“Excellent. Let us proceed.”

Eric nodded. He drew the Blackfang Sabre and strode forward, toward where the sun beamed down, no longer restricted by tight walls. A hulking shadow loomed there, washed to black by the sun’s brilliant light. Eric lifted his hand, blocking out the sun, and stepped into the arena.

A crowd cheered. The drum of a thousand feet stomping shook the ground. Eric looked around, startled. A moment ago, he’d been alone with the looming shape and nothing; now, a veritable sea of goblins filled the seats that wrapped around the central arena. They chanted and beat their spears against the ground, stomping, drumming, and making all kinds of noise.

“Are they real?” he asked.

“Eyes forward,” Cy chided him.

He turned, taking in the shadow yet again. The massive beast loomed over the center of the field. He hadn’t noticed from the walk-up, since the glare of the sun was in his eyes, but it wore a collar and was bound to the earth with a heavy chain. Despite the chain’s weight, it strained as the beast roared again and clawed at Eric.

And what a beast it was. It was a minotaur, but instead of a bull’s head, it had the three-eyed, three-horned head he’d seen the skull of earlier. Its body was proportioned slightly wrong, too, its barrel chest wider than any human Eric had ever seen. Its bull legs stomped the earth, thumping dinner-plate sized hooves into the stone and kicking up dust just from moving around. A long, whiplike tail thrashed behind it. It was unarmed, but it didn’t need weapons; its arms were as wide around as Eric was, and every inch of them roiled with more muscle than he’d have in his life, even considering his recent change in profession.

A disembodied voice sounded, coming from the center of the arena, though Eric saw no source, speaker or otherwise. “Contestants, take your marks!”

A glowing blue square appeared on the floor not far from where Eric stood. Another appeared around the minotaur, which roared again, as if in indignation.

“What happens if I just attack now?” Eric muttered under his breath, pacing toward the box.

“You’re not strong enough to deal with the fallout. Take your mark,” Cy muttered back.

“What is this, anyways?”

“The Game has its theatrics. Right now, all you can do is play along. For the most part, they won’t deliberately disadvantage you.”

For the most part. Eric understood the unspoken part without anyone else stepping in and clarifying it. “Why? People like it?”

“Indeed. The pageantry mostly exists to promote viewership, so it works in your favor. I can’t say if you’ll end up on the all-important episode,” he spat the final word like venom, “but at the very least, these matches with a little more prelude tend to be popular among those who watch individual streams and clips.”

Eric nodded. “Let’s get this party started, then.” He walked over and took his mark in the blue square.

The disembodied voice sounded again. “Ladies and gentlegoblins! I present to you… the Trinotaur!”

The goblins roared. Eric frowned. “I… don’t think the “mi” part of “minotaur” is a number.” This wasn’t a unicorn-tricorn situation; they’d just tacked three onto the front of a non-numeric name.

Cy drew back to give him a look. “You think goblins can count?”

“You know? Fair.”

“And on the other side, the Trinotaur’s next victim… the pathetic Player known as Eric!”

“Oh, hey,” Eric complained, while the goblins booed. A few of the more effusive ones threw trash and rotten food onto the arena floor.

“He’s featured… no, should I say appeared, in a grand total of one episode! Shall we show his highlights? Oh, I’m sorry… highlight.” A flickering screen appeared over the center of the stage. The clip of Eric hissing back at the snake played on loop, blown up to a hundred times its size. The goblins laughed riotously, hooting and hollering and smacking their thighs at the sight.

“There’s only been three episodes,” Eric commented to himself, not even trying to be heard over the goblins. As embarrassing as the snake clip was, they at least weren’t replaying him in a slap fight on loop, which was an option for some people in the Tutorial right now. In fact, compared to the really stupid drama going down in the Tutorial, he would much rather have a silly snake gif tied to his name.

At the same time, he was talking to himself a little. There had only been three episodes. Like Joe pointed out, there were still billions of contestants, and most of the viewership right now wanted to see the Tutorial. There’d only been three episodes. It was an honor to appear in one of them, not an embarrassment that he’d only appeared once. With how much stress he was under, his perspective had gotten a little skewed. He needed to stay humble and keep his eye on the prize, not worry over every little appearance.

“We’ve all seen enough of that. Let’s get on with the meat of it! Contestants, are you ready?” The disembodied voice shouted the last three words with the enthusiasm of a game show host or a race announcer.

The Trinotaur roared.

“I guess,” Eric said. He had his sword-focus-thingy and his mana was still in good shape. He had everything he needed, probably.

On his shoulder, Cy snorted.

“On your marks… get set… Fight!”
