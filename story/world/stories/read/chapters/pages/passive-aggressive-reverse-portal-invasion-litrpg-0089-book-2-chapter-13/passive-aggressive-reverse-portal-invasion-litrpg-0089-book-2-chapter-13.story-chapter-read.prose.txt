The next day, we got back to marching. Well. Less marching, more our eclectic mix of walking and training our skills and proficiencies. Watching the mixed species of Last Chance move about had taught me that self-improvement, for any but the laziest bums, was a fact of life here. If you were walking, you might as well run, to improve your running skill. If you were running, you might as well jump off rocks and climb trees to improve those skills. Or… manipulate the air, craft cat’s cradles, whittle bone whistles or practice your swordplay. With a very few exceptions, every person would have skills dear to them that were a part of their own much-rehearsed rotation.

We’d spent a while to really get into our own rotations back with Nix and Haddock. Yet, where the pair had tolerated the necessity of training, Victoria got it. She heard our explanations, saw the point and assured payout, and got straight into it. I absolutely got how she’d achieved her sculpted physique. The woman was a true gymaholic.

Still, certain things took a few attempts to really sink in.

“You’re such a bad liar. Nobody’s that insane.”

I shrugged. “Well. I am? Just… think about it. Yesterday, I got myself hit a bunch of times, and got a point to Dodge, Armor and Resilience, two to Pain Tolerance and a full five to Acid Resistance. Since then, my Self-Heal’s also jumped another point. That leaves me just one point short of the Level 25 evolution for both Dodge and Self-Heal. And all it cost me was a bit of pain. Hell, my health is almost back to full already.”

She looked at me like I’d just said I wanted to be a career politician. Y’know, that particular blend of disgust and disbelief that she’d even heard me right. “But… poison you? Why don’t I just skip the middleman and go straight to stabbing you in the kidney?”

“Heh. I saw a Tavungor who trained just like that. There was this little bloke who’d just slash at him constantly with his daggers. Then a mage used some healing talent for a bit before they’d start over.” I saw her look of disbelief. “I mean, that’s not what I’m planning. Yet.” Truth be told, I could see the point of it. Pain Tolerance, even though it was still below Level 20, was effective enough that minor cuts and hurts were more annoyances than actual hindrances. Hell, I wasn’t even sure it was any worse than the common bruises I’d always end up with after my sparring sessions. I believed there was some lovely overlap happening between the Pain Tolerance skill and the pain receptors of my body, that had already been professionally dulled or deadened over years of abuse.

And once the pain was out of the equation? The world looked a lot different. Every moment I spent at full health and stamina was a moment I wasn’t improving. Endurance. Self-Heal. Stamina Regeneration. Whichever defense skill I could improve by getting myself hurt. I doubted that I was ever going to like pain, become one of those masochistic weirdos from tattoo shows who got themselves hanging from the ceiling with a hook through their pecker. And I definitely didn’t want to end up like some Deadpool-wannabe, covered in scars. But I was not going to let something as… as irrelevant as pain keep me from becoming strong.

So, we travelled. And I hurt. And we slowly expanded on our skills and mutual rapport.

Steve, beyond a doubt, was going to end up having a much higher Running skill than mine. He’d circle us constantly, sometimes with his invisibility engaged, scouting the near vicinity, looking for new species to Identify, and ensuring we weren’t going to be jumped by something. When he was around, he’d update us as to the status of our surroundings and possible threats. We’d engage in a few back and forths, with him attacking with his spear and other piercing weapons and me defending. We’d critique each other as best we could, footwork, reaction time, that sort of thing, to the best of our knowledge. Then, he’d take off again.

The area we were in was not exactly thriving. Not only did the vegetation struggle more, grass stalks veering more for the brown and withered look, there were also less access to water. The only water hole we spotted that day was encircled by a ring of capillaries, just like the very first encounter I’d had with monsters in this place. There were no Core Leechers here, though, which was a pity. I’d have loved a rematch with the ugly abominations of nature now that I’d grown stronger.

On top of that, we didn’t encounter a lot of opposition. Steve learned the secret behind the large pillars studding the plain, as he spotted an animal on top of it. Apparently, the winged, furry mammals were ambush predators, who used the pillars to launch themselves at any enemies. They weren’t fliers, but more like those flying squirrels, only with the size and temper of an annoyed wolverine.

We avoided the pillars.

Victoria was trying to find her own routine, figure out which skills to keep evolving. Using her magic was an easy choice, of course. She kept her mana constantly regenerating, using her talents on the go, all the while she kept my health ticking down and my Poison Resistance improving. On top of that, dedicated a lot of time to getting used to moving with her Ramping Up talent, practicing jumps, sprints, dodging and everything at different stages of the constantly changing state of the buff.

We worked hard throughout the day. We did have to plot in a few extra breaks to regain mana or stamina, but nobody grumbled over the delays. Humanity as a whole might be on the clock, earning enough strength to stand up to the rest of Ademia, but this wasn’t dawdling. This was part of the prep.

Just like back on Earth: When you put in the hours? You reaped the rewards. Every single movement or regeneration-related skill of mine saw at least one increase, with Athletics, Running and Stamina Regeneration earning two. Then there was the matter of Poison Resistance, which leapt from eleven to eighteen in one day. Even Victoria had to admit that was worth the pain. The idea that some day soon, she could have a poison web trigger right on top of me, and I’d just shrug it off, while her poison killed off every surrounding enemy – yeah, that was a goal worth striving for.

Steve found enough beasts to identify in the distance that his Explorer’s Strength earned another increase. An additional +1 to Strength. It wasn’t the optimal choice for any of our builds, but it was bloody lovely.

Some rewards, meanwhile, arrived automatically. We were just traversing a tricky passage, where it looked like something had perforated the soil, leaving crumbling footing and holes to trap the unwary, when the notification hit me.

I stopped and flung my hands into the air. “Yes! One step closer to immortal!”

Steve was just catching up from behind. He and Victoria approached and I elaborated without being prompted. “Higher Self-Heal. It finally hit Level 25.”

Victoria smirked. “I’m not even going to consider how much you’ve bled to hit that.”

“No comments.” I replied drily, and segued onward into the options.

Your skill Higher Self-Heal has reached the first evolution threshold at Level 25. This earns you a choice between two different evolution options.

Please note. Due to Higher Self-Heal reaching Rare, the evolution options are improved.

Option one:

Level 25 evolution:

The first debuff each day is automatically prevented, regardless of rarity.

Option two:

Level 25 evolution:

The healing factor of Self-Heal is improved from 10% per point to 20%

“No me chingues. Regardless of rarity? Does that mean if you went up against, like, Medusa, and she petrified your white ass, you’d just unpetrify right away?”

“I… think so? Only, if I stubbed my toe two minutes earlier, that’d be the debuff that would be prevented, and I’d still be turned into stone.”

“Rock hard, eh? A lot of-“

“Please don’t finish that sentence.” I groaned.

She chuckled. “All right. Still, that’s what you’re getting, ain’t it? The other one’s boring.”

“Boring. Yes.” I mused. “But. Steve. Help me do the math, would you? Say I make it to… let’s say seventy-five in Self-Heal. What’s that in regeneration per hour?”

“What’s your Health right now?

“Twelve hundred, right on the money.”

Victoria made a tiny noise in the back of her throat.

“At seventy-five… that’s got me at a hundred and two health per hour. Without that evolution.”

“No me jodes!” Victoria said. “I am, without trying to brag or anything, one tough bitch. Are you seriously telling me that, if you take that evolution and got your skill to seventy-five, you’d be able to heal more than my full health in forty-five minutes?”

“That’s what I’m thinking. And then, who knows? With a good alchemist, the right enchanted items, or the next evolutions, maybe I can improve it even further. And, obviously, I’ll be improving everything else at the same time. Shield, Resilience, Armor, all my resistances. At some point, I might be able to take everything head on and keep going.”

“Only, the other evolution might save your life outright, where any possible healing rate can’t help you.”

“That is also true.”

For a while, there was nothing but silence, and the awe-inspiring image of a Floating Earth-Caster throwing itself off a pillar in the far distance and hang-gliding several hundred meters on its furry wings before slamming down onto its unsuspecting target.

Victoria huffed. “I say go for it. The healing one. The other one might be better right now, but a lot of the things we pick are planned for long-term. If you pull this off? It’ll be glorious. If you end up petrified? Eh. I can keep a statue of you around as a coat rack.”

I groaned. “I just realized this means you’ll be able to poison me twice as much in the near future.”

“Well, you’re the one getting kicks out of that, not me.” She smirked. “Hey. What did Navi say to Link when he couldn’t open the door?”

I squinted at her, one eyebrow slowly rising by itself. “I don’t know. What did Navi say?”

“Triforce!” She said, then broke down laughing.

I looked at the utter loon, shaking my head in dismay. Then my head swivelled to see Steve who was clearly hiding a grin behind his hands. “Nutters. Every one of you!”

“I know!” Victoria said happily. “Speaking of force, by the way. I actually did gain a level earlier. And I got something new. Something lovely, I think.”

You have earned a new general skill:

Passive skill

People are used to pushing their physical limits to make the most of their muscles. Yet, when it comes to magical talents, many do not push further than what is granted to them by the talents themselves. This skill helps you consciously push the limits. Every level in Magical Impact will marginally improve the impact of any magical talent that is not bound by hard limits.

I scratched my head. “That’s… confusing. Hell’s that mean?”

Victoria shrugged. “I think that came from messing around with Ramping Up.”

“Your movement talent?”

“Yup. It’s got a bit in there about also improving reaction speed, and when I was playing around with that stupid Am Bush.” She stopped to titter. “I love that name so much. Anyway, I was messing about with the sensations of it, trying to move faster, to really make the most of it, improve my dodging skills and whatnot.”

“Ah.” I started to see it now. “So, you won’t be able to go any faster, but you might be able to… direct it better. I like that.” I pulled up my talent list and considered things. So, I could probably direct Deflect a lot better. Maybe aim Wave of Reflection better, or use it to better control Mirror of All. Yeah, I’ll absolutely be trying to learn that one as well. Though my track record with magical skills isn’t too impressive.” I trailed off, realizing Victoria was waiting for me with a raised eyebrow.

“You done?”

“Stage is all yours, ma’am.” I bowed.

She snorted. “Don’t make me feel old.” She cleared her throat. “Okay. This time, I got three choices for new talents. One’s right out, because it’s an instant debuff and my quirk will nix that. Shame, cause it would’ve been damn good too.” She paused before asking. “You said the system adjusts to the different persons, right? Why can’t it adjust to sort out that shit being useless?”

I shrugged. “Obviously, I have no clue. My guess is that it’s to make the cost of your negative quirk feel real. Like, your quirk is Tier 3. If your selection wasn’t reduced from taking a weird-ass quirk, you’d be a lot less impacted by it.”

“That’s exactly what I’m complaining about!” she said.” But okay, I guess. Tier 4. A lot of shit this time around. Only two real options this time. Luck of the Draw and Selective Force.

I sucked at my teeth. “Yeeeah… I probably wouldn’t.”

Steve nodded sadly.

She looked back and forth between the two of us and sighed. “All right. Hit me. What am I missing?”

“Looking at it, it’s pretty amazing. Especially with your quirk, right? And I would absolutely recommend it… if you were from Ademia. Cuilcis said that people in this place who didn’t get any help would usually only unlock ten to fifteen skills in their entire lifetime. But we have Starved Growth! How many skills do you have right now?”

She counted. “Eighteen.”

“Right. And you’ve been enslaved for a month! Meanwhile, I’ve been running free, and I’ve got thirty-four right now. Just think about it. Three months from now, what are the odds that you’ll activate that talent and actually hit a skill that’s useful?”

Victoria’s gaze was distant, clearly still caught up in her skill list. Her face scrunched up, looking like she’d had a drink of homemade slivovitz. “Hijole, I hate that you’re right. I’ll be more likely to just increase some lame-ass resistance skill or, or, knitting or something, than anything useful.” She exhaled and nodded. “I guess I’m saving up for Selective Power then.”

“Yeah, about that.” Steve began.

“Oh, come on!”

“I’m sorry. I really am.” Steve said. “I don’t mean to be that guy, and it really is an amazing talent.”

“But.” Her comment was as dry as watching a House of Lords session.

“But look at the choices. Now, if you were Liam, you could safely pick Magical Defense or Physical Defense and be happy as a clam.”

“You haven’t really made your choice yet, have you? As in, what you want to be. How you want to improve. Obviously, Magical Damage will work for your poison, and Magical Power for your buff and debuff. But which way are you going?”

Victoria’s eyes opened wide.

“My suggestion right now would be to save your points. When you’ve got three, you can make your choice between Selective Power or the stamina to mana talent. Or save them for something even better.”

She sighed, but nodded. “Okay. Point taken. I’m saving up for the future. Where’s a magical career councillor when you need ‘em?”

We moved on. Eventually, we did stumble on the reason for the lack of wildlife. Or, at least, so we assumed. A mile-wide hollow with a shallow pool of liquid that looked more like thin mud than actual water was ringed by capillaries on three of four sides. Nothing wrong with that scene, in theory. If it weren’t for all the bones. And the napkins.

Menial Servant

Level 21

Health 205/205

Path: Mental Power

They looked like napkins. They really did, crease at the center and all, as if they’d been folded at some point. Meter-wide napkins, nicely rectangular and flat. Except, the flattened whatever-they-were just lay flat on the ground with not a care in the world, as if that was normal. There wasn’t even any active camouflage or other attempts at blending into the background. The backdrop of at least a dozen skeletons of various species didn’t do anything to help the atmosphere.

“Menial Servant.” Victoria mused. “Servant to who?”

“Whom.” Steve said.

She waved him off. “You don’t get a servant unless you have a master. Where’s the master? Where’s the fucking master, Steve?”

I grunted. “I’m calling it. We’re not messing with this. Even if we had an idea what the damn things do, nearly two dozen of them, all above Level 20? That’s bad news. And the fact that they’re on a Mental Power path just convinces me even further. I’m not going anywhere near that. I’m sure if I go down there, they’ll fly right up and attach to me like flypaper or something, before they suck me dry of blood!”

Victoria placed both hands on her hips. “Is that what it’s come to? The King of Carnage, afraid of a bunch of wet blankies?”

“Are you volunteering to go down there?” I countered.

She held my gaze for all of five seconds. Then she burst into laughter. “Oh, shit no. There’s no doubt whatever those creatures down there are, they’re just waiting for a chance to go all Monster Party on us.” When I didn’t react, she added. “Not a good thing. Very not a good thing.”

Steve raised his hand. “I, for one, am all for leaving that death trap untriggered.”

Thus, we continued, enjoying the idea that, for once, we’d made the clever choice.
