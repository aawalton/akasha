Chapter 178 / B3-23: A Party of Three

“Trait sharing? Huh?” Margen pondered. “You know, Tintze is the only other bloke I know who had that ability, and he was a changeling.”

‘Tintze was a changeling?’ Yuk blurted as a surprised Joe mirrored those words out loud.

“Sure was. Some fey out of the Kingwald swapped him when he was a babe. Didn’t go well for him either. When it was discovered, his parents tossed the lad out onto the streets. They were a bunch a tight-arsed idiots who couldn’t stand that the smartest kid in their whole clan wasn’t blood kin. Fools, all of em. He went from urchin to merchant prince all on his own. And they never saw a copper penny of it.” The big man stopped and stared at Joe before adding, “You know … he also was somebody I could never assess,” Margen added, letting his voice trail off.

“Yes, sir,” Joe stated, picking up the question. “I’m a changeling too.”

“Thought so. Not to worry. Tinz was the best man I ever knew. He had that hint of fey in him. I sure ain’t going to hold it against you. Oh, and drop the sir.” Margen scratched his bearded cheek and thought. “Alright. Racial or honor trait. Well, against these deaders you're going to need strong mental resistance. I have [Indomitable Rage] I can share with you. The more pissed off I am, the higher my mental resistance gets.”

“I already have major resistance to {Fear}, {Confusion}, and control effects. I also have the [Stun Block] skill.”

“Well, I’ll be damned. At your level, three cooperative majors is quite a feat. No wonder you were able to keep your head around the deaders.” Margen stopped and considered before adding, “Well, if you want attributes, I’ve got [Lion Heart], [Titan’s Might], and [Champion of Steel]. Here, take a look.”

[Champion of Steel] The blade and being are one. +3 to Vigor. +30% to your primary combat skill.

[Lion Heart] Fierce on the inside. +3 to Vigor. Gain moderate {Disease} resistance.

[Titan’s Might] Move the Mountain +3 to Strength. +25% to your Strength efficiency.

Joe’s eyes flew open. [Titan’s Might] immediately appealed to him. It would up his actual Strength by 50% but, even better, his effective Strength would be double what it was now. If he tossed all of [Morphic Form] toward his muscles, his practical Strength would be almost as high as his Vigor.

His build, so far, had been focusing on Spirit and Vigor, making the other two very tempting, but he already had ways of dealing with disease, and he was sure to learn more. Additionally, he would see an equal boost to his claws from the Strength alone as he would from a bump to his weapon mastery skill.

The ability to break free if he was overrun by another wave of wights, to be as strong as Kendell, stronger than Tez, well, those were just too good to pass up.

He accepted the trait and felt an immediate change sweep through his body. In the past, when he bumped up his Vigor, he felt like he had become more solid, but this much Strength actually swelled his body. Joe could feel layers of muscles building onto his bones. It was a good thing his shirt had been shredded by the [Bug Bomb] because it suddenly would have been far too tight. The semi-intact seams around his biceps ripped apart.

As Joe reveled in his newfound brawn, he realized he had already been sensing the presence of one of the other traits, [Lion Heart]. There were times during Joe's ministrations of Margen, where the man’s body seemed to be helping him deal with the mummy rot. It was more than just a very high Vigor. Having passed on the trait, Joe knew he would have to make sure he never let the Erlking touch him.

“So. Now what? Do you have a plan, Joe?” the swordsman asked.

“Well, that depends. Can we get out of here through where the water comes in or goes out?”

“Your familiar might be able to, but we sure as Hells can’t. They’re just cracks in the rocks.”

‘That’s not a terrible idea,’ Joe sent to Yuk. ‘If you head out and tell the others, maybe Vexor could locate us.’

‘No! We’re not leaving you guys. Way too much chance we won’t be able to find our way back. Our sense of direction is as bad as the bugs that make us up … as bad as yours.’

‘What about the critters you left on the others? How close do you have to be to connect to them?’

‘Again, no. They’re dead. Once a creature becomes part of us, it stops being an independent thing. After it's part of us, it’d be like you losing hair or blood. The stuff is no longer alive in any real sense after it's away from your body. While we were close together, we could choose which bugs would contain our consciousness, but as soon as the other insects were separated from us, they croaked.’

‘Damn. Hold on. Margen is staring at us … me.’ Joe cleared his throat. “Sorry, I was testing something with Yuk. They are a new power for me. I don’t think I could maintain control over them at a long distance. Hold on, we’re going to try something else.”

“Do what you must,” Margen replied with a shrug, turning his attention back to his long-lost sword.

‘Oh, and just so you know, we can’t actually be in two extreme range spots at the same time,’ the living swarm conveyed. ‘You know what sucks? We were offered that skill a while ago. We just didn't think we’d ever need it. We’ve never really been a team player before. We were always simply the guy with unusual powers that Myllo foisted on other guilders. If we had to scout, we would have used all of us. [Parasitic Bond] is long range, and so it's always been good enough. Now we’re regretting it. If we had taken [Roving Roaches], we might have been able to send a part of us and keep the rest here. Sorry, Joe.”

‘No apologies. How would you know that this would come up?’

‘Kenda might have if she really knew what I was.’

‘Dude! Really, don’t sweat it.’

‘We can’t sweat. And what’s a ‘dude’?’

‘It means friend, mate, bud.’

‘Dude. Ooh. We like that!’ Joe heard echoes of ‘dude’ reverberate through their mental connection as his companion rolled the new word around in his mind.

‘Yuk. Focus. I’m tired and punchy, too. Either you have to go for help, or we have to try and find the path out and get Margen through it.’

‘That’s Margen, the Blademaster. If we find the way out, he will be the one clearing the way for us,’ Yuk sent emphatically.

‘I’m not so sure about that. I have seen what is going on inside him, the sickness that has been killing him over and over again. He has got to be in unimaginable pain. I’m not sure we can count on him being the hero of the ages.’

‘Pffff. You’ll see. And I’m staying with you two.’

‘Fine,’ Joe grumbled silently to his collective partner. “Well. It looks like we are walking then,” Joe stated to the founder. “How much of this place do you remember, Margen?”

“I know where a set of stairs are, but not where they go. The one time I tried them, I ran into a pack of the Erlking’s deaders. Didn’t have Dooms, and I was about halfway through a rot cycle. It was a bad battle. I knew I’d win, but it was tough to get them all. I worried that if one got away, the Lark might have sent more down here to try and track me down. If they found my cave, I’d be back in the fey lord’s clutches again,” the big man stated, grinding his grip on the sword’s hilt so forcefully Joe could hear the magically-preserved leather creaking.

The warrior sighed before adding ruefully, “Never tried it again. But now that I have Dooms back and you’re with me, I think it’s time we go take a look. What’d ya say?”

“Let’s do it,” Joe agreed.

As they climbed out of the stinking grotto, Joe was mentally drowning in the complete geek-out Yuk was pouring through their connection.

Margen left the small cave behind without a single backward glance. The hulking warrior led Joe and Yuk through a series of twisting, turning caverns with complete surety. It was pretty obvious the founder was very familiar with this stretch of terrain. In less than an hour, the trio arrived at the first bit of worked stone Joe had seen since fleeing the cavern.

A flight of stairs crept up an incline in a rough, open, cavernous space. These steps were not like the clean, dark blue, dressed stone of the ziggurat, where magic had obviously been used to shape and shade the rock. This work was more irregular, and the stone was unaltered in color from the natural cave floor and walls. They also were not cut in straight lines. The stairway meandered its way upwards, following the natural curves of the cleft they were ascending. Joe assumed some sort of {Earth} magic had been used to create the stairway, but whoever had done the work were almost surely not the architects of the pyramid.

“These look older,” Joe noted. “Are we even under the ziggurat anymore?”

“Not sure. But we’ll find plenty of deaders on these steps. I’ve never made it to the top ‘cause of the number of wandering wights I kept running into,” Margen stated. “I’ll take the lead.”

“Actually, Yuk should. They can sense movement, and the undead ignore them,” Joe countered.

“Good to know. Keep that light low. The wights will still see it. Let me know if your familiar hears anything.” Margen mounted the stairs a dozen steps behind the swarm. Joe brought up the rear. The staircase was cut straight through the rock, with a landing and a switch-back after about forty feet.

They zig-zagged up the long, irregular flights before they reached a semi-worked hallway. It was still not the blue stone of the Lord of Lark’s prison, but it felt like they were getting somewhere. Joe felt a small smile reach his lips, but Margen looked anything but happy. A grim scowl furrowed his brows. These halls clearly held dark memories for the warrior. Joe wasn't sure what to say, if anything, but before he could decide, Yuk issued a warning.

‘Lots of feet. Moving this way quickly. Somebody sure knows we’re here.’

“We have incoming, Margen,” Joe reiterated in a hushed voice, teleporting a rock from his bullet-bag into his left hand. His right one, he curled to be ready to cast or slash with.

Sure enough, a second later, roughly twenty wights swarmed out of the darkness. Joe had started charging [Grit Razor], but he never had a chance to release it. Margen fell on the feral ghouls like an avalanche. The huge soldier’s ferocity and might were stunning to behold. Joe and Yuk simply stood gawking as the swordman hewed through the undead. He drove DoomHerald through limbs, torsos, and skulls, effortlessly. He hacked away chunks of gray flesh and gouts of oily blood spattered against the walls. Joe found himself sprayed by droplets of necrotic fluids as he stood awestruck.

In seconds, it was over, but it only took Joe another fraction of a second to realize what that exhibition had cost them. Margen, whom Joe had brought to a reasonable facsimile of health, once again looked like death warmed over. His muscles had withered away. His skin was ashen, with new weeping sores splitting his skin everywhere visible.

Assessing the man, Joe noted Margen’s stamina was almost gone. Without that energy pool to power the man’s resistance to disease, the rot had reinvaded all the tissue Joe had cleansed.

“Son of a bitch!” Joe swore. He touched the fur and willed it to slide off his patient. It was as bad as he feared. The mummy rot was everywhere again. “Damn it! Ok, no more berserker rushes for you, sir. This is going to take me an hour or more to fix. If we try to do it your way, we’ll never make it out of here.”

“I lost my head there for a second,” the man wheezed ruefully. “Sorry, son, but it was worth it. Felt so good to swing Dooms against those bastards again. I will be more conservative from here on.”

“Hope so.” Joe helped Margen into a cross-legged sit and then lit a [Halefire] under him. ‘Yuk, make a wide scout, both ways. Let’s not have any more surprises.’

‘Got it. Is he going to be ok? He looks really bad again.’

‘Yeah. It’s gonna take time, though … and stink the hallway up to high heaven. Now that I know what I’m doing, I’m sure I can get him back to where I had him.’

‘But did you see him fight?! THAT WAS AWES...’

‘I saw it,” Joe cut in.  ‘Scout, Yuk. I really want to get moving as soon as we can.’

‘Grouch,’ the tatterdemalion harumphed as the swarm of insects slid into the darkness.

‘They’re not the one who has to stand in the rot-stink for another hour fixing something we JUST fixed,’ Joe thought to himself before wondering just how much of his thoughts Yuk had access to. Shrugging, Joe began the horribly nauseating task of pulling out as much of the relentless blight as he could from the great man.
