The blast of fear magic plunged Nora deeper into memories she’d spent years trying to bury. Not faded echoes this time, but vivid scenes that swallowed her whole, dragging her away from the cathedral’s chaos into the darkest corners of her past.

Cleft Hill Manor, seventeen years ago.

Rain lashed against leaded windows as Nora and Seryn stood over their father’s bed, both trembling with a mixture of terror and resolve. Mordecai Lancaster slept soundly, unaware of their presence. Unaware that his years of calculated cruelty had culminated in this desperate act.

The signet ring gleamed on his finger—the same ring he’d used to sign countless documents that had ruined lives. It was the same ring that had left its mark on both sisters’ faces more than once when his rage turned physical. The latest bruise still darkened Seryn’s cheekbone, and it was a purple testament to Mordecai’s displeasure at her questioning the choice of husband he’d arranged for her.

“I can’t,” Seryn whispered, her knife wavering in her slender hand. At nineteen, she was everything Nora wasn’t—gentle, refined, patient. The perfect nobleman’s daughter, except for the spark of defiance she kept hidden behind courtly smiles. “Nora, I can’t do it.”

Fifteen-year-old Nora tightened her grip on her own blade. “Then I will. For both of us.”

She raised the knife, moonlight from the window catching the metal. One thrust. That’s all it would take. One moment of courage to end years of torment.

Her father’s eyes opened.

“My daughters,” he said calmly, as if finding them looming over him with blades was expected. “Come to kill the monster at last?”

Nora’s arm froze mid-descent.

“Do it,” he urged, his voice oddly gentle. “You’ve wanted to for so long. Both of you have. I’ve seen it in your eyes. The hatred. The fear.”

He sat up slowly, making no move to defend himself. “But you won’t. Because deep down, you know the truth. You’re just like me.”

The door splintered behind them. Three of her father’s personal guards burst into the room, swords drawn.

“My lord!” The captain’s eyes widened at the scene.

“Ah, perfect timing,” Mordecai said, his voice shifting from calm to theatrical outrage. “Restrain them! My own daughters, attempting to murder me in my sleep!”

The guards hesitated for just a moment—long enough for Nora to see the flicker of doubt in their eyes. They had served House Lancaster long enough to know what kind of man Mordecai truly was. They’d seen him drag Nora by her hair across the dining hall for spilling wine. Heard him berate their mother until she wept. Watched him break a servant’s fingers for delivering news he didn’t like.

But duty prevailed over conscience. They rushed forward, disarming the sisters within seconds, twisting their wrists until pain forced them to release the hunting knives they’d requisitioned from the stables a few days ago. Nora fought like a wildcat, landing a solid kick to one guard’s groin before another pinned her arms behind her back. Beside her, Seryn offered no resistance, her face a mask of quiet despair.

“This confirms everything,” Mordecai declared, rising from his bed with calculated dignity. He projected his voice so that the other servants would overhear him, Nora would later realize.

Blood trickled from where Nora’s blade had nicked his chest in the struggle—a wound so minor it barely qualified as a scratch, yet he clutched at it as if mortally wounded. “The rumors about Seryn must be true. For what virtuous daughter would attempt to silence her father with murder?”

“What rumors?” Nora demanded, struggling against the guard’s grip. “What are you talking about?”

Mordecai circled the room, every inch the wounded patriarch. His eyes, cold as winter frost, gleamed with malicious satisfaction. She knew that look. She hated that look. It was the same one he’d worn when he’d ordered their dog drowned after it had barked during one of his headaches.

“Why, the rumors that your sister has been entertaining men in secret. Giving away what should be reserved for her betrothed. Lord Graffin deserves an unspoiled bride, not damaged goods.”

Seryn’s face drained of color. “That’s a lie!”

“Is it?” Mordecai’s lips curled into a sneer. “Lord Harwick’s son claims differently. As do several of his friends. They say you were quite... enthusiastic.”

Nora felt sick. Lord Harwick’s son—a spoiled, vicious boy who had cornered Seryn at the midsummer festival. The same boy Seryn had embarrassed by rejecting his advances publicly. The same boy who’d sworn she would regret humiliating him.

“They’re lying,” Nora spat. “And you know it.”

“What I know,” Mordecai said, retrieving a document from his desk, “is that your sister is no longer welcome in this house.”

Dread filled Nora’s chest. It made everything dark. Every breath was like trying to lift an anvil.

He’d been ready for them, she realized. He’d prepared for this.

Lord Mordecai pressed his signet ring into hot wax, sealing the document with the emblem of House Lancaster. The ring left its perfect imprint in the red wax—a raven wearing a crown over a silhouette of their city of Cleft Hill.

“By my authority as head of this house, Seryn Lancaster is hereby banished from Cleft Hill and stripped of her name and privileges. Let her find shelter with the sort who share her... proclivities.”

“You can’t do this,” Nora struggled harder, earning a painful twist of her arm from the guard. “Mother won’t allow it!”

Mordecai’s laugh was like broken glass. “Your mother is halfway across the kingdom attending her sister’s wedding. By the time she returns, this matter will be settled. Besides,” he added with a dismissive wave, “Velda has never had the spine to oppose me. She’ll weep prettily and accept it, just as she accepts everything.”

He nodded to the guards. “Take Seryn to the gates. Make sure she leaves with nothing but the clothes on her back.” His eyes raked over his elder daughter’s form with a contempt that made Nora’s skin crawl. “Though that nightdress is practically transparent. Appropriate attire for her new station, I suppose.”

He turned to the other guard. “As for Nora...” His eyes narrowed, a promise of future pain in their depths. “Lock her in her rooms. I’ll deal with her defiance in the morning. Perhaps a month without meals will teach her the obedience she lacks.”

“Father, please,” Seryn begged, her composure finally cracking. “Where am I supposed to go? It’s the middle of the night!”

“That’s hardly my concern.” Mordecai glanced at the window, where the first hints of false dawn were beginning to lighten the sky. “Though I hear the women who work this hour of the morning are always hiring. You should feel right at home with the other whores.”

The casual cruelty of the words struck Seryn like a physical blow. She staggered, would have fallen if not for the guard’s grip on her arm.

“You’ve always been a disappointment,” Mordecai continued, his voice taking on the quiet, venomous tone that both sisters had learned to fear more than his shouting. “Too soft. Too questioning. Too much like your mother. At least now you can be useful to someone.” He smiled, the expression never reaching his eyes. “I hear Lord Feldings pays well for fresh diversions for his son. Perhaps you can earn your keep that way.”

The guard dragged Seryn toward the door. She looked back once, her eyes meeting Nora’s with a silent promise: This isn’t over.

As they pulled her sister away, something cold and terrible settled in Nora’s chest. Not fear. Not grief. Something darker. Something with teeth.

They locked her in her room, the key turning with a final click that echoed like a death knell. Nora stood motionless in the center of her chamber, letting rage build until it threatened to consume her from within.

Then, with methodical focus, she began to gather what she needed. A sturdy cloak. Her hunting knife. A pouch of coins she’d hidden beneath a loose floorboard. The silver pendant her mother had given her on her twelfth birthday—the only time Velda had dared defy Mordecai by giving her daughter a gift he hadn’t approved.

The window was her only option. Three stories up, with nothing but decorative vines clinging to weathered stone. A deadly drop for most—but Nora had been climbing these walls since she was eight, sneaking out to watch the stars with Seryn on countless nights when sleep wouldn’t come.

The rain made the descent treacherous, stone slick beneath her fingers as she found each familiar handhold. She nearly fell twice, saving herself only by a desperate lunge for the next outcropping. By the time she reached the ground, her fingers were bleeding, and her clothes were soaked through.

She found Seryn half a mile from the manor gates, huddled beneath a willow tree, rain plastering her golden hair to her pale face. When she saw Nora, something between a sob and a laugh escaped her.

“I knew you’d come,” she whispered.

Nora pulled her sister to her feet, wrapping the spare cloak around her trembling shoulders. “As long as we’re together, we don’t ever need to be afraid.”

Seryn’s smile was fragile, but real. “Where will we go?”

“Anywhere,” Nora said fiercely. “Anywhere but here.”

They walked through the night, two shadows merging with the darkness, heading toward an uncertain future that, no matter how harsh, promised one thing their past never had: freedom.

The memory shifted, colors bleeding into one another like watercolors in rain.

One year later. The Whispering Ruins, Outer Territories.

“Seryn?” Nora called, her voice echoing through crumbling stone arches. The decrepit ruins had been their shelter for nearly three months now—far enough from civilization to avoid Mordecai’s searchers, yet close enough to a small village that they could trade for necessities.

Lately, though, Seryn had been distant. Changed. Her gentleness hardened into something cold and brittle. The Dreadcaller cult had found them two months ago, offering shelter and protection. Seryn had embraced their teachings with disturbing fervor, while Nora kept her distance.

Their argument that morning had been vicious—Nora accusing Seryn of losing herself, Seryn calling Nora naive for not seeing that power was their only protection in this cruel world.

Now, as sunset painted the ruins in shades of blood and gold, Nora searched for her sister, hoping to mend what had been broken between them.

The ruins were unnaturally silent. No sentries guarded the perimeter, no acolytes tended the communal fires. Just empty corridors and vacant chambers, dust motes dancing in slanting sunlight.

Then she heard it—a rhythmic chanting that raised the hair on her arms. Not the usual Dreadcaller prayers, but something older. Darker. The cadence was wrong, the syllables guttural and harsh against her ears.

Nora followed the sound, moving silently through familiar passageways that suddenly seemed alien and threatening. The chanting grew louder as she approached the central amphitheater. It was a half-collapsed structure that had once hosted public ceremonies centuries before. Now, it housed a darker purpose.

Roiling storm clouds gathered overhead. They were unnaturally swift, as if drawn by the ritual below. Nora crouched behind a fallen column, peering into the amphitheater with growing dread.

Twelve figures in black robes formed a perfect circle around an intricate seal carved into the stone floor. At equidistant points around them, thirteen black candles burned with flames that cast no light. In the center of the seal stood the High Priestess, her face hidden beneath an ornate mask fashioned to resemble a screaming face.

Nora scanned the circle desperately, searching for Seryn among the hooded figures. But her sister was nowhere to be seen. Fear clawed at her throat—not the ordinary fear she’d lived with all her life, but something primal and overwhelming.

The chanting reached a crescendo. From a darkened tunnel at the edge of the amphitheater, a figure emerged.

Seryn.

She wore a simple white dress that seemed to glow against the gathering darkness. Her golden hair hung loose around her shoulders, adorned with black flowers Nora didn’t recognize. Her face was serene. But that wasn’t what troubled her. It was her unfocused eyes as if she was walking in a dream.

“No,” Nora whispered, realization dawning with horrific clarity. “No, no, no.”

She followed the straight line her sister walked. There, in the center of all the esoteric runes written in a red liquid Nora didn’t want to know the origins of, a black altar awaited. It was just large enough for a grown woman to lie across.

The seal wasn’t for a summoning. It was for a sacrifice.

Seryn stepped into the center of the circle, directly onto the seal. The magic flared to life as she passed, some sort of dome-like ward crackling into existence. The High Priestess bowed to her with disturbing reverence before backing away to join the circle.

Terror seized Nora’s heart—not for herself, but for Seryn. Fear that had paralyzed her for years under her father’s rule now transformed into something else entirely. It bubbled up, lending strength to her limbs. It was a desperate, primal need to protect the only person who had ever truly loved her.

She moved without thinking, exploding from her hiding place with a feral cry that tore from her throat. The nearest cultist whirled, dagger flashing in the storm-light. Nora ducked under the blade, driving her shoulder into the woman’s sternum. Bones cracked. The cultist crumpled.

“Interloper!” Another cultist lunged at her, hands crackling with black energy.

Nora was barely sixteen, untrained, but terror for her sister made her dangerous. She rolled beneath the spell, swept the cultist’s legs, and kept moving. No time to fight. Only time to reach Seryn.

Two more blocked her path. One grabbed her hair, yanking her backward. Pain lanced through her scalp. Nora twisted and bit down hard on the cultist’s wrist. Blood flooded her mouth, the coppery tang nearly enough to make her vomit. But then the grip loosened. She tore free, leaving strands of hair in the cultist’s fingers. She spat the blood out, wiping at her lips with a gray sleeve.

The chanting never faltered, the dark ceremony continuing despite the interruption. Black energy began to coalesce above the seal, twisting like smoke into a vague suggestion of a face etched into obsidian.

“Seryn!” Nora screamed, her voice cutting through the chanting like a blade. “SERYN!”

For an instant, Seryn’s eyes focused, clarity returning as she recognized her sister. Horror replaced the serene acceptance on her face.

“Nora? No! You can’t be here! RUN!”

The seal beneath Seryn’s feet began to glow, black light rising from etched lines like ink bleeding in reverse. Tendrils of darkness wrapped around her ankles, crawling up her legs with hungry purpose.

Three cultists rushed Nora simultaneously. She ducked the first, took a glancing blow from the second that split her lip, and drove her knee into the third’s groin with desperate strength. The distance between her and Seryn seemed impossibly vast—ten yards of stone that might as well have been miles.

Blood dripped into her eyes from a cut on her forehead. She wiped it away with a trembling hand and charged forward again. A spell sizzled past her ear, close enough to singe her hair. Another cultist moved to intercept her, but Nora was beyond reason now, beyond fear for herself. She feinted left, dove right, and tumbled past the last defender.

The invisible barrier at the edge of the seal hit her like a wall of fire. Agony blazed across her skin as the ward rejected her intrusion. Nora screamed but didn’t stop. She pushed through, muscles trembling with effort, skin blistering wherever it contacted the magical barrier.

“I’m here,” she gasped, fighting through the pain as she grabbed Seryn’s arms. Blood trickled from her nose, her ears, but the worst was the singed line across her chest. It had been the final price of forcing her way through the ward. “See? We don’t have to be afraid as long as we’re together.”

With every ounce of strength remaining, she yanked Seryn toward the edge of the seal. The tendrils of darkness fought her, clinging to Seryn’s legs like hungry vines. Sweat poured down Nora’s face as she pulled harder, muscles screaming in protest.

They stumbled together toward the edge, Seryn half-dragging her sister as Nora’s strength began to fail. The barrier loomed before them, crackling with malevolent energy. Nora pushed Seryn ahead of her, using the last of her failing strength.

“Go!” she urged, shoving her sister through the barrier.

Seryn tumbled free, rolling across the stone outside the circle. Nora let out a breath. The ward was one-directional. She stepped forward, but slipped on her own blood. Seryn reached out a hand, her fingernails nearly scratching against the edge of the crackling energy.

A deafening crack resounded through the clearing, and Nora whirled to see the black altar tear down the center.

The black energy above her condensed into a single point of perfect darkness. Lightning struck the amphitheater with blinding force. The tendrils that had been focused on Seryn suddenly whipped toward Nora instead, wrapping around her legs just as she was about to cross the threshold to freedom.

They yanked her backward, her fingernails carving bloody furrows in the stone as she tried to resist. Nora’s feet left the ground, her body suspended in the center of the seal as darkness poured into her through every pore, every breath.

She couldn’t scream. Couldn’t move. Could only endure as something ancient and terrible flooded her being, rewriting her from within.

Then she was falling—not down but in, spiraling through endless darkness until she landed on what felt like solid ground. Opening her eyes, Nora found herself in a void so complete it seemed to swallow light itself. Before her stood a figure that defied description—a suggestion of antlers, of bone, of shadow given form.

“Child,” the entity spoke, its voice resonating not in her ears but in her mind. “Such unexpected bravery. Such delicious fear.”

“Who are you?” Nora demanded, backing away instinctively.

“I am Dread,” the figure said simply. “And you, little one, have taken what was not meant for you.”

Understanding dawned with sickening clarity. The ritual had been designed to channel this creature… this Dread’s… essence into Seryn—to make her a vessel for the god of fear himself.

Had she known? Or was this yet another deception from the Dreadcaller sisterhood?

“I can feel your fear,” Dread continued, circling her with predatory interest. “Fear for your sister. Fear of your father. Fear of the future. Fear of yourself. So much fear, little one, and yet you possess a remarkable strength despite it all.”

He extended what might have been a hand. “For your courage, I offer you a bargain. Accept what has begun. Become my vessel willingly, and I will give you power beyond imagining. Strength to protect your sister. Strength to ensure neither of you need ever fear again.”

Images flashed through Nora’s mind—herself returning to Cleft Hill, Mordecai cowering before her as she unleashed his worst fears upon him. Seryn safe at her side, untouchable. A future where they dictated their own fates, feared by any who would harm them.

“And your father,” Dread whispered, his voice honeyed with promised vengeance. “I can make him suffer as he made you suffer. Every nightmare, every terror, magnified a thousandfold. I can make things right, Nora. All you need to do is let me in.”

Nora hesitated, the temptation almost overwhelming. To never be afraid again. To have the power to protect Seryn, to punish those who deserved it.

Just as she opened her mouth to accept, blinding light tore through the darkness.

The vision shattered, sending Nora crashing back to physical reality. She hit the ground hard, the impact driving the breath from her lungs. Around her, chaos erupted as figures in silver and blue charged into the amphitheater—Fateweavers, their weapons gleaming with divine light.

“Kill them all!” a female voice commanded. “Justice for the deserving!”

“Justice for the deserving!” came the resounding echo from the dozens of paladins sweeping into the clearing.

Nora tried to move, to cry out, but her body refused to respond. The partial binding with Dread had left her paralyzed, darkness still coursing through her veins like poison.

Through blurred vision, she saw Seryn scrambling toward her, the same knife they’d brought to their father’s bedside clutched in her hand.

She set her feet, planting herself directly in front of the Fatweaver’s charge and Nora’s suspended form.

“Stay away from her!” Seryn screamed, at the advancing warriors.

Unlike that night in their father’s bedroom, Seryn’s hands didn’t tremble now. Her stance was solid, her eyes fierce with protective fury.

The Fateweaver who led the charge—a tall woman with steel-gray hair and a face carved from granite—assessed the situation with a single glance. “The ritual was interrupted,” she observed coolly. “The girl is corrupted but not fully claimed.”

“Please,” Seryn begged, desperation cracking her voice. “She’s innocent. She was trying to save me.”

“Step aside,” the Fateweaver commanded. “We’ll decide that ourselves, cultist.”

The paladin raised her greatsword with oceanic striations across its blade. It loomed high into the sky, suspended over Nora’s form.

Seryn’s response was to lunge, knife slashing toward the woman’s exposed throat. But she was no warrior, no trained fighter. Just a desperate sister with nothing left to lose.

The Fateweaver’s blade—Wavebreaker, though Nora wouldn’t learn its name until much later—moved with lethal exactness. Seryn never had a chance.

The knife fell from nerveless fingers as Seryn crumpled to the ground, blood spreading rapidly across the white dress that had been meant for her sacrifice. Her eyes, already dimming, found Nora’s one last time.

“I’m sorry,” she mouthed, before the light in them faded completely.

Something broke inside Nora then—something fundamental and irreparable. She screamed, the sound tearing from her throat with such force that several Fateweavers staggered back. Darkness exploded from her body in a concussive wave, bowling over cultists and Fateweavers alike.

Only the leader remained standing, Wavebreaker held before her like a shield. She approached Nora with careful steps, her expression neutral as she assessed the sobbing girl.

“I am Angela,” she said, her voice surprisingly gentle as she crouched beside a young Nora. “And this is not your end, child. Dread has touched you, but not claimed you. There is still hope.”

She placed a hand on Nora’s heaving shoulder. “Hey, as long as we’re here, there’s nothing to be afraid of.”

The same words Nora had spoken to Seryn so many times. The same promise she had failed to keep.

Darkness took her then, merciful unconsciousness sweeping her away from the sight of her sister’s lifeless body, from the reality of having lost everything she had ever loved.

The memory faded, leaving Nora gasping on the cathedral floor as Dread loomed above her, skeletal fingers still extended in offer.

“Now you remember,” he said, his voice like velvet sliding over broken glass. “All that pain. All that loss. And for what? So you could become a weapon for Fate instead of for me? Look at you now—abandoned again. Cast aside by the very Fateweavers who ‘saved’ you.”

Nora struggled to her knees, each breath a battle against the weight of memory. Around her, the battle continued—Cade desperately working to alter the ritual, her friends fighting for their lives, Evie still pinned beneath Augustus’s massive hand.

“They’re not your family,” Dread continued, his voice dropping to an intimate whisper. “They’re just convenient allies. When they no longer need you, they’ll discard you. Just like everyone else.”

He gestured toward Evie with a skeletal hand. “Even your precious ward. You think she loves you? She tolerates you. She Needs you. But love? No one has loved you since Seryn.”

“That’s not true,” Nora whispered, but doubt crept in with the memories, cold and insidious.

“I can give you what you’ve always wanted,” Dread purred. “Not just power. Belonging. Purpose. No more wondering if you’re worthy of love. No more fear that you’ll fail those who depend on you.” His form shifted, becoming more substantial, more tempting. “I can make you strong enough to protect them all. Strong enough that you never have to lose anyone again.”

The offer hung in the air between them, seductive in its simplicity. Nora looked toward her friends—Cade working frantically at the ritual circle, Elena and Evie fighting desperately against Augustus, Gavin pursuing a fleeing Rufus into the shadows.

“They will all die,” Dread continued, his voice almost sorrowful. “Just as your sister died. Unless you accept what you are. Unless you accept ME.”

He leaned closer, his antlered crown scraping the cathedral ceiling. “One word, Nora. Just one word, and you’ll never be powerless again. Never watch someone you love die while you stand helpless. Isn’t that worth any price?”

Nora closed her eyes, feeling the pull of his words. With Dread’s power fully embraced, she could save her friends. She’d be able to unleash terrors that would send Bernard and his minions fleeing in a mindless panic. The worst part was that she could already feel that power—his power—gathering at her fingertips. He could follow through on this promise.

But at what cost?

An image of Seryn rose in her mind—not the broken body on the amphitheater floor, but her sister as she had been before. Gentle. Kind. Determined. The person Nora had loved more than anyone in the world.

What would she want for me?

Seryn’s voice seemed to whisper in her mind: “He’s lying, Nora. You’ve never been powerless. Never.”

And suddenly, with crystal clarity, Nora understood. Dread wasn’t offering freedom from fear. He was offering another cage—more comfortable, perhaps, but a cage nonetheless. He wanted her to believe that fear defined her, that she was nothing without it.

But that had never been true.

“You know,” Nora said, her voice quiet but steady, “for someone who feeds on fear, you don’t understand it very well.”

Dread’s form rippled with surprise. “What?”

“Fear isn’t weakness,” Nora continued, struggling to her feet. “It never was. Fear is what made me climb out of a third-story window to find my sister. Fear is what drove me through that barrier to save her. Fear is why I’m standing here right now, refusing to let my friends die.”

She took a step forward, and for the first time, Dread retreated a fraction.

“You’re right about one thing,” she admitted. “I am afraid. Terrified, actually. But that doesn’t make me yours. It makes me human.”

Golden light began to gather around her clenched fists—her own magic, not the borrowed power of fear.

“Foolish child,” Dread snarled, drawing himself up to his full, terrifying height. “Your humanity is your weakness. Your fear has shaped every moment of your existence. You cannot simply reject it.”

“I’m not rejecting it,” Nora replied, the golden light intensifying, flickering with hints of silver. “I’m embracing it. Fear kept me alive in my father’s house. Fear drove me to save Seryn. Fear brought me here, to these people who need me.”

She gestured to her friends, still fighting desperately throughout the cathedral. “They’re not just allies. They’re my family. The one I chose. The one I fight for.”

“They will abandon you! They will die!” Dread hissed, his certainty wavering. “Just as everyone else has that you’ve cared about! And what about when this is all over? What then? When they no longer need your sword—”

“Even if they did,” Nora cut him off, “I’d still choose them. Because that’s what family means. It’s not about what they can give me. It’s about what we build together.”

The golden-silver light spread from her hands, flowing up her arms, across her shoulders. Nora felt something shift within her—a barrier breaking, a wound finally beginning to heal.

“I am Nora Lancaster,” she declared, her voice ringing with newfound strength. “Daughter of Velda. Sister to Seryn in life and in death. Guardian of Evie. Friend to Cade, to Orro, to all who stand with me.” Her voice softened. “I am afraid. I am flawed. I am human. And that’s enough.”

Radiant black and gold energy started to spill forth from Nora’s broken and battered form. It was like a watery cocoon, and yet it felt oddly comforting as it encompassed her there in the darkness.

From within the darkness, she saw a face. It was murky and distant, yet undeniably feminine. At first, she thought it was Seryn, but the shape of it was wrong. Too smooth. Too elvish in nature. It drew closer, and Nora gasped. It was the nymph from what felt like an eternity ago.

“You draw close to the end, oathbreaker. Stay strong. We will aid you, as you will aid us. Look for us, dearheart. Look for us when the dawn of fire comes. All of those you’ve helped awaken are gathering what strength they’ve forgotten,” the nymph spoke quietly, yet in a rush.

Nora was too overwhelmed to respond.

The nymph started to fade away into the shadows of the watery energy around her, and it was as if time snapped back into motion.

The god before her sneered and raised his hand. Dozens of black spears lanced at the barrier, but snapped as soon as they struck.

Dread recoiled as if struck, his form wavering as the ritual’s pull intensified, Cade’s alterations beginning to take effect.

“You will regret this defiance,” he spat, venom replacing the seductive purr. “When all you love lies broken before you, remember that I offered you salvation. Remember that you CHOSE this pain.”

“No,” Nora said, tears streaming freely down her face even as she smiled. “I chose freedom. I chose family. I chose life with all its pain and all its joy.” She shook her head. “And honestly? Your sales pitch needs work.”

“YOU DARE—”

“Oh, shut UP,” Nora snarled.

She lunged forward, intertwined gold and black light blazing around her fist as she delivered a haymaker directly to Dread’s obsidian face. The impact sent shockwaves through her vision, fracturing the nightmare landscape around her.

Dread’s hold on her mind shattered like glass. The fear-induced memories dissolved, leaving Nora gasping on the cathedral floor, back in the present moment. Dread remained partially materialized through the fissure, his skeletal form now wavering as Cade’s ritual modifications began to take effect.

The god of fear howled in rage as the ritual’s reversed flow began to pull on him instead, dragging him back toward the spirit realm. His skeletal hands clawed at the edges of reality, trying to maintain his hold.

“This isn’t over, daughter,” he snarled as his form began to dissolve, drawn into the ritual’s hungry maw. “We will finish this conversation soon. Very soon.”

Nora stood on trembling legs, her fist still glowing with power she hadn’t known she possessed. A power not granted by gods or rituals, but forged in the crucible of her own pain and resilience.

For the first time since Seryn died, she felt truly herself—neither defined by fear nor running from it, but standing firm in spite of it.

Around her, the battle raged on. Origami soldiers clashed with skeletal abominations, while her friends fought for their lives against Bernard’s corrupted allies. At the center of it all, Bernard stood frozen in shock as his carefully crafted ritual turned against him.

Nora took a deep breath, picked up her fallen greatsword, and stepped back into the fight. There would be time for grief later. Time for healing. Right now, her family needed her.

And this time, she wouldn’t fail them.
