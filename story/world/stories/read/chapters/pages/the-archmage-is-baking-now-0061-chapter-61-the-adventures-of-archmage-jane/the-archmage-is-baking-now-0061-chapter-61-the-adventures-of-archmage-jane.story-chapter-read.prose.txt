Jane’s bath routine had grown over time. She stood beside her tub, staring at a table filled with a dozen different soaps, shampoos, and lotions, and wondered where to start.

Thanks to the gift of traveling in a carriage, she was not nearly as filthy as she normally was after a journey, which made the first part easier. She scrubbed herself spotless before draining and refilling her bathwater. Then she soaked for a long time, thinking of nothing at all. Eventually, she turned to the wonderful products that would calm her wild hair, soften her skin, and let her sleep in perfect, blissful comfort.

About halfway through that, a realization came to her. She wouldn’t be able to bake the next day, at least not in the sense of reopening her shop and seeing all her customers again. There wasn’t so much as a single egg in the house, and several other ingredients had been running low when she left to solve the world’s problems. Tomorrow, she could put in an order. But she would still have a day off, whether she wanted one or not, and little to do in it.

She decided not to worry about that. Trying to plan a day off was a fool’s errand, she had learned. She had tried it many times, and some unexpected event almost always occurred to change her plans.

Life in Glenfall was slowly teaching Jane how to handle free time. Tomorrow would be another opportunity to practice the trick of just getting up, determining what she felt like in the moment, and then doing that.

When she finally slipped into bed, it was in perfectly laundered sleeping clothes and in between perfectly clean sheets. It only took her a moment to fall asleep.

She slept like a log all the way through until dawn.

—

Jane awoke slowly, drifting up from sleep in stages rather than all at once. It was truly the best way to wake up, and a rarity for her. Usually, the morning light woke her up right away. Today, it was a gradual, gentle process. She allowed her body to take as long as it wanted to. Even when she woke up in earnest, she lay there for a while, savoring the sensation of being in her own bed, in her own room, and at home in her own house.

Eventually, though, her stomach reminded her that she hadn't eaten since dinner with Allen the night before. She got dressed in comfortable around-town clothing, then added a light jacket that was just enough to keep the mild chill at bay. Her in-town walking shoes were waiting by the door where she had left them before her trip. They were broken in perfectly, molded to the shape of her feet after months of use. There was nothing quite like them, and she blessed the simple cobbler who had made them for her..

Her first thought was to head to Bella's stand for breakfast, but a short walk revealed that Bella hadn’t been able to reopen her business yet, either. Bella herself was also nowhere to be seen, which meant Jane was still on her own as she moved about her morning. She decided to find breakfast elsewhere, and had a good idea where.

There was a small café several minutes from her house, one she had discovered a few months back during one of her morning walks. It was nothing fancy, just a handful of tables and a counter where the owner served simple food to early risers. The main attraction was the view. The café sat overlooking the lake, and from the right table, you could see the whole expanse of water spread out below.

Jane walked there at her slowest, most relaxed pace. A few other early risers were out and about, and she exchanged nods with some of the people she recognized from her regular trips to the market. Nobody stopped her to talk, which she appreciated. She wasn't quite awake enough for conversation yet.

The café was almost empty when she arrived. An older man sat at one of the tables near the back, reading something and mostly ignoring a cup of steaming tea. The owner looked up as Jane entered and smiled.

"Morning, Miss Jane. The usual?"

"Please." Jane took her favorite table, the one by the window with the best view of the lake. "And some drink to wake me up, if you have it."

"Always."

The food arrived a few minutes later, a simple yet exquisite meal of poached eggs with toast and homemade jam. Jane ate slowly and sipped her favorite chocolate-y stimulant drink, watching the lake through the window. The water was filling up with fishing craft already, each one ready to draw from the lake’s endless supply of swimming things to feed the town.

She thought about Allen as she ate, wondering what he was doing this early. Her best guess was that he was working already. He was always up before dawn to get a head start on whatever project he was currently involved in. She would find him later, she decided, to avoid the risk of interrupting him. There was all the time in the world to see him more. She suspected he would find her after his workday was over, anyway.

What does that leave for me to do today?

After a bit of thought, she decided to visit the library.

It had been a long time since she had been there. Months, probably. Her early days in Glenfall had prompted several visits to the library, and she had kept up the habit for a good while. But as she’d settled into her new life, the library had faded from her routine somehow. That felt wrong in a way she would be delighted to correct.

Jane finished her breakfast, left some coins on the table, and headed out.

After a long but leisurely stroll, the library came into view. It wasn't particularly large compared to the mammoth library in the capital, but it was well-maintained, with clean windows and a neatly kept garden out front. The heavy wooden door was worn smooth from being pushed open by generations of hands.

Inside, it was the smell that hit her first. There was something about libraries that always made her feel safe. Maybe it was the quiet, maybe it was the books themselves, but Jane suspected it was mostly the mere smell of paper.

She wandered through the stacks for a while, not looking for anything in particular. Her fingers trailed along the spines of books as she passed. She was perusing titles in a remote back corner when a familiar voice made her jump.

"Jane? Is that you?"

Jane turned and found her friend Emily standing at the end of the row, looking surprised.

"Emily! Hello." Jane greeted the librarian with a smile. "It's been a while."

"It has!" Emily came closer, her eyes bright with curiosity. "I didn’t expect to see you back so soon. I heard you went off to fix some town's water problem. I thought you'd be gone for weeks to come."

Jane shrugged. "The work got done a little faster than I thought it would. We were lucky. The problem turned out to be more straightforward than it could have been."

"Well, that's good news." Emily leaned against the bookshelf, settling in for a conversation. "You have to tell me everything. I've been dying to hear about it."

So Jane told her, starting at the beginning and giving her a full dose of details. The librarian soaked up all the information like a sponge. She was truly one of the few people Jane knew who could understand almost all of the story, and the only person besides Cecelia with whom Jane could use fairly technical language when discussing her magic.

Emily’s expression shifted from interest to concern to amazement as the tale unfolded. When Jane finally finished, she let out a low whistle.

"That's a lot," the librarian said. "You've been busy."

"It didn't feel busy at the time. It felt like a lot of waiting and worrying, mostly."

"Well, either way, I'll have to add all of this to my book. The chapter on your water adventures is getting quite long."

Jane felt her cheeks warm "Your book. Right."

Emily was writing a book, she claimed. Jane was never entirely sure if it was real or just a complex form of librarian-type teasing. Supposedly, it was called The Adventures of Archmage Jane and chronicled everything Jane had done since arriving in Glenfall.

Jane hoped desperately that it was fake. The thought of her stumbling, uncertain efforts being immortalized in print made her want to die. She had made so many mistakes. It was mortifying to imagine even one person reading about them, let alone many future generations of Glenfall residents.

But she was grateful enough to Emily that she would allow it, either way. Emily had been a good friend since Jane arrived in town. She had been one of the first people to treat Jane like a normal person when Jane still wasn’t sure if that was possible. The librarian had also provided the cookbook that actually taught Jane how to bake.

The memory of Felicity Cast’s scientifically structured baking tome sparked a new thought in Jane’s mind. Though she hadn’t ever considered it before, the idea immediately moved to the top of her priorities.

"Emily," she said slowly, "my cookbook. The one you gave me when I first started learning."

"The scientific one? What about it?"

"It's signed." Jane had noticed that months ago, when she first started using it. A small signature on the title page, faded with age but still legible. "By the author. Felicity Cast herself.”

Emily nodded. "Oh, yes, I remember. I had to shelve it when it first came in, you know. The signature was one of the reasons we never got rid of it, even though nobody seemed interested in borrowing it. Signed copies are sometimes the only copies of a book in existence, so we try to give them special privilege."

"I figured." Jane hesitated, not sure how to phrase what she wanted to ask. "The thing is, I would like to know more about her. That book taught me everything I know about baking. The way it's written was exactly what I needed. I’ve never found another cookbook like it. She’s brilliant."

"I don't think she ever wrote anything else,” Emily said. “At least, nothing that I know of. I've never seen another book with her name on it."

"Is there any way to find out more? Who she was, where she lived, anything like that?"

"Maybe.” Emily thought for a moment. “It would take some time. The book is old, and the author wasn't famous. There might be records somewhere, or mentions in other sources. Something I could track down.”

"I feel bad asking," Jane said. "It's not urgent or anything. I just wondered."

Emily shook her head with a grin. "Don't feel bad. Honestly, it sounds like an interesting project. This would be actual detective work. Tracking down information, following leads. It’ll be fun!"

"Are you sure?"

"Positive. Leave it with me. I'll see what I can dig up. No promises on how long it'll take, but I'll let you know as soon as I find anything."

"Thank you, Emily." Jane meant it. "I really appreciate it."

"Don't mention it." Emily glanced toward the front of the library, where the sound of the door opening signaled that other patrons were arriving. "I should get back to work. But come visit more often, all right? I miss seeing you around here."

Jane smiled. "I will. I promise."

Emily disappeared toward the front desk, leaving Jane alone among the stacks.

She stood there for a moment, thinking about the cookbook and its mysterious author: the woman who had somehow known exactly how to explain baking in a way that made sense to someone like Jane. Then, shaking her head, Jane started making her way toward the exit.

She had a whole day ahead of her still, and plenty of ways to fill it.
