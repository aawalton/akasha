As Logan entered the guild hall, he sensed Nugget over the bond. The dragon was letting him know he was doing fine. Bromlin had found an entire bag of pickled newt’s feet and given it to the dragon.

That made Logan happy, but he would be happier once Nugget got to hang out in the open air. Which was another reason the upcoming quest excited him.

To his surprise, he wasn’t the only person from his team in the guildhall. Standing in front of the pillared busts was Cassandra.

But Agnes, the receptionist, noticed Logan first.

“Ah, Logan,” said Agnes from her desk. “Horace is expecting you. Just a moment and I’ll go get him.”

“Thanks.” Logan made his way over to Cassandra. She smiled at him distractedly. “Everything okay?”

“Hm? Oh, yes.” Her eyes drifted back to the statues. Specifically, to her brother’s. There was a faint red smudge at the corner, but Logan couldn’t tell from what.

Logan pursed his lips. “Well, I’m here if you need someone to listen.”

She nodded distractedly, then lifted her fingers to wipe part of the smudge off. Some instrumental music drifted from the lofty heights of the guildhall’s atrium. When Cassandra finally spoke, the song had cycled through a dramatic series of scale changes before returning to a familiar bridge.

“Do you see those cleaning crystals up there?” She lifted her head, indicating some light green glowing crystal spheres. “Those are supposed to tidy anything that’s out of place.” She reached into her inventory and pulled out a shadeshrub stem with a bit of dirt still clinging to its roots. She shook it lightly until loose dirt fell.

The nearest crystal brightened and drifted down from its position on the wall. It sucked the dirt up, glowed with a staccato pulse, and suddenly the ground where the dirt had fallen was now polished.

Finished, the magical little vacuum returned to the wall.

“That’s neat,” said Logan. “Do they make any that fix leaking pipes? I could have used one back home.”

Cassandra smiled, but she looked sad. “Yesterday, somebody spilled red paint all over the place. Nobody knows who. But the coloring stained, and the crystals spent over an hour cleaning up. They’re programmed to take care of anything out of place.”

“Clearly not enough. I saw you just remove some of that residue.”

She nodded and faced Logan. “That’s what has me worried. If the cleaning bots didn’t remove that, I think someone programmed them to ignore the stain.”

Logan wasn’t sure why someone would go through the trouble, but he didn’t rush her. She looked worried. When the overhead song changed to a different tune with a marching beat, he reached out to grab her hand, giving it a gentle squeeze.

“What if they were marking him, Logan?”

Logan raised a brow and was about to ask who him was, then noticed just whose statue was affected. Her brother’s.

“A mark? You don’t mean like an…” He dragged a finger across his neck.

Her silent stare meant that was exactly what she thought.

Chills prickled Logan’s spine. “Why would they do that here in such a public place?” Right after asking it, he answered his own question. “You think it’s a statement against your father elevating Stetson’s station so quickly.”

Her lower lip trembled as she nodded.

Logan let out a deep breath and squeezed her hand tighter. “We’ll figure something out.”

Her small laugh showed she appreciated the comment, but doubted it was possible. And she wasn’t entirely wrong. How was Logan supposed to help figure out how to keep her brother out of political assassination attempts? He had just kind of offered it.

Cassandra brought his attention back to the present as she returned his hand squeeze.

Congratulations! Another has accepted your quest.

Did I just extend that quest to her, too?

Logan gave Cassandra an encouraging smile, but now he was getting worried about how to protect Stetson. He came to the guildhall to accept a quest that would take him out of the city. But Stetson would remaining in Caerwyn. He didn’t know how quickly the threat-givers would act on their plot against Stetson. He suspected he would have to rely on a lot of communication over chat, using Mishki as an intermediary to connect with Cassandra.

Agnes returned meanwhile with Horace slithering behind her, plus Jorek and Elias right behind. Logan was only expecting to meet with Horace, though.

Then again, Jorek and Elias weren’t paying Logan any attention. They were headed toward Cassandra, who straightened her posture.

Right. She was already waiting here. She probably wanted to tell them about the plot she suspected.

Horace extended his hands wide in a welcoming gesture. “If it isn’t my prized pupil. I can’t tell you how happy I was to hear you accepted the quessst to take on the quilled brillybills. It will be a splendid opportunity to advance your Poison Resistance skill, too.”

“Oh? Well, good thing you already helped me earn the skill.”

Horace gave Logan a questioning look. “The way you say that makes me think you didn’t realize quilled brillybills have a rather toxic venom.”

Logan gave a sheepish grin.

The serpentine narrowed his eyes on Logan. “Have you even seen a quilled brillybill before?”

When Logan slowly shook his head, Horace frowned and glanced over at Elias, who had stepped off to the side with Cassandra and Jorek. “Perhaps I should speak with the first reconjurant about reassigning this case, then.”

Logan couldn’t let that happen. He had raised Nugget’s hopes too high about soaring around outside. “Wait. I can see if anyone from my team wants to join.” He hoped he could persuade at least one teammate.

“Hm. Perhapsss.” The serpentine tapped his fist against his marginally exposed scaly chest and cleared his throat. “Firssst Reconjurant? Might I have a word?”

Elias turned away from Cassandra, clearly annoyed by whatever they had been discussing, although Jorek tapped his lips thoughtfully. Cassandra looked like she was frustrated by their conversation.

Her father probably doesn’t believe her. Jorek seems on the fence, though.

“What is it, Horace? I assume you don’t have an outlandish claim to present me? It seems so many others want to waste my time lately.”

Horace’s eyes flicked to Cassandra quicker than his tongue flicked from his mouth. He raised both his hands, palms forward, in a calming gesture. “I’m sure your daughter gets her strong will from you.”

The statement could have been cutting, but the way Horace said it, he clearly meant it as a compliment. Even Logan, for some reason, felt bolstered by the words, and he realized there was probably some bardic charm at play. If he hadn’t suddenly felt so relaxed, he would have been tense about Elias detecting the serpentine’s hidden charm.

“Hmm. I suppose I cannot fault her for inheriting my traits. What do you need, Horace?”

Logan blinked. That actually worked? If Horace had just used a charming skill, the battle bard must be pretty strong with it to have it work on the first reconjurant.

“My pupil has undertaken the quilled brillybill quest, yet he does not have experience dealing with the birds.”

Logan felt suddenly foolish. He hadn’t even realized it was a bird.

Horace continued. “I know it’s a high-profile quest with the temple concerned as well, but I also believe it will be good for his training. I would like to request an accommodation so that someone with more experience can join him. But that would require increasing the quest rewards.”

“Is that all? Approved.” His eyes lit and a wide smile stretched across his face. “In fact, I believe my daughter would be well-suited for the task. If you’re worried about the boy taking damage, I’m sure he would benefit from having such a talented healer with him.”

“What? Father, no. If nobody else is going to watch out for Stetson, then I am.” She planted an emphatic foot. “I’m staying within Caerwyn’s walls.”

Elias’s cheeks puffed out as he exhaled and rolled his eyes to the ceiling. “For the last time, your brother is safe. Stop being so hysterical.”

Logan’s neck hairs bristled.

Cassandra’s fist shook at her side, but her voice remained steady. “Why won’t you even consider it? He’s not just my brother. He’s also your son. If someone is targeting him, you yourself could be next!”

“I said, leave it be, woman!”

Logan’s jaw dropped. Without thinking, he interrupted. “Why don’t we have Stetson join us?”

Elias turned in confusion to face Logan, apparently having forgotten he was still there. Even Cassandra appeared surprised.

Dammit. Why did I just suggest that? I wanted this to be a trip where Nugget could just relax and be free. But seeing Elias battering Cassandra down over her concerns for her brother’s safety had just brought the words out. Nugget would understand, so Logan leaned into it.

“You said this is a high-profile case with the temple too, right? Wouldn’t this be a great job for your son to take on?”

Cassandra’s brows furrowed as she heard Logan’s suggestion, but then she seemed to warm to the idea.

While Elias gave Logan an incredulous and-who-do-you-think-you-are look, Cassandra seized the opening. “Yes, that would be a wonderful idea.”

“No. My son is busy with other tasks. You will take Cassandra and Cassandra alone.”

Cassandra raised herself tall. “Nobody is taking me anywhere. If Stetson does not go, neither will I.”

Elias’s eyes narrowed spitefully. “Then I will just send a different healer!”?

Jorek, who had stayed silent until now, cleared his throat. “Excuse me, first reconjurant, but if you’ll allow me to make a suggestion”

Elias inhaled deeply, but stayed silent as he turned to Jorek and raised an eyebrow.

“Perhaps,” said the chief inspector, “you should send both of your children. If the brillybill outbreak has anything to do with the blight, there is nobody in the guild I would trust more than your daughter to handle the healing. You’ve seen how good a job she does with stabilizing Krett. And this presents a wonderful learning opportunity for your son out in the field, too. He can’t learn how to lead if he’s always stuck behind a desk.” He coughed and looked apologetic in advance. “Others in the guild might even think of you as foolish to pass on the opportunity of sending both your children on such a prestigious assignment.”

Elias’s chest swelled with a slow inhale. The idea of being a fool clearly didn’t sit well with him, but neither did giving in to his daughter’s demands.

“Fine,” he finally said. “Send them both.” Then he turned and left the room.

Cassandra took a deep breath and faced Jorek. “Thank you, Chief Inspector Hane.”

Jorek nodded.

Logan respected the man for standing up for Cassandra, too, but by the look on his face, the chief inspector still had something else to say. After Horace slithered off, Jorek beckoned Logan and Cassandra to walk with him.

“I think you’re right, Miss Varesh,” said Jorek when they were out of earshot from anyone else. He agreed with Cassandra about the strangeness of the cleaning devices not detecting the stains and the possibility of an inside attempt on Stetson.

He glanced over his shoulder and lowered his voice. “If it keeps Stetson out of their reach longer, take your time investigating the brillybills until tensions die down. I understand your former mentor, Grissom, also works in the same branch as your father and brother. Or at least he did. He was recently removed from his position after the other day’s events, and he may have a grudge against both you and Stetson right now.”

Cassandra nodded slowly, and Logan sucked on his teeth. Would Grissom really be upset enough to stage an assassination? He supposed people murdered others for a lot less than just embarrassment or political gain. And Grissom had both motives.

“Thank you, Chief Inspector,” Cassandra said. “We will stay alert while we’re outside the city walls.”

Jorek nodded. “A wise decision. I will monitor Grissom from inside the walls too.” They turned a corner where they would typically split paths, but Jorek lingered. “Do you have any updates on our other quest in progress? With the mages’s guild?”

“Actually, yes,” said Logan. He explained that there might be more clues back near the original blight source where the blast had gone off. He wasn’t about to mention the unleashed curse in the Ruined Wilds, but Jorek had been investigating the blight from the beginning and might have more information. Any little piece of info could help Logan figure out what he was going up against.

Jorek’s eyes squinted as he considered some possibilities.

“This ought to work out well, then. I’ll share a report with you regarding the details of where my blight investigation led me, and where I believe it is strongest. It’s quite a distance away, though. Back towards Gnashridge Heights, in fact. But honestly, I wouldn’t be surprised if your brillybill investigation leads you in that direction, too. If I’m right, the creatures will get even stronger the closer they are to the blight’s source.”

Cassandra grunted. “My father won’t be happy if we take Stetson too far.”

The chief inspector waved his hand and winked. “Well, fortunately you have the chief inspector on your side.”
