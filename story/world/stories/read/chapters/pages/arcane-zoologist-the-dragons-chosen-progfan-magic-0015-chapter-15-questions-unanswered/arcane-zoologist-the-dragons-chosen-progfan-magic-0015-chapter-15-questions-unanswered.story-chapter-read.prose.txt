"Thank you, thank you!" Callie said to Snowglow. "I wouldn't have been able to do this without you. I… I need to get these to my mom, but can we meet again later?"

Yes. Callie friend.

"Stay safe!" Callie waved at the glimmerhare and ran to where she had last seen her brother.

"Callie!" She heard Dad calling.

"I'm here!" She responded, not hiding anymore.

Before long, she was reunited with her brother, who looked relieved, and her father, who looked red from anger.

"Callie, where have you—"

"I found the dandilynx flower!" Callie hurriedly announced, opening her pouch and extending it towards them.

Dad's face instantly changed to one of immense relief.

"I… Callen, run ahead and get these to Granny Ethel at once! We'll follow after," Dad said.

Callie handed the pouch to her brother, who instantly ran off, disappearing from sight within a few minutes. This was the best decision. Callen was by far the fastest one in the family, right after Mom. If anyone could get there quickly enough, it was him.

But that also meant that Callie was left alone with Dad. She looked up nervously at him, expecting him to get mad again, since they were out of immediate danger. However, instead, she saw a lone tear roll down his cheek.

"Dad?!" Callie exclaimed. She had never seen her Dad cry. Ever.

He hurriedly wiped at the corner of his eyes, then crouched down to be on the eye level with Callie. "What you did today—I'm not even sure where you went—was extremely reckless! I was afraid that in my haste to search for the flower, you had gotten lost in the woods again. But instead, you brought back the flower that will save Maeve's arm. Thank you!"

Then he pulled Callie into a hug. Callie just stood awkwardly for a moment until she dared to put her arms around Dad. He smelled heavily of iron and ash and also a bit like dirt. But it was a comforting smell.

"Does this mean you're not mad at me?" Callie said, enjoying the hug.

Dad released her and straightened, looking at her with the same strict gaze as always. Almost the same. His eyes appeared a little bit brighter.

"I am mad, and I have many questions for you as to where you disappeared when I was searching for you for hours! But that comes later. For now, let's go see your mom," Dad said, taking her hand.

Callie winced. It appeared that she had a long scolding ahead of her. Again. Nonetheless, she couldn't help but smile. She had managed to grow the flower Mom needed. Now she just had to trust in Granny Ethel's skills.

When Callie and Dad had made it to the clinic, Granny Ethel had already finished making the medicine. Callen was standing behind Mom, holding her unconscious form in a sitting position.

"Anything we can help with, Ethel?" Dad asked as they entered.

"You're right on time! I was about to administer the antidote, but I need to wake her for a moment. Rowan, help Callen hold her up. Grab her head in case she tries to dodge me. I'm going to give her a sniff of crysara steeped in alcohol," Granny Ethel warned.

Everyone scrunched up their noses. The pungent concoction was so strong that it could bring a dead person back to life. That was largely how it was used, given to unconscious patients to wake them up for long enough to give them medicine.

"Bleh!" Callie exclaimed, pinching her nose shut.

Callen turned his head away as far as he could, and even Dad looked like he wanted to get away as Granny Ethel opened a tiny bottle containing the deadly concoction. The odor instantly permeated the whole hut. Callie felt like she could taste the smell on her tongue as she breathed through her nose.

Mom's eyes snapped open the moment the bottle was brought to her nose.

"What?" She exclaimed weakly, but the disgust was apparent on her face.

"Drink this, dear. It'll heal you," Granny Ethel sealed the bottle with crysara leaves and instead offered her a small cup.

Mom looked barely conscious and bleary-eyed, but noticing Callen, Dad, and Callie nearby, she relaxed and drank the medicine in one gulp. After that, she slumped down and fell back into unconsciousness.

"W-Will she be alright?" Callen asked nervously.

"I believe so. The ingredients Callie brought were of excellent quality. I've never seen mireling slime so clean and pure, and the dandilynx flowers were in prime bloom. Admittedly, each person is different, and I cannot guarantee how well Mauve will take to the antidote. We'll see the results tomorrow morning," Granny Ethel said.

Callie slumped a little, as did Dad and Callen. But Granny Ethel was right to say that one could never know how each medicine would work on a patient. She once witnessed millefollia leaves cause a horrible rash in one of the village children, when nobody else had ever had a similar reaction.

They remained and watched Mom silently until late at night, observing whether the medicine was working. Mom would occasionally groan in pain, at which point everyone looked at her with concern. Eventually Granny Ethel sent them all home, where they each collapsed in their respective beds and slept until morning.

The next morning, before even eating breakfast, the whole family rushed back to the healer's hut. To their relief they saw Mom sitting up, gingerly sipping tea with Granny Ethel next to her.

"Maeve!" Dad ran up to her, his usually stern expression turning gentle.

"Mom!" Callen and Callie ran up to her, giving her a gentle hug.

"Sorry to worry you all," Mom said, giving everyone a pat on the head in turn. Even Dad. "I heard you worked hard to get me the necessary medicine."

"It was all Callie. She was the one who got the mireling slime and the dandilynx flowers," Callen said proudly.

"Thank you, dear," Mom said, pulling Callie into a hug. "I really appreciate it."

Callie felt immense relief. She clung onto Mom for a good while, feeling how her strong hands patted her head.

"The ingredients Callie brought really made a difference. I think if they were any less potent, I would have had no choice but to cut off your arm, Maeve," Granny Ethel said.

"About those ingredients," Dad said, his voice growing stern.

Mom released Callie, looking down at her with a dubious look. The time for scolding had come.

"Would you care to explain exactly how you obtained the mireling slime and a flower that supposedly isn't supposed to bloom just yet? Callen and I combed through the whole forest, and none of the flowers even had buds yet, forget about full-blooming ones," Dad said, crossing his arms over his chest.

"I… I just got l-lucky…" Callie muttered, staring at the floorboards.

"Callie…" Dad said, his voice low. This was the "you better tell me the truth, or there will be consequences" voice.

Callie could feel her hands turn clammy. What was she supposed to say? Admitting that she could talk to arcane beasts certainly wasn't an option. Even Callen had looked at her as if she were crazy. Not to mention, telling Mom about Wiggles and Snowglow could mean their death. Admitting to having magic would likewise endanger the dragon. What could she do?

"Callie has always been good at finding medical ingredients. There's no need to glare at her so viciously, Rowen," Granny Ethel interjected, stepping up to Callie and placing her hands protectively on her shoulder. Callie took a step back, closer to the older woman.

Dad reached his hand up to his forehead, rubbing it slightly while muttering something too quietly for Callie to hear.

"What's so unusual about mireling slime?" Mom asked. "Those little pests can be found everywhere. I'm sure finding their slime isn't that difficult."

"She brought half a jar filled with the stuff, and it was absolutely clean to boot. Not a single speck of dirt in it," Dad explained.

Callie could feel cold sweat beading on her forehead. He was right. Callie and Tristan had been sent to collect mireling slime before, usually in the early morning while the dew was still around. It could be found sticking to half-eaten plants, or sometimes, globs could be found on the soil near gardens.

But getting pristine, clean slime in such large quantities. Yeah, Callie could see why Dad would find it suspicious.

"I… I…" she stammered, trying to come up with a response and inadvertently sinking deeper into Granny Ethel's skirt.

"It's because of me," Callen suddenly said.

Everyone looked at him in confusion.

"I saw the mireling sneaking into the chicken coop, so obviously I tried to kill it so it doesn't harm the chickens. Callie saw me and—"

"She tried to stop you, didn't she?" Dad said.

"Yeah. I did manage to hit the mireling severing a bunch of its slime. After that, we heard what had happened to Mom and ran straight to the healer's hut. There was no chance to clean it up. That's where Callie got it. She just didn't want to tell you, because you would be mad that she let a mireling get away," Callen said.

Callie had to admit that she was amazed at her brother's ability to lie with such a straight face. Even she almost believed that it was what happened.

"Makes sense why you would want to hide that. Mirelings could seriously hurt our chickens with their acidic slime," Dad reprimanded.

"It didn't hurt any chickens!" Callie objected. She didn't have to lie about this. Wiggles truly hadn't hurt a single feather on any of the chickens. He was a good slime.

Dad massaged the bridge of his nose. "Perhaps, we need to check the coop for any stray holes once we get home. But more importantly, where did you get the blooming dandilynx flowers?"

"I can answer that, too, since I am just as guilty as Callie. I distracted you so Callie could go into the Deep Forest," Callen said.

Mom and Dad gasped audibly.

"You did what?!" Mom exclaimed, leaning closer to Callen. But she then winced in pain and leaned back into the bed.

"Callen, have you lost your mind?! Letting your little sister wander into the Deep Forest alone. That's where the swamp king lives. What if she had been caught in one of its mires? She could have died," Dad bellowed.

"As the smallest one, I believed that Callie was least at risk of getting caught in a mire. Besides, she did find the flower there in the end," Callen said.

Dad's face grew red with anger while Callie glanced nervously between her father and brother. Why was Callen telling such a horrible lie to protect her? Surely he knew that Dad would be incredibly mad.

Everyone in the village knew to never head into the Deep Forest. An arcane beast lived there that could create mires and swamps with merely its steps. Callie had tried asking the villagers about what kind of creature it was, but nobody really knew. Nobody had seen the swamp king.

But it was a fact that many who ventured into the Deep Forest never returned. At that moment, Callie wondered if perhaps the dragon was the swamp king. But there was no real way of knowing. Besides, the dragon didn't exactly give her an impression of a creature that enjoyed living in swamps.

"There, there, Rowan. You know that the children were merely trying to help their mother," Granny Ethel tried to pacify Dad.

"I understand why you went to such lengths, Callen," Mom said, her lips pursed into a thin line. "But sending Callie to the Deep Forest is not something that can be forgiven. She could have died. We will discuss your punishment later. This isn't something that can be decided on a whim."

"Make no mistake that your punishment will be severe, young man!" Dad growled.

Callie felt so bad. Callen shouldn't have lied like that just so Callie could escape punishment. For a moment, she wondered whether she should just tell the truth. But... she couldn't. She was too scared. Not for herself, but for Wiggles, Snowglow, and the dragon.

Callen, on the other hand, looked fearless, staring at their father with a steady expression. He didn't look afraid to shoulder all the blame. In fact, he looked incredibly strong and brave at that moment. Callie wished she could be that strong.

"Brother did nothing wrong!" Callie exclaimed and ran to Callen's side, clutching onto Callen's arm. "If you're going to punish him, you will have to punish me too!"

"Oh, make no mistake. You're getting punished too. Young you may be, but not so young that you wouldn't understand that going to the Deep Forest is dangerous!" Dad said firmly.

Geh! She should have expected that. But nonetheless, she stood by her brother's side, clutching his hand firmly. As always, Callen has proven to be her strongest ally. Looking up at her brother, she saw a radiating grin on his face as he looked down at her.

They didn't need words to understand each other. At that moment, Callen had accepted Callie's ability to speak to arcane beasts and had decided to protect her secret and the arcane beasts. Most importantly, it was thanks to arcane beasts that Callie was able to save Mom from losing her arm.

"Honey, would you be so kind as to speak with Mister Blackthorn tomorrow. I'm afraid the children here have gotten too brave for their own good. The glauxis is likely still nearby. If somebody can make them understand the danger of the forest, it's him," Mom said.

Dad nodded. "You're right. I'll ask him to give you and the other girls a special lesson tomorrow."

Callie shrank back. The lesson about making fire was still fresh in her mind. In fact, the blisters on her hands still hadn't fully healed. She couldn't even imagine what kind of horrors the scary man would make them endure. Not to mention, that she would have to see Aurelia again.
