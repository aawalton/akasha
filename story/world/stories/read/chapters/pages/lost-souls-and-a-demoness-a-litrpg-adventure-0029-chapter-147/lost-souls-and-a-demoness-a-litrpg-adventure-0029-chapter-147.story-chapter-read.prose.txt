One of the first things Jade did upon their return to Illysport was stop by Ashar's shop. The streets were busier than ever as people flowed in from the surrounding countryside, sheltering from the imminent demon invasion. That the city housed and fed all comers, regardless of their affiliation with the city-state, caught Jade by surprise. Earth had no shortage of refugee crises, and seldom were her people so generous. When she'd voiced that opinion, Fenrin had explained that there was a culture of unity among the Labyrinth's population. When bizarre monsters or mysterious magical events could appear out of nowhere, it paid for people to band together in response. That this threat was potentially greater in magnitude only made that behavior more important. The result was a city that felt like it was preparing for siege, complete with congested streets and overflowing inns.

"I'm surprised so many people are here." Jade said, pushing her way through the crowded street towards Ashar's shop with Siora. "There have only been a few demon sightings in the area, right?"

"I heard a local wayfarer guild uncovered an incubus that had infiltrated the city." The elf replied, clinging to Jade's back and using her to break through the flow of people. "And that's just the latest one. People are scared."

"I'm glad we're not the only ones doing something about it. But where I come from, people always seem to ignore when the government warns them about some oncoming disaster. Floods, fires, whatever it is, there are always people who think the rules don't apply to them."

"Oh, we have people like that here, too." Siora said, speaking loudly to be heard over the noisy thoroughfare. "But I don't think they last very long."

They finally broke through the crowd, entering the smaller side street that held Ashar's shop. Making their way inside, they were greeted by Ashar's assistant."Oh, yes, welcome!" Yulie said, hurriedly arranging a collection of crystals in a display case. She closed and locked it, turning to give them a somewhat flustered smile. "Jade, right? And you're Siora?"

Ashar's assistant was a human woman with long, dark hair, and an excess of nervous energy. With the number of wild experiments the mage was always up to, Jade completely understood her demeanor.

"That's right, we're here to see Ashar." Jade said. "Is he in the back?"

"Why does it smell like something is burning?" Siora asked, cocking her head to one side."What do you-" Yulie paused to sniff the air, and then a look of alarm crossed her face. "Lost gods’ grace, Ashar! I leave you back there for five minutes!"

Jade watched as the irate assistant dashed into the back room, pushing her way through the curtain of beads that acted as the divider. A hissing sound emanated from the doorway, quickly followed by a faint plume of smoke. Ashar emerged, waving his hand through the air in front of him and coughing. He noticed Jade and Siora and stopped, looking back over his shoulder.

"Sorry, Yulie! Would you clean that up? I have guests!"

The reply was too muffled for Jade to make out, but she got the gist of it from the tone.

"I think she needs a raise."

"Probably." Ashar agreed, straightening his jacket and brushing soot from his pants. He stood up straight, greeting them with a smile. "Welcome back. I take it the expedition went well?"

"About as well as our search for the demons in Estelhelm." Jade said wryly.

Ashar winced.

"That bad?"

"We got the job done, and we all came back. Can't ask for more than that."

"I can." Siora said, raising a finger. "I could do without the frequent brushes with death."

"You know, Siora." Ashar said thoughtfully. "If you're going to be adventuring with Wayfarers, you should find your way to an apprentice mark."

"Those are the marks some wayfarers can give out that give people classes, right?" Jade asked. Aylin had one, as did Nythis, the elven druid from Siora's home.

"That's right. Any wayfarer who's completed their one hundred quests can grant them, though I don't know the details. They don't help much when it comes to levelling up since you still don't get quests of your own, but the expanded features would help you be prepared."

"There's a retired wayfarer in the town I grew up in. That's where Jade can get the last ingredient she'll need for the potion we're making for her brother. I might ask him if he'd be willing to grant me one.""That sounds like a good idea, Siora." Jade said, putting a hand on her shoulder. "I can come with you for that, if you like."The elf gave Jade a warm smile.

"I would. Thanks.""Speaking of ingredients." Ashar said, stepping behind the main counter and ducking down to access a drawer. "I have something for you two."

Keys jingled and a lock clicked. A moment later, Ashar held up a small, crystaline vial. It was similar to a potion container, but smaller. Most of the vial was empty save for an iridescent droplet clinging to its bottom. It shone with rainbow light, and Jade's magical senses picked up a brilliant warmth emanating from it. The sensation was oddly familiar, resonating with the new luminous quality of her own aura.

"One phoenix tear." He said, holding it out to Jade. "I hope it does the trick."

Jade reached to accept it and then hesitated. She didn't have a price, but she knew this was a rare and valuable ingredient.

"Ashar, I…"

"No buts." He interjected, holding up a finger. "Besides, I need to keep you and your friends spending all your money here." He grinned. "Nothing better than a sense of obligation for that."

Jade didn't buy his greedy merchant act for a second. For some reason, he liked to pretend his generosity was a screen for selfishness. Jade didn't understand it, but if he wanted her to play along, then she was more than happy to indulge him.

"Of course." She said, the corner of her lip curling up in a slight smile. Another plume of smoke wafted out from the back room, accompanied by an acrid odor. "Seems like you need all the help you can get."

"Oh, that?" He asked, glancing over his shoulder. "That's just the development process. New enchantments don't invent themselves, you know."

"Does the process always smell so bad?" Siora asked curiously.

"I'm still working out the quirks."

There was a muffled thud from the other room and Ashar winced.

"I'll be right back…"He hurried back through the beads, calling out to his assistant.

"Yulie? Are you okay? Was that the mana condenser again?" And then, a moment later. "No, the front isn't supposed to fall off!"

Jade stifled a giggle, then held up the shining vial and inspected it more closely. The surface of the phoenix tear shimmered, prismatic light filtering through it like sunlight refracting through water. She pulled up its information.

Phoenix Tear

Rarity: Legendary

Drinking this droplet induces a short lived but massively powerful regenerative effect capable of healing almost any injury. The magic will linger in the body, preventing potions and other restorative effects from working for a moderate period.

This was the first legendary item Jade had ever seen. She read the effect again, cupping the vial in both of her hands. Intellectually, Jade knew she didn't need to worry about breaking the precious item. Like all potion vials, the container was magically reinforced to prevent anything but a concerted attempt to damage it from having any effect. Still, she couldn't shake the instinct. When encountering healing magic, one of the first thoughts she'd had was how it could help her brother. It turned out to be more complicated than that, her initial dreams of buying a cleansing potion and returning home with it shattered by the reality of what it took to treat complex ailments. Even in a world of magic, it turned out, the body could be a delicate thing.

But now she had this. A dream come true. Almost.

"So, what do you have to do with it?" Jade asked, carefully stowing the precious item in her dimensional pouch. "Do you know how long it will take?"

She tried to keep her tone casual, but inside she was filled with anxiety. It suddenly felt real. Possible. And she knew from painful experience that a thread of hope was a dangerous thing to grasp.

"No need to worry." Siora reassured her. "It's a variant of a cleansing potion recipe I've brewed hundreds of times, just with more powerful ingredients. It only takes a day to make. The hard part is getting what we needed.""We still need the blossom from the tree in the sacred grove, right?"

"That's not actually a requirement for the elixir, strictly speaking." Siora said, waggling her hand back and forth in an uncertain gesture. "But imbuing it with growth essence will act as an enhancer. It will make the potion more effective for a longer period. Maybe not necessary, but for complex illnesses, you can never be sure what's enough."

"If it gives it a better chance of working, then I'm all for it." Jade said. She surprised Siora by pulling her into a warm embrace, giving her small frame a grateful squeeze. "Thanks, Siora. For doing this for me."

"You're my friend, Jade. That's what we do. We help each other out."

"I think your idea of friendship is a little more involved than most people’s. But I think we'd all be better off if more people were like you."

Jade mentally added Ashar to that list as well. The enchanter had even less of a reason to help her than Siora did, and yet he'd procured what she could only imagine was a near-priceless ingredient and simply gave it away. Whatever the two of them told her, she was in their debt. And it wasn't one she would soon forget.

"So, you actually found one?"

Jade and Naomi sat across from Braston, the Ebon Eye go-between they'd dealt with before leaving on the most recent corespace expedition. They were in a bustling restaurant, tucked away in a corner booth that gave them more privacy than the densely packed space looked like it could afford. Before answering, Jade took a circumspect look around. No one at any of the nearby tables was paying them any attention, and the general clamor would make their conversation indistinct.

"We think we did." Jade shrugged, returning her attention to the man. He had a large frame and rugged features, taking up nearly as much space as she and Naomi did on their side of the booth. "But that's just going off the description you gave me. We could've gotten it wrong, your notes were a little vague.""Well, let's see it then." He said, gesturing to the table. "I have a way to check."

Jade wasn't sure how good of an idea it was to pull out a potentially volatile magical relic in the middle of a crowded restaurant but complied without complaint. Judging by the emotions she was sensing from the closest tables she suspected the Ebon Eye owned the establishment. Potentially being surrounded by members of a shady organization didn't give her the best feeling, but the rest of their party was nearby to help if they needed it. After fighting their way through multiple corespaces undergoing some manner of magical catastrophe, she was confident in their ability to handle some rough looking men in a pinch.

The crystalline orb thumped onto the tabletop, nearly knocking over Naomi's drink. Faint colors illuminated the interior of the glossy sphere, shifting and glimmering like a distant nebula. It rolled across the table to Braston, who stopped it with his hand.

"We went to a lot of trouble getting this for you." Naomi said, crossing her arms. "I hope it was worth it."There was genuine bite in the idol's voice. Jade suppressed a smile. She was playing her part well.

"It definitely wasn't easy." She agreed, watching as Braston withdrew a small rod from a coat pocket. He passed it over the orb, and the device blinked a sequence of colors.

"Looks like your hard work paid off, kid." He said, tucking the rod back into his pocket. "This thing is giving off some powerful dimensional readings. Still willing to let me take it off your hands?"

"Could we actually say no at this point?" Jade asked, giving him a flat look. "How many of the tables in here are filled with your people? Six? Eight?"

"Enough." He said, and Jade sensed a level of satisfaction from him. "But we don't just take things from people. That's not how you cultivate a good business relationship."Braston said it with a straight face, but Jade's emotional senses picked out the lie. Not that she cared, they'd come here to hand it over in the first place.

"You told my friend here that you'd be able to get Earth wayfarers good discounts on gear if we got this to you." Naomi said, giving the man an appraising look. "How do we know that you'll hold up your end of the bargain?"

"Like I said, it's about building a relationship. If we go back on a deal, there won't be another. That's not profitable for anyone. Besides." He shot Jade a glance. "Nobody wants to make enemies with a succubus."

Jade glowered, taking another furtive look around.

"Advertising that doesn't endear you to me." She hissed at him, leaning across the table. "If you haven't noticed, we're trying to stop a demon invasion. If people find out that I'm… not what I look like… they'll try to lynch me."Jade's upgraded shapeshifting ability protected her identity from being uncovered by most forms of magic, a choice she was increasingly glad she'd made. In Estelhelm, Aylin helped her procure documentation that explained her circumstances and granted her a legal status in the city. Now that news of the imminent demon invasion was spreading, they'd decided to keep her identity firmly under wraps in Illysport. Approaching the Mystic Order or the civic authorities just wasn't worth the risk.

"Like you said, these are our people." He grunted, crossing his arms and leaning back against the wall. "Your secrets are safe here."

"I'd rather not test that."

"Fair enough." Braston picked up the orb, tucking it away in his coat. If the relic's weight bothered him, he didn't show it. "Regardless, we'll hold up our end of the deal. Earth Wayfarers coming through Illysport will get supplies sold to them at cost from all of our affiliated merchants.""And how do we know who those are?" Naomi asked.

"They'll advertise it as a gesture of goodwill to a newly integrated society. Starting our relationship with your people off well."

"And Lord Ledkin?" Jade asked. "I don't want him causing trouble for us. The faster we destroy the cores, the less likely it is the demons can use them to invade."

"I doubt even he would try anything with the city in crisis, but we'll make sure he doesn't." Braston scooted out of the booth, squeezing free from the tight confines with a grunt. "You and your friends know how to get things done, Jade. There's always work for people with your skills."

"We have our own priorities." Jade said, also standing."Like saving our world from the Labyrinth, the demons, and whatever else is out there." Naomi added, joining her.

Braston smiled.

"Those aren't mutually exclusive. There's a lot of profit to be made in war."

Each passing day brought more refugees in from more rural settlements, people taking shelter as word of the new danger continued to spread.
