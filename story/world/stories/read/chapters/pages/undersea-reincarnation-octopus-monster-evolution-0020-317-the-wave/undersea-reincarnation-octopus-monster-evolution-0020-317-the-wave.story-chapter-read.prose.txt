From a few tables away, Henry had kept an eye on the chaotic mixing competition while Velistraine, Zerathstra, and Arisia sat around and chatted about mundane matters as they downed one drink after the other under the eyes of the curious locals. It was an enjoyable evening, and for the first time in a while, Henry just… let loose.

He showed them sights from his world. Images of places and cities he had seen, either in person or in pictures, and as he went from one to the next, he wondered what was going on back over there. Did something magical happen? Was his disappearance just a blip? Clearly, that turtle had arrived in that world by magic, and it had left and carried him along to this place, which in this hypothesis, was another world. That meant Earth either already had magic in its raw, unmanaged form, or that it had been recently introduced.

At the end of the day, I’ll only know if I’m ever able to step foot there again, he thought. It was an interesting question to explore eventually, but for now, he just enjoyed the impressed looks his drinking companions were showing. He showed them the sprawling cities with their sky-scrapers, the pyramids, the giant cargo ships and submarines, but what had interested the captain the most had been the music, and interestingly, Henry had had a lot less difficulty than he expected to recreate some of the songs he’d spent hours listening to what felt like years ago.

Unsurprisingly, the captain had really enjoyed the theme of a popular pirate-themed series of movies from back home, though to be fair, most musics he’d played had gotten some head-nods and foot-taps out of her

Over a soft soundtrack one might hear in a lounge, the conversation about his old home continued and evolved. It started with what kind of cuisines and liquors Henry had tried and how they were. How they were made. Then the discussion shifted to what would happen if magic was introduced in Earth’s culture, which then circled back to food, right as a platter full of appetizers was procured by Zerathstra. He’d disappeared for a few seconds before reappearing with a platter in hand, and as Henry and eyed the arrayed appetizers, Velistraine squealed and jumped in. They were small slices of bread that had been dipped in a spicy sauce before being covered with a smoky, thick spread of peppers, garlic, and onions, all topped with flaked fish.

It was delicious.

As they ate and drank, Henry tuned them out for a short moment and listened to Ash and Maurice’s talk about A-ranks. It wasn’t news to him, really. Becoming an A-rank himself was not a hope or a dream. It was very much a target. It was one of the steps toward his goal of figuring out what was going on in this world and how he found himself within it. Still, he was pretty happy to see how well they were getting along.

Watching his friend squabble with someone who felt more his age was enjoyable, especially if he and Maurice ended up spending some more time with Velistraine and her crew. While they hadn’t really talked about it much considering the looming threat of the wave, Henry assumed they were going to travel a bit together to get their hands on the remaining mysterious orbs. With one of the locations known, the trajectory was set.

It wouldn’t be for the long term, and it might not happen soon, but it was a possibility, and it was nice to know the crab could make a friend out of most people he met. Just in case I get myself into a dumb situation, he thought. Generally, Henry wasn’t too worried about his own safety, though seeing Maurice with his new friends, he felt a pang of guilt about putting him in so much danger. But then but he reminded himself that while Maurice was young, he was no child. The crab could grasp the gravity of most situations just as well as he did. Still, it was a little hard to balance the part of him that wanted to keep his companion safe against the part that respected Maurice’s own initiative.

After all, even before they’d met, Maurice had been fending for himself. And speaking of meetings, Henry glanced at the other table and made a note to check with Rocky and Stormsong. To see what they wanted to do once Henry and Maurice were ready to leave. So far Stormsong had already expressed wanting to stay, but maybe she changed her mind. As for Rocky, Henry wasn’t sure. But it wouldn’t hurt to clarify soon.

Henry’s musings were interrupted by the sounds of a chair scraping. Zerathstra groaned and got himself up to his feet, stretching an arm above his head. “Alright, this won’t be long,” the guardian said, glancing up at a particularly low branch that was only a couple dozen feet up and with smooth hop, he landed on the branch, as easily as it would be to take a step up the stairs. There was a pulse of mana that immediately rippled across the crowd, quieting down the chatter while Zerathstra’s posture straightened. The ape’s eyes grew sharper, and in a heartbeat, the effects of the liquor the A-rank had allowed to affect him instantly dissolved away.

I can probably do that, Henry thought, glancing down at the cup in his hand. He eyed the pale cocktail for a second, then threw it down his gullet. The liquid went down his throat, then down toward a little pouch that faced his real mouth, and he drank it all up and sighed.

He was sure he could flush the buzz out of his system if he wanted to, but he wouldn’t, and just for that thought, he resolved to drink some more. He was taking a break, and he was making the most of it.

Zerathstra already had everyone’s attention, but he still clapped, and if anyone had been distracted before, they weren’t now. “People of Thalis, guests,” called out the ape. The A-rank looked around, a serene look on face for a couple of seconds before he grinned. “Well, let’s keep it short so we can get back to the party, right? Enjoy your time! Laugh, drink, and feast, because we have work to do. Any day now, the wave will begin in earnest. Some of us will spend days without sleep, but once it will pass—like all the waves before it—we’ll have another one of these parties! So!” the ape exclaimed as he raised his cup. “Eat! Drink! And have fun!”

A roar filled the space as everyone cheered back, and as the clamor mellowed down, Maurice popped into existence right in front of him with a pink, quartz-made cup in his claw.

“Henry! I made you a drink. Rocky made the cup. Try it!”

Henry chuckled and took the cup, cheering with the rest of the table and as he tried the drink, he had to admit, it wasn’t bad at all and as the party picked up once more, he grinned, sighing contently as the stars filled the sky above them.

He could get used to this.

***

The party continued deep in the night, and while Henry sensed some apprehension from a few of the attendees, it was overshadowed by the general optimism. The folks of Thalis seemed reinvigorated by their leader’s confidence. Also, the presence of an increasingly drunk Velistraine certainly seemed to help and through the night, the party kept going, while Zerathstra disappeared a couple of times here and there to chase away some monsters, giving the Treeguards an opportunity to relax and enjoy themselves, and every time he came, they cheered for him but eventually, it got late, and soon enough, everyone had gone to sleep. Even Henry, who’d been chained self-improvement work and real security work for days now. He remember slipping into the pool, and he almost immediately dozed off.

In the night, it happened.

Henry’s eyes had shot open at the same time as Maurice and Rocky, instantly thinking they were under attack, but no attacks or skill came at them. The alarms of Thalis did not ring, but once the initial shock had faded, they realized what had woken them up, and they all knew it was here. Rocky confirmed it as well, having lived through at least one of the phenomenons that he remembered.

The wave had officially begun.

It was, in a way, as if there had been a dam blocking the energies over the last few days. Some of it still leaked through, increasing the levels of ambient magic every day, and as of a couple of minutes ago, the dam broke. It was nearly a physical thing. The difference was as stark as swimming into the Current for the first time ever, and it set Henry’s nerves on edge. All of a sudden, it was as if there were heavy, dark, clouds hovering above all of them. They weren’t crackling. They weren’t rumbling. And they weren’t raining.

They were just there. Still. Looming over them while waiting for a spark to unleash the thunderstorm of the century.

It had been difficult to go back to sleep with that heavy air about them, but Henry did eventually manage a couple of extra hours of rest before wind began buffeting the trees and canopies of Thalis, somehow managing to make the gigantic trees move and by morning, it was almost a full-out storm. By morning, Henry had been dispatching enraged sea creatures for a couple of hours, and the body and cores were piling up.

One thing was for sure. He was going to earn his keep.

***

The next day, around noon, Thalis saw its first serious threat. When the alarm rang, Henry had been resting between shifts. The horns were ominous, and it was blown only once. As soon as the baleful signal went quiet, Zerathstra’s voice followed.

“Every non-fighter to the inner shelters. All B-ranks with range capabilities to the upper platforms.”

Zerathstra’s voice was clipped, unlike his usual tone, and Henry knew he had work to do. He wasn’t B-rank just yet, and he didn’t have the best ranged attack himself, but he was ready to get some levels and some practice with Echoing Arm.

With Maurice surrounded by a particularly tough arm—which Henry had created by fusing two limbs and by significantly increasing the density of the muscles—the two flew up the trees and though they didn’t see them at first, they could heard the calls filling the sky. Then Henry saw them. Flying above the canopy. Hundreds of them. Swarming in the sky. White and gray wingspans, some as small as a school bus and some as large as three lined together, with the largest of them sporting nearly a two-hundred feet wingspan.

The largest of them swooped toward the canopy, accelerating dangerously as it screeched before it flapped its gray wings and two giant barely visible arcs sliced down and cut through one of the larger branch like a hot knife through foam.

[Windscythe King (?) - ?]

The bird has done damage, but flying so low opened it up to retaliation. Not that it couldn’t defend itself. Henry watched as the giant bird flapped its wing and easily dodged the flying red dots that tore through the air, aimed at its throat. Velistraine fell back down, kicking the air to find herself a new branch and while Henry would love to watch more, he quickly realized that couldn’t help with that fight—though he had to keep a vigilant eye on it—and instead, he focused on the smaller gulls he could do something about. Especially considering their numbers.

“[We deal with the ones swooping on the Treeguard first, Maurice. Then we focus on the ones helping the A-rank.]”

“[On it!]”

Henry scanned his surroundings and quickly found his first target.

[Stormgull (B) - Level 33]

Without wasting any time, Henry whipped out an arm, activating Echoing Arm and watched as an arcane copy of his arm flew up and clipped the bird, which let out a shriek before it dove at him and as it quickly closed in, Maurice’s voice chimed into his mind.

“[I’ve never had bird before,]” the crab mused, and Henry grinned. If things went well, they’d have all the birds they could eat soon. They just had to work hard for it.
