Several guards waiting outside in the hall burst into the chamber. Elise immediately rushed toward the baron, while the others attended to the viscount.

Near the wall, a window was broken, but there were barely any shards inside the room. It looked like someone had broken out, not in.

The viscount lay pale and trembling, his eyes closed and his breathing shallow. Beside him, the baron sat propped against the wall, his chest rising raggedly. The baron was wounded, but it seemed he would probably live.

The viscount… Finn wasn’t so sure.

“Elise?” His voice cracked. “He—he’s not going to—”

“Hold it steady!” she snapped, ripping a strip of linen from the medical kit at her side. “The baron will survive. The viscount is worse off, so we focus on him first.”

Finn stood frozen in the doorway. The scene in front of him was completely unexpected. He wasn’t ready for such a gruesome sight, but he also couldn’t look away.

And perhaps that morbid curiosity was what allowed him to realize something vitally important.

As he looked from the injured nobles’ faces to the guards and then to Elise, he noticed something odd. Everyone’s breathing was becoming shallow and ragged, as though they were slowly contracting a deep cold. Standing in the doorway, Finn didn’t feel much of it at first. But the moment he took a few steps inside, panic rose within him.

His mind began to reel. He blinked, trying to clear it away, but the dizziness only deepened. His chest rose and fell faster, yet each breath felt shallow, as though the air itself were stealing the oxygen from his body.

Another guard nearby began to sway as he worked. Even Elise, who was bandaging the baron, had started taking short pauses.

Something was wrong.

And he’d seen it before.

“Elise, something’s wrong.”

She turned to look at him. “What are you talking about?” she said, her voice airy and almost gasping.

Her voice confirmed it for him.

Finn shook his head, steadying himself. He glanced toward the doorway and made a decision. He might look like a fool, but it was better to be safe than sorry.

“I’ll be right back,” he muttered.

“You—!” Elise snapped, but Finn was already running.

The guards turned as well, yelling after him. “Hey! Get back here!”

Finn staggered into the hallway, sucking in a deep breath. Immediately, he looked left and right. He chose a random hall and ran down it. As he turned the corner, he spotted what he wanted: a brazier set along the wall, with a torch resting against its edge, the tip still burning.

Finn grabbed it and ran back toward the room.

“Hey! What are you doing with that?!” a guard barked from inside, spotting Finn from the corner of his eye.

Elise also turned around, her hands bloodied and ready to yell at him. But Finn caught everyone’s attention first.

“Everyone! Look!” he shouted.

He dipped the torch low to the ground. The flame flickered for a brief moment. It struggled to maintain its shape, and then it went out.

Just like that. Without any sudden movement.

Finn furrowed his brow.

He was right.

“Dead air,” he said aloud. He looked back up at everyone. “Get them out of the room! It’s filled with bad air! Like in the mines! You’ll die if you stay in here!”

Greystone children learned early not to play too deep near the old mine vents. Dead air gathered low, the miners would tell them. If a flame died near the ground, you ran.

The guards hesitated for half a second. A few turned toward the baron’s maid.

“Elise—?” one started. “What should we—”

Elise pursed her lips, her gaze scrutinizing Finn. A second later, she gave the command.

“Listen to the boy! Get everyone out of this room!”

Quickly, though slowed by the buildup of dead air, the guards hoisted the viscount and baron into the hallway, then carried them toward one of the secondary guest rooms. Finn helped carry the viscount’s legs despite the blood dripping down them. They laid both men on the floor outside the room and began bandaging their wounds once more.

Almost immediately, everyone felt slightly better. The headaches and sluggishness began to fade.

It was also at that moment that Elise noticed the viscount was wearing an artifact.

There was a ring on the viscount’s hand. It glimmered with a green glow. Something about it gave off a pleasant feeling, almost rejuvenating in a way. Whatever the ring was, it was probably enchanted with some type of recovery field. A defensive artifact. It wasn’t rare for nobles to carry things like that, especially a viscount.

That ring might have been the only reason both men were still alive when they burst into the room.

Finally, Elise tied off the last strip and pressed down firmly on the baron’s abdomen to apply pressure. Her gaze flicked toward Finn, sharp and urgent.

“You need to run. Find Steward Hadrian. Tell him assassins have attacked the baron and the viscount, and that Claire is missing. He must alert every guard and call up the militia. And tell him to find Sir Nolhan. One of the viscount’s servants had called him to check some of the food the chef was making in case of poison. He shouldn’t be far. Whoever they are that did this must have known how strong Nolhan is.”

Finn scrambled upright, blood still sticky on his palms. “O-okay! I’ll tell him!” He hesitated for only a second, then blurted, “Also! Veronica said the town might be attacked by cultists too! So be careful here.”

He knew that Elise was a mage, so she should be fine even if they did attack.

Elise froze mid-motion, her eyes narrowing. “Cultists…? You were speaking about demons before. How do you two know this?”

But Finn was already backing toward the door. “Just—just trust me! Trust her!”

He bolted out of the chamber, lungs heaving, his palms still tacky and moist with the viscount’s blood. The hall blurred past him as he charged down the steps two at a time, boots echoing across the marble.

Behind him, Elise stared after the boy, brow creased. Then she turned back to her work, pressing her palms down on the viscount’s wound as the faint hiss of his labored breathing filled the silence. She couldn’t afford time in worrying about needless things. Not right now.

The mansion courtyard stretched before Finn, lanterns casting dim, pale glows across the hedges. In front, the large iron gate loomed at the far end. He sprinted straight for it—only to slam to a halt.

It was locked.

“Elise didn’t—ugh!” He kicked the bars once, teeth clenched. She had left the gate locked and not given him the key.

He backed up a step, then sprinted forward and vaulted. His fingers caught the bars, shoes scrambling for purchase as he scrambled up the black iron. For a moment his chest caught on the top rail, sharp points scraping his side, then he twisted and dropped down hard on the other side. His knees jolted from the impact, stumbling, but he immediately moved into a sprint.

The air was cooler outside, brisk and welcoming against his sweating face. He raced across the cobblestone streets toward the town square. Lanterns still hung from the festival, paper shades glowing muted orange. The square itself, so crowded just hours ago, was thinner now. A few drunkards lingered near taverns, laughing and idly engaged in small conversation.

The clamor of the day had dimmed into the harsh stress of the night. Everyone else was winding down, yet, a few minutes from now, that would change entirely.

Finn tore past them all. He knew exactly where to go. Everyone knew where Hadrian spent most of his time.

The steward’s building loomed ahead on the far side of the square. Finn hammered on the door with both fists, shouting, “Open up! Please! It’s urgent!”

A few moments later, the door opened. The clerk stared down at him: a boy with a busted lip and a bruised cheek.

“What is it? Do you know the hour, boy?”

“There’s no time!” Finn gasped. “Assassins attacked the baron and the viscount! They’re hurt—badly! And Veronica—she’s fighting a demon right now in the forest—”

The man blinked at him, overwhelmed by the shouting. A moment later, he noticed the blood on the boy’s hands. “Slow down, slow down. You’re just spouting gibberish at me. Who are you, anyway?”

“I don’t matter!” Finn snapped, both fists balled. “What matters is that Veronica needs help! The whole town might be attacked!”

At that name, the man’s brows lifted. “Veronica… the mage?”

Of course he knew her. He worked with the steward and was one of the few who knew directly that Veronica had slain the ogre a few days ago.

The man exhaled sharply and pulled the door open. “All right, inside. Quickly. Hadrian’s office is the door in the back.” He pointed toward the far right. “You’d best not be lying about this. The steward’s fine with putting even children in jail.”

Finn bolted past him without another word.

The steward’s office was cramped, lit by a single standing lamp. Steward Hadrian sat behind his desk, spectacles perched on his nose as he scribbled in a ledger. He looked up in surprise as Finn burst inside.

“What is the meaning of—”

“The viscount’s daughter has been kidnapped!” Finn blurted. “Assassins stabbed the baron and the viscount—Elise is with them now—but they’re in critical condition! And Veronica—she’s in the forest fighting a demon!”

The words struck like hammer blows, one calamity stacked on top of another. The boy’s bruised, panicked face, combined with the blood smeared across his palms, left no room for jokes. Hadrian’s face drained of color at once. He shot to his feet, knocking over his chair.

“Repeat that. A demon?”

The rumors of people dressed in robes and lurking around town weren’t new to him. However, none of his guards had ever caught sight of such people. Despite this, villagers had gone missing left and right, but there wasn’t anything he could do besides tell people to remain alert.

He had, of course, suspected that some people were taking bribes or that there were corrupt guardsmen, but there was no solid proof. Old Thom had been hounding him, but there simply wasn’t anything he could do besides interrogate every single person in Greystone.

“Yes!” Finn replied breathlessly. “Elise told me to tell you to ready the town guards. She might need a medical team with her too. The lords’ injuries—both of them—they looked very bad. And Veronica said the town may be attacked soon, so you need to prepare everyone to fight, just in case!”

Hadrian didn’t waste another heartbeat. He strode across the office and lifted some type of curved object from a stand. He pressed his finger against its surface. A blue light shone, forming a misty haze. Finn realized it was some type of magical artifact. A sharp buzz rang out as a connection formed.

“Captain Luthen,” he barked the moment the line clicked, “mobilize all guards immediately. Sound the bells, arm the registered militia, and distribute weapons at once. I want a detachment sent straight to the baron’s mansion. They’ve been attacked. Yes—assassins. I don’t care if it’ll take ten minutes! Stop asking questions. Move!”

Finn stood frozen, huffing as he listened to Hadrian’s orders crackle across the device. Relief washed over him. He had done it. He had delivered the warning. The town would be ready.

But even as the steward spoke, Finn’s thoughts began to drift. His pulse still raced, but not from running.

The cultists. They needed a sacrifice. Claire—she was the one they wanted. According to Elise, she must have been in that room with them.

That meant they had kidnapped her.

Finn didn’t know who Nolhan was, but Elise had mentioned that he’d taken his break only a few minutes ago. That meant the cultists had waited until he was gone. The attack must have happened recently.

Which meant they couldn’t have gone far.

They would be moving toward the ruins. Toward the ritual.

Finn clenched his fists. He turned and ran from the office before the steward had even hung up.

“Where are you going?!” the balding clerk shouted after him as he darted through the front door.

“I’m headed toward the old ruins to save Claire from the cultists!”

There wasn’t time to explain anything else. He couldn’t wait for them to prepare a team and begin searching for her.

Ten minutes, according to what Hadrian had said over that magical device?

By then, it might be too late. Veronica was already fighting a demon on her own. Elise was too busy helping both lords. Captain Luthen had to call up the guards and militia and issue orders.

There was no time. No one else to call for help.

That meant only he was fast enough to hike through the woods right now.

His feet slammed hard against the dirt as he bolted down the street, past glowing lanterns and shuttered windows.

I warned the town. His chest burned, but his stride didn’t falter. Now I just have one thing left to do. Save Claire.

Greystone was a poor mining town, too far from the kingdom’s heart to benefit from most magitech advances. Annesheim, Maftem, and most other major cities relied little on manual labor when magitech handled the hardest work. For Greystone, that meant no automated wagons. No mana-driven constructs hauling people where they needed to go. At least, none freely available to anyone but the town lord.

All they had were old solutions. Manual ones that required labor.

“How many times is the limit…?” Finn murmured to himself.

He stood inside one of the old storage houses at the edge of town, where people mainly kept their mining equipment. All sorts of things could be found there, from pickaxes and mining helmets to old minecarts.

If Claire had been kidnapped only minutes ago, then he didn’t have time to give chase on foot. He also didn’t know how to ride a horse, nor could he easily steal one from a stable. Instead, he relied on something that shouldn’t have been used the way he intended.

The crankbarrow.

He’d seen miners use them before. He’d even climbed into one once before being chased off by a miner.

It was essentially a modified wheelbarrow, one that could move on its own by burning coal. By cranking the lever on its side, its speed could be adjusted. It was mainly used to transport ore or other heavy equipment. It was popular among older workers who weren’t strong enough to trek the entire way back to town while carrying their haul.

However useful it was, it wasn’t used often. It was bulky, like a miniature carriage, and couldn’t hold as much as a normal wheelbarrow. That, and the coal exhaust was dangerous and gave miners lung disease from the fumes.

“Seven… eight… nine…”

Finn looked left and right, making sure no one was around. A large commotion was building near the center of town, presumably from the guards rallying together.

“Fifteen.”

He hopped over the side and landed inside the crankbarrow. Reaching over the front, he hovered his finger above a small notch. “Okay… okay… and…!”

He pressed down on the notch and heard the gears beneath him shift. Several crackles followed. For a brief moment, he thought it hadn’t worked, but then—

“Whoa!” he screamed.

He blasted forward through an opening between the fence posts and descended into the forest. He quickly shut his mouth and gripped the sides as though his life depended on it.

It was fast, that was for sure. If the cultists were on foot, they couldn’t have gotten far. As long as he didn’t crash, he’d make it to the ritual grounds in no time.

Veronica should be fighting further west, he thought, glancing through the trees. If anyone could kill that monster, it’s her. That just leaves the ritual and cultists.

As he descended deeper into the forest, he steeled his gaze and gritted his teeth.

If Veronica can fight a demon, then I can at least stop a stupid circle and rescue Claire.

The night continued to drag on, the sound of several explosions soon reaching the ears of the townspeople. Flashes of light and the echoes of earth tremors.
