Klepto girl—I shouted inwardly to myself like she was some type of super villain from some cheesy comic book. She was the one who’d stolen my evolution stone!

My feet scurried even faster to catch up with her. She was hiding her level, so I didn’t know how much stronger I was than her. Even so, I told myself not to engage. I remembered what Nolan said about item thieves—they were well armed, and squaring up against one alone was suicide.  Especially seeing as our last encounter didn’t go well, I had no doubt that the girl had something in her stolen bag of tricks that could leave me on the roadside for the wolves. A sleeper, paralysis, or even reversal crystal could put me down quickly if I wasn’t careful. With this pro thief, I had to distance myself—not too close, not too far. Just enough so I could tell where she was going. Maybe then, I could find out where she might be staying so I could head back for help. Hopefully, with the help of the village, I could get our stuff back….

While the plan seemed foolproof, there was a problem, and that problem was a man in a white suit waiting for her under a bridge. As soon as I saw him, I tucked myself behind a tree and continued to watch on. For a short while she seemed hesitant to walk closer to him, but he beckoned her with a shifty smile. Something about his posture made my senses fire up. Even from this distance, I could sense a dangerous aura radiating from him—the kind that reminded me of apex predators in the wild.

“You’re late,” his voice carried in the night air, smooth as silk but cold as ice. He looked pretty wealthy, almost like royalty, and he was a pretty boy, too. He had long wavy blonde hair that was fixed in a loose low ponytail, broad shoulders, and some height to him. The mystery man was fae, but even though he was handsome, there was something rotten about him that made me instantly hate his guts. “I don’t appreciate the tardiness.”

The klepto girl shifted nervously, clutching her bag tighter. “I got what you asked for,” she replied, her voice smaller than I remembered. Gone was the cocky thief who'd snatched my evolution stone with a smirk. This girl seemed... afraid.

The man in the spiffy suit reached his hand out. “Well, I’m waiting,”

“You promise you’ll let him go?” the girl in the hood asked him softly.

“You have my word,” he reassured her.

With a short sigh, she slung the bag off her shoulder and handed it to him. He told her to unzip it, his voice stern. She did as she was told, and inside her bag sparkled with glitter and gold.

Could that be all of her valuable stolen items?

The fae reached into the bag, rummaging through the glittering contents impatiently. He inspected each piece closely, his cunning eyes flicking over shards of color that caught the dim light. Each artifact seemed to wash a new wave of satisfaction over his face. “Good work,” he muttered, pulling out a blue stone and holding it at eye level. “Ah, the prismatic shard from the northern temple. Excellent work.”

My heart skipped a beat when I recognized my evolution stone poking out from the bag’s unzipped top. I get that she was a thief, and that she’d get her hands on everything and anything she could, but I highly doubt that she’d fork over all of her stolen goodies, because what would a man like him want with my evo stone? Did he have a baby dragon to evolve? Or did he want it as a collector’s item?

“The Elementium Stone,” he admired, his eyes gleaming over the precious item. “One of the rarest catalysts in existence. Quite priceless for those without natural affinity. This blue stone could trigger a metamorphosis, reshaping body, spirit, and mind, and amplify radical powers tenfold. A magical push that many would die for….”

He looked over to her with a dark smirk. “You’ve done well. Better than I expected from someone with your... background.”

“Can’t you drool over your crap later? I’m waiting for—”

“Impatient tonight, aren’t we?” he interjected, and then reached into the bag, rummaging for something else. But then he paused as he inspected one particular item closely. It looked like the Ulmah that Kauli gave Nolan!

Before the man could clasp the bag shut and throw the bag over his shoulder, he flashed the girl a look of disappointment and said, “This is a fake.”

“What?” She winced slightly.

“Are you hard of hearing? I said this is a fake. The Ulmah isn’t what you’ve presented to me here.”

“But I stalked your target!” she said with a hint of desperation in her voice. “I followed your instructions perfectly and this is what he had!”

“Clearly not,” he replied coldly, tossing the fake ladybug back into the bag. “Unless you’re holding out on me.” There was a dangerous glare in his eyes as he sized her up.

“I have no idea what it is or how to make a fake anything! Do you seriously think I’d risk my brother’s life by stealing from you?”

His eyes trailed south, admiring that ring around her finger with obvious jealousy. “It must feel like less of a blessing and more like a curse, to have that gem fused to you. Any smart mage could just cast a separation spell, and any capable barbarian could just chop those little fingers and claim his reward. But no, that klepto ring was fused to your soul by your very own people, in the hopes of you making them riches, so they could claw out of their poverty stricken lives. All it really did was make themselves a target, including you. The greed of some people.”

She scoffed. “You’re one to talk.”

“You must play me for a fool.”

“I told you, I tracked your guy!” she insisted. “If he was carrying anything less than what you’re after, that’s not on me!”

“Then you’ve been as careless as I feared. Consider your brother’s freedom withdrawn until the real item is retrieved.”

Her face dropped, crumbling like paper in a fist. Speaking of fists, she clenched her own, and then boldly threatened him. “You’ll give me my brother back!”

The girl pulled out a blinding white crystal from her inventory and hung it at her side. He glared at her with a warning, “I wouldn’t do anything you might regret.”

His warning went through one ear and out the other. Her arm cocked back and she slammed the crystal on the ground, the crystal immediately shattering into pieces. Right after, a ball of energy suspended in the air like a miniature sun.

I could feel the energy radiating from here. Whatever she had in her back pocket was powerful, powerful enough for that shady man to snap his fingers and retaliate. All of a sudden, two brawny orcs appeared behind the girl and snatched her back and covered her mouth before she could finish spilling out what I suspected were some magic words that came with her light show. She kicked and squirmed as one of them locked her up in his grasp, carrying her to the dirty elf.

“Shame on you for summoning a Kundarta Crystal on me,” he said behind a grimy smile. “If you cast me to the endless void of space, how would you ever find where I’m having your brother’s organs harvested?”

“MMmmmMMm!!” she cried behind the hand over her mouth.

Just like that, the miniature sun she created began to fade. Without a victim, it powered down. The fae said something to his two orc goons, but his voice was too soft for me to hear. The free-handed orc reached over for the bag filled with precious goods, while the other one continued to wrestle with the girl who was giving him a hard time trying to free herself. The scene was hard to watch, and every bone in my body wanted to help her, but I couldn’t budge a single muscle. Then, without warning, a cloud of black smoke appeared, separating into three parts. It swallowed the two orcs and the girl. As for the scummy guy, he stayed behind. With his hands in his pockets, he lifted his chin in the air and surveyed the area. I jumped behind the tree, completely hiding myself. My little stubby legs went numb as my heart raced, afraid that he’d catch me snooping. Did he sense he was being watched?

For a long while, I didn’t dare look back. The seconds seemed like minutes before I heard a swallowing vortex sound that quickly dissipated into silence. When I looked over my shoulder and toward the bridge, I noticed everyone was gone.
