This place was different.

Where Rhogann’s spire had led into a full-on cathedral, complete with pews, altars, and massive symbols of the faith, the interior of the spire we had all nearly drowned in was not a place of worship.

If I didn’t know any better…

I would call it a library. But Korvoks being Korvoks, they of course had to taint even the idea of knowledge preservation with their own brand of personal sickness.

It didn’t take my battle-group long before we encountered our first evidence of this. As we waded into the blood-flooded tower, just past the exit to the room that we had all nearly been swept away in, I paused and oriented my light Spell downwards. It was unusually dark in here, as I believe all of the candles and lanterns had been doused in the flood. But still, I could see numerous odd little cylinders floating on the surface of the blood. Slowly, keeping an eye open for anything that might leap out of the darkness, I bent down and scooped it up. The sensation of it as I did was…odd, to say the least. The object was smooth and supple in my hands, with an odd warmth that I wasn’t sure was coming from being immersed in literal blood…

Or the item itself.

Bringing it up to the light, I could see that it was a cylinder of some kind. I had to shake and wipe the blood off of the surface to see that it wasn’t just an odd tube.

No, this was a scroll.

For a single moment, I was immediately excited. But then, I looked closer at the scroll. I was fairly used to parchment by this point in my life on Vereden. This planet didn’t really have the industrially created paper I had been so used to on Terra. Parchment creation was not only a bespoke profession, but it was a Profession as well. In fact, it was a Profession that was very closely related to the Tanner and Leathermaking Profession trees.

Because parchment was skin. Animal skin, of course, coming from all kinds of different beasts. Sheep, goats, cows, and sometimes there were particularly skilled Parchmenters who could process Monster skin before they dispersed. That always fetched a high price.

But it was all skin, and now that I was looking down at this scroll, I had a bad feeling that this parchment wasn’t sourced from animals.

“Is that…?” I heard Renauld whisper from my left. The Gnoll had a very distinct note of disgust in his voice that I didn’t blame him for. But curiously, all of us had started to speak in a whisper as we delved deeper into this spire. I’m not even sure why, really.

I nodded silently to him, and then undid the small…leather tie on the scroll. My eyes fell on the writing within, and my brow furrowed. My eyes skimmed the contents for a few moments before a grimace of disgust crossed my face, and I dropped the scroll back into the blood below without even rolling it back up.

“What did it say?” Meia asked quietly, having waded up to my right elbow.

“Nothing important,” I said shortly, before sighing at the looks both of them shot me. I relented. “It was a series of poems by a guy calling himself Thurok Flesh-Boundary. He went on and on about the incredible taste of Dwarven liver roasted over an open fire, when accompanied by a glass of something ‘Crimsherry’. Happy, now?”

It was literally impossible for a Gnoll to go green in the face, considering all the fur, but Renauld gave it a good shot. “Ecstatic,” He mumbled out, falling behind me.

I exchanged a glance with an unfazed Meia, and then silently gestured forward. As my battle-group moved out once more, I paid less attention to the dozens and dozens of other scrolls floating in the blood all around us. No doubt they were filled with more inane and monstrous trifles. I had more important things to do than judge the poetry of a bunch of crazed cannibals.

Like trying to navigate the halls of this damn place. It was nearly a maze, to the extent that I was afraid we were going to get lost in here. Where the cathedral had been one massive auditorium filled with pews of docile Skarned and worshiping Karvokar, this place was a branching path of many different hallways that all split off from each other. Each and every one of those paths, though, led to a small open room that was filled with racks upon racks of those same scrolls that even now floated all around us. It looked like the flood that had nearly swept us away had reached deep into this complex. Almost every room that we came across had been ransacked by the flood ‘waters’, tossing everything inside all around.

Including the residents. There were dozens of Karvokar, dotted all around what I was even now starting to refer to as the ‘library’. For the most part, they were just as flayed and mutated as all the other Karvokar we had encountered up to this point. However, there was one major difference.

Beyond the simple, uninteresting cloth fare that most Korvoks wore, these guys were all wearing full-body concealing, hooded robes. Made from what exactly, you might ask?

Take a wild guess.

These mad bastards…what glorious ‘Truth’ could possibly entice so much that you would flay the very skin from your body, only to drape it in robes hewn from the same flesh?

I tried not to dwell on it, and instead focused on the fact that they were all either unconscious or dead. Drowned in the same blood I had been worried would drown us.

This was, contrary to expectation, encouraging to me. It was entirely possible that every single Korvok in this ‘library’ had been knocked into a torpor from the sleep-inducing effect of the blood lake. If that was the case, then we could deal with those that were sleeping all in the same way.

By opening their throats and piercing their hearts.

I instructed my people to make sure that all of the Korvoks we came across were to be executed on the spot. There was an ever-growing disgust in my heart for these ‘people’ and their society, and I was finding it hard to manifest an ounce of mercy for them. Logically, I knew that they were as much victims of the culture that the Rendarchs and their Korvokahn had inflicted upon them. But in my heart, I knew.

These people were a boil on the breast of Vereden, and it needed to be lanced. How, I wasn’t sure.

But culling them like this didn’t elicit a flicker of doubt in me. I’m fairly sure it wasn’t even bothering my soldiers, either. Normally, considering the proclivities of the people that made up the expedition, I think it would have bothered them to so coldly execute sleeping opponents, without even giving them a chance to fight back. However, we had all seen too many horrors that had been orchestrated by the Korvoks by this point.

In both of the Holds we had explored.

My orders were followed with no question.

I think this complex might have been larger, and gone deeper into he bedrock of Karag Korvok, than any of us had been expecting. However, we weren’t really here to cleanse these already tainted halls.

We were here to find another Rendarch. And eventually…

We found him.

I believe we got lucky. It was entirely possible that there was an end point somewhere in this archive. Somewhere that the Rendarch ruled this place from, the seat of his power. But at the time of the flood, he-it, must have been roaming the shelves. Who knows what he had been doing at the time.

The important thing was that it had been caught off guard just as much as we had, and been momentarily drowned by it.

It was good to know it wasn’t just regular Korvoks that were affected by the blood.

We found it in the middle of one of the larger archival halls. This one had shelves that were nearly twice the height of most of the others we’d found, and they were packed with far more care. Possibly this was a more important area, as much as these monsters in Dwarven form placed any importance on anything more than flesh, and blood, and pain.

We almost didn’t recognize him as anything more than just another Korvok, at first. It was the robes, you see. We’d gotten so used to seeing Dwarven skin leather robes that we nearly passed the Rendarch like all the rest without a second thought. Or at least, attempted to.

I had a feeling things would have gone south fast if the soldier who found him had actually tried to kill him.

“Marshal!” I heard called out to me from across the room. Fade’s ears perked up, and we both followed the call with our heads. Through the stacks, I saw a collection of my men clustered together in a circle, the light of their own light Skills congregating to shine like a star in the night sky. They cast long shadows on the far wall, and even those shadows had tense shoulders. “Over here!”

I waded through the nearly calf-high blood that had filled the space to reach them, while Fade loped through it as best he could. The thick liquid was harder for him to navigate than it was for us two-legged, but the Spirit Wolf had yet to complain about it.

My soldiers shuffled to make room for me in their circle when I reached them. “You should see this, Marshal,” The man who had shouted for me murmured in a hushed whisper. “We think this might be the guy.”

At first, I didn’t see what they did. This just looked like another Korvok face down in the blood, complete with skin robes.

And then his chest moved, sucking in a breath even though his face had been submerged for over half an hour at this point. Somehow, this Korvok was breathing, or perhaps respirating, the blood somehow. As I watched, its chest moved up and down on the surface of the pool, causing the body to rock in a gentle motion.

My eyes narrowed as I understood. This wasn’t something we had seen on any of the other Karvokar.

Without taking my eyes off the suspected Rendarch, I reached up and patted the soldier on his shoulder. “Good work, Lieutenant Kourvan,” I murmured. “Now, if you don’t mind, can you go find Meia Itzelan for me? I think I saw her going into the room across from ours. For the rest of you, give us some room.”

The Lieutenant saluted before rushing out of the room as quickly as he could in search of my Half-Orc Sorceress. As the rest of the soldiers around me backed away, I glanced down at Fade. “What do you need?”

The Spirit Wolf studied the slowly breathing body in the blood for a moment, before letting out a wolven sigh. “You’re not gonna like this.”

I fought off the urge to rub my forehead. Just those words alone were enough to make me feel a migraine coming on. “Hit me.”

“I need access to his throat,” Fade told me bluntly.

“Which means we need to turn him over.”

Splashes in the blood signaled the arrival of someone else. Someone who, apparently, heard what I had said. “Which will wake him up,” Meia said tiredly, coming to stand next to me.

Over her shoulder, I noticed that not only had the Lieutenant followed her back, but Walter had as well. I exchanged a nod with my Squire, who chose to linger near the watching soldiers, and then addressed the Half-Orc. “Will it? Does the robe count as contact enough to wake these assholes?”

Meia nodded. “I believe so. One of the men nearly had his throat torn out when he didn’t see one of the Karvokar and nearly tripped on one. It woke him up, and I only barely reacted in time to kill it. So. You’ll need to be quick about whatever you’re doing, dog.”

Said ‘dog’ just fixed her with a stare full of palpable disdain. “I’m plenty quick, you cucumber-skinned, up-jumped, would-be pig monkey.”

My eyes widened, and I barely restrained a barked laugh. “Who’s teaching you these things?” I said under my breath, smiling.

“What did he say?” Meia asked with narrowed eyes.

I just smiled at her like butter wouldn’t melt in my mouth. “He said ‘thank you for the advice’.”

If anything, that just made her eyes narrow even more.

The banter died off quickly, though. It was good to lighten the atmosphere sometimes, but none of us had forgotten why we were here.

Slowly, I got into position, hovering over the form of the suspected Rendarch. I made sure I was careful not to touch him before Fade was ready, though. Who knows how this guy would react to being awoken after being drowned. I made sure Might of the Wyrdwood was running at a full sixty-five percent, as I looked up and met my Familiar’s eyes.

“On three,” I said lowly. “I’ll spin him around and jerk him up. Do what you need to then.”

Fade nodded seriously, and I took a deep breath.

“One.”

The Spirit Wolf crouched into a lunging position, and I saw an oddly mint colored mist start to form in his jaws.

“Two.”

The soldiers all around us shifted in place, and I heard leather gloves creak as hands tightened on drawn blades. Meia merely stood silently in place, already deeply focused on the task at hand.

“Three.”

My hands plunged into the surface of the blood, looped under the forearms of the Rendarch, and dragged him upward.

Fade blurred forward.
