The elemental converter turned out to be much less interesting than actually inscribing the talismans in the first place. The talisman was placed in the middle of the plate and then Jia poured mana into the formation until she was almost entirely out. It took most of her mana and several hours to finish even a single talisman, but Lee Jia had finally finished her first spell talisman—a simple short range shock spell.

“That was exhausting. I don’t think I can make more than one of these in a day. It’s going to make practicing with them kind of difficult when they are so precious.”

Eunae and Takeda—who had returned while Lee Jia was working on the talisman—nodded along with Lee Jia’s complaints. After a moment of pondering, Eunae offered a suggestion.

“For sparring, would it not be adequate to practice with blank talismans? I’ve never heard of it being done since talismans are never in short supply in the colleges, but in your case it might be a good alternative.”

Takeda nodded in agreement.

“I think that could work. It helps that you’re using lightning, which is notoriously hard to dodge. We can just assume for the sake of the spar that any successful spell cast is a hit. We’ll have to test the range, though.”

Lee Jia was a little hesitant at the idea of wasting one of her precious spells just to check the range, but had to admit that it was probably important to know that for the duel anyway.

Lee Jia spent the rest of the week completely focused on preparing for her duel with Yan Zhihao. She fell into a routine—every day she would meditate with Eui to replenish her mana and cultivate a greater reserve, then it was either martial arts lessons with Ienaga and sparring with Guan and Takeda, or magic lessons with Hwang followed by tutoring with Dae.

The martial arts days were spent focusing on refining the basics of ki-empowered movement and working spellcasting into her practical combat. The shock talismans turned out to have about the same range as Guan Yi’s spear, which turned their sparring into a game of whether she could activate a talisman while avoiding his strikes. It turned out to be much harder than she thought to split her concentration between watching her opponent’s attacks, moving with the flow of her ki, and pushing mana into a talisman.

In Ienaga’s lessons, her hand-to-hand combat was built up slowly as she learned proper striking and parrying movements. A proper strike or parry had to be timed with a forceful explosion of ki, which was yet another layer of ki manipulation that Lee Jia had to think about during her sparring.

According to Ienaga, the purpose of practicing the forms and meditating was to internalize those things until they became second nature. Unfortunately, Lee Jia didn’t yet have the time to spare meditating on her martial arts forms.

The magic focused days were spent learning more about spells and magical theory as well as inscribing as many talismans as she could. There was a limit to the number of shock talismans she could make because of the converter, but she could make mana-shield and blank talismans at will.

One important lesson she learned over the course of the week was that activating talismans took a fairly significant amount of mana. A mage could prepare any number of talismans, but they were still limited by the strength of their aura. This was why she was focusing all of her meditation time on mana for the time being.

While Eui always joined for Ienaga’s lessons and the sparring that took place afterwards, Jia was never successful in convincing her to join for Dae’s tutoring, and her attendance for Hwang’s classes was spotty at best. Eui seemed to greatly prefer focusing on martial arts, and even spent most of that extra time on ki meditation.

Thanks to their daily joint-meditation sessions, Jia knew that this was partly because of her hatred for mages, which always brought her back to those disturbing memory fragments and the mysterious Sun Jaehwa. Jia was glad that her training was keeping her too distracted to dig deeper about it.

The day before the fateful duel, Eui spoke to her over their daily meal—they had found that since their awakening, they needed to eat and sleep far less often than they used to.

“So tomorrow’s the big day, huh?”

Jia glanced up from her rice and nodded. She swallowed her food and then sighed.

“I’m not going to lie, I’m pretty nervous. I’ve done everything I could to prepare, but it doesn’t feel like enough, you know?”

“I think I get it. I had to survive out in the wilderness after my exile, and I always felt like that whenever…”

Whenever she had to attack travellers for supplies—Lee Jia sighed. They had spent a lot of time on joint meditation, and sometimes it felt like they could read each other's minds, but it wasn’t always convenient or comfortable. They had found that the best solution was just to be as open with each other as they could.

“What was it like?”

Eui mulled the question over for a moment before answering.

“It was always scary. I never knew if some random traveller was going to be a mage or not. I had a pretty good intuition for guessing, but I didn’t always get it right. Even if it was just regular mortals, a simple robbery could easily become a fight for my life.”

It was a little uncomfortable discussing their pasts. They had both been thieves, but Eui’s methods were a great deal more violent than Jia’s had ever been.

“How did you overcome the fear?”

Jia ignored the intrusive thoughts filling in the answer as she listened to Eui. She wanted to hear it directly.

“Well, my options were to risk death or definitely die. When I thought of it that way, the decisions got a lot easier. Besides that, while I’m not exactly proud of it, it got a lot easier after a while. I did what I had to do to survive, and I’m never going to apologize for it.”

Jia nodded—she felt much the same way about her own past and couldn’t really judge Eui for it. As it often did, ruminating on their pasts reminded Jia of that frustrating question that had been burning in her head recently.

Who was Sun Jaehwa and what did they have to do with Eui’s past? Jia was also concerned about the connection between herself and this person who had something to do with Eui’s darkest hour. The silence stretched on awkwardly, and Eui gave Jia a reluctant look before coming to what seemed like a difficult decision.

“Ugh, ok. I know it’s been bugging you, Jia, and I really appreciate you not asking about it, but I don’t want you to be distracted tomorrow so I’m going to tell you...a little bit about how I got exiled.”

Jia shook her head and waved her hands urgently.

“No, you don’t have to, Eui. It’s fine, really—I’m getting better at ignoring it.”

Eui crossed her arms and hit Jia with one of her signature scowls.

“I already made up my mind, Jia, now just shut up and listen. Back home I was from a wealthy family—we weren’t nobles or anything, but we had pretty good status. I was kind of spoiled back then, to be honest. I had a good education and I was on track to be accepted as a student by the mage college in my city. To that end, I was attending a prep school and I hated it. The only reason I kept going was to please my parents and because I had—a friend.”

“Sun Jaehwa?”

Eui looked askance at Jia but shrugged and nodded.

“It’s weird that you know her name without me telling you, but I guess it makes sense. I did the same thing with that Lee guy. Anyway, yeah, Sun Jaehwa and I were close. She was from a noble family, so she had a lot of guys that were interested in her.

“One of those guys was a mage who didn’t really take no for an answer. He got really angry when she rejected him, and he attacked us. I knew he was a mage, so I didn’t really hold anything back fighting him. I guess I must have caught him by surprise because—well, I survived and he didn’t...”

Jia listened with rapt attention to Eui’s story—It was a half truth—but Jia knew that Eui wasn’t trying to deceive her. There were probably just details she didn’t feel comfortable sharing.

“So, that’s my story I guess—or some of it. Enough to ease your concerns, I hope?”

Jia nodded. There were still some pieces missing, but she wasn’t going to press any further. It was at least good to know that the person Jia reminded Eui of wasn’t the person that she had killed.

“Thank you, Eui. I appreciate you sharing that with me. I think I do feel a bit better now.”

“Good, now go get some sleep. You probably didn’t even notice since you’ve been so caught up in training, but you haven’t slept in a week and that means that neither have I. Get some proper rest before your big day.”

Jia got up and stretched, feeling the fatigue of the past week of training now that Eui mentioned it. She had a comfortable bed now and she’d barely even used it—it felt like such a waste. Soon she was curled up in the soft blankets and drifting off into a comfortable, dreamless sleep.

The day had finally come. After all the preparations Lee Jia had done, at last it was time for her duel with Yan Zhihao. The boy had been surprisingly quiet over the course of the week, apparently satisfied to simply issue his challenge and leave her to her own devices. Lee Jia had been half expecting him to harass her like a debt collector, but it seemed that not everything was exactly the same as her old life.

Before she made her way to the training field, she had one stop to make.

“Good morning Miss Lee! Are you excited for your big debut?”

Lee Jia grimaced at Dae as he greeted her cheerily.

“So excited I could throw up. I would have been perfectly happy to just quietly train my magic and martial arts without anyone ever knowing my name. Whether I win or lose, this entire duel is an endless pile of attention I did not want.”

Dae chuckled at Lee Jia’s griping.

“Hehe, a common sentiment among the wise, powerful, and talented. Sadly, it seems to be the destiny of those wise enough to avoid attention to have it foisted upon them. Perhaps this will make you feel a little better.”

Dae presented her with an impressive looking talisman. Unlike the paper talismans she was accustomed to, this one was made of a thin slip of some kind of fine, pearlescent wood. The inscription had been painstakingly burned into the wood, which had then been polished to a brilliant sheen. Lee Jia stared in awe at Dae’s handiwork.

“I might have gone a little overboard with it, hehe. I barely managed to get it finished in time, the finest lightning bolt talisman I’ve ever made. Eh, not that I’ve made very many, hehe.”

Lee Jia looked up from the talisman at Dae as he chuckled nervously.

“Dae, this is amazing! This is way more than I expected—and why is it so fancy, wouldn’t a regular one be fine?”

“Oh, uh, two reasons. First, while mages are capable of forcing low quality materials into accepting elemental infusions for our spells, it’s far more efficient to use higher quality materials—especially if the intended caster is a lower stage.

“Second, there is more to a talisman than simply the inscription. Ideally, every facet of a talisman represents the element contained within. In the case of lightning, which is considered to be the cleansing fire of heaven, I burned the inscription into a piece of thunderstruck birchwood and polished away any imperfections.”

Lee Jia realized she still had a great deal to learn about magic. She hadn’t known about any of that. She beamed at Dae, appreciating just how much of a favor he had done for her.

“I guess this is going to be my trump card, then. Is there anything special I need to know about using it?”

“Well, activation should be simple enough. It will be the same as any other talisman or formation. Ah! One thing—using a formation or talisman above your level does carry certain risks. You’ve done very well training your aura—actually ridiculously well, what did you do? Anyway, even so, this talisman will be very mana intensive by your standards and once you begin the activation, it will draw mana from you until it either has enough, or you run out entirely. So use it with caution.”

Lee Jia nodded and immediately felt a little bad for only half-listening. She’d been spending a lot of time with Dae during their tutoring sessions and he had a bad habit of never using one word where three would do. Could he not just say it would use a lot of mana?

“Thank you, Dae. You’ve gone way above your obligations with this, and the tutoring too. I don’t know how I can ever repay you.”

Dae shook his head and chuckled.

“Hehe, I wouldn’t be too sure Miss Lee. Thanks to you, I’ve already nearly achieved a second awakening—I think it will be any day now. That’s an immeasurable amount of potential right there. Besides, you shouldn’t underestimate just how incredibly talented you are. I suspect you’ll be a good friend to have in the future.”

With the last piece in place, Lee Jia and Dae made their way to the training field. There was an enormous crowd gathered there, to the point that Lee Jia wouldn’t have been surprised if most of the disciples had skipped lessons to watch this event. She recognized her small group of supporters waiting for her. Takeda Rika, Seong Eunae, Xin Wei, Guan Yi, and even Eui were waiting for her as she made her way to the stone platform in the center of the field.

Across from her, Yan Zhihao waited with his own much larger retinue, his grin as smug as the day she first saw him. After receiving some words of encouragement and support from her friends—and a sarcastic ‘good luck’ from Xin—Lee Jia stepped up into the ring and began to stride across towards Yan. His grin widened as he walked across to meet her halfway.

“I’m almost surprised you even showed up, Miss Lee. A few of my peers were under the impression that a beastkin wouldn’t honor their agreements. I had every confidence, of course.”

Lee Jia wanted to ask why he was ‘almost surprised’ then, but instead she simply smiled and nodded.

“Of course. I’m unfamiliar with your formal dueling procedures, so please bear with me.”

“Then allow me to explain for you and our impressive audience. We will begin on opposite sides of the arena, and a third party will signal us to start. The duel is full contact and will end when one side yields or is unable to continue fighting. You will be wagering the secret behind your second awakening against any demand which you can name after the duel is concluded. Understood?”

Lee Jia nodded.

“Then let’s not waste any more time!”

Lee Jia and Yan Zhihao took their places on opposite sides of the dueling ring—which was actually more of a rectangle. Lee Jia took stock of her resources—five shock talismans, the lightning bolt talisman, and as many mana shields as she could cast.

Unfortunately, mana shield wasn’t very efficient to cast and would only be partially effective against the semi-physical manifestations of fire that Yan Zhihao would be using against her. It wasn’t much, but she had always known she wouldn’t be able to overwhelm him with offensive power.

Lee Jia knew that the combination of her martial arts movements and her powerful, but limited spellcasting made her greater than the sum of her parts. She only had to hope that it was enough to overcome the huge advantage that Yan Zhihao had both in cultivation and in experience. Once they were in place, one of the lackeys from Yan Zhihao’s group called out.

“Ready!”

Lee Jia lowered herself into a mobile stance as she saw Yan Zhihao’s fiery aura begin to envelop him.

“Fight!”

Accustomed to her spars against Guan Yi and knowing that Yan held the advantage at range, Lee Jia immediately sprung into action, dashing straight towards him and activating one of her mana shield talismans.

The bubble of mana sprung into place around her, offering some limited protection. In her sparring, the mana shield had been able to negate one of Guan Yi’s long-ranged attacks completely before collapsing. Knowing that Yan’s fire wouldn’t be blocked entirely, they still considered that a hit.

Yan Zhihao smirked as Lee Jia ran straight for him, hurling a ball of fire to intercept her simple charge. While Lee Jia lacked Takeda’s uncanny ability to instantly change her momentum, she did have the natural agility and reflexes of a half-spirit, and her tail allowed her to keep her balance in positions that would be too awkward for a regular human.

Seeing the attack coming towards her, Lee Jia shifted her momentum slightly and dropped to all fours. The fireball flew harmlessly over her and by the time Yan had adjusted to throw another, she was back on two feet and running straight at him again.

“Not bad! I guess that tail of yours is more than just window dressing!”

Yan taunted her as he conjured a whip of flame and lashed out at her. The whip was a difficult weapon to dodge, especially since it didn’t entirely follow the normal path of a whip—controlled in part by his qi. Lee Jia hadn’t spent the week on mana meditation for nothing, however.

Her focus was razor sharp as she watched with her mana sense. The movements of his hand were a feint—it was the way his qi moved that would determine the behavior of the whip. In an instant, she figured out the trajectory of the whip and jumped through a safe space, twisting in mid-air to keep her extremities away from the whip and landing on her feet with a shock talisman drawn.

Now in range, Lee Jia quickly activated her talisman as Yan Zhihao reared the whip back for another strike. His attack was interrupted by a sharp crack as a small thunderbolt arced from the disintegrating talisman in Lee Jia’s hand and into Yan Zhihao, causing him to jolt and stagger backwards.

His fiery aura flared out at Lee Jia, but from this distance she was able to dance back away from it. With the gap between them widened once again, the fight was back where it had started, except that Lee Jia was unscathed while Yan had taken a direct hit from one of her spells—maybe she could do this after all.

“Hahaha, good hit! You seemed so intent on closing the distance that I didn’t expect an attack like that. I underestimated you! It won’t happen again.”

Lee Jia narrowed her eyes at Yan. She recalled from his duel with Takeda that he seemed to be a talker, but she hadn’t realized until being in Takeda’s position just how annoying it was. She didn’t waste time on banter, instead charging forward for another exchange even as Yan conjured a second whip.

With both whips flailing, the safe gaps were much smaller and it became difficult to slip through to get into range for another attack. Difficult, but not impossible. Lee Jia was so focused on dodging the two whips that she almost failed to notice a third, snaking out towards her from behind Yan Zhihao’s back. There was no avoiding it, so Lee Jia steeled herself to take the hit. The fiery tail whip broke her mana shield, letting some of the flames through to singe her as she retaliated with another shock spell.

Yan was staggered by the spell once again, and Lee Jia took the opportunity to dodge out of the way of his aura’s retaliation. This time, Yan predicted her movements, and she was caught by a fireball thrown to intercept her escape. Lee Jia attempted to parry the fireball as she had seen Takeda do, but she hadn’t practiced the technique enough and mistimed the burst of ki, receiving the fireball full on.

“Agh, fuck! Ow, ow!”

Lee Jia was forced to dive and roll to put out the flames, springing to her feet just in time to avoid a second fireball. Her right arm was badly burned, though she had managed to protect her face. She cursed as she realized that while her uniform was fine, two of the shock talismans hidden in her right sleeve had been caught by the flames and destroyed—she hadn’t even considered that possibility.

Across from her, Yan Zhihao held a pair of fireballs in his hands and grinned at her. While she’d managed to do some damage, he had clearly come out ahead in that last exchange, and things were not going the way she had hoped they would. Lee Jia gritted her teeth as she activated another mana shield and found that her mana was already below half. At this point she wasn’t sure she would even be able to cast Dae’s lightning bolt.

Lee Jia charged again, and Yan held nothing back. Having already revealed that he didn’t actually need hands to manipulate his fiery whips, he now used three at once to prevent her from closing the distance while at the same time pressuring her with a constant barrage of fireballs.

Just avoiding his attacks was taking every ounce of Lee Jia’s concentration, much less getting close enough to attack. Worse, Yan Zhihao was starting to advance on her, slowly backing her into a corner where she would have no choice but to face his whips head-on.

“Have I still not worn him out enough yet?”

Lee Jia grumbled through gritted teeth as he boxed her in. Yan Zhihao’s qi supply was unbelievable, and it was clear that she wasn’t going to draw out his trump card at this rate, which she knew would be her only hope of victory.

In an act of desperation, she drew her last shock talisman and began casting as she ran to the side. Predictably, one of Yan’s whips lashed out to intercept her. As it did, Lee Jia flung her hand out and unleashed her spell. The miniature lightning bolt struck out at the whip and then traced back along it, far surpassing the spell's normal range.

When the spell struck Yan Zhihao he was surprised but uninjured—most of the spell's power having dissipated by that point—but it had disrupted his concentration enough to destroy the whip through which the bolt had travelled.

He rallied quickly, catching Lee Jia with a combination of another whip and a fireball. To his surprise, Lee Jia avoided the whip and charged straight through the fireball, coming out the other side with minor injuries and no more mana shield.

“Hahaha! Fantastic! You’re nothing if not persistent, Miss Lee. Still, I’ve dueled mages before. You don’t have another one of those in you. Not if you plan on casting that spell you’ve been holding in reserve.”

Lee Jia froze momentarily, shocked. She quickly recovered in time to dodge another fireball, but narrowed her eyes and frowned at Yan Zhihao.

“What are you talking about?”

Yan Zhihao chuckled and shook his head, staring down his nose at Lee Jia condescendingly.

“Do you think you’re the only one who prepared for this duel? I know you’ve been scuttling around, collecting favors from the strongest mages among the students. You had one of them make a spell for you, and you’re holding it in reserve, hoping to catch me off guard with it, right?”

Lee Jia tried to school her expression, but couldn’t help the hairs on her tail standing on end, or the cold sweat at the back of her neck. As she watched Yan’s smug grin grow even wider, she growled and spat.

“Fuck my ancestors! This entire duel has been a farce! I can’t even get one edge without it being ruined. Fine! To hell with it, I’ll just have to rip your damn throat out with my own claws!”

Without hesitation, Lee Jia took out another talisman and activated it, before charging straight towards Yan Zhihao at full speed with a face full of fury and her claws fully extended. Yan Zhihao was taken aback but responded quickly, holding out his arms and beginning to inhale deeply.

He had barely even begun to collect his qi together for his ultimate attack before Lee Jia stopped her charge short, grinning—he’d fallen for it. The talisman she had activated was a blank, not a mana shield, and her angry retort and threats were a bluff to throw him off guard. From the moment she’d started her charge, she’d begun to power the lightning bolt talisman in the hopes that Yan Zhihao would try to put an end to the duel with his most powerful technique.

Lee Jia pulled out Dae’s talisman and held it out towards Yan Zhihao, grinning maniacally. She was far beyond the range of her shock spells, but she doubted it mattered for this spell. All of this happened in the space between heartbeats as Yan absorbed his flame aura to power his fire breathing technique.

A look of horror dawned on his face as he stood momentarily defenseless against Lee Jia, dumping mana into her spell. Her entire mana reserve drained away in an instant and then—

…

—nothing. The spell failed to activate. Lee Jia didn’t have enough mana to power the demanding talisman. However, just as she was about to resolve herself to getting roasted alive, she felt a pull within her—her ki!

She pushed her ki towards that pull and it immediately began to channel through her hand and into the talisman. Time seemed to slow down as it turned into a race between who could finish powering their ultimate attack first. As Dae’s talisman tore away at the meager remnants of her ki, Lee Jia started to worry that she’d self-destructed with her own attack. Then, with only a scrap of energy left in her body—a mere instant before Yan Zhihao’s preparations were complete—the talisman exploded.

KRAKOOM

The deafening boom was accompanied by a blinding flash of light as Lee Jia was knocked backwards and fell sprawling to the ground. Her vision was white and there was a loud, high pitched ringing noise in her ears as she coughed and tried to stand.

As she blinked away the white spots in her vision she saw Yan Zhihao lying face-down on the ground, smoke rising from his motionless body.
