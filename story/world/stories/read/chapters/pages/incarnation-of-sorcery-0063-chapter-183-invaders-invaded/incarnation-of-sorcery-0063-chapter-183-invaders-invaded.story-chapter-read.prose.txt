Warning Leaflet

Azugho G.

Normally, Ryland wouldn’t particularly care about things like attacks upon his person. He could shrug off the vast majority of magic that anyone might use against him, teach them a lesson, then move on. It was a tremendously rare case when an individual or a group actually gave him a reason to retaliate or seek them out.

Like when those insurrectionists had kidnapped Viren or when Valorous from the Flourinus Order had caused all those nefarious shenanigans to put everyone in danger.

Or, such as right this moment, when the Astral Invaders were determined to continue invading a world full of innocent people.

Which was why Ryland had travelled across the cosmos with a [Translocate] to appear right in front of their home world.

Realm 61 was much smaller than Abarosk. In fact, it was one of the smallest worlds in existence, though its total area wasn’t as small as might appear at first, thanks to the decently sized moon that orbited the planet.

Ryland deliberated how best to tackle the issue. He had practically destroyed their last invasion, and while that ensured no one had returned to confirm the tale of what had happened, word of just what had gone down had undoubtedly reached them. By this point in time, the forgean invaders had developed long-distance communication Glyphtech.

Glyphs of Communication—and any associated spells—had their range increased by every Tier the Glyphs went up. However, having seen evidence that the strongest invaders were Primordial-ranked at best, Ryland was pretty sure their Glyphs were Tier [15] or [16] at best.

Maybe Tier [18] if they had a hidden Empyrean they refused to risk unless it was a massive invasion.

Not that information relay was that important. They already knew he was present near their home. The moment Ryland had appeared, he had noted that a ward had been triggered.

There was a wide forcefield around the entire planet. But it wasn’t simply a hollow sphere or even a misshapen membrane surrounding the combination of the Realm and its orbiting body. Instead, they had constructed a three-dimensional miasmic field filled with mana acting as the trigger whenever a foreign presence entered the area.

“Impressive,” Ryland murmured. In truth, he was taken aback by the complexity of the Invaders’ defensive sorcery. Perhaps he would do well not to underestimate them.

That said, he noted the origin of the spell in no time at all. A powerful Inscription was etched on the entire moon, with the major Subglyphs sending out all that mana while others ensured they were ready to receive any signal to emerge from the mana field.

Ryland promptly attempted to destroy it.

His manasoul writhed into greater existence as he attempted to simply create a large enough rift across the moon’s surface. Breaking the Glyphs making up the Inscription would be enough to break the Inscription itself.

But it turned out there were more defences than he was aware of. Magic rose into the air around the atmosphere-less moon, more miasmic spells that drew away and ate his rifts. Hmm.

It didn’t take long for Ryland to note the mages taking action. They were placed right on the moon itself. Forgean Warmages. Not quite the sort that had accompanied the invaders on their meteors, but powerful sorcerers nevertheless. It seemed Ryland would need to deal with them personally before he moved on.

He wasted no time. “[Dragongod’s Spinespear].”

An enormous rift cracked open. The familiarity was almost nostalgic. A gargantuan bony tail emerged from the rift. It was large enough to dwarf the entire moon. Just as he remembered. There was no reason for Ryland to hold back, so he was holding nothing back really.

He was also too far away to hear what the verbal responses of the Warmages were, and honestly, since he had stopped using [Omniscience], he was hardly aware of what spells they were firing in response. Mostly because it didn’t really matter. The tail of the Dragongod wasn’t something most mages had a response to. Not even when they were working together.

A powerful crash sent dust and debris flying into the airless cosmos to remain suspended there as they slowly spread outwards. The enormous draconic appendage tore through the mages and their Inscriptions, ripping apart its surface, crushing any opposition.

It took only a minute or so longer before the entire tail had emerged from Ryland’s gigantic rift. Only a minute for it to wrap around the entire moon.

Unfortunately, it couldn’t destroy the moon entirely. Ryland didn’t want to go that far anyway. With how large the moon was in comparison to the planet it orbited, its destruction would cause the entire habitability of the planet to crater. Much as he was annoyed by their constant invasions, he didn’t want to cause these forgeans to go extinct entirely.

That said, he did need a permanent solution to their constant attacks. Destroying the magic they had on the moon was one thing. But he still needed a solution to their assaults.

He figured it out quite easily.

Most of the invaders would be dealing with the fallout of having their moon overtaken by a giant undead dragon tail. No doubt, they were trying to figure out how to deal with the stolen moon and all that, which meant Ryland had a perfect opportunity.

He [Translocated] yet again, this time appearing next to a large fountain in a lush forest. It had only taken him a few minutes with [Omniscience] to find the location.

Ryland placed down another rift. One that led to a very different Realm that was far, far off.

He waited a minute. Then another.

And only then did the first creature emerge. The beast was monstrous. As big as a house, with spiderlike legs that trawled over the ground and dragged bony protrusions along, its dark, emaciated body at severe odds with its new surroundings.

Yet, it didn’t seem to hate its new location. Not as much as Ryland had feared it might.

The beast turned to look at Ryland, almost questioningly. Its fang-filled maw growled, and its purple eyes glowed malevolently. Ryland raised a single eyebrow, then the creature turned and fled deeper into the forest. Not long after, more of the monstrosities emerged from the rift, sniffing the air for only a short moment before pelting into the trees.

There were a lot of problems with introducing new fauna and flora to a new world. Aside from the new entries’ compatibility with the local atmosphere and climate, there were issues for the hosts too.

For instance, bringing in new diseases the locals had no resistance to, disrupting local ecosystems and unbalancing the food chains, and so on. Problems that Ryland mostly eradicated himself.

He just wanted the Astral Raiders to deal with a bit of their own medicine.

Since a single rift wasn’t going to be enough, he went around creating more all across the small planet. No doubt, the invaders would start finding these rifts and start closing them with their miasmic magic. Ryland could have placed trapping Inscriptions and the like as strong safeguards against the invaders, but why bother? Sheer numbers would keep them busy.

By the time Ryland was done, he had already located the local town near the first forest he had visited. It was already dealing with a couple of beasts invading its outer barriers, taking them down without much trouble.

Satisfied, Ryland left them to it. This had gone on for long enough. With their new situation fully established, the invaders would be far too busy protecting their own homeland to consider attacking him.

He found Viren waiting at the Ivail Trade Guild. Just as he had promised the Guildmaster had treated Viren like he was a long-lost guest. They were happy to hear that the Astral Invaders were no longer going to be a major issue. The Guildmaster thanked Ryland over and over again, and constantly invited him to meet with all the dignitaries and others, which he also had to reject multiple times.

More importantly, their operation to collect the accretion streams was underway. Everything was in perfect order and by Ryland’s estimate, they had so far gathered approximately a third of what he needed for the ritual. The rest would come in time.

So for now, Ryland departed Abarosk, promising he’d visit them again before long. He wasn’t quite sure how long “before long” was supposed to be, so he didn’t bother elaborating.

“What exactly did you do to them, sir?” Viren asked when they were just the two of them again. His words emerged with a bit of bravery, as though he was afraid to hear Ryland’s answer and feared the worst. Poor boy still had a soft heart. Which wasn’t necessarily an awful thing.

“I did not annihilate their entire world,” Ryland said. “In case you were wondering.”

“Well, I guess that’s a relief.”

“Instead, I essentially set several dungeons all across their Realm, which endlessly pour forth a lot of monsters and other dangers that should keep the invading forgeans busy for the rest of their lives.”

Viren blinked hard several times. He didn’t seem like he could decide whether that was a good thing or not. “I guess that’s an interesting way to handle things.”

“Do you truly think so?” Ryland asked.

“Yes, sir. At least now they have their hands full with problems on their own world. But it isn’t a terrible setback, and their fate is in their hands again.”

Ryland looked up into the Abaroskian sky. This far away from the star being slowly eaten by a black hole, it all looked like one large star, thanks to how the lights from the two sources were overlapping. A star with a bit of a twist in the middle. Not that most people would have been able to note that, what with how blinding sunlight was.

“The sad thing about a great many people,” Ryland said. “Is that they often need something to constantly occupy their attention. For without something productive to do, they will often get up to mischief. Their thoughts will turn in negative directions.”

Viren looked a bit grim at that realization. It wasn’t a cynical thought. That was just the reality of things. Certain people had more proclivity towards evil, and besides outright ending them, the only other option was ensuring they never got the chance to exercise their malintent. It was a sad but inescapable truth of the universe.

“Are you ready to leave, Viren?” Ryland asked. “Our adventures on Abarosk have come to an end for now.”

He nodded, rather quickly at that too. Maybe this adventure had been a bit too much for him. “Yes, sir. I’m ready whenever you are.”

“Good. Let’s go. [Translocate].”

Another rift, and another step later, they were back on Rhel. It was nighttime, and Ryland didn’t spot anything untoward, so he asked Viren to take a break and relax for the time being.

Over at another end of the enormous Inscribed spell circle, Ryland started placing the accretion stream matter he himself had gathered. He would need a lot more for the purposes of the spell’s ritual, but that would come later through the efforts of the Ivail Trade Guild.

The whole Inscription glowed a shimmering white and blue when Ryland let the star’s energy drop down onto the Glyphs he had prepared. It was so bright, the sky of Rhel itself turned almost into early dawn several hours before sunrise. The blinding illumination even drew Viren away from where he had set up camp.

“Sir…” he said as he came over, marvelling at the lightshow he was now witnessing.

There was a question in his tone too, though he didn’t quite voice it. Maybe he had surmised that Ryland was working on his Inscription after gathering one of the most important ingredients to power the ritual.

“Can’t sleep, Viren?” Ryland asked. He paused. “Of course, that’s probably my fault because of all this light. Don’t worry, I’ll bring it down soon.”

Viren shook his head. “I’ll be fine, sir. I’m more curious what’s next.”

Ryland slowly wound down the spell. He could place the rest of the accretion matter where it belonged when the Trade Guild arrived with it. “Regardless, get some rest. We’re going to acquire the final piece we need for the ritual tomorrow.”

That brought a glint to Viren’s eyes. “You got it, sir.” He gave a salute before starting to head off. Though, before he did so, he apparently had one last question to ask. “What exactly is this final piece you need, sir?”

Ryland smiled. No doubt, Viren was wondering whether it would match up with their last ingredient. After all, there were few things in the universe that could surpass a stream of starry matter, the very origin of all life in the universe, slowly being dragged into and consumed by black holes, the greatest obliterator of the cosmos.

“I’m going to find what’s called an Ephemeral Colossus,” Ryland said. “Who are said to hold powerful, ancient magic. Including other Incarnations.”
