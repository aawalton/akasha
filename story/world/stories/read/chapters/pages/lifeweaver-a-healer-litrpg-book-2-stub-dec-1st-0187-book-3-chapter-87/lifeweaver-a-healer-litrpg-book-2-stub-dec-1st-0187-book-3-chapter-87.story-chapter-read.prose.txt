Nothing had changed. Luke told himself that as he forced his knees to function. Pulling himself up to the bars in the cell door, he showed his teeth in a mirthless grin. Yes, he had done all those things, but they needed doing. He was still the same person, not evil, not a psychopath. Telling himself that over and over again worked, at least a little. Like a mantra, he repeated himself.

"Not a monster. I'm not a monster."

Pressing his face to the bars like a madman, he kept going. "Not a monster."

"Not a monster," he said again as he reached with a thread into the lock and cut it apart.

The door swung open, and he stepped outside, gathering a bit of momentum. Luke's body listened to him. The shock was wearing off.

"Not a psychopath."

A demon stood at the far end of the corridor. It was pear-shaped and looked as if it were covered in some sort of growth. Even hefting a war hammer, the demon looked weak. Luke had been worried that the block being removed would make him afraid to face opponents in combat, but found that was not the case. The demon charged with a roar.

"Not a killer," Luke muttered, killing the demon as soon as it reached him. It was nothing compared to the greater demons that walked the streets of New York.

He peeked through the window-slot of another cell door and found it empty inside. Unsure why he'd even turned up in a cell block, Luke turned and walked toward the guard station where the demon had been rustling with something on the table. Turned out it was food. At least Luke guessed it was food to them. Congealed, misshapen pieces of... something. He didn't want to know. Sitting down in a rough-carved chair of some dark wood, he attempted to message people outside of the dungeon. It didn't work, of course. Not that he had expected it to, but he was desperate for news from Earth now that he knew what was going on. Was everyone up there going nuts? Like Luke?

"I'm not a monster," he hissed to himself.

This was bad. Really bad. Come on, Luke, calm down. You can do this. Luke closed his eyes and steadied his breathing, trying to control the chaotic storm of thought and emotions using him to battle it out against peace, quiet, and calm. Neither side was coming out on top, but the moment of silent struggle meant more mana regeneration. Perhaps his mind was falling to pieces with no way to piece itself back together, but at least he had mana. Too bad he couldn't heal this sort of thing. How the hell had the Singers managed to build something like this?

A scream bounced off the naked stone of the corridor, and Luke turned to face the darkness beyond the nearest set of flickering torches. That hadn't been far.

"I'm not bad," he whispered, pushing to his feet.

Another scream rang out. It was decidedly human. Someone in pain. A lot of it. Questing along the corridor, hurrying from torch to torch, Luke wondered if he'd entered some sort of endless corridor. As it turned out, it wasn't. It opened up into a larger chamber, the purpose of which became apparent even before he saw what went on in there. The smell of it. Blood, and a lot of it. Three people, two men and a woman, were stuck to wide pillars of wood, their hands and feet run through with thick, crude nails. A fourth person was in the process of receiving the same torture, with two humanoid-looking demons, their skin a deep red, working on her with an air of professional expertise. They never finished the job.

"They deserved it," Luke said, setting his jaw as he felt their disgusting infernal mana surge within him. He'd drained them for all they were worth before ending their miserable existence. The pain of infernal mana felt kind of good. Gave him something to focus on. Luke pushed outward with threads of mana, pulling the nails free even while healing the four victims. Only two of them were still conscious, one of whom was the woman with nails halfway through her feet. They looked up at him with wide, frightened eyes. Wanting to know more about them, Luke attempted to inspect the little group, but found he could not.

"Not a dungeon," he whispered.

"What?" the conscious man, a guy Luke's age wearing a leather jerkin, asked.

Luke eyed him. "Earth?"

"W-what's that?"

"Never mind," Luke said, shaking his head. He turned away from them and pulled up the hood of his robe. Having his face hidden felt good. "Not a monster," he whispered.

"T-thank you!" the woman shouted after him. "There are more of us here!"

The man added a couple of words that made Luke pause for a moment. "We're Lilineese!"

He turned back and shouted through the darkness, since he'd gone too far to see the small group. "You're Lilineese?"

The question hung in the air for a moment until the people came into view through the gloom. "Aye, we are."

"How long have you been down here?"

That question got a lot of confused looks, but the man who'd answered the first question answered. "We have always been here."

"This is the first time we didn't die," the woman who'd been in the process of getting nailed to the post said.

Luke frowned. "What do you mean?"

"We wake up here," the man said, "then we die. Over and over again."

"Forever," one of the silent Lilineese said.

"Forever," they all intoned.

This was more than a little creepy. What was he supposed to say to that? Another wave of guilt hit, and he had to struggle to stay upright. "I'm not a monster," he hissed. Looking up at them again, he realized he couldn't leave them. Before, he might have, since they would be nothing but a liability. But now? That line of thinking made him pause. Was this some kind of test? Were they watching him? No. What kind of question was that? Luke’s head was scrambled. Paranoia among all the other things? No, he couldn't abide by that. Earth couldn't afford him falling apart. Not now. Not ever.

Still looking at them, he realized he must look like some bogeyman out of a nightmare with the hood up, and lowered it. Some tension left their shoulders, but not all of it. Luke wanted to help them. He really did, but he couldn't.

"I'm sorry," he said. "I am not a monster, but I can't help you. Not now. Not like this."

Luke expected pleading or curses, and wasn't sure he would have been able to keep to his decision if that was what had happened, but none came. Instead, they nodded in understanding, and the man at the front spoke, "We know."

With that, Luke fled. He couldn't stand to look at them for a moment longer. Before, he'd grown to resent the coldness spreading through him, the detached and the uncaring. Now he wanted it back. It wasn't until it was gone that he realized how much of a crutch that mental block of Guide's had been. Now, he had to feel things, feel everything. Overwhelming, in a word. That didn't mean Luke was about to quit, however. If anything, it made him more determined to see this through. Even the thought of losing everyone was like a hot knife through his chest. Unacceptable, just unacceptable.

More demons guarded the cells and various torture chambers. Luke killed them all. Losing the mind block had done nothing to increase his fear or apprehension in a fight. A good thing, that, because he knew many more of those lay ahead of him. Drawing on infernal mana was a painful process that knocked the breath out of him each time, but his own Meta-Heart grew more used to it little by little, and if he wasn't mistaken, the conversion from infernal mana to his own type grew smoother and smoother.

Finding a set of stone stairs leading down, he took them. The next floor didn't have torches, but the air itself seemed to glow orange, casting the place in an eerie light. The demons grew stronger here, but were still nowhere near as skillful as the greater demons who had come to Earth. Something that looked like living chambers appeared on this floor as well, with the occasional demon sleeping in rough beds. The idea of a demon sleeping seemed ludicrous for some reason, but it made them a lot easier to kill, and he did so with no feelings of remorse or apprehension. That part of Luke, it seemed, was all him, and not something brought on by the mental block.

In one such room, Luke sat down to meditate. While his own mana was full, he wanted to make sure his Meta-Heart was topped up with healing mana and that at least one Mana Orb had some mana stored as well. But, more than anything, he needed his spiraling thoughts and emotions to calm the hell down. The urge to press on was still there, but there was something else he needed to do as well. Luke leveled up twice in the strange, pink dungeon. While the attribute points were spent, he had yet to pick out new skills, and he had a lot to choose from.

Meditation was... difficult. Emotions kept getting in the way, as well as memories in the form of images flashing by. It was like learning to meditate all over again. When he did get it after a lot of sweating and focusing, it was like a balm to his ravaged soul. Calm. Quiet calm. In that pool of tranquility, his mind made sense again.
