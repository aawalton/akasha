What made Lira Velora herself?

It was not being a maid in the castle: Dusting, cleaning, and organizing.

It was not working for Pallan: the years she spent forging lies and living in the shadows.

It was not magic, even if it was all she had been focused on lately.

Lira had been shaped long before all of that.

She learned to read at the age of three.

By that same year, she could already write, her small hand guided by her father’s patient instructions. The letters had not been lessons forced upon her, but something she had reached for on her own, drawn to them as if they had always belonged in her grasp.

Some people were born bathed in blood.

Lira Velora had been bathed in ink.

Ink, paper, knowledge, mind, and books.

That was her foundation.

That was what she understood.

That was what she should build upon.

Lira sat in her alcove, her father’s ledger open across her knees. The worn pages rustled softly as she turned them, her eyes scanning the familiar glyphs and their meanings.

She already knew some of the words she was looking for.

“Pika.” was "mind.", a component from the second spell she had learned.

“Asatan." was "paper.” She learned this one during her failed attempt to create the burn paper spell.

That left the rest.

"Books." Knowledge." and "Ink."

She searched more carefully this time, slowing her pace, reading each entry instead of skimming.

Her father’s notes were incomplete; she only had around 500 words rather than a full vocabulary.

“Linasara.”

She paused.

The translation beside it read: written work, most likely books.

She turned another page.

“Cognar.”

Understanding: knowledge in its simplest form.

Lira nodded slightly.

That left one.

"Ink."

She almost missed it.

“Tir.”

Lira leaned back slightly, closing her eyes for a moment as she let the words settle.

"Pika. Asatan. Linasara. Cognar. Tir."

Those felt right.

More right than fire ever had.

And especially more right than wind.

Those were not something she was forcing herself to learn.

Those were something she already understood.

The next step was to design a spell.

A simple one for a start: A cantrip.

Her first instinct was straightforward.

"Mind. Ink. Manipulation.":“Pika. Tir. Furshum.”

A substitution of one word, from a spell she already knew. A spell to move ink as if it were water.

It made sense. She already understood a similar structure from the elemental magic she had practiced.

But she dismissed it almost immediately.

Moving ink like water would be versatile, yes, but it would require constant control and focus. It would behave like the wind spell, unstable, demanding, and draining.

No.

That was not the path.

She thought back to the candle spell.

“Candira. Fila. Curad.”

The spell had a restriction; it only worked on candlewicks. But that made it one of the easiest spells to cast.

She concluded that the more narrow the purpose, the easier the execution.

The spell did not try to control fire as a whole.

It just ignited a wick.

That was all.

Lira opened her eyes.

If the first spell was candle, fire, ignition: target, concept, action…

Then hers should be similarly structured.

Paper. Ink. Writing.

Her gaze dropped to the ledger again.

She searched for the word she needed.

Writing.

It was not there.

Not directly.

She frowned slightly, flipping through a few more pages before finding something that should work.

“Kisar”, meaning “Symbol”. With the language using glyphs as writing, she figures symbols and writing were synonymous.

It would do.

Lira nodded once, satisfied.

Now she had the components.

"Asatan. Tir. Kisar." as in "Paper. Ink. Writing."

For the next step, she needed to understand them, not just as words, but as concepts.

She needed to enter the trance.

She pulled a blank sheet of parchment toward her and set a quill beside it.

She would be making a faux primer, just like before.

This time, she did not reach for another book about writing to copy the knowledge in it, even if her grand uncle's book was right there.

She did not need it. She was as much of an expert as any scribe.

She dipped the quill into ink and began to write.

“Writing is permanence.

A spoken word vanishes the moment it is uttered, lost to memory and time.

But ink endures.

It carries thought beyond the limits of a single mind.

It allows a man long dead to speak to the living.

It turns knowledge into something that could be preserved, copied, and passed around.

Writing is Truth. When memory fades writing defines the truth.

Writing is Art. In every curve, in every line, in every empty space hid beauty.”

Lira remembered to add the magical words before she got lost in her own writing.

Asatan. Tir. Kisar.

The moment the final stroke touched the parchment, something clicked.

Lira was about to start on the next passage when she froze in realisation...

Seriously, it was that easy…

It barely took her a minute to learn it.

There was no strain, nor any resistance.

She simply learned the spell.

It was there, complete and whole, as if it had always existed.

She stared at the page, then let out a slow breath.

So this was the difference.

This was what it meant to learn something that was already part of her.

Compared to wind, which she had to wrestle into shape, this came naturally to her.

Lira leaned back in her chair, her gaze drifting to the shelves around her.

If this were true…

Then what about other crafters?

A baker. with Flour. Fire. Transformation.

A blacksmith. with Metal. Fire. Shaping.

Old Marga, the cook. With cooking. Cutting. Fire. Taste. Fullness.

Lira’s lips curved slightly.

Perhaps magic was not meant to be studied by stuffy mages and old wizards but was just the next tool for master crafters.

It was meant for those who understood something deeply enough… and those who were passionate about something.

A power so easy to obtain for those who already struggled.

And a struggle for those who obtained everything easily.

She spilled a drop of ink on the paper

“Asatan. Tir. Kisar.”

The drop of ink began to flow across the paper, changing form from a blob to a fine script:

Lira Valora, maid, scribe, and now Ink Mage.
