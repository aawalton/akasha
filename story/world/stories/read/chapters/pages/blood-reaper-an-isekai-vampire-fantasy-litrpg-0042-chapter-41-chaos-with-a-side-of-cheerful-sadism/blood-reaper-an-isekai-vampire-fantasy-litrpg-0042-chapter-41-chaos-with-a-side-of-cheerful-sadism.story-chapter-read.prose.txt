This thing was ugly as sin.

Ace stood on his boulder, the Highland wind whipping past him as he studied the shuddering forest canopy at the bottom of the hill. A colossal form burst through the treeline, and its towering bulk snapped hundred-year-old trunks like matchsticks. It rose fifteen feet tall, a nightmarish fusion of primeval deer and ancient trees. Bark armor plated its massive body, and pulsing veins of emerald light flowed like sap beneath the wooden skin. Atop its elongated skull rose a crown of golden antlers that twisted toward the sky in impossible patterns. It curled its massive claws into fists and roared.

Great.

A pissed-off, horned forest spirit, or at least whatever passed for one in this hellscape.

“Oh, look, Sergeant!” The System floated beside him and happily clapped her tiny hands. “One of my favorites!”

In answer, he groaned.

Of course.

The creature halted at the forest edge, those antlers swaying as it surveyed the open hills. Ace stared into those piercing eyes that glowed like twin emerald pools—ancient, intelligent, and unmistakably predatory.

Those eyes found him. Locked on. Recognized him as a core threat.

Behind him, Tara’s urgent voice echoed off the cave walls as she shouted for the others. Panicked shuffling and confused voices filtered through the vines dangling over the entrance, but they weren't moving fast enough.

"Everyone up!" he shouted, his voice echoing off the hillside. “NOW!”

The monster's wooden limbs tensed, bark plates shifting and locking into place like organic armor. Dark fluid dripped from wounds along its flanks—evidence of some previous battle—sizzling where it struck the earth and sending up curls of acrid smoke.

And here Ace was, with no weapon. His Soul Meter was almost at zero from all his practice with Abyssal Armory—something he would never allow to happen again.

Yet another lesson learned the hard way.

It wasn’t even enough to summon a Duskblade, let alone slip into the Shadow Realm to mount a proper defense.

The Behemoth pawed at the earth, gouging deep furrows in the soil, its claws like gnarled roots. Its massive chest expanded as it drew in air, and moments later, it released a roar that shook the very hillside. It was a sound somewhere between splintering wood and primal fury.

Then, it charged.

The Behemoth moved with surprising speed for something so massive. Each thundering step shook the ground, sending vibrations through Ace's boots that rattled his bones. The open hills offered no cover, no defensive position except the boulder he stood upon—and that would provide only seconds of advantage.

The System twirled in the air beside him, her childlike form unnervingly calm. "Ooh, it's so fast for a plant-thing!" she cooed. "I bet those antlers would look splendid mounted on a wall. Or mounted through your chest. Either way!"

He heard scrambling behind him as his team got their shit together, but their confused voices were drowned out by the monster's approaching thunder. There was no time to coordinate. No time for complex tactics.

Just the rapidly closing distance and the certainty of impact.

The creature lowered its golden antlers, their sharp points aimed directly at his chest. If those connected, even his newfound vampire durability wouldn't save him. He'd be impaled, lifted, and torn apart—or worse, left to dissolve slowly in that acidic sap that dripped from the monster's wounds.

Fun.

With barely twenty feet between them, now, Ace held his ground until the last possible moment. His mind raced as he calculated the monster’s trajectory and velocity with cold precision.

Three seconds to impact.

Two seconds.

One.

At the last instant, he twisted sideways and hurled himself off the boulder. The monster’s antler scraped along his Thornplate Armor, sending tiny sparks of void energy shooting into the air. A muted thud slammed into his ribs—a blow that would have been lethal if he hadn’t been wearing the armor. The obsidian-infused plates shifted upon impact, distributing the force across his torso rather than allowing it to penetrate. Microscopic blood channels in the armor flared to life, glowing crimson at the point of contact before darkening again.

He landed hard against the ground and rolled. A rush of displaced air buffeted his body as the massive creature thundered past, its momentum carrying it forward—toward the cave.

The ground shook with each of the monster’s steps, and as Tara pulled aside the curtain of ivy, Ace’s gathered team froze in place. Their reactions varied. Rachel gasped in horror as the monster skidded to a halt just thirty feet away from them. Marcus swore loudly. Olivia held her staff in one hand and remained eerily silent, her eyes narrowing as she assessed the situation. Victor stepped out of the cave last with his whip at the ready, his expression shifting from annoyance to predatory interest when he saw the enormous beast.

The Thornhide Behemoth pivoted and once more locked eyes with Ace, apparently uninterested in the others.

"What the fuck is that thing?!" Marcus called out, still half-dressed and disheveled from sleep.

Rachel clutched her hands to her chest like a shield. "Look at those energy pulses beneath its bark. That… I don’t know why, but I can sense that they’re important."

“Real helpful,” Marcus quipped.

Victor didn't bother with analysis. He immediately inched to the right, circling toward the creature's flank, his eyes gleaming with the prospect of a kill. He didn't even look toward Ace or the others, already focused solely on his own agenda.

Wait.

The man was using Ace as bait.

That bastard.

"Victor, get back here!" Tara called.

He ignored her completely.

“How do I kill this thing?!” Ace asked the System.

“I can’t tell you that, silly goose,” she chided, wagging her finger back and forth. “That’s cheating.”

“You—”

With a frustrated grunt, he opted not to finish that thought.

Ace ducked and rolled as the beast rumbled past yet again. This time, it slipped on the steep Highland terrain, and the hulking beast went tumbling. It couldn’t stop before it hit the treeline, and its massive form smashed a tree at the edge of the forest to splinters. The ground shook beneath his feet when it finally skidded to a halt, gouging deep trenches in the earth as it turned. Dark fluid dripped from wounds along its flank—injuries from some previous battle, he figured—and the pitch-black goop sizzled against the forest floor. As it hit the dirt, a puff of acrid smoke rose into the air.

The Behemoth shook its massive antlered head, its emerald eyes flashing with rage at missing its target. It pawed the ground, wooden claws tearing chunks of soil and vegetation, before lowering its head for another charge.

But its target shifted.

This time, it aimed directly at the cave entrance where Tara stood with the others.

"Scatter!" Ace shouted. "Don't let it corner you!"

As it rumbled past, Ace ran toward the treeline. He snatched a fallen branch as long as he was tall and hoisted it over one shoulder.

“Ooh, a makeshift spear!” the System said as she appeared beside him. “Rather primitive, I suppose, but clever nonetheless!”

“Will you shut up?” he asked.

“Nope.” She closed her eyes and, with a chipper tilt of her head, smiled.

At the top of the hill, his team dispersed as the monster charged, but they weren’t fast enough. Rachel stumbled, evidently overwhelmed by primal fear. The Behemoth adjusted course, sensing the weaker prey.

"Rachel, move!" Ace sprinted toward her, his hand gripping the branch tightly as he pinned it to his shoulder.

The creature closed the distance with terrifying speed.

Fifteen feet.

Ten.

Five.

With no other choice, Ace aimed for the back of the thing’s head and hurled the branch like a javelin. It missed by a margin and slammed into the beast’s shoulder blades, splitting the bark plates with the sharp crack of a falling tree.

The Behemoth arched its back and screamed, the thunderous shriek echoing across the hills and forest alike. Dark sap erupted from the wound, splattering the grass and instantly devouring everything it touched.

Rachel stumbled again, but she managed to scramble away on all fours, instinct apparently overriding her dignity as she clawed her way out of harm’s reach. Droplets of sizzling goop fell inches from her heels as she kicked up grass and dirt alike, until she finally righted herself and sprinted out of reach.

The victory was short-lived.

The branch poking from the Thornhide Behemoth’s back dissolved in seconds. The bleeding sap ate away at it with stunning speed, and the damage healed before their eyes. A flash of green light knit the wound closed, and it was like the hole had never been there.

It peered over one shoulder at Ace, and those glowing emerald eyes narrowed.

"It regenerates!" Rachel called out, her voice shaking in fear.

“No shit, Rachel!” Marcus yelled.

“Okay, okay,” the System raised her hands in mock surrender. “I’ll help you out, Sergeant. Spear it through the eye, and you’ll kill it.”

He cast a sidelong glare at her, and that mischievous smirk on her face made him doubt the advice.

However, there wasn’t much else to go on, and it was worth a shot.

Ace scanned the battlefield, his mind racing. The monster's momentum had carried it past the cave, giving him precious seconds to form a plan. His gaze locked onto a cluster of boulders near the edge of the clearing, and then to the massive fallen tree the Behemoth had knocked over in its first charge.

"Alright, everyone!” he called. “Time to face this together! Olivia, Marcus—drive it toward those rocks," he called, pointing to the formation. "Tara, stay back. Rachel, identify weaknesses. Victor, you—"

But Victor was already running, circling behind the creature with a predatory focus—and ignoring Ace entirely.

Of course.

The Behemoth turned again, its wooden skin flexing and shifting with a series of sharp cracks. It charged toward Ace, who waited until the last possible second before diving sideways. He rolled to his feet and sprinted again toward the fallen tree.

"Tara, stay back,” Ace ordered, doing his best to coordinate amid the chaos. “We need your healing.”

“Right!” she replied.

He scanned the hilltop again as the beast prepared to charge once more. “Rachel, I need information—analyze that thing, find weak points. Marcus, Olivia—flanking distractions, keep moving, but don't engage directly. Even if Victor’s going rogue, the rest of us need to work as a unit."

“Got it!” Marcus shouted.

Rachel nodded, and though her voice shook with terror, she scanned its body. "Hit the joints! Also, the underbelly where the bark is thinner!"

"Good!" he shouted, grabbing a sharp, broken branch from the tree's splintered trunk. “Keep it distracted!”

Olivia darted forward with fluid grace and slammed her staff against its legs before dancing away from its retaliatory swing. Marcus, despite his fear, managed to hurl stones at its face, each one striking with uncanny accuracy.

Ace wedged the improvised spear between two boulders, angling it upward. If he could just get the monster to charge again, he might be able to end this.

"Hey Antlers!" he shouted, circling until he stood directly behind his trap. "Too slow to catch one measly vampire?"

The Behemoth shook its massive antlered head and charged again, but instead of coming for Ace, it barreled directly at the closest moving object—Marcus. The man barely dodged a sweeping thorn-covered limb that would’ve skewered him right in the skull if he hadn’t moved.

Phew.

Close call.

“Over here, you overgrown log!” Ace shouted again.

The monster's emerald eyes swiveled toward him just as Victor launched himself at its back, slashing at its neck with his whip. It wrapped around the beast’s neck, and the shadow-drenched coil cut deep into wooden flesh. Acid blood sprayed in a wide arc.

Right at Ace’s team.

Some of the bubbling sap hit Marcus's arm, and he screamed as it ate through his sleeve and into the skin beneath.

"Victor, get out of the way!" Ace shouted, his anger rising as the rogue vampire continued his reckless assault.

"Not my problem if the telepath is too slow," Victor snapped.

Tara ran in a wide arc around Victor and the beast, heading for Marcus—good. Maybe she could heal him before the man lost his arm.

Victor launched into another strike, and another wild spray of acid sap coated the ground inches from Tara’s feet. If Ace didn’t know better, he would’ve thought that the man had deliberately tried to hit her.

Olivia darted in from the opposite side, her movements graceful and precise as she struck at the behemoth's knee joint and danced away before the counterattack could land. The monster roared, the sound reverberating through the clearing like thunder.

"That's it," Ace called. "Target the joints, stay mobile!"

The beast suddenly shuddered, its bark plates shifting as something emerged from its back—dozens of wooden spikes that quivered like porcupine quills.

“Fuck,” Victor said as he jumped off and rolled across the ground, pulling his whip free of the beast’s body.

The System clapped her hands in delight. "Ooh, projectiles! Duck!"

The warning came too late. The Behemoth flexed, and spikes shot outward in all directions. One grazed Ace's shoulder, another embedded itself in a tree inches from Rachel's head. Two speared the ground at Tara’s feet. Marcus, still recovering from the acid burns, took three to the leg, the impact throwing him backward to crash against the cave wall.

"Marcus!" Tara screamed, immediately rushing to the fallen vampire.

Ace watched his coordinated plan collapse in seconds. Victor continued his solo assault, more acid blood spraying in every direction. Rachel had frozen in terror, and Marcus lay unmoving with wooden spikes protruding from his thigh. Only Olivia maintained her composure, dodging another swipe seconds before it could cave in her skull.

"We need a new plan!" she shouted.

The Behemoth turned its attention to Tara as she knelt beside Marcus, its massive form looming over her. Acid dripped from its wounds, hissing against the ground inches from her feet.

"Tara, move!" Ace shouted, desperation fueling his sprint toward them.

He knew he wouldn't make it in time. His Soul Meter was nearly empty, his team in chaos, and a fifteen-foot regenerating monster was about to crush the only person who'd shown him genuine kindness since he'd arrived in this hell.

The cold, tactical part of his brain tallied the odds and came up with one answer:

They were screwed.
