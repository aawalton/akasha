Thorfinn walked around the mine. Already, his men had been dispersed to find and kill all the native inhabitants surrounding the island. Of course, Relas Touch had already briefed him on the situation of the Tora mine, and the location, in his opinion, was a prime one.

It was almost unheard of in all of Ra’ahal for a Tora mine to be situated on an island of its own. Which was why the moment his feet touched the ground of this baby world, he understood why the Relas Touch was reluctant to part ways with the mine.

Even as far away as he stood from it, the pressure that the Tora ores emitted brushed against his aura, and with the amount of pressure it put on his aura, he could tell that this mine alone was worth its weight in gold.

But he digressed. The mine wasn't his to claim. It was Relas Touch, and he knew that if he claimed the mine and decided not to return it to them, they would simply double down by sending another mercenary to retrieve it from him.

Of course, he would kill that one, but he didn't doubt the resources of Relas Touch were enough to keep sending more until he buckled and died, and then the mine would inevitably be returned to them. Besides, Thorfinn didn't care much for the mine, just for the rewards that he would receive for delivering the mine into the hands of Relas Touch.

A gateway to the obsidian throne.

Relas Touch had offered him a clear path into hell, straight to the throne room of the demon he had hunted for his whole life.

He’d plotted, planned, and prepared for this moment from the very first day he had escaped the invasion of the demons on his homeworld via Relas Touch.

Thorfinn had seen them in their droves as they had attacked them in his homeworld, Narbana.  They had been merciless, bitter, unrelenting, unyielding, and most of all, brutal.

The armies had been led by Demon King Zephyr the VII, the conqueror of worlds. It was impossible for him to ever forget after witnessing the demon's power and brutality firsthand.

Narbana had been new to the apocalypse. His people had still been learning the ropes of the brutal lifestyle imposed on them by the system. They’d just begun getting used to the skills, the thrills, the death, the power that the apocalypse had given to them.

How could they have known that it was all just a ploy by the system to fatten them up for the reaping?  The apocalypse had been used to make them more appealing for the invasion of their homeworld.

In the end, they were ill-prepared for what was about to come.

They had received the warning from the system that the demons had won the bid to invade, but they hadn't paid it any heed.

The infighting caused by the apocalypse amongst the different ogre tribes was fueled by the power that could be gained by killing another. The distrust for each other had eaten deep within the short time that the apocalypse had begun, and now the co-operation that had once filled the different tribes had vanished into thin air.

Many who had different vendettas hidden within their hearts finally struck as the apocalypse gave them the power to act on their desires.

That was how it went, until each and every ogre with enough ambition sought to kill and dominate. And so the message for the invasion hadn't been treated with the urgency that it required.

If only the strongest of them who hadn’t been so self-centered and had done what was necessary by coming together to prepare for the invasion, then maybe they would have had a chance at surviving.

But no.

Each lord and lady stuck to their own stronghold, believing that they could survive the invasion by their own power.

What a folly that was.

A few had indeed managed to survive the invasion, just like himself. But others did their best to flee with each opportunity granted to them, while some of those branded as the strongest of their world had realized that they could not win such a war, and so they swore fealty to the Demon King, selling over their soul and their own kind just so they could live.

Thorfinn was generous to call it an invasion; the truth is that it was anything but. It should have been called a massacre. And it lasted for a handful of weeks before it was all over.

All of a sudden, Narbana, which the ogres had ruled since time immemorial, had experienced a regime change—with the demons becoming the new rulers of the land.

Of course, the majority of demons themselves had withdrawn to their homeworld once victory was assured, installing ogres who had sold their souls to the demon king to govern the land while reporting to the demon prince, who had been given reigns over the entire world.

Just the thought of his homeworld and race being under the rule of demons was enough to make Thorfinn’s blood boil.

He could still remember his short stay under the rule of the demons.

He remembered how they behaved—pillaging the lands, killing ogres, desecrating and wiping out some tribes, forever.

Thorfinn had watched chief after chief that he had respected bend the knee to the demons. He’d been young then, a young man, barely into his 20th cycle when the invasion had begun.

And how naive to think that the strong could ever care about the weak. He now knew that they only cared about themselves.

So he played his part as prisoners of hell, watching as they stole all the resources of their people.

The Tora mines that had appeared on their homeworld changed ownership to the hands of the demons.

The infernal creatures still gave them enough to improve their weapons and armor so they could fight more beasts in the land. But that was it.

He also remembered how they were required to pay tax to the regent demon prince.

Horrid times, but all that was about to change, and it's because that young version of him, still twenty cycles old, had taken the unknown risk of world hopping as a miner, an opportunity offered to some by Relas touch.

Thorfinn believed that even if he died in the process of changing worlds, it would be a more worthy death than being a slave to the demons themselves.

As the All Father would have it, he survived, and the world that he had hopped to was named Palanthon.

Palanthon. A world teeming with creatures, native inhabitants, just as odd to him as the demons had been. But Torfinn hadn't minded. He had bided his time, getting to know the native inhabitants even as he completed his contracted ten cycles of mining.

He learned their ways and how to fight, earning a name for himself in the process.

Occasionally, he was required to hop onto other worlds to mine for Relas Touch, but that came to an end as soon as he finished his ten-cycle contract with the mining powerhouse.

Relas Touch had heard about how ruthless he was when it came to capturing baby worlds, and so they offered him an improved contract, but he refused, preferring to pursue his own advancement until they started offering him a mercenary contract.

That worked for Thorfinn because, as long as he did his job, he was allowed to live wherever he wanted to. And with the way their jobs occasionally gave him opportunities to vent the rage growing in his heart, he continued to work for them.

Thorfinn was the one they talked to whenever t their escapades on a baby walk went wrong.

His job was usually one of two: to protect a mine or to retrieve one reclaimed by native inhabitants. Either way, Thorfinn did what he had to do.

Baby world after baby world. He went killing, and killing until his entire frame was soaked in blood. But it was what was needed so that he could earn enough to build his private band army of mercenaries, and so he had worked for Relas Touch for another ten cycles more before putting down his axe.

At 40 cycles, he had grown indifferent to the deaths. But his rage towards the demons only burned hotter, forcing Thorfinn to ask questions and dig deep into what the demons were as he tried to understand how he could eradicate them.

They were nothing but scourges that he needed to clean both for himself and for future other worlds that they were going to invade.

Thorfinn had no illusions that he was better than them, as he knew that he himself had killed countless inhabitants of different worlds, but he had a good reason for doing so. He needed to shed their blood so that he could end this scourge that was the demon.

He had to destroy their roots, and to do that, he had to get into hell. But he hadn't had a way to do that… until now.

Rela's touch had given him an offer too good to pass up, which was the only reason why he had decided to come out of retirement once more to kill. The offer was worth every muscle he stretched.

Killing a bunch of Native inhabitants in the baby world for a route straight to hell was a better deal than he could have ever imagined, and he would take pride in doing it.

Very soon, he would have his revenge, and the entirety of hell would feel his wrath. They would know the retribution for their actions.

Thorfinn would be the answer to so many prayers; he would gladly be the tool used to grant the dying wish of the many whose lives had been taken away so that the demons could continue to expand across the multiverse.

His left eye twitched at the thought. Thorfinn couldn't wait, but it wasn't going to be much longer now. Already, this was the second day, and in a few hours, the system would offer him the chance to claim the mine as his own. All he would have to do then was return it to Rela Touch.

As far as he was concerned, the matter was already settled.

The absence of his ogres, whom he had sent out to scout, did not so much as bother him.

Amongst the five who had come with him to the native world. Only two were worth even noting, while the other three had been the bottom barrel of his roster.

He had decided to give them a chance to experience bloodshed in another world and improve themselves. And even if they all did not return by the time it was over, he did not care.

As long as he could claim the mind when the time reached, that would be enough for him.

Thorfinn nodded to himself as he walked back out of the mine and onto the open courtyard, waiting, his mind wandering until he heard the footsteps of his men.

Thorfinn waited until they reached him, and only then did he turn to look at them. Only three had returned.

“My Lord, these are all the native inhabitants we found on the island,” Yaris—the highest leveled ogre that had followed him to the native world—said as he kicked three hideous-looking creatures in front of him to their knees.

Thorfinn stared at the three native inhabitants who had been forced into a kneeling position before him, with a rare smile that didn’t reach his eyes.
