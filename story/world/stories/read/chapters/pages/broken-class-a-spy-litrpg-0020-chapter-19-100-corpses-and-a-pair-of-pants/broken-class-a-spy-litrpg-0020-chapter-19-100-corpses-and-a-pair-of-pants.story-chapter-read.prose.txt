Arthur skewered his 100th beast and checked the leaderboard.

Arthur – 100 kills

Rowler – 3 kills

…

…

…

He snorted. This wasn’t a hunt, it was XP farming on easy mode. The real grind wasn’t the killing. It was the post-murder paperwork: core extraction, body cleanup, inventory juggling. At this point, he was less monster slayer and more magical janitor.

But the results were clear: his lead was untouchable.

“I could take a nap,” Arthur muttered, “and still win this damn thing.”

“Don’t tempt fate, boy,” Drogan grunted. “Also, naps are for the weak.”

“Duly noted, O wise and muscular one.”

With the sun dipping low and his lead firmly locked in, Arthur decided it was monster snatching time.

Arthur had already cleared this part of the forest. So he sprinted east, kicking on Mana Body for speed and activating Mana Sight to light the way. The world flared to life in pulses of blue and green, mana trails dancing like ghostly threads across his vision.

After a few miles, new signatures emerged. But they weren’t monsters. Five concentrated glows moved together in a tight, coordinated cluster. Not wandering. Not hunting. Marching.

Guardians.

“Careful,” Drogan warned. “If they’re Hunt participants, they’ve got every reason to gut you on sight. Fewer contenders, better odds. And you, my boy, are painting a target the size of a dragon’s ass with that kill count.”

“Relax,” Arthur muttered. “I’m not picking a fight. I’m just watching.”

He darted forward, staying low. Leaves rustled, but the wind was on his side. Always upwind, always out of sight. When he drew close enough, he pushed mana to his ears. The Mana Body skill flared subtly as he focused. The world sharpened, filtering in conversations from over a hundred feet away.

The group was made up of three men and two women, all dressed in elite gear - custom leather, light chainmail, engraved cloaks, even an archer with a rune-carved bow. Their formation gave them away: one warrior in front, two in flanks, a mage and rogue covering rear. Professionals. Confident. Young, but not reckless.

“…we’ve been walking for hours,” the mage said. Her voice had the snappy pitch of someone running out of patience. “And not a single beast. This place is supposed to be crawling with them.”

“I know,” the warrior in front replied, sweeping his sword in a wide arc through the air, “which is why we’re keeping formation.”

“We shouldn’t be keeping anything,” the rogue muttered. “We should be leaving.”

“Seconded,” said the archer, scanning the trees with glowing eyes. “Something’s wrong. There should be monsters by now.”

Arthur crouched behind a mossy ridge and grinned.

Of course there were no monsters. He’d taken them all.

This area used to be the most popular hunting ground because the monsters here were Earth-aligned: high defense, slow offense. Great for practice, terrible for trauma. But now, it was empty.

Because Arthur had systematically captured every monster dumb enough to nap outdoors.

“Leader,” the mage said, her voice lowering, “this area feels… abandoned. Like everything ran away.”

The party leader’s frown deepened. “You think something bigger scared them off?”

“I know something bigger did,” the rogue snapped. “This is a predator-free zone. The only time beasts avoid a region this large is when there’s a stronger apex around.”

“Then we leave,” the archer said.

The leader hesitated but finally nodded. “We double back. Formation hold. No noise.”

They turned.

Arthur followed.

---

By nightfall, the team had set up camp beneath the towering redwood-like trees - giants with bark like old stone and branches lost somewhere twenty feet up. Their bedrolls formed a loose circle near a small, carefully banked fire. Two members took their first watch, eyes sharp, nerves sharper. The forest was too quiet, and they knew it.

Arthur settled behind a tree a couple hundred yards away. Through Mana Sight, the five pulses still glowed below. Flickering like trapped stars.

He waited until the sentries grew bored and started chatting about who had the best odds in the Great Hunt. Then, quietly, he reached into his ring.

He summoned a sleeping Grubtusk. It snorted awake, sluggish and confused. Arthur didn’t give it time to adjust. He hurled a rock at its flank, then darted past, letting the stench clinging to his clothes waft straight into its face.

The Grubtusk caught a whiff, recoiled and then bolted straight toward the Guardians’ camp.

“What the...MONSTER!” one yelled.

The entire camp jolted awake.

“I see it!” shouted the archer, drawing her bow.

The Grubtusk squealed and bolted, right past the campfire.

Excited chaos erupted. For the first time since arrival, the team had prey. They surrounded the beast with practiced movements. The mage flung earth spikes; the warrior charged with his sword blazing. The Grubtusk, now wide awake, fought back.

Arthur observed, fascinated. They were good. Not terrifying, but efficient. A clean kill was maybe thirty seconds away.

Which was when he released two more monsters.

Right behind their supply packs.

“Behind us!”

“Two more!”

“We’re surrounded, leader?!”

“Fall back! Leave the bags. Regroup at ridge marker three!”

They vanished into the night, their polished boots pounding into the soil.

Arthur waited five minutes.

Then stood up from his hiding spot among the trees.

---

The packs were of good quality. He rifled through the main one and found survival tools, flintstones, dried rations, and a small alchemy kit. He didn’t need the food anymore; monster cores gave more mana than dried jerky ever could.

But tucked into a side pouch was something far more valuable: coin. Real currency. Good for bribes, transport, gear upgrades.

And then came the treasure.

Clothes. Real clothes.

A shirt. Trousers. Even undergarments. All standard-issue Guardian travel wear - soft, sturdy, and blessedly intact.

Arthur looked down at himself.

What used to be rags from the Darvaza mine had all but disintegrated. His body had healed and grown stronger, but his clothes hadn’t kept pace. At this point, he was basically butt-naked.

“By all that is sacred and swole,” Drogan groaned. “Finally. Put something on.”

Arthur laughed as he pulled on the trousers. “You’re just mad you don’t have glutes anymore.”

“You’re lucky I’m dead,” Drogan muttered. “Back in my day, walking around half-nude was basically a duel invitation.”

Arthur slipped into the full set, tightened the belt, and tucked the coin pouch away. The clothes were clean, solid, and surprisingly comfortable. Not custom noblewear, but miles ahead of the shredded disaster he'd been parading around in.

He stood straighter.

For the first time since arriving in this brutal world, he didn’t feel like an escaped prisoner. He looked like a hunter.

“Well, look at you,” Drogan said with mock solemnity. “A proper warrior. Clothed, dangerous, and marginally less embarrassing.”

Arthur smirked. “Marginally’s generous.”

He looked out into the trees where the Guardians had vanished. He’d gotten what he needed: food, coin, clothes. More importantly, intel. He’d seen how they fought, how they panicked. Guardians weren’t invincible, they were just trained.

So was he. Just in different ways.

“You planning to go after them?” Drogan asked.

“Eventually,” Arthur said. “But not to fight. Not yet.”

He tapped the ring on his finger. Dozens of monsters still inside. Mana cores waiting. Strength to be harvested.

“I’m going to get stronger first.”

He walked back toward the mountains, boots crunching underfoot.

“Then?” Drogan asked.

Arthur smiled.

“Then I go say hello to civilization.”
