- Kivamus -

The temple bell had already rung nine times by the time Kivamus approached the northeast gate.

The sun had risen above the Arakin mountain range in the east, but the morning was still reasonably cool compared to what would come later. In another few hours, the roads, roofs, and open ground would be baking beneath the peak summer heat.

Duvas walked beside him. Hudan stayed on Kivamus's other side, with four other guards walking around them in the usual box formation.

Syrene, Lucem, and Clarisa walked a short distance ahead. Trevalo had joined the group as well this time. He had been present in the manor to check his horses and wagons, so Kivamus had told the merchant that he could join them to visit the northeast which would also give them time to talk on the way. The merchant had agreed, more than willing to see the sight of three dead adzees in daylight.

No one in the group had risen early though.

Most of Tiranat had remained awake till the early morning hours, Kivamus included. He had heard about the gathering in the new market block and gone there to see it for himself. People from all four longhouse blocks had filled the open ground around several fires, eating, talking, and celebrating the village's victory. And even then, the new larger market block had more than enough empty space to let even more people sit there. That space would become vital in the future as the population of Tiranat grew.

The festive atmosphere was contagious and the cheering had grown louder when Kivamus arrived. He had sat with the guards, while some other villagers came to talk to him or to thank him and the guards for protecting everyone. It had felt… nice, that he had been able to help these people—his people—live to see another sunrise, so their families would not have any reason to cry, so their children wouldn't grow up as orphans.

It had worked, and nobody had died, this time at least. Only time would tell whether it remained like that in the coming months.

He had stayed longer than he intended, mainly because he was relieved to see how quickly the fear of the previous nights had turned into celebration. He had heard the village merchants had done lucrative business last night, selling food, ale, snacks and what not.

That same mood had continued outside the manor that morning.

The crowd waiting for work assignments had been larger than usual, even though most of the villagers directly reported to their foremen these days. They had come to thank the manor guards and hear the final account of the attack. The guards had been more than willing to regale them in stories of how they had bravely defeated the beasts—without any mention of the scorpions, of course.

Even the newest refugees present among the workers had cheered just as loudly as the rest after hearing a blow by blow account of the battles while they waited for Duvas to allot them to whichever worksite needed them the most today.

Kivamus had been glad to see that enthusiasm. The sooner the refugees began thinking of Tiranat as their home, the less likely they were to repeat everything they saw to passing merchants or travelers, whether for coin or even just for the usual gossip sharing. More importantly, people who felt that they belonged here would care far more about helping the village survive, including by joining up as manor guards whenever the next round of recruitment was undertaken.

Feroy had also returned from the southwest before the daily work assignments began. He and a group of guards had followed the wounded adzee's trail for several hours. The beast had apparently been far stronger than it had looked during the night. Its blood trail had led deep into the forest before becoming too faint to follow safely.

Feroy hadn't found the wounded beast, but he had confirmed that it was nowhere near Tiranat.

Whatever remained of the pack had moved on.

The workers and foremen had heard that, and the farms, coal mines, road construction crews, and the industrial area would return to normal work from today. People were still talking about the attack, but they were no longer expecting an adzee to emerge from the trees every time they left the walls.

Trevalo had also been telling about his recent trip to Kivamus as they walked. The coal barge had already left Cinran. It would return in roughly two weeks, and Trevalo expected to complete two more journeys before then, carrying six wagon loads of coal each time.

His caravan would remain in Tiranat today so the horses could rest. Any animal that still looked exhausted tomorrow would be replaced temporarily with one from the village stables. Trevalo would then leave the following morning with another six loaded wagons.

After that, six wagons would no longer be enough.

Coal production had risen to an estimated 60 wagon loads per month now. So Kivamus had told Trevalo to rent a seventh wagon from Cinran before his next return journey.

For now, the caravan would continue travelling with an escort of eight well-trained guards. If it became much larger, Hudan would have to assign more.

Thankfully, Tiranat now had 120 guards, including the women. Nearly a third were still new recruits, but Nesser trained them every day, often from morning until evening, despite the persistent weapon shortage. At least there were enough wooden training weapons, which meant the shortage of real swords, spears and such wasn't affecting the training. During the hottest hours, he shifted the recruits into the shade or allowed them time to rest before continuing.

Kivamus was no military instructor, but he often shared whatever modern training principles he could remember from a friend in London who had gone through military training. His knowledge was incomplete, and he knew that it wouldn't immediately turn Tiranat's guards into modern professional soldiers, certainly not without having any kind of equipment which armies on Earth used, but the training was still making them stronger, more disciplined, and better able to work together.

They were already improving.

Last night's victory had proven that.

Clarisa stopped near the open gate, gawking for a moment. "They are huge…!”

Three enormous carcasses lay outside the northeast wall.

Kivamus slowed as the group passed beneath the gate.

He had heard Hudan and Feroy describe the beasts, and he had even seen the lone adzee which had attacked last winter, but the descriptions hadn't fully conveyed their size or how large a fully grown adzee might be. Even lying motionless on its side and partly butchered, each adzee was still nearly the same height as him. They really were huge!

Their bodies were thick through the shoulders, their legs sturdy and powerful, and their paws large enough to tear a man apart with a single strike. Maran had been lucky last night to only get a gash in his legs. The guard had already woken up in the morning although it would take weeks before he could walk on his feet.

Lucem stared at the nearest adzee with his mouth slightly open.

A small crowd had already gathered outside the gate, kept well back from the work by a line of guards. Most were elderly residents, women, and children who didn't have to go to work that morning.

They were speaking excitedly among themselves, pointing toward the bodies whenever the butchers exposed another part of one. A few of the children looked more impressed than frightened.

The butchering had begun during the night.

Once Feroy became confident that the remaining adzees wouldn't immediately return, village hunters and butchers had been sent outside under heavy guard. They had opened the carcasses, removed the entrails, and spread the bodies enough so the trapped heat could escape. Leaving animals this large intact until morning, especially during the peak of summer, would have spoiled a great deal of the meat.

At first light, more workers had joined them.

Duvas had provided every laborer the butchers requested. Some workers were helping strip away the thick hides. Others carried off bones, organs, and sections that couldn't be used—although the bones and fangs would still be kept after cleaning to give to Pydaso for selling in Ulriga. Several more pushed wheelbarrows filled with cuts of dark meat toward the smokehouse inside the northeast part of the village.

The butchers themselves had been working continuously.

One of the beasts had already lost most of its hide. Another had been divided into large sections, with men cutting those into smaller pieces that could be salted, cooked, or hung inside the smokehouse. Work on the third one had begun more recently. Similar work was ongoing outside the southeast and the southwest gates, each having another adzee carcass there.

The smell of blood and opened flesh was strong, but none of the observers appeared interested in leaving.

Hudan stepped closer to the guarded work area, and Lucem followed him.

"I want to fight it next time!” Lucem said excitedly.

Hudan looked down at him, then toward the nearest adzee.

“Hah! You are still tiny, kiddo.”

"I'm thirteen!”

"Exactly."

Lucem frowned. "I could still help…”

"You could help by staying behind the wall and not making the guards rescue you."

The corners of the captain's mouth had lifted slightly, making it clear he was teasing the boy.

Lucem folded his arms. "You don't know that I'd need rescuing."

"The beast's mouth is wider than your shoulders. Much wider.”

Lucem studied its exposed fangs and seemed to reconsider his options now.

Hudan snorted and rested one hand on the boy's shoulder. "When you're older, you can join the manor guards if you still want to. Until then, learn to use a training-spear properly."

"I already know how!”

Syrene glanced over from beside the closest carcass. "You dropped one when you were playing that make-believe game of fight-the-bandits last week with your friends."

“It… It slipped!”

"It flew behind you when you were walking."

Hudan laughed as Lucem sputtered, glaring at his sister.

Clarisa had already turned her attention to the opened carcass. She stood beside Syrene, watching one of the butchers cut around the ribs.

"Was that where the bolt entered?" Clarisa asked quietly.

Syrene followed her gaze.

"I think one of them struck close to there, but the butchers have already widened the wound while cutting."

Clarisa leaned slightly to one side, trying to see more without stepping past the guards. "Could it even reach the heart from that angle?"

"It might have struck some other important organ instead, which would be just as deadly. Can't say at this point."

The two of them continued talking in lowered voices, Syrene patiently answering the questions of her protege.

Clarisa had been frightened while the horns sounded during the night, but now that the beasts were dead, her curiosity had taken over. She wanted to understand how the wounds had affected them, how thick their hides were, and why the butchers divided some parts differently from others. It seemed likely that she would follow in the footsteps of the maroon-haired researcher.

Lucem was more interested in getting a trophy instead.

"Can I keep one of the fangs?" he asked Hudan.

“No, kiddo."

"What if the butchers take it out first?"

"No.”

“But it’ll be wasted otherwise!”

“Still no.”

Lucem looked toward Kivamus for support.

Kivamus shook his head before the boy could ask. Those fangs were sharp, and the boy could easily hurt himself in excitement, not to mention Pydaso would be able to sell them for real money, since he had eventually managed to find a buyer for the bones and fangs of the adzee they had killed last winter.

A group of children ran along the edge of the crowd.

Kivamus recognized the adopted orphans Maisy and Timmy among them. Elsie ran beside Maisy, while a younger boy followed Timmy hesitantly.

Merchant Kigeir stood some distance away, leaning slightly to favor one knee while he watched them. His thinning blonde-white hair was easy to recognize even among the crowd.

The younger boy must have been his other son. Leif, his eldest, would already be working elsewhere in the village.

Kigeir noticed Kivamus and gave him a respectful nod.

Kivamus nodded back. He knew the middle-aged merchant was a trustworthy one. He might have to include the man if any plan needed the help of a local merchant in the future.

After a few more questions, Lucem and Clarisa moved away to join the other kids. Within moments, all of them were comparing the three carcasses and arguing over which beast had been the largest.

The guards had to warn them twice not to cross the marked line.

Kivamus turned back toward the butchering.

Before the butchers were allowed near the bodies, the guards had removed every scorpion bolt in the night.

But Trevalo had seen the weapons' results when his caravan passed them last night. The bolts had still been protruding from the adzees then, and there had been no practical way to hide them before the wagons passed.

The scorpions themselves had been concealed again before the villagers were allowed outside.

Kivamus had checked all three weapons in the morning before that happened. The new steel components had survived repeated use without bending, cracking, or slipping out of position. That was enough for him. From now on, the trained crews could inspect the weapons themselves after each use. Kivamus no longer needed to check every component personally, knowing that the work of Darora and Cedoron was top-notch as usual.

Trevalo approached the nearest carcass as far as the guards allowed and studied the wounds.

"I saw the short-spear-things jutting from the beasts last night," he said quietly. “I'm no expert, but those weren't ordinary spears or pikes broken off in half, were they?”

Hudan glanced at him with a frown then looked at the merchant.

Kivamus met Trevalo’s eyes. "Our guards fought the beasts where the village defenses could reach them. But the exact weapons we used are not something I want discussed outside Tiranat. You are smart, and I know you notice things, but I'm not willing to say any more about it at this point. Make sure you don't mention anything unusual you saw to anyone else.”

Trevalo glanced back at the top of the nearby watchtower, looking at the covered up shape of the scorpion mounted there, then nodded.

"You have my word."

Kivamus believed him.

Trevalo had earned enough trust to establish the new Ulrigan trade route and handle increasingly valuable shipments for Tiranat. It helped that Trevalo hadn't asked for an early payment on the debt the village owed the merchant, despite knowing that the villagers were being paid in coin now, which meant the village’s financial situation was better than it was in the past. The merchant still couldn't be told anything about the village's weapon designs, but Kivamus didn't expect him to betray something he had been directly asked to keep private.
