No, no, sir! This is no simple sword. Once belonged to a soldier in the first Pemolaran Army, it did. Tis a relic, made by the Bondcrafter herself! Never loses its edge! No. No it doesn’t! Test it? Well. No, better not. It doesn’t lose its edge, but it might still snap, y’know.

— Nicolan Trader of Pemolar The Month of Brittle 321 AR

***

I snapped the second beacon two hours ago, but no one had appeared. Mom, Uraleka, and Nemmikel would have to come back the old-fashioned way. By foot.

I’d started to feel better over the last week, but the improvement was soured by the continued fortification of Eschal’s army.

Neogon had read them right. They did try a few more half-hearted attempts to break into the town, but they were handily repelled. None with so devastating an effect as that first assault on the wall, but they hadn’t tried for the wall again.

As the days faded into a week, the army completed its encirclement of the town, while the defenders had dug trenches in lieu of walls. Both beaches on the outer edge of town were lined with soldiers from both sides, each waiting, hiding behind the tall rocks. The north and west entrances looked tempting, but the wide Denarlan Road leading up towards the center of Pemolar was lined day and night with Neogon’s remaining soldiers. The open plain was peppered with hundreds of wooden palisades to prevent Eschal from using cavalry.

The west, the one area still densely surrounded by trees, was protected by the hunters, who knew the land so well that any advance was noticed almost before the enemy general even planned it.

Older children and the elderly were interspersed with soldiers on the wall. The sheer number of crossbows made the entire town feel like a porcupine, ready to skewer anything that dared come near.

It amused Neogon to no end when he considered the enemy commander, and he never failed to bring it up to Mera. The Eschal leader was certain we had to be running out of bolts, and he kept operating under the theory that sooner or later our deadly barrages would slow down. Every new attempt showed him the error of that thought, as his soldiers paid in blood.

On the other hand, evacuation was no longer an option. Those who had escaped, had long since fled up the highroad, to fates unknown. For us, it was either break this siege or die trying.

Our town smelled terrible. We didn’t have the option to give mercy. We didn’t dare risk it. The Eschal had thus far given none. No quarter. An entire nation, hell-bent on killing us, for some unknown slight.

The dying lay outside our walls, crying until they went silent. We used skills and warm earmuffs to drown them out, and thanked the gods when they finally lay still. Some soldiers took it into their own hands and fired on those surviving few. Crows had been circling Pemolar’s Hill for what felt like years. An attack from any direction would have to scatter hordes of the things.

“Where are you, Mom?” I thought. I couldn’t think of anything that might prevent her from teleporting to me, save perhaps a desire to keep her secrets from Uraleka and Nemmikel.

Not for the first time, I wondered if I should’ve fled with the evacuation groups I’d spent so much time organizing. Solidarity and stubborn defiance looked good from the outside, but now we were staring down a slow, cold death by starvation. No amount of crossbows would save us from that.

I couldn’t sit and mope all day. Mom had told me that the beacon would fade within an hour. If she hadn’t used it by now, she wasn’t going to.

Sighing, I made my way toward the wall.

I didn’t have a set spot like everyone else, and was free to roam between the various defenses.

I had two hundred and nineteen free points, and no idea what would be best to put them in. Public speaking, if I added enough, might give me an attack like Freyna had used to disable the enemy army. It was already quite useful with its ability to let me speak in the ears of soldiers in a local area without needing to shout, but–!

I paused, noticing something.

“Soldier!” I said.

The man turned, eyes widening a bit as he saw me.

“What is your name?” I asked.

The man — boy really — was younger than me. Almost certainly didn’t have his talent yet.

“Malken, M’Lady,” he said. “Lancer Malken.”

I rolled my eyes. “Come now! I’m a farmer for pity’s sake! What’s a girl got to do to stop being ‘M’Lady-ied’ everywhere she goes!?”

Another soldier beside him clapped him on the back. “Don’t mind the lad. He calls all the young lasses in town that.”

I turned to the grizzled man. He was probably in his mid-thirties. His light armor and long spear signaled him as one of the dragoons who specialized in fighting airborne rift monsters.

“Oh,” I replied, feeling slightly embarrassed. Not everything was about status.

“Uhm. He’s wrong, M’lady,” Malken said, emphasizing the title. “I called you that because of what I saw you doin’ for my friend the other day.”

“You knucklehead. The girl is clearly uncomfortable! Why’d you go and say that?”

I blinked.

“Ma always told me to tell the truth,” he replied.

“Uhm… What was I doing?” I asked, both out of curiosity and to make sure not to do it again.

“Chett. You were helping him. Talking to him. Went and got him some soup. He… needs that. Words, I mean. He’s been having a tough time of it. So thanks.”

Right. The bald man. Not the water mage, but the other one. The soldier, who’d been dragged in on a litter when Neogon’s army first entered the town. He was actually probably about my age, but the bags under his eyes made him seem so much older. He looked dead tired. I’d sat and talked to him, and it seemed to make him feel a little better. He’d needed something similar to Burnom’s. Something to uplift himself. Making it had been impossible though.

Me being me, I’d really been looking to use his hands rather than his head, but apparently I’d made an impression. I remembered his story. The army was all he had, and he’d lost almost everyone he’d known in it, one death after another, over the course of this war. Neogon apparently had used his talent quite a lot, but I’d sensed that bringing it up probably wouldn’t help.

I was glad to know he still had at least one friend.

“You’re welcome!” I beamed. It was good to know a little kindness and a little care were paying off. “Well, come with me. We need to get to my house.”

I paused practically feeling the innuendo the older soldier was about to allude to, and interrupted before he could.

“My talent lets me craft for people and yours is surprisingly easy to make.”

The older man pouted like I’d just ruined his day. I probably had.

“Well, fine. Get you gone then. Get yourself a pretty trinket,” said the sergeant. “Report back as soon as you’re finished.”

Malken gave a salute, and turned to give me a bow.

“Lead the way, Lady Elmerina,” he said.

Rumors of my ability and the things I could make were spreading faster than the Eschal Army. Sometimes I was sure the kindness and generosity of these men was simply a front put forth in the name of greed. They wanted a shiny of their own. My talent allowed me to see who truly liked me and who was just pretending, based on how difficult their needs were to make.

We quickly made our way to my house. More specifically, we went to the barn where Stoutgruff used to lived. Now it had been repurposed to hold all the supplies the villagers had provided me with, and getting him back in here when he’d reappeared with the wagons had been a pain in the ass.

I made the man a sword, and earned seven points for his Swordsmanship skill.

Quality Longblade

This weapon never dulls.

When wielding this weapon, gain ten points in the Swordsmanship skill.

He, predictably, needed the sword to kill humans, but I’d become somewhat numb to that at this point.

Two hundred and twenty six free points. It felt like a mountain and a mole-hill at the same time. Enough to do something amazing, but not enough to do everything. Would I need more points in Footwork? Some melee or weapon skill? Would I need to improve my Archery? Public Speaking for relaying orders? Running? Fire magic and the mana skills? Jumping?

My fire magic had been steadily improving as I used it constantly to keep myself warm. No one ever asked why I didn’t need to wear heavy clothes. I tried to fool myself into believing that they thought I was just naturally warm… but most of them remembered me bundling up like a caterpillar in a cocoon last year.

They must know that my talent was at least Masterful. Perhaps some even suspected Legendary, but there hadn’t been any calls for my head yet. The old wisdom was a fucking death sentence for me, but I didn’t see any way to stay alive without drawing attention to myself.

“Amazing…” Malken said, holding the blade reverently.

My talent didn’t seem to feel like scabbards were necessary when creating swords. Two for two without one, now.

“Use it well,” I said. “Defend your friends, and allies. And me, if you spot me in trouble, okay?”

The man blushed. “I… will, My Lady.”

“Mera. Just… call me Mera.”

The man nodded but didn’t say anything as he turned to leave.

“And get a scabbard for that! It’ll hurt someone if you don’t!” I called after him.

He tipped his helmet in my direction, before jogging off down the road, snickering. I rolled my eyes. I was actually feeling somewhat thankful that Reid wasn’t here. I didn’t know if I could bear it if he started treating me this way.

A horn suddenly echoed out across the plains from the west. My immediate thought was an attack, but I realized suddenly that it wasn’t one of ours.

I took off at a sprint. I was slowly getting better and only had to stop to cough a few times along the way. The pear Akkiwa had gotten me had worked wonders.

The horn called out again as I joined a throng of people climbing the ladders up to the top of the wall.

The Eschal army was signalling. Another attack? If so, why would they alert us?

“What’s happening?” I asked, breathlessly, as soon as I made it to where Dobretin was already standing. Neogon wasn’t here. The man tended to stay awake for nights and he’d left Arc Sergeant Vovel, and Lieutenant General Nadir in command in his absence. The Sergeant seemed to like me. The General thought Neogon was wasting his time with me, but both nodded at my approach.

“We don’t know,” Dobretin said. He seemed equally breathless. Almost all of his time these days was spent rationing food. A small guard had to be placed around the granaries, and they’d needed to use force to deter thieves more than once. He must’ve gotten here just before I had.

He looked tired. We all did, I supposed.

I looked out to the distant treeline. The Eschal had picked up where Travis had left off, making even more cut ground so they could set up outside our range.

I could see a woman wearing a deep green set of leather armor. Even at this distance, I could see her blonde hair.

“Oh gods,” I breathed, as a phantom pain echoed from my healed eardrums. “She can attack you with sound! Guard your ears!”

I gave the order with no direction, but was obeyed as many men began donning earmuffs. Those without, did what they could but none of them would drop their weapons.

The general glared at me, but I ignored him, eyes focused on the woman who was powerful enough to attack a small army.

She stepped out of the treeline, well outside the range of even our mightiest longbow, but she was accompanied by… something.

It took a few moments to realize what I was seeing, but eventually, I figured it out.

Men and women were floating in a line beside her. Unconscious. She walked forward slowly, each of them held aloft by what I could only assume was wind magic, until she was close enough for us to understand.

Tacurian soldiers. All of them were either unconscious or dead.

“People of Tacuria,” came a sudden voice in my ear. I shivered. It wasn’t loud, but I knew it could be. My own earmuffs weren’t much protection. Was that how I sounded when I spoke to people half way down the wall?

“I am Queen Freyna. Daughter of Emmaline, better known as the Chemist. While I will never forgive, nor forget what your people have done to me, and mine, on this day, I am willing to allow you your freedom and your lives!”

I glanced across the soldiers. Hundreds of them that I didn’t know, but one that I did.

Captain Berkenem.

I focused on the woman, and saw the sword in her hand. She was a long way away, but even here, I could see the chunks of rock hovering around the blade.

Sudden rage swept over me. That wasn’t hers. That sword shouldn’t be in her fucking hand. Was she even an Earth Mage? Gods help me if she was an earth mage too. I hadn’t been able to learn healing or water magic, so surely even Freyna wouldn’t be able to learn wind’s counterpart.

Murmurs had broken out among the soldiers, but they held crossbows ready, in case of some trick.

“Our army focuses on your capital. Even now, the majority of our people make way to lay siege to Denarla! We would prefer to be there! Instead we are here, stomping out cockroaches. But, it seems, you’ve hidden a gem away. So I propose a deal!”

My eyes widened as a mild hope began to settle in. They’d… leave? Was that possible?

She lifted the sword high. It was a dull blade that didn’t shimmer in the sunlight, but nevertheless exuded a presence that commanded the eye.

It was beautiful, but it didn’t belong in her hand.

“Give me the one who crafted this sword! If you do, I shall release these prisoners, and our army will turn north towards your great Duchy! Your town will never face Eschal again. Merely give me the Bond Crafter!”

I didn’t move. But my hands trembled on the wooden spikes.

“Otherwise, you will all die today.”
