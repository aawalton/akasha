Chapter 49: Hot Spring Inspiration

When Ambrose woke up a couple hours later, it was with a start. He couldn’t quite recall what he’d been dreaming, but as he regained consciousness, the musky scent of the room clashed with his memories of home. He jerked straight up, breathing hard. The last two days came back to him, and he relaxed. Mostly.

“What a fucking mess,” he sighed. Everything. His entire life, leading up to this point, all just a series of messes, fights, and complications, leading him to being on the run, and relying on the kindness of a bunch of stinky mole people. He was hungry, had no clothes, and cranky beyond belief.

But at least he wasn’t exhausted anymore.

Ambrose slid out of bed and stretched. He wore only torn dress trousers, and didn’t bother to put anything else on. They had promised to deliver him clothes after they’d spent a good fifteen minutes taking his measurement and poking him with needles. He had no shoes, but the floor of this Imperial safehouse was smooth enough. The healer had fixed the sores and blisters, but the ghost of that pain still lingered, too fresh to forget.

He stumbled out of his temporary room to find that the other three bedrooms were both open and empty. Wandering through the house, he found a small kitchen, a sitting room with a bookshelf and small table, where a note waited for him.

Ambrose, we woke up before you. Mulch is still with us, and is showing us this place’s bath. We’re relaxing for a bit while waiting for clothes.

There was no invitation to join, but that never stopped Ambrose before. He went to the very back of the safehouse, where a door opened up into the first part of Crag he found appealing: a hot spring.

“There he is,” Raeleq said, waving enthusiastically and flinging water around. “We were beginning to think you’d sleep until dinner.”

He and Luthor were submerged up to their chests, and looked comfortable. It was a small spring, capable of fitting at least a dozen humans, or about five molekin, judging by how much space Mulch took up. This small cavern was dim, lit by a glowing, hanging flower that cast dancing lights around the walls. The water bubbled invitingly, and those ghost-pains begged for salvation.

“How long was I out?” he asked, pausing at the lip of the spring. The heat washed over him, tantalizing and close. “Hell, how are you up, Graves? I thought that arm wound was bad.”

Luthor shrugged, leaning back against the spring’s side indulgently. “It was bad, but I guess I’m made of tougher stuff than you. You’ve been out for two and a half hours. We only just got in here about fifteen minutes ago, if you care to join us.”

Gods, did he. But something bothered him. “Where’s the kid?” he asked, looking around. “We’re not letting him run free, right?”

Mulch let out a belly laugh. “Don’t you worry about him none. I had a buddy of mine who’s on our side, and he’s watching out for him. As far as Elder Marphy and the others are concerned, he’s in deep shit with you guys and you’re responsible for handling his punishment.”

That was good enough for him. Without an iota of hesitation or insecurity, he dropped his ruined trousers, kicked them to the side, and stepped into the fantastically frothy water. It hurt at first, in that way he appreciated, slow and ramping up until his body finally adjusted. He made himself comfortable, sitting opposite his friends and the mole.

“So,” he said, “Our side. I’ve got some questions about that.”

“Oh, we did too,” said Raeleq. “He’s probably already answered them. Do you want us to catch you up on what we know now?”

It was actually kind of annoying, Raeleq knowing more than him. Even if it was only for a few minutes. The water helped soothe it away, though. “Yes, please do,” he said. “While we still have some privacy.”

Mulched submerged for a second, then popped back out with a splash, nose-tentacles wriggling around. “Whoo-ee, that feels good. Alright, Ambrose, here’s the important bits. The Empire’s been here for about sixty years or so, and they’re in control of just over half the world. Enough that the difference doesn’t matter. They have control, because a lot of people believe that they stopped the world from ending.”

Ambrose blinked. SNAC had said something along those lines, but this was way more engaging. “Well, damn Mulch. That’s one way to start a story. I’ll bite. Why do people think they stopped the world from ending?”

“Well, a long time ago, back when all the villages were friends, we joined forces for two expeditions. One of them was going to see how deep we could dig before we hit magma, and the other…” He paused, clearly for effect. “The other went to discover the surface. And discover it, they did. And with it, horrors none of us could’ve ever anticipated.”

“I feel like we should have drinks or a meal while we listen.”

“I agree,” said Luthor, stirring. “I am starving, but this is important. Can you be a bit more straight forward and less theatrical for the second telling?”

Mulch sighed and flopped his claws on the surface of the spring. “I guess so. They found the surface, and there were monsters up there. The entire place was riddled with monsters, in fact. And with our expedition having opened up a path, they had a way down to us. These monsters were big, much bigger than that spider you took out. They tore open new tunnels and devoured moles, bats, and kobolds alike.

“But right when things looked most bleak, machines came from the surface and started slaying all the beasts. They took them all out in just a few days, hunting them down to the depths of Stonskarr. One of the very first battles, the one that saved my grandpappy, was down where you said you landed, the Forlorn Pit. That place is said to still be haunted by that first monster’s awful progeny.”

The ghouls. It made sense, as far as mythological stories went. But there was nothing natural about what this Empire did, as far as he could tell. They used local superstitions and customs, then overwhelming strength and magic to insert themselves into worlds like a parasite, overtaking its host. It was the way of most empires.

“Yeah, that totally happened.” Ambrose snorted, sitting up straight. He’d fully acclimated to the heat now, and it was loosening him up, soothing away all the stress.

“I knew it,” Raeleq said with a deep chuckle. “I told you he’d call it crap immediately.”

“So,” Mulch continued, “when the Empire took over, they did it while helping rebuild, and as they did, they started asking for little favors. Repayment for them helping out, you know? And then it kept getting to be more, more, more. And some of the villages, they didn’t like that. They said no, and the offworlders didn’t like that. So, it turned into a fight. Out of the ten villages, three of them still resist assimilation. And they’re in a great defensive spot, so they haven’t been squashed out just yet. Mychele is from one of those villages.”

Ambrose laughed, a little light headed. “Of course the bastards didn’t want a group of mind readers interfering. I’m surprised they didn’t try to wipe them out from the beginning. But I suppose they thought they could maybe use them, and Mychele’s people took offense.”

“Yessir,” Mulch grunted. “So, there are pockets of resistance, but a lot of the population is already beaten down and compliant. It’s all some of these people know. My Grandpappy saw how they closed the trap slowly. By the time he believed what others had warned about, it was too late. And it was him who made sure we knew the truth: the damned Empire needs to get the hell off our world.”

“The general plan,” said Luthor, “is to have Mulch take us by train to Bedrock Spire. We’re going to need to pretend to be Imperial Agents, like we’ve been doing, to infiltrate the Hermanic Quarter and see if we can get what we need for repairs. Dexi’Lynn says she can help there, if we think we can trust her.”

Mulch snorted, nose wriggling wildly. “The only thing you can trust about kobolds is that they’re mostly concerned with their own comfort an’ pleasure. Keep her indulged and safe, and she probably won’t stab ya in the back.”

He thought about it more. It wasn’t the time for a full plan, but he had a scenario to try out in his imagination, at least. “We’ll need information from Dexi’Lynn and Jack for this work. The little shit won’t want to help us, but we can get whatever info we need to fake being agents better this time. I assume we still want me to pretend to be in charge of things?”

“You are the best at being a pompous ass,” said Raeleq cheerfully. “It’s the condescending sneer that sells it.”

Ambrose’s lips curled into a smile. “Condescending’s an awful big word for you, champ.”

They laughed together, and then Raeleq splashed scalding hot water on his face. It hurt for a few seconds, but mostly stung his dignity. All in all, he approved.

“With me and Dexi to guide you, it should be a breeze. Offworlders who are here often get help from us locals. We’ll get your ship out of this world, and maybe on the way out, you help us kneecap the bastards’ operation.” Mulch’s lips pulled back in an unsettling toothy grin.

“That’s secondary to us getting out of here. We’re not in any condition to do real damage against a much bigger foe, and we need to stay moving so Vanderborn doesn’t get us. We still need more and better cards, and to grow more powerful before we can do anything. We need to find a world opposed to the Empire and see what they can offer us.”

Luthor nodded thoughtfully. “Yes. But the way that Mulch was talking about Bedrock Spire, there’s a lot of good, expensive cards there. Enough to possibly make a difference to outfit some rebels here, and keep some for ourselves.”

“Now you’re speaking my language,” said Ambrose. “I’m always down to steal or cheat some cards away from undeserving fools. Okay. We’ll help the local insurgency on our way out. Why not?”

The door to the hot spring opened, and a young molekin waddled in.

“Hey folks, your new clothes have been delivered! Come on and try ‘em on, and then come get some grub!” He waved them in, then retreated.

“Do you think it will be actual grubs?” Raeleq asked thoughtfully.

Ambrose’s stomach turned at the idea. “Gods, I hope not. I’m starving and terrified at what might pass for food around here.”

“That’s a bit hurtful,” said Mulch, “but we’ll show you. Let’s get a-move on!”

Luthor stood and slowly, deliberately climbed out and grabbed a towel he wrapped around his waist. Raeleq, on the other hand, jumped out and didn’t bother.

“Oh come on, you’ll put someone’s eye out,” Ambrose groaned as he held up his hands to ward him off.

Raeleq let out a low belly laugh and strode inside the house, proud of his form. As he had every right to be. Ambrose shrugged and got up and followed his friends inside without any worries, right back to his room.

Just as promised, there were clothes waiting for him. Ambrose had half expected them to be thick, rough overalls, the way many of the molekin seemed to wear. Instead, they were dark bluish violet silken robes and undergarments. Smooth, soft to the touch, but surprisingly durable when he tugged at it. Ambrose got dressed, impressed with the comfort and fit. They came with similar socks, and sturdy, heavy boots. That would definitely come in handy when climbing up and down those steep chasms.

All in all, he kind of liked the look, and a quick glance into the small, dirty mirror next to the bed showed he looked pretty good. He combed his fingers through his wet hair, doing his best to tame it and slick it back without anything to keep it that way. Next came his beard, smoothing it down and hating the need for a trim. Maybe there was a barber in Crag he could see before they left.

Ambrose took a deep breath. They had the start of a plan, but they would have to discuss it further later. For now, he was hungry, and the people of Crag were curious about them. They had a role to play, and information to gather. This wasn’t just about having fun and relaxing, it was about exploiting the situation to their advantage to get the hell out of here.

He understood that well, but wasn’t so sure about the others.
