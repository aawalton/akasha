Callie, Callen, and Mom all looked at the frost caterpillar, observing them closely. They had small teeth with which they seemed to gnaw at the frost, slowly but steadily eating it.

"Amazing!" Callie marveled. "There must be something within this frost that sustains them. Something more than just water. I wonder what it is."

She wished she had her journal with her, because this was something definitely worth writing down.

"I'm just glad they are not eating hay. That would be troublesome to say the least," Mom sighed in relief.

"So I suppose it's fine to leave them here?" Callen asked.

"I see no harm in it, if they're eating the frost. In fact, they are doing us a favor by eating it," Mom said and returned to brushing Gale.

Hearing Mom's words, Callie had an idea. "What if we take these little guys to the firewood? Maybe they can eat the frost from it?"

Mom and Callen both stared at Callie in surprise.

"That… That would be great, if they did that," Mom said sheepishly.

"Hey, little guys. Would you like some more frost?" Callie said to the frost caterpillars.

"Wait a moment, Callie. How will we get them to the house? The wind will just blow these little guys all over the place," Callen said.

Food?

Tasty?

Eat!

The caterpillars, having heard Callie's offer, all perked up and began crawling up to her.

"We could carry them in a crate. Cover it with some old cloth and shield them from the wind with our bodies," Mom suggested.

With that said, that was exactly what they did. Callie carefully gathered all the frost caterpillars into the crate while Mom wrapped some old cloth around it. There were some holes in it, however, so they would have to be very careful when carrying the caterpillars.

Or rather, Mom and Callen would have to be. Callie could barely make her way to the house herself with how horrible the wind was. As Mom and Callen did their best to safely carry the caterpillars to the house, she could hear their little voices call in fear.

Storm!

Storm!

Hide!

Hide!

Thankfully, they managed to get inside before the caterpillars could truly panic.

"Sorry, little ones! Is everyone safe?" Callie asked, uncovering the crate once they were inside the house again.

Safe. Safe. Safe.

It looked like none of the caterpillars had been blown away, although some did look a bit frazzled. Without further ado, Callie took the caterpillars and placed them on the frost that had formed on the firewood. The layer was even thicker now than before.

Tasty, tasty, tasty!

Little bugs instantly began to munch on it while chanting happily. Unlike in the stables and coop, they spread out instead of clustering together. Maybe it was because there was so much of the unusual frost that there was no need for them to cluster together.

"They really are eating it!" Mom said with a chuckle.

"Isn't that great! If they eat all the frost, we won't have to worry about our firewood not burning," Callie cheered.

Just a moment later, the door opened, making everyone shudder from the cold breeze. Dad had returned, covered from head to toe in snow.

"Oh, so warm in here…" he sighed in relief as he unwrapped himself from the many layers of clothes he had on.

"How are things in the village?" Mom said, taking his coat and hanging it near the fireplace to dry.

"Bad! Very bad. This weird frost is appearing in seemingly every house. Many are having trouble keeping their fireplace lit. The chief doesn't know what to do. We might have to house another family with us if this continues," Dad said grimly.

"I know how to get rid of the frost!" Callie announced triumphantly.

Dad's expression grew stern. "Callie, we can't have you exposing your magic to everyone…"

"It's not magic," Callie shook her head.

Mom and Callen both grinned, which clearly made Dad pause.

"Just take a look in the storage," Mom said.

Callie led him to the storage and showed him the frost caterpillars.

"Are those—"

"Frost caterpillars. We found out that they eat this weird frost," Callie announced.

Dad stepped closer and observed the little bugs more closely. There were tiny holes in the frost that the caterpillars had already managed to eat.

"I can't believe it… How did you discover this?" Dad asked.

"There were a bunch of them inside the coop and stables. We just brought them inside," Callen said.

Dad nodded thoughtfully. "This… This might be the solution everyone needs. I'll be going again to warn the chief."

"Stay and eat breakfast," Mom insisted. "I'll go, along with Callie."

"Why Callie, too?" Dad frowned.

"Because her magic is clearly responding to this strange storm," Mom said, rolling up Callie's sleeve and showing the still flickering mark. "Maybe Granny Ethel might be able to explain why it's happening."

"Yes… I suppose she might. But the weather… Callie, will you be able to walk through it?" Dad asked.

Callie looked outside. She didn't feel confident about getting anywhere with the blizzard being as strong as it was.

"Don't worry. I'll carry her if I must. We're not leaving the village after all," Mom said.

So they huddled up and went into the blizzard. Callie couldn't do anything. Maybe it was because she was so small, but she was completely reliant on Mom. Holding tightly onto her hand, they trudged through the village, slowly making their way to the village chief's house.

Callie's mark was itching like crazy the whole time. While inside, it was a bit annoying but tolerable. However, the longer she remained outside, the stronger the itch got, almost to the point where it was unbearable.

When they finally got inside the chief's house, ushered in by his wife, she instantly felt relieved. What was it with this storm? Why was it making her mark itch so badly?

"Heavens, Maeve! Why are you trudging all the way here in this weather? With Calluna in tow, no less. Are you alright, child?" The chief's wife asked, looking surprisingly worried.

"I'm okay," Callie said, brushing the snow out of her eyes.

"Rowan said many are struggling to keep the fire going. We might have a solution," Mom said.

"You do? Please, head inside. We're barely able to keep our fire going. This weird frost is appearing inside in the most unusual places."

Callie noticed that a few more people were inside the chief's house. All of them wore deep frowns. Their talking was reduced to muted whispers when they saw Callie come in. Although they clearly weren't talking about her. They were likely worried about the unnatural frost.

Among the people, she also noticed Granny Ethel, looking rather tired. "Granny Ethel, are you alright?"

"Callie, dear. What are you doing here? Shouldn't you be home?" The elderly woman said, but still wrapped Callie in a soft embrace.

"I could ask you the same thing," Callie retorted.

"I'm afraid the fire has gone out in my hut during the night. I couldn't get it going no matter how hard I tried. Heavens, I just hope the herbs don't get damaged by that weird frost."

Callie wanted to tell Granny Ethel about everything she had discovered, but with so many people around she didn't dare. What if somebody overheard her magic? And speaking about the frost caterpillars out in the open wasn't smart either. The chief had to know first so he could organize people properly.

Mom went to talk with him, while Callie waited with Granny Ethel. After a while, the chief summoned them to talk privately. Callie insisted on bringing Granny Ethel along with them, too, and the chief agreed.

"You'd better have some good news, Aurelwynns. Fireplaces are going out in the village one after the other, and frost is forming indoors. No matter how much people scrape, it just won't come off," Chief Aemon rubbed at his temples.

"We do in fact!" Mom said, turning to Callie. "Let him, dear."

"Have you found a… magical solution?" The chief narrowed his eyes.

"Initially, yes, but I found a better one. Frost caterpillars!" Callie announced proudly.

"Frost caterpillars?" The man blinked in surprise.

"I observed them and noticed that they eat the weird frost that forms indoors. We placed a bunch of them on our firewood, and they have been munching the frost away," Callie announced.

"The caterpillars… eat the frost?" The chief rubbed his chin thoughtfully. "I never would have guessed that. Why would they even do that?"

"There must be something in that frost that can sustain them," Callie added.

"This storm isn't natural, Aemon. The frost isn't the only weird thing. Callie, show him your mark," Mom urged.

Callie rolled up her sleeve, shuddering from the cold air. Her mark was glowing lightly and then going dim again, just like before.

"Why is it doing that?" The chief asked, inspecting the mark closer.

"We don't know. But it started as soon as the blizzard began," Mom said.

"It itches too…" Callie whined.

The room fell into contemplative silence as everyone wondered what could be going on. After a while, Granny Ethel finally said, "I believe I can offer a theory. But I have to insist that this is merely a theory based on the facts Callie has brought to us."

"I'll take anything. Tell us your theory," the chief insisted.

"I believe that this blizzard might also be a mana storm," she announced.

"What's a mana storm?" Callie blurted out.

Mom looked just as confused as Callie, while the chief seemed to recognize the term.

"I've heard of this phenomenon once," the chief said, looking at Callie. "It is not commonly known that the dragonbloods are trying to keep it under wraps so as not to cause panic among people. But I, as village chief, have been warned by the mayor of Drymon."

Callie swallowed hard. Something that dragonbloods were trying to hide from people. It sounded serious. Was it alright for Callie to know this?

But Chief Aemon continued his story. "Mana storms, in essence, are like any other storm. Abnormally strong weather phenomena. The difference is that mana storms carry with them an extraordinary amount of mana. This mana causes strange things to happen. Things that often result in disaster."

"The Mayor of Drymon told me of a mana storm that happened in the sea on the shores of the port city not too far to the south. The mana storm caused the waves to be twice as strong as in a normal storm, and they were filled with weeds and grasses that feed on mana, making the fish suffocate. It even caused a fearsome sea monster to appear… Many ships were lost during that mana storm…"

Callie gasped in horror. A storm filled with mana… That sounded horrible. The same thing was happening in Greyfen.

Granny Ethel nodded. "I've heard of a mana storm far to the south, which caused extreme wildfire in previously lush forest. It was burned completely to the ground…"

"What is going to happen to us?" Callie asked in fright.

Granny Ethel patted Callie's head gently. "I believe you've already brought us the solution."

Chief Aemon nodded. "Frost caterpillars. If they really eat that unnatural frost, then the village might just be able to hold out. The problem will be finding them. Calluna, do you happen to have any ideas of where to find them?"

"Yes! It appears that during the storm, they are crawling inside buildings, barns, coops and such to safely eat the frost without being blown away by the winds. If we search corners of buildings and places close to the ground where caterpillars might feel safe, we might find a lot of them."

Chief Aemon nodded. "I'm going to organize the villagers to do just that. But in the meantime, I'm going to need your help, Callie."

"My help?" She blinked in surprise.

"You managed to get the fire going in your home, right?" He asked.

Mom instantly jumped to her. "You can't suggest that! It will expose her magic to the whole village."

"Caterpillars alone won't be enough! Many homes have already gone without a fire for a whole day. Any longer and they might freeze to death," the chief said firmly. "This isn't a request. This is an order! Callie must go to the houses where the fire has gone out and get it lit again. Do what you can to hide that it was Callie who could do magic. Bring Rowan with you. Present that it was he who lit the fire. But you must light the fire. The secret about magic isn't worth more than people's lives."

Callie looked at her Mom. She was clenching her fists tightly. Was it really alright for her to do this? Obviously, Callie didn't want anyone in the village to freeze to death. But using her magic? It was very difficult to control in this weather.

The chief's expression softened a little. "I don't want to do this any more than you do. I wouldn't ask this if there was a better option."

Callie understood this, too. After all, it was the chief who ordered her to keep her magic a secret. But if it came down to exposing her secret to save the lives of other villagers, Callie would do it.
