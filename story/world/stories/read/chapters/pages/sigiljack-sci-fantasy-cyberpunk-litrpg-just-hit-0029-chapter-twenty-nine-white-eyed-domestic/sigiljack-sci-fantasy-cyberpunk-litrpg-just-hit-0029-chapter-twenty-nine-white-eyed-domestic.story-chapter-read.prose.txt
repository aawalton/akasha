The frost was already melting off the cooling pod. Boots pounded somewhere distantly in the hall.

Athena was still in him. Not fully. Not the usual clean, honed rhythm of Synchronicity, but enough that their thoughts were bleeding together.

The Bound Titan's weight still pressed faintly against his soulcore. But it was fading somewhat.

Realspace felt loud.

The hum of the cooling unit, the drip of condensation, his own breath. It all pushed too hard against his ears, still raw from cyberspace’s simulated overstimulaiton.

His head throbbed from the strain he’d taken in cyberspace, the kind his newfound biomancy had mostly patched but not completely. He reached for Athena's control over his perception of pain, and together they dulled the ache.

John forced himself upright. His cyberarm's data cable was still buried in the pod’s netport. He reached across his body and manually retracted it, the mechanism whining as it spooled back into his forearm.

He stepped over the body of the watchman he'd just shot. And turned toward the disabled aerial drone in the middle of the floor, remembering the Grin’s datapulse had dropped it. Its fans spun once, stuttered—then steadied. It floated off the ground.

He hesitated to approach the drone. But his cyberyeye quickly highlighted it as an ally. It was still his.

The military droid beside the now hovering drone also twitched, servo-joints creaking, then looked up at him. Its optics focused.

“You alright?” John asked. “Because we’ve got to go.”

The droid’s head tilted, almost confused. Then it stood, shaky but ready.

John flicked open the hallways' hacked security feeds in his cyber-eye.

And froze when he saw what he'd been hoping he wouldn't.

Dozens of white-eyed and pupilless civilians were coming down the hallway toward the server room. Not all of them had guns; most gripped kitchen knives, hammers, likely just whatever they’d found in their apartments. Their faces were disturbingly not blank as he would've expected, however. Their mouths moved in casual conversation. Almost like they were chatting like neighbors in any hallway, except they were running at break neck speed.

He took one step toward the door. The world buckled under his feet. Glitched.

[Skill Deactivated: Synchronicity – Body Lv. 1]

He staggered sideways. The droid caught him before he hit the floor.

“John… I’m—something is—” Athena’s voice quavered in his head, her thoughts abruptly tanked from his own.

Foreign memories hit him like cards flung into his face. A puppy with an adorable spiral tail. An awkward first kiss with a boy. A student dorm suite with furniture nicer than anything he’d ever even owned or borrowed.

“Athena?!” His fingers dug into the droid’s armor for balance.

“I can’t—what is—” Her voice frayed into static.

One more image surged forward, shuddering in his mind. A young Japanese woman, beautiful, adjusting her perfect hair in a mirror. Then the image cracked, broke. Flashed between that and a screaming woman looking down at her hands, her body almost entirely replaced with machinery, sitting locked in a chassis surrounded by racks of data-servers. Cables plugged into her spine and fingertips.

“Wh—who is that?” Athena’s voice pitched toward panic.

The image vanished.

He righted himself with the droid’s help. “Athena… you there?”

“I… I…”

He’d never heard her like this. “Athena, breathe.”

“You know I can’t… John--I’m scared.”

He frowned. “Is your stability dropping?”

“No. Just… those memories. They weren’t yours.”

“No, they weren’t. Whose?”

“I don’t know. John, I don't know."

His jaw locked. The camera feed was still running in his cybereye. The crowd was closer now.

“Athena, not to cut this short, but we’ve got incoming.”

“Yes,” she said quietly, shame edging her shaking words. “I can manage. I’m sorry.”

“We’ll figure it out later. I promise."

He stepped into the hall.

“Hey, get over here!” A middle-aged man waved a cleaver at him like he was calling John to help unload groceries.

A woman who looked like somebody’s tired mother raised a kitchen knife. “We have to kill you!” she said in the same tone someone might announce that dinner was ready.

The droid moved to block the incoming crowd.

“No,” John snapped. “We’re not doing that. Come on.”

The droid hesitated, then followed, the airborne security drone trailing over its shoulder.

“They’re closing,” Athena said.

“We’re faster.”And they would be even faster if they were synced, but after her episode he wasn’t going to risk it. He wasn't even sure that Athena, in her current state, wouldn't actually hinder his ability to think clearly if they merged their thoughts fully again.

He pushed himself, ducking the sharp pop-pop of pistol fire from behind.

He slammed into the boiler room and found more civilians waiting. One had a gun already raised.

“Can you please stay still?” the man asked politely.

John ducked behind a water heater. Bullets punched through the tank, water spraying hot and loud onto the floor. Footsteps shifted position. They were flanking him.

He activated [Hardbody]. And he came out swinging, rounds sparking off his armor. He caught the gunman’s arm, ripped the weapon aside, and slammed a fist into his face.

“Hey! You can’t do that!” one of the other men yelped. John’s boot caught him in the gut, folding him.

He pistol whipped the final person in the room, other than him, that was still standing upright with Skill-enhanced strength.

Voices spilled in from just outside, as the final man fell to the floor. The crowd had caught up.

John swore and made for the collapsed section of wall he’d used to breach into the building from the Undercity. Bullets hissed past as he dropped through.

The Grin-victims followed. Always talking like neighbors on a stairwell. “Come back!” “You’re making this harder!” “We just want to kill you!”

The droid limped behind him, surprisingly fast now that it had to be. The aerial drone skimmed the low ceiling.

Shots pinged from behind as they wove through the Undercity service tunnels.

“John—” Athena started.

“I know!” He didn’t have to hear the rest. Sooner or later, one of them would get lucky. They were too fast for civilians, too steady--their bodies moving like they’d learned how to ignore pain.

His stomach turned. Memories that were actually his resurfaced from where he'd locked them away tightly. He remembered the Verge girl’s dying scream, the one he'd accidently killed in cold blood as a new soldier, her voice breaking around the wailing of her impending death.

He turned, took a knee. He could've ordered the drone to fire on the crowd. He didn’t. He would at least be the one who pulled the trigger.

His shots were clean. He only aimed for the armed ones. Mag, reload, mag, reload. His hands trembled by the third iteration of firing.

“He’s shooting us!”

“Is he crazy?”

All of the gunmen--and women--were down.

The rest kept coming.

He turned a corner and ran again.

After a few moments, more distant shots echoed after him.

Why did you have to pick the guns back up, he swore internally.

He sent a command to his aerial drone to open fire on any gun-armed combatants. The drone turned, still floating behind him, and began to shoot with precise bursts of semi-automatic fire.

“I’m so sorry,” Athena whispered.

He didn’t answer.

Even when some of the civilians dropped mid-stride from old hearts giving out. Even when others tripped, broke bones, vomited and kept running because they couldn’t stop.

He kept going. Past the place where the mana leeches had attacked him before. A part of him mourned the lost of the monsters' soulcores, but the other part wasn't willing to turn back to slaughter the Grin's victims to make the time to claim them.

Eventually his drone stopped firing every other minute or so. He glanced back and there was no one there besides the droid and drone. Just the echo of his own boots and the droid's armored feet in the tunnels.

He'd lost the survivors. Even if they were immune to pain and able to push through their bodies' protests, they couldn't match his Reflex attribute--or the superior endurance that his Body attribute gave him.

“John… are you alright?”

“I’m fine,” he panted.

“You don’t sound—”

“Because I'm not, but we're not talking about it,” he snapped.

He jogged until the sealed door to Kaito’s contact’s net parlor came into view. He entered the code to the Undercity exit with exhausted eyes. The lock disengaged.

He stepped through.

The door shut behind him and his new robots with a pneumatic wheezing.

“John… please talk to me.”

“I can’t—” he started.

And froze.

The Angel he'd seen in the Grin video log flickered into his vision. Glitching in the service hallway before him. Multi-winged. Thorned halo and all.

“You… I can’t keep our briefly-formed connection… rescue me… I am owed…” Its voice warped around him, its more-direct digital presence causing his cybereye HUD to sputter. “The wastes outside your New Cascadia… they keep me there… give to me what I have already given you... grant me freedom."

John reached for Ghostwind and primed <Black Fang> within his cyberdeck. But the net vision blinked out.

Athena’s anxiety rippled against his mind.

The Angel’s earlier words crawled back through his memory: "I freed you".

And now it had said: "I am owed".

A disturbing suspicion began to crawl up John's spine.“Athena… was that thing talking to you?”
