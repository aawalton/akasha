Asher was lucky that Complete Resilience seemed to work for muscle cramps as well, because as much as he loved spending time with Samantha, lying on top of her back for hours on end wasn’t exactly the most comfortable way to travel. They’d toyed with the idea of her flying on her own and then him simply Recalling to her when she arrived where she was going, but it didn’t work out very well for long distance. Samantha could fly, but she had no method of navigating. Even if she tried to head in a straight line, powerful winds would often blow her off course. On the other hand, Asher had maps of the entire continent Incorporated into his head, and he was able to use his many Eternal Marks as waypoints, effectively triangulating their position and using that to make sure he knew roughly where they were.

There was also the fact that Asher didn’t want to leave her alone in the off chance she ended up getting shot down again. They hadn’t seen hide nor hair of the Head or their Knuckles since that eventful day, but there was no telling when they would strike again. Owl had already proven himself capable of sniping Samantha out of the sky, and if he could do it, there was no telling how many other people might spot her flying up there and decide to do the same. Winged Savior would save her from getting shot, but it wouldn’t do a damn thing about her falling to her death afterward.

With that in mind, Asher was able to ignore the uncomfortable position and focus on getting them where they were going safely. They did have to stop and stretch every so often anyway, simply because flying was a constant drain on Samantha’s tritessence. Her Long Flight skill helped with that immensely, but even with that, she couldn’t stay airborne forever.

“Here it is!” Asher said, leaning forward and whistling as he pointed toward a massive expanse of forest stretching out as far as the eye could see. “Wow, that is a big forest. This might be harder than we thought.”

“I’ll do what I can,” Samantha said, angling toward an empty meadow on the edge of the treeline and diving toward it. Once they were down, Asher stretched, taking in the large trees before them. Most of the trees reached a good couple of stories tall, which made him feel on the smaller side as he stared up at them.

“I know it’s just my imagination, but I almost feel like I can sense the monsters waiting within,” he admitted. “I still think monsters are way too boring to fight against, so I hope this is at least a little exciting.”

“Only you would walk up to a lich’s lair and hope for excitement,” Samantha chuckled as she checked on her bow. The two of them had paused during their flight when it got dark and Recalled back to the palace only a few hours ago to sleep, so it was fairly early in the morning still. “Ready to grab the others?”

“Let’s do it!”

Asher found Rulfar and Kroult in the library, right where they said they’d be. Moxy was a bit tougher to track down, as she still refused to have an Eternal Mark placed on her, but he eventually found her beating the hell out of a few fresh-faced royal guards in their practice grounds easily enough. Once he’d managed to drag her away with promises of deadly monsters, he Recalled back to the forest and handed out their helmets.

“You guys know the drill,” he said, securing his onto his head as he looked between everyone. “Protect Samantha so she can focus on actually finding the lich, and kill anything that gets close. That said, this is a pretty famous monster-hunting zone as well, so there’s a chance we might run into other people deeper into the forest. Obviously let’s avoid killing them if we do. Moxy.”

“Hey, you’re the assassin,” Moxy snorted, her voice muffled from the glass helmet.

The plan in place, they all let Kroult walk around and lay his hand on them, bolstering their immune systems just to be safe. If any sort of disease did manage to make it past their helmets, they’d need whatever edge they could get to fight it off. Once that was done, it was time.

Rulfar took the lead as he headed into the forest, his large battle axe held at the ready as he sniffed the air and kept his eyes peeled for any movement. Despite the fact that his Scent skill was pretty much nullified due to the helmet, Asher supposed old habits died hard. Samantha and Asher followed immediately behind, her with her bow out and arrow nocked, and him already holding Crescent and Nail. Kroult was directly behind them, and Moxy brought up the rear, the warrior the best one to react to surprise attacks with her ability to sense sudden changes in the air.

As they walked, Asher couldn’t help but once again admire just how naturally Samantha took to the new environment. It had been ages since the two of them had last gone hunting together within a forest, and he’d almost forgotten just how impressive she was. Woodland Stride allowed her to practically flow through the dense undergrowth of the forest, even walking straight through trees if she so chose rather than bothering to go around. Every so often she’d lay a hand on the scratched-up side of a tree, triggering Tree Mender and healing the damage. All the while, her eyes were peeled, staring forward through the dense treeline as if she could see leagues further than any of them could.

“Okay,” she said after a couple of minutes. “Natural Guide is picking up on a couple of different structures that have been here long enough that they’re considered part of the forest now. If the lich is hiding out in something he built, we’ve got eight different options.”

“Assuming it doesn’t have some way of fooling your skill,” Rulfar added. “It is only in the second tier, so we shouldn’t rule that possibility out.”

“How close are we to the first one?” Asher asked.

“It’s about thirty miles into the forest,” Samantha said, pointing off toward the structure. “We can probably make it there by tonight if we move quickly.”

“Wait, why don’t we just fly?” Moxy asked, furrowing her brow as she looked between them. “As much as I’d prefer fighting our way there, it seems sort of stupid to stay on the ground.”

“Well, thank you for admitting you weren’t listening to any of our earlier conversation,” Rulfar drawled, shaking his head. “One reason is the monsters. Samantha needs to stay pretty close to the canopy in order to be considered in the forest for her skills to work while she’s flying, and there are a number of very dangerous monsters who live in the tops of the trees and prey on anything in the air.”

“You guys don’t think you can handle a monster or two?” Moxy asked, raising an eyebrow as she looked between them. “Seriously?”

“The monsters are definitely one thing, but the main reason is we don’t want to give ourselves away,” Asher explained. “Don’t forget, we’re here to assassinate the lich living in this forest. We don’t have any idea what kinds of defenses he might have in place, or if he’s monitoring the forest itself. For all we know he’s aware of the fact that three of his fellow council members have already fallen and is now on high alert. At the moment, we just look like a normal team of monster hunters, trekking through the forest in search of a quick but dangerous payday. If we take to the air and start flying in a direct line toward each and every structure hidden within the forest…”

“It’ll give us away, and the lich might make a break for it,” Moxy finished with a grunt. “Alright, fair enough. Though I don’t know how normal we look with these glass balls on our heads.”

“Adventurers are weird folk,” Rulfar said with a chuckle. “Trust me, if the lich has been here for centuries, he will have seen far weirder things than this. At least try to pay attention when we’re talking strategy, would you? I—”

“Wait!” Samantha said, cutting Rulfar off and holding up a hand as her eyes narrowed and she focused on the forest before her. “Something’s coming. Get ready!”

Sure enough, barely three seconds later, a lizard the size of a small school bus burst out from the line of trees, screeching as it barreled straight for them. Asher didn’t have the faintest idea how it had managed to get so close to them without making a single sound, but that didn’t matter. His Shard Detection skill flared as well as the lizard approached, confirming that this was a monster. Before the lizard could even reach them, however, there was a streak of purple as an arrow slammed straight into one of the monster’s eyes.

“Nice shot!” Asher laughed, before realizing the monster wasn’t slowing down. Already the arrow was being forced out of its socket by the lizard’s natural regeneration as the eye regrew in an instant.

“Extreme regeneration,” Rulfar said, shaking his head as he stepped aside and motioned for Asher. “If you would?”

Asher almost felt bad for the poor monster that didn’t know what it was getting itself into, but he didn’t let that stop him. Lunging forward through a tunnel of compressed space, Asher elongated Crescent and went to slice through the monster’s neck.

Only to miss as the thing reared up on its hind legs, spinning around and attempting to slam its tail into his side.

Irked by his missed strike, Asher slipped into the astral and let the attack phase through him. The monster almost seemed caught off balance when its tail hit nothing but air, and before it could recover, Asher returned to the material realm and lashed out with Nail, cleaving straight through the lizard’s closest leg.

The monster crashed back to the ground, unable to stand on its hind legs any longer now that one of them just so happened to be missing. Screeching again, the monster rushed forward, opening its maw of jagged teeth as it went to bite him in half. Again, Asher slipped into the astral, easily dodging the attack before returning next to the monster’s head and finishing the job with a simple slash from Crescent. As the lizard and its head both fell to the ground, Asher wiped the blood clean from his daggers.

“A lot faster than I expected,” he admitted. “If the rest of the monsters in the forest are like this, this place is no joke.”

“There’s a reason why people come here,” Rulfar shrugged, picking up the lizard’s head and examining it more closely. “Tougher monsters normally means a bigger payout. How are the shards looking?”

Asher grabbed the glowing orb of shards hovering over the monster’s body, making a so-so motion with his hand. “About three and a half thousand. Not bad for a single monster by any means, but nothing crazy.”

“We need to be careful,” Rulfar said, pointing to the monster’s fangs. Secret Seeker helped Asher spot the telltale signs of a slight discoloration at the tips of its teeth, even if Identify didn’t warn him of anything out of the ordinary. “I hate not being able to use my Scent skill, but I think this thing’s bite was bad news. Either it was venomous, or a method of inflicting disease on wary adventurers.”

“We’ve barely gone three feet into the forest at this point,” Moxy said. “You seriously think the lich has turned every monster in here into some sort of carrier?”

“I think it would be wise for us to assume that he did,” Rulfar said, tossing the severed head to the ground and wiping his hands on his pants. “It doesn’t hurt to be cautious.”

“No, but it’s boring as all the hells!” Moxy laughed as she pushed past him and took the lead. “Come on, let’s keep going! I got dibs on the next monster! I only hope it’s bigger than the last one!”
