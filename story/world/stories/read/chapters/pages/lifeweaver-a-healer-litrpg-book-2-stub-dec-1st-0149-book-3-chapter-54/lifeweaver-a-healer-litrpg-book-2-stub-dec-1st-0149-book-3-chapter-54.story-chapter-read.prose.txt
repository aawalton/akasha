The following hour or so was filled with a lot of rage, cursing, and worried looks. In the end, the few guild leaders in attendance got together and set a meeting for the following day, where they would plan and prepare together as best they could. It turned out that the man Peter had spoken to, the one who wore sunglasses indoors, was a DIA agent, and he would be attending as well.

Luke made sure to fill Penny and Mateo in on everything they'd learned from Lara, but neither of them would be able to attend the set meeting. Other messages went out from most people in the room, he knew, and Luke figured more meetings would be taking place soon all over the world. With society half-collapsed all around them, it was a weird feeling, knowing they were about to be attacked by what were essentially aliens. He still didn't understand the reasoning behind it. Why attack your supposed allies?

The Singer disguised as an old woman had called him naïve. What did they know that Earth's Integrated didn't? How did they even have the technology, or whatever you wanted to call it, to cross between systems and even launch an assault? Did that mean the Singer system was older and more advanced? What did Guide think about all this? What did he know? Luke decided it was time to try to get in touch with the enigmatic bastard again. But there was something else he wanted to do first. Luke wanted to talk to Lara.

The Architect had been crowded since the moment she made her proclamations. People wanted assurances. Some weirdo even cried out that she was a witch before he fled the scene. With that sort of attention, it was hopeless to get a moment alone with her. So he waited.

When she was about to leave with Peter, Milla and Curtis having long since gone back to guild headquarters to plan, Luke found and seized his moment.

Lara watched him approach, a twinkle in her eye, like she'd been expecting him.

"Hey," Luke said with a glance at Peter.

"I'll leave you two to it," Peter said to Lara before taking his leave.

"He's fine with you being on your own?" Luke asked her once Peter left.

She smiled, showing a row of coffee-stained, crooked teeth. "I'm not someone he can boss around, nor am I someone in need of protection."

"No?"

"No," she said with a laugh, pointing to the corner where a pair of Integrated kept an eye on everyone in the room, including Luke. "I do have protection, though."

No longer able to contain himself, Luke held up the palm of his hand and brought out a few threads of mana, re-creating the pattern he'd seen the goblin mages hold to keep the portals open. "Do you know what this is?"

She studied his image for a moment, but shook her head. "No."

Luke frowned. "But you can create portals?"

"I can," she said, and a person-sized, light blue portal opened right beside her, to the horror of everyone still in the room. Once she'd calmed everyone who hadn't hurried out of there, she closed it again.

Luke hadn't seen a spellweave when she created the portal. "How do you do that? I figured it was some kind of spell, but I guess not?" He'd been about to drop his representation of the spellweave when something about it drew his attention. There was a solidity about it, like it was... right.

Then Lara spoke, and the moment passed. "Dammit," Luke said as the pattern broke apart. He turned his head up to look at Lara. "Sorry, what was that?"

"What I said was, creating portals is nothing more than a part of a different skill for me. It's called Formation."

"What else can it do?"

"With this skill, I can play God."

Luke raised an eyebrow. "Really?"

"As long as we're in a space I created, sort of."

"Like in here?" Luke asked, looking around. "You've dungeonized this room."

She shuddered again at his use of the word, but then smiled. "Like in here, yes. Want to see?"

"Sure," Luke said, not sure what to expect.

The fluorescent lights in the ceiling blinked, and Luke felt a, for lack of a better word, shift in the air. When the light returned, Lara was gone. Or rather, Lara, as she'd been, was gone. It was still her, but she looked at least ten years older, and her figure was... not as generous. The dress she wore hung off her. That wasn't all. Her bodyguards were gone, too, just gone, as were the other stragglers who'd stayed in the room by the object. Only Luke and Lara remained.

"What just happened?" Luke asked.

She shrugged, and when she spoke, Lara's voice was different. Whinier, sort of, and more nasal. "Welcome back to reality, Lifeweaver."

He turned and looked around again. "What?"

"I've withdrawn my space."

"Illusion?"

She shook her head. "My creations. As long as a space is SAR:ed, I can even create people and alter a person's appearance, my own included."

"So you have your own domain that you can do whatever you want with?"

The lights flickered again, and when they came back on, Dot had taken Lara's place. "Yes, Luke." She bit her lip, her freckled cheeks reddening. "Whatever I want."

"Stop that," Luke said, his voice as cold as ice.

"Sorry," Lara said, returning to herself. "Didn't mean to offend."

He drew in a deep breath. "You were in control of Alan's office. It had been dungeonized."

"Stop saying that word," Lara said with a sigh. "Yes, I did that."

"If you were in control, why didn't you help Alan kill me?"

"I'm not a killer."

"But he could brainwash people."

"Not me. Not in my domain. He was scared of me, I think. Saw very little of the man before you put him in the ground."

"Fair enough," Luke said, wanting some clarification since he saw no thread leading away from her last portal. "What about that portal from before? Where did it lead?"

"This," she asked, summoning the portal again. No thread this time either. "Want to try?"

"I'd better not, I suspect."

"No fun, Lifeweaver," she tutted. When he didn't take the bait, she just pointed to it again. "It leads nowhere. You can't enter. But that could change if you want. The sky is the limit."

"Please."

He kept a close eye and his senses primed. Something shifted within the motes of light in the portal. There it was. A thread. Interesting.

"Can you create actual dungeons?" he asked.

"Not yet, but I suspect I will. My level is still low."

"I noticed. Why is it so low?"

"Entering dungeons is not an option for me."

"What do you mean?"

She snapped her fingers and closed the portal. "I can't. It won't take me. My class is not allowed outside of this system, I guess. That's where the portals take you, to other systems, no?"

"Yeah," he said. "So you're stuck here?"

"Until I can create my own way out, at least. For now, I make do with simulated monster breaks from my own portals. They give me very little experience, so it has been a grind."

"That's an interesting class," Luke said.

"Thank you."

"So," Luke began, considering his next question. "What else can you tell me?"

Lara leaned back against a workbench. "You'll have to be a little more specific than that, Luke."

He nodded at the countdown object. "About that?"

"I've shared everything I've been able to get from it."

There was something about the inflection in her voice that made him press a little. "Are you sure? Sounds like you aren't sure."

She looked down a little, as if thinking, then glanced up at him from under her bangs. "About that object, I'm sure."

Luke took a step toward her, putting her within arm's reach. "But there's something else you can tell me?"

"Perhaps."

He took another step. Now, their shoes touched. "Perhaps?"

"Perhaps," Lara confirmed, her lower lip pouting a little. "Come a little closer, and I'll whisper."

Luke took her face between his hands, leaned forward, and tilted it up so their eyes met. "Tell me."

She shuddered again and squirmed a little, then grew still. Her breath caught, and Luke found fear in her eyes. "You're carrying something dangerous with you, Lifeweaver."

Luke shook his head and blinked several times, taking a step back. "What the fuck?" Realization dawning on him, he barked a command. "Pull the skill down. Now!"

The lights blinked, and the older version of Lara returned. She looked sullen and spoke in a low murmur. "Just wanted to have a little fun."

"Don't you ever do that again," Luke said, turning to walk out of the room.

"Wait," she said. "What is that in your inventory? The readings are terrible!"

"You must be confused. There was a nuke in there a while back, but it's gone now. Maybe you aren't as all-powerful in your little playground as you think."

Simmering with rage at having been made a fool of, Luke left, stomping up the stairs. He needed to talk with the others and compare notes, or he wouldn't trust anything he had heard down there. What a shitshow.

Lara, he left down there. She could wallow in the dark by herself, for all he cared.
