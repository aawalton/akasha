“WRRRRAAAAHAahahahaAAAaaaaaa.”

As the creature wailed, its chest expanded, and ribs tore through its white, maggoty flesh, dripping grey fluid. Then, as its breathing calmed, the ribs slid back under the flesh as it continued to lumber around.

However, only a moment later its eyes set on a Skeleton Brute that had wandered into its path. The Pale Wailer froze and then screamed again, charging at the brute that looked up too late.

It lifted its axe, but before getting it up over its shoulder, the Pale Wailer dove face first just like a T-Rex and scooped up the brute.

Its jaws mashed through, crunching and snapping bones, shaking the brute like a rag doll, and tossing its shattered bones in a spray around it as it rose back onto its massive hind legs. Its plump, maggoty tail waved back and forth across the ground almost like a Hell Hound wagging its tail, but leaving streaks of that grey rot on the ground with each wag.

My stomach turned inside out as I stared at that thing.

“You see now?” Themiel asked. “You’re going to want my help.”

“No,” I answered immediately, looking at him through the corner of my eye.

“No?” he asked.

“I’ve overcome worse odds before.”

“No offense, but I’ve seen what you can do and—”

“You haven’t even begun to see what I can do,” I shot back at him. “I said I have some other powerful skills, remember?”

I then began scanning what would be our battlefield. While Cat Footed could give some advantage to my Hell Hound form, only my human form could take full advantage of it, and only in human form could I utilize this environment effectively. Hell, this thing didn’t seem to have much in the way of ranged attacks, so if it were possible to just jump from building to building while launching magic at it, this fight would be all too easy.

Unfortunately, I didn’t have access to both at the same time.

The Pale Wailer was currently lumbering around a fairly open area, its feet crunching past piles of shattered and scattered bones, leaving the sick grey stuff that seemed to sweat from it anywhere it went. In that open area, we’d have some mobility on our side, but I could easily see getting forced to the sides and chased into an alleyway. While this thing was big, it seemed just the right size to move between buildings while blocking off entire streets. If it were to chase one of us into a dead end, we’d be completely screwed.

“Even if you think you can handle it, Luke is only level 16,” Themiel pointed out.

My ears folded down. “I know… But it’s also an opportunity. If he can make a significant effort in this fight, it will give him a crazy amount of experience.”

Not to mention there’s no doubt tackling a fight like this together will open up Pack Bond for him, I thought.

"Just keep an eye on us,” I said to Themiel. “Mainly, make sure if Luke gets out of my reach, you step in to help him. As for me, I’m sure I can handle myself, but this fight is completely pointless if you swoop down there and take care of it for us.”

“Fine,” he said. “But no complaints if I do step in. Alright?”

“As long as you don’t do it prematurely.”

“I’ll try.”

I figured that was the best I’d get from him, so I turned to look for my brother. Themiel moved ahead of me, and I followed. We jumped down, scaled back across some rooftops we’d come from, and found Luke still hunkered down right where Themiel had told him.

As the Umarii and I dropped down from above, Luke’s tail wagged, looking back and forth between us.

“Come on, Luke,” Themiel said, nodding over his shoulder. Luke’s tail instantly stopped wagging, and he got a serious look in his eyes as he followed. It seemed that he understood a fight was coming just from those brief words. He really seemed to understand Themiel well.

We continued below on the streets. “As I mentioned before, I have a more permanent transformation I can do, one that I can’t just switch back from. I’m most likely going to have to do that during this fight, no matter how things turn out,” I explained to the Umarii. “Therefore, I won’t be able to communicate using language with you for two days.”

“I see,” he answered. “I have to admit I’m curious to see this in action. You seem quite confident, so this must be a pretty powerful skill. Not to mention I’ve never heard of a skill with such a limitation.”

“Yeah, well... Anyway, I’d also appreciate it if you collect my clothes for me once the battle is over. I will need them when I do change back.”

“Understood.”

We rounded a corner, the vibration of the Pale Wailer under our feet. It stopped and let out another of those horrifying screeches.

“WReeEEAAAAAAGHGGGGgggg!”

Luke’s hackles raised, and if I were in Hell Hound form, mine would be as well. I felt the hairs on the back of my human neck stand on end.

I checked my vitals as I began mentally planning how to go about things.

Health: 1100 / 1100

Mana: 1073 / 1250

Stamina: 690 / 862

I didn’t like the idea of just starting with an Overdrive Times Two Pentaclysm for the fact it cost 480 mana. While that would be the strongest start, I could only use it twice before running out of mana, and I didn’t actually know how effective it would be against this thing. I could hurt it, no doubt, but if the Pentacyslm didn’t critically injure it, then watching my remaining mana pool would become a real concern. Basically, starting like that was somewhat of a Hail Mary, and I wasn’t one to just leave my fate in the hands of chance.

Actually, realistically, I wouldn’t even get to use my ultimate attack more than the one time, because the second use really would drain me of mana and I’d likely need to use more abilities before this fight was over.

Then there was Luke to consider…

My stomach curled at the thought of biting down on that fleshy, rotting monstrocisty, and Luke didn’t really have any ranged attacks of his own as far as I could tell. He was certainly on an up-close and personal kind of path.

As we came around the corner, I quickly doubled back upon seeing the shadow of the monstrosity. I put my back to the wall and peaked around the corner with a single eye a little more carefully as Luke peaked around by my knees.

Themiel quietly came to Luke’s side. “Okay boy, listen. Run around it and use Rumble Step. Do you understand? You’re not going for the kill; you’re a distraction. Just like you do for me.”

Luke’s ears folded back as he looked at Themiel.

Could he actually understand Themiel?

Well, either way, once he got Pack Bond he would have better communication with me than Themiel could ever have with him. The way it should be.

“So what’s your plan?” Themiel asked me.

“I’m going to start off in this form; do what damage I can with my daggers since I won’t have to waste much mana while getting damage off that way. Then, I’ll transform and start using magic.”

“Magic?” Themiel turned his head. “You know, I thought it was strange. You didn’t seem to be on a magic Path yet you had a large mana pool.”

“So your Analyze can tell you that much, huh?” I kept my eyes on the monster as it disappeared around a corner, another street down. “I need him in a more open area,” I said. “We’ll both need mobility.”

I nodded to Luke, then ran out onto the street. The Pale Wailer’s heavy footsteps continued to shake the ground.

I slowed as I came to the next corner and peeked around, along with Luke coming up by my knees just like we had at the last corner. It seemed to be wandering around on an almost set path, I realized. Actually… the piles of bones scattered along the streets spoke to this fact as well.

“Luke,” I said, looking down at my brother. “Wait for my lead, okay?”

He turned his head, tongue lolling from his mouth in a happy smile. I had no idea if he knew what I wanted or not.

I just had to trust him.

I then turned to the wall and started climbing, scaling up its side, using whatever ledge or roof stone I could find until I was on top and could see the Pale Wailer from above.

Now that I thought to pay attention to the ground, it was clear, the streaks of grey rot from its tail moved in a visible pattern through the city, with piles of bones all around those areas. I looked ahead to the large open area that I knew would be the best place to take this fight, then started running across the rooftops, getting into position.

When I found a good spot to drop down on the monster, I searched the shadows of the adjacent street, spotting Luke, lowering down with his eyes fixed on the target and his hackles raised. The Umarii must have been hiding somewhere else, as I did not see him.

Alright… good… we got this, I thought as I drew my daggers from their sheathes and gripped them in my hands.

I rose from a crouch, standing on the edge of a building as wind blew at my cloak.

All felt still except for the thump, thump, thump of the Pale Wailer’s slow steps.

Then, as it finally came into my range, I jumped, aiming to start things off with a plunge from above.
