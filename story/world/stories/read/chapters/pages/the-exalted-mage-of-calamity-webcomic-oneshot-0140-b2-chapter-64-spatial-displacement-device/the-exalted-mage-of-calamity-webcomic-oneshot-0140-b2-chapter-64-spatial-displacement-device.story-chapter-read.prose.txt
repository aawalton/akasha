Everyone was inside Sibyl’s house. Surprisingly, it was as cozy inside as it looked from the outside. Despite Sibyl being a dryad made of earth and plant life, she lived in a regular home that wasn’t much different from their own. They were all sitting in her living room on cushioned couches, discussing their next plan of action. Everyone except Phillus, as he was too large to fit inside. Instead, he stayed outside, peering through one of Sibyl’s windows.

Throughout her home were a large number of trinkets and odd artifacts scattered around the place. There were crystals, broken contraptions, numerous metal pieces, carved stones, old jewelry, and several half-disassembled items sitting across shelves and tables.

Theo kept glancing around at them. “Are you some type of collector?” he asked.

Sibyl had her legs crossed, her cheek resting against the hand propping up her head. “No.”

Theo waited for her to elaborate, but when she didn’t, he was forced to ask another question. “Then… what’s with all the stuff?”

“I’m a researcher,” Sibyl said. “I study magical artifacts. Mainly things that happen to catch my interest.”

Veronica glanced around the room, then back toward her. “Phillus said you were the one who helped Morgan create the seal that trapped the Last Gold.”

“I helped set it up,” Sibyl replied. “Morgan handled most of the actual spellwork and magic. I mainly set up the physical array and put everything in place.”

“Can you recreate it?”

“No.”

Everyone looked at her.

“What do you mean you can’t?” Veronica asked.

“The seal used to contain the Last Gold was made using four artifacts. They don’t have a name, so I’ll refer to them as binding rods. Only with those can I make another seal.”

“Do you not have any more?”

“No. The only ones left are inside the castle. And unfortunately, we cannot forge new ones since the forge is also sealed beneath the castle. Reusing the old ones won’t work either. The moment the barrier falls, they’ll likely shatter.”

Orban frowned. “What makes the rods so special?”

“The material, mostly. And the way they were forged. One of Avalon’s forgemasters made them specifically for Morgan’s array. Without those, there’s no way to make another seal.”

“Can’t ya use somethin’ else?”

“No.”

Sibyl said it so quickly that Orban’s brow twitched.

Theo leaned forward. “So there’s no way to seal him again?”

“Not with what’s available to me. Unless you outsiders have something.”

Everyone looked at one another, but no one came up with a solution. Everything they had on them was fairly mundane. Weapons, some potions, nothing that would help contain a person inside an array.

“Um, do you have any ideas for how we could defeat the Last Gold without fighting him?” Gwen asked.

Sibyl shrugged. “We could try and lure him outside of Avalon’s barrier.”

“If he survives, we’d just be releasing him back into the world,” Veronica said. “He came here with an Asvarian expedition in the first place. If he manages to return to his people, Avalon could end up dealing with another invasion.”

Phillus gave a slow nod from where he stood outside. “I would prefer not to repeat that mistake.”

Sibyl sighed. “So many stipulations.”

“Was there another method that Morgan mentioned? Some backup plan you have in case he ever broke out?” Veronica asked.

“I did come up with some alternative plans. The seal in the castle was the only one available at the time that would guarantee most of our safety.”

Theo glanced around the room again, toward all the artifacts and strange mechanisms scattered across Sibyl’s shelves.

“If you’re a researcher, then do you have anything else that could trap someone?” he asked. “Like some other kind of prison? Maybe a pocket dimension or something?”

Orban slowly turned toward him. “Are ya still on that, boy?”

Theo frowned. “No. I was just asking if there’s a similar method.”

A mix between a groan and a sigh came from Orban.

Sibyl looked over at him with a raised brow. “Something I’m missing?”

Orban shook his head and then pointed a thumb at Theo. “Boy had the bright idea of stickin’ the Last Gold inside a storage ring. We already told ’em it won’t work.”

Sibyl stared at Theo. “That’s ridiculous. Storage rings aren’t designed to hold living organisms.” She paused. “But… her eyes moved across the room before settling on Veronica’s hand. “Is that the storage ring in question?”

Veronica looked down at her fingers, then back up at her. “It is. We found it on one of the Asvarians’ bodies.”

“Let me see it,” Sibyl said, reaching out.

Veronica frowned slightly at the blunt demand, without even a single please, but she passed it over anyway.

The Great Dryad studied it closely, flipping it over and inspecting each side. Then she put it on and emptied everything that had been inside, mainly the random assortment of items that were already stored within.

For several moments, she said nothing.

“The boy’s idea won’t work. But it isn’t too far off from what we could use this for.”

Veronica narrowed her eyes. “What do you mean?”

Sibyl turned the ring slowly around her finger. “This storage ring could be used to defeat the Last Gold. With some modifications, of course.”

“How?”

Sibyl raised her hand and looked at the ring. “You can’t put the Last Gold inside this, but I might be able to turn it into something that’ll kill him. A collapse of space. If he’s caught near it, I doubt even someone like him would come out unharmed. Whether it actually kills him, I can’t guarantee, but it has a decent chance of working. However, it’ll be risky for you lot. You’ll also lose the ring in the process.”

Veronica frowned.

Storage rings were quite rare. Even as a mage who had reached the tenth tier, she had only ever owned four of them. They were nearly priceless artifacts, and not many people knew how to safely create one.

Mainly because creating a personal pocket dimension was an extremely dangerous endeavor. One small mistake could turn the ring from a priceless artifact into a deathtrap.

“Are you suggesting we use the storage ring as a spatial bomb?” Veronica asked.

“Spatial bomb? What’s that?” Gwen asked beside her.

“It’s what happens when someone fails at creating a storage ring, or when attempting to travel between large spaces or worlds unprepared. Since you’re essentially ripping open space, one failed step can lead to making the space unstable, resulting in a massive detonation.”

Sibyl looked at Veronica thoughtfully. “Hm. As expected, you know quite a bit. But yes, that is the plan. I can alter the ring so that the spatial pocket collapses outward instead of remaining contained.”

Veronica narrowed her eyes at the Great Dryad, not missing the fact that she had said ‘as expected.’ For some reason, Sibyl was adamant that Veronica was the one she had been looking for.

“How big will the radius be?” Nasha asked.

The dryad held her hands out and made a circle with her palms about a meter and a half wide.

Orban frowned. “That small? We’ll practically have ta punch ’em with it.”

“Not my problem. You came here for a solution to dealing with the Last Gold, correct? There’s one,” Sibyl said.

“Can… we do that?” Gwen asked slowly. “If we just have to get it close to him, we have to throw it, right? Because we can’t be inside the radius either, or we’ll die.”

“The Last Gold is a formidable opponent. Getting that close could mean disaster,” Phillus added.

Veronica pursed her lips, thinking it over.

On one hand, Sibyl was confident she could turn the ring into a spatial bomb. With that, they could deal with the Last Gold. The issue was that they’d still have to fight him, and they would only have a single shot at pulling off the trap. Not to mention, she’d be losing the storage ring in the process.

As far as priceless artifacts went, a storage ring was something she would absolutely need in the future. However, she also needed an outstanding weapon that would last her until she reached the tenth tier again, along with whatever other artifacts might be hidden within the castle.

“I vote that we go,” Nasha said suddenly.

Everyone turned to look at her.

“With our current team,” Nasha continued, “we are probably strong enough to at least stall the Last Gold in a direct fight. We don’t need to beat him. Just lure him into a trap.”

“Are you as strong as the woman beside you?” Phillus asked.

Nasha glanced over at Veronica. It took her a few moments to come up with an answer.

“Close enough,” Nasha said casually.

Gwen tilted her head at the comment but didn’t say anything.

Veronica, on the other hand, wasn’t too surprised by that answer.

Nasha had been quite obvious in hinting at her current strength. Sage had even caught on back in the Starfault Archives. Nasha was somewhere around the fourth to fifth tier now in aura usage. The only confusing thing was… why? How had her strength increased so rapidly? And why was she keeping it a secret, at least verbally? She had mentioned breaking through to the third tier, but never the fourth or fifth. Instead, she simply let Veronica feel her aura on numerous occasions.

“If the lasses are that confident, then count me in, too. I might not be able to do much ta the old fogey, but I’m as durable as they come,” Orban chimed in, tapping a fist to his chest.

Theo simply nodded beside his master, and Gwen gave a thumbs up.

Veronica turned back to Sibyl. “Then it’s decided. We’ll fight the Last Gold and try to lure him into the spatial bomb. How long do you think it’ll take?”

Sibyl took off the ring and looked at it one last time.

“It’ll take me a few hours. I just need to alter the inscriptions and remove some of the safeguards from this thing, and it’ll be primed to explode.”

Veronica stood up, and everyone else followed. “Let’s do that, then. We’ll wait outside and continue exploring the island in the meantime.”

Sibyl stood up and looked at Veronica. “Stay for a moment. I need to speak with you.”

Veronica glanced back at the Great Dryad.

Nasha turned around as everyone began leaving. “Everything alright?”

“Yeah,” Veronica said softly. “Go ahead without me. I’ll catch up.”

Nasha paused for a moment, then nodded and left with the others.

Sibyl turned and walked toward a back room within her house, gesturing for Veronica to follow.

They entered some type of workshop, with numerous trinkets and mechanical devices lining the tables and walls.

Sibyl grabbed something from a drawer and walked toward her.

Veronica extended her hand, and Sibyl dropped a small badge into her palm. It took her a moment to realize what it was, but then things began to click.

She looked up at Sibyl. “You… were an acolyte?”

“You recognize the emblem, then?” Sibyl asked, not directly answering her.

Veronica glanced back down at the image on the badge. Of course she knew the emblem. It represented a reverent deity. One of the major worshiped goddesses.

“The Goddess of Stories,” Veronica said softly.

Sibyl hummed with a smile. “She requests an audience with you whenever you are able.”

“With… me? Why?” Veronica asked.

A strange feeling settled over her. A Goddess wanted to meet her? Someone without an identity in the world? Someone who shouldn’t have existed in this timeline? That could only have meant one thing.

The Goddess knew about her.

Veronica scowled. Was the goddess upset that she was changing history? Was a reverent deity powerful to even know about changes to the timeline? And for what reason did she want to meet?

At the very least, it didn’t seem like she was a target for apprehension. Otherwise, the goddess would have told Sibyl to capture her, not request an audience. Whether attracting the Goddess’ attention was a good thing remained to be seen.

“I don’t know why,” Sibyl said. “But I suggest you don’t ignore the summons. Unless you die on this expedition of yours.”

Veronica furrowed her brow even tighter.

Was that a hint? Precognition? A warning hidden as a prophecy?

Or was she just overthinking it?

“Now leave. I have a bomb to make.”

Without waiting for a response, Sibyl ushered her out of the workshop and shut the door behind her.

Veronica remained outside for a moment, staring down at the badge in her palm. The emblem of the Goddess of Stories. The goddess who supposedly knew everything.

And now, she knew about her.
