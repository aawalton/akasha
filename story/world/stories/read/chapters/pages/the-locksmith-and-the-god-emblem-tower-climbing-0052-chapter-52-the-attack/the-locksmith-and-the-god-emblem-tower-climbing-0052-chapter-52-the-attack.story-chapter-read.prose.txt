He wasn't a sailor, much less a Bedouin, so what reason had he had to put himself in danger like that? Before he could be persuaded otherwise, he headed towards the horizon; the best ideas he'd had were instinctive. That was how he'd beaten Dracno, and that was how he would end up speeding things up.

The sounds of the crowd could still be heard, indicating that the battle continued. I hope there's still time... I need everyone distracted. He added inwardly.

His steps, now heavy, flooded his shoes with a thin layer of sand. I hate the desert. He muttered to himself as he noticed strange vibrations in the sand. He then began to walk faster. The vibrations, almost as if following his rhythm, also changed.

Through all that ocean of sand, there was a creature that had learned to swim through the dead terrain and that, somehow, resembled a boat. Matthews, and possibly the rest of the criminals on the floor, were unaware of the nature of the Metas; They thought the creature always reset like them. Harry and the others, however, knew that wasn't true.

A creature of such colossal size would certainly have to have an extremely efficient organism and internal system to stay alive on sand alone. That's what Harry initially thought, but what if it wasn't that? What if it was all an illusion? That wasn't why he was preparing this invasion, but curiosity didn't diminish his motives.

The vibrations soon began to feel like sand quakes that destabilized Harry. Looking back briefly, he saw the coliseum with its pygmy stature amidst the vastness of the desert. A blessing and a curse, isn't it?

Suddenly, Harry felt his foot slip as he seemed to fall into an endless pit of sand. They've arrived. And then he created a platform with its doors, anxiously facing the abyss that formed in the sand.

He wasn't very happy with his plan. It wasn't safe. It wasn't easy. It wasn't sane. Yes, Harry thrived in this kind of madness; he wallowed in chaos. This, however, was dangerous even by his standards.

Remembering his last encounter with the creature, Harry had a terrible stomach ache; he could barely concentrate. Now, however, he seemed healthy enough to tire out the creature that was about to lunge at him. 4, 5, 6... He used a calming method Devon had taught him. What would it say if it saw me here? A melancholy thought vanished as quickly as it came.

From inside the hole that was forming, Harry could see a white, gleaming point. Moving against the natural formation of the sandy abyss, Harry jumped out of the hole.

Smash! Like a crocodile in a swamp, the living boat leaped from the sand, aiming at Harry with deadly precision. Dodging only by a hair's breadth, Harry felt his entire heart freeze. Holy shit.

At that moment, the young thief realized what could've happened. He had experienced near-death situations before, but none had been as brutal as this one. Dodging the reaper was something he did daily, but unlike other times, this time he managed to see the scythe of death. If I were a second. No. Half a second later, I'd be being chewed up right now.

Without looking back, Harry measured the distance of the violent Meta by the frequency of the vibrations in the sand. Besides, of course, the agonizing scream the creature made. Thump! Thump! Like when he almost died from Emblem Exhaustion, Harry's heart seemed to be racing itself, pulsing at intervals different from Harry's desperate steps.

He jumped through doors that quickly disintegrated and then passed through a passage that saved him a few meters from the gigantic mouth of the boat. Can I keep this up? And he knew it was his anxiety talking, not his rational side. He ignored his worries and slowly turned towards the side of the Colosseum.

Unlike last time, when the Meta gave up as soon as they reached the Colosseum, Harry would do something different. He would bring her around while still reducing the distance to the Colosseum, circling it. He didn't know the exact reason why the Meta didn't want to attack the Colosseum, but he couldn't waste time looking for one.

Soon the announcer's sounds became audible once more. Something has to happen. Brun's words echoed in his mind. If I don't do this, how long will we be stuck here? And he imagined the countless loops they would go through, constantly fighting against their will. If even Matthews didn't escape this fate, what about Harry?

He yielded to his instincts and looked back. Besides the Meta's intimidating appearance, he noticed that the muscles around his body seemed to stretch more than necessary. Perhaps it was just the elastic mechanism that allowed the boat to travel great distances without tiring, but Harry had another idea.

He also noticed some other curious changes, such as — between the rows of teeth, each the size of the Locksmith's head — some teeth moving strangely, as if they were an alive part of the creature; a malfunctioning gear.

What if the singular Meta was actually just a whole bunch of Metas strung together? In all the previous floors, there were a lot of Metas. Why would this place be different? Adding to the fact that the boat was basically made from hatches from other floors, Harry seemed to figure something out; what if this is just a bait to lure prisoners and what not to the desert, in search of the true hatch? What if all of this wood isn't actually wood just as much as this boat isn't exactly a boat, but a junction of hundreds of inoffensive Metas? What if the real hatch is inside one of them?

Now's the time. Harry then set about creating higher passages, creating more vertical distance from the living boat, which seemed to grow even more from the sand to try and catch him. Getting even closer to the Colosseum, Harry decided to begin the second part of his plan.

Spinning around the creature, dodging its hungry teeth by mere centimeters, he gained speed, twisting his doors at funny angles, confusing the boat. Like a cat trying to catch a butterfly, the creature moved its triangular head towards the Locksmith.

It's all or nothing. And then he took a leap of faith. Gaining momentum, Harry created a passage above him, too high for the boat to reach. Come on... Stretch that body, child. And as if telepathically ordering the creature, he saw Meta extend its body like a spring, if springs had teeth.

Seeing a tunnel of flesh approaching his location, instead of being terrified, Harry felt a strange excitement. He glanced to his left and saw the Colosseum, realizing he still lacked height. I didn't want to have to do this, but it'll be interesting! And he fell directly into the creature's mouth.

He felt the Emblem Exhaustion rapidly taking over his body. I'm getting more and more unstable... And he sighed deeply to make sure he didn't faint. Only a few seconds left... It's now.

Struggling to avoid hitting the teeth, Harry looked up again and created a doorway towards the Colosseum. From the young thief's perspective, the sun was seemingly being devoured as he himself plummeted into the boat's throat. He realized he didn't even need to try so hard to avoid touching the creature's flesh, since it was wide enough for several Harry's to gather there.

He closed his eyes and created another passage, connecting it to the place above. The doors then vanished, and Harry was once again airborne, a missile heading straight for the Colosseum. Now comes the hard part. He looked back and exhaled wearily.

Where did he go...? Madeline wondered, looking back through the dark corridors of the circular venue. She turned around, seeing the end of the fight. As competitors, they had a more reserved space in the Colosseum. Still, she felt uncomfortable being in such a "privileged" position.

Bodkin and Manes were finishing the fight against one of the participants who seemed to use ropes to move and grab enemies. Despite the numerical advantage, it seemed difficult to catch the man, who was quickly becoming exhausted. It should end in a few minutes...

"Did you see where Harry went?" Madeline asked Vela and Goldman.

"Um, unfortunately not, dear..." Goldman said, giving her an apologetic smile. "But with everything that's been happening with his brother, I imagine it'll be a while. All this happening right after Prism died..."

Madeline then shifted in her chair and began tapping her foot. "...Yes." That was all she could manage to say. She looked back again and then stood up. "Wait, girl!" Goldman urged her to sit down.

"Mrs. Madeline, please," Vela said calmly. "Let's wait. Surely Mr. Locksmith must have important things to think about... alone."

"You don't know him like I do, alright?" She blurted out, but quickly looked away from the two, visibly embarrassed. "Something's happening, and that means..."

Like a mosquito drawn to light, Madeline turned to the sky, speechless. Her eyes widened and trembled at the spectacle she saw. The two, confused, looked at each other.

"Madeline? What are you—"

"There." She then pointed outside the coliseum. "I found Harry."

"What are you talking about..." Goldman said as he stood up and immediately spotted the same thing Madeline had. Vela did the same, the three now looking at the sky, completely absorbed.

"...It's not possible." Madeline added. "What, why..."

From his perspective, it was possible to see some kind of living boat, tens of meters long, flying. Like a speck of dust, a young man with a neatly arranged tie and long hair was near it, looking back with his arms wide open. Madeline couldn't properly see it, but she could already imagine his big smile.

In a moment, a gigantic door opened and began to swallow the entire creature. The three of them, instinctively, then looked up and saw another door of the same size. Oh no. Madeline thought.

Daniel, now known as Bodkin, was pressing down on the throat of the criminal who had previously tried to strangle Manes. The viking pressed his boot against the man's trachea, who desperately gasped for air. Daniel knew he couldn't kill him, but he could make him suffer. After all, that's what the public wanted, isn't it?

"You're not so brave with me, are you?" He knelt and stared him in the eye. "Well, everything has its price. Next time..."

And then he stopped when he saw the man's eyes widen and his muffled cries become more erratic. What are you trying to do? You've already lost... Bodkin, perplexed, searched for reason in his movements; they weren't survival instincts, but fear. Pure fear.

He took off his boot and grabbed his wrists at the same moment, realizing they seemed to be pointing at something.

"There..." He said while coughing violently.

Daniel then turned and looked up. He then came upon a stretched-out creature leaping into the Colosseum, like a frog leaping onto a rock.

Without saying a word, he turned to Manes, who seemed oblivious to what had happened, tending to a wound with a tourniquet made from the clothing of another fallen competitor.

In a leap, he ran to him and grabbed him by the shoulder. As expected, the audience's shouts could be heard, even the announcer was surprised. The dome was then removed.

"Hey!" Manes slapped Daniel's back, to no effect. "Let go of me, what are you..." And then he fell silent when he looked up.

Daniel, also curious, decided to look at the creature again, but to his surprise, near the mouth of the boat, a man was gracefully falling with his arms outstretched and a maniacal smile.

...It had to be you, didn't it? And so, Daniel smiled too.
