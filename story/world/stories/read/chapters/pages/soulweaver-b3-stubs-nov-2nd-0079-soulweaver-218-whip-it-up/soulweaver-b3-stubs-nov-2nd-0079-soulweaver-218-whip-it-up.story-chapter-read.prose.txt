Even still, I managed to convince them in due time, with Aerion throwing in her support once she got a handle on the plan.

The truth was, we didn’t have that many options. Hitting the compound from the air was a possibility, but as I had said before—a dangerous one. Not only would it compromise our surprise attack on the core when we did eventually decide to take it, but Yashas had confirmed the shield generator was deep underground. Which meant we would still have to find a way to infiltrate the place, assuming that our parachutes weren’t shot out of the sky by their anti-air defenses, which seemed all but inevitable.

As for the trains, it just so happened that, as part of Yashas’s scouting, he had located the nerve center of the Tokyo Metro system, where all the trains were controlled from. From there, we’d have options.

Or so I hoped. I suppose we’d only find out once we got there.

This time, though, we did not rush out blindly and without preparation. We were going to take the day getting ready, with the plan being to leave tomorrow morning.

This would be the final battle, after all. Or the start of it, anyway. I had no doubt in my mind that what we were about to face would be the most grueling fights I’d ever seen upon reaching Axius. We were talking about days and days of traveling through enemy territory, fighting through an army of skeletons and hordes of zombies.

So it was paramount that we went in prepared. Not just physically, but mentally.

I was personally good in the mental department, having had my fill the previous night. But in terms of capabilities, I had to up my game.

After breakfast and after deliberations died down, Aerion and I excused ourselves and headed up to the roof.

I spared the tied-down Bell helicopter a single glance before shaking my head, letting Galia out from my inventory.

Thankfully, the phoenix seemed to handle staying inside my inventory much better than Aerion did. After all, anything living put inside there was fully conscious, just unable to breathe, move, or even think.

I honestly couldn’t even imagine what it was like. It sounded pretty horrible to me, but Aerion was a champ in putting up with it. Even still, her limit was about a day inside there, and even then, she was quite visibly shaken when she emerged, needing several minutes to reorient herself.

Galia, on the other hand, seemed to buzz right out, blabbering and flapping her wings without a care in the world. Which, while fortunate, had become something of a crutch. We needed her to be useful in the upcoming fight—not as a front-line combatant, but for her unique and honestly miraculous ability to save lives with her tears.

I really wanted her to develop an ability that let her produce the tears on demand, or perhaps even a healing ability based on that, which was why we had been meticulous about selecting only soul crystals from monsters with such healing abilities. It had been a chore and a half to get her to actually eat them, though, despite the rarity.

“I swear,” Aerion said, giving the clucking Galia an exasperated look. “Why is she so obsessed with Fire Crystals? She’s like a magnet to them.”

I chuckled. “Yeah, well, she is a little firebird,” I said, stroking her head.

“It’s no help that you pamper her this much,” Aerion huffed, her hands on her hips. “She knows that anytime she wants something, she can just come to you.”

“Aw, it’s not that bad,” I said, scratching my nose before looking into Galia’s cute eyes. I felt my resistance melt. I stopped myself, realizing my hand was already halfway into my inventory, about to grab another soul crystal with fire abilities.

“Well… huh. Okay, maybe it is that bad,” I muttered. “But hey, her fire abilities are the other thing we want to develop.”

“Well, yes,” Aerion said, crossing her arms with a frown. “It concerns me that she has not developed a proper healing ability. I fear that we will absolutely need to rely on that for the fights ahead.”

“Well,” I said, pulling out a small heap of fire crystals mixed in with crystals from healing abilities, “there’s no time like the present to gorge her.”

Galia squawked and flared her wings the second she saw them, nearly tripping over herself to get to the pile.

“Galia?” I said, looking at the bird. “This is your lucky day.”

“I can only hope this will be enough,” Aerion said, looking exasperated.

“It better be,” I replied, giving the bird a sidelong glance. While she had certainly expanded in size recently, she still wasn’t quite large enough to ride. More worryingly, our once vast supply of crystals was now dwindling at the rate the gluttonous bird consumed them.

If she didn’t manifest something soon, I feared we’d soon run into a roadblock—especially considering how none of the zombies or skeletons manifested soul crystals of any kind. Only the brain creatures and the commanders did, and those had been few and far between.

I sighed, shaking my head. “It’ll be what it’ll be,” I said, moving some distance away as our gluttonous bird started chomping away at crystal after crystal. I kept one eye on her as I pulled out the gigantic forge the elves had given me. “In the meantime, I’ve got some forging to do.”

For one, I needed to repair Aerion’s armor that had been utterly trashed in our previous fights. Thankfully, my stock of Mythril and my absolute cheat ability to forge things with far more skill and efficiency than I had any right to came in clutch here, allowing me to repair the thing in less than an hour.

It was not lost on me that even Rogar would have taken at least a few weeks to manage that feat. But hey, what good was magic if you couldn’t use it to cheat?

After that, I went to work repairing my own armor, which had significantly less damage but was still in need of some repair. And once I was done with that, I focused on the real reason I was here—bringing out the shards that I had Initialized before the Humvee raid.

As effective as they were, the things still had no minds of their own, which meant they relied on my control. Something that I hoped to rectify.

The problem, of course, was that the shards were small, and that meant they were barely big enough to house a soul crystal. Which restricted me to Common crystals. Anything bigger and the shards would become too large and unwieldy, moving around far too slowly to do any real damage without the aid of [Launch] to boost them.

That was okay, because all I really needed them to do was target the nearest enemy, smash into them, and repeat the process on nearby enemies before returning within collection range of my inventory.

In this way, I could focus solely on Launching and recovering my projectiles rather than actively aiming them, which I currently had to do. My brain was the bottleneck right now, but with this, I would become, if not a machine gun, something closer to a fully automatic rifle.

Because right now, what I needed more than a mega ability capable of destroying bosses—my Siege Bolt arsenal was plenty for that—was an area-of-effect ability. Something that was closer to a machine gun, though even that wasn’t really ideal.

While my shards did have some ability to identify friend from foe, they could only bend the laws of physics so much. If I happened to aim at a zombie that Aerion happened to be behind, there was little the shard could do but crash headlong into her after punching through the zombie.

It was the bane of all projectile weapons. They more or less went in a straight line. Between Initialization and soul crystals, I could make them curve in mid-air—something I imagined that points in Wisdom would have allowed me to gain if I hadn’t had my unique ability set—but they were still fundamentally directional.

As such, after imbuing a dozen shards with Common and Uncommon souls, I turned my attention to an entirely new weapon. One that I’d been mulling over for some time.

What I wanted was something that could rip through hordes of enemies in close vicinity. Something that had much more ability to change direction while still being extremely effective against a crowd.

Projectiles were out, as were most forms of conventional area-control melee weapons, such as greatswords or polearms. For one, my skill with those things was next to non-existent. But for another, they were simply too dangerous and too rigid to wield in confined spaces.

“You’re grinning,” Aerion said from across the rooftop. “That means you’re up to something sinister.”

My grin widened, showing teeth. “Maybe.” But also, how could she see that from way over there? Sometimes, Aerion’s intuition was absolutely terrifying. Especially when it came to me.

She was absolutely right, but I also had no idea if it would work, which meant there was only one thing to do.

I began by bringing out the Voidsteel I’d stored and chucking it into the forge. The nice thing about my inventory preserving everything was that the forge was always up to temperature and ready to use.

Furthermore, the forge the elves had given me was more akin to a blast furnace, with air being continuously pumped in via a magically powered turbine pump.

That meant it could heat steel way hotter than a regular furnace, which was necessary when dealing with exotic materials like Mythril or Voidsteel.

Once the hunk of metal was glowing red, I chiseled off a chunky strip on my anvil about two feet long and four inches in diameter.

After several such reheatings, I had several dozen such strips arrayed before me. They looked almost like cylindrical silver ingots, but that changed before long as I heated them one by one, bending them over my anvil to form a U-shaped object, interlinking one with another before the metal fully cooled.

“A chain?” Aerion asked, catching on immediately.

“Uh-huh,” I said. “But not just any chain.”

If I was right, the fact that the links of the chain weren’t physically fused to each other meant that each link was its own entity. And while a link of a normal size was far too small to hold a soul crystal, I didn’t need this to move fast at all.

Each link was far beefier than a regular chain would be—so beefy, in fact, that it would be impossible to hold if it didn’t power itself. Even then, I’d need my enhanced stats to properly wield it.

Slowly, I reheated the chain, embedding a Common soul crystal into each link for a total of about thirty links, all while focusing on the stats—or rather, colors—I wanted via my Soulweaving interface. While that number didn’t sound like a lot, considering that each link was a foot in length, it gave me plenty of chain.

But the final touch came at the ends—with one end terminating in a spiked mace that I forged from a sphere I stuck spiky Voidsteel pieces into, and the other being a large handle.

Inside both the mace and the handle went Rare soul orbs.

And when that was done, I finally Initialized the thing, nearly gawking at the cost.

At 122 points, it was the single most expensive thing I had, aside from Aerion. Even my Heavy armor suit wasn’t even that much. Only the fact that I’d leveled to C-7 after our recent battles made it affordable, even if it did nearly max out my capacity at 1342/1410.

“Oh, yeah. This is gonna be good.”
