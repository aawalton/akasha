Hayden found the spirit beast’s appearance charming. It looked like a mouse, but it was the size of a bunny, and it had beautiful, angelic wings and fur as white as fallen snow.

Hayden lifted his hand, and it landed on his palm. Then it stared at him with its beady black eyes.

“I think I’ll call you… Gluttony,” Hayden said. “Is that okay?”

Gluttony squeezed his eyes shut and squeaked in delight. Hayden felt winsome as he stroked its wings.

“You don’t even know how special you are, do you?”

Gluttony cocked its head, but then turned when it heard a series of fawning cries. An entire group of women had gathered to see the horix, and they were trying to convince each other to approach Hayden.

“Allow me to demonstrate a fraction of your power,” Hayden said. He placed the little horix onto his shoulder and walked to the nervous women with a friendly smile. “Would you like to meet him?”

The women squealed and rushed Gluttony, who instantly took to loving women. He was clearly a ladies' man—and a glutton for attention.

Rosen saw the scene—and was furious. That night, he forced himself into Ms. Narin’s office.

“What the hell was that?” he demanded.

“What do you mean what was that?” Ms. Narin asked. “I gave Hayden the spirit we agreed upon.”

“You didn’t say how much it’s worth! Do you even know how much that thing’s going up on auction for?”

She scoffed. “What part of can’t sell it don’t you understand? Nothing’s changed—he has a pet. He’s screwed for the tournament, and probably for life. Isn't that what you wanted?”

Rosen took deep breaths through his nose. “I get what you're saying—but I don't buy it. Did you see how fast he turned down Thunder Whip? It was just like—” he snapped his fingers—“that. He knew exactly what he was getting—and he liked it.”

Ms. Narin looked around and shrugged. “I don’t know what to tell you.”

“You don’t know… Well, I suppose that’s how you ended up teaching in this D-rank shit heap.” He turned and stormed out of the office.

The next morning, Kayline and Lina met in the meadow for the third day of the ceremony. Of course, as desperate and immature as it made Kayline feel, the first thing she did was look for Hayden.

It didn't take long for her to find him. Unsurprisingly, he was just as expressionless as he had been every other day she had seen him. That was a trait that she found generally unattractive, but was strangely attractive in his case. It wasn’t visibly attractive. She just admired the fact that he had so much control over his emotions. It was as though he didn't like people to know what he was thinking and had the willpower to hide it. She found that fascinating—and unnatural.

There’s something wrong with that guy, she thought.

As she was thinking that, Loricks walked on stage and delivered the proclamation the students had been yearning for.

“Although anyone can make this announcement, I always feel great pleasure making it myself,” he said. “The work year has ended, and the time for redemption has come! In just two weeks’ time, we will be starting the Redemption Festival and—”

The students’ applause and whistles were so loud that he was forced to pause—such was the thrill and effect of the announcement.

Loricks paused and started over once the riotous crowd slowed.

“As you know, the Redemption Festival is more than just a series of tournaments. It’s an opportunity for those who worked hard to get early scholarships to rifter academies. Those who take first in either the martial arts or spirit tournaments will obtain a full-time scholarship to Junolyx University—a B-rank academy.”

The strongest men beat their chests like mindless primates, and nearby women laughed.

“Those who take second and third place will obtain a placement at a C-rank school, cutting a year off their work contracts. In total, there will be nine who will move on from Galma early: three for each gender in the martial arts tournament, and three from the coed spirit tournament.

“This is a very exciting opportunity, and I’m sure everyone is planning to compete and win. That said, I'm equally sure that most are just excited for the three week vacation.”

Kayline cupped her ears playfully when students released battle cries. It was funny to see them jumping up and down, high-fiving each other, and bumping fists. It was an overdramatized celebration that made Loricks chuckle.

“That’s right—everyone gets three weeks off,” he said. “That said, I recommend that you spend it training with your spirits. As a reminder, Galma doesn’t provide rifting licenses. Its purpose is to provide you opportunities, and prepare you for the entrance exams of real institutions. If you do not train, you’re working hard for nothing. Do you understand?”

Loricks scanned their nodding faces, then pursed his lips when he saw Hayden’s stoic mask. Loricks looked pained when he glanced at him. Kayline didn’t fail to notice.

After the ceremony, Lina followed Kayline back to the dorms, complaining all the way.

“You really need to stop looking at that guy,” Lina said, “it’s creeping everyone out.”

Kayline puffed out her lower lip. “It doesn’t seem to be creeping him out.”

“And that’s what creeps me out. You’re out there giving flutter-eyes and he’s just standing there like a… statue. I’m pretty sure you could flash the guy and he’d frown.”

Kayline shrugged. “So? I kinda like that.”

“You like that? Okay, hold up.” Lina jumped in front of her sister. “What’s your trauma? He’s weak, poor, and ugly, and there’s no universe in which you could even date him.”

“But he acts like he’s strong, rich, and handsome. And I’ll date whoever I damned well please.”

Lina rolled her eyes. “Yes, sure… whatever. I’m a strong independent woman who’s going to introduce my scrub boyfriend to galactic leaders. Grow up. Next month you’ll be back at Junolyx and pizza face’ll be cold typing in Loricks’ office. That’s how it is, so chill.”

Kayline laughed sharply. “What’s your trauma? It’s like you want him to fail. What's up with that? You bully him or something?”

Lina turned away. “Of course not. I’m just trying to prepare you for disappointment. ‘Cause you’re going to ooh and ahh and ogle and then Goren’s gonna juice his skull like an orange. And you know why? Because Goren's seven feet tall, and Hayden hasn't even opened his gate. That's why. It's over, sis. Get over it.”

Kayline bit her lip. What Lina said was true: there was no way Hayden could fight a seven-foot behemoth naturally. He could have won if he had opened his gate. Once a rifter opened their gate, their muscles rapidly fused with cora. Then, Scrawny Boy Hayden could out-bench Goren. It could be a powerful equalizer. Problem was—Hayden didn't open his gate. He didn't have a chance at victory—and it was his own fault.

Lina was right. Hayden Reskco was staying at Galma—that’s just how it was.

Kayline sighed. “Yeah…you're right.”

“As always.” Lina flicked her hair and walked off, clowning around to make Kayline smile. Kayline didn't need the encouragement, though. Something was off about Hayden—and she had a strange premonition he would come out on top regardless.

Hayden knew he would come out on top, but it certainly didn't feel like it. He couldn't even do push-ups properly.

How did this cretin survive? Hayden wondered. He had only done one hundred eighty-three push-ups, and his arms felt like they were approaching physical damage.

Hayden rolled onto his back, catching his breath. This is unbelievable… He lay for a minute and then sat up. At least it's good for infusion.

Coran infusion required weavers to break down their muscles and circulate cora into them. Hayden had no problem ripping up his muscles, so it was an ironic boon.

Hayden also had Gluttony—the greatest boon of all.

He summoned the winged mouse on his palm. It chittered and sniffed as it looked around. Then it locked his eyes on Hayden.

“Hey, Gluttony. Are you hungry?”

Its eyes lit up, and it nodded rapidly.

“Well, I’m poor and there’s no beasts around to kill.”

Gluttony’s ears drooped.

“But… I do have this little trick.” Hayden grinned when the horix’s ears perked up. “And if you learn it… well, you’ll never be hungry again.”
