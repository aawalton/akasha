The screams of the dead mingled with those of the dying, to Crow. Ephemeral souls twisted with their desire to have vengeance poured out on the Witchhunters. Though he’d expected it, none of the spectral hounds appeared, perhaps indicating these hunters specialized away from that capacity.

The spirits of the dead ignored the counterattacks dealt to them, immaterial even against enchanted blades and special abilities.

They don’t belong here to begin with, and they don’t have long. Long enough to have made their ire known, that’s what counts.

True to Crow’s will, the spirits seemed entirely unable to attack his quarry, Arthen. They clearly wanted to, crowding around him, as he spun around and swung his weapon at them uselessly. But they were rebuffed as if by a reverse polarity, unable to close in.

When Crow thought he was unobserved, he tossed a dagger for Arthen’s throat, but a sudden shift made it a glancing blow. Even this did nothing, as a wound negation was spent.

Arthen spun around and pointed his blade with a snarl. “You’re the source of this! What are they? Who?!”

“It doesn’t matter,” Crow replied, “but sure. Stop me, stop them. Have a try. There are always rules to deal with, aren’t there?”

A growl behind Arthen presaged the sudden charge of a giant wolf, likely having avoided the unleashing of spirits by being far enough back. The warg came right for Crow, naturally.

Crow dove out of the way of the warg’s snapping jaws, rolling and coming back up — right into a thrown dagger aimed at him from Arthen. His balance was such that he couldn’t avoid it, though he tried twisting at the last moment.

A wound negation was shredded as the dagger bounced off of his coat.

“Touché,” Crow muttered.

The warg pivoted quickly, turning around and baring its fangs, ready for another charge… and then several spirits came down on it at once, screaming, biting with too-sharp and too-long teeth, clawing with too-horrible talons, their eerie eyes glowing bright and hot in the dark of the underground.

“Flee, Winterseed!” Arthen commanded. As he spoke, he threw another dagger at Crow.

Dodging it easily, Crow did the same, and Arthen rolled out of the way as well.

The warg ignored the command. It snapped its jaws into ephemeral nothing, and the spirit half inside it, unperturbed, bit its face and clawed for its throat. Simultaneously, three more were ripping into its torso as if feasting.

A hunger that can’t be quenched, no doubt.

Growls of pain turned to whimpers as the warg’s flesh was shredded and its blood spilled open and freely, cut through like cheesecloth. More attacks, and more spirits piled on, and he quickly went still.

Arthen had his own teeth bared, growling in obvious distress. Nonetheless, he didn’t otherwise seem to grieve. He moved forward cautiously, his head flicking around at the surrounding spirits staring hungrily at him, their hands outstretched.

By now, everyone else in his party was already dead or had fled. Crow wasn’t sure about the last part, but he thought he caught sight of some magic slung around. Perhaps some wizards got away, perhaps not.

Arthen stalked toward Crow with his blade pointed, constantly eyeing his flanks, but the spirits couldn’t reach him, just snarl and stare at him with hatred and desire.

Crow pulled out two long daggers, one with a handguard and an extensive hilt — a parrying dagger. He nodded to Arthen as he whistled. “I’m surprised you aren’t running. Wouldn’t that be pragmatic?”

Scowling at Crow as he approached, Arthen replied, “A man has to feel out his tests. I feel them waiting for me to run, and I’d as soon deny them cutting me down from behind. You’re the source. With luck, I take you down, and they vanish. Aside from that, I’d rather not suffer you to live.”

Crow smiled faintly. “Perhaps too-wishful thinking.”

“We pick our battles. I’ve chosen mine!” With that, Arthen dashed forward at heightened speeds, his blade flashing.

Crow managed to dodge the first strike with his man-like reflexes, and parried the follow-up with his off-hand. He pivoted to dart in and stab with his good hand, and Arthen jerked away at the last moment, the blade merely forming a cut in the leather at his breast.

They both recoiled slightly, and quickly moved back in, inevitably falling back into each other’s gravity wells, inevitably circling and feeling each other out.

Crow whistled and said, “No love lost for your dear warg, hmm? I suppose your kind just see animals as tools.”

Arthen sniffed derisively. “Only the weak-minded mourn a stupid beast. He’ll be replaced. Perhaps I’ll bring you to heel, feline. You’d make a fine pet.”

Crow formed a predatory grin. He’s a liar, and just seeks to goad and unbalance me with taunts. “I wouldn’t. I’ve been known to bite the hand that feeds!” With a short whistle, he charged.

Arthen attempted to interrupt Crow’s dash forward. Still, Crow was ready for it, leading with his off-hand to lock the blade successfully and attempting a wide-spanning slash overhead with his other, made to be difficult to dodge. Arthen did not try — he swatted it with his armored back hand, knocking the strike aside.

Twisting the locked blade, Arthen managed to free it as well as attempt an ensuing, haphazard stab. Crow was so off-balance that his attempt to dodge got him poked in the face, wiping out another wound negation. Nonetheless, Crow jammed up the blade again and jerked it aside to throw his opponent off-balance. Instead of going for a kill-shot, as Arthen might suspect, he swept up low for a leg.

He scored a solid slash with his main dagger, slicing through leather armor and what should’ve been a nasty bleeding wound. But no — another wound negation.

Crow dodged quickly away from a follow-up slash, and they soon were circling each other again.

Arthen looked at him appraisingly. “You make up for what you lack in skill with sheer speed and agility. I suppose I should’ve expected it.”

Crow whistled and shrugged. “I’ve always been a quick guy.”

Arthen frowned. “Why do you keep whistling? It’s an annoying habit. Is that a cat thing?”

“You know, I’m really not a fan of stereotypes,” Crow replied balefully.

“Is that so? Then I’ll be sure to continue, Catboy!” He moved in with another quick barrage, as Crow parried and countered once more.

Again and then again, once more, they made similar exchanges, until wound negations were cut through and each was cut in the flesh a little, too — just barely. They bled as they circled each other yet again. There was a lot of annoying whistling, to say the least.

“This has been fun, Catboy,” Arthen said with heavy irony, “but it’s time to end it!”

“Funny, I was thinking the same thing, Apeboy!” Crow cried.

They clashed blades once more, but this time, Arthen, blade and all, turned into smoke. Simultaneously, Crow felt a presence behind him going for his throat.

Ah. An illusory double. Classic.

Without time to turn around, Crow instead summoned up the particulars of his actual, unique class: [Requiter], the death-centric bard.

Having fully gotten the measure of his opponent by whistling in different tones throughout the fight, he knew exactly the correct tone to cause him not only distress and pain, but immediate spiritual and physical harm.

So he whistled again. This time, though, it was supernaturally loud and echoing from a distance, passing through the spirit world, and focusing directly on the heavily pinpointed target of Arthen’s soul.

A choked-off scream came behind him as Crow spun around and backed away. He kept whistling as he saw Arthen twisted up in agony, his body shaking, his face a breathless rictus. He dropped his blade as he fell to his hands and knees.

Crow paused in the ‘death-whistle.’ “At least now you know why I was doing it.”

Gasping for breath, hands curling around dirt as he continued writhing, Arthen asked with straining effort, “Were you just… playing with me?”

“No. It was tactical. The slow charge-up targeting worked better, so I stalled and committed to the risk of fighting you. In fact, I don’t think I could kill you with this. It’s the opposite of my slow-damaging harp. With this, you’ll gradually get more resistant.”

“I…” Arthen pivoted suddenly, trying to fling a throwing dagger he had in his boot. “Look forward to it!”

Crow whistled right before the blade came from Arthen’s hand, spoiling the aim and not needing to dodge as the blade went spinning into a wall. Meanwhile Arthen pitched into the dirt and writhed in agony as his flesh tried to tear away from him in every direction. His wide-brimmed hat fell off his head.

Walking up even as he kept whistling, Crow dropped and put his knee into the man’s back, as he grabbed his hair and pulled his head up, slipping his dagger around to Arthen’s exposed throat. Finally, he stopped whistling and said, “I’m afraid that won’t be in the cards. This is where you die.”

Arthen, wheezing, managed to whisper, “Figured as much.”

Despite himself, Crow grinned at the cavalier response. Briefly, he whistled to make sure Arthen couldn’t fully recover, causing another brief seizure of sorts and a pained groan. He asked, “Any last words, Arthen?”

Arthen wheezed a bit before asking, “Can I take a minute to think?”

“No. Last chance, you fuck. Now or never.”

The body briefly jerked under him, but Arthen was still too weak and disoriented, and Crow easily kept him down. Arthen finally sagged and eked out, “May the next life be like this one. I regret nothing.”

“I see. I’d wish you luck, but you were a bastard, after all.” With nothing further, Crow sliced Arthen’s throat ear-to-ear, slow and deep to be sure. If the gurgling, choking sounds that followed weren’t enough clue to the success, the torrent of blood that spilled forth into the dirt was.

With Arthen thoroughly and quickly dying at that point, Crow wiped his blade off on the man’s back and stood.

The spirits gathered around them in a circle were peering at him, entirely silent and calm, most of their bodies transparent, nearly invisible. Only their glowing eyes were clear. They were all still dark and bitter to the core, but at least there seemed some measure of satisfaction.

They seemed to be waiting.

Justice is a fine and precise blade. Best not to wield it wantonly. It is not made for bloodlusting. My quest is finished, and that is enough for me.

Crow waved his hand as he put away his daggers. “Go. We’re done here. I’m leaving, too, and not slowly, either.”

The spirits parted as he dashed away down a corridor. He felt them fade away behind him, but he did not look back.

In his head, Death mused to himself. ‘A part in the play has been played, Sister. But even winning the war will not win the battle, will it? So many times, so many ways, we’ve seen it end and begin…’
