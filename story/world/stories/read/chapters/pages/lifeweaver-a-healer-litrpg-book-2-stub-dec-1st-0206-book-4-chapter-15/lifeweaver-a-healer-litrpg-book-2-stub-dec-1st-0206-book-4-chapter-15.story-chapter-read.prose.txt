Perwen shared some of what he knew. Perhaps Luke would have been able to eke out a little more information, but pressed for time as he was, the answer he got would have to do. The entirety of the Singers' political landscape was not something of interest, but Perwen reiterated what he had shared up to that point, adding some interesting tidbits. In short, there were two sides with one led by Luom's father, wanting to keep humans around as pets, slaves, or whatever you wanted to call it, while the other wanted them exterminated as vermin. The first side wasn't doing it out of compassion or anything, but to make sure there were as many Integrated on Earth as possible, since that correlated with the number of portals opening, and the Singers were starved for experience. Now, with Luom's death, the kill-them-all side would gain a majority. It would take a little time, according to Perwen, but weapons like the one that had reduced New York to rubble would be deployed all over, with execution squads mopping up whoever was still alive after. Bleak, to say the least. Other than a few names of Singers in leadership positions, Luke didn't have time to stick around. Not far from their positions, humans were fighting Singers, and even from a distance, Luke could tell their ambush had devolved into utter chaos. They needed him.

Running down the slope after finishing Perwen, leaving them all where they had fallen, Luke sent the information to Milla, but also to Ray, hoping the Assassin could delay proceedings over there a little further in a fox-in-the-henhouse situation. The distance to the fight below was farther than he'd estimated, and things didn't look so good. Curtis was doing his part, acting like a wedge and spreading himself across the road, almost blanketing it in darkness, but the others were struggling. More Singers than they had planned for remained in the back part of the column, engaged with The Band, and Curtis was doing nothing to help them, even after providing them with a challenge above what they could rise to meet.

Smudge came up next to Luke, matching his pace. The voidling shimmered and grew to a size that almost matched that of a horse while keeping his dog-shape.

I carry.

The sending took Luke by surprise, and he almost stumbled over a rock outcropping. "Won't I fall into your void?"

No.

Well, there was no use arguing. The Band needed him, and by the time he got there on foot, fast as he was, it might be too late. While Curtis acted as a sort of shield, or perhaps a river of darkness, parting the strong force of Singers, almost all of those at the front end of the column had turned around to face the threat, meaning the small group now had a small army to beat. Not great odds, at least not for Nash, Martin, and the others. To get there faster, Luke jumped and pushed himself off the ground with a thread of mana as an extra boost, landing on top of Smudge while holding his breath. He trusted his companion, but Smudge wasn't the most well-reasoned being. His thinking Luke wouldn't fall in didn't necessarily make it so, but to Luke's relief, it worked. Straddling a void, perhaps the void, had not been something he anticipated doing when he woke up that morning, but life carries you through strange and wonderful experiences.

And it was wonderful. Smudge wasn't just quick. He was fast to a ridiculous degree, flowing over the ground as if powered by silent jet engines. After closing the distance in a matter of seconds, Luke's Weaver's Perception picked up on the status of those fighting right in the middle of the large crowd of Singers. Things were dire, even worse than he'd imagined. No one had died as of yet, but it was a close thing.

"We have to jump!" Luke shouted as he pointed over the heads of the Singers. The tall, lithe, and humanoid figures milled about, pressing in against the human Integrated. Most of them seemed like martial types, hefting different sorts of weapons ranging from spears and daggers to rifles and even more futuristic gear. Somewhere in there, Luke thought he saw a lightsaber, but that couldn't be it, could it? If it was, Luke called dibs as he and Smudge more flew than jumped over their heads.

"Smudge! Your job is dealing with those!" Luke shouted, pointing to a small group of Singers standing off to the side. By the spellweaves over their heads and the clothes they wore, he identified them as healers and support casters. Cutting them off from the other Singers was important. "You can eat their meta-hearts!"

That got Smudge going, and rather than continue the downward arc of the high jump, the voidling pushed forward in excitement, almost like he'd just realized he could fly. Shaking his head in disbelief, Luke rolled off his companion's back and fell right into the middle of the large group of Singers, landing in a small, clear area right in the middle of the human Integrated. Stunned, Singers had stopped fighting for a moment to look up at the Lifeweaver surging through the air atop a creature so dark you could only see an outline, but when Luke landed, all hell broke loose again.

"Mas!" Martin shouted as he phased out of the physical plane to dodge a blow before ramming his fist into a Singer and phasing back in. The Singer's chest tore itself apart, unable to keep its form with the Phase Blade invading it, and it fell dead on the spot.

Luke didn't need to be told about the condition of the Integrated he'd allowed to follow them into a battle they weren't ready for. Other than Martin, the humans were in terrible condition. The other standout wasn't human at all, but a half-demon. Lemira had brought out her full force of lions and wolves, and they protected her while tearing into the enemy. Mas was almost dead. He would be already if not for his sister, Sam, directing all the vitality she drained from Singers into her brother. Even that wouldn't be enough. Without intervention, the Conduit would die within a minute or so, Vitality alone unable to regenerate the gaping wound in his shoulder. And he wasn't the only one. Nash had turned into a hulking behemoth who radiated power, but his right arm hung limp at his shoulder, and several piercing attacks had found their way to his internal organs, rupturing them. He might not look it, but he wouldn't last long either. Savannah wasn't injured, but the constructs she used both for protection and attacking were faltering, going in and out of focus, like they would burst apart at any moment. Heaps of dead Singers were at their feet, but many more surrounded them. It was good Luke had come, or this fight might have been over in another couple of minutes.

Using The Healer's Moment, Luke bought himself time to make sure his friends would last a little longer. This was the reason he hadn't used it in the previous fight. Weaver's Renewal was necessary to save Mas, and it ate into his considerable mana supply, and still more mana was necessary to deal with Nash's internal injuries. Cuts and scrapes he ignored, but once time resumed, all mortal wounds had been taken care of. Wanting to protect the others, Luke triggered a skill that hadn't seen much use before.

Lifeweaver's Sacrifice [Toggleable]: Absorb damage from anyone touched by your threads.

For it to stay active on all of them, he needed to keep the threads connecting himself to the rest of the guild members active, but that was a small price to pay. Lemira received a cut on her arm, and a wound opened up in Luke's instead, closing almost the same moment it appeared. Small spatters of fire in a cascading shower rained over Sam, who cried out before realizing she was safe. The burn wounds transferred over to Luke, who stepped forward and grabbed hold of a Singer, draining her of mana to heal himself. Wounds were opening up all over his body, and it didn't take much to understand how The Band was too outnumbered to inflict any meaningful damage themselves.

Luke: "Curtis! Can you drag your ass over here and help the crew?"

No answer. More and more wounds opened up, and to Luke's horror, a Singer lashing out with spells that looked a lot like darts made out of purple-glowing light struck a thread of mana and even managed to break through it, severing his connection to Mas just as several darts struck him. One blew through his skull, and the other punched into his heart. It was too much for his body to bear. The Conduit died in an instant. Luke reached out and cleaved the offending Singer's head with Weaver's Whip, but that couldn't reverse what had just happened. Sam let out a cry of anguish and despair as she lunged to her brother's side.

"Heal him!" she shouted, looking at Luke, her eyes wet with tears. But it was too late.

Having seen the effect of Luke's threads being cut, the massing Singers pressed in, focusing their attacks on the remaining threads as a trio of melee fighters stepped past Martin and charged Luke, daggers dripping with some ominous green substance.

Seeing Mas crumple to the ground had made Luke freeze. It hadn't taken him more than a second to come back to himself, but that brief lapse had been enough to put them all in danger. A different Singer reached the thread connecting Luke to Nash, and a third the one to Lemira, just as the three Singers reached him. Cold steel pierced his chest, and the poison began its dark work.

The pain, he almost didn't feel. In the grand scheme of painful experiences, being run through with knives was nothing, but Luke cried out as the Singers lashed out at the remaining human Integrated. It was not a war-cry, per se, nor was it borne of fear or despair. No, what Luke felt, more than anything, was anger. At himself? Yeah, sure, but most of it was not directed inward, but toward the Singers.

How dare they?
