Karav had been waiting on a hot Chitrakutan rooftop for nearly an hour when the Chitran Raja’s guard detail entered the square below.

Even from this distance, he could see that Matiman was animatedly chatting with another kothi advisor, utterly unaware of his surroundings.

Any halfway experienced Warrior would balk at the obliviousness, but then, Matiman was no Warrior. The four bodyguards in full seric armor were responsible for that.

As was inevitable with any outing of someone as important as a Raja, passersby congregated around them. Not in any way that troubled the guards, of course. They were clearly used to working with crowds such as these. The Chitran loved their Raja, after all. They wanted to be close to him.

While that served his ends splendidly, Karav would be lying if he said the sight didn’t bother him.

A mere five years under Matiman’s rule and the Chitran hailed him as a demon they would die for. The Akh Nara had certainly chosen well, placing that kothi on the throne. Which only made Karav’s mission here all the more important.

A beloved leader was the most dangerous kind.

He lifted the spyglass to his eye and focused on Matiman.

A civilian merchant was animatedly talking to the Raja, who smiled and nodded intently, giving the conversation his full attention when other leaders would have long since moved on. Not Matiman.

Meanwhile, Karav’s operatives moved through the crowd, moving ever closer.

Their human disguises—currently hidden under hooded robes—had been created by the Order’s most experienced makeup artists. Not even Karav could have seen through them. Not even if he were looking, and he was no neophyte in that department. Demons in the Human Realm either learned to look human or they died miserable deaths, after all.

His operatives wore robes common to the Garga. An everyday sight in these lands. No one paid them heed, and the only ones capable of seeing through their disguise—Iksana ghaels possessing Clarity—were as rare in these parts as humans.

Under those demonic robes, of course, his operatives wore very different garb.

The Chitran would remember the attackers as human from Sai. Karav had picked the country specifically because a merchant delegation was to visit Chitrakuta today. Their guilt would be further cemented in the eyes of all those watching today. One last push to tip those delicately balanced scales.

The operatives closed within range.

Karav’s mind was oddly calm in that moment. If anything, he was grateful that Fate had been kinder to him than he had any right to expect.

The Akh Nara’s reunification of the realms—still an unbelievable feat Karav would forever marvel at—had ironically also removed the only thing stopping the Order from accomplishing their dream.

As a single nation in the Voidlands, they’d been under constant threat of annihilation. A small fish in the vast ocean that was the Human Realm.

But now? Now, only land stood between them and demonkind. Now, they could pass freely to and fro without the Akh Nara’s knowledge.

Jagath had come to their side, bringing the Aindri’s might to bear, and the Iksana had not been far behind.

It was almost too easy, though he had to admit it made some sense. Reuniting the realms was bound to cause friction sooner or later, and on a scale the Order could never have manufactured on its own.

Karav was simply hastening the process.

The operatives were within ten paces of the honor guard now, and not one demon had even thought to question them.

Falguna had failed because his operatives had treated Tia as a low priority. This was different. This operation bore the full might of Order intelligence. Perfectly planned and expertly executed.

His operatives charged as one, drawing their weapons in a coordinated strike. This was the difficult bit of the operation. His operatives could use neither their superior demonic physique nor their arts, or the whole charade would be for naught.

Matiman’s guards caught the motion, with the Raja himself realizing it a moment later.

Through the spyglass, Karav saw the kothi’s expression shift from joviality to one of shock and alarm. He only had time to take a single step back and widen his eyes before the first dagger slit his throat.

The second pierced his chest a half-second later, while the third was blocked by a guard who took the blade for his charge.

It mattered not. More than enough damage had already been inflicted.

The guards moved quickly, but Karav’s forces had the element of surprise.

They moved too late, and his operatives had already begun the second phase. All three threw off their robes, revealing their human faces and their Saian garb for the world to see.

“Your god cannot protect you now!” one shouted in a perfect Saian accent.

The crowd began to panic.

“We will hunt every demon in this realm! For Sai! For humanity!”

By then, the guards had regained their wits, finally reaching the operatives.

His forces did not resist. Did not try to escape.

Karav had ordered them not to. Their job was to deliver the kill and the line. They had accepted that they would not leave the square alive.

Each of them activated precharged orbs. Orbs created specifically by Pagan Order Thaumaturges to overload.

The ensuing explosion took them in seconds, along with every nearby guard, leaving no evidence of the perpetrators’ bodies to study.

As for Matiman, not even the Panav’s Yuma’s Embrace could help the mangled Raja now.

Karav idly wondered if the Akh Nara’s resurrection magic might, but it was moot. He’d abandoned the realm to their own devices, and that suited Karav just fine. This was their singular moment of opportunity, and they’d exploited it beautifully.

The square was the very definition of chaos as civilians screamed and ran for their lives. What guards remained had their hands full trying and failing to contain it.

Karav turned his back on the scene. He’d accomplished what he had come for. The operatives’ bodies had been disposed of, and not a single soul would pin this on the Order.

No, it was quite clear to him and everyone present that Saians had slaughtered a demonic Raja today in a declaration of war.

Karav crossed the rooftop and descended the staircase to the street, ensuring he put on a suitably panicked expression.

There was much work left to be done. For while today was a good day… it was nothing but the beginning.

Devar removed his helmet and set his spear next to it, taking a seat on a low wall at the edge of the central square. He watched the crowd gather for the announcement, wondering when he’d get some sleep.

Probably not for a while, but at least he was not alone in that. Many of the faces he saw looked quite haggard, and it was little wonder why. The day and a half since Raja Matiman had died in this very square had passed in a waking blur.

The riots had been nonstop ever since the assassination. His squad had spent the night stretched thin, running continuously around the city without rest.

It didn’t help that, after several death threats and having their skyship burned, the Saian trade delegation had barricaded themselves in a warehouse against the mobs outside.

Luckily, his squad had just barely reached them before they burned alive. The terrified traders clearly had nothing to do with the assassination, but the rioters were out for blood. They had set fire to the whole building. A fire that had nearly spread to nearby buildings. Only the Asura’s timely arrival had stopped that.

Yet they were so few in number. Despite their vast strength, they could not hope to quell an enraged city on their own.

By the time Devar had escorted the Saians to the safety of the castle, two other parts of the city had erupted in conflict, and what little sleep he managed had come in short naps, nodding off where he stood guard.

Devar had been a city guard for four years now, and between the reconstruction and the Ash Tears, he thought he had seen it all.

How wrong he’d been.

His thoughts drifted back to his sister but quickly he shook it off. Thinking about Asha while on duty was a good way to ensure he became completely useless.

She’d been on a tour of the human lands prior to Reunification—a tour she’d been dreaming of for years.

She’d nearly chewed his ear off talking about Sonam’s walls and the wonders of the Altani floating city.

He had told her she was a fool. That the human lands were no place for a young kothi woman to wander alone. That humans had spent five thousand years hating on demons. He had said she was naive.

She’d listened to his worries, but in the end, she’d gone where her heart called. At least she’d sent letters. Bright-eyed and full of detail and wonder. Devar had even come to admit he’d been wrong about the whole thing.

But the letters had stopped abruptly a week ago.

Devar had spent every spare moment since praying. To the Akh Nara, and to any god who would listen. He did this even as he told his mother he was certain Asha was alive. He’d even almost managed to convince himself, but deep down, he knew the truth. That he was unlikely to ever see his dear sister ever again.

The crowd around him began to quiet. The projection apparatus at the center of the square activated, and the air above materialized into an image.

A murmur passed through the crowd, and Devar soon saw why.

The figure that appeared was unmistakably Raja Jagath, clad in his Aindri riding armor.

Devar’s fatigue vanished as he realized the implication.

Clan Rajas rarely appeared in projections on other clans’ land, let alone someone as reclusive as the Aindri Raja.

The murmurs grew louder, and for a moment, Devar wondered if he might be called upon to quell the crowd.

When Jagath spoke, however, it was as if he’d cast a spell over the audience. His voice was loud and deep, and Devar found himself breathing a sigh of relief at the effect it had on the people.

“People of Chitrakuta. People of the Chitran! Of every clan, you must now have heard what transpired in your city yesterday.”

The crowd went stock-still.

“The humans have indeed slaughtered your Raja. They have declared war on demonkind, and through all of this, the Akh Nara remains silent. Yes, his consort consoled you. Offered kind words and swore that the perpetrators will be caught. I tell you here today… this is not enough! The humans have done this because they believe demonkind is unprotected. They have done this because they believe we will not answer!”

The silent crowd immediately broke out into heated whispers, looking at one another.

“But answer we will,” Jagath continued. “My Aindri Warriors are mobilizing as I speak. To every Chitran city and village. Let it be known that the Aindri stand with the Chitran, as they have since time immemorial.”

Devar’s heart beat faster and his heart began beating faster without him realizing. By the looks of the crowd, the same was true for the others.

“The Akh Nara cannot protect us this time. He has done more than any demon for demonkind, but this fact remains. We cannot afford to wait for him to return!”

The whispers from before had now turned into full blown discussions, even as the Raja spoke.

“People of Chitra! The Aindri are mobilizing, as are our allies, the Iksana. The humans believe we are weak! They believe the Akh Nara’s absence is our undoing. Little do they know how wrong they are!”

He paused once more, and the crowd did not disappoint. Several raised their voices in assent, and several others clapped, and Devar found himself clapping with them.

Wasn’t this exactly what they needed? To take the fight to the enemy?

“Let us carve this lesson into their bones! Let us rip open their hearts and conquer their lands! For we have two gods on our side and they have none!”

This time, deafening cheers erupted through the crowd, but Devar hardly noticed.

His voice was among the loudest.

“Go, good people,” Jagath continued, nodding at the display. “Go and arm yourselves. Go with our blessings and prepare for war!”

The projection cut out.

The square held its breath for one long second, and just when Devar thought that would be all, they erupted into a deafening din.

This time, however, Devar did not join them. He sat on the low wall and thought of Asha. Of what could have happened to her.

It wasn’t just the Chitran. The Aindri were mobilizing, as were the Iksana.

As a guard, he had no hope of finding his sister.

But a Chitran soldier? A soldier on the front of a war in human territory, no less?

There, he could make a real difference. There, he could search for his dear little Asha.

He was standing before he knew it, the spear on his shoulder and helmet on his head. The crowd around him surged with the same energy as before, and Devar realized in that moment that he was not going to be a guard for much longer.

Tonight, he would rest.

And tomorrow? Tomorrow, he would enlist with the Chitran forces. He would march with them when they crossed into the human lands, and he would carry his spear into the very Hiranyan markets and Kin’jal cities Asha had dreamed of visiting.

If she were alive, he would find her. And if she was not, well, then he would find the humans who had killed her. He would kill them, and then he would go after their sisters and their brothers. Their fathers and their mothers.

No one would be safe from his spear.

Devar raised his weapon to the sky and joined in his brethren’s roar.
