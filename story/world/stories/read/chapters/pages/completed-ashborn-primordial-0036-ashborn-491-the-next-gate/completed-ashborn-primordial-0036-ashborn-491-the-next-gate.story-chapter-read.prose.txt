It was with much fanfare that, after familiarizing themselves with their new weapons and receiving their pranite injections and Ashani her upgrades, the group was ushered onto a magic carpet and taken to the Vimana closest to Mahādi. Except, unlike in their universe, this Imperium had expanded past the perimeter of the original city, enveloping its surroundings to become a sprawling metropolis the likes of which Vir, Maiya, and Ashani had never seen.

Furthermore, the Vimana they arrived at was not the lifeless corpse they had entered before but a bustling hub chock-full with thousands of Imperium researchers, families, and staff, all rushing through its vast halls in the course of daily life.

It felt as though Vir had stepped back thousands of years. The sight before him was beyond precious, and even if he hadn’t known it himself, a single look at Ashani, who wept silently as they walked the halls, told him all he needed to know.

“I’m sorry if this has been difficult for you,” he muttered to Ashani.

She wiped away her tears and shook her head. “No. It is I who owes you an apology. I apologize for being so remiss during our time here. It’s just, I never expected…”

“I know,” Vir said, nodding. “Even I’m overwhelmed. I can’t imagine how this must feel for you. Is there anything you wish to do here? Anyone you want to speak to before we leave?”

Ashani shook her head again. “No. As similar as these people are to my own, they are not the same. I know none of them. Janak is not here. They might as well be an entirely different civilization.”

Vir understood. As amazed as he was by this Imperium, who’d vastly outstripped the Imperium of their universe, it was not the same. This was not a civilization who’d unwittingly trodden on Fateweaver territory.

This was not the Imperium who’d paid the ultimate price.

Guided by the goddess Yuma, they took the lift to the top, just like last time, and after saying their farewells, the three stepped into the chamber with the wolves. Once again, a projection of Janak’s avatar appeared before them once the door behind had sealed shut.

“This is a pre-recorded message,” the grizzled, long-bearded avatar of blue and white said. “If you have come here, then all proceeds according to my design. You have succeeded upon the first step of your journey, but that which awaits you will test your mettle in more ways than one.

“It is my hope that, with the boons bestowed upon you by this civilization—this Imperium that was—you will be prepared to combat the challenges ahead. For there will be many. The Gate to this universe was one I was able to influence. The next, however, are beyond my reach.

“You may encounter any number of perilous situations, but your goal remains unchanged. Reach the Vimana in the next world. Enter its chamber. And proceed to the next universe.

“If you follow this breadcrumb trail, you will find your way to me. I must apologize for this difficulty, but it is the only way I can ensure our safety. We must meet, desperately, for time runs short.”

The recording cut out. The projection vanished. The group stood in silence.

“Do you know anything about what he’s talking about?” Vir asked Ashani.

The more Vir heard from Janak, the more his suspicions grew that they seemed fixated on different goals entirely.

The goddess shook her head. “I’m afraid not. There is little Janak can reveal, and given how he hasn’t contacted me for millennia… I once assumed he couldn’t. Now, I suspect there is a greater reason.”

She fell silent. Vir thought she was holding something back but decided not to press her. She would share what she wished in her own time.

“Well then,” Vir said as another Gate between universes opened before them. “Let’s see what this next world has in store.”

He stepped through. Maiya, Ashani, and the wolves followed close behind.

On the other side, Vir found not another Imperium world, but a landscape that looked familiar. Too familiar.

“We’re in the demon realm?” Maiya asked in confusion, looking up at the blood-red sky, the reddish soil confirming it as the only possibility.

“It seems that way,” Vir said, scanning the surroundings. “Regardless, our first task should be to descend from here.”

They appeared to be atop the summit of a jagged peak, with no clear path down. A treacherous descent for most mortals.

Vir, however, was anything but. Holding Maiya in his arms, he floated downward with the power of Balancer of Scales, which he’d spent the last years honing and perfecting. His control, while not quite rivaling Cirayus’, had grown considerably.

Ashani used her own powers to descend, while the wolves bounded down the jagged slopes to meet them at its rocky base.

“But why here?” Maiya asked as she stared up at the peak.

“I’m not sure,” Vir said. “Janak said he has less control over where we end up. I’m not convinced this location was intentional.”

“I would doubt Janak placed us here deliberately,” Ashani replied, seemingly recovering now that they were no longer in the alternate Imperium. “Most likely we were dropped here by random chance, much like an Ash Tear that opens to unpredictable destinations.”

The explanation made sense, and the Gate between universes felt very much like a Tear… just more profound.

“What needs to be done, then?” Maiya asked. “We need to find a Vimana, but if that means what I think it means…”

Ashani nodded. “This universe doesn’t have our Ash Gate network linking the two Realms together, and I am unable to form a link to the Gates I’d set down in our universe. It seems we will have to rely on more conventional means of travel.”

“I’d grown so used to our Gates that I’d almost forgotten how difficult travel between the Realms truly was,” Maiya muttered. “Amazing how fast you can get used to something, huh? We’ve only had them for a few years, and we already take them for granted.”

“True,” Vir said. “Crossing into the human realm will be quite the challenge, though Ashani and I have already charted a path through the Ash once. It won’t be impossible to do it again. It will, however, take time. Weeks, at the very least. Weeks we do not have.”

Even now, Vir had to block out thoughts of the Ash Tears opening up in their universe. Without Vir and Ashani, who knew what chaos it was causing?

“Say,” Maiya asked, “do you think time flows the same here as in our world? Or do you think it’s like the Ashen Realm?”

“I’m afraid it is impossible to say,” Ashani replied. “Even my internal clocks are skewed when in a region of dilated time. Without a reference back to our universe, I cannot know for certain.”

“Whatever the case, we must make haste,” Vir said. “For now, let’s journey toward the Ash Boundary.”

They set out, with Vir bounding across the realm with Maiya once again in his arms, Ashani using her own powers to descend slopes and cliffs, and the wolves sprinting to keep pace.

Traveling over the desolate landscape reminded Vir of just how impoverished the demon realm had been only a few years ago. Judging from the terrain, they were somewhere in the far east of the realm, well away from the boundary. This demon realm had seen none of the revolution theirs had, continuing to waste away with barely any prana to be seen.

“You know,” Maiya said as the landscape blurred beneath them, “this isn’t that bad. We may be universes away from home, and I know we’re on a mission, but… how long has it been since we’ve spent this much time together?”

Vir glanced down at her, meeting her hazel eyes that entranced him even to this day. “I can’t remember. Years, possibly.”

“Yeah,” Maiya murmured. “Is it bad that I’m hoping this lasts a little longer? I know the Realms are in dire need, but still…”

Vir chuckled as he leapt across another ridge. “Honestly? I feel the same. Between meeting a living, breathing Prime Imperium and having time alone with you… this has been a surprisingly refreshing journey.”

“Sure beats closing Ash Tears every waking hour of your life, doesn’t it?”

Vir could only laugh, until his expression froze.

In the distance, nestled among jagged peaks, crisscrossing its way up the mountain, was a city he recognized instantly.

Vraj Parah. The Panav capital.

But it wasn’t the city that held his attention.

For floating above it was a massive structure. An unmistakable citadel in the sky. Except in their universe, there were no Vimana in the demon realm, and Vir had been unable to find an answer as to why.

But there was something else that surprised him more.

“That’s…” Maiya trailed off.

“Fully operational, yes,” Ashani completed.

Unlike all the Vimana of their universe, this one wasn’t dormant at all. It was powered, blazed with lights, and to Vir’s horror, firing continuous beams down upon the city.

“I’m going to go faster,” Vir said, tightening his grip on Maiya, though even as he accelerated, he wondered if there was anything he could do against the destructive might of such a terrifying machine.

“One moment!” Ashani called suddenly. “We must heed Janak’s warning. We cannot interfere with the affairs of this universe. Doing so could draw the wrath of the Fateweavers.”

Vir’s jaw clenched. “I understand. But I cannot stand by while people die from those blasts. We must do something. And besides… our journey requires us to reach the Vimana anyway. I’d say this is a necessary part of our journey. Wouldn’t you?”

Ashani bit her lip. “I understand. Then let us go, but we should attempt to conceal ourselves wherever possible.”

In agreement, they shot forward together.

Vir could only pray they weren’t too late.
