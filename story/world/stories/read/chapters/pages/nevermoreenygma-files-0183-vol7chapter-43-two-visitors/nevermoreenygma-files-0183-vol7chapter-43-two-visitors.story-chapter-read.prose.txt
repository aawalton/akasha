Chapter 43

Two Visitors

Weeks passed, and Mai tried to find her rhythm again, but each day felt like a new battle.

The house—now crowded with Miho’s traces—seemed a bit strange to her, even though every corner was filled with memories of the time both spent together.

Her friend lingered everywhere: the messy stacks of books, scattered work papers, her favorite tea cups. The feng-shui charms. Her personal training equipment. Miho's white cane and glasses, which Mai put away safely.

Everything spoke of Miho, and every time someone arrived looking for the detective or medium—unaware that she had been involved in the disaster—pain swelled in Mai’s chest. Clients grew saddened when they heard the news, some even apologized, as if fate were something they could have prevented. But Mai knew there was nothing anyone could do to change it.

Miho was gone, and life moved on. Exactly as she had said it would.

With time, the pressure to keep going began to suffocate her.

Miho had left her so much. Not only the house, the office, the other property—but also the responsibility to continue the work she had begun. Mai didn’t have the luxury of letting everything crumble, so she made a painful but firm decision: she would not let her friend and boss’s legacy disappear.

With new resolve, she began delegating the more complex cases to other Kuchiyose priestesses and to monks who specialized in divination and tracking. They took on investigations that required more advanced skills, and although Miho’s friends were grieving, they had no objection to continuing the work she’d left behind. It was their way of honoring her—and the work was always welcome. Mai understood she had to let them carry on what Miho had started.

As for herself, Mai threw herself into the daily routine with the police and the Inoue Section, looking for cases where an extra pair of eyes might be useful—something Miho had done countless times, and had taught her to do as well. Even without spiritual abilities, cases could be solved, she had learned that from Miho too.

Theft in temples, magical creatures escaping confinement, or helping the Inoue Section catching criminals trafficking captured feys or person with special skills… the cases were as varied as the dangers they faced.

But she no longer had the one who had guided her through the off-the-books craft of dealing with the unseen world.

At first, focusing was difficult.

But as the months passed, she began to find some comfort in the work. The constant pressure, the need for quick decisions, the adrenaline of solving what others couldn’t… it all helped smother the grief that clung to her. Little by little, the chaos of daily life helped her adjust to her new reality—though it never erased what she had lost.

In time, Mai’s life settled into something resembling stability, but nothing was the same.

Miho’s memory hovered in every case she took, in every decision she made. And although her presence was gone, the legacy she left behind lived on in small gestures, in the jobs Mai completed, in the way she confronted each new day. Despite the ache in her chest, Mai kept moving forward, hoping that one day she might understand the purpose behind everything that had happened.But for now, she was content to continue—to keep Miho’s memory alive while building her own path.

During the months following the tragedy, Mai started forging a new life for herself—one that couldn’t be compared to the one she shared with her friend, yet full of its own challenges. The trafficking cases at that time and the investigations had necessitated a secret collaboration with the special forces. The horrific nature of these cases meant that she and everyone involved in capturing or dismantling the networks were forced to make quick decisions, focusing on the victims and pull the trigger when it was necessary.

Though the pain still lived inside her, the new cases she faced helped keep her mind busy, and in each one—no matter how small or insignificant, or how overwhelming—she found something that reconnected her to her purpose.

Facing loss, embracing memory, refusing to let fear consume her. Looking forward.

Keep flowing through life.

***

One gray autumn afternoon, as the city slid beneath a thick layer of fog, Mai was called by the police to help investigate a strange theft at an old temple on the outskirts of town—an abandoned place long out of religious service.

A set of valuable scrolls containing arcane rituals had vanished without a trace. The temple, seemingly untouched, showed no signs of forced entry. Despite the calm exterior, Mai felt a wrongness beneath the surface—and her intuition did not fail her. While examining the grounds, she found strange tracks, signs that the thieves were no mere humans, but likely fey raiders who had discovered a new source of power in the stolen scrolls.

During her investigation, Mai discovered that the fey had managed to bypass the temple’s protective barriers using a long-forgotten ritual, and their plan was to sell the scrolls on the black market because of their magical power. The abandoned temple had once been dedicated to a deity enshrined in a rock within the temple, which had disappeared during World War II.

Although she was not an expert in arcane rituals and could not wield magic herself, she used her knowledge of spiritual energy and her tracking abilities to follow the magical currents of the scrolls through artifacts like Miho taught her. With the help of a Kuchiyose priestess friend, she managed to undo the enchantment on the scrolls and capture the thieves.

Despite the tension, Mai returned the stolen scrolls and restored peace, though a lingering sense of unease persisted, as if something remained unresolved in the shadows. When questioned, the thieves admitted that although they had found the place almost as if they had been drawn there, something had guided them to the book that helped break the sealing barrier. Why had the old sanctuary lost its power now after being abandoned for so many decades?

It was during this case that she came face to face with something the Inoue Section had been investigating for some time: what they called Dark Events. Apparently, in various parts of Japan, many shrines and locations protected by old magical barriers had begun losing their power, resulting in spiritual energy leaking into the outside world. This could be related to the recent rise in supernatural incidents across Japan and the rest of the world.

According to experts, many Dark Events could be classified as either strange incidents breaking into peaceful environments or unusual occurrences in places with an existing supernatural tradition. In a way, they represented a disruption of order.

For example, a supernatural entity such as a ghost might appear, or there could be sudden alterations of time or space in a place that had never experienced anything like it. On the other hand, if the event occurred in an environment with established magical traditions, it could lead to the disappearance of entities, people, or the fading of barriers or spells.

Sudden climate or geomagnetic changes, tremors, chemical alterations in the earth or atmosphere, unusual movements of animals or insects, or changes in human behavior could all signal that something strange was occurring, once conventional explanations were ruled out.

Some locations with a strong magical tradition in Japan had disappeared or shifted entirely. Some linked this to the Dragon Lines, which govern many of the earth’s magical and spiritual currents, but the cause remained unknown. Certain magical creatures in the forests, classified as fey by the Inoue Section, had vanished from their usual habitats to appear hundreds of kilometers away, even on islands, requiring new observation and research to ensure these habitat shifts did not trigger public alarm.

A few months later, Mai was called to investigate a series of mysterious disappearances in a residential area—people vanishing without a trace, no witnesses, no evidence.

As she delved deeper, Mai began noticing strange patterns: all the disappearances occurred on rainy nights, and all the victims had something in common. At first, she thought it was coincidence, but she soon discovered that a group of mystical creatures, known as the “Dark Kitsune,” was involved. These beings, mischievous yet dangerous, fed on human despair and used their powers to pull people into a parallel space, where they consumed their deepest fears.

With the help of an old monk, an expert in mystical creatures, and a few members of the Anomaly Prevention Office, Mai devised a plan to rescue the trapped victims. They entered the parallel space, confronting the Dark Kitsune, who, like their less dangerous kin, sometimes toyed with their victims’ minds—but this type actually devoured them.

Mai, managed to save the captured people, using amulets to see through illusions and restore clarity to the prisoners’ confused minds.

Although she had defeated the Kitsune, the experience left its mark. The creatures had toyed with Mai’s mind in ways she had never experienced before, forcing her to relive Miho’s death repeatedly—until the amulet worked and she was able to defeat the deceptive fey. It was a painful lesson on how even the most mischievous beings can lurk in the shadows, waiting to trap the unwary.

But the real enigma came from the legends. According to tradition, Dark Kitsune never devoured their victims, limiting themselves to feeding on fear. The legends said they liked to hunt lost travelers at night but left them in a state of deep depression for weeks afterward. That Dark Kitsune were now feeding on the flesh of their victims was something new—something unheard of, or at least never officially recorded.

A deeper investigation revealed that, indeed, in the place where the first victims had been found, there had been legends of foxes deceiving humans centuries ago, but there was no reference to feeding on the victims. The ley lines running beneath the area were stable, but the guardian deities were gone, and it was unknown when they might have disappeared. For that reason, the case was classified as a Dark Event.

At another time, Mai became involved in the capture of a criminal who had specialized in kidnapping newcomers feys to sell them on the black market. The rescue operation was tense, and Mai had to contend with an internal betrayal from an informant within the police.

The criminal, a man known as “The Merchant,” had managed to evade capture for years, but finally, thanks to a coordinated effort by multiple agencies and the police, he was apprehended in a raid organized by the police and members of the Anomaly Prevention Office.

During that time, Mai was tested both mentally and physically, having to face ethical dilemmas regarding investigation methods and laws concerning the treatment of fey and people with special abilities. It was a gut-wrenching case, and no one complained when she and the fey of the Anomaly Prevention Office eliminated the criminal and the traitor. The circle of over eighty degenerates who controlled the group began to fall in the following days, including the mysterious deaths of two government deputies. One was run over in a parking lot, and another had his head split open as if struck by a projectile—though no projectile was ever found, only an ancient stone arrowhead nearby.

The string of deaths continued in the following days, spreading to other prefectures. There was no proof for the government, but it was obvious that the Inoue Section and other agencies had acted independently, taking justice into their own hands when they saw how far some of the tentacles reached.

Amid all this, Mai later changed her car and spent every afternoon driving just for the pleasure of roaming Tokyo. She began to feel a strange connection with the city. With each case she solved, with each life she touched, she felt more rooted in her new life, even though the pain still throbbed deep in her soul.

The adventures and challenges kept coming, but Mai was no longer the same woman she had been before the attack. She had changed. And although Miho was no longer there to accompany her, she had found her own path, and with every step she took, she moved forward with the strength that only those who have suffered a great loss can possess.

Thus arrived the winter of 2008.

***

The wind brought a chill that ran down Mai’s spine when she heard a knock at the door. She had been so focused on reading the reports that the section had sent her for consultation that the sudden noise startled her. At first, she thought it was a mistake—perhaps the sound of a window closing improperly, or a branch tapping against the house. But then, a second knock snapped her out of her reverie.

She put out her antennae and scanned the area using echolocation, detecting only two people outside her door. There seemed to be nothing at the back, and the wolf barrier was on guard as usual. Something inside her, that sharp intuition that had accompanied her in recent times, made her approach the door cautiously.

She opened it slowly, the wood creaked as she slid it, and there they were: two women standing before her. One was almost her height, with short blonde hair streaked with green, as if the moonlight had fallen upon her head, and the other, with a cascade of dark purple hair down her back, looked at her with the intensity of birds of prey that would have made anyone else avert their gaze.

Almost involuntarily, Mai felt a shiver run down her spine. It wasn't fear. For some reason, they made her feel strange, and she felt a blush rising to her cheeks. She didn't know why, but she felt a strange familiarity with them. Did she know them from somewhere? She tried to remember, but they didn't appear in her memories. Even so, she was sure she had seen them before. Perhaps in some file in the Inoue section? For their part, the two women had opened their eyes a little when they saw her, almost as surprised as Mai. Didn't they know where they were?

Both seemed out of place in that quiet neighborhood, dressed entirely in black, and the sense of strangeness they radiated was not just physical. They were fey, and Mai knew it almost immediately without needing to see how they kept their pointed ears pressed to their skulls.

“H-Hello,” said the blonde woman with a slight nod. “I’m Sari. This is Milena.”

The purple-haired woman barely inclined her chin, not taking her eyes off Mai.

Mai did not open the door fully. “Hello. What do you need?”

“We came because we need help,” Sari replied, her tone neutral but revealing a certain nervousness. “We know what you do. That you handle cases others don’t touch.”

Mai crossed her arms. “And you—what kind of cases do you bring?”

Milena interjected. “We can’t tell you everything here. We don’t even know if we can trust you yet.”

She said it naturally, but her steady gaze made it clear this was not an empty comment.

Mai pressed her lips together, weighing the situation. She had learned to recognize when someone was asking for help and when someone was testing her. “So you came all the way to my door with nothing concrete. What do you expect?”

“Just listen to us,” Sari replied, clasping her hands as if pleading. “Something’s happening. Something we can’t handle alone. And we can’t go to just anyone.”

The silence stretched for a second. Outside, the wind rustled the branches of a tree against the house.

Mai tilted her head. “What kind of problem?”

Milena shook her head slowly. “If you decide to join us, you’ll find out. If not, it doesn’t matter.”

Mai snorted. She didn’t like being kept in the dark, but the tension in their voices was real, with no hint of exaggeration. And if there was one thing she had learned in recent years, it was that people who came knocking at her door at this hour rarely played games. From what she could tell, Milena was the one analyzing the situation, and Sari was the more talkative and expressive one.

Sari held her gaze. “We’re not asking for an answer right now. But if you say yes, you have to be prepared. This won’t be just any job.”

“Can we come in?” Sari spoke again, now clearly quieter, weighing each word. “We need to talk in private.”

Mai studied her for a second. She didn’t like the idea of letting strangers into the house, not after the a certain incident months ago. It wasn’t the first time someone had come seeking revenge for a case, nor the first time she had to fight inside her own living room. That had happened a few months earlier. She remembered the sword marks on the wall she had to repair, the shattered window panes she had to replace, the police who had interrogated her afterward, and the curious neighbors. It wasn’t wise to let someone in so casually.

But at the same time, she looked out toward the street. A couple of neighbors were walking by, someone peeked out from the window across the street and then disappeared. She didn’t need rumors, or prying eyes, or curious neighbors asking why two fey were at her door at this hour.

“I don’t like surprises,” Mai said. “What guarantees me this isn’t a trap?”

Milena remained unflinching. “We didn’t come to you by chance. We couldn’t risk speaking anywhere else. If you agree, we’ll tell you something we can’t tell just anyone. If not, we leave.”

Mai hesitated. She had traps set around the house—small seals under the flowerpots, a couple of devices that vibrated if someone tried to force the locks—and inside the study, there were defenses Miho had left, like the mirror system and shikigami. If this went wrong, she wouldn’t be caught off guard. Still, she had no interest in turning the street into a spectacle.

Despite keeping her guard up, she couldn't explain it, but she had the unfounded feeling that they weren't a danger to her. Mai felt a little annoyed by that feeling. Where did she know them from? Maybe it was just a feeling.

She took a deep breath and stepped aside.

“Come in.” Her tone left no room for discussion.

“Sorry for the trouble.” Sari smiled faintly and stepped in first, Milena followed with a bow, and Mai led them to the living room. They didn’t look for comfort: they sat on the floor, straightforwardly, facing Mai as if the room were an interrogation chamber.

Mai closed the door carefully, checked a seal on the frame with the tip of her finger, and let the lock click into place. For a moment, as the noise from the street faded, she felt the familiar mix of tension and control. She knew this could end badly. She also knew that if something stirred her from within, it was curiosity.

She looked at them sitting on the floor. Why not the couches?

“Sit there, you’ll be more comfortable,” she said, gesturing toward the sofa.

They did, though they didn’t seem comfortable. Milena crossed her arms and settled at the far end, while Sari leaned forward, hands clasped. There was something odd about their posture—a tension that hadn’t shown at the door. Now, inside the house, it seemed both were on unstable ground.

Mai watched them for a moment and raised an eyebrow. “Don’t sit like that. I’m not going to bite,” she said with irony. Then added, “Tea?”

Their response was an exchange of glances. Milena opened her mouth to refuse, but Sari interrupted with a brief nod. “Yes, thank you.”

Mai nodded and headed to the kitchen. The routine of preparing tea gave her a few seconds to organize her thoughts. But she wasn’t distracted. From the kitchen, the magical mirrors installed in the corners—tricks Miho had left, which Mai had reinforced—showed the visitors clearly.

They didn’t stay still. Sari let her gaze roam over the living room objects: books piled on the table, boxes with amulets and talismans, the lamp engraved with symbols at its base. Milena seemed more restless, she leaned back, then forward, as if searching for the best way to sit without showing discomfort. Her eyes lingered on a photograph still hanging on the wall: an old image of Miho with a crooked smile, taken a couple years ago. Milena looked at it a second longer than necessary.

Damn it! Where I have seen them? Mai though.

Mai pressed her lips together and dropped the tea leaves into the teapot.

When she returned with the tray, the tea steaming in the cups, she found the two fey more serious than before. She set everything on the table and sat down across from them.

“All right. You’re inside now. You have tea. Let’s talk.”

“Thank you,” Sari said softly. “We didn’t expect you to agree so quickly.”

“I couldn’t leave you at the door either.”

“Mr. Rackhenhell sent us.”

“Mr. Rackhenhell? Franz Mr. Rackhenhell?”

Both nodded.

Mai sighed. They should have said that earlier. Two years had passed since that encounter with the vampire. He had sent her a few rumors but no certainty about who had perpetrated the attack.

A brief silence followed. Milena was the first to speak:

“We both lost our lovers in the hotel attack.”

Mai went blank for a few seconds. She lowered her gaze to the cup in front of her, untouched. The sentence had been stated without drama, almost like a report, yet the weight was there.

“I’m sorry to hear that.”

“We had a group relationship,” Sari continued, her voice sad, though a slight tremor escaped on the last word. “They were on the lower floors keeping watch. We weren’t part of Wishper back then.”

Mai swallowed hard. Her moth-like antennae twitched involuntarily. The memory of the collapse, the smoke and fire in the hotel, and Miho’s shattered body stabbed into her mind like a knife.

“Since then,” Milena went on, “we’ve been investigating. Not alone. With Wishper. Mr. Rackhenhell has been helping a little. But finally we have more hints about the responsible.”

“Who are they?” Mai asked dryly, though she already knew Wishper wasn’t just any group. She was merely testing the waters.

“A collective from the occult world,” Sari explained. “We aren’t many, but we operate as a network. We moved under the radar. We gather information, cross-reference data, prevent governments from using us as weapons or experiments. And when necessary, we hunt the bastards. Thanks to that we discovered more now.”

Mai sighed a little more relieved. It was quite possible that she had seen their faces in some file in the Inoue section after all. That explained her feeling, but for some reason they still made her feel a little strange. And that warmth in her cheeks hadn't completely gone away.For some reason, Sari also seemed to feel strange, and Milena could hardly take her eyes off her. Mai took a sip of her tea without breaking eye contact. “And what do you need from me?”

“At first, we thought you’d be seeking revenge,” Milena said. “That you’d move on your own to track down those responsible for what happened at the hotel.”

Mai gritted her teeth. Miho’s name wasn’t spoken, but it hovered in the air, inevitable.

“I’m alive,” she said finally, in a low, sharp tone. “That doesn’t mean I’m running around with a knife between my teeth or a gun hunting culprits. Moving without a clear idea of what you’re looking for could harm us all.”

Milena nodded. “We know. That’s why, after losing our partners, we joined Wishper.”

“For revenge?”

Sari leaned slightly forward. “We’re not seeking just revenge. We seek understanding. And what we’ve found doesn’t match the official version in the hidden world.”

“What exactly did you find?” Mai asked.

The two looked at each other again. It was clear that there were parts of the information they still weren’t ready to share.

“What do you know? The last time Franz Rackhenhell sent me any data, it was related to names erased from reports, financial movements that didn’t add up, check-in and check-out records from the hotel that had been tampered with. Someone planned that attack to make it look like an accident, or an inevitable consequence of the encounter.”

Milena leaned forward, her voice lower than before. “We’ve found a few more things. But we can’t do it alone. And we can’t trust just anyone. Mr. Rackhenhell said we should try to contact you because it might interest you. We’re very close now.”

Mai took a sip of her tea, which had cooled too quickly. The words felt like hooks embedding themselves in her.

“We hope you’ll want to hear what comes next,” Sari said calmly. “And if after that you decide not to, we’ll leave and won’t bother you.”

Mai set her cup on the table, sigh and speak calmly. For some reason, the two women seem a little tense. If they came to clarify things a little better, it would be best to listen to what they have to say. “Ok. But don’t give me speeches, please. Tell me what you know, what you suspect, and what you expect from me. Nothing more.”

Sari nodded. “That’s what we’ll do.”

The two women recounted their story and investigation, and Mai listened attentively.

She closed her eyes for a brief moment, feeling the weight of the decision about to crush her. When she opened them, she was no longer so certain of what she thought she knew.

The whole situation was far too complicated.
