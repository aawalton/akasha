“First, let me say thank you to everyone for your contributions in the battle. We couldn’t have won without everyone working together,” I say honestly to the mirrors that hold my allies’ images. They all look tired, but satisfied – as well they might. Taking down a Tier four is never easy, and when the match was as bad as this one – a Tier four Air-Roc at sea – getting through it mostly intact is certainly an achievement.

“We couldn’t have won without you driving the beast down to the ocean,” Layton praises, and the look in the eyes of everyone else – Elarion included – indicate that he’s not the only one thinking it.

“Perhaps, but without the Water Mages holding it there, and everyone’s fighters piling on the damage, I wouldn’t have been able to keep it there.”

Satisfaction and pride flicker in the eyes and expressions of those present. I didn’t just say it to stroke the egos of my allies – I said it because it’s the truth. And it’s probably more impactful for that fact.

“Thank you for saying so, Lord Markus,” Josiah replies, the flicker of satisfaction in his eyes quickly disappearing as his face once more takes on a grim cast. “On that note, I was wondering how the harvesting of the roc will be apportioned? According to losses suffered, I hope.”

Teams are currently riding the bobbing carcass of the roc, harvesting everything they can reach. After the last of its life escaped its grasp, the roc’s body became fully corporeal, the air-magic that had suffused it and surrounded it largely disappearing. But the materials from its body will still be incredibly useful to craftsmen capable of teasing out inherent aspects of its nature. Items that offer efficient flight, some control over air-currents, and maybe even the ability to go incorporeal for a short time should all be possible. There is a time limit, though – unless preserved with specialised methods, the majority of the materials’ potential will be lost within two days of its death.

“I think the feathers, meat, and bones should be split equally among all those who have crafters capable of using them – whether that be cooks, enchanters, leather-workers, armourers, bowyers, or the like. Perhaps bonuses can be paid to harvesters according to how much they harvest within the time frame – that might incentivise extended effort that will benefit everyone.”

Josiah’s expression darkens.

“Then there is no compensation to those who have lost so many of their crew and fighters?”

I gaze at him thoughtfully.

“I can understand why you are upset,” I start soothingly. “But will extra feathers or bones replace those you’ve lost?” I allow a hint of sadness to enter my voice. “I know they will not replace those from my ship lost in the fight too.”

He’s silent for a few moments before nodding slowly.

“I can certainly agree that no resources will replace my retainers lost in battle. However, materials to produce enchanted equipment would help to avoid future losses.”

I hesitate, then incline my head.

“That’s certainly true. Let us consider the beak and claws to start – does anyone have the means of transforming those into equipment that could help us?”

No one responds immediately.

“I…my lord, I have a weapons-crafter who works primarily with materials from beasts.” Valence turns out to be the one to break the silence. “He would very much appreciate such materials – and would likely be able to make some powerful weapons out of them.”

I nod at Valence in appreciation.

“Then, unless anyone else wishes to argue the point, I suggest we grant the beak and claws to Valence.”

“I do not wish to argue the point,” Elarion starts smoothly; I do my best not to sigh, “however the beak and claws are a significant part of the beast. As you said, we fought the Tier four together, yet this rather seems to advantage Heir Fell disproportionately.”

I gaze levelly at Elarion.

“Please recall how we distributed the earnings after our battle with Kyrian and his fleet: according to need first, and desire afterwards. If you take the beak and claws, what will you do with it? Anything? Or just put it in storage?”

Elarion holds my gaze, but, after a moment, twitches his chin down fractionally.

“Point taken,” he replies, the sheer lack of emotion showing how much that cost him to admit. I release him from my gaze, glancing back at Valence.

“That said, perhaps we can work out an arrangement whereby the other ships benefit from some of the weapons your weapons-crafter is able to make out of these materials. For example, if he’s able to make powerful arrowheads, perhaps some of these can be shared with other ships.” I glance at Josiah and give him a nod. “As Josiah mentioned earlier, preventing future losses is something we would all benefit from.

Valence bows his head briefly, not needing to struggle with his pride nearly as much as Elarion does.

“I will speak to my crafter about what he thinks he is able to make and then we can return to the topic, if you feel that works?” he suggests.

“Good idea,” I praise.

“There is still the question of the Core,” Josiah interjects bluntly, clearly not satisfied with our earlier exchange.

“That should surely go to Lord Markus,” Layton protests, sending his cousin a chiding look. “If he had not driven the beast down to the sea, we could not have defeated it and there would be no Core to debate about – or probably ships to hold it.”

Silence falls again, though this one holds the weight of unspoken thoughts. Based on the way Josiah’s jaw works silently and Elarion carefully doesn’t look at anyone directly I suspect that they don’t want to agree with Layton, but are finding it challenging to come up with a decent argument against it.

“It is a question,” I agree finally, “but we have more important ones right now. I suggest that when the Core is found, we put it to the side in Sarran’s Inventory – as Expedition Steward, he seems to me to be the best choice for caretaker – and work out what to do with it when we have more freedom to debate about it. For now, I know that my ship has taken heavy damage from the attack – what of yours?”

“I can see that your mainmast is down,” Valence agrees with concern in his eyes and tone, “and that your other two masts seem to be alarmingly angled. Is that the total?”

“I wish,” I sigh. “We have a hole in the hull below the waterline, a crushed wheel and control hub, and a dead captain,” I summarise succinctly. “Our shields were temporarily down, but I’ve managed to reactivate them.” Sure enough, there were secondary controls in my cabin, but they are basic in comparison to what has been lost. “So, in short, we currently have no steering, and no propulsion.”

I see grimaces on several of my allies’ faces at the summary.

“Ah’m sorry te hear ‘bout Captain Tolly,” So’raz interjects, his expression grave. “He was a good sort.”

I sigh heavily.

“He was,” I agree, sadness seeping through me again. “He died because he refused to leave his post while he was still needed there.”

“A brave man. He will be remembered,” Layton pronounces stoically.

“Along with many others,” Josiah adds, his face twisted in grief.

There’s a moment of silent reflection as we think of all those brave dead who were alive and cheerful this morning, not knowing what lay in store for them. Can any of us say anything different? I wonder dismally to myself. Maybe we’ll all be dead by tomorrow evening.

“My ship’s steering has been compromised too,” Valence admits a little awkwardly, breaking the moment. He flicks a brief look at Elarion. “The collision of our ships caused significant damage. I have my carpenters working on it already, but several parts will need to be replaced and that takes time. At least our masts are working, but we have lost several key members of our crew to the roc’s predations.”

My mouth presses itself into a thin line – it sounds like his command structure has been disrupted too, then.

We continue the discussion, each of the captains chiming in with their own damage reports. Even Captain Garous, the slave captain of the ship I seized from Kyrian, nervously reports his own damage.

As it turns out, the damage is both worse than I’d hoped and better than I’d feared. We’ve certainly lost a lot in this battle, probably more than we’ve gained even with the Tier four crafting materials taken into account, but with a bit of time and some urgent repairs, our fleet will survive this.

My ship has definitely come off worst overall in terms of physical damage – I think that my attempt to protect the masts must have drawn the roc’s particular ire as mine is the only ship without any sail capacity at all. However, Layton’s, Elarion’s, and Captain Sor’az’s ships are also at reduced sail capacity for various reasons.

No other ship lost its captain, but it seems that almost all the ships are down on crew members, many missing key officers. And we’ve all suffered the loss of fighters, up to almost twenty percent in Josiah’s case. Interestingly, Captain Garous’ ship came off best in that regard – Kyrian’s former ship has excellent shields and the final mana shield only broke briefly before I attacked the roc and drove it to the sea. They only lost a few fighters or crew members to wind that drove them overboard.

It’s disheartening. In the battle with Kyrian, we barely lost anyone – though some people were wounded, magic was able to bring them back from the brink. Here, however, people were whipped off decks with wind and then sucked up by tornados and devoured by the Tier four. There’s always the possibility that we’ll find some alive inside the roc, but it seems highly unlikely.

Elarion’s ship also has a hole in its side, caused by the collision with Valence’s vessel. It’s bigger than the one in mine, but he’s managed to block it with unmelting ice for now. He’s drawn his magic from the ice floes he formed in the sea, so those have practically all melted away now, removing at least one threat from the environment. His steering capacity remains undamaged.

Josiah’s has, ironically, come off the best when it comes to physical damage, though he’s lost the greatest number of people due to his mana shield breaking early on in the fight. Perhaps the roc was pleased enough with the smorgasbord that it didn’t focus any of its efforts on damaging the ship. He has neither hole, nor damage to his sails or steering.

“We need to make the repairs we can while the harvesting crews do their work,” I summarise grimly. “And even that probably won’t be enough time. We’ll just have to hope that we don’t run into anything until we’ve at least all got our sails and steering back online.”

Captain Sor’az clears his throat and my heart sinks as I turn to see his rueful expression. He’s been somewhat distracted during this discussion, carrying out multiple inaudible discussions with people on his ship.

“Captain,” I warn him, though without much hope in my voice. “Please don’t give me bad news.”

The apology in his eyes grows.

“Sorry me lord. Can’t promise tha’.”

I close my eyes and take a deep breath. Whatever it is, we can face it, I tell myself. Then, pep talk over, I open my eyes once more.

“Go ahead Captain. What is it?”

“Well, some o’ me crew have noticed tha’ we’re bein’ pulled by a current.”

“There are plenty of ocean currents,” Elarion interrupts irritably. “What of it?”

I send him a quelling glance, then look back at Sor’az.

“Go on. What is concerning about this particular current?”

“Well, ah don’t rightly know,” he admits. “But me soothsayer be worried. Says t’ omens are bad. That the current’s out o’ position. Wrong. An’ ah trust me soothsayer – she’s never led us wrong afore.” He looks grim for a moment. “An’ the sea’s too silent. A Tier four goes down an’ no scavengers’ve appeared yet? Concernin’.”

Wonderful, I think grimly.
