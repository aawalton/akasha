I followed Lord Ceonwin into the hall, keeping my hood drawn and my gaze low until we reached the back. We disappeared through a door beside the throne, then stepped into the back hallway where the thegn’s rooms lay.

They used to be my rooms, but I hadn’t actually stayed here long enough to get used to them.

“Sir Kenelm isn’t home, huh?” I whispered.

“He rarely is,” Lord Ceonwin replied. “He prefers his estate in the adjoining countryside. Barely within ‘his’ lands.”

After I had supposedly died, these lands were given to Sir Kenelm, a thegn with relatively small holdings, and lands adjacent to mine. He was loyal to the Warlord, and we’d done our best to keep him in the dark.

“And the portreeve?”

“Usually bumbling around the town center, looking for people to nag about something he can’t change, trying to make them follow a rule that no longer exists.”

I chuckled. “You’d think Kenelm could undo some of the laws I’d gotten Cuthward to strike down. I mean, he could at least reinstate them.”

“You’re lucky he’s terrified of your legacy,” Lord Ceonwin replied. “Without the cultists to keep control, the rural councils you gave power aren’t likely to give it up without a fight. He can’t assert absolute control again without a fight. And I think he’s beginning to understand that he can’t control the army. He thinks they’re loyal to me.”

“The Warlord could reinstate those laws if he wanted to.”

“He thinks you’re dead, and he only cares that we fall. The local laws are of no concern.” He kept walking. “Which brings me to my next point...”

Lord Ceonwin brought me to a meeting room at the end of the row of rooms. Inside, there was a small table laden with maps. The windows were closed, and a pair of rushlights cast a flickering glow through the room.

He wasn’t alone, though. The Drillmaster stood at the end of the table, and Saeglaf, one of the Greencloaks who’d come to help us, hunched in the corner, his arms crossed.

I leaned over the map, staring at the tiny wooden chits that represented armies. Orange for ours, black for the orcs.

“Much to the Warlord’s chagrin, the orc push has halted three miles east of Hyrdford,” Lord Ceonwin said. “They are too spread out, and we put up enough of a fight to stall them.” He drew his sword and pointed to the map, tapping a line that had formed east of Hyrdford. “But it won’t stay that way for long. Now that they have us on the ropes, they’ll keep pushing.”

It was still chillingly close to the city I was supposed to be the thegn of. I was still its protector, and orcs were just as dangerous as cultists.

“The orcs won’t just give up,” the Drillmaster said. “Worse, we expect them to be using suppression collars. The Warlord will surely try to push them to attack sooner than later.”

“He has that much control?” I whispered.

“He has, it seems, a temporary alliance with the Dark Lord. But the Dark Lord will not share power for long.” Lord Ceonwin shook his head. “Kharzon has stabbed greater kings in the back before, and he will do it again.”

“Rumour is, the Warlord is already disappointed with how long this has been taking,” Saeglaf said. “He was expecting us to crumble by now, but Hyrdford has been a strong point.”

“Not to mention Slowbend,” I said, pointing at the one spar of the front line, still near the Thelea River, that hadn’t fallen yet.

“We haven’t received word from them in weeks,” Lord Ceonwin replied. “We should not get our hopes up.”

I grimaced. There was no way the 294th had just fallen. They’d held for this long, and they were better armed than most. Sure, they were probably under siege, and taking heavy casualties, but I refused to believe that they’d been wiped out.

“How’s our army doing here?” I asked.

“We’ve gathered about a thousand regular Dupes, who are currently scattered around the nearby countryside,” the Drillmaster replied. “Along with a thousand mortal militiamen and five-hundred mercenaries.”

I glanced at Lord Ceonwin. “That’s just in the local area?”

He nodded. “Only in the local area. There are more spread north and south along the line, helping to hold it.”

“And everyone has equipment?” I asked.

“One helmet and at least a chainmail hauberk for everyone,” the Drillmaster said. “As well as a weapon and a shield. We’ve been rearranging them into units with their preferred weapon. Spears, swords, axes, bows. The squad model won’t help us fight large pitched battles.”

“And we have been running training exercises to prepare them,” Lord Ceonwin added.

I nodded. “What about the other thegns of the Domain?”

“Three quarters of them have pledged their support to us.” Lord Ceonwin marched around the side of the table. “We’ve sworn them into our little shadow council, with soul oaths and all to pledge their secrecy, but they are busy dealing with incursions of their own.”

I inhaled sharply.

“When you reveal yourself as Ealdorman, and when we’ve pushed the orcs out, they will support you,” Saeglaf said casually. He held up the emerald-green edge of his cloak, which was tattered and shredded from months of assisting with combat.

“Alright, now, what’s the status of the Greencloaks in southern Gate?” I asked.

“I haven’t had prolonged contact with any of them,” Saeglaf replied. “The invasion scattered us, and I haven’t seen many signs of the rest of my Order. However, Lord Ceonwin’s scouts report that most of the Hangmen have been slain. Therefore, I suspect there are many Greencloaks, including Askan, who are still active. We believe some of them are assisting Slowbend, but I don’t have contact with that city, either.”

“Most of the Hangmen?” I tilted my head.

“During the night of the invasion, we counted ten sightings,” Lord Ceonwin replied. “We have only seen reports of nine slain Hangmen, and no new reports on their status for weeks, either.”

“It’s possible that we miscounted, right?” I replied.

“We should plan for the worst,” Lord Ceonwin said. “And for what happens when we wipe out an entire squad of Hangmen.”

“I’m afraid I don’t know what you mean,” I said. I leaned on the edge of the table, pressing my hands into the wood. “Killing all of them would be a good thing.”

“We expect that, if the Dark Lord actually registers a threat capable of slaying his Hangmen, he will deploy the Fallen Queens. He would prefer to keep them as his personal guards, but he will use them if necessary.”

I gulped. That’s not good. But…I had no idea what those were.

“I take it we should be even more worried about those.”

“Correct,” Saeglaf replied. “Between them and Askan, I would not place my money confidently on either. They are fallen noble-ladies from other conquered lands, corrupted, and turned to his will. Consider them to be an especially powerful and well-formed wraith”

I exhaled. “So it would be best to leave the last Hangman alive.”

“They aren’t likely to let us,” said the Drillmaster, pointing to the map just east of Hyrdford. “There’s a large buildup of orc forces here. Our scouts believe they’ll attack by the end of the week. We also believe they’ll deploy their last Hangman.”

I stepped over to the room’s closed window and peered out a crack, looking out across the city. “How long ‘til we finish the wall?”

“We can have it ready,” Lord Ceonwin promised. “So long as everyone chips in and lends a hand, that is.”

“I’m always willing to help,” I said.

“If you revealed your identity, it would boost morale,” the Drillmaster replied. “For the average person to know their true thegn is still alive?”

“There’s a rumour that your soul has not yet made the journey to Welkinmere,” said Saeglaf. “That you are still watching over the town you swore to protect.”

“Well, they’re not entirely wrong.” I crossed my arms, then turned away from the window and leaned on the wall. “But I can’t reveal myself until we’re ready to march on the Warlord. I’m not strong enough to face him yet, and I won’t survive another duel with him in this state. I’m certain that he would find me again and try to kill me.”

“Do you figure we can defeat the orcs here?” the Drillmaster asked Lord Ceonwin.

Ceonwin kept his chin high. “I make no promises. We estimate their force to be in the five-thousands.” Lord Ceonwin flicked his sword to the side, then sheathed it. “But that gives us good odds for surviving a siege, so long as our combined strength can overwhelm a Hangman. We are expecting it to be high-up in the Gold tier.”

I leaned over the map. “And once we do defeat them, we need to capitalize on it. Keep pushing. They don’t know how many of us there are. If we lure them into attacking our walls, we can hold them off, then break the offensive and liberate the rest of the southern domains.” I looked at them with a hopeful grin. “We will need to make a push and rescue the 294th.”

“What then?” Saeglaf asked. “You think that, after a battle like that, you would be ready to face the Warlord?”

“It would reward us a lot of Presence…”

“You are not in the Labyrinth anymore, my lord,” the Drillmaster said. “They won’t guarantee a Presence drop, and Skills will be much more difficult to gain. You’re still a boy, like it or not, and a Gold against the Warlord is still a losing battle.”

“I’ll…figure something out,” I said.

“What if you don’t? Not everything has an easy solution. You will hit a wall. Not everyone can advance as quickly as you did.”

Saeglaf cleared his throat. “Forgive my intrusion. It may not be easy, but there may be a way.”

My eyes lit up, and I turned to him, head cocked expectantly.

“It can wait,” Saeglaf said. “There’s nothing we can do about it now.”

“I hate to be pushy,” I replied, “but no good can come of waiting. What if you die in the coming days? If there’s information, I’d appreciate it if you shared.”

Saeglaf sighed. “Don’t complain to me if the burden is too much.” He stepped over to the map. Although the main map only depicted southern Gate, we had a few smaller maps in the corner of the table, which depicted the rest of the nation, and the lands to the west of Gate. He pointed at one of the smaller maps. “The Great Pillar. Where Welkinmere’s eye points, there is an ancient tower. We believe its doors are opening soon. Warriors from far and wide will be arriving, attempting to test themselves against it. It rewards all who challenge it. And…should a warrior manage to reach the top, the Pillar would empower him.”

“Empower me?”

“We do not know all the details. We do know that it was there where Aelfwine reached Emerald.”

“I’ll…keep it in mind,” I said. “But for now, we need to get to work. We have a fortress to finish.”
