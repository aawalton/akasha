The room Erhan had chosen for their little meeting was far from pretty much everything of interest in Castle Matali, tucked away in a section no one ever visited.

The space itself was nothing fancy, either. Just a small windowless room with a couple of chairs and a simple table. The thick stone made it nearly impossible for anyone to overhear, which was the point.

It seemed their mother had used it for certain private audiences when she’d ruled, or so she’d been told. Their parents had died when they’d been quite young, after all.

Erhan was already there when she entered, his back turned to her. For a long moment she just stood there, unsure of what to say.

“Han,” she said finally. “I’d say you’re looking well, but…”

He finally turned to face her, and she almost gasped.

She barely recognized her brother. His hair, which had been a rich brown, was streaked with grey and the skin around his eyes had a sunken, papery quality to them.

Tia noticed only now that he stood with a slight hunch, as though straining under the weight of the responsibility he bore.

The responsibility I forced him to bear alone, she corrected herself.

Tia’s heart almost stopped when he returned her greeting with a scowl.

“And yet you look as if you haven’t aged a day.”

She had no response to that.

“Five years, Tia,” he rasped in a hoarse voice that was nothing like the vibrant brother she remembered. “For five years, I thought you were dead! Do you have any idea how hard I searched for you? I tore up the damn realm! Spared no expense from our dwindling coffers, even when we couldn’t afford the cost.” He took a step toward her. “And now you show up as if nothing happened. As if you left for an afternoon stroll.”

Tia’s grip on the chair’s backrest tightened, but she said nothing. What could she possibly say now that wouldn’t sound like an excuse? The Tia that had had recklessly thrown herself into the Ash all those years ago would absolutely have argued, telling him she had her own path to pursue, and that she never wanted the throne in the first place.

Ten years in that blighted realm, however, had a tendency to change people.

Even still, even knowing all that, she’d at least hoped for, if not a warm welcome, then at least some sign of love, but it had been nothing more than an idle hope. They both knew she didn’t deserve any of it.

Tia had always known that her past would catch up with her eventually. It would seem that day had finally come. Rather fitting, she thought, for it to be the day when the realms miraculously mended themselves and she escaped from the Ash. Thanks to Vir, of all people, if the rumors were to be believed.

She’d only just begun reciting the apology she’d been rehearsing on her way here when Erhan suddenly crossed the room and wrapped her in his arms, foiling her plan in a single move as he sobbed into her shoulder..

Her mind went blank, entirely at a loss for how to react.

She’d braced herself for a scathing admonishment. She’d been fully prepared for him to refuse to see her at all. She hadn’t expected this.

For her elder brother, the King of Matali and the man who had ruled the country alone while she was off galavanting as an adventurer to come apart like this…

After standing around awkwardly, she finally gave in, wrapping her hands around him.

He was… thinner than she remembered.

“I’m sorry, Han,” she said quietly. “I’m so sorry.”

He pulled back after a long moment, wiping his face with the back of his hand.

“Might not be much, eh? But you’re all I have left. Despite everything, you’re still my stupid little sister.”

“Guess so,” Tia laughed, shaking her head.

“But where have you been?”

She gave him a sad smile. “Would you believe me if I said the Ash?”

He stared at her. “So it’s true, then.”

“You knew about that?” she asked, raising an eyebrow.

“Only rumors,” he said. “That you had sought out those Artifacts said to protect their wearer in the Ash. I dismissed them, mostly. The accounts were unreliable, if such convenient Artifacts even existed in the first place. I never thought…”

“Never thought I’d find them?” Tia asked, the edges of her lip tugging up. “Or never thought I’d be crazy enough to actually venture into that cursed realm?”

“Both,” he said with a knowing smile. “But even with those, how could you possibly survive? The monsters that lurk within those depths… And what of Haymi and Vason?”

“They’re fine,” Tia assured him. “Though, well, they very nearly died. We all did. So many times, especially early on, when we were just three lost Ash’va in a place where the very air is lethal to our kind.”

“Yet you managed to survive…”

Tia nodded. “The first year was the hardest. We didn’t know the lay of the land, didn’t understand which beasts to avoid at all cost… gods, we didn’t even know how to stay alive without our Artifacts. But we learned and we adapted. The Ash forces you to learn. Either that, or you die.

“Suffice it to say that our prana capacity increased, as did our magic. We at least had the foresight to take along A and B rank orbs, even if we had no hope of using them initially. That, and Haymi’s Life affinity saved us more times than I could count. But we grew into them over the years. We also got a lot better at working as a team to take down bigger and deadlier beasts. Not the really deadly stuff, mind you. There are some monsters in there that no human will ever match. Or were, I suppose. I can’t believe they’re all gone now, just like that.”

Tia realized her mistake when her brother’s expression steadily warped into one of horror and shock.

“You mean to tell me,” he said, “that you spent the last five years there?”

“Oh no, not at all,” Tia said, failing to stop the smile that crept onto her face.

Erhan heaved a sigh of relief. “Good. Because I—”

“I spent the last ten years there. Well, at least that. Time runs differently in the Ash. We lost track, but the effect gets greater the deeper you press. I’m older than you now, brother.”

Brown and red flames poured off her shoulders and arms, mixing as they rose into a vibrant blonde that came close to that of her own hair.

All this time, she’d been holding her prana in. Ever since she’d been returned to the Human Realm. Not only was it wasteful to let it dissipate, but letting her prana loose was a great way to attract attention.

That, and scare the life out of anyone who happened to see it. Which was, of course, her goal.

Erhan fell onto his ass, staring up at her with the most wide-eyed, open mouthed expression of shock she’d ever seen from him.

“Impossible,” he whispered, scrambling back several inches. “That’s… Only he can do that!”

“We all can, Han. Vason, Haymi, and me. And while I can’t speak for him…” Tia trailed off as Vir’s face appeared in her head, making her want to bury herself under a mountain and never come out.

She shook off the feeling and took a deep breath. “Him aside, I’d wager I could at least give Maiya quite a challenge in a straight fight. With the three of us together, well…” She ceased the flames. “Who could say.”

“What do you mean?” Erhan’s voice was tight. “You can’t possibly mean to fight her?”

“A tempting idea, I must admit,” Tia said with a devilish grin. “To think that lowly handmaiden has somehow become humanity’s leader in the time I was gone… It’s almost hard to believe only five years have passed in this realm.”

Erhan stared at her from the floor, trying and failing to respond to that. Eventually, he managed to grab the back of one of the chairs and pull himself up.

“This is…” he said, shaking his head. “This is a great deal to take in. But for you to appear before me now, wielding such power…. you did not come back to merely greet me, did you? You wish for the throne.”

Tia sighed, placing a hand on his shoulder. While he didn’t exactly resist, he did flinch at her touch, which hurt her more than she thought it would.

“You’ve nothing to fear from me, brother. Truly,” she said, looking directly into his eyes. “While I am not the same person who entered the Ash that day, I have not changed so much that I would usurp your throne. It seems like you’ve done a fine job of managing our country while I was gone. Better than I ever could, frankly. I’d be a frakking fool to change that.”

By the deep frown and the narrowed eyes, it was quite clear he didn’t believe a word of what she’d said. Even still, she felt as though the tension between them eased somewhat.

“And now that the Ash is truly gone,” she continued, “am I right in assuming Matali’s border concerns are a thing of the past? There is more land in the world now than any of us know what to do with, and with Ira on the throne in Kin’jal, our greatest enemy has vanished. The country has never needed a battle-crazed lone wolf like me less.”

“That’s…” Erhan cleared his throat. “What is it you intend to do, then?”

“For now? I want to apologize,” she said, fidgeting for a moment. “To you, first and foremost, for abandoning you when you needed me most. I should not have left. No, even long before that, I should have helped you when you were forced to take the throne after mother and father died.

“I… told myself I was doing it for noble reasons, but the truth is that I was just running away. In the end, you were right. I was a spoiled brat of a princess, and I behaved like one. After that, I wish to apologize to our country for the same reasons. To undo some of the damage I caused before I left, in whatever way I’m able.”

Seeing the disbelief in her brother’s eyes, however, gave her pause. Even more than when she’d flaunted her strength earlier.

“I want to help our people,” she repeated. “Give them courage and assure them that everything will be fine, even if I’m not sure of that myself. Beyond that, I honestly don’t know. This power I have must be useful for something, right? If nothing else, as a political tool? We can figure that out together. If you’ll have me, of course.”

Erhan looked at her for a long moment.

She fully expected a lengthy debate. Trust was never regained in a day, after all, and Han had survived five years while governing a dying country. That kind of strength did not come without a healthy distrust of goodwill.

And yet, for the second time in their meeting, Erhan Matali proved his sister wrong when he reached out and took her hand.

“You have no idea how long I have wished to hear those words, Tia.”

Despite everything, despite all the walls she’d built over the years and the toughness she’d cultivated, Tiyana Matali broke down in tears.
