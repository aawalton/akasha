Crowlis kept looking directly at Sakura. For the first few seconds, she still tried to convince herself it was a coincidence. Maybe the man had simply turned toward the spring, heard the water, and happened to be looking exactly where she was standing. But then his eyes narrowed slightly, and he walked straight toward her. It was getting harder to believe in coincidence.

"Sakura," Gilbert said quietly.

She heard him, but did not look away from Crowlis. The other students continued talking among themselves. Nobus was saying something irritably to the mage beside him, while another man flipped through notes at the table. No one even noticed that Crowlis was no longer listening to the meeting and was walking toward three people who should not exist in this time at all. This was all just a memory from the past, right?

He stopped in front of Sakura and reached out. She tried to pull back, but Crowlis’s fingers had already closed around her wrist. This time, his hand did not pass through her.

"What the..."

Sakura did not have time to finish. The cave disappeared along with the students, the sound of water, and all the voices. She was suddenly pulled forward, lost all sense of her body for several seconds, and then everything stopped just as abruptly.

Sakura yanked her hand free and immediately stepped back. There was nothing around them but blackness. No floor, no walls, no ceiling, though somehow she was still standing and could see Crowlis perfectly a few steps away. There did not even seem to be any light here, but that did not stop her from seeing. Sakura cautiously looked down and immediately decided not to do that again. Her feet seemed to hang above an endless void, even though some invisible support clearly existed beneath them.

"Where’s Gilbert? Where’s Lir?" That was the first thing that mattered to her.

Crowlis did not answer, and Sakura reflexively raised her hand, ready to use a skill. Nothing happened. She tried to call on the System, bring up her status, or at least open [Inventory], but again, nothing worked. There was no response at all. As if the System simply did not exist here.

Sakura had long since forgotten what it felt like to be in a world where nothing obeyed that mysterious System!

She stepped back again and raised her hands. Crowlis remained where he was, watching her calmly. Without the hood, he looked exactly as he had a few minutes ago in the cave: dark hair with gray at the temples, high cheekbones, attentive eyes. Nothing obviously threatening, though after he had pulled her into some unknown place with a single touch and cut her off from the System, appearances did not mean much anymore.

"I won’t hurt you," he said.

Sakura did not lower her hands, even though her skills were clearly blocked in this place.

"Every suspicious person says that."

The corner of his mouth twitched slightly.

"That is a reasonable point."

"Where are my friends?"

"Exactly where they were. They’re fine. And we don’t have much time, so it would be better not to waste it on pointless arguments."

Sakura still did not trust him, but slowly lowered her hands. If Crowlis really wanted to hurt her, he probably would have done it already.

"What even is this place?"

He looked around at the blackness with mild boredom.

"I call it the in-between."

"The in-between? Between what?"

"Between a memory and the person observing it. I don’t have a precise definition. We aren’t supposed to be here at all."

Crowlis explained it with surprising patience, like a patient teacher. Sakura had not expected him to speak to her this way.

"Much clearer now..."

"I’m afraid we don’t have enough time for a proper explanation."

Sakura tried to clarify.

"We’re in the past, right?"

"Not exactly. I think you’re inside someone’s memory. One preserved by magic and strong enough to outlive the person it belonged to."

Sakura frowned.

"Ater?"

Crowlis said nothing.

"And you can talk to me from someone else’s memory?"

"Technically, I shouldn’t be able to. One way or another, this breaks the natural order of things."

That made her wary again.

"Then how did we manage this at all?"

"Because we both hear the water. Apparently, that connected us."

A faint tremor passed through the darkness around them. At first, Sakura thought she had imagined it, but a few seconds later it happened again. Crowlis immediately raised his head and spent several seconds listening to something she could not hear.

"That’s why we don’t have much time."

"What’s happening?"

"The memory wasn’t made for me to speak with someone who was never part of it."

He looked at her again.

"And there are several things you need to know, Sakura."

She immediately tensed.

"Wait. I never told you my name. How do you..."

"That isn’t important right now."

Sakura frowned. Everyone around here kept giving her answers like that. Crowlis continued.

"I’m already dead."

Sakura said nothing. Goosebumps ran over her skin. It was strange hearing words like that from a man standing right in front of her, one who had held her wrist only moments ago, even if they were true.

"So there is no point looking to me for answers. What you see in front of you right now is only what remains of the man I once was. A small piece of consciousness inside a magical memory. I can warn you. Maybe point you in the right direction. But you will have to fix what happened after this day yourself."

"Why me?"

"Because you became the Obsidian Hierophant."

"I didn’t choose that."

"I know. But the class belongs to you now, and the sooner you learn how to use it, the better."

Sakura frowned but did not interrupt.

"There will be people who need your power. Some will try to use you. Others will decide it would be safer to kill you. From what I’ve managed to see, some of them are already close..."

Crowlis took a step nearer.

"The Obsidian Hierophant will give you the power to defend yourself. But remember one thing. You need to master the class before the class masters you."

"This sounds like another riddle."

"Just consider it a warning."

"Can you be more specific?"

Crowlis was about to answer, but the space around them trembled again. This time, Sakura felt the support beneath her feet drop for a moment, and a low rumble rolled somewhere in the distance.

"We have very little time left."

"Then stop talking in riddles."

Crowlis suddenly laughed quietly.

"You remind me of someone."

Sakura immediately latched onto the words.

"Who?"

"It doesn’t matter yet. If you meet him, maybe you’ll understand on your own."

Crowlis’s face grew serious again.

"I’m sorry," he suddenly said.

Sakura drew her brows together.

"What?"

"I’m sorry you’ll have to fix what I left behind."

Sakura studied him more carefully. Before, Crowlis had spoken about the future as if he only knew it vaguely, but now there was too much certainty in his words, as if he had been there and seen everything with his own eyes.

"Then at least help me avoid repeating your mistakes."

"That’s exactly why I’m talking to you now. Pay attention to the people around you."

Sakura immediately thought of Gilbert and Lir.

"I trust them."

"I’m not saying you shouldn’t. Just remember that everyone who stays beside you will have their own reasons for doing so. Especially when ambition is involved."

Sakura remembered the underground hall and Nobus, who had only moments ago been trying to convince the others to move on to integrating the new class.

"Nobus?"

Crowlis’s face revealed almost nothing, but his silence was enough.

"You think he’s dangerous."

"I know what he was like."

"Then say it clearly."

Crowlis looked her in the eyes.

"Beware of Nobus."

Sakura frowned.

"Why?"

"Because the result always mattered more to him than the risk."

"Does he have something to do with what happened to you?"

Crowlis did not have time to answer. Thin white cracks appeared in the darkness behind him, as if someone had struck glass. The space rocked, and Sakura had to take a step to keep her balance. A ripple passed through Crowlis’s figure.

"Time’s up," he said.

"No, wait. I still have a ton of questions! What happened to your class? Where did the obsidian creatures come from? Why are they coming to me? What even is [DEVOURER]? And why does the water call me the Heiress? Who is Ater?"

Sakura rushed through the last questions, afraid he would disappear before he could hear even half of them.

"You’ll have to look for answers among the living."

"Very convenient. You’re the only person who even understands what I’m asking."

"That’s why I can tell you who to look for."

The cracks around them widened. Through one of them, Sakura saw the cave with the students. Then Ater’s room appeared, and then there was only blackness again. Crowlis raised a hand as if he was about to touch her shoulder, then changed his mind.

"I’m glad I was able to see you after all."

Sakura immediately grew wary. There was far too much warmth in his voice for someone who had supposedly met her for the first time.

"Did we know each other?"

Crowlis did not answer.

"Did you know me?"

"No."

He answered suspiciously fast, and Sakura narrowed her eyes.

"You’re lying."

Crowlis looked at her for several seconds and smiled faintly.

"Maybe."

A new crack split the space between them, and part of his arm disappeared for a moment.

"Find the Flying Tenno," Crowlis said quickly.

For a second, Sakura was not even sure she had heard him correctly.

"Who?"

"The Flying Tenno."

"Where do I find him?"

"I don’t know where he’ll be in your time. If he’s even still alive. But if you find him, he can teach you."

"Teach me what?"

"How to be a Hierophant."

Sakura froze.

"Is he a Hierophant too?"

Crowlis was already beginning to dissolve along with the surrounding darkness.

"Find him, Sakura."

"Wait! Crowlis!"

He looked at her for a few more seconds, then said:

"Good luck, Heiress."

The blackness vanished. Sakura was suddenly pulled sideways and down. For one second, the cave flashed before her eyes, Nobus by the table, Lir and Gilbert nearby, and then everything cut out.

Sakura opened her eyes and did not immediately understand what was happening. For some reason, the room was lying on its side. The floor was right in front of her face, shards of a clay cup were scattered nearby, and Ater’s table lay overturned a few steps away. Only then did Sakura realize the room had not turned sideways. She was lying on the floor.

Her ears were ringing, and the air was full of dust and smoke. Sakura coughed and pushed herself up on one elbow. One leg of the table was broken, dishes and food were scattered across the floor, and a wide crack ran along the far wall. Apparently, while they had been inside the memory, an explosion had gone off here.

"Sakura!"

She turned toward the voice. Gilbert stood in the smoke, supporting Lir under the arms. Lir was almost completely hanging from him, his legs barely supporting his weight, while his eyes kept opening and closing.

"Lir..."

The house groaned, dust fell from above, and somewhere near the wall a shelf crashed to the floor. Gilbert was staring toward the door that had been blown open by the blast.

"He caught up with us!"
