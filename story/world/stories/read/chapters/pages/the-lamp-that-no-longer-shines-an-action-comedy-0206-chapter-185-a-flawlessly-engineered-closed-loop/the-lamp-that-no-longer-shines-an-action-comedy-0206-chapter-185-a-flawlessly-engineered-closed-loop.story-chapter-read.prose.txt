[Time]: Summer Break, Day 49 — 2:20 PM[Location]: White City · Royal Rosas Club · Courtyard

Hathaway's voice came out dry, scraping against the back of her throat.

"What was that box for?"

Liandra looked down at the crushed aluminum can in her hands. The afternoon sunlight lay across the courtyard stones with complete indifference to the conversation.

"That ring box at the altar," Liandra said, her tone carrying the flat, clinical cadence of someone reading from a case file, "originally held a Legendary-tier [Imprisonment] spell." A short, mirthless exhale. "Unfortunately, Evangeline was one step ahead. If she hadn't been, this entire catastrophe would have been resolved six years ago."

Hathaway went silent.

A wave of colliding variables surged through her cognition, rearranging every cached piece of data she had accumulated over the past three days. Then the final puzzle piece dropped into place, a quiet, definitive click, the sound of a lock engaging.

Of course. Of course it was.

Cecilia Wellington had never intended to drag anyone else down with her. Her sole operational objective, from the beginning, had been to solve the problem herself. The presence of an imprisonment spell in that box clarified one critical parameter: she had only ever planned to keep Evangeline.

She had been giving her chances, not once or twice, but the entire time.

From the day Evangeline first abandoned the estate, all the way to the suffocating confrontation in the hotel suite barely twenty-four hours ago, when Cecilia had stood at the very edge of the abyss and still asked: Why didn't you even want my eyes?

That question hadn't been for Cecilia's own closure: it had been Evangeline's exit ramp. A door, held open. If Evangeline had offered an answer containing even a microscopic fraction of genuine human weight, Cecilia would have stood down.

Hathaway closed her eyes for one second.

Evangeline simply lacked the hardware to register what was being offered. A hollow vessel merely exists, and the warmth poured into it vanishes without trace.

As the logic tree resolved, a profound, unexpected calm settled over her own chest.

My romantic trajectory, she noted, is structurally safe.

Rhode and Bella had looked at the Wellington bloodline and seen one thing: the suffocating grip of obsession that refused to release. But Hathaway had the complete dataset now. The Wellington women loved without reservation. They gave their entire capacity, absorbed withering damage, and still, impossibly, retained their gentleness.

Evangeline was the outlier, the unprecedented achievement of starting a campaign at maximum affection parameters and single-handedly brute-forcing every available route into its worst possible Bad Ending through sheer, unyielding emptiness.

Truly a milestone. There will not be another.

Then she thought of Ash.

Liandra was still waiting. Sixty-five years. If Hathaway combined the full span of both her lives, it still wouldn't reach that number. Even for a Witch, who belonged to a long-lived species, the duration of Ash's absence had vastly eclipsed the brief window of time they had actually shared.

Perhaps that was precisely the mechanism. Witches appeared aloof and capricious to the outside world, but their few, genuine obsessions didn't weather with time: they crystallized.

Her linguistic processor returned zero viable outputs. Whatever was keeping Ash away operated on a tier far beyond her current clearance level. All she could do was stand in the sunlit courtyard and feel the gravitational weight of sixty-five years pressing down on the woman standing beside her.

Liandra was quiet for a moment longer. Then the vulnerability sealed itself away behind blast doors, efficient and practiced, and when she spoke, her voice had returned to its habitual, unbothered register.

"I should go. Otherwise Alucard's going to come nag me again."

Hathaway watched her walk away, the dark coat disappearing into the shadows of the arched corridor.

It's fine, Hathaway thought. Witches do not lack for time. And in the end, they still have their family.

Then, exactly one microsecond later, her developer brain executed a violent, unprompted system interrupt.

Witches have time. You, however, do not. Your development bottleneck is critical. The patent royalties are sitting idle. The alchemy equipment is on order. Today is the operational window. Move.

She reached into her pocket and closed her fingers around the cold, enchanted coin.

[Time]: Summer Break, Day 49 — 4:15 PM[Location]: White City · Outer District · Irene's Spell Shop

Irene's emporium didn't appear on any map and ran every visit as a private instance: no shared queue, no waiting room, just Hathaway and whatever the coin chose to unlock.

Hathaway had taken a Griffin carriage to the outer district, disembarked, and walked the remaining distance on foot to a tasteful, understated storefront nestled beneath the sprawling canopy of an enormous, ancient oak.

She pushed the heavy door open.

The sharp, clean scent of lemon zest arrived first. A delicate incense jar exhaled a faint cooling mist on the counter. To her left: a sleek, automated self-service requisition terminal, blinking quietly.

To her right: Irene Habsburg, sitting comfortably on a velvet sofa with a ledger open across her lap.

Hathaway's boots froze to the floorboards. She inhaled a sharp, involuntary breath.

Does the geopolitical underpinning of the Inner Sea's entire financial architecture genuinely have this much free time? The Fifth Seat is personally working the cash register?!

Irene looked up. She registered the full-body flinch with the warm, completely unfazed composure of a woman who had catalogued this reaction several thousand times.

"Welcome." Her smile arrived without effort. "Please, have a seat."

Hathaway navigated to the armchair across from the sofa with the careful, slightly stiff movements of someone acutely aware of being under-leveled for the current room. Irene rose, crossed to a small kitchenette in the corner, and returned bearing a porcelain tea set and a plate of delicate, layered pastries, both preserved in a [FreshRevert] stasis box at the exact temperature and texture of their baking.

"Please," Irene offered, setting the cup before her.

Hathaway accepted the teacup with both hands and took a measured sip. Her sensory array returned a single, immediate assessment: catastrophically expensive. Do not spill a drop.

Even if Irene had some mystifying surplus of spare time, she was not going to monopolize the schedule of a sovereign financial apparatus.

"I would like to purchase the feat scrolls for [Dismiss Self-Cast], [Iron Will], and [Spell Conversion]," Hathaway stated, going straight to business.

Her shopping list had been finalized long before she walked through the door.

Both [Spell Rebound] and [Echo Casting] activated on residual spell matrices: the leftover mana signature after a casting completed. [Dismiss Self-Cast] let her manufacture the precondition on demand, converting two situational feats into reliable tactical tools.

She had stood locked in place once, unable to cast, unable to move, because a standard Will save had failed at the worst possible moment. [Iron Will] was the non-negotiable patch. It was a feat virtually every Witch possessed, which made the scroll marginally cheaper than the esoteric options, and made her marginally more embarrassed to still need it.

Watching Allison absorb Irene's own casting and redirect it back had planted a specific idea. [Spell Conversion] wouldn't approach that level, but the underlying principle was the same: absorb a spell, expel a variant of the same attribute and origin. Swallowing a basic fireball to spit out a pyroblast, or cannibalizing the residue of her own spells into a secondary payload.

Irene named the figures without consulting a ledger. "[Iron Will]: eighty-five thousand Solars. [Dismiss Self-Cast]: one hundred and sixty thousand. [Spell Conversion]: two hundred and forty-five thousand."

Total: four hundred and ninety thousand. The figure settled cleanly at the lower end of her pre-compiled research estimate. A professional courtesy discount from someone who recognized the client.

Irene's golden eyes drifted downward, resting for exactly one unhurried microsecond on the [Ring of the Dark Sun] on Hathaway's finger. Her smile deepened by a fraction.

"Have you ever heard of [Concealed Fluctuation]?"

Hathaway shook her head, a slight hesitation in her voice. "I already have [Silent Cast]..."

Irene's head tilted in a gentle negation. "[Silent Cast] eliminates the mana overflow during execution, what we typically call 'noise.' It does not mask the energy field distortion that occurs the moment the spell matrix actually forms."

She leaned forward slightly, the instructor's gravity in her voice effortless. "If you ever intend to participate in cross-planar incursions against spellcaster empires, or if you ever need to strike with genuine surprise against a top-tier Witch, this feat is mandatory. The mechanic is direct: pay an additional fifty percent mana cost, and your casting signature is completely erased."

There is no reason to do it halfway. Hathaway confirmed the addition, then handed over her premier banking card. The transaction finalized.

A phantom, rhythmic pain established itself in the center of her chest.

Irene laughed: a soft, genuinely amused sound. "Your expression."

She gestured toward an ornate standing mirror near the coat rack.

Hathaway turned her head. Her reflection verified the current operational status of her own face: lips pressed into a bloodless, defensive line, brows arching skyward in a steep V of concentrated distress, cheeks puffed out with the involuntary, subconscious commitment of a creature preparing to triple in volume as an emergency defense mechanism.

"Ah, that is..." Hathaway started, and found she had nothing to follow it with.

"The expression of someone who has just spent a vast sum of money," Irene provided, with the seasoned expertise of a veteran merchant who had documented this specific display across several thousand client interactions. "Every Witch who walks through those doors eventually wears it."

The acute awareness of financial catastrophe redirected Hathaway's cognition toward its primary coping protocol: alchemy. Once I master Alchemy, I will be a wealthy woman.

"If I develop an original formula through my alchemical studies," Hathaway said, leaning forward slightly, "and I register the patent through your distribution channels, what does that process actually look like?"

She heard how it sounded the moment it left her mouth. "I have only just started reading the theory. I am merely asking about the parameters in advance."

Irene considered the question for a moment. Then she raised one hand.

From deep in the back storeroom, a massive leather-bound manual floated through the air and settled on the coffee table with a heavy, deliberate thud.

"[Alchemical Vision]," Irene introduced, her voice dropping into its professional register. "This feat cannot be compiled into a consumable scroll. It requires prolonged, deliberate exposure to specific reagents and rigorous cognitive training, with the objective of forcing the evolution of a supplementary neural pathway: a dedicated secondary thought-channel that runs a permanent overlay in your visual field, tagging, logging, and managing alchemical materials in real-time."

She tapped the cover. "Because it involves literal neurological modification and the construction of an artificial cognitive channel, its acquisition difficulty is rated 'A,' possessed by a microscopic fraction of the practitioner population."

Hathaway stared at the book.

It is a heads-up display hardcoded into the optic nerve. It is, without any qualification whatsoever, a cheat code.

"What is the training manual's price?" Hathaway asked, carefully.

Irene's golden eyes lit with a bright, crystalline, profoundly pleasant amusement.

"One million, two hundred and sixty thousand Solars."

Hathaway's internal abacus caught fire. 1.26 million. She had the liquidity. The transaction was executable. But purchasing the manual would compress her remaining operational budget for raw alchemy materials into a suffocating bottleneck. Yes, the patent royalties deposited monthly, so she wouldn't starve, but 1.26 million was only the training manual. Mastering an A-rank cognitive mutation required months of secondary reagents, neurological modification compounds, and experimental material cycles. None of which the Royal Rosas Club was going to approve as a reimbursable operational expense.

As the calculations cascaded, Hathaway's eyebrows knotted into an expression of concentrated, genuine misery. She weakly pressed one hand over her heart, reached for her card with the other, and swiped it across the terminal without looking at the screen, because if she actually read the remaining account balance, she was going to burst into tears and beg the Fifth Seat of the High Council for a refund.

The confirmation chime sounded. In the ringing silence that followed, the realization arrived with the clean, unavoidable finality of a closed audit.

Every Solar from the patent buyout contract. The lump sum. The monthly royalties. Every coin she had routed through Irene's distribution network had now been efficiently, completely, and irrevocably returned to Irene's vault.

A flawlessly engineered closed-loop economy.

The light evacuated Hathaway's eyes completely. "Thank you for your recommendations, ma'am."

Irene tilted her head, the soft chime of her earrings accompanying the motion. "You may call me Irene."

Hathaway's lips parted. Her vocal cords refused to compile a response.

Irene didn't press the issue. The mercantile warmth faded from her expression, leaving behind something quieter and more direct. She looked at Hathaway with a steady, unhurried sincerity.

"I was truly honored," Irene said softly, "that when the sky was falling, I was the one you thought to call."

Hathaway's heart executed a sharp, involuntary skip.

She knew exactly who was sitting across from her. Fifth Seat. The architect of the slave contract. A woman whose depth Evangeline herself had explicitly characterized as a specialist in the management of the foolish. Every fact Hathaway possessed about Irene Habsburg pointed in the same direction.

She lowered her head.

"...Goodbye, Irene."

She pushed through the heavy oak door and stepped out into the late afternoon sun. Her legs felt hollow. Her bank account was a smoking crater. Her arms were loaded with restricted feat scrolls and a brain-modifying A-rank training manual.

She stood beneath the canopy of the ancient tree and ran a quiet tally of everything the day had contained: the courtyard, and Liandra, and sixty-five years, and crystallized obsessions, and the box that had always been a trap. The shop, and the card she hadn't looked at, and the name she had said.

She thought of the townhouse at the end of the carriage line. Silver hair at the dinner table.

It's fine. It's all fine.
