Unlike his time spent ‘training’ with Moxy back in Whikoga, which really was little more than desperately running around while trying not to get the flesh blasted off his body from her air blasts, Asher’s days spent with Mornamol were far less exciting. Sure, they had the occasional practice bout with one another here and there, but true to his word, the majority of their time was spent trying to correct all of the little things Asher had been doing wrong all this time.

How to hold a dagger if he was going for a forward thrust. How to adjust his grip if he needed to stab down. How to deflect a ranged projectile without getting hit in the face. How to utilize both daggers at the same time to block a more powerful opponent’s attack. No matter how many hours the two of them spent together, Mornamol never seemed to run out of things to critique about Asher’s technique, or new moves to teach him that might potentially save his life one day.

Which made a lot of sense, seeing as Asher’s technique really did boil down primarily to ‘stab until they stop moving, and then sometimes stab some more.’

By the end of their fourth day of training, Asher had already begun to feel like a more competent fighter. At first, all the different ways Mornamol demanded he adjust his grip felt wrong, but the muscle memory he’d built up over the last few months was quickly rewritten. The more they sparred, the more Asher came to realize Mornamol really knew what he was talking about, and the more he decided to just trust in whatever the dagger expert told him to do, regardless of how weird it felt. He shifted how he placed his feet, how he utilized his legs and core to put more power into each thrust, and even learned how to throw a dagger to ensure it struck either blade or pommel first, giving him a nonlethal method of taking down enemies at range. Of course, knocking someone unconscious for even just a few seconds would probably result in serious brain damage, but in a world filled with magical healing, he could only hope that was something which could be easily solved.

By the time his package arrived at its destination via pegasus, he was feeling more confident than ever in his ability to stab something until it stopped moving. When he told Mornamol this, the slayer had only laughed and informed him he had a long way to go before he could even be considered halfway decent with a blade, but had admitted he was realms better than when they’d first begun. With the open invitation to reach out to him via the slayer guild whenever he wanted more lessons, Asher thanked his instructor and went to meet back up with his team in their usual lounge room.

“Everyone ready?” he asked, taking in his three teammates. As usual, Samantha stood at his side, hefting a quiver filled with some sort of new arrow he’d never seen before. Rulfar stood across from them, his battle axe looking freshly polished and ready to split some undead skulls. Moxy was lounging off to the side in one of the bean bags, her crooked grin all the answer Asher needed as to whether she was prepared to put her life on the line. “Do we need to go over the plan again?”

“Stick to the outer edges of the Death Aura and kill as many undead monsters as we can to give you cover as you sneak in and shank a walking corpse,” Moxy drawled, getting up and holding out her hand. “We’ve gone over this plenty. Come on, let’s go!”

Rolling his eyes, Asher grabbed her hand and pulled her into his rift. A moment later, Rulfar joined her. Turning to Samantha, Asher smiled and held out his hand.

“Ready to shoot down some undead?”

“Ready as I’ll ever be,” Samantha said, returning her smile as she tilted her head toward her new quiver. “Ended up picking up some living wood arrows. Not cheap, but the shafts are supposed to be incredibly durable, and they should be extremely effective against undead.”

“You don’t think your piercing enchantment is enough?” Asher asked, recalling how it had blasted holes straight through the lesser demons’ thick heads.

“After experiencing how the greater demons’ presence weakens enchantments, I’m trying to limit how much I rely on it,” she admitted. “I don’t have any skills to help me out, which means all I can do is change up my ammunition.”

“Fair enough. I’ll leave a couple of your prepared quivers with you just to be safe, seeing as I won’t be able to act as your mobile resupply while I’m off running head-first to my death.”

“Just be careful,” Samantha said, putting her arms around him and giving him a tender kiss. A small part of him found it entertaining that his Belt of Strength finally allowed him to withstand the crushing force of her powerful hugs, but he decided not to mention that thought, for obvious reasons. After a few seconds, they broke away, and she gave him a pointed look.

“If you go down, the Death Aura will probably keep you down for good, and we can’t exactly go in after you. I don’t want you coming back to life a thousand years in the future when some other suicidal maniac actually does manage to put the lich down and Eternal Regeneration finally kicks back on.”

“You know, that would be kind of interesting though,” Asher said, doing his best not to grin as Samantha rolled her eyes. “Imagine, traveling into the future in the blink of an eye…”

“We better not keep the others waiting,” Samantha sighed, giving him one last smile as she took his hand. Pulling her into his rift as well, Asher took a deep breath, running through his mental checklist and ensuring he was ready. Yes, he’d taken down one of the liches already, but that had been little more than a cheap shot, almost entirely orchestrated by Zygrul. This was the first time they were going after not one, but two liches all on their own. Technically, they were using the information contained in Zygrul’s journals, but the actual plan and killing of the liches would be entirely on them.

It was time to truly strike back against The Council of Death.

Focusing on his Eternal Mark, Asher activated Recall, and the world shifted all around him. In an instant, the comfy, silent lounge within the palace was replaced by a dark, musty smelling storeroom. Seeing as they hadn’t actually had anyone to deliver a package to, they’d instructed the pegasus courier to drop it off at the town hall, figuring it would be put into storage while some poor clerk did their best to find 'Rulfgoxy', who the courier would no doubt insist was visiting the town.

Taking the package back and leaving a thank you note that it had been collected, Asher slipped out of the storeroom, quickly making his way out of the small town. He supposed it didn’t actually matter if anyone ended up spotting him, but he also wasn’t really in the mood to stop and answer a dozen questions about who he was and where he’d come from. Once he made it to the nearby forest, he pulled Samantha back out of his rift, hopping onto her back and holding on as she took off.

Despite how terrifying the Deadlands were, the undead monsters roaming around within never left the Death Aura keeping everything else out, and the Deadlands themselves remained static. Because of that, the village sitting a mere few miles away had never bothered to up and move, and the two of them spotted the edge of the Deadlands just a few minutes later.

The vast swath of green from grass and trees and whatever other plants that grew across the ground ended in an instant, replaced with dark, almost black dirt that stretched off far into the distance. The land didn’t just look dead, it looked sick, and Asher scrunched up his face as they touched down barely a dozen feet from the strange border. This close, they could even spot a handful of zombies and other unnerving monsters roaming around the Deadlands, aimlessly wandering despite the fact that there was nothing to attack.

“No wonder they call it the Deadlands,” Samantha shivered, rubbing her arms as she peered into the land completely devoid of any and all life. “My Forest element doesn’t like this one bit.”

“The good news is that if we have our way, it won’t be like this forever,” Asher said, pulling Moxy and Rulfar from his rift. “Though based on how it looks… People are going to need to wait a few centuries for everything to grow back, or hire a hell of a lot of druids.”

“I don’t like druids,” Moxy said, frowning as she began stretching. “I don’t know what it is, something about how they talk to plants or seem to like plants more than people just sits wrong with me. I’ve met three druids in my life, and all of them gave me a bad feeling.”

“I can see a handful of zombies just from here,” Rulfar said, gazing out into the Deadlands. Inhaling through his nose, he made a face before coughing slightly. “It’s probably for the best that you’re heading into the center on your own, I can’t smell a thing in there. The scent of death is too powerful, it blocks everything else out.”

“If the undead can’t leave the Deadlands, can I just shoot them from out here?” Samantha asked, nocking a living wood arrow as she judged the distance between her and her targets. The nearest cluster had about ten zombies, all slowly shambling about without any real purpose. “Will they just act like there’s an invisible wall when they hit the edge of the Deadlands?”

“No, that’s the only time the undead actually will leave the Deadlands, though they’ll return back as soon as you’re dead,” Asher explained, recalling what he’d read about the strange zone. “The Death Aura keeps their bodies intact and ‘healthy,’ so they don’t like to leave it for long.”

“Might as well see what we’re up against,” Moxy shrugged, cracking her neck and bouncing on the balls of her feet in anticipation. “Take the shot, Wings!”

Seeing Rulfar and Asher nod in agreement, Samantha took a deep breath, brought her bow up, and let the first arrow fly. The team watched as the arrow shot across the Deadlands, before slamming directly into the head of a distant zombie. The zombie, which should have been rather unfazed by a small sliver of wood shoved into its brain, dropped like a sack of rocks, the effects of the living wood making themselves readily known as a pale green smoke began drifting out of the arrow wound. While that zombie wasn’t getting up anytime soon, the rest of the wandering group somehow managed to figure out where the arrow had come from, and they began running toward their position.

“Wait, zombies can run?!” Asher asked, shocked at the sight of the once shambling corpses sprinting rather quickly toward them.

“Of course they can run,” Moxy laughed, waiting for them to get within range before throwing out two quick jabs, each one firing out an accompanying blast of air that snapped the zombies’ heads clean off and sent them sprawling. “And they’re damn fast, too. They don’t get tired like we do.”

Though even if they were fast, the zombies weren’t intelligent enough to even attempt to dodge the incoming attacks. Samantha managed to shoot down two more, and Moxy knocked the heads off another four with her rapid-style strikes. That left only two zombies to actually make it to the edge of the Deadlands, though if they could hold so much as a fraction of a thought, they probably wished they hadn't. A single, massive cleave of Rulfar’s battle axe was all it took to bisect both zombies along the waist, and Asher wrinkled his nose as the two severed torsos began crawling their way toward them, still moaning all the while as they dragged intestines behind them.

“Alright, that’s probably the nastiest thing I’ve ever seen,” Asher said, putting the zombies out of their misery with twin thrusts of his living wood dagger to the temple. “So even if they can run, they still follow the same zombie rules? Headshots to take them down for good?”

“For these basic ones, yeah,” Moxy said, peering down at the finished zombies. “The further you get into the Death Aura, the stronger the zombies will be. You’ll probably find a good number far more powerful than this.”

“Remember, when you feel the Death Aura taking its toll, Recall out to safety,” Samantha said for the third time, giving him one last quick hug for encouragement. “Good luck in there,” she muttered into his shoulder before leaning up to kiss him.

“Same goes to you,” Asher said, smiling at her before nodding toward his other teammates. “Even if you guys are going to stick close to the edge, you’ll still be under the effects of the Death Aura. Be careful.”

“With my Life element, we should be okay for the most part,” Moxy pointed out. “Don’t worry about us, Runt, go crack some undead skulls.”

“Will do,” Asher laughed, giving them all one last wave before turning and crossing over the border into the Deadlands proper. As the weak, icy aura of death fell over him, currently little more than a whisper of what was to come in the back of his mind, he ignored it as he made a beeline straight for the center of the Deadlands. After all, he had a few dozen miles of death and monsters to cover, followed by what very well might be the most dangerous opponents he’d ever fought.

He didn’t have a second to waste.
