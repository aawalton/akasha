Chapter 392

“Why is it so freaking humid here?”

Michael shrugged. “Japan’s humid. I went once when I was a kid, and it’s actually not quite as humid now as it was back then.”

“That’s… nuts.”

At this point, all major settlements around Earth had been connected to Forest via fracture halls, and some of the largest ones were even connected independently of Forest. Humanity was slowly but surely reclaiming Earth.

The Japanese settlement had already been doing pretty well for itself, but a few years after establishing an amicable relationship with Forest, they had really begun to thrive. They were probably the second most prosperous Earth settlement after Forest.

“Are you hungry?”

“Don’t you have to go and do interviews with your new employees?”

“Potential new employees, and not for another… hour and fifteen minutes, no. We have plenty of time to get something to eat. Is there anything you’re interested in?”

“Sushi!”

“All right, let’s take a look aro—”

“Ramen!”

“Okay! Maybe we can find a place that has a bit of both—”

“Kaki goori! Dango! Senbei!”

“All right, all right, I don’t even know what any of that is. Where did you hear about all these dishes?”

She shrugged. “Old magazines.”

”Maybe we had better split it up into two meals. One now, and one after my business is over with.”

“Sushi now, then!” she proclaimed and started walking.

International recruitment was perfectly possible now that translation abilities made language barriers trivial and the collapse of political borders made immigration a case-by-case judgment.

The sushi was absolutely delicious. Apparently, someone had developed an ability set that specialized in animal husbandry, and quickly bred a type of salmon that rapidly reached ideal size combined with meat of staggeringly high quality.

Michael thought that the theory behind that could possibly be utilized in medicine as well, so he was already planning a second visit to the Japanese settlement to talk to the breeder. Bin reminded him that something like that had probably already been explored by other healers, but the man insisted on wanting to figure it out for himself.

“I should buy a lot of stuff to bring back to my Grandpa,” Bin said as they left the restaurant. “He needs inspiration to update the restaurant’s menu.”

“Why?” Michael asked with a raised eyebrow. “Doesn’t he basically have a full house every night?”

“He says it’s boring to make the same stuff every day.”

“Reminds me of a certain someone,” Michael muttered.

***

Michael’s interviews had gone very well. Even now, among humans in Forest, there was a serious lack of knowledge of basic medical principles and practices.

It wasn’t particularly worrying at this point in time, because they had access to incredible experts via Gimleh and the other Alliance-affiliated territories, but it had been a severe issue before their encounter with the Nidafjeld Alliance. This Japanese group had been slightly… luckier, perhaps?

Michael hadn’t researched the matter very thoroughly, but his best theory was that their settlement had simply been more fortunate than Forest and that not quite as many medically trained individuals had lost their lives before eventually joining up.

At low levels, Awakened specialized in healing in no way trivialized the skills of a conventionally trained doctor, nurse, firefighter, or paramedic, and it had allowed them to weather quite a few minor problems that could have developed into something worse without proper care.

Furthermore, with more medical practitioners Awakening, there was also a naturally greater inclination to opt for the path of a healer, which had allowed them to completely avoid the shortage of healers to venture out with warrior that Forest had initially faced.

In the end, Michael had met with three candidates and actually decided to just hire them all. They were all Awakened, specialized in healing and with conventional medical backgrounds. Two had been nurses and one had been a firefighter. One of the nurses had even gone further into specialization and focused on anesthetics and pain management via mental skills.

Michael was giddy like a pony on grass after a long winter when they made their way to a restaurant, recommended to them by the firefighter.

It was when they were about to turn a corner onto the street where the restaurant should be, when someone called out to them.

That was strange. None of them knew anybody here. Well, as far as they were aware.

“Excuse me! Could you wait for a moment?” the voice called.

It was a Japanese woman. She looked to be in her mid-thirties or something along those lines, and she was jogging up to them, two friends hanging back slightly and looking right about as confused as Bin felt.

A massive bow was strapped to her lower back. It looked like it would be a real pain in the ass to navigate through a crowd with that thing attached. Effectively, it made her almost two meters wide.

They stopped and let her catch up, Bin shooting Michael a quick glance, but he didn’t appear to have any more of a clue than her. She felt his fingers grasp the back of her shirt and inconspicuously pull her slightly behind him protectively.

“Can I help you?” Michael asked the woman. He didn’t show his nerves, but the tightness of his grip on her betrayed him.

Surprisingly, rather than directing her words at him, the woman spoke directly to Bin, eyes boring into her. The translation was perfect, as always. “I’m so sorry to disturb you, but could it be that you are… Bin? Bin Magnasen?”

Bin was so taken aback that she lost the ability to speak for a moment, which was enough for the woman’s expression to shift from hesitant hope to unease. When Bin wasn’t quite quick enough to respond, the woman seemed to change her mind and take a couple of steps back. “Ah… I apologize. It appears I’ve made a mistake. I didn’t mean to bother you,” she said and turned to her two friends with a tilt of her head. “It wasn’t her. Let’s go.”

“No, wait!” Bin piped up, voice coming out considerably louder than she had intended. “I’m her. I’m Bin Magnasen! But… who are you? Do I know you?”

The woman’s face brightened once more and she dashed back, going into a deep bow of respect. “No, we have never met, and I never thought we would. I only know of you from what I’ve heard.”

“What you’ve heard?” Michael repeated, some of the wariness she had felt in his hand now clearly entering his voice.

The woman’s head snapped up—although she maintained the bow—as if she had just now realized how this whole encounter might come off to them. “I’m not handling this very well, am I…? My name is Honda. Honda Kotoka. I spotted you and I couldn’t help but notice that you look exactly how I’ve heard the daughter of Eik Magnasen described in the stories.”

Stories? Was that what her dad had become? Stories and legends? But it wasn’t even a year and a half since he had been serving piping hot pancakes with melted chocolate for breakfast, only to be scolded by Mom for giving her and Goo sugar first thing in the morning.

Michael’s fingers relaxed notably, but inside, Bin was the tensest she had been during this entire encounter. “Eik is my dad,” she muttered mechanically.

At that, her two teammates joined Kotoka and bowed deeply, they weapons clinking metallically against the armor plates on their thighs and knees as they moved.

“Please let me express my gratitude for your father’s sacrifice. There is not a single person here in Fukuoka who is not aware of what he has done for the people of Earth. What he did, and what he had to pay, will be remembered forever. You have my word.”

The settlement had actually been established in the ruins of Fukuoka City in the Fukuoka prefecture on the southernmost of Japan’s four main islands, and they had naturally kept the name.

A few people nearby who had overheard the conversation stopped and mirrored the gesture, mumbling their own words of thanks, but most just wandered by, faces full of confusion.

“Thank you,” Bin managed to get out, eyes wide to keep the tears from flowing. She pulled on Michael’s sleeve as she started to walk away, offering an awkward bow of her own to Kotoka. The distance to the street corner felt kilometers longer than it actually was, and the Japanese didn’t look up from their bows until after Bin and Michael had turned down the other street.

After a couple dozen paces, Michael pulled her gently to a bench by the side of the road and sat her down. He knelt and pulled her into a hug. “What are you feeling?” he asked quietly into her ear.

She swallowed a sob before speaking. “Sad.”

He squeezed her a little tighter. “That’s okay, Binny. That’s okay. It’s okay to be sad. You’ve had a really terrible experience.”

She couldn’t hold it in anymore and cried into his shoulder. “I feel really lonely.”

“What makes you feel lonely?”

“I don’t know. I just feel like I’m the only one who can’t get over it. It feels like everyone else is just moving on with their lives, but only I can’t move on.”

“Have you talked to your mom about how you feel?” Michael asked, not letting go of the hug.

She shook her head, the movement wiping snot and tears into his shirt. “We talked about it a lot in the beginning, but the more time passed, the less we talk. Now we almost never talk about it.”

“You don’t talk about your dad?”

“No, we talk about him, but we don’t talk about him being dead, you know.” Just saying the words made it feel like ice-cold water was being flushed through her brain. She felt dizzy.

“Well, I can tell you who else have difficulty moving on.”

She sniffed. “Who?”

“Me. And I know for a fact that Heath and your Auntie Olivia can’t, because they’ve told me so. Robert has missed more days of work than I’ve ever seen him do. I saw Grandpa Rasmus cry in the kitchen last week when he thought he was alone. Goo speaks even less than he did before. Your mom probably thinks she’s protecting your feelings by hiding her own. Maybe it would be okay if you told her about your feelings. I’m sure she would be really happy that you want to talk to her.”

“You think?”

“Of course!” he said. She could hear the smile in his voice. “What parent wouldn’t? But for now, you can tell me.”

The tears returned.

“People just talk about my dad no matter where I go. I feel like I can’t escape. I know that he’s a big hero, but he’s also just my dad.”

“That’s the one thing he will always be. Even when you grow old and have kids and grandkids of your own, you will always have a dad named Eik.”

“But he won’t be here to be my dad.”

Michael squeezed her even harder than before. “No. No, he won’t.”
