We wait tensely for the array to decide whether it will continue to work, or whether it will instead throw a fit in some dramatic way.

A minute or so later, when nothing has exploded, imploded, cracked, snapped, or otherwise indicated that there is a significant issue here, Lizil breathes out an audible sigh of relief and releases her white-knuckle hold on the control box. The rest of us immediately relax – we trust the Master’s assessment.

Closing my eyes, I focus on what’s happening with the mana.

Immediately, I see a massive difference in the earth within the circle compared to the earth without. Inside, the mana is a variety of murky greens, browns, and the odd flicker of blue. Outside, it’s a sea of bright green – the plant network’s influence. Already, it’s trying to press inside the circle, but the array is operating as intended in that respect – any green mana that approaches within a short distance of the array is sucked into it.

I then examine the array more closely. Though the influence of the runes makes my mana sight go fuzzy – like I’m looking through thick, darkened glass – I am able to see the flow of mana around the circle. Interestingly, it goes anti-clockwise. Then again, I don’t know why I’m expecting it to go clockwise, especially when clocks aren’t even known in this world! I say to myself.

The Enchanters were obviously expecting that to be the direction, as the spiral itself is also anti-clockwise. When the mana reaches it, it’s pulled through the curves of the spiral, as expected. The box at the centre – now one of the only things visible above ground – is so thickly-clustered with runes that I can’t see anything inside it.

Opening my eyes, I step forward to stand next to Lizil.

“Is the array working as intended?” I ask her. I’m pretty sure it is, but the creator would know better than I, even with my mana sight.

She hesitates, then nods her head slowly.

“The mana flow is correct,” she relates briskly. “And the integrity of the condenser box is so far within expectations. However, time will tell whether it functions as intended. Condensing mana to this extent is not something I’ve ever done.”

“It’s not likely to explode, is it?” I ask with some concern. I recall clearly the way the Pure Energy stream, when blocked by an Energy Heart formed in the wrong place, exploded and created a tunnel that was so big Kalanthia wondered at first whether it had been caused by an elder dragon.

“It’s…not impossible,” Lizil admits with some reluctance. “However, we have put in several back-up arrays – one to vent the pressure if material integrity drops below a certain point, another to deflect the explosion upwards, a third to monitor both of those to ensure they remain active.

“Good.” While venting the pressure means losing whatever is built up, I’d rather that than the explosion destroying everything within a wide radius of itself. “How long do you think it will take for the condenser to create a natural Core?”

“That will depend entirely on the amount of mana absorbed and the efficiency of the transfer. We estimate anything from three days to three months,” Lizil replies.

I have to admit to some dissatisfaction for the upper end of the scale. Three days would be impressive; three months, though…. Then I remind myself that there is no guarantee it will work at all, or of the quality of the results even if it does. Anyway, the point is to ensure good growing conditions for our crops. The possibility of getting some Energy Hearts out of it is a bonus.

With those thoughts in mind, I paste a smile on my face that becomes increasingly genuine as I congratulate everyone on the feat of actually getting an experimental array working and, to all appearances, doing what it was intended to do.

Elarion’s Plant Mages and farmers soon sweep in under the watchful eyes of Lizil and her fellow Enchanters.

“Treat this field for a bi-tenday harvest,” I hear Elarion instructing the head farmer and a Plant Mage.

“Yes, my lord,” they acknowledge crisply. I nod silently in response too. Speeding up the wheat harvest to be done in five days rather than over a whole season is probably a good balance between speed and consequences for the land. Though it’s possible to get multiple harvests in a day, it’s very potion-intensive and damages the land. If we did it on a systematic basis, we’d end up with large tracts of land that were all but worthless for anything growing – with magic or naturally. As it is, the farmers have to rotate fields after a couple of harvests to give the land time to recover.

To the relief of all of us, the planting and initial enhancement goes well. I watch the systematic movement of mana with interest, a flicker of the sense of inspiration I had earlier returning. I think I’ll stay here for a bit longer – maybe if I get just a bit of peace and quiet, I might be able to finally crack the Skill I’ve never been able to learn.

The Enchanters and their apprentices spend a few more minutes taking readings and examining the array before all but one of the apprentices returns to the settlement, the one left behind to monitor the progress taking it with good humor.

The farmers and Plant Mages soon move on too. I see two of them going over to the small version of the massive plant monster that my companions and I fought to get through to Artio and the deris. They feed it with chunks of meat, tossing the food into its two flower-like mouths.

Suddenly reminded of my companion, I look around for the owlbear. After a moment, I see him beyond the newly-planted fields of wheat. He seems to be experimenting with his new ability to grow plants – he has several around him. I’m tempted to go over and find out exactly what he’s doing, but rein myself back – Plant-Shaping.

Sitting down at the edge of the array, I close my eyes.

“My lord?”

Alyna’s voice makes me jump half out of my skin. My eyes shoot open and I’m halfway to my feet before I get hold of myself.

“You are far too quiet on your feet,” I tell her, half-joking, but also half-serious. “I might have accidentally attacked you.” This forest is as dangerous as the one on the samurans’ world that I almost died in – many, many times – and my reactions have sharpened again accordingly.

“Sorry,” she replies, her eyes looking at her feet, tension in her shoulders.

“It’s fine,” I sigh. So much for time to myself. “What did you want to talk about?”

“If it’s a bad time…” she trails off.

“It’s fine,” I repeat, more firmly this time. “Alyna, look at me.” She does, though with some hesitancy. I’d hoped we were past this. “There we go. Now, what can I do for you?” A thought suddenly occurs and I quickly glance around to make sure no one is in earshot. “Did something happen that you need to report about?”

“No,” she quickly answers and I relax as I see the certainty in her expression. She shrugs. “Always people complainin’ sure, but now there’s enough food, the beasts aren’t attackin’, most people’re just glad fer a bit o’ peace. Lots o’ people’re happy the temples’re finally gettin’ set up.”

I nod – that was something Loran reported to me in our most recent meeting. I was firm in my decision that everyone should have a house of their own before the temples got set up, so the priests have mostly been operating out of people’s houses up until now. But now that that benchmark has been met, the Earth Mages and other builders are now at the service of the various priests to set up their own places of worship.

Personally, I’m ambivalent to the whole thing, but that experience with Binder indicated that gods here are a lot more active than back on Earth, and I recognise the importance of religion for morale. Fortunately, only one of the priests was annoyed at having to wait – the one for Justice, ironically. Apparently Justice doesn’t include social justice.

“Any complaints you’ve been hearing? Especially repeated ones?”

Alyna looks thoughtful.

“Biggest complaints now’re about not havin’ enough places te spend their money. No taverns, no whorehouses, no theatres. But that’s been gettin’ better recently with the food stalls that’re setting up an’ the games that’re happenin’ in the marketplaces each eve.”

“Well, if anyone wants to start a theatre troupe or tavern, they’d be welcome,” I comment. “If you hear of any who are interested, send them my way. We don’t have much alcohol right now, but it will come. Brothels, however, are something I’m less keen on,” I comment wryly.

In some ways, I’d prefer not to have taverns either – alcohol is definitely a drug of its own, and the abuse can cause all manner of problems. I know that first-hand. However, I’m not foolish enough to think that by preventing taverns from setting up, I’d prevent alcohol from being sold – the Prohibition in the US was evidence enough that it just drives the creation and sale underground. Between the two options, I’d prefer to be able to ensure that whatever alcohol is being sold is held to at least some standards.

At least the magical level of treatment available here means that the physical symptoms of alcohol abuse can be treated better. I’ll just have to make sure that the other issues with it are also dealt with.

Or delegate that to one of my stewards. That sounds like a good idea.

Alyna shrugs.

“Not officially,” she hedges, and my thoughts are drawn back to the topic at hand.

I raise my eyebrow at her.

“So what’s happening unofficially?” I press.

“There’s a group o’ women – most slaves – who’ve set up a…welcome house. Company for them that pay – drinks, singin’ that kind o’ thing.” She shrugs. “Though from the sounds comin’ out o’ the windows at night, there’s plenty o’ physical company too, if ye get me.” She sends me a sly look, then quickly sobers, perhaps fearing for my reaction.

My immediate desire is confusion. Why, when there are so many other things to do, are these women choosing to…sell themselves? Are they being forced? Because if so….

Alyna shrugs again when I ask her, though she eyes me a little warily, perhaps sensing the anger I’m doing my best to keep off my face.

“Haven’t heard o’ any pimp.”

“Then why…?” I trail off, deflating a bit.

“Pays well don’t it?” she replies pragmatically. “Without many ways te spend their money, lots o’ hunters an’ soldiers an’ crafters an’ sailors are willin’ to pay plenty fer a night o’ company – or a mark. An’ I guess they don’t have skills to do much else. Or it’s just work they’re used te doin’, an’ they don’t mind it so…”

She trails off, but I don’t mind – my thoughts in a whirl. If someone was forcing them, then I’d be sure to rain down fire on them. But if they’re choosing to do it themselves…isn’t that up to them?

I sigh. Another thorny dilemma.

Even back on Earth there'd never been a consensus on prostitution. Different countries took different approaches, but history seemed to show one thing consistently: like alcohol, it was always present in society – whether people approved of it or not. Much like alcohol, trying to ban it often just drove it underground and removed protections for the workers. And it’s not like the people here would understand my qualms – brothels are perfectly legal on Moriaxar. And common.

This case might take a bit more care, though, with the women involved mostly being slaves. I decide that I’ll personally speak to the…ladies of the ‘welcome house’ and ensure that they know what other options are available to them – that they don’t have to continue ‘doing what they know’ unless they actually want to. Otherwise, I’m reluctant to exercise the authority Moriax would say I have over those whose contracts I hold. I certainly could regulate their activities – and will if they’re breaking the settlement laws – but I don’t want to if I can avoid it.

Arguably, they are providing a service to the settlement, I think with some tired amusement. Loneliness is a disease of its own.

Given my position on alcohol, and Moriax’s approach to prostitution in general, the best option is probably to keep it legal as long as there’s no coercion or force involved. Though ensuring that without a Justicar might be a challenge.

“Alright, thanks for letting me know,” I tell Alyna finally, trying to keep my frustration out of my voice. It’s not her fault that this has opened a can of worms. “Was there anything else?”

She fidgets, and looks away.

“What is it?” I ask, doing my best to stay patient. “I’m not going to shoot the messenger even if it’s bad news,” I continue, trying to interject a little levity into the conversation that’s got way too heavy.

“It’s…not about the settlement,” she finally mutters to her feet, though I can see her peeping at me warily through her hair.

“Do you have a problem?” I ask, becoming more serious. “We might no longer have a Bond, but I’ll always do my best to help you if you need it, Alyna.”

“That is the problem,” she mutters.

“What?” I frown.

“That we don’t have a Bond.”

I stare at her blankly for a long moment.

“Are you saying…that you want a Bond again?”

She nods.

It’s hard to believe. Though Alyna didn’t fight me much when I invoked the Battle of Wills the first time, I sensed that that was mostly her characteristic pragmatism. She knew she couldn’t win – both literally, and because even if she somehow broke free in the Battle of Wills, she was still a slave, my slave. But she certainly hadn’t been enthusiastic about it. Yet now, she’s asking for it?

“Why?”

“Because…” she hesitates, and I know her well enough to see that she changes what she was going to say for something different. “It’s useful. Te be able te contact others when needed.”

“Alyna,” I chide. She’s being truthful, but not fully.

Her eyes dart to mine and then fall again at the warning in them.

“It’s quiet, is all,” she admits. “Got used te havin’ all them voices in my head. Te knowin’ that I could call fer help if needed. Now? There’s silence.”

“The Bond isn’t supposed to be a crutch,” I remind her quietly.

“I know that!” she fires back, glaring at me, then looking away. “It’s just…I felt I was part o’ somethin’...special. An’ now I’m not. I’m on the outside. Alone. Again.”

I think over it for a moment, but the answer’s clear.

“Alyna,” I say gently, then wait until she looks back at me to continue. “I’d be happy to have you back in the pack, if that’s what you actually want – and for more than just security.” After all, though her motivations are self-serving, she was a perfectly good pack member the last time. I trust that it will be the same this time.

“It is,” she attests, certainty in her voice.

I smile, then reach out to her with a Tame offer. She’s so guarded – I wonder how much she will actually trust me with.
