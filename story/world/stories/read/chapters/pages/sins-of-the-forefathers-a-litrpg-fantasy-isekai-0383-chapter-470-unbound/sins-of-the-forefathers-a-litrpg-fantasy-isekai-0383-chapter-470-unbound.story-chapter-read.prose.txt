With a palpable sense of disdain in my heart, I curled the fingers of my left hand into a claw and then raised it above my head.

Across the field of battle from me, the maddened, fearless screams of the very last living Korvok abruptly ceased as his head was removed from his shoulders. With another twitch of my fingers, the three thorned vines that had facilitated the euthanasia dissipated into sparks: two on his legs to hold him in place, and the one looped around his neck.

I relaxed my hold on Grasp of Calithorne with the end of the battle, as my soldiers began to collect themselves. I cast my eyes about as Olag’s mercenary knights wandered across the plaza, silencing those Korvoks that had only been wounded instead of slain. I ignored the dying wheezes of the madmen as they were culled, stepping around the pools of blood that the disgusting creatures had reduced the Varlayans into.

Instead, I gazed down at the enormous sinkhole in the middle of the courtyard.

That…looked pretty deep. I couldn’t see the bottom of it, in fact. But I could make out the general construction. It looked to have been dug up from inside, created to include tiers that rickety-looking ladders had been laid upon. Taking a guess, I think the Korvoks had undermined the Hold, approaching from underground, and then dug upwards, laying ladders as they ascended. And then, once everything had been in place…they’d breached the surface, and come pouring out like they were nothing more than bloodthirsty ants. Or Antium, I suppose.

I wonder how Venex was doing? Assuming, of course, that the Antium ronin had survived the Skyfall with Grey, that is.

I grimaced and shook my head to clear it of such thoughts, as Olag and Walter wandered up to join me in my observation of the breech. As front-line, melee combatants, both were covered in far more viscera than I was. With Grasp of Calithorne, I had rapidly discovered that it required a great deal of concentration on not just my part, but on all of my Lattices, if I hoped to control so many thorned vines at once. It was worth it, though, considering just how much of a deciding factor I truly became in a large-scale battle. The Skill was just so versatile. Not only could I fight and kill with the vines, but I could occupy enemies so others could do it. There wasn’t exactly a lot someone could do to stop a Dwarven axe speeding toward their face if they were wrestling with a vine that was trying to steal yours. While also tangling around your feet, of course.

Couldn’t have them running away. Not after what they had done.

Olag seemed to have the same thought I had running through my mind. There was a look on his blood-streaked, tattooed face as he stared down at the sinkhole below us. “Interesting…”

We shared a look of unspoken agreement, while Walter glanced up from where he was cleaning his claymore. “What is? Is there something wrong with the hole?”

When Olag raised an eyebrow at me, I just shrugged and nodded. It was my responsibility to educate the young man when it came to the art of war. “Since this is very likely where the Korvoks originated,” I explained patiently. “This hole is actually going to be very useful to us in the future. You see, Walter, we were…concerned about just how we were going to get within the confines of Karag Korvok once we had arranged matters with the Varlayans. From what Olag has told me, there very likely is a connecting tunnel in the Long Road between Karag’s Varla and Korvok, considering they weren’t always flesh-mad cannibals…”

“But it’s very unlikely that my oaf of a cousin would have cleared that path, like he did with the one that led us here,” Olag spat off to the side. “No doubt the Hold very thoroughly destroyed those tunnels. It would have been a time-consuming task to clear them. But with this…”

Walter’s eyes finally lit up in comprehension. “We can just use their own tunnel against them! This would take us right into their mountain!” He paused, visibly torn for a number of reasons. “But…why aren’t we just taking it straight there, then? This is why we’re here…right?”

The look that Olag shot me then was…complicated, as he waited for my answer. Part warning, part resignation, and part pleading, I could read what he was telling me with his face like a book.

He needn’t have worried.

“Normally, I would,” I smiled slightly at the anxiety spike that caused in the Dwarf. “But I’m not willing to leave the Varlayans to their fate, especially not when they’re being assaulted by a force that would be just as willing to tear our throats out as theirs. If they lose, and we leave our backs unguarded to the Korvoks trying to murder this Hold, they’ll just sink a dagger into it.”

Which was the practical reason, of course. But another one, that spoke to the more idealistic side of me, just wanted to save the poor people of this Hold from the murderers on their doorstep.

At least, those who might have survived till now.

An Order meant to help, and not to hurt.

No, the Korvoks had to die before we made for their Hold, and the Spirit trapped beneath it.

“Speaking of, Walter?”

My Squire straightened up at my address. “Yes, Sir Nate?”

“I’m going to leave twenty men here with you, and I want you to watch this sinkhole,” I said. “I’ve already signaled Bleddyn to come to this location via his Sprite, so his squad should reinforce you soon. But we’re moving out. Our business with the Korvoks isn’t finished. Hopefully, we won’t be long.”

Walter visibly hesitated for a moment at the unexpected position of command I had just hoisted onto his young shoulders. But to my quiet pride, he firmed almost immediately. “Of course. I won’t let you down.”

I reached out to lay a hand on his shoulder, squeeze once, and smiled at him. “I know you won’t. Olag, gather the men. We’re going.”

………………………

The group of Korvoks we encountered at the plaza might have been the largest one we stumbled across…but it was far from the last. For whatever reason, there were tons of roving packs of the barbarians as we pushed deeper into the heart of Karag Varla. To my frustration, those were the only Dwarves we had found in our search for the Hagia Dynamis. There wasn’t a single living Varlayan to be found anywhere in this city.

Which struck me as deeply suspicious. It just didn’t make any damn sense that we didn’t find a single terrified civilian, huddling in the back of any of the local centers we searched, as Olag and I explored the broken Hold of Karag Varla. I could tell I wasn’t the only one getting frustrated, too. Endlessly stumbling on the casual atrocities that the Korvoks carried out thoughtlessly on these people…I was getting deeply sick of it, and the men were as well.

We made sure to take our frustration out on any Korvok we found. Most of them seemed to function in packs, like they were some kind of wild animal instead of heavily mutilated Dwarves. Compared to the discipline of the new Herztalian expedition, combined with our classer levels of strength, there just wasn’t any question as to their fate.

We put them down like the mad dogs they were, and we did so by the dozen. They had no hope against us, not when a force of hundreds had fallen to our blades without anything more than a few injuries. And even then, those had been quickly Healed up by Renauld.

It made me wonder. Had it just been the element of surprise that had allowed the Korvoks to so easily overwhelm the Varlayans? I refused to believe that the vaunted Legions of the ancient Hold of Varla had fallen so easily to the chaff that we were funning into. They were just so…so pitifully weak. I’m not sure I would have put any of the Korvoks we’d cut down past the Level of fifty. At the most.

They were all too insane to organize a true fight against us. It was like something had been broken in them, leaving the Korvoks with little actual will.

No thoughts.

No desires.

Only a ravening thirst for blood and flesh.

There wasn’t a professional force on Vereden that would have been overwhelmed by just those things.

It made me suspicious. There must be more to the Korvoks than just this. But if there was…

Where the Hell were they?

………………………….

On one hand, we did eventually find the Hagia Dynamis.

That was good, because from the feel of my blood sense, there were still people holed up in there. Hopefully, non-insane Dwarven hearts.

That was the only good part.

On the other hand, I also discovered just what had allowed the Korvoks to so easily conquer Karag Varla.

My two platoons almost blundered into the area where we found the Hagia Dynamis. Maybe it was a bit arrogant to do so, but I had started to mentally dismiss the Korvoks as an appreciable threat to us. We had yet to encounter anything other than mindless, bloodthirsty berserkers that simply ran screaming at us. They were a non-threat, really.

What was pounding on the door of just another one of the domed, marble buildings here in Karag Varla was not one of the Korvoks we had slaughtered by the dozen. They were larger, for one, startlingly so for a Dwarf. But it was hard to even call them a Dwarf anymore; they were so…so different. I would almost call the thing mutated.

Azarus was what I thought of as the upper limit of Dwarven kind. I had never once encountered another Dwarf larger than him, and his head reached up to about shoulder height on me.

This thing was a little taller than me, and yet it still had Dwarven proportions. It must have been six feet tall at a minimum, and it looked to have been halfway skinned. The entire right side of its bare torso had been flensed of all the flesh that would have normally been present, right down the central line of its chest, and I do mean all of it.

Including the skull.

What was left behind…I hesitated to call it musculature, because that wasn’t quite right. It had the basic look of what clean, skinless muscle should appear as, from what I had seen of anatomy sculptures from back on Earth. Only…it wasn’t wet, as if blood was constantly welling to the surface, like I would have expected flayed muscle to do. Instead, it was almost waxy-looking, as if it had been preserved in some way. Hardened, perhaps, in a manner that caused it to grow several shades darker, to an almost maroon. It bulged and contorted oddly as I watched the…thing bang its monstrously sized fists against the door that I could feel those quickened, frightened heartbeats huddling within. It was only made worse by one of the largest concentrations of regular Korvoks we had seen, huddling behind the broad back of the mutant. Easily over a hundred of them, swaying in place in an oddly docile manner for them.

Then…

The banging stopped, and the creature twisted its head to stare right at our group, standing at the mouth of this street.

And I got a good look at its face. I immediately tensed. Not because it had all of the other features that the regular Korvoks had, on top of its own mutilations. No eyelids, no lips, no nose, no ears. Not because of any of that, no matter how horrifying it was.

But because of the intelligence I saw within its gaze.

Then the thing opened its mouth and spoke. “More of the Hidebound? And visitors, at that. Not one of you are of these Apostates. No…you’re from much, much farther off, aren’t you? Humans and Velancians…how interesting.”

I violently repressed a shiver at this thing's voice. The lack of lips caused a very noticeable lisp, but that didn’t make it any less menacing. No, on the contrary, it made this…monster more intimidating.

Needless to say, I didn’t like it.

“Who are you?” I asked, narrowing my eyes and projecting my voice. I had no interest in actually approaching this…thing. Not unless it was with a blade in hand. “What are you doing in this Hold? What do you want?”

“Want?” The thing mused, almost philosophically in tone. “What an odd question. But perhaps I shouldn’t expect anything more from one of the Hidebound. I want what all true Karvokar desire…and that is Truth. Complete and total freedom from all that is false in this world. I wish to be truly unbound…and wish for all of those that are bound by the falsity of skin to be granted this blessing as well. Ah…what a glorious world that would be…”

I shifted my eyes over to Olag, a desperate question in my eyes, as the ‘Karvokar’ waxed poetically across the lane from us. From the wide, baffled gaze he gave me, the Florens Dwarf didn’t have a clue what was going on either.

Wonderful.

“But that is something for another time,” It said, locking its gaze with mine. I think it would have been smiling if it still had the lips to do so. Instead, it just clacked its teeth together twice. “You’ll understand as soon as you’re brought before a Skarnweaver. And then, once you’ve come to realize the Truth…you can tell us what you’re doing here, Human.”

I tightened my grip on my staff. “No. I don’t think I’ll be going anywhere. You have one chance to retreat, abomination. I have business in that building.”

The thing just laughed at me. “Is that so? So do I, Hidebound. Very well. The Rendarchs teach that the Hidebound sometimes need a forceful hand to become enlightened. Yours will be firmer than most.” It lifted its skinned, deformed right hand…

And snapped its fingers.

“Go, Skarned. Bring me these Hidebound.”
