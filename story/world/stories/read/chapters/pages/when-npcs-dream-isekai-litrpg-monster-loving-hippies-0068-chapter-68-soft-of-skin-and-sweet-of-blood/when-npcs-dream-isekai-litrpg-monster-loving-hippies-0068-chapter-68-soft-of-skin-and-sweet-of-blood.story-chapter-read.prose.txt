“Anna,” I called out, blinking slowly awake from a good night’s rest.

The door to my room opened immediately, and my sweet Anna stepped in, wearing a simple white gown and a red scarf wrapped continuously around her neck.

“Good morning, Zylil,” she smiled at me warmly. “Have you slept well?”

“Most excellently, thank you,” I answered, stretching slightly.

“Should we begin with the grooming?” she asked.

“Oh, yes, definitely,” I answered. I liked Anna’s grooming very much; she had a touch unlike any other.

Anna lifted me gently from my bed and carried me to the dressing table. She placed me upon her lap, retrieved a fine-bristle hairbrush from the table drawer, and ever so gently began to stroke my fur. Anna knew exactly how I liked it—firm enough to untangle, but soft enough to soothe.

“Now the tail,” she said softly.

I gladly raised it for her, extending the appendage so she could run the brush through the long, silky strands until they shimmered.

Then she turned me onto my stomach and brought out a different brush, this one smaller and softer, specifically suited for the delicate membranes of my wings. I closed my eyes and purred deep in my chest. I loved it when she stroked my wings… just like that…

When she was done, and I was looking pristine, I looked up at her. “May I feed now?”

Anna smiled, and slowly removed her scarf, revealing her white, soft, long neck, marred only by a constellation of small red dots. She then took me gently in her hands and raised me to her neck.

I took a deep smell first, inhaling her scent, and a smile spread across my face. Naked Nula were soft of skin and sweet of blood. But Anna’s blood... hers was the sweetest of them all.

I took a small nib, making the smallest of holes with only the very tip of my sharp teeth. I began to suck slowly at the thin stream of blood. It was nothing that would get me full, of course; this was just for the pleasure of it. I closed my eyes, taking it slowly, savoring the metallic, rich taste. This was the best…

“Now, go clean the neck wound,” I told her after I was done. “Don’t forget to use the ointment for disinfection. Then return the scarf. After that, come and play the flute for me.”

Anna finished cleaning herself at the basin, then she placed me on a soft, luxurious cushion. She picked up her instrument—a tube-like music instrument native to the Naked Nula—and played a nice, calming melody.

“That is a new melody,” I remarked, “I have never heard it.”

“Yes,” Anna smiled, lowering the instrument. “I wrote it just yesterday. I meant for it to be calm, but to tell a story, too.”

“What story?”

“It is about the feeling of sitting by a warm hearth,” she said, her eyes distant, “watching the cold rain lash against the windowpane, knowing the storm cannot touch you.”

“Do you like it?” she asked.

“Yes, very much so,” I said.

Suddenly, the door to my room burst open. It was an NPC bird-folk servant, looking flustered.

“Nurr asks you to come to his study,” he said, panting slightly. “He said it is a matter of great urgency.”

It was always a great urgency with him. I sighed heavily; it looked like the fun part of the day was over.

“Carry me,” I said, turning to Anna.

She picked me up and began the long walk across the palace to Nurr’s study. There were many staircases, endless corridors, and large halls to cross. Flying was an exhausting matter for my kind; if I flew all the way there, I would have no air left in me to breathe upon arrival. Walking was laughable; we couldn’t walk past a single stair with our small, legless feet, as this architecture was designed for large bipedal creatures.

When we finally got there, I saw Nurr buried in his papers behind a large wooden table, flanked by two controlled bird-folk players.

“Close the door,” he said, not even giving me a glance.

“It is awfully stuffy in here,” I remarked, sniffing the stale air. “You ought to air the place out a bit.”

He ignored me.

“We are going to pull out resources from the Western faction,” he said in a boring, monotone voice. “We will concentrate on putting more pressure on Ire-Ish.”

“But we just received information about important research facility,” I said, my voice rising. “The Order of the Phoenix is building something new. This must be addressed.”

“We are at a stalemate with the Order,” Nurr explained, using slow speech as if he were talking to a child. “While Ire-Ish is a kingdom ripe for the taking. After the conquest is done and settled, we can then allocate resources to push against the Order.”

“There is no stalemate!” I retorted. “I am in charge of spies and intel. They are putting their forces in strategic places; they are planning an attack. And as I said, they are... excited about this new device they are making.”

“What new device?” he asked, giving me a skeptical look.

“It’s a gas mask, we think,” I said.

“You think…”

“Yes,” I repeated firmly. “We should get more spies for more intel, or go right through and build a special assault force. If they have effective gas masks, all our advantages from the spore bombs become null…”

“Gas masks are old-world technology,” he said dismissively, waving a tail. “So I don’t want to hear more of that nonsense. We allocate resources to infiltrate the Ire-Ish palace and get forces ready for a direct assault on Tnu-Sha. We will take advantage of the Order’s absence in the area.”

I looked at Nurr with cold eyes.

We were supposed to lead together. That was what had been established in the beginning, when all the agents were scattered and lost. We each rounded up about half of the agents on the continent and decided to collaborate as equals. But at some point along the line, it appeared that Nurr thought of me as an equal of a lesser sort.

“Zylil,” he gave me a sharp look, “have you located the Storm-crows gas mage and the NPC Lori yet?”

“They seem to have escaped. But we are still looking.”

“They offed Hash,” said Nurr, shaking his head. “If I remember correctly, this is the same gas mage that killed Uul. We cannot just let Naked Nula run around picking us off. Nemm said they are injured and should still be in Raz-Rash. You must find them and kill them. This is your responsibility.”

“Yes, we will try.”

Nurr seemed to return to his papers, and it looked like this meeting was blissfully over. But as I turned to leave, Nurr spoke again.

“Oh, and one more thing.” He gave Anna a long, measured look of disgust. “People are talking. You are to cease with your… perversion. You are fooling no one with that scarf.”

It was indeed considered a bad habit, or even a perversion by some, to feed upon a bipedal intelligent creature—at least, back in our old world it was. But I didn’t give a shit, and Nurr was making my blood boil.

“Yes, sire,” I said with a bright smile on my face.

Nurr gave me a strange look, then went back to his reading.

We walked out of the room, and the moment the latch clicked, I signaled Anna.

She nodded happily, pulled a large, nut-like object from her pocket, cracked the seal, threw it back into Nurr’s room, and immediately slammed the door shut.

We heard a distinct CRACK sound, a gasp, and then coughs—endless, horrid, wet coughs.

He really ought to have aired the room a bit.

We saw white smoke seeping beneath the door, curling like malicious snakes along the floorboards.

Oops, I think we overdid it. Better we leave before the seep beneath the door poisons us too.

“Anna,” I said.

“Yes, Zylil?”

“Go search for any agents and players in the palace,” I commanded. “Get them in the main hall. We have an announcement to make. Change in leadership, and all that.”

“Sure,” said Anna. “Should I first go outside and throw a rock at Nurr’s study window? You know, so we do not poison half the palace?”

“Uh, yes, good thinking.”

Anna was my lady-in-waiting, my tail-maiden, my partner. She was extremely intelligent, and I could trust her with everything. She was beautiful and sweet, too.

We gathered all twenty agents who weren’t currently on any mission and were present in the palace, along with some of the more important players. When they were all present, I flew in circles around the room, beaming.

Flying was tiring, but it was necessary for making an impression.

“I am terribly sorry to inform you…” I started, a smile spreading wide across my face. “There was a horrible accident! A death spore device exploded in Nurr’s room! It was a very accidental event! And he is so very much, completely, utterly dead!”

I let that sink in for a moment, enjoying the silence. Then I added:

“So you see?” I did another flip in the air. “Now, I, Zylil, am your only leader!”

I then landed gracefully on Anna’s head.

“And I may do things a little differently,” With a quick tug of my tail, I let her scarf fall, exposing the red dots on her neck.

“So, if anyone has a problem with how I do things,” I said, my voice dropping an octave, “horrible accidents might happen to them, too.”

I let that one sink in as well.

“Other than that,” I continued brightly, “not much changes, really. Just that we stop the Ire-Ish advancement and conquest, and move most resources to the west to fight the Order of the Phoenix—our most powerful and dangerous enemy.”

I looked at their faces. At least the agents among them showed quick understanding.

“Rel,” I turned to one of the smaller pink agents. “That means all your units come back here. Wait for orders.”

“Yes, Zylil,” he responded immediately.

“Till,” I turned to another agent. “All spies and intel, bribes and what have you—move them now to the Lithurion and Aldioli kingdoms.”

He nodded.

“Shenn,” I added, “All scorpion-cat tribes, bring them close by. Keep them ready, on standby.”

“Vii,” I turned to her. “Your… uh, what exactly were you doing?”

“Managing player control,” Vii said. “Keeping records of all controlled players, acting as help, and training new agents in player control. As well as handling players controlled by deceased agents. Like, um… Nurr. For example.”

“Oh, okay, sounds important. Keep doing that,” I said.

“Te, Kamynathur, Rejish,” I turned to an agent and two high-ranking bird players I recognized. “You are in cloth management and ship construction, right?”

“Yes,” Rejish replied.

“You are to give me a daily report about ship building progress and cloth stock.”

I didn’t have any more ideas at the moment; I knew more or less what everyone else’s work was. I think this concluded the main issues regarding the changes.

“Everyone else,” I addressed the room at large, “Anna here is my tail and mouth. I will pass orders through her, and you do exactly as she says.”

They all nodded.

“Keep working hard, and let us win the war and bring our masters here as soon as possible!”

The agents nodded again with seriousness, eyes bright. This was truly the most important thing in the world to them.

I dropped into Anna’s embrace. As she turned to carry me out, I looked back over her shoulder. “Oh, and Nemm,” I called out to the gas mage agent among the crowd. “Come to my room. I need to talk to you.”

“Yes, Zylil,” he said.

I think the change of leadership wasn’t hard for them. It wasn’t like Nurr was an inspiring leader admired by all. Not that I was, either.

The agents' main objective was to bring the Bamboo back ASAP. they were okay with any leader who was rational enough and looked like they knew what they were doing.

But they did want a leader, as they knew by heart that without being an organized group, our chances of accomplishing our objective were slim.

And I was that. It looked like I knew what I was doing… maybe I did, too, to some extent.

We were back in my room with Nemm tugging along, Anna shut the door.

“Nemm,” I turned to him. “You were close to Nurr.”

“Very much so,” he said. “Not his second, but… dare I say, I had his trust.”

“Good,” I said. “How do you feel… about this sudden tragedy and the… ah, Management transition?”

Nemm just gave a small tail rotation, a gesture that said he didn’t care much.

“I think your priorities are much more… educated,” he said. “Yes, you may be better suited than Nurr.”

I didn’t get any flattery from his tone. He sounded honest, so I was happy for the compliment.

“Yes, I will do my honest best,” I said. “But I must confess, me and Nurr didn’t always see eye to eye. And even though we were meant to rule together, I fear that he sometimes… neglected to share information that he felt would be of uninterest to me.”

Nemm didn’t responded, so I gave him a pointed look. “You could start about now. Tell me fucking everything.”

“Of course,” he said. “Not that he told me everything, but as he felt the Order of the Phoenix was on some level deterred, he wanted to complete the conquest of Ire-Ish. This was top priority for him.”

“That I already know.”

“He was very interested in the Aldioli group, the Stormcrows.” He continued, “He tried to invest resources in data on them, tried to find everything about them, almost to a religious length. As he heard from me about the Geshem and Lori assault last night, he wanted to catch them. He lost interest in anything else.”

“Well, you were at the fight,” I said. “They managed to kill one of our strongest fighters. Tell me about it.”

“Oh, it is that imbecile Hash,” Nemm said in annoyance. “‘The warrior code takes precedence…’” he imitated him in a mocking tone. “What a fool! They were in our town! We could have had them surrounded in minutes!”

Nemm looked really worked up by the memory of it.

“I barely made it out alive, too,” he said. “But I must say, Hash fought like a demon. They looked equal, him and Lori, both demons. I confess, if Hash wasn’t there, they would have killed us easily, calling backup or not.”

“Shooya was there too, right?” I asked.

“Yes,” he said. “She didn’t participate much in the fight but…” he felt his cheek with his tail, wincing. “Hit me with a rock, she did. hurt too.”

I smiled at that. I had met Shooya for a short time before, when she was still with Uul.

“Lori got injured in the fight, correct?”

“Yes, badly,” he said. “She was all but dead. She had more cuts than skin. By now, Best case scenario, she already died from her injuries, but even if not, she couldn’t be walking around anytime soon. Unless some miracle is involved, or magic unknown to me.”

“I see,” I said.

Geshem, Lori, and Shooya were a risk; they could make trouble, burn cloth, and kill agents. But as they were injured, they would want to go back to Kamatouu. They were probably hiding, too. It wasn’t an immediate risk, and I wanted to invest all my resources against the Order of the Phoenix.

“They are not of imminent importance,” I said. “I will have some NPC soldiers keep looking for them, but not agents or controlled players. All our resources go to the west.”

“Of course,” Nemm said. “But you might want to know, Nurr discovered something else of importance from the Stormcrows.”

“I am not of a patient personality,” I said sharply, nudging him to get to the point.

“They discovered a book…” started Nemm, “A very special one.”

“With special magic diagrams, you mean?” I asked.

“No magic,” he said. “It is about the System. About the world we are in. Nurr wrote about it to the Bamboo, and they were heavily interested. They ordered him to get his hands on it.”

“Oh…” This baffled me. “But we are in a war. And when the Bamboo arrive, the war ends. There surely is no urgency to this. When the Bamboo are here, they will have all the time to research this.”

Nemm looked very troubled by what I said. “But… this was an order from the masters,” he said. “We must do what we can to accomplish the new objective.”

Oh yes, for a moment there I forgot we were controlled puppets.

“Of course,” I said quickly. “What I meant was, this effort will be simultaneous to the war effort, as our objective to bring the Bamboo with the airship is our main objective!”

“Of course,” he said.

Interesting. So the Bamboo have something else they are fixated on. Very interesting.

“Thank you, Nemm,” I said, dismissing him. “If you have other information that you may feel is remotely of interest to me, don’t be shy, do share.”

“I will,” he said, and left.

I was back alone in my room with Anna.

May I be so bold as to hope to have another pleasurable moment in what looked like one of the more annoying and busy of days?

I gave Anna a look, and she smiled lovingly at me.

“Should I prepare a bath, Zylil?” she asked. “Murders and revolutions may leave you with a need for relaxation of a sort.”

Oh, Anna knew me so well! I wanted to kiss her… or suck her blood.

“Yes please,” I said. “And a massage after?”

“Of course,” Anna said.

Then the door opened again, and a controlled bird player hurried inside without knocking.

Damn, being a leader seemed like an awful lot of work suddenly. I hoped I wouldn’t regret it too much.

“Zylil, I wanted to talk to you.” It was Ankher’salm, our spore bomb specialist.

“Come in again without knocking and I’ll bite half of your neck off,” I snapped.

“Oh, I’m terribly sorry,” he was taken aback for a moment, but recovered quickly. “I am not sure how often Nurr found the time to update you on a matter of—”

“Nurr told me shit,” I said. “You will update me on everything. And don’t miss a single detail.”

“So, the new spore device shipment from two days ago?” he asked.

“No!” I got angry.

That Nurr, hiding spore device shipments from me! I was so glad I offed him!

“You might want to see it,” He was excited about it, very much so. “It is something else, really!”

“But do I really must come?” I asked.

He gave me a lost look.

“Fine,” I groaned.

Anna took me in her hands and walked after Ankher’salm. There were two soldier NPCs waiting outside, and they tagged along too. We walked to the palace garden, to an area that looked like a training ground for spore devices.

He walked to a small storage house and brought out some small nuts, triangular in shape, and some arrows.

“Look how small they are!” He basically shoved the thing in my face. “And look, they are meant to be mounted as arrowheads!”

He pointed and showed me that the arrow he brought had the triangle nut as its tip.

“So instead of dropping them from the birds…” I started.

“No, no, that is not the point,” He smiled happily now, all his feathers vibrating in excitement. “I will show you.”

He handed the arrows to one of the NPCs and walked some distance away.

“I happen to know some basic positive air pressure magic,” he said, and drew a circle in the dirt. “Now, any spore bomb outside my bubble won’t harm me.”

“Okay,” I said, not getting what he was up to.

“Now,” he continued, “Ruseth, Berington,” He called the NPCs. “Aim at me!”

Immediately, the NPCs raised their bows and drew the nut-headed arrows, aiming directly at him.

Wait, wouldn't he… die or something?

“Now, shoot!” he squeaked happily.

They immediately released the arrows. They hit the ground near his legs, and immediately white smoke spread. While some of the smoke reached the edge of the bubble and dispersed, most of the white payload filled the sphere completely.

Seconds later, Ankher’salm dropped to the floor.

“Did he just kill himself to show off?” I asked Anna.

Anna shrugged her shoulders.

But a few moments later, he seemed to awake. He looked confused and disoriented, but after some more moments, he came to his senses.

“Did you see? Did you see?”

I gave him a horrid look.

“Oh, the spore?” he said, brushing himself off. “I forgot to tell you, the arrowhead devices are only for sleep-inducing spores. Death spores need some critical mass to be effective.”

I still thought of him as an idiot.

“But the Bamboo ship us mountains of these little things,” he said. “We could shoot volley after volley, make all the positive pressure mages fall asleep, and then attack, or drop death spores from above! It is a game-changing tactic for sure!”

It was huge. I could see that as well, but…

“Unless they have anti-spore gas masks,” I said slowly.

“A what?” Ankher’salm’s smile disappeared. This clearly shook him.

“Masks,” I repeated.

“Oh no,” he said, his feathers drooping. “But it won’t do! We will lose our edge; we will lose all of our edges! It will be a disaster!”

Yes, the silly bird-folk was right on that. It wouldn't do. We must find the location of that gas mask research facility and destroy it. I knew the Order was readying their forces for an attack, and I was sure they were waiting for those gas masks for the assault.

As things looked right now, it was probably a matter of a week, a week and a half at most.

I went back to my room with Anna, and she immediately started to prepare the bath.

“We need to destroy the gas mask facility,” I said. “We should do as they do—make a small specialized elite force to infiltrate and do a surgical strike.”

The bath was ready, and Anna carefully laid me in the steaming water.

“But you don’t know its location,” pointed out Anna.

“I will put all our resources into it,” I said, sinking into the warmth. “Move spies, bribes, offer prize money for information.”

“It won’t do,” said Anna, as she soaped me gently, her fingers working through my fur with practiced ease. “It could take weeks.”

“So what would you have me do?” I asked. “Attack with all our forces now, before the gas masks are ready?”

“That won’t do either,” Anna smiled mysteriously.

I gave her a look; I felt like she was going somewhere with this.

“You need to send me,” she said simply. “You know I can do it.”

“No!” I said without a thought. “I need you here, and… you are not suited for this type of work.”

Anna lifted me from the bath and began to dry me with a plush towel.

“Yes, I am suited,” she said firmly, her eyes locking onto mine. “I am the only one. I am your trump card, Zylil. And now you need to use me.”

Anna was special. Anna was my trump card. And the reason was simple.

Anna wasn’t controlled. She was loyal to me of her own free will.

Of all the players on the continent, Anna was the only one who would side with an agent without being controlled or bribed.

The strategy with Anna would be simple. She would go with an agent and a small NPC force on an apparent mission, deep in Lithurion. They would encounter the Order of the Phoenix, who would chase off the agent and the NPCs, and ‘rescue’ Anna.

Their Bug-folk would detect control spores on her—I would put some on her in advance. They would put her in the five-day isolation, as their procedure dictated for controlled individuals. Then she would be control-spore free, but of course, ever so loyal to me.

Yes, Anna was indeed perfect for the job.

When I arrived here, I had a rough landing. The seagull I was riding was barely conscious from the long trip and crashed on the land hard. I was injured and lost consciousness, only to wake up in Anna’s house.

She didn’t know what I was, but she guessed. She treated me anyway.

And I did.
