Back in Abyssos, Magda shaded her eyes with a palm, scanning the endless carpet of snow for any glint of a Grand Abyssal Pin. It had been hours since they'd split from the main group to comb their quadrant, but aside from the two grand pins Aodhán had found—judging from the leaderboard—no one had located the third yet.

And her partner was getting restless.

Borevin stood beside her, arms crossed as she scowled at the white expanse, frustration and annoyance evident in her features.

"How many hours has it been now?" she demanded, her tone clipped.

Magda sighed. This was the fifth time Borevin had asked her that question in the last half hour, and despite her deep respect for the woman, her patience was fraying.

Pasting a smile on her face, she glanced at the clock ticking at the corner of her vision—a clock Borevin also had—and muttered, "Just a bit above five hours."

Borevin made a sound between disgust and annoyance, turning to glance sharply at her as if the loss of time was somehow her fault. "And how much time is a bit?"

Magda exhaled deeply. "Borevin, this isn't necessary—"

"It is if our search for the last five hours has been practically futile." Borevin cut her off, her tone sharp enough to slice stone. "Listen to yourself, Magda. We've been scouring this quadrant for five hours with absolutely nothing to show for it. This is a waste of time."

Magda glared at the woman, battling the urge to lash out at her. No one had forced her on this mission, after all. Instead, Magda forced down the urge and said through gritted teeth, "We still have a stretch of land to cover. And until we scour it, we cannot give up. We may just get lucky and—"

"And find nothing," Borevin drawled scornfully, gesturing behind her. "Look at all the miles we've covered."

Magda looked back and grimaced. Borevin wasn't exaggerating—miles upon miles of snow had been transmuted into a sea of verdant grass that stretched farther than the eye could see.

Nevertheless, the fact that they had covered so much ground without result didn't mean they could simply give up. There couldn't be more than a few miles left in the quadrant. The grand abyssal pin could literally be right in front of them. And the faster they got to searching for it, the faster they could be done with this whole mission.

Whispering a quick prayer to Tihamun for patience, Magda said evenly, "I understand your frustration, Borevin. I really do. But we only have a few miles left. We can't go back without searching the remaining miles first."

"Ugh!" Borevin threw her hands out in sheer frustration, a wave of prismatic energy exploding from her palms to wash over the frozen expanse, transmuting snow into rippling water in a wide arc ahead of them—an act equal parts anger and demonstration.

“There!” She barked without looking. “Nothing but frozen—”

Borevin cut herself off when Magda suddenly stiffened, eyes widening in disbelief as a nearby mound of snow collapsed into water, revealing—impossibly—a grand abyssal pin covered in purple runes.

Magda’s breath hitched, her voice bright with shock and triumph. “By Tihamun, it’s a grand pin.”

"What?" Borevin turned, disbelief flickering over her face as her gaze landed on the pin as well. Embarrassment colored her expression, but before she could speak, Magda had already sprinted toward the pin, a wide smile on her face as she caressed it.

"Can you believe it, Borevin? It was right in front of us. Imagine if we had given up?"

Borevin scowled at her, her expression wavering between reluctant pride and irritation before settling on the former. "It seems you were right after all," she muttered darkly. "Tihamun really does enjoy humbling people."

Magda chuckled, savoring the victory, and without hesitation, she yanked the pin out of the ground, steadying herself in expectation just as a tremor hit, rumbling within the earth like vibrations trapped beneath the surface.

But unlike the previous tremors, this one was different.

The earth cracked, fissures tearing through the surface in jagged lines, racing outward like lightning in frozen stone. And from the depths below, a thunderous roar ripped out, tearing through the air like a herald of destruction.

Magda felt her entire body grow cold instantly, her eyes widening in fear and realization as a fearsome aura suddenly blanketed the skies.

The dragon had been unleashed.

Deep within the earth, Serythion roared as he shook off the last of his shackles, energy flooding his core as the chains hooked into his ribs suddenly dissolved into nothingness. And like a dam burst open, strength and power flooded his body, scales rippling and muscles spasming as long-deprived tissues instantly received a baptism of essence and energy.

Wounds closed in seconds as void essence burst out of his sinuous form, lighting the entire chamber in its grayish-black glow. Power coursed through his withered pathways like a river of boiling currents, rejuvenating him in an instant, and with a roar of excitement, Serythion flapped his massive wings, a beam of pure void building within his throat.

He raised his head to the rune-covered ceiling that had served as the only hindrance between him and freedom for the last decade, and with a thunderous roar, he unleashed the beam—a wave of pitch-black fire that erased absolutely everything in its path.

The beam slammed into concrete with the force of a collapsing star. Runes flared desperately… then ruptured with a psychic shriek as they were devoured. Thunder boomed, and the entire cavern shuddered as the roof exploded, debris raining down in a cascade of destruction to reveal a burning hole punched straight through several layers of bedrock.

But it wasn't enough. Serythion couldn’t see the sky yet, and without hesitation, he unleashed another beam, enlarging the ragged wound in the world’s flesh.

The cavern shuddered once again—more violently this time—walls cracking and debris raining. Support pillars splintered like bone, and slabs of rock the size of carriages tore free from the ceiling, crashing down in murderous avalanches.

But Serythion's lips only stretched into a fanged smile. Already he could taste it—the scent of open sky, the memory of wind, and the promise of freedom.

Debris hammered his scales, and rocks shattered against his hide as the cavern collapsed around him in a thunderous ruin. But Serythion paid the destruction no attention, his gaze fixed on the blazing hole above him, wings flaring in anticipation of flight.

All he could think about was freedom. And a certain Fury he had seen only a few hours ago.

After coming to an agreement to meet again an hour before the duration deadline, Aodhán and his group detached from the main gathering, eager to return to their mission now that the battle for pins was over.

They, however, now had two new members—Daruk and Mirith—and a tense silence stretched between them as they walked, the dynamics of the group thrown slightly off-kilter until Baxter decided to do something about it.

His mystical solution? Small talk.

"Well," he cleared his throat awkwardly, "I guess congratulations are in order, Aodhán. You now have ten abyssal pins. Six more and you'll gain immunity." He paused. "Though I doubt you intend to use it for yourself."

Aodhán chuckled, happy to diffuse the tension himself. "You never know, Baxter. Perhaps I'll give it to you."

Baxter snorted. "That'll be the day."

The tension eased a notch, a few chuckles breaking the silence before Brennon leaned in with a grin. “I would volunteer myself if I thought there was even a chance you’d give it to me.” He paused, eyebrow lifting toward Aodhán. “Is there?”

“No. There isn’t,” Mirith replied before Aodhán could speak, her tone raising the tension straight back to a hundred. "If there's anyone Aodhán would give the pin to, it's Daruk."

Everyone turned to glance at Daruk, except Aodhán, who turned to glare at Mirith instead. "I don't need my intentions announced, thank you very much." He glanced at the rest of the group. "And no—I don't intend to give it to anyone. I intend to keep it just in case."

"That makes sense," Daruk nodded carefully. "However, I doubt it calls for much celebration considering we're only six pins away from the destruction of the realm."

His words plunged the mood of the group even further into gloom, and as if that was not enough, Destra decided to make things worse by stating the obvious. "If Magda and Borevin find the last pin, we'll be five pins to destruction."

"Okay, that's enough." Brennon scowled at them both. "We're already tense enough as it is. Your words of wisdom aren't helping."

Destra glared at him. “I’m only stating the facts. We really have nothing to celebrate, and if you’re about to suggest otherwise, Brennon, please—don’t. This is not the time."

"This is exactly the time!" The illusionist disagreed. "We have a solid plan in place and a team of absurdly powerful individuals. What better time is there to celebrate?"

Every eye turned on him in an instant, ready to list out a dozen examples of what they considered better times. However, before any of them could speak, the earth heaved, a loud tremor rumbling through it like a beast trapped within.

Everyone froze immediately, and Aodhán quickly checked the leaderboard, searching for Magda's name. His gaze landed on it a moment later, and a wide smile bloomed on his face. Magda and Borevin had found the last pin.

Relief washed over him, brief and sweet, until a deafening roar suddenly split the air, shaking the entire realm to its core—an aura of deep purple erupting to paint an entire quadrant of the sky.

The smile vanished as quickly as it had come.

"Serythion…" Aodhán breathed, his words lost in the noise of the rumbling earth as fissures spread across the realm, widening like chasms of hunger given life.

“By winter’s tits!” Destra cursed, fear evident in her eyes. "Are we sure the dragon remembers our deal?"

Varéc growled within Aodhán's spirit, and he frowned. "We can only know for sure if we get close."

Without hesitation, he acted, summoning Varéc from his spirit in a burst of storm and chaos essence. “Go!” he commanded. "See if you can reason with Serythion and report to me."

Varéc growled low, taking his mission seriously. He teleported forward an instant later, his form disappearing in a crackle of purple electricity.

Aodhán glanced back at the group urgently. “Come on, guys. We have to go. If Serythion is awake, we have to make sure we are the first group of humans he sees. Any other group will attack, and that will rip this entire plan to pieces.”

“But what if it attacks?” Destra asked. “What if it’s so consumed by the idea of freedom that our deal means nothing to it?”

Aodhán swallowed nervously, not even wanting to think about that. “Let’s hope that doesn’t happen. And if it does, then Baxter can always teleport us out of there.”

They all exchanged nervous glances, and after a moment of hesitation, they all nodded. Hopefully, Serythion would be on their side.

Baxter stretched out a hand to open a portal, but Aodhán shook his head. “No, Baxter. We can't afford to get there before Varéc has a chance to speak with it." He glanced at Mirith then, a hint of bitterness lining his words. “You are the fastest person here. Take us to it.”

Mirith nodded, and a wide platform of azure mental energy instantly appeared before them. They climbed onto it immediately, and a moment later, they were zipping forward at a speed so fast, the world around them was a blur of white.

However, they'd barely crossed a mile when another tremor ripped through the earth, fissures racing across the surface like spiderwebs, widening as a chorus of explosions suddenly filled the air. Snow and ice disappeared as the earth caved in multiple places, destabilized from the constant tremors.

And then, as if that wasn't enough, a blood-curdling cry suddenly split the air from beneath them, an aura of deep crimson rising to paint the sky in blood.

“By Raol.” Aodhán cursed, but the words had barely left his lips before another aura rose from the east, staining its quadrant of the sky green. And from the west, a piercing howl echoed, even as an aura of inky darkness rose to shroud the sky in death.

The creatures had all woken up, and they were far from ready.

Another shriek tore from beneath them, and Aodhán felt a chilling terror suddenly grip him as core sense brushed against a blazing blood core hundreds of meters below them.

His eyes widened, and he gasped in shock.

The phoenix wasn't at the 75th tier as they'd assumed. It was at the 90th—its core radiating a killing intent so tangible, the air shuddered with it.

“What is it?” Destra asked him urgently, tears pooling in her eyes at the terror she saw in Aodhán’s.

If Aodhán—a man she had never seen express fear so openly—was so obviously terrified, then what chance did any of them have?

What chance did she have?

A continent away, in a chamber of storm and lightning, High Elder Zarxan Al’ruhn meditated, unaware that fate was about to drag him into the chaos above.

At Tier 189, he stood eleven steps from ascension—eleven steps from leaving this realm entirely and transcending to whatever lay beyond mortality's veil. He was an apex member of the Storm sect of Gordon, and in the last three years, he had not opened his eyes once, so lost in his meditation that the mundane affairs of Gordon—wars between sects, political machinations, even the rise and fall of entire kingdoms—were all becoming ephemera, barely worth acknowledging.

Even now, his consciousness floated within the origin plane of Storm, lost among the primordial truths of electricity, magnetism, and the spaces between as he searched for an inspiration—that understanding that would push him over the edge into the 190th tier.

He was so close now he could feel it, just beyond his grasp—a fundamental principle of storm that would revolutionize his entire cultivation base.

And then something foreign brushed against his consciousness—a knot of willpower, anchored deliberately in the Origin Plane's weave. Crude. Desperate. Screaming with emotional resonance.

Zarxan scowled at the spark of impurity, irritation rising within him as he realized what it was—a will imprint so crude it was no different from trash. This was why they never taught people how to interface with the origin planes. Some fool had just merged their less than stellar understanding of the affinity with the plane’s weave, creating a nuisance for all who came across it.

His first thought was that an ambitious mythic must have done this to garner the attention of a high elder. However, that thought shattered the moment he probed the imprint and realized it had been left there by an advanced class cultivator.

It should have been impossible. No one at that tier should have been able to think within the Origin plane, much less leave an imprint.

Who was this person?

Curiosity rose within him—not the mild, academic curiosity of a scholar. But a deep, instinctive pull that he hadn't felt in over a century. The kind of curiosity that had originally driven him to pursue the Calamity-class in the first place.

How was this possible?

Against his better judgment, Zarxan coalesced a portion of his awareness and pierced the foreign imprint. The moment his consciousness made contact, emotion slammed into him with physical force.

—MY NAME IS AODHAN BRYSTION. PLEASE, WE ARE TRAPPED IN A REALM CALLED ABYSSOS DEEP WITHIN THE ORDER OF THE WATCH. I HAVE NO IDEA WHERE EXACTLY WE ARE, BUT I’VE ANCHORED OUR LOCATION TO THIS IMPRINT. PLEASE FIND US. THE LIVES OF MANY ARE IN DANGER—

Zarxan jerked his consciousness back, a scowl on his face as the words of the imprint resounded in his mind. He analyzed the location embedded within it, and his scowl deepened.

Lutia? That cursed continent across the northern straits?

He should ignore it. Return to his pursuit of Tier 190. This wasn't his concern—hadn't been his concern for centuries. The affairs of his home continent meant little to him. Not after the way they had manipulated history—painting his mentor as a slaughterer just to further their wicked goals.

Still, he couldn’t ignore it.

It had been so long since he’d heard anything about Lutia. So long since anything in this realm genuinely surprised him. And with a thought that would have atomized a tier 100 cultivator, Zarxan dove into the imprint. Not gently—he didn't have the patience for that—but with the systematic precision of someone who'd spent centuries dissecting the fundamental nature of reality.

He peeled back layers of emotion, sorted through the chaotic resonance, and filtered past the layer of pain and terror to examine the essence beneath it.

And stopped.

No.

That's not possible.

The signature woven into the will's foundation—the particular resonance of the storm affinity, the unique flavor of the soul itself—was one he knew intimately. One he’d spent dozens of years studying before his relocation to Gordon.

One he’d considered his mentor.

"Az'marthon," Zarxan whispered into the Origin Plane, eyes wide.

The man had ascended nearly four hundred years ago, and yet, this flavor of willpower was undeniable. The sharp crackle of electricity that never failed to remind him of deep pain and sadness, yet bearing the unmistakable mark of power, and torment turned strength.

This imprint bore the exact same signature. Except… it was also different.

Zarxan studied the imprint with the focus that had carried him to Tier 189. This wasn't Az'marthon himself—the signature was similar in origin but fundamentally different in expression. Like comparing a child's first attempts at writing to a master calligrapher's work.

He realized then what this was. A Legacy.

Specifically, Az’marthon’s Legacy.

The implications cascaded through his mind like lightning.

Once upon a time, he had hoped to bear this legacy, completely willing to discard his own path for Az’marthon’s. But Az’marthon hadn’t left a legacy, or at least, he hadn’t seemed interested in the idea.

In fact, he had been against it.

And yet, nearly four hundred years after his ascension, a legacy seemed to have appeared and was now trapped, to be killed by cultists.

Zarxan couldn’t allow that.

Pulling his consciousness out of the origin plane, Zarxan opened his eyes, revealing a pair of runic irises as bright as stars. Thunder rumbled around him, roiling within the fragment of primordial storm he used as his cultivation chamber. Winds howled, and lightning flashed.

But despite the noise, Zarxan’s voice was crystal clear when he spoke, eyes crackling with electricity and reality rippling.

“Hold on, Aodhán Brystion. Far be it from me to fold my arms and watch while the legacy of my mentor struggles. I am coming.”
