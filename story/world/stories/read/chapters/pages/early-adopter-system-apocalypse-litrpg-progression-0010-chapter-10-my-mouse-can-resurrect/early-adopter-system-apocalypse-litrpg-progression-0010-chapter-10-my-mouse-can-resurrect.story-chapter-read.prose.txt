The CCTV footage mirrored what she’d seen. Nothing. She still wasn’t going out there. She didn’t know what was making her so uneasy, and did not feel remotely inclined to check.

"Curiosity is a disease," she said to Maus as she prepared to go through her bond’s Status sheet. Habitually, she made a cup of tea before settling down with a notepad.

It was time to understand this bond, and the companion it was attached to.

The Interface responded immediately, pulling up her Stat screen. The moment she focused on the final entry, the interface changed, expanding into what could only be Maus’s Status. This time she focused on not just seeing the information, but the details as well.

Name: MausSpecies: Spatial MouseLevel: 3Tier: 0Stats Per Level (SPL): 3

Free Stats (FS): 6

Bond:

Titles:

Traits:

Stats:

Skills:

Scarlet read through it once, then again more slowly, her attention moving in a deliberate pass from top to bottom as she organised what she was reading into something she could work with.

[Bond Revival: If bond dies, their form dissipates and reforms within the bonded partner’s soul space. They will be available for physical reconstitution after cooldown.Current Cooldown: 5 days]

Scarlet had adopted six mice in the past ten years. Her first were a pair of house mice from a research lab on the Island: Algernon and Algernon the Second. After them came the Desperaux trio, three fancy mice rescued from a hoarding situation. She’d heard about Maus soon after they’d passed. He was her first deer mouse, a litter runt destined for termination before one of her contacts got in touch. He had now been with her for a year and a half.

Unfortunately for Scarlet, mice had short lifespans. Maus had already been roughly halfway through his lifecycle before Integration. Now he had Bond Revival.

[…If bond dies, their form dissipates and reforms within the bonded partner’s soul space…]

“So long as I am alive, you are immortal,” Scarlet whispered, stroking his tiny head. She couldn’t imagine the face she was making right now.

This Trait meant she’d never have to lose a companion again. Never have to watch them die of old age, or induced illness, or even mutated raccoons with magic powers – Skills. She reminded herself. Those magic powers were Skills, and this new world meant anyone, and anything could possess them.

Yes, the world was dangerous now, but the world had always been dangerous. That man at the library hadn’t needed superpowers and a magical reward system to justify trying to brain her with a brick. The world had always been dangerous; System Integration just expanded the list of predators, and the ways she could combat them.

Bond Revival meant Scarlet could plan more daringly. It meant new possibilities, with the only permanent loss being time. Of course, time was precious, and so was Maus. Scarlet would never willingly put him in mortal danger, but choice wasn’t always a factor.

The weight of responsibility warred with relief and Scarlet took a moment just to breathe.

This was… a lot.

“So,” she said after a moment, “you are considerably more dangerous than you have any right to be.” Her tone was measured, but her heart was delighted. Sure, she wasn’t enthused that while he had short range teleportation and an increase in lethality her unique contribution to the team was ‘feeling feelings’. However, considering they were a team, her boosted Perception and Intelligence was a good offset to the more physical build Maus was shaping up to be.

Sensing her focus, Maus turned to face her. His whiskers shifting at the appreciation.

Her gaze lingered briefly on the species line. Spatial Mouse. She tilted her head.

It would explain the teleportation, though she was sure he hadn’t been anything other than an average deer mouse before the Initialization. Perhaps the episode they’d experienced during the Integration process had something to do with it?

Her attention moved on to the bond section. She studied it for a moment.

Bond:

Subordinate. Meaning she was what, the superior? Why wasn’t that on her Stat sheet?

Scarlet exhaled softly, her fingers tapping against her thigh. Also, the bond type was ‘Soul Bond’. Was that good? Were there better types of bonds? She didn’t dwell too long on the possibilities. Instead, she focused on what information she’d been provided.

At least Bonded Growth offered some joint benefit. “We scale together. What’s mine has always been yours anyway,” she said, reaching out to stroke his fur. He promptly began grooming her fingers, and she smiled, letting him have his way as she refocused on the Stats.

STR 4, DEX 4, VIT 3.

“I suppose those numbers are relative to your size and build,” she mused. “It would be strange if 4 in Strength for me was the same as 4 in Strength for you.”

She queried the Guide just in case:

[All Stats are relative to the individual]

She was shocked the Guide didn’t require her to ‘Advance’ before it gave her the information. However, in retrospect it made sense. That wasn’t exactly something that couldn’t be verified just by comparing with other people, and now creatures.

Maus’s Stats were clear.

“You are not built to take a hit,” she said, scooping him up and raising him to eye level. “But you’re not meant to.”

PER and DEX being some of his highest Stats, coupled with his movement Skill meant he was great at sensing danger, and avoiding it.

Being great at escaping didn’t mean he wasn’t meant to deal damage. It just meant he wasn’t meant to get hit doing it.

Ravaging Bite coupled with his STR and mobility meant he could sense the enemy, deal a blow, and be away before they even knew he was there. Well, within reason.

Then there was Blink. It was the only Tier 2 Skill she’d encountered so far. Only Tier 2 anything, and it was a huge mana sink.

No wonder he’d gotten sloppy mid-fight. He was running out of fuel. She was glad they’d fought before she went through the screens because it had become much easier to figure out a build for her companion. Her only real struggle would be boosting Stats and getting the guy to train. Though if their growth was linked somehow, maybe she’d be able to help with that as well?

Bond Communication required no clarification. She’d yet to have a need for it as there was nothing she’d wanted to tell him that their normal methods of communication didn’t convey. However, there was no reason not to test it out.

Scarlet focused, and as if by instinct the Skill came to her. The sensation was like faint background noise, something she could easily tune out until she focused on it. Now that she was paying attention, she could clearly feel the link between her and Maus. When she activated the Skill, she felt the connection strengthen into something far more extensive and complex.

Having nothing profound or urgent to share, Scarlet just focused on Maus himself. On feelings of gratitude, warmth, and curiosity.

Maus froze, and for a moment nothing happened. Then a flood of disjointed feelings rushed back at her. They weren’t overwhelming in the way the roidcoons had been. Their wave of bloodlust and greed had been obtrusive and foul. The sensations were nothing alike. These feelings felt like they were her own, and yet not. There was no other way to describe it.

She also got the feeling she could shut him out if she wanted to, or push to feel what he was feeling.

She closed the Interface and gave her full attention to mapping this new shared connection with Maus. For a quiet minute, it was just her and her Maus. Then the little guy decided he required popcorn, and he needed it now.

She rolled her eyes and stood. The moment broken. Well, at least she had a direction to follow.

“Going forward you will handle mobility, long range scouting, and opportunistic damage,” she said, placing him on her shoulder and heading for the pantry. “For now, I will handle everything else. We will reassess once we understand more.”

She pulled out the kettle corn and decided she would have some as well. Handing him a piece, she took the bag with her as she made her way into the command centre.
