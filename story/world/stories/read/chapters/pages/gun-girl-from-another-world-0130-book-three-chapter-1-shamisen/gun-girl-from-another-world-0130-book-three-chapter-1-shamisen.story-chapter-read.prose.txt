Chapter 1

Shamisen

*Remmi*

The marketplace of the city of Lurei is a bustling, busy place, the sheer mass of people seeming to force the stalls back into the brick and mortar by raw presence. It's like somebody fused Black Friday with a convention during a festival. And not only are there an unfathomable number of shoppers here, but many of them are my first sight of people from outside of Furinshao!

According to Ayre, Lurei's Indian-like grand bazaar used to be part of the Western Demesne, but the Demesne is actually a significantly large area spanning a number of provinces. Lurei was grown into the hub of trade it is today by its location at one end of the “Moon-Crossing Chain of One Thousand Golden Links,” the Tolestean equivalent of the Silk Road.

Yes, I complained intensely about the name, but when I pointed out it was a mouthful, my friends turned on me like I was the crazy one! Apparently, I'm the only one that has to sit through eleven syllables of Wuxia woo-woo. For most everyone else, it's a tolerable five. Essence translation, yay.

Anyway, this position transformed it into a cosmopolitan, multicultural melting pot unlike anywhere else in the empire, and earned its overseeing regent a great deal of wealth and influence for very little personal effort. However, when the Darkness swept through the Demesne, somehow, Lurei was left outside of the sphere of influence.

While it survived the collapse of the Demesne, its regent did not, and with so much of the original province lost, the decision was made to dissolve the position, and Lurei was incorporated into the neighboring region it bordered.

… A cynical part of me wonders how much that political maneuver cost Lurei's new lord. To be honest, though, it's drowned out by the part of me that's squealing like a kid on Christmas morning that found out her present was a shopping spree at Toys-R-Us.

I've spent a small fortune on books both foreign and exotic, which I categorized as travel expenses, two fortunes on spices and seasonings, which, of course, are camping supplies, and three fortunes on magical constructs and other devices that caught my eye. These got listed as research materials.

Ayre scolded me for wasting money, and dismissively ignored my pleas that they were absolutely essential purchases in the pursuit of our mission. However, he went silent somewhere around the foreign silks when I passed him a bag of bars and hasn't bothered me since. He even got tagged by an alchemist selling facial cream when she told him it was the best for keeping skin moisturized, firm and glowing.

I'm not even going to remind him that he already bought something like that in Kaiwoku. Heck, maybe he's already used it up, that has been a while ago. I'm just penciling it all under esprit de corps.

Honestly, I actually feel like the books are the biggest haul. I unexpectedly found something resembling light novel series, and grabbed a bunch of them. I'd call most of them Xianxia, but there's also some about Heroes like me. Fictional ones, sure, but the genre still caught my interest. Disappointingly, science fiction doesn't seem like much of a genre here, probably because science isn't much of a thing here. The closest thing seems like mages researching dark magic or building golems.

Speaking of pseudo-fi about golems, I may have given into guilty temptation and picked up one book called, “The Artificer's Carved Lover.” I have a suspicion, just from a quick flip through, that it has a rather different climax from the Jewish Golem, and if it's as steamy as I think it might be, I'll be keeping it far away from Korrigan.

In my defense, not all of the books I picked out are for entertainment. The bazaar has a thorough collection of intellectual literature on artifice, enchanting, medicine and crafting. I even picked up one on herbology and medicinal alchemy, it might give me more ideas on what to plant in my Essence garden. The one I'm looking forward to reading the most, other than Carved Lover, is a book I found on siege weaponry: “Go Big or Go Home: An Examination of Engineering Solutions to Walls, Giants, Officers and Other Impediments.” The title was particularly eye-catching to me, but Ayre couldn't intuit the nationality of the author's name. If we were on Earth, I'd guess Ahk'Ari might be African, or a very obtuse pen name, but such a guess doesn't do much good here.

In the spirit of keeping up party morale, when I noticed Korrigan starting to get mopey and counting her tins too many times, I swooped in and topped her off, too. After all, while most of our group's wealth is from my crop sales and the loot from dungeons Ayre and I had cleared by ourselves, she's been pulling her weight ever since. I don't mind giving her a little forward bonus for a special occasion like this.

And the look on her face when she came out cradling three magic books and a stuffed doll made it worth every bit.

Still, if we're going to make this an extended stay, we'll have to find a good place to pawn some more of that loot. We're fine for a day or two of this, but if we try to hit every good store in this open market, we're going to need at least a solid week and a couple big, fat sacks of dosh. Though the mental image of that comically sized sack slung over my shoulder makes my inner cartoon villain giggle. And maybe my outer one, too, which gets me the standard weird look from Ayre.

“I'll explain it when you're older.” I grin at him.

“I think I'll find a way to endure,” he snarks back, then returns his attention to a rack of foreign outfits.

But then I see it, and everything else in the entire city disappears from my mind.

The rack before me holds, in a wooden display case lined with felt, a truly lovely shamisen. Its pale wood is trimmed with cherry pink, in an eye-catching contrast to its pure white strings and tuning pegs. The skin is etched with cherry blossoms and curling swirls like the winds that carry them aloft. It’s paired with a shimmering ivory bachi.

Ayre raises his head and looks over in my direction when he realizes I’ve fallen uncharacteristically silent, and after a moment, he comes over beside me, tilting his head to see what, exactly, I’m looking at.

“... The instrument?”

“It’s beautiful,” I breathe.

The elf’s eyes drift down to the base of the display case, and then his mouth starts to look like a downward-facing horseshoe. “Remmi, at least get stunned by the price before you get stunned by the product!”

I turn to face my friend. “We can afford it! Besides, imagine how nice it would be for long trips or beside the campfire!”

He sighs, crossing his arms. “I don’t think it would stand up to that kind of life. This thing’s definitely far more meant for the eyes than the ears.”

“It’s a work of art,” I insist with passion.

“Yes, it is,” he agrees, unmoved, “and, as such, is intended for a wall or table, not a campfire.”

While I’m pouting at him, Korrigan pops up between us. “Hero Remmi can play the shamisen?”

At that, Ayre peels open one eye to send it my way. “That’s actually a good question. Can you play it? Or are you just going to cheat and buy the skill?”

“Rude!” I plant my fists on my hips. “I’ll have you know that I absolutely can play it!” Their doubting gazes make me sweat for a moment before, less confidently, I clarify. “Well, I can play a guitar, and they’re very nearly the same thing!”

“... What’s a guitar?” Korrigan asks predictably.

I hold up a finger with a smug look on my face. “Like the shamisen, it’s a plucked string instrument with a long neck and a hollow body! Its big differences, though, are that it has twice as many strings and a somewhat different, skinless body, and it has cross-bars down the neck called frets! It also comes in more varieties for different specialties and effects, like the steel or electric guitars! I play an acoustic guitar, which is the closest to the shamisen, but if you can play one, then you can play most of them.”

Ayre turns toward me fully now. “If you know so much about them, why don’t you just make your own guitar, instead of buying a shamisen that’s not what you’re familiar with, and wouldn’t stand up to the rigors, anyway?”

… Okay, yeah, that’s a really great point. Is there any reason I couldn’t just build my own? “... Yeah, now that you mention it, I could do that,” I muse as my mind starts sorting out the construction details.

That puts a victorious grin on my friend’s face. “You could make it in the same style if you really like it that much, too, and for way less money!”

Even Korrigan seems excited at this new project. “Yeah! I want to hear Hero Remmi play a guitar! I wonder if it sounds much like a shamisen?”

“Hero Remmi Lee!”

I’m halfway through inhaling to answer the little oni when I hear my name shouted out, and I turn to see a young woman in the uniform of the messenger corps heading right for us. I raise my arm all the way up so she can better see us, but the way she’s heading, I don’t think it’s really necessary. She’s already got a clear bead on us despite the crowds, so I just wait for her to reach us.

“Wow,” I praise her once she’s suitably close to do so without blowing out my throat, “you’re a really good tracker! I’ve had messengers only able to get to the right building in a place with a fraction of the population as this place has. Only a dozen people in the inn, and he still had to call for me!”

She grins proudly at my compliment, not even winded. “Eheh, thank you for your kind words! Only the most skilled messengers are employed in the big cities! It’s the only way to make sure the job gets done!” She reaches into her satchel and pulls out a scroll with a seal on it I recognize as Yorin’s, which she passes to me. “Here you go! Hope it’s good news! More deliveries to make, so off I go!”

“Thanks,” I say, waving. “Have a great day!”

Once she leaves and I unroll the letter, the other two gather around to catch a peek.

“That was Sacred Yorin’s seal, wasn’t it?” Ayre asks, trying to curve his eyes around my shoulder. “What’s it say? Do we have new orders? Is there news from Dabun?”

I push against his pretty elven face with my elbow. “Gimme time to read it, and I’ll tell you! And, technically, you don’t get any orders! I’m the only one that’s gotta follow them!”

Korrigan comes up from the other side. “Can they really make you do whatever they want? Just give you orders and you have to do it, even though you're not from the Empire?”

I sigh at that, but shake my head. “Honestly, if I wanted to opt out of all of this, they'd probably let me, if not out of common decency and the goodness of their hearts, then because they can't really keep me from running off somewhere and disappearing if they say no. The Empire's just the biggest nation on the continent, not the only one.”

I lift the scroll back in front of me in order to start reading again. “But I'm pretty sure the summoning ritual filters for stuff like that. If I was the kind of person to see others in trouble and run away, I wouldn't have been chosen. So, if I'm going to stick around and help, anyway, I may as well not do it running around blind and with no support, right? It's not about what I could get away with, it's about give and take. I take on a little accountability, and they give me resources and direction.”

They’re both silent as I read for a bit, but Ayre notices when I tense up partway through.

“Remmi? Is something wrong?”

“Eheh,” I shift nervously with a stiff smile, “I … think I might be in trouble …”

The elf gives a deep, long-suffering groan as his head drops. “... Again?”

At least Korrigan has the decency to look confused. “This happens a lot?”

“Depends,” I try to joke, “is it a day that ends in Y?”

But it backfires as the girl’s nose wrinkles. “What’s a wuai?”

“Nevermind.” I reflexively reach down and pat her head. “I just mean that it might happen a bit more often than it should …”

“How do you know you’re in trouble, then?”

“She woke up this morning.”

I pout over at the recalcitrant elf, who is doing his best to pretend I’m not. Soon, I give up and turn my attention back to answering Korrigan.

“It’s a verbose and eloquently written lecture from our favorite Elven Shrine Maiden. She's saying something went wrong with the dungeon in Dabun, and she's assuming it’s my fault.”

“Is it?” Ayre asks.

“I mean … maybe?”

“Yeah, that sounds right.”

Korrigan takes the opportunity of my distraction to peek past at the letter. “I don’t see where she’s saying it’s your fault.”

I start to jerk away on sheer reaction, but then sigh and lower the letter back down. “Oh, it’s there, it’s called subtext.” I motion to the section where Yorin is talking about people reporting on anomalies without her going into any actual detail on what those anomalies are. “You see here, where she starts getting really talkative without actually saying much? She does that when she’s angry, and this spot where she’s piling on super-proper adjectives? I’ll bet she went through an entire pot of tea writing that section.”

The little girl looks bewildered by my assertions. “You can tell she’s angry at you by how she doesn’t say she’s angry at you?! How is that even possible?!”

“Practice.”

Again, I shoot a glare at Ayre, and again, my best friend is completely unpenitent.

So I turn back to the oni again. “Also, I have the diplomacy skill, and it's going off like a blaring alarm.”

“Okay, so there’s something wrong with the Forest Cavern,” Ayre finally decides to be helpful, though he does it by crossing his arms across his belly with a cock of the hip. “That’s bad, sure, but what does she expect us to do about it? She does know we’re at least in the area of Lurei, right? Even if we burned through magically-enhanced horses in the maddest sprint possible, we’re at least a week out, maybe two. At a bare minimum. And those horses would have to be put down when we were done with them.”

I reach down and rub the strap to my seemingly empty leather backpack between my fingers. “Apparently, it’s bad enough that she wants us to use the Wanderer’s Stones.”

Ayre looks at me like I just announced that I entered us all into a swimsuit competition. “The Wanderer’s Stones? The ones she told us were incredibly expensive and only to be used in the utmost emergency? Those Wanderer’s Stones?”

“No, a different stone that only points north, of course those stones,” I confirm, but look around the crowd. “We should probably go somewhere we can be sure it’ll be safe to return to.”

“Remmi, they don’t have a return function!”

This time, I meet Ayre’s eyes directly with my frown. “I know that! But I’m not killing horses to get back here!” My eyes start moving around toward nothing, a sure giveaway I’m flipping through menus.

“You’re going to buy the Beacon Teleport spell?!”

Ayre’s words cause Korrigan’s face to perk up. “Hero Remmi’s buying a new spell?!”

“One that she said was way too expensive when she found out about it!”

I just roll my shoulders as I get to the page in question. “That was a long time ago for the way we’ve been going. That number’s not nearly so impressive anymore.”

Ayre holds his hands out in front of him like he’s grasping for reason. “Doesn’t that mean you should be spending it on leveling up instead of wasting it on a spell?!”

“I’ve been leveling up,” I counter, “and I’m not wasting it. It’ll also allow us to get out of dangerous situations. You know, like dungeons where we can’t get back to the exit, or fights against unbeatable monstrosities of the Darkness.”

My friend gives me a look like I just made a dirty play. “Yeah, okay, that’s true.” He sighs and turns away. “Then you’re right, we should head somewhere else to use it. Maybe even outside the city gates. It’s a good thing we haven’t paid for an inn yet, I guess.”

“Not yet,” I stop the elf before he can sashay away.

That confuses him, and he looks back. “Not yet?”

I hold up a finger and close my eyes as if I’m delivering great wisdom. “I may not yet know what I’ve done, but I know the first step in making amends: A peace offering! Before we leave this shopping utopia, we must first find the finest herbal blend for the ancient elven palette, suitable for release of tension and enhancing focus!”
