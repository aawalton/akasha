After finding a rhythm, we ended up having to pause every hundred metres or so.  I would either unblock my last water spout or drill a new one with the daggers.  It turned out that Manducare Barnacle was a particularly stupid spell to use on multiple casts of Conjurare Barnaculum.

As a dragon, it was no big deal.  A foot-wide plug of barnacles, that was maybe a foot deep as well, didn’t even register as a mouthful.  As a human, it was a bit more intimidating, as I’d discovered the hard way.  So now I burned out the poor barnacles that I’d magically kidnapped from the nearest ocean instead.  The smell of flash-fried seafood wasn’t quite able to overwhelm the acid-stink of melting rock and Deep One, but it added a relatively pleasant after-scent to the pervasive odour.

“How much further, Granvil?” I was taking aim to drill another borehole up to the mystery river above, the dagger held above my head and carefully slanted.

“Another two hundred metres or so?  Then there’s a big cavern, called the Womb on the old maps, before the Descent.”

“So the Damp Passage led to the… never mind.  I give up on the names in this place.”  I hit the river on my second go this time, and I ducked back towards my minions as high-pressure water flooded out and filled the tunnel with steam.  “I’m surprised the next section isn’t called the Fallopian Tube.”

“That lies way over to the east.  Past the Cunny and Baby Factory, but those are still clear, though.  The Deep One was waiting for us at the heart of the mountain, not in the outer tunnels.”

“You know he wasn’t waiting for you, right?  He was conjured into being by the system because—”

“LALALALALALA!” Granvil chanted, hands over his ears.  I shrugged.  Dwarves were weird.

“What system do to make monster?” Bighorn enquired.

“The Dwarves have a curse, same as all the sentients.  Some Pixie asshole fucked everything up thousands of years ago so all the species have their oddities.  Humans have their weird territory thing, plus racism.  Pixies are hated, Dwarves always awaken some ancient evil that shouldn’t see the light of day, Elves… What the hell did it do to the elves?  I mean, those guys are long-eared wankers, but I can’t remember the specific curse.  Something to do with shagging trees, or something.”

“They mount the trees?”

“Maybe?  Is that so weird, compared to every dwarf clan always finding some uniquely horrifying monster in their caverns?”

“What’s our curse?” Bighorn rumbled slowly.  He sounded a little forlorn.

“Dunno, dude.  Maybe it’s not having magic?  But you seem to do ok without it.”

The minotaur hefted his axe into both hands and nodded firmly.  “Makes the furless weak.  It’s a crotch.”

“Crutch, but I take your meaning.  Right, I think that should have done it.”

“No, crotch.  Ever been kicked there?  Definitely a weak point.”

He had a valid point.  More barnacles were snatched from the salty embrace they thought of as home and jammed into a pipe.  Not a euphemism.

“All the species seem to do ok.  It’s almost as though… Hmmph.  Not far now.”  I scarcely dared to even think it in my own head.  IMPS was worse than the gods for reading your thoughts, and I loved IMPS; it was great.  I chanted the glory of IMPS in my mind as we moved forward.  No point taking unnecessary risks.

And so we proceeded steadily forward.  It only took two more burns to reach the end of the Damp Passage and get a look at what lay beyond.

“I think whoever owns this place is menopausal.”

The space was wide.  My heat vision let me see fine, albeit in shades of blue and orange, but Bighorn was a grazing creature and unused to life underground.  As a result, I’d had a couple of light orbs floating along in my mage hands, and as I sent them out into the Womb, I saw a lot of dead eggs. Something had been hatched here, a lot of somethings.

The first ones I reached were brittle with age and crumbled to dust in my hands.  The shells were thin, maybe simply due to gradual decay over time, perhaps they had been that way originally.  Each egg was maybe two feet tall, or would have been before they had hatched.

“Eggs usually mean something to lay them… Any ancient myths about this place, Granvil?”

“The shadowpedes,” he whispered.  “But these are all ancient.  Ha.  Nothing left of them.  Besides, the Deep One would have taken them for biomass.  I’m sure.  Definitely.”  He had moved over and was practically hugging Bighorn's leg, placing his face uncomfortably close to the minotaur's plumbing, but he didn’t seem to mind.  He wanted to be close to another living thing.

“I am hoping the name has nothing to do with urination or inappropriate touching.  Preferably both.”

“Big many legged insects made of darkness.”

Bighorn reached down and put a hoof on top of Granvil's head. They were starting to resemble mismatched Siamese twins.

“I guess you’re scared of insects as well?  Thinking about it, I didn’t see any insect dudes on Broke Dick Island.”

“Killed them.  Moon Shiver did it, centuries ago.”

“She certainly gets about, does our Alicya.  Maybe that’s why all her nicknames make her sound like a slag?  Look, both of you, there’s nothing to be scared of.  These things are long dead.”  I waved a chunk of shell the size of my chest at them, and it broke off in my grip and shattered on the stone.

“What are you looking at?” I asked wearily.  The looks of absolute terror on their faces were obvious even in the dim illumination of the pair of orbs.  As always, confidently declaring we were in the clear had summoned some monster to appear behind me as if by magic.  Fucking system—I mean, I love the system so fucking much!

I turned slowly and looked up, then up some more.

“Well, you never missed a meal, did you?”

I couldn’t actually see the thing.  It was more of an absence, swallowing the light that touched it.  Shadow made flesh. Disturbingly insectile.  Midnight mandibles hung down in front of a face that had too many eyes, all of them drinking the light like a scotsman at last orders.

“I haven’t eaten for a long, long time.”  Deep, with a rustling chitter in the background.

“Have you ever considered doing voice-over work?  I know a guy in Ankmapak, he might be able to set you up.”

“Why have you come here?”

“Just passing through.  No need to get yourself in a tizzy.”

Tens, no, hundreds of legs shifted slightly, rippling away from me down the impressive length of the thing's body.  Many, many legs that dug little holes in the stone whenever they landed.

“Why should you be permitted ot pass?  And where are you headed?  Down into the depths is suicide.  Dave won’t tolerate your sort.  He didn’t tolerate mine either.”  The damn thing sounded sad.

“Been keeping you in a box?”

“I am Artackelsprix the Void!  I have never been put in a box!  I am a terror of the depths!”  The voice boomed, and the mandibles snapped open and closed repeatedly.  The milipede shade reared back, and a dozen legs waggled menacingly down the front of his body.

“Ok, Art, so why are you still here?  You could have just strolled down the horror-vagina and gone on a rampage against the short arses.  Plenty of meat for more eggs down that way.”  I pointed a thumb behind me at the tunnel we’d cleared.  Art was big, but he should fit down the Damp Passage easily enough.

It would be a disturbing thing to see from the other end.  Like being conscious while in the womb and your parents… I shook off the nightmare fuel and considered the monster glaring down at me.

“Bob, shut up about the food, eh?  Those are my people!” Granvil whispered, aiming an awkward smile up at Art.

“I have feasted on dwarves in the past.”  The massive head snaked down, and I nodded in appreciation.  As someone who regularly practised that move when I needed to intimidate mammals, the professionalism impressed me.  “And will again in the future.  Dwarf tartare is a fine dish.  Some roast mushrooms on the side.  Mwah.”

The creature lacked the limbs to make a proper chef's kiss, but the mandibles moved in a way to suggest it did its best.

“You’re a foodie?” I asked, mentally running through the contents of my storage space.

“I’m very, very hungry.  All my young either went to Dave, or I ate them myself.”

“You ate own calves?” Bighorn sounded horrified.  He mooed unhappily.

“I’ll eat anything.  They don’t become sentient till they’re a century old.  It’s more like pest control before then.  Feral little monsters.  You look juicy.  Do you prefer to be roasted, or is it better to pan fry your kind?”  Art’s head dropped lower still as he looked Bighorn up and down.

“I’m chewy.  No fat.”

“You’re not going to eat my… friends, I guess?  We’ll say friends for now.  It sounds better than minions.  I’m happy to offer you some grub in exchange for safe passage.  Hell, it’s in your interests, I’m off to rip Dave's heart out.”

“I’m not so sure.  Kat is one of those system-assigned outsiders as well, and she reckons she can die.  I’m not planning on killing the bastard anyway.  Just reclaiming something he stole.  But what about you?  I can offer you some Ancient Wyrm steaks in exchange for not making me kill you?  Option B is also fine by me.”

“A few rounds.  Maybe three hundred kilos each.”

“I could just take them from your corpse.”

“Proud too.  Arrogant.  I think I shall eat you again and again.”

“Bob, if you’ve got some trick up your sleeve, use it?  Or turn into a dragon and—”  Granvil began.

“It’s been many centuries since I last ate a dragon.  The taste is subpar, but the flesh fuels excellent evolutions.”

“Tell me about it.  I ate Umbraxis and had epic roles for days.”

“Bob, maybe we should go back?” Bighorn clutched his axe nervously.

“Yeah, sure.  No point forcing the issue, there’ll be another way down.”

The minotaur and the dwarf began to hurry away, but my loyal bunnyborgs stayed by my side.

“What if I don’t wish to let you go?”

“It doesn’t matter, I just wanted to get the squishies out of the way.”

I unleashed one of my most terrible moves.  Of all the many ways to hurt and maim I had learned when I received access to Dragon Fu, this was among the worst.

Forward-down-down/forward-forward-press-A.  I took off like a rocket, my fist raised to slam into Art’s chin at the same moment my borgs opened fire with their mana-cannons.
