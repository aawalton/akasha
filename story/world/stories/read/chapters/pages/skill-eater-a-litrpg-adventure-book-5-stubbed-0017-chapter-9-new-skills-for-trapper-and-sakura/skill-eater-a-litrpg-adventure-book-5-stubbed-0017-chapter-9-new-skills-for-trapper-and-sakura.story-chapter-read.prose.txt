The crew had been back for less than a day, but they had accomplished a great deal in that time.

After Trapper switched on the room’s privacy device, the hunters reported on their progress. Jumo had stopped by Lowtown to meet with the Syndicate. The warrior had grown quite friendly with the organization over the last few weeks, judging by the lipstick smudging the collar of his shirt.

He confirmed that the Wardens hadn’t been seen in or near the settlement while the team was hunting monsters. No one had any leads on the Coalition’s activities. Their operation in World’s End had been dealt a crippling blow, although they might have some personnel who were flying under the radar. In other news, the tension was tighter than ever between the haves and the have nots, but other than a handful of disappearances, the dynamic hadn’t changed much while Edge was away.

Melenia had gone barhopping with some acquaintances from the city watch, and Sasha had visited a friend who worked in the Magisters’ citadel high above the mixing bowl. Both organizations had their hands full making sure the sudden surge of monsters was contained while keeping the situation in the free city from reaching the boiling point.

The crew hadn’t had any luck unveiling the Ancient’s identity, but that wasn’t likely to happen simply by listening to rumors. Once everyone evolved and had room to equip Monster Slayer, the crew would work together to track the traces of corruption the creature left in its wake.

In the meantime, Trapper and Sakura were mapping every place they crossed the monster’s path, although only the scion could tell how fresh the trail was. It was safer than asking probing questions during the early stages of the investigation. Even if they couldn’t locate the Ancient by studying the pattern, they hoped to deduce its objectives and start crossing people off their list of potential suspects.

They intended to focus their efforts on the matter after meeting with King, but today, the women had spent most of their time consolidating their gains from advancing to stage three.

They had stopped by the Mortium exchange, and from the smiles on their faces, both hunters had found something good. They wound up purchasing two skill gems each. That left them with some empty slots to fill, but they didn’t want to run out of room before visiting auction house on the gold terrace, which they were eligible to enter after evolving.

“It might be a good idea for Trapper, Sakura, and myself to leave at a slot open for a while,” Jumo mused. “We’ve pulled off enough crazy shit recently that we have got to be close to earning a Rare or Epic skill the old-fashioned way, just like Sasha and Mel.”

“I was thinking the same thing,” Trapper agreed. “And I’m hoping that all three of you will increase your cores’ rarities when you evolve. Since it happened for me and Earl, I figure the odds are high.”

“That would be fantastic.” The warrior grinned in anticipation. “But we can worry about that later. Right now, I’m dying to hear all the juicy details about your sexy new skills.”

The women happy to oblige. The crew’s leader shared her newest powers first. “With Manifest Trap, Jab, and the devices I can set by hand, I’m satisfied with my selection of physical traps for the time being. Now that I have more skill slots to work with, I’m ready to broaden my horizons.

“Alarm is a versatile skill that I can customize with a vast array of trigger conditions and alerting mechanisms. In addition to placing wards on the ground like traditional traps, I can cast Alarm on myself or the wagon to create a mobile-detection system. It will warn us when enemies are approaching our position, defend us while we’re asleep, and guard our supplies in the field.

“Going forward, we should be a lot safer while operating in extreme-threat biomes, particularly will all the stealth upgrades we’ve obtained over the past few weeks. The second skill I slotted today offers some fantastic utility.”

Trapper had been planning to add a mobility power to her skillset for some time now. She wanted something that would pair well with her crossbow and longbow, rather than a power that was geared toward melee combat. Fortunately, the exchange had something for sale that checked all those boxes.

Edge’s jaw dropped when the woman ignited her core and starting pacing in midair. The skill was called Airwalk, and it let Trapper stride across the sky as if it were solid ground. At rank one, the spell could only operate up to an altitude of twenty feet, and it was too expensive to maintain for long periods. Even still, Airwalk was a fantastic power that would help the hunter cross rough terrain, make it easier to scout, and provide an elevated position to fire bolts and arrows.

When the team was done asking questions, the scion of Lore-Weaver shared her latest gains.

Since Sakura had begun hunting monsters and beasts on a regular basis, she had devoted two of her new skill slots to ranged attacks—one for defeating opponents that were weak against energetic powers, and one that was purely physical.

Her first skill was Lightning Bolt. The spell worked as advertised, creating an arc of electricity that fired from her fingertips. Her second power was called Pierce. It was designed to synergize with her penetrating boltcaster, which the woman had grown fond over the last few weeks. Rather than adding a magical effect, Pierce dramatically accelerated the bolt in the same instant it fired, increasing the weapon’s range and damage all in one go. The skill’s magic was spent long before the missile landed on target, which meant Disruption wouldn’t impede it at all.

While they were at the exchange, both women had purchased a personal magitech privacy device that ran on gold-grade aether. They would let the crew strategize in the field or chat while walking through town without having to worry about anyone listening in. They were critical acquisitions given the situation with the Ancient, but the devices were insurance against the Coalition too and were certain come in handy from time to time going forward.

The crew’s leader had also upgraded her mana-muting device to boost its strength and duration. In addition to helping the crew hide from deadly predators, it would prevent their prey from sensing the hunters’ alpha strikes before the team was ready to let loose with their strongest skills. Mana berries were hard to come by, but Trapper had managed to source enough to issue one to every member of the crew.

Since everyone had earned a ton of Credits on top of their massive windfall of lifecoins, she and Sakura would check out what goods and services were available on the gold ring soon. They also planned to attend the next scheduled auction, which sold items and resources that couldn’t be found anywhere else.

“Before we go our separate ways for the rest of the evening,” Trapper said when show and tell was over, “I want to review our strategy for dealing with the Coalition. It will take twelve hours to reach the coordinates we were given, which means we have a little over a day to complete our preparations before setting out from World’s End.

“Since Edge needs to evolve and Overdrive is on cooldown, we are going to make a play to buy more time under the pretense of needing to think the matter over before committing to an alliance. We need to decide who will attend the meeting, what the rest of the team will be doing in the meantime, and come up with plans for a range of contingencies so we’re prepared whether or not they give us the time we request.

“We already know the Coalition is willing to hold our people hostage to get what they want. It’s best if they don’t learn the full details of who is on the crew, which of us are scions, and which lords they bear. I think Edge and Sakura should attend the meeting with the rest of us on standby in the Lakelands—close enough that she can contact our Guides and we can regroup in short order if needed.”

“What should we do if they ask about our Unique cores?” Edge asked.

“Given how resilient you are and what they already know, it’s ideal if they believe you are the only scion. I would send you in alone, but Sakura’s informational and perceptual skills will let us learn a great deal once she’s inside their base. If she can identify their heavy hitters, gain some insight into their skills, and evaluate their overall strength, I’m sure it will be useful at some point. While you do the talking, she should act as Puppet Town’s liaison, which will explain her presence without arousing suspicion.

“Before we leave, try to learn more about their conflict with the Wardens, as it might present us with bargaining chips that will help us achieve a favorable outcome. I want to find a way out of this alliance, but if that’s not in the cards, we will have to bite the bullet and make the best deal we can.”

With that, everyone left the Lost.

Edge had some beast parts he needed to sell and made his way to the Association with Rue at his side. They stopped by the appraisers’ station and unloaded all the resources that weren’t valuable enough to auction, which he’d been too tired to deal with yesterday.

When he returned to the inn, Trapper and Sasha did their best to polish his negotiation skills, preparing him for different lines of questioning and coaching his response. He answered their queries honestly, acting under the assumption that King would be using skills that could detect lies or read body language.

Edge didn’t try to mislead or create false impressions either. Whenever the women asked about something he didn’t want to discuss, he simply stated that he wasn’t willing to divulge additional information until they had agreed to form an alliance.

Fortunately, he didn’t have to worry about mind-reading magic or skills that could manipulate his emotions thanks to Mental Fortress, which gave him some significant advantages despite his lack of diplomatic expertise. He still had a long way to go, but he was hopeful that by the time he was staring King in the eyes, he wouldn’t put his foot in his mouth or give any critical information away.

He replayed their words as he slipped between the covers, wondering what the future would hold. Edge didn’t know what would happen when he met with the Coalition, but it was finally time to find out.
