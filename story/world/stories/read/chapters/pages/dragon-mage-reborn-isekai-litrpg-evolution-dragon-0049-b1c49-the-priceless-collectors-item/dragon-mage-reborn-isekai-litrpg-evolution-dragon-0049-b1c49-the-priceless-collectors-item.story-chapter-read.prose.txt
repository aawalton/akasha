“Well, would you look at that,” Nolan muttered to himself. There Klepto Girl was, in the middle of a room filled with valuable items, in a life sized glass tank with gradually rising liquid sloshing up to her knees.

She was unconscious, but was still standing there straight and still. At first glance, I couldn’t understand what was holding her in place, and then I remembered I was in the mansion of a sick and twisted fae—the magical possibilities were endless.

“What the hell is that?”

“It looks like a harvesting tank,” Lun whispered. “Zev uses something like this to extract magical essence from particularly potent specimens. The liquid temporarily paralyzes the subject while the tank processes the extraction.”

“Shit, harvesting organs, and now magical essences, too? Except this girl isn’t a fae.”

“That’s why I said looks like. I’m not entirely sure what it is.”

‘Either way, it doesn’t matter. She needs our help,’ I insisted.

“How do we get her out?” Nolan asked.

“I can get her out, as long as I can find my scepter.” Lun said as he looked around for his scepter. Nolan, on the other hand, was searching for his precious Scarlet.

“All right! Jackpot!” Nolan cheered, opening the casing where his precious Scarlet was mounted on display, and his ammo wasn’t too far from it. As for Lun, he spotted his scepter mounted on a wall display between a jewel-encrusted dagger and what looked like a mummified dragon’s claw. He snatched it up, the magical weapon humming with hot energy as it reconnected with its master.

And here I thought Nolan called the scepter mediocre at best! Clearly it was special, otherwise, it wouldn’t have been up here in a private collection.

“They must have just dropped these off! If I’d seen my baby earlier, I might have been tempted to snatch it and risk it all!” Lun tweeted excitedly as he stroked his scepter lovingly.

“So you don’t fix the haul in his private room?” Nolan asked.

“Not always. That dumb orc El’knagn doesn’t trust goblins. Hence why I’m downstairs and not up here.”

“Well, while I’m grateful that I found my gun and my katars, I need to find my scrolls.”

“Most likely, they won’t be here. Scrolls aren’t rare items. They are common trade.”

“Not these scrolls.”

“I doubt there is anything special about your specific scrolls,” Lun said skeptically.

“They are in fact special, with amplified runes through lore activated extensions. I had someone very special to me craft them. There’s nothing common about my skill scrolls.”

‘I found some scrolls,’ I said as I rummaged through a drawer under the row of display cabinets. ‘Not really sure which one is yours though, Nolan.’

I backed up as Nolan went through the drawers. Lun urged Nolan to take any and roll with it, insisting that we didn’t have a lot of time to be picky. But Nolan was very particular with his magical scrolls.

We wouldn’t get everything back. For instance, our coins were definitely gone. While being broke should have bummed me out more than it did, I was used to it. What I’d actually miss more were my special dungeon raid rewards, like my x3 EXP Chitin Armor, spider strings, and spider eye.

“Hurry up!” Lun cawed at Nolan. I kept busy as he searched, and I couldn’t find any common items, like mana restoration vials or any type of attribute boosters. What did Zev do with them? Toss them in an incinerator? Or trade them off?

“Got them,” Nolan said, but I noticed he snatched all of the scrolls that were inside.

I gave him a skeptical look. ‘Were all of those yours?’

He smirked. “No.”

‘Hey I am feeling something back here,’ I said, pointing my nose at the far end of the collection room. My eyes zeroed in on an area underneath a crimson runner along the polished floor. When I stepped on it, a very faint current vibrated gently under my feet. My claws scratched at the runner, until Nolan crouched over and flipped the piece of fabric aside to reveal nothing special.

‘It has to be underneath the marble floor,’ I insisted.

“Then allow me,” Lun said as he snatched his scepter with both hands, the aura suddenly coming off of him forcing me to take a few steps back. He spread his legs wide and stabbed the area with the butt of his scepter ever so lightly. Lun looked like he was struggling to fish out what was underneath there.

“You alright there, lizard?” Nolan asked. “Looks like you’re ready to pop a blood vessel.”

“Need concentration!” he stressed through his breath. He was outpouring so much power that furniture was starting to tremble. I was afraid he’d make enough noise to alert the guards.

‘Please, be careful!’ I prayed inwardly, and finally, a sign of progress. Lun managed to carve a rectangle in the floor, the chore making his face bead with sweat. It was worth it though, because as soon as he lifted the block of flooring to the side, we spotted the real reward for our perilous trip to the mansion of madness.

“Is that—”

‘Myrim’s Magical Base Elixir!’ I cheered.

It was encased in a glass square container, in a small carved out pocket of the floor.

Nolan looked over to me baffled. “How did you find this?”

‘I don’t know, I just felt it.’

“What do you mean you just felt?”

“Well, it makes sense,” Lun added as he took in a long tired breath with his hand on his knee and his back arched. “When dragons are around a lot of magical artifacts and knick-knacks, their senses tend to become overstimulated, and in turn, sensitized.”

“Damn it, Freya. You really scored big time. There’s a reason Zev tucked this in a special area. He knows if in the right hands, this shit could potentially reverse the effects of Khunum’s parasites. I thought for sure we lost this….”

I smiled proudly as I wagged my tail.

“Everyone, stand back!” Lun warned, approaching the glass tank with the girl inside of it. He swung his scepter and said, “I’m going to break this one out, but I need some space.”

I gave him the space he needed, while Nolan did the total opposite. He tucked his things in his inventory and straightened up again, before he took a step toward Lun and said, “Hold on, how the hell are you able to use your scepter without scrolls?”

Lun grinned ear to ear. “Oh, but I do have scrolls. Just watch closely, and I’ll show you.” Lun tapped the butt of his scepter on the floor and instantly, his scales began to glow. All over his body there were inscriptions, small runes activated at his will.

“Flesh scrolls,” Nolan whispered, sounding genuinely impressed. “I only have scroll amplifiers. True flesh scrolls are not easy to get.”

He gave a smug smirk before his inscriptions faded. “Like I said, I am the strongest mage of the Empora District! I have trained under the guidance of nobles, mastering every arcane discipline there is!”

Lizard boy was beginning to sound like a broken record.

He raised his scepter high, the tip glowing with radiating energy. “Shattering Prism!”

The scepter flashed brilliantly as a beam of concentrated magic struck the glass tank. Instead of shattering outright, the glass began to crystallize, fracture lines spreading like lightning across its surface. The liquid inside stopped rising, shimmering with an iridescent glow before solidifying into jelly.

“What the hell?” Nolan muttered.

“I’ve transmuted the properties,” Lun explained quickly. “The paralytic will release her now.”

With a final crack, the glass tank splintered completely. The solidified gel broke apart into glittering shards that dissolved into harmless mist before they hit the floor. The thief girl collapsed forward, and Nolan rushed to catch her before she hit the floor.

She was limp in Nolan’s arms, but she wouldn’t be for long. Nolan began shaking her like a tambourine, not sparing the girl and slaps, pats, or jerks. “Hey! Wake up!” he shouted, and Lun ran to his side to tell him to shut up. With the last shove against her shoulder, the girl was finally coming to. At first, it was gentle, a light flicker of her eyes, and then chaotic, the little thief gasping for air before she startled poor Nolan with a hook jab!
