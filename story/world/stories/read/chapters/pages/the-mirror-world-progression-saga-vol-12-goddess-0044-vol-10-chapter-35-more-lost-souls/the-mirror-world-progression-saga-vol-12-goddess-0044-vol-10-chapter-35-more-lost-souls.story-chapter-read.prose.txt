His assistant waiting patiently at his desk after delivering the latest news, Unobus rubbed his forehead with his eyes closed, sighed, and took another sip of bitter, strong spirits.

Damn that bastard boy. Palthennius has weakened us with this. How did he find out? How? Why wouldn’t he come to me? How could his trust be so compromised? What in blazes are they teaching among the Heaven Knights these days, that they turn to such insanity? Insubordination, mutiny?! It’s unthinkable!

And yet, it had happened. Crushed, of course, and those who’d spoken out suitably killed or cowed. Many had already made statements about being deceived and dropped hints of demonic influence. There was no easier thing to blame under the circumstances. With origins for the mutiny in The Proper, and so many present not technically of Matrazere, confidence in the Unbroken Fortress was not particularly compromised. Obviously, the failure was due to corruption generated in the south, after Rivermount.

The plot had a lot more to unravel. How Palthennius had gotten wind of Plan Z… There were many possible culprits, with numerous officers fleeing around the same time. Some of those had been caught, but Unobus was under no illusions: that was entirely a screening maneuver. None caught would be the one to blame. Of a handful that fled, one of those was the leaker. That or someone still present and hiding in plain sight — the most awful of possibilities.

Well, it wasn’t even his concern anymore. The Inquisitors would deal with that. That they would was enough reason for the guilty to run. Who hid the truth from them? No one.

I’m left to deal with the fallout of their precious plot. They wouldn’t need to dig to hear my opinion, but I imagine they won’t seek it.

“Orders, Your Ascendancy?” his assistant asked.

Unobus sighed again and regarded him. “I still believe Palthennius had a trick up his sleeve with what he pulled, and it is terribly convenient to leave no body or artifact. He may yet live. It is not as if his trust in us was high in the end. Even if he didn’t, we must conduct due diligence. Seek him out as if he lived, and capture all who flee. Failing this, kill them.”

“Yes, Your Ascendancy. And what of the contingency for a… leader, if Palthennius isn’t found?”

Unobus grimaced. “Contingencies remain wise, as always. Have recommendations for replacements sent to my desk. From elsewhere, even. We have time, and we require the best.”

The assistant bowed, muttered his platitudes, and left.

The Sage of Judgment took another sip of his drink and brooded. This would reflect on him regardless of where the blame truly lay, but despite the setback, the upcoming victory would wash it all away. Ultimately, a few soldiers and a champion were irrelevant in the grand scheme of things. Matrazere was no less unbroken than before. If Palthennius was so corrupted by false thoughts, he might not have made the best for what they wanted, anyway.

More common bodies were pouring into camps on the daily. The simplest of levies, yet when Plan Z was executed — unclear, now — each would gain multiples of their humble effectiveness.

Come and wade through a sea of bodies, Witch Queen. The ocean always has more to fill it in.

During the northern advance to Matrazere, Samantha and the allied forces’ advance spies and scouts began to catch word of what happened in progressive bits and pieces. First, the troubles had apparently been a sizable, brief revolt of some sort that resulted in internal strife, arrests, swift ‘brain wipe’ punishments, and deserters fleeing every direction with the Dominion in heavy, hot pursuit. The details had not yet gotten widespread, likely due to the rampant fear that existed anywhere even close to the massive sphere of influence the Unbroken Fortress had over the region.

Samantha quickly seized on this to form small, elite ‘rescue teams’ of sorts, to go find and grab them before the Dominion. The first people found, a group of four, were more like prisoners, fighting against their would-be rescuers like cornered rats. One of them was a lieutenant, and the whole lot of them remained sullen and tight-lipped.

They ended up fighting Dominion groups repeatedly, in one case almost immediately after they wiped out some deserters in the forests southeast of Matrazere, which a lot of groups were trying to reach, apparently to disappear into them.

Another duo of soldiers were survivors of a big slaughter in that same forest, from which they’d barely escaped. Harrowed and shellshocked, they spilled some of the beans on what happened in Matrazere, finally. One of them was a sergeant, and he shared that word had first been going around, filtering down to leaders, that the mages were planning on feeding hordes of soldiers, mercs, and citizens into some mass sacrificial spell. In other words, they were going to kill their own.

The sergeant said his slain captain almost certainly knew more, but that was the gist given down to the lesser officers, in attempting to brew what amounted to a revolt or even a civil war. The captain had been tight-lipped about who, but supposedly ‘very big names’ had already become a part of the revolt and stirred it up in the first place, thanks to insider knowledge.

But no sooner than the whole thing began, it got brutally repressed. Fighting had been quick and fierce, with the Dominion far outmanning and overpowering any uprisings. Their large group beat a fighting retreat out of the city. Interestingly, their captain had been a strong dual-classer with [Wizard] levels, who ultimately bought the rest of them the time they needed to flee. Still, only two of them were left out of dozens, after an ambush in the night found them.

It was all-in-all fascinating and horrifying to Samantha. How many would the sages kill, now, with rumors no doubt flying like crazy in their camps? And those who actually believed it could still do nothing, now almost certainly under stricter lockdown and paranoid discipline. Matrazere was notoriously difficult to flee, with flat terrain for leagues and leagues in every direction. Only the sheer magnitude of the chaos had allowed some to desert at all, on foot or horse at least. One would need some tricks up one's sleeve even then, like an invisibility potion or the like.

Survivors only got more sorry, unknowledgeable, and uncooperative from there. They also got rarer, as the Dominion still rapidly cleaned up their little messes before rescue groups could get to them. Diminishing returns quickly set in. At least she had some nasty rumors to spread around, now with extra potency and closeness to the horrible truth.

But the actual spellcraft… not knowing what it was remained a very bad thing. They had to get more information.

Samantha finally had to stop participating directly, but kept agents out there, just in case more could be found. She was busy catching up with realm and army management inside the greater camp when she got word of a potential lead for more information from a group of three rescued souls.

It was composed of two soldiers and, of all things, a pregnant servant girl. The father didn’t really know anything but fled rather quickly with his girl due to their association with one of the ‘rebel groups,’ and fear of reprisal. The other soldier had gotten mixed up with them somehow along the way and stuck around to help. Not much had been shared before they’d been taken to the infirmary tent to be healed and get food, but the father had claimed the other soldier was extremely capable, well-equipped, and had given hints of knowing more about the ‘plot.’ He was likely to be an officer. But he’d also been badly injured while constantly defending them.

Interesting.

Samantha decided to pay them a visit personally, to draw information she badly wanted more on, so she gave instructions to send for her once they — particularly the supposed officer — could be questioned.

An hour later, they were unsure about the officer, but the other soldier, the dad-to-be, was lucid and fed.

Clad in simple cleromage robes with a hooded cloak, Samantha found herself pushing through an infirmary tent flap to a cordoned-off section with two beds. One had the couple curled up together, and the other had a man turned practically into a mummy with bandages. Samantha frowned a bit at that. Her frown deepened as she looked aside to see heavily damaged and scorched, but quite enchanted armor sitting aside, together with an enchanted blade. Strong.

Nothing legendary, obviously, but that’s good gear.

As the young dad-to-be reacted, sitting up slightly with his eyes widening at her appearance, the mummy-man appeared unresponsive. Meanwhile, the young soldier’s girl remained curled up next to him, asleep with her head swallowed by a pillow.

The young soldier next to her appeared hale, though bits of dried blood were still evident on his cheek and his bared shoulder. Samantha slowly and quietly glided over closer to him, at which point his eyes widened even more, staring at her revealed face with his mouth open. He looked as if he wanted to say something, but nothing would come out.

Awe, it seems. It’s hard not to be impressive, humbler clothes or not. I guess I should’ve come in the guise of a hag or something. Holy shit! Now I totally see why deities do that kind of thing…

Samantha tried a small, friendly smile and whispered, “What’s your name, soldier?”

The young man’s lip quivered briefly, but he swallowed and began in a too-loud, cracked voice, “Hu-” He cut himself off, glancing and wincing at his sleeping baby-momma, then regulated his voice to a whisper. “Hugh, milady. Hugh Matchie.”

“We’ll get you and your girl here to Rivermount, where it's safe, I promise.”

He stared at her in wonder and disbelief. “B-beggin' your pardon, milady, but aren’t we… prisoners?”

Samantha raised an eyebrow. “Have you even fought against us?”

“Ah, no, milady… not technically.” His face reddened slightly as he looked away and back. “I-I would have, eventually, if, well… you know.”

Samantha smiled. “I appreciate your honesty, my child. In any case, being willing to fight someone isn’t a crime. Will you swear an oath not to oppose us or undermine us?”

The boy blinked and tried to sit up more. With a sober gaze, he nodded firmly. “I swear it now, milady. On my honor, on my rebirth, I will not undermine or oppose you all, who helped me and Lana and our unborn. I’ll serve you, milady. I can fight. Just please, send them where it’s safe. If it pleases you.”
