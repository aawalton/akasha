Ethan expected his final two weeks on Earth to go by far too quickly, but every minute ended up feeling like an eternity. It was like he was about to take a flight somewhere far away, and he was constantly worried he’d forget something. He neurotically made lists, and generated increasingly unlikely scenarios in his mind where he might require the most unreasonable things.

It was when Selina caught him loading the Fable Train with mountain climbing equipment that he realized he might have a problem.

“Ethan, you have a power that lets you climb walls. You can also teleport…and Deevee is big enough to fly you wherever you need to go. Plus you’re a Twilight, you could probably jump your way up Mount Everest in a half hour.” She’d been watching a lot of TV with Agent Larkin.

“But what if my Bonds are severed, and I’m out of mana, and I need to sneak up the mountain so that I don’t alert anyone, and–”

“Go to sleep, Ethan,” she’d commanded. Then she’d threatened to put him to sleep with a Rune Circle he suspected didn’t exist. But he didn’t push her on it.

After that he’d shifted to a different kind of panic, thinking about everything on Earth he’d never see again. That led to binge-eating a disturbing amount of food, followed by loading the Fable Train with hundreds of takeout containers. That was nothing, however, compared to the ambush Michael prepared for him.

Ethan still visited his older brother regularly, checking on his progress, and catching up. He’d started doing this every Wednesday, and on the final visit before his departure, Mary-Ellen Bishop was waiting for him at the dinner table. Ethan’s mother.

Despite being back on Earth, he’d avoid her for nearly two years. Add the time he’d spent on Nexum, and the two years before that, and it had been a considerable time since he’d last seen her. It had been far longer than that since they’d last gotten along.

Mary-Ellen was the kind of parent who lived vicariously through her children. Worse really, as she always managed to somehow take credit for anything Ethan and his brothers accomplished. She was a proud Bishop, despite marrying into the family, and had spent their childhood bragging to any who’d listen that she’d intentionally brought four more brave soldiers into the world.

Ironically, Ethan’s father, who was an actual soldier, had never pressured his sons to follow his path. He was a joyful man, who seemed perpetually surprised to even have a family. Ethan didn’t learn until he was older exactly how many close calls the man had experienced, leading to him simply feeling lucky to be alive.

Ethan’s relationship with his mother had soured more each year after his father died. As his brothers left home, and there were fewer buffers between them, it slowly became impossible to avoid her attention. When he expressed his desire to become a doctor–what a less mature version of himself had called “the opposite of a soldier”–things had really fallen apart between them.

It had only infuriated Ethan more when he actually did get into medical school, as she quickly began telling anyone who would listen how she’d always known he was meant for it. Eventually he’d just stopped speaking to her, focusing on moving forward with the demanding life of a young physician.

When Ethan was honest with himself, he admitted that she was one of the biggest reasons that he didn’t go back to society after returning from Nexum.

Michael had always been the better son to their mother. Not the favorite–that was Dean–but the most responsible. Ethan used to tease him that “being a good son” was something he scheduled after push ups and breakfast. Michael had never denied it.

The dinner started out awkwardly, but ended extremely awkwardly. She’d apparently been coached not to chastise him for staying away after returning home, as she didn’t mention it. At first. But an hour, and three glasses of wine later, and she was going on about “how utterly devastating it was to hear about her own son from the television”.

Ethan had tolerated it, spending the dinner trying to see if Twilight rank would allow him to stare actual holes into his brother. When Mary-Ellen exhausted her lecture on being abandoned by her son, and shifted to talking about how she always knew he’d be better as a soldier, Ethan had just gotten up and left.

Michael chased him down on the path outside their uncle’s farmhouse. To his credit, he had Orion, his Follower-bred Familiar, hold the door closed so Mary-Ellen couldn’t follow.

“Well?” Ethan asked, glaring at his brother’s impassive face.

“Roast was dry,” Michael said with a shrug.

Ethan glared, then barked a laugh. “Yeah, it truly ruined an otherwise perfect meal.”

Michael almost smiled, an achievement. “At least you get to leave knowing you said goodbye. More or less.”

Ethan nodded, looking around at the farmland. “How are you progressing?” he asked, not wanting to talk about his mother any further.

“Quite well,” Michael said. “I think the blood rain helped, strangely. It made Orion feel even less scattered, and I haven’t felt the cravings in months.”

“Glad to hear it,” he said. “I’m sorry you can’t come through the portal with me, Noctem can barely manage two of us, and Selina has a right to fight for her world.”

“I don’t mind you going with a healer instead of an unstable killer Bonded to an alien,” Michael said.

“They’re all basically aliens,” Ethan said with a shrug. “Besides, you’re doing what I need you to do. Keep putting the work in, because when I see you next, I expect you to be ready for the real fight.”

Michael did smile then, though it seemed forced. “I’ll be ready.”

Neither one acknowledged how unlikely the reunion would be.

Ethan pulled his brother into a hug, then said goodbye. He hoped it wouldn’t be forever.

Ethan didn’t feel like Master Ulric’s undisguised irritation was entirely justified.

“I’ve spent four months on these Rune Circles, and thousands of years learning how to structure them. We’ve tested them. Many. Times. They work. They will continue to work. They should be nearly indestructible, but in the unlikely case that they’re not, I have provided you with three backups for each, as well as detailed instructions on how to make more.”

Ethan looked back at the man, who’d finally deigned to put down his book in order to emphasize his point. Ethan tried to keep his mouth shut, he really did. But he was about to essentially go to hell, and to say that hell didn’t have cell-service was a wild understatement.

“I just want to be sure–”

“I will kill you. I will doom all these worlds, and all future worlds, Doctor Bishop, and consider it a worthy sacrifice for the moments of blessed silence that it brings me.”

“That…seems like an overreaction,” Ethan said softly.

Master Ulric surged to his feet, and Ethan fell backward through a rift, already apologizing when he appeared at the other side of the room. “Message received!” he called loudly.

Ulric sighed dramatically, running a hand over his face while shaking his head. His unusual outfit always made Ethan feel like he was talking to a magical cowboy, and the man’s hand on his hip had Ethan wondering if he might have once reached for a gun at this point.

Thankfully, Ulric really did understand Ethan’s worries, and he’d calmed himself by the time he looked up. “Ethan,” he said, for once using his first name, “we’ve done everything we can to prepare you for this. I’ve done more than I should, really. I’ve experienced a pain you cannot yet fathom for stretching the bounds of my purpose as a Master. But I did it gladly, because your cause is everyone’s cause.”

“I know,” Ethan said, moving back to his usual seat, and forcing himself to sip the cold tea. “But the second I walk through that portal, all my lifelines are gone. My options are reduced to nearly nothing, and I’ll be alone in a world of nightmares, with the highest stakes I can imagine.”

“And you’ll be prepared,” Ulric said again. “The plan is a good one; I helped you come up with it, and it has the approval of a demon and two gods. You’re also as powerful as I could make you in such a short time, and we’ve turned the Fable Train into a true fortress. You’re uniquely suited to this endeavor. Just do what you’ve always done, and your chances are good.”

“Unless we run into a single Champion rank monster…” Ethan said glumly.

Ulric ground his teeth, and his hand even went to his chest for a moment. “I’ve told you: if you follow the plan, that won’t be an issue.”

“But you can’t tell me why,” Ethan said. “That’s a hell of an unknown to ignore.”

“You’re right, I can’t tell you why. So the only real question is whether you trust me or not.”

Ethan sighed. “You’ve earned my trust, but maybe I haven’t earned yours.” His hand went to his own chest, to the blank spot between his Bonds where an evolved Soul Rune should be.

“I’ve told you, Bishop, it takes most Bonded a decade–decades, even–before they find Twilight’s gift. And even still, you’re closer than you think.” He put a hand on Ethan’s shoulder. “This isn’t a short mission. You’ll be at this for months at least. Spend that time pondering my lessons, searching for who you truly are, and perhaps you’ll find the one tool you couldn’t pack inside that train of yours.”

Ethan nodded, but it wasn’t good enough for Ulric. “Is that it? Is that how you’ll leave this world? After everything you’ve been through, everything you’ve achieved, you’ll slither off to Potentia on your belly? Is that really who you are, Doctor Bishop?”

He looked into the Master’s silver eyes, and felt the warmth and support of his three Familiars rising up from within. Ulric was right. Hell, everyone was right. Ethan just hadn’t been used to actually having time to prepare. He’d raced across Nexum to find out what the Church had been doing, then rushed to Dawn without hesitation. He’d done the same when Twilight finally appeared, charging in head first.

Then he’d been faced with a return to Potentia, and just so much damned time to think and stress and…whine, if he was being honest.

Ethan gritted his teeth, then dropped to one knee, slamming a fist into the floor, and shattering the ancient stone.

“On Nexum, Ellevaro did everything she could to make me dance to her tune. Then Elias and Lathander both tried to play me for a fool. Here, I was lost, a new player in an ancient game, struggling to clean up someone else’s mess. But on Potentia…” He paused for a deep, calming breath. “On Potentia, it’ll finally be my game everyone else has to play.”

Ulric nodded. “Then go, apprentice.”

Ethan nodded back. “Thank you, Master Ulric.” Then he spun on his heels, and went for the door.

His lips split into a grin as he heard a quiet “my gods-damned floor,” muttered behind him.

***

Ethan stood on the Rune Circle that Selina had carved into the ground, under the guidance of Noctem himself. He didn’t leave his cavern, of course, but he opened a small rift and spoke to her through it. She’d handled it fairly well, all things considered.

They were nearly alone for their final moments on Earth, despite the monumental nature of the task before them. Selina was taking a final moment with Agent Larkin, and Emily stood awkwardly, waiting for her own goodbye with Ethan.

Newton had sent him a text message, Leonardo, a painting. King Rothavaro had returned from the front lines to give Ethan an enormous sword, and an expression of confidence he’d really needed. But Ethan hadn’t wanted anyone else nearby–the only exception being Calevaro, who was fighting underground somewhere in central America.

“I’m glad to have met you, Agent Rowan,” he said to Emily.

She smiled back. “I’m still on the fence,” she said playfully. The last six months had been strange for the two of them. They crossed paths a little more frequently once they’d both moved to Atlantis, and…some things had happened, but it had never evolved into anything more.

Ethan’s mind had certainly not been any clearer with Potentia looming, but they’d taken comfort in one another, and neither regretted those captured moments.

“I’m sorry I can’t come with you,” she said.

“This is where you belong,” he said. “Better that there’s only two tickets for this ride.”

She nodded, then embraced him. They stayed like that for a long moment before she spoke. “I lied. I don’t think I want to deal with the monumental crap-pile you do.”

“Neither do I, let's forget it all and get icecream.”

She laughed. “Good luck, you madman.”

“Madmen don’t need luck, we have unearned confidence.”

She smiled, moving away from him. “It’s earned,” she whispered, then turned and walked away.

Ethan watched her retreating form, then started turning back toward Selina. He paused when two more figures appeared, looking something like holograms.

“Maria, Miguel!” he said happily. “I didn’t expect to see you again, your CFC minders are remarkably strict.”

Maria nodded. “They fear for us. They may not be wrong to do so.”

Ethan resisted cracking a joke about how many times the twins had died. He doubted that was the “look back and laugh” kind of thing.

“Well, I appreciate you seeing me off. Take good care of your father while I’m gone.”

Miguel spoke next. “Be well, Ethan, and when all seems lost, try to remember what truly started all this. Why you fought in the first place.”

Ethan nodded. He’d grown used to cryptic advice from the two future gods, and knew that dwelling on it never helped. Seeing that Selina was ready, he turned and walked to the center of the Rune Circle.

“You ready for this?” he asked.

“To fight for my people, and my world?” she asked. “To put my power to its greatest use, and help save us all? Probably!” she said the last with absolute conviction, and Ethan found himself laughing with her.

It pained him to imagine making this journey without her. She reached out and took his hand, and he squeezed back, knowing they both needed the other’s support right now.

“We’re ready,” he said, and a rift barely large enough for the two of them appeared.

Ethan Bishop took one last look around, taking in the Master’s Tower, the looming city of Atlantis, and the world he’d never see again.

Then he marched into hell, head held high.
