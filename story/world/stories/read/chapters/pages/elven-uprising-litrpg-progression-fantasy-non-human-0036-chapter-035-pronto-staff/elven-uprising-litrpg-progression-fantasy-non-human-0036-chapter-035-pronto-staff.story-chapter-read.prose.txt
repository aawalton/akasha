“Grahhh!” Pronto’s screams echoed through the forest as Pinaka casually twisted his wrist. He jabbed his index finger into Pronto’s head and curled it around like he was playing in mud.

Then, with the same calm ease, Pinaka waved a hand through Pronto’s neck, slicing clean through it like it was nothing. A moment later, he reversed the motion and restored the severed section, making the neck look untouched. “Looks fun, right?”

“It was just a demonstration. No need to look at me like that,” Pinaka said, turning to face another version of Pronto—this one looking like a tightly wrapped dumpling. His limbs were bound into a cocoon that covered most of his body, leaving only his head sticking out.

“W-What the heck are you…?” Pronto stammered, panic rising in his voice. He wasn’t the one being tortured. He was just trussed up under the shade of a tree, watching.

The real horror was that the one being mutilated was a clone of himself—and that was what truly broke him.

“Stop! Please, spare me! I’ll tell you everything!” the clone begged before collapsing onto the ash-covered road, lifeless. Blood streamed from his eyes, the vessels in his skull burst from the strain. Four Pronto clones now lay dead on the path.

“Whatever you want from me, I’ll cooperate. So please…” Pronto bowed his head as much as he could, voice shaking. “Please, don’t make me watch anymore. It’s terrifying.”

“Oh, I apologise,” Pinaka said, then touched the four corpses. Grass sprouted over them as their bodies were quickly absorbed into the soil. He rolled up the patch of grass and tossed it to one of his nearby clones.

“It’s my first time appearing on Gangnea in this form, you see.” Pinaka gave a wry smile and crouched down in front of Pronto. “So, I didn’t know the best way to get a Human’s cooperation. I apologise if I startled you.”

Seeing Pronto give a terrified nod, Pinaka continued, “Does this appearance seem familiar?”

‘It’s… that Elf!’ Pronto remembered the fight between Pinaka and Nunaka. “I’ve seen your fight.”

“He was a son I doted on quite a bit, you know,” Pinaka said, carrying the same calm, all-knowing presence Pronto had felt from the World Tree when he was first reincarnated to Gangnea. “He starved to death in your prison. He chose that over living as a slave. It was his final wish, you see.”

“That’s why I took on his appearance.”

“W-What was his wish?” Pronto asked, barely holding it together. He kept hoping this was all just a nightmare he’d snap out of, but it wasn’t. The fear had gripped him too hard for rational thought.

“He wanted the Elves to live the way they used to. So, tell me, child.” Pinaka’s face remained blank as he leaned closer, eyes locked on Pronto’s. “Do I grant his final wish, or not?”

“N-No, I… it’s not… my place to say…” Pronto wanted to say no, but the words wouldn’t come out right. It felt like the question wasn’t meant to be answered.

“It’s good that you know your place.” Pinaka gave a small clap. “So, here’s what we’ll do. We’ll be killing a large number of Humans from now on.”

“And you…” He patted Pronto on the cheek. “You’ll be my mount. If you serve me well, I’ll grow a vast forest, teeming with life, right in Human territory when all of this is over—enough to meet their needs three times over.”

“If you cooperate, the Human Race will benefit in the end. Otherwise, only complete destruction awaits them.” Pinaka tilted his head slightly. “So, what’s your answer?”

“I, Pronto, swear to heed the World Tree’s every command willingly,” Pronto said.

“Good. Then you’ll use your powers whenever I tell you to.” The pressure from Pinaka’s palm on Pronto’s cheek increased just a bit. “To help you cope, I’ll gently shut out your senses—except your hearing—when I give the signal.”

“Gah!” Pronto gasped, and then... nothing. It felt like the world had vanished. In reality, Pinaka had sealed his eyes, ears, and mouth shut.

“Phew, that was nerve-wracking.” Pinaka flopped down onto the ash-covered road, a bit drained from the whole encounter. His nerves were frayed after everything he’d done. If even a single moment had gone differently—or if Pronto had managed to steady himself—this would’ve dragged on much longer.

Pinaka had prepared enough to yank Pronto back down if he tried to take off. But if his timing had been off, even slightly, and Pronto had taken flight, he would’ve soared out of reach.

Beyond a certain height, Pinaka’s arrows wouldn’t be able to touch him. That was the worst-case scenario.

That’s why Pinaka had created a crowd of himself—to rattle Pronto and stop him from tapping into his full strength. ‘Thankfully, that worked in my favour.’

Most of all, the Ogre Race’s Relic was his trump card. Ever since he trapped the metal carriage in the pit, Pinaka had been subtly steering Pronto’s attention toward it, feeding into his reluctance to leave without the Relic he’d worked so hard to secure.

Now that Pronto was both physically and mentally drained, Pinaka could finally move on to the next part of his plan. He turned to face the crowd of his clones. “How does it look?”

After Pronto passed out from staring at the fake Status Window Pinaka had crafted, Pinaka used the time to create as many clones of Pronto as he could. That way, they’d at least have basic thinking and reflexes.

That was how they could scream when tortured.

Only after he was satisfied with the results did Pinaka wake Pronto up. From there, everything had played out in his favor.

Meanwhile, the group of Pinaka clones had been busy scrubbing away any sign of a fight. One of them, who had access to his authority, focused on restoring the forest—removing the scorched patches, regrowing vegetation, collecting the arrows, and piling up any remnants.

Pronto’s companions were dead. They’d suffocated inside the metal carriage—some of them skewered by the expanding arrows fired in.

The Pinaka clones disposed of the bodies after the one with authority converted them into manure and fed it to the surrounding trees. Now, all that remained in the pit was the metal carriage and the Ogre Race’s Relic.

Stone Relic of Ogres—Sky Boulder!

It was a stone shaped like a pyramid, with rough, chiseled edges and a coarse surface speckled with patches of moss. About a metre in size, it hovered gently in the air, tethered to the floor by thick metal chains.

The chains creaked nonstop, strained to their limits. They were visibly worn, some nearly torn from the constant rocking the metal carriage had taken during Pinaka’s assault.

The Sky Boulder weighed just over a tonne, yet it could generate enough lift to carry ten times its own weight. It was the ideal core for building a flying ship. Unlike Sun Stones, which required a Human to activate them, the Sky Boulder’s power remained active on its own. It didn’t need an Ogre’s touch to function.

That made Sky Boulders incredibly valuable to the Human Race—and also why no one had managed to secure one until now. They were rare, and every last one was fiercely guarded by the Ogres.

They could only be found deep within Ogre territory.

“It’s a floating rock,” one of the clones remarked after opening the hatch that led into the chamber housing the Sky Boulder. It walked up and pressed a hand against the Relic. “Feels about as hard as granite.”

“Pronto, my dear.” Pinaka unsealed Pronto’s ears and mouth. “My kids are clueless. Why don’t you explain the Ogre Race’s Relic to them?”

“Yes,” Pronto jolted awake, the sudden return of his senses making him feel less like a corpse. He began explaining dutifully, “From what I know, a Sky Boulder’s power is passive. It can lift ten times its own weight. If it’s left untethered, it’ll drift into the sky and eventually find its way back to Ogre territory.”

Stone Authority of Ogres—Heavenward Mountain!

An inverted mountain that stretched endlessly into the sky, its peak hung low near the ground. The moment anyone stepped into Ogre territory, it loomed overhead, blotting out the sky.

“Even if ground to dust, fragments of a Sky Boulder will eventually settle on the Heavenward Mountain. According to Ogre records,” Pronto explained, “that dust will gather and form a new Sky Boulder.”

‘That’s good enough for me.’ Pinaka thought, then sealed Pronto’s senses once more. He gave a command to one of his clones, using his Authority. “Break off a piece that’s a tenth of Pronto’s weight.”

“Okay,” the clone said, walking up to the Sky Boulder. A small shrub sprouted from its hand and latched onto the floating stone. The roots slowly spread, wrapping around the surface until they found weak points to pierce.

As the roots grew, they absorbed faint particles from the stone. During this process, those particles reacted chemically—and in doing so, lost their floating properties.

A World Tree Fruit could be burned to ash. A Sun Stone could be melted by extreme heat. And a Sky Boulder could be undone through chemical reaction. Every Relic had its vulnerabilities.

Once the right method was found, the roots burrowed deeper, expanding inside the boulder. They formed a layer beneath the surface, and as the roots thickened, they fractured the stone—cleanly breaking off a portion.

Two clones quickly grabbed hold of the broken piece and were lifted slightly as it reacted to their touch. After adjusting their grip, they steadied themselves and hauled it out of the chamber.

A second group of clones carefully placed the fragment into a large mortar and began grinding it down with a heavy pestle. Meanwhile, another group held up an umbrella-like sheet to catch the drifting Sky Boulder dust as it formed, collecting it into separate containers—each weighed down enough to stay grounded despite the lift generated by the dust.

While all this was happening, Pinaka got to work reshaping Pronto.

He stripped away the excess mass—limbs and all.

The nervous system remained surrounded by the core organs, now protected by necessary tendons and muscle tissue for cushioning, all encased in a light bone structure. Around that, he added a layer of wood, so it wouldn’t feel grotesque to the touch. After all, he was turning Pronto into a staff—bulbous at the top, with two irregular bumps along the shaft.

The two bumps housed the lungs. The bulbous head contained the brain. And at the very top, above the rounded crown, Pinaka embedded the Sun Stone. Pronto’s eyes were fixed just below it, facing outward, so they could see wherever the staff was aimed.

All orifices were neatly arranged along the staff. The mouth, ears, and nose were spaced along the stem. And at the very bottom tip—yes, the rectum, left intact to handle waste. Every basic function of Pronto’s body was preserved. The wooden casing simply allowed Pinaka to seal the openings when needed.

“Pronto, my dear.” Pinaka said, unsealing the staff’s eyes and ears while pointing at a pile of arrows and half-burnt tree bark. “Burn it all, will you?”

Pronto couldn’t speak—the mouth was sealed—but he responded. Fire seeped into the Sun Stone, and a stream of flame burst out.

“Thank you,” Pinaka said once the pile had burned down enough. The remaining flames were drawn back into the Sun Stone, leaving behind nothing but a small heap of ash. Then, he asked, “Show me your status.”

‘Good thing I attacked his mind more than his body,’ Pinaka thought as he sealed Pronto’s senses once again. ‘He really is an absurd monster for his age.’

“Well,” Pinaka said, rubbing his cheek against the staff, “that monster is now my staff. And since he’s alive, he’ll keep on growing.”

“I’m officially a magician now—with a growth-type artifact!” For a few seconds, Pinaka lost himself in the fantasy, fully leaning into the magician cosplay.
