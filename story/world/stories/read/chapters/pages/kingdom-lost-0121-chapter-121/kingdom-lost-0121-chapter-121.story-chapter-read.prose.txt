Chapter 121

Bang, bang, bang against the door.

"Warden. It's first light."

Riley's eyes opened. She lay still for a beat, the ceiling above her, breath slow. Then she sat up.

"Yeah. I'll be down."

The bed released her the way a bed like that released someone. Reluctantly. The mattress had held the shape she'd left in it overnight, and the wool along the outside edge was still warm. She swung her legs over the side and put her feet on the floor.

That bed is going to be a problem, she thought. She had known it the first time she'd put her hand on the blanket. She had known it again in the dark. She knew it now.

I want to spend the whole day in it.

She stretched once, dressed fast, and went down.

The stairs were still new under her boots. Second landing, third, first floor. She crossed the great room where the hearth sat cold and clean and she pushed through the inner doors, then the outer.

She pulled the outer door open.

The yard was full.

Every soldier they had picked stood in the yard, in three ranks facing her door, in full battle plate, helms on. Thorne and Valrik stood at the front. Behind them a saddled horse waited, held by a groom, empty stirrups turned out toward her.

She stood in the open doorway a moment.

Thorne saluted. Valrik saluted a half-beat behind him, cleanly. The three ranks saluted after them, all together, and the sound of gauntlets meeting cuirasses came up out of them one time, and then it was quiet.

"Warden," Thorne said. "The soldiers are ready. We wait your command."

Riley did not move for a moment.

She had drawn the plan. She had named the count of archers, the count of infantry, the count of cavalry. She had said at dawn. And here they were in the yard because she had said it. What happened in the country between this yard and the depot, and inside the depot after, would happen because she'd said that too.

She stepped forward.

"We ride out."

Thorne's mouth moved into the smallest of smiles. Valrik nodded once. The ranks broke together, the horses came up behind them, and the column began to form on the gate.

Riley mounted.

***

They rode out through the gate and headed north.

The sky was a pale pearl overhead, thin, and the light was still low enough to sit under the trees without much colour in it. Thorne rode at her right. Valrik at her left. Behind them the column and she could hear the low sound of them talking before the settlement gate was even out of sight.

"As you ordered we hand-picked them," Thorne said. "Level Twos. Equal split. Archers, infantry, cavalry. The saddles will all ready for return load, whatever you decide to bring back."

She nodded.

She was listening but part of her had already gone to the men behind. Someone had said four the way you say a thing you weren't supposed to say aloud, and someone else had picked it up. It was moving up the column in small pieces.

Her face did not change.

"Valrik and I have been working on something," Thorne said. "For launching the pots further than a hand can throw. We had one of the Hoshin carve an end onto a spear. Curved. Like the bowl of a spoon. The pot sits in the cup and the spear throws it clean of the arm."

"How far."

"Twice a man's throw, in the tests. Better with the right man on it."

She was about to answer when the word came up from further back. Not a mutter now.

"Rank four?"

Thorne stopped talking.

Valrik, on her left, spoke without turning his head.

"We should decide on arrangements for carrying the fallen back."

Riley’s face hardened and she pulled back on her reins.”

"Column, halt."

The command echoed back through the men, first from a voice at her shoulder, then from halfway down, then from further behind, until it had walked the whole length of the column and the hooves came to a stop.

She sat in the saddle a moment. Both hands on the reins. She was looking at the trees along the road and she was not seeing them.

The men she had lost at the raiders' compound. The count Nikola had brought her the morning after, quietly, in the tower. The reflex it triggered. Valrik going down on the longhouse floor. What Valrik had told her at the bedside about the dark and the glow, about the things he had seen there that were not only the things that happened.

She let a breath out.

Then she turned her horse to face the column.

"Listen."

Not loud. Loud enough.

The muttering had already stopped when she'd halted. Now the quiet was total. Even the horses were still.

"I heard the word four in the column. So, we're going to say it out loud. Where we are going, some of the men on the walls are level four. That is true. That is why we picked you. That is why we drilled you. That is why the saddles are packed the way they are, and the pots are packed the way they are, and the plan is what it is."

She held the moment then continued.

"We've hit superior forces before. The raiders were more than us and we took the day. This one is a bigger climb. I am not going to pretend otherwise."

She looked along the ranks.

"But we ride with Valrik. We ride with Thorne. We ride with weapons the country ahead has never seen. We ride with surprise. That depot is not ready for what we are bringing to it. We are what it does not expect. That is why we win. Not because we are lucky. Because we brought the fight we chose."

She did not raise her voice at any point.

She looked along the column and waited.

What came back was not what she'd expected. It came quieter. A settling. Shoulders coming down. Chins coming up a small measure. Men who had been holding a number in their chest putting it down.

Thorne had been watching her the whole time. His face had held the same expression as usually when she spoke, complete attention. But something in it had begun to change while she was speaking, and by the time she'd finished it had changed all the way. He turned his horse in one clean movement and faced the column.

"You heard the Warden."

He looked down the line.

"Too long has the north bled for the Clawborn. Who will fight for the Hoshin?

He drew his sword.

Who will rise against the lawless country? Who will stand for what is owed? When does the tyranny end?"

The column moved at each question. Chests came out. A yes came from somewhere in the middle, then a few more.

Thorne lifted the sword.

"Let me hear the cry that will put the fear of the gods in Clawborn hearts."

The roar came up out of the column in one wave. Thorne roared with them, sword up, his own voice inside theirs.

Riley watched from her saddle.

Right, she thought. He does the speeches from now on.

***

They rode the rest of the morning and through the afternoon, and the sun began to sink low in the sky.

By the time Thorne raised a hand from his saddle and slowed his horse, the light had gone long and yellow along the road and the cold had come up out of the ground. The trees had thinned. The road ran on into a stretch that opened onto flat country beyond the last of the alders.

He nodded at the tree line ahead.

"Close," he said. "It sits just beyond that."

Riley slowed. The column slowed behind her.

Above them the sky had gone a low grey without weight to it, a sky that was not going to rain and was not going to clear. A single bird crossed it and did not call. The wind came thin along the road at their backs and did not stop and did not start, only stayed, cold, steady.

Valrik moved his horse up alongside her.

"Warden. The men we planned to engage on foot should dismount here. Tie the horses off. Leave a guard detail."

"Do it."

He turned in the saddle and gave the order, sharp and short, and the men peeled out of the column in the pattern they had been drilled in for it. Horses were led off the road into the trees. Ropes came out. The guard detail formed without being named twice.

Riley sat her horse and watched the last of the sun.

Thorne came to a stop at her right. Valrik came back to her left when the dismount was clean. Neither of them spoke.

She looked past the trees at the flat country and the darker line she could just make out through the alders that would be the depot's south wall, and the four small shapes at the corners that would be the towers.

"We wait for nightfall," she said. "Then; we take them."
