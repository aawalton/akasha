Ethan had been holding on to a vain hope that this moment might not come, that he might be able to take The Throne without facing Prince Calevaro, one of his closest friends. He might have been able to take out the Emperor with the Leviathan, or just find that the Prince had otherwise been unable to join the battle.

He’d hoped for the best, but he’d planned for the worst. Experience had taught Ethan again and again that it took more than wishing for something to win. Now he’d have to face a man that had trained him, and fought beside him on two worlds.

He refused to let it end in Cal’s death.

Ethan had seen the prince before in this state, seen him fighting against the Basilisk, covered in the inky black Familiar of the Emperor, himself. Yet Cal hadn’t been transformed, for whatever reason.

He’d fought with his normal techniques, and considerable skill. Ethan refused to believe that such ability could be perfectly carved from a mind, or a soul. If he still fought like Calevaro, then there was a good chance that somewhere inside, he still was Prince Calevaro.

Ethan had considered various methods of bringing his friend to the surface. From somehow attacking the darkborne Familiar directly, to just fighting it out with his old friend and hoping for the best. But in the end, he’d felt there was one way above the others that seemed to have the best chance.

He trusted Cal, and the man’s remarkable will. So Ethan chose to trust that the man could still fight against the Emperor’s control. He just needed the right circumstances to jolt the prince’s might back to the surface.

And Ethan had prepared, as best as he was able. He’d searched deep inside for the fragments of an ability he’d sacrificed some time ago, in order to gain the dimensional ascendancy he was now counting on.

It wasn’t enough, not on its own, but Ethan had more than one connection to rely on. He had Revan, he had Valanor, and he even had Tomo’s mystic abilities to bridge a few gaps. All together, he believed it would be enough for a single roll of the dice–he wouldn’t have the mana for more.

“[Unstable Bleed].”

***

Valanor was in the fight of his life. This “Emperor” was proving to understand his own Master rank power to a disturbing degree. He moved exactly as fast as he needed to in order to avoid every significant attack. He countered with the precise strength needed to push Valanor off balance, or send Savilar sprawling away.

Every few moments, he would attempt a killing blow, and it was all Valanor could do to keep them alive. All his own mana was going toward his defenses, and it still wasn’t enough. Only the arrival of Dean Bishop had kept the fighting going for these few, desperate minutes, and now even that advantage had been lost.

Prince Calevaro was here.

There could be no doubt who the man wielding twin longswords must be. Valanor had helped train the prince for many years, and fought beside him as well. Valanor couldn’t imagine how he’d been captured by these… “Followers”, but it was one more reason why the Emperor had to die.

If only Valanor truly believed that was likely.

Prince Calevaro had more than evened the odds, and he moved through the battle like a raging zephyr. He’d reached Twilight rank–Valanor still felt a hint of pride at that, even under the circumstances–and the prince was proving every bit the remarkable threat Valanor had known he would become.

There had to be some way to snap the boy out of this…

Valanor saw none, and for a few more heartbeats could do nothing more than struggle to keep them all alive, putting himself continually between the two deadly opponents, and Valanor’s less sturdy allies.

In truth, only Dean’s clever use of those gravitational orbs was preventing this all from coming to an abrupt end.

Hopelessness was beginning to set in when a flash of blue-white came from behind, and Valanor risked a glance back. His eyes widened at the sight.

“Prince Kentevaro?” he whispered, unable to believe what he was seeing.

But there could be no doubt. He’d known Calevaro’s older brother for his entire life. He’d been there when the boy had died… The hulking man in deep purple armor was every bit his father’s son–at least in appearance. His temperament had always been more his mother, but she was absent in the aggressive stance the prince took, burning two-handed blade raised in challenge.

“Brother!” Kentevaro bellowed. “Face me! If you’re truly a son of Viridus, then face me and prove it!”

Prince Calevaro didn’t move–in fact, he froze entirely, staring at the armored figure, slowly approaching. But Dean hurried things along, a gravity orb slamming into the prince from behind, then dragging him toward his waiting brother.

Kentevaro’s heavy sword came down in a brutal arc, and only a sudden gust of wind saved the possessed prince. He flew up, and over his opponent, landing behind his brother, then shooting forward, weapons raised.

Kent was already moving, his sword whistling through the air to intercept the attack. With a ring of steel echoing, the duel was joined.

Valanor knew that he should focus on his own battle. They were back to three on one, but the Emperor was no less dangerous. He moved with a palpable hunger for blood, clawed hands raking against shield, armor, and demonic flesh, and each strike seemed ready to end the battle.

Yet time and again, Valanor felt his gaze drawn to the unlikely duel taking place nearby.

The princes were locked in a deadly exchange, yet to Valanor’s trained eye, neither one seemed to be fighting with quite the expertise they were known for. Calevaro moved in a somewhat stuttering way, absent his usual grace. He seemed unsure of himself, and loathe to commit to a real attack.

Kentevaro on the other hand… he was fast, and determined, and his technique appeared correct, yet he only displayed a fraction of the skill with a blade that Valanor remembered. He didn’t take proper advantage of the weapon’s reach, and never seemed quite prepared for its weight–and Twilight rank, that blade would be staggeringly heavy.

The fighting went back and forth, Calevaro’s confused assault meeting Kentevaro’s desperate swings. And all the while, the older brother seemed to be taunting–or at least challenging–the younger.

“Is this truly who you are, Cal? Someone’s puppet?”

“Are you lost in one of father’s rages? You were always the one who kept your temper before.”

“Do you truly seek to kill me, brother? Is that what you’ve become?”

The accusations and demands continued in an endless stream–Valanor couldn’t remember Prince Kent talking so much–but whatever the lost prince was hoping for, he didn’t appear to be getting it. Calevaro showed more and more of his true skill, his speed and precision increasing as the duel raged on.

Soon, Kent was in a stumbling retreat, and injuries began appearing. Anywhere that a blade could slip between plates of armor, blood trickled down, and Kentevaro slowed more and more.

Valanor longed to step between them, yet his own part in this battle remained clear, however hopeless it might seem. Prince Kent–or the man Valanor was near-certain wore the man’s form–was on his own.

***

This was not going according to Ethan’s plan.

Calevaro had seemed hesitant, at first. Ethan had met the man, blade against blade, and even seemed to be winning him over, for a time. But the longer they fought, the more the cracks in “Prince Kent” began to show.

The problem was, fragmented ability or not, there simply was no possible dimension where a real, living Prince Kentevaro was on Potentia, in this place, at this time. That meant reaching further, and grasping only hints of the man’s real presence.

Ethan offset that with memories from Revan, and illusions and conjurations from Tomo, but it just wasn’t enough. As the fight continued, Calevaro grew more and more dangerous, while Ethan only grew slower.

He bled from a dozen small wounds, and his recently broken left arm still ached. He’d burned through nearly every drop of mana he possessed, and his disguise hampered Prime–his sentient, Familiar armor.

Slowly but surely, he was losing, and seemed no closer to drawing the real Calevaro out.

But this should have worked! Ethan knew of no one the prince had been closer to, no one that the man would be more hesitant to raise a blade against. Ethan had, of course, considered King Rothavaro, but the father and son had a complex relationship.

Even beyond that, Cal had trained against his father his entire life, as well as a considerable amount in the last year, on the road to Twilight. There was little to suggest that the Prince would hesitate to attack the king.

Ethan tried to make himself think, but he was just so, damned tired. He was retreating, swinging wildly with a weapon he was unfamiliar with, and his limbs were numb from blocking a hundred frenzied strikes from the dogged Calevaro. Yet he needed a new plan, he needed to–

The clang that ran out made Ethan’s mouth drop in surprise. The prince had lulled him into a pattern, then backed him up against a boulder. The moment he came to an unexpected halt, the prince had performed a complex disarming maneuver, and the two handed blade had gone sailing through the air.

Ethan could still solve this, he could conjure another blade, he could–

The feel of cold steel running through his middle was as much as shock as it was painful. Ethan had been in so many battles, been slashed and stabbed so many times, but so rarely truly run-through. He froze in disbelief, looking down at the sword buried up to the hilt in his flesh, dug so deep it pinned him to the rock behind.

He looked up to see the second blade poised for the finishing strike, this one aimed for his throat. As Ethan’s concentration faded, [Unstable Bleed] ended, and the illusions fell away.

There was nothing left but that blade, driving toward his throat. Ethan met it with his eyes open, knowing he’d given everything he had this day.

The blade halted, the tip drawing the tiniest bit of blood as the inky black, featureless face of Cal stared down at him.

“Brother?” the man whispered, the sounds slightly muffled beneath the flesh of the alien Familiar.

In his confusion and pain, Ethan couldn’t make sense of the word. His illusion had fallen off, hadn’t it? Why would Cal possibly see his brother now? Then it became clear.

“Brother,” Cal repeated, sounding confused. “Brother…Bishop…Ethan?”

The sword withdrew.
