Dozens of dirty, flea-bitten swarm scouts sniffed at the wall of rock. It was undoubtedly solid stone, and their keen noses picked up no way through, but that couldn’t be.

Rakataks, the scout captain, knew this was the carrion wyrm’s lair. It was clearly marked on their maps as a no-go area. It had been that way for generations, and there was no way a master pathfinder like Rakataks could have misled them.

Impossible. Unless… no, that couldn’t be.

The scout captain passed over possibilities in his mind, but none made sense. The surface invaders had come this way, but there was no way they could kill a carrion wyrm brood mother.

The wall was stone, but his senses told him it wasn’t particularly thick. Thick enough to keep him and his scouts out, but not the wyrm.

Even more troubling, they had been trying to find the path taken by the surface dwellers, and every passage they came across was unpassable. Of course, cave-ins did happen, but this was too much. How could every passageway to that cavern have collapsed?

No, that was impossible—not without an earthquake that would have been felt by this entire section of the Undercavern.

“Ssswarm, move out!” Rakataks shouted over the squeaks and squirming.

He was troubled by this development, but there were still other old and hidden passages to try, and he would not stop until the swarm lord’s word had been fulfilled.

The great white rat sniffed the air as he turned to leave. The leader of the scouts was one of the few dire rodakin within their swarm and was almost twice the size of his kin. Towering above men and possessing unnatural strength and durability, he was a rat to be feared.

But at this moment, it was he who felt fear trembling beneath his fur. Who were these strange outsiders who had, in no time, blocked off so many passages and found a means of dealing with a dragon?

It wasn’t just rodakin who avoided the great wyrm. Neither the dwarves nor the Arcanites trespassed here. It wasn’t worth it. But these outsiders, only a few thousand ragged people, based on the reports, dared? It was inconceivable.

If this is truly what they are capable of now, what might they achieve with time?

Alarm bells rang through the scout captain’s thoughts. He needed hard evidence. Something he could bring back to the Swarm Lord, and he needed it now.

“Force march!” He screamed at the top of his lungs, ordering his hundred or so scouts into an exhausting pace.

The time for dallying was over.

Gradually, ore started flowing into the smithies to be worked into tools and equipment, and coal-fuelled their forges.

On the other hand, timber still eluded them. Carmack had spotted trees in the large cavern above, but they hardly constituted forests. Exploiting such resources would not be efficient and likely wouldn’t even provide them with enough timber for their needs.

Carmack himself had spent his entire life beneath the ground, and living without timber was hardly new to him. However, there were more than a few growing voices sharing their discontent. Many people wanted to return to the surface despite what had been provided for them. And the inability to provide creature comforts they were used to like timber furniture, only served to elevate these feelings.

One solution was to put more gatherers to work in the lower tunnels, searching for giant mushrooms. The fibrous mushrooms at least provided materials for decent mattresses and pillows, even above what the average commoner possessed in the old city.

Still, more work was required to quell the chatter, and even then, they would likely lose people to migration once trade routes with the surface were secured.

If we are to convince people to stay here, three things will be required: security, wealth, and comfort, Carmack mused.

Before he could risk opening a trade route, valuable goods and well-paid work were required. Otherwise, people would leave en masse.

The minithral beneath them could make them a fortune, but Carmack was determined to ensure their military needs were fulfilled before trading any. And that could take some time.

What can we leverage?

Carmack’s thoughts wandered to the market floor he had hollowed out and the craftsmen who had started selling everything they had. All food was still required to be handed into the store to be rationed, but everything else, including ration tokens, was fair game. Although, metal tools were prioritized for, and provided freely to craftsmen who needed them.

Carmack eyed the dozens of tightly packed stalls manned by riffraff harking their goods to bypassing citizens. His eyes perked as he spotted a door along the wall at the market’s end.

“Cecil’s Emporium?”

Huh, I wondered what he was up to.

Filled with all kinds of goods, wheelbarrows, vases, chests, and cabinetry, the inside of Cecil’s Emporium stood in stark contrast to most of the settlement. There were a few dark rumors about the store, but many witnesses also spoke of the wagons Cecil and his guards had brought down with them into the caverns. And as such, no proof could be provided that the goods were not legally Cecil’s.

At the far end of the store, behind the counter, a crooked-nosed noble with a straight bowl cut was craned over by two brawny guards.

“Damn it,” Cecil hissed and threw away the letter he had just read. “They're not going to compensate us. Not a single copper! Tis not I who failed to guard that damned city!”

“Very unreasonable,” the guard at his side said and shook his head.

“I know, right? Everything I put into that cursed city is gone, and not so much as a thank you. Curse them, the lot of them. This is totally unfair!”

“For shame,” the guard agreed.

“They have no understanding of what we’re going through. Despicable, really. But…”

The guards nodded as they listened to the noble’s complaints for the millionth time.

“If that scoundrel can put action behind his words, then perhaps not all is lost. But we can’t just sit around and wait,” Cecil sighed.

Bells chimed, and when Cecil turned to see the Great Hero stepping into his store, he almost jumped out of his chair.

“Ah—boss—the hero,” one of the guards pointed.

“I know, damn it, lower your finger!”

“Hello, it’s Cecil, right?” The hero said as his heavy, long strides carried him across the store in seconds.

“Yes,” Cecil forced a greasy smile. “But to you, it's Lord Cecil Bartholomew the third of Damchester Abbey.”

“Damchester Abbey? Was that somewhere in Lydrus?”

“Of course, it was in Lydrus, you peasant! It was the largest town under the crown besides the great city itself!”

“Oh, sorry to hear that. I suppose that means you're no longer the lord. You know, since this Damchester Abbey no longer exists. Considering that, would it be okay if I just stuck to Cecil?”

“Why y–”

“Ah, gemstones,” Carmack said, pointing past Cecil to a sign that read, “Ask about gemstones.”

“What?” Cecil swung around as his face turned red.

“Pardon me, but I wasn’t aware anybody had gemstones to sell here. By happenstance, I’m actually in the market for one at this moment. Do you, by any chance, have a large ruby?”

The absolute nerve of this peasant! Who does he think I… The thought trailed off. Cecil had promised Dimitri—The Gray Talon of Lydrus—that he could sell his gemstones. Unfortunately, that was proving more difficult than he had expected. It seemed the refugees of Lydrus didn’t have much coin, and what they did have was spent on necessities.

“Perhaps,” Cecil said, almost choking on the word.

“Brilliant,” Carmack said, dropping a heavy pouch on the counter. “Please, show me this ruby.”

Gingerly, Cecil pulled the string, and the pouch poured open, spilling gold coins onto the table.

“H-how?”

“You don’t need to mind that. Now, please, if you wouldn’t mind showing me the ruby.”

Forcing a twitching smile, Cecil nodded and then snapped his fingers. A moment later, one of his men brought a chest out and placed it on the table. Opening it, he revealed a sparkling ruby the size of a knuckle.

“Hmm, impressive quality,” Carmack said, picking up the ruby between his fingers and raising it to a light as he inspected it.

“It is. Precisely why it won’t come cheap.”

“I'll take it.” Carmack’s large hand closed around the ruby. “Nice doing business with you, Cecil.”

The noble couldn't force out a response in time as the hero turned to leave, the gold coins still on the benchtop. The truth was, as much as he hated doing anything for that hero, he needed the coin.

“Boss, are you alright?”

“Just shut up,” Cecil hissed.

Carmack was cautious about doing business with the noble. He didn’t want to enrich someone who had clearly set themselves against him. But he needed to develop this city, which required law, order, and commerce. But more importantly, he couldn’t run around like a tyrannical thug. He would lead by example, but if Cecil and his kind were unwilling to follow the rules, they would experience his uncooperative side soon enough.

But for now, he had the ruby he was after and one step closer to providing the magical item he had promised Aphexia.

As he put pieces together, an idea started brewing. There was one thing better than having strong trade routes: having something people would travel to you for. If they had something rare and valuable enough that others would travel down to them to purchase, that would be far more valuable than just opening trade routes.

He thumbed the monster core and ruby in his hand. Both items were immensely valuable on their own, but it was the sum total of what they could become that stole his attention.

There were four elements that went into magical items that determined their value: the quality and size of the gemstone, the power of the monster core, the purity of the minithrel, and the skill of the enchanter. And now, Carmack possessed the highest level enchanting skill.

While they had yet to be secured, all of these ingredients should be sourceable within the Undercavern…

A spark of power crackled across Carmack’s hand as he held the items. He was channeling mana through them and testing their makeup.

These will make for a fine blade. All I need now is some minithrel.

Through the long discussions he regularly held with Managra, Carmack had learned that few spell casters on this world were competent enchanters, and the skill itself was highly prized, since it usually required someone with craft abilities to unlock the skill tree. This was rare because most spellcasters focused on magical pursuits and not crafting.

The thought brought a smirk to his face. He might have to spend more skill points on the enchanter tree to get the most out of it, but even now, what he had was valuable.

We’ll show them the true value of the Undercavern… once we’ve become the masters of magical items in this world.

Carmack’s thoughts swiveled back to the minithrel needed. First, he would provide for Aphexia and expand its reach.

Heading down the spire, he listened for the precious metal humming through his magical senses. Traveling deep into the cavernous bowels beneath their settlement, he reached a place far too deep and treacherous to risk sending his miners.

In the dark ahead, he sensed creatures, but Carmack was one with the earth, and on its rocky surface, his footfalls fell softer than those of an assassin.

Through several chambers, he passed before reaching the rock glowing with an unnatural violet hue.

This confirmed it—there was no doubt in his mind that this minithrel was the same metal used to forge magical weapons and armor on Avalock.

He only needed a little to be folded into the steel used to craft the blade—just enough minithrel to draw the power of the gemstone and magically enchant the weapon.

Placing his palm against the rock, he pulled on the metals within. Separating minerals was difficult at the best of times, and attempting to draw out a heavy, magical mineral like minithral with limited mana reserves truly tested him.

Gasping, Carmack fell forward, catching himself on the rock. He had drained himself, but the ball of purple metal humming with magical power in his palm told him it had been worth it.

Beautiful. This is just the beginning.

Carmack swung around as a furious roar echoed through the tunnels at his back. Closing his hand around the minithrel, Carmack inched backward as another grosteque roar chilled the air.

Seconds later, heavy, scuttling steps followed.

Okay, that guy sounds angry.
