Focusing into the darkness in front of me, I concentrated harder, picturing my body… nothing more than a sculpture or a shadow. In moments, a body emerged in front of my non-existent eyes, though I still couldn’t see it fully, just my hands and a leg—but I was fairly confident it was mine.

Slowly, I turned my gaze down, focusing at the center of my chest. Bright light emerged suddenly, a mixture of violet and crimson. My core was perfect… though slightly dimmer than normal.

Maybe I haven’t recovered properly… I murmured inwardly.

Since focusing on the mana vessels wasn’t really important for the task at hand, I looked to the wand clenched in my right hand.

A stream of violet energy flowed into the wand; it drew my mana just as intended.

Just push the mana to the right, I thought inwardly, attempting to change the flow of energy.

The violet mana obeyed my call. I felt the sensation shift—the mana that had been seeping into the solid wand now moved into the air.

The task was simple enough; releasing mana in the chosen direction was something everyone learned as a kid. Was I ever taught it, though? I couldn’t recall.

My vision wavered, distracted by the thoughts in my mind. I lost the ability to see the mana flow. My eyes pressed harder, then relaxed. The flow of mana returned before me, and I looked at it calmly, careful not to think of anything else.

Just as I had envisioned, the bright violet mana didn’t move straight, nor fully heed my call. It moved somewhere in between. And as soon as it exited my range, the mana answered only to the pull of the wand, moving straight to the stone along another diagonal line.

"It works, huh…" I whispered, slowly opening my eyes. Everything around my non-existent eyes vanished as my real eyes adjusted to the little moonlight seeping through the curtains.

I let out a long exhale and placed the wand at my side. My eyes felt hot, as if my eyelids were on fire.

I wasn’t sure whether it was because of burnout or a cold I had caught, but the quick shiver running down my spine gave me the answer. I was probably going to be sick the next day.

Not wanting another burnout while struggling with a cold, I called it quits.

"Such a failure I am," I murmured, frustrated at myself. I mean, who gets a cold in the rainy season without rain? That’s borderline pathetic.

With a frustrated sigh and an overdramatic yawn, I left the bed and moved to the study table, placing my wand there before heading to the Icelure, feeling a strange yet familiar sensation in my nose.

I looked up at the Icelure. Cold air blew at my face. Though slower than usual, it felt colder than normal.

If tomorrow were a normal day, I would’ve turned the Icelure to max speed—to get sick willingly and take a few extra days off from school. Being sick was fun… till it was a cold or flu. Though the flu sucked, I’d probably sleep for two days straight if it were the flu.

"Why is the battle fest so soon, sniff, sniff," I protested, holding back tears. Raising my hand, I grabbed the bigger thread and pulled it seven more times, turning the Icelure off completely.

There’s no way I could take tomorrow off too! I thought. Learning Dark Pulse was important, something I could even sacrifice my comfort for!

Also, the sniff had turned into sniffles. Fuck this cold!

With the Icelure off, I turned around. A small yawn escaped my mouth. Too tired to care whether the cold would worsen, I crawled back to bed, pulled the blanket over me, covering everything except my head, and closed my eyes.

Focusing on the faint humming from the garden, maybe an insect? The sound slowly faded into the background, and I fell asleep.

The next time my eyes opened, it was because of a loud sound, one I couldn’t clearly recall, though I was certain it was loud.

I swallowed. In front of my eyes was a beautiful starry sky, something I would’ve loved to see if I had known it was real. Though it wasn’t, I raised my right arm, bringing it in front of my eyes. It was smaller than usual, like a kid’s, though it was mine—the birthmark on my ring finger confirmed it.

"I’m dreaming, aren’t I?" I murmured, my already high-pitched voice even higher. Why do kids sound like this! I protested in my mind, pushing myself off the ground and dusting myself.

My eyes darted around as I tried to assess my location. Everything around me burned, enveloped in cracking blue flames; the town’s buildings crumbled one by one.

Maybe a collapsing building woke me up? I thought to myself, strolling. There was no reason to hurry—seeing her would only end the dream, anyway. My gaze swept along the landscape as I walked.

I noticed a familiar detail from previous dreams—a Doll-Corpse, as I had called them after the fourth encounter, lying on the ground.

The Doll-Corpses were the only inhabitants of the burning town. I looked around. There were three corpses, but I chose to check the one beside the crumbling building. There wasn’t any specific reason; I just wanted to look at that doll, though I didn’t know why.

Moving closer, I crouched and took hold of the lonely doll’s hand. I pried open her palm and peeled off the white glove she wore. Her hand was smooth, without a single crease; her fingers, featureless. Of course—the doll had no identity.

The girl’s arm was as small as mine. She had probably been separated from her parents before death. My eyes turned to the other doll couple slightly away. They didn’t look like her parents. Her clothes were far prettier… maybe she was a noble of some sort? I thought, looking at the doll’s face.

A face with no eyes to see, no mouth to speak, no nose to smell, no ears to hear… absolutely nothing. I stared for a moment, then averted my gaze, still terrified from the last experience from the previous dream.

With my curiosity satisfied, I let go of the corpse’s hand and moved away, only to stop as the building crumbled behind me, falling over the doll.

Though… it wasn’t as if she had been alive to begin with.

I resumed walking, covering several streets before coming across another Doll-Corpse. This time, it was more of a cluster of Doll-Corpses.

In front of me on the street lay several Doll-Corpses. A few had pale skin pierced, while others, like the previous one, had no visible injury. Perhaps they died from smoke, I presumed, tiptoeing through the cluster to the next street.

The next street was familiar from previous dreams; it was one that was near the first intersection. It didn’t take long for my walk to turn into running.

"Maybe I’d see her better this time," I smiled, excited to meet her.

The only other individual in my dream, who wasn’t a Doll-Corpse and was also the trigger to ending it.

I turned left at the first intersection, ignoring the wreckage. In front of me was a long street, one I could’ve crossed in moments with my actual body, though with my dream one?

All I managed was running with tiny legs.

By the second intersection, I was already huffing continuously, my body tired, so I slowed to a walk, taking long breaths as I walked.

At the second intersection, I turned right. The town center came into view—the stone sculpture of an angel pouring water into a dried fountain stood ahead.

I took a few steps forward, eyes fixed on it. And that’s when I saw her.
