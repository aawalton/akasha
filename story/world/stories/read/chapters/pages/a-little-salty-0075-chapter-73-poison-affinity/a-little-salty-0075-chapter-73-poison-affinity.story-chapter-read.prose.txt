I whirl around, immediately locking eyes with Gugora. He must be able to see something in my expression, because his face softens with a sad smile. “Time to go?”

Most of my stuff is already in my Apothecary, and I don’t have time to grab whatever I’ve left in my room. It will have to wait until I come back—because I will be coming back.

I dash across the room and throw my arms around Gugora; my hands don’t even come close to reaching each other on the other side. He rubs my back.

“I’m sorry,” I say, voice muffled from where it’s buried in his shirt. “My friends are in trouble and they need my help.” He pats my shoulder and I reluctantly let go of him.

“Then you better go help.” Gugora turns his head toward the kitchen. “Iski.” He doesn’t even have to raise his voice for it to boom through the tavern.

A moment later the goblin appears in the doorway. Her hair is pulled back with a wrap of cloth and she’s wiping a knife off on her apron. “What?”

I rush over to her and fall to my knees to wrap her in a hug as well.

“Hey!” she cries, throwing her hands back and out of my range. “I’m holding a knife here!”

Before I can even start to explain, however, she lets out a sigh, patting my head with her free hand. “Don’t wait so long to visit next time, you hear?”

“I won’t,” I promise, letting her go. I look between her and Gugora, two people I never deserved in my life. Two people I’ve only ever caused trouble for, and who have paid me back with unimaginable kindness. “I’m going to make it all up to you, somehow.”

Iski snorts. “Kid. Just go make us proud.”

My throat is too tight to say anything else, so I just nod. I want to say more—there’s a lot more I thought I’d have time to talk about before I had to leave—but life’s never seemed to care about what I want. I stand and race to the entrance, only pausing to look back as I push the heavy door open. Iski has her fists planted on her hips with an encouraging smile, and Gugora lifts a hand in parting. I raise mine as well, then I dash from the Starlight Inn, sprinting down the worn road as the tavern vanishes into the woods behind me.

I run faster than I’ve ever run in my life. Faster than I’ve ever run in both bodies I’ve had. I have faint memories of playing tag and jumping around a playground from when I was a kid, before I got sick, but that was a literal lifetime ago. And now I’m stronger than I’ve ever been, hardened by months of training and dozens of level-ups. It’s clear I’m faster and stronger than I should be, but I’m not sure if it’s the System or the fact that I’m Shirasil’s champion that is giving me the extra stamina I need to keep running. But whenever I start to flag, feet pounding against the dirt, a stitch stabbing into my side, I think of Cyros and his mapa, and I dig deep and find the well of energy I need to keep going.

It still takes me two hours to race all the way back to Fairwood. By the time I make it to Cyros’s tree, my lungs are on fire and I’m gasping for breath. The door to his home is open and I skid through it to find a small crowd gathered around Teltora’s bed.

Cyros, Álvaro, and Nera are all gathered around them, along with an unfamiliar dryad that Echo identifies as a healer. Álvaro looks up as I catch myself on the doorframe, planting a hand to the stitch in my side and struggling to catch my breath.

“Sal!” he cries. “Holy crap, you got here fast!”

Cyros turns to look, and I’m startled to find his eyes bloodshot and watery, as if he’s been crying. I’ve never seen Cyros cry.

“Sal?” he asks, his voice croaky. “What are you doing here?”

I guess Álvaro didn’t relay my invitation to Cyros.

“Let me see them,” I say, shouldering my way into the cluster of people around his mapa.

“Careful,” the healer objects. “They’re in an extremely fragile state!”

That’s an understatement. When I’d last seen Teltora, they’d been bright-eyed and smiling. The dryad I’m looking at now is barely recognizable. Their eyes are closed and their breathing is rapid and shallow. The woody texture of their skin has taken on a yellowish tone, and the leaves in their hair are all wilted. Every once in a while their whole body twitches, but they otherwise don’t seem to be aware of what’s going on around them.

Check, I think.

[Status effect: Major Poisoning,] Echo reports. Their health is low and ticking lower by the minute. When Echo doesn’t provide any elaboration, I activate my Toxic Intuition skill.

Since originally obtaining the ability, all my experiments with poisons (especially by poisoning myself) have leveled it up to lucky number thirteen. Not only can I intuit what poison is being used, I can also figure out what ingredients are needed to brew a cure. As long as we can get all the ingredients in time, I should be able to save them.

[Mana poisoning,] Echo reports as my skill kicks in. [An insidious affliction that contaminates an individual’s mana and corrupts them from the inside out. Only observed in rare circumstances, often manifesting spontaneously, little is known about the cause of such diseases. While there is no known cure, the patient can be sustained longer by cycling fresh mana through their body from an external source.]

I swear, my stomach knotting itself up. Of course it’s magic cancer. I can’t even escape it here, can I?

I instinctively glance toward Cyros; he’s already watching me, and when our gazes meet, his face falls.

“Sal,” he says, before choking on whatever words he meant to say next. But I can see it in his eyes. Can’t you do something? Anything?

“It’s mana poisoning,” I say, looking to the healer in case she knows something my Toxic Intuition doesn’t.

“What?” She looks at me sharply. “How do you know? Are you sure?”

“Is there anything you can do?” I ask.

Her lips press together in a pained look, and she gives the smallest shake of her head.

Cyros sways, and Álvaro jumps to his side, catching him before he can fall.

“Woah, dude!” He helps Cyros stagger to the floor, where he sits heavily. “You’re okay, man, you’re okay. I’ll get some water.”

“This is my fault,” Cyros says, his gaze unfocused and distant. “It’s all my fault. I just wanted to heal them. I thought—maybe this time—”

For a moment, when I look at Cyros, I don’t even see him. Instead it’s me, sitting in a pool of Talia’s blood. The metallic stench still haunts my dreams.

“No,” I growl, turning back to Teltora. “No. I’m not gunna let them die.”

I pull my vial of Attuned poison mana from my waist. It’s all I have, painstakingly harvested over a hundred different sessions, each time funneling my meager amount of mana into a few drops of Attuned liquid. It’s potent—deadly. But so is chemotherapy, depending on the dose.

“What are you doing?” the healer demands as I uncork the bottle. “What is that?”

She wouldn’t like the answer, so I don’t bother explaining. Responding to my mental commands, the Attuned poison lifts from the bottle and hovers over Teltora’s body.

“Hold on,” the healer starts again, but Álvaro stops her.

“Let her try,” he says. “It can’t hurt, can it?”

The healer hesitates, looking at Cyros, but he’s completely shut down. I don’t know if he’s hearing anything we’re saying.

Hesitating a moment more, the healer finally steps away. I spread my hands, and the poison spreads with it, flattening into a thin film of liquid. I press it against the dryad’s body, and feel the mana begin to sink into his body.

I close my eyes at that point, focusing on my Attuned poison. I can feel it spreading slowly through him, and through that, I can see Teltora’s body in my mind as well.

Something zaps me, and I jump. No, not me—it zapped my magic. It happens again, like a firecracker going off in my head. It’s the corrupted mana coming in contact with my Attuned mana. I can almost feel them vying for control, each attempting to infect or poison the other. I try to collect some of my magic into denser patches, and this works, overwhelming the corrupted mana in those spots and neutralizing it. But other parts of his body are left unprotected, and the corruption continues to spread. I could do this if I just had more mana to work with.

I open my eyes and look at my wrist, where Shirasil’s bracelet rests just above the remnant bracelet Álvaro made for me. For some reason, the idea of using it doesn’t fill me with as much disappointment as it had before.

[Noxious Gauntlet activated.]

The healer and Nera gasp. I ignore them as I lay the ghostly green claws against Teltora’s chest.

I can hear the smile in Álvaro’s voice. “That is so sick.”

[Mana Drain activated.]

The mana inside his body shifts like a changing tide, drawn toward the claws. It fizzles and spits as it meets the Noxious Gauntlets, sucked into the claws even as its corrosive touch tries to eat away at the new source of magic. Once again I can sense the two magics warring, both types of magic designed to absorb the other.

The corrupted mana in Teltora begins to leach from his body. Concerningly, however, that’s most of his mana. I’ve experienced mana exhaustion before—scraping the bottom of my well and feeling fatigued as a result. But pushing past that, extracting every drop of magic one has in their body, can be dangerous. And given Teltora is already so weak, pulling all the corrupted mana out of his body might end up killing him anyway.

As I’m mulling this over, trying to figure out what my next move should be, a staticky sensation begins to crawl through the Gauntlet. The corrupted mana is collecting inside the gauntlet, turning the magic a sickly yellow color. That’s probably not good. What happens if it corrupts the Gauntlet before the Gauntlet can absorb the mana? Shoot, I didn’t think this through. I could deactivate the Gauntlet, but then the corrupted mana would no longer be in check, and I have no idea if it would just let that mana loose in the room, or if it would all get pulled into me—I also have no idea which option is worse.

I try to pull my Attuned poison back toward the gloves as well, hoping the combination of poison mana and Mana Drain will be enough to erode the corrupted mana. Mana starvation is still a risk for Teltora, but right now the corruption spreading to me or someone else in the room is quickly becoming the more pressing issue.

The creeping static reaches my wrist and starts to prickle at my hand. I pull even more of my Attuned poison back, trying to cover my skin like a glove, but I’m not sure how effective that will be. I chew my lip, quickly going through my inventory of potions to try to think of something else that might help. I have to have something!

Calx jerks to life on my wrist. Its mind surfaces with distinct irritation, like a cat prodded rudely awake. The corrupted magic is nibbling at its enclosure, which it somehow finds offensive.

“Calx,” I think, focusing on the remnant. “I need your help.”

It only half pays attention to what I’m saying. Helping seems like effort, and more importantly something is keeping it from falling back asleep.

“That’s what I need help with,” I urge. “It’s corrupted magic. Can you do what you did before with the lindwyrm? Can you eat it?”

The suggestion to eat intrigues the remnant slightly more. Calx is pretty hungry—it’s always hungry—and eating is the second best thing after napping. Its presence finally rears up out of the refiner and comes in contact with the corrupted magic.

Calx swallows it in a single bite. It floods through the Noxious Gauntlet, consuming every scrap of diseased mana as if it were as benign as a bowl of noodles. Reaching the edge of the Gauntlet, and still sensing more mana to eat, it begins to sink into Teltora as well.

“Wait!” I think in a panic. “You can’t eat all the mana here. You’ll kill him.”

Calx doesn’t understand what I mean. How much is not all?

“You can only eat the corrupted mana,” I explain.

Once again, the remnant doesn’t understand. Mana is mana. Some have different flavors. It’s all meant to be eaten.

“You can only have one of the flavors here,” I say, trying to meet its thought process in the middle.

It still finds this unsatisfying. Eating some but not others seems like effort. And which is which?

“I’ll show you,” I think, tugging on my Attuned poison that’s still inside the claws. Calx can feel it swirl through the Gauntlet. “I can tell you what mana to eat.”

The remnant still feels dubious about this entire situation, but being led to its next meal sounds convenient.

Taking that as agreement, I start spreading my magic through Teltora’s body once more. Calx hitchhikes along with my Attuned mana, and as I encounter corrupted mana, I give the remnant the OK to chow down. It does so with such efficient laziness that it’s almost scary.

“Gods above,” I can hear the healer say. I’m barely paying attention to the others in the room, too focused on carefully guiding Calx through Teltora’s body, but I can start to hear them stirring.

“I told you,” Álvaro says.

I’m not sure how long I stand there, scraping through every inch of Teltora’s body in search of corrupted mana. Calx eats it up as we find it, and sometimes it even notices tiny scraps that I miss. I start to feel in sync with the remnant, its thoughts reading more clearly to me, like I’m working with it rather than tugging it around.

Eventually, finally, we can’t find any more hints of the corruption. The amount of natural mana left in Teltora is worryingly low, but it’s not zero. That’s not something I can do anything about anyway, and I don’t think Calx knows how to do anything but eat.

“Thank you,” I think, gently guiding my Attuned poison back out of Teltora’s body. There’s significantly less of it now, much of the magic annihilated from coming in contact with the corrupted magic—and it didn’t help that Calx took a couple nibbles from my magic as well, just as a treat. I can’t be mad at it, though. It saved Teltora’s life.

Calx doesn’t acknowledge my thanks, instead curling back down into its refiner for a well-deserved nap.

[Major Poisoning status effect expired.]

[Noxious Gauntlet ended.]

And their HP finally ticks one point up.

I open my eyes, and my lids crack from disuse. Teltora is still unconscious, but now their breathing is slow and steady, and their complexion has darkened back to its normal shade of brown.

“This shouldn’t be possible,” the healer says, her hands lighting with magic as she runs them over the dryad’s prone form. “How did you do that? What did you do?”

I push off the cot, and immediately feel woozy. Teltora isn’t the only one with dangerously low mana levels. I make it to a nearby chair and slump heavily into it, starting to think Calx’s plan to take a nap doesn’t sound so bad after all.

“I knew she could do it,” Álvaro says, his voice giddy with excitement. “I knew she could heal them!”

Heal? I guess so. I wasn’t even thinking about it like that. I was just focused on getting the poison out. I guess that counts as healing, in a way.

“Sal.” Cyros’s voice is close.

I open my eyes to find him back on his feet, standing in front of me. His shoulders are slumped and his face is haggard, but there’s a light in his eyes that wasn’t there before.

“Thank you,” he says, his voice cracking. “You saved Mapa’s life.”

I saved a life. I healed them.

I smile wearily and try to wave Cyros off. But when I go to speak, a sob bubbles up out of me instead.

“Sal!” Cyros grabs my shoulders. “Are you okay? What’s wrong?”

I wipe at my face, but the tears won’t stop, and they don’t bother me this time anyway. I laugh and I cry all at once, looking up at Cyros’s blurry face.

“I healed them.” My chest hurts, and I don’t want it to stop.

Cyros seems to understand what I mean. His grip loosens. “You did.”

I saved someone. My magic did that. Poison magic that I thought would only ever hurt people.

I look over at Cyros’s mapa and I laugh and sob until I have nothing left to give.
