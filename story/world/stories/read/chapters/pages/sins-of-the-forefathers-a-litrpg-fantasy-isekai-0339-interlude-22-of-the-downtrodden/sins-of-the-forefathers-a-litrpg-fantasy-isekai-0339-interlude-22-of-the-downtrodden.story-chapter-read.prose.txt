It began with a bang.

Through the far-eye that Maria had lent her, Sylvia could watch the entire affair from above. Her and her forces were currently camped out inside a tower they had occupied. With the city still on high alert due to the presence of the Savoy forces, this particular example of Rhoscara’s famous belltowers had been abandoned. It was more centrally located, rather than being positioned around the walls, where most of the Rhoscaran armed forces were still camped. Instead of being in a useful location to spot outside forces trying to get in, this tower was somewhere else.

It was between the docks…and the palace.

From above, Sylvia’s trained eye could spot the surreptitious movements of the Unchained as they snuck around the port that Nathan had been abducted from only hours previous. She couldn’t see exactly what they were doing, but she knew regardless. Bleddyn hadn’t exactly held back on telling her just what he and his people were going to do to create a suitable distraction.

Sylvia held her breath in anticipation when the distant, skulking figures finished their jobs, right under the noses of the Savoy standing guard. With incredible haste, they vanished back into the tunnel system from which they had appeared in the first place. They, too, knew what was coming.

In only moments, a bright flash lit up the overcast clouds of the Skyfall. The sudden illumination was great enough to reach the frozen bolts of magenta lightning that was Fynneas’s supposed Mantle of Divinity, casting ominous shadows over the surface of the clouds.

Then came the noise. A sudden crashing sound, akin to the greatest rumblings of thunder that Vereden had ever heard. Rolling, it was loud enough that even miles away, the sound was enough to set her Mithril ears to ringing. Sylvia had only heard a noise louder than that once in all of her short life, and that had been when the entire mountain of Gorenzan had erupted into a fiery, Aetheric conflagration.

It was followed right after by a wave of pressure. A blast wave strong enough that it collapsed buildings all around the docks themselves, which had otherwise survived the explosion itself. It traveled out across the city in a visible burst, quicker than most Classers could even react to. The force of it was great enough to shatter glass all over Rhoscara. Even the lens of the far-eye that Sylvia held in her hands cracked, rendering the device useless.

And finally…the fire. Rising up from the docks was a great ball of white-hot fire. It spread upwards in a strange manner, growing a long stem and unfurling high above the now former port. The combination strangely reminded Sylvia of a great, ancient tree, the rounded bough of it held up by a great trunk.

Only this tree was made of flame.

The Unchained had set off a bomb in the port of Rhoscara. A strong one, too, Alchemical in nature and Mystically enhanced. The only reason that the entire docks weren’t covered in flame was that it had been outright destroyed by the force of the initial blast. The entirety of the port was now shards of scorched timber and shattered stone, floating upon the waters of the Fiume d’Oro.

Many Dwarven bodies could be seen floating within it.

But…

That wasn’t all. The Unchained, in particular Bleddyn, had been trusted with the knowledge of something else in the lead-up to the battle that never was. Though they seemed to have strangely welcomed in the Savoy forces, the military leadership of Rhoscara had created many fallbacks for the defense of their city beforehand.

Including the depth charges that had lain with the river itself.

Sylvia hadn’t even known what those were until a savagely satisfied Bleddyn and a reluctantly impressed Bella had informed her.

Aquatic bombs, weighed down and floating within a watery home, just waiting for the correct signal to detonate.

And detonate they had, all up and down the waters in front of the now ruined docks. Five great plumes of water had shot in the sky as the bombs within the river itself had exploded, at the same time the Alchemical bomb of the Unchained had. The water of the plumes, combined with the fire of the bomb, had mixed just enough to create a scalding, misty rain that fell over half of the city, so great was the force of both strikes.

Every single Savoy ship that had weighed anchor before the port of Rhoscara, iron-clad or not, had been instantly decimated by river-bound mines. Great hulks of timber and iron now listed in the waters, shattered by the pressure of the bombs. Some had been reduced to shards, while others were sinking into the deep waters of the d’Oro.

One thing was for certain, however. The entire breadth of the river before the port was completely inaccessible, now. It was occupied only by shattered bulks and the dead bodies of hundreds of Dwarves. Sylvia wasn’t sure she had ever seen such an instantaneous loss of life before. There had to have been nearly a thousand Velancian soldiers on those now destroyed ships.

Including the ship that Leonard Ashran had been upon, to the best of her knowledge. Sylvia dearly wished that the traitorous former Warden of Caer Drarrow had been annihilated in the blasts, but she unfortunately doubted it.

The man had a near cockroach degree of tenacity when it came to survival. No doubt he had escaped, Fynneas’s crown in tow. He was too powerful to have been assassinated by something as mundane as a bomb.

“Gods…” Sylvia heard from behind her. Lowering the now useless far-eye, she turned to see that the words had come from Renauld. There weren’t many people in their conspiracy up at the top with her, as most of them were stationed at the ground floor of the bell tower, watching for enemies. The Gnoll was shaking his head in open wonder at the force of the conflagration. “Look at that…where the hells did Bleddyn get bombs like that?”

“It doesn’t matter,” Sylvia heard Maria say from her left, where she had been crouching with another far-eye. It seemed to have been shattered as well, so she collapsed and stored it in a pouch. Sylvia was…surprised at the frown on her face when the other woman turned to look at her. “Sylvia…did you see it?”

“…see what?”

“The reaction from the survivors,” Maria answered seriously, drawing odd looks from the rest of the squad. “There weren’t many, but…”

“There wasn’t a reaction…” Sylvia whispered in uneasy surprise. She couldn’t stop herself from folding a single Mithril knuckle and holding it to her lips briefly, before she forced it down. It was an old tic of hers she’d thought had been trained out of her, but it still popped up when she was most stressed. “At least, not from the Rhoscarans. I would have expected to have seen hundreds of them pouring out of the fortifications nearby. There were more than enough of them to swarm the scene in moments. But…”

“Nothing,” Maria finished for her, frown transitioning into a scowl. “Not only that…but I saw something else. In the midst of it all…there was a single soldier. He looked to have been far enough from the explosion to survive it, but not enough to escape the flames. He just…stood there as they were crawling up his body, completely unresponsive, and yet I saw the rise and fall of his chest. He was alive and still up until the moment he suddenly collapsed, cooked alive in his own armor. He never once reacted otherwise.”

An uneasy silence stole over the tower, then, as everyone looked at each other. It was only Sylvia, Maria, Renauld, and one other up here who had been waiting for Bleddyn’s signal. And while that was obviously it, there was a collective sense that something wrong was happening.

The last person in the tower was Kierla, the young Elf…and she had yet to speak. Instead, the scout had been watching the scene at the docks with narrowed eyes of her own.

And then she broke her silence. “Seen thing like that before,” She grunted, in her oddly deep voice for her age. “Warriors, devoted to Progenitor. Barely aware, barely speak. Only thought is of kill. Not good see. Not at all.”

Sylvia took a deep, deep breath, filling her lungs in an extraneous gesture…

And sharply shook her head.

“Enough. It doesn’t matter, nor does it change our plans,” She said with finality. “We move out. No matter how oddly the Dwarves are acting, we still have our mission.”

No one argued with Sylvia as they began to abandon their position. As everyone descended the stairs to the ground floor where the rest of the squad awaited, there was instead an air of unease among them.

Sylvia tried not to let it bother her. But still…

Her gaze lingered on the flaming docks in the distance, and she couldn’t help but note the silence. She would have expected some kind of response from either the defenders or the occupiers of the city. Shouts, or perhaps screams of some kind.

Instead, silence hung over the city like a gavel, ready to pronounce the sentence on the guilty.

Sylvia shivered…

And turned away.

………………………………..

There were no guards at the palace.

None whatsoever.

As far as they could tell, the entire palatial terrace had been emptied. There were no servants, soldiers, or civilians anywhere to be found in the entire complex that comprised the Scarlet Palace. The command staff directing the Rhoscaran Backdropped by the fire still ranging over at the docks, casting a grim, orange cast to the sky…

The silence was incredibly unnerving. So much so that there were whispered, unsure comments from the other members Sylvia was unfamiliar with that maybe…maybe they should turn back. There was obviously something wrong with the entire situation. It could even be a trap.

But Sylvia…didn’t think it was, and neither did Maria. And Sylvia trusted the former spy’s instincts more than she did her own.

Yes, something was off…but this didn’t stink of a trap.

More like apathy and neglect, as if the palace wasn’t worth the effort to guard.

They pressed on.

According to the intelligence Bleddyn had given them, there was a prison built into the back of the palace. Typically, it wasn’t occupied at any one point in time. It was really a temporary holding spot for politically important prisoners.

Such as the Marshal of a Herztalian Martial Order.

Sylvia and her squad barely bothered with stealth as they crossed around back to the prison. There, they found the large wooden door hanging wide open. If it hadn’t been incredibly heavy-looking, Sylvia was sure it would have been banging open and closed in the wind, from how neglected it was.

Instead, the portal to the underground prison loomed large and dark. They could only see a few feet into the hall on the other side, and it looked narrow. As if maybe three people could fit inside shoulder to shoulder.

Sylvia shelved the ominous feeling the sight of the doorway instilled in her and turned to face everyone else. “Maria, Walter, Renauld, and…Kierla, with me,” She said, in a low voice. “We’ll go in. Everyone else, secure this area and wait for our return.”

Immediately, there was at least one protest to her decision. Fade shoved his way to the front of the squad and stared up at Sylvia with accusing eyes. Sylvia could see an actual frown on his lupine lips, a distinctly human gesture.

Smiling softly, Sylvia knelt to his level and rested her forehead against his, careful to avoid his antlers. “You wouldn’t fit,” She said quietly, for his ears only. Fade winced in response, and Sylvia nodded. “You know it. That corridor isn’t wide enough for. Please, wait patiently. I promise, we’ll be back soon with him.”

When Sylvia pulled back, Fade eyed her suspiciously for a moment…

And nodded, fading back through the crowd.

She was touched by his trust. Sometimes, it was easy to become unsure of such a thing. Especially when we couldn't understand him the way Nathan could.

Sylvia and her chosen squad didn’t waste a moment more. Instead, they advanced down the darkened hallway of the prison. Walter, as the most armored person among them, took point, with Sylvia and Maria following closely behind. Renauld and Kierla took the rear, as primarily ranged combatants. But they barely needed to be even that cautious.

Inside, they found…nothing.

At least, at first. The palatial prison seemed incredibly empty, their footsteps echoing off the stone.

Until suddenly, they weren’t alone.

Appearing as if from the void itself, two of the Rhoscaran Corvid knights slunk out of the darkness. Their avian-themed, crimson plate mail was eerily silent as the two members of Elysael’s personal Knights barred the way, standing abreast in the middle of the hall.

Judging by their bare swords and presented shields, the Raven of Florens evident upon them, they meant to stop their advance.

And yet, they didn’t say a word.

Sylvia’s eyes darted to the door to their left, which they appeared to be guarding. It was blank to her senses, utterly and totally.

That said something in general. None of the other cells down here had any kind of mystical defenses, and there were two of them within reach. The cell just down from where the Knights were was also warded in some way.

Her eyes narrowed, and without a word, Sylvia gestured forward with one arm.

Everyone understood, though the Knights still didn’t react.

An arrow glowing a murky, forest green streaked over Sylvia’s shoulder, and impacted the raised shield of the Knight…

And bounced off his shield.

It did its job, though.

Walter charged with a shout that echoed up and down the hallway, and both Maria and Sylvia followed after him, followed by more arrows from Kierla.

The Corvid Knights had a reputation. They were known well for being stout defenders, paragons of the Rhoscaran ideal…

But compared to a squad of battle-tested veterans, all of them Classers in one way or another, they weren’t enough.

The moment Sylvia caught even one of them in a custom-crafted illusion of her own design, it was over. People had the wrong idea about Illusions. Especially Cultivation, Art-based Illusions. They imagined an entire school of ‘spells’ for every situation, a box of tools to fit the lock that was combat.

Instead, it was all freeform. It was more than just a Cultivation Art.

It was art in general. You had to overwhelm the senses with additional stimulus until they experienced what you wanted them to. It was, in a way, similar to Mind affecting Spells and Arts. You had to custom craft everything you did, and assault a mind with an additional layer over reality, unique to their senses.

It was one of the hardest forms of Mysticality available to Classers. Not just because of the intrinsic difficulty in custom-crafting your own Arts and Spells.

But because you needed to have the imagination to create them in the first place.

Every Art…every Spell…was a canvas.

A deadly one.

And so, even though the rest of her squad saw nothing, to the Knights, they were suddenly facing something entirely different. Instead of five humanoid assailants, they were facing five different monsters.

Odd ones, at that.

Though it didn’t have a name, Sylvia liked to think of this as the ‘Clay Person’ Illusion.

For this one, the people she layered this illusion saw five blobs of stretchy, grey clay in the place of whoever was at her side at the moment, instead of who they were in actuality. These blobs had several different abilities. For one, they were larger, with more mass. Typically, twice the size of what they actually were. For two, they were very oddly stretchy and bendy. Their arms could extend past their reach, and sort of curve around the way a normal person wouldn’t. They had no features, as well. No visible eyes, no ears, no mouths. Nothing to give away tells.

And so, whenever someone tried to fight one of these ‘clay people’, they were fighting something that their blows went right through, their blocks and parries didn’t actually land, and they couldn’t maintain a good position.

Faced with that, and the refined abilities of everyone else?

It was over in under a minute.

They didn’t kill the Corvids, though. There was still a hope that matters could be settled with Elysael and Rhoscara itself without bloodshed.

Once the Knights had been shoved to the side, Kierla and Maria approached what they assumed to be Alveron’s cell, while Sylvia, Renauld, and Walter did the same for Nathans.

Sylvia didn’t waste a moment and struck out at the ward scheme etched into the stone around the door. Her short sword, made of the same material as her Father’s, easily parted the rune-carved stone, and the wards upon the cell fell. Sylvia didn’t know what to expect when she opened the door. For all she knew, Nathan could have been chained up or even injured during an interrogation.

She wasn’t expecting the Precursor to be sitting in the middle of the bare stone cell, cross-legged, eyes closed, and hands cupped in front of himself. It almost seemed like he was peacefully meditating?

Looking…totally fine?

Sylvia…

Blinked.
