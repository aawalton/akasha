“Alright,” Viktor said. “I won’t tell anyone.”

Jeanne nodded. “Same.”

Calyssa swept her gaze across the hall, then gestured toward a corner where the nearest cluster of adventurers stood well out of earshot.

If this were truly as sensitive as she was making it sound, Viktor thought, then a private room would have been a more sensible choice.

But he was not going to complain, not when she was already offering something. And so, without a word, he and Jeanne followed her and Cedric across the hall until the four of them stood together in that quieter, emptier stretch of space.

Cedric cleared his throat. “In the stone building, we found a wooden case...”

Ah, that.

Viktor could already see how this would play out. It was disappointing, no doubt, since this was not the discovery he had hoped to find, yet at the same time, he couldn’t help but feel a little amused, precisely because he knew where this conversation was heading.

“A wooden case?” Jeanne asked.

“Yes,” Cedric said. “Inside, we found some needles. And a vial.”

“What kind of vial?”

“Poison,” Calyssa said. “And, after a thorough analysis, we concluded that it’s very likely... the same one that was used to kill Gideon.”

“What?” Jeanne stared at her. “The Guildmaster has been injected with poison using some sort of needle. You’re saying the murder weapon came from there?”

Actually, no, Viktor thought, pressing his lips together to suppress a smile. The real murder weapon had already been destroyed a long time ago. What was found in that building was a mere recreation, made by Kazyk at his order. As for how it had found its way into the hideout of those masked bastards... well, that was a total mystery.

“Yes, that’s our current thinking,” Cedric said. “These men must have something to do with the late Guildmaster’s death. They had such a well-hidden lair, so it would not be a surprise if they had been here for years without anyone being the wiser.”

“You found only one case?” Jeanne asked. “Were there any other poisons?”

“Speaking of poison, there were three men inside the building, and all of them had taken their own lives. By poison, as it happens, though not the same type as what was in the case.”

“It’s not strange for poisoners to keep several kinds on hand,” Viktor said with a shrug.

Cedric nodded. “True.”

“But why?” Jeanne said. “Why would they want to kill Guildmaster Gideon in the first place?”

“And why would they want to kidnap and torture the pyromancers?” said Viktor.

Cedric slowly shook his head. “Madmen...”

“Now you understand,” Calyssa said, “why we cannot allow this information to reach the public yet.”

Viktor looked at the woman. She was wearing a mask of calm, but he didn’t miss the faint tremble at the corner of her lips or the flicker in her eyes. A storm of emotion must be raging inside her right now, one she could barely contain. After all, at long last, she had finally found the culprits—wrong culprits, though—who had taken Gideon from her.

“Yes,” Jeanne said. “The last time, when people suspected Overseer Rennald was behind the murder, the adventurers had come to the caravan station and made a mess of things. This time would be worse. Much worse.”

Calyssa nodded. “Even though there’s no proof the Iskora men were behind the outpost attack, the fact is, most people simply do not care. In their minds, the verdict has long since been delivered. And what those adventurers did afterward certainly didn’t help, either. They marched into town and occupied the west side. Then one of them...” She glanced at Viktor. “...attacked Claire. And now, there are masked men and monsters and dead pyromancers. The entire town is losing its mind. Everyone has already decided that the kidnappers and the Iskora adventurers were one and the same.” She let out a slow breath. “So this... this could be the straw that broke the camel’s back. There’s a real possibility our adventurers and the townspeople would go to the west side and start a fight. Daelin could tear itself in half.”

“I... understand.”

“You’re right,” Viktor said. “But what’s the plan? Are you going to withhold this indefinitely?”

“Of course not,” Calyssa said. “But we must tread carefully. Isadora wants to gather more concrete proof before we make our move. And once we have it, we take it to the Guildmaster’s Conclave. Those responsible will be punished, and their henchmen will be evicted from Daelin.”

“And how is that proof-gathering going?”

“We’re working on it. The whole place is being examined thoroughly. We will turn over every stone until we find something that points to them clearly and undeniably.” Calyssa looked at him. “I know that you want to make them pay for what they’ve done to Claire. I understand that. But I’m asking you, please, be a little more patient.”

“If you find anything, can you let me know right away?” Viktor asked.

Calyssa thought for a moment, then she said, “It depends on what it is. But yes, if I’m able to, I will.”

“Thank you.”

Well, that was about as much as he had reasonably hoped for, all things considered.

“Is that building the only thing you’re investigating?” Jeanne asked. “Or is there anything else?”

“We’re also looking into the party who discovered the place and reported it,” Calyssa said.

Jeanne’s brow arched. “Why?”

“Because they’ve always been strange, if I’m being frank about it. They seem to have a particular talent for finding themselves at the center of things. First the incident with Brynhildr and Dagnar, and now this. Sure, Ekon being targeted by those masked men makes sense on its own. He’s a pyromancer, and we know what they were doing with pyromancers. But hiding from them by living inside the dungeon for over two weeks? That is, to put it mildly, a very weird decision. And then, his friends stumbled upon the hidden lair. It all sounds a bit too... convenient, for lack of a better word. Those people had apparently been here for a long time without drawing any attention from anyone, only to be found out just like that.”

“Isn’t that just the nature of staying hidden?” Viktor said. “To avoid being caught, you have to be careful every single time. But to get caught, you only need to be careless once.”

“Fair point,” Calyssa conceded.

“I don’t know much about Ekon personally,” Jeanne said, “but I’ve met one of his party members, Renee, a couple of times. She struck me as a friendly and honest girl.”

“I know Renee as well,” Viktor said. “And Mandragora the Druidess, too. I’ve been to her hut several times. Not to mention, she’s the one who has been helping Claire with her potions.”

Cedric grinned, looking at him. “I’ve heard the story from Fiora.” Then he turned to Calyssa. “From what she’s told me, I believe that they are not bad people. I could vouch for them, if it came to that.”

“Well,” the bespectacled woman said. “If all of you are saying so, then I’ll give them the benefit of the doubt for now. I guess we will divert our resources to investigate the ‘dark spirit’ instead.”

“The dark spirit?” Jeanne asked. “Is there really a dark spirit?”

“We call it that because everyone else does. Just a name used out of convenience. In all likelihood, it’s a person, someone who possesses a number of powerful Reliquaries, one of which allows them to conjure a veil of shadow around themselves to conceal their identity.”

“But...” Jeanne said. “By calling them that, are you implying they’re the same person? The one who attacked the secret lair and the one who burned down the Iskora outpost?”

“Very likely,” Calyssa said. “According to Ekon’s party, what they saw was a humanoid entity shrouded in shadow who flew around and rained fire and ice down upon their enemies. Quite similar to what the survivors from the outpost described, don’t you think?” She paused. “Of course, Ekon and his friends could be lying. But you three have just told me they’re trustworthy, so.”

“I wonder what the dark spirit’s motive could be,” Cedric said.

“I’m sure you’ve all heard the rumors that either the Guildmaster or the Overseer has hired someone to carry out the attack on the outpost,” Calyssa said. “And I will tell you plainly: that’s complete nonsense. In my opinion, the more likely explanation is a personal feud of some sort. The dark spirit is simply going after their own enemies, who happen to be near our town.”

“But...” Jeanne said. “If they attacked both the Iskora adventurers and the masked men, doesn’t that suggest those two groups are indeed connected to each other?”

“You’re not the first one to draw that line, and yes, it does make them look suspicious. But as I keep saying, suspicion is not proof.”

“There’s a rather popular theory going around at the moment,” Cedric said, “that the dark spirit is actually the Protector of Daelin. I’ve even heard that some people are starting a cult to worship them.”

A what?

Calyssa frowned. “A protector? If that were the case, one might reasonably ask what they’ve been doing up until now.”

“Well, it’s not like Daelin has needed much protecting before all this,” Cedric said with a shrug. “The story goes that the dark spirit was slumbering in the old castle, and that the adventurers from Iskora disturbed it when they arrived.”

“The ghost of the Dark Emperor...” Jeanne murmured.

“That’s enough.” Calyssa waved a dismissive hand. “We’re not discussing local superstition. We will never get anywhere if we allow such things to derail the conversation. Frankly, even the theory about the Guildmaster or the Overseer hiring someone, ridiculous as it is, has more merit than this.” She straightened. “Anyway, I think that’s everything for now. I should get back to—”

“Chief Secretary!”

A sharp, urgent voice came from across the hall. A man in Guild uniform was cutting through the crowd toward them.

“What’s the matter?” Calyssa asked.

“The Guildmaster... do you know where she is?”

“At Overseer Rennald’s office, I believe. Has something happened?”

“Yes.” The man paused just long enough to catch his breath. “We’ve just received a message from Guildmaster Khalid of Iskora.”

In an instant, the hall went quiet. Every conversation stopped. Every head turned. Every eye was now locked onto the man.

“What does it say?” Calyssa asked.

“He’s coming here tomorrow,” the man replied. “He wants to parley.”
