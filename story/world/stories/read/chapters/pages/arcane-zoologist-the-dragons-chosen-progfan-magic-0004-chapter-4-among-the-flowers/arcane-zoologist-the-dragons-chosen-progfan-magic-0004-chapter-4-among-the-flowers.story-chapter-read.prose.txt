Callie looked at Granny Ethel, whose usually calm face was scrunched with worry. The elder woman reached down and gently rubbed her thumb over Callie's magical mark.

"That is indeed magic… There's no doubt about that. It appeared after the dragon offered you water to drink?"

"Yes! Out of a flower that grew on the ground," Callie confirmed.

Granny Ethel sighed heavily. "That's even worse…"

Callie's heart sank. Worse? She had thought that getting magic from a dragon would be amazing. She obviously couldn't tell her parents, because they could hurt the dragon. But if nobody found out where she got her magic from, then it would be alright. No?

"Why is it worse?" Callie asked, her eyes inadvertently filling with tears.

Granny Ethel flinched and hurriedly grabbed both of Callie's hands, pressing them between her calloused but incredibly warm palms. "Oh, child. I'm sorry! I didn't mean to scare you. Magic from a dragon is a wonderful thing. In fact, I'm proud of you! While I don't fully understand how the dragon could bestow you with magic, I know that it is a great honor. The dragon must have recognized that you have a good and kind heart."

Callie's tears stopped as some of the tension melted away. However, she knew that Granny Ethel wasn't finished just yet.

"Are you worried that hunters will hurt the dragon?" She asked.

"There's that too, but… I'm more worried about you," the woman said.

"Me? Would people hate me if they found this?" Callie asked nervously.

"Hate you? No! It's quite the opposite. Haven't you noticed how much people praise your parents for knowing just a little bit of magic?"

"I have. That's why I was confused as to why it would be such a bad thing…"

"It's a bad thing if somebody who is not supposed to know finds out. Like a dragonblood," Granny Ethel leaned down to whisper the last word. As if saying it aloud was a bad omen.

"What exactly are dragonbloods? I know they are always hunting for arcane beasts, and I think Mom said that they are very powerful people, but I don't think I've ever met one," Callie pondered.

Even though she didn't know much about dragonbloods, she didn't like them. The fact that they were always hunting arcane beasts was bad enough in her opinion.

Granny Ethel threw a glance around the room, as if to make sure that nobody else would be listening. But obviously, there was nobody there. They could hear Tristan rustling about in the adjacent room, with an occasional angry stomp or clatter as he swept a bit too enthusiastically with the broom.

Eventually, the elderly woman spoke again. "Dragonbloods are the ruling class in our country."

"Ruling class?" Callie cocked her head.

"They are people who are in charge of important things. Like the village chief, only even more important. And they can all use magic, very powerful magic, which is passed down through the bloodline. It is said that dragonbloods are descended from dragons, their last mark on this world. Although after what you witnessed, I'm not so sure about that," Granny Ethel explained.

"Magic? Should I perhaps learn how to use magic from one of these dragonbloods?" Callie asked.

"NO!" Granny Ethel snapped, making Callie flinch back. She held the girl's hands firmly and looked Callie straight in the eye. "Never trust a dragonblood!"

"Wh-Why not?" Callie dared to ask.

"If they found out that you have magic, they would take you away from this village. They would take you away from your family and use your magic for their own gain. You would never be able to see any of us ever again," the woman warned sternly.

"What?! Why would they do that?" Callie exclaimed in shock.

"They believe that magic, real magic, should be kept to only a select few. So please, keep this and your magic hidden. At least for the time being. I'm sure we can figure out a safe and discreet way to use it, okay?" Granny Ethel said.

Callie nodded, although she didn't manage to hide her disappointment. She was given this power, but wasn't even allowed to learn anything about it, because some stupid dragonbloods would take her away. What right did they have to even do that?

Frankly, Callie doubted that Dad and Mom would ever let anyone take her away. They could be really strict at times, but they certainly wouldn't let anyone hurt her. Not to mention that they were really strong. They could probably beat those dragonbloods anyway.

But there was no need to create conflict. What if somebody got hurt while fighting over her? Callie wouldn't be able to live with herself, so she promised not to do any magic where somebody could see her. If she figured out how to do magic, that is.

"I'll keep it hidden," Callie assured.

"Good. Does anyone else know about your mark?" Granny Ethel asked.

"Brother knows, but nobody else."

"Okay, then we have nothing to worry about. Callen is a reliable young man. Now, I'd really appreciate it if you could go to the flower meadow and pick up some fresh herbs. My stocks are running a bit low," Granny Ethel said kindly.

"Of course," Callie nodded.

"Do be careful when you go out. Remember that compassion is a good thing, but compassion without caution can be dangerous," Granny Ethel warned.

Callie nodded. She wasn't sure if she fully understood what the elderly woman meant. Likely that following the glimmerhare into the night wasn't the smartest thing Callie could have done. Perhaps, it would have been better if she had called Callen to help.

She would do that next time, although Callie sincerely hoped that there were no more traps in the forest. Taking one of the baskets used for herb gathering, Callie carefully placed the wooden boards with her drawings at the bottom, and headed out.

While picking herbs normally wasn't her favorite activity, after spending so much time at home, it sounded like a wonderful idea. Not to mention that she wanted to think a little about everything Granny Ethel said.

But as Callie hurried towards the flower meadow, her worries slowly dissipated, and she went with a bounce in her step. It wasn't that she didn't care about dragonbloods. Judging by Granny Ethel's reaction, they seemed like scary people.

She had compared them to the village chief, and he was definitely a scary man. Scarier even than Dad when he was angry. Imagining people even scarier than the chief made Callie shudder. She definitely didn't want to meet them. However, in her childish mind, it was fine as long as nobody found out about the mark.

With the sleeve of her tunic firmly pulled down, Callie had no worries. After all, who would be interested in seeing her forearms all of a sudden? Not even the biggest bullies in the village wanted to know what was happening underneath her clothes. So Callie walked on without much worry.

Her steps came to an abrupt halt as she heard familiar girlish voices nearby. Callie dove behind one of the nearby village houses and listened.

"Really? Serves her right!" A girl cackled.

Callie recognized those voices. The other village girls. Once again they were too eager to talk about things others did, rather than do their own chores. Callie didn't want to run into them.

So instead, she crept behind the house, through the backyard, making the chickens cluck incredulously at the random stranger appearing in their yard. But with a gentle shush, Callie managed to get through before truly disturbing the birds.

Having avoided the girls, Callie carefully made her way to the flower meadow. It was a truly beautiful place, the only one like it in the whole area. Their little village of Greyfen was located deep in the forest, and thus, there were trees everywhere. They didn't have large areas for farming, only as much as each family managed to clear out on their own.

Flower meadow was the only exception. It was a rather sizable area where trees and bushes didn't grow for some reason. Nobody knew why—it was as if somebody had drawn a large circle in the forest and declared that trees couldn't grow past it. As a result, the place was abundant in medicinal herbs and flowers that couldn't be found anywhere else.

Apparently, many villagers wanted to make the flower meadow into a field for their crops, seeing how abundantly plants grew there. But Granny Ethel was ready to beat them with the stick for even thinking that. After all, the flower meadow had herbs and flowers that didn't grow anywhere else.

In the middle of summer, the place was most abundant. She and Tristan had gone to clear it out just two weeks before, but Callie could already see that the place had once again regrown most of its bounty.

"This might take a while…" Callie sighed. On the bright side, this meant that nobody would complain about where she was and what she was doing. This was proper apprentice work.

With a determined nod, she began picking the herbs. Crysara leaves, millefollia leaves, and matricaria flowers were just some of the many she managed to find. At this rate, she would have to return to the healer's hut early.

"I guess I'll have to head back for now," Callie announced, and was about to head back when she noticed something move among the flowers some distance away.

Curious, she crouched down and stilled, watching carefully. The flowers rustled about, indicating that something was moving them. Then one of the flowers disappeared. Callie focused her gaze and crept a little closer.

Another flower disappeared, and Callie finally saw what was disturbing the plants. A creature made up entirely of green slime with the exception of a red core at the very center.

"A mireling!" She exclaimed, unable to contain her excitement. This was the first time she had seen one so close.

The mireling wobbled from her voice, likely frightened. Callie ducked deeper into the grass and stood completely still. The creature continued to wobble for a while longer, but soon settled down.

Having made sure that nothing would disturb it, the mireling resumed its meal. Callie watched with fascination as it encased a matricaria flower with its slime, in which the flower dissolved almost instantly. Curiously enough, the natrea leaves that happened to be right next to the flower remained untouched.

It must not like the taste of natreas. I can't blame it. They make the skin itch really badly if touched with bare hands.

Callie took in each movement the mireling made. Each wobble of its slime, which appeared firmer than she thought. But she would have to touch it to be sure. It definitely looked to be slightly moist, much like a snail. She wondered whether the core was hard or just differently colored slime.

In the end, Callie had no clue how long she watched the mireling, but each wobble was committed firmly to her memory. She would draw the creature in her notes later. A thought that plagued her while she watched the mireling was how its slime worked exactly. What would happen if she touched it? Would it feel squishy, slimy, or something else entirely?

"Carrothead! Carrothead!" Tristan called loudly, startling both Callie and the mireling.

"Shh! Don't yell!" Callie chastised the boy, rising slightly out of the grass.

"There you are! Granny Ethel was wondering where you had—"

The boy stopped mid-sentence as he noticed the mireling, which was now nervously wobbling away from its feeding spot.

"Get away! It's dangerous!" The boy screamed and rushed towards Callie.

"No, stop!" She called desperately, but it was too late.
