Chapter 3 – EXP from TearsARC I: THE ACADEMIC EXILE

SYNOPSIS: Livia is now the accidental therapist for Guild #1. Bron drags her into the guild camp for back-to-back sessions before she can object. Phina wants a direct confession strategy for a slime. Nyx needs a visualization. Alaric needs divine probability logic explained to himself. Miles watches from a chair like he owns the place — which, to be fair, he does. Livia levels up by fixing lunatics. She has complicated feelings about this.—————————————————

Day 2 as Emotional Buffer 2.0.

Survived: one crying immortal tanker, one slime-related romantic consultation, one ghost rogue who needed to talk about the dark but couldn’t yet say why, and one paladin who had bet his sacred sword on a goblin race and felt this was primarily the goblin’s fault.

Casualties: one bench (structural failure, Bron-related). Zero progress bars (800/1000 EXP, sitting there, judging her).

Current status: cross-legged on a salvaged bench, Therapist’s Log open to a fresh page, attempting to look busy enough that nobody would approach her for at least twenty minutes.

She had written Day 2 as Emotional Buffer 2.0: Survived crying tanker. Guild is insane. Send help.

Below it, after a moment of consideration, she had added: No, seriously. Send help.

I failed practicum. I have no idea what I’m doing. The system is giving me EXP for it. This is the most alarming part.

[Achievement Unlocked: Chronicler of Chaos. +5 Note-Taking Points (Cosmetic)]

“Cosmetic points,” she read. “Five cosmetic note-taking points.” She looked at the notification. The notification looked back, placid and certain. “Give me a fireball spell. Or give me a nap.”

The notification dissolved without comment.

She pulled up her HUD and stared at the progress bar.

[Mental Supporter Lv. 1: 800/1000 EXP]

There are no swords in this class. There are no spells. There is only my questionable expertise, this itchy robe, and two hundred EXP standing between me and whatever Level 2 looks like.

A shadow fell across her notepad.

She knew that shadow.

Bron Ironslam stood over her, blocking the sun with the practiced ease of someone who had never once considered that other people might want sunlight. His dented armor clanked as he leaned forward. He was beaming with the specific energy of a man who had discovered something wonderful and was going to make it everyone else’s problem.

“Buffer!” he announced. “You are a genius. That breathing thing? Changed my life. I tanked a wyvern this morning and actually felt something.” He paused, apparently for emphasis. “I felt something, Buffer. Do you understand what that means? I felt.”

“I didn’t fix anything,” Livia said.

Bron blinked. “You made me feel better.”

“That’s not the same thing.”

“What’s the difference?”

Livia opened her mouth.

She had an answer once. Somewhere in a lecture hall, half-asleep, underlining a paragraph about symptom relief versus structural change.

She wasn’t sure she still believed it applied here. Not to a man who tanked dragons for a living and cried about it on a Tuesday.

He genuinely thinks I’m competent, she thought. This is terrifying.

“Gotta fix the others now,” Bron continued, with the certainty of someone announcing a simple logistical plan. “All of them. They’re a mess since you showed up.”“They were a mess before I showed up,” Livia said.

“Yeah but now they know there’s a fix.” He grabbed her arm. “C’mon, Buffer.”

“Stop calling me—”

He had already started walking. She was being walked. She was being walked by an immortal tanker who outmassed her by approximately four hundred pounds, and the notepad was still in her hand, and the guild camp was getting closer with every step.

The guild camp was exactly as she had thought about it, which was to say: a monument to organized chaos that had given up on the organized part.

Runes glowed at irregular intervals on every surface. Enchanted weapons had been abandoned at angles that implied their owners had left in a significant hurry and not yet returned. A slime near the east tent was attempting cartwheels. It had graduated from backflips. Livia noted this with the detached appreciation of someone watching a peer advance in their field.

This is a psych ward with swords.

Phina was in the far corner, composing something at volume. Glimmer sat beside her, jiggling at intervals that may or may not have constituted feedback.

Nyx had located a crate and was inside it, flashlight on, hood up, the pink beam visible through the gap in the lid.

Alaric was nearby, muttering about that damn slime race with the specific grievance of a man who had not yet accepted that the outcome was final.

Miles Vexley was in a chair. Not a bench. An actual chair, the kind that suggested it had been here first and the camp had built up around it. He had one ankle crossed over a knee. He was watching her arrive with the expression of someone who had been expecting this and found the timing satisfactory.

“Back for round two, Emotional Buffer?” He tilted his head. “Or you planning to bolt?”

“Don’t tempt me,” Livia said.

She looked at the guild. The guild looked back. Nyx’s crate didn’t technically have eyes but she felt it looking.

She straightened. “Fine,” she said. “Who’s first?”

― SESSION 1: PHINA ―

Phina glided forward with the bearing of someone who had prepared an opening statement and was going to deliver it regardless of audience readiness.

“I require your counsel, Sentiment Processor,” she began.

“Please don’t call me that,” Livia said.

“My heart yearns,” Phina continued, undeterred, “for a being of extraordinary resonance. How does one court an entity of such… fluidity?”

Her eyes moved to Glimmer.

Glimmer jiggled.

You are in love with the slime, Livia thought. You are genuinely, sincerely, completely in love with the slime.

She kept her face professionally neutral. “Tell me more about the bond,” she said, which bought her approximately three seconds to compose herself.

Phina took a breath. Then she spoke. For a long time.

Livia learned that Glimmer had first caught Phina’s attention during a raid on the Amethyst Caverns, when the slime had absorbed a stray lightning bolt and refracted it into seventeen colors simultaneously. Seventeen, Phina emphasized, as though this were a significant number. Most slimes managed four, perhaps five. Glimmer had done seventeen.

“It was in that moment,” Phina said, her voice reverent, “that I understood. This was not mere biology. This was a being of extraordinary resonance.”

The tale has been taken without authorization; if you see it on Amazon, report the incident.Livia wrote: Phina. Fell in love at lightning bolt refraction. Seventeen colors. This is real. This is happening. Do not laugh.

She had learned that Glimmer communicated through jiggle frequency — slow oscillation for contentment, rapid tremor for excitement, and a specific sideways wobble Phina had designated as Glimmer’s laugh after eighteen months of observation. She had charted it. She produced the chart. It was color-coded.

Livia looked at the chart for a long moment.

Eighteen months of jiggle frequency data. Color-coded.

“Have you considered,” Livia said carefully, “expressing your feelings directly? Just… talking to Glimmer?”

Phina’s eyes widened. “A direct confession? Without poetic intermediary?”

“Maybe start without the poetry.”

“Simple,” Phina repeated, like the word was in a language she had studied but never spoken aloud.

“Simple,” Livia confirmed.

“Let’s practice,” Livia said. “Pretend I’m Glimmer. What would you say?”

Her introversion screamed. You are volunteering to be a slime, it said. In front of Miles Vexley. Who is watching from his chair.Yes, Livia told it. I know. We’re doing it anyway.

Phina straightened. Looked at Livia with total sincerity. And said:

“O radiant one.“Thy viscous beauty captivates my soul. Wilt thou join me in an eternal dance of starlight and goo, that our essences may intertwine across the fabric of cosmic time, thy gelatinous grace and my arcane fire merged into a singular harmony that defies the mortal understanding of love—”

“That’s a lot,” Livia said.

Phina paused. “Too much?”

“It’s beautiful,” Livia said, because it genuinely was, in a way she would need to process later. “But Glimmer jiggles for bread. Let’s match the register. What’s the core of it? If you took away all the poetry?”

Phina was quiet. The runes on her robes stilled, which Livia had never seen them do before.

“I see it,” Phina said, finally. Quietly. “Not as a creature. Not as a curiosity. I see Glimmer, and I choose to be near it. Every day. On purpose.”

Livia stopped writing.

Oh. That’s actually quite profound. She is in love with a slime and it is actually quite profound.

“Say that,” Livia said. “Exactly that.”Phina turned to Glimmer. Knelt — actually knelt, robes pooling on the camp floor — and said, very quietly:“Beloved. I see you. I choose to be near you. Every day, on purpose. Shall we journey together?”

Silence.

Glimmer oscillated slowly. Then — a warm, golden glow, faint, spreading from its center outward.

Phina’s breath caught.

Livia wrote: Glimmer glowed. Unknown if this constitutes reciprocation. Sufficient. Phina did not break. Result: success. The slime didn’t flee. In this guild, that counts as a win.

[You inspired an archmage to confess to a slime. +100 EXP. Mental Supporter Lv. 1: 900/1000 EXP.]

Phina immediately began composing an ode. She had the first three stanzas done before she left the session area. Alaric, who had been listening from behind a tent, started a betting pool on the final stanza count.

Current over/under: seventeen stanzas. To match the seventeen colors.

Livia chose not to engage with this.

― SESSION 2: NYX ―

Nyx shuffled forward from his crate, flashlight in hand, hood doing the work of hiding most of his face except for the lower half, which was doing the work of looking like someone who had a lot to say and very limited infrastructure for saying it.

“I need help,” he said. “The dark. It’s bad.”

His voice cracked on bad, which he appeared to find mortifying.

Livia’s empathy activated before she could decide whether to let it. Something in the way he stood — back to the nearest solid surface, flashlight angled to cover the largest shadow in the area, free hand doing something complicated in his pocket — told her more than the words did.

“Okay,” she said softly. “Let’s try something. Picture the dark as a quiet friend. Something that’s just there. Neutral.”

Nyx looked at her with the expression of someone who had been told to picture a bear as a quiet friend.

“A teammate,” she revised. “Not the threat. Backup. The flashlight’s the primary. The dark is just — present. Not hunting you.”

He turned the word over. Teammate. Testing it.

“Yeah,” he said, slowly. “Okay.”His grip on the flashlight loosened, fractionally. It was not a breakthrough. It was the first centimeter of distance between him and the wall he’d been pressed against.

That counted.

[You eased an assassin’s fear of darkness. +200 EXP. Skill Unlocked: Verbal Hug Lv. 1.]

[LEVEL UP! Mental Supporter Lv. 2. New Passive: Emotional Resonance Lv. 1 — empathy amplifies trust in patients, +10% breakthrough chance. Robe upgrade: slightly less itchy.]

Livia looked at the level-up notification.

Then at Nyx, who was examining the flashlight with the expression of someone trying the word teammate on for size.

I am actually getting better at this, she thought.

The thought scared her more than anything else that had happened today.

― SESSION 3: ALARIC ―

Alaric staggered over with his flask and his holy aura, both of which were operating at approximately seventy percent capacity.

“Buffer!” He pointed at her. “I bet my holy sword on a goblin race. Lost it to a slime. Fix me.”

Livia opened her notepad. “Maybe… stop betting?”

Alaric’s expression went through several phases, arriving at genuine offense. “Stop betting? You don’t understand — betting is strategy.”

“It’s really not.”

“I’m a PALADIN.” He drew himself up with divine dignity, which was undercut by the swaying. “We calculate divine odds. It’s practically sacred math.”

Livia pinched the bridge of her nose. “Okay. Fine. What are the odds you’ll actually listen to me right now?”

Alaric paused. His eyes moved in the way eyes do when someone is doing real mental arithmetic.

“…Twelve percent,” he said solemnly.

“And the odds you’ll lose your next bet?”

Another pause. Longer this time.

“…Eighty-eight percent.”

Silence stretched between them. A tent flap blew somewhere. A distant fireball popped and faded.

Alaric stared at the ground.

His flask lowered, slightly. His holy aura steadied — stopped flickering, held at its seventy percent, which was not good but was more stable than before.

“…I’m going to go pray now,” he said. Quietly. With the weight of someone who meant it.

He walked away. Still swaying slightly. But with something different in his steps — a direction that wasn’t just the next bet.

[You redirected a paladin’s gambling urge through divine probability logic. +100 EXP]

Apparently paladins respond to numbers more than feelings. Good to know. Filing that.

By midday, Livia’s notepad was in a state she could only describe as aggressively annotated.

The guild’s chaos had continued in the background throughout all three sessions — as it always did, as it always would. Bron had decided that his new purpose was to guard Glimmer from all threats, and had spent the intervening time positioned between Glimmer and various camp elements he deemed threatening. The threat list currently included: two NPCs, a supply crate, and the concept of overcast weather.

Phina’s ode had reached twelve stanzas. Alaric had updated the over/under to twenty-two.

Nyx was back in his crate. The flashlight was on. This was normal. What was slightly less normal was that the crate lid was propped open an additional two inches compared to this morning.

Livia noted this. Wrote nothing about it. Noted it again.

[You stabilized a guild of lunatics. Achievement Unlocked: Accidental Savior. Skill Upgraded: Group Therapy Lv. 2.]

“I’m either a genius or doomed,” she muttered.

Miles materialized beside her with his usual timing — always for the aftermath, never quite for the moment itself. He sat on the edge of the bench she’d claimed. Not close. A considered distance.

“Not bad, rookie,” he said. His voice was missing the usual sarcasm. Not warm — just even. “Keep this up and we might not implode.”

His smirk softened for exactly one second.

One second was enough for Livia’s barely-functional therapist instincts to clock something underneath it — not amusement, not performance. Something quieter. Like a person looking at something they’d been worried about and finding it less destroyed than they’d expected.She filed it. Said nothing.

“This guild needs you,” Miles said. Not dramatically. Just factually. “More than they know.”

He stood and walked back to his chair before she could respond. This was, she was beginning to understand, simply how Miles Vexley operated: he said the true thing and immediately created distance from having said it.

Livia looked at her notepad. At the three sessions. At the messy progress notes. At 200/2000 EXP on her new level bar.

They needed her.

And that need scared her more than any dragon.

But underneath the fear — barely audible, unfamiliar — something that felt, cautiously, like purpose.

She was not going to examine that right now.
