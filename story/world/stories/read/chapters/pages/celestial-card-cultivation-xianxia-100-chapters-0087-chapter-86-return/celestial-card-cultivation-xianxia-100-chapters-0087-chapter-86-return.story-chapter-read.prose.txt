The Wild Typhoon Sect has vanished from the face of Tianqiu. The seneschal has deployed Imperial Investigators—I am looking forward to their report.

— Diary of Xu Ziqing, Imperial Scribe, Tianqiu Plane – now known as The Origin, Outer Realms

Five days. That was the time left until the mid-year spear assessment.

That was the first thought Jianyi had when he entered the outer sect with Minghao, coming back from the month they had spent in the wilderness.

While the changes from the second stage of the Soul-Forged-Body Path weren’t quite as impactful on his spear fighting as the first stage had been, he still had to do some work adjusting to his new capabilities.

The next few days would be busy—but he was very much looking forward to it.

First, though, they would return home.

It felt a bit childish, but Jianyi really wanted to show Elder Sun the progress he had made.

They greeted the guards at the entrance to the inner sect, and Jianyi immediately felt the higher qi density compared to the outer sect. He realized that he was truly spoiled from living there—and the time in the wilderness had done nothing to wean him off the expectation of being surrounded by unlimited qi.

Jianyi had to smile when the compound came into view.

Over the last nine months, it truly had become his home—maybe even more so since his mother had sold his childhood home. He was incredibly happy for her, but the new place didn’t feel like home to him yet.

Their first stop was the kitchen to grab a bite to eat. That was where Minghao found a note from Elder Sun. The retainer raised an eyebrow, then crumpled the piece of paper in his hand. “It looks like the elder is still on a mission, and it might last for a while…”

He wondered what kind of mission that was, but then was consumed by the disappointment that he couldn’t show his progress to his teacher. Hopefully, Elder Sun would be back before the spear assessment.

Jianyi cleaned himself up, wrote a letter to his mother, a note to Shuhua, and then made his way to the tailors in the outer sect. He had destroyed two out of his three robes, and the one he had left was too small and had seen better days.

His mother would be appalled to learn how he looked.

While cleaning up, he had the first chance to look at a proper mirror, and it wasn’t just his height that had changed.

His shoulders had broadened further, and while he was still far away from the ethereal look advanced cultivators shared, his features seemed more symmetrical, and his skin was flawless. Not that that was important.

***

Jianyi had received new robes and also visited the Mission Hall to sell some of the things he had collected over the month in the wilderness. He had been surprised that some of the spirit beast materials had yielded quite a few points. As expected, the spirit grass and other natural treasures had been less profitable, but he was fine with it.

The way things stood, he would soon be able to afford another technique; however, he was hesitant to spend anything, given that he had yet to learn how expensive the elixirs were that he needed to complete the Body Tempering Realm. His suspicion persisted that Elder Sun and Minghao had let him off cheaply, and he doubted the sect's alchemists would do the same for him.

Dressed in his new robes, Jianyi was on the way to the lecture hall in the outer sect—after skipping lectures for a month, he was keen not to fall too much further behind.

He was early and found a seat in the lower third of the hall easily.

Not long after, he heard a squeal from the entrance, and upon turning, he found Shuhua running towards him, Miyao following at a more dignified pace.

Shuhua grabbed him by the forearm, and he was confident that the only thing that protected him from a hug was that he remained seated. “You are back! We missed you. How have you been? What did you do? I can share the notes from the lectures! Will you join our training today?” She paused at the last question, looking at him, eyes wide. “Will you?”

He felt slightly breathless from the assault, but nodded decisively. “If you will have me, I will join.”

Her smile was wider than the sun. “What do you mean, ‘will have you?’ We have been missing you!”

Something in Jianyi’s chest loosened at those words, and he smiled.

Miyao had joined them by now and took the next seat down the line, welcoming him back as well. After a moment, she squinted at him. “You do feel different. Have you progressed your cultivation?”

Jianyi’s smile widened slightly. He knew that pride was an undignified emotion—and one that could lead to a swift grave—but he couldn’t hide it completely. He had worked hard for his progress, and that it was noticed felt good, deep down in his chest.

Not wanting to give too much away in the presence of other disciples, he just nodded and promised to explain more to her later. He knew that she would be incredibly keen to learn about his first steps into body cultivation.

Elder Zhang entered the hall through the entrance behind the stage. She looked as impeccable as always, with her hair braided in multiple strands that were then pulled together on the top of her head.

He had the impression that she noticed his presence, but he might have been wrong as she launched into the lecture right away.

“Today’s topic is the strategic selection of techniques. As you hopefully know by now, we differentiate offensive, defensive, mobility, and utility techniques. The only category that might need elaboration is utility. Those include everything that doesn’t easily fall under the third one and, most importantly, crafting-related techniques. It might be something that helps a carpenter fuse two pieces of wood, or a butcher keep meat fresh for consumption. But there is a myriad of techniques out there, from those that help set up a camp in the forest, to those that lure fish into a net,” she paused, looking at the disciples, daring anyone to ask a question about something she considered trivial.

Jianyi thought that she had a point; however, he resolved to get a better understanding of the utility techniques. Whenever he had read up on cards, he had very much focused on those directly relevant to combat.

“The natural instinct of many young disciples is to add as many techniques as possible to their upper dantian. Without a doubt, there is value in having a diverse set of techniques for all the different enemies one can face as a cultivator,” Elder Zhang continued.

“There are, however, a few counterarguments. For one, and we have discussed this before, as you gain additional insights into specific cards, the qi density you can apply increases. Furthermore, there are few things that are more important in a fight than consistency. You do not want to be in a position where you need an offensive art and only draw utility techniques. While for a crafter to have a few offensive cards is only a matter of waiting another breath, for a cultivator on the frontlines, that can be the difference between life and death.”

That made a lot of sense to Jianyi. He had already been in situations where he had drawn Verdant Mending when he would have rather drawn Bolt of Fire.

“One of the few points to take a large number of cards into your spirit is Qi Cards, of course. If those make up a large share of your cards, it could spell your doom.”

Jianyi couldn’t help but take a quick look at his lower dantian, where his Celestial Core Card rested. It was easy to forget how much of a boon it was that his Qi Cards had been absorbed. Not having to worry about drawing them at the wrong moment would make him a lot more powerful in the long run.

The lecture helped refine Jianyi’s thoughts on what he wanted to achieve with his techniques. He currently had three in his upper dantian and, like all Qi Gathering cultivators, had a theoretical maximum of ten.

He owned a few more thanks to his mother conserving the one card from his father, and the ones he had gained from Elder Sun—those were currently taboo as they would give away his ability to use cards from all elements. In the short-term, he would probably stick with what he had, but of course, he wouldn’t say no if the right technique knocked on his door.

When leaving the lecture hall with Shuhua and Miyao, Hong waited for them. Apparently, he had followed the lecture from one of the back ranks.

Jianyi had not seen him that often between Jun’s death and his leaving for the wilderness. Multiple emotions warred within him.

There was the guilt and anger triggered by the reminder of Jun’s death, a surprising amount of happiness to see the quiet young man again, and relief that Hong seemed… better. He had never been as outgoing as his cousin, but it felt like the month that had passed had also done wonders for Hong’s ability to deal with the grief of losing a family member who had felt more like a brother than a cousin.

“So tell us, where have you been?” Shuhua asked with the same level of excitement she had shown before the lecture had begun.

From the way Hong and Miyao focused on him, he could tell that they were curious, too.

“My teacher thought it would be good for me to get out of the sect for a while and focus on my growth, so his retainer and I spent a month out in the wilds, and I managed to get a few things done with my cultivation.”

“You are… Something definitely changed, but… You are not on the fourth stage, yet, are you?” Miyao asked; it seemed like her eyes tried to bore into his spirit to unlock a mystery.

Jianyi shook his head. “No, I did manage to advance to the third stage of spirit cultivation, though…” he looked around, making sure there was nobody else listening. “I did start out on Body Tempering as well.”

Miyao’s head rocked up, and she stepped almost uncomfortably close to him. “Tell me about it!”

Hong chuckled softly at the reaction, and it was good to see him like that.

Jianyi took a step back. “You know that I was always interested in body cultivation, and after a discussion with my teacher, we decided that it could be worth it, so I chose an art that fit and went through with it. The experience is very different from spirit cultivation…”

Now it was Shuhua’s turn to step closer. “Is that why you look different? You grew, didn’t you?”

He took another step back and nodded his head. “Indeed.”

The satisfaction he felt at the thought that somebody noticed disappointed him in himself.

“A few more stages and you will be as tall as me,” Hong joked.

“I need to see this in action. You must join the training today!” Miyao asked, uncharacteristically forceful.

“I would be happy to. For the next five days, I wanted to do little but focus on the spear.”

“Good. We have heard rumors about the test,” Shuhua said smugly.

Jianyi raised an eyebrow.

“Okay, okay, I will tell you!” Shuhua broke right away. “There is lots of wild stuff out there, but the one consistent thing is that it is going to be more than just spars. Apparently, some of the inner disciples have been drafted to help prepare for whatever it is. Of course, they are not telling, but somebody’s sister’s friend said confidently that it shouldn’t be anything we are not comfortable with.”

“Everyone is going crazy right now. You wouldn’t believe the speculation about prizes as well. The dormitories are abuzz with it,” Hong added.

Prizes. Jianyi had been so focused on achieving Elder Sun’s goals that he had temporarily forgotten that there were prizes from the sect as well for a strong performance.

That reminded him of the leaderboards, too, but given that he had been gone for a month, he suspected he was far behind on merit points.

“Very well, if you tell me when you want to meet for training, I will be there,” he said.

“Now!” Miyao demanded, pulling on his arm in the direction of the training grounds.
