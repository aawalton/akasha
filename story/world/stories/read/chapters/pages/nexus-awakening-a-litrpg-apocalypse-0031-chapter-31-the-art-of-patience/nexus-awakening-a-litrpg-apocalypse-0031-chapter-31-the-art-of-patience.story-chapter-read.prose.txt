Chapter 31: The Art of Patience

I waited on top of a tree branch, perched like some mechanical vulture among the alien foliage. The twisted bark beneath me was warm to the touch, pulsing with its own internal rhythms that had nothing to do with any earthly tree. These impossible plants grew within the dungeon's constructed reality, part of the Flux-created environment that defied normal understanding. But it held my weight, and the height gave me a clear view of the approaches to Keth's camp. That was all that mattered.

I knew the base would send others to look for their companions. It was basic tactics, the kind of thing anyone who'd survived this long would understand instinctively. When a patrol didn't return, you sent another patrol to find out why. Simple, predictable, exploitable.

The initiates I'd killed hadn't had much worth taking. I had looted their bodies methodically, searching through their meager possessions with the thoroughness of a man who couldn't afford to overlook anything useful. But there had been nothing of importance worth claiming. A few healing potions of questionable quality, some dried rations that tasted like cardboard, personal trinkets that meant nothing to anyone but the dead.

I wasn't even sure what I had been looking for. Something better than this backpack, maybe. Some piece of equipment that would give me an edge, make the hunting easier. But the truth was that these were bottom-tier initiates, the kind of people who followed stronger leaders because they lacked the strength to lead themselves. Of course they had nothing worth taking.

Keth likely didn't have many initiates left under his command anyway. From what I'd observed during my reconnaissance, I was reasonably sure there had only been a dozen or so to begin with. With four down, that left eight. Basic mathematics. I was guessing he would send another four to look for the missing patrol, leaving half his remaining strength to guard the camp.

I couldn't be sure, however. I didn't know Keth personally, had never studied his methods or tactical preferences. I was merely operating on what I thought a reasonable person might do in his situation. It was educated guesswork at best, and in a place where mistakes meant death, guesswork could be dangerous.

It was some hours before I felt Flux aspects approaching through the forest. The dungeon's artificial lighting had shifted subtly, the way it did to mark the passage of time in this place where day and night were just concepts from a world far away. The ceiling of dark stone that hung impossibly high above us remained unchanged, weeping its eternal tears of crimson. My respirator continued its mechanical rhythm, filtering the strange air with steady efficiency. The sound had become so natural to me that I barely noticed it anymore, like the beating of my own heart.

As it turned out, Keth only sent three more. Either he was more cautious than I'd expected, or he was running lower on manpower than my estimates suggested. Either way, it worked in my favor.

"Idiots probably got lost," a female initiate snorted as they approached the area where I'd left the bodies. Her blonde hair bobbed as she shook her head in disgust, dark robes flowing around her like liquid shadow. "I told Keth we should have sent someone more reliable than those four morons."

A living shadow whispered something in a language I didn't recognize, the words flowing like smoke given voice. The sound was unsettling, like hearing voices from beyond the grave. Its form shifted and wavered as it spoke, never quite solid, never quite gas.

"No one knows your language, bud," a leonin initiate said with obvious irritation. The cat-like humanoid's features were twisted into a permanent scowl, golden fur matted with dried blood from previous fights. "Speak something the rest of us can understand or shut up."

"Oh, Zar preserve us." The woman's voice dropped to a horrified whisper, her brown eyes widening as she caught sight of the corpses scattered through the underbrush. "What the hell happened here?"

I wasn't paying much attention to their reactions, the shock and fear that radiated from them through the Flux. Instead, I was studying the living shadow initiate with the clinical interest of a predator sizing up unusual prey. I didn't think my resonance blade would do much damage to something made of animated darkness. After all, how could one cut through shadow itself?

But through the Flux, I could feel the shadow initiate's aspects, the energy patterns that held its unusual form together. Cold void, mostly, the absence of light and substance that defined its existence. But there was echo as well, temporal energy that let it exist partially outside normal time. And at its core, barely visible to my enhanced senses, was something else entirely.

A heart of sorts. A crystalline core that pulsed with the vital aspect, the spark of life that animated even this creature of living darkness. Almost every living thing had at least a little vital energy, it represented life itself, so it only made sense that even a shadow would need some connection to existence.

I reached out through the Flux and promptly crushed that gem with focused force.

The shadow initiate burst apart like smoke in a strong wind. There was no blood, no dramatic death throes, just the sudden dissipation of darkness as whatever held it together failed catastrophically. One moment it was there, whispering in its alien tongue, and the next it was gone, leaving only empty air where a living being had stood.

I would have done the same thing to the others, reached into their chests and crushed their hearts with the Flux. But targeting multiple individuals with my crushing ability was somewhat beyond my current capabilities, and these two appeared to have higher Containment than the shadow creature. I could feel their defenses, their ability to resist Flux-based attacks, making it difficult to reach their vital organs directly.

Their shock and surprise radiated from them like heat from a fire as they tried to process what had just happened. But though I could not crush their hearts, I could still target their weapons. Resonance blades were external manifestations, easier to affect than internal organs.

One by one, I crushed their resonance weapons, applying focused pressure until the crystal matrices that contained their energy collapsed into useless slag. The leonin's massive two-handed blade crumpled like foil. The woman's elegant curved sword folded in on itself until it resembled abstract art more than a weapon.

Then I dropped down from my perch, landing on the forest floor with predatory grace. My own blade ignited as I moved, white light touched with crimson cutting through the dungeon's eternal twilight. A perfect ten slash, the heavenly cut, bisected the leonin man before he could react to his sudden disadvantage.

Blood sprayed like a crimson zipper opening across the landscape as whatever flesh my blade touched steamed and burned. The resonance energy didn't just cut, it cauterized, leaving wounds that would never heal properly even if the victim somehow survived. The leonin's two halves toppled in opposite directions, his feline features locked in an expression of terminal bewilderment.

The blonde woman was screaming, a high-pitched sound that echoed off the twisted trees and probably carried back toward their camp. But I froze her vocal cords with my Command Presence, cutting off the noise mid-shriek. She stood motionless, eyes wide with terror, watching me approach with the mechanical precision of death itself.

My voice emerged from the respirator's speakers, mechanical and distorted, barely recognizable as human speech.

"Has your group found a key?"

I had planned on asking Keth this question directly, but if I could extract the information sooner, then all the better. Intelligence was a weapon like any other, and the more I knew about what waited ahead, the better prepared I would be to take it.

I released her from my hold so she could answer, though I kept her body frozen while allowing her to speak. Her mouth worked frantically, trying to form words through lips that trembled with terror.

"What the fuck? Who are you?" She managed to gasp out the question while reaching instinctively for where her blade had been. Her eyes widened further as she realized it was nothing but crushed metal now, useless as a weapon.

"How did you...?" She gulped, looking up at me with the kind of fear reserved for forces of nature, for things that couldn't be reasoned with or escaped from.

I waited, saying nothing. My only response was the mechanical sound of my breathing, the steady rhythm that had replaced my human respiratory system. The silence stretched between us, heavy with implied threat.

"We think a Flux beast is guarding one deep in a cave nearby," she finally stammered out, the words tumbling over each other in her haste to appease whatever I was. "But we can't get to it. There's a group of Molech's initiates camped outside, blocking the entrance. We've been watching them for days, trying to figure out how to get past."

She swallowed hard, throat working against the terror that threatened to choke her. "There! I told you what I know. Now please, let me live."

A tinge of hope crept into her voice, the desperate optimism of someone who believed that cooperation might be rewarded with mercy. That providing useful information would somehow earn her continued existence.

It gave me no pleasure to crush that hope. I wasn't a man who relished the killing itself, I had come to understand. The act of ending a life held no particular appeal for me, no sadistic satisfaction in watching the light fade from someone's eyes. That didn't make me any better than those who did enjoy it, of course. I was still a killer, and just because I didn't feel good about taking lives didn't make that fact any less real.

She was experience. She was points. She was advancement toward whatever I was becoming. The universe had reduced all human interaction to these simple equations, and I had learned to think in its terms.

My blade removed her head in a single flash of light, so quickly that her expression of desperate hope never had time to change. The corpse fell to the ground with a wet thud, joining the growing collection of bodies that marked my passage through this alien forest.

Three more down. That left five back at the camp, assuming my initial count had been accurate. Manageable odds, especially now that I knew what I was looking for.

It was time to finish Keth's little base off completely, eliminate whatever remained of his faction before moving on to bigger prey. Then I would hunt down that cave, deal with Molech's people, and claim the key they were all so interested in.

The hunt was just beginning, and I was finally learning to enjoy it.
