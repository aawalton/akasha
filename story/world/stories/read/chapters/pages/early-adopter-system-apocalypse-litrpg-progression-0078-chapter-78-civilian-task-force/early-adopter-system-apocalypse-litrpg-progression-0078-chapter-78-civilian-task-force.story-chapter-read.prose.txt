Kain P.O.V.

The sound of fighting could be heard before the truck even turned the corner. A screeching wail, somewhere between a rooster’s crow and tearing metal. Worse were the screams and shouts of the people obviously fighting whatever it was that had produced that sound.

The group turned the corner, barely waiting for the truck to stop before all five piled out and ran towards the barricades.

Perhaps thirty or forty townspeople surrounded the perimeter, with six active combatants fighting in rotation. Boxes, crates, overturned cars and other debris made up barricades protecting zones for healing, coordination and recovery.

Kain’s eyes were immediately drawn to the most fortified one.

Behind it stood a woman he recognized. She was average height and a little thick, but in the way obviously sturdy people tended to be. She looked slightly younger than her late fifties. Her curly, salt-and-pepper hair was pulled into a clean bun as she ladled something from a pot.

The arrival of an obviously organized group seemed to perk up the townspeople who weren’t too busy or exhausted to care.

“Military?” one called out as they approached the woman’s well fortified position.

Sombra looked over, then back towards Kain.

“Well, boy, are you going to answer them?” Said the woman, barely glancing up.

“Mags.” He grinned.

“That’s Ms. Margaret to you.”

“Of course, Ms. Margaret,” he said. His cheek dimpled before the smile dropped as he looked towards the creature.

There was a man holding a flame-spitting chainsaw. A few people held spears, one a pitchfork. Others had gone in with axes. It was easy to tell who had skill, who had Skills, who had both, and who was basically distraction fodder.

“You guys are with the military, right?” The man asked again. He was wielding what appeared to be a proper System spear.

Behind him someone was pulled from the field as another person rotated on, this one physically weaker but harder to dismiss. It was rare this early to see someone with what appeared to be a pure mana focus. The young woman rapidly discharged sustained bolts of electricity at the tree creature, toggling the Skill on and off to avoid friendly fire.

Even in Kain’s group, everyone bent towards weapons or hybrid builds except Myka, and Kain didn’t really consider the guy part of his team yet.

“We’re something of a civilian task force.” Kain answered the man, then turned back to Ms. Margaret. He knew who was really in charge here. “It looks like this isn’t the first battle you’ve seen.”

“Nah, we’ve been fighting since the world went wonky.”

Kain nodded, then turned to Raj. “Range?”

“Mostly Level 3. A few Level 4.” She looked towards Ms. Margaret. “And one Level 5.”

Kain’s eyebrows rose before he schooled his expression. “And the creature?”

“Level 7. It’s called a Lillow. Identify says it has nature mana and regenerative properties.”

“The thing doesn’t really have a brain or central nervous system,” Myka added. “It’ll be a pain to cripple the normal way. It does have a mana core. I think that replaces the mana heart in beasts? And a super-protected nucleus that shifts around inside it. If we hit that, no more regen, then we can– We...”

The group was giving him a look he didn’t know what to do with.

“Yeah, I have a...” He scratched the back of his head. “I’m pretty sure I told you I have an identification type Skill?”

Sombra patted him on the back.

“Right.” Kain nodded stiffly before turning back to Margaret. “Well, it’s more information than I could have told you.”

Margaret turned to the flustered healer. “That’s a lot more than the ‘big, ugly, and doesn’t go down easy’ we had on it before.”

Kain’s smile stiffened slightly at the corners.

“Well,” he said. “We’re here to help. Team,” he called.

They turned to face the lillow.

It had six limbs on its upper half and six on its lower, like a centaur gone wrong. Its upper torso had two arms at the front and two jutting from its back. Four lower limbs rooted into the ground, tearing up earth and asphalt as it trundled along, with another two limbs protruding from its lower back.

Instead of bark and branches, the lillow was made of overlapping corded vines that shifted like snakes and flexed like exposed muscle fibre. It had no head or face, just arms, legs, a torso and a gaping maw.

The screech was one more thing to add to the nightmare fuel.

“All right, everyone, gather up.”

Sombra, Dani, and Raj huddled closer while Myka dithered on the outside. He’d been in a few skirmishes with them, but Kain had kept him to healing after the fact. A minuscule part of him regretted that now, if only because it meant this Lvl. 7 would be their first actual fight together.

Sombra re-read the description of his newest Skill, Charged Shot.

[Charged Shot (Tier 1): Active. Channels mana through a weapon to empower projectiles when fired, increasing damage and penetration. Effect scales with mana invested.This Skill has growth potential.]

"Sombra. Tactics?" Kain requested. The older man dismissed his Interface, then turned his attention to the group.

Within a minute Sombra had outlined sight lines, weaknesses, and reiterated the kill condition. The Target Weakness Skill came in exceptionally handy.

“Raj, you’re on tank duty. Stay back from the legs so it can’t kick you and keep aggro in the front. Sombra, ranged DPS. You said that new Skill would help with the whole actually-doing-damage-with-a-gun issue," Kain said, and Sombra nodded in confirmation.

Two more defenders went down while they hashed out the plan. Those on the back line quickly pulled them from the battlefront as replacements rotated in.

“Good. Dani, damage dealer.” Kain fist-bumped the girl. “Stick to targets of opportunity. Don’t bother with easy shots when you can take the big ones. I’ll be behind you, okay? We stick to the plan, and if that goes sideways we work with what we’ve got.”

He unsheathed his sword. Dani bounced on her toes and Raj rolled her shoulders before slinging her giant hammer over one of them. Sombra pulled his weapon out of its case, while Myka shook out his legs.

Just before they broke off, Ms. Maggie grabbed Kain’s arm.

“Now hold on. Where’s Scarlet?”

“What?”

“Your sister?” Worry crept into Margaret’s eyes.

Kain scoffed. “Ran off to do her own thing before we even got to the fight.”

The older woman frowned. “She’s safe, though? She made it?”

“Yeah, she’s around.”

Myka frowned at the exchange but followed the others to their positions.

“All right, let’s bring this beast down.” Kain vaulted over the barrier and the rest of the team took up their positions.

For a while, everybody just did their job.

Kain’s sword, coated in wind mana, sliced through limbs with ease. Raj dodged tentacles, fist slams and attempted stomps as she smashed the lillow’s limbs aside with her hammer before ducking and rolling away. Whenever its attention deviated towards her teammates, she hammered into the creature until it focused on her again.

Dani cackled as she sprinted beneath its stomping legs, juking vines erupting from beneath her feet as she combined her attacks with the chainsaw guy Except his chain roared with magical flames.

He’d hack at a limb with his flaming chainsaw; she’d reinforce Skillit and smash through the next.

Anytime either she or Raj got into trouble, Kain was there with his wind blades.

“Dani, stay left!” he called. “Raj, stop just dodging. You have to actually tank its blows if you want to keep its attention.”

Wherever Kain couldn’t be, Sombra was, sniping with his new gun. Mana-charged bullets smashed into the limbs. Excess kinetic force discharged on impact, turning each shot into a tiny grenade.

Amidst it all, Myka cast his spells.

“Wind works, but it regenerates too quickly,” he said from where he and Sombra overlooked the battlefield. “Earth only slows it down, and it’s too strong for Raj’s earthen walls. Water’s the same as wind. I even tried pulling water out of it, but the mana inside its body makes it too difficult to control. Fire should work, look at chainsaw guy! But the thing won’t stay burned.”

“It’s green wood,” Sombra said.

“Green wood?”

“Water content’s too high. Best you’ll get? Scorch marks and acrid smoke. Could probably cauterize a wound, but those vines are unlikely to catch.”

They looked towards the chainsaw guy, one of the few Lvl. 4s on the battlefield. He and Dani were doing significant damage, slowly chipping away at it. Myka wished the thing had one of those video-game health bars, and internally cursed whatever stingy System this was that was happy to send him a pop-up box of attributes, but no actual Health Stat.

“Maybe I’ll try more concentrated flames. I think I could make, like, flame bolts. Like your bullets.” Myka glanced at him. “Really cool, by the way.”

“Thanks.”

Myka wove fire-aspected mana. His wand glowed as he used the focus to pump more and more mana into the construct before firing off the flaming projectile. It wasn’t enough. The creature just absorbed the damage.

Whenever it felt itself weakening, the lillow would send fine root tendrils burrowing into the soil, then it would regenerate. They couldn’t get anywhere near its core, let alone the reinforced nucleus shifting around inside it.

Haphazard attacks were ruthlessly punished. In the time since they’d joined, two more townspeople had been rotated out, and Raj had a nasty, whip-like wound along one shoulder and arm.

“We need to do something else. This regeneration is too much,” Myka said.

“Suggestions?” Sombra asked.

Myka watched as the lillow rooted itself into the earth again, he tracked the process.

“I think I can turn its strength into its weakness.”
