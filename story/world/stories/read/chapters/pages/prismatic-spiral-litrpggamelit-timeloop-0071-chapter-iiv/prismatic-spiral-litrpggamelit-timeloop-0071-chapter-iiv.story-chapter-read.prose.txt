Chapter II.V

After recovering from hypothermia, Holly was still steadfastly set on joining Mitsuko to Porcelain Island. Once thawed out, Holly took charge of arranging everything for Mitsuko. She negotiated with the captain, helped stock up on supplies in the city, and even ordered more books for Mitsuko from the local book shop.

With everything else being taken care of for her, Mitsuko had plenty of time to dedicate to experimenting with her spells. But, instead of dedicating her time solely to Deteriorate, Mitsuko decided to take a trip into the town center and visit the other sage she had access to. Escara, Sage of Retrospection.

Juniper approved of this decision, agreeing that it would be wise to meet with Escara. She didn’t say it outright, but Mitsuko got the impression that Juniper was very pleased by this turn of events. Likely, she wanted them to meet together without Sterling on hand to interfere with their conversations.  They probably wanted to tag team her in an attempt to convince her to cut Sterling out.

While Mitsuko expected Juniper to use her shoulder as a perch to ride around on, the sage never made any movement to do so. Another case of the sage probably trying to stay on her good side. It wasn’t going to work. And Mitsuko wasn’t about to offer or comment on it. The seagull continued to hop along on the street behind her until they entered the clock tower.

A cacophony of clockwork noise greeted Mitsuko as she entered. While she ascended the stairs to the side, she kept an eye on Juniper, curious if the bird would get herself crushed between gears rather than ask her for a favor.

But, despite it not being her natural body, Juniper still deftly avoided death by cog. She somehow made it to the top of the steps unscathed.

Escara bowed and greeted Mitsuko as she stepped up onto the loft. Behind him, the massive glass window showed the entire city sprawled out beyond them, with the hands of the clock stretched across the glass.

Escara’s hands blurred with movement. The mute sage signed a message incomprehensible to Mitsuko.

“He wishes to greet the Champion and asks what he’s done to deserve the pleasure of your company on this fine day,” Juniper translated for him. “He also wishes to express his regret for his inadequacies as your guide. And says your new guide….” She trailed off, then rephrased what she was saying. “He is pleased to see I am here.”

“Sterling is my friend,” Mitsuko said bluntly. She faced the bitelas man, unflinching. “Show him respect or do not speak of him at all.”

Escara bowed once again, but Mitsuko noted that this time his face’s expression was stoney. She was getting better at reading bitelas people and knew enough to know that her words had not pleased him. Good. Him bad mouthing Sterling didn’t please her. Or…was it bad-signing? Since he didn’t speak with his mouth? Regardless, those were semantics that Sterling would argue about. Not something to get into with these two sages.

“He asks what he owes this please today,” Juniper repeated as Escara signed once again.

“I asked a question of Sterling and as an answer he told me that Retrospection unlocks the ability to rewind my mind at level 5.”

“That is accurate,” Juniper translated. “Retrospection is a spell of memories. It looks to the past and lets the spellcaster access insight into what once was.”

“The spell of memories,” Mitsuko repeated. “So it’s related to mental magic then?”

“All magic is related to all other magic. It’s united within our blood.”

“I can’t cast all magic though,” Mitsuko pointed out. “I can cast temporal spells though. Does that mean that I can access mental spells as well if I had a teacher?”

Escara shrugged, then continued to sign.

“Just as he told you in your last visit when you asked about divination spells, there are relations, but we don’t know about your natural talent for any branch of magic save for temporal.”

Her natural talent for other normal branches of magic was nonexistent. Mitsuko had been secretly hoping they had more of a connection to mental magic so she’d have access to another branch. But she supposed that was greedy. At least she could manipulate memories tangentially. In fact, she was fairly confident that if she could obtain the ability to undo her memories, then accessing those of another would be on the table for higher levels. But she didn’t ask. It would doubtlessly cost her a precious question.

“Can you give me advice on how to level up Retrospection? With Mend it was easy. Retrospection I feel like I’m spending a lot of effort to just stare at unmoving rocks for hours on end.”

“You should push yourself to watch things with a wide variety of changes in a short amount of time,” Escara advised through Juniper. “For example, you used Retrospection on that cog over there in a previous loop and witnessed it spin around backwards for hours. While a moving item is good, redundant movement is not nearly as valuable as unique movement.”

Mitsuko considered that. Unique movements. She once again thought back to the heating stone used as a torture device on the sculpt aboard The Terror. That would probably count as a ‘unique’ movement.

“Sterling also mentioned that using Mend in stressful situations would advance the level faster than using it while safe. Is it the same for Retrospection?”

“It is the same for all of our spells,” Juniper answered. “My spell, Deterioration, likewise will advance in levels far quicker should you wield it in combat.”

“I don’t think I’ll be able to use Retrospection in a fight,” Mitsuko replied. “Not unless I get some major upgrades at the next level.”

Escara’s mandibles twitched. The bitelas equivalent of a grin. Then he signed some more.

“I cannot say that,” Juniper scolded the other sage. “You’ll be as bad as Sterling if you give away information like that for free.”

Mitsuko held back her own grin. The sage was defending his spell. Good. That fact alone gave her a bit of extra information. There likely would be at least some way to utilize the spell in combat.

Mitsuko continued to try to mine for information from the sage. But since it was going through a second layer of censorship with Juniper translating everything, it was even more difficult to get anything useful.

After a long discussion of the usefulness of Retrospection and how to level it up faster, the clocktower donged around them, shaking the room and signifying the passing of another hour. With her ears now ringing, Mitsuko decided to depart. She felt like Escara had revealed all he was likely to at her current level without her using one of her few questions.

“At least Sterling doesn’t have to deal with that every hour,” Mitsuko muttered to herself as she descended the clocktower’s stairs. A buzz continued to rattle her ears from the hourly noise from the clock. Sterling’s treasure room on Ashen Island might be painfully dull, but it was also peaceful.

“My room is preferable to the other two,” Juniper agreed. She hopped down the steps in Mitsuko’s wake. “It might be redundant, but at least I can spend my hours admiring my plants and coaxing their growth in unique patterns. It’s kept me busy these loops.”

“Am I able to ask you about your hobbies? Or would that require a question?”

“Ask away!” Juniper said, sounding delighted by the idea. “I’ll make certain to stray away from anything sensitive that enters into the territory of the Prismatic Spiral’s functionalities or my magic. No need to worry about me duping you. I wish only for your success.”

Mitsuko ducked under a low cog. If that caught her hair she would be scalped. Not an experience she was looking to explore in the timeloops.

“You’re a gardener?” Mitsuko asked. “How did that all start?”

“It’s custom among fauns. We grow our own food and live within the trees of the forest. I’m assuming druids still exist in the world?”

“Yes. Actually, there’s one in the local circus. But they’re rare.”

“Oh yes, her. Well all fauns are born with a natural understanding of the forest. And our elders can…” Juniper’s words slowed for a moment. “Excuse me, could shape the trees while they still lived.”

“All of your people are gone now?” Mitsuko asked.

“That is a question I should level at you, not the other way,” Juniper teased with a note of sorrow. “Do you know of any fauns in the modern world?”

“No,” Mitsuko admitted. “I’ve heard stories. But not of any modern civilizations.” She paused. “I’m sorry.”

“The world moves on. People, places, history. It all succumbs to time. I am the sage of change.”

“That might be true, but the world also is a big place,” Mitsuko offered. “I haven’t been to all of it. It’s possible that fauns still exist out there.”

“Yes. It is possible,” Juniper acknowledged. “Thank you for your kind words.”

They exited the clocktower and Mitsuko stopped speaking to the seagull. But, rather than make it hop on the ground in her shadow, Mitsuko scooped up the bird and set it on her shoulder.
