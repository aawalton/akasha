Yue was staring down at a table with three slips of paper on it and a stone that had a formation array carved into it. The array was a holographic projection array that was displaying the instructions for this exam. There were no examples.

“What the hell does it mean, inscribe the spell for fireball?” She asked the stone sitting in front of her.

Deciding to give up on that one, she moved on to the next. “Inscribe the spell for a shield formation array” she read out loud as she squinted at the small text.

She glanced down at the slip of paper in front of her and cursed the examiners for only giving her one chance at inscribing anything. The next moment her eyes started glancing around herself nervously as the thought that if she failed even one of these she might wash out of the recruitment ceremony and be forced to live as a mortal until the next one where she would have less of a chance of being accepted even if she did study her ass off.

She turned her attention to the next spell out of six that they had suggested she inscribe onto one of these slips of paper. She breathed in, suddenly deciding to centre herself. She could smell the faint fragrance of sandalwood smoke in the air, feeling the calming effects, she opened her eyes. They landed on the braziers around the edge of the curtained off section, watching the smoke slowly rise and float over the area. She turned back to the hologram in front of her.

“Okay, there has to be at least one thing I can do. Let's read this carefully.” She whispered to herself.

She took a deep breath and turned her attention to the ink next to her hand, noting it was cinnabar ink, she could smell its metallic scent, sharp and heady. A brush lay next to it. It looked a little dinged and roughed up, but undoubtedly had the tail-hair of a spirit fox. It was decent equipment for talisman work provided by the sect for her. Her eyes fell to the slip of papers, taking a proper look at them, she noted that they had a subtle texture, woven with silk threads and powdered jade dust.

“Damn, each one of these slips, on their own, would cost more than my family could make in a year.” She lamented, feeling how shameful it would be if she wasted even one of them.

She decided to approach this the same way she would approach her formations, only they would activate when broken instead of completed. With this in mind, she picked up the brush and slowly dipped it in the ink. Her other hand held the sleeve of her robe out of the way before she glanced at the holographic image and selected a spell that she felt slightly comfortable being able to complete.

Her first stroke dragged across the paper. The line wavered, too thick at the start, too thin at the end. The rune’s shape already felt wrong. She clenched her jaw, refusing to give up.

The next symbol required a tight curl. Her hand shook slightly, the brush scratching faintly as it faltered. She narrowed her eyes, trying to concentrate as she pushed her Qi into the ink, tracing the curve until it closed. A faint shimmer flickered, then sputtered out.

She cursed softly under her breath before she lifted the brush, dipping it again, she tried the next mark. This one was sharper, a diagonal slash meant to anchor the flow. She pushed slightly too hard, causing the paper to shift slightly, the ink spreading unevenly.

By the time she neared the final stroke, her forehead was damp with sweat. She paused and inhaled shakily, breathing in the sandalwood to help calm her nerves again. She gathered the tiny amount of Qi that still remained. Slowly, carefully, she pressed the brush down, dragging it across to finish the pattern.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened. Then the lines gave off a dull shimmering flicker like a dying ember before it began to glow steadily with a dull blue colour. A few breaths later, it receded into the lines and vanished.

“Does that mean I succeeded?” She asked, looking a little confused.

She turned, looking around, only for a robed person to appear next to her out of nowhere. It took a moment before she recognised the girl was the one that had led her to the talisman exam area. She lent down to look at the talisman before making a ‘tsk’ sound.

“Crude.” She said as she narrowed her eyes. “Almost a waste but, you managed to scrap through, congratulations, you passed this exam. You may prove your talent by doing another, or retreat to your cabin and prepare for tomorrow's exam.”

When the Disciple vanished, Yue lept from her chair like it was on fire and dashed for the curtain exit wanting to be far away from this money pit, that made her feel like she was playing with riches far above her. At least with the alchemy exam that she was looking forward to. The expensive nature of it was a little hidden by the fact she had no idea what the resources she was going to be using were worth.
