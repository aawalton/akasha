It had been some time since Vin last felt like a celebrity anywhere other than Terra itself, but they quickly learned that Fritz hadn’t been exaggerating in the slightest about not really having to deal with visitors. The moment the three of them touched down on the creature’s back with some help from Slow Fall, they were instantly swarmed by a group of beastkin.

“What are you doing here?!” an elderly man shaking a gnarled cane shouted at them as he tried to get close.

“Fritz, are they dangerous?” a woman asked, keeping her son safely behind her. “You should throw them off God’s back!”

“What happened to all your fur?!” a young girl shouted, before someone who looked like her older sibling grabbed her and dragged her away from all the commotion.

“Everyone calm down!” Fritz shouted, and Vin was surprised when the throng of beastkin actually listened to him. Sweeping the crowd with his sharp gaze, Fritz grunted. “Thank you. These two are known as Vin and Alka. They’re from beyond God’s back, from an entirely different world fragment. They are exploring the world of Edregon, and I am going to show them around town some. We’ve got important things to talk about, so if you have any questions for them, wait until they’re on their own. If you see me with them, find something else to occupy yourself with. Spread the word.”

There was a heavy amount of grumbling, but nobody spoke up or argued. Slowly, the crowd began to disperse, and while plenty of people were still staring at them from afar and whispering to one another, they kept their distance.

“Damn, talk about impressive,” Alka said, whistling as she turned toward Fritz. “Are you in charge around here?”

“In a sense,” Fritz grunted, currently staring down that old beastkin until the man finally lowered his cane and clicked his tongue, walking away. “I’m God’s Tender. It’s a special role within my town that comes with a lot of weight and responsibility. The town has an actual elder in charge of town business, but when it comes to anything related to God or the world beyond, my word supersedes hers.”

“And seeing as we definitely come from beyond, that means you are pretty much in charge,” Vin surmised. “Out of curiosity, what was your base class?”

“Scout,” Fritz said, motioning for the two of them to follow him. “Though these days I’m a Guardian of Beasts. Let’s find somewhere a little quieter where we can talk. Without a few hundred people constantly staring at us.”

Fritz led the two of them through the compact town, and Vin couldn’t help but take in the curious choices in architecture clearly designed to maximize space. Buildings were close together and many of them had second and third floors connected with one another, allowing people to walk around on God’s back while still providing extra room on the upper floors. He had to keep reminding himself that they were actually walking around on the back of a giant animal, because at the moment, the ‘ground’ beneath his feet felt as sturdy as ever. The beast’s hard, dry skin looked almost like cobblestone, and he shook his head when he spotted a few children kicking a ball around over in that tiny park that was sectioned off for such activities.

“And this… God. It seriously doesn’t mind you all running around on top of it? How did you even manage to build up here in the first place?”

“It’s a symbiotic relationship,” Fritz explained, pulling open a door for them and motioning for them to go inside. “We ensure God is healthy and has plenty to eat, and God offers us protection from things like monsters or those who would harm us.”

“So what’s with the giant chain?” Alka asked as they headed into the small room. “I feel like chaining your God would sort of go against, you know, worshiping them.”

“The Sacred Chain was not our doing,” Fritz said, frowning as he took a seat at the table. The room he’d led them into reminded Vin of a cluttered veterinarian’s office. There were detailed sketches of the giant beast lining the walls and a bunch of alchemical equipment sitting off to the side. Tomes were lying open here and there, and a quick glance in one of them revealed it to be a historical journal documenting treatments for their God.

“According to our records, it was a terrible act forced upon our God nearly eight hundred years ago,” Fritz continued. “Our people were once plentiful, and our Gods far greater in number. Our lands were invaded, and our towns razed one after another. Our ancestors fought with everything they had, but in the end, even with all the might of the Gods, it wasn’t enough. The other Gods were slain and our brother and sister towns destroyed before our very eyes. Eventually, we were the last. Then, through some miracle, our attackers decided not to vanquish the last of the Gods. Instead, they fashioned the Sacred Chain, restricting our God’s movements to prevent it from enacting vengeance for its fallen kind. We were told if we ever so much as touched the Sacred Chain, they would return and send us to join our brothers and sisters. Our people have remained here ever since.”

“Wow…” Vin muttered, looking at the sketches of the giant animal in an entirely new light. Based on Fritz’ story, it sounded as though the people who attacked his ancestor’s lands had decided to keep this ‘God’ around for one reason or another. Either as some sort of living trophy, or some last-minute guilt about wiping out the entire species. Whichever the case, it sounded as though the beastkin had ended up being saved because of that. “That’s a pretty crazy tale.”

“It has been passed down from one generation to the next over the years,” Fritz said, sighing as he motioned around the room. “And like my mother before me and her father before her, I was raised to be this generation's God Tender. Of course, no amount of preparation could have ever prepared me for our God’s domain being whisked away to Edregon.”

“Yeah, that’s one thing I still don’t quite get,” Alka said, fixing Fritz with her glowing green stare. “You guys know there are actual Gods, right? Or do you just not believe in them?”

“Just because there is one God, does not mean there cannot be another,” Fritz shrugged. “There are the Gods in the skies, and the Gods on the ground. Some are unseen, and some literally carry us on their backs. That is the way of the world.”

“Huh. Fair enough,” Alka said, leaning back in her chair. “So you’ve seen how Edregon works?”

“As the God Tender, it is my job to know what threats our God faces,” Fritz nodded. “I have not gone far, but I have catalogued the six adjacent fragments to our own. Each one is dangerous in its own right, but none of them seem overly hostile.”

“In case you didn’t notice yourself, you’re going to want to be careful about the fragment directly south of you,” Vin said, shuddering as he remembered their time running through the death fragment. “It’s filled with radiation. An unseen poison that will kill you if you spend too long within it.”

“I was able to tell something was off about the land, but I didn’t know what,” Fritz said, inclining his head slightly toward him. “You have my thanks.”

“Death fragment, capillans and their frozen wasteland, those prick-ish dwarves who won’t let anyone into their town, the flower fields, and the Necromancer compound,” Alka said, ticking the surrounding fragments off her fingers. “And one we haven’t been to yet. Overall, you could have worse neighbors.”

Vin was about to ask about the Druids Fritz had mentioned, before the entire room began to violently shake. His eyes widened and Alka shouted, but Fritz didn’t move so much as a muscle, continuing to sit back in his chair as he watched the two of them with a slight grin. That made it all the more obvious what was happening, and Vin laughed when he felt the beast under them slowly begin to move forward.

“Now that is going to take some getting used to!” Vin said. The ground slowly swayed side to side, and he smiled as these people’s God started walking.

“God is not able to walk far, due to the length of the Sacred Chain. But God does like to stretch their legs every now and again,” Fritz said, spinning one of the open tomes around and sliding it across the table. “Upon finishing off a forest, God will often stretch and find a spot to lie down and rest. Our Druids take advantage of that time to hop down from God’s back and get to work on regrowing the trees God will need upon waking. It’s a lengthy process, but one we have completed without fail all these centuries.”

“That’s amazing,” Vin said. Pulling the book closer, he skimmed through the detailed records of how the beastkin Druids had optimized their cycles of growth and regrowth.

“...What do you do about the building-sized clumps of poop?” Alka asked, staring pointedly at Fritz. “That has to be a problem, right?”

“Alka!” Vin snapped, before blinking when he got to the bottom of the page he was skimming. “Oh, looks like that’s actually part of the regrowth process. The Druids utilize it as fertilizer. It seems to even have slight magical properties, so they spread it out to help the forest grow back stronger than it was before.” Reading that, Vin gasped as everything finally clicked into place. “Oh, duh! It’s not a giant animal, it’s a giant magical beast! That explains how it can survive being so ridiculously large! Well now I just feel silly for not realizing that sooner.”

“It is as you say,” Fritz nodded. “We have been trapped in this small zone no more than a few miles wide for far longer than Edregon has existed. It was critical we devise a way to keep nature in a healthy state, else our God would have grown ill and perished. Things did get a bit dicey when we lost our levels, but we managed well enough. Thankfully Rapid Growth, our most important spell, is also only a tier 1.”

“Sounds like we need to come back here one day with Shia and Lumel for sure,” Vin said, closing the book and sliding it back across the table to Fritz. “We’re friends with a high-level Druid and another mage who is super interested in how different cultures work. Unfortunately they’re both busy with their own things right now.”

“I suppose so long as you do not touch the Sacred Chain, I can’t stop you from returning in the future,” Fritz said, looking like he wasn’t sure how he felt about such a thing. “So long as you do not go around spreading blasphemies about God or threaten anyone with physical harm, you are welcome here. Though if it wasn’t obvious from the earlier crowd, I feel it only fair to warn you that you will be mobbed without my presence. Most of my people have never seen any of the other races, and they know little of the world beyond our God. You shouldn’t be in any danger, but I imagine it will grow old quickly.”

“Our Druid friend would probably love that, but yeah, I’m not really into the whole ‘crazy mob’ scene,” Vin admitted, before looking at Alka. “Well, your call. Hunt down the dungeon in this fragment and head back to Terra, or spend the night here. Assuming that’s alright?”

“You’re welcome to stay,” Fritz shrugged. “We don’t exactly have guest rooms, but you can sleep on the table.”

“Let’s stay until tomorrow,” Alka decided, surprising Vin until she shifted over to the moaning language of the ghosts and continued. “I want to wait until night and see if I can’t climb out onto this God’s head. Should be cool balancing practice!”

“It’s your funeral,” Vin sighed, smiling at Fritz’ confused look before switching back to the beastkin’s language. “If you don’t mind, we’ll take you up on that offer. I’d love to get the chance to go through a few more of your books and maybe talk to your Druids before we head out!”
