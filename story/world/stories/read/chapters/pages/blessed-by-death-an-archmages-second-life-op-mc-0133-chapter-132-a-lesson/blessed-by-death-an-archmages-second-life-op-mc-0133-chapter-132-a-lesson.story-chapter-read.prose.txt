“If you know of the Old Ones, then you should know full well just how hopeless this situation is for you,” Heimler said, his expression cold. “The Old Ones predate the stars themselves. They are an aberrational existence that originate from a fractured universe, banished to the Outer Realms for unspeakable crimes long forgotten. It is not an exaggeration to say that even a single Old One is enough to wipe out an entire world.”

Levi didn’t move, didn’t even breathe as he regarded the two before him. The Old One radiated neither magic nor killing intent, yet despite that, its very presence seemed to herald unparalleled destruction.

“So,” Heimler said. “Will we do this the hard way again? Or will you choose to surrender this time–?”

The Old One screamed. A terrible, unholy sound so powerful that it would’ve instantly ruptured an ordinary human’s internal organs. Every piece of glass in the lab shattered, shockwaves rippled outward, and fissures split down the walls.

A sound of pain.

Heimler whirled to the side, face blanching in alarm. “What?!”

A pillar of scorching hellfire had blasted upward from below the Old One, enveloping the entire creature’s form in malevolent flames. The sickly scent of burning flesh filled the air as the Old One’s tentacles began disintegrating into ash.

Levi wasted no time with follow-up banter or negotiations. Now was not the time for theatrics. Instead, he went straight for the kill.

[Purple Lightning!]

Blistering violet lightning shot forth from Levi’s palm, instantly separating into thousands of branches that pierced through the air like the roots of an ancient tree. The lightning slammed into the Old One before it could react, punching through its flesh like paper. Thousands of holes formed, rendering the Old One practically see-through. Dark crimson blood sprayed the wall behind it.

The Old One let out another screech of pain, only for the sound to be abruptly choked off as Levi twisted his hand in a forced wrenching motion.

[Catastrophic Gravitas!]

The holes disappeared as quickly as they’d formed, collapsing shut with sickening squelches as the Old One imploded into itself. Another wave of Levi’s hand, and the Old One was sent flying backward, crashing into the blackboard. Raw, dense mana engulfed the Old One, pinning it into place against the wall.

The Old One tried to resist, but Levi raised his mana to its maximum output, imposing the full might of his magic unto the creature. He stalked forward, his steps graceful yet predatory, uncaring of Heimler who stood utterly frozen off to the side, until he came to a stop right in front of the Old One.

The Old One squirmed as it was crushed further into the wall, its tentacles spasming in every direction. The wall cracked and cratered beneath it, and the only reason the room didn’t collapse altogether was due to its hefty adamantine construction and runic reinforcement. The Institute had spared no expense. Even still, the entire room began shaking as Levi poured more and more mana into the skill.

[System Notification: Warning! Mana output has reached the maximum safety threshold!]

[System Notification: Warning! Mana output has exceeded the maximum safety threshold!]

[System Notification: Warning! Overclocking the skill [Catastrophic Gravitas] may result in catastrophic consequences!]

Levi ignored the System screen as he steadily overloaded the skill beyond the System’s built-in limits. Slowly, the Old One’s body caved inward, its tentacles getting compressed with such overwhelming force that the flesh began glowing white-hot and emitting crimson steam. Then there was a flash of bright light, and its tissues started ablating into superheated plasma that dripped onto the floor.

“You dare enter this world?” Levi said, his voice filled with barely restrained fury. “You dare crawl out of the Outer Realms and think to face me in battle? Look at me, Old One! Do you not recognize who I am? Who I served?”

The Old One roared in defiance, struggling to break free from the magic’s hold–

It stilled.

Even after Levi had retired, he still possessed a lingering connection to Death. The residual link was faint, nearly imperceptible, but definitely there. It was why animals instinctively hated him, though monsters – and eldritch creatures – tended to be far less attuned. However, if Levi used his magic to amplify the connection, like he was doing right now…

YOU… HOW ARE YOU HERE? WHY ARE YOU HERE?

The Old One did not use sound to speak. Instead, its voice resonated directly into Levi’s mind, deafening yet shocked, even fearful. Heimler flinched, his hand flying up to his right ear as blood began leaking out.

“I’m here because this is my world! Because this is my home.”

WE WERE TOLD THAT YOU NO LONGER SERVED YOUR MASTER.

“You were told…?” Levi shook his head. “No, that doesn’t matter. Your sources were right. But just because I’m a free agent now doesn’t mean that I won’t still fight for the principles I once swore to uphold. Your very presence upsets the natural balance of this world, you who act as a harbinger of endless death and destruction.”

MY NAME IS YEVANTA.

Reality trembled. Names held power, and the True Name of an Old One was no exception. The light in the room dimmed, gravity weakened, matter itself seemed to begin falling apart as the electromagnetic and nuclear forces that held atoms together came undone–

“I don’t care who you are!” Levi shouted. He slashed his hand across the air and his magic flooded the room, washing away the effects of the True Name in one fell swoop. “You do not belong here. You do not belong anywhere! You were banished to the Outer Realms for a reason – banished by my predecessors!”

Death had been the one to originally lead the effort to seal away the Old Ones. Levi didn’t know who Death’s servants had been at the time – Death had always refused to tell Levi about the others who’d served him – but Levi knew that the fight had been a great and terrible one, with so much destruction and pain and misery and a world that burned away beneath innumerable dying stars, the scent of ash in the air and the taste of blood in his mouth, his magic thrumming beneath his skin as he prepared to cast the Old Ones into the farthest edges of the ceaseless void–

Levi’s eyes widened and he jerked back. What… What was that? A vision? No, that was too vivid to be a vision. A… A memory? But from where? And why did it feel so familiar?

He was interrupted from his thoughts as the Old One spoke.

PREDECESSORS? THERE CAN ONLY BE ONE.

“One?” Levi narrowed his eyes. “One what?”

Yevanta ignored his question. RELEASE ME AND STAND ASIDE, EMISSARY. THIS DOES NOT CONCERN YOU. COMPLY, AND YOU WILL BE SPARED–

“Do not insult me!” Levi roared. “This does not concern me? Have you not heard a single word I said? Let me make this perfectly clear to you. You are not welcome here. You are not permitted here. This world is under my protection, so drown in my magic and begone!”

[System Notification: Critical Warning! [Catastrophic Gravitas] has entered skill meltdown!]

The skill burst apart with an earsplitting boom akin to thunder, all the magic compressed within the spell structure detonating all at once. Ordinarily, this would’ve vaporized everything within a hundred feet radius including the caster; however, Levi had anticipated this with his True Sight and used his magic to instead redirect the power straight into the Old One.

Yevanta exploded. Dark crimson blood and chunks of tentacles splattered outward at hypersonic speeds, painting every surface with gore. Levi and Heimler remained spotless, Levi having formed a barrier and Heimler having activated his [Stillpoint].

Levi let out heavy breaths, adrenaline coursing through his entire body.

Slowly, he turned to face Heimler.

Despite himself, the professor took a step back.

“What are you?” Heimler whispered. “How did you just–”

“Are you an idiot?” Levi snapped. “You summoned an Old One to this world? Have you any idea how dangerous they are, how much devastation they can wreak? You could’ve destroyed the entire world!”

Heimler recovered remarkably quickly. “Well, yes. That would be the whole point.”

“What?”

“Well, okay,” Heimler amended. “Not the entire world, just select parts of it. I summoned Ye– the Old One to tear down the majority of the world’s ruling structures, obliterate a large number of institutions, and massacre every adamantine-tier adventurer and above. With some exceptions, of course.”

There was a moment of silence.

“Shit,” Levi said. “For real?”

“Quite real,” Heimler said. “I’m surprised you didn’t know. Your superiors didn’t tell you?”

“... superiors?”

Heimler blinked. “You’re not from the Crucible?”

“No?” Levi said. “Definitely not. Though, they did try to recruit me recently…”

Heimler blinked. “They did?” He examined Levi for several moments, but must’ve seen the truth in Levi’s eyes. “Fascinating. And you refused to join them?”

“Of course.”

Heimler nodded, and though his expression remained impassive, Levi sensed a faint approval wafting off him. “A sound decision. They tried to recruit me once too, several years ago.”

Levi’s expression became carefully blank. “Did they, now?”

Heimler nodded. “They explained their plan to me and asked me to help. I refused, naturally. Sowing chaos, manufacturing tribulations, forging men into heroes, all to save the world…” He chuckled. “How very quaint. There is no point to such a plan. This world is already beyond salvation. Much better to simply tear it all down and start anew.”

Levi stared at him. “You rejected their offer because their plan wasn’t extreme enough for you?”

“Effectively, yes.”

“So you chose to summon an Old One instead? You’re mad.”

“Of course I’m mad,” Heimler said calmly. “The world is mad, and I am but one man. I don’t even have a secret organization on my side. The Old Ones were the only way for me to gain the necessary power to accomplish my goals.”

“At the cost of your own soul?”

Heimler shrugged. “A worthy sacrifice. I would gladly sell my soul a thousand times over.”

“Why? You’re a Mythmaker, a war hero, Qorbin’s former partner. What could’ve possibly driven you to this?”

Levi couldn’t reconcile the fact that the man in front of him had once been Qorbin’s partner. That Qorbin had spoken so highly of him.

Heimler was silent for a second. “It is funny you mention the war. Even funnier you mention my old partner. Do you know what our role was during the Third Blood War?”

Levi furrowed his brow. “Frontline fighters?”

“Initially, yes. When the war first started, we were dispatched to the front lines, acting as the vanguard against Arcedia. But when it became clear that Luminarche was losing… Qorbin and I were relegated to the role of cleaners. Do you know what that means? You’re Qorbin’s apprentice, are you not? Or pretending to be, at any rate. Did he ever tell you what we did?”

Levi’s face darkened. “You took measures to prevent the creation of vampiric thralls…”

“Don’t euphemize it,” Heimler snapped. “We massacred civilians. With every village that fell, the vampires turned the population into their thralls and added to their numbers. We were already losing against the vampires themselves; if they added an army of thralls to their numbers, then our defeat was all but assured. Fortunately, dead people could not be turned into thralls. So, the government sent us.”

Heimler exhaled, his gaze growing distant even as his tone remained clinical. “While I held back the vampires, Qorbin would sneak into villages and slaughter the entire population. He’s a Grand Assassin, after all. Fast, agile, undetectable… He could wipe out an entire town in a matter of minutes. Over the course of the war, Qorbin murdered tens of thousands of innocent people.”

Levi let out a long breath. “Right. He… He did what he had to do. What is your point?”

Heimler’s voice was colder than ice.

“My point is that he didn’t have to do that!”

Levi paused. “What do you mean?”

“Luminarche was not losing the war because Arcedia was too powerful; it was losing the war because it refused to fight properly. The government didn’t dispatch any of its Royal Sentinels or most of the army to the front lines. Half the adventurers didn’t care about the war, too busy with their own missions and grinding, and the other half were hired as guards by the nobles holed up in safe inner cities.”

“Huh? But… Why?”

“Because this country is pathetic. If the government had sent the Royal Sentinels to help, if the nobles had sent their soldiers to the front lines, if the adventurers had stopped prioritizing themselves for one moment, then we would’ve been able to fend the vampires off. Perhaps not fully, but we would’ve at least been able to evacuate the towns and villages in time. Instead, we were forced to fight an artificially outmanned and outgunned war for no reason other than stupidity and fear and greed.”

Distantly, Levi recalled how the adventurers stationed at Canum had chosen to run away, or how Hawke’s daughter had died because the reinforcements were too busy gambling, or...

“The worst part,” Heimler continued, “is that after the war, the entire country denounced and ostracized Qorbin. Village-Slayer Ravenbane, they called him. Murderer, they called him. They demanded that he be executed for his ‘crimes’. Have you any idea what Qorbin felt? To have an entire country call for his head?”

“Yeah,” Levi said softly. “Yeah, I do.”

“Don’t be absurd,” Heimler said. “How can you possibly know–” He fell silent at the weary, harrowed look in Levi’s grey eyes. He swallowed. “Well. In any event… This country disgusts me. The Crucible believes they can save the world, but I disagree. The only cure here is to raze the world to the ground and hope that the new generation will learn from the mistakes of their predecessors. And until my will is fulfilled…” His eyes gleamed darkly. “The Old One will not rest.”

Levi tensed up as all the clumps of viscera suddenly leaped into the air and converged next to Heimler. He immediately fired off a salvo of hellfire arrows, but the flames had no effect; the tissues weren’t even singed.

In less than a second, Yevanta fully regenerated, its tentacles undulating in fury. It had no visible injuries or wounds.

“Did you not learn your lesson?” Levi growled. He cast another [Catastrophic Gravitas], his magic rushing forward to crush the Old One to pulp once more–

It had no effect.

Levi blinked. “Wait, what?”

He scanned the Old One with his True Sight. Was it some trick? No… He could detect no barrier or defensive spell. Levi’s skill was working as intended. The Old One was just somehow immune, as if he’d gained some sort of ontological resistance.

It hadn’t been affected by the hellfire earlier either…

Oh fuck. Yevanta could acquire absolute immunity to attacks it had already experienced once before? Adaptive defense?

Then, Heimler spoke.

“I do not know who you are, Levi Ironwood, or if your name is even Levi Ironwood. But if you insist on standing in my way, then I’m afraid I have no choice. Old One… kill him.”

WITH PLEASURE.

Levi narrowed his eyes, his magic swirling wildly in preparation–

He blinked. His face abruptly became devoid of all emotions as he looked down at his left arm. Ash fluttered around it like snow.

Slowly, his arm detached cleanly at the shoulder and fell to the ground with a thud. Blood immediately followed, gushing forth in great torrents and painting the floor a deep crimson, iron and salt and ash.
