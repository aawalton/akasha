The following days slipped past quicker than a current swept eel. The fifty-something men and women, soon-to-be defenders of the Ember Republic, walked along roads and slithering fractures from daybreak to dayfall, after which they put their exhausted muscles through further abuse under—or behind—Lieutenant Brownweather's barely watchful eyes. If someone hadn't broken in their military issued boots beforehand, they definitely did throughout the journey. Groans, blisters and a general gray atmosphere had become just as commonplace as the visage of forever slanting wheat, bean, and corn mounds, something the mountainous landscape had no shortage of—at least at the start. The ground slowly evened out the farther they marched east, and it was a fact that didn't go unappreciated by the recruits.

Though he'd never explored this side of Újtavak, Kalsin's expectation of a sparse local population was quickly proven wrong. Standing on high ground, one could see settlements scattered across the region, tiny dots of civilizations sitting between the archipelago's web of fractures. They passed anything and everything from small huts to lone travelers' inns, religious shrines to disorganized yet bustling settlements. And the people, well, the people of the region were a lot of things, but boring wasn't one of them.

If Kalsin wanted to describe them in a single set of words, he'd have gone with 'farmer marauders'. Imagine this; a wide brimmed hat, bad teeth, white linen shirt with dark—and significantly dirtier—pants, topped off with a handcrafted axe or spear tied across the back. Practical, slightly intimidating. Judging from the number of narrow-eyed glares they had received, the people around these parts were either deeply suspicious, or looked into the sun for so long the expression had stuck on their face permanently.

Speaking of the sun, Blindlight had come, stayed for two days, and was gone without much issue. Their group had waited out the radiance in a little town called Hammershine, safely tucked in near-empty barns and chicken coops, then quickly resumed their journey. The only downside was the unpleasant odor of animal filth that accompanied them afterwards, especially true for Youthere, who had a pig defecate on his feet during the night. Ironically enough, it was Batshit giving him the most, uh, shit, about the unfortunate event. Even if this soldiering gig didn't work out for them, those two were definitely going places.

A few times Kalsin had caught sight of the Clenn recruits marching ahead. They were perhaps a quarter of a day's time ahead of them, similarly being escorted toward their destination by their own delegation of Clenn soldiers. The group was too far ahead for Kalsin to make out individuals, though it wasn't for the lack of trying. He could only see their golden uniforms glinting in the sun as if mocking the lesser creatures who followed in their footsteps.

Kalsin missed Cinder and Jack, and being so close yet separated only made the feeling intensify. What started as a sluggish fly buzzing in the back of his mind soon grew into a bloody trumpeting elephant with wings. He wanted to see them. He needed to know if they were fine. He’s been out of loop for too long, been stepping to the beat of others too obediently.

So, after making sure he could slip away unseen, Kalsin sneaked out of their camp every night when he had the chance, closed some distance between him and the Clenn squad, and waited.

Kalsin lost track of time as he adhered to the routine of practice, walking, and the snippets of sleep he caught after crawling into his bedroll, returning from yet another fruitless attempt to meet his friends in the night. He thought it unlikely, but if the people around him managed to look past his scars, they might've noticed the circles under his eyes—but to be fair, the growing stress was not exclusive to Kalsin. Many of the recruits also had it in spades.

Later arrivals, those who'd only had a couple days of training before the march, like Deplomacy, Quiescent, Leather and Yesfellow, could barely keep on their feet by the time the group stopped to rest. Even Uggo and Joe, two men who'd been there from the start, weren't immune to the mounting pressure, evident from their bloodshot eyes. And those were just the physical signs. Complaints. Sour attitudes. Bitching and moaning in the morning, switched up by the suddenly soulful confession to get off the chest around the somber campfire, like lost men clinging to mortality.

Whether he wanted or not, Kalsin learned many things about his companions during those evenings. Littleguy had lost a family. Pike had a knack for gardening and loved the smell of blooming lantana. Ringtail—who still maintained her bratty rivalry toward Kalsin—had worked as a thief-for-hire before the army, but funnily enough, the arrest that landed her in the military was based on false charges.

So, yes. They had it rough. It showed, and Kalsin was hardly an exception in that regard. Some caved, others coped, but they all put one foot after the other, because it was something they all signed up for. Coerced for the most part, but still. The illusion of choice had to count for something.

It was during one evening, after they had camped at the shore of a wide pond with ashen dirt and vegetation sparse around the edges, when Kalsin snuck out under the moonlight again. The water was calm and the night peaceful. The flap of the tent Kalsin shared with Crawl and Littleguy barely disturbed the silence as it swished back into place behind him. He looked around, took a moment to inhale the musty scent of still water, and moved out.

Creeping around the camp was easy on most nights; one or two days a week, Brownweather would remember to bark some orders and single out a couple of recruits for guard duty, but everyone knew it was more for the sake of training than actual watching for threats. This deep into the Republic, nobody would dare mess with the army. Not even for the potential haul of dirt-expensive swords and horses. It made Kalsin's former self as a hunter a little jealous. Vultures had many things; fine articles of steel, good leather packs, a mount to get around. But the fearsome reputation of a collective system of authority to scare off thieves wasn't one of them.

Kalsin soon left their tents behind and fell into a comfortable rhythm toward the Clenn camp. The moon was out in its full glory, casting a pale light on the shrub-lined wilderness and dirt road cutting through it. A high note of katydid—bush cricket—accompanied him at first, but even that much faded as he trudged to the thump of his heavy boots along the road. The wind held its breath, summer air warm, unmoving and unexpectedly pleasant.

The next hour swept past in a dreamy state, just walking, thinking, talking out loud, and more walking.

Though he acted the opposite, and few looking in would've guessed it… but Kalsin wasn't a people person. Not really. Not for long, at any rate. To maneuver the snare traps of conversations, to say the right things when the right things needed to be said—anyone can learn that. Easy as popping a leg out the socket. But the sound, the crunch, the feeling of bones slipping out of place—they wear on your mental if you're not wired for it. The pressure builds up, bit by bit, until it either breaks open with an explosion, or you let it out in a controlled environment and without judging eyes.

Kalsin preferred the latter.

He never remembered much of his ravings. It suited him well enough. He often emerged with burning eyes and the last pulses of a splitting headache, or curled on the ground, head tucked deep under his arms. One time, he woke in complete darkness only to find out he'd tore up his shirt and tied the ruined cloth over his eyes. Things like that. Everything was a blessed blur afterwards. The one detail he knew, that he was absolutely sure of, was that he never let other people see him in such a state. Shut it in a deep corner of his mind and keep going until he found solitude.

So when he felt a hand suddenly grip his shoulder, his senses returned like a sharp arc of lighting. He grabbed the arm, twisted with the entire weight of his body and pushed off to gain some distance. His knife was halfway out by the time he recognised the woman stumbling back, one arm cradled against her chest with another that ended in a stump.

Kalsin stared at his sister, breaths coming in gasps.

"Slow it," Cinder said in a calming voice. "Slow it, Kalsin. It's just me."

Kalsin felt his fingers go numb and he barely noticed his knife hit the ground. Tears that hurt with each drop—how long had he been crying?—poured down his face as he nearly tackled his sister in a bone grinding hug. He could delude himself all he wanted, but there was no denying how much her absence had shaken him.

Cinder let out a strangled laugh, hugging him back as much as she was able. "Missed your ugly mug, too. Augh, 'right, let me out please. Off of me with you." She took a step back, smiling at Kalsin. "You've grown at least two more layers of muscles on those arms."

Dressed in a similar uniform than that of Kalsin, except gold instead of gray, Cinder looked good under the pale light. She always did. Amidst all the confusion and uncertainty, Kalsin was glad to find some things never changed.

"I, uh... Yeah." Kalsin said. He cleared his throat. "Been getting lots of practice to up my form."

"I can imagine. They must be driving a hard routine over there, with and how you were no slouch to begin with." Cinder said. Her eyes fell on Kalsin’s jaw. On his new scar.

Just that single look made Kalsin wince, sharply turning his cheek away.

"Shit, Kalsin,” Cinder said quietly. “I'm... shit."

Kalsin snorted, though it was a weak sound. He wiped his eyes, and even through his clothes he felt the sick texture of his old scar. He didn't want to think about the other, the one perched right along his jaw, the one that used to belong to his friend and bodyguard. "Good enough summary. That's about as far as I got, myself."

"How did it happen? How could it?"

"Dunno. Magic, maybe?" Kalsin tried for a nonchalant tone, even though he was aware that his sister could see deep below the surface. "Woke in a cell with this thing on my jaw. Turns out, these rotten things are contagious via touch. Funny. It really is. I always wanted to be unique and stand out from the rest, but this wasn't exactly the talent I had in mind."

Cinder stayed silent for a while. "And still no...?"

"Nah." Kalsin said. He didn't have the surge. But that was a hope Kalsin had long given up on. He had no wish to pry open that particular lid. Instead, he asked, "What about Jack? How's she doing?"

Cinder searched his face with a troubled look, and Kalsin suspected she wouldn't let a change so significant in his little brother's life go that easily. But she acquiesced a moment later. "She's doing good. Better, actually, better than I've seen in a long time. Whatever you did to her—willingly or not—it put a storm in her sails. I’ve never seen her more determined."

Kalsin felt some of his strain ebb away hearing the news. Jack was well. That counted for something. "Determined to do what?"

"Just, determined in, eh, a fuck-if-i-know sort of general sense. Driven, you know? To get up in the morning, push things that can be pushed."

Cinder gave Kalsin a playful shove, then sat on a clear patch of grass left open between the jumble of brushes. She patted the spot next to her. She fidgeted as Kalsin joined her, excitement almost rolling off of her like drifting smoke. When Kalsin was about to ask, Cinder said, "She advanced to root."

At first, that threw Kalsin for a loop. Then he remembered Jack was Clenn now; previously dirt, the next step would've been rot. But those were surge names for the Scarrd. A Clenn dirt was a nail, and a nail would be called a root when advancing.

Cinder, who had a stupid grin plastered on her face, faltered for a second. "So. Are you ecstatic or what. She's not one for celebration, 'specially not when marching for war, but news like that are bigger than bull balls."

"No, I am. I'm happy for her." And he was. But Kalsin found himself searching for words. "I'm just thinking… why now? She could've advanced all the way to hive any time she wanted, as a Scarrd. I asked her, before, I asked many times. Her answer was always to, um, use her physical surge and put me on my ass in an instant. I figured it meant she was content with her dirt powers."

Cinder snapped off a nearby branch. "Well, it's not like I asked her. She still, you know. Treats me as if I..." She fiddled with the leaves, tearing off one or another. Kalsin could tell something was nagging at her. Cinder butchered another set of leaves. She sneaked a glance at Kalsin, an intense and thoughtful look. As if holding back something important. As if wondering if he was ready for it.

But then the moment was gone, and she shrugged. "Anyway. You don't need a degree in deep-fracture mathemations to guesstimate Jack’s motivations. Before, as a Scarrd, her ceiling as a hive was set in stone. But now?" She tossed a fistful of leaves in the air. "Now the limit is well and truly the shining nipples of the night sky. As Clenn, she has the chance to be overworld. From root to abyss, and beyond. It's not an easy journey, that. It could take years and years and even more years—not to mention the risks. But imagine. She's finally been granted the chance that life denied her so long ago."

His sister smiled at him. Kalsin returned it, unsure what he was feeling. Cinder put an arm around him, giving him a squeeze and bumping him in the chest with her stump. "You, Kalsin. You gave her back that chance."

Kalsin carefully, slowly disentangled the arm from behind his neck. But he didn't move otherwise. He stayed like that, aware of his sister next to him, her questing gaze on him. Kalsin spoke, eventually, voice quiet. "What do you want me to say?"

"I want you to say exactly what you want to say. No more or less than that."

Kalsin dug his heel in the ground. He kicked at the soft soil bunched under his boot, sending a spray of dirt everywhere. "Fuck it all," he said, sudden anger making his voice unsteady. "Pointless remarks, useless sisterly wisdom. Is that why you came all this way to meet in the dead of night, out of the Clenn camp?"

"No. I came because I wanted to see you, but most of all, because I knew how much you needed to see me. Anything above that is just a plus," Cinder said. "Things are a mess. I get it. I get it more than anyone else ever could. So talk to me. Or don't. I'll understand either way."

Kalsin's anger had no foothold against that. It seeped away, and he was left with something resembling peace.

"Back when we were kids, when I got my scar and left for Gulling, I never thought you'd come after me. Bad times. The worst, maybe, days of my life I can remember. But what did you do, Cinder? Instead of the rational course of action, after you recovered from being crippled by a mythos, you up and defy your father and follow the adopted kid he sent away? Your brother who was at least partially responsible for what happened to you? A mad choice. Find him, stay with him. A Clenn siding with a Scarrd, and in turn, rejecting the acceptance of both worlds. I don't know... how many times have I told you to leave my side? To find something better for yourself?"

Cinder smiled. "Many."

"Many. Yeah. But your stubborn ass wouldn't budge, no matter how much I pleaded or screamed or smashed silverware to pieces over the years. But for all your blind loyalty, you didn't see what it was doing to me. Yes, I needed you. You kept me going, but it was a double edged sword to watch you pay the price. It wasn't fair, Cinder. In throwing everything away for my sake, you also gave me a front row seat to watch the life you didn't get to live. I wanted it to stop. To make it right.

"So we searched for a cure. And, after pushing through hell and razor storms, we found it." Kalsin laughed, raising his arms in a mocking gesture. "Behold! The savior of all, except for himself, the one he really needs to save to make things right. A cosmic fucking joke, eh? And I don't even get to be selfish, because curing myself was never about me. It was about us. Siblings made equal. It's just, Cinder..." He laughed again. It came out as a hitch. "It's not fair. Not fucking fair."

Cinder never interrupted. She just sat there, listening to him rant, cry, then finally talk about whatever came to mind. Inconsequential things.

She had no solution to offer and no advice to give, offering no more input than a gray boulder jutting out and above the restless sea. But maybe that's exactly what she needed to be, for him; unmoving and safe, a constant within an unpredictable world full of terrifying changes.

Kalsin told her about the new people he'd come to meet. The friendly duo of Crawl and Littleguy, their asswad of a lieutenant, the eccentric blue-haired Cyanfish. His troubles learning the halberd. His unexpectedly newfound love for a fruit part of the military ration called jégberry.

Cinder, in turn, spoke about the difficulties of shield formation with one hand, and the story about convincing her officers to train her as a scout instead. They compared the two divisions, how the Scarrd practically ignored the surge while the Clenn did the opposite with mandatory training, categorizing men based on their powers.

They recalled the sights they'd seen, people they pissed off, shared rumors about the East. Where they stood right now and where they were going.

It was good. Simple.

Kalsin wished that night would never end.
