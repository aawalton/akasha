A couple of days passed, and so far, things seemed to be working fine under the new rulers.Well… depending on who you asked.

Juliette was having a bit of a hard time handling Hina.

Apparently, she came with a full package of royal expectations—expectations this “peasant accommodation,” as she called it, couldn’t meet.

She wanted glass windows in the main hall of the manor—to let the light in, but keep the heat, and fend off bugs.

She also complained about the furniture, about the decorations, about the ugly paintings, and the absolute crime of having no heated bath.

Though Juliette tried to be all stoic and Spartan about it, I could tell from her tone that she wouldn’t exactly mind a heated bath herself. I mean, neither would I.

According to Juliette, Hina was like Duchess—just with fewer poetic curses… and also, you know, her boss.

But more than the luxury demands, the real issue was the BFF situation.

Hina wanted to have girly bonding time. She wanted them to go shopping for clothes, check out jewelry stalls, and gossip about cute town NPCs.

Yes, Pedro—her boyfriend—was the first line of defense, and she also hung out with the NPC maid who came with her from Liliverth. But a princess apparently needed a best friend, too.

Now, Juliette was a short-tempered girl who often spoke her mind. Being sweet to a royal, needy boss was an uphill battle. From what I gathered, she’d had... limited success.

One strategy we were seriously considering was sacrificing Charlotte. Hina seemed to like her too.

Now, me and Juliette were on our way to the tavern to meet the rest of the Stormcrows. We were having a little celebration—Santiago had finally reached level two.

He hit it yesterday after hunting some kind of large furry snake with Elrik. He’d put his new XP into archery and had been insufferably excited ever since. He insisted we celebrate.

“So I told her I’d be leaving early today,” Juliette continued her rant, “because we’re having Santiago’s party at the tavern, right? And she goes, ‘Oh how exciting! I just love the peasant atmosphere there!’” She did a decent Hina impression.“Like, dude! You weren’t even invited! You’re NOT a Stormcrow!”

“It’s fine,” I said. “She’ll come with Pedro, and Santiago loves Pedro. They’ll talk about soccer the entire party.”

Santiago had secretly, desperately longed for a soccer conversation partner ever since coming to this world. And when he found out Pedro was into it, I swear he almost cried. After that, it was like watching newlyweds. Couldn’t separate them for days.

“Then Charlotte will complain endlessly about her boyfriend being stolen,” Juliette muttered.

“Such is the harsh daily struggle of a mercenary band leader,” I said solemnly.

Just then, I felt the buzz of vibration in my pocket.

I was carrying the phone today. The remaining starving survivors from Farhad’s group were on their way here from the keep, and they’d been calling me often for help navigating the forest.

“Hi, sup?” I answered.

“We passed the waterfall,” came Ananyan’s voice. “There’s a clear road, but ahead is this huge zebra creature with multiple horns. Is it… dangerous?”

“Oh yeah! Definitely is. Don’t go in the giant zebra direction.”

They were hopeless.

“You’re supposed to see a thick bush thicket near the road. That’s actually the safest path, even if it doesn’t look like it. Just stay away from flowers that look like roses, and you already know how a snake tree looks?”

“Yes!” she said. “When snake falls on you and gives you heart attack—it’s not something you forget!”

“Good,” I said. “Make a wide circle around the zebra monster. Don’t lose sight of the main road. Once you see three big boulders next to each other, that’s your camp spot. Should be late afternoon by then.”

“Okay, thank you,” she said. “Do you think we’ll arrive tomorrow?”

“You’re supposed to, yeah. If you keep up the pace, maybe by late noon.”

“Thanks again!”

“Sure. Call if you’re not sure where you are. Bye.”

“Bye.”

They were really grateful. It was… kind of touching. They sounded so small and miserable, lost and desperate—and I think we all agreed at this point that Farhad’s group had been punished enough. It was hard to see them as enemies anymore.

“So, they’ll be here tomorrow?” asked Juliette.

“If they don’t get eaten,” I said. “But yeah.”

“Good. I’ll talk to Hina. She mentioned a merchant group is supposed to arrive from Liliverth soon—on their way back, they can take the survivors with them.”

We arrived at the tavern. Most of the Stormcrows were already there.

Also—two uninvited intruders.

“Juliette! Here!” Hina waved cheerfully. “Come sit!”

“She talks like we haven’t hung out two hours ago…” Juliette muttered under her breath.

“Hey Santiago,” I said, locating him in the corner mid-heated soccer debate with Pedro. “Congrats on the level-up! How do you feel?”

“Really awesome!” he grinned. “I can shoot like a demon now!”

“Cool! Did it have any side effects? Like, arm strength or stamina?”

“Yeah! My arms got magically stronger. I can suddenly do 100 pushups—before I could barely hit 60. I swear I can see better now too—especially in the dark.”

“Can you shoot like Lori now?”

“Not like her! She’s, like, arch-demon level. But I’m getting there. At least I won’t be a total embarrassment next time I hunt with Elrik!”

“But how does that work?” asked Pedro. “If you kill a game animal, doesn’t it disappear? You’re probably the worst hunting apprentice ever!”

“Well,” Santiago admitted, “it wasn’t a problem since I mostly didn’t hit anything. And if I did, I just wounded it and Elrik finished it with a knife. Sometimes I made it disappear—and that’s how I got XP. But Elrik didn’t seem to mind. He’s a really cool guy.”

“Please go back to talking about soccer,” Samar begged. “No one needs to hear how you murder cute animals for fun.”

“It’s… not for fun…” Santiago muttered, but happily returned to soccer talk.

“Anyway,” Santiago continued, “we were talking about how Flamengo dominated the continent. Like the 2019 Libertadores final in Lima? Gabigol scoring twice in the last three minutes against River? That was history.”

“That was one final,” Pedro retorted. “Boca Juniors has six Libertadores trophies. Six. And we didn’t need oil money or billionaires—we did it with garra. With the Bombonera shaking every rival to pieces.”

It was the first time in this world I’d heard a conversation and couldn’t understand a word of it.Stupid system. Why doesn’t it translate soccer?

But they looked happy enough, so I let them be.

“Hey, Geshem,” Hina said. “Your girlfriend couldn’t come to the party?”

Ever since word got out that I had an NPC girlfriend, people had been very curious.Reading on Amazon or a pirate site? This novel is from Royal Road. Support the author by reading it there.I guess the story had made the rounds—NPC girl bravely fighting monsters to rescue her lost lover.Yeah, it was a good story.

Also, I’d moved in with her.Not in the spicy way—I just got a room at Elrik’s cabin. It felt too weird to sleep at Juliette’s place when I had a girlfriend now.

“Ah, Lori doesn’t like crowds,” I explained. “Makes her nervous.”

“Too bad,” said Hina. “I’d love to meet her. From what I heard, she’s really cool.”

“She is,” I agreed. “You’re welcome to visit the forest cabin. You could buy stuff from her—she’d like that.”

“I’ll definitely do that,” Hina said, then added with a curious smile, “So… what’s it like? Dating an NPC, I mean. Is it similar to dating a normal human?”

“I’ve never dated a human,” I said plainly. I wasn’t embarrassed. We weren’t in high school anymore.And anyway, a total of one girlfriend was already ahead of the curve in my book.

“But no,” I continued, “she’s not like a human. She’s an NPC. But you know how they are.”

“Not really,” Hina said. “For me it’s mostly work or business with them. And honestly… after all this time, I still don’t get them at all! So what’s it really like, being in a close relationship with one?”

That was clearly a personal question. Borderline chutzpah, even.

But I didn’t really mind answering. And judging from the sudden silence around the table, and everyone now staring at me—even Santiago and Pedro had paused their eternal soccer debate—it seemed everyone wanted to know.

“NPCs aren’t robots,” I began with the basics. “They’re people. They have feelings, needs. They can be just as smart and capable as anyone. But they’re also… not human. And not humans trapped in robot bodies either. They’re something else. Different—at a fundamental level. They think differently.”

They were watching me closely now, captivated.

“It’s like… we’re fluid people, and they’re quantized people, discrete.” I explained, “Their minds shift between very specific states—and they can only be in one at a time. So their speech is limited to those states. It’s why their vocabulary sounds simple.”

“They also value their roles.” I continued, “Duty is important to them. They can get angry, even hateful, but they’re always calm. They don’t act on impulse, and they don’t get bored. An NPC can stand behind a counter for hours, doing nothing—and be completely content, because that’s their job. That’s why they sometimes seem robotic.”

“You said they think differently,” Hyun-woo asked, fascinated. “What did you mean?”

“Well, I can’t know for sure,” I replied. “But I think they’re more task-oriented than reflective. They’re not always pondering life’s meaning or wondering if they’re good enough. They focus on what needs to be done. That’s probably why they don’t get bored.”

I paused, then added, “But as I got to know Lori, I realized they’re not as different as they seem. Not in the important ways. For example, she’ll get pissed if I leave a mess.”

People chuckled.

“As for talking, it’s not a big issue. Their vocabulary is limited—not meant for complex conversations. But NPCs are smart. Lori will use a sentence that sounds generic—about the forest, or spirits, or whatever—but depending on context, it means something very specific.It took some time, but now I can usually tell exactly what she means—especially in context.

It’s kind of like learning a new language. Actually, it’s really fun.”

“Incredible,” Hyun-woo whispered, mouth slightly open.

“Wow,” Samar breathed.

“So no, I’ve never had a ‘real’ girlfriend,” I concluded. “But NPCs are just fine. Especially when they’re as smart and badass as Lori.”

“I see,” said Hina, smiling now. “Sounds kind of fun! Maybe I’ll dump Pedro and find an NPC boyfriend instead. Bet he won’t talk about soccer all day!”

“Hey!” said Pedro.

“You should totally check out the blacksmith’s lad,” Charlotte offered. “He has a jawline to die for.”

“Take me to visit?” asked Hina, eyes lighting up. “I need a new set of knives anyway.”

“Sure,” said Charlotte. “I’d love to.”

Oh good. That’s a little less Hina time for Juliette.

The next morning, I was on the phone with Ananyan for most of it. Her group was getting close to the edge of the forest, but there were still two known Scorpion Suricata tribes in the area, and I really didn’t want any last-minute disasters.

By noon, they finally emerged from the forest—just near Elrik and Lori’s cabin.

We went to meet them: me, Lori, Juliette, Santiago, and Charlotte.

They looked awful.

Their clothes were in tatters. They were filthy, pale, and thin in the way that clearly came from malnutrition.But they were also smiling—relieved to be out of the forest and out of danger.

They each took turns grabbing my hand and thanking me. They thanked Juliette too and greeted Santiago and Charlotte like old friends. I sighed. Yeah… I guess we had kind of saved their lives.

They introduced themselves properly.

Ananyan, the Indian woman, was the most senior—she’s the one I’d been on the phone with most. We already knew Yusuf—he was the first of Farhad’s we met when we arrived in Kuriolis. There was also a dark-skinned woman named Chantal, a pale redhead named Harper, and a young Asian guy named Yichen.

First, we took them to the local NPC physician. He didn’t say anything about malnutrition—probably should have. Honestly, he wasn’t a great physician, but he had managed to fix my shattered leg, so he wasn’t completely useless.At least he treated their bruises and minor cuts.

Then we brought them to the bakery.

We had prepared a second room in advance—enough beds, new clothes, and most importantly: actual food.

We didn’t give them much at first. After starvation, refeeding too quickly can be dangerous—you need to slowly ramp up calorie intake to avoid serious complications. But even the modest rations brought tears to their eyes.

And seeing them cry, almost made me cry too.

Three days passed.

Ananyan’s group began to regain color in their cheeks. They started smiling again. They even came with us to the tavern one night for drinks. Bit by bit, they started to look like people again.

That day, me, Juliette, and Charlotte sat down for a meal with them at the bakery.

It felt like they had something on their minds.

Ananyan cleared her throat. “We were talking,” she began. “We really think Farhad’s way of doing things was wrong. We… should’ve seen it coming. And stopped him.”

She glanced at the others, who all nodded.

“In our time here,” she continued, “we’ve seen how you handle things. Looking back… I can see what went wrong with the Arjun Group. About Miguel…” she trailed off.

“It wasn’t justified,” Juliette said. “We’re assholes for killing him. We know that.”

Ananyan nodded slowly. “What I’m trying to say is—we’re grateful. You saved our lives. The caravan’s arriving today, and when we travel to Liliverth, we won’t go back to Farhad. Some of us may join the Order of the Phoenix. Some may start new lives there.”

“That’s a smart decision,” I said.

“Also…” Ananyan hesitated, then gestured at two of their group. “Chantal and Yichen would like to stay. Here. In Kamatouu.”

That got our attention.

“Only if you’ll allow it,” said Yichen quickly. “Of course. We’d also be honored to join the Stormcrows.”

“Did you…” I began.

“We didn’t kill any guards,” Chantal cut in. “During the keep assault, we stayed back. We didn’t fight. Everyone who took an active role in the keep assault, also join the attack on Kamatouu. We didn’t.”

“She’s telling the truth,” Charlotte added. “I didn’t know them super well, but they’re not fighters.”

“We know your rules,” Yichen added eagerly. “No monster grinding for XP, respect NPCs, bird-people, goblins—”

“Ah—actually,” Juliette interrupted, smiling, “you’re not required to respect Duchess at all. But yeah—basically, treat anything that talks like a person. That sums it up.”

“And don’t mess with the system,” I added. “Also, respect Juliette’s decisions—she’s our leader.”

“Of course.”“Absolutely.”

“Great,” I said. Then added, dead serious: “Now all that remains is the Goblin Fight Trial. To test your courage and strength.”

“Oh…” said Yichen, eyes wide.

“He’s joking!” Juliette elbowed me. “Stop being mean! Yes, you can join.”

“Really? Just like that?” Chantal asked.

“It’s not exactly an exclusive club,” Juliette said. “If you’re not an asshole, you’re in. That’s basically our recruitment policy.”

And just like that—our mercenary band’s membership increased by two.By two? I meant by more than 20%!

Later that day, the merchant caravan from Liliverth arrived—another exciting event for our otherwise quiet town.

They came with four wagons, each hitched to a pair of horses and driven by NPC riders. Two Lithuron NPC soldiers accompanied them, and a player from the Order of the Phoenix was leading the operation.

He was a cheerful, brown-skinned young man with curly hair and a dazzling smile.

Before business began, the player was invited to a welcome meal at Hina’s mansion. Naturally, we were invited too. Of course we came. We were curious.

The curly-haired player introduced himself as Omar. He was very excited to talk about trading—and Hyun-woo was very excited to listen. Honestly, I was too. It was fascinating stuff.

“So, in the Order of the Phoenix, we do trade systematically,” Omar began.

Hyun-woo’s eyes practically lit up at the word systematically.

“We maintain a database of shop inventories and pricing across every town in the kingdom we’ve visited. We even have an algorithm to compare every product by profit margin, volume, and travel distance. That way, we always know the most profitable trade routes. Kamatouu’s actually expected to turn into a solid revenue source.”

“Could you give some examples?” Juliette asked.

“Sure! Candles, for example—sold here for half a silver each. In Irelteh, a town far north in Lithuron, they sell for 3–4 silver each. We buy 800 units from the chandlery for 400 silver and resell for over 1,800. That’s a net gain of around 1,400 silver—or 140 gold.

"Same with high-quality ceramics from the potter, or luxury textiles from the clothier—great profit margins. Even large snake horns are rare elsewhere and go for double or triple the local price.

“Bottom line—this trip alone will earn about 450 gold in total profit. A chunk goes to the Lithuron treasury, of course—but the Order still gets a big share.”

“Holy shit,” said Samar. “That’s a lot of gold.”

“Wait a minute!” I said, eyes widening. “I made those luxury textiles from the clothier! Don’t I get a cut?”

“You get a paycheck,” Omar said cheerfully. “Because you’re an employee, not an owner.”

“But it’s only like five silver a day!”

“You should become a merchant,” Omar said. “Best job in the world. You make a ton of money, see this beautiful world, and meet new interesting people every day.”

“That actually sounds kind of amazing,” Juliette said.

Apparently, the Order of the Phoenix didn’t just conquer towns—they were also making a shit ton of money doing it.

After the meal, Omar and his crew spent the entire day running around Kamatouu—buying, selling, loading wagons and negotiating deals, and filling every bit of wagon space they had.

By evening, they were packed and ready to head back to Liliverth.

“I think it might be a good idea to actually join that caravan,” I said to Juliette. “Like Felix suggested. Visit Liliverth. Meet John Smith. We could learn a lot from the trip.”

Honestly, it sounded exciting. Maybe Lori could even come too. But even if not—it wouldn’t be for that long.

Juliette considered. “I think you’re right. It’s a good idea. Hyun-woo would definitely want to go. Maybe Santiago and Charlotte too. But… is it a good idea for you to go? Crippled and all that?”

“We’re joining a caravan,” I explained. “I can ride in one of the wagons. And in a about week, they’ll send another caravan back this way—we can return then. Worst case, we borrow a wagon and a horse.”

“The roads to Liliverth aren’t great,” Juliette said. “From what Omar described, they’re barely wide enough for wagons. It’ll be a rough ride.”

“It’ll be an awesome adventure!” I said happily.
