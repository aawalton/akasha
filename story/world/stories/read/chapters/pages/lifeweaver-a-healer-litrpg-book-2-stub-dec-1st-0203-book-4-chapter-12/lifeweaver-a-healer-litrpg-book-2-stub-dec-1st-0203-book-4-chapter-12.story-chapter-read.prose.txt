A few quick questions from Milla gave them what little information was available. The Singers had launched some sort of artillery strike, but with glowing energy rather than a mortar or missile. Curtis couldn't be sure about the intended target, but it stood to reason that the destination would be New York. How long until it hits? Minutes at most. Far from enough time to evacuate anyone.

Milla (Guild): All of you who are in dungeons, stay there. Don't leave.

Milla (Guild): Our allies have been notified.

Milla (Guild): If you're outside and near headquarters, you'd better hurry up and come home.

Milla (Guild): The rest of us will stay in the guild. We have measures to keep this place safe.

Milla (Guild): Good luck, everyone. Stay safe.

And that was it. Shelter in place. Luke looked up into the sky as he lobbed another monster core at Smudge, who jumped up and snatched it out of the air. As far as he could tell, there was nothing up there, but maybe it was getting a little warmer? Part of him wanted to stay in the city rather than go through to the relative safety of whatever dungeon waited for him through the portal. If this were a powerful weapon, it made sense that they tried to learn as much as possible about it. There was, however, the small chance that it was so strong that Luke wouldn't live through it. However unlikely, dying like that would be a shitty way to go. Even if he didn't die, healing himself would cost a lot of mana, which meant the Guardian Weaves on both himself and Smudge might break down. Not a fun time.

The temperature was rising, and it was rising fast. Sweat trickled down the side of Luke's face as he looked up again. Something wasn't right. The sky was turning orange.

Luke: "Hey, Curtis, how large was the glow-thing the Singers launched?"

Curtis: "Couldn't tell. It blinded me for a little while."

Big, Luke guessed. Real big. The echo threads connected to people in the city were all in safe locations, as far as he could tell, and there was no one calling out for help, meaning he didn't need to jump around with Weavestep and get himself hurt in the process. It was good timing, with most of The Band working their way through various dungeons or having already left the city for one reason or another.

"Let's head inside, Smudge," Luke said, turning to walk through the portal as the air outside turned red and almost too hot to even breathe in.

The dungeon was the opposite. A freezing cold wind buffeted him, and Luke's shoes crunched into fresh snow as they found themselves on a mountain. At least he thought it was a mountain, since the snow was a steep downward slope and pieces of rock jutted out from the otherwise white landscape. Thick, heavy flakes of snow filled the air and landed on his robe and hair. The blizzard made it impossible to see much beyond the portal's opening, but Luke still took a few steps to the side, wanting to get away from whatever blast might come through when the attack landed on the city he'd just come from. While the robe helped a lot against the wind, it did little to staunch the cold, and Luke shivered as the snow threatened to bury him in minutes. Smudge didn't seem to care. Whatever snow touched him disappeared into his personal void, and the little voidling jumped around sniffing anything and everything.

Then the launched attack struck its target. Energy Luke didn't recognize beamed out from the portal, continued straight ahead into the far distance, and spread to the sides like a flower blooming. Its incredible heat melted the snow in all directions to the point it seemed to pause the blizzard for a moment. That heat wafted up against Luke, who took a step back, then another, shielding himself from the undirected secondary warmth of the attack. Even that was too much to withstand, and Luke ended up grabbing two big handfuls of snow to press against his face.

"Much better," he mumbled, careful not to get the melting snow into his mouth. He was glad now that he hadn't stayed behind to witness the blast. It kept going even through the portal for several seconds before dissipating little by little. Once it was gone, new snow danced through the air, and the mountain, exposed by the heat, turned white again. A rumble from above meant he didn't have much time to check his surroundings, and he pushed through the deepening snow to get back to the portal, yelling for Smudge to join him.

The avalanche would have crushed them both if they had waited even a second longer, and Luke stepped back out into New York in the middle of a jerk-filled dance to get the snow that had fallen down the back of his robe out. Right in the middle of it, he stopped and let his hands fall to his sides. Devastation didn't feel like a strong enough word. Annihilation? Maybe. Luke shook his head as he scanned the utter madness stretching out in all directions. The city was gone. Not gone as in reduced to rubble, but as in nothing but dust remaining. As far as he could see, not a single house remained. Visibility was limited, but from the surrounding area, he could be pretty sure it was the same all over. The building next to the portal just wasn't there anymore. Same with the one next to it. Even the road was gone, not a single piece of asphalt remaining.

Curtis (Guild): "I could see the blast from a full day's travel outside the city. The skyline is gone."

Penny (Guild): "Oh, no. Did everyone make it?"

One by one, the guild members checked in to report their status and their experiences. A single member in the city had been too far from both headquarters and a portal to hide out in. Rolf, the barrier mage from Martin's group who helped battle The Fallen Shepherd, was dead. Martin himself wasn't sure what his friend had been doing, but it added to the subdued nature of the meeting once everyone had returned, entering the guild headquarters through the still intact plaque that now rested on the ground. Most were gray-faced from the dust, and quite a few had wet lines going down their cheeks, like almost dried-up streams through an arid wasteland. Luke just felt numb. If there had still been people living in the city, the Singers would have killed millions. Some must have remained, along with quite a few Integrated still hiding out.

"What about Phoenix Phalanx?" someone asked.

Milla's gaze looked blank for a moment as she checked in with the other guild. She nodded, more to herself than to the guild members. "They are fine. A little singed, but they made it to a portal just in time. Nick, Curtis's brother, has decided they'll leave the city and head down toward Washington."

"Not much city left to leave," Nash said. Tears were still coming down his face where has next to Priyah's still unconscious form.

"What's in Washington anymore?" Lana asked.

"Singers."

As they talked, the numbness had spread through Luke, but at the mention of more Singers coming to finish the job after the blast, he stood, anger surging through him. "How many?"

"I stopped counting at a hundred. Probably half again as many. They've come to finish the job."

Milla got off the couch, glanced over at the injured, then turned to face the remaining members. "We need to leave."

"We need to fight," Curtis countered.

Glaring at him, she turned to Luke and gave him a look beseeching him for help, since it appeared most guild members were on Curtis's side this time. Their new home was gone. The idea of fleeing after that was like a punch in the gut and a humiliating slap in the face rolled into one.

Luke shook his head at his sister. "Curtis is right about this one."

"Nick has turned around. They're on the way back," Curtis informed them.

No matter what happened, Curtis wouldn't step back. Not this time. Luke understood the sentiment.

"The Band should retreat and take care of the wounded," Luke said. Curtis turned to him with a frown, about to speak, but Luke held up a hand. "Find us a new place, and those of us who want to stay will join you afterwards."

Milla: "They'll die when they could have run to fight another day."

Milla: "You and Curtis are on a level the others can't match."

Milla: "Don't let them join."

His sister was right, of course, but who was he to tell grown men and women what they could and couldn't do? For a moment, he wanted to tell her off, but his conscience got the better of him, and Luke shook his head at the group who had gathered in front of him, all of them rearing to go, with Nash at their helm. As it turned out, neither Luke's little speech nor Milla's stern orders were enough to keep them all back. A few chose to stay and take a stand against the Singers. With every single building in New York gone, they risked the battle turning into an open melee. Luke looked out across those who had stayed with a growing lump of worry in his gut. Nash, Mas, Sam, Savannah, Lemira, and Martin. A small group to stand up against so many, but more Integrated from Earth would come. Luke just hoped it would be enough.
