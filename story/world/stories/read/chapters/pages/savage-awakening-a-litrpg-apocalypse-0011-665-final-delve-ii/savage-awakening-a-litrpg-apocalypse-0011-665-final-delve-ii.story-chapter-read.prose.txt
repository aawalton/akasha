90 clicks away from the Superdungeon, there was a field tent cloaked under the same conditions as Dragonspire's Command—the absolute maximum secrecy Command could muster. They needed certainty a certain occupant of the Superdungeon couldn't listen in.

In the distance a cluster of units were already in position—arranged in a circle above the Superdungeon's shattered higher floors.

There was a burst of portal light, and Zane Walker touched down at the teleportation station constructed just outside the Command tent. He strode in and was greeted with the respectful nods and inclined heads of those present—which included ten of Dragonspire's top-20 rankers. Ten of the very folk who'd taken part in that initial attack on Malzareth.

He nodded to Reina, then greeted the man at the head of the long table—"Sage."

"Disciple." Sage Noughtfire nodded as Zane took his position.

Two more top-end elites entered, and that made the complete strike force.

The Sage surveyed the rest of the room. "Shall we get underway?"

At the silence—

"I hadn't imagined we'd meet under these conditions," the Sage said. "But here we are. You all know your teams and your entry points."

A projection shone behind him, mapping out the Superdungeon and all the places Dragonspire's forces would soon enter.

There was a scattering of entry points near the shattered top, and fewer as they neared the tower's firmer middle.

But a few routes existed near the base—striking into the floors of the Superdungeon known only as the 'Troubled Zones.' Their walls were totally blackened by Corruption from the outside; they didn’t expect the interiors to be any more welcoming.

The Sage went over their respective paths. The bulk of the elites would attempt to find each other on their floors and form units before they continued further.

Reina locked eyes with him here. There was a single unit, consisting of him and an avatar of hers, that didn't plan on doing that—that didn't need to.

They had an idea of the floor layouts, but it was difficult to predict exactly what they'd find in there. The idea was this entire force would meet at the very end—just before the Throne Room. Clearing anything they met on the way.

The speartip of that force, and the one scheduled to drop into the lowest floor, was Zane’s unit.

The entry points were Noughtfire’s work. They were short-distance spatial tunnels, and they partly explained what the Sage had been up to in the Superdungeon all this time—why he'd been gone for such long stretches. This was one of the more secret elements of his work, and the Sage had been quick to tell them they weren't silver bullets. They were what he said—just entrances.

After their units were dropped in, the true work would begin.

The concept was that this way, the true elites would skip hundreds of floors of Superdungeon between them and their final targets—they’d avoid slogging through all the associated defenses and staircases.

And with layered entries—including dozens of entry points by the elites already stationed outside into the more easily penetrable upper floors—Dragonspire could effect a divide-and-conquer strategy, opening up dozens of fronts at once, top-to-bottom.

Suffice it to say, Dragonspire wasn't here to enact a days- or weeks-long siege.

Ideally this would be fast and efficient. Although there was a silent understanding that this all rested on the success of the elites placed deepest into the Superdungeon, the ones who would ultimately take on the crux of the mission.

The Sage finished and placed his hands behind his back. "If any of you requires any final clarifications," he said. "Now is the time to task.”

Silence.

The elites glanced at one another and saw they shared an understanding.

“All that's left is this,” said the Sage.

The Sage opened his palm. And there, floating in a circle, were a series of flowers whose petals seemed made of pure, cleansing white light. They bathed the room in that color, bypassing all soul defenses and drawing a slight calming reaction in all their souls.

They were called holyflowers—treasures that carried a kind of essence that simply wasn't present in Dragonspire, but which countered they influence of Corruption quite effectively. There was an extremely limited supply of these flowers—the only ones that existed, as far as he knew, had been in the Sage's garden.

The Sage distributed them to all twelve, who'd take them the instant they entered; these treasures would grant temporary protection against the immense Corruption-staining environments of those lowest floors—it’d make it so they didn’t even notice it. Reina received a single petal; she only needed a petal since she'd send in just her avatar, not her true body. She clasped it firmly.

Zane placed his in his Ring.

"It should prove more helpful for the rest,” the Sage said wryly. “These plans were not, shall we say, made with your current skillset in mind. It will still prove of slight help, but I expect you'd survive fine without."

He nodded; he'd pretty much figured that.

"I still don't believe you saw as little of this coming as you say," Zane told the Sage.

As far as he was concerned, the routes and treasures the Sage had prepped for this felt kind of prophetic. But the Sage just seemed amused.

"If a plan is well made, it's rather unlike glass, disciple. When it shatters one can easily pick up the pieces and remake it anew. This—"

The Sage gestured to the maps. "Is recycling."

The Sage then considered the Superdungeon—glancing at the tent walls, which were rendered transparent, showing it there in the distance. "I expect that will not be necessary during this final stage."

Zane nodded.

As far as he could tell, this final plan would only shatter if he did. They stared silently at the Superdungeon for a bit—that dark colossus, ringed by streams of starkly white Destruction lightning. The top-half strike force was stationed just above that zone, just waiting for the command.

"You've mastered the movement with Stellar, I presume."

"Yes," he confirmed.

The Sage seemed satisfied with his answer. "I shall be curious to see how your Stellar plays," he said. "With the degree to which it's touched by spatial Laws... I’d like to see the Concepts that shall follow it, for that matter. What higher Laws they may be tinged with."

The Sage seemed to believe the nature of Zane's Stellar Collapse Concept was due to the influence of his Title—like it allowed his Concepts to 'touch on' higher kinds of Laws, more than would normally be the case. In this case, spatial Laws.

"There will be time for that," the Sage said at last.

The other elites had already gone to take their positions. Reina stuck her head in and gave him a wordless glance—he understood that glance; she didn't want to rush him. It was just a reminder of the time.

"I sense I should leave you to your task," the Sage said, with a ghost of a smile. "Until then, disciple.”

Zane bade the old fellow goodbye and left to take his position.

***

It started with the highest floors. The order was given, and they descended on the Superdungeon—he saw thousands of large-scale shields popping up, covering the units as they entered range of the lightning. Included in that attack force were nearly all of the top Hegemon Rankers, outside of the highest 20 or so.

The finale had truly begun.

The top few floors were breached, and the rest quickly followed as more and more of the operation made their moves.

Then it was the top elites' turn.

Floor by floor, the crews entered—The Rage Knight, Thalia, and the Huntress in a flash far above. Then the Lord Commander and the Nameless King, and Striker and the Carved Saint—all vanishing in bursts of space Law.

Zane clenched the charm in his hand. The lower the spatial tunnels went, the narrower they had to be. The Carved Saint, for instance, went in alone.

If not for Reina's avatar at his side, Zane would be the same.

Her true body had spent the last two hours alone with him here, at his station—they'd had a private chat about the plan, but much of the time was silent, with her head on his—there simply wasn't much that needed to be said at this stage. She just kept him company, and he kept her company.

Then she'd had to leave to administer the War. But not before she left her avatar.

She met his eyes.

"Whenever you're ready," she told him.

With that, he crushed the charm.

They were gone.

He made landfall and inspected his surroundings—a landscape filled with silver mist, mist laced with twisted lines of Corruption.

Lines that bounced off the new holy essence in his aura.

You could see the walls in the far distance, mostly by the shining Corruption scars running all throughout them—smudges of thin light to his eyes.

This will certainly be interesting.
