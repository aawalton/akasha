After three days of sleepless nights and nonstop effort, Vir was both pleased and frustrated with his progress.

Pleased that he had, in fact, uncovered the secret to channeling prana under these new laws. And frustrated that even with that knowledge, generating prana from one’s own body seemed nigh impossible.

For there was but one change the Fateweavers had caused. One, tiny change that rippled through everything related to magic and prana.

Namely, in this universe, prana was not found in the air or in the ground… It was generated within one’s own body.

The concept was utterly alien to Vir, who’d traveled further down that very path than most, with his prana-acclimatized body that stored and powered many a spell of his.

Even then, all he’d done was made his body a more efficient conduit and battery. He’d never actually created prana out of thin air.

Yet that seemed to be exactly what was required here. Vir had more or less stumbled upon that when he’d been trying to get Prana Vision—the foundation of his insights into prana manipulation—working again.

For the longest time, he’d focused on different ways of channeling his prana, but it was as though all that reserve he’d built up had vanished.

It hadn’t, of course—he’d have felt far weaker and lacking in vitality if it had—but that he couldn’t touch it at all provided the first clue.

He couldn’t touch it because it hadn’t come from him. Acting under that hypothesis, he’d focused on the creation of prana, but while he had been successful, the rate was so abysmal he might as well have failed. Even now, after nearly three full days of what Maiya had dubbed ‘cultivation’, he’d barely accrued enough usable prana for a single display of magic, and Maiya had barely managed to charge a single C grade orb.

And unfortunately for them both, the Altani entrance exam was not some bout of physical combat or simple display of magic. A duel, Vir could have overcome with ease. His raw strength and wealth of combat experience would have carried him through any such trial even without magic, and he was sure he could fake the usage of magic when observed at a distance with what he currently had.

But these were Altani mejai, who prided themselves not on just their magic, but their minds as well. The tests, therefore, proved far more nuanced.

The Altani tested not only for orb activation speed, but activation efficiency, prana conductivity, and a long list of other metrics, all of which required charging orbs in the full view of a proctor and countless others.

The tests were so rigorous that they put even Maiya’s disguise to the test, and she was at least able to do regular magic, even if her scores were low.

Vir wouldn’t be able to perform a single one to any acceptable degree. Something no amount of overnight effort could remedy.

And so as the minutes passed as Vir stood in line with the other students, waiting for his turn and watching Maiya struggle. Not because she did especially poorly in relation to the others, but because her standards were so high, Vir knew she found her performance nothing less than atrocious. He might have found it amusing, were had he not been in an even worse situation.To think these orbs were simple toys for the Imperium… it seemed even toys were capable of stymieing Vir, and he found himself growing increasingly certain that the exam would end in disaster.

He had ended armies, fought mythical Automatons a hundred paces tall, and killed countless thousands of Ash Beasts. He had overthrown a clan and united a realm.

Not one of those things had made him as nervous as he did in that moment, waiting to take a simple entrance exam for an Academy of mejai so weak, they might as well have been children.

There wasn’t a hopeful around him with a Balar higher than the low hundreds. Even ignoring his vast wealth of experience, his raw power easily put him in the tens of thousands. It was absurd. Comical, even. He could fight them all combined blindfolded with one arm tied behind his back even in his current state.

But Vir wasn’t laughing.

If he failed, if he was forced to reveal himself or caused a commotion, everything they’d worked for could be undone. They could end up stranded in this universe with less than a year left to live. All while their own universe drowned in an ocean of Ash Tears.

Vir wracked his mind, but of course, no solution presented itself. None had the night before, and so expecting one to materialize now was nothing but a fool’s hope.

His relatively weak magic wasn’t the problem. If the others were passing with equally lackluster showings, he’d do just fine there. Better than fine, even. However, for all the changes the Fateweavers had wrought, Ash prana was still every bit as incompatible with normal affinities as before. In the best case, nothing happened at all. In the worst, the orb cracked entirely.

And while a cracked orb might be spun as a sign of overwhelming strength, the Altani were too experienced to miss the real implications. Maybe they would take him as a curiosity. Maybe they would fail him outright. Or maybe they would brand him an abomination to be eliminated, or worse—studied as some specimen.

What frustrated him most was that he’d made no progress on that front. No results to show for his efforts.

He was now cursing himself for not pursuing it more all these years. The problem had simply become irrelevant so long ago that he hadn’t thought about orb charging in ages.

Yet here it was again. The very first obstacle he had ever encountered on his path to magic, back when he thought himself pranascorned, come back to haunt him.

To make matters worse, Fate, it seemed, had dropped the very worst companions right into line behind him. Though he’d not seen the likes of Ira, Mina, or Tia, Vayren had spotted him and had dragged over Daya too. They stood in line beside him, chatting animatedly as they commented on each examinee’s performance.

“Did you see the blonde girl? The blue-eyed one? To think she’s regained such command over her magic, even after the Shift. She has to have peak Affinities, right?”

Vir closed his eyes for a moment.

Of course they were talking about Maiya.

“Oh yes, the Ice and Wind mejai,” Vayren said. “Impressive, to be sure, but she seemed to struggle to my eyes. Likely just another refugee.”

“Can’t say,” Daya said, frowning. “But something about her speaks of real power. I got a sense that she’s more than she lets on.”

Vayren snorted. “Yes, her score was passable, but nothing that impressive. I think you’re imagining things. Perhaps it’s nerves for the exam? Aren’t you confident in your own skills?”

Daya scoffed. “Please. I’ll have the highest score among us.” She offered Vir a polite smile. “No offense intended.”

“None taken,” Vir said, returning the smile. Given the pressure he was under, it took more than a little effort to make it seem natural, and though his extensive experience acting allowed him to pull it off, inwardly his mind was scrambling.

“It’s no lie,” he added. “I’ll almost certainly perform worse than either of you, given my background.”

Soon Vayren’s turn arrived. The boy performed abysmally by any regular metric, but compared to the others, his activation speed tests were relatively good, though he came up short in the efficiency department. His strategy seemed to be to just throw as much prana as he could into the orb and hope for the best.

Though Vir also relied on his overwhelming prana to some extent—easily a thousandfold more powerful than Vayren’s—his real specialty was in prana efficiency. He utilized every drop of that prana better than Vayren ever would. It was why even the morsel of prana he’d managed to generate within his body could manifest any magic at all.

Then Daya stepped up, and to no one’s surprise, she passed with ease and near perfection in all departments. Her prana control especially caught Vir’s eye. As she was, she was nothing impressive. But if she were put through the Asura training Maiya had undergone? If she learned from the best… then she might very well be a force to be reckoned with.

Something that would, sadly, never happen.

Finally, it was Vir’s turn.

As he approached the oversized testing orb and the wizened proctor holding it, he made his decision.

“I’d like to request a special consideration for this exam,” Vir said quietly. “A trial of pure combat.”

The proctor scoffed, looking him over. “You are neither royalty nor upper echelon Sawai, so why, pray tell, do you believe you deserve such treatment?”

“Because of this.”

Vir held up the fire orb Maya had given him and allowed a fraction of his preciously generated prana to leak out, taking the form of a tiny wisp of black fire, barely visible to the naked eye.

His signature move, but a pale shadow of what it once was. He didn’t channel the prana into the orb, of course—that would have been pointless. Instead, he guided it with a feat of sublime prana control that was all but lost upon the proctor’s amateur eyes, surrounding the orb and making it seem as though it had been lit with a weak flame.

Not the most conventional display of magic, considering orbs had preset abilities, but it was not without precedent. According to Maiya, Fireball spells could be cast in a similar manner if one had enough control. Not that there was any reason to risk burns by summoning a ball of pranic fire directly in front of one’s body, but it could be done.

He canceled the display well before the proctor could see through the ruse, and before he ran out of prana. He had to save his meager reserve for the real thing, after all.

The old man’s eyes widened until they looked ready to fall out of his head. “Yes, this way, if you please.”

Vir followed the man, aware of the astonished looks Vayren and Daya were giving him. It was why he’d been reluctant to use this method.

He’d been wishing not to cause a scene, but now, his little trick would likely be the talk of the Academy.

And though he had taken a risk, perhaps it was better this way. With this, he avoided a public scene, and now, only one person had seen his magic up close. A risk, to be sure, but one that need not be repeated now that he had his trial by combat.

With steady steps, Vir followed the proctor through the halls of the Altani Magical Academy to a training field set within a courtyard.

— —

Passing the test turned out to be far easier than he’d expected. Once a special exam was approved, they gave him far more leeway, even letting him set the exact nature of the test.

After that, it only took a single demonstration.

Namely, burning a test dummy with every last bit of prana he’d accumulated.

And though none of the proctors had seen black fire before, that didn’t seem to cause any suspicion whatsoever. Rather, they simply treated Vir as a promising hopeful. One of the ‘best of this batch’.

It wasn’t suspicion in their eyes, but hope. Hope… and more than a little desperation. For what, they hardly needed to say. Every new recruit was another tiny ray against the crushing darkness of the encroaching Ash, promising doom with each passing day.

After that display, the proctors didn’t even bother examining his efficiency or anything else.

So when Vir walked out, he did so under the bewildered, envious stares of the other students waiting in line.

Still, he felt no satisfaction at the feat.

Vir knew he had only passed by luck and circumstance. Eventually, someone would start asking questions, especially if he continued using his fake Fire Affinity abilities in view of others.

He needed a real solution. He needed to unlock his full prana reserve, he needed to learn how to convert his affinities, and needed both fast.
