We were lounging in oversized chairs while a Naga woman danced lazily like a snake charmed by an invisible flute. Soft, calming music drifted through the air from every direction, slow enough that it barely registered in my active thoughts. It blended into the room, becoming part of the atmosphere.

I felt half awake and half asleep, like being visited by a sleep paralysis demon, except pleasant.

I didn't really know how to describe it.

It was as though every muscle in my body had stopped existing. My thoughts drifted in and out, melting into one another as time itself seemed to lose meaning.

Then Nina blinked, and the dream dissolved back into the preset tavern. I frowned as I immediately felt the hard bite of the wooden chair beneath me.

"Why did you do that?" I protested. "We just got there."

"We already spent almost an hour of real time there." Nina looked amused.

"Really?" I blinked. "It felt like we had just arrived."

"Yeah." Nina nodded. "This kind of dancing is famous for that. People go there to have a restful sleep."

She leaned back in her chair.

"If you experience the full dream instead of simply remote viewing it like we just did, people claim they only need four hours of sleep afterward and still wake up completely rested."

"That sounds incredibly overpowered."

"It is." She sighed wistfully. "Sadly, I don't have enough influence to receive a proper invitation into a Naga dancer's private sanctuary."

Another way rich people got to have an advantage over everyone else. It might look minor compared to the Bloodline Realm's soul power disparity, but I bet a farmer would rather have a few extra hours in the day than be able to fling fireballs.

"So..." I stretched my arms. "What are we doing now?"

"Riri should be asleep by now." Nina smiled and reached for my hand. "Let's visit her room in the Melody of Gossip."

She blinked, and the tavern disappeared once again.

We appeared inside what looked like the bedroom of a luxury resort. Half the room consisted of a circular bed roughly the size of a tennis court, garnished with mountains of impossibly soft pillows and layers of colorful silk sheets. The enormous nest resembled an ocean of fabric rather than a place to sleep. It reminded me of Nina's nest, except absurdly more lavish.

The other half of the room had been transformed into an enormous crystal aquarium. Coral reefs covered the floor while schools of brightly colored fish drifted peacefully through perfectly clear water. Tiny glowing jellyfish floated among decorative sea plants, providing most of the room's gentle lighting.

I slowly looked from one side to the other in amazement.

A mountain of pillows shifted. Several silk sheets rose before a woman slowly emerged from beneath them. She wore black lace lingerie that left very little to the imagination.

The moment she noticed us, her eyes widened.

"Oh."

She immediately grabbed the nearest silk blanket and pulled it over herself.

"Hi."

Nina sighed with the exhaustion of someone who had expected something like this.

"I probably should have seen this coming."

"I'm so sorry for interrupting," I said immediately. I grabbed my friend's hand. "Nina, let's leave and come back in an hour."

She ignored me completely.

"Hi there." She waved casually. "Where's Riri?"

"She stepped out to gather some gossip." The half-dressed woman smiled sheepishly. "She should be back any minute."

"I'm really sorry we interrupted your date." I rubbed the back of my neck. "We'll leave now."

"Oh." The girl laughed. "This isn't a date."

"It isn't?" I asked, my eyes dropping to the blanket covering her.

"Riri doesn't do dates," Nina explained matter-of-factly.

I looked from Nina back to the nearly naked woman.

"Then why is she dressed like that?"

"We fooled around for a little while," the woman shrugged. "But since I'm not a Dreamer..."

She tugged awkwardly at the blanket.

"I find it hard to change my clothes by myself."

That actually made sense.

Nina blinked.

The black lace lingerie instantly dissolved into one of Nina's familiar pastel sarong dresses.

The girl visibly relaxed.

"Oh, thank you."

She lowered the blanket before giving us a polite little bow.

"Allow me to introduce myself. I'm Zira Ira."

"I'm a sound mage currently employed by Riri the Songstress."

So she was fooling around with her boss... Different world, different standards, I guess.

"You're from the Ira Clan?" I asked.

"The Light affinity family?"

"Yep." She nodded cheerfully. "I got Light and Kinetic. Those often combine into sound magic."

"Makes sense." I nodded. "I'm Alice Hecate."

I gestured beside me.

"And this is Nina Rockfeather."

Zira's eyes immediately lit up.

"Wait... you're that Nina?"

Nina stiffened slightly.

"Maybe?"

"Oh!" Zira clapped excitedly. "Riri talks about you all the time."

"And the mandolin you created," she gushed. "The sound it makes is simply incredible. I've never heard an instrument produce such beautiful harmonics."

Nina's shoulders visibly relaxed.

"Thank you." She smiled. "I'll admit I was a little worried."

"Worried?"

"A lot of Riri's lovers tend to be hostile toward me."

Zira burst into laughter.

"Oh, please." She waved dismissively. "I've worked for Riri for six months now."

She grinned.

"I've watched her cycle through lovers about as often as I go through kanta."

She pinched the tiny bump of her stomach.

"I'd die of heartbreak if I got jealous every time."

"Nina!"

The voice echoed through the room like the opening note of a song. I turned and saw a mermaid glide gracefully through the enormous aquarium.

Her lower body shimmered with overlapping pink and violet scales. Long side fins stretched elegantly from her hips like those of a flying fish, thin and nearly transparent. She wore a feathered bikini top, soft and reddish-pink like a pair of Valkyren wings supporting her chest. Her vivid magenta hair floated weightlessly behind her like silk drifting through water. She looked ethereal, like an idealized person rather than something real.

When she spotted Nina, her entire face lit up with unmistakable delight.

Without slowing down, the mermaid planted both hands on the edge of the aquarium and launched herself gracefully into the air. She sailed clear across the room in a glittering arc, droplets of water scattering behind her like tiny gemstones. It looked like the rehearsed entrance of a performer taking the stage.

She aimed herself directly at Nina, arms spread, and lips already puckered in anticipation of a dramatic kiss.

Nina, however, knew her friend's antics well enough. With the reflexes of someone who had clearly dealt with this exact situation many times before, she tilted her head a few centimeters to the side.

Instead of a passionate kiss, Riri planted a loud smack squarely against Nina's cheek.

"Oh, you're no fun," Riri pouted dramatically as she landed lightly on her tail fin. "You used to fool around with me all the time."

"That was before I realized I preferred my partners to be monogamous," Nina replied, wiping the exaggerated kiss from her cheek with the back of her hand.

So they really had been lovers once. That explained why they were so comfortable with each other.  They looked like two people who had spent years learning each other's habits. It also sounded as though the relationship hadn't ended because they had stopped caring about one another. They had simply wanted different things.

Riri tilted her head, studying Nina for a moment before her bright orange eyes drifted toward me.

"And is this your new flame?" she asked with a playful grin, looking me up and down without the slightest hint of subtlety. "She's gorgeous."

"We're just friends," Nina answered a little too quickly, a faint blush creeping onto her cheeks.

Riri's smile widened into one of complete satisfaction. She lowered herself to the ground, and her half-fish tail shifted into a pair of legs covered in pink-purple scales along the outer sides while the inner skin looked completely human. Smaller fins protruded from the scaled portions of her thighs, while her modesty was covered by a pink feathered bikini bottom matching her top.

"So..." she said slowly. "You wouldn't mind if I made a move, then?"

Before either of us could answer, she confidently stepped toward me, leaning in with the same theatrical confidence she had shown Nina only moments earlier.

I instinctively stepped back and raised a hand between us.

"I'm sorry," I said politely, "but I'm going to decline."

She pouted immediately.

"Why?" she asked. "Are you not into women?"

"First of all," I replied, "when it comes to something like this, 'no' is already a complete answer. It doesn't need to come with an explanation."

Riri paused. A second later, she nodded.

"I guess that's fair."

"But," I continued, "if you're genuinely curious, I'm fairly certain I'm demisexual."

All three women looked at me blankly.

"What does that mean?" Nina asked.

"It means I only experience sexual attraction after forming a deep emotional connection with someone first."

Nina frowned thoughtfully.

"Isn't that just everyone?"

"Your friend here is a pretty good example of why it actually isn't," I said with a small laugh. "Even then, most people might prefer having an emotional connection before acting on attraction, but they can still find complete strangers physically attractive."

"I can't." I shrugged. "I can recognize when someone is objectively beautiful," I clarified. "I just don't experience any real sexual attraction unless I've already developed a strong emotional bond with them."

"Oh." Nina tilted her head. "That... actually explains a lot."

"It does?" asked Riri.

She nodded.

"She doesn't look at anyone the way you practically look at everyone."

"Hey," Riri protested dramatically. "I have standards."

"You have a pulse check," Nina replied.

Zira doubled over laughing.

Riri placed a hand over her heart in mock offense.

"I feel attacked."

"You flirted with Alice within ten seconds of meeting her."

"I was introducing myself," Riri corrected proudly.

"With a kiss?" I asked.

"That's the best way to do it."

I couldn't help laughing.

Riri turned back toward Nina with a grin that hadn't faded in the slightest.

"I like this one," she declared. "Where did you find her?"

Nina smiled.

"This is Alice Hecate. She wandered into my workshop one day and accidentally became my muse."

"A muse, huh?" Riri repeated.

"I even entered a Creation State while making her spear."

Riri's eyebrows shot upward.

"Oh..." The playful expression dimmed slightly, though it didn't disappear completely. "I didn't realize you'd finally replaced me."

She dramatically pressed the back of her hand against her forehead.

"Oh, poor me. Forgotten by my childhood friend."

"You haven't been my muse for years," Nina replied, sounding more amused than guilty. "Not since you moved to the Sky Islands."

"I suppose that's true."

For the first time since we'd arrived, Riri's smile softened. I couldn't detect any resentment in her voice, only acceptance.

Watching the two of them was oddly comforting. They clearly still cared about each other. That much had been obvious from the moment Riri had thrown herself across the room. But caring about someone wasn't always enough. Sometimes two people simply wanted different things from a relationship. One wanted freedom while the other wanted commitment. Neither was wrong. They just weren't compatible.

From the way they spoke, Nina had accepted that years ago.

Surprisingly, Riri seemed to be the one who hadn't completely let go. She hid it well beneath endless flirting and playful jokes, but every now and then there was the faintest hint of nostalgia in her smile, as though part of her still wondered what might have happened if she had made different choices.

"Welcome to my humble abode." Riri bowed theatrically. "I'm Riri Windrider. How can I help you?"

"It's actually we who might be helping you," Nina said. "Alice showed me something in her sanctuary, and I thought you might find it interesting."

She glanced toward Zira.

"But I'm afraid it's a delicate matter, for your eyes only."

"Really?" Riri looked intrigued. She turned toward her assistant. "As for Zira, I often let her relax in pleasant dreams before I start working on my music anyway."

"Yep, it's one of the perks of the job." Zira nodded cheerfully. "Having a boss who can control your dreams is pretty convenient; I'd much rather dream of calming music than sleep on my own."

"Okay then, that settles it," Riri said.

She took her sound assistant gently by the wrist, dipped her low like a tango dancer, and kissed her deeply before the two of them vanished.

Three minutes later, Riri returned alone.

"Okay, let's go."

Without waiting for an answer, she stepped beside Nina and gracefully dipped beneath one of her wings. The movement was so practiced it looked almost instinctive. Nina's wing immediately folded around her in a protective embrace without her even seeming to think about it.

"Still doing that?" Nina asked with an amused smile.

"I was getting cold," Riri replied. "It's soft and warm."

"This is your sanctuary. You can control the temperature here."

"Let's not waste time arguing semantics."

Nina rolled her eyes before reaching over and taking my hand with her free one. She gently pulled me beneath her other wing until I found myself standing in the same sheltered position as Riri.

A heartbeat later, she blinked, and the room disappeared.

We reappeared inside Nina's sanctuary, right beside the doorway leading out into the Great Dream. Above and below us floated hundreds of rocky islands, each carrying a different workshop dedicated to one of the countless crafts Nina had explored. Some drifted lazily through the sky like clouds, while others slowly rotated in place.

Riri looked upward and smiled nostalgically.

"It's been a while since I visited your dream."

"I'm not the one living on a floating island where the day and night cycle shifts as it travels around the planet," Nina replied. "You know roughly when I'm asleep. You're always welcome to visit."

Riri scratched the back of her neck.

"You know what it's like being a Songstress. If I don't keep networking, people might forget I exist."

"I know." Nina smiled warmly. "I'm not blaming you."

She gently nudged her shoulder against Riri's.

"I'm just reminding you that this place is always open if you need a friend."

Riri's teasing smile softened.

"Thanks."

Rather than visiting any of the floating workshops, Nina led us toward the far side of the Great Dream Gate, where a single ordinary apartment door stood completely alone.

Compared to everything surrounding it, it looked almost laughable in its mundanity.

Before opening it, Nina turned toward Riri with an expression I had yet to see on her.

"Before we go through this door," she said quietly, "you need to promise that whatever you see inside never leaves this dream."

Riri immediately nodded.

"Of course." She shrugged. "A Dream Sanctuary is a private thing. I understand that much. If word got out about Alice's sanctuary, it could become dangerous if she ever fought another Dreamer."

Nina slowly shook her head.

"No."

Riri blinked.

"No?"

"You don't have the faintest idea."

The Songstress looked from Nina to me.

"What could possibly be behind one ordinary door?"

Nina answered without hesitation.

"An Eighth Realm."

Riri laughed.

"A what?"

"You heard me."

Her smile slowly disappeared as she looked toward me for confirmation.

I nodded.

"I'm from beyond the Seven Realms."

"You mean... the Sealed Realm?"

"No." I slowly shook my head. "My world isn't governed by Doubt. There isn't any time travel where I come from, which is the only thing we know for certain exists in the Sealed Realm."

"Frankly, there is barely any magic back home," I continued. "There are no Soulbooks, no monsters, and only the faintest traces of Mythic and Kindred powers."

Riri simply stared at me. Her mouth opened slightly before closing again. She looked toward Nina as though waiting for the punchline.

Instead, Nina quietly gestured toward the door.

"It's easier if you see it yourself."

I stepped forward and turned the handle, opening the door inward.

The three of us stepped into my college flat.

Riri looked around slowly. Her eyes wandered across the bookshelves, the sofa, the kitchen, the framed photographs, the refrigerator covered with magnets, and the countless little details that made the place feel lived in.

"It looks..." she said carefully. "Different. But not 'Eighth Realm' different."

Nina smiled and reached toward the wall, flicking the light switch.

The apartment instantly flooded with bright white light.

Riri jumped.

"What was that?"

She hurried over to the switch, staring at it before pressing it herself.

The lights vanished.

She pressed it again, and the room lit up.

"Okay..." She slowly looked at us. "That's new."

She pointed toward the ceiling.

"This definitely isn't a lightstone."

I picked up the remote and switched on the ductless air conditioner.

A quiet hum filled the room as cool air drifted from the unit.

Riri immediately lifted a hand into the airflow.

Her eyes widened.

"Wind." A second later, she smiled in surprise. "And cold."

She looked around suspiciously.

"You can make this with magic."

"But it isn't magic," I said. "It's just harnessed lightning."

She stared at me.

"That sounds exactly like magic."

I shrugged.

"I suppose it's magic in the same way alchemy is magic."

She clearly wasn't convinced.

Fortunately, that wasn't why we had brought her here.

I walked over to the Tarot table. Sliding the Fool card aside, I replaced it with the Moon.

The apartment dissolved around us as the walls melted into drifting mist before reforming into my father's home cinema.

The love seats, fluffy carpet, beanbag chairs, and enormous LED screen all returned exactly as I remembered them.

Without waiting, Nina fluttered her wings over to the wall and pressed the control button. The blackout curtains silently parted.

Beyond the glass stretched the night skyline of [redacted] city.

Thousands upon thousands of windows glowed across an endless forest of steel and glass towers. Elevated roads wound between skyscrapers like rivers of light, while countless moving headlights traced glowing paths through the darkness below. Neon signs shimmered in every direction, and far beyond them the horizon continued almost without end.

Riri couldn't speak.

She slowly walked toward the glass until she stood only a few inches away.

One hand reached out almost involuntarily, her fingertips resting against the window.

Her reflection stared back at her alongside the impossible city.

"I..." Her voice caught in her throat. "...How many people live here?"

"About seven million," I answered quietly. "Give or take a million."

Her eyes widened even further.

"That's even bigger than Hano... and Hano's size is considered an anomaly."

"It's only possible because of portal magic," Nina said. "They managed it here without any."

Her gaze drifted from one skyscraper to another, trying and failing to comprehend their scale.

"They're like mountains."

"They're homes," I replied. "Just like this one."

She slowly turned toward me, her expression completely stripped of its usual confidence and theatrical charm.

For the first time since we'd met her, Riri Windrider looked genuinely speechless.

"Alice..." she whispered. "What kind of world did you come from?"

I couldn't help grinning.

"The kind," I said, "that has more kinds of music than you can ever imagine."
