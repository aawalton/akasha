We made our way back to the subway station in silence, both of us lost in our own heads.

Aerion kept shooting me concerned glances, probably worried I was going to spiral into some existential crisis about divine conspiracies, which, to be fair, wasn’t entirely off base.

The revelation kept going around in my head in circles. The gods created the Cataclysms, or allowed them to continue. Either way, everything I’d been fighting for, every Axian who’d died, every tragedy caused by each cycle… was all by design. All part of some cosmic plan I was only just beginning to understand.

The question was what to do next? And whether I ought to tell the others? Richard would believe me, probably. He trusted my judgment. Arianna would be a tougher sell. Maybe she’d be willing to at least listen, maybe not. But Yashas? As congenial as the guy was, he wouldn’t believe anything I said without solid evidence.

No, we had a mission to complete. Then, I’d have to gather more evidence. Both to convince myself and the others. I’d deal with the metaphysical implications of divine manipulation after we weren’t actively in a zombie-infested apocalypse.

One crisis at a time.

The fourth and final train run went smoother than I expected. We’d refined our tactics to an art by this point, and with Aerion piloting while I cleared the path ahead, chain whip chomping and flying swords slicing, the zombies didn’t stand a chance.

And then, of course, we hit our first snag.

“Commander ahead!” Aerion called out over the PA. “Big one!”

I could see it in the tunnel. One of the zombie commanders, larger than the regular shamblers and significantly less rotted. Its flesh still had some color to it, and it moved with disturbing coordination as it directed a horde of regular zombies into a deliberate barricade across the tracks. Almost like the thing was still alive.

“Shit. We need to stop,” I said, already moving toward the door.

“Already slowing down,” Aerion replied.

The train screeched to a halt about fifty meters from the commander. I jumped out, Light of the Fearless in hand.

The commander let out a guttural roar that echoed through the tunnel, and the zombie horde surged forward, but not in the chaotic horde as usual. This was more a blitz march, with clear columns and rows. Which somehow made it all the more terrifying.

The only thing that terrified me more was the weapon I unleashed to take care of them.

The chain whip uncoiled from my inventory like a living thing, extending to its full thirty-foot length. [Buzzsaw] activated, its hypersonic vibrations creating a lens-like distortion around the links. The weapon weaved through the horde like a snake, and everything its links touched pulverized into gore.

The commander tried to retreat, its enhanced muscles allowing it to move faster than any shambler had a right to. But bullets maimed his legs, taking him down with an otherworldly howl, giving my flying swords time to approach.

The whip was faster.

The chain wrapped around the commander’s torso and squeezed. The creature’s scream cut off abruptly as [Buzzsaw] turned its enhanced flesh into something that honestly resembled chunky salsa.

I’d seen my fair share of gore upon coming to Axius and I thought I was immune to it.

I nearly puked as the body collapsed, and the remaining zombies lost coordination, stumbling over its remains.

[Soulwoven Voidsteel Alloy Chain Whip of the Damned]’s ability [Buzzsaw] has leveled up to Divergence-3.[Soulwoven Mythril Shortsword]’s ability [Blade Projection] has leveled up to Divergence-8.

I grinned. Between all the levelups, it wouldn’t be long before I leveled again, hitting C-MAX. Unfortunately, that also meant I’d need to do another Trial if I wanted to get to the next tier.

“Clear!” I called out, recalling my weapons and jumping back onto the train. “Let’s keep moving.”

Aerion threw the throttle lever forward and I tried not to think too hard about the vibrations as the train shuddered over the remains of the zombies, crushing them into paste as we ran them over.

We encountered another commander about ten minutes later, but this time, Aerion didn’t even need to stop the train, just slowing down instead.

The moment I saw it in the tunnel ahead, I extended the whip through the broken front window and let it do its thing. The weapon shot forward eagerly, homing in on the commander like a heat-seeking missile.

The creature didn’t even have time to roar. The whip’s spiked mace end opened into that horrifying jaw and just… chomped. One bite, and the commander was gone, reduced to chunks that the chain absorbed as it retracted.

As for the rest, well, they didn’t last long against the spinning blades and my bullets.

“Well,” I said, staring at the whip as it coiled back around my armor. Despite lacking a face, the thing looked disturbingly satisfied. “That was uh, efficient. And terrifying. Mostly terrifying.”

“Your weapon is becoming disturbingly autonomous,” Aerion muttered, giving it a distrusting glance.

“Yeah, I noticed.”

The whip was becoming more efficient with each level it gained, and for the first time, I didn’t know whether to be excited or horrified. Yet one more problem to deal with once I was out of here.

At least it was still under my control for now. Mostly.

The rest of the run was thankfully uneventful. No more commanders showed up, and the scattered groups of zombies were dealt with easily enough with my little arsenal. We stopped the train and the one following us crashed into the horde we’d attracted.

I’d almost come to like the sound of bursting zombies. It was therapeutic in some fucked-up way.

“Well done, my friends!” Yashas’s voice crackled, and not even the PA system could drown out the happiness in his voice. “The path is clear and we are coming through.”

The whole plan had been to carve a path from Ginza down south to Asakusa, and if the damned trains had been automated, this two-day-long initiative would’ve taken no more than 30 minutes.

Then again, Aerion and I might never have gotten the chance to explore Akiba. Silver linings, and all.

Aerion and I hitched another train back, and by the time we reached Asakusa Station, I almost couldn’t believe we’d made it.

We pulled into the station to find Richard, Arianna, and Yashas already waiting on the platform, having cruised there on their own train on the route we’d pre-cleared. Richard was grinning, Arianna looked relieved, and Yashas gave us an approving nod.

“Four successful runs,” Richard said as we disembarked, shaking his head. “I’ll admit, I had my doubts about this train plan, but my hat goes off to you two. That was quite the show.”

“I’ll say,” I said, grinning. “We had more than a few close calls there. And it’s good to see you all in the flesh again.”

“The route to the military compound is now significantly easier,” Yashas said. “We eliminated an estimated fifteen to twenty thousand zombies through the train collisions alone. That we’re standing at this station chatting as we are is a miracle. The path ahead should be easier now.”

“Should be?” Arianna raised an eyebrow.

“There are still challenges ahead,” Yashas said diplomatically. “Come. I’ve found a suitable location for our final briefing.”

Our tactically armored and heavily armed friend led us through the station to an abandoned ticket office, no doubt using his awareness ability to ensure we didn’t run into any shamblers. I’d kill to have an armor that could do that, but I suppose he did pay a hefty price for it, being as weak as he was. He’d certainly decked himself out with Earth’s best to make up for it, though.

The main area of the ticket booth was a mess, with overturned chairs and some desiccated corpses that were thankfully past the point of stinking.

But there was a backroom that was relatively clean. Yashas wiped the coffee mugs and stationery off a large table before spreading a map of the area across it.

“Here’s what we’re facing,” the general said, pointing at the map. “The military compound is approximately one kilometer north of this station. With what limited reconnaissance I was able to conduct, we can expect heavy fortifications. Primarily tanks and artillery emplacements, though we will be too small a target for those. It’s the machine gun nests and helicopter gunships we need to worry about.”

“Gunships,” Richard said, shaking his head. “Never dreamed I’d ever fight an army.”

“With luck, we won’t,” Yashas replied. “And this is not all. The skeleton warriors patrol the streets, and they are coordinated by the tentacled brain-creatures.”

Yashas tapped a section of the map that I recognized as the Kaminarimon. “Expect organized resistance all the way to the Senso-ji. Our best approach is stealth for as long as possible. Once we’re detected, and we will be detected, we run. Fight only when necessary. Prioritize speed over engagement.”

I raised my hand. “I, er, might have an alternative that could help with the stealth part.”

Everyone turned to look at me.

“So, you know how I have a spatial inventory, right?” I said. “Well, I can store people in it too. It has some downsides, but sneaking around with Yashas would be a lot easier than all five of us.”

Yashas’s eyes narrowed thoughtfully. “What are these downsides you speak of?”

I glanced at Aerion. “You want to tell them? I still have no idea what it’s like.”

Aerion nodded. “Time freezes whilst inside Greg’s inventory. You cannot move or breathe, or even think properly while in there. But you are aware, to some extent. It’s… quite odd. Like being trapped in a moment that never ends.”

“That sounds horrific,” Arianna said flatly.

“It was at first,” Aerion agreed. “But you quickly get used to it. The real problem only comes after extended periods. A few hours is tolerable. A day is pushing it. Any longer than that…” She shuddered. “It begins to affect your mind. It takes longer to recover the longer you are inside.”

“Recover from what, exactly?” Richard asked, frowning.

“It… takes some time to make yourself believe you’re free to move and think again,” Aerion said with a shrug. “But again, this is only an issue if you stay in for longer periods. You will barely notice the effect on the time scale Greg intends to put us in.”

“And I need your consent,” I added quickly, looking at Aerion who nodded, guessing what I was about to do.

I touched her shoulder and pulled her into my inventory, eliciting everything from light gasps to widened eyes from my little audience. “I can’t just shove people in there against their will. You have to agree to it first.”

Richard stroked his beard. “Quite an interesting option you’ve put forth. I admit, it would be simpler to sneak around without all of us…”

“Exactly. When we inevitably get detected, I’ll pull you all out and we fight our way through together.” I looked around the room. “I, er, wanted to mention this before, but as Aerion explained, it’s a weird experience, and I figured people wouldn’t be interested. Given what we’re facing, though…”

“May I try it first?” Arianna asked, looking clearly uncomfortable with the idea. “Before committing to it for the mission?”

“Of course,” I replied. “That’s actually a good idea.”

Yashas nodded. “I would also like to experience it, in case circumstances require my own storage in the future.”

“Sure,” I said, bringing Aerion out. The moment I did, all eyes turned to her, examining her, like they were making sure it was really her and not a double or something. “Who wants to go first?”

Richard stepped forward immediately. “Might as well get it over with.”

I placed my hand on his shoulder. “Okay. This might feel a little weird.”

I pulled him into my inventory, and he vanished.

Arianna’s eyes widened. “I can’t believe you can just disappear them like that.”

“It looks like that, but he was just pulled into my inventory really fast.” I gestured vaguely at myself. “He’s in there between my forge and my ingots.”

“That’s deeply unsettling,” Arianna said, her frown growing deeper.

“Your turn?”

She hesitated, then nodded. “Fine.”

I touched her shoulder, and she vanished too. Then Yashas, who disappeared without so much as a flicker of surprise on his face.

“How long should I keep them in, you think?” I asked Aerion.

She considered for a moment before holding up five of her fingers. “Five minutes should give them a good sense of it. Anything shorter and they’ll likely not feel anything at all.”

I counted down in my head, going over the plan again with Aerion to kill the time. When the five minutes were up, I pulled them out.

All three of them staggered slightly as they materialized, looking disoriented.

“That was…” Richard trailed off, searching for words.

“Profoundly wrong,” Arianna finished. “Like being conscious but not existing. I was aware of the passage of time, but also… wasn’t? It’s hard to explain.”

“Fascinating,” Yashas said, his tone clinical despite the slight tremor in his hands. “How long were we contained?”

“Five minutes,” I said.

“It felt like an hour,” Arianna muttered.

“It only feels that way because you can’t think in there,” Aerion explained. “Your perception stretches. It becomes worse the longer you’re inside.”

Richard took a deep breath, steadying himself. “Well. That was… if not unpleasant, at least uncomfortable. But I’ve endured worse. If it gives us a tactical advantage, I’m willing.”

“As am I,” Arianna said, though she didn’t look happy about it. “You kept your identity from us, and I still wonder what other secrets you hide, but this mission is too important to let discomfort stop us.”

“Good,” I said, not quite meeting her eyes. It wasn’t like I didn’t feel bad about hiding who I was from them, even if it came from a place of healthy skepticism. “Then before I store you, make sure you’re battle-ready. Weapons drawn, whatever abilities you have activated, etc. Because when I pull you out, it’ll probably be because our cover’s blown and we’re in deep shit.”

“Comforting,” Richard said dryly.

“Just setting expectations.” I looked at each of them. “And I promise I won’t leave you in there longer than I have to. You have my word.”

Richard grinned. “Better not. I’d haunt you if you did.”

“I’d find a way to kill you even from inside the inventory,” Arianna added, her expression deadly serious.

“Noted,” I said, trying not to show how much that statement unsettled me. “Okay, then. Let me know when you’re ready.”

The group scattered to check their gear and mentally prepare themselves. I watched them for a moment, then turned to find Aerion looking at me with an unreadable expression.

“What?”

“You’re taking on a lot of responsibility,” she said quietly. “Carrying us all like that. Literally.”

“It’s just tactical efficiency.”

“It’s trust,” she corrected. “They’re trusting you with their lives in a very literal way. We still don’t know what happens to what’s in there if… if something happens to you. Don’t take that lightly.”

I nodded. “You know I don’t.”

It was honestly humbling how readily they trusted me with their lives, and that was not something I ever intended to betray.

Five minutes later, everyone was ready. Aerion had her sword drawn, as did Arianna. Richard just looked the same as ever.

“Alright,” I said. “See you on the other side.”

I touched Aerion first. She gave me a quick smile before vanishing. Then Arianna, who set her jaw and disappeared without a word. Finally Richard, who winked at me before blinking out of existence.

Just like that, I was alone with Yashas.

“It’s our show now, general,” I said, changing into my Grace suit.

Yashas nodded seriously. “Indeed. This is likely to be the most danger either of us have ever faced. Are you prepared, Greg?”

Was I? I was about to sneak into a military compound filled with skeletons, tanks, and eldritch brain monsters. I was carrying three of my teammates in my pocket dimension, and somewhere in the back of my mind, I was still processing the revelation that the gods might be responsible for creating the very Cataclysm we were fighting.

Yeah. Totally prepared.

“As ready as I’ll ever be,” I said, mustering a halfassed smile.

“Then follow me.” Yashas moved toward the exit, either not noticing or choosing to ignore my expression. “And Greg? Try not to get noticed.”

“That’s the plan,” I muttered, wishing I had a Cunning suit for situations like these as I followed him to the stairs that led to the surface.

Toward the military compound… and whatever hell awaited within.
