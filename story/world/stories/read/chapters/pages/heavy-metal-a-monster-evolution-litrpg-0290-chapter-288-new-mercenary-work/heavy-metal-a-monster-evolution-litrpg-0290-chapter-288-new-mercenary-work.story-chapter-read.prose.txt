“Not bad at all... but I'd better level up before trying to fight a C-rank.”

Rusty watched the monster disappear around him, then looked down at his own body. One of his arms was missing, and several holes pierced his frame where icicles had skewered him. Even though it had all been an illusion, he had learned a great deal about what his body was capable of. Now he could safely venture outside.

The damage faded as the simulation reset, leaving his alloy shell completely restored. He had not felt any pain, but when magic was involved, his core still suffered. However, with his new skill reinforcing it with a layer of physical and magical protection, he was finally able to hold his own in battle.

“Maybe I should add another layer of protective enchantments. I didn't have enough time during my evolution.”

Rusty had spent day and night inside the soul forge recreating all of his frames, making more variants than ever before. His main body, however, was still the one presented to him in the main chamber, one he had been unable to enchant before completing his evolution. Now he could. Adding more layers of defensive enchantments would make his core almost completely safe from magical attacks, at least from enemies of his own rank. To block C-rank or B-rank spells, he would need wards of a similar level.

“I've got more work to do, but first I'd better see what that humanoid woman wants.”

With that, his time was up. Just like in the other chambers, his surroundings began to fade, and a moment later he was back in his own body with Gleam perched on top of his helmet.

“And I'm back!”

“(˶ᵔ ᵕ ᵔ˶)”

“How long was I gone?”

“(˶ᵔ ᵕ ᵔ˶)”

“Oh, not even a minute? That's good.”

Gleam raised one tiny leg and let out a soft chirp, confirming the brief lapse in time. Rusty nodded and stood from his seat while Gleam climbed down to his shoulder before hopping onto the workbench.

“Now then...”

His armor shifted as he selected one of his new loadouts. His adventurer armor had also been upgraded with the new metal. It now carried a slightly different tint, though it was only noticeable under bright light.

Next came the experience talismans, which he activated one after another until he gained a full level. Satisfied with the small increase in strength, he turned his attention to something else.

'I should probably let those two out. They must have been bored inside the soul forge.'

With that thought, he summoned his two guides.

“Finally. That place has become rather boorish.”

Aburdon appeared first and began complaining almost immediately.

“It would be better if you weren't there talking the whole time.”

Alexander appeared next, his glowing form drifting away from Aburdon, who, as always, loved talking people's ears off whether they wanted to listen or not.

“Will you two ever learn to get along? Just look at me and Gleam.”

Rusty pointed at his companion. She immediately hopped onto his shoulder and began happily bouncing in place. Alexander and Aburdon looked at one another before replying in perfect unison.

“Never.”

“I'm sure you'll forget all about your hate for each other with time. I even forgave the dungeon that imprisoned me.”

Rusty said this as he opened the door to his workshop. Ever since his last summoning, he had managed to let go of much of the hatred he once held toward dungeons. If he could move on, then surely these two could eventually put their shared history behind them as well.

“Forgive this hero? The same one who ruined my life? I don’t think so.”

Aburdon said while Alexander did not even feel the need to comment.

‘Some grudges are not that simple, huh?’

As he thought, he noticed the children in his workshop. It was already afternoon, but there were not many customers in the shop. Rolo sat behind the counter and perked up in surprise. Two days had passed since Edmund had last been there.

“Mr. Edmund? Were you in the smithy this whole time?”

Rolo asked, clearly surprised. He had probably assumed Edmund had gone out adventuring instead.

“Something like that. Do not worry about it. I will be back later, but for now I need to go.”

The other children were running around, and the moment Natia poked her head out, she waved with a bright smile.

“Welcome back, Edmund!”

The youngest, Natia, was as energetic as ever, but her older sister Ria immediately scolded her.

“It’s Mr. Edmund!”

“It’s fine. She can call me Edmund if she wants.”

He replied as he walked outside. There he spotted the second boy, Isan, sweeping the ground in front of the shop.

“Keep up the good work.”

The boy looked a little sleepy, but when he saw Rusty, now dressed in his adventurer's suit of armor, he straightened up and nodded with a smile.

“Thank you, sir!”

With that, he confirmed that everyone was safe. Gleam remained behind to watch over them. The city was much safer now, and the Mercenary Association offered him some protection, but it was still better to have her there in case anything happened.

“Now, what could Galiena want from me?”

He wondered as he walked through the city's streets. The difference between this place and the Rose Knight Academy, or even the other cities they had visited in Gwen's world, was immense. This settlement looked as though it lagged a hundred years behind. There was barely any magic supporting its infrastructure. The streets were dirty, and the dirt roads quickly turned into mud whenever it rained.

“Probably something annoying. Why not use this chance to get rid of her? In your current form, you are more than a match for her, Rusty. After you become the leader, we can move on to the second phase of the domination plan...”

Aburdon cackled, apparently having come up with yet another scheme to take over the settlement.

“Domination plan? Did you forget that Rusty became second in command specifically so he would not stand out? Why would he become the leader now and throw away all that effort?”

Alexander replied with a sigh. Rusty shook his helmeted head as he stepped around a puddle of stagnant rainwater in the middle of the unpaved road.

“Alexander is right, Aburdon. I am not planning on becoming the mercenary leader, at least not for now.”

There were certainly advantages to leading the mercenaries, but there were just as many drawbacks. Meeting the local noble could be considered one of the benefits, yet he had no desire to enter that estate until he had reached the proper C-Rank. With stronger knights stationed there, he still did not feel entirely safe.

Soon, he arrived at the mercenary barracks. The grounds were sparsely occupied by armored men wearing matching equipment. The old Hounds insignia had been removed from their armor. Some trained on the practice field, running through drills, while many others simply lounged around. A few had even passed out drunk in the corner. It was far from a well-run organization, and it was obvious they could use much stricter discipline.

“Well, well, if it isn't the mysterious second in command.”

The voice came from nearby. It did not belong to their leader, but to the man who had effectively become third in line after losing to her in the tournament that had determined the group's pecking order. He was a heavy swordsman, a few levels below the current leader of the Steel Wolves, and it seemed he had decided to set his sights on a different target.

Before the orc siege, he and several other D-ranks had formed a small faction out of resentment at having to take orders. Rusty was not sure whether they had genuinely grown attached to their new leader afterward, but it was hardly surprising that the man who considered himself third in command was trying to climb higher. Rusty, after all, stood directly above him.

“Jakub, was it? Is this important? The boss wanted to see me.”

He spoke dismissively. As second in command, he had no reason to stop for someone like this. Yet the moment he tried to walk past, another man stepped in front of him. It was the very same opponent Rusty had defeated in the semifinals, the one who was perhaps fourth in line.

“Can't that wait, Second? Won't you have a little chat with us before you make your report?”

Rusty paused, glancing between Jakub and the other man. Neither of them was a match for him anymore. At this point, he could defeat both of them at once. However, he was still concealing his true strength, and crushing them too easily would only invite unwanted questions.

“Get out of my way.”

“Oh, I think you misunderstand. We just want to have a little talk about our boss.”

“…”

At first, Rusty thought they were trying to provoke him into a fight. It did not take long for him to realize this was about something else entirely.

“Aren't you annoyed with her too? She can't seriously call herself a leader. The boys told me you're the one stuck writing all the reports while she sits around drinking and taking credit for everything. You do all the hard work while she acts all high and mighty.”

“Oh my, these hoodlums.”

Before Rusty could reply, Aburdon's sarcastic voice echoed in his mind.

“These two want to win you over so they can betray that woman. How interesting, but painfully obvious. They are not even trying to hide it. You are supposed to arrange something like this in a secure location and send someone else with a message instead of coming in person. How amateurish. Why didn't they pay an orphan to deliver a letter and arrange a meeting at night like any sensible conspirator would?”

Alexander let out a quiet huff.

“Not everyone lives in a world of shadowy backroom politics, Aburdon. These are mercenaries, not spymasters. Though I agree their approach is foolish.”

Rusty looked from Jakub to the other man, taking in their arrogant smirks and relaxed postures. They clearly thought he was a quiet, obedient lackey who had grown tired of handling all the administrative work while Galiena lounged around and took the credit.

“If you have concerns about the leadership, take them up with the boss directly.”

Rusty kept his voice calm and cold.

“I have no interest in this.”

Jakub's smirk vanished, replaced by a scowl. His hand drifted toward the hilt of his heavy broadsword.

“You think you're above us? You might be second in command, but you're just a lone adventurer who got lucky!”

The two were not very secretive about their dissatisfaction, and they were loud enough for even some of the lesser mercenaries to notice. Only when more eyes turned toward them did they finally realize that making a scene would only make things worse.

"...Consider yourself lucky, but don't think this is over."

Jakub said before giving him a cold glare and finally walking away. Rusty simply stood there without replying. He glanced at the nosy mercenaries around him, who quickly looked away the moment they realized he had noticed them.

"Oh, Second, there you are! Where were you for the past day?"

As if on cue, their leader finally showed herself. The large woman wore a broad grin and failed to notice that something was off about the people walking away. Instead, she waved him over and led him into her private office, which had once belonged to Varkas.

Rusty followed her inside. The office looked very different from the last time he had been there. Varkas had been a man who liked to flaunt his wealth, a trait that had probably led him down the path of corruption. Galiena, on the other hand, was a much simpler warrior. She cared little for lavish decorations, and most of them had been removed. In their place were a bear rug and several mounted monster trophies.

"Those are..."

"Oh, a new merchant arrived yesterday. They have a lot of stylish items!"

"Stylish..."

While Rusty was neither human nor a being of flesh and blood, even he could tell that the room made no sense. Strange mugs covered the desk, which was cluttered with mismatched wooden carvings, a half-eaten smoked ham, and map scrolls being used as improvised coasters.

Galiena dropped into the heavy oak chair behind the desk and rested her boots on top of a stack of documents. She took a long drink from a silver tankard before wiping her mouth with the back of her forearm.

"Sit down, Second. We've got business."

Rusty looked at the only remaining chair in the office but decided to stand beside it instead.

"What kind of business?"

He wanted to head back to the dungeon to continue leveling up and make use of the summoning chamber to gain more power. For now, however, maintaining his life among the mercenaries was just as important, so he was stuck with whatever this woman had planned.

"It's about the Orc tribes. Here, I'm sure you'll be able to figure it out."

She pointed toward a stack of scrolls and documents at the edge of the desk. Rusty picked them up without hesitation and began reading. At the same time, his guides, hidden within his helmet, helped him process the information.

"They want us to go into the Orc-infested forest?"

"Something like that. It seems those noble bastards got cold feet after the recent attack, or something like that."

Rusty glanced over the papers again while his guides quietly shared their opinions, unheard by the mercenary leader.

"Maybe those humans aren't as incompetent as I assumed. Or at least someone among them knows those Orcs are planning something."

Aburdon commented, his voice carrying little interest.

"I'll have to agree with Aburdon. Maybe they want us to thin their numbers and prevent them from amassing an army."

Alexander's words got Rusty thinking. If they were sending him to the forest, it was a great opportunity. He had been itching to test his new body, and what better opponents than the orcs? Some of them were even C-rank, giving him worthy enemies to fight.

Those creatures offered far better experience than the monsters in the dungeon, and the mission would also give him a chance to map the area and turn it into a proper hunting ground. Even after the operation was over, he could continue hunting orcs if he knew which camps were too dangerous to attack.

‘Even if that Orc leader shows up, we can just escape like last time... This is great!’

"They want us to kill orcs?"

"Yes. We'll have support from the adventurers, and we'll be able to order them around."

"I see."

As always, he replied with few words while reading through the assignment. They would receive some support from the adventurers, but not nearly enough to make Rusty feel confident. The mission did not look very appealing. They were marching into enemy territory with little information, and their mercenary force was far from united. There was also no guarantee the adventurers would not flee at the first sign that their side was losing, making the entire operation a risky one.

Yet none of that really mattered to Rusty. What he needed was to level up and grow stronger. There was not much left before he reached C-rank, only twenty-three more levels, and that alone was enough to capture his attention.
