I stepped through the gate just as Kaela emerged from the opposite entrance across the arena. Her golden eyes swept over the dense foliage before settling squarely on me.

She grinned at me with interest, as though she were looking at prey. “Black Phantom, was it? Hope you don’t disappoint me like the others.”

It was clear she didn’t recognize me, or at least not yet.

“Ladies and gentlemen, brace yourselves for what may be the most anticipated clash of the round!” the commentator announced. “On one side, the undefeated dark horse—Black Phantom!”

A wave of cheers and excited murmurs rolled through the stands.

“And on the other, the Mad Beast of the Silver Fang, a force of nature who has crushed her way through the tournament—Kaela!”

As the final syllable echoed, the barrier began to rise around the arena, sealing us within the jungle battlefield. Taking advantage of the brief moment before the match began, I focused my gaze on her and activated Inspect Eye.

Steel met claws. Her strike slammed into my crossed blades, the impact rattling my arms and driving my boots deep into the earth. Even without entering her transformed state, her raw physical strength was as overwhelming as ever.

Ugh… as expected, I’m hardly her match in direct combat! Even with the stat boost from possessing Selene, I couldn’t overpower her. It couldn’t be helped; Selene was an assassin, and her strength lay in assassination, not head-on clashes. Not to mention that Selene was strongest when she was in the night below the moon. Perhaps if I had chosen Uldrek the Blood Berserker instead, I could have overwhelmed Kaela’s base form and stood on equal footing with her transformed state.

I quickly disengaged and called out, “Kaela, why did you join this tournament?”

“Hah! Is that what you’re worried about?” She lunged at me with a feral laugh, claws slashing toward my throat.

I deflected the strike using Lunar Sword Dance, my blades flowing into position as I adeptly parried. “Then what’s your true purpose?” I pressed. “Are you trying to become the commander of Istellia’s divine warrior army?”

She paused for a moment, staring at me, then threw her head back and burst into laughter. “Hahahaha! I don’t give a shit about being commander! All I want is to fight strong opponents!”

A true battle maniac through and through. If that was the case, then I might be able to convince her to forfeit this match—provided I could offer her a more enticing alternative.

She shoved forward suddenly, overwhelming my guard and forcing me to brace. The impact reverberated through my arms as she chained her attacks together—wide slashing arcs with her claws, a brutal rising swipe, followed by a spinning backhand strike that came in like a hammer. I had no choice but to rely on my two daggers to block and redirect each blow, my wrists screaming with strain with every impact. The jungle around us splintered under the shockwaves.

“You think I care about leading humans?” she continued, eyes gleaming with delight.

She lunged again, closing the distance entirely. But then, her nose twitched, and she froze for a couple of seconds.

“…That smell…” Her eyes widened in sudden recognition. “You are Ma—”

Before she could finish the word, I activated Moonlight Blade. Both daggers shone with pale white radiance as I poured strength into my stance and drove them forward in a sudden burst. The force caught her off guard, blasting her backward several meters as roots shattered and soil exploded beneath her feet.

Not bad! I thought, genuinely a little amazed by the effect of Moonlight Blade.

She slid to a stop and stared at me with narrowed eyes. “Ha! No wonder you’re asking me these questions,” she said, lips curling into a sharp grin. “What’s the deal with the disguise?”

“That’s not important right now,” I replied. “What matters is why you’re here.”

She snorted. “Didn’t I already tell you? I’m here to fight.”

“Then that’s all the more reason you shouldn’t win this tournament.”

Her ears twitched slightly as she paused, golden eyes locking onto mine. “What?”

“Think about it. If you become the commander, you’ll be chained down. You’ll have to deal with all sorts of nuisances like meetings, giving orders, and playing politics. You’ll be responsible for thousands of divine warriors who can’t even survive a single exchange with you. You won’t be charging into the thick of battle anymore; you’ll be standing behind the lines, giving commands.”

Her grin weakened.

“You won’t get to fight freely,” I continued. “Every move you make will be restricted and questioned. You’ll be told where you can and can’t go. Who you can and can’t fight. Can you really tolerate listening to weak humans who can’t even pose a challenge to you—anyone other than me?”

Kaela’s claws slowly curled into fists.

She clicked her tongue. “Tch. That sounds like shit.”

“Exactly. So, here’s my offer,” I said. “Forfeit this match.”

Her eyes snapped back to me, sharp and dangerous. “Human, are you trying to trick me?”

As I thought, Kaela wasn’t as simple-minded as her battle-hungry demeanor suggested. Whatever she had endured before being drawn into the Divine Will had forged her into someone far more perceptive than she appeared at first glance.

“Hear my offer first,” I said quickly. “In return, I promise you this: When the Battle of Divine Will begins, I’ll make sure you’re placed where the fighting is thickest. Against the strongest enemies. No humans on our side will become a nuisance to you. I can guarantee that.”

Her eyes widened with interest. A silent tug-of-war must have been playing out inside her gaze as she hesitated a lot. After all, if she endured holding back here, she could let loose completely later, just like the saying: “endure the bitter now and savor the sweet afterward.”

Slowly, her grin returned, wider than ever.

“…You better not be lying,” she said, excitement bleeding into her voice.

“When did I ever lie to you?” I replied with the most convincing smile I could manage.

Kaela squinted her eyes at me for a few seconds as if trying to see through me. Then she suddenly threw her head back and laughed.

“Fine!” she declared loudly, retracting her claws. “I’ll bite.”

Without another word, she stepped back and raised one hand. “I forfeit.”

For several long seconds, the words didn’t seem to register at all, and the arena fell into stunned silence. But then—

“What?!”

The commentator nearly choked on his own announcement. His voice echoed across the Grand Colosseum in full disbelief. “W-Wait, contestant Kaela has… forfeited?! This is completely unprecedented! The Mad Beast of the Silver Fang has voluntarily withdrawn from the match!”

In truth, I could have asked her to forfeit later, perhaps even in the final. However, the real reason I needed her to withdraw now was far simpler. If I won this round, I would walk away with nearly one hundred thousand soul coins from the bet I’d placed on Black Phantom, which was myself.
