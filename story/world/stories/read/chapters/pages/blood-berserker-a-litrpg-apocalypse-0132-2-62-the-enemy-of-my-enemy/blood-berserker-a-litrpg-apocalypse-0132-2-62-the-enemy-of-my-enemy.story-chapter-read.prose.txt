Nathan began to feel lightheaded and woozy as his vision suddenly began to double up, with his balance feeling off as the world began to swirl even though he stood still. His skin tingled, and everything seemed out of place.

Wait, isn't this the type of thing Hidden Mind is supposed to protect me from? Nathan thought.

[Hidden Mind (I)] was supposed to protect him from mental attacks, and Nathan certainly believed that whatever skill l the ogre had used on him certainly qualified as a mental attack. But apparently, the system hadn't recognized it as one.

Or maybe [Hidden Mind (I)] was too low-level to actually handle such a skill. In the end, all that mattered was that the skill had affected him.

Joke's on you. I'm a teenager from Earth. Nathan thought as he tightened his grip on Oblivion.

He'd been high before, because in his youthful days—when he had worked for Sparrow—he had been allowed to indulge in some vices that he wasn't really proud of, but he had been clean of all those things. Nathan hadn't even had a sip of alcohol since he had begun working for Mr. Wong. Which meant he had been sober for a few years, but he knew this feeling.

In fact, he had done jobs under the influence. So this wasn't really much of a new thing to him. He just needed to keep his head in the game, and he would win this.

Against the berserker's better judgment, he charged at the ogre. Eager to prove to himself that he could indeed handle this, his sword came up in a vicious arc as he swung at the side of the ogre who was standing in front of him, but he completely missed.

The leader of the ogres did not even make a single attempt to move as he watched Nathan chase shadows. The berserker struck again, but his aim was just off enough to narrowly miss the ogre again. And then, Nathan got the reward for his troubles as the ogre elbowed him in the face, knocking him into the dirt.

Nathan was in a worse situation than he had been in at the beginning of the fight due to the blow he had received from the ogre moments ago.

The Berserker got up to his feet and once again charged towards the ogre. This time, he preferred to thrust his sword rather than swing it to avoid a repeat of the last time, when he had narrowly missed it.

It turned out that that decision had been correct; his aim was actually on point this time. Unfortunately for Nathan, his sword did not manage to reach his target as the ogre brought his massive broadsword down on Oblivion, knocking it off target.

The ogre chuckled in the berserker’s face.

The sound infuriated Nathan, who quickly pulled himself together and barreled into the ogre, knocking him off his feet. Before the ogre could retaliate, Nathan rolled away and immediately put distance between them.

Nathan was too disoriented to know how much space was between them, but he hoped that it was enough.

The berserker didn’t beat himself up too much about his performance in the fight because, even as disoriented as he was, Nathan had managed to avoid being skewered by the ogre. Of course, much of that outcome was due to the fact that the ogre hadn't seen fit to truly attack him, probably because it didn't consider Nathan to be a threat, and was something Nathan planned to take advantage of.

I've done this before, I can do it again. Nathan thought.

Fighting under the influence wasn’t a new thing for him. But the last time he had done it, it definitely hadn't been with swords. The thought made Nathan consider ditching his swords, but quickly he realized that that would be a death sentence, as the ogre armed with his massive broadsword would not care if he was armed or not. That meant Nathan would have to keep Oblivion in his hands if he planned to keep his hands intact.

Nathan cursed and charged at the ogre again. This time, he added a little twist to his attack.

As soon as he got within range, he released an overhead strike, planning to hit the berserk ogre from the front. The ogre was quick to raise his massive broad sword, and a loud clang! echoed in the mine as their blades met mid-air. It was clear that a straight sword clash like this wasn’t in the berserkers' favor, but Nathan ignored that logical part of him and pressed the attack by pushing everything he had into his arms.

Even if he wasn't able to break through the defenses of the ogre, being locked in this position would at the very least buy him a little bit of time, for this disorientation to begin to wear off. At least he hoped that the ogre would be dumb enough to allow him to buy that time.

Turns out the ogre wasn’t that dumb. The ogre kicked at Nathan once again, knocking him off his feet.

Is one day of peace and quiet too much to ask for? Nathan cursed..

The berserker quickly scrambled to his feet, the action a lot more difficult than it should have been because of how disoriented he was. Regardless of his special circumstance, Nathan still managed to dive out of the way, evading an attack by the ogre as it swung its massive broadsword, which sank a good amount of inches into the dirt where the berserker had lain moments ago.

The massive broadsword had kicked up a significant amount of debris once it landed, and once it cleared, Nathan, still in his disoriented state, saw a deep groove that lined the earth that he had been on a moment ago.

The berserker clicked his teeth at the sight. He had gotten dangerously close to losing his life, but he had come to realize something within the first few clashes against the ogre.

Unlike the demon prince, who had been ruthless, the ogre seemed not to be a good fighter. In fact, it seemed more like it preferred to overwhelm Nathan, before crushing him, which meant that the ogre was used to fighting foes a lot less leveled than him. And Nathan was willing to bet his life on that being the case. That was why the berserker once again charged at the ogre.

He had already made peace with the fact that the ogre wasn't going to let him outlive the two minutes that the [Disorientation (IV)] had been inflicted upon him, which meant that he also had less than two minutes to get this fight out of the way, or else he was probably going to die.

So spotting his opportunity, he charged at the ogre.

As soon as he got within range of the ogre’s broadsword, he made a wild slash at the ogre. It was nothing more than a bait for the ogre. And it was one that the ogre fell for.

The leader of the ogres brought up his broadsword to defend against the attack, but that proved to be a mistake as Nathan activated his Dao-tied skill. [Dissipate (I)]

Nathan's entire body instantly disappeared as his skill took hold, and he immediately reappeared behind the ogre.

Of course, the ogre was fast enough to realize what had happened, but it wasn't fast enough to move its body out of the way.

To the ogre's credit, it had managed to twist its upper body out of the way, but Nathan's strike wasn’t aimed in that direction. The Berserker swung Oblivion with all the strength that he could muster as he cleaved through the right leg of the ogre in a single strike.

Great.

Now we are both disoriented.

The ogre screamed as its severed leg fell to the floor, red blood leaking from its wounds and drenching the dirt beneath it.

It took a minute, but the ogre managed to pull himself together before swinging his broadsword at Nathan. Unfortunately for the ogre, the berserker had been smart enough to hop back after his successful attack, so the ogre's broadsword met nothing but air.

The ogre’s recently severed limb left it rooted to a spot, opening up opportunities for Nathan. In fact, from the way the Berserker was seeing it, this fight was basically over the moment he had succeeded in that attack, which had permanently put the ogre off balance, so he could now strike at it. But it went without saying that the berserker would have to keep an eye out for its blade.

Nathan held back the urge to puke as his vision spun as he charged at the ogre.

Oblivion went up in the vicious arc as it swung towards the arm of the ogre. As expected, the ogre tried to defend the attack, which turned out to be another grave mistake on his part, as Nathan simply let go of the hilt of Oblivion, watching as the ogre's broadsword knocked the sword into the dirt.

It seemed like a big mistake on the berserker's part, but Nathan already had a plan in mind.

Without missing a beat, the berserker summoned King's Wrath into his free hand and slashed at the remaining leg of the ogre, severing it. Nathan watched with satisfaction as the ogre fell to the ground, helpless, both legs severed.

The ogre writhed in pain on the floor. “Curse you!”

He continued to yell curses at the berserker, but Nathan was so disoriented by the pounding headache he had and the way that his vision swirled that he simply ignored the ogre as he picked up Oblivion with his other hand, and without a shred of hesitation, he slashed at both arms of the ogre with both swords.

Nathan's aim was a little bit off, as he was still a little bit disoriented. So he unfortunately did not go all the way through the ogre's right hand with Oblivion, which was in his left hand. That meant there was a little bit of skin still attached to the demon's right arm once Nathan had finished the attack.

But the berserker didn't really think it was much of an issue since the black blade had cut through the bone, which meant the ogre wouldn't be using that hand, just as the other one that King's wrath had completely severed.

Nathan stared at the bleeding ogre. Even disoriented as he was, he had managed to cleave through three of its limbs, and the only one remaining, which was his left hand, was hanging on by a strip of flesh and a bit of muscle.

He crouched to the level of the ogre still on the floor, writhing in pain with his teeth clenched.

There was no way that this ogre had just magically appeared on his island, going straight for the mine and not even bothering with his stronghold, and the only reason that could be was if someone had told him about the mine or sent him to reclaim it.

And if Nathan had a target painted on his back, he wanted to know who the painter was. “Who sent you?” He asked.

The ogre looked Nathan dead in the eyes and spat in his face. The bloody spit hit the berserker on the chin.

“I've really just had a terrible day fighting and killing a demon prince. You do not want to test what else I can do to you, even in your state.” Nathan said.

As if Nathan had said the most incredible thing, the ogre stopped writhing as he stared up at Nathan, his eyes wide as he tried to figure out if the berserker was lying. The ogre then did something that Nathan found very weird: he sniffed at the berserker.

“Sulfur.” The ogre spat the words with so much venom that he almost choked on his own spit.

Where is he going with this?

The ogre’s eyes shone with something that Nathan couldn’t put a finger on. “If what you are saying is true, then maybe there really might be hope for the multiverse.”

“Take this.” The ogre said, as he summoned an item Nathan was vaguely familiar with—a coin pouch.

Of course, without limbs, the pouch could only appear beside the ogre, dropping with a clinking sound. “That is all the gold I have amassed in my life. Take it, it is yours as is my broadsword, but you must promise me that you will kill the Demon King Zephyr the VII.”

Nathan looked at the ogre skeptically. He wasn't sure if this was a trap because it was very odd for someone who he had maimed as brutally as this to offer him such a thing.

“No,” Nathan said as he began to piece together what the ogre was trying to do. “I will not fight any demon King, or any other being for you.”

He made a weird sound that sounded suspiciously like chuckling mixed with wheezing. “You are free to reject fighting the demon king, but take my items. I am already at the end of my rope, and they are all worthless to me, but they might help you.”

“I planned to use it to hire an army to take on hell, but all these coins will not serve me in death. So they are yours, and so is my life.” The ogre said. “Go on, kill me, and gain the levels you need. Because, like it or not, more demons will come here until your world becomes unrecognizable.” Nathan paused, the reality of the situation dawning on him as he finally comprehended what was going on.

The ogre had realized its fate was sealed, and whether Nathan decided to deliver another blow, it was surely going to die. Which was why it was offering Nathan the coins that he had spent his life amassing.

“Why are you really doing this?”

The ogre did not answer, and it seemed the lights in his eyes were starting to dim.

Nathan briefly considered not killing the ogre, but it would be a waste of the ogre's life.

With his mind made up, Nathan gripped the head of the ogre with his right hand, just as it closed its eyes. [Consume (I)].

The ogre thrashed around for a minute, but Nathan's hand remained firm on the ogre's skull as Consume finally began the process of transferring the ogre's entire essence to the berserker.

Nathan's veins around his wrist bulged as the entire essence of the ogre flowed into him, rejuvenating him. And within a few breaths, it was over.
