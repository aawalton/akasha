The silence of the freezing night was shattered by the sharp, unforgiving thwack of a bowstring. Perberos’ arrow vanished from the string and materialised a fraction of a second later, buried deep within the eye socket of the furthest, eyeless beast atop the merchant’s wagon. The creature didn't even have the chance to vocalise its pain. The force of the elven longbow snapped the monster’s neck backwards with a sickening crunch, sending its elongated, hairless body tumbling lifelessly over the far side of the canvas roof.

Instantly, the remaining three beasts reacted. They did not flee. They moved with a terrifying, insectoid suddenness, their heads snapping toward the source of the attack, their serrated jaws parting to release high-pitched, warbling screeches that tore through the quiet camp like jagged glass.

"Now!" Perberos shouted, the elven stoicism breaking as he reached over his shoulder for another shaft.

Josh didn't hesitate. He felt the familiar, fiery surge in his core as he activated his movement skill. The magical system-assisted energy flooded his exhausted muscles, overriding the cold and the fatigue. His heavy plate armour, which moments before had felt like a frozen iron prison, suddenly felt light. He pushed off the frozen mud, his boots digging deep into the frost, and exploded forward with unnatural, blurring speed.

Josh didn’t do subtle engagement. He needed to be the loudest, most obvious threat in the vicinity to draw them away from the sleeping civilians and the compromised perimeter.

"Over here, you ugly bastards!" Josh roared, his voice booming across the laager. As he closed the distance to the wagon, he swung his heavy shield in a wide arc, smashing it violently against the iron-rimmed wheel of the cart. The resounding CLANG rang out like a temple bell.

The tactic worked, mostly.

Two of the beasts, their barbed foot-pads gripping the canvas, practically threw themselves off the roof, launching their bodies through the air directly at Josh. Their double-jointed limbs extended, claws glinting in the starlight, ready to pin him to the frozen earth.

But the third beast, the massive specimen with the whip-like tails, ignored the noise. Driven by a predatory instinct that bypassed simple distraction, it slithered down the side of the wagon with horrifying fluidity, dropping not toward Josh, but directly onto the overturned bucket where Kaelen sat sleeping.

The heavy thud of the beast landing directly on the adventurer’s chest violently woke him from his slumber.

Kaelen’s eyes snapped open, wide with a sudden, uncomprehending terror. He barely had time to draw a breath, let alone scream, before the beast’s jaw, a nightmare of jagged bone-plates, clamped shut over his throat and collarbone. There was a wet, tearing sound, followed by a sickening crunch of armour and bone giving way.

"Kaelen!" Josh bellowed, his heart stuttering in his chest, but he couldn't stop. He couldn't turn. The other two beasts were already upon him.

Josh raised his shield, bracing his back leg, just in time to catch the impact of the first leaping monster. The sheer weight of the creature was astonishing. It slammed into the steel face of his shield, driving Josh backward, his boots skidding through the frozen mud and carving deep trenches. The beast scrambled wildly, its hook-like claws screeching against the metal, trying to reach over the rim to find flesh.

The second beast landed lightly to Josh's right, immediately circling to flank him, its whip-like tail thrashing the ground.

Josh shoved the first beast off his shield with a grunt of exertion, swinging his longsword in a brutal, horizontal cleave. The blade bit deep into the creature's slick, hairless hide, drawing a spurt of thick, dark blood, but the monster contorted its spine in an impossible angle, rolling with the blow to avoid a fatal strike.

As it rolled back to its feet, its jaws opened wide, and its throat convulsed.

A stream of thick, pale-green liquid shot from its maw, spraying across the dark air. Josh cursed and brought his shield up, tucking his chin behind the rim.

The liquid hit the steel shield and his left shoulder pauldron. Instantly, the night air filled with the acrid, eye-watering stench of sulphur and burning ozone.

The acid was terrifyingly potent. It hissed furiously as it struck the metal. Where it hit the face of his shield, the steel immediately began to pit and bubble, the surface tarnishing to a dull, corroded black. But worse was the splash that caught his shoulder. The acid ate straight through the thick leather straps of his pauldron, seeping instantly into the steel rings of his chainmail and the padded gambeson beneath.

"Argh!" Josh grunted, his teeth grinding together as a searing, blistering agony bloomed across his left shoulder. It felt as though someone had pressed a white-hot branding iron directly against his skin. The pain was blinding, threatening to buckle his knees.

The distraction was all the second beast needed. It lunged, its jaw snapping shut on Josh’s right greave. The bone-plates failed to pierce the heavy steel plate, but the sheer force of the bite sent a shockwave of pain down Josh's shin, knocking him off balance.

Josh stumbled sideways, his vision swimming from the agonizing burn on his shoulder. His skill had faded, leaving his limbs feeling slower. He brought the pommel of his sword down hard on the head of the beast biting his leg, forcing it to release its grip, but the first monster was already convulsing its throat for another volley of acid.

He was too slow. The exhaustion of the past few days, compounded by the chemical fire eating into the flesh of his shoulder, was slowing his reflexes. He raised his corroded shield, preparing for the worst.

Before the beast could spit, a silver blur streaked through the air.

Perberos’ arrow took the spitting beast straight through the open maw. The arrowhead pierced the back of its throat and drove upward into its brain. The creature jerked violently, the acid gurgling uselessly in its ruined throat, before it collapsed into a twitching heap in the mud.

Josh didn't look back to thank the elf. He used the brief respite to pivot, driving his boot into the chest of the remaining beast, creating space. He swung his sword in a desperate, overhead strike. The blade caught the beast perfectly on the collarbone, biting deep into the chest cavity. The monster shrieked, thrashing wildly, its whip-like tail whipping around to strike Josh in the ribs, but the blow lacked its earlier power.

Suddenly, Perberos was there. The ranger had discarded his bow, drawing his twin, curved elven blades. He moved with a terrifying, silent grace, stepping into the beast's blind spot. With a fluid, scissor-like motion, Perberos drove both blades into the creature's neck, severing its spine in a single, devastating cut.

The beast went limp, sliding off Josh’s sword to land heavily in the dirt.

Josh collapsed onto one knee, his chest heaving, his breath coming in ragged, white plumes. He reached up with his right hand, desperately tearing at the clasps of his left pauldron, trying to pull the acid-soaked metal away from his body. The gambeson beneath was smoking, the fabric practically melted to his blistered skin.

"Hold still," Perberos commanded quietly, kneeling beside him. The elf produced a small flask of clear, magical water from his belt, pouring it directly over the smoking wound.

The water hissed violently as it neutralised the acid, sending a fresh wave of blinding pain through Josh, but the burning sensation finally began to recede, leaving behind a dull, throbbing agony.

Around them, the camp was erupting into utter chaos. The screeches of the beasts and the clash of steel had woken the laager. Refugees were screaming, mothers were frantically shushing crying children, and the sounds of heavy boots were rushing toward their position.

"Kaelen," Josh gasped, forcing himself to look towards the merchant's wagon, leaning heavily on his sword.

Perberos' expression was grim. "I was too late. He was dead before I could draw my second arrow."

Beyond the wagon, the massive fourth beast lay dead in the mud, three elven arrows protruding from its thick neck. Beneath it, half-obscured by the shadows and the mud, lay Kaelen. The adventurer’s chest was a ruined, bloody mess, his armour peeled back like tin foil. His spear lay precisely where it had fallen from his sleeping hands, entirely unused. Right next to his limp fingers lay the shattered remains of a dark glass bottle, the sharp stench of cheap, high-proof spirits mingling heavily with the metallic smell of blood.

"By the Light," Carcan’s voice cut through the darkness. She jogged into view, her staff glowing with a soft, revealing luminescence. Behind her came Bhel, his axes drawn, his beard bristling, and Brett, who had a sphere of crackling fire hovering above his palm.

They stopped short as the light from Carcan's staff illuminated the gruesome scene: the corrosive smoke rising from Josh’s armour, the dead beasts, and the mangled corpse of Kaelen.

Carcan immediately rushed to Josh’s side, her hands glowing with a soft, golden healing magic. "Let me see the burn, Josh. Quickly."

Josh waved her off, gesturing towards the wagon. "I'll live. See to him. Make sure..." He swallowed hard, the bile rising in his throat. "Just make sure."

Carcan approached Kaelen's body, kneeling in the blood-soaked mud. She placed two fingers against his neck, then bowed her head, her shoulders slumping. "He is gone."

Bhel stepped forward, prodding the carcass of one of the beasts with the toe of his heavy boot. He looked down at Kaelen, then noticed the broken glass and the strong smell of alcohol. His expression twisted into a mixture of pure disgust and simmering anger.

"The fool," the dwarf rumbled, his voice low and heavy. He kicked a shard of the broken bottle, sending it skittering across the frozen mud. "The absolute, bloody fool. He wasn't just asleep. He was drunk."

Brett stepped closer, the fire blinking out in his palm as he stared at the gruesome remains of his fellow adventurer. The mage looked slightly green as he caught the scent of the spilled spirits. "He was trying to numb it. The trauma of the past few days... the exhaustion. It was clearly too much for him to face sober."

"Exhaustion is an excuse for missing a swing in combat," Bhel snapped, his knuckles white on the hafts of his axes. "Trauma is an excuse for a trembling hand. But drinking on watch? That is a death sentence you write for yourself and everyone behind you. He doomed himself, and he nearly doomed over a hundred people. If Perberos hadn't heard them coming, these things would be feasting on children right now."

Carcan stood up, drawing her cloak around her. "Speak gently of the dead, Master Dwarf. He failed, yes. Terribly so. He sought an escape in the bottle and paid the ultimate price for his failing. May the Light grant him the peace in death that he could not find in these last, dark days."

"Pity a drunkard if you must," Bhel grunted, turning away to secure the perimeter. "But I'll not sugar-coat it. The frontier doesn't care about trauma, and it certainly doesn't forgive a man who drowns his duty in cheap ale. It only cares if your eyes are open."

Josh remained silent, leaning against the wagon wheel as Carcan finally turned her attention to his shoulder. Her magic washed over the burn, soothing the worst of the blistered skin. The pain dulled to a manageable ache, but the psychological weight of the encounter pressed down on him heavier than his armour.

He had put Kaelen there. He knew the man was struggling, knew he was combative and terrified. He should have smelled the alcohol on him, or at least recognised the desperate glint in a broken man's eye. He had trusted a broken man to hold the line anyway. It was a leader's mistake, and a man was dead because of it.

"We need to set a double guard for the rest of the night," Josh said, his voice hoarse. "Bhel, Brett, wake Geth and anyone else who can hold a weapon. Nobody sleeps. We form a hard perimeter around the wagons until sunrise."

For the remainder of the night, the camp existed in a state of hyper-vigilant terror. The refugees, now fully aware of the horrors lurking in the darkness, sat huddled together in the centre of the laager, wrapped in blankets, their eyes wide and staring at the shadows. The adventurers paced the perimeter, weapons drawn, starting at every gust of wind and every shifting branch.

But the forest remained silent. The probing attack had failed, their scouts had been slaughtered, and whatever intelligence guided the beasts seemed to decide that the defences were too thick to break tonight.

When the dawn finally broke, it offered no warmth, only a bleak, grey light that revealed the true, miserable state of the camp. The mud was frozen solid, the air was bitterly cold, and the faces of the survivors were hollow, painted with exhaustion and grief.

There was no time for a proper burial. Kaelen’s body was wrapped in a spare canvas tarp, hastily tied with rope, and loaded into the back of one of the sturdier merchant wagons. The mood was incredibly sombre as the refugees began the arduous process of harnessing the shivering draft horses and packing up the camp. Every movement felt sluggish, every command required immense effort to articulate.

Josh stood near the southern perimeter, holding a cup of lukewarm, bitter tea, watching as Brett and Bhel helped secure the final crates. His left shoulder was heavily bandaged beneath his armour, the movement of his arm stiff and painful.

Perberos approached silently, stopping beside Josh. The elf was staring down at the corpses of the four beasts they had slain the night before. The morning light revealed their horrifying anatomy in full detail: the slick, grey skin, the barbed pads, the unnatural, elongated proportions.

Josh followed the elf’s gaze, waiting to see the familiar sight of the monsters dissolving.

When a creature spawned directly from a portal was killed, its physical form could not be sustained by the ambient mana of this world. Within moments of death, the body would break down, dissolving into a shower of faintly glowing, golden dust, leaving behind only an arcane core, perhaps a valuable material component, and whatever loot the system dictated.

It was a clean, albeit bizarre, process. It was the only reason the battlefields or portals of this world weren't choked high as mountains with rotting monster corpses.

Josh stared at the dead beast at his feet. The green acid saliva had dried crusty on its jaw. Its blood had pooled and frozen black in the mud. It smelled of sulfur, offal, and death.

It was entirely, physically whole.

"They aren't turning," Josh murmured, a cold chill running down his spine that had nothing to do with the winter air.

Brett drifted over, pulling his fur cloak tight. He looked down at the bodies, a deep frown creasing his pale forehead. He crouched, his hands glowing with a faint analytical light as he passed them over the nearest carcass.

"I was wondering about that," Brett noted, his voice devoid of its usual arrogance. "There is no residual dimensional magic clinging to the tissue. None at all."

"It has been over six hours since they were slain," Perberos said softly, his elven eyes narrowing. "If they were portal-spawn, they would be dust on the wind by now."

Josh slowly lowered his cup, his mind racing through the terrifying implications. "If they don't turn to dust... They are made of actual flesh and bone, not mana. They're not from a dungeon break."

"They are native," Perberos confirmed grimly. "Or, more accurately, they were born here. Their ancestors may have spilled from a portal years ago, but these creatures... they gestated in the forests of this world. They have established a breeding population. They belong to the ecosystem now."

Josh felt a sickening wave of despair wash over him. If the monsters were breeding... if they were adapting to the cold, hunting local prey, and giving birth to new generations that were native to the soil...

It wasn't just an invasion anymore.

"They're colonising," Josh whispered, the true scale of the apocalypse finally dawning on him. They weren't just fighting an army; they were fighting a replacement ecosystem. "If this is happening here... it's going to happen everywhere there’s been a portal break."

"Yes," Perberos said simply, turning away from the corpses to look at the long, muddy road stretching ahead of them. "The war just became a fight for the very soil we stand on. We must get these people to the stronghold, Josh. Before the woods spit out anything worse."

Josh nodded, tossing the dregs of his tea into the frost. He adjusted his heavy shield, wincing as the strap pulled against his chemical burns.

"Mount up!" Josh roared, his voice echoing across the bleak morning plains, forcing down the despair. "Wagons roll in five! Keep tight, keep moving!"

The caravan lurched forward, the iron-rimmed wheels grinding against the frozen earth, leaving the native dead behind them as they marched further into the dying world.
