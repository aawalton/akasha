The separation of Planes caused a separation of energy as well. When the reality inhabited by the universe was split into differing regions by the powers that be, certain proclivities in their very integral sorcery came about.

For instance, the cosmos was flooded with mana. This was magic of the soul. Magic of life. It helped beings who were mundane “ascend” through different realms, though in truth, the separation was merely a formal barrier. Something that could be crossed if the requisite conditions and requirements are met.

Similarly, the Great Gods’ Overrealm became filled with divine energy. Where mana worked with one’s soul to cast magic, divine energy used one’s potential. It erodes one’s time. As such, it is tremendously deleterious for anyone to cast, save for those who can claim immortality, like the Great Gods.

Chapter 2: Divine Energy

Of the Great Gods

Norial M.

Ryland was once again confirmed of his success by a notification from the Grand Frame. He was potentially growing a bit too reliant on it.

[ Achievement Recognized: Divine Bulwark

You’ve survived the assault of a Great God, despite being merely Empyrean-ranked.

Rank up from Empyrean [63] to Empyrean [87].

Reward: Divine energy is now easier to channel. ]

Ryland would have considered the rewards if he wasn’t at least somewhat astounded when the Wound Corridor spit him and his companions out onto Rhel once more. He had thought that escaping from a Great God would grant him some reprieve, but it was not to be. His base of operations was under attack.

“Ryland…” Xyke said. “I thought I was going to get a breather here.”

“So did I, Xyke…”

The third Incarnation Ryland had dragged along just stared mutely.

It was hard to tell the exact identity of the mage who had decided to attack Rhel. Of course, Ryland’s suspicions fell on a very specific target—the Flourinus Order. He was quite certain that closer inspection would reveal it to be true, and in fact, [Omniscience] proved his hunch correct.

Having decided that Ryland was trapped elsewhere, the Order had decided to launch a direct assault on Ryland’s base of operations. They had even succeeded to some extent too.

Ryland’s heart wavered for a moment. He shifted [Omniscience]’s gaze, searching all over the battlefield before he could finally breathe out a sigh of relief. Viren was fine. He didn’t even look scratched, besides some minor singeing and burns on his clothes.

It soon became evident how he had survived. Ryland frowned. Devas was here, fighting against the Order mage furiously.

How long had the annoying Turnalian been present on Rhel? Had he been here since before the moment Ryland had sent Viren back? Or had he arrived after this battle had started and Viren had been forced to fend off an Order mage all by himself?

As unlikely as the latter seemed, Ryland had full faith in Viren’s resourcefulness and grit. The boy would have found a way to survive. It was why Ryland had brought him along, after all.

Regardless, this had gone on long enough. Much of the Inscription had been broken and shattered, much of the ground it rested on now lay ruined and pockmarked, and much of the fortification spells Ryland had cast were damaged and in disrepair. Ryland was not about to let things stand any longer.

The Order mage was unleashing devastating waves of lava magic, springing storms of volcanic eruptions from a gargantuan cloud that could easily have swamped a whole city. In response, Devas was relying on his trusty spatial magic to evade and defend.

They seemed to be locked in a stalemate, where Devas found no room to attack and the Order mage couldn’t find a way past her adversary’s defence.

Ryland decided to end the matter then and there, before sending forth the Incarnation he had brought.

One look at Xyke got a nod from the Glyphmaster. One of the Glyphs she still had on the Incarnation himself—a Glyph of Tranquillity, Ryland realized after a moment of observation—now disappeared.

The subdued Incarnation of Growth now straightened. His form blazed with power he refused to keep dampened, the air churning around him until it was growing into a storm. While he didn’t immediately start causing the same devastation as he had when the Order had unleashed him in that unstable state, Ryland could feel how he was capable of such again.

Except, thanks to Xyke’s efforts, all that power was tightly leashed and controlled now.

“Would you kindly take care of the Order mage?” Ryland asked, pointing to the offending enemy.

His fellow Incarnation considered the matter for a moment, almost like he was deliberating. Had stability brought back a measure of intelligence? Had the essence swirling chaotically within him finally returned to a certain level of balance and cohesion?

The Incarnation nodded, then flashed away. Of course, the battle that followed was almost a massacre.

With the Order mage now beset on two sides, one of which was a shrieking Incarnation of all things charging at her with bloody bones and the air itself turning into a storm, she was quickly being overwhelmed. Her magic was counteracted and the Incarnation of Growth began getting in quick, powerful hits that she either barely avoided or actually took some damage from.

Ryland left them to it and quickly rifted to the Inscription with Xyke in tow.

“What a mess,” Xyke lamented. “You’re lucky I’m here. I can get this back in tip-top shape before long.”

“How much divine energy do I pay you with?” Ryland asked.

“You’re also lucky I’m doing this out of the goodness of my heart.”

“Is that so? And it’s not at all because I saved you from the Order and from the Great God they brought along?”

That clearly troubled Xyke. Her complexion paled, her scales turning a little more colourless. “This isn’t just an Inscription for breaching the Overrealm, is it, Ryland?”

Ryland had to admit that it was impressive she had already figured it out after just a cursory look at best. “Is my work that easy to read? Or are you truly that much of a genius?”

“Both? Why not both? I’d like to go with both.”

Ryland shook his head bemusedly. “But yes. There is a reason the Order seems intent on breaking down my magic, yet they don’t seem to have anything against me making my way into the Overrealm. Which proves your supposition correct—the Order wants to stop this Inscription from activating at all costs.”

Xyke’s face darkened a bit, but she began focusing on the Inscription itself. She muttered and mumbled to herself as she pored over the Glyphs, their arrangement and quantity and Tiers, and every other detail about the entire, city-wide magical etching.

Meanwhile, Ryland visited Viren. As he had suspected, the boy had found his way to the interior of one of his fortification spells.

While the defences itself had been battered, both the Inscription within and, more preciously, the boy were fine. He got another resurgent flash of relief at seeing Viren standing more or less safe, even after he had already spotted it thanks to [Omniscience].

But he did frown at the burning smell in the air.

“Sir, you’re back!” Viren said. “And you brought friends!”

He sounded a little too ecstatic despite facing off against a mage who could have wiped him out quite easily. Had he survived her for a time on his own, or had Devas been present all this while?

“I’m glad you’re safe, Viren,” Ryland said. He found himself sniffing slightly again. “Although, I hope you didn’t get burned or anything…”

Viren flushed, which was hard to catch thanks to his already-present excitement. “No, that was me, sir. I, um, set fire to myself and everything inside the fortification spell.” He quickly raised his hands and looked panicked. “Don’t worry! I made sure to immunize the Inscription against fire damage before I did it. I was just trying to trick Bhusine.”

That was a lot to take in, but Ryland ended up focusing on the final detail. “Bhusine, is it? Can you tell me what happened?”

Viren nodded, then quickly explained the story. It was quite odd to calmly listen to his tale while wild, cataclysmic magic blasted the sky apart far above them.

Ryland had been casting barrier spells all over the Inscription as soon as he had landed with Xyke in tow. This ensured that the occasional bolt of lava-lightning or a burning bit of flesh from the Incarnation never struck them. They just bounced off and burned away upon contacting his barriers.

Viren’s tale confirmed Ryland’s fears. He had indeed been forced to deal with Bhusine on his own, a high-ranked mage from the Flourinus Order all by himself. An academy student who was barely Silver-ranked. The idea felt a bit preposterous.

Eventually, Devas had arrived out of nowhere to reinforce Viren’s defence, giving him some much-needed reprieve. Annoying as it was, Ryland would need to thank the Turnalian.

“I’m sorry you had to deal with an Order mage by yourself, Viren,” Ryland said.

Viren shook his head vehemently. “No, no, sir! This is what I came with you for. I intend to help any way I can, and if that means stalling mages from the stupid Order until help arrives, then I’m going to do that as best as I can!”

Ryland slowly smiled. “Your bravery and determination do you great credit, as they always have.”

He was pretty sure he saw Viren flush again.

Ryland looked up. The battle still hadn’t ended, for some reason. Devas had chosen to remain on the defensive, likely because he had no wish to interfere in a battle where an Incarnation was actively causing great chaos. But that just meant Bhusine was able to repel said Incarnation with a storm of living lava.

Magma constructs soared through the air, their wings crackling with sprays of orange lightning and their tails trailing long lines of smoke. Waterfalls of lava were colliding with the fleshy protrusions that the Incarnation kept sending Bhusine’s way, only to get destroyed by the liquid heat she commanded.

He had resorted to causing growth in other mediums in no time. Not just the air itself as well as rippling space, but in the very lava sent towards him. Little explosive bursts blasted out whenever the magma sorceries got anywhere close, which was why Bhusine hadn’t made much headway against her opponent. Once again, they seemed locked in a stalemate, especially since the Incarnation wasn’t being as aggressive as he had been.

Hmm. Had Xyke still kept up some Glyphs to prevent the Incarnation from going all out? Or had a return of stable understanding and intelligence also granted it a great deal more restraint?

Whatever the case, Ryland was glad for the opportunity to act. He could claim another little sub-ingredient. And since it was three-on-one, Bhusine had no choice but to lose.

“[Translocate],” Ryland incanted.

The rift took him right into the midst of their furious battle, where heat washed over him with sizzling fury and the world warped as energies it could barely contain coursed through reality.

“[Absolute Null],” Ryland cast next.

The magma constructs fell apart. The lightning flashed out. The rest of the smoke, flames, and lava all immediately began dissipating. Bhusine was too shocked to move or cast anything in response.

Even the Incarnation’s power was affected. It could no longer twist reality with virulent growth.

“You,” Bhusine growled. “You’re back! How?”

“Perhaps you can ask that of your fellow Order operatives,” Ryland said. “If you ever make it back, that is.”

Bhusine’s eyes widened. She immediately realized that, for her, all hope was lost. Fighting was going to get her nothing here. So, she tried to escape.

“[Dimensional Lock],” Ryland incanted.

Bhusine hadn’t uttered any spell, but he had sensed the shift in her manasoul anyway. He had caught how she intended to depart, how the signature of her mana had started growing faint. If Ryland’s eyes weren’t mistaken, then he had also caught how her form had started glowing too.

None of which mattered now because she was trapped. Caught by Ryland’s spell. Unable to escape what was about to happen next.

“I don’t approve of attacking little children,” Ryland said, his eyes holding Bhusine’s own. Since Bhusine was caught, Ryland took his time enunciating each and every spell. “[Endless Evolution]. [Everlife Spectre]. [Soul Stone].”

Ryland’s arm shifted into the ghostly skeletal version he used often, which he then stabbed into Bhusine’s torso. She hadn’t been able to move a single muscle since [Dimensional Lock] had gone into effect, which made the process very easy.

As soon as Ryland had clutched her manasoul, he began to feel the sheer fieriness of it. It felt like holding liquid fire, like Ryland was touching lava itself.

Then he ripped her manasoul free from her body.

She couldn’t even scream. The light simply went out of her still eyes, and the ferociously rippling essence of her very being immediately began compressing and solidifying, until it was finally a condensed pearl that glowed as radiant as the moon.

Ryland didn’t wait for a single second. He [Translocated] away, appearing right on top of his rifts and immediately slapping the stone onto the renewed Inscriptions Xyke had created.

She turned around to face him with a curious expression. “Don’t think I didn’t catch what happened.”

Ryland never took his eyes off the earth, where the pearl was now embedded into the Inscription, almost like it had turned into quicksand before partially swallowing the stone. Light bloomed, runnels of gleaming magic running along the carvings and etchings. “I hope you did, for that is the fate of the rest of the Order. Whenever I get to them, that is.”

“Disturbing,” Xyke said bluntly. “But far from the worst thing you’ve ever done, Ryland.”

He was somewhat glad she had said it before Devas and Viren arrived, the latter of whom would probably have exploded with questions at that.

“Is it done, sir?” Viren asked. “I can still see her high above, but everything’s weirdly calm now.”

A sad day indeed, when any sort of calm was called weird.

“Don’t worry,” Ryland said. “She’s no longer a threat. In fact, she has been a blessing in disguise. I’ve completed another step of the ritual Inscription, which is excellent.”

“And what about…”

Viren didn’t finish his question, but Ryland got what he meant. However, it was Xyke who responded.

“I’ve resumed my Glyphic control over the Incarnation,” she said. “It’s distasteful and I abhor having any sort of dominion over any living creature, but for the safety of everyone present, I’ve been forced to keep it up. Don’t worry. The Incarnation of Growth will no longer present any danger.”

Viren’s eyes widened at that. Right. He still didn’t know who Xyke was, and the revelation that she could casually stop Incarnations was no doubt starting to make him wonder.

So, Ryland performed some quick introductions and got everyone getting along.

“Master’s endeavours always leave me wide-eyed and stupefied,” Devas said. “I would be honoured if Master Ryland allows this humble Devas to remain here till Master’s great work comes to fruition.”

“Fine, fine,” Ryland said. “I do need help.”

“You do.” Xyke glanced once or twice at Devas, giving him a quick searching look, before turning back to Ryland. “This isn’t the last of them. There will be more Order mages coming here to tear down your Inscription and this entire planet you’ve overtaken.”

Ryland took a deep breath. His ritual ingredient gathering was almost complete. Now, he just needed to ensure the actual breaching of the Overrealm went without a hitch.
