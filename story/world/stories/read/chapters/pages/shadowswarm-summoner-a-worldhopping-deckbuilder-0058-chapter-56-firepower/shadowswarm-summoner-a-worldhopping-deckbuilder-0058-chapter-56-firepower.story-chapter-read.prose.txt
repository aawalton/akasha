Chapter 56: Firepower

Raeva was a little annoyed with Ambrose, and not for the usual fun reasons.

Upon arriving at the Hermanic Quarter, they got out of the walkway and scanned the area for a few minutes. And then, rather than go to a shop, Ambrose had instead taken them to get food. And no one other than her blinked at the suggestion. She’d argued against it for a couple of minutes before Mulch told her it was actually a good idea.

And then they went to a place with outdoor seating, largely on account of their guide being non-human. They ordered food, and had to part with a couple of common cards for it.

“You going to tell us your reasoning now, or is it just so you can eat something without an exoskeleton?” Raeva demanded. Despite being angry at him, she wasn’t too proud to avoid ordering a bowl of vanilla ice cream. It was hot and stuffy there, and the sweet, cold treat helped cool her temper a bit.

“I can’t have multiple reasons for doing things?” he scoffed. “We’ve got several hours, and we’re supposed to be seasoned travelers here. Take a moment and act like it. We just got in, we’re tired, we want a snack and to rest our feet.”

“We’ve been on a train for two hours,” Luthor pointed out.

“Yes, and then we had to climb a million steps,” Ambrose shot back. “I won’t deny that I wanted real food, but it’s about the appearance. I guarantee you, someone or multiple someones are watching, especially after giving the gate guards a spoonful of shit. Relax, enjoy yourself. We’ve got time.”

“Why didn’t you say so in the first place?” Raeva asked. “I wouldn’t have complained if you had.”

“Because this is more fun. You look good when you want to kick my ass.” Ambrose lifted his beer to his mouth and waggled his eyebrows at her.

Everyone but him sighed. Most knew better than to keep poking. Most.

“Why do you all let him be such a jerk?” Jack asked, unable to keep the frustration out of his voice.

“What’s that, Junior?” Ambrose asked. “You want to be thrown off the side of the city?”

“Ambrose,” Luthor warned. He held out a hand to keep Jack from getting up. “Stop antagonizing him when he’s mostly behaving.”

He started to say something, but Raeva slapped her hand on the table, making her ice cream bounce and the spoon clatter to the ground. He wisely shut up after that.

“No, Jack has a point,” she said. “There’s a time and a place, and this is neither. Don’t act pissy to us just because you don’t like being afraid.”

Oh sweet, sweet satisfaction. His face turned a deep red, and his eyes blazed with indignation. But again, Luthor stepped in.

“Enough. Finish your snacks, and then we’ll get to shopping.”

Mulch, who had been uncharacteristically quiet during this, finally spoke. “I don’t know the shops here too well, mind you. Most of what I know’s by reputation only. Do you wanna start small, work our way up, or go right for the best they got?”

Raeva knew the answer instantly, even if she didn’t entirely care for it. “We go for the biggest and best. If only so we can potentially be in a good position if things go sour. Once we’re set up, we’ll start picking at the smaller spells. It’s not enough to be able to use combat cards, you need to work up to having the deck space and Affinity Levels to make use of better spells. If we’re going to help, you need mostly level 1 and 2 spells.”

The molekin looked around to see if anyone overheard, but they were the only ones sitting around the table. It was funny; it was likely his presence that kept people away to begin with. “And some non-combat cards that might be useful for breakin’ things. We’ve been resistin’ without being able to fight directly, and it’s worked out well.”

Ambrose finished eating his steak sandwich and drained his beer. “That’s a good point. Well, I’m no longer in danger of passing out due to lack of food, so why don’t we get moving?”

Raeva somehow resisted the urge to throw the melted remains of her ice cream at his face.

The first place they tried was a clean, well-lit shop that screamed “genuinely legitimate”. Their selection wasn’t bad so much as basic. The greatest thing they had to offer was a level 6 Shadow card that Ambrose spent a few minutes drooling over.

[Twist the Knife: Rare. You’ve already hurt them, why not make it worse?

Spell Effect: Target takes damage equal to the amount of damage done by the caster in the last 30 seconds. 5 Mana.]

“You can’t even use that yet, and it’s expensive as hell,” Raeva complained. “We’re not wasting our limited resources on a vanity card. Especially when we don’t know what will happen to it with…You know. Your unique problem.”

That had gotten under his skin, but he agreed and they moved on.

The next shop was the opposite, dim and dirty, with a proprietor that kept glancing at the door, like he expected but didn’t want company. Ambrose seemed a lot more comfortable there, but Raeva and Luthor shared a look and kept their eyes open for trouble. The problem with this stop was that the stock was basically all commons and uncommons and noncombat cards, which didn’t get them closer to their goal, but they vowed to come back later for Mulch.

“You said you knew some places by reputation,” Raeva said to the molekin after they left that place. “What’s the place with the best prestige?”

Mulch kicked the metal floor, clunking softly. “That’d prolly be Javon’s Emporium. Ain’t no way they’re gonna let me in, though. And I don’t think that leaving me outside with the kid is a good idea either.”

Luthor spoke up, “I’ll stay outside with you and Jack. Ambrose and Raeva can go inside. They work well together, and I trust them to get it done.”

Just as he likely intended, it shut them all up and forced the two of them to look at each other.

“I can behave if you can,” she said, a sardonic smile on her face. “Probably better, in fact.”

“Probably,” he agreed, “but I guess this means I have to play along and try to prove you wrong.”

“When all of this is over, you two need to get a damned room,” said Luthor with a fond but tired sigh. He emptied his pockets of all his spare cards, splitting it up between the two of them.

Javon’s Emporium was exactly as ostentatious as it sounded. Unlike a lot of the other vendors, this one had guards inside and out, as well as no competition for at least a hundred yards in any direction. It was the only card shop in its square, and was easily the largest building on this level, as far as either of them could see. The color changing letters of the sign were unintelligible at first, but then their translation cards kicked in and they became the Amaric alphabet before their eyes.

Raeva almost expected to be searched, but as soon as they entered the Emporium, a weird heaviness settled over her mind. She reached out for her [Fiery Hatchling], but couldn’t call her out at all. She looked at Ambrose, panicked for a second, but he shook his head.

“I don’t think we can use most of our cards while in here,” he said. “And I imagine that’s common among the Empire. It’s probably an Edict of some kind, bound to the location. I can’t imagine how difficult it must be to pull off.”

“Be impressed later,” said Raeva, looking around at the countless glass cases stacked together, lining each wall, the front counter with still more cards behind it. They were far from the only customers in there, but the place was big enough that crowding it would be difficult. “After we get what we need, preferably.”

Javon’s Emporium presented an entirely new challenge neither of them were prepared for: too many good options. Unlike the other places they checked, which tended to top out at Affinity Level 6 or so, they went all the way up to Level 9 spells, for those who were approaching true mastery. It didn’t matter that they were years away from even considering such cards, they both had a good look.

Raeva found one she immediately wished she was powerful enough to use.

[Dragon’s Breath: Rare. Harness the power of the primordial wyrm’s greatest weapon.

Spell Effect: The caster breathes a gout of flame in a cone in front of them. Everyone damaged by the flame discards a card. If a card would be deactivated this round, cast it for free. 7 Mana]

Aside from being powerful, it would synergize well with Ambrose’s favored strategy, and…Well, Raeva liked dragons. The problem was it was Affinity Level 7, and in one of the more secure looking cases.

She loved working with fire, but Stonskarr had a specialty, and the more strategic part of her knew it would be smart to lean into it, and not rely on what she was used to. Luckily, she found exactly what she needed, and at her Level too.

[Dirt Drill: Uncommon Hybrid Card. Pierce the earth and break it.

Spell Effect: Caster fires a magic bolt in up to a twenty foot line. Any dirt or stone is destroyed and displaced violently to the sides. 3 Mana/4 charges per hour]

This was the kind of thing Mulch wanted for his people, and it was the first card she took for herself. Ideally, they’d leave Stonskarr by tomorrow. But just in case…She paired it with a simple creature she could see being effective.

[Dust Devil: Uncommon. Hybrid Earth/Wind Elemental. A semi-intelligent, definitely malicious gust of wind, out to ruin days and clean clothes.

Special: Nuisance. All spells cast in this creature’s aura gain Gamble 3. 4 Mana]

Raeva made it a goal to grab two Level 3 or 4 cards for each person, and see what Ambrose came up with later. Unlike most shops, one didn’t take the cards up to the counter and just pay for them. Instead, there were little pads of paper and tiny pencils, and she quickly made a list of ten cards. And then, seeing one final card, she added it to the list too, trying not to smile when they met up again.

“How many are on your list?” Raeva asked, holding out her pad.

They traded lists, and a second later laughed.

“Maybe Luthor was right,” said Ambrose, shaking his head at the list. “Maybe we can be trusted with this after all.”

Over half their lists were identical. There were hundreds of cards around the shop, but they’d picked seven of the same cards, which made it a lot easier. Until he pointed at the bottom choice.

“What’s [Devil Form]?” he asked, a hint of excitement creeping in.

“Something perfect for you, to make up for your weakness in battle,” she said cheerfully. “That one’s on me. A gift to you, to make you a little less useless outside of your imps.”

“Thanks,” said Ambrose, lips twitching. Thinking for a second, he offered up his arm. Raeva took it. They got in line behind the other six parties ahead of them and waited.

And waited.

After fifteen minutes, only one of the parties had been served. There was only one clerk, a pale, thin man in his mid-sixties with wild white hair, and Raeva suspected he may have been the owner of the place. He had the smug smile of someone who was used to being important and making others wait on him. Or maybe she was just impatient.

Ten more minutes, and another person left with a cloth bag full of their purchases. They stepped up a couple more feet, mindful of the six or seven feet between each party, given out of courtesy.

“Think Luthor and the others are getting worried?” Raeva asked under her breath, watching the proprietor waved someone away. They left, clearly disappointed. Only one more party ahead of them.

“Probably a little,” Ambrose answered. “He’s not the type to ever relax when there’s fretting to be done. It wouldn’t surprise me if he found worrying about things to be relaxing.”

She chuckled, shaking her head. “He’s not that bad. He’s serious, but he knows how to have fun too.”

“Oh yes,” said Ambrose, schooling his face into the most dull, bored expression possible. “Fun means reading old history books and drilling for four hours a day. Look out, I’m a wild child.”

She swatted his shoulder. “Stop that. Someone has to be the boring, unfun one. Ancestors know it’s not going to be me, and it’s not going to be you either. Who else does that leave? You want Little Ms. Nervous, or my soft, sappy brother?”

Ambrose faux-shuddered. “Okay, point made. I’ll appreciate him and all his hard work, like keeping our guide and prisoner safe.”

Just as Raeva was about to remind him to be less of a bastard to the kid, the last person finished and left, empty-handed. That didn’t bode well for her.

The two of them strode up to the counter, and set their pads down. “Good evening!” she said pleasantly. “How’s your day going so far, Mr. Javon?”

“Hmph. No, I think not. Next customers, please.”
