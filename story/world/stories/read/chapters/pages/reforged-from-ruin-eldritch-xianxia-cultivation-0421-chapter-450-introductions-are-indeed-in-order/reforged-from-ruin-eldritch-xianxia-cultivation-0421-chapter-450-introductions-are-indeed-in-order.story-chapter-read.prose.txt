Power goes hand in hand with disregard.

That’s not the same as saying it’s evil. It doesn’t cause evil. Power is no more evil than art or water– it just is. But there are many things we do because we must, and power above most things allows us to disregard them.

Salting and curing meats is something one does to compensate for scarcity. If there is no scarcity, it can be disregarded. To some, people are much the same as salt. Useful when needed, absolutely, but something simple to toss aside when they aren’t. Rules are things that some listen to for self interest, to protect themselves from reprisal or consequence, to gain access to societal goods, and when those things can be achieved in another way, they are eager to do so.

I have heard it said that power does not corrupt, but it does reveal. I agree, but it’s missing a piece. It also refines. It heightens what you already are, unburdened by the things another you would be forced to carry. The powerful disregard not due to villainy, not due to loathing, not even due to disdain– they disregard because it’s less effort, and they have something they can replace that care with. It just so happens that people are in just about everything, and so disregard for anything comes with consequences of their own.

Consequences that power often allows one to disregard in turn.

Hand in hand.

It interests me to note that to most, this does not disincentivise the pursuit of power. Quite the opposite, actually.

What a pity that without burdens we find ourselves so often vile. What a pity that the ability to be underburdened often comes most easily to those who benefit from it the least.

Raika’s smugness feels pretty well smothered under all the boring.

It’s annoying to have to listen to all their little voices.

In some ways they’re still interesting, but honestly, her interest keeps dwindling the longer they go unharmed. It’s hard to pay attention to so many things that feel like they don’t matter at all.

Either her acolytes will find a way to bring her into the screaming un-sun above, or she’ll do it herself. Which comes first is just a matter of circumstance. The purpose of their expedition was to get her here undetected, and now that it’s happened, there’s so much that’s so much more interesting to Raika than the words of little things that don’t particularly matter.

There’s the screaming sun, obviously. While she might not remember the exact events surrounding it, it’s easy enough to recognize that it’s a part of Her, and likely one of the stronger ones. Other acolytes are approaching other sites, but none of them are quite as loud as this one, though others tend to mirror it in terms of scope and scale. Enough safeguards have been eliminated to know that the Watchful Fields Sect was close to Her before her Death, and it’s easier still to track the smell of older pieces that have emerged from here. Blood and bone and other bits have left the barest hints of their presences, even months gone, smelling less of Raika and more of the woman that was the Raika before her.

This is very likely where she was unmade.

Then there’s the beast tide, coming in at a close second for interest. Even from miles and miles away and behind a barrier, they are fascinating. Shapes and ideas that have no business intermingling or being organized as they are, somehow managing to exist separately from what existence conventionally allows. Like puzzle-pieces built into works of art that move and become more complex and artistic as they continue through time. Most of the swarm she can taste isn’t all that special, but all at once, in such numbers as to flow like water across the horizon? Delicious.

And there are particular bites in that oncoming tide that she can’t help but salivate about. Things whose presence in existence makes the soup of all-that-is taste different, even with their proper scents and flavors blocked by the arcane defenses she’s kept under. Things whose presence rivals the greatest of the cultivators behind the barrier, and whose flavors rival the screaming sun for complexity and uniqueness.

Then there’s the absence of the Fengs. The site is entirely public as far as she knows, and comparative to the weapons she knows they can wield, its defences should be negligible to the full force of the emerald legion. And yet, it’s been untouched by them. No part of her detects that evergreen poison anywhere near the site.

Why?

She’s not sure. Some political thing, maybe, or saving the best for last, or some entirely unknowable reason she has no chance of predicting.

But it’s interesting.

The voices of people around her are not.

They’re talking about jobs, about places to be, jockeying for power and authority, being escorted here and there, waiting and explaining and babbling. Sometimes there’s even small talk and pleasantries, as disgusting as they are wasteful.

On some level she likes helping people. She can recognize that much about herself, in spite of how little she seems to like people. But helping can be inspiring and fun and engaging.

Except she’s already helped these people, and they’ve already helped her, and she likes other things too. She might like other things more than helping people.

Actually, that’s not a possibility. It’s just true.

Some part of her feels uncomfortable about that, but there’s no safeguards in that direction and she’s too busy to waste time examining that.
