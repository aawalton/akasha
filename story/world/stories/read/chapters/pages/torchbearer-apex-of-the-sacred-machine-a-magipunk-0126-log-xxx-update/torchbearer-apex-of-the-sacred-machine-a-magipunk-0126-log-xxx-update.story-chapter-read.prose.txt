Hey everyone, this is that update that I promised!

As I'm writing this, I'm sitting in a small hotel room in Japan, on the second to last day of my vacation. I'll be flying back home on Sunday, which I am both looking forward to, and regretful about at the same time. I wish I could have stayed longer, and maybe seen Osaka as well, but to be honest, after 2.5 weks of travel, I'm ready to sleep in my own bed again.

It's not just living out of a suitacse that's bothering me, but not being able to speak the language. I always feel as though I really shouldn't inconvenience anyone, and so I have perfected both my use of google translate, and pronounciation of "gomenasai".

Did take some damn cool pics, though, and had an absolute blast meeting John Bierce (Mage Errant) and Mike We (Lord of Goblins), who happened to be in Tokyo at the same time as I. What are the odds of three litRPG authors deciding to travel to Japan on the same weekend, each on their own volition? One of the craziest coincidences of my life, so far!

Now, I know you're all here for writing updates, so I shouldn't e beating around the bush much longer.

my backlog is now about 39.000 words big, which is about 35% through the next Arc. There is a bunch of stuff I need to fix, numbers and readouts to add, and maybe even flashbacks to insert, but I am comfortable with what I have written, and it works. To put the amount of words into sperspective, I have about 12 scenes written, and there are about 20 left to write until I can call it a wrap. These 20 scenes have mostly been plotted out already, however, down to short one-liners for each paragraph.For my speed, I have written about 12K in March, up from 5K in February, and 10K in January. I hope that I will be able to kep up a healthy routine when I get home, but reality will probably going to be different. I will ahve to catch up on about a month of work, after all, and also several social responsibilities I shouldn't neglect.

What does that all mean?

Well, I'm still chugging along, for starters. I have a pretty good idea how this arc will end, and even a good end point for the series, so far, that will hopefully not disappoint anyone too much. In fact, I think it's a good ending, but I can't talk about it too much without risk of spoilers. The one thing I want to say, though, is that while the end point will mark the end of me writing Torchbearer for now, and provide some closure, it will leave things open enough to allow me to pick up the series again at some point in the future. Even cutting over 9 arcs, there's still so much to tell. I honestly underestimated just how much work it would take to write all those stories, especially while working a full time job, as well as an agent, a podcast host, and another job I can't talk about. (You'll hear more about it in the summer, and you'll like it, I promise!)

Speaking of agent stuff!I recently had the great honor of David Niemitz hiring me to represent him. If you haven't read Guild Mage, I strongly urge you to give it a try. I am not joking when I say that I wish I could write like David does. It's concise, gripping, and engaging. The plot never feels rushed, nor does it feel like a lull. Essentially, he's writing a trad-publishable novel, but as a serial. It's insane. Absolutely, mind-boggingly insane.We have sold his rights to Mango Media and Podium, in a very nice deal. Manifest Fantasy in and of itself is already great. It's literally Stargate SG-1 meets Dungeons and Dragons. But with Brook Aspden's help, I believe we can turn it from great to extraordinary. It hit Rank 25 on RR before the downvote faires got a whiff of it, and it absolutely deserves it.
