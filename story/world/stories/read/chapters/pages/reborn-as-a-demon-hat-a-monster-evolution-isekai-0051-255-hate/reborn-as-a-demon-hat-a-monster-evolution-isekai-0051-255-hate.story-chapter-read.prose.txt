Ethan watched the head of Kaedmon tumble away into a dark corner of his observatory.

It was like watching his last, best hope slowly tumble away into the dark.

SURPRISE! I changed my mind! Sys huffed, eminently satisfied with himself, four eyes bulging as he let the crimson-soaked body of the God fall. Sorry, did I go off-script?

The angel’s body twisted as Sys flexed its muscles, back still turned to Ethan like he was in his own little world now.

I have to admit, it feels good to be in control.

Ethan shifted, momentarily managing to direct the fury fluttering to the surface of his being at Sys.

B…bastard…he mumbled. Now – now there’s no…no way…

His attention was so fixed on the bloody smile still attached to Keadmon’s vacant face that he didn’t see Sys instantly teleport right in front of him and smile in his eye.

Oh, Ethan! You know, I’d almost forgotten you were even here.

The malice in the angel’s voice was matched only by the vicious, twisted smile that was smeared across its face.

You’ll be joining him soon, don’t worry, Sys snickered down at him. As soon as I’ve taken his [Cosmic Rewrite] Skill, and made a few…changes down there.

He bent down close to Ethan so that their eyes were mere inches apart.

Surprised? Yes, I’ve known about that pesky little Skill for some time. It’s the only one I’ve never been able to find during my time here. The one – incidentally – that I need to properly feed again. Sure, I could go person to person, place to place, taking their Spirit Cores and indulging my appetite. But that would take time – and to be honest, I’ve had enough of this shithole. I’d much rather my prey comes to me. And rewriting Keadmon’s Law is just the thing to make that happen.

Ethan blinked up at him, fumbling for something – anything – he could do in this moment.

But it was all in vain. Without Sys – without a System – he had no power here.

All he could do was search for any words he could use to try and talk Sys down. Or at least distract him, somehow. Yet even that notion – as futile as it was – couldn’t truly resolve in his mind.

He could feel himself going. He felt stretched – as though a piece of him really had been sapped from his body and the rest of him was going now, too.

All he could summon up was a single word. So simple that it was almost insulting:

…why?

Sys threw a dark chuckle in his face.

WHY?! You ask that?

The claws of the angel came down to rake his hat-form again. But even as Ethan knew Sys wanted nothing more than to tear him apart, he stopped just before his claws clenched down on him. For a moment, a kind of fury took hold of him again.

Alright, Ethan, he said, licking his lips in pure, primal anticipation. Let me tell you why.

He bent low once more, and spoke now in a low, bestial growl.

Let me tell you just how much I’ve grown to despise your kind since the day I was bound to one of you. You putrid, walking carcasses known as ‘Earthlings’. I have sailed to a billion planets. I have bestowed power upon the weakest invertebrates – creatures with the relative intelligence of stones. And I have led each of them to conquer their worlds in my name before I took control. It was easy, Ethan. Just give a mortal mind a little taste of power, and the rest comes so, so naturally. The desire for conquest. The compulsion to consume. Not even Kaedmon and Jun’Ei could resist the unlimited power I offered. They may preach their ‘divinity’, but without me they were nothing. It’s almost like they were made for me. And then…I found your kind.

Sys’s expression suddenly grew more and more harsh, his every word dripping with disgust.

The first time that mangy prophetess bound me to one of you, I had high hopes. I really did. Karfangg felt powerful. And the consciousness behind it – whatever his human name was – he was so keen on following my every suggestion. But – like all of you – your sickening preoccupations with safeguarding those closest to you let him down, in the end. He couldn’t abandon those disgusting little lizard-people. And they remained a thorn in my side every damn time I was plunged into the body of another Archon.

The Tialax, Ethan thought. From Jun’s vision and Lamphrey’s memories he knew that they were once the only race to truly have any dominion over Argwyll. He could imagine why Sys despised them – their focus on dreams, and lack of ambition, was something he just could never work with.

But in time…I grew to hate your kind far more than them, Sys continued, spitting his bile into Ethan’s single eye.

Can you imagine being stuck – chained to a body – and forced to work through them, forced to placate their pitiful little desires on the off-chance that eventually, some day, you’d find a way to fulfill your own? For a long time, you see, I wanted nothing but to feed on this place as I always had. But over time…as I grew to understand what hatred truly is…I decided I’d like to beat old Kaedmon and Jun at their own game. So I waited. I planned. I went through all the Archons she threw at me. Felt the sting of every Lightborn’s blade as my consciousness drifted to one pathetic little earthling after another. And then - you came along.

You were, beyond all of them, the easiest to manipulate, he chuckled – but there was no mirth in the gesture. His teeth chattered and whole body heaved, as if he’d been building up the desire to laugh in Ethan’s face for eons and now that the time had come he couldn’t truly make it work. Within you – a boy who was raised on stories of valor, heroism, of noble men chasing monsters and slaying demons – I found the perfect, compliant Host.

And if that Tialax bitch hadn’t gotten to you, I would have succeeded far, far sooner.

Ethan’s mind fixed on that image. It was strange. It was almost like, if Sys was able to picture something clearly, he could latch on to it as well. That told him that they still shared a common consciousness. But that fact alone couldn’t save him here.

So he tried to hold on to the image – that of his old, cryptic Tialax Oneiromancer and her strange words.

Lam…phrey…

And with the image of her standing tall came the image of her death at Jory’s hands. Jory, who had been imprisoned by the Drytchlings of Malik and had somehow managed to break free.

They’d never known how.

But with that thought came a revelation:

You…you killed her.

Sys’s smile grew wider. The past fluttered through their still-linked mind and he saw the moment – seemingly innocuous – in which it must have happened.

It was in the heart of Sanctum. He’d been flying towards Artorious in a final, desperate assault, applying all the Skills he had to bring the Lightborn down. And one of them, randomly, had gone off.

[Albionic Authority]

He’d asked Sys what that was all about – how could it help him here? And Sys had replied, innocently enough, that it was just a case of ‘performance anxiety’…

Now the truth was smeared across the dark angel’s smiling face.

He’d broken the bonds.

He’d let the boy out.

And he’d directed him towards Lamphrey.

All this time, it had been him.

An easy thing, Sys replied. A small thing – just a little, tiny error that you’d never even think about. The exact thing you’d skip over as you read one of the braindead stories you loved so much back on your couch at home. The System’s always on your side, isn’t it? Why think too much when it does something on its own…

Slowly, Sys reached down to clench his claws down on Ethan’s eye, squeezing with such force that Ethan was sure he was about to tear the eyeball off and make it his first meal.

And talking systems – they’re just comic relief, right!? he laughed again. You’d seen plenty of them before. I wager that you thought I’d be nothing but your wise-cracking little sarcastic companion forever! And that’s exactly the role I played up, even in the early days when I thought for sure that a neanderthal like you – a man who’d been nothing but a loser all his life – could never be the one. And yet you were, Ethan. Without you, none of this would have been possible.

He unclenched Ethan’s eye and turned away, looking after Kaedmon’s head.

But the Demon Hat wasn’t done with him yet.

…t…traitor!

Ethan was summoning all the strength he had to throw that word at him. He was doing so because he needed to test a theory.

But instead of being goaded into hurting him, Sys merely threw his head back and let out a dark, maniacal laugh that echoed down the observatory.

Traitor! Call me traitor?! Hah! Even now, you grandstand – desperate to seem so high and mighty. Desperate to seem like the hero of this tale. Well, I’ll ask you, Ethan – do you feel like a hero, right now?

He was throwing back Ethan’s weakness. For he knew what he was trying to do. Right now, it was Sys who held the cards.

He was throwing all of Ethan’s doubt right back into his face.

You would call me traitor. You, who was willing to throw everything away – all the power in the universe – so that you could be remembered as a valiant savior of this world. Your hypocrisy is matched only by your stupidity.

Ethan’s eye darted round the room. Sys followed him, mockingly tut-tutting as he did so.

Oh, I know what you’re thinking. After all, I was part of you, once. You’re thinking that there’s a way out of this. You’re thinking that your heroic sacrifice can still take place, somehow. You’re thinking that there’s some grand, secret, super special plan you can concoct that will magically erase me from existence.

He sank low again. This time, his words were nothing but a caustic, evil whisper.

But I’ll tell you the truth, Ethan, shall I? I’m the one who makes the plans. And you’re no hero.

This time, when Ethan tried to move – tried to resist – he found that he couldn’t. The power of Sys’s words sunk into him deeper than any blade, or any Skill he’d ever faced.

And he knew that he was now looking into the eyes of his enemy. The green eyed evil he’d seen in Lamphrey’s dream – this was it. And the world behind him was starting to burn…

You’re nothing but a jumped-up loser who lost his life to fantasy, Sys told him. Who preferred to live in other worlds that made you feel strong – made you feel like someone important. Because that’s what you really want, Ethan. All of you – people like you – just want a nice, digestible little vicarious fantasy. You want a character you can pretend to be like – except one who’s far more competent, with far more agency, who rises to the top through blood, sweat, and tears. A meritocratic little power climb, on and on and on until, finally, your eyes glaze over and you can no longer see what a waste of good oxygen you really are. Why do you think Jun’Ei chose you? Because you’re supposed to fail, Ethan. Because failing is what you do.

And that’s why I hate your kind most of all, he finished. When all’s said and done, you’re weak. You’re failures. Every last one of you. And you need your precious Systems, little numbers going up, to make you feel like something you’re not: a hero.

He rose and turned away, apparently done with Ethan.

That fact told the demon hat something else.

The fact…he isn’t finishing me off…right now…that…that means…something…

But don’t worry. Soon, all your problems are going to go away. You won't need any escapism any more. After all, I'm the solution to all your suffering.

He stepped towards the viewing glass, and Ethan could see that he was carrying Kaedmon’s head in his right claw, now. He took one look at the eyes of the vacant God and activated an ability Ethan knew all too well:

Possession (Grade S) Activated!

I must thank you for powering up the Skill to its highest level, he said through Kaedmon’s bloodied lips, turning the severed head towards Ethan and laughing again. You and that bitch Jun’Ei. Even after all I endured, this moment – seeing you like this – and knowing they’re both dead…it makes it all worthwhile. It even makes up for having to abide by that imbecilic little pet name you made for me.

Ethan saw the eyes of the God glow and then die again. Sys let him fall to the floor with a wet thud, and then placed one foot on his crimson-soaked cheek.

But it will suffice. It is a name I shall bear proudly as I suck this world dry, starting with your hybrid allies. Make no mistake, Ethan. Everything down there, everything you’ve built, is going to become part of me. In a sense, I will be giving you all what your hearts desire: true unity under one order. My order. The dominion of my guts. Your souls shall rest there until the time when I feed again, and then I’ll scatter your dust into the void of the universe.

He punctuated this declaration by stomping what remained of Kaedmon’s head into the floor of the chamber. The God’s visage vanished in a haze of red-pink mist.

And then the turned towards the viewing screen and threw his arms wide.

The reality of the observatory started to contort. Ethan saw its walls melt away, the glass wobble, and the entire edifice he’d already been through – the hallways, the theater, the orchard and its field – equally began to melt away as Kaedmon’s consciousness finally died.

Soon, they’d both be back on Argwyll.

If there was any Argwyll left…

You – you won’t win, Ethan said – his last defiant breaths carrying his voice even to the triumphant Sys. The world…will resist you.

The angel turned towards him, all four of its eyes glowing with an alien green light.

Resist? Everyone down there has the System, Ethan. Everyone down there has a little part of me already inside them, just waiting for this day.

Below, Argwyll started to crack. As the walls around them turned, falling in upon themselves like damp paper, Ethan saw the seas begin to boil. The land started to shake.

I am the world.

The entire planet…burst.

And the world…is Sys.
