The earth, where it wasn't covered by the red-and-yellow grass, was rough like a mountainside hammered and flattened on Hadúr's godly anvil. Fractures wrinkled the ground, some flowing together to form tiny gullies that sneaked in and out of grassy patches, completely disappearing toward the lush east. No winds blew above the plains. The only movement was the slow herd of clouds drifting across the sky, edges lit up by the halo of Warmlight hiding behind them.

Kalsin crouched atop the earthen wall, throat dry and eyes roving the horizon. To his left crouched a man who'd introduced himself as Rolce; thin hair, good smile, a little jumpy. To Kalsin’s right was Ringtail, with Shoreline to her right. The base point of their shield rested in front of them like an extension of the barricade they perched upon, jutting out above the ground. Defenders stood as far as the eye could see, but the wall was left unattended at several spots like gaps in a row of neglected teeth, and it was for the first time Kalsin had truly realized how much the Old lacked in numbers.

"Shit," Ringtail said, doing the same calculations.

"There's no chance we can prevent a breach," Kalsin said.

"It's a matter of when, rather than a matter of if," Rolce said. Like everyone else around them, he was an older boy proper. When he heard the three of them were recruits caught away from their group, he just shrugged and said it was no big deal. People moved about the Old, and they defended where they could. Happened all the time. "I remember my first time, I remember well, and they're lying if anyone says they don't. Was caught apart from my mates, too. But I won't bore you with the minutiae. I suggest you leave your expectations in the dust. There's no guarantees when it comes to fighting these cannibals. You gotta have eyes front, sides, and back." His lips twitched as he paused. "That, and don't mind the birds. Just cut the ropes."

And so they waited, eyes in the distance, breaths loud and some hitched with nerves. Kalsin's sweaty clothes stuck to his skin under his chainmail, and there was an itch below his vambrace that he couldn't scratch no matter how much he squirmed. He'd gotten used to wearing light armor most of the day, but now, in the face of mounting tension, all these little annoyances assaulted him at once. He checked his forearm again. The straps of his shield were drawn tight.

Then he looked up—and saw the lonely herald not a hundred paces away.

She stood still and alone like a bloodied willow tree, her red curtain of hair stirred softly by a breeze that didn't exist. Her arms hung limp at her sides, body angled toward the east, but her head watched him as surely as the hard earth beneath his feet. The patterns of her dress couldn't be discerned from such a distance, but Kalsin knew what they depicted anyways.

Kalsin knew the others couldn't see her. He knew he was crazy—but maybe it was a useful kind of insanity. Somehow, some way, the red girl had a purpose that he simply couldn’t afford to ignore anymore.

She was a herald of death.

She had only appeared to him when standing before a life-threatening crossroads. In Atlas’ office before his sentencing to become a soldier. At his poisoned bedroll. And now—a calm sentinel prophesying the slaughter at hand.

Kalsin waited, watching the red girl standing in the lonely tundras. The defenders waited, too, and they didn't need a ghost to know that things of savagery were on their way.

It was a man, voice scratchy as he called out, who first spotted them. Kalsin squinted in the distance and saw nothing, but as he focused on the reddish expanse in front of him, there was an almost invisible impression of movement. It was subtle—tiny spots of grass bowing and bending as if isolated streaks of wind were rushing toward the west. With the air standing completely still, however, that would have been impossible.

The movement reached the edges of the red sea where grass had been cut to leave patches of dark earth, and the shapes that had blended in with their surroundings now gained human form.

Wild strands of red-and-yellow robes rippled behind them as the Veiled poured from the east in the dozens, running in a low stride that supplied more endurance than speed. Though it covered them from head to toe, the red cloth worn close to their skin didn't appear baggy or cumbersome; instead it was fastened to their bodies with hardened leather straps dyed a color of scorched mahogany, most of which looped around in doubles from shoulder to waist, like empty belts. The material around their head was lighter in texture, tightly woven around the neck, face, and forehead, only leaving the eyes open to the wind.

At first Kalsin thought they came unarmed. But closer now, he spotted the glint of metal around their hands.

Claws. Hooked, wicked looking claws peaked from between their curled fingers, with a pair of rings at the base to secure the weapon reminiscent of brass knuckles. A blade as big as his forearms was attached to the lower end of the weapon. With the two parts combined, the thing looked like a gods-forsaken brigand's dagger poised for a deadly downward motion, with a hilt that, if used for punches, would tear through meat and muscle.

Most of the Veiled also carried a tiny sack around their waist, and as their approach reached close enough for Kalsin to make out the sound of their thudding footsteps, they reached down as one and yanked the sacks off to reveal—birds.

Small and with long beaks shaped like a screw, the winged creatures took flight and sped toward the defenders, each dragging a narrow line of rope tied to their feet. Kalsin pulled his neck down on reflex as the birds sailed over them. Seconds later the ropes went taut, and Kalsin looked behind to watch in astonishment as the birds dived toward the ground like dropped anvils. They spun and burrowed down into the earth, only leaving tiny holes behind—and a large number of anchored ropes now stretching above and across the earthen wall.

"Cut!" Rolce shouted, already in the middle of swiping a blade against the lines near him.

Kalsin, Ringtail and Shoreline quickly followed the example of the older boys around them, and the next minute was a blur of tempered steel tearing against woven strings.

Heft, swish, sever.

The ropes were weak, but what they lacked in durability they made up in numbers and the sheer frustration they caused. The lines that went taut were easy to cut, but several the Veiled kept slack on purpose as they ran, which forced the defenders into the laborious work of slowly sawing away at them.

The fight had not ever started yet, Kalsin's breaths were already deep, hacking at ropes. Part of him was occupied by an internal debate—going over the rights and wrongs of leaving the ropes to let them give a small advantage to the climbers, or sever them as they came and exhaust himself before the battle could even begin.

More birds landed. Strings crossed and tangled across their shoulders, not enough to become a serious issue, but enough to fray against their nerves. Few of the defenders cursed as wayward birds tried landing on them, pecking out a clump of hair or bruising a shoulder before sliding off. The occasional arrow flew out toward the invaders, courtesy of the two archers the defenders had, coming slow and with precision due to the low amount of munition they had. All the while—the Veiled advanced, the ropes came, and the older boys cut.

"Iron headers!" someone shouted. For a moment Kalsin thought it was some elaborate curse, but then he saw the slowly lumbering figures among the Veiled.

Instead of robes, they were fitted in sets of rustic full-plate armor, and instead of the claw-like weapons, they carried sturdy looking hooks in their gauntleted hands. From the waist below, their armor appeared lighter, but their iron boots had sharp spikes sticking forward. Their most notable feature, however, was the wide and circular plate attached to the top of their helmets, discs bobbing as they moved like an always-present shield braced for a hail of arrows.

The Veiled only had a handful of iron headers among them, though Kalsin didn't have time to count. The first wave of the invaders was about to hit.

Kalsin's vision tunneled in on the pair of robed figures directly ahead. They didn't slow down like he expected, but quickened their loping gaits as they approached. They let momentum carry them, bracing their feet and launching from the base of the vertical wall to immediately clear most of the barricade.

Kalsin barely had enough time to yank his shield up in a slanted, backward position and take the murderous lunge on its surface. Metal scraped on his shield like nail on a chalkboard. He shoved forward instantly, but the Veiled had found a handhold on the wall—facing off against Kalsin from a hanging position—and hooked his claws behind the frame of his shield with his other hand.

Kalsin pulled his mind from the terrified sludge it had fallen prey to, and acted, not allowing the invader to have the initiative. In one smooth symphony, Kalsin moved his claw-hooked shield to the side, slammed the tip down into the earth-wall to anchor it, and thrust his sword through the fresh gap. He felt more than saw the blade find its mark. An increased resistance as steel went from slicing air to biting skin and meat, then slipped on bone, bit some more, and finally stopped to rest with dead weight pulling it down. Kalsin drew his sword back. It came with a wet squelch and red droplets splattered on his hand.

The Veiled didn’t fall. Limp, dead eyes staring at Kalsin in accusation, he remained hanging from the wall, his claws keeping him anchored like a gruesome painting slanted on a crooked nail.

A rope nearby tensed, and the crimson sash of another Veiled rose into Kalsin's vision. He angled his shield toward the invader and pushed. He felt the clung, metal connecting with skull, making the invader disappear below, but another took its place almost instantly. Kalsin remained still as he felt Ringtail vie for an opening, her shield sliding over his to borrow room for a strike she directed at the invader scaling the wall at her front.

Kalsin lurched forward, his shield suddenly a multitude times heavier as two sets of iron claws had been wedged over the rim of his shield and pulled his strapped arm with the strength of a mule. Only Ringtail’s hand on his shoulder managed to keep his position before he would dive headfirst over the edge. His shield’s pointy bottom dug a furrow into the wall before he could stop it. Kalsin grunted, maybe screamed while he struggled, and reared up to hack his sword blindly above and over his shield.

Slash, tug, thrust. All sounds were molded into a singular, continuous, indistinguishable mass of chaos.

All the while, between the gaps of their shields, the hanged carcass of the first Veiled he’d killed watched the slaughter with unseeing eyes.

Kalsin's world shrank to a blur of red and black, the sound of clashing metal and ragged breathing. Weights pulled and pressed against his shield, flesh caught on his steel when he sliced forward, his body attuning to the rhythm of mindless bloodshed. The Veiled continued to climb and attack, their claws digging furrows in both the earthen blockade and the minds of the defenders. Morale bolstering hive-surge wafted in and out like a stray gust within the landscape of their minds, a bubble of hope flaring up and away like liquid resolve.

Gasping, Kalsin gained a moment of calm just in time to see the iron headers arrive at the wall's base, their hooked arms latching onto the lip and pulling themselves up with gruesome ease. They moved like an unstoppable landslide, securing their positions with their spiked boots, the defenders’ blows deflected by the wide brim of their disc-helmets.

Further down the line, he saw multiple spots getting overwhelmed, small gaps opening up as older boys fell back or got hooked out of position, falling headfirst into the Veiled with screams.

"Back! Back and defend," the hoarse calls rose above the battle, and Kalsin saw the defenders start pulling back from the wall, retreating with their shields up.

A hand grabbed his shoulder, making him snarl and his sword-arm jerk violently before he realized it was his mates tugging him backward. He inched back on the sloped footing under his boots, keeping a steady line with the others until he felt the ground even out below him.

A blessed reprieve descended upon them as they retreated from the wall, the attackers quickly pulling themselves up the earthen barricade. He felt Ringtail by his side, with a new face—a stocky middle-aged woman—having replaced Rolce at some point on the other.

But that was all the time they got. Despite the orderly line with which their section had retreated, a chaotic melee was breaking out all over the battlefield. Isolated fights were spreading out, and those standing at the sides of the formation had no choice but to turn and latch off toward the flanking enemy, each man fighting for their own survival.

Kalsin felt a hand grip his arm.

“We fight together, Ghost,” said Ringtail, yanking him closer with fire in her eyes. He had to lean down until their foreheads pressed together. “Come on. Let’s give those fuckers a bloody theatre.”

“Together,” Kalsin said, feeling her breath on his face before separating with some effort. He turned and planted his feet next to an equally unmovable Ringtail in the midst of a raging maelstrom.

Any and all order from the defenders was gone. From this point forward, they could only rely on themselves.
