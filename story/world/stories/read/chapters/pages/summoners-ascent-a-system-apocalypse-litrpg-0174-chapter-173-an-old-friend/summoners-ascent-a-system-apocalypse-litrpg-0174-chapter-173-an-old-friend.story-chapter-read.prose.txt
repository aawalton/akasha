The world around Ashton faded away into nothingness, and what surrounded him was a complete black void. For a moment, it seemed like he would still be blind even in his dreams, but then, a speck of colour appeared in front of him.

A seed was pushing through the ground, and was rapidly growing.

"Hey there, little buddy," Ashton squatted down and touched the trunk that was slowly growing thicker. It was weird; Ashton felt completely conscious, like this was a lucid dream. But even then, it was much clearer than when he's had these in the past.

He could see his hands, and he could feel the stump of his index finger aching ever so slightly. Frankly, Ashton didn't have the slightest clue what was happening right now, though he was definitely quite on edge.

The sapling continued growing, and soon reached up to Ashton's chin even when he stood back up. It wasn't done growing, either, and simply kept on going. Before long, there were other things pushing through the ground as well, though. Grass was sprouting on the ground, sprinkling bits of vivid green around this complete darkness.

Soon, there was more, too. Flowers began to blossom, and by the time Ashton turned and saw that the tree had turned into a massive, ancient oak, the world was truly filled with colour. But the sky was still that endless, dark void.

But well, it was pleasant, anyway. He didn't know where it came from, but there was sunlight that was fairly warm on his skin, and a slight breeze was pushing past his face.

Ashton smiled and sat down beside the tree, leaning onto it. It had stopped growing by now, or at least wasn't growing that fast anymore. This was nice. A bit weird, but nice.

Sure, it was possible that something was about to jump out to scare the shit out of him, or use his trauma to open up old scars, but until then, Ashton would enjoy this to the absolute fullest extent he could. He figured he deserved a break, even for just a little while.

And after resting for an hour or two, he could use this time to study up and try to plan out things, and make use of this weird 'dream', where he wasn't surrounded by any dangers.

"Ahh, if only I had a drink and some snacks, this could be a real great day," he pointed out. And then, something decided to throw a wrench in his plans. It was a voice that would not let him sit here in this peace and quiet, and it wouldn't let him use this time smartly, either.

"Oh, wow, sorry for not being able to serve you correctly, your majesty," they said sarcastically, and Ashton jumped up without a moment's hesitation. He turned, and in front of him, he saw a man. Well, not a human man, nor was there anything about him that would necessarily guide Ashton either direction, but he knew this was a man. Because Ashton knew him.

He stood there wearing a long, waving cloak that hid his arms—wings with feathers made of dyed glass—and a colourfully painted mask that covered his face. His hair glittered in every colour in this sunless light.

The colours and patterns on the man's mask were shifting rapidly, but Ashton knew exactly what that meant.

"I... is it really you? Seriously?" Ashton asked, walking forward confused. "Is this some stupid fucking trick again? If this is my mind playing a prank on me, then–"

He said, but soon, the man interrupted him by rushing forward and pulling Ashton into a hug. "It's not a trick or a prank. I promise. Just... after hearing that message, 'Ashton is safe', I just couldn't not come here, could I?"

Ashton laughed slightly, almost coughing it out. He pulled away and smiled broadly. "But how did you do that? I didn't know you had the ability to interfere with dreams."

The masked man laughed with a sharp cackle. "Of course, Elder Spirits tend to have a lot of different abilities."

"... What? What do you mean, 'Elder Spirit'? You're a middle grade spirit."

"I was, back then," the man explained, starting to walk around him, as if trying to actually get a proper look at his old friend. "Ashton, a long time has passed since we last saw each other. Though, I'm surprised. I thought you said that the people of your realm live to at best a hundred years, in rare cases. I figured that time may flow different in the Spirit Realm, but not to this degree. You barely look a week older than when I last saw you."

Ashton stared at the spirit. "I... It has been barely a week for me. A little more, but yeah. For me, the Summoner died a week ago."

"A week? How much time had passed in your realm."

"None? I mean, maybe a few seconds, but I was right back where I was when I was first dragged away," Ashton explained. "And want to know the biggest 'fuck you'? I didn't get to keep any of the power I gained throughout my time as a summon, and a few hours later? The fucking Interdimensional Council invaded."

The colours on the spirit's mask started to jump around furiously, turning into sharp spikes. "What?! Are you serious?"

Ashton smiled a little. He was happy that his old friend was so concerned, but at this point, there wasn't anything they could do to change it anymore anyway.

"I'll tell you everything that's happened so far, okay?"

The spirit nodded his head, as Ashton and him walked back over to the tree, sitting down there side by side. And then, Ashton started recounting everything that had happened since he was pulled back to earth.

It was actually pretty fun for Ashton, since his friend's mask always showed his emotions in the most clear way imaginable. Well, at least to Ashton it was very clear. He had seen this mask change numerous times, after all.

"... So you became a summoner yourself?"

Ashton nodded. "It was the only way I could see myself growing powerful enough to really make it through to the end."

"But even they didn't make it all the way..."

"Yeah, but that wasn't because the Summoner was too weak. They were strong enough to take down the entire system if they wanted to. They died because they failed one of the final quests. It was tailor-made to make them fail in the first place."

The spirit seemed to think about something intensely. Their wings shifted back and forth under their cloak. "Do you remember what the quest was?"

Ashton scoffed. "I never knew most of the Summoner's quests in the first place, all I know comes from talking to other folk when I was travelling around on my own. I have no idea what killed them, but I do know that they were pissed when they saw the quest."

"Urgh, tell me about it," the masked spirit groaned. "Well, I'm sure you'll be able to figure it out. If there's anyone that can, then it's you. I mean, you're already so different to them. You're forming contracts the right way, actually saving people, and basically doing anything you can not to follow in XXXXXXX's footsteps. I'm proud of you, Ashton."

Ashton twitched slightly as he heard that word. That name. "What... did you just say?"

"Oh, don't make me repeat myself, you know I'm not for all this sappy stuff. I'm just proud that you're walking the right path, that's it. I–"

"No, no, that's not what I mean. You just said a name there, right?"

"Hmm, did I? I just said their name. It leaves kind of a bad taste in my mouth so I try not to."

Letting out a yelled groan, Ashton hit his head against the tree trunk behind him. "Oh come on, seriously?"

"What's going on?"

"I wasn't able to hear their name."

"... Huh?"

"I don't know why I didn't realize it until now. I've been drawing a blank every single time, and it didn't even seem weird to me. I've just been calling them 'the Summoner', like some constellation's modifier," Ashton grumbled. "I'm sure you can tell, but my soul is absolutely fucked up."

The spirit shrugged. "I figured you didn't want to talk about it, but yeah, I noticed."

"Essentially, it looks like the Summoner was only able to grab me on complete accident, and my soul became a bit torn up when I was thrown back. Maybe the whole time shenanigans that were going on made it worse. But either way, their name is a complete blank to me," Ashton said, clicking his tongue.

"So? I'd be happy if I didn't remember their name."

"I remember saying my own first words. But of all the thing that I don't remember, it's the name of the being that tormented me for a century? Yeah, no, that's sketchy as fuck."

The spirit laughed a little and nodded. "Well, you're not wrong about that. Don't worry, I'll ask some friends to look into it."

Ashton smiled lightly. "I'd appreciate that. But I guess that means we'll see each other again?"

"Duh, of course we will. There's no way I'd leave you behind. You started from nothing, but in just a bit more than a century, you became a being that none of us could ever forget," the spirit said, looking off into the distance. "A lot of us see our lives back then differently. We all hated it, but we can't agree on a lot of other parts. Except for one thing. Ashton, without exception, all of us found you to be a radiating beacon of hope. Without you, I don't think as many of us would have made it through to the end."

Trying not to show that his cheeks were definitely flushed in a bright pink. "Thanks, I'm glad that I'll be able to tell my friends that I'm not being cocky for no reason. I mean, I've got the official approval of an Elder Spirit."

A little embarrassed, the spirit also turned his head away before clearing his throat. "Well, you've got the approval of multiple of them."

"Hm?"

"What, you think I'm the only one that ascended to this point? It's been thousands of years for us. Many of us hid away after being freed, but a lot of us tried to reclaim the power that we were forced to gather. Just for ourselves this time. A couple of us made it to Elder, and the rest that tried made it to the level of a 'Greater' spirit at least. Hell, I'll let you guess three times who the new Spirit King is."

Ashton stared at his friend, startled. "Hold on, are you serious? Did the Sword Spirit actually make it?"

"Yup! They've got a new title now, but 'Sword Spirit' just feels right. And trust me, everyone was surprised as hell to see them rise up like that. I mean, object-born spirits like them don't tend to even make it to middle grade. Without a clan, all on your own, it's kind of hard to ascend like that."

Ashton grinned broadly, "I'm so happy for them. I know that it was their dream from the moment they found out it was even a thing."

"You can say that again. It was a huge celebration back then. Oh, but it's not just us spirits that changed and grew. A lot of the fairies banded together afterward, and they've created one of the largest countries in the Feywilds. I heard that some of the elementals have risen to 'Highest' grade, and a couple have become lords. Most of the demonic and angelic beings tend to stick to themselves, but there's exceptions there, as well. Like..." Ashton was listening intensely, happy to hear that they were all doing better than the thought. Ashton looked at the spirit that finally remembered what he wanted to say. "Oh! You might be able to see them around sometime, actually. You remember that small blood demon that fell through when... uh, the Summoner ripped open a portal to hell sometime? The one that didn't even have a name?"

"Yes? What about her?" Ashton asked, confused where the spirit was going. How would Ashton see them around?

"She's become a constellation! Isn't that crazy? I was a bit angry at first when I heard she actually became a 'patron' of these stupid games, but I heard that she's not like the others. She hasn't bought a single 'product'. Hell, she's never even offered someone a sponsorship. Still, she's a huge deal somehow."

Ashton felt his heard skip a beat. He had a weird feeling about this.

"Do you... know what her modifier is?"

The spirit thought for a moment. "Hm... something-something, crimson heart or whatever?"

A light smile appeared on Ashton's lips. "Is it the 'Daughter of the Scarlet Heart'?"
