Morning greeted me with pale light and the music of distant songbirds. One side of my body was pleasantly nestled into what might have been the softest mattress I’d ever felt. Both knees lay curled under a heavy quilt, while both hands stretched languidly ahead. Surrounded by crisp air laden with the smells of pine, morning dew, and something pleasantly floral.

As close to bliss as I’d had in a while.

Dawn, or just about. Mm. Over-sleeping again. Figures. I used to be much more responsible.

I yawned, nuzzling my cheek against the pillow as half-lidded eyes settled on the window. Its thick drapes glowed vaguely pink, concealing the world beyond. A few slivers of hazy light graced the floorboards beneath.

Huh. I could’ve sworn I left the curtains open. And I… wait. I’m awake. It’s morning. It’s morning! I didn’t dream about-

Parchment rustled from behind me.

Every muscle locked into place. The floral smell sharpened with awful familiarity, and recognition turned my blood cold.

The sound came again.

I left the curtains open. I did, I’m certain I did, oh saints, oh no, no-

“Please don’t scream.”

I bolted upright. Turned in wild, slack-jawed terror.

Velidan sat beside the door. Black hair swept back, skin hued dusky gray in the pale light. Full, red lips pursed and glittering eyes fixed on me. Garbed in verdant emerald and sinful black. A visage of arrogant, cold beauty, almost comically out of place in the patched red armchair.

My runebook lay open in his lap. My pack was overturned atop the little table, belongings stacked around it in tidy piles. The shard lay on the armrest with a familiar, folded piece of parchment resting beneath it.

“I’d rather not fend off the deluge of concerned humans that will surely come to save you.” Silver-flecked eyes narrowed, pinning me to the bed. “And, just to be clear, my ‘fending’ will likely be quite bloody.”

I shook my head. Inched back until the headboard dug into my spine. “I won’t scream,” I whispered, pulling the blanket to my chest.

“Good. I’ve had a miserable enough night. Although,” a positively wolfish grin flashed across his features, “I have a feeling it’s about to improve.”

My knuckles turned white around the cloth.

How long has he been here? Ashes, going through my things while I slept? The casual invasion of my privacy brought a sickening wave of nausea right alongside the terror. The sheer, impossible nightmare that he was here.

Not a dream. He’s here. He’s actually here.

“I’ve read all this several times, and it’s… mystifying.” The Fae’s deep voice rippled through the room. “I assume it’s what you had hidden in your bedroom.”

I bit my lower lip hard enough to taste copper.

He turned a page, gaze falling. “Fellbrook… are there any other runekeepers there?”

I swallowed. Shifted slowly to one side. Towards the window. Traced the distance with my eyes. Once my feet hit the floor, safety would lay two steps away.

“Was your mother one?”

The drawn curtains glowed with invitation. A thin barrier between me and salvation. Could I reach them before he stopped me? Tear them wide, flood the room with dawn’s light?

“When did you first use magic?”

The quilt hissed against my gown as one leg slid down. Salt, maybe I’ll just throw myself out the window. It’ll hurt like anything, but he can’t follow me outside. I’ll… probably survive. My bare toes pressed into cold, hard wood. I shifted my weight to ball of that foot, muscles coiled-

“Get back in bed.”

I froze. Counted my heartbeats. Curled my toes against the floor.

“Brin.” The book snapped shut. “If I came with ill intentions, I wouldn’t have waited until you woke up.”

The claim was far from reassuring. I didn’t move.

“I’m not here to hurt you.” He waved a hand. Casual. Dismissive. “You’re harmless. Actually, you’re… well.” A soft rumble of laughter sent a shiver down my spine. “You’re exactly what I need right now.”

My pulse jumped. I swung my gaze towards him, holding the quilt like a shield. “W-what?”

“You,” carved features bloomed into a wicked smile as he rose, runebook in one hand, the other holding the letter and shard, “Might just be the solution to a little problem of mine. I have a gift for you.”

My shoulders stiffened. “I don’t want anything from you.”

“I didn’t ask. Are there any other runekeepers in Fellbrook?”

Hateful, defiant silence was my answer.

His lip curled. “Don’t be stubborn. It’s a simple question.”

Quiet snaked its way between us.

Dark eyes rolled skyward, as if he was searching for patience. “Fine, mortal. Shall I start making threats? There’s other people in this building. Ones whose lives seem to motivate you.”

I sank back onto the mattress, my world narrowing to the predatory figure now drawing near. Slowly, languidly, as if he hadn’t just flippantly threatened to kill people.

What do I do? What do I do?

Fire. Veth. Probably… probably wouldn’t end well. Not for me, at least. Probably wouldn’t be enough to really hurt him. Might ruin his hair. Will definitely make him angry.

Another thought chased the swell of panic. A calmer, fiercer one.

He likes it when I’m bold. He enjoys it. So… be bold.

I tucked sleep-tangled hair behind my ears, chest swelling with a deep breath. My nostrils filled with the smell of fragrant, sweet roses. “W-will you… how about a question for a question?”

The book landed with a thump in front of me. A moment later, the mattress sank beneath his weight. “I’m not here to play games, Brin.”

Too close. He was too close. I curled my knees up. Drew the blanket tight, hands knotting in front of my chest.

Velidan scoffed, then turned a glower downwards, regarding the bed as though it had grievously offended him. “You slept on this?”

“Is that your first question?”

The Fae was silent for a moment. Then… my pulse stuttered as familiar amusement danced across his features.

“I… I thought this was a very nice bed.” I smoothed one palm atop the blanket. Tried to ground myself in the feel of soft, thick fabric even as my body shrank beneath the sheer strength of his presence. The words tumbled too fast, one after another, tripping and slipping from trembling lips. “My… my m-mattress at home is stuffed with straw. It used to get itchy, sometimes. Kind of damp, too. Especially during the Harvest season. That’s when the air gets warm some days, and really rainy on others, you know? Straw doesn’t fare well when the weather is erratic.  But I… I think this is wool. The stuffing, I mean. It’s… it’s very comfortable.”

A crease between his brows deepened with every word. He flicked some lint off the quilt. “It’s abysmal.”

“It’s small, but I don’t need a lot of… and it’s bigger than mine. The blanket was warm. I… I can’t ask for more, can I?”

“Is that your first question?”

“N-no. Why… why did you… why are you…” I looked around helplessly, fighting to calm my nerves. Searching the room as though I could find sanctuary somewhere in the too-small space. A thousand questions bubbled up, but… perhaps starting with something simple, something unimportant, was the wiser way to approach this. It would give me a moment to collect myself, if nothing else. “Why… why has your night been miserable?”

The Fae was silent for ages. So long that I wondered if he would simply refuse to answer. Finally, with a heavy exhalation and a crack in the arrogant visage, he muttered, “I fell in a lake.”

“You… you… what?”

“From very high up.”

I blinked, waiting for him to elaborate, and took note of the state of his clothing. Velidan had always appeared immaculate in form as well as in how he dressed. I was used to smooth, dignified outfits; vests of ethereal satin, pressed silk, and flawless hues. He wore a shimmering dark green tunic now, with black trousers made from dense, rippling fabric. But he was also… rumpled. There was no other word for it. The clothes were bunched and crinkled, with spatters of mud staining the outfit. Even his hair was unusually wavy and mussed in places, as though it had just recently dried, and was that a leaf sticking out of it?

“Apparently being bound to you makes crossing the realms… well. Much easier, but much less accurate. My destination lay deeper in the valley, but you were here.” He grimaced and swept an arm out, vaguely indicating the outdoors. “I appeared in the middle. Mid-air.”

“Um… sorry?”

“You’re forgiven.” Those perfect lips twitched into a smile. “Are there any other runekeepers in Fellbrook?”

“N-no.” I waited, pulse thrumming like a trapped bird, but he just made a thoughtful little noise before looking at me expectantly. “How… h-how are you here?”

“In your realm, or your room?”

“I… both?”

“Through magic drawn from the strength of blood sacrifices and a frustratingly convoluted ritual.” He cast a displeased glance towards the hallway. “And your door doesn’t have a lock. Did you have any idea the shard would return to the mortal realm with you?”

I expected the bite of anger, perhaps even the threat of danger, behind that question, but all I heard was genuine curiosity. I shook my head, hair hissing along the quilt. “No. No, I swear.” I waited with baited breath. Ready for this veneer of cordiality to shatter beneath rage and threats.

It did not.

“Hm.” Velidan drummed his fingers against the bedspread for several long moments, seemingly lost in thought, then murmured, “It’s your turn.”

I swallowed. Steadied myself with another breath. “You… why have you been leaving me baskets of food and watering pails?”

Black brows rose as he said, in a tone that clearly indicated the answer should have been obvious, “Because you said you missed gardening, and I assumed the exertion would make you hungry. When did you first use magic?”

The question barely registered. I was too busy staring. Listening to the echo of that bewilderingly offhanded answer.

“Brin?” The corners of his mouth turned downwards. “Brin?”

I blinked. “You… what?”

“When did you first use magic?”

This again. Though at least he actually believes me, now. And I don’t suppose there’s any point in lying, is there? He’s got the book, he’s read my letter. Saints, my letter…

“When I was twelve. I went snooping in Durst’s bedroom and found the runebook. My mom’s letter was inside. I read it over and over again, for hours, and then… and I tried…” My throat thickened. The room grew blurry. “Please don’t take it. Please don’t. She… she didn’t l-leave me m-much and-”

“Was that why you hid it?” Velidan’s deep voice held a slightly baffled note, and no trace of sympathy. “Simply because you thought I’d take it away from you?”

“Well, yes, but… it’s magical. It’s powerful. I didn’t want… someone like you shouldn’t have it.”

He snorted. “Valiant of you, but I can’t use these. I don’t even recognize most of them.”

I blinked. Chewed the words over. Knit my brows together.

Starlit eyes studied my expression. “Yes?”

“You… you don’t recognize ‘most of them.’ But… some? Other than… than yours, I mean.” I straightened, leaning forwards as fervent curiosity inched past the fear. “Do you know what they do? And why is your name one of them? That’s what I really don’t understand. Is it a… a word or a name that used to be common? What does it mean?”

“That was a lot of questions.” He was smiling slightly.

“I’m just curious.”

“And that wasn’t one at all. You don’t seem to understand your own rules.”

I spread my hands. “Well, aren’t you?”

“Oh, very. As I said, I’m mystified. Again.”

“I told you I was a runekeeper.” I gave a plaintive shrug. My gown rustled against the quilt. “You’re the one who didn’t believe me.”

He shook his head, dark hair swaying leisurely. “Because you shouldn’t be. You can’t be. Was your mother one?”

“No. Not that I know of, and I think Durst would’ve told me.”

“Durst. Your father?”

“Sort of.” One shoulder rose, and my mouth twisted to the side. “I don’t know anything about my real father. Durst raised me after my mother died.”

“Another thing I don’t understand. She claimed the ability of foresight, yet died?” He scoffed, nose wrinkling and tone soaked in scorn. “Either she was a liar, or her powers were an embarrassment.”

“Don’t talk about her like that!” My sudden spine seemed to surprise him as much as me, and we spent a quiet moment regarding each other. “Durst said she’d been having visions since she was a child. He swore they were true. And… I mean, she was right, wasn’t she?”

“About?”

I dragged the book to my chest, half-afraid he would tear it away. He didn’t, and I lowered my nose to inhale the familiar, earthy smell of leather. “About me. About the magic in this, and the fact that I can use it. About… salt, even about you.”

“Me?”

“It’s no use hiding… you read the letter. The part about Durst asking me to do something. She told Durst about you coming to Fellbrook. I mean, not everything, but… she told him there’d be an attack one night. She said when he woke up and saw the fire, he was to leave me at the house.”

The Fae’s expression grew more puzzled with every word.

“It’s the whole reason I took the shard. Durst said I was supposed to leave town with it. He told me to keep it safe.”

“Brin. You…” pale fingers swept up, pinching the long bridge of his nose. “Just so I’m clear. You’ve been living in a state of terror, nearly died several times, and have been frustrating my designs simply because some long-dead, absent human-”

“Don’t talk about-”

“-Told you to? With no explanation of why? Mortal, are you-”

“She loved me! I suppose a monster like you wouldn’t understand a thing about that, but it’s enough for me! She loved me, and Durst loves me, and they asked me to do this! I don’t need any more reason than that!”

He seemed baffled. Staring at me with knotted brows and parted lips, head cocked to one side, as though I were speaking a different tongue.

A thump from near the doorway made me freeze. I started, glancing over in sudden fear that my outburst might have stirred one of the others.

But, no… the soft thuds continued past the door and down the hallway. I sank back against the wood, pulse thrumming, then rubbed my palm across the leather. “Can you… you said you recognize some of these. Do you know what they mean? What they do?”

“Some. One is of particular interest, especially given my original destination tonight. Do you believe in fate, Brin?” Amusement threaded every word.

“That’s an… incredibly complicated question, given…” I clutched the book, offering a little shrug.

“I can imagine.” He chuckled, extending one palm.

I hesitated, leery of handing my most precious possession over again, but… He could just take it, if he really wanted. He’s being… ‘nice’ isn’t the right word, but something like it. His version of cordial, I suppose.

A grim certainty chased the thought. He wants something.

I was sure of it. The Fae’s previous moments of generosity or charm had been attempts at bribery or bloodless coercion. It stood to reason, then, that this interaction had an ultimate goal.

I handed the book over. Tucked the letter, with deliberate slowness, behind and beneath me, between one hip and the headboard. Watched him carefully all the while, making a statement with my movements if not my voice.

‘This is mine. You can’t have it.’

The Fae’s fathomless gaze traced the movement, but he made no comment. Just flipped leisurely through the book until he landed on Velidan and, still watching me, slowly tore the page out.

I closed my eyes against the awful sound, nails digging into my palms. Fought back tears at the violation of my mother’s gift.

“I’d like you to attempt some magic, Brin.”

I sniffled. Dried my eyes and, after taking a moment to collect myself, leveled a glare up at that cruel face. “Sure. Want me to set you on fire?”

Rich laughter filled the room. “Tempting, but I have something else in mind. I told you earlier that I brought you a gift.”

“And I told you, I don’t want anything from you.”

Broad shoulders shook as he chuckled, flipping another page and sliding the book towards me. A moment later, to my surprise, he handed me the shard. “It’s not for you to keep. Just… something I need you to hold onto.”

He’d stopped on Faer. A pattern of swirling, night-black lines inked the shape of an eye with a hooded lid. A shape that, every time I’d looked at it, I’d felt certain looked right back up at me.

“Language,” he murmured, tracing one long finger along the page, “Changes over time. But some things are substantial enough to retain their meaning.”

“Is it a name? Like yours?”

“Not a name.” The smile sharpened, and his deep voice dropped to a purr as one hand dipped  into a pocket. “The beginning of an invocation. A rare, violent, and powerful ritual. Faer’yll Illyrium. ‘Forged of the soul.’”

I leaned forwards, eager to ask more questions, but they all died on my tongue as Velidan withdrew his hand. Nestled within it, larger than mine and just as bright, was a second piece of sunlit steel.

He held it out. “I’d like-”

He said more, but I didn’t hear it. He moved, too, but I didn’t see it. Every fragment of him was a distant echo, as if he was beneath deep, dark water.

Everything was the rune. Every scrap of my vision, every pulse in my eardrums. Hot and bright, a blinding gold that commanded every shred of my attention and cast the rest of the world into distant haze.

I was vaguely aware of the pleased grin spreading across Velidan’s face as my fingers lifted of their own accord. Traced mindlessly, effortlessly, as if I’d been born doing so. My lips parted as light carved itself into the very air. He leaned forwards, eyes lit with triumph and a thousand pinpricks of fiery gold, as-

My world exploded into agony.
