Tradis ran a hand through his hair like someone who could not believe what was happening. He looked visibly livid but confused. It was as if he was angry while knowing that he did not have the right to be angry.

Elaswit watched patiently from her chair in what turned out to be a very comfortable guest room in Valdan’s manor.

The floor was covered in a wide carpet that reminded her of the color of a particularly old tree; a scattering of grey and brown. It was an odd choice for a carpet if anyone asked her. At least the bed seemed normal enough. It was clearly not a room designed to host dignitaries or the like, perhaps the room of a friend or unimportant guest.

Considering the fact that the maid who had guided them had referred to it as her uncle’s room, it made little in the way of sense.

Tradis sat back on his own chair. A sigh left his lips. He threw his head back and let it hang over the chair’s low backrest.

“This is turning out to be a shitty day,” he groaned. “I swear I’d wring her neck if Valdan didn’t care very much about her.”

“Uncle!” Elaswit chided before she could stop herself.

Her uncle’s eyes moved in their sockets. He looked at her from the corner of his vision. There was something cold and deadly in them, something that bade her to remember her place before he reminded her of it.

Elaswit looked down and away, unable to meet her uncle’s gaze.

It was a stark reminder that he no longer treated her like his beloved niece, but like a child who needed to understand the wicked way of the world and needed to be hard enough to survive it.

How a man could change from so much love to the absence of any love at all was terrifying to think about. It was even more terrifying to witness.

Her uncle looked away from her in her silence and stared up at the ceiling. It was nothing but stone crafting, devoid of design. Grey and blue and black stared back at her uncle.

“This is just insane,” he muttered, talking to himself. “And Bran does not even know if Valdan is alive. Only the gods know what she would do with such a weapon if he no longer lives.”

He frowned, sighed again.

Elaswit could see the gears working in his head. The problem was that she didn’t know if he was looking for a solution that led to the spear, Valdan’s beloved, or Valdan himself.

Taking his hand away from his face, he folded it over his chest.

“Elaswit,” he said in that voice that she had come to see as his teaching voice.

Elaswit straightened on her chair. “Yes, sir.”

“What strategy would you employ if you were currently in my position?” he asked. If he had noticed her straighten and felt bad about her new reaction to him, he did not show it.

Elaswit ignored her constant desire to find a shadow of the uncle she’d once known and put her mind to the task he had set her on.

The question was a tricky one. What exactly was the position her uncle felt that he was currently in? Was he looking for Valdan’s beloved or was he looking for the weapon?

“I will find a scryer,” she answered. It was always the solution to every problem that involved tracking someone when you did not know when to start.

However, her uncle’s response told her how wrong she was.

Snorting in derision, he looked at her. “I swear, why do strong men raise their children the wrong way?”

Elaswit wasn’t sure what he was talking about specifically. Her father had raised her to the best of his abilities.

“He should’ve sent you all to the adventurer’s guild like our father tried to,” Uncle Tradis said with a touch of displeasure. “At least he should’ve sent you all out to see the world.”

None of those options made any sense. To send them off on their own out into the world was not a reasonable decision for a king. Such an act was to risk their lives. And to risk their lives was to risk the throne.

If the king had no heir then the kingdom would fall into chaos.

Despite her dislike for the throne, her lack of control had almost led to the death of her brother, Derenet, which already put her freedom in jeopardy. Elaswit knew that the only reason she could comfortably reject the throne as she always did was because Derenet had complete interest in being king. Vaskot, her youngest brother was still too young, even if her mother was grooming him for the position of leadership.

The thought made her wonder how Derenet was doing. Had the [Healer]s found a way to wake him up; a way to save him?

It was a moment before she realized that her uncle was looking at her.

“Try it,” he said when their eyes met. “Be defiant. I will not punish you for it.”

In his past life the [Spear of Valsin] had ended up in the hands of somebody that was not from Bandiv. Or from Nel Quan.

Aiden could still remember how powerful the weapon had been. Some polymaths claimed that part of the reason the war had raged on for so long with so many casualties was because the weapon had eventually fallen into the hands of the demons.

The Order begged to differ. If anything, regardless of what happened, the Order was of the opinion that the entirety of Nastild—both sides combined—were in place to lose the war. The realization had been a shaker to Aiden at the time. Why? Because it implied that single handedly, Ted had raised an army capable of taking over an entire world with nothing but violence.

That was power.

Could he do the same?

Aiden banished the thought very quickly. He was trying to go back home, not rule Nastild. He looked at his arm as he and the rest of the team walked through the forest, quietly making their way back to Nosrath.

The enchantment that Alobam gave him was an illusion enchantment, and a very powerful one. The question was what it was hiding, and if it was hiding it from him, too.

With no answer, Aiden’s mind returned naturally to the [Spear of Valsin] still strapped to Melvet’s back as she walked beside Valdan like a woman who had not been more than ready to draw blood not too long ago.

The [Spear of Valsin] was one of twelve heroes relic from his past life. Or, to be more precise, twelve known heroes relic of his past life. Here was the interesting thing, there were more than twelve. If the Order information was to be believed, there were many more. What most people in this generation did not know was that the hero relics were simply powerful weapons crafted over time since the birth of Nastild, using magic the kinds of which the current era did not know. As for the church, they propagated the belief that they were weapons given to Nastild by the gods to help protect themselves from the rising darkness.

Seeing as they had never been in possession of more than two of these relics at any point in time in their history, they could not claim ownership of all the relics. There was also the fact that they had been foolish enough at one time to claim that there were only two such relics, just for a kingdom somewhere at sometime to discover another relic in some unknown ruin.

The [Spear of Valsin] was the latest known discovery, found some three hundred years ago and rumored to be the strongest of all the known relics. Aiden could say for a fact that his experience from his past life certainly backed those rumors up.

And here it was strapped to the back of Melvet.

It was insane.

“We’re not far now.” Ted said, Elami walking uncomfortably beside him.

He was right, the sounds of fighting and pain and dying was loud enough to agree with him. If Aiden strained his eyes just a little, he could even see Tanor and Telma crouching behind a tree, watching the chaos.

As expected, fighting Nosrath without the proper planning and teammates was an endeavor that would last forever.

“They probably rushed this,” Valdan said, the sword Aiden had given him at his hip. “Fights against Nosrath would usually have auras of different kinds flying around. I don’t even sense one. They are probably as powerful as I am or weaker.”

Aiden nodded, thinking more about why Tradis was going to kill Valdan than the monster they were about to face.

“I still have the Wielder of Nosrath if things go south,” Elami said.

Valdan paused. “What’s that?”

“An item designed to restrain Nosrath for a period of time, numbering in seconds.”

Everybody paused, staring at Melvet in confusion and surprise.

“Most people used it as a last resort,” she continued, seemingly unaware of the attention on. “A final blow. One last barrage of attacks. It’s an old item, lost to time, really. How…”

“What?” she asked.

It was just one of those things you knew but never really remembered until someone brought it up. It was one of those things he knew because he had stumbled upon it. Even the thought of fighting Nosrath did not bring it to mind.

Elami knowing it had not really sent him on a chaotic thought because Elami had already proven himself to be a mystery and knew that he was a mystery. Melvet, however, was not supposed to be a mystery. She was supposed to be the beautiful woman Valdan loved.

A woman who had a deep affection for the church.

“Babe,” Valdan said carefully. “What just happened?”

“Remember when I said I once knew an [Apostle]?”

Aiden’s jaw dropped. Melvet once knew an [Apostle]? The church kept the knowledge and locations of [Apostle]s more secretly than countries kept the locations of all their nuclear weapons. And Melvet had just stumbled upon an [Apostle]?

Just how deep in the church had she been before meeting Valdan?

“You met an [Apostle],” Elami said, slightly suspicious. “Aren’t they very special resources of the church?”

“Designed to keep the [Saint]s in check,” Melvet said, unfazed. Then she paused very suddenly and looked at Aiden. “You’ll have to be extra careful now, Saint killer. You’ll probably have an [Apostle] coming for you soon.”
