Theo watched, bemused, as the magician asked one of the small monsters to pick a card before putting the arcane object through their neck. The arcane cards were sharper than knives and seemed to move as if they had a life of their own. Max then hopped onto a summoned chair before then landing on a cannon that appeared out of thin air. After he struck a pose, the large weapon blew a cloud of confetti across the monsters with a blast.

Plenty of the affected horde looked stunned or bewildered. Most outside of the area of the magician’s influence had readied their weapons and wanted to get closer. Probably not to get an autograph.

He turned his gaze to the elf, who was lining up a shot. With a crack only slightly muted by the fanfare put on by her equal, the bullet snapped across the courtyard. Bursts of red came from four or five monsters shredded by the high caliber ammunition, before the projectile bloomed into a wide area of grasping vines. Green roots surrounded a dozen small creatures, holding them in place.

Theo smiled and put a hand in his pocket. He knew the pair were strong from the duel, but seeing them in action was something else. The Outsiders definitely needed both magicians. Not only to repel the coming Radoch threat, but for future adventures as well. He of all people knew that Sally wouldn’t be satisfied once Sanctuary was saved, and… he had much to learn from the universe as well.

Stepping down the stairs to the courtyard, Theo twisted the sledgehammer in his right hand. The short monsters from the left side were moving in to surround the showboating magician, and that wouldn’t do. Upon registering the vampire’s arrival, several turned to face him.

Slightly too late.

With one hand still in his pocket, he darted forward with a jab. The end of the hammer struck the eye socket of the bipedal fish, breaking through bone into its brain. Theo turned, gliding forward to crush a second with a wide sweep of his weapon. Ren had been right about these being low level monsters. Even with his reduced stats and lack of access to his best abilities, this was a breeze.

The area where Max was fighting was a distracting array of lights, sounds, and objects flickering in and out of existence, intertwined with sprays of blood and screams of death. Occasionally a sniper rifle shot would add to the cacophony, blowing away several monsters and scouring the courtyard with a new elemental damage type.

Hardly any time had passed, and they’d killed nearly half of the courtyard already. The magicians usually had Wolf with them—who was apparently a juggernaut of an offensive tank—and had spent most of their adventure with two other companions to offer support. Much like some of the other Outsiders, they had now retired from active duty, but it was no surprise to Theo that the group had managed to save their world.

He struck another monster with a kick, before avoiding the stab of a spear. The creature’s basic weaponry was doing them no favors, but the growing feeling that there was something more to this had started scratching at the inside of Theo’s head. He glanced over at the temple.

The tall doors, previously shut, were now starting to open. Theo took his hand out of his pocket and held his weapon with both hands.

Things were about to get interesting.

Sally stood with her hand outstretched, pointing her dagger toward the treeline. This was getting boring.

It was clear the Radochs were almost upon them. A billowing cloud of smoke rose from the forest, darkening the approach of the survivors. Trees buckled and fell out of view. Some odd humming sound resonated through the air, irritating her ears.

But she had called for the dwarves to ready their weapons slightly too soon. Now they waited for her signal to fire, but the blasted cockroaches hadn’t shown their faces yet. She eventually deflated and turned to the troop in wait.

“Just shoot them as they appear.” She sighed and lowered her dagger.

“Not full of your usual spirit,” Archie noted.

Sally shrugged. “The emergency protocol requires us to live. I’m not exactly worried about dying, but this trip has been a tiring one.”

“It could be worse.”

She took a moment to consider the statement. It was easy to imagine ways in which this could be a more dire situation. That didn’t make her feel any better, though. Coming back from lower odds might even be more rewarding. She weighed Skeleton Key in her hand and gave the wizard another shrug.

“These ones got some power behind them.” Hunter’s bright white glasses shone as he tilted his head. “Can almost taste it.”

“I’ll be tasting it when I tear their useless brains from their heads.” Sally smiled and adjusted her position into something less resigned. “With my mouth. When I eat them.”

Sentiment back on the Omen was hopeful. Jackie had her work cut out relaying a distilled account of the back and forth between herself and the other two Outsiders on board. Mostly because Edward kept winding Humphrey up. Up on the rocks, Bully looked a little sick, but motivated enough to see things through.

Archie shared her calm exhaustion, but was a little more confident about the imminent battle. It was difficult to guess at what amount of power he had left.

“You should learn to create some healing spells or something.”

He raised an eyebrow. “The breadth of things I could learn is certainly wider than that of what I do know. I used to be rather solitary, so I didn’t have the need for supportive spells.”

“Well, you’re an Outsider now.” She shook her head and looked back over the shaking forest. “You’ve been a part of our lives for years. Time to pick a lane.”

“Hmph. Speaking of that.” Archie raised a hand up in front of him. The ground swelled as water pooled up from the rocky surface. Within three seconds a waist-high wall stretching twenty feet across bubbled and shimmered in front of them. With the flick of his wrist, the wizard sent the short tidal wave off down into the treeline.

Sally clicked her tongue. “I suppose after such a long walk, a little feet washing might improve their mood. Maybe they won’t kill us as quickly.”

“It’s to make them a little more conductive. I assumed you’d be getting some use out of the potion bottles Bully passed you.”

“Or I could save them for the sequel.”

The wizard sighed. “Well, you’re the expert here. It is far beyond me to question your methods. I may have once been a master of magic, but I was never an adventurer. I’m not exactly a killer.”

“You’ve done alright so far.” She grinned and gestured out to the forest. “I’m sure you’ll be plenty busy with Sanctuary and studying once we’ve saved it. We’ll just have to deal with these pests first and-”

Hunter tensed up. “Get down.”

Sally pulled the wizard to the ground. A bright light illuminated the treeline slightly below their position, right before a beam of yellow energy slammed into the mountain behind their position. The six-foot wide attack burned against the rock for three seconds before flickering away, only leaving super-heated air in its place.

“That would have taken my upper half clean off,” Archie mused as he clasped his hat to his head.

“Good thing one of us is an adventurer at least.” Sally glanced back at the charred mountain, where three of her dwarven squad had been vaporized by the attack. With a grimace, she returned her red eyes to the shadowy man. “You didn’t try to dodge?”

“Hmm? Oh. It wasn’t aimed at me.” Hunter frowned and held up his gun. He stared at it for a few moments. “That jogs something of a memory, however.”

While the others dusted themselves off and stood back up, he leveled his revolver to point down the burning hole punctured through the trees and into the darkness of the lower mountain.

Graken the Miserable growled from atop his palanquin. The trek from the crashed ships had been long and uncomfortable, but it had been the wave of water from the mountaintop that really cut his fuse short. The wet mud just made his carrying servants buckle and slip, making the last leg of his vengeance tour enraging.

Setting off the beam cannon this early was a mistake born of his anger, but the thick forest was as much a help as it was a hindrance to the attack.

He leaned over the side of his golden throne to glare down at the pale Radoch half a dozen feet away. Arcanist De’z stood, idly scratching at the glowing metal mask permanently screwed into her head.

“Did we at least get them?” he asked, his claws drumming on the armrests of his carried chair.

“Several smaller stars have been extinguished in the night. The brightest which drew my gaze has a way of masking their brilliance.” The smaller alien continued to look in the direction of the Outsiders.

Graken clicked his mandibles together in frustration. They had almost removed the threat of the unclean from this world. If that cha-lek hadn’t crashed the ships, then there were enough Radochs to squash or capture the zombie… but the appearance of this powerful magic user was a huge problem.

Yet also such a curiosity.

It was, of course, why he had taken all the troops his force field had saved and marched them over here. Killing the nuisances was great, but taking them for his own collection was something so alluring he couldn’t ignore. Especially since his ship had been blown up, and he needed to work out a way off this dying world.

“Want us to fire again, Your Eminence?”

He turned his eyes to the head engineer in charge of the cannon. The weapon itself was one of the few remaining pieces of his collection on this planet. A technological marvel when most System worlds were the backwater medieval type. It resembled a large lightbulb, with enough battery power stuck in the back to make it unwieldy in anything but a siege. The heat coming off the infernal weapon had wilted and charred any vegetation for over a dozen feet away from it.

Graken waved off the lithe, part-mechanical engineer dismissively. “That should be enough to put fear into them. Once we approach, you can change the aperture to erase a wider area. They will have nowhere to run.”

“As you wish, Your-”

The head engineer didn’t finish his sentence, as a foot-wide beam of crackling black energy cored through both him and the cannons internal mechanisms.

An explosion echoed out, heard for miles around.
