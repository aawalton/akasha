Aira blinked, waiting for the message to be passed through her friends, a little surprised. “Yes. I’ve always used a warhammer in battle. It’s... part of who I am. Or… was. I’ve lost it when I was transported to this world and never acquired a new one.”

Ronorrh stood up and started walking back and forth in agitation, her smirk returning, but this time with a more thoughtful edge. “Of course! It suits you! But I’ve been thinking about your path in this world, Aira. It all comes together. Destruction and creation. Fire and metal. And even something else, from the old world.”

She leaned against the anvil, crossing her arms. “You’ve lost your abilities, at least most of them, when you came to this place. But you’ve started to recover aspects of your power, haven’t you? And not through magic alone, but through something... different. Technology.”

Aira frowned slightly, but nodded. “It’s true. Tech has become... a kind of guide for me. It’s strange. I never relied on anything like it before, but now it feels like a focusing point, helping me regain some of what I’ve lost.”

Ronorrh smiled, her eyes glinting with understanding. “Exactly. Your connection with the Nexus runs deeper than you realize. But you’re not fully there yet. You need to strengthen that connection. To blend your past abilities with the new world you’ve stepped into.”

Aira tilted her head, intrigued. “What do you have on your mind? Stop teasing me, please spill it out!”

Ronorrh pushed off the anvil and began pacing slowly, her mind clearly racing. “You need a new weapon. One that reflects your journey in this world. Not just any weapon, but something that fuses who you were with who you’re becoming. Technology and the elements, working together. A warhammer, yes—but one that’s more than just a tool for battle.”

She stopped pacing and turned to Aira, her expression serious yet excited. "Oh, I'll craft you more than just a warhammer," Ronorrh's lips curved in a secret smile, eyes dancing with mischief. "I'll forge you a key—one that unlocks doors even Ainorrh doesn't know exist. A little tech miracle that speaks both your language and the elemental language of this world. The old wind-witch thinks she knows all the currents, but some flames burn hidden, even from her sight."

Aira raised an eyebrow, intrigued but unsure. “Tech... in a warhammer? How? Why?”

Ronorrh's lips curled into a mischievous grin as she danced her fingers through the air, conjuring little flame-butterflies that fluttered between them before dissolving into sparks. "Oh, it's not just possible—it's inevitable," she practically purred, circling Aira with the predatory grace of someone who'd just spotted a fascinating new toy. "You’ve seen some examples of, as Lila called it, magitech during your stay here.”

“We did, your advances are miraculous,“ said Aira through Lila. “But a warhammer? What sort of tech it could have?“

”Well, are you willing to wait one more day?" She snapped her fingers, and a small flame erupted from her palm, coalescing into a miniature warhammer that spun slowly above her hand. "I have a few ideas, but I don’t want to reveal all of them right away. What I can say is that it would be able to work as this sort of fulcrum you described. Focus your connection with the Nexus."

Aira felt the Shards pulsing in her pouch, the Rune on her wrist answering and joining with the rhythm that was dictated by the power of the Fire element Source. The tiny hammer in Ronorrh’s palm seemed to feel that connection and replicated the strange dance of arcane energies that went through Aira.

"Think of it as..." Ronorrh paused for dramatic effect, "an amplifier that turns your whisper into a roar, connecting both your tech, the elemental powers, and your Shards into something..." The flame-hammer exploded into a shower of embers that danced around them. "...transcendent."

Aira took a deep breath, absorbing everything Ronorrh had said. The idea of a warhammer that combined both technology and elemental magic was unlike anything she had ever imagined. In her precious life, in her previous world, nothing like that even existed. She could be angry at the System or whatever malevolent forces that put her through this ordeal. But she was absolutely sure at least in one thing: during the past year or so since she came to this world, she had learned more about its working and the cosmology than during her whole previous life.

And it felt... right. It felt like the next step in her journey.

“You’re sure this will work?” she asked, passing her words through Lila, as usual. Her voice was steady but filled with curiosity, which would probably be lost in the transfer of the information. “And what about the connection with the Shards?“

Ronorrh smirked, as if guessing Aira’s emotions. “Trust me, Aira. I’ve walked the path of a blacksmith my entire life. I’ve shaped metal, fire, and energy into things you wouldn’t believe. If anyone can forge a weapon like this, it’s us.” She gestured toward the forge, where the heat of the Emberwood Grove pulsed in rhythm with the glowing trees. “We’ll use the power of the Grove to start the process. You felt its power. You know our Sources are capable of much more than we can even think of doing with them… through them. The Shards are proof of that. And when we’re done, you’ll have a warhammer that’s unlike anything this world has ever seen. Probably even your world, if I’m allowed to make a wild guess like that!”

Aira nodded, feeling a surge of determination. “Let’s do it.”

Ronorrh clapped her hands, excitement filling her voice. “I’ll get my most skilled blacksmiths to join me immediately. We’ll start with the Smoldering Ember, and from there, the path will be yours to walk.”

As the forge fires roared to life before them, Aira felt a strange mixture of excitement and responsibility settle over her. The warhammer would be more than just a weapon. It would be a symbol of her journey, her connection to both the Nexus, the elements, and… her past. And with each shard, that connection would grow stronger—until the final piece was in place.

Aira's heart raced as she gazed upon the magnificent warhammer before her. It was a weapon she had longed for, but never thought she would find in this unfamiliar world. For over a year, she had explored ways to craft her favorite style of weapon, but to no avail. She had that poor substitute she had found in the ancient facility. But she didn’t even have a chance to test it in a battle. Not that Aira really wanted to complain about that. Still, she knew something was brewing. Ainorrh’s ambitions couldn’t lead to anything besides a bloody fight that would probably involve more than just Aira and her friends.

She chased off the stray thoughts and returned her attention to the warhammer before her. Human blacksmiths didn’t know magic, and enlightened people of Wentouk were so focused on their affinity, so that their tools weren’t good enough for Aira.

That is, until she stumbled upon Lamcada.

Aira hesitated to touch it, feeling an uncharacteristic uncertainty wash over her. Was this weapon truly the answer to their struggles and a path to their future? Or could it be a deceptive ploy from Ronorrh, waiting to turn the tables once all four shards were obtained? Should she wield it with pride or reject it entirely?

Despite her doubts, Aira took a step forward and grasped the warhammer tightly in both hands. It felt strangely familiar, as if it had been made specifically for her. The local blacksmiths had truly outdone themselves, fulfilling her every request with precision and skill. The Smoldering Ember was already embedded within its head. Aira was hesitant to forfeit one of the most powerful artifacts she had ever held in her hands to Ronorrh, but in the end, she decided that the proficiency of the local masters should be more than enough to balance the structure of the weapon and create the right connection.

Even now, her fingers paused over the Smoldering Ember, protective instinct warring with practical necessity. The Shard pulsed against her palm, almost sentient in its resistance to separation. Was it right to allow Ronorrh to decide where to put the Shards and how to use them? But then, she knew much more about elemental magic and energy than Aira ever had a chance to learn. These people had the power of the elemental Source behind them. If anyone could bind the Shard's power without doing something terribly wrong, it would be them.

From there, when the Shard was already embedded, they just had to replicate and iterate to create the slots for the other three Shards, one of which was waiting in Aira’s pouch. But even now, she could feel the powerful connection of her new weapon to the Rune hidden within her own body.

The time had come to give it a name—one worthy of its purpose and power. It was a key, as Ronorrh powerfully described it. But it was also something that had to bind all Shards, bind Aira’s power, and this world’s peculiar System together.

She looked at the powerful weapon, deep in thought. "Shardforger," she murmured, testing out the warhammer's name on her lips. "I like it." She ran her fingers along the handle, admiring its simple yet efficient design. This weapon would soon become an extension of herself in battle.

“Aira, we included something special within this weapon,“ said Ronorrh, addressing the woman directly. Aira was startled to hear her without Lila making anything to transfer the words through their group chat. “As I told you,“ Ronorrh continued, “we had some success in making communication with humans easier. That allowed us to build in within this weapon magitech that will help you to communicate with the enlightened without the need of interpreters.“ Ronorrh nodded to Lila.

“Oh…“ mumbled Aira, speechlessly amazed. “Oh…“

“What?“ said Ronorrh. “It should be working! Why can’t I hear you?“

“Thank you!“ said Aira. “I just don’t know what else to say! When I came to this world, I didn’t have any ability to talk to people. My Two is a Crowd skill helped a bit, but still, communication with the enlightened was quite stunted. You are truly making me whole with this warhammer. I can’t be more grateful!“

“Ah, I was joking,“ said Ronorrh. “I heard the strange sounds that came from you right away. If just couldn’t miss that opportunity. Anyway, the time you spent here was quite entertaining for all of us. And I hope that you take to heart the balance we were able to build with humans. If that’s what what we get out of this partnership, it would be more than enough.“

The warhammer gleamed in the light of Lamcada's eternal flames, its surface reflecting the dance of fire that surrounded them. Aira hefted Shardforger in her hands, feeling its perfect balance and the gentle hum of power resonating from the Smoldering Ember embedded within. The weapon felt like an extension of herself—something she'd lost when she was torn from her world and thrust into this strange one.

"It's magnificent," she said, her voice reaching the enlightened people without translation for the first time since her arrival in this world. The freedom to speak directly sent a surge of emotion through her that she hadn't anticipated. "I don't know how to thank you."

Ronorrh's eyes danced with flame as she circled the weapon, admiring her work. "Use it well. That's thanks enough." Her fingers traced the air near the empty slots where the other Shards would eventually rest. "Though I confess to a burning curiosity about what will happen when all four are united."

Lila stepped closer, amber eyes reflecting the weapon's gleam. "By the twisted branches, it's like watching forest and flame dance together. Never thought I'd see something so... balanced."

Alliot maintained his distance, arms crossed, as he studied the warhammer with a mixture of awe and apprehension. "Powerful. Dangerous." His eyes met Aira's. "Be careful."

Aira nodded, understanding the weight of his words. The warhammer represented more than just a weapon—it was a convergence point for everything they'd discovered, everything they'd fought for, and perhaps everything that was yet to come.

"Two Shards claimed, two more to get," she said, carefully inserting the Splinter of the Worldroot into the second slot. The effect was immediate—a pulse of energy that sent ripples through the workshop, causing the eternal flames to flicker momentarily, as if in acknowledgment. Earth and Fire, now united in a single vessel, their opposing natures finding harmony through her will.

The Rune embedded in her wrist flared with answering power, pulsing in accord with this world’s elemental currents. For the first time since her arrival, Aira felt something close to completeness—a sense that perhaps her presence here wasn't merely an accident or cosmic cruelty, but purpose. Step by step, she got her voice back, she was getting her powers back, or at least some semblance of what she had before. She was getting answers. At least to some of her questions. And her quest finally had a definitive goal, a final destination.

Ronorrh watched the interplay of energies with a knowing smile. "The path ahead won't be easy. Veskai may welcome you with open arms, but water can drown as easily as it sustains." Her eyes gleamed with mischief. "And Ainorrh? Well, the wind carries secrets even fire can't burn away."

"We'll be ready," Lila said, her hand instinctively reaching for Aira's shoulder. "High on the cliffs or lost in the shadows, we'll find our way through."

Alliot nodded, his usual stoicism softened. "Together."

Even Al squeaked his agreement from Aira's pocket, tiny claws gripping the fabric as if to emphasize the point.

As they prepared to leave Lamcada, gathering their few possessions and the knowledge they'd gained, Aira felt the weight of Shardforger at her side—a comforting presence that balanced the uncertainty ahead. The weapon wasn't just a tool for battle; it was a symbol of the bridges they'd built between worlds, between species, between elements that should have opposed one another but instead found harmony.

That night, she stood at the edge of the city, watching the flames dance against the obsidian walls, lava flowing in its perpetual motion. Veskai awaited, its watery depths holding the third piece of a puzzle she was only beginning to understand. Beyond that, Wentouk—and Ainorrh, whose plans remained as elusive as the wind itself.

"Whatever you're planning," she whispered to the distant stars, "I'm not your pawn anymore."
