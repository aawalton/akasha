I silently move through the dark house, crossing a hall without casting a shadow. The invisibility potion I took just before I broke in will last sixty seconds, so I need to move fast. My Soft Step skill triggers to avoid creaky floorboards.

That tells me this hit is for a mid-ranking gangster. In the desert city of Moonfall, wood floors are a status symbol—but creaky boards mean they got it second hand. My hit is no kingpin.

Not that I would expect them to be. The job postings left on the Blackcloak’s Guild wall are never for the leaders—that’s a recipe for a gang war. But second-in-commands, low level armsdealers, they’re all free game. I don’t know if the kingpins have a deal with the Assassin’s Guild, or if it’s just some sort of unspoken “thieves code” they all honor, but the hits are always for those who are replaceable. Maybe the real dangerous hits only take place at higher ranks.

Soon, I tell myself, silently easing a vial from the bandolier of potions I have strapped across my chest. In the two months I’ve spent in this city, I’ve managed to rise from Rank F jobs to Rank D. Tonight should get me to C, if everything goes off without a hitch. And the more dangerous jobs I can get my hands on, the faster I can Level Up and get stronger.

Hopefully—eventually—strong enough to kill a god.

I bite the cork stopper between my teeth and work it out of the vial. It opens with a quiet pop. I drop the cork into the palm of my left hand and hold it with my pinky and thumb—the only fingers I have left on that hand. Silently easing from foot to foot, I slip through the doorway and into the bed chambers.

A shadow is snoring lightly in the bed. There are no guards here, no traps—why would there be? Their precautions were to keep people from getting into the house, and I’ve already made it past all of that. Scoping out the scene, I’m relieved to find a pitcher of wine on a tray next to their bed. I make my way over and glance inside; half empty. Perfect.

Pouring it directly into their mouth, or leaving the poison on food or a toothbrush, is equally effective, but much more difficult to pull off. And when it’s left on food, it has the potential to kill someone else, first. But I recognize this pitcher as Lord Tena Dal’s personal chalice, which means no one else will be drinking from it. This kill will be a cakewalk. I hold my vial of poison out over the wine.

And I hesitate.

I narrow my eyes, mentally berating myself. I can’t get all this way and back out now. I’ve already taken the job. Messing up here will reflect poorly on my ranking. Besides, Tena Dal isn’t exactly a good guy. All I have to do is tip this vial over, and there will be one less thug in the world.

Talia’s lifeless face flashes through my mind.

“Are you sure you want to do that?”

I jump, very nearly dumping the entire bottle onto the bedside table. Slapping the cork back on top of the vial, I whip around, already reaching for my knife.

A shadow that hadn’t been there a moment ago is now seated on the dresser. One leg is casually folded over the other, elbow on her knee, cheek propped on her fist. Even in the dim moonlight, I can make out the white of her grin.

“Lisari,” I whisper through clenched teeth.

Tena Dal snorts, stirring beneath their sheets, and I freeze. Then they roll over, their breathing leveling off as they settle back to sleep. I release a silent breath, letting the tension out of my shoulders.

Lisari laughs. “Careful there! Don’t want to wake them up before you kill them. That would just be rude.”

I glare at the god. It figures she would show up now. Over the last few months she’s popped up a handful of times, always unannounced, completely invisible to everyone else, and right when I’m in the middle of something. I’m convinced she’s doing it just to mess with me.

As usual, I try to ignore her, and turn back to my hit, again lifting the vial of poison.

“I mean, it’s totally your call,” Lisari says. Or, rather, Shirasil. Sometimes the god shows up in his divine form, and other times he looks like the human scholar I came to know before he revealed his true identity. I prefer it when he looks like a god; every time he takes the form of the girl I thought I’d been friends with, it feels like a slap to the face.

“I’m not the expert in assassinations, here,” Shirasil continues. “You probably know something I don’t.”

I grind my teeth, hand hovering over the pitcher. After a couple seconds of extremely annoyed indecision, I draw my hand back and round on him.

“What do you mean?” I sign.

Cyros has been teaching me some basic sign language. Apparently it’s common to know among the Blackcloaks; communicating in silence is useful for assassins for obvious reasons. Unfortunately, missing most of the fingers on my left hand makes some words difficult, but I can usually get the point across.

The faintest tickle of a breeze brushes against my skin and stirs the covers on the bed. Shirasil is almost certainly to blame. The god is still wearing her—I mean, his—round black glasses; when I thought he was just a human alchemist, he’d claimed to be blind, and told me he used his wind magic to “see” his surroundings instead. It annoys me that he’s still keeping up the charade.

“I mean that the pitcher there could be artificed with a poison or hex-detecting spell,” Shirasil says. He lifts his shoulders in a casual shrug. “But I’m sure you already checked for that.”

He’s messing with me. He’s definitely messing with me, right? I narrow my eyes at the pitcher. Check, I think.

[Check,] Echo says into my mind. [A pitcher of wine.]

Great, super helpful. I’ve developed a bunch of poison-based powers now, which help me sense and identify types of poison, but nothing that lets me sense something that can sense poison. Would Tena Dal have an anti-poison pitcher? The gang leaders all would, but this grunt is further down the chain. It would be expensive to keep spells like that constantly charged in all their cups and utensils.

“To be honest, I’m not really sure if this is your calling,” Shirasil continues, hoping off the dresser. He meanders over to the other end of the bed, hands clasped behind his back as he peers down at my hit. “I mean, I’m all for you developing your alchemic abilities. What you did to Maru was absolutely delightful. And you’ll need to keep working on these skills if you have any hope of taking revenge on Widengra.”

I try to ignore him, instead bending down to look under the bed and Check anywhere else in the room where there might be hidden spell circles. I’d scoped the house out earlier this week, but Shirasil now has me feeling paranoid. So far, nothing is out of place.

“You’re destined for great things.” Shirasil’s glasses flash as they catch the dimmed light leaking in through the room’s window. “As my champion, you have incredible potential you’ve yet to embrace—a well of power you can tap into. But becoming a common street assassin?” He shakes his head. “You’re an agent of justice, not a murderer for hire. I don’t think you have it in you.”

My eye twitches. Oh, I don’t, do I? In one swift movement, I turn back to the pitcher and dump the poison into the wine.

A gem set into the handle begins to glow a deep, blood-red. Then it gets brighter. And brighter.

Crap. He was right about the anti-poison hex.

“So distrustful,” Shirasil exclaims, though his smile somewhat undercuts his wounded tone. “Well, at least this should be fun to watch!”

The glowing gem is now painfully bright, and the room has become stained red—it’s almost certainly visible through the window. It’s time to go.

Stuffing the empty vial into a pocket, I draw my knife. Tena Dal stirs beneath the blankets. I squeeze the handle of the blade, steeling myself. The hit didn’t have to be done by poison, it would just be less messy that way. I raise the knife.

Images of Captain Enrold slam through my mind. His twitching body. My hands tacky with blood. The metallic smell—

Footsteps pound against the floor of the house, rushing my way, and Tena Dal sits bolt-upright in bed. I flinch back as they look around the room wildly, but I’m still invisible. Not for much longer, though. I’m standing there, knife still drawn, only one lunge away from finishing this job.

A figure bursts into the room behind me, and I dash away, racing for the window.

“What’s going on?” Tena Dal demands. “Where—”

A throwing knife whistles by me, embedding itself in the wall right next to the window. They must have based it off my footsteps, which means they’re going to be a whole lot more accurate once they can see me. I instinctively grab for a smoke bomb, but my bad hand slips off the bottle. Swearing under my breath, I sheath my knife and grab the smoke bomb with my good hand. At the same time, I make it to the window.

Without looking back, I throw the bottle at the floor. A second knife flashes past, this time with a burn of pain across my right shoulder.

[3 points of Slashing damage sustained.]

I hiss in pain, but the damage is superficial, and the System will heal it soon enough. I put a hand to the glass as the room erupts in black smoke.

Tena Dal cries out in surprise, their body guard swears, and Shirasil laughs. The smoke obscures even the tiniest hint of light, and the bottle it was in hisses as it begins to disintegrate. Rule number 1 of being a Blackcloak: don’t leave anything behind that could be used to track you down.

I punch through the locked window, and blinding daylight stabs into my eyes as the glass shatters and falls to the stone streets far, far below. I hoist myself up and through the gap, adrenaline coursing through my body, and scramble up a pipe fixed to the sandstone wall outside. Like most houses in Moonfall Kingdom, the city is built vertically, running up the cavern walls of a hollowed out plateau. The ground level is several stories below me, but one of the city’s countless stone bridges crosses overhead just one story up, and it’s that escape route I’m making for.

[Invisibility potion expired.]

I hardly pay the notification any mind as I scurry up the wall, rough stone scraping against my fingers, the hot desert air wicking moisture from the corner of my eyes, which have watered in response to the sudden and painful sunlight. Beneath me, black smoke continues to vomit from the broken window. Shirasil hasn’t followed me out. But knowing him, he’ll already be sitting up on the bridge waiting for me, sipping a cold drink as I scramble over the ledge. That would just—

A burst of piercing damage numbers explode in my vision as pain sears through my right shoulder.

[You have sustained Minor Poisoning.]

[Poisoning expired due to Poison Resistance.]

That guard tried to poison me? I’m not sure if I’m more amused or offended. The knife stuck in my shoulder is no laughing matter, however, and when I reach for the ledge, white-hot pain jolts through my arm, and my grasp slips. My weight falls on my left side, jerking my weak grip from my handhold. My stomach leaps into my throat as I start to fall.

[Noxious Gauntlet activated.]

A glowing green mist swirls to life around my left hand, forming the visage of an oversized claw. I dig the ghostly hand into the stone, and it bites into the rock like talons sinking into wood. The weight of my body falls painfully onto my shoulder as I’m yanked to a stop. Blood rushes in my ears, and my heart beats like a drum in my chest. That was too close.

Hanging from the Gauntlet, I brace my legs against the wall and grab another potion from my belt with my freehand. I rip the cork off with my teeth, then pour the contents down the face of the wall. The potion starts to hiss and spit the second it comes in contact with the stone, and I don’t stick around to watch it eat through the wall. There was enough in that bottle that it should drip down to the windowsill I escaped from. With any luck—

Someone screams from below.

[10 points of Acid damage dealt.]

Bingo. Whether it got on the guard’s head or hand, I shouldn’t be dealing with more throwing daggers anytime soon. I stash the empty bottle and resume my climb.

I can’t dislodge the knife in my shoulder while clinging to the side of a building, so I grit my teeth and do my best to ignore the pain as I haul myself upward. Luckily the sandstone buildings are covered in cracks and nooks, so I can almost scale it one-handed anyway.

I haul myself over the side of the bridge without any further interruptions. Mercifully, Shirasil isn’t already here to gloat. But I don’t have time to be relieved; the smoke is going to draw attention. The second my feet hit the ground, I’m off running, grabbing a second invisibility potion to lose anyone who might be on my tail.

I grab the cap with the Noxious Gauntlet, then snort in disgust, dispelling the god’s blessing. The claw disperses into a puff of green smoke, which condenses around my wrist in the form of an unassuming bracelet. It looks like it should fall right off my wrist, but it can’t actually be removed. Or burned, bent, or broken, for that matter. I know; I’ve tried.

The ‘gift’ from Shirasil is annoyingly stubborn. And it irritates me all the more that I keep using it. Granted, only when I’m desperate, but that somehow makes it worse. Every time I activate the weapon, it feels like Shirasil wins.

Taking my frustration out on the invisibility potion, I once more bite the cork with my teeth and yank it free. The potion evaporates into the surrounding air in a burst of pressure, enveloping me before the white mist vanishes—and I vanish along with it.

So does a portion of the bridge, but it’ll be back in a minute.

I yank the dagger out of my shoulder next, and my vision flashes white with pain as I stumble. I manage to keep my feet beneath me, however, and I toss the dagger into an alley as I run, so it can’t be used to track me. Heat trickles down my arm and back. The System will heal the wound given enough time, but I don’t want to leave a trail of blood—plus it really hurts. I uncork a healing potion next, haphazardly splashing it over my shoulder. The pain dulls, and I keep running.

I take a sinuous route through the city, making sharp turns, dashing across more bridges, sliding down to lower levels. I don’t think anyone followed me, but it can’t hurt to be careful. If a Blackcloak inadvertently leads an outsider back to a Guild location, they are immediately expelled—generally by means of death.

I’ve slowed down and my breathing has settled by the time I reach the Guild’s entrance: an old crumbling statue of Rinviu forgotten to time on the backside of the god’s temple. Even so, I take a moment to comb a hand through my hair, tightening the ribbon that keeps it pulled back from my face so I look a little less frazzled. Nothing I can do about the blood in my clothes, but wearing all black helps disguise it. I pull my scarf down from around my face, and step into the stone effigy.

It’s an illusion, of course, but I wouldn’t be able to step down into the hidden staircase without the magical badge on my scarf that permits me access. I have three now: one that lets me into Blackcloak locations, a second that gives me access to the alchemic tools cache, and a third that marks me as being qualified for D rank jobs. I wrinkle my nose as I step out the bottom of the stairwell, and a large stone hall opens up before me. Guess I won’t be getting that C rank tonight afterall.

The room is a giant sandstone cavern, full of stalagmites, stalactites, and pillars of natural rock that obscure most of the room. Small red lights drift around the ceiling, casting long, shifting shadows across the walls. They’re bright enough to see by, but dim enough not to kill your night vision, though many of the nocturnal inhabitants of this city don’t even need this much light to see. Whispers echo through the chamber as a handful of darkly clad members and clients find remote corners to speak in, and doorless openings spackle the walls, spellcircles engraved in the stone above their entryways, glowing with the subtle warning of magic. The room smells like clay and sharp herbs.

I release a sigh, letting my shoulders slump. Home sweet home.
