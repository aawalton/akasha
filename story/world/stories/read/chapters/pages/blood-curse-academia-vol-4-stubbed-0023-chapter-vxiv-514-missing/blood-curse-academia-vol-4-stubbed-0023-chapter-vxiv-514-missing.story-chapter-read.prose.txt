Chapter V.XIV (5.14) - Missing

Kizu growled at the summoning circle sketched on the stone he kneeled on. The chalk snapped in his fingers.

“All attempts to glare the summoning circle into submission have proven folly in my experience,” Sene said beside him. “Though continue on if it makes you feel better. And let me know if it results in anything more than a headache.”

Kizu bit the inside of his cheek, then turned his attention to his girlfriend’s twin. The only other student in the class who lacked any affinity or ability to conjure anything.

“I can’t be ranked 800 in this,” Kizu said. “I need to be better.”

“I’ve been saying those same words for three years. And last semester fate finally let me rank up. Not because I became better, but because someone worse appeared.” Sene knelt down next to him to examine his circle. “You need to connect those marks at a sharper angle. At least 8 degrees less. And this section has one too many loops.”

“I don’t think I can count on hundreds of other students becoming worse than me,” Kizu replied while he used an elemental spell to extract the chalk dust from the stone, erasing the mark.

“I am simply relaying the only positive result from my personal experience.”

The other students in Conjuring F all had mice, of varying levels of success, emerging from their summoning circles. With the second half of the semester now in motion, Professor Oasaji had moved their material forward into conjuring living creatures. Most students still failed to summon a perfect mouse. Many had extra limbs, eyes, tails. But they all managed something. Everyone save for him and Sene.

“My mother mentioned you’ll be having dinner with her tonight,” Sene said conversationally. “Are you bringing your father along as well?”

“Absolutely not.” Kizu’s heart rate doubled whenever he thought about the upcoming dinner. He shuddered to think of the hypothetical meeting with his father in tow.

Sene shrugged, a very Ione-like gesture that felt wrong on the uptight girl. “Just a thought. Has my sister met your father yet?”

“Briefly during spring break.”

“But not since you became romantically entangled?” Sene pressed. “Or were you two seeing one another last semester as well?”

Kizu bit back a sigh. This felt more like an interrogation than a conversation. “I mentioned it to you like a week after we started dating. Do you think Ione would have gone to the dance with Faible last semester if she was seeing me?”

“I will not pretend to understand my sister’s motivations. The thought did occur to me that this had been kept secret in an attempt to avoid confrontation with my parents over spring break. I know many find them intimidating individuals.”

“Why would I do that? I was scheduled to meet with your parents at the end of Parent’s Day but Dragons invaded the island. Or did you miss that?”

Sene tensed. It took Kizu a moment to understand why. The perfect student, the top combatant in the academy, had missed the invasion. While Kizu and the professors fought back the sudden attack and even Ione had vanquished undead, Sene had been nowhere to be found. Likely practicing a speech for the visiting parents she never delivered.

The bell dismissed them, shattering the suddenly uncomfortable atmosphere. Kizu silently thanked fate for watching out for him for the first time ever, then said his farewells to Sene before a hasty retreat.

“Kaga, clean up your station,” Professor Oasaji demanded.

Kizu turned on a heel, and with a wave of his hand, washed the stone clean from afar with splash water, then escaped before the professor could voice any further complaints.

Basil had promised Kizu to help him with his outfit for tonight and so he made his way over to the third year boy’s dormitory. It had been a long time since his last visit to the dorms.

As he stepped through the painting that acted as the dorm’s entryway, he was greeted by several students in his year. They lounged around in the common room on the plush furniture, a fire crackled in the hearth. A layer of clouds floated by outside on the other side of the windows.

“What are you doing here?” one of them asked with a sneer. It took Kizu a second to clock the boy as the one he’d decked in the face on his first day at Shinzou Academy. In hindsight, it probably hadn’t been the most friendly way to clear up that misunderstanding. And it clearly hadn’t taught his peer any sort of meaningful lesson, as he seemed to be repeating the same greeting from their first encounter.

“I have a dorm room here? We’ve been over this,” Kizu said.

“After all these weeks you just happen to show up now of all times? Yeah, right.”

“Now?” Then Kizu realized what the student was implying. He rolled his eyes, exasperated. “I’m not here to steal anyone’s tokens. I just want a change of clothes.”

“A means in which to deceive someone and rob them!”

“Are you…dense? Do you not recall me casting illusions when we met? I don’t need clothes to disguise myself.” Kizu snapped his fingers. But rather than alter his own clothing, he changed the appearance of his interrogator's academy uniform.

In the student’s place was a dopey looking human woman in a barmaid’s outfit Kizu had seen down in the town a few times.

Raucous laughter erupted from the other boys in the room. The victim of the illusion jolted and looked over his shoulder out the tall windows swiftly scanning the clouds below for any sign of illusions. It took him several seconds before he realized the laughter and all eyes were directed at him, not some illusion behind him.

“What have you done?” the boy squeaked, looking down at the scantily clad clothing he now wore. Kizu’s attempt to alter his voice was a valiant effort, but it came out far too screechy for a natural voice. He needed a bit more work on his auditory illusions.

The boy tried to pull his illusionary skirt down to cover up his thighs, but of course his hand passed through the imaginary cloth.

“I—I won’t forget this!” the boy squealed. Then he hurried off to his dorm room, laughter and cat calls following him. He tripped on his feet as he exited. Then scrambled up and disappeared from sight.

“Nice work on the facial expressions,” a Tainted third-year with scarlet scales said to Kizu. “You managed to translate over a lot of Minato’s emotions while keeping up the illusion the entire time.”

“Thanks. It helps that I didn’t actually change his face very much,” Kizu replied. “Now am I good to go meet up with my roommate? Anyone else suspicious of me being here?”

“Oh, we’re all suspicious.” The Tainted boy who’d complimented him flashed a grin. “But we won’t stand in your way. You’re allowed here as much as any of us.”

Kizu nodded his thanks and passed through the common room on his way to the dorm rooms. All the boys watched him. Some with amusement, others with nervous expressions. Kizu ignored them all.

He swung open his dorm room and found the place in a familiar state of disarray. Stacks of clothing piled high to either side of him. What once had been his bed now served as an extra closet, with an enchanted hanging rack that rotated dresses, gowns, and suits. The scent of different colognes and perfumes assaulted his senses and he rubbed his nose.

“Basil? Are you in here?” Kizu called into the clutter.

“Yes, yes. One second Kizu.” While he could hear Basil’s voice, Kizu couldn’t see the shapeshifter anywhere. It reminded him a bit of Yvonne’s ability to haunt Vincent’s property. But then Basil finally tumbled out from behind a mound of cloth. He wore the body Kizu had become accustomed to him using, and held up a fresh set of clothing like spoils of war.

“Thankfully I have all your measurements memorized,” Basil said. “No time to get an enchanter to add the final touches on this. Not with the time table you asked for. But it should serve well enough to make a decent enough impression with a family of minor nobility. You at the very least won’t embarrass yourself in this. Though I still have a few finishing touches I want to apply to the costume. Now, lift your arms.”

Kizu did as instructed and Basil clipped something to the sleeve of his academy uniform.

“This is a little something I’ve been experimenting with,” Basil said. “It should allow you to swap between outfits swiftly. Replaces whatever I have on that mannequin over there. So I’ll keep working on it for another hour and when you’re ready to wear the outfit, simply remove the clip.”

Kizu gaped at the mannequin Basil had gestured towards. Little more than a torso with legs and stumps for arms. But now that he knew what to look for, the object blazed when he flared his spellsense.

“Basil. Is that spatial magic?”

“Yeah. I got the idea when I was helping out with your portal for our Enchanting C final. It’s a really convenient branch of spellcraft when combined with enchanting. But I’ve mostly been focused on expanding it in ways that can be applied for clothing. Don’t worry, I have no interest in stealing your speciality. It’s a pain to practice anyway.”

Kizu stepped up to the mannequin and ran a finger over its bare chest. “Who taught you how to do this? This isn’t a novice’s work.”

“Oh, same as you. I asked Wakino. Aoi convinced her to help me realize my designs.”

“Why couldn’t you put this much effort into our project last semester?”

“You and your brother more than had it all under control. Plus, it wasn’t nearly as interesting as this,” Basil replied nonchalantly.

Kizu closed his eyes and bit back a retort. Right now Basil was doing him a favor. Better to not get back into this argument. Instead, he reached into his jacket pocket and brought out two strips of paper. “Here, I brought you something as thanks for helping.”

Basil’s face lit up. “A gift! You shouldn’t have!” In a swift motion, the papers in Kizu’s hand were replaced by a bundle of clothing. Basil lifted one of the papers and his eyes darted across it as he read.

“It’s a ticket to a griffin race,” Kizu said. “I thought you could take Aoi and I would take Ione.”

“A double date!” Basil said, delight clearly sprawled across his face. “Are you able to get all four of us into Port Kallis? Ah! This will be so fun! And the date listed is for this weekend! Luckily, my schedule just opened up. The party I planned to attend was canceled due to a Dragon damaging the venue!”

“Speaking of Port Kallis, will there be any problems getting Aoi into Tross?” Kizu asked, the problem just now occurring to him. “She is a princess of a different county.”

“Doubt it. Animosity is between Tross’ federal government and more local entities. A civil war. Not an international conflict as far I’m aware. Hon and Tross are both technically buddies at the moment.”

“Oh, good.” Kizu glanced down at the clothes in his hands. “Do you want me to dress the mannequin before I leave?”

“Nah, I’ll take care of that. You just try on the clothes. I’ll modify it based on how you look.”

Kizu’s eyes wandered to the exit. He dearly hoped this wouldn’t turn into an hour of being poked and prodded by Basil. But he still did as instructed and stripped out of his academy uniform.

As Kizu removed his pants, he frowned and patted his pocket. It felt…empty. That couldn’t be right though. He reached inside and found nothing within. His eyes widened and he checked the other pockets. Then crouched on the ground, scouring the menagerie of clothing around him.

“You lose something?” Basil asked.

“Yes. You could say that,” Kizu replied, his heart sinking. “My token. It’s gone.”
