While he hadn’t mentioned it to the Magisters or the city watch, Edge had a theory about who had flung those stones, pouring fuel on the fire at the worst possible moment.

There wasn’t a single person living in World’s End who would benefit from the city’s collapse, which led him to believe the instigator wasn’t a person at all. The timing between the near riot and Rue destroying the dimensional anchor was too tight to be a coincidence. His gut was telling him the Ancient was still in the free city, and the creature had chosen another tactic after its invasion was thwarted.

It might be behind the disappearances in Lowtown too. There have been rumors of monsters inside the walls for weeks. What a clusterfuck. Dealing with the factions is trouble enough, but we might be the only ones who are aware of the Ancients’ existence.

In light of this development, Edge decided to head to the inn instead of engaging in his usual nighttime activities. Concocting plans could wait until morning, when he had a clear head and a pot of coffee to think the matter through.

His team must have felt the same way, because everyone was there when he arrived.

Trapper had invited Astil and Lanna to join them for dinner. He took a seat in the common area, relaxing and playing with Rue while the crew’s leader worked her magic in the kitchen. The food was delicious. The young guide and the motherly innkeeper excused themselves after clearing the table. The crew needed a break before he added another crisis to the pile, so he didn’t mention what had happened just yet.

Edge chatted with his friends for another hour or so before taking a long shower and heading to bed. He held Mel tight when she slid between the covers, letting the warmth of her love lull him into a deep and dreamless sleep.

The next morning, everyone gathered around the table. Before they discussed other matters, he brought them up to speed with the incident in the mixing bowl.

“Shit,” Jumo swore. “I need to stop by Lowtown before I do anything else. I won’t mention the Ancients or the monsters’ dimension until we figure out who we can trust, but Frederick needs to know that someone is creating friction between the rulers of the city and its poorest residents.”

“We need to get a handle on the situation without drawing attention to ourselves,” Sakura added. “I doubt the Ancient is involved in the Syndicate’s activities, or Lowtown would be a living hell already, but I’ll head down there soon and see if I can sense tainted magic near their base of operations. I’ll look into the disappearances while I’m there, since it’s the only lead we have at the moment.”

“When you’re done,” Trapper said, “I want you to investigate the Hunters’ Association. They are our best bet for reliable allies in dealing with this mess. I doubt their leadership has been infiltrated, since they don’t have the same kind of power as the city’s other factions, but I need to be absolutely certain before I approach them. I would do it myself, but even with the trait I got from the System, I can’t sense corrupted magicytes clearly enough to clear their names. We’re lucky you’re mana sensitive now or we would be flying blind.”

“I should be able to help after I evolve and equip Monster Slayer.” Edge sighed and turned to his fellow scion. “Be extremely careful. My instincts are screaming that the Ancient will eliminate it catches investigating its activities, and Skill-Eater said we can’t hope to defeat it. That might change after we form an alliance or two, but right now, we need to do everything in our power to stay off its radar. We already have more trouble than we know what to do with.”

With that, the hunters moved on to other matters.

They didn’t know how the situation with the Coalition was going to play out, let alone the Wardens. There wasn’t much the crew could do about the planet-wide war looming larger by the day, let alone an invasion from another plane of existence. Although they had accomplished incredible feats and grown stronger at an unprecedented rate, they were small fish in an ocean filled with sharks, and leviathans dwelled within the unfathomable deep.

Edge intended to advance enough to challenge the Ancients, the Wardens, and anyone threatened his home. If he wanted to live long enough to make that dream a reality, he had to keep closing the gap and focus on the goals he could achieve here and now.

Cycling up, ranking up, gearing up, and earning Credits was an ongoing priority, as was obtaining the information they needed to stay one step ahead of their enemies. At the same time, the crew needed to keep their eyes open for opportunities that could benefit their people back home.

He wasn’t ready to hunt big game until he had some empty skill slots, but he needed to advance in other ways while he waited for his Epic skill to finish digesting. There wasn’t anything he wanted to buy until the next auction, but he knew another way to get his hands on some interesting items that weren’t generally available, pick up some valuable combat experience, and investigate a vital component of the free city at the same time.

In short, Edge intended to visit the Grand Arena of World’s End and register as a gladiator, something he’d hoped to do since long before coming to Ord but hadn’t been practical until now. Melenia was just as eager to step into the ring, so they decided to head out together, complete an assessment match, and obtain their starting rung.

Rubbing elbows with the free city’s gladiators was also a fantastic opportunity to forge relationships with powerful warriors who weren’t affiliated with any of the settlement’s gangs, guilds, or factions, so their efforts might be rewarded in other ways too.

After his friends shared their plans for the day, he bid them farewell and left the Lost. Edge held Mel’s hand as they walked toward the sprawling structure rising from the center of the mixing bowl—the beating heart of the most famous city in inhabited space. Along the way, they reviewed everything they knew about the establishment and the contests it held.

“I wouldn’t mind participating in some challenge matches to hone my technique,” he said, “but I’m mostly interested in taking home the grand prize in one of the major exhibition matches.”

“That would be awesome,” she replied. “I would love to join you, although I’m more interested in fighting skilled weapon wielders and climbing the specialty ladders. I was an elite fighter in Puppet Town, but the top gladiators make me look like a novice by comparison. Either way, fighting in the arena will let us engage in life-and-death battles without having to worry about killing anyone or being killed ourselves. It’s an opportunity to pick up real-world combat experience unlike anything else on Ord, and I’m eager to cross blades with my betters.”

“I know exactly what you mean.”

Edge had mostly been fighting monsters and beasts during his early adventures, but the war with the Claws had revealed the need to broaden his horizons. The factions’ elites were all skilled warriors, as were the high-stage hunters, jailbirds, and gladiators living in World’s End. In fact, almost anyone he came across in the extreme-threat biomes surrounding the Lakelands was likely to be more proficient with their weapon than he was.

He needed to be able to hold his own against professional killers, and despite his astonishing rate of advancement, he was woefully lacking in technique, knowledge, and experience. Battling gladiators in the arena while being trained by Beelo was his best bet at bridging that gap, although he would keep an eye out for powers that could help with the problem as soon as he evolved. However, that would have to wait a little longer, so he returned his attention to the task at hand.

There were thousands of gladiators who called the free city home, and it was the biggest commercial enterprise in the settlement. The arena was always hungry for fresh blood to keep the audience entertained, and assessment matches were conducted daily. Once Mel and Edge were assigned starting rungs, they could start climbing the ladder, clearing the requirements to qualify for exhibition matches and challenge other fighters.

Some of the events were restricted by stage, and Edge was at the peak of stage two, giving him a significant advantage in those bouts, or an “edge” as Melenia called it, giggling at the wounded expression on his face after hearing her latest pun.

They wrapped up their discussion as they stepped into an annex he hadn’t visited before, which dealt with gladiator administration, challenge scheduling, match sponsorship, and the like. An entire floor was dedicated to new-member registration and orientation. The duo grinned as they climbed the stairs and stepped into a room filled with would-be champions.

As it happened, today’s group was unusually large. Attendants holding clipboards were walking around while scribbling down notes, overseen by a grizzled clerk who looked like he’d gone through this a thousand times before and probably had.

While they waited to complete their registration, they chatted with their fellow applicants, sizing up people they might be facing off against before the day was through. Edge spent most of his time talking to a beefy fellow who went by Bison.

Bison had been living in the free city for years. He had managed to scrounge enough Credits to purchase a core seed two months before the anomaly. He had gone on some curated hunts in a medium-threat biome that used to border the Lakelands, evolving to stage one in the process.

“When the disaster occurred,” the man continued, “that avenue of growth was cut off. I’m not strong enough to tackle the beasts around World’s End, and while I’m lucky enough to have a job in the industrial sector, I can’t afford to buy the gear I need or pay to cycle up under the protection of experienced crews.

“While it’s risky in its own way, I want to fight in the area, earn enough creds to reach stage two, then resume my hunting career, picking up some combat experience and skill ranks along the way. I can earn some cycles in fights against monsters, since they let you kill them, and pick up a few Mortium once I’m strong enough to take on stage-twos.”

Edge listened with one ear, learning more about life in the free city before an attendant arrived to take down his information. He asked why they were using pencils and paper instead of magitech recordkeeping tools. It turned out that half of all applicants never returned after their evaluation match, and this saved room in the database. She jotted down his name, stage, and society ranks, then then moved on to Mel with a brisk efficiency.

They were handed a number to pin to their clothing, and not long after, everyone was processed and a senior staff member addressed the crowd.

“Welcome, prospective gladiators, to the Grand Arena of World’s End. My name is Krix. I’m going to provide an overview of today’s proceedings, offer a quick tour of the facilities available to low-rung members, then take you to the area where your evaluation matches will be held. By the time we’re done, you will have been assigned a starting rung and learned everything you need to know about participating in our competitions.”

Krix’s tone grew serious as he finished his speech. “Before we continue, there are a few ground rules. I know many of you are hotblooded but save it for the ring. If you give me lip or cause a scene, I’ll kick your ass out and blacklist you until the start of next season. Follow me, save your questions until the end of the presentation, and we’ll get to the fun part soon.”
