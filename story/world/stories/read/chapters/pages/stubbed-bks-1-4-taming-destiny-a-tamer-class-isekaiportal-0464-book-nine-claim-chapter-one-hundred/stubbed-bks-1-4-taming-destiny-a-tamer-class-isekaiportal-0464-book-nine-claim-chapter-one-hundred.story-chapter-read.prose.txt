A chime rings through the air as Elarion taps his glass gently with his eating knife. The sound should be limited to the immediate area, but, thanks to a friendly breeze, it carries over the whole area. The faint smile in the corners of Josiah’s mouth indicates that he is the source of the magic.

In response to the signal, the mass of people filling the District one marketplace quieten.I swallow as all eyes turn to fix themselves on us. On me.

The other Great House heir sends me a look that is equal parts encouraging and amused at my expense.

“Lord Markus, you have the floor,” he invites quietly.

I send him a quick glare, then slowly rise to my feet. I know this speech was planned, but that doesn’t make giving it any easier.

The marketplace is filled with hastily-made tables, with only just enough space between them for the servers to move – and now all the food has been delivered and is mostly consumed, even they are filling up tables to the side nearest the command centre kitchen. As many people as we could squeeze into the space are here – slaves and free, Classers and non-Classers sitting cheek by jowl.

Unfortunately, some have to miss the celebration – a skeleton crew are needed to man the ships, and the wall cannot be left unguarded. However, even they have received a delivery of plenty of food and some of our remaining stores of drink – not enough to prevent them from carrying out their duties, of course.

Even without those worthy souls, it’s a lot of people. Still, as my gaze flickers over those arrayed before me, I find my nerves lessening rather than increasing.

Here, I see people I’ve healed; there, are people I’ve fought with. On this table there are those I’ve laughed with; on that one, there are those whose tears I have done my best to stem. These are not strangers: they’re my people.

If the king called an end to the competition today, I would still most likely stay. And it would be because of these people who hoped that coming to this land would change their fortunes – or for those who never chose to come, but have made the best of it nonetheless.

Through starvation, illness, beast attack, and even parasitic possession, these people have never lost faith, have never turned from our shared objective. And now, feasting on the meat harvested from the very army of the beast that sought to destroy us, they are revitalised. And with them, so is my hope that we’ll get through this. That we can make something amazing here.

The realisation that I am speaking to those who are already willing to listen unlocks my throat, allows me to speak where before I was frozen. I nod to myself, deciding in that moment to almost completely throw my carefully prepared script out of the  figurative window.

“When you imagined settling the Lost Continent, I doubt any of you thought it was going to be quite this difficult,” I start with a wry tone to my voice. “Did anyone accidentally forget to sacrifice to Lord Luck before our journey?” I ask, half-joking. “Or offend a deity or five?” There are a number of rueful chuckles in response. At the same time, I can already feel the exasperation of Elarion and Layton at my ad libbing. They’ll have to get over it.

The amusement dies away as my expression turns serious. “I have to admit that I, too, underestimated just how challenging it would be to create a foothold,” I continue, my words carrying easily even without magic. “Our food situation alone has been more difficult than anticipated, and worsened by the other circumstances, at least some of which were purely due to bad luck.” The manastorm, for example. “However, we’re still here.”

I pause for a moment, my eyes playing over the silent crowd filling the large square.

“When we decided to have this feast, we announced that it was to celebrate the victory over the Tier four that has caused us so much trouble. But now, I prefer to consider it as a celebration of the victory of every one of us.”

Several people look confused at that. Understandable.

“The victory that is never giving up. We sit here today not because of the actions of one or two people, but thanks to the whole community pulling together and doing what needed to be done. And we are all the stronger for it.”

I pause for a moment, letting that sink in. “We sit here thanks to the healers who faced increasing illness, dwindling healing resources, and a threat they had no idea how to manage, and yet continued to offer the help they could – healers who now know how to detect and deal with any other parasite that might try to overcome us, as well as more about how to heal without recourse to magical aids.

“We sit here thanks to the alchemists who faced a land of unknown reagents and increasingly urgent demands on their time, and yet who kept on moving forwards methodically – who re-discovered how to craft several of our potions when we needed them most.

“We sit here thanks to the fighters, who every day face a dangerous forest that could easily kill them – fighters who are so much stronger now than they were when we arrived.

“We sit here thanks to the crafters who have adapted and developed their craft to repair and replace tools, weapons, and armour, learning about new effects the local materials can have on the finished works.

“We sit here thanks to the fishermen who contributed much-needed food – who are now accompanied by a local Tier three sea serpent when they journey out to sea.

“And we must not overlook the staff who have kept everything running even when they were exhausted, sick, or starving. The farmers whose animal husbandry and continued efforts in farming are the bedrock of our new settlement. The leaders who have offered steady guidance and direction.

“We sit here thanks to each, and every one of you. And so, to all of you, I say, from the bottom of my heart, thank you.”

I dip my head low to the crowd, the move eliciting several murmurs. Slowly, the murmurs turn into cheers, and then the cheers spread until the whole cavern seems to ring with the sounds.

Lifting my head again, I smile, turning slowly to try to meet the gaze of as many people as I can.

This isn’t the end of my speech – though I’m genuinely grateful to every single person here, there are some whose efforts need to be individually recognised and rewarded. River. Layton. Thessalanor. Sera Kerst. Sera Isolde. Elarion. Loran. Josiah. Valence. Sarran. And so many other heroes who may not have been obvious, but whose efforts truly were invaluable.

More sadly, there are some names we need to honour in their absence – those who were killed in the line of duty. Though casualties were amazingly low for the number of beasts we faced when attacking the Tier four, there were still some.

But in this moment, I’m just grateful to be here, to be with these people, and to be able to explore another amazing – and dangerous – land of opportunity with those I love.

We had incredibly bad luck in our starting weeks – and I know that I dropped the ball in certain areas and swear to do better – but we’re still here. We’re still fighting. And we’re all the stronger for it.

Whatever this continent has to throw at us next…

We’ll be ready.

As the moon rises over a silent hill, a pale-furred paw steps onto a blood-soaked ground, its owner making no effort to hide. A muzzle scrunches up as a massive beast inhales the scents that linger upon the charnel ground.

Blood. Death. Fire.

And unmistakably, the traces of an old foe. One who he had thought dead long ago.

The beast snarls, his domain exploding and scaring away any that had dared to come to feast on the scraps of battle.

It dares to encroach on his territory, believing that because its base lies beyond his control, it will be left alone. It had to know it had declared a challenge the moment it sent a worm-ridden beast across the river.

Padding forward purposefully, he begins to prepare his magic. This time, it will not escape him – he’ll blast it until the rock beneath its slime turns to glass.

However, as he reaches the gaping mouth that he knows leads to a warren of tunnels below ground, he slows. The scent of fire is strong here – stronger than he had expected.

Caution makes him pause at the threshold. There is little of the slime he was expecting – almost all of it is blackened ashes that coat the tunnel in a blackness darker than the night sky.

Grim amusement drifts through him. It seems he’s too late – his old foe’s ambitions have been a quicker end of it than he was expecting. Reaching out with his domain, he confirms it – he feels the distinct lingering traces of an Immortal’s death.

Good news or not, this bears investigating – his old foe was not powerful in the same way he is, but those who can kill an Immortal might pose a threat even to him.

The beast inhales again, finding the newest traces of the victorious force, intending on following them back to their den. As he does so, he scents something oddly familiar, yet…different.

He inhales more deeply, tasting each scent as he identifies it. Finally, he separates the one that had caught his attention.

He stills, his eyes going wide.

Then, narrowing his eyes into slits, he shifts into an unconsciously threatening position, his domain once more snapping out ferociously – though there are none left to scare away.

Kalanthia! he snarls.
