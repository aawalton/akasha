Chapter 367

“I, uuh… Oops?” Eik muttered as he looked at his bloodied hand. “I swear that wasn’t on purpose! She just came out of nowhere, man! She scared the shit out of me!”

Not only Eik and Andihar, but the entire room was absolutely covered in blood. The extreme force with which Eik’s hand had struck the Oracle had propelled everything outward in a high pressure burst.

“I have told you a million times…” Andihar groaned, fingers pinching his forehead in frustration, smearing the blood around his skin even more. “to control yourself better. You’re way too reckless, Eik.”

“I didn’t mean to do it! Oh, my god, I killed someone!” Eik was taking deep, calming breaths.

“You’ve killed literally thousands of people before.”

“Yeah, but those were cultists!”

Andihar raised an eyebrow pointedly without saying the obvious.

“That was different!” Eik insisted. “They were hostile!”

“You do realize she leaped at you like a raving beast just a moment ago, do you not?”

Eik rolled his eyes. “She was obviously under the influence of some manner of outside power. And if I had to guess, that looked an awful lot like the work of our dear boy, Mr. Moon. That miasma of his is pretty unmistakable at this point.”

“And who’s to say she didn’t invite him inside her body and mind out of her own free will?” Andihar shot back. “You seem to be forgetting that she is the Oracle of the cult that released him into our world. The spiritual leader of the whole organization.”

“Was the oracle of the cult that released him into our world. I’d say she rejected that role rather clearly when she helped us kill her former cohorts and thwart their plans to summon the Worldbreaker.”

“But we didn’t thwart their plans. Sure, the cultists died, but they were a step ahead the whole time, and in the end the Lord of the Moon is alive again. How can we know that she wasn’t responsible for that end result?”

Eik rolled his eyes. “We obviously can’t, but she still didn’t have to die.”

“Nevertheless. She attacked us, infested with miasma. When it comes to the Lord of the Moon, we can never be too careful. You did the right thing, Eik.”

“She could have had a seed of his power in her spirit this whole time for all we know,” Eik admitted. “I mean, yeah, we checked her and stuff, but with the Lord of the Moon and the theme of his abilities, I wouldn’t really be surprised at all if he was able to cast some kind of obscuring effect on her. She might have been crawling with parasites and miasma since the day I first met her in her chambers.”

“Damn,” Heath said. “So she just exploded, or what?”

“Yeah,” Eik said sheepishly.

“Damn.” He went back to inspecting the blade he was working on. Again and again, he recreated the same project with minor alterations here and there. But it seemed like he was getting close to a result he was satisfied could pass his exam.

Eik raised an eyebrow. “That’s all you have to say to it?”

The smith shrugged nonchalantly. “I don’t think I ever met her, so… You’ve killed plenty of other people before, no?”

“That’s what Andihar said.”

“Then it might just be because it’s true. She flew at you with an unknown power, likely from one of the strongest beings in the universe, so of course you have to kill her. Leaving her alive could have meant infesting the entirety of Gimleh with some parasite before we can stop it. It's just not worth the risk, is it? We went through this back when we first integrated properly into the Unified Mass. This is not old Earth, unfortunately.”

“Yeah…”

“And you still don’t know exactly what happened?”

Eik shook his head. “Nah. Andi’s got some people looking into it. Not sure if it’s really worth it, but I guess we’ll see.”

“All right, cool.”

“Give it to me straight, bro,” Eik continued. “Is it terrible that I don’t really feel much after killing her? I’ve been trying to force out some guilt since it happened, if you hadn’t noticed, but I’m not getting much. Like, obviously, I would rather it hadn’t ended quite like it did, but at the same time, I’m like, ‘yeah, okay, what’s happened has happened.’”

“Don’t know, man. Probably? If you had asked me twenty years ago, I’d have probably been reconsidering our friendship, but now…? Maybe we’ve all just become a little bit fucked in the heads.”

Eik snorted a laugh. “Hah, yeah, you might just be right about that. Given the experiences we’ve had, the things we know, and the powers we wield, I guess it’s only natural to go a little crazy after a while.”

“Speaking of going crazy,” Heath began. “what’s the reaction of the Gohkmorian elite now that your… request is in effect?”

Eik snorted. “Deafening silence, dude. I’ve never seen them quieter. Any time I met them, they always tried to make me back down with not so subtle threats, political pressure, imagined consequences, and crap like that, you know. But now that I’ve actually gotten the whole thing started, they’ve started to keep very low profiles.”

“Is it working?”

Eik grinned, the expression filled to bursting with malice. “Not at all. I’ve gotten a ton of reports already.”

“So, what, you got them all?” Heath asked, forgetting about his project for the moment. “We got ‘em?”

“Nah, not quite,” Eik admitted. “I’m convinced that this is not nearly the extent of it. While the act of sending monsters to us could have been carried out by a somewhat small team, there must have been a host of government decision-makers and military officers who supported the aggressive invasion. I want them all, Heath.”

“I couldn’t agree more. What’s the plan, then?”

”I’m going on my first sweep today, actually.”

“Sweep?”

“Yeah, that’s what I’m calling it.”

Heath raised an eyebrow. “That’s what you’re calling what?”

Eik grinned again. “That’s what I call going to Gohkamoria with a big ass sack and stuffing all of the shitheads into it like an X-ranked Krampus.”

“You don’t pick them up as you go? What if they run away?”

“Oh, plenty of them have. I just pick them up wherever they go. I have Living Manifestations quite literally everywhere and they are very good at sticking to their targets. I have them do thorough investigations of each accused. Most of the now numerous members of Eik’s Great Group of Guys are working on that as well.”

“So the sweep is…”

“To get all the ones that have stayed put in one, big, dramatic yoink.”

Unlike his last rather informal visit to Gohkamoria, when he had come through an unofficial and covert fracture opened by one of the members of Eik’s Great Group of Guys, this time Eik stepped through the primary portal that connected Gohkamoria and Gimleh.

There were no soldiers or generals ready to object to his arrival either.

Commander whatever his name was from the Gohkamorian something or the other, who had led the charge the first time Eik had come, certainly hadn’t bothered to show his face again. The sweet fact that he had been one of the first individuals to be identified by Eik’s scheme might well have had something to do with that absence, but who could really say for sure?

Eik snickered at the thought.

The tough guy had attempted to flee pretty much immediately after Eik had first splashed his final messages across the Gohkamorian sky. Unfortunately for him, however, Eik had planted a Living Manifestation in his ear the moment they had met and had been able to track and know where he was the entire time since then.

When he had eventually sent a small army of Profound Toxic beasts after him, Eik had located a smaller but not insignificant group of the Gohkamorian elite, hiding away on some remote world covertly owned by the government.

Eik couldn’t say exactly what the exact purpose of this world or its maintenance was, but he had a relatively safe guess considering what he saw there through the Eyes of the Beast.

There didn’t seem to be anything of note besides sandy beaches and a few coves dotted with absurdly large and luxurious castles brimming with service staff and whatever else one might find necessary to live the life of a greasy bastard. All in all, it had been an indescribably satisfying catch.

Frankly, it was puzzling just how surprised they seemed to have been when a fracture had been ripped open in the sky above what was essentially a beach resort. Drinks in hand and food in mouth, all color had drained from their faces when they witnessed almost fifty thousand high-ranked troops in faithful service to the man who swore himself the bane of their existence pouring out of the portal.

Had they thought that an X-ranker like Eik, who had already proven his capability several times before, would be incapable of locating them here or something? Even as they faced their own destruction, they couldn’t shake that sense of superiority that they carried.

Where had he seen arrogance like that before? Oh, right, freakin’ everywhere.

In the beginning they had sicced their guards and even servants on the Living Manifestations in a desperate attempt to keep them at bay while they escaped, but the blue beasts had plowed through the lot of them like a concrete mixing truck through a field of delicate dandelions.

None had been killed, however, and rigid suits of crystal had been sufficient to render them all incapable of fighting back as the Profound Toxic beasts rounded up the actual targets. Nuts as it sounded, Eik had actually bought the rights to a smaller remote world under the control of the Nidafjeld Alliance where he had erected a number of sectioned domes to contain his prisoners until the time was right.

And, as it happened, the time was right now.

Eik walked through the streets of Rorgherd, the capital city of Gohkamoria, while the messages he had left on his last visit to the lovely city still hung menacingly in the sky above. It had been very effective, but it could be more effective still.

Eik was going to get his fingers around the throats of every last one of those responsible.

As predicted, there had been a number of attempts to use Eik’s reporting system to falsely identify innocent Gohkamorians as guilty, but Eik was as certain as he could be that he had caught them all. He was confident in his investigation of the targets, but the reality was that it could never be a hundred percent foolproof.

Tyrannical or not, that was a price he was willing to pay.

And today, he would show, in no uncertain terms, that he wasn’t the only one with something to pay for.
