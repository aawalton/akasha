Sally paced around Big Joe’s palm, rubbing at her temples. Her normal method of showing up somewhere and chewing through antagonists didn’t look like a valid plan at present. With three of the Outsiders in hell, and her brain aching from maintaining her horde, she was struggling to come up with a battle plan.

She glanced over at the hovering Hunter and gave him a brief shrug. “With Archie gone, I guess we’ll see if we can pick Chuck’s brains for the next step.”

As if in answer to her spoken thought, a faint wireframe projection of the Architect appeared in the air close to them. It flickered constantly.

“I’m s-sorry,” it stuttered. “The person you are trying to contact is unavailable. Please leave a m-message after the t-t-t-” The projection disappeared.

Sally groaned. “This is truly our darkest hour.”

“So far,” the shadowy man added. “Isn’t this your bread and butter?”

She waved a hand dismissively. “Traditionally it’s always been a team effort. The Law of Fives. Even when it’s been on my head, others have pushed me to that point. But now, who will help if I’m not strong enough?”

After a moment of silence, Big Joe raised up his other palm.

“Sweet of you.” Sally gave the giant zombie a tired smile. “But, I’m not sure that-”

A blue flash illuminated them, as Theo teleported onto the open palm held out ready for him. Despite the grin across his face, blood soaked through his shredded uniform.

Sally shot him a relieved smile, ignoring how Big Joe had anticipated the vampire’s arrival. “Fangs. I thought you were locked up?”

“If the antagonists don’t show up in fifteen minutes, I’m allowed to leave.” He adjusted his glasses.

She rolled her eyes. “You hid the moon key in your metal coffin. The anti-ability effect only affected new spells, so you had pre-prepared your escape beforehand.”

“Tch, am I getting that easy to predict?” Theo sighed and turned to look up at the floating man. “Pleased to meet you, Hunter. I’ve heard a lot about you.”

“Likewise.” He doffed his hat at the vampire. “You here to help us come up with a plan?”

“Already sorted.” A wry smile crossed Theo’s lips as he glanced back at Sally.

“Oh, no.” She shook her head. “I can see those thoughts, Mr. Danger. That’s too risky when we’re already down to the wire for attempt one.”

“Pretend I make a convincing argument.”

Sally pinched the bridge of her nose and sighed. Without needing to ask, Big Joe moved his hands together so that the pair were on one platform. “If this doesn’t work…”

“Then I’ll see you in Hell, my dear.”

She stretched her arms out and gave the vampire another disapproving shake of her head. Rather than say anything further, she stepped up to him. Staring Theo dead in the eyes, she lashed out forward with her hand, burying it in his chest. With a burst of blood and broken bones, she tore his heart out, leaving his body to flop over dead.

Hunter sucked at his teeth. “That, uh, meant to help us?”

“Who knows?” Sally gave him a tired glance. “Nine times out of ten, Theo’s right on the money when it comes to dabbling in System bullshit. This, though…” She held up his heart. “I’m yet to be convinced.”

The shadowed man sank from the air to land on the giant zombie’s hands. “Some kind of new ability?”

“He seems to think that one of our Ultimates could be related to our opposing Aspects. Life and Death, you know? I’m not sure why taking and consuming his heart would be the key to that, however.” She frowned, as the organ continued to beat in her bloodied grasp. “I mean, I’ve eaten it a few times in the past, of course, and nothing interesting has happened.”

Hunter nodded slowly. “Anything I can do to help? As I’m unbound from the System, I might be able to waltz into their base and destroy it.”

“Too risky. We don’t know if they have a way to capture you, and we can't resurrect you if you die. Now that I’ve killed one of the few remaining voices of reason in my life, I might need you to stick around and guide me.”

She shook her head again and glared at the vampire’s heart. Surely brain would have made more sense? At least this was the correct time for her Aspect, anyway. It could assist in controlling an army this size.

“Bottom’s up.” Sally raised the heart in cheers. “Oh, and you’re lucky as I don’t have a System Voice. The others all yap in a super dramatic way to farm aura once they've changed, but I guess I already talk too much in my normal form.”

With a shrug she leaned backward and fell from Big Joe’s hand. Before she struck the milling zombies below, she became the Aspect of Life.

Her body tore apart, as a giant bulb grew from within. Thick, snake-like roots burst from the core, striking and burying into the sand. Thick petals of deep red unraveled, revealing a large maw of fleshy green. Hundreds of sharp teeth glistened within the mouth of her new form.

Yet within the STAR+, this wasn’t where her Aspect ended. More roots and vines formed, slithering across the ground or reaching into the air. Four smaller mouths grew from her main mass. Now as large as a two-story house, Sally raised Theo’s heart up to one of her hungry plant mouths with a slim root. She ate it, gulping down the still-beating organ.

Hunter watched in quiet awe as the vegetative form sat writhing in place. He saw how hundreds of zombies surrounding them grew less listless and seemed to pause, waiting for instructions. At first, Theo’s plan hadn’t done anything. Before he could decide what to do next, a thick smoke gradually rose from the buried roots.

For Sally, the change was more obvious, even if only internally. It wasn’t just the heart of her vampire husband nourishing her form, but the essence of death itself. An overwhelming and confusing feeling washed over her senses, before she realized what it was.

She could no longer only feel her undead pals. Subconsciously, she was aware of every corpse on the planet. Some close. Most far.

Yet, within her reach.

Blood bubbled up from the turned sand, where her roots dug deeper. Her many smaller mouths thrashed back and forth as the mist covered them. Everywhere this fog touched turned monochrome.

Hunter adjusted his hat and took a deep breath. Sally was wrong about being silent in her Aspect form. While her voice didn’t come from her Aspect form, he could hear it as a whisper, spoken through every risen body around them. Thousands of zombies muttered her will. A simple desire.

To consume.

It wasn’t something Sally was aware of. In fact, she knew very little at present, other than briefly wondering if this was what Chuck felt like sometimes. That thought was washed away by a wave of cool emotion.

She reached out across Sanctuary. Mourning every loss. Dead Adventurers and Monsters alike. Although she didn’t have the power to return their souls, she had something better. The peak of necromancy for a dying world.

Dark electricity sparked around her form as she cast Extinction Reversal.

Across Sanctuary, every dead body stood back up, glowing yellow light in their eyes.

“Told you.” Theo leaned against the doorframe, his fangs catching the light.

Edward continued tapping his fingers on the desk, only giving the vampire a dull glare.

“Should we return now and help Sally out?” Ren asked.

The most powerful people in the world were now stuck in a small meeting room in Hell. It would be putting it lightly to say that the atmosphere was tense. For most, sitting idle while Sanctuary was still under threat by the Radochs was maddening.

Archie shook his head. “Unlike her normal zombies, these attack indiscriminately. It is best we stay out of her way and pick up the pieces once her Aspect has run out.”

Crimson flames lapped wildly behind Humphrey’s helmet, as he sat with his arms crossed. “While I dislike that reasoning, I will admit it is the correct course of action. We don’t know how well this new ability will work.”

The various blue screens showing different parts of the world told a previously unthinkable story. Even as thousands of zombies near the pyramids made their move toward the Forest, roving undead filled every area of Sanctuary.

Risen bandits, goblins, and wildlife. Raptors and Giant Spiders. Outposts previously filled with Orcs, Kobolds, Lizardmen, or many others now stumbled their way toward the Tropical Isles. The only thing not affected by this Ultimate were the dead Radochs.

Theo continued grinning to himself. Most of Sally’s abilities had no range limit - they were just boosts to summons she controlled. While the newly risen weren’t a one-to-one match to most Radochs, in groups they stood a chance of whittling away at the invaders.

But perhaps the best thing about being right about her absorbing his Death power temporarily was that the opposite must be true. He could unlock a new Ultimate with her Life powers.

Selling the idea to Max and Ren had been difficult, but maybe organ consumption wasn’t the only way.

“It depends on how the Radoch react to this new threat,” the magician added, placing his top hat on the table. “If they have something that can get rid of the undead easily…”

“Then we’d best hope Chuck is being productive.” Archie gave them a strained smile. “Not that I doubt him, of course. There are just too many unknowns, and I wasn’t able to get as much information from their leader as I’d hoped. Other than they don’t underestimate us as much as I’d like.”

“Getting rid of those on the Isle is the priority,” Max agreed. “But they seem to have all sorts of technology to counter us.”

“Not all of us,” Theo interjected. He stepped further into the room to stand by the table, hands in his pockets. “Hunter doesn’t even have a System. Quite an anomaly, huh?”

Ren furrowed her brow. “None at all?”

“Nope. Sent a chill up my spine.” He shivered, just thinking about it.

The others exchanged looks. Theo always had an almost apathetic confidence to him, so for the new ally to be so concerning… it was worth taking that into account.

Archie steepled his fingers in front of his beard. “Regardless, we cannot depend on one factor. Spreading our resources across the world is not going to work in phase two. We must accept that some parts of the continent are lost, as are any islands. Instead of fighting on many fronts, we will have to act as one. A sharpened spear to take out the Hive King and sow disarray within the Radoch ranks.”

The world was at war. Every corpse rallied against the orchestrators of their death. Radoch groups that thought themselves in safe territory were now beset by the undead once more.

A cycle of death and life gripped at Sanctuary, as it teetered toward oblivion.
