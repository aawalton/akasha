The mist was thick, a choking white vapor smelling of flash-boiled mud. I stepped into it, my visor sliding down. The world turned a flat, digitized grey through the infrared overlay.

Heat bloomed in the mist. Bright, localized suns. The Cinderfall knights.

Tes, target lock.

[Processing thermal signatures. Seven targets in immediate proximity.]

The silhouettes sharpened on my HUD, rendering in sharp, skeletal wireframes over the glowing red cores of their bodies.

I willed the motion. Two plasma katanas snapped from the halo hovering behind my shoulders. They dropped perfectly into my waiting gauntlets. My right boot thruster kicked, pivoting my momentum. I brought my left arm up, the azure plasma hissing as it cut through the superheated steam.

A knight lunged from the fog, his heavy broadsword raised for an overhead strike.

My HUD flashed. Tier 6.

He telegraphed the swing, putting his entire weight onto his forward foot. I slid under the arc of his heavy iron. I swung my left katana in a tight, rising diagonal.

The plasma sheared through the tempered steel of his breastplate, through the padding, through the collarbone, and out the opposite hip. A clean, cauterized line. The two halves of the man separated, the top half sliding backward into the mud.

Phoenixes resurrect. I needed to burn a full cycle to keep him down.

I spun on my heel, bringing my right katana around in a flat, horizontal arc. The blade sheared cleanly through the neck of the fallen top half. The head hit the ground.

My left boot thruster ignited, a short, sharp burst. I kicked the severed, helmeted head like a soccer ball. It sailed through the mist, striking another advancing knight square in the chest.

The second man stumbled, his heavy shield dropping a fraction of an inch as he tried to regain his balance in the muck.

Tier 5.

I threw the left katana.

I pulsed the micro-thrusters housed in the pommel right as it left my hand. The weapon became a spinning disc of azure light, rotating fast enough to hum a deadly, high-pitched note.

It struck the staggering knight dead center. The spinning plasma tore vertically down his centerline, bisecting him from the crown of his helmet to his groin. The two halves slumped apart in a shower of sparks and hissing, cauterized flesh.

The spinning blade arced left, its momentum carrying it toward a third knight who had just raised his sword to parry the incoming projectile. The plasma sliced effortlessly through the enchanted steel of his blade and was inches from taking his arm.

Tes, retrieve.

The katana snapped backward, the thrusters reversing instantly. It zipped back to my left hand with a solid clack against my palm.

At my feet, the decapitated body of the Tier 6 knight erupted in a sudden, blinding flash of golden fire. The resurrection cycle was initiating, drawing raw mana from the environment. The blinding light served as a beacon in the fog.

Shadowy outlines began rushing toward me from all sides. The skirmish had drawn the attention of the surrounding squads.

I dropped into a low crouch. The Reaper armor groaned as I channeled a massive surge of Kaelus’s raw, untamed magic. The suit acted as a conduit, the runic circuits absorbing the draconic power and venting it outward.

Freeze.

I slammed my gauntlets into the mud.

A wave of staggering cold radiated outward. Icicles the size of spears erupted from the ground, condensing the moisture in the air in a fraction of a second. The jagged ice lances shot up in a tight, defensive circle around me, a brutal, frozen barricade.

The charging knights slammed into the ice wall. Several were impaled, screaming as the freezing cold bit through their armor. The rest scrambled backward, slipping on the suddenly frozen mud.

They would thaw quickly in this environment.

I focused on the Tier 6 knight, his body now fully restored by the golden flames, his sword swinging toward my flank.

I stepped into his guard, deflecting his blade with my right katana. The plasma chewed through his weapon, melting the steel and slicing cleanly through his shoulder in a single, fluid motion. I saw his eyes widen now no longer wearing a helmet, the shock registering even at this close range.

Three more knights were already hacking at the ice lances, breaking through my defensive ring.

Blink.

I pulled on the space element. The world stretched and compressed. I vanished from the center of the ring, appearing instantly behind one of the knights hacking at the ice.

I drove a heavy, thruster-assisted kick into the small of his back. The contact triggered a localized burst of ice magic from my greave, freezing his armor and the moisture on his skin solid. He stiffened, petrified, falling face-first into the dirt as a solid block of ice.

This was the infuriating reality of fighting Phoenix Knights. They were easy enough to drop individually, but the sheer volume of them, combined with the necessity of killing them multiple times, made it a grinding, exhausting process.

I needed area-of-effect suppression. I needed to thin the herd while they were disoriented.

I flared my mana again, subtly altering the ambient temperature of the battlefield, pooling more water beneath the feet of the struggling knights before flash-freezing it into a slick sheet of ice.

Tes. Target the stationary knights. Use the halo.

[Target locks acquired. Engaging automated weaponry.]

The nine remaining plasma katanas hovering at my back shot outward in every direction. They moved with algorithmic precision, independent drones of death controlled by the ASI. They tore through the ranks of the knights struggling to keep their footing on the ice, slicing through armor and limbs, initiating multiple resurrection cycles across the immediate area.

Space magic was my most versatile tool, allowing for unmatched mobility and defensive positioning. But lightning... lightning was pure, unadulterated destruction.

It was chaotic. Unpredictable. It devoured mana and lacked pinpoint accuracy, usually functioning as a wide, indiscriminate blast.

But I had a workaround.

I raised my left hand toward the sky, pulling heavily on the electric affinity in the sky.

A violent, jagged bolt of raw azure lightning ripped down from the clouds. It tracked directly toward the plasma katanas currently embedded in the ground or lodged in the armor of the fallen knights.

The katanas acted as lightning rods, channeling the massive electrical discharge with terrifying accuracy.

The lightning struck eight targets simultaneously. The resulting explosion of electrical energy scrambled their resurrection cycles, frying their nervous systems and turning the immediate area into a blinding, chaotic storm of blue light.

My old man’s brutal sparring sessions were paying off. He had taught me how to control the flow, how to bridge the power, and most importantly, how to blend my swordsmanship with my elemental affinities. I still lacked fine control over the lightning, but utilizing physics to guide the strike was a solid compromise.

The electrical storm subsided. I moved on to the next target—a Tier 7 knight who had just recovered from his death.

He had lost his sword in the chaos. He roared, his gauntlets erupting in golden fire, and charged me, relying on close-quarters brawling and the sheer, overwhelming heat of his fists.

He swung a heavy right hook aimed at my helmet.

Blink.

I folded space, slipping three feet backward.

The knight, expecting resistance, stumbled forward, his momentum carrying his flaming fist into the chest of another Cinderfall soldier who had just finished reforming from the ash. The fiery blow punched straight through the newly formed armor, initiating yet another immediate death cycle.

Friendly fire. I noted dryly, stepping back into the gap he had just created.

I swung the right katana in a tight, downward arc, decapitating the Tier 7 knight cleanly. His head rolled into the mud. The golden flames didn't erupt. His phoenix's reserves were depleted. The other knight, the one struck by the flaming fist, also remained still.

I checked my HUD.

Ten confirmed, permanent kills. I felt a surge of pride, a grim satisfaction in my own efficiency.

Then I glanced at Bob’s telemetry data ticking in the corner of my screen.

Twenty-five.

He had chewed through more than double my number in the exact same timeframe. He didn't have the mobility of space magic or the rapid-fire drone capability of the floating katanas. He was just a massive, unstoppable wall of black steel swinging a silver broadsword with terrifying, mechanical precision.

I was still so far behind him. No wonder my father constantly lectured me about my incomplete training.

I tightened my grip on the plasma katanas. The hum of the blades vibrated through my gauntlets. I was getting better. The combat flow felt more natural, the integration of magic and steel more seamless.

The thick curtain of superheated steam finally began to thin, shredded by the fierce winds blowing across the valley.

The battlefield resolved into stark, horrifying clarity.

A large section of the Cinderfall infantry had entirely bypassed the melee in the center. They were ignoring the swarming infantry automata tearing at their flanks, focusing entirely on dismantling the automated defense grid. They were hacking at the base of the rapid-fire turrets, overwhelming the static defenses through sheer numbers. There was a visible, gaping hole developing in the trench lines.

We finally had their attention.

Five massive thermal signatures burned brightly through the dissipating fog. The Tier 8 knights in the vanguard.

They were staring directly at us.

Two of them possessed smaller, intense heat sources perched on their shoulders. Tier 8 phoenixes, acting as magical batteries to double their knights' destructive output.

That meant the remaining three Tier 8 phoenixes were currently ascending into the cloud layer. It was three Tier 8 phoenixes against two Tier 8 dragons in the sky. It was going to be a bloodbath up there.

The Cinderfall command had drastically miscalculated. They believed Kaelus was still a Tier 7. That misjudgment was going to cost them dearly. I had absolute faith my brother would tear them apart and return to reinforce the ground.

The five knights on the ground split their formation.

The two with the shoulder-mounted phoenixes turned their massive tower shields toward Bob. They were flanked by two of the knights without mounts. Four Tier 8 combatants focusing entirely on the General of the Steel Tide.

The final knight, lacking a mount, turned his attention to me.

Their strategy was glaringly obvious. They were underestimating me. They viewed the kid in the red armor as a distraction, someone they could tie up with a single combatant while they focused their overwhelming force on the larger, more imposing threat of Goliath.

It was time to prove them wrong.

I willed the nine hovering katanas back into a tight defensive halo behind my shoulders.

I ignited my main back thrusters, rocketing forward at an angle that brought me directly into the path of the two knights charging toward Bob.

The two heavily armored warriors saw me intercepting their line. They raised their broadswords, the blades engulfed in roaring, golden-white flames, intending to cleave me out of their way.

I opened a portal of white light directly in front of me and dove through.

The two knights slid to a halt in the ash, their flaming swords swinging through empty air. They stared at the spot where I had vanished, completely stunned.

The exit portal opened seventy yards behind them, depositing me directly into the rear lines of the Cinderfall vanguard.

I was standing amidst a tight cluster of Tier 7 knights who were completely focused on attacking the automated defense turrets, their backs turned to me.

The nine plasma katanas snapped outward from my halo in every direction. They moved like streaks of blue lightning, a storm of automated death ripping through the unprotected rear echelons of the Tier 7 knights.

The two Tier 8s, finally turning around to witness the sudden carnage erupting behind them, realized exactly what had just happened. I was returning the blatant indifference they had shown me.

I tore through the ranks of the smaller fry, my thrusters whining as I blinked and weaved through the disorganized formations. I was prioritizing speed. I sliced through armor, severed limbs, and initiated one resurrection cycle after another, turning their back lines into a chaotic sea of flashing golden light and screaming men.

The Cinderfall knights, watching their comrades fall from an attacker they couldn't even track, stared in dawning horror.

They were becoming acutely aware that I possessed space magic. They realized, far too late, that they had picked the wrong target to ignore.
