Archie had dreamt of reviving Petrichor many times. The menu changed from dream to dream, but it was always heralded as a masterpiece by the people that traveled across the world to taste the famed food.

The best part of the dream was always the part where Arty smiled at Archie. Sometimes he’d say good job. How proud he was. Other times he would just load all of those emotions into a simple smile that made Archie wake up feeling warm inside.

Although the dream changed in many ways, it always held one constant—his smiling father. A true smile, not like the one he had forced for so many decades as he resented his gifts, his Gluttonous father, and his paralyzing suffering. A realization of a dream. Freed from a lifetime of hardship.

Blood poured from Arty’s mouth as he collapsed in the grass. Tataki watched his prey fall. He started to turn toward Archie…

…who had wrapped noodles around stone pillars on either side of Tataki. Archie yanked and retracted the noodles at the same time, slingshotting his hardened leg into Tataki’s back. Tataki stumbled across the ground as Archie continued his assault, unwrapping the noodles from the pillars and onto his forearms. When he punched, the noodles followed a moment later to whip Tataki, who conjured chitinous shells to protect his body.

Archie found the soft spot. Tataki had been burned by flame, burned by acid, punched, kicked, and thrown around, but his most prevailing wound was the one he had suffered earlier in the day—a gashing wound on his side. He couldn’t cover it with a shell, leaving it vulnerable to Archie, who punched it repeatedly, his knuckles sinking to split the wound further with each strike.

Archie delivered several powerful punches per second, his sugar rush empowering him to deliver brutal strikes. Normally, his power would wane after a few of such hits. But this time, his power grew. Essence flowed into him faster than he could expend it. He felt Nori’s familiar essence, then Julienne’s, then Oliver’s. Each of his friends groaned and rolled around on the ground as Archie only grew more powerful.

He unleashed one last punch that shattered Tataki’s ribs and sent him stumbling. Archie would have pressed on—he would have killed Tataki—if a group of guards and fighters hadn’t spilled out onto the courtyard at that moment.

“Get him!” the Glutton screamed, pointing at Tataki.

Tataki took one last look at Nori before slapping a tentacle arm onto the roof of the walkway, suction cups clinging to the tiles and allowing him to swing out of sight. Archie had a thought to chase, but his father’s pained groans broke his bloodlust.

“Dad!”

The guards watched Archie with hesitation. They kept their spears at the ready as they attended to the fallen Glutton.

Archie kneeled down by his father. Arty’s eyes fluttered as the color left his skin. The spike was as wide as a fist and had pierced all the way through the right side of his chest. Blood leaked from the wound with each pulse of his heartbeat.

Archie thought of healing noodles that he had never learned to use and health tonics he had never learned to create. “Nori!” He screamed for help. Nori!” He looked back. Nori sobbed in a slump against the tree. “Nori, get over here! Hurry!”

She hobbled over on a twisted leg, wiping blood off her forehead as she approached.

“Do something! Use acid to cauterize the wound!” Archie pleaded. “Stop the bleeding!”

“Archie…it’s…”

He couldn’t wait for her. He conjured a noodle with the intention of giving it healing properties. As he put it across Arty’s skin, he knew it wouldn’t work. But he had to do it anyway.

“Ar-Archie…” Arty whispered, his voice gargling with the blood that filled his lungs.

Archie wiped away tears.

The pain in Arty’s face disappeared. His eyes struggled to stay open as a sort of peace washed over his expression. His lips curled up into a soft smile. His hand rested on Archie’s knee. His voice came out with his breath, airy and unclear. “Yamaykem…uhproud.”

Proud. It was the only word Archie truly understood just before his father took his last breath.

The Glutton gave his report.

The students were delivering a gift to Prince Waldorf when they were attacked by Tataki—the Glutton knew him by his face, as did most of Ambrosia City. Tataki seemed to be waiting for someone, likely Prince Waldorf, and had already sustained a major injury, likely due to his failed assassination attempt earlier in the day. He was surprised to see the other Urokan and focused his attack on her—this part of the Glutton’s testimony kept Nori from detainment.

The students defended the Glutton and were defeated one-by-one. At some point, an older man joined the battle. The man held off Tataki until the guards got there, taking a fatal wound in the process. The boy that screamed as guards pried him away from the corpse was his son.

Prince Waldorf saw the potential assassin not as a threat, but as a legitimization of his power. Prince Waldorf was about to rise to the highest seat in Ambrosia, and over the coming days, rallied the common people with speeches about how he posed a threat to the established ruling class. The assassin just proved that he was onto the right people.

Anise and Aubergine had their hands full that night. Anise had to keep four injured students alive, and Aubergine had to make sure the right political spin was put on the night’s events. Considering the delicate balance of power between crown and academy, Anise was glad to only have the fate of four students on her hands and not the entire student population.

Yarrow and Julienne got it the worst.

Yarrow’s hands had melted to just bone and sinew. Anise sedated him and picked out glass shards. She soaked bandages in one of her rarest potions—a regenerative potion made from the moss of an igpa turtle—and wrapped what remained of his hands.

She couldn’t linger. Julienne’s wounds, while much simpler, were just as potentially fatal. His lower oblique had suffered a gouging wound. She used a blood-replenishing potion on him and commended him for stopping the bleeding with his cheese rind technique. She said if he hadn’t, he would have died before she had a chance to treat him.

On top of his other wounds, Julienne had bitten off his tongue. Anise spared some of the regenerative potion for him, pouring it into gauze and making him suck on it. It regenerated, but he claimed it felt numb and strange in his mouth.

Oliver had a serious concussion that prevented him from telling Anise about his broken arm. He screamed when she touched it and felt splinters of bone move beneath the skin. He screamed again when he tried to stand and found that he had a broken leg to match.

Nori had a mild concussion, lacerations to the face, and red, raised skin in large circles all over her body from the tentacle’s suction cups. Anise gave her only a moment to clean up her wounds. As the Head Chef returned to the more dire students, Nori slipped away to look for Archie, who had refused treatment.

She went to his room. Not there. She went into their usual kitchens. Not there. She didn’t think he would be wandering around the city. Unfortunately for Archie, he had been through enough bad times for Nori to know that he sought solitude in times of crisis. She started to go toward the greenhouse, but then she caught a nagging feeling that she knew where he was.

She went to the abandoned room above the kitchen and found Archie on the other side of a heap of old, unused furniture. She heard him before she saw him. He fought to contain his sobbing once he heard Nori’s footsteps.

She sat next to him, letting him have the first word. He sniffed several times and wiped away tears that never stopped flowing.

“I’ll kill him,” he said with a depressed conviction.

Nori wasn’t sure if he meant Waldorf or Tataki. She didn’t ask. To acknowledge the statement would be to make it real, and if it were real, Archie would die trying.

“I can’t…I can’t do this without him,” Archie said, breaking into another sob.

“Archie…”

“If I…no matter what I do…If I got my white jacket. If I…” He sniffed. “If I saved the world, it wouldn’t matter, because he’s not in it. Nothing matters without him.”

Nori rubbed Archie’s arm.

“None of…this,” he gestured at the Academy around them, “matters. I have nothing.”

“That’s not true, Archie. You have people that rely on you. Your mom.”

“My mom’s gone…”

“What if she isn’t?” Nori leaned in. “What if you could do something for her?”

Archie scoffed. “There were a dozen Veratores there, Nori. They specialize in healing magic. What can I do that they can’t? I can’t even…” He swallowed his words. His voice quieted. “I can’t even bandage a wound.”

“Okay, maybe you can’t heal her, but you might still be able to help. You could find some old ancient healing method. We’ll get Sutton and set that little nerd to work on some history books.”

Archie laughed. A true laugh, made louder by his exposed vulnerability. As the joy surfaced, it pushed his remaining tears down his cheeks.

Nori didn’t let the feeling go. “He’d love that. We could pay him by indulging him and listening to him ramble about history. I’ll help with the burden. We can trade off every hour.”

The laughter continued. So did Nori. “You know, one time he passed out in the library because he hadn’t left to get food or water for two days. You know what had him so focused? He thought he saw an extinct bird.”

Archie laughed and sobbed and wiped away tears as he remembered the tale.

“And it wasn’t even, like, a big bird. No half-dragon this or magic that. It was, like, a sparrow with some pattern on its neck. Can you imagine how excited he’ll be to help on something that actually matters?”

Archie laughed. And then he stopped. And then he cried. “When they…when they took me away, they were talking about what to do with his body…”

Nori offered a sad smile. “Anise said Aubergine has the—the body. They’re going to bury him out in the fields. I think they were waiting on you.”

“What? Here?” Archie sniffed and sat up. “He needs to be buried at Petrichor.”

“Well…Aubergine’s afraid that Waldorf will demand the body. If we don’t go now…”

Archie sighed. “Okay. Let’s go.”

That night, during the witching hour and as the fireflies hunted, they took Arty’s body into the forest. Aubergine still had to navigate the situation with Waldorf, leaving only Quince to help Archie and Nori. The Head Chef brought an ox around with a sled to drag the body.

Archie walked in front of the ox, unable to bear the sight of his father’s body. Nori kept herself between Archie and the body. They walked through the trees in silence for a while, Archie waiting on Quince to stop, and Quince waiting on Archie to stop. Eventually, after traveling through the forest by torchlight for several minutes, they both felt like they had gone far enough.

“Here is good?” Quince asked. He kept his voice low, minimizing his presence in the forming memory that Archie would hold onto for the rest of his life. Archie nodded. “It’ll just be a few minutes.”

Quince put a hand to the dirt. Vines rose from the dirt and unfolded like a zipper in front of him, pushing muddy dirt to each side. The vines went back into the earth and reemerged in a straight line again, unzipping to shovel away dirt.

Archie smiled. Something about burying his father with magic felt right. He watched the hole form, occasionally taking a glance at his father’s body. After a couple of minutes, the grave was ready.

“Uh…look away,” Quince said. “No graceful way to move the body.”

Archie looked up at the night sky. The stars seemed to dance behind the rustling leaves of the trees. He heard the body drag across the dirt and plop into the grave.

“Alright,” Quince said. Archie and Nori turned to face the body as it lay in its grave. “Um…would you like to say anything?”

Archie stood in silence. He hadn’t thought about last words. He had been out burying the body for half an hour. He regretted not using that time to think of something.

“I…”

He thought about what he wanted to say.

When they look back at you and me, they’ll say we were the redemption of the Kents. We’ll make your father a footnote in our history. You won’t be known as the son of a monster. You’ll be the father of a legend. All of this time I’ve wasted, these trivial pursuits, I’m done with them. I will elevate my name so that your name will live forever. I’ll make you great through my achievements.

And he thought of what his father wanted to hear.

“I’ll do it for me,” he said.

As Quince covered Arty’s body with dirt, Archie wondered if the dead could detect lies.
