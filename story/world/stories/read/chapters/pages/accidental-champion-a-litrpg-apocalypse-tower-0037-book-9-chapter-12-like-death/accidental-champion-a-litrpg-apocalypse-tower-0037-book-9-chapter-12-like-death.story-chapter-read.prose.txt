Xavier couldn’t cultivate. He didn’t have Bones, his soul bound weapon. His Storage Ring—none of his several Storage Rings—were anywhere to be found. And it would be a long time before his soul reserve was replenished enough to scatter the energy of Arrack’s Locked Down spell.

So, inside the time dilation field, Xavier did the only thing he could do. Sitting on the stone floor of the steel-walled circular room, he waited.

As a C Grade Denizen and a Tier 3 Cultivator, Xavier had long grown beyond the need for water and food to sustain his life. What he hadn’t grown beyond, however, was the need for energy.

Weeks passed.

With his damaged and blocked cores and energy channels, he was unable to draw Celestial Energy into his body. Even his newfound ability was useless toward this end. He could shift the Celestial Energy in the air, even make it pass through him, but in his current state he couldn’t make the energy become his. It wouldn’t flow through his channels or enter his cores.

He did find that he could take advantage of the energy that was already his—not in the traditional way, with his cores blocked, but with his new ability. In the same way he’d manipulated the refined Celestial Energy that had been bonded to the molecules in his hand.

Slowly, as the weeks turned to months, Xavier pulled more and more energy from his cores to maintain his body, using up several points of Soul Energy in the process, though fortunately he gained more Soul Energy than he needed to use. His wounds had healed long ago, but his body needed far more energy than he’d realised to sustain its powerful muscles.

He’d never been cut off from cultivation to this degree—even when his cores had been shattered, he’d at least been able to eat.

Soon enough, even the energy in his cores started to become depleted. He couldn’t draw more energy in, and the energy he did have wasn’t merely being lost to sustain himself—it was leaking out of him due to the acute soul sickness he was suffering from.

A year must have passed before the symptoms of the soul sickness abated. A year of his cores being slowly depleted until there was nothing left inside them.

The time dilation field, running through a spell pattern, functioned perfectly the entire time. Arrack’s shocked look had shifted ever so slightly, as though it was moving toward becoming a scowl, but even the A Grade wasn’t fast enough to do anything outside Xavier’s powerful time dilation field.

The soul energy he’d replenished in this time wasn’t sufficient to scatter the A Grade Locked Down spell. Not yet. And without any energy in his cores, his muscles started to shrink, the nutrients inside them cannibalised for his body’s survival.

More months passed in such a state. As his soul strengthened, his body weakened, until finally he thought he had enough.

The first time he’d scattered an A Grade spell, his Soul Energy reserve hadn’t been close to full. He’d expended a great deal of it when he’d encountered the threats in the Memory Room, then even more when manifesting spell patterns outside his body to use against Arrack.

This time, he hoped he would have enough not only to scatter the spell, but to do it without suffering from soul sickness afterward.

Because if he suffered from it again, it wouldn’t matter that Locked Down was disabled.

His cultivation still wouldn’t work.

If only that Void Energy hadn’t been so thoroughly dispersed. I could have used it again...

But he had no more Void Energy to disrupt his enemy’s spell.

He had only this.

The stillness was deafening. He couldn’t train. Couldn’t fight. Couldn’t read or write. Couldn’t even speak to Roln or Bones—his constant companions were gone.

The only company he had was the corpse of his friend and his enemy all but frozen outside his time dilation field.

Roln might be gone forever for all he knew. Xavier’s head no longer hurt like needles were stabbing into it, but he worried that whatever damage had been done to him when Arrack had attempted to have his memories extracted had ended up destroying the mental construct.

Maybe after the Locked Down spell lifts, my mind will clear further, and Roln will return.

All Xavier really had during that time was the Immortal Soul lens.

So as he waited, he looked through it, studying the energy around him, inside him, inside the walls, inside his enemy—delving as deeply into his new ability to see as he could.

When he was finally ready to disperse the Locked Down spell, he’d become so emaciated he looked more like a skeleton with wings than a dragonkin.

Like Death...

Xavier took a ragged breath, looked through his Immortal Soul lens at the Locked Down spell’s energy twisting around and inside him, then drilled deep into it and scattered the pieces, bit by bit.

Setting fire to the spell with his will, the energy drifted like ash in the wind.

The Locked Down status effect has been lifted.

Your energy channels, cores, spells, spiritual sense, ability for higher thought, and System notifications are no longer blocked.

As a lingering effect, all your spells have been cooled down.

Xavier took the clearest, deepest breath he had in almost two years.

Disabling the A Grade spell hadn’t taken as much Soul Energy as he’d expected. He’d been able to execute the technique far more smoothly than he had the first time—a consequence of having come to understand the energy better, after examining it with his Immortal Soul lens for so long.

With the Soul Energy remaining in his reserve, he could have performed the scattering twice more before becoming susceptible to soul sickness.

He blinked at that realisation.

I waited longer than I needed.

But it was better to have more than necessary than less.

Xavier stood. His bones creaked. He’d become so damned skinny and weak. But the instant his channels and cores were free they began feasting on the energy around him like a starved peasant roaming free at a royal banquet.

And there was more energy in the room than there should have been. All the A Grade spell energy he’d scattered hovered in the air free for the taking.

He took it all.

With the rune-inscribed walls, he was blocked from feeding off or even seeing the Celestial Energy outside his dungeon cell, but that was an easy fix now he was free to cast and manifest spells again. Altering the runes was a simple process. He’d been examining the runes he could see with his Immortal Soul lens for over a year. He knew exactly how to manipulate them toward his ends.

The fact they’d been inscribed by an A Grade didn’t hamper him in the least.

He’d also discovered the mechanism that allowed Arrack to slip in and out through the floor.

Xavier wasn’t trapped anymore.

With the Locked Down spell disabled and his soul sickness cured, the options at Xavier’s disposal expanded exponentially, and there were a few things he needed to puzzle out before he took his revenge.

Drawing in more and more energy, able to push his spiritual sense through the walls and beyond, he used his Immortal Soul lens to observe the other Denizens in the base, quickly recognising a few of their energy signatures.

Dybal and Lydad stuck out like sore thumbs.

This entire base was a trap. Arrack had been waiting for me. Management hiding among the agents. But that doesn’t mean this place isn’t what it seems.

The contracts had been real. Alica’s memories had been real. And the Memory Room, with thousands of memory orbs stacked on all those shelves, had definitely been real.

Xavier closed his eyes and focused on his mind, entering the central room. As he did, he found his thoughts still did not move as swiftly as they should, and that the rooms in his mind—though he could once again access them—had been damaged.

He searched through them, trying to find out how they’d changed and what had happened to them.

On his way, he was able to review his memories with a perfect clarity that he’d lacked while suffering from Locked Down. He inhabited the moment he’d reached the Memory Room. Watched as Arrack moved to stab him.

What he noticed was the shock written not only on Dybal and Lydad’s faces, but on the faces of every single memory-wiped agent there—excepting two, the agents who’d been closest to Arrack back in the Social Rooms.

Arrack didn’t know about my ability to erase contracts. Me trying to free his agents had not been something he’d anticipated.

Xavier smiled.

Well, isn’t that interesting.

After reviewing the memories, he kept exploring the rooms of his mind. When he entered the hundred and fiftieth room, his mental apparition halted in the doorway.

A faded figure huddled in the corner.

“Roln?” Xavier stepped forward.

The figure curled inward and covered their ears.

It was definitely the dead Wanderer. Except there were deep gashes through his flesh—flesh that was merely a representation of the mental construct, and yet...

“What happened?” Xavier took slow, quiet steps toward the corner, as though trying not to startle a wild animal. Roln curled deeper inward as Xavier knelt beside him, reaching out a tentative hand.

The mental construct flinched.

“Roln?’

“Tried. Tried.” Roln rocked back and forth. “Wouldn’t let ‘em but they tried. Fought them off. Tried. Tried.”

“Who? What did they try?”

“Tried. Tried.” Roln kept rocking.

Xavier placed a hand on the mental construct’s shoulder. “It was you, wasn’t it? You’re the reason they couldn’t take my memories.” He swallowed. “Thank you, Roln. If they had...”

Roln kept muttering, over and over, the mental construct too damaged to register Xavier’s words.

“I’ll fix you.” Xavier almost added, I promise, then Palini’s face flashed in his mind and he stopped himself.

Xavier sat beside the dead Wanderer for a long time, trying to talk to him, to soothe him, but Roln keep muttering the same, barely coherent things, over and over.

In the end, Xavier split his mind and left a portion of it in that room.

Then he moved on, examining more of his memories related to the base in perfect detail.

When he returned to his body, he let out a long breath. Thoughts of what happened to Roln lingered in the forefront of his mind. Usually, he would push those thoughts away, compartmentalise them, but he’d found that the deeper he allowed himself to feel his emotions, the more powerful his Immortal Soul lens became.

So, he let the thoughts remain.

I’d be dead if not for what he did. Dead, or a memory-wiped agent. Arrack would have gotten what he wanted. No advanced interrogation techniques necessary.

Still pulling Celestial Energy from the wider base, Xavier looked down at his body. It was no longer a shrivelled, skeletal form, though it hadn’t returned to its former mass. He flexed the muscles in his right arm. His hand and forearm no longer carried the refined Celestial Energy it had before—that had been redirected to his chest and heart.

His right hand and arm felt on par with the rest of his body, and his muscles appeared more compact than before he’d suffered from the lack of energy.

He looked thinner, more... chiselled. But he hadn’t lost an ounce of strength.

Xavier stared at Arrack. The scowl on the A Grade’s face had finally formed. Xavier tilted his head to the side and manifested several spell patterns. His spells were still in cooldown from Locked Down’s lingering effect, but he solved that with a Pocket Time Stream spell pattern.

Drones appeared all around him. As many as he could fit in the room, and as his drones could fly and the ceiling was quite high up, that made for a lot of drones. His time dilation field expanded to encompass them all, leaving the A Grade locked outside of it.

Then he took some further precautions, making one of his drones create a time dilation field of their own and trap Arrack inside it—a slow-moving time dilation field.

“That ought to keep you a good while,” Xavier muttered. He knelt by Palini, placing a hand on the man’s chest. “I’ll get you back home. Just not yet.” He glanced at Arrack. “First, I need to set up a little surprise.”

With half his drones at his back, Xavier slipped through the wall, using the same mechanism Arrack had used—except, just as extra insurance, he keyed the altered runes to only follow his commands.

Even if Arrack somehow broke free, which was near-impossible, there was no way he was getting out of that room.

Xavier once more walked through the base a hundred galaxies away from this own.

There were people he needed to talk to.
