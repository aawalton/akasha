As the waves crashed against the docks nearby, Ethan pulled up his interface as he braced for the list of choices he’d have to sift through. But then he recalled that pretty recently, he had splurged. In fact, as he parsed through his available upgrades, he managed to narrow his attention on his Prime Arcanum as both of the other two were nearly fully upgraded, with each having a single upgrade that he couldn’t afford anyway.

Tier upgrade (Tier-4 → Tier-5): Increase the Arcanum’s tier, empowering its capabilities and unlocking more upgrades - 15000 fragments

Suffice it to say, he wasn’t anywhere near that. He had upwards of 11,000 fragments and exactly 512 essence at his disposal, but it wasn’t enough to push any of the lesser Arcanums to tier-5. Not that he would right now and of course, that didn’t mean he had nothing to spend it on. His Prime Arcanum had a few options available so with a move of his hand, he pulled those all up.

Tier upgrade (Tier-3 → Tier-4): Increase the Arcanum’s tier, empowering its capabilities and unlocking more upgrades - 10000 fragments, 500 Essence, 3 Riftcore (tier-3)

Spellform Capacity (2 → 3): Increase the Arcanum's spell capacity by one - 3000 fragments, 150 Essence

Spellform Bonus (Tier-2 → Tier-3): Increase Spellform bonus from 20% to 30% - 2500 fragments, 120 Essence

Mana Regeneration (Tier-2 → Tier-3): Increase the Arcanum's mana regeneration - 1500 fragments, 50 Essence

Spell Capacity (5 → 6): Increase the Arcanum's spell capacity by one - 1500 fragments, 50 Essence

Prime Echo Slot (1 → 2): Increase Echo slots by 1 - 1500 fragments, 100 Essence

Trait Upgrades - 5 available

“A decent list,” Ethan hummed as he eyed the tier upgrade for a moment. If any of the past tiers was of any indication, then the tier upgrade should come with a new ability. While it’d be something situational like Echo or better he couldn’t tell, but he hoped it’d be good. It wouldn’t be for now, though, as tier-3 riftcores were not on the table just yet.

“I guess that leaves the traits to upgrade. I’m pretty sure I should be able to afford at least one of those.”

Ethan tapped the last item, and as the text floated in front of him, his brows lifted. Aside from the fact that there were two new traits eligible for upgrades, he was pretty sure that one of them—[The Vanguard]—had grown cheaper. The essence-part, at least. In the past, it had cost only fragments and 500 essence considering it aligned well enough with his path of the Burning Juggernaut, but with all the primagems he had encountered, he was happy to see that one of them could be used to reduce the essence cost, and while he did have enough essence for one full upgrade, if he reduced the cost with his primagems, he could maybe afford to upgrade two traits instead of one. And for that, he needed to grab a couple of things.

Shoving the menu to the side for a moment, Ethan took a seat and reaching into a ripple of air half an inch away from his chest, he began pulling un-assimilated primagems out of his [Soul Space] arraying them in front of him which, by now, he had plenty of, especially after the last couple of days of rift delving.

By now, Ethan was pretty much sitting on a pile of primagems. At least, the low-grade kind. He had all sorts of ‘em, and in good numbers, too. There were hardening Primagems. Crystallization. Plenty of Lightning, Barrier, Water, and thanks to the last couple of rifts, he had added some more variety. Now he had poison as well. Growth. Heat. Mirage. But while he was looking forward to finding a use for all of the low-grade ones, there was no helping that they were being out-shined by the larger specimens.

As for mid-grades, he didn’t have as many of those. But he did have a pretty respectable amount and compared to the simpler, pebble-sized low-grades, they certainly felt much more potent.

“We got Draining, Eldritch, Conviction, Heat, two Lightning, one Storm, and Growth.”

Ethan hummed, arms crossed as he eyed the fist-sized gems. Every single one of them was distorting the air around it, their nature affecting and clashing with their surroundings which forced him to space them out a little further. But one of them he kept in the center.

The Eldritch mid-grade primagem.

It was powerful. Clearly. It had potential. But there was no denying how creepy it looked. How eerie it was to see the air and dust around squirm like half-visible worms. The cement below it seemed to shudder and somehow, it almost felt as if something was peering at him from beyond the stone.

Ethan shuddered. It would be nice if a trait could consume the Eldritch mid-grade primagem and purify it somehow. But that still felt risky. For spells, the effect of the primagems used strongly affected the result of the upgrade. Would that be the same here? If so…

He shook his head, then picked up the Eldritch gem and stored it away once more. It was too big of a risk, and he didn’t need it to muddy the options. He’d need to confirm it first. He needed to make sure that the primagems used in the trait upgrading process wouldn’t affect the nature of the trait. And if it did, the effect would need to be minimal if he were to take the risk. Otherwise… he’d have to find another use for it.

“Or just keep lugging it around until I figure a way to get rid of it,” he spoke. He sighed, then loaded the list of upgradeable traits again. “Oh well, we’ll see.”

Enduring Brawler - Multiple combinations available

Child of Flames - Multiple combinations available

The Vanguard - Multiple combinations available

Harvester’s Flames - Multiple combinations available

Enhancer Specialist - Multiple combinations available

Ethan leaned forward, interest piqued, and over the next minute, he tapped all but the first trait to see what the price combinations were. It turned out that thanks to his wide array of available primagems, the cost of essence had been reduced. Not the fragments, though. All upgrades still required the same amount of fragments, but while before some of them cost 500 essence, now that was lowered, replaced by multiple combinations of primagems. And if a trait required a mid-grade primagem, that could also be replaced by ten low-grade ones.

“Conviction’s a bit of a wild card, I see,” he mumbled, scanning the prices. It made sense that the primagem was this versatile, considering Zacharia had used his Prime Manifestation both offensively and defensively. So it was no big surprise that every single upgrade could use it. But of course, Ethan could use it just once and with how valuable it was… he was tempted to keep it aside for a potential spell upgrade instead.

“Yeah… I think that’d be better,” he said with a nod. Then he focused on the remaining cost and after a few minutes, he managed to pick the two upgrades he wanted and that didn’t have too much overlap in cost.

Child of Flames - 5000 fragments, 100 Essence, 1 Growth Primagem (mid-grade), 2 Barrier Primagems (low-grade), 3 Crystallization Primagems (low-grade), 5 Hardening Primagems (low-grade)

Enduring Brawler - 5000 fragments, 100 Essence, 1 Storm Primagem (mid-grade), 1 Draining Primagem (mid-grade)

Ethan eyed the two for a few seconds, then with a flick of his finger, he loaded up their description.

Child of Flames: The blaze knows its own. While it scorches the world, it stitches you whole. Your flames protect you and accelerate your recovery.

Enduring Brawler: Harder to break, quicker to mend. Get more out of your Toughness stat and recover faster.

Considering the supposed danger of the rift he was about to delve in, these would be good upgrades to get. At least, so long as the rift didn’t disable them. They were some of his earliest traits and he wouldn’t have been here without them. They were more defensive than offensive, which he felt would be needed in case some of his abilities were taken away and while the cost of upgrading them was pretty damn high, he was pretty sure it’d be worth it. As for the primagems themselves… well. He be might using quite a few of them, but so long as rifts were around, he’d replenish his stash and in any case, he still had plenty that wouldn’t be used here. So with his decision settled, he accepted the first of the two.

Instantly, there was a pulse of heat around him. Then the air ignited into warm, bright golden flames that were soon joined by the dissolved essence of the primagems the upgrade had called for. In fact, he decided to get up and move away from the rest of the primagems still arrayed in front of him, just in case the fire could damage them. And there might have been a chance that they would have, as the flames kept growing and spreading around him, surrounding him like a thick, cocoon that he still could see out of. The heat seeped into his flesh and spirit, burning away his fatigue and worries, while the flames continued to swell and increase, to the point that soon, Ethan found himself frowning as it a nearby half-submerged boat caught fire. It was like he was caught in the midst of an inferno, and slowly, the heat seeped into his flesh and bone, searing him from the inside out. Ethan grit his teeth and endured though thankfully, before the heat got too bad, the process mellowed down and in just a few seconds it extinguished, leaving the cement glowing under his feet, while the still burning boat continued to burn.

“At least the fire got nowhere to go,” he grumbled, raising both arms so the breeze would help cool him off as he eyed the notification.

Child of Flames has been upgraded to Child of Flames (I)

Ethan raised a brow, then chuckled. “Simple enough.”

Taking a look at the description, he found it to be unchanged, though he felt it, deep within, that the trait was stronger. He considered testing it first, but then seeing the mess of primagems still ahead of him, he opted to finish the second upgrade then test everything together and so, he tapped the second upgrade and closed his eyes.

He was looking forward to seeing how these changes affected him.
