Epilogues

I. The Trickster

It had been so long since the last time the Trickster felt the tug at his Core, calling him to convene with the other members of the Thirteen. And after what they had just witnessed take place, this was going to be quite the eventful meeting! A seam unzipped in the air with a sound like shattering glass, and he stepped through on a burst of starlight, sandals touching nothing for a heartbeat before reality remembered it was supposed to hold him. The Space Between Realms clung to him in afterimages, cold shimmer around his shoulders. A faint scent of old, well-traveled roads heralded his arrival. After all, he may be the Trickster, but his origins ran so much deeper.

The Trickster smiled as he came through the portal, already savoring the moment he would press his thumb into the Silent One’s smug pride and twist it as hard as her could. I told you so, he would say. He loved being right. Only a little less so when it meant they were all in peril as a result.

Chernobog had reached into the Game. Their Game. That was confirmed now. An indisputable fact that he and his brothers and sisters would need to wrestle with. The black stain in Tartarus had been moments away from forcing a collision between the Nether and Earth’s reality.

If that was possible… then what else could that monstrosity do while imprisoned?

It should have been impossible. Deep within Tartarus, behind bars of pure, endless eternity, under the weight of the Pact and the Thirteen’s own seals, bolstered further by most of the power of a World Seed.

And yet…

Influencing the Game at that level could only be done at very specific seams in the System’s integration with a Realm. He knew the seams well. He knew where the stitching was thin. He had tugged on a few threads himself during the User onboarding process. Just small nudges that mortals would call coincidence and gods would call a good laugh.

He stepped fully into the gathering and found ten of the others already there, arranged in a loose ring within a chamber of black stone and drifting constellations. Not a place that existed anywhere, and therefore a place that existed everywhere. Their shared meeting ground, their courtroom… their kennel (at least until the Game had concluded and their access to the Ascended Realm was re-opened).

Nine of his siblings turned their attention toward him at once:

The Lady of Chains

The Arbiter.

The Forge Father.

The Maw.

The Mother.

The Starcatcher.

The Storm Singer.

The Dancer.

The Endless Field.

And with the Trickster, arriving at the same instant through a portal of vines and brilliant foliage, was the Verdant Keeper, green light spilling across the stone like spilled wine.

That makes eleven, he thought, taking his place.

Which meant the absences were loud. The Radiant Herald was not here. And neither was the Silent One.

The Trickster donned his mask, delicately placing a grin upon the face he’d chosen to wear for this momentous meeting of the Thirteen.

He’d arrived mid-conversation—he could tell by the posture of the Arbiter, the way the Mother’s gaze was angled toward an empty space, the faint tension threading the room like wire. They were speaking about him, then.

He gave them a slow look, letting it linger on each face.

“Oh?” he said lightly, spreading his hands. “That’s adorable. Please, do go on. Do not let my arrival interrupt you… Did you all miss me?”

The Forge Father rumbled, voice like stone scraping stone. “How can we be sure this isn’t one of your tricks?”

A fire erupted before the Forge Father, and within the flames was an image of a gigantic fist pulverizing a demonic avatar.

The Trickster feigned offense so perfectly even he almost believed it himself.

“Me?” he asked, pressing a hand to his chest. “Brother, that wounds me. Truly.”

The Forge Father’s eyes narrowed. “The timing is quite strange, with your recently being freed. And there is no way Chernobog can influence the contest from imprisonment. It’s simply impossible. And so, I am of the belief that this is nothing more than one of your tricks, attempting to get a rise out of us. Petty revenge!”

The Trickster tilted his head, the rooster-tail feather behind his ear catching starlight from the swirling constellations.

“And yet,” he said, voice bright as a blade, “something tried to drag the Nether into Earth. That’s a difficult trick, even for someone of my talents. And I know you all felt it too. That was a real threat.” He let the words hang for a beat. “If you’re accusing me, then you’re accusing the Silent One as well. He and I personally went to confirm Chernobog’s imprisonment.”

He smiled, all teeth. “Unless, of course, one of you has been sneaking off to do a little redecorating in Tartarus without inviting me.”

The Arbiter’s gaze sharpened. The Storm Singer’s hair crackled. The Maw’s grin widened like it enjoyed the taste of paranoia.

The Trickster leaned in, tone almost conspiratorial. “So yes. Strange indeed.”

As if summoned by the mention of his name, a portal of gold light blossomed behind them. The Silent One stepped through, white robes untouched by even a single particle of filth, his presence pressing down on the chamber like a law of nature.

He said nothing. He simply looked at the Trickster.

And the Trickster returned the look with a grin.

“Brother,” the Trickster greeted. “Just on time.”

That left only one missing: the Radiant Herald.

A deep quake rolled through their Cores, through the very essence that made them gods, through the ancient architecture of their being. The chamber shuddered in reprise.

The Trickster’s grin faltered.

That was the System, the Vestiges of Creation. Its declaration hit them like a hammer hitting a bell. It was impossible to ignore. It was the same sensation they experienced every Game at this phase. The System would explain the rules that applied to the Thirteen. The only problem was, it was doing so unusually early. Typically, it would only happen when all of the gods had arrived to their meeting place.

The System Message began.

It outlined the Blessings—how they would be granted, how they would bind, how the gods were otherwise not permitted to “directly interfere.” It reiterated the end condition, the only condition that mattered:

The winner will be decided by whoever’s Champion claims the power of the World Seed at the end of the Game.

Then it began listing them, as though taking attendance.

Lady of Chains.

Arbiter.

Forge Father.

Maw.

Mother.

Starcatcher.

Storm Singer.

Dancer.

Endless Field.

Verdant Keeper.

Silent One.

Trickster.

And then… the System glitched.

ERROR!

. . .

Silence.

Radiant Herald has been removed.

Reason: elimination.

The Trickster’s mind raced. Eliminated? Gods did not simply get eliminated. Not in the Game.

The System continued, as if nothing had happened.

. . .

The Beast.

A pause that felt like the universe holding its breath.

“…the Beast,” one of his siblings breathed.

Dread filled the Trickster, sudden and thick as tar.

Not the playful dread of a wager gone wrong. But the kind of dread that whispered, This isn’t your Game anymore.

II. Illrune Abascal

Illrune Abascal had just killed a god.

Or, at least, the closest thing to one this cursed Realm of theirs could spit out without the universe coming to smite them all out of existence for their hubris.

Pride of the Cardinal Hand was dead. And by Illrune’s blade.

That should have felt like a clean, crystalline moment. A triumphant spike of victory cutting through the noise of war. Instead, it felt like the aftermath of a terrible and awkward sexual encounter: laying there with ringing ears, and the weird, floating disbelief that it had actually happened.

Of course, he wasn’t stupid enough to pretend it had been just him. It had taken their entire legion to bring the demigod down far enough for a mortal strike to matter. Bodies of his comrades had been broken like kindling. Lady Sloth’s training and tutelage had made him stronger than he had ever believed possible, but Pride had been… Well, Pride. Some believed he was the most powerful of the Cardinal Hand. Less a man, than a thing that had worn divinity like a tailored suit.

And yet… Divinity bled all the same, it seemed. Illrune knew that with intimate certainty because he was soaked in it.

Pride’s blood pooled behind him in a thick, black-red slick that steamed faintly against the scorched stone. It coated Illrune’s arms, his chest, his throat. It made his hair stick to his forehead and his clothes cling like a second skin. The stench was sharp and wrong, like incense burned in a slaughterhouse.

Worse than the blood was the weight. Pride’s dead body lay on top of him. The demigod’s eyes stared lifelessly into Illrune’s face from inches away—empty now, but still carrying the echo of arrogance, the lingering I should not be here expression that even death couldn’t fully erase. Blood dripped from the man’s lips and onto Illrune’s chin. The corpse was heavy in a way Illrune hadn’t expected. It was the weight of what the thing had been. When the two had tumbled to the ground, Pride ready to kill him with his bare hands, the man’s weight had felt different. Now, the corpse was a stubborn force of gravity. Illrune was beginning to understand the meaning of the term ‘dead weight.’

His arms were wedged awkwardly under the dead man. His hands were still locked around the hilt of his blade, which was buried deep within Pride’s body, sunk up to the crossguard. The Skill he’d used—[Liar’s Blade]—had been intended as a last-ditch effort. A cruel little trick for the moment you were out of options and out of mercy, and needed a fighter’s shot at survival.

Pride’s own arrogance had allowed it to work.

The demigod had assumed Illrune had run out of tricks, and magic, and was unarmed.

And that assumption had been the last lie Pride ever believed.

Illrune tried to shift and failed. Every muscle in his body screamed. His ribs ached, and his lungs felt bruised. It was difficult to catch his breath. Especially when choking on Pride’s blood.

“P-Please help,” he gasped, voice coming out thin and pathetic in the aftermath of the greatest accomplishment of his life. “Get this asshole off of me!”

A chuckle answered him.

Dain. The silver-haired elf moved into Illrune’s line of sight like a nightmare made useful, poking over the corpse’s shoulder and casting Illrune in shadow. The lieutenant’s crimson eyes glowed faintly with the remnants of active magic. Dain wore battered armor and a grin too calm for the carnage around them. The armor was grafted to expose his one enchanted arm. The metallic arm bestowed by their Lady Sloth gleamed in the blazing, afternoon sun, catching the light like a blade’s edge.

“You always know how to make a moment poetic,” Dain said, amusement curling around the words.

Then, he obliged.

The metallic arm slid under Pride’s corpse like it was lifting a sack of grain, and Dain hauled the dead demigod up with insulting ease. The body came free with a wet sound, and Illrune sucked in a breath like he’d been pulled out from underwater.

He scrambled, rolling to his side, then pushing himself up with shaking arms. The moment his weight was on his feet, the adrenaline hit him again like a second heartbeat. He wrenched his blade free from the corpse Dain held one-handed, blood suctioning around the steel. He didn’t even bother wiping it before storing it in his Inventory.

Pride’s body then began to dissolve into light. It unraveled into threads of radiance peeling away into the air towards Dain. Dain stepped closer, palm open, and the last remnants of the corpse vanished, stored away into his Inventory.

Illrune’s eyes narrowed at that.

Dain’s new Skills permitted him to store living and once-living creatures in his storage space. That wasn’t possible with ordinary Inventories. But Sloth didn’t hand out ordinary gifts.

Dain brushed non-existent dust from his metallic fingers and looked at Illrune with a satisfied tilt of the head.

“Our Lord-lady will be pleased,” Dain said. “That leaves only the three now.”

Illrune nodded.

Sloth. Greed. Gluttony.

The only three living members of the Cardinal Hand that remained after nearly two years of conflict.

Each with a legion. Each now securing one of the three spots that their world was being given in the new divine contest.

Illrune felt the shape of that truth settle into him. A new horizon. The war hadn’t ended; it had just changed its rules. The easy targets were gone. The pretenders were dead.

He’d killed a demigod.

So, what came next?

He glanced at the pooling blood, at the scorched battlefield. At the lingering, sparkling flecks of light where Pride had been erased. He could still feel the phantom weight of the corpse on his chest, the echo of those dead eyes inches from his own. And beneath the exhaustion, beneath the pain, beneath the sticky clench of terror that came after surviving something you shouldn’t have… There was hunger.

There were names in his mind. Faces he’d left behind when he’d only been the younger son of the Abascal crime family. Before his old world had been burned to the ground, and Lady Sloth’s hand had reached down and pulled him from the ashes.

There were people he wanted to be reacquainted with. There were debts that had never been collected.

Illrune wiped Pride’s blood from his lips with the back of his hand and felt himself smile.

III. Sarah Zorbas

The private bar at Pegasus Los Angeles had been designed to make powerful people feel comfortable with the idea of not being comfortable. It was a subtle display of power, as were all things Pegasus Guild. It was all dark wood, brushed steel, and soft lighting that pretended it was warm. Everything expensive, everything tasteful, everything exclusive.

Typically, the wall of screens behind the bar would be formed into a single screensaver-like image of non-descript lights and graphics. Tonight, the wall behind the bar was nothing but chaos. The wall was synced into a single image. One feed, one story. The footage from Cleveland, Ohio, and the recent Mass Outbreak Event.

Sarah stood at the counter with a drink in hand that she hadn’t tasted in ten minutes. The bartender—a Level 35 Mixologist with the ability to craft potion cocktails offering excellent buffs and recovery effects—stood off to the side, silent and patiently waiting to tend to her needs. Ice clinked softly in the glass in Sarah’s hand. Her fingers were tight around it, knuckles pale as frost.

On the screens, Cleveland’s downtown city center… If you could call it that… looked like a devastated war zone. Ash drifting through the air like snow. Buildings torn apart and crumbling. Fires burning and imps choking the sky. And in the center of it… a single man.

He stood in a large crater, broad and muscled like he’d been carved out of industrial equipment and stone. Steam rolled off his body in visible waves. He wore a cape and a pointy wizard’s hat with a wide brim that threw his face into shadow. He also wore three-inch inseam jean shorts. And a fanny pack. Despite how ridiculous that all should have all been, he seemed so… epic. Like the Greek and Roman heroes captured in sculptures across Europe.

A faint blue mist drifted from where his eyes should be, leaking out from beneath the shadow of the hat brim like his gaze was still burning, even if the cameras couldn’t quite catch it. His face was stoic. Not triumphant in a moment of heroic victory. Just… set. Like a man who’d done what he had to do and didn’t want anyone clapping about it. The camera focused before panning out. The whole thing made Sarah feel like she was watching one of the old Westerns her grandfather always enjoyed. And this was the America hero who just saved the town from the outlaw in donned in black.

The caption at the bottom of the screen screamed in bold white text:

MUSCLE MAGE HERO SAVES THE DAY!

Joseph Sullivan of the Harvest Guild faces off against Extinction-Level Interdimensional Threat!

Sarah’s drink tilted in her hand. She was so shocked she almost dropped it.

Her throat went tight and her heart stuttered. Her mind flicked through the footage again and again, trying to convince itself it was a coincidence. Some other man with the same name… From the same city… But when the camera finally closed in with a tight shot on his face, she knew it was undeniable.

“Hey,” a voice said from behind her, lazy and amused. “Why does that man look familiar?”

It was Jase.

The young black Captain of Pegasus Guild, known publicly as the Crimson Pact (though he’d insisted everyone just call him Crimson) was sprawled on the couch behind her like this was a movie night and not a tragedy. His boots were up on the coffee table. He was all grins and confidence.

He watched the footage of the gigantic magical fist descending from the sky and annihilating the large demon. The report credited the footage to Stephanie Wolf. Of course it did. Of course she would have her hands all over this.

“Wait a sec!” Jase suddenly sprang up like his body finally caught up to his brain. He jogged to the bar, hands landing on Sarah’s shoulders, in a familiar and playful sort of way. The Pegasus Captains had all grown close since the early days of the private selection process Silver ran to form the Guild. He pointed at the screen like he’d discovered buried treasure.

“I’ve seen pictures of that guy!” he exclaimed. “No fucking way. That’s the guy you dumped! Left at the altar, right?”

Sarah’s jaw clenched so hard it ached. She’d broken off their engagement months in advance of their wedding date, but that wasn’t going to stop Jase from describing it like there’d been a dramatic walkout in front of sobbing guests. That’s probably how Joe felt about it, at least.

“Shut up,” Sarah snapped, voice low and sharp.

Jase laughed anyway did. He could smell being right, and having found a sensitive spot, the way some hounds smelled blood.

“Wow,” he said, leaning closer to the screen. “Was he always that ripped? Might need to get his number so I can hit him up for his shoulder routine.”

Sarah placed her drink on the bar countertop with controlled care. The ice clinked, a tiny polite sound in a room full of dead people on screens. The shots quickly glanced over the carnage, and attempted to not show it straight on, but the words on the screen filled in the gaps.

RESHMA MURMU AND MATTHEW BRUCK, OF THE PEGASUS GUILD, SLAIN IN BATTLE. DIED FIGHTING TO PROTECT INNOCENT LIVES IN MASS OUTBREAK EVENT.

“We’ve lost two of our Captains,” she said, voice trembling just enough to make it worse, “and you’re joking right now? Really, Jase?”

Jase shrugged, utterly unbothered.

“Workplace hazard,” he said. Then he grinned wider. “They knew what they signed up for. And anyway, Matt was really pissing me off with how much he was trying to steal my spot.”

He gestured vaguely at the screens, as if death was just a metric.

“I’ve gained two thousand new followers since this happened. All fans jumping ship back to the Crimson Crew! So…” He shrugged again, like that settled the moral math and was happy enough with the results.

Sarah stared at him with something cold in her chest. She let her aura leak out, just enough to hopefully give him frostbite on the tips of his fingers. The System would regenerate the damage, but he’d feel the numbing sting first.

“You’re disgusting,” she said. She meant it.

She was about to say something else—something that would leave a mark—when a new voice cut through the air.

“Ms. Zorbas.”

Sarah turned her head, just enough to see who it was. One of Geraint Silver’s assistants stood at the edge of the bar area, posture stiff, expression careful. He was kind of person trained to deliver orders without appearing to enjoy it. A young man who also probably didn’t realize that this was as far as life was taking him, no matter how ambitious he was. Poor loser…

“Mr. Silver would like to speak with you in his office,” the assistant said. “Now, please.”

Jase’s hands gave her shoulders a playful shake, like he couldn’t help himself. He relinquished his grip, shaking out his hands with a mock expression of pain.

“Boss man is calling,” he sang. “Go get ’em, girl!”

Sarah didn’t dignify that with a response. She just picked up her drink, thought better of it, left it on the bar, and followed the assistant toward the hallway.

Behind her, Jase laughed.

On the screens, the footage was looping. A man in the crater stood like a monument carved out of muscle and blue, magical light. The last remaining golden fireflies of Reshma’s magic clinging to his shoulders before fading into the dark gloom and haze.

IV. Geraint Silver

Geraint Silver was a numbers man. Everything… Everything could be distilled to a simple value and run through a cost-benefit analysis.

Reshma Murmu: a high-value asset with an astronomical cost of replacement. Public goodwill loss was also moderate. Operational impact? Significant.

Matthew Bruck: a moderate-to-high value asset, though easily replaceable. Practically half the applications for Pegasus Guild were System Users trying to maximize speed and damage output. Public goodwill loss was also moderate. Operational impact? Moderate.

The numbers were unpleasant. It was never ideal to lose valuable assets. He had spent time, resources, and prestige cultivating them. He didn’t like waste. This, however, wasn’t waste. It was a loss leader.

After all, he had gained something potentially far more valuable.

Geraint’s eyes flicked to the System window floating in front of him, re-reading the messages he had received in the wake of the Cleveland Gate Incident. He savored the words.

Trait: [High Priest of Chernobog]

Description: You are the most-favored of Chernobog.  Your [Soul-Touched] Trait has been enhanced to its maximum potential. Your [Soul Hunter] Skill has been upgraded from (Immaculate) to (Mythical). You have gained additional Gifts of Chernobog. See your [Skill] Menu for additional Skills.

QUEST UPDATE!

Quest: Soul-Touched

Description: You are one of the 100 Soul-Touched of Chernobog.

Objective: Be the last remaining Soul-Touched of Chernobog actively participating in the God Game.

A Soul-Touched (Chosen Priest) has been eliminated.

Elimination Type: Death

Elimination Type: Opt-Out Selected (Removed from Game)

. . .

Additional Soul-Touched (12) have been eliminated.

Elimination Types: Death (1), Out-Out Selected (Removed from Game) (11)

. . .

QUEST COMPLETE: Soul-Touched

You are the last remaining Soul-Touched of Chernobog actively participating in the God Game.

Final Soul Confluence in process…

. . .

Final Soul Confluence complete!

Please see Attributes to observe all increased Stat Gains.

Reward: Divine Boon of Chernobog

You have received [Eye of Chernobog] (x1)

[Eye of Chernobog] added to your Inventory

Trait: Soul-Touched (Mythical)

Divine Boon has been improved. Divine Boon increased to three!

You have received [Tongue of Chernobog] (x1)

[Tongue of Chernobog] added to your Inventory

You have received [Tome of the Beast] (x1)

[Tome of the Beast] added to your Inventory

Geraint basked in the glory of it all. In fact, he was as giddy as a child on Christmas morning. He would need to further examine his new Divine Boons, but that would wait. He had business to attend to after all. He was divinely selected and had a purpose greater than anyone could imagine. No one had the vision he had. He could see where this was all leading. It was all to serve him on his journey to a higher realm. A place where he would finally walk among peers.

And there was the final System message that he’d received…

You have received the Blessing of the Beast.

Check your Status Menu for more information on [Blessings].

Geraint’s smile was small, practiced, and entirely sincere.

He let himself chuckle, only a little. Excellent.

He could feel it already—the subtle shift in his internal architecture, something tightening into place as it settled onto something in his very core. It was god placing its hand on his soul and whispering, You are mine.

Geraint leaned back slightly, letting the satisfaction wash through him. He belonged to no one. And by the time Chernobog, or these faint echoes of this lost entity, caught on, it would be far too late. Chernobog had laid the groundwork for his divine ascent. After all, wasn’t Chernobog a Participant long, long ago? Someone who had attempted—and failed—at the same goal Geraint himself had?

Chernobog: a high-value asset with astronomical cost of replacement (but not irreplaceable). Public goodwill loss? Zero. Operational impact: Mitigate by the commencement of Phase 2 of the Game.

There was a knock at his door.

He closed the System windows with a thought, sealing them away behind the pleasant mask he wore for the world. He adjusted his posture by a fraction, softening his expression into the version of himself that people found reassuring.

“Come in,” he called.

The door opened.

Sarah Zorbas stepped inside.

Ah, yes. Good.

He’d already received a detailed intel report on this Joseph Sullivan. Muscle Mage, Harvest Guild, new public darling with momentum and myth forming around him like a halo. The report had been thorough.

How fortunate, then, that Sarah happened to have history with this man. Life came easy to Geraint. Fortune always seemed to bend to his will, like a branch bowed under a hand. It did so here, too. He watched Sarah cross the office, her posture controlled, her face composed, and he saw the tension behind it.

“Sarah,” he said warmly, rising just enough to signal importance without wasting effort. He gestured to one of the chairs in front of his desk—the good chair, not the one meant to make visitors feel small. “Come in and have a seat, please.”

Sarah sat.

Geraint folded his hands on the desktop, a picture of calm authority.

“I have a task for you,” he said, voice smooth as polished stone.
