The following few days were strangely normal, all things considered. Despite the fact that Donvath had made it clear the Council of Death was going to be moving onto the Noala Kingdom before long, there wasn’t really a whole lot the coalition was able to do about it.

At this point, the Council was down to only five members, and they didn’t have any leads on a single one of their positions. Only one of the remaining five had a journal with a potential location, but Donvath was able to confirm that a few glimpses into the future showed that the lich had either up and moved in order to protect itself, or Zygrul’s hunch on where they had been staying was incorrect. While Donvath’s ability to view the future surrounding wizards was flaky at best, it seemed his ability to view futures that didn’t include them worked just fine.

With no further leads on the whereabouts of the final five liches and no real way to track them down, all they could do… was wait.

“I don’t like this,” Moxy grumbled as she punched a few rapid Air Blasts in Asher’s direction, each one of which was easily dodged via flickers into the astral realm. “I like being the one doing the attacking, not the other way around. How many times have I told you, the key to winning a fight is to be acting instead of reacting.”

“If you’ve got some way of finding any of the five liches who have managed to remain hidden for the past few thousand years and you just haven’t told us about it yet, by all means, we’d love to hear it,” Asher drawled, turning and hurling his dagger toward her with every intention of lodging the blade directly between her eyes. If he fought with anything less than his best, Moxy would get angry with him. He’d learned long ago that the only thing worse than fighting against Moxy was fighting against angry Moxy.

Twisting to the side and batting the blade away with a pelt-covered hand, Moxy went to throw another punch his way before he vanished, reappearing directly beside her at the mark he’d placed on the dagger. But rather than get the drop on her, Asher cursed as the living pelt he hadn’t seen leap off her landed right in his face, sinking its claws directly into his eyes.

“God!” he shouted, reflexively slipping into the astral to dislodge the undead pelt. Of course, he’d already been blinded, and he frowned as he returned to the material realm and held up his hands. The world was nothing but darkness, but Asher didn’t let that stop him from trying to glare at where Moxy had been standing a moment ago. “Was going for the eyes really necessary? I thought you didn’t like to use your blended skills during sparring! That’s a waste of a pelt!”

“I’m trying to prove a point,” Moxy snorted. Asher heard her walk over and felt her hard, calloused hand press against his forehead. He didn’t have to see to know that there was a bright violet glow coming from her hand, and within seconds, he was blinking through watering eyes again as the sensory organs were repaired good as new.

Moxy’s healing wasn’t nearly that fast on anyone else when it came to complex injuries, but combined with his own healing and his amulet, he was a special case. “You’re getting predictable. The trick with the daggers is neat, but anyone who's seen or heard about it is going to be able to turn that trick into a liability. There’s a split second where you have to get your bearings after you use Recall, and that’s more than enough time to shove a blade through your chest or stab you in the eyes if your opponent knows you’re coming.”

“You know most people are capable of making points without maiming the ones they’re teaching,” Asher grumbled as he wiped the tears from his new eyes.

“Probably. But I find teaching by example tends to leave a stronger impression,” Moxy grinned. “Just be happy I stopped there. With your ability to heal, you should really start practicing fighting blind one of these days.”

“I can do the same thing with a blindfold!” he argued. “I’m not going to start gouging my eyes out before sparring sessions!”

Yet even as he argued with Moxy, he realized that, like always, Moxy had a good point. He’d been relying on his Eternal Mark and Recall combo more and more lately during his battles, and as she’d so helpfully just showed him, the move really did open him up to a split second of retaliation that he couldn’t do a damn thing about. Ideally he’d try to alternate more between relying on that and Distortion to close the distance between him and his opponents. Maybe start throwing daggers as feints instead of always Recalling to them when they were dodged. Giving Moxy a begrudging thanks for helping him come to the realization now rather than in the heat of an important battle, he called it a day on the sparring. After Recalling back to the palace to get cleaned, he moved onto the next portion of his day.

Training his skills that were lagging behind.

With nothing to do beyond waiting for the liches to make a move, Asher had been spending a significant portion of his days hard at work improving his non-combat skills. As eager as he was to try and get Sever the Thread and Astral Dip over that last little precipice and into their third-tier evolutions, he knew from experience he was going to need an actual battle with some serious stakes in order to make it happen. Instead, he focused on his other skills. Hitting the Stage of Power required all of one’s skills to reach the third tier, which meant if he wanted to figure out what secrets were being held from all of them, he had a lot of work to do.

Fighting two birds with one stone, Asher spent a lot of his time in the palace library, Incorporating anything and everything that he thought might be useful one day, as well as using Comprehend to read through the things he thought might clue him in on what Gran and her strange pact-members might be hiding. Now that he knew what to look for, he was able to confirm that there was something odd going on regarding their entire continent.

While there were plenty of histories about the founding of different countries, the amount of information on the founding of their continent as a whole was pretty much nonexistent. The history books just… never went into detail whenever the topic came up. Based on what they said, it was almost as if people had always been on this continent. Which, while Asher supposed that could very well be the case, he highly doubted was quite that simple. But even if his hunt for knowledge didn’t bear any fruit, the days of scouring the library and all but downloading books into his head for his future perusal did what he wanted it to.
