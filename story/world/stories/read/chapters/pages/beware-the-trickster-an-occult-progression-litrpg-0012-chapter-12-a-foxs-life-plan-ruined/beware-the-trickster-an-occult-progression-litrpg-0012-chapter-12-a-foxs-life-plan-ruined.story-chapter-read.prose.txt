Well, I was just wondering how Julia planned to explain how the different Aspects actually worked. She could “communicate” with me… well, not in any telepathic way, but in the sense that I could understand her words. A full year had been enough for me to pick up the language she spoke. Even stripped of my past-life memories, I was still me: a sapient soul stuffed into the body of a fox. So, I learned. And I learned fast. Julia taught me the way you’d teach a toddler, patiently, repeatedly, and with lots of pointing and gesturing.

The difference was that I wasn’t some slow-developing human baby; I picked things up far quicker.

Written language, however, was another matter entirely. My education never stretched to the written word. But that didn’t really matter, Julia knew I could understand her spoken language perfectly. I mean, once your fox starts reacting appropriately to every word you say, it’s hard not to notice she’s actually parsing the sounds rather than just responding to tone.

And Julia talked. Oh, she talked. The woman could fill the silence of a monastery library with her running commentary, and frankly, that constant stream of chatter only sped up my learning curve.

Right now, I was tilting my head at the latest of her “teaching aids”. It was a stick figure sketch depicting one triumphant, beam-shooting hero of holy solar justice immolating a crowd of clearly evil stick figures, their tiny arms raised in agony. She narrated over it like a tour guide, explaining in enthusiastic detail why the Sun Aspect was, without any contest, the most superior choice among the three options available to me.

Her “teaching method” was questionable at best, but I didn’t exactly mind. I’d already done my own digging into Aspect lore once before, so this was more of a recap than a revelation. Still, it was useful for spotting her glaring bias toward the Sun Aspect, something she clearly intended to save as her grand finale.

“Wouldn’t it be brilliant if you could do it too, Clover?” she said, eyes shining. “Just like me! We could team up and blast those baddies who tried to snatch you earlier!” She flailed her hands in mock battle “Swoosh! Woop! BURN HERETIC!” like she was half-instructing, half-rehearsing for a children’s puppet show.

Agnes, standing behind her, was quietly chuckling at the spectacle.

But for me the Sun Aspect was completely off the table. Julia’s bias wasn’t without merit, it was indeed powerful, versatile, and undeniably straightforward. But that was exactly the problem. That brand of straightforwardness didn’t resonate with me, not in the slightest. Worse, I had a gnawing instinct that it was fundamentally incompatible with me. Even now, just looking at that golden, pulsating heart-shaped core floating in front of Julia made my skin prickle. A faint burning sensation crept through me. It was a visceral warning I wasn’t about to ignore, especially after what had happened earlier.

So no, picking it was out of the question. That left the other two options, each strong in its own right. But this wasn’t a decision I could walk back. Once I picked an Aspect, I’d be locked in for good. And the real kicker was that these Inquisitors only had information up to Tier 2 for the Moon Aspect and Tier 1 for the Chain Aspect… barely scratching the surface.

[It would be fair to assume they didn’t even have a method for advancing those paths any further.]

Elaborate on that.

[Well, from the information I just processed, advancement requires a second set of Aspect-specific organs once your current ones are fully assimilated and refined to their peak potential. The process is termed an 'advancement ritual.' The moment you absorb your first set of organs and enter the tier 1 cultivation stage, you immediately unlock two additional slots within your soul world. Technically, you could absorb the next tier's organs right then, but every text I scanned vehemently advised against it, labeling it a catastrophic trap and a desperate gamble. Until your tier one organs are completely refined, attempting to ascend risks a horrifying mutation, transforming the cultivator into a thing of the dark. It did not seem like a good experience.

The archives included grim records of individuals who attempted it out of desperation; they now serve as cautionary examples.]

I thought about that…

So when you state the Inquisitors lack a method to advance, do you mean they are ignorant of the specific organs required? Or is the ritual itself a complex, undiscovered procedure?

[You've hit the core of it. The Sun Inquisitors here appear to lack the fundamental botanical, anatomical, or astrological knowledge to identify the correct tier 2 organs for the Chain Aspect, and likewise for the Moon Aspect beyond tier 2. Even for their own Sun Aspect, their comprehensive data seems to cap at tier 3. The 'ritual' itself is likely the act of finding and properly integrating those correct components.]

That revelation spawned a cascade of questions. From my observations, the Sun Inquisitors presented themselves as a powerful and influential organization. So why was their repository of essential knowledge so… incomplete? It spoke volumes about how severely gatekept the path to true power must be in this world. Was this fragmentation intentional, a result of lost knowledge, or… simply the immense difficulty of discovery?

[Well, it's a combination of factors. Cultivating an Aspect is inherently a slow, meticulous process of bodily refinement. It might explain why the Captain is only at tier 3 after who knows how many years. We still don't know the upper limits of these tiers; that aberrant flesh entity Lucian summoned operated on a completely different, and likely higher scale. Therefore, I conclude it would be prudent to acquire more information before we irrevocably commit to an aspect.]

I yawned, my foxy jaw snapping shut with a soft click. Well, that settled it. Procrastination was officially the most strategic choice available.

I shook my head decisively at Julia’s hopeful expression.

Julia’s face fell. “So… not the Sun, then…” She moved her finger to hover over the second, Moon one. “What about the Moon?!” Another firm shake of my head prompted her to finally indicate the sketched chains of the third option. That, too, received a refusal.

My consistent denial finally gave her the hint. “Oh. You don’t want to choose anything right now.”

I nodded in firm agreement.

Agnes, who had been waiting patiently in the background, finally chuckled and stepped closer. “That is the most rational choice. This isn’t a decision to be rushed. Once she picks her Aspect, she will be permanently locked into that aspect. There is absolutely no pressure for her to choose immediately.”

I internally sighed with relief. That would solve the immediate problem, granting me the necessary time to learn how this entire intricate system truly worked.

Julia’s eyes drifted down to the luminous, golden beating heart hovering before her. “So… should I just keep channeling energy into it? Without stopping? That is the most efficient way to assimilate it…”

My gaze followed hers, noting that only a small fraction, perhaps eight percent of its surface, had yet transitioned to that stable, soothing azure hue. The rest still pulsed with a volatile, aggressive gold.

“Well, that’s how it would normally go, yes. But…” Agnes gestured toward the strange suitcase holding the golden heart and the pair of eyes, “this trinket is both enchanted and blessed with the Sun’s power. If you store the organ inside after your cultivation sessions, it’ll bleed out your mana at a much slower rate.”

She paused, thinking. “Normally, every two to three hours you’d lose about one percent of your assimilation progress. But with the heart inside that case, the decay slows to a full twenty-four hours. That means you can stash it away when you’re too exhausted to keep going, take the time to regenerate your mana, and then pick up right where you left off.”

Julia let out a visible sigh of relief. Agnes noticed and chuckled. “Well, cultivation is a dormant practice by nature but that doesn’t mean us mortals can’t improvise and invent clever workarounds.”

The door to the workshop swung open, and the attendant from earlier, Elara, if I remembered correctly, strode in without hesitation.

“Pardon the intrusion, Agnes. Hopefully your work here is just about done?”

Agnes gave a small nod. “I’m wrapping up now. Is something wrong?”

“Nothing urgent. A few things, though… first, I’ve finished processing Julia’s employment papers.” She muttered something about “Captain always dumping last-minute jobs on me,” then produced a neatly stacked set of parchment. “Since she’s here, why not have her sign them now?” She grinned.

Even I raised a brow when the papers hit the table with a thunk.

“Twenty Lumen per week, plus two Solaris stipends quarterly,” Elara read aloud. “Extra benefits for commissions completed and delivered.”

Julia’s eyes went wide. “That’s… a lot. When I left my parents, I worked as a potion maker, filling commissions for local pharmacies. I made that much in nearly two months.”

“Well, the work is far more dangerous now, is it not?” Agnes said smoothly. “It’s only prudent for the Church to care about an Inquisitor’s welfare.” She leaned in and murmured in Julia’s ear, “I could give you tips on earning more through the Indemnity Allowance.”

Elara’s head snapped around, glare locked on Agnes. “You can’t start corrupting the newbie this early!”

Agnes only laughed.

Julia’s attention drifted back to the parchment, though her expression shifted into a frown. “Uh… ‘Sanctioned Asset’?”

“Oh, right.” Elara’s glare switched targets. “Captain told me to add a clause for your familiar. It allocates an extra ten Lumen per week for her upkeep and… involvement.”

Julia’s face immediately lit up with unbridled joy. She scooped me into her arms, squeezing me in a happy hug. “Clover! You got a job too! We’re officially colleagues!”

Meanwhile, my brain completely short-circuited. A job? Why in the world did I have a job?! My life’s ambitions were elegantly simple: to nap in sunbeams, eat generously, and mess with people for my own amusement. Nowhere in that plan was there any provision for steady employment, a weekly salary, or being a ‘Sanctioned Asset’!

I wiggled in protest, but my diminutive size betrayed me, I was just a perfectly portable, utterly helpless ball of fluff in Julia’s ecstatic embrace. My gaze drifted back to the offending parchment, which now seemed to stare me down like a smug predator that had already won. And then, with a flourish, Julia signed her name on the dotted line.

Before I could even muster a squeak of protest, she grabbed a nearby ink pad and, with terrifying efficiency, forcibly stamped my front paw onto the document right next to her signature.

I went limp. I was defeated. By bureaucracy.

I was now… an employed fox.

Elara took the freshly signed and stamped papers, cooing softly at the tiny, inky paw print beside Julia’s signature. Before the moment could settle, Agnes cut back in. “And the second thing you mentioned? The other matter.”

“Oh, right, yes.” Elara’s posture straightened. “The intelligence you requested before you headed out to the Phantom Glades has finally arrived. The local watch has confirmed detecting movement linked to The Chiaroscuro Order. Our sources report their operatives have been seen loitering around Bakers’ Square market for the past several days. Their presence was confirmed again as recently as today.”

Agnes accepted the update with a slow, thoughtful nod. “Thank you for the information, Elara.” Her brow then furrowed. “Bakers’ Square, you said.”

Julia caught her frown. “I’ve heard of the Chiaroscuro Order before, from my parents. They just said they were a bunch of lunatics who believed they needed to ‘hatch’ the moon to free the true Moon Goddess they claim is trapped inside.”

“That is indeed the core of their deranged doctrine,” Agnes confirmed. “They first arrived in Crieffton almost three years ago. And while they have always been a significant pain in the arse, they were, to put it charitably, rather blunt about their actions. Wherever they went, they would cause such loud, chaotic incidents that it made tracking and purging their operations straightforward, if tedious. But recently… they haven’t acted up in months. This new quiet is, in our line of work, far more suspicious than any overt riot.”

“But that doesn’t entirely seem to be the reason for your personal distress,” Julia observed.

“Indeed, it’s not just the Order. It’s the coincidence,” Agnes clarified. “I spoke with the Captain earlier. Since you’ll need a permanent place to stay in the city, he took the liberty of booking a house for you. It’s located just two streets past the Bakers’ Square. It’s one of the more… exquisite properties available to us, his way of ensuring his people have proper facilities for both work and recovery. The subsidized rent is quite doable on your new salary, and in time, you could even purchase it outright. But now, this report means the notoriously quiet Chiaroscuro Order is suddenly lurking in the very neighborhood meant to be your safe haven.”

Julia wore a thoughtful expression. “Well, do we know for certain who attacked us on the road here? Could they be connected?”

“We have no definitive identification yet,” Agnes admitted. “By all logical means, our ambushers must have been affiliated with the Deathless Cult. Perhaps a few scattered members we missed managed to call upon reinforcements. But…” She paused, choosing her words carefully. “I have my doubts. Those individuals never fought with the intent to kill us. Their entire strategy was to stall for something… I felt a distinct mana wave pulse from their direction at the very end of the skirmish, right as they broke and ran. We still have no idea what their true objective was, but their methodology feels entirely separate. I do not believe they are related to the Chiaroscuro Order in any direct manner.”

“Well,” Julia laughed a little, “I think it’s likely just an unfortunate coincidence. And in a way, wouldn’t it be better if I am stationed around there? As a soon-to-be cultivator and a new official resident, my moving in won’t raise any alarms if the Order is cognizant of their surroundings. I’m just another citizen. It might even provide a perfect cover to observe the area without drawing suspicion.”

“It might indeed present a unique opportunity for observation,” Agnes conceded. “But the answer is still no. Until you have fully assimilated both of your primary organs and solidified your foundation, you are expressly forbidden from meddling with anything remotely supernatural, Inquisitor Julia. Your only mission is cultivation for now.”

Julia offered a casual shrug and a nod, accepting the directive without argument.

“Well, now that all this is settled, it’s been a while.” She glanced toward a large, elegantly ticking clock on the wall. Its hands showed it was nearly 2 PM. We had been sequestered in this room for over six hours. “And I’m certain none of us have had anything to eat. I skipped breakfast, and it’s practically lunchtime.” Agnes facepalmed. “Right. Sustenance. I keep forgetting most mortals can’t just eat one large meal and be set for the week.”

“I can have something brought here if you give me about half an hour?” Elara offered, already half-turning toward the door.

Agnes shook her head, waving off the suggestion. “No, I needed to head up to file this acquisition report anyway. We’ll all go out. Bakers’ Square should be less bustling now, past the midday rush.” She clapped her hands together decisively. “And it presents a perfect chance to show Julia the new place she’ll be residing in. Plus,” she added, “I’ve heard a particular restaurant there makes absolutely exquisite bacon. Thick-cut, smoked over applewood, the works.”

At the mere mention of the word bacon, my little stomach betrayed me with a loud, plaintive growl that echoed in the sudden quiet of the workshop. The entire room fell silent for a beat before bursting into unified laughter.

But I wasn’t thinking about bacon. My thoughts kept circling back to that supposed coincidence.

What were the odds that it wasn’t a coincidence at all, and Julia was walking straight into trouble?
