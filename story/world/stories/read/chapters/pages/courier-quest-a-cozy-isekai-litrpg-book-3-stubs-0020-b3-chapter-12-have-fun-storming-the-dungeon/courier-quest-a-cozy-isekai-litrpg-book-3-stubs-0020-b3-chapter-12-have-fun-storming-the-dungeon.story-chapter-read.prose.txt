They had moved to the entrance of the cave. The sound of waves echoed from inside, and the wind came with an eerie moaning noise that made the hair on the back of Trevor’s neck stand on end. With the mist swirling around them, it looked like the kind of place you would yell at characters in a horror movie not to go into.

So, of course, they were going to disregard that completely.

“They’ve been settled in there for a while now,” Arny explained as he crossed his arms and stared into the cave with them. “We were told to hold off, so the normal Summoned haven’t come down to handle the restless spirits in a while. I’ve got to warn you, they tend to get a bit… weird the longer they’re in our world.”

“Weird in which way?” Adabelle asked. Her voice was even with no trace of fear.

“They get chatty,” the orc snorted. “This ain’t your usual monster den. They aren’t spawning from the magic swirling about, all hungry and ready to jump anyone who enters their lair. These were once actual pirates, bound here in death.”

Trevor looked over in surprise. “Wait, really?”

“Yep. The Dread Pirate Tanazi and the crew of his ship, the Calamitous,” Arny answered with a straight face. The courier tilted his head, trying to decide if he should say something. He was too slow, and their Quest Giver continued. “Died on these rocks a few centuries back in a shipwreck. Stories say that there’s some artifact they picked up that won’t let them rest, reviving them a couple of weeks after the usual Summoned come to slay them.”

“Has anyone tried to find the artifact and let them go on to their eternal slumber?” Trevor asked, feeling more concerned than scared at this point.

“Oh yeah, tons.”

“I wonder if Clay would be able to find it,” he said, patting his familiar absently. The earth elemental was still in front of them like a guard.

“Huh?”

“I mean, it’s gotta be bad for them, right?” Trevor asked, glancing at the cave. “Doomed to haunt this place forever? Clay here’s an earth elemental, so he’ll probably be able to search this place better than most.”

“Oh, don’t do that,” Arny said with a laugh. “We already know where the artifact is. Found it centuries ago. The crew just doesn’t want to pass on yet.”

Adabelle shared a look with her husband. “They enjoy being in a dungeon?”

“According to them, yes. That’s one of the reasons why I called them weird,” Arny replied, shrugging. “They enjoy repelling invaders, heckling them, and we’re on good terms. The captain knows that the armor they create when they reform has beneficial properties, but he’s not really a good enough guy to really care much. He just likes fighting. Whole crew’s the same way.”

“That is certainly an interesting twist,” Trevor said, putting his hands on his hips as he stared into the cave. “When I heard that this was a dungeon with a bunch of spirits inside, I imagined tortured souls wandering with an extreme jealousy of those who were living that had gotten so bad that it’d become straight up hatred. Now I learn that they’re just doing it for the love of the game? I’ll be honest, I’m not sure what to think.”

“That they’re willing to provide us a challenge, and we’re here to meet it?” Adabelle offered before turning her attention to Arny. “They will challenge us, right?”

“I don’t know how strong you two are, so I can’t really answer that question,” he replied, scratching his neck. “They will go for the throat, though. Dungeon’s gotta be scary, they say. They’ll let you flee, though. Captain Tanazi’s told me that it’s great for morale.”

“Not quite ruthless pirates, then,” Trevor stated.

Adabelle nodded in agreement. “You’ve spoken with their leader, Arny?”

“We go way back. My father was the Quest Giver for this dungeon as well as his father before him, almost all the way back,” he said with a proud grin. “Pretty sure I’m descended from someone who made it out of the shipwreck alive. Me and mine have been greeted with nothing but good cheer whenever we come to visit. It’s the biggest reason I’m in charge of giving out the quest. Not for long, though. Going to retire soon. My son’s traveling in preparation for the next Quest Giver qualifiers.”

The courier smiled. “That’s cool. Congrats!”

“Thanks. It’s been a long time coming,” Arny replied before nodding towards the cave. “We’re way off topic now, though. Once you’re in, they’ll hunt you down. If you need to flee, then do it, they won’t chase you out of the dungeon unless you’ve really angered them, but it takes a lot to get to that point. They’ll heckle you pretty much throughout, and they can take it as well as they can dish it.”

Adabelle reached over and took Trevor’s hand. “Ready to go?”

“I’m still trying to get over the heckling pirate ghosts, but I suppose so,” he answered as he reached out and put a hand on Clay. “Ready, little buddy?”

The elemental raised their shield hands into the air before bringing them back down in a boxing stance. Taking that as a yes, Trevor activated Quick Change to don his armor. The process, as its name implied, only took a second as darkness appeared around his body. When it dispersed, he was in a completely different outfit.

Trevor wore chainmail over a blue gambeson. It was comfortable despite the weather being warmer than he was used to at this time of year. His left arm was armored from shoulder to hand, while he had a metal bracer on his right forearm that left his skin exposed from the wrist down. Thick pants with plates above and below his knees protected his legs, and his steel-toed boots had been remade by a cobbler after the soles had finally been worn down from all his travel.

Adabelle’s chainmail was much more intricate, and she wore hers over a white jacket. She drew her sword, gripping it tightly in her leather glove, and gave it a few practice swings. It whistled through the air with enough speed that her husband could actually feel the wind despite the blade being so slender. To Trevor, it appeared as though she wasn’t wearing any armor under her waist, but he had learned that the black skirt she wore had multiple layers, some of which were meant to dull strikes.

“The quest, if you please,” the noblewoman requested as she held her sword out in front of her.

“Quest Master, I’d like these three to take care of the usual,” Arny said. “Now, if you’ll excuse me, I’ve got some reading to do. Have fun storming the dungeon.”

Trevor snorted and waved goodbye to the retreating orc. “Okay, they’ve got to be doing that on purpose.”

“What do you mean?” Adabelle asked, her eyes scanning the space in front of her.

“Just some movie references. Nothing major,” he answered as he looked at the screen the Quest Master dropped.

“More often than you think,” Adabelle answered, offering her husband a smile. “Adventuring parties are usually more than just a few people attacking without any strategy. A well-rounded one would have clear roles, like how I’m the damage dealer, Clay here is our defender, and you’re the support.”

“DPS, tank, thief. Right, right. I’m familiar.”

“I thought that was Clay?” the demonkin giggled. He rolled his eyes, smiling, and she patted him on the shoulder while her tail swished behind her. “The Quest Master usually passes out duties like this depending on the situation. Everyone should be doing something, after all, and having separate tasks can help keep adventurers focused. It’s also telling, sometimes.”

“Yeah? What’s it telling us?” Trevor asked, leaning towards her as he received more of an explanation than he had ever gotten from Wayne.

“That I can handle this Dread Pirate Tanazi myself, though it might not be easy,” she replied, turning her focus back to the cave. “It’s why the quest singled me out. I could have fought him with Clay while you looked for an opportunity to grab his armor, but this way helps me grow and possibly even grants me a boon from it.”

“Okay, I can see that,” he said, looking at the screen again. “I can also see someone getting too fixated on it and getting hurt because they tried to solo the boss. Not that I think you’d fall into that trap.”

“Not anymore,” Adabelle agreed as her smile became wry. “The Quest Master has a great grasp of every Summoned’s personalities, habits, and capabilities, often knowing more about them then they do. It doesn’t give out missions thoughtlessly. If it deems a task impossible, then they simply roll the quest in with the rest of the dungeon. Unless, of course, there’s an emergency situation.”

“Because it’s an emergency?”

The noblewoman nodded. “Sometimes a well-timed quest can change the tides of battle. Help push someone past their limits. That kind of thing. It doesn’t come often, but the Quest Master is nothing if not magnanimous, if a little sassy sometimes.”

Trevor smiled as he watched his wife’s eyes read a message that appeared in front of her.

She put her fists on her hips, careful with the sword still in her hand. “Yes, you can be sassy, and I refuse to do this with you right now. I’m about to go into a dungeon with my husband and I will not be waylaid any longer.”

Taking Trevor’s hand, Adabelle gently pushed Clay forward and the trio started moving. The wooden walkway was swallowed by the entrance of the cave, plunging them into darkness. With a flick of her wrist, the noblewoman’s sword started glowing just as her husband pulled out a bright lantern from his inventory and set it on top of his familiar.

“That’s handy,” they said to each other, smiling at the shared phrase.

Clay’s body shifted slightly and a rocky lip rose over the bottom of the lantern, holding it in place so that it wouldn’t fall. They had become a mobile lighthouse as they pondered forward, but that wasn’t all. Once they left the walkway, there was nothing but damp, natural stone beneath their feet. The earth elemental flattened out a path for them with every step, displacing the water while making it easier to walk.

A burst of wind blew past them, causing Adabelle’s braided hair to whip violently behind her. She raised her sword, sensing something in the air. Her eyes darted through the darkness for any hint of enemies.

“GEEEEEET OOOOUUUUT!” a voice yelled. Its volume was so great that Trevor felt it in his very bones.

Adabelle was quick to check on him out of the corner of her eye. It definitely felt like he was out of his wheelhouse here. At least the earth elementals in his last dungeon had the good sense not to inhabit a spooky cave.

He offered her a nervous smile before clearing his throat. Fake it until you make it, they had always said. Well, Trevor was good at that.

“No thank you, spooky dude,” he announced into the cave, directing his voice nowhere in particular. “We’re going to… Banish you, I guess? Is that what we do to ghosts? Maybe I should have thought this through.”

The demonkin had smiled at her husband’s bravery, but it had become half-strained, half-amused as he flubbed his chance at a big line. Silence followed, interrupted ever so lightly by the sound of dripping water.

There was no wind this time as the voice started laughing. It was accompanied by a slapping and stomping sound, as if someone was striking their stomach while repeatedly kicking the floor.

“Alright, well that seems like a bit much,” Trevor complained, sulking.

“Oh, we’re going to have fun with you kids,” the voice stated as soon as it was done laughing.
