But given that I may end up against Kate or an enemy like her in the Tower, perhaps it’s a bad idea.

As Beck had described earlier and Emelia had told him in the past, the Tower of Mirrors was given its name because it reflected each player’s journey through the Trials up until that point, pitting them against twisted versions of past encounters and threats. The severity of each floor of the tower depended on how many floors a player decided to traverse in a turn. The options were one, two, or three floors, but as the Tome Keeper had said, there was a high likelihood of running headfirst into a Voidspawn if he went up three floors at a time. Beck had also theorised that Lasse and Adam would encounter the Cloud Leviathan if they went up three floors. Beck was himself worried about having to fight a lot of Nwetrou worshippers, given just how big of a threat they had been to him so far.

The Tome Keeper had told Adam a bit more in-depth about the mechanics, at least as best as he was able to without violating the System’s rules. If a player only travelled up one floor at a time, then the challenges would only reflect the simple encounters of the past and the threat would be minimised. It wouldn’t be a cakewalk, but it would be the safest path. When players went up two floors at a time, the challenges often reflected close calls that they’d had. For many, this would be fights against other players. The form these encounters would take wasn’t always completely clear though, and going up two floors often introduced a lot of puzzles that required a strong ability to retain the memory of past stages and fights. Going up three floors would pit players against encounters they had lost or which represented an insurmountable threat.

Beck had said that it was players’ arrogance that got them killed in the tower, because many traversed three floors at once to get a head start. However, Adam knew it couldn’t just be arrogance. The tower had twenty-one floors to beat, with the twenty-second being the stage boss. Everyone moved up simultaneously during each ‘turn’, with their distance depending on how many floors they were willing to traverse at a time. To add to the stress, there would only be eighteen turns given to reach floor twenty-one, meaning it was impossible to reach the top by only taking one floor at a time with every turn. Therefore, its design incentivised taking risks, which was a slippery slope.

But, the Tower of Mirrors was not a competition between players, and in fact it would lead to adverse results if someone got far ahead of the rest, given how each player’s tower was linked to another’s. For example, when a player beat floor fourteen, the linked player’s tower would lose its bottom seven floors, and if the linked player was still on those floors, they would perish. Something similar would happen again at floor twenty-one. Once a linked tower’s player perished, the links would readjust to those with living players, excluding the dead tower from the chain. It was possible to create a cascading effect of death as a result, which could lead to just one or two survivors reaching the top, where they had to take on a difficult scorn-inflicting boss to win the stage.

Additionally, floor seven gave players the ability to help or hinder their linked player if they hadn’t yet reached the seventh floor, and apparently the player making the choice would have an overview of the linked player’s journey. For Adam, this could include such things as how he’d killed Jun, Juliette, Aeran, and Heiner. It wasn’t clear if the context would always be included, but Adam doubted it. Beck attributed a lot of deaths to this mechanic, and for good reason. It preyed on people’s worst impulses.

Adam took a deep breath.

The odds were stacked against him, but it wasn’t unwinnable. He planned to use the orb of insight to find out who the other players were in his stage, and then he would seek them out and try to explain how the stage would work, in order for them to all make it through together.
