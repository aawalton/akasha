Eric, the older baker from the original meeting, spotted Jane and waved her over. He led her to a series of tables near the middle of the square and pointed to the one at the end.

"This is yours," he said. "Set up however you like."

"What's the plan?"

"We're keeping it simple. The royal family will make a late breakfast of it, moving from one end to the other, sampling as they go. When they're done, I'll get an informal vote from them and announce the results to the crowd. After that, anyone who wants to can eat whatever's left. Saves us from having to carry it all back."

Jane nodded and started unpacking her crates. The burnt-top bread went in the center, sliced into neat pieces for easy sampling. The keln flanked it on one side, arranged in an overlapping fan. The meat buns went on the other side, still warm from the oven and filling the air with their savory aroma.

She had just finished arranging everything when Emily appeared at her elbow.

"Good luck," Emily said. "Not that you need it. Your bread looks amazing."

"Thank you." Jane straightened up and surveyed her display. Nodding once in satisfaction, she turned to her friend. "How did you get away from the library?"

"Closed for the showcase. Everything is." Emily looked around at the crowded square. "I guess the town is basically on a running holiday now. Everyone is getting their work done as they’re able, around the festivities. I can’t remember anything like it."

Jane followed her gaze. The entire town seemed to have turned out, packed into every available spot around the showcase tables. Even some of the rooftops had people on them, all sitting on the edges to get a better view.

"You don't seem very nervous," Emily observed.

"I'm not." Jane was a little surprised to find it was still true. "This far in, it's like being midair over a pool. The water might be cold, but it doesn't matter much when I can't do anything to turn around."

"I suppose that's one way to look at it."

A hush fell over the crowd. Jane turned to see what had caused it. The door of the meeting hall had opened, and Xand was stepping out onto the raised platform that served as a stage. He held something in his hand. It glinted gold in the morning light.

An amplification device, Jane realized. He must have borrowed one from somewhere.

Xand cleared his throat, and then his voice rolled out over the square.

"Citizens of Glenfall," he announced. "It is my honor to present His Majesty the King, Her Majesty the Queen, and His Highness Prince Brand."

The royal family emerged from the meeting hall, looking rested and surprisingly casual in their morning clothes. The king waved to the crowd, the queen smiled graciously, and Brand managed a passable imitation of both. The crowd cheered.

And now, Jane thought, we watch the royal family eat breakfast.

The absurdity of it almost made her laugh. All this preparation, all this anxiety, and it came down to watching three people eat their way down a row of tables while a thousand spectators looked on.

She supposed there were worse ways to spend a morning.

The royal family started at the far end of the arc, working their way slowly toward Jane's position. She could see them stopping at each of the other two tables, sampling the offerings, and speaking with the bakers. Though she couldn’t hear exactly what was said, the general attitude of the event seemed to be positive.

The crowd watched as if the act of eating had been transformed into something sacred. Jane wondered if they would find it so interesting if they had met the actual people involved. They royal family were certainly fine folk, as far as Jane could tell, but they weren’t nearly so unusual as the average person might expect.

Jane's table was at the end of the row, which meant she had time to watch and wait. She used it to observe the royal family's reactions. The king seemed to favor the heartier offerings, while the queen gravitated towards more delicate pastries. Brand ate everything put in front of him with equal attention, which wasn’t a huge surprise at his age.

They reached her table sooner than she expected. One moment they were two tables away, and the next they were standing in front of her, looking at her display.

"Welcome," Jane said, slipping into the most professional manners she could muster. “This is my burnt-top bread, made with a blend of seeds and herbs that are seared into the crust. This is keln, a traditional flatbread that was common in Glenfall before becoming something of a lost recipe for a while. And these are savory buns, filled with spiced meat and vegetables."

The queen's eyes lit up at the mention of keln.

"I've heard about this," she said. "But I thought they said you invented it."

Jane shook her head. "No, Your Majesty. It was a well-known thing in town long before I came. The original baker passed away, and her recipe was thought to be lost. I was just lucky enough to rediscover it."

"Modest." The queen picked up a piece of keln and took a bite. "This is excellent."

The king tried the burnt-top bread and nodded approvingly. Brand sampled one of the meat buns, chewing thoughtfully before swallowing.

"Different," he said. "But good different. It’s like a whole meal in one package."

Jane smiled. "Thank you, Your Highness."

Having completed their circuit, the royal family doubled back, sampling a few offerings one more time. Then they made their way to the platform where Xand and Eric waited. The group huddled together for a few minutes, speaking in unamplified voices Jane had no chance of hearing.

Despite her earlier calm, Jane found herself growing anxious. She wasn't sure what she wanted the outcome to be. She just knew she wanted the whole thing to be over.

Finally, Xand stepped forward and raised the amplification device to his mouth.

"The royal family has made their selections," he announced. "It is my pleasure to announce the results of our showcase."

The crowd went quiet, waiting for the announcement.

"In first place, for her exceptional honey cake, Marta Denning!"

The crowd erupted in cheers as the older woman Jane had voted for at the original competition stepped forward to accept the honor. Jane clapped along with everyone else, genuinely pleased. The honey cake had been excellent, and Marta deserved the recognition.

"In second place, for his remarkable sourdough and seed loaves, Thomas Albrecht!"

More cheers, more applause. Jane's clapping slowed as the math caught up with her. There were only three of them. First place was announced. Second place was announced. That only left one space.

"And in third place, for her burnt-top bread, keln flatbread, and savory buns, Lady Jane!"

The applause that followed wasn’t just polite, but warm. Nobody in the crowd seemed to think there was anything wrong with the outcome. Third place out of three was still a place, after all. She had still been chosen to showcase. She had still fed the royal family.

“You did it!” Bella slammed into her from almost nowhere. “Good job, Jane! Unless getting third bothers you.”

It didn’t, Jane realized. Not one bit. The fact that she had made it this far was a bit of a miracle. Out-baking everyone in town wouldn’t have been realistic. In fact, it would have worried her so much that the experience would have seemed fake from the bottom to the top.

Now she could simply enjoy it. She had cooked for a king. After today, she needn’t ever be worried about whether or not her bread was good enough for anyone.

All in all, not a bad day.

“I’m not bothered,” she assured Bella. “Really. I’m glad.”

Before she could say anything else, the crowd descended on the showcase tables like a wave. Everyone was trying to get their own little piece of food the king had eaten. Jane had barely stepped back from her display before hands were reaching in, grabbing pieces of bread, keln, and buns with an enthusiasm that bordered on frightening. She watched her morning's work disappear in a matter of minutes, consumed by people collecting the shortest-lived souvenirs imaginable.

"They're hungry." Bella laughed. "I suppose standing around watching other people eat works up an appetite."

By the time the crowd had finished, there was nothing left on Jane's table but empty plates and the clean cloths she had used to cover everything during transport. She gathered them up and folded them neatly, tucking them under her arm. It was was a much more manageable load to carry back, all told.

Saying goodbye to Bella, Jane made her way toward the platform. Xand and Eric were still standing there, shaking hands with well-wishers and accepting congratulations. A few people stopped Jane along the way to offer their own kind words, and she accepted them with as much grace as she could manage. Though she genuinely appreciated their support, it had already been a long day. She could feel her stamina starting to wane.

Xand spotted her approaching and waved her over.

"Lady Jane," he said, using the title she still wasn't comfortable with. "Congratulations on your placement. The royal family spoke highly of your keln, in particular."

"Thank you." Jane accepted the rolled certificate he pressed into her hands. "I'm glad they enjoyed it."

Xand smiled. "The king said it reminded him of something he ate as a child. High praise, coming from him. He seems to have enjoyed being a child much more than he likes being the king. You should be proud of yourself."

Jane supposed she should be, and did her best to encourage herself in that direction. She tucked the certificate under her arm alongside the folded cloths, begged her leave, and made her way back through the thinning crowd. Dave was waiting near the edge of the square with his empty handcart, ready to help her transport whatever was left. When he saw her approaching with nothing but fabric and paper, he laughed.

"That good, huh?"

"That good." Jane climbed up next to him on the cart's bench. "Nothing left to carry. They ate everything, and I see you already collected my crates. That’s all there is."

Dave turned the cart toward her bakery. "Then my job is easy. Just a passenger run."

The streets were quieter now. Most of the crowd was still lingering in the town center to enjoy the aftermath of the showcase. Jane watched the familiar buildings pass by and felt her satisfaction and relief grow as she drew closer to home.

It was done. Her part in all of this was finished. For a little while, at least.

Dave dropped her at her door with a cheerful wave and a promise to check in later. Jane let herself into the shop and stood for a moment in the quiet, letting the silence settle around her.

Nobody expects me to bake today, she realized. It was almost a shame. There was enough time to make some of her usual stock, but she doubted anyone would ever guess it was there to buy. Her best bet was simply to start afresh the next day, this time with a third-place certificate gracing her wall.

She climbed the stairs to her living quarters and sat down heavily on her bed, letting out a long breath. Perhaps it was a good thing that she didn’t need to bake today. After such a morning, she could use hours and hours of time to just sit there, gathering back her scattered sense of normalcy. Maybe she could even take a nap.

No sooner had she lay down, though, when a knock sounded on the downstairs door.

Scowling, she sat up. Perhaps some unseen force had heard her silent desire for peace, and was determined to ruin it.

Who is it?

She made her way back down to the shop, smoothing her clothes as she went. The knock came again, more hesitant this time, and she quickened her pace before the person gave up and her scramble to get to them was for naught. Straightening her shoulders, she threw open the door.

The prince of the realm was standing on her doorstep.
