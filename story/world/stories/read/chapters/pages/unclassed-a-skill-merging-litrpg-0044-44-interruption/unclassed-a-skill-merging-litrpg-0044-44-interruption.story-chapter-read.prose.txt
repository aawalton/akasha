“Where the hell’s my gift?”

It was. I’d missed her cooking daily.

Marcois hadn’t said much. He’d smiled at me when I’d returned, but he’d refrained from speaking more than a few words. Same with Finn. He seemed relieved to see me initially, but hadn’t turned into a chatterbox after.

Maybe those two didn’t feel as close to me. That said, both were usually on the quieter side, and Finn in particular?

I dunno. He seemed to be in a bad mood. Maybe I was reading into nothing, but since we’d gotten back to camp, he’d basically spent the entire time sitting alone and maintaining his tools.

I wondered if the tension around the camp was just generally higher these days. With everything that was going on, that’d make sense. Once the high from my return had settled, Jackal and the others might start to become more withdrawn as well.

Ceri was about two minutes from done with my meal when a shadow descended over the camp. From the distance, there was a whistle, and moments later, a guard stumbled onto the scene, clutching a wineskin.

He had a patchy beard, and bags under his eyes like he wasn’t sleeping enough. He stank of sweat and alcohol. He upturned his lips, revealing yellowing teeth as he sauntered over.

“We’re good, Samson,” Toar replied for us. “Did you want to check our progress?”

“Who exactly—”

“Who the fuck’s talkin’ to you, boy?”

The guard cut me off before I could get three words out. Before I could stand, Toar pressed a firm hand against my knee.

“Don’t,” he whispered.

I didn’t like this. I’d seen drunks like this before. They were wild. There was no telling what he’d do if we didn’t get rid of him.

“What can we do for you today, Samson? Is there anything you’d like?” Toar asked, sounding more like a butler than I’d ever heard him.

“I don’t like that,” Samson hissed. He’d let go of Maisie at this point and was leaning on Finn, spilling wine down himself. “I think you should call me sir. No!” He hiccuped. “Lord.”

“Already eaten…”

He leaned fully against Finn’s shoulder. He closed his eyes.

Toar stood; Samson’s eyes snapped open.

“Don’t stand up too quickly.”

“I was just going to get you a gift,” Toar said. “Something for you to take with you on your way.”

Samson seemed to think about it for a moment. He took another gulp of wine, spilling some on Finn.

“Alright… be quick about it.”

Toar walked to one of our carts. Without him to hold me back, I felt myself feeling for my [Hoard]. I couldn’t tell how strong this guard was, but he was drunk. If he tried anything. Anything violent, I wasn’t sure I’d be able to hold myself back. Toar needed to defuse this quickly.

“Why the fuck are you lookin’ at me like that,” Samson growled, turning to face Finn, eyeballing him as he scooted away. “You think I didn’t try for you, Jason? You think I wasn’t good enough?”

Beside him, I saw a small tint of blue in Maisie’s mana signature. Was it something to do with her emotions? Was she starting to channel a spell?

“What do you think of these, Lord Samson?” Toar asked, coming back with two hunks of rocks that were almost completely encrusted in gems. One set was blue. The other pink.

“The fuck are those?” Samson asked, pointing at the clusters in Toar’s hands. “It’s fucking pink.”

“Lovely gems,” Toar said. “Very valuable. You could sell them, or maybe give them to a lady you like.”

“I agree,” Maisie said, the blue tint in her signature becoming sharper. “They’re very lovely. You should have them.”

“A lady…” Samson straightened in his seat. “Yes. I do like the sound of that.”

“You’re sure you don’t want some food?” Toar asked.

“I said I didn’t want food.” He snapped. “What are you, fuckin’ deaf?”

“Jason. Help your father back to camp. I’m feelin’ dizzy…”

Finn flinched at that. He clutched the pick in his hand.

“Maybe we should all go with you,” Maisie offered. “To make sure you get back safely.”

“Maybe we should,” Marcois weighed in, walking past the fire and closing the distance between him and Samson. “Would that be helpful?”

“No… I think I’ll be able to get back on my own.”

Toar smiled.

“That’s wonderful, Lord Samson.”

“It’s Lord Samson…”

He corrected Toar for no reason, then began to drag his way out of camp. Once he was gone, each of us released a collective sigh. Finn began to scrub at his shoulder with a bucket and sponge.

“Crazy freak,” Finn spat.

“I was two seconds from throwing hot coals at him,” Ceri added.

“What the fuck was that?” I asked.

“Samson,” Toar answered. “One of the guards that checks we’re meeting quota.”

“He does this every time.” Finn shuddered. “One day I think he’ll snap.”

“What Tier is he?” I asked Toar, my mind processing. “Can you see?” Finn had returned to sharpening his pick, though he kept glancing around himself.

“Tier 2,” Toar answered. “He’s got some kind of fighter class. We could take him if needed.”

“Take him?” Maisie asked, eyebrow raised.

“Well, we’re all thinking it.” Ceri turned sausages in a pan as she spoke, her posture now slumped.

“I was trying to defuse the situation,” Maisie said. “Why are we talking about fighting him?”

“Because if he attacked one of us and we didn’t do anything about it, it’d get worse,” Ceri replied.

“He wasn’t going to hit anyone,” Maisie countered. “He’s drunk off his ass, as usual. If he didn’t leave, I’d use Tranquil Sea and make him leave.”

“You mean like you did with Selsor?”

“Don’t be a bitch.” Maisie pointed a finger. “You really think we’re better off assaulting a guard?”

Ceri shrugged. “I dunno.”

Maisie looked to the rest of us, her eyelids batting. “Seriously? Is this a conversation we’re actually entertaining?”

No one responded. I could see Jackal dithering like he wasn’t sure whether to speak, but the rest of us were all silent.

After two seconds of no response, Maisie threw her hands up. “Alright. Whatever. You guys have fun with that.”

Ceri started laughing. She cackled as she placed the pan down.

Maisie stormed across the campground and grabbed her by the chin.

“What? What’s funny about this?”

“Maisie—” Toar started.

“No! We are constantly going through it, and she thinks it’s hilarious! She’s always laughing!”

Ceri pushed her hand away. Maisie turned to face the rest of us, fury in her eyes.

“Do I need to show you all the bodies hanging in the central cavern? Because I think we’ve all seen them!”

Ceri laughed some more. Maisie’s hand glowed blue.

“Damn. Are you gonna heal me to death?”

Maisie closed her mana-encrusted fist and swung for her.

She missed. Ceri dodged back, and Maisie tumbled in the direction of the campfire. Ceri swiped her to the floor with her tail.

“You wanna know what’s really funny?” Ceri asked.

Maisie didn’t answer. She grit her teeth as she pulled herself to her knees.

“You’re supposed to be the kind one.” Ceri advanced on her. “The one that stops us from doing stupid shit. You act like you don’t care, but you really do. Whatever.” Her tail hammered the ground. “But when it comes down to it, you’re just looking out for yourself.”

“That’s bullshit,” Jackal shouted.

“Is it?” Ceri’s eyes flashed to him. “I’m not stuck here like some of you. I’m not working off a debt. I’m not poor. Adam came back with the only thing I wanted from one of these shitholes, and I’ve got zero reason to stay anymore. I could get up and leave a month from now.”

No one knew what to say to that. It sounded so cold.

“But I’d still fight for any of you if I saw you being hurt in front of me. Any of you.” She returned her gaze to Maisie. “Because I don’t give a shit about my life. And because you all put up with me for so long. But you’re right, Maisie, I am a bitch. How dare I suggest protecting one of our own.”

“Maisie’s right,” Jackal argued. “It’d be fucking stupid to attack Samson.” He walked between the two of them, blocking Ceri off. “We survived what happened with the doctor, but other people died because of it. If we fought him here, how the fuck would we cover that up?”

“So if he flew into a blind rage, decided he wanted to beat his son Jason to death? What then?” Ceri snickered. “Sorry, Finn. We don’t wanna get in trouble. Better yet, should Toar have let Finn die? What about you, Jackal?”

Finn didn’t reply; he’d put down his tools and was sitting with his hands on his chin, staring into space.

“That’s way different,” Jackal said. “We’d kill him if it meant saving Finn. Right?”

Jackal looked at Maisie for a response. She said nothing.

“See what I mean?”

“We’d all die…”

Maisie choked out the words. Tears were streaming down her face. Her bangs hid her eyes from view. Her ears sagged low.

“If we killed him,” she continued, “we’d have no way to cover it up. They’d kill all of us. And then we’d all be dead.”

“What if it was you that he went for?” Ceri squinted. “You’d want us all to—to just watch? Let it happen?”

“I don’t wanna die…” Maisie sniffled. “But I don’t want any of you to die either…”

Jackal looked between the two of them. He didn’t seem sure what to say any more.

Maisie’s voice was small. “Yes. I’d want you to let me die.”

Ceri blinked. She moved toward her. She chuckled.

“That’s stupid,” she said, placing a hand on Maisie’s shoulder. “You’re really stupid.”

She sobbed. She placed her hand on Ceri’s.

“I’m scared. I-I’m really scared. I don’t know how to help anyone…”

“But we all help each other,” Ceri said, meeting the girl’s eyes. “I cook. You heal. Marc and Finn keep the lights on. Adam thinks up clever ideas. Toar protects us. Jackal… exists—”

“Hey, fuck you.”

“None of us want to die,” Ceri said. “Well, I don’t anymore. But honestly, I had you all wrong.”

She sat down beside Maisie. “I take back what I said. You care about all of us. Honestly, I think you’d be the first to rush into danger if shit had hit the fan.”

“I wouldn’t have,” Maisie defended.

“You mean like with Selsor?”

Maisie snickered. “You really are a bitch.”

Ceri petted her hair. “Nah. I’m just right.”

Maisie leaned into the touch a little. Her ears drooped further. She sniffled again. “Well, you’re not always right.”

“And neither are you. How do you know things won’t get worse if we do nothing?”

Maisie shrugged.

“Can I say something?” Marcois asked.

He’d been watching the entire time. Funny that he’d interjected in this before I had. I’d wanted to defuse the argument at first, but after a moment, I’d wanted to hear their sides. I didn’t like seeing any of them upset, but I used the argument as a moment to check myself, to consider how committed I was to the idea me and Toar had discussed.

I imagined he hadn’t weighed in for the same reason. Both of us were attempting to assure ourselves that the path we wanted to take wasn’t an awful one.

“Go for it,” Maisie said.

“I’m inclined to agree with Maisie,” Marcois said. “Fighting him might have made everything worse. I felt the same way when I went to pull straws.”

I didn’t know what that meant. I listened anyway.

“I don’t like fighting,” Marcois continued. “Ever since I…” He took a deep breath. “Ever since I killed a boy and found myself down here, I swore off fighting entirely.”

Everyone turned to look at him. Even Toar. I stared at Marcois, who appeared level, calm, almost graceful despite his grand stature.

“I told myself that I was evil. That if I wished to grow as a person, I had to swear off violence and use this punishment to build inner strength.” He sighed. “I haven’t built anything. I haven’t grown at all. Standing still has only caused me to allow the people I care about to be hurt. For a long time, it was Fallos. I told myself I couldn’t do anything. I didn’t try. I was scared to become a monster like him.”

Jackal looked away. At the floor.

“We were the poorest family amongst Deyvat’s upper class. The only orcs. Beggar lords, they called us.”

He swallowed. “I was always warned not to get into fights as a child. Orcs are bigger than humans. Bigger than most beastkin. It’s dangerous when an orc loses their temper.

“I was hounded throughout my childhood. Through my schooling, whenever I left the manor, wherever I went, the other children followed me. They called me names. They hit me and threw rocks.

“I could handle all of that. Then one day, my grandmother died. She was my favourite person in the whole world. We buried her.”

He looked up at us. It was like the stirring of an ancient colossus. His yellow eyes were tinted red.

“A week after, I went to visit the grave. Alden Caster was there, a noble with two vassal sons that followed him around like fleas.” He grimaced. “He was pissing on her grave.”

Marcois’ jaw clenched. “I broke her headstone with Alden’s head. He was pulp and bone by the time I was finished. One of the vassal sons tried to stop me. I’m not sure if he’s learned to walk again since. The other ran for help.”

Marc huffed a large breath. “I’m telling you all this because I needed to confront it. What I did was wrong. Alden Caster was a disgusting bully, but he was a child. There was no excuse to kill him. I could’ve stopped before I killed him. I just didn’t want to.”

I took a step back. I felt shaken by the admission. The brutality of it. I tried to imagine ever feeling so angry. I remembered Carrow.

Would I have killed him on that day? I’d pressed a knife to his neck. The rage inside of me had been broiling to a horrific peak. I’d dropped the knife.

Then there was Selsor. The bastard put as through so much in such a short time, and he was a liar. A con artist. I’d been ready to fight him after I’d severed my finger. I’d felt indignation rising inside of me like a swirling tide.

But would I have beaten him to death? Even if he was clearly outmatched?

I didn’t know. It scared me that I didn’t know. The closest feeling I had to what Marcois described was the one time I’d been affected by the mist, plus the Power Stone I’d injected into my body. When I’d beaten that feral Drassian to death.

That feeling had been exhilarating. It felt disgusting to admit it, and I fully attributed it to the psychoactive effects I was under at the time, but it didn’t change how overwhelmingly powerful I’d felt in the moment. How reluctant I’d been to stop.

“Going from rage to stubborn pacifism was wrong,” Marcois said. “Instead of working on my anger, I’ve buried the problem. Instead of using my strength to protect others, I’ve punished myself, and now everyone around me.

“Maisie might be correct. It’s possible that all of us would die fighting back against this place.

“But Ceri might be right too. How do we know that we won’t die down here regardless? That doing nothing won’t result in us or people we care about being dragged to their deaths?

“If the cost of survival was watching everyone I love die. If the cost of survival is staying true to my beliefs, and doing as I’m told, so I can atone for my sins and see my parents again…”

Marcois closed his eyes. “Then I’m sorry, but I am a sinner. And I cannot continue to hide.”

“We should make a plan.”

It was Finn’s voice. Everyone besides Marcois turned to face him.

He was still staring at nothing. His face was still blank.

“A plan?” Ceri asked.

“We should find out when they sleep. What their shift patterns are. See if we can make deals with other group leaders.”

“Are you saying we should assassinate them?” Maisie asked.

“If I was, would you object to it?”

“Yes,” Maisie admitted. She wiped her face. “I still don’t see how we’d come out on top, even if we took them by surprise. But maybe I’m wrong. Maybe if we got help, and we had a good plan, we’d have a better chance than I realise.”

“I’m not saying we definitely do it,” Finn clarified. “Just that we should think about how we could. Set things up so that if it felt necessary, we could execute.”

“That sounds smart,” Toar said.

Finn’s eyes flicked to him; he didn’t say anything.

“Adam and I were talking about something similar,” he turned to face me. “Do you wanna tell them, or should I?”

Toar was right. This was as good a time as any to have this conversation. I’d been wondering about how I was going to bridge into it, but the opportunity had arisen naturally. With how oppressive things were down here, I was hardly surprised.

“I’ll talk.” I rose from my seat. Sausages sizzled in a forgotten pan. Besides that, silence.

“See this?” I pulled the rifle from my pack, presenting it to the group.

They all stopped to stare at it.

“It’s some kind of musket,” Jackal said matter-of-factly. “I saw you shoot Toar with it earlier.”

“That was a test,” I told them. “Toar has an ability which allows him to harden his skin. He’s also a beastkin with a Tier 2 core. The fact that I was able to hurt him with this, and that this gun can fire multiple rounds a second…”

I let the implication hang in the air.

“Wait. It’s not just fire once, then reload?” Ceri asked.

“How the hell does that work?” Finn drew closer to inspect the weapon as he asked.“So… you think we could kill the guards with this thing?” Maisie asked, sounding a little skeptical.

“There’s thirty bullets in a magazine,” I said to Toar. “You think you could survive being shot by this thirty times?”

“I doubt it.”

“Isn’t this assuming you land every shot?” Finn asked—he’d begun pacing. “How many bullets do we even have for this thing? It makes a lot of noise, too. Wouldn’t they realise we have it and try and take it from us?”

“You’re right to wonder those things,” I started. “And, yeah, you’d need to be shooting accurately to do damage with one of these. Well, you would if you only had one person shooting.”

“How is taking turns to shoot gonna make any difference?”

I laughed at his misinterpretation. My mouth settled in a grin. “Buddy. Do you think we’ve only got one gun?”

He stopped dead. “How many.”

“Over a dozen like this.”

His eyes widened.

“And dozens of smaller guns.”

“And… they all work like this?” Finn asked. “Holding multiple bullets?”

“You’re damn right they do.”

The group seemed more palatable to the idea of rebellion after this. I mentioned Drassia to all of them, and Maisie proved her devotion to the group. She had zero interest in running away to an alien paradise, and denounced the idea before anyone else could.

We started to talk through different plans. Jackal had the idea of taking one of the guards hostage and using him to find out more about the enemy’s position, but I deemed it too risky. The moment we did that, there’d be a retaliation, and there was no guarantee we’d be ready for it.

We started talking about how we’d communicate with other groups. Giving them guns was risky. Naska’s group were likely the most trustworthy, but we didn’t know Ghrinda’s group. As for the other leaders, Toar seemed to think one named Garrett would be on board with the idea, and that Vulrak likely would, assuming he was freed of his confines.

“There’s a chance one of them tells the guards,” Marcois pointed out.

I chewed on burnt sausage in a bread roll—Ceri apologised for the ‘welcome back’ meal being ruined, but it still tasted pretty good, and I made sure to tell her that.

“Then we need to feed the other groups fake info,” I said to him. “The ones we don’t trust at least.”

“How so?” Toar asked.

“Lie about where we hid our weapons and supplies,” I said. “We’ll booby trap the location. If anyone tells the guards, they’ll assume the group that snitched were the attackers and they’ll punish them, not us.”

The colour seemed to drain from Toar’s face as I rattled off that plan. Might’ve brought back some memories for him.

Jackal, meanwhile, was staring at me.

“Dude. That’s pretty brutal.”

“We’re trying to stay alive here,” I said bluntly. “If I could just not involve the groups we don’t know, I would, but that’s over half the workers in this mine. If some would rather snitch on us than fight for their freedom, the least they can do is take a couple guards with them before they’re executed.”

“So… we’re gonna set a trap for every group we don’t trust?” Jackal asked.

“We’re gonna set a bunch of traps.”

“What if no one snitches?”

“Then we tell everyone to snitch on the day.”

“What if someone just name drops us?” Finn asked. “We’re going around and inviting these guys to help us, right? Wouldn’t it just take one person calling us the ringleaders for our heads to be cut off?”

“We don’t have to worry about that,” I told him.

“Why not?”

“Because we’re not the ringleaders. Vulrak is.”

“Vulrak?” It was Toar who said it. He sounded in disbelief. “But he—”

“Took down three guards before they could subdue him, was too valuable to kill, and still controls the largest group in the mines.” I finished, recounting what Toar had told me. “Everyone saw them spare him, even after he fought back. Which group would be crazy enough to defy him? Who ever has?” I shrugged. “If anyone snitches, they’ll do it because they’re hoping for better treatment from the Association, but they won’t drop Vulrak’s name. Even if they do, there’s a good chance it won’t matter. I doubt they’d march on his group unprepared, and we’ll be ready if they do.”

“Vulrak’s on board with this?” Toar asked. “But he’s—”

“He will be once I remove his collar.”

“Once you…”

“You heard me.”

I didn’t explain how I was going to remove Vulrak’s collar. I just told them I would. By now, my group had gotten quite used to trusting me with impossible tasks, so it wasn’t hard to handwave that part of the conversation.

There were still two major unknown elements in all of this: the guard named Maxwell and the Overseer himself. It was my hope that by communicating with other groups, I’d find people who were able to help fill in the gaps in our knowledge, as well as people to assist with trap-making and other preparations.

“How often do guards check in on you guys?” I asked.

“It’s two or three times a day for us,” Toar answered. “When we wake up, during the day, and evenings. More frequent with larger groups, from what I can tell.”

That was gonna be difficult.

“I need a way into his camp. What’s a good excuse?”

“The replacement doctor’s less skilled than Mansol was,” Toar said. “Some have been going to Vulrak’s camp to get healing from Mercury when they’ve gotten hurt or ill. He’s cheaper, and, a lot of the time, better.”

That was all I needed to hear. I pulled two fingers on my left hand back until the bones snapped.

“What the fuck?”

“Adam!”

I felt a stab of pain rush through me, enough to push tears into my eyes, but I still managed to smile through it.

“There. Let’s go pay Mercury a visit.”
