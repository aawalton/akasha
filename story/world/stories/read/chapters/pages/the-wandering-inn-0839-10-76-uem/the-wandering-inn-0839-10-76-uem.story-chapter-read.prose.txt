It was time to leave Lemoste-Under-Cliffs.
Aside from pertinent reasons of Bowom, everyone had already known the moment was coming. The ant swarms were all but eradicated or chased away from the damaged Fraerling city, and it was just…time.
It was the itching of Eurise’s feet. The [Explorer] grew restless. In truth, only a certain duo’s fight had moored the Fraerlings from Dretonamis so long.
The [Explorer] explained all this to Vision Tiregal and expected the usual pushback.
‘Oh, Eurise the Explorer, can’t you stay and kill another thousand monsters bothering us? Please, Eurise, you’re our only hope despite us having a million Fraerlings in the city and enough magic to blow a hole through a hill! Please do all the things the Tallguard and our security forces can’t!’
It was a sign of how things were changing that the young Vision just nodded and heaved a sigh.
“I can’t say it wasn’t overdue. We’ll have a small celebration; please don’t take offense if it’s modest, but shouting that we’re losing one of the Village Protectors makes Fraerlings nervous. No one could say you haven’t done enough, though.”
“We must insist—uh—okay?”
Eurise might have been whinging to Roja and the other Fraerlings about how hard it would be. The lack of a fight made him hesitate, but Tiregal just called for Commander Leafin to hear the news and for someone to tell the Architects. He noticed Eurise’s look of confusion and smiled painfully, but with a determination palpable to all.
“We cannot rely on high-level Fraerlings alone. Lemoste has paid for our arrogance, and believe me, upon the Gnomes, we shall not allow it twice! Nor are we blind to your two Tallfolk friends’ own quest. Give us this evening to whip together something, and you’ll be on your way at dawn with as much as we can throw together to speed you towards…where were you thinking?”
Eurise fumbled for a reply as the others, Zemmy, Mera, Roja, all turned to him.
“Well…we were thinking the city of Cillencreith. Konor’s closest, but Cillencreith is a magic-specialist city. If anyone has the resources to cure whatever shrinking spell is ailing Erin and Ulvama—”
“Good choice. They’ve gone silent, but it might be they locked down due to insect swarms. Any answers you gain would be welcome, if only to tell them what happened to us.”
In the worst case scenario, the City of Cillencreith, Lemoste’s ‘neighbor’ a good trek northwards, might have been overrun by insects too. But Fraerling cities were hardy, and Eurise bet it was just Fraerling politics. Vision Tiregal was consulting a list.
“We’ll spell anything you haven’t already enchanted. Can you spare a few of Zinni’s amazing giant aphids? We’ll replace them with roaches or other good haulers.”
“Of course. We, ah, could trade you most. It’s not like enchantments don’t do most of the work.”
Eurise spent a few minutes talking over what they could use and retreated. The lack of any protest made him rather embarrassed at how much he’d moaned over having to leave.
It was time. Battle of the Bands 4 was still the talk of the city despite having been a few days ago. Erin and Ulvama had mended their rifts, and they had to go. Eurise just—
Well—
It wouldn’t hurt to get people begging his group to stay. Not that he wanted it. It was annoying as gnats, but he might have stuck it out another week if they needed him. He was over Level 50, and Zemmy, Mera, Roja, and a number of other Fraerlings were all over Level 40! It wasn’t like they grew on bushes, even in Fraerling culture!
As it so happened, there was an objection as news spread of the convoy’s departure. Just…not quite what Eurise might have hoped for.
 
——
 
Lemoste’s citizens had seen strife. They’d lost friends and loved ones in the ant waves that had crashed over their home, and they’d rebuilt amidst tragedy and strife.
They were changing. It took the peaceful Fraerlings out of their shells and broke some. Unearthed mithril in others, or even harder metals. But they were still a city who loved music, diversion—it had been a certain [Innkeeper], a [Punk] with a guitar, and a shy Hobgoblin who’d helped remind them that such things existed.
So, it was a mix of Fraerlings who appeared in a crowd to protest the departures. Vision Tiregal met them with Eurise outside the home where their company was quartered.
“Good citizens, please! We cannot prevail upon Dretonamis so long! More Fraerlings are coming from the villages, and we are safe!”
Tiregal earnestly pleaded with them, but a [Shopkeeper] who sold knives pushed forwards to the head of the crowd.
“Eurise the Explorer? He can go! All respect to him and the, uh, Brawling Twins and Roja! Credit to them, but we don’t need another pair of idiots breaking every window in creation when they brawl!”
“Hey!”
Zemmy and Mera chorused. However, Shopkeeper Thist just jerked a thumb at the sizable crowd of over four hundred Fraerlings.
“They can go. But not Ulvama! She’s a Goblin.”
“Rest assured, she wouldn’t ever tell anyone about Lemoste’s location, if she could even find it, Thist—”
Vision Tiregal had the sense he was barking up the wrong piece of grass already, but Thist’s look of incredulity stopped him.
“Tell anyone? If she’s got more Goblins like her, they can come too! You want to send her out into the Tallfolk’s world where we all know what happens to Goblins? What about honorary citizenship? She doesn’t even need to contribute—I’ll sponsor her!”
“And us!”
The mob of four hundred Fraerlings were all in agreement. All of them wanted Ulvama to stay. Tiregal tried to argue with them as Eurise folded his arms.
“What makes Ulvama so much better?”
Thist eyed Eurise.
“Aside from the fact that she can tolerate a conversation for more than ten minutes before she goes off to climb a tree or kill something? She’s good with kids, knows magic even our spellcasters aren’t familiar with, looks like she actually enjoys good food—”
“Wipes her feet when she enters buildings.”
“Doesn’t break windows kicking other Fraerlings about every day?”
“Actually remembers our names? Go on, what’s my name? You can’t tell me, can you. None of you.”
The more martially-inclined Fraerlings were getting a touch offended. Dretonamis was sort of a live-as-you-please kind of village. So what if you didn’t remember a neighbor’s name despite knowing them for four years? When the cats came, you were ready to fight.
It was just that this mentality didn’t exactly jive with the cityfolk. A rather offended Eurise took some pleasure in telling the others that Ulvama’s departure was non-negotiable.
“She’s got to go. We’re curing her and Miss Solstice. You want her too?”
To his surprise, the Fraerlings in the mob glanced at each other and wavered.
“Eh…if it’s a package deal, we’ll take both.”
“We’re not saying she’s bad—just the fighting?”
“I’ve got armpits. Everyone I know has armpits, even if it’s only one after losing an arm. I don’t want to be judged.”
“The Battle of the Bands was great. I’m still replaying that saxophone song old Alack performed. But listen, you put them on the same scale and it’s like—do I want an acorn or a nali-stick? No offense.”
It was amazing that Ulvama had won over the Fraerlings more than even the [Innkeeper] herself. Then again, given how moody Erin had been and the long and public fight between her and Ulvama…Tiregal took over as Eurise stomped back.
“I’m afraid both are leaving. They have lives to be about, families. If you want to help enchant their transport, or give a gift, we’re sending them off tomorrow, early. But that’s final!”
The Fraerlings grumbled, but most of them were adults, and it had more been a show of support. However, Shopkeeper Thist pointed a warning finger at Tiregal.
“You think we’re not serious? Tell Miss Ulvama it’s an offer from all of us. We’ll feed you to the fish if not, Tiregal, and that’s all we have to say. But we’re not who you should fear.”
“And who’re we to be afraid of?”
Roja had also had enough of the non-warrior appreciation. She folded her arms, and Thist gave her an alarmingly knowing smile. He jerked a thumb over his shoulder as his crowd trooped away, grinning. Then they heard the chanting.
“Matha stays! Matha stays!”
Mera and Zemmy were cocking their heads, glancing around. It was when they saw the first rank of the six-thousand strong mob of almost exclusively female Fraerlings that the two [Brawlers] groaned. Mera slapped her forehead.
“Oh come on.”
 
——
 
It wasn’t just Fraerlings of various ages who had fallen in love with the [Punk] guitarist who’d sent the musical scene into overdrive in Lemoste.
A majority of them probably did fit that camp, but there were plenty of Fraerlings who just loved the music and didn’t want her to leave. They showed up in force, and Lemoste was a city.
It had a lot of Fraerlings; these were just the ones who weren’t on labor duty. The protests took all evening to sort out, and by the time Tiregal and the others were greeting dawn, there wasn’t a lot of pro-Matha sentiment in Dretonamis’ group.
Not that it had ever been exactly a popular opinion. However, Eurise did see it as an opportunity.
“You could stay, you know. They won’t love you forever, but being respected is probably novel for you. Give it a decade, two, and you might be another Zinni. Of music or something. And the offer is serious, Ulvama.”
He had Ulvama and Matha considering the offer over breakfast. Matha had seen the crowds still protesting outside her home and appeared a bit overwhelmed. She kept glancing out the window at Fraerlings dressed in punk clothing, holding up guitars, or asking her to go on dates.
It sort of annoyed Eurise, who’d never been that popular even when he’d been a famous Fraerling [Explorer] slaying animals in the wild. He didn’t see the appeal in the young Fraerling woman with short-cropped hair, a few scars from surviving in the wild, and aphid-leather clothing suited to her raiding days. What kind of self-respecting Fraerling liked a rebel Fraerling who rode Aphids, broke the law, could shred on a guitar…
His thoughts caught up with him at some point, and Eurise sighed. Matha had always been popular, even in Zinni’s village. Being the leader of a large Fraerling raider gang hadn’t hurt her ability to charm people, and she was better at leadership than Zemmy or Mera.
“It’s not a light offer. You’d be safe enough here. Thoughts?”
Both Matha and Ulvama considered the question seriously and with some hesitation. Ulvama kept glancing towards the kitchen, and Matha did the same.
“I’d like that. Only…I can’t. I—we—couldn’t. Even if—”
“I mean, do I want to stay here and be the coolest Fraerling around? Sure. But I feel like I’ve just started this adventure and I’ve not even begun it. I have to—”
Both began, trailed off, and looked at each other. Then at the humming figure who was in the kitchen, blissfully ignorant of the other’s struggles. No one had begged to keep her. Part of that was, again, the armpit allegations, but it was also just because Erin Solstice was different. Eurise got annoyed again.
The most popular Goblin and Fraerling in Lemoste were glancing at her back, and she didn’t even notice. Eurise didn’t know what had happened at the Battle of the Bands 4, not exactly, but he had noticed a change.
No more fighting. Everyone being upbeat, and the tension and strife of earlier reversing. Dead gods, Erin’s aura made everyone visibly cheerier; even the Battle Hamster was smiling as he snacked on nuts and oat pancakes for breakfast.
But were she and Ulvama…? And if not, why was Matha giving Erin the kind of look that Eurise had seen on most of her admirers’ faces? She and Ulvama kept staring until they glanced at each other.
Matha smiled weakly.
Ulvama’s face went blank—then she jabbed Matha in the stomach.
“Hiyah.”
“Argh!”
Matha doubled over. Ulvama leapt up.
“I will go say goodbyes. Like a good person. Matha can’t say goodbye to all her admirers. We leaving soon?”
“Yup. An hour before Tiregal throws a little celebration, then we’re out in two hours or I’ll just start throwing people out of the way.”
They’d hit the road soon. Eurise saw Ulvama nod and smile sadly. She rose, and he realized she had a bunch of gifts she must have spent all night making for her friends and acquaintances.
Matha had nothing. She sat there guiltily.
“I…I don’t know what to say. There’s a lot of cool girls out there. What do I—? Can I put on a performance or something? A final farewell concert?”
Eurise patted her shoulder as she turned to him for guidance.
“Whatever you want. I’m not helping, and we’re leaving in three hours. If you’re not there, you stay behind.”
He got up to go grab breakfast. He was done with all of it, and Matha sat there, embarrassed, forlorn. Unused to being, well, beloved. She peeked out the window and winced.
“That’s a lot of Fraerlings I’ve dated.”
Zemmy threw a shoe at her. He and Mera, in the way of people who’d never been popular, glared at Matha. The [Raider] wasn’t being egotistical, though. She was genuinely guilty. Not sure how to…say goodbye.
Lemoste was a good city. A fun one. Matha could stay there. She could imagine making a life here. There were certainly a lot of very cool Fraerling women she hadn’t had a chance to, um, get to know. But adventure and the unknown called her forwards. And…her promise.
Matha’s gaze strayed to the figure in the kitchen. There she was, oblivious to all the drama over leaving. The sun was shining through the water-barrier hanging around Lemoste’s outskirts. It surrounded her with a nimbus of light, and she was relaxed today.
That was it.
For the first and perhaps only time that Matha had ever known her, Erin Solstice was at a kind of peace. That was such a new experience that Matha stared. She saw something unlike the wounded, dangerous woman who had broken her home—and that one glimpse was all it took.
She couldn’t stay.
But…Matha’s heart hurt. She glanced at Ulvama, who had gone out to say goodbye to her friends, tears in her eyes. A proud [Shaman] who hugged every child and knew every person’s name she had chosen to befriend. A woman carrying magic, a Goblin and tribe of two. Then Matha gazed at her reflection in her cup of water.
How could she hold a candle to that after the Battle of the Bands? After all she’d heard? The two weren’t a thing, and Erin was as dumb as Zemmy and Mera, but…Matha gazed at the humming [Innkeeper] working on the cutting board with some dough.
“Yeah. Grandma warned me about people like her. Guess I didn’t get what that meant. I’ve never been the minnow on the line before.”
She touched her chest.
“Don’t feel so bad. Just like I’m dying, but I want to be hooked. But maybe…”
Matha turned back to the crowd outside, and she could have six thousand dates out there. The fondness of a city. Her class…it all called to her. She wavered, and she did stand at a true crossroads for a second. Unlike Ulvama, who had known her path and saw the other option as just a lovely dream.
Then…Erin Solstice turned her head.
“Hey, Matha. Whatcha doing? Also—why is there a crowd of girls screaming your name?”
The [Punk] peered up, and [The Wandering Innkeeper] smiled.
“Want to help me make bread?”
 
——
 
Erin Solstice was happier.
That was all.
She hadn’t healed, physically or spiritually, much more than when she’d come to Lemoste. She was more rested, yes. But that was not…
It was something about how she moved. She was working at the kitchen counter, making dough.
Bread. She was mixing it simply: flour, eggs, butter, a bit of salt, water…a recipe unknown to Fraerlings, but Erin had shifted the ingredients to ones she knew, and the foundation was simple.
However, today…Matha drifted over and tried to understand what it was.
It was the way Erin moved, perhaps. Not slowly, but as if each motion, each process of the breadmaking were something she had time for. She made a mound of flour, then a divot in the top and poured some chicken egg into it.
“It’s like a volcano, see? An eggcano. Do Fraerlings know what volcanos are?”
“Sure. We’re not stupid.”
The [Innkeeper] turned her head, and she smiled. It looked so easy. A week ago, when she was fighting with Ulvama, or before that, Matha would have said that a smile from Erin was a rare thing you’d see once a month if that. Something to be treasured, a tiny sliver of happiness from a woman who was adversity and strife and amazing deeds, but not happiness.
Today, and the days after the Battle of the Bands, Erin was different. She didn’t create glowing dough like her favorite [Baker], nor did her eyes flash with the magic of [Witches]. She just rolled the dough up and then sighed.
“I dunno if this will be ready before we go. Guess I should have made it rise earlier. Flatbread. Flatbreads are nice. You going to stay, Matha?”
She wasn’t deaf either. Matha awkwardly leaned on the counter as Erin worked. She inspected Erin’s fingers, skin still slightly discolored by magical damage, divots taken out of her arms, scars on her wrists, hair still regrowing and also changed—but she had an apron on. Her hazel eyes were just at peace with the world.
Happy, yes. Relieved. Matha remembered Erin’s conversation with Ulvama.
“I’m…I don’t think so.”
“Are you sure? They love you here.”
Erin nodded to the Fraerlings chanting Matha’s name. Fresh [Punks], [Rockers], [Musicians]—classes born of the new trend in Lemoste. Music was in the city’s blood. It might change everything.
“Do you think I should? I know I’m not that important. This place likes me. I should stay. But…it’s not even that far from Grandma Zinni’s. I feel sort of stupid, wanting to stay here already.”
Matha ducked her head shyly. Erin glanced at her and then divided the dough in half with a chop of her palm.
“You take this half and knead. Wash your hands first. I think it’s not a bad thing to want to stay, Matha. I can see you being great here. Ahead of us might be more trouble. I dunno. I think if you come with us, you might not do better than Lemoste. But I think…yeah. I think I wouldn’t mind you tagging along.”
Another smile. Matha glanced at Erin as she awkwardly began to fold the dough. Erin was punching it, flopping it over, compressing it, whirling it around in one hand…just having fun with the process. She had a Skill; the dough seemed to stretch and grow translucent as she thinned it out. Matha’s just sort of clumped together, but the [Raider] punched it with a will.
“You sure? You didn’t like me before.”
A guilty expression crossed Erin’s face as she ducked her head.
“No. That’s my fault. I’m sorry, Matha. I was mad about you bothering Ulvama—that’s how I saw it. I was in a bad place, and I…I either like someone or I don’t. I’m bad that way. If you’re my guest, I’ll do all I can. But if you’re not, good luck, buddy.”
“I get that. It’s fine, I deserve it.”
Erin tilted her head and stopped kneading her dough for a second.
“Maybe you did. But you tried to help patch things up with Ulvama and me, even when I was a jerk to you. You earned more than me being mean to you. So, I’m sorry, Matha. I’ll be nicer, I promise.”
Argh, she really wasn’t making this easier. Matha could have stayed. She knew she wasn’t going to beat…but then Erin went and said that and Matha had to huff over the bread.
“Well, hanging with you is fun. I get to ride a Corumdon Beetle and make friends with a Battle Hamster, and they don’t like anyone. This bread stuff’s cool too. I might stick around. But it’s hard.”
She expected Erin to give her some tough love like Eurise had, but the [Innkeeper] just sidled over and nudged Matha with an elbow. Gently.
“Of course it is. That’s how you know you’re loved, dummy. Look outside.”
Matha’s head rose, and she almost flinched as she saw Fraerlings gazing at her. With avarice, pleading in their eyes, calling at her through the thick, enchanted walls. And yes, lust…it was scary. However, Erin just smiled.
“Look. You might never be so popular ever again. Look and feel it, Matha. This is a chance some people never get in their entire lives. If you want it, if you wish to know what it’s like—this is it. That’s you, Matha the Punk, Matha the Rockstar. Matha of the Battle of the Bands.”
Her eyes gleamed a second, and Matha imagined she could see that hat made of fire upon Erin’s head. Then the [Innkeeper] smiled again.
“You don’t have to be the person they want. But you should understand what they think you are. Now—the bread’s ready. If I’m not letting it rise, I can bake it in a pan or even a frying pan, no sweat. But it’s sort of boring as it is. What goes well with bread?”
Matha had had some of Erin’s Tallfolk bread before. She wavered.
“I, um, I dunno. It’s pretty plain, but in a good way? When it’s hot and chewy, it could be all kinds of things. Savory, sweet, umami, catship—all kinds of flavors.”
Erin’s face screwed up.
“Okay, I know ‘umami’ because Ryoka explained it to me—but how the heck do you know that word? And what’s ‘catship’?”
“That’s…the opposite of umami. So instead of something that’s really savory and delicious, it’s sharp and sort of hurts. Like cats. But you can’t stop eating it, even though it tastes sharp and stuff.”
“…Weird. Is that why you called it ‘catship’?”
“Yep. It’s sort of like ‘cat shit’. That’s what we all said in my gang. Plenty of stuff’s capship. Like…centipede guts. But you could also do it with a nice sweetberry jam—I bet Lemoste has some. And savory? You know, those dragonfly eggs you guys harvested in Dretonamis might make a really good jam.”
Some of the food from the other villages had been arriving as the other Fraerlings sent what could be spared to help Lemoste in their hour of need. Matha had seen the frozen Dragonfly eggs, and she jogged towards the door.
“I could get some if that’s okay? Just to taste?”
Erin tilted her head, then nodded.
“Okay. Get all the stuff you can and we’ll do a taste-test. Bugs are sort of gross, but…hey, you eat some with bread, I’ve heard. They’re growing on me.”
Matha ran out the door, ignoring the cheers, and was so preoccupied with requesting resources from some of the people who kept Lemoste’s food stores that she didn’t realize how weird it was for her and Erin to have a civil conversation until later. When she came back, Erin was finishing making bread.
“It’s not the greatest.”
She apologized; she was flash-frying the flatbread in a pan rather than an oven given the time constraints. She’d coated the pan with oil, then let it get screaming hot. Then she’d tossed the dough in—ducked—and begun furiously moving the dough around.
She had a piece of hot flatbread done within a minute. Just in time for Matha’s little spread of samples.
A glowing red Sweetberry, the magical berry filled with delicious sugars, a green, dull Dragonfly egg with an unborn nymph inside, frozen solid, and some centipede guts in an unappetizing paste with bits of chitin mixed in…Erin made a face.
“Ugh. Do you guys really eat it with the shell inside?”
“It adds to the bad flavor. I’ll strain it. Just give me a second.”
Matha began heating up the various gels, and Erin produced a little pink flame that heated up the bowls. She smiled, but said nothing nor performed great magic beyond that.
In short order, they had some bread to taste test. Not just each respective topping, mind you. For the sweetberries, Matha had pieces of cinnamon and other wild berries that would go well with it.
She had cheeses, pieces of meat, dried and fresh, and even a bit of wild bird egg, a real luxury. They loaded up the flatbread with condiments and sampled their creations with the other Fraerlings of their group who were preparing to head out.
“Hey, Erin, is this more bread? I love it. Hey, this is Sweetberry! It’s great! Did Matha help? It’s still edible despite that!”
Mera gave Erin a thumbs up as she chewed down a loaded piece of bread with sweet jam and a bit of wild nut butter. As close to a ‘PBNJ’ as Erin could make.
The [Innkeeper] politely coughed as she stopped nibbling at the first offering. She hated sweets these days, so she handed it off to the Battle Hamster, who devoured the bite. She stuck her tongue out and ruefully eyed Matha.
“I still can’t do sweets. Okay, Dragonfly jam with egg and meat…mm. That’s not bad…”
She was just a bit grossed out by the bugs, even when the Dragonfly eggs were a green paste on the bread. Matha thought it was great, and Zemmy did too. He and she devoured the hot bread and savory fillings. They had downed three each when Erin slapped their hands down.
“Stop eating all my bread! Ulvama and Eurise haven’t gotten any! There’s just enough for the, uh, catshit stuff. Let’s try it…”
She loaded up a sandwich with some centipede mash and roach meat. It was not a nice bite of food, even by catship standards. Matha winced as she took one bite and coughed.
“Feels like someone’s poking my mouth with needles. Blegh.”
“Ugh, this is horrible. I get the ‘hating it but liking the pain bit’. Can you make this better? There’s a bit of savory…Bowom would love this, I bet. Anyone see him?”
“He’d better show up before we leave or he’ll get left behind. Eurise is raring to go.”
A grimacing Erin tried to salvage the catship product. She ended up heating up the bread over the pan again, giving the roach meat a good sear until it was crisped on the outside. Then she took another bite.
“Ergh, it’s still not great—but hold on—it’s sort of addictive. It’s not great, but you get just enough taste…it’s like eating pine needles. Only, flavorful. Argh, it’s a bad burger, but I sort of like it when it’s hot. Yum. Yuck. Yum. Argh!”
She was so dismayed she kept eating the sandwich. Grimacing, laughing—and Matha took a bite of the underside, grimaced, and felt the semi-addictive qualities too until Erin swatted her away.
“Get your own, Matha. I sort of like this! Whew, it’s too hot in the kitchen. Hey…if it’s our last day in the city, then we can’t eat here. Come with me.”
 
——
 
She found the roof of the house where they were all staying and sat there with Matha as the crowd of Fraerlings chanting her name surrounded them. However, Erin’s eyes were on the city around them.
Lemoste-Under-Cliffs. The districts below them were still being rebuilt, reduced to rubble and death by the ant attacks. It hurt Matha’s heart to look at. So many Fraerlings dead or wounded or…
“Hey.”
Erin drew the young Fraerling’s attention higher. She pointed up, and Matha saw the rest of the city. The commercial districts rose above their location, buildings of steel and magic. Some literally floating above enchanted foundations.
So much waste. So much excess that could have been spent defending the city. Matha, as a village girl, saw things that her home could never have, even with Zinni. Part of her resented it. Got mad at the complacency that had led Lemoste to being attacked and nearly overrun.
But Erin? She just sat there, legs splayed out on the rooftop, nibbling at the cooling sandwich with a bit of roach leg sticking out. She grimaced from time to time, but her head turned to the magical barrier of water falling across Lemoste’s outskirts. To the Fraerlings, and even to Matha.
Then she smiled again.
“Sometimes, I’m terribly sad, Matha. When I see tragedy or think of all it’s cost us. All the people from my world. When I think of my parents…”
Her face fell, then she closed her eyes and took a breath. Erin opened them, and they sparkled as she looked around.
“It doesn’t hurt less when I remember them. But I came to this world by means outside my control. Sometimes, when I see things like this, it makes it a bit worth it. It does not change the suffering, the deaths. But it just…makes my heart feel bigger. This city is something I’ll remember until the day I die.”
“It’s not even how it should be. It’s not that great.”
Matha protested softly. Lemoste was more amazing than any place she had ever been, but she knew it could be better…Erin Solstice just shook her head. Then she flung one arm out to take it all in.
“That’s what they all say.”
“Who?”
“Everyone, Matha. Drakes tell me the Walled Cities used to be better. Gnolls talk about the days when their tribes were glorious. There was the Goblin King that I missed, the terrible Antinium Wars—Klbkch talks about True Antinium, and I have never known them. To hear them tell it, I missed everything. It was always better, more dire, harder, and more magical. That’s the problem with talking to Dragons. To me, it’s the most it’s ever been, and that’s okay. Look.”
She pointed, and her hat burned with a thousand fires. The Human woman sat there, and her eyes shone. She gazed down at the sandwich in her hands and laughed.
“I’m eating a centipede-gut sandwich in a Fraerling city, and I’m six inches tall, Matha! Look!”
She stood up, and Matha saw the [Innkeeper]’s eyes gleam. Then, she thought she saw the world as Erin Solstice did, sometimes. A tiny Human on a beautiful home, surrounded by watching Fraerlings, in a magical city built into the side of a rocky cliff face.
Impossible, all of it. Her eyes burned with wonder. She wore a delighted smile on her face, for the novelty of it all, and Matha thought—
Oh. So that’s how much they hurt her. This was the real Erin Solstice who found magic in what to Matha was so mundane. The [Innkeeper] turned.
“I made it here. A place no one from my world has dreamed of. Mom, Dad…I wish I had a picture to take for you. Look. See where your daughter’s gone? How much further will I go? I hope you’re proud of me, when I come home. Don’t be sad. I’ll come home and apologize for making you so worried. I have so many friends I want to show you. Not just the inn’s family, but…everyone! Anazurhe, Rags, the Quarass—yes, Ulvama was conscious there was a lot of danger depending on who it was, and the rule about secrets was dangerous. The more you shared, the more the other party felt like they had to reply. And since you could only speak truth here, you might lose something important.
There was a bit of nuance with the ‘truth’ being whatever you believed to be true. It was a perfect Skill for a [Witch] and [Innkeeper] with so much power. Just maybe not Erin Solstice specifically.
But that was why Ulvama had been so upset when Erin failed to include her. She wanted to be part of Erin Solstice’s adventure, her life. Now she was here at last, and she sat in the gazebo and finally, finally laid eyes on the [Pavilion of Secrets] in its fullness.

All real, and none of it was. Including the copy of Erin Solstice. Pavilion-Erin sat there, smiling at Ulvama, as the real Erin Solstice glared at her.
“—And that’s the door that probably leads into the [Palace of Fates]. We can change the scenery and get rid of this loser if you want. Anywhere you want to be. How about it, Ulvama? Ulvama?”

Beyond Cognita Truestone. A thinking Skill with Erin’s face. It hit her, suddenly, how terrifying and unique that was. An Erin who was not on your side? The real thing didn’t get how scary that was, because she’d never met herself. Erin eyed the Pavilion and flicked her fingers.
“Shoo. Don’t look so freaked out, Ulvama; I’ve gotten the hang of this place. You keep telling me how to do it better, so…go ahead. Floor’s all yours. I’m sort of excited, having you in here. Nervous. I’m afraid of the Quarass. You annoyed me with all your big talk about how much smarter you’d be, so this is your chance.”

It had begun. Unseen, the [Pavilion of Secrets] began to cackle like the [Innkeeper]. The Hobgoblin opened her mouth, took a sip of tea, and murmured.
“This place is, um, scarier than I thought. I was wrong. I shouldn’t have made fun of you. I’m scared.”

You’re the only person I’d trust with my back in here at the moment. I want you to be best friends with me…forever. So of course I was gonna bring you in here. I trust you with all my secrets. See?”

It meant a lot. Especially to Ulvama, who doubted everything. It made the Hobgoblin feel a lot of things she’d unpack later, but unfortunately, the main thing that popped into Ulvama’s head was the need for reciprocity.
And the problem with that?
‘I think I want to be more than friends, Erin. I think you do too. I just don’t think you know it yet.’
The Hobgoblin bit her tongue, wrestling her mouth closed before she said that. Not because it wasn’t true; it was absolutely true, and that was the problem.
What would Erin do if she heard that? Freak out? Deny it? Ulvama didn’t know, and that worried her.
If Erin was anything like most Izrilians, be they Human or Drake, there was a chance she didn’t take that response well at all. She might embrace it. Or it might make her balk, resist, and twist it all.
Ulvama had seen that happen too. She was too afraid to say it, so she forced the words down and found a lesser truth.

She came, she came.
The door opened, and the sands of Zeikhal blew inwards, a storm across the void. The winds howled, and the [Pavilion of Secrets] itself shuddered.
This was no Revenant of a few hundred years.
Nor the highest-levelled [Strategist] of this mortal era.
Nor even a cunning [Rogue].
The Pavilion put the invitation across the world in a second, and the two women inside waited not long for a reply to be received.
The moment she saw the door, the ruler of Ger stepped towards it. And she was smiling before she put a hand on the handle.
It did not matter when she was called or what condition she was in. If she were in a raging battle or lying sick with the ague, she would have taken the invitation.
That was just the being who she was. Countless lives compressed into one frail child’s body. How frustrating. How necessary to spite death itself. She had oh-so-many regrets already. Things she wished to do, but which her nation, level, or frail form did not allow.
Lifetimes of regrets, of poor choices and forms and personalities who led her astray. But always, the Quarass returned. She crawled her way back across the cycle of rebirth with the will of the first woman to take her class.
And this place…
She remembered this place.
The moment the girl saw the door and heard the words being read aloud to her, she knew what must be done. For she was, again, the Quarass.
She had been here before. Once in ignorance and defeated by the first owner she had met. Then she had been invited again and again, sparring wits with the greatest Reinhart of her era. Polishing her own mental blades and gaining experience until she was so dangerous the other owner had invited her in once—and never after.
Now, a third time they called for her, and she had far more knowledge accumulated over the aeons. Moreover, the Quarass’ mind was already in full-gear before the door opened.
This door is Erin Solstice’s. That it would be anyone else was ludicrous; Erin Solstice was the inheritor of the [Garden of Sanctuary]. It could only be her. Moreover, it was the real Erin Solstice.
Fetohep of Khelt was not that canny, nor was the imposter a perfect actress. The Quarass had no real intentions of blackmailing Erin Solstice anyways. The Titan had paid a small sum for her silence, though he hadn’t answered her on whether the fake was a Fraerling in disguise or that Hobgoblin, the Quarass’ assumptions. One could not know everything…well, unless the Quarass were to gain the [Pavilion of Secrets].
Sadly, despite lifetimes of trying, the will of levels had never given her even the [Garden of Sanctuary]. As if it were reluctant to hand her the tools she believed she could use to maximum advantage…
The door swung open, and the Quarass breathed in and out. She walked forwards, and the child vanished.
A taller woman with a fully-formed brain halted in the walkway towards the gazebo and saw a new structure waiting for her.
So this is what she thinks of the [Pavilion of Secrets]. So much less grand than her peers. It is neither a stifling room nor a window into the infinite, but the infinite around us all. Yet we reside in a humble building. Fitting of her.
She was surely damaged. Perhaps dying. If she had survived her wounds, then the scars of what she had done were scarcely healing. The Quarass advanced towards the gazebo and saw the young woman sitting there.
“Uh oh. I’m having second thoughts.”
The moment Erin Solstice saw the Quarass in the flesh, she paled, and the Quarass of Germina smiled. Her eyes swept towards the second figure who sat there, her confidence gone. Borne away by the winds before she saw the woman striding towards them. The Quarass of Germina dipped her head.
“Be at ease, Erin Solstice. I am still the Quarass of Ger you witness on scrying orbs. That I wear a more fitting form to speak as equals, with my full faculties, is but a weakness upon my part. I come to you in friendship now, with no intention of harm or malice. May I sit with you?”

Yet they had not had time to converse properly until now. The Quarass had been cognizant of the risks. If Erin Solstice had used her [World’s Eye Theatre], even then, the Quarass would have been circumspect. But this place…she had been waiting for Erin Solstice to gain custodianship of this place. For the Quarass had known it was possible. So she had waited, a patient player playing a thousand games in the sand against the world, and this moment had been but one she had anticipated.

She?

Anyone else in the world.
Here, in this place, in this moment—
The Quarass was unmatched.
Witness her.

“Search me. I didn’t know that was a thing. You going to show her the evidence? You might as well; she won’t be able to get the rest, but at least it’ll get you over your door-phobia.”
Erin and Pavvy conferred as Ulvama got increasingly suspicious and annoyed behind them.
“Hey, what is the conspiracy? How are you showing me?”
Erin gave Ulvama a wretched look.
“I’ve been trying to tell you, but you keep forgetting.”
Ulvama’s expression grew alarmed.
“Impossible. Unless you’re protected? How big is the Sariant Lamb thing?”
“Super big, and no one but me, Ryoka, and Nanette understand. I tell anyone else and it just goes in one ear, out the other. I had to have the [Pavilion of Secrets] to become immune to it.”
Erin hoped that would register, and Ulvama’s eyes flickered. To her relief, the Hobgoblin did get what this was. She put a hand to her forehead.
“Ugh. This one of them stupid mind things. You learn something bad, your head explodes. Bleh.”
She grew markedly less interested when she realized she wasn’t able to help, but Erin did have a conciliatory prize of sorts. She walked over to the rightmost door in the gazebo, a worn bathroom door with a brass handle. It was the least impressive of the four doors, and Ulvama’s eyes widened.
“Erin?”
“This is my door. I think I know what’s behind it, Ulvama. Don’t worry…”
Erin pulled at that familiar handle and swung open the door to her version of the [Pavilion of Secrets] at last. Pavvy sighed as Erin walked through it.
“…It’s just me.”
 
——
 
There was no version of Erin Solstice inside this room, which was a huge relief; she didn’t think she could handle a third her. In fact, this room was totally unlike the one she’d seen with Cormelex.
That had been a kind of august sitting room, if clearly dark and totally old-money ostentatious with an evil-overlord theme. Erin remembered a roaring fire and high backed chairs and the Lucifen sitting around. Cormelex had been the innocent-looking young man, not the imposing Lucifen she’d pictured.
She hadn’t seen Sheta and Aleieta’s rooms, but Erin suspected it was some living room or place central to who they had been. A parlor where you could meet…past reflections of them? People important to them?
She was still working out what each owner’s rooms did, but her own rooms were a good hint, even if they were visibly unfinished.
Literally. Erin emerged into another black void with a few articles of furniture scattered around. A huge office desk, a worn chair behind it, a giant cork board on one ‘wall’, and a fireplace of her own. Even a simple fan on the ceiling with a dim yellow glow, one of the bulbs missing, a chain twirling as the blades lazily spun around in the air.
Erin recognized everything but the ludicrously huge cork board. The fan was from her family’s office. The desk not hers, but Erin’s lips quirked as she saw more objects floating around the room.
“Oh, come on. This is me?”
“I don’t make the rules. This is all your subconscious. Lame, right?”
“I just—I have to have better taste than—okay, this is actually sort of cool.”
Erin wandered around the room as Ulvama poked one head in, chewing suspiciously on more imaginary donuts. Erin knew this place. It wasn’t her common room of the inn like she’d thought, her private bedroom, or even another part of her inn. This was, in fact, a place she’d been to a few times, mostly to get yelled at.
Watch Captain Zevara’s desk sat at one end of the room, and across the walls was black-and-yellow tape that read ‘under construction’. Clearly, this representation of Erin’s pavilion wasn’t complete because she wasn’t done with it.
Her mind had just organized it how she’d see it. As for the decor…Erin sighed as she stared at the cork billboard.
“Detective theming all the way down. Yup, yup.”
An image of a scowling Sariant Lamb was pinned to the board with a red tack and connected with some string to Ryoka Griffin’s face. Underneath Ryoka was a little factsheet.
 
‘Ryoka Griffin, Rulebreaker. That’s why she’s able to talk to Nerry and remember everything, no doubt about it. Unless it’s also because she’s fae-touched? Got to find more Rulebreakers.’
 
There were more details below, mostly just details about why Nerry had sought Ryoka out. Reminders about Ryoka’s weird perspective powers and how they worked. There was another line to Nanette. This listing was even less helpful.
 
‘[Sariantfriend]. I bet she’s dedicated to helping. Plus, she renounced her class so she figured it out, then got a class.
Nanette’s cute but gets stubborn, and she’s not a full [Witch] yet.
What? This is literally all I have.’
 
Detective Erin was not working with much. She did have another image of Erek, which made the real Erin slap her forehead.
“Of course! Erek might know about all this! Genius work, me. Hey, Ulvama, you—”
The Hobgoblin had wandered right past the cork board, as if she were unable to see it. She was poking around the office, annoyed.
“This place weird and stupid looking. I just know it’s because of something from your world, Erin. This like those movies where those idiots smoke on pipes and solve crimes even Apista could figure out?”
“Hey, hey. Inspector Gadget was a genius. Aw, who am I kidding? Penny was the real brains. Even the dog was smarter than that guy. It’s my attempt to solve this mystery. It’s a work in progress—”
Barely begun. But it made Erin think out loud.
“Are the other rooms like this? A kind of library of data the others were working on? So if I go in there, do I get access to Cormelex’s secrets? Sheta’s?”
That could be fascinating and, indeed, dangerous. But why was Aleieta’s door the worst…?
Well, one question at a time. Ulvama had found the only actual ‘being’ in the room, and Erin Solstice sighed. This time, she avoided Ulvama’s accusatory stare. She didn’t have a good explanation for this one.
The figure with their feet up behind Zevara’s desk would have probably eaten a full kick to the stomach if Zevara had seen her doing that in real life. However, she was smoking on one of Palt’s cigars, and she sat up dramatically as a rain-lashed window lit up with lightning behind her, illuminating her face.
“It wasn’t a dark and stormy night when Erin Solstice called me into their [Pavilion of Secrets]. In fact, it wasn’t any of those things. But there was a conspiracy afoot, only it was the hoofed kind, and I was in need of help. So was she. The dame needed a body-double, and I needed a voice. Happenstance meant we got to trade places…”
Nerry, in Erin-form, steepled her fingers together as she pretended to be a detective. Erin covered her face with her hands.
“I hope this confuses the heck out of anyone who inherits my Skill. Do I end up here?”
She turned to Pavvy, and the being smiled. A touch sadly.
“When you’re dead, a copy of you will be here. Nothing like the real thing in its complexity. It’ll probably be more straightforward, even if you die tomorrow. This is just how your brain likes to think of things.”
“You’re so lame.”
Ulvama wandered out of the room, and Erin didn’t respond. But she did change Ulvama’s next donut from a cream-filled puff to a nasty rotten raspberry one that Erin had once eaten.
Ignoring the exclamations from outside, Erin peered around her room. It was sort of a let-down. True, it explained the problem with the Earther revelation.
“Proof, huh? This is a nice way of limiting the [Pavilion of Secrets]. Very give-and-take. Almost like Sheta was shaving down the Skill a bit to get everything she wanted.”
“It was a delicate negotiation. You think this kind of power was allowable at merely Level 50 without drawbacks? It’s not even a capstone. Me, not even a capstone.”
Pavvy seemed put out about that. Erin raised her brows. That also gave it more nuance indeed. Erin walked around her room. Still, something was off.
“Weird. I guess the Earther secret has to rest a moment. The Quarass said she needs proof and that I could figure it out. If I think about it…”
She closed her eyes, then opened them slowly. Her face grew calm, still, and Erin gripped her tunic in one hand.
“…I guess I have to have one of those conversations I really don’t want to. Tell a man about some sins.”
Pavvy grinned in pure delight, but Erin just glanced sideways.
“The problem is, once you play a hand like that, you’re marked twice. And if it’s both that and whatever else…bleh. I need to take the piece or be ready to duck the entire board coming at me.”
“Mm, so you’re not just lazy.”
“Oh, no, I’m lazy.”
Erin ignored Ulvama shouting for her to come out here! She tapped a foot and stared around the room.
“Pavvy, question.”
“Ask.”
“This room, unfinished as it is. If it’s just a reflection of Grand Revelations and people who mattered to me, that’s lame. Sheta? I don’t think she actually thought of her heirs this much. She wanted something more than this.”
“She was a woman who planted trees she would never rest under.”
Erin sighed.
“Yeah. And also more dangerous than Teriarch ever knew. Those other rooms…is there something they have that I don’t have yet?”
She turned, and Pavvy’s smile widened before she covered it with a hand.
“Not that you’d notice right away. Now that’s interesting. Aleieta reasoned it out in a week. Cormelex saw it in a second, but he cheated. How did you beat Aleieta?”
Erin Solstice shrugged, slightly pleased.
“I’m not as trusting as she is. Plus, I just had the Quarass beat me up. Also, if this is my room, I leave clues for myself. There’s a lot of stuff in this office that fits. But playing find-what-stands-out—I’m not bad at those games.”
So saying, she walked over to the one thing that really didn’t fit. The cork-board made sense. Adventurer’s Guilds, the Watch House, all had them, if not in Zevara’s office. Same with the desk, the other stuff. But…Erin glanced up.
The fan and light switch fit a detective’s office from Earth. Not here. Erin slowly stood on her toes, reached up, and pulled the chain.
Click.
The yellow lights went out in a second. But it did not turn completely dark. Instead, the ‘missing’ bulb illuminated, a pale, purple outline glowing.
Like a black light. Nerry vanished. The desk vanished. Erin Solstice turned in the dark room, and her breath caught.
“What’s that?”
“The opposite of a Grand Revelation.”
Pavvy grinned in front of the empty…Erin Solstice’s eyes flickered up, and then she saw it. Perhaps it was just how she saw it. She had to compare it to—
“Erin, what was that donut? Was it nasty?”
Ulvama kicked the door back open. She had a bunch of cream puffs ready to throw. She whirled, and Erin stopped trying to force-feed Pavvy another rotten jelly donut.
“Hey, Ulvama! C’mon, it was one joke! You were being mean!”
The [Innkeeper] fled out of her room as Ulvama followed, cursing her. That left Pavvy alone. She munched on the bad jelly donut and smiled.
“I’m waiting for when you need me.”
But the [Innkeeper] already knew that.
 
——
 
Erin let the Hobgoblin chase her around and felt a bit better after the Quarass incident.
“Okay…who’s next?”
Ulvama hesitated and lowered her arms.
“Ceria? Colth? Yvlon? You promised.”
Erin had promised Pisces that. She bit her lip.
“I will call them in. But maybe I should meet them privately. Ceria, at least.”
“If you haven’t met them already.”
Pavvy mouthed, but too softly for Ulvama to hear. Erin twitched. She would meet all her friends in time. She owed them that. Some conversations had to be hers and hers alone, though. Ulvama nodded and cast around, awkward.
“I could do a list—”
“No.”
In the silence, Erin ran through the many peoples she wanted to speak to. She made her choice without asking Ulvama. But she would be glad if the [Shaman] could be there.
“Pavvy. Can you get Ryoka in here, please? I mean, Ryoko?”
A pause as Ulvama stopped eating donuts. She put the box down and came over to Erin. They stood there until Pavvy reappeared.
“She’s banned. Remember?”
“What? Oh yeah, shit. But I want to get in contact with her or…she’s one of the people I really want to talk with. Some Skill if I can’t talk to one of my best friends. Fine, who’s with Ryoka? Can I ask about that? Get me the closest person to…no, you’re just going to get some random person. Shaestrel—she’s not going to come in here. Nerry? Can I at least figure out where Ryoka is?”
Pavvy’s eyes glittered.
“Who knows? I’m not a cartographer. But go ahead and try to triangulate her position. Reminds me of when I couldn’t have gotten you Cara, Geneva, Daly, or most of the Bushrangers all at the same time. That’s the kind of thing your Grand Revelation is meant to uncover.”
The hair stood up on Erin’s neck. That was too…Pavvy gave Erin a wink.
“That’s my purpose, piercing obfuscation Skills. I could ferret tons of stuff out that’s being kept under mental locks. Like people or places trying to hide from public consciousness. The Bloodfeast Raiders, for example.”
“Motherf—”
Their name made Erin clench her fists.
“I might have to take a mulligan on the Earther mystery to kill some people, Ulvama.”
“Okay.”
Erin cast around, thrown by that. Then she exhaled.
“I…if not them? Then…I promised to check back in, and I didn’t. I’m so stupid. But I was embarrassed, and he’s easier. Get me Rabbiteater, please.”
She smiled, and Ulvama straightened and relaxed. Pavvy nodded and vanished. Erin Solstice wiped at her brows.
“Let’s have a good conversation, get some important stuff settled like where they are now, and then we can go tell the others about this. Matha wants to come in here.”
“Eh, you don’t have to let her. Rabbiteater. He all fancy now, a [Knight]?”
“Goblin Lord.”
“No.”
“That’s what he felt like.”
“Psh, Erin, I’ll be the judge of that. Hold on, where did you say he was last time?”
“Uh, shipwrecked and running from Dullahans. But he’s a tough guy, and they had an army. They said there were some kind of ruins ahead.”
“Nice. Goblins, ruins, easy to get away.”
The two nodded, and Erin was smiling. She waited for her beloved [Knight]. Of all the far-flung peoples she was worried about, she had felt that Rabbiteater wouldn’t die so easily. He was too high-level. Too strong. And she was right about that.
Erin Solstice called for Rabbiteater long after his company had left the Synectic Maze. She summoned Ser Solstice to her [Pavilion of Secrets] as his weary, footsore, decimated company of Terandrians, adventurers, friends, and allies of convenience reached some kind of settlement out of the jungles at last. The port-city of Benoix.
She called him the hour after he placed Ser Markus’ body in a shrouded casket and sent him home.
