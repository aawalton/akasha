Finally, a gaggle of men barred my path, their allegiance marked with bandanas that had been dyed the telltale orange of burning cigarettes. Some of them wore T-shirts and jeans, several wore police uniforms, many were smoking, all of them were pale beyond what could be considered healthy. Maybe it was loyalty to the person providing their fix, or maybe it was the dangerous foreigner that welded them together. Either way, the cops didn’t even pretend anymore, which made me square my shoulders and stare down anyone who dared meet my eye.

Once I came to a halt in front of the group and crossed my arms, they shuffled and obediently made way for their leader. He emerged from their group like pus from a wound, cigarette in his mouth and still wearing his uniform cap.

“Baker,” I said, tilting my head in something that could be interpreted as a greeting, but definitely held no surprise.

“That’s Sergeant Baker to you, bitch,” he said, exhaling a cloud of smoke. It hung in the air like volcanic ash, orange lights flickering within the dull gray mass. He flicked his cigarette, then casually pointed it at me. “Why do I hear you’ve been causing trouble?”

So far, so good. He was following the bully-guardsman script to a T. I was about to play right along when things went off script for a second. Some poor sod, a middle-aged man with a receding hairline and a rusty pistol tucked into his ruddy jeans stepped up behind me.

“She’s been helping, actually,” he began, but before he could say another word, Baker’s eyes bore down on him like dying suns. My would-be hero crumpled under the weight of that gaze like an aluminum can under a boot. The cop didn’t even need to say anything. Still, I thought I could hear the man mutter, “Been more help than you assholes have ever been…” as he made his escape into the crowd.

I didn’t hold that against him. Someone trying to stand up for me was more than I usually had going for me. Here, I actually had quite a few men and women at my back, I noticed. A crowd facing armed thugs and the city guard with that much spine usually meant there was someone holding down a counterweight to the Cracked. And that meant there was someone I could negotiate with besides Baker. I just had to find them. Or wait until they found me.

“Out there doing business?” Baker, oblivious to my thoughts, inhaled another lungful of smoke before going on, “…killing Ferals, perhaps? To protect us, of course.”

I couldn’t help but smirk. Did this jerk think this was my first time coming face to face with the sickly pale arm of the law?

“Just out for a stroll,” I said, brushing some Zombie gunk off of Arx. “I wouldn’t dare dream of killing anything without your permission.”

Baker scowled. Took a pull from his cigarette. The glow briefly cast his pallor into fractured orange.

“How dumb do you think I am, slag?” he asked, and took another pull.

“I honestly don’t know. You tell me.” I should have been more circumspect, I knew. But I was tired, and exhausted, and covered in blood and mechanical fluids, and this guy rubbed me the all the wrong ways from here to Sunday and back. Good thing he couldn’t arrest me for wisecracking.

“Bitch thinks she’s so smart, but doesn’t even bother to hide her gun.” He snorted and spat on the street. His snot was pitch black.

“Search her.”

Awh, fuck. Turns out he could, after all.

Three of his flunkies stepped forward.

“You are under arrest for the possession of unlicensed weaponry. Anything you say or do will be used against you…”

I frowned, which made the gang members still surrounding us reach for their clearly licensed guns. Still, the sudden tension made the cops freeze, which bought me precious seconds to come up with a plan.

On instinct, I almost reached for Pharus, and barely stopped myself in time. While I was sure I could take them, murdering police on my first day would destroy the professional image I was trying to cultivate. Besides, there were far too many spots of bright orange in the crowd. Sure, I could kill them. But that would decimate the amount of people who could stand up to the zombies, putting everyone in danger, and me on 24/7 cleanup duty. So that was out, too.

What else? I could run, sure, but then what? Take Nexus back to my Domain and risk getting locked out? Hide in a city with 50% of the population hunting me? I’d done that before, and no thanks.

As much as it pained me, if I ever wanted to sleep again, I needed the gang’s help to hold back Latirae. And that meant playing cops and robbers with a bunch of drug addicts.

Great.

I unfolded my arms and, with a grimace, raised my hands in the air. Perhaps they wouldn’t even find anything. Manus should be untraceable, and Pharus just looked like a flashlight, but who knew what was going on in the code behind the scenes. That thought worried me further. How deep were they going to search me? And what would a search reveal of my DPM, my Domain, my secrets?

Before they could touch me, though, a new voice cut through the crowd like a razor whip.

“Baker? What are you doing?”

A woman stepped through the crowd as though she owned the place. She was tall, with red hair flowing over her shoulders like a liquified bird’s nest. Her outfit was anything but regal, just a white T-shirt over ripped blue jeans, but she made it work with the way she moved. Self-assured, determined, as though the world should bow to her. The moment she arrived, the cops straightened, and the thugs took a step back.

“My job,” Baker replied without looking at her.

“You should let me handle this,” the woman said as she came to a halt so close to the officer that he had no choice but to look at her. “You always fuck up these sorts of things, anyway.”

I tensed, expecting sudden violence, but everyone just took it in stride.

“Just keep out of this Ma’am. I—” Baker began, but she rode right over his half-hearted attempt to regain control.

“No, no, no. You’re doing it again! Your job is to serve and protect, Baker. Just go.” When he didn’t move immediately, she made a shooing gesture. “Go! Go man the barricade and make sure the Ferals don’t eat our faces!”

Baker condensed all his arrogant hatred into a suffocating silence, but if he intended to intimidate her, it didn’t work. The woman just kept staring him down, until he scoffed, flicked the ash off his cigarette, and took another hit as his eyes searched for something in the distance. His macho energy, probably. Or perhaps his dignity.

“That won’t be a problem, Jacky,” he said eventually, face clouded. He glanced at me. “Bitch over there killed all of them and took the Cash.” When he turned back to Jacky and took another pull, however, his expression changed. It didn’t soften, exactly, but there were traces of some sort of grudging respect.

Jacky, on the other hand, no longer paid him any attention. “Oh, you did?!” Smiling, she approached me with long, urgent steps and grabbed me by the shoulders.

My whole body tensed involuntarily, and I made a gentle effort to shrug out of her grip. “I can neither confirm nor deny—”

“Baker, you need to get back to your duties!” She said over her shoulder.

While the cop hesitated, however, he didn’t budge. “She’s a threat, Jacky. Strong enough to wipe out an entire horde. What if she starts getting unruly? What if she decides she deserves what isn’t hers?”

“Oh, she won’t.” Jacky grinned at me like I was her best friend, and we were about to steal horses. “She will be a good girl, yeah?”

While the two of them had been arguing, my thoughts had been running through possibilities like cracks through a frozen lake. With Jacky’s subtle invitation, it all came together with a mind-shaking rumble.

I smiled back. “Oh, the best, for sure.”

Jacky laughed, shrill and excited, and squeezed my shoulders. Wincing at the sudden noise, I took a step back and out of reach of her hands, and she let it happen.

Baker, in the meantime, clearly wasn’t buying it. He took another draw from his cigarette and exhaled the smoke upward. Bright reds and oranges crackled angrily within.

“Look, officer,” I said, wrangling down my desire to pour salt into his wounds. “I know you don’t like me, and fair enough, I don’t exactly like you, either. But you can’t deny that I get results.”

Jacky grinned as if I’d just made her a present. For his part, the cop just took another long hit from his cigarette. If this had been a meeting room, at this point I would have sent for coffee to show good faith. Let things cool down a bit. Since I couldn’t risk losing the momentum, though, I had to barrel through.

“There’s no reason why you I couldn’t help you out,” I pressed. “I mean, every Feral I kill will keep your people safe, right?”

“Sure. I could use a guard dog. But you’re a wolf, bitch,” The cigarette between the Sargent’s pale lips blazed bright one last time, but then he finally flicked it to the floor. “And I ain’t got a leash on you.”

One of his cronies stepped on the butt, ground it under her boot. She was trying to make eye contact, but I ignored her.

“Could always deputize me,” I suggested, forcing my most non-threatening smile.

Baker’s lips curled into a contemptuous smile.

“Cold day in hell. And don’t think the Dread Captain will make it happen, either. He’s too busy running his fancy academy.”

While I was sure I could find the Captain and pry some time out of his schedule, it felt like Baker had a point. If Khance was going to deputize me, he would have done so when we met. It was a shame. Being incorporated into the structure would have solved a lot of my problems.

Still, Dread Captain? I would have loved to hear the story behind that. Seemed like the good Captain Khance had been a bit of a wild card in his youth.

“Alright,” Jacky said as if that was amazing news, and wrapped her arm over my shoulders. “If you don’t want her, she’s going to join me instead.”

I flinched, but didn’t move out of her grip. She wanted to help, and I wasn’t going to turn that down. Instead, I crossed my arms and as Baker’s smile withered, my own bloomed, thornier and sharper than his had ever been.

“She’s going to be the end of you, Jacky,” he said, fishing in his breast pocket for another cigarette. “All of us.”

Jacky squeezed my shoulder. “Oh forget that! She knows she should fit in just fine, right?”

My smile strained a little, but held. “Absolutely. In fact, let me make you a deal.” I finally shrugged off her hand, ignored the goosebumps, and stepped forward, arms spread. “Why don’t you move the barricade further up north? Reclaim some lost territory.”

Baker shook his head, cupping the flame of his lighter as he brought it to the cigarette on his lips. Around it, he mumbled “Told ya, bitch is crazy.”

It was loud enough for everyone to hear, but neither me nor Jacky reacted.

“I can hold them off. You don’t need to send a single guy into the breach.”

“…and in return you want pay,” Baker said, his cigarette burning angrily. “Pay that my guys deserve after staying on their feet for a week straight.”

“Yep. And a place to stay.” There was no sense in playing coy. I needed more Logic if I wanted to keep up this pace. More importantly, though, focusing on the funds veiled my real objective. I needed room to breathe, and by God, I needed time to sleep. Creating more distance between the Kids and any Ferals would get me enough of both, even if it wouldn’t allow me to return to my own Domain.

“Come on, we could start tonight. You’d shorten the wall, too. Less people to keep it protected that way.”

“We should do it,” Jacky added. “Don’t be so selfish all the time, Baker. Think about what it would mean to the city if we had someone of her caliber at our side.”

He took one step toward me, hand on his chin. He glanced at Jacky, then at me again, before he shook his head in exasperation. “Bitch is crazy…” he mumbled to himself and took another deep hit from his cigarette. Then he blew a lungful of smoke in my face. It smelled of burned hopes and tasted of ashen realities.

“You’re not getting a single fucking cent out of me. If Jacky wants you so bad, let her pay your whore ass.” Through the haze of the fog and the tears in my eyes, I could barely see anything. When I stepped out of the cloud with a hand on Manus, however, he just flipped me the bird and strode off, taking most of the cops with him.

The gang members glared at us for a while longer as the smoke faded. But when Jacky made a shooing motion, they dispersed, muttering amongst themselves.

“You need to ignore him,” Jacky said, while I was still trying not to cough and ruin my tough-merc image. “He’s getting paid to pay attention, and he’s worried about the blue signal. That’s reasonable.”

“Blue signal?” I wheezed, even though I probably knew the answer.

“Oh, usually, there’s a blue signal before the red one. A kind of warning. This time, it was the other way around, so maybe the warning system is breaking.”

“Oh,” I said. “I mean, can’t you fix it?”

“That’s a silly question!” Jacky said with a grin. “We don’t issue the warnings. They’re being sent from abroad.” She laughed, shrill and loud. “You’re probably the only person in this town who doesn’t know.”

She took my hand and pulled me along before I had time to think about what I could say to that.

“Anyway, come on, I’m going to take you home. You’ll meet Darryl, and he will pull some strings to make you a full citizen.”

“I’m not looking for a new place, actually. Just a hotel or something, I have—”

“No, no, no. You must become a citizen. As a citizen, you can be part of the militia, and get paid for every Feral you kill.”

“But I don’t have any place to st—”

“You will stay with us for a while, then you will find a place of your own!”

I wanted to protest, but there was still smoke in my lungs, and I didn’t want to fall into a coughing fit. I decided I liked her, though. She was just trying to help, even if she was a bit too energetic about it. I really should try to repay her efforts in kind.

Besides, it was just for a while.

“What about my pay?” I asked.

“Oh, you’ll get your share once we are done with the harvest,” Jacky said. “We had to stop after it became dangerous to dress our kills, and we kind of ran out of reserves.”

Alright, that made sense. They harvested the Ferals for their Logic, and turned it into local currency. What I needed to know, though, was how fast they could do it, and how much I’d get.

“So, are we talking hours here, or…?”

“Oh no, silly! We should be done by tomorrow.”

That wasn’t ideal, but far from the worst. If you wanted to last as a mercenary, you ideally got paid before you even started the job. In this case, however, that clearly wasn’t an option. Also, it wasn’t like a safe place to stay was worthless. Getting kicked out of town after you did their dirty work happened occasionally, and it was never fun. It meant hardtack for dinner and sleeping on even harder forest floors. But what are you supposed to do? Threaten pitchfork-wielding villagers until they let you sleep in their inn? That meant poison in your stew and knives in your dreams.

“Alright,” I finally said, but my reluctance must have fluttered in my voice, because Jacky squeezed my arm to reassure me.

“I know it’s not great. You just need to be patient, okay?”
