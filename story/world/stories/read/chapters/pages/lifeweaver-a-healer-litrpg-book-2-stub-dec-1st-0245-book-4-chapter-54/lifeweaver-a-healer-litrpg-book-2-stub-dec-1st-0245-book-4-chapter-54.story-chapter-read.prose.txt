Another hour. That was all the quiet time he got before the alarm bells started ringing again. This time, it wasn't from the weave being under attack. Well, not just because the weave was under attack. Smudge called out to him through their bond, and Luke answered without hesitation.

Stepping through the weave, he found himself in a small town. Judging by the look of things, they were still somewhere in the US, but the streets were empty except for Smudge himself and the army of monsters pouring through a portal right in the middle of the street. They were... fish-people, kind of. An upright trout with slim, almost human-looking legs and arms was a sight to behold. They fought with war-hammers, which was a ridiculous choice. Or it would have been if they hadn’t been able to harm Smudge each time they connected. Even while they swung, their fish mouths kept opening and closing, like they weren't able to breathe, but either they didn't need air, or they'd found some way to make do, because they didn't die from asphyxiation. If not for the seriousness of the situation, Luke might have laughed at the sight. Instead, he charged to Smudge's aid. He had never seen his companion struggle in a fight due to his void-like nature, but this time it seemed like he had bitten off more than he could chew. A massive hammer blow struck Smudge's head, and the voidling let go of a fish-man he was tearing into. Staggering, Smudge whimpered.

Luke called his companion back into the Stable, getting him out of trouble. It was good Smudge had called him over, because the presence of the monsters in Earth's system pulled on the void's attention. Attacks on the weave would come soon if he didn't deal with the situation. Throwing himself at the massing crowd of sea-smelling creatures, he wondered why dungeon breaks didn't happen more often, seeing as portals opened all over the world. Were Earth's Integrated just that good? Luke doubted it.

Dodging a war hammer blow, then weavestepping past a row of monsters, Luke reached forward with several threads of mana, slamming them into the monsters in his way. One or two resisted his threads, but Luke didn't give up. Instead, he pushed forward with his will, and the threads slammed home, whipping through their necks, cutting through flesh. A fish-man to his left found an opening and raised its hammer. Rather than use threads to attack or dodge, Luke broke its face with a fist. Several bones snapped and popped, sending a jolt of pain all the way up his arm. Worth it. Another blow followed. This time, Luke took a page out of Magan's book and pushed it back with nothing more than his will. That took a lot out of him, more than a lot. His knees almost buckled, but Luke kept his footing and pushed on, forcing his way through the monsters to reach the portal. Not because he wanted to go through, but because that's where he saw a fish with a crown. Obvious target if ever there was one.

Water-based spells threw up barriers around their king. They stopped Luke dead in his tracks, not budging even an inch. Dodging more blows, now from the occasional trident as well, he side-stepped and found that the barrier, while fantastic at stopping physical attacks, did nothing to prevent his threads from moving through. Weavestep brought him through. A fish clad in ornate jewelry stepped forward, a spell at the ready. When its spellweave fell apart to Luke's advance, it blubbered wordlessly before dying to a well-aimed Needle of Life piercing its monster core. The spellcaster's death brought the barriers down, but it was too late for the king. It raised its hands in surrender.

Once, that might have given Luke pause, before he'd been burned by devious plots and back-stabbing dungeon dwellers more times than he could count. A swift thread to the head and the king slipped to its fish knees, its crown clattering to the ground.

That, unfortunately, didn't stop the rest of the fish-monsters from coming. It took him almost fifteen minutes of constant fighting to kill them all. Not a single fish-man retreated through the dungeon. By the time he was done, Luke was bleeding from a dozen wounds and some damn fish had crushed his foot with a war hammer. His foot!

"Asshole," he grunted as he healed himself. The bastards hadn't been great as mana batteries either, meaning he'd been forced to conserve mana through the whole ordeal. Now, he was covered in fish scales and smelled like a pungent fisherman's wharf. All in all, he gave the fight two out of ten fish heads. To make matters worse, he hadn't been quick enough, and the void had come calling again. At least the portal closed on its own.

Luke: "Dimitrios? We've got company."

Dimitrios: "Yes, I know. I'm there. You should come. Now."

Even in his tired, somewhat mangled state, Luke had no difficulty reaching out to the weave. For a moment, he considered an attempt at going in the flesh, like Dimitrios, but figured he didn't have much to gain from that, even if he managed it. It was something to experiment with during a quiet moment, maybe. Not when it was void o´clock. Traversing the weave in the metaphysical realm, he could appear wherever he wanted in an instant, using nothing but a thought. Wouldn't that be something if he could do the same? Instant teleportation anywhere in Earth's system and beyond would be an improvement, to be sure.

Appearing next to Dimitrios, Luke saw that the weave wasn't damaged at all, which was strange. No void creatures waited outside the weave either.

"What's going on?" Luke projected.

Dimitrios glanced over to him. "It's very strange to talk with you when you are like this. Like a ghost." He pointed. "Look."

At first, Luke didn't see. When he did, a chill ran through him. The outline was faint and so large that it hadn't registered in his mind as a single entity.

"I know this one," Luke said.

The void being. It was the same one that had harbored such a keen interest in Luke ever since he first glimpsed the weave and what lay beyond. The same one that had communicated with The Fallen Shepherd.

"A friend?" Dimitrios asked, faint hope in his voice.

"I wouldn't say that exactly."

"Do we fight it?"

Luke sent a mental shake of his head. "Not unless it tries anything. Doubt we could do much."

The void being - hadn't Luke heard its name at some point? - approached, leaning forward and moving so its tentacled, Lovecraftian face came into focus. Its gaze bored into Luke, and for a moment, it almost seemed about to speak. That moment passed, and the being withdrew, disappearing into the void.

"Holy shit," Dimitrios said, letting go of a breath he had been holding. "I would not bet on us in that fight."

"Haven't seen that one in a while," Luke said, wondering why it had made another appearance. Could it be that Relian was around somehow? There was no way that cockroach had gone down with the Singer system, which meant he had either made his way into The Greater System somehow or was on Earth. Luke hated always having that lingering sense of a job not completed. Relian needed dealing with, but he didn't have a clue how, not yet. As strong as Luke had become, he was still far from being able to reach into the weave, find whatever he wanted, and then crush it. He wasn't godlike. Not like Guide. Perhaps that was the answer. Guide.

"I've got to go," Luke said.

"Thank you again for healing my brother," Dimitrios said. "Until next time."

Returning to himself in the physical realm, Luke put away the small mountain of clothes and leaned back against the bench. He drew in a deep breath and closed his eyes.

"Guide!"

Nothing.

"Hey, Guide!"

Nothing.

"I know you can hear me!"

Nothing.

Luke: "Guide?"

No such Integrated.

"Guide!" he tried again, exasperation growing. Demanding access to the being in charge of administering Earth's system and everyone in it was perhaps a little presumptuous, but Luke didn't care. "Guide, you damn asshole! Answer me!"

"Yes?"

Luke opened his eyes and found himself back in Guide's office.

"Oh, hi," Luke said, clearing his throat. "Was starting to think that wouldn't work."

Guide raised an eyebrow, a monumental facial expression for him. "During my stay here, I have learned that young humans do tend to shout until they get what they want."

"Are you calling me a child?"

"Maybe."

Luke nodded and leaned forward, putting his elbows on the desk separating the two of them. "You're starting to show some personality. Is that allowed?"

"Maybe."

"Maybe," Luke mimicked, pitching his voice to mock the all-powerful entity. Only after did he realize he had just proved Guide right about the whole immature thing. "So, about The Fallen Shepherd."

Guide narrowed his eyes.

"He's alive, isn't he?"

"What makes you say that?"

"Just answer the question," Luke grunted.

"No."

"No, as in he's dead, or no, as in you won't answer the question?"

"Yes."

It was Luke's turn to narrow his eyes. "You're a bit of an asshole, aren't you?"

"I believe you have questions for me?" Guide asked. "Believe it or not, but I do not have much time to spend in conversation."

"You won't answer questions about Relian. Why not?"

"He is outside the parameters regarding what information I am allowed to share."

"Cool," Luke said with a sigh.

"What I can share is that the one you call Relian is in no position to harm you or anyone on Earth at this moment."

"So he's not here?"

Guide didn't answer.

"Well, I guess I'll take your silence as an answer," Luke continued. "Tell me about that void being. What does it want? Why is it so much bigger than the ones we've been fighting?"

Guide seemed happy enough about the subject change. "Those who dwell in the void are many and varied. Once they grow enough to leave the initial stage, the creatures begin developing personalities and motivations. That can change their form in various ways. The one you have referred to is old, very old, and one of the few who are able to resist our efforts."

"Your efforts?"

"To siphon its power and make it our own."

Luke nodded. "And turn it into experience."

"Correct."

"What does this one want?"

"It seems to have taken an interest in Earth, and in you in particular."

"Why?"

Guide shook his head. "Who can tell?"

"You?"

"I cannot."

"Does every system have one of... you?"

Guide considered Luke for a moment. "Why do you ask?"

"I'm curious about what you are and how these things function."

Nodding, as if having weighed Luke's answer and come away pleased, Guide explained. "All systems must have someone guiding them. It is vital. Without my kind, as you put it, Integration would mean pure chaos. That, in turn, would mean handing over that system to the void, since such a young system would not be able to protect itself adequately."

"Oh."

"However, there are many seed worlds out there ready for integration. Unfortunately, we do not have more of... my kind available."

"That sounds strange," Luke said, frowning. "Wouldn't integrating new worlds be at the top of the list? More people to fight the void and whatnot?"

"There is a cost as well. Experience, power. It takes a while before a system can join the fold and become part of The Greater System. Your system has evolved at an exceptional speed. That is not always the case. More is better, yes, but you need guides, and there are few who can accept the requirements of the task."

"Sounds like you need to unionize and demand better pay," Luke said, mulling over the answers.

Guide looked faintly amused. "Our time is running out. I will soon have to take my leave for other matters." A second later, his eyes widened in shock, which shook Luke to his core. What could have happened for such a reaction? Rather than explain, Guide shook his head in disbelief. "I must go now."

"Wait!" Luke shouted, but it was too late.

Darkness took him.
