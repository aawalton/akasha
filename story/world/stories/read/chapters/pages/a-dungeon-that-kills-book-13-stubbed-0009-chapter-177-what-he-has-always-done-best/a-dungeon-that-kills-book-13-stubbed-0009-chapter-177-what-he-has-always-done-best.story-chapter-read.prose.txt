Jeanne’s sobs ebbed little by little, the storm of emotion gradually exhausting itself until the only sound left in the room was the occasional hitch in her breathing. Then, she slowly leaned back from Viktor, brushing the back of her sleeves against her red-rimmed eyes.

“I’m... sorry...” she murmured. “You’re the one who has been hurt the most from all this, but instead of helping, I only made things harder for you.”

“It’s fine,” Viktor said. And, to be honest, it really was. After everything he had experienced in both his lives, dealing with a crying woman for a few minutes barely qualified as an inconvenience. “It’s late already. You should rest. Whatever else there is can wait until tomorrow.”

He turned toward the door, but before he could even take a full step, Jeanne’s voice stopped him.

“You said you wanted to talk, didn’t you?”

He glanced over his shoulder. “Yes, but it doesn’t have to be now. There’s no need to rush.”

“I’m alright now,” Jeanne said, tapping softly against the mattress. “Just sit here.”

With a shrug, he sat down beside her on the narrow bed.

“Well, it’s about what happened today. I want to hear your account again. Tell me everything.”

Jeanne drew in a slow breath.

“This afternoon... I was at the Guild. Like... usual. Chatted with Rhea, chatted with... Claire, too. One thing led to another, and in the end, we decided to have dinner together at Rhea’s house tonight. Since she was buried in work while Claire had more free time, we agreed that Claire and I would head there first and get the cooking started. Rhea would fetch you later after she finished at the Guild. And that’s why the two of us ended up on the west side today.”

Viktor didn’t particularly care about those details, so he just nodded along, prompting her to continue.

“It’s been a while since I last visited this area. You know, I’ve been spending most of my time at Alycia’s shop these days. And with those men taking over the west side, I didn’t exactly feel inclined to come here anymore. Still, both Rhea and Alycia told me it wasn’t as bad as I thought, so Claire and I just went without really thinking too much about it, completely unaware of what was waiting for us once we arrived.”

“That man,” Viktor said. “He just came out of nowhere and stopped you on the street?”

“Yes,” Jeanne said with a nod. “And he was the same large man with the sideburns we saw when we went to the Mayor’s Office. Hold on... I once heard he was a Mithril... Did I get that correct?”

“He is. Name’s Xander.”

Jeanne’s brows drew together at once. “Then why? Why would a Mithril-ranked adventurer do something like that? I thought people of that standing were supposed to behave according to their status, instead of, I don’t know, acting like some common thugs?”

“There are assholes at every rank and station.” Viktor shrugged. “In fact, people in high positions abusing their authority is hardly unusual.”

She let out a long, tired sigh at that.

“What happened next?”

“He... demanded me to go with him. I refused. We resisted. And then he—”

Her words caught in her throat. Her face tightened as the tears she had managed to hold back threatened to spill once more.

“What happened afterward? I mean, how did you manage to escape, taking Claire with you? I doubt he just let you go.”

“I... don’t know. He... threw her down. She was on the ground and she was not moving. Then, something in me just snapped. I couldn’t think straight anymore. I glared at him, I yelled at him, and... my mind went blank. It’s like... there’s a gap in my memory. When I came back to myself, he was already gone.”

Huh?

That was the explanation?

She got so mad she somehow scared the bastard into running away?

How did that even work?

Viktor kept his gaze fixed on the red-haired woman, scrutinizing every detail of her expression, every twitch across her face, searching for even the slightest sign that she was hiding something or altering the truth in some way.

And he found nothing that suggested deception.

If anything, she looked genuinely confused by her own story.

Well, it didn’t matter whether she was lying or not. He could tell this was all he could get from her, and pressing her further would likely accomplish nothing. Maybe he would need to seek out other witnesses, or perhaps, extract the answer directly from the bastard himself.

“How is... Claire?” Jeanne asked.

“She’s still the same. There’s no way to know when she will wake up.”

“I...” Jeanne said, lowering her gaze again.

“As I said, it’s not your fault. If anything, I should thank you. You’ve gotten her here safely. If not for you, Claire would’ve been left lying out there on the street, and things could’ve turned out far worse than this.”

“You’re very kind,” Jeanne murmured. “But even if no one blames me, I still can’t stop blaming myself. Ever since it happened, I’ve been thinking about all the things I could’ve done differently to prevent it in the first place. What if I hadn’t suggested we have dinner together today? What if I hadn’t asked Claire to come with me? What if I simply hadn’t visited the Guild at all?”

Oh, that. That was a feeling Viktor knew all too well.

Because he had been there once himself.

For a good long while, his own mind had been tormented by the exact same kind of useless questions. What would have happened if he had brought Leo with him to Marcus’s house that day? What if he had run into the burning house to save him instead of standing there watching while the flames consumed everything? What if he had never told the boy to hide in the cellar to begin with?

But, eventually, he stopped. Because he had grown up, and he had learned how the world worked.

In the end, whose fault it had been was utterly irrelevant.

What was done was done. The dead stayed dead. The broken remained broken.

There was no way to change the past, so what was the point of dwelling on it? You kept moving forward, without ever looking behind. That was all there was.

“Other than being mad at myself,” Jeanne said. “I’m also angry at those people. So angry that horrible thoughts just keep looping inside my head, over and over again, like they have nowhere else to go. They tell me to let go of everything, to just... unleash it all on them, to make them pay for what they have done.”

He already knew that she wielded a destructive power that was beyond comprehension. He had seen it back in the dungeon, and even that was merely a fraction of it. Should she ever lose control and release everything at once, it would turn into an unstoppable catastrophe. If she directed her inferno at Zubayr’s headquarters, for example, there wouldn’t even be ash left behind. But of course, the collateral damage would be equally staggering.

And, more importantly...

“Are you capable of killing someone?” Viktor asked. “Even if they were truly scum deserving of your wrath?”

“I’m... not sure. You may not know this, but there’s something inside me, something... terrifying. I’m always afraid of it, and I have to keep it locked down all the time, so that I don’t hurt anyone by accident. But today, I actually thought about using it on someone. For a moment, I really did. And now, I’m scared of myself for even thinking that.”

“It’s normal to have such thoughts. As long as you don’t let them take control of you and push you into doing something you would later regret.”

Jeanne went quiet. Then, slowly, she nodded. “You’re right. Hating someone is normal. But I should not cross that line. Because murder is wrong...”

No, Viktor thought, what I mean is you must never kill someone on impulse. You plan it properly. You execute it clean. You leave no loose ends. There’s a proper way to do things, so don’t make a mess that could come back to bite you later. Nevertheless, I don’t think you’re cut out for this kind of job, so you should leave this whole murdering business to professionals. For example, the guy who is sitting in front of you right now.

But of course, he was not going to say that to her face.

“You’re being consumed by those thoughts because you’re sitting here alone with them,” he said instead. “How about, I don’t know, you focus your attention elsewhere? Give yourself some goal to aim at.”

“Like what?”

“If you feel guilty about what happened to Claire, then you can try making it up to her. Like, look after her. She’ll probably be in that condition for an unknown amount of time, and there will be many things she can’t do herself. Someone else has to handle them for her.”

“Right.” Jeanne nodded. “Claire matters more than anything else right now. It’s not the time to dwell on such pointless thoughts. We have to help her. We have to save her.”

Actually, there is no “we” here.

“I won’t be able to spend much time here for the time being, so I’ll have to leave everything to you,” Viktor said.

Jeanne blinked. “Huh? Why?”

He gave no answer.

After a beat of silence, she exhaled and nodded once more. “Alright, I don’t know what you’re planning, but leave it to me. I... Rhea and Alycia too... we will take care of her. Just...” She hesitated. “Just don’t do anything dangerous. You said it yourself. Murder is wrong, right?”

I didn’t say that. Don’t put words in my mouth.

“I know what I am doing,” Viktor said. Then he stood up and walked toward the exit.

It’s better this way, he thought as the door closed behind him.

Claire should be taken care of by people who actually cared about her. He didn’t, so he shouldn’t be here.

Instead, he should return to what he had always done best.
