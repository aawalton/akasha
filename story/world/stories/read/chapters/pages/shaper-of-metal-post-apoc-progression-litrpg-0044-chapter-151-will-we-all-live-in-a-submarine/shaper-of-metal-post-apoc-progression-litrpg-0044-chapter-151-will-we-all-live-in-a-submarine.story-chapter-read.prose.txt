Chapter 151: Will We All Live in a Submarine?

After the ‘fun’ battle with HRVR’s super-tough mutasaur, they pivoted to undersea operations, beginning with drills on what to do when there was an issue aboard a submarine, which, thankfully, were more technical and sufficed as a palate cleanser for the all-out fighting they’d been engaging in.

All in all, drills about the process after conflict occurred were fairly bewildering, and the newbies were told to be ready and obey orders from those who did know what they were doing. Fortunately, the actual submarine crew would be battle-seasoned veterans, and on the ANP side, they had numerous familiar vets, including their leader himself, Solvent.

The distances involved with first engagements remained mostly beyond Jack, though his range did improve while under the buffs. He was unsure what his exact role would be, possibly defensive-oriented, reinforcing the sub. But if there was a giant squid-thing grabbing the vessel, well, then maybe he’d go to town with giant blades?

Maybe we're about to find out...

The submarine was absolutely stacked with imaging systems feeds, so if something was close-range and warranted going all-out, he’d have eyes. He also got used to ‘anchoring’ his sensory field around a moving vessel, which had a feel vaguely like magnetizing to it. But he was pleased that he could do this at any speed — at least, according to the HRVR sim. Even if he generated memorite fresh while they were moving, it didn’t suddenly zip and fall behind him due to the speed he was going. It worked relative to his speed.

Nonetheless, it was still a bit harder to get a sense of bearing ‘out there’ while in a relative abyss. This was especially true in a ‘cold’ scenario without apparent enemies, as the rule was to be as quiet as possible, and certainly not to be blasting active sonar if they could avoid it. Passive sonar wasn’t terribly useful for most ANPs to operate through.

They’d rely on a plentiful surrounding escort of underwater piloted drones for long-range information and warning. As a last line of defense before the sub was threatened, Crash would be out there in the water at all times, ready to respond, though by default, he’d be going as ‘cold’ as possible, like they were. Power use was unwise if not needed, which sucked because Crash could make the sub go faster, but that was at least a good tactic to have in the back pocket. Granted, they were kind of stuck with some power use at all times simply due to the necessity to distribute Jack’s Anchors to the separate teams. This long-range link was seen as both a necessity and a liability due to increased risks of drawing attention.

In any case, none of Team Escort were considered passengers in this scenario. In the sub, they were all given seats and free movement in the albeit cramped command center, while being told to keep certain well-marked ‘routes’ clear for sudden crew movements. As such, most of them were standing in different crannies, observing monitors and listening to the chatter of the simulated crew.

Action finally came from the charge of a large number of sea monsters coming for the sub, as picked up from the outer drones. Imagery was captured as the drones attempted to lure the group away, which unfortunately didn’t work.

They were something maybe in the size range of dolphins, but built like long arrows, zipping through the water at unnatural speeds with swaying motions, and a nose point that could probably spear right through weaker prey. Some sense of a network of folded ligaments could be discerned as well.

A drone deliberately antagonized one of them, and that caused it to move even faster, emitting piercing cries as it darted in like a blur and ripped apart the drone, ending the viewpoint. The captured images had to be slowed down to show the creature’s attack, which revealed a sudden bloom of unfurled, thin blades that it spun end over end with, in a veritable storm, at the end of its charge. Along the edges, there was a slick gleam, and these blades cut through the metal with ease.

Holy unholy shit!

“What in a fresh hell are they?” Lighthouse asked.

“We know,” Solvent said with his arms crossed, as he shook his head. “But this is a simulation, after all, and we want to learn what we can, so we’ll save that for after. We’ll try to pretend we know nothing. Crash will not be able to contain them all, so expect the worst case to occur here. Also, let’s stick to comms from here on.”

“We’ve got multiple readings from several headings!” a crew member called.

“Hit them with everything we got! Off the bow priority!” the captain called. He popped onto a sub-wide intercom and began barking into it. “Enemy engagement at hand. All crew to stations.”

When Jack saw Solvent taking a seat while also putting on his helmet, Jack followed suit. Everyone else did the same.

Crash came in through the team gestalt. “I’ll dive toward one group and deal what damage I can, then hurry back to mitigate whatever gets to the sub.”

“I’ll try to hit a leader, I suppose,” Snapper said. “Maybe that’ll scare off groups?”

“Do it. It’s worth a shot. But you’re not a good amp target in this situation.”

“I’m starting by amping Perma and you, Crash,” Solvent declared. “I’ll pull from all logical idle members. That won’t include Snapper or Ferro. I’ll pivot to Lattice when they get too close. Be ready and willing for isolated boosts if there’s an opportunity you shouldn’t pass up.”

With active imaging going into overdrive, they got significantly more information feeds from monitors. Due to the fact they were simulating being ‘out there,’ they couldn’t get those feeds in their head, so they had to make do, but at least mental gestalt comms were still possible thanks to Jack’s specialty.

A super-buffed Crash hit the first group before it got to the sub, sending a slurry of sudden waves that sent the group tumbling into the abyss. Simple and efficient work, essentially, but meanwhile, multiple other groups were coming at them.

The submarine crew sent explosive torpedoes forward in one volley — about all that would be safe for an area they were approaching — and detonated them in the midst of a large group of the charging schools of beasts, clearing the way so they kept up their speed instead of needing to change headings. The whole world shook slightly from the distant detonations.

He noticed Délight’s buff go off, causing that weird strobe effect to begin pulsing within the submarine. It took on a very even, predictable beat that everyone could time themselves around, gaining perceptual speed benefits in momentary windows as the pulse built up and went out.

Sweet. I love being on the other side of this now.

Spiral did something too, but it required no timing and was practically unnoticeable, especially after everyone had more or less gotten used to it in training together. They were just all marginally faster, basically.

The immediate situation was difficult for Jack to get his ‘paws’ into due to the initial range of the engagement. Experimenting, he slapped an Anchor on a zooming torpedo and tried to scatter more fragments when it detonated, but it was so far and out of his range, his senses were all but blind. He might’ve helped; he wasn’t sure.

Solvent, multitasking a dozen things at once, also sent to Jack, “Do what you can on starboard.”

Huh?

Right on the heels of this, a voice cracked into his natural comms through the helmet. “Sir. Drone pilot here, under your command. Got metal for you. Starboard side fifty meters, matched speed and heading. Feed B7. Incoming; we’re looking right at them. Fired torpedoes, but a fish broke off and kamikazed them. Didn’t do much. Got two kinetic, fast-fire torpedoes reserved for you.”

Interesting behavior… I look forward to knowing more about these bastards.

Jack flickered his eyes around for Feed B7. As it would figure, his seat was not at an angle to see that feed, so he began getting out of his seat to find a new one. “One second, soldier.”

Hope this shit is straighter in reality, yeesh.

As he was moving, the soldier continued, “Yessir. They’ll be intercepting us in an estimated… thirty seconds.”

Muttering a curse under his breath, Jack found a seat in proper front of the sensor feed, which showed a continually adjusting zoom on a pack of an unknown number of the creatures zipping toward the viewpoint. They were keeping a generally diagonal bent to stay locked on their flank, slightly from behind, referentially. “Alright, I see them, soldier. How long does the firing sequence need?”

“A second after you call it. T1 and T2, either, or both will fire. They get going quick.”

“Got it. Keep your position locked referentially: that’s the best bet for me to do my thing. Standby.”

“Roger that, sir.”

Jack reached out with memorite until he found the pilot’s drone, which didn’t take him long. He found it had a small but nice variety of goodies that were no doubt power-intensive to move at those speeds: a large steel ball, three flooded compartments with different-sized balls en masse, one compartment of spear-like spikes, and the two kinetic torpedoes the pilot mentioned.

Scrambling under the time constraints despite slight benefits from the global buffs, Jack interlaced all of his memorite into one torpedo’s inner substance and fragmented it, forming a network of as many sharp darts as he could manage.

As he worked, a bit of chatter was in the team gestalt, which he paid only half a mind to, just to note what he could of the situation.

“I stopped a leader,” Snapper reported, already with a tone of disappointment. “It didn’t stop the group even slightly. I just threw a pebble in the ocean, picking one of a horde off, basically.”

“Figures,” Crash replied. “Might’ve learned something from this, eh, boss killer?”

“I assume I’d better.”

“At least you did something,” Highfive said sullenly. “I’m totally useless right now.”

“Oh, shut up, you big baby,” Lighthouse retorted. “Everyone is contributing, even if just by getting debuffed to boost others.”

Once he had enough of something to work with, he called, “Fire, soldier! Uh, T1!”

“Roger. Torpedo away!” A moment later, the torpedo could be seen zooming away, making for the group, leaving a glowing trail from its propulsion. With his eyes glued to the feed and his senses continually straining to maintain a bead on the torpedo, Jack watched and waited.

The deadly entities made the sort of move he expected from the pilot’s report — one of them shot ahead in front, and the rest began to separate with the intent to go around.

Rather than wait for that result, Jack preliminarily ‘burst’ the metal shell around the torpedo, unleashing the darts as it reached the limits of his range, and they rapidly slowed from the drag. He distributed the darts and locked them into a referential position with the path of the sub, basically stuck right into the intervening space of their flank.

If the beasties wanted to come for it, they’d have to go through that field of control.

“Ohhhh,” exclaimed the pilot in his comms. “Nice one, sir.”

“Thanks,” Jack replied, though he frantically continued working as the actual torpedo kept going, albeit slowed by the increased drag of being gutted. Jack was able to pick a little more material on the straaaaaaining far edge of his range before he simply lost the ability to trace where it was. At that point, he let it go, figuring the entities would waste at least a little time on it regardless.

Once he’d locked a bunch of controllable darts in place on the far edge, he said, “Fire T2!”

He didn’t wait long at all before acting this time — instead of letting the torpedo continue far, he shredded it completely, pulling the metal away and making haphazard shards in a chaotic assortment. He knew this was about all he could do before the creatures reached the edge of his ‘dart zone’ and he’d need to begin actually attacking. Meanwhile, the remaining propulsion system of the torpedo had nothing to push and thus balance its movement, so it went spinning randomly into basically nowhere.

With the oceanic beasties fast approaching and not adjusting their route to the flank, Jack spent what time he could making an irregular, secondary ‘shrapnel zone’ immediately behind the dart zone. It wasn’t great, but it was something, and it gave him metal to manipulate in an area that was his ideal range. With the great speeds the entities were moving, he’d need every advantage to act quickly enough to interrupt their charge.

Once they were passing directly through the edge of his covered zone, Jack took hold of a small cluster of darts and activated Throw, launching them at the head of the pack.

Reactions were mixed — some dodged with ease and kept on going, some slowed down to ‘attack’ the darts in a swirling parry of sorts, and some were pelted. With the range difficulties and the lower quality of material at that far edge, they all bounced off of the tough hide of the beasts. On the plus side, several beasties — including two that took shots angled from below — twitched in annoyance and unfurled themselves in reaction to a threat, and this slowed them down enough that they fell behind in the charge of the pack.

But there were many more in the pack.

In the gestalt, Jack crammed in words as best he could and said, “In the next few ticks, if I get a dose of boost, might stop this flank.” Fortunately, thanks to it being at the higher speeds of communication, it didn’t take much time at all.

“Roger,” was Solvent’s harried yet even reply. “We’ll see in a sec.”

Steeling himself for whatever was to come, Jack prepared to hit the beasties again as they moved closer into his range. Though at the farther reach of it, he also had the best darts there.

He began launching them at the charging creatures in small pockets, one after the other. On the plus side, his focus suddenly drastically improved, as Délight’s buff triggered at an advantageous time.

With improved perceptual speed and acute aiming possible, Jack went for what he perceived was a vulnerable, or at least sensitive spot for these things: the underbelly.

This time, several of the darts seemed to find purchase and lodge into the body of the beasts, causing a heap of chaos in their ranks, and they thrashed about. Jack didn’t have time to focus on any one wound to attempt to go for more extensive damage; instead, he ‘gripped’ all of the darts in play and jerked them one way and then the other, hoping for multiple aggravated wounds at once.

It was hard to tell, but he thought he saw blood in the water, and at the very least, he’d just shaved off a sixth or a fifth of the numbers from maintaining the charge.

The submarine rocked again from an unseen issue.

“Ooh!” Highfive called in the gestalt. “Nice one, Perma.”

Damn. No time to look, even if I could see the feed.

One last launch of darts was possible, and he focused hard on firing from underneath in general. This time, off the buff period, his speed and control were not as great.

The creatures also pulled something adaptive: they flipped backwards, presenting their tougher top hide to the angle of attack.

I don’t like that at all.

His attacks were negligible this time, slowing down only a few, as the greater mass moved past the dart range into his shrapnel zone.

Just as he was about to set up some chaotic ‘from every angle’ sort of attack to avoid their new adjustment strategies — which would unfortunately steal away from his potency a great deal — he felt a sudden surge of power in his fiber and bones, as all of his branching control mechanisms ballooned with growth and reinforcement. He identified it as the ‘mid-tier’ boost, coming just from Solvent rather than Solvent+Conflux.

“Go for it, Ferro — quickly!” came Solvent’s command.

With the sudden massive increase of raw effective Allotment, and thus Create, Jack used at least half for sheer breadth and division of his control, to grab a large radius of both shrapnel and darts, the latter of which were now behind the charging creatures. It didn’t matter when he was boosted, though: he could launch those quite hydrodynamic darts far faster than they moved, he was sure.

Amping himself up to 105% output, he made the radial mass of metal surrounding the beasties collapse on them. Immediately, it sent waves propagating outward under the great pressures of the deep ocean simulated there.

In the chaos of angles and the unexpected speed increase they faced, the creatures didn’t react well. Some presented their backs correctly from their position, as flanked by others of their number, and others seemed to make the wrong call. But it hardly mattered. The shrapnel sliced gouges even in their tough hides, and if it found their softer tissues, it burst right through them. Meanwhile, the darts coming from behind totally blindsided them and ripped right into their innards, decimating those in the trailing position of the group.

Half of their numbers were out of pursuit in one fell swoop… and then Délight’s buff dropped again. Jack took advantage, grabbing loose metal already in the vicinity and firing it again, ripping into more of them.

Something between ten and twenty percent of their numbers was what was left as they neared the sub. He couldn’t quite get them all, sadly.

As he was grabbing for the material onboard the drone right before the creatures would rip into it, he felt the buff fading rapidly.

“Gotta pivot,” Solvent said, “but good work.”

A shaking in the sub was all he felt, though in the gestalt, Lattice reported, “Crystal up.”

Jack continued with what he was doing even as the Allotment boost slipped from the ‘fingers’ of his powers. He shot the spears out of their compartment with the last of the extra strength available, getting a semi-boosted Throw effect in.

The spears got mostly dodged by minute movements, but he scored multiple nasty penetrations of their hide, as well as slowing down others.

Nonetheless, in the next instant, those creatures barreled and spun themselves into the drone, ripping it apart and ending the feed.

In the comms of his helmet, the pilot said, “Switching your feed to the sub, sir.”

In credit to the soldier’s simulated prowess, the switch was almost seamless as he watched a portion of the charging creatures rip apart the drone and fall away in mutual drag increases, while the remainders left continued.

Jack noticed the feed was slightly distorted due to zooming through Lattice’s thick crystal barrier surrounding the sub.

I guess we’ll end up seeing how well this’ll take their charge, after all…
