Alex, in a white room…

This wasn’t the first time I’d found myself in one of these rooms. At least this time I was seated at the table, and wasn’t staring at myself across the way. No one else sat in the space. Just before I had arrived, I’d felt something.

Like a cold breeze across my toes, when there shouldn’t be.

Recognition of something.

The cube remained stuck to my hand, weighing almost nothing, but I couldn’t let go. It was frustrating.

[Can you describe what happened to you after you left known space?]

The voice attached to the notification inside my head felt soft, like they were being as careful as possible.

“Afterward, can you explain what happened?” I asked.

[Potentially.]

Noseen telling me to be smart echoed in my head. Then again, he hadn’t seen that thing.

I sighed, then started to talk, first about how I found myself in outer space outside of what I was sure had to be Earth. Then I described heading to the base, and how it seemed Forgers had taken parts and technology from the remains on the moon.

I paused before I continued, then spoke about the lab, then about the entity that I had, ultimately, devoured.

“They uploaded their memories into it. So many people, once it was clear how much was to be lost,” I added.

Then I explained the fight, and devouring the thing, this ultimate artifact of the last of my people. Then I held up my hand that still held the small cube. “There is this as well. I cannot get it off, and I don’t know what it is.”

[It is blocking the memories you have absorbed from the information focus. It is beyond your ability to host the memories of so many.]

My fingers tingled. “You mean it might make my head explode.”

[Yes.]

I clutched the cube then, like it was the most important thing in the world, as my heart rate sped up.

[An alternative is to give the memories to the System.]

“Why do you want them?” I asked, thinking of the last of humanity resting inside my head. Though, I wasn’t sure how much of it remained, really. How much of it was human, if any of it was.

[You shouldn’t be destroyed for helping.]

“I mean, not dying would be great.” I didn’t know what to do. The cube was attached to my hand so my head didn’t pop. But did I really want to have all of those memories in my head?

Not a freaking chance.

“So, you don’t want them?” I tried again.

[I care less than you do.]

An idea came to mind, so quickly that I wondered if it really came from me. “What about if I trade them to the System, in exchange for the locations of the other colony ships?”

[What would you do with that information?]

“Bring them to Lakeside,” the answer rolled out without me thinking about it. Each ship had thousands of colonists. There were scientists, engineers, musicians, artists, the best our planet had to offer, sent off in the hope that our people might survive. If I could bring them into the System, it would be another chance for those who had reached out to the stars. “I am the First.”

No response.

[It will take you several years to bring the closest ship to your city.]

“That’s better than them crashing somewhere and not having anyone to guide them.” All of us who survived knew what that was like. They didn’t deserve that. Plus, at Lakeside they had a path to settle down, or to grow. “It will give us time to prepare for them.”

[You will need to travel with the ship.]

My heart clenched at the thought of needing to travel through space yet again. This time I wouldn’t be asleep. And it would be a long time. I closed my eyes, thinking of my family. It wasn’t like I could stay with them anyway. The gap between us was too large. I wasn’t even sure I could spend more than a few hours at Lakeside, at this point.

“What about the rest of the ships?” I asked, almost not wanting the answer.

[There is only one remaining.]

The notification hurt. It shouldn’t at this point, but it did. Out of the ten ships that had managed to launch, only two had survived. Twenty percent. Still, it was something. There were people just floating through space, asleep, and I could help.

Hopefully the others didn’t feel any pain when it had ended for them. Or fear.

These people had no one right now.

I could do this.

I closed my eyes for a moment and thought about everyone. My Dad, my brothers, and Abby, then Lenna, and Strange. They’d need to forgive me.

“I’ll trade them to the System, then,” I said, opening my eyes and looking down at the cube.

[Good luck.]

The room rippled, and the cube started to glow. A heavy presence pushed down on me, and everything inside me screamed. Then, it suddenly stopped, like someone put up a wall. Someone pitch black stood between me and the bright light attached to my hand.

“Control yourself,” growled the First.

The bright light dimmed a little. Then the cube vanished, along with the bright light.

“Are you okay?” he asked.

I flexed my fingers, then nodded, unable to speak.

They gave me a sharp nod, then I found myself standing next to Noseen and the First, still in the white room.

“It is done,” he said to Noseen. “She has a long adventure in front of her, made shorter by her return.”

Noseen buzzed lightly and pressed his lips together. “Thank you for protecting her.”

“She is young,” he said, then turned and disappeared.

“I need to return you to where I found you,” said Noseen, clearly unhappy. “Near the edge of the System.”

“I’ll be fine, Noseen,” I said, trying to not sound worried. I had some concerns, but fewer than I thought I would. I could do this.

“The edges require you to be smart and strong,” he added, as his form shifted and he vanished from sight.

“Are you saying I’m not smart and strong?” I asked, then continued. “I saved the First, own a planet, and now I am going to go save thousands of humans floating lost through space.”

“I don’t care about those humans,” he said with a grumble.

“You can talk to me in my dreams. I’ll keep you updated,” I added, trying to get him to see I’d be fine. At least with my Dad, he didn’t get a choice.

“The bond is gone,” he finally said in a small voice. “Burned away.”

The edges of the room darkened.

I shrugged. “Add it back if you want, I don’t think it will be a problem.”

Before he could respond, we were somewhere else.

“I cannot stay here,” he buzzed. “Be smart, and strong.”

Then his presence also vanished.

My wings flared out behind me, and I steadied myself in the darkness of space.

[You have accepted a quest: Find the Colony ship and escort it to Lakeside Landing.]

A star appeared on my map, and a glittery path appeared in the darkness. The sun burned in the far distance, and where Earth once stood, along with the moon, now a new planet, lush with green life and bright blue bodies of water, orbited peacefully. Two smaller moons circled the orb.

With a smile, I turned and followed the path.

Lenna, at Lakeside Landing…

“She really did it,” said Lenna, in a hushed voice.

“What did she do now?” asked Abby, as she stirred the soup. The heavy scent of herbs filled the space, and soon a party would be in full swing.

Everyone wanted to celebrate the return of the team, plus several newcomers, like Kabi’s parents.

I smiled, reading the notification again.

[Do you want to be the leader of the planet Gassamer? Alex, the First, has offered you this position.]

“She owns an entire planet,” she said, in a soft whisper.

Abby paused and looked at Lenna. “She did what?”

“That planet where we saved Strange, she owns it.” Somehow, she really had done it, even given how young and, well, low leveled she was. Then again, there wasn’t much left on the world, besides those few who had rejected the clan, and a few high leveled, uncivilized beasts.

“Better you than me,” said Abby, in a low voice.

Something in her voice caught my attention. One of my skills hummed.

“You don’t need to stay here,” I said softly. “You can give the position to someone else. Someone Alex trusts. The system would allow it.”

Abby pressed her lips together, then shook her head, rejecting the idea. “At least this means that Alex is okay.”

“She’s fine, it’s just going to be a long time until she makes it back here,” I said with full confidence. The whispers from the stars were very clear about that. And once she did return, everything would change for Lakeside. “Lakeside will radically change on her return.”

“That’s why I need to be here,” she said in a quiet voice. “These people need me.”

“Do they, really?” I asked, pressing. “Give me your hands.”

Abby held out her hands, and I took hold of them, then triggered my favorite skill. One I’d gotten very recently.

Flashes of different paths in front of her appeared between our hands. Each was a burst of silver light. The only downside to this was that I couldn’t see what she did. The reading of the paths was for her, and only her.

Abby’s eyes widened, and I knew multiple images flashed in front of her, even though I couldn’t see them clearly. She leaned closer, and one even paused. Her face paled, and she yanked her hands away.

“What was that?” her voice trembled.

“Potential paths you could take.” I couldn’t do that very often, but Abby deserved the chance to see what she could become. I just wished I could use it on myself. Though, if I became the leader of Gassamer, I could help all those who needed to find a different path. Those who were left.

I could even see if I could connect a portal to Lakeside.

Ideas and potentials swirled inside my head, and I opened the notification and accepted. It was too good of an opportunity to pass up. Now, I needed to build a team around me.

And I already had some ideas of who to recruit first.
