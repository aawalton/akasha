The final days of preparation passed slowly for Ethan. He’d already done all he could for the Nator, and with mixed results. He still had a hard time accepting that they were all so ready to march to war, but he understood that it wasn’t his decision to make.

Bishops had no right to tell anyone to avoid sacrifice. In his family, dying of natural causes was practically taboo.

Still, he found that just being around the people of Bastion as they prepared for battle was like poison for his mood. Every one of them appeared entirely convinced of their own doom, and seemed almost to hope for it. Ethan had a hard enough time keeping his own sliver of hope alive, without being surrounded by the last remnants of a people, to whom hope had all but ceased to exist.

Thankfully, Allyara had agreed to help him pass the time, while Fable, Steelbinder, and Apollo prepared the train. Integrating Steelbinder’s technology was relatively easy, given she was the origin of the Fable Cube, but the adjustments made to integrate Apollo and Ethan’s spirit complicated matters. They would be ready soon, but the hours couldn’t tick by quickly enough for Ethan.

Try as he might, searching for Twilight’s Gift with Allyara wasn’t proving as distracting as he’d hoped.

“You keep losing concentration,” the Champion said flatly. She said most things flatly these days. Her own endless quest to keep Potentia from boiling over had burnt away much of her charms, and she reminded Ethan of family members he’d seen after too many tours of duty.

She needed peace, normalcy, and time, and this world offered none of those things.

“I’m trying,” Ethan insisted. “There’s a lot on my mind.”

“This cannot be rushed,” Allyara said. “If you’re not in the right state for it…”

“I’ll try harder,” he insisted. Inwardly, he knew he could make no promises.

There was just too much to worry about. He’d already been given the near-impossible task of tracking down Dusk’s power, made worse by the complication of the Followers. But he hadn’t been prepared for Calevaro.

Ethan had been trying not to think of that unexpected piece of madness, that his friend might somehow be here, and enslaved by the Emperor.

The discovery came at a time when he was so exhausted and overwhelmed that he’d almost managed to put it aside. From losing the Basilisk, to his rescue by Allyara, and finally everything Bastion represented, the life of one more friend on the line was almost something he could forget.

But Calevaro deserved more.

Ethan had spoken with Selina about it, though there wasn’t much to say. The prince had been captured somehow, and didn’t appear to have been transformed, despite being encased in a Follower Familiar. But they knew little else. Certainly not enough to come up with a rescue plan.

Cara refused to even discuss the matter. She didn’t say as much, she simply left the room whenever anyone brought it up. They all knew that they didn’t have the power, or the information to just go and steal the prince back, and no one was willing to say the harder truth.

That no one, not even Prince Calevaro, could be elevated above their goal. The world–the worlds needed to come first.

And so Ethan had one more reason why he couldn’t concentrate on looking inward.

Allyara let out a frustrated breath, then began speaking again. They’d already been through the typical warnings that Ethan was rushing this, and it could take years, now she merely spoke in calming tones, hoping to open his mind and spirit.

“Twilight’s Gift is something beyond simply power, and growth. It is similar to becoming a Master, or Champion, in that it represents the process of stepping beyond the limits of our Bonds.”

Ethan made his breathing even, and looked inward, sensing his soul, and the friends contained within. Allyara’s voice seemed distant, but not beyond hearing.

“Searching for the gift is searching within oneself. It’s trying to find a unity that cannot be forced, or rushed. It is an awareness of who you are, not merely as a person, but as a Bonded. As someone who has become more because of the Familiars they have welcomed within themselves.”

Ethan felt the gentle acknowledgement of each of his friends as his awareness touched them. Revan growled, and volcanoes seemed to erupt in this section of Ethan’s soul that held the Guardian Beast of Flare.

Deevee split into a dozen hydras, then back again, sailing and swimming through the skies of a thousand shifting worlds.

Tomo merely gave a gentle nod, and Ethan took some joy in seeing that his own little world was clearly a version of Potentia from before the corruption. It was beautiful in its own way, though it was a stark, pointed beauty, that what might be found in a desert.

“You must look beyond yourself,” Allyara intoned. “You must find something that is more than just the sum of your parts. You must truly be one being. Your Familiars are as much a part of you as any limb. Their power is your power, their will is your will. Their–”

Ethan was miles away, feeling like he was floating in an ocean of blackness. He could see the little “worlds” of his Familiars in the distance, but they were so far apart. The distance felt overwhelming, and insurmountable. How could they be one being, when they were separated like planets in a solar system?

The stench of failure seemed to overpower his calm, and he heard Allyara let out a sigh as he came back to himself.

“I think that’s enough for today,” she said.

Ethan wanted to argue, but couldn’t bring himself to. He’d gotten no farther today than he had in the last five days, and he knew that the Champion’s own patience was far from infinite.

Yet when he only gave a nod, she sighed again, and didn’t head toward the door of the small, candlelit room. “This method might not be for you,” she said at last.

Ethan raised an eyebrow. She hadn’t said anything like this before. “What do you mean?” he asked.

She gestured around them. “Peace and tranquility, patience and time. Introspection. It is the most common method, yes, but so is careful mastery of one’s Soulscape, and their inner world. How is yours coming along?”

He felt his cheeks heat up. He’d still never managed to do more than touch his Soulscape. He understood it represented great utility, and was a key part of the path to higher ranks, but after his stumbles at Dusk rank, he’d never really managed to make it work. Allyara knew that, of course.

She smirked slightly. “For some, this is the only path, but for others…? I’ve avoided bringing this up, as telling someone there might be another way will only prevent them from embracing their current path. But give your unique challenges… Let me ask you this, Chosen, are you someone who moves slowly–incrementally, in times of peace? Or are you forged in the heat of battle?”

The answer seemed obvious. Ethan had never stopped rushing forward. He’d gone from one fight to another since the moment he arrived in Potentia–the first time. He’d pushed himself forward at a breakneck pace, always looking for the next advantage, knowing his enemies were more powerful, and always closing in.

“Battle,” he said confidently.

Allyara nodded. “Then that will likely remain true. Perhaps you’ll find your Gift someday, but I doubt it will be in a room like this.”

Abruptly, she got to her feet. “Come, let’s see if Steelbinder and her wayward daughter have killed one another yet.”

Ethan chuckled, then followed. Fable and her maker weren’t quite as contentious a pair as Allyara made it sound, but they were close.

Steelbinder seemed to think that Fable should worship her like a god, yet Fable saw her as little more than a distant connection–and one that had abandoned her, no less.

They didn’t exactly bicker, but their disagreements were frequent, and enthusiastic.

Ethan followed Allyara up the tunnels and out into the forest, eventually coming to where the Fable Train rested, enormous, yet largely hidden among the tall trees.

Its external appearance hadn’t changed much since Steelbinder began her changes, but the changes were there. For one, it looked sleeker and more like a fortress. The windows and gaps were gone, and there was almost no space between the three “train cars” if they could still be considered as such. It looked more like a bullet train from Japan than ever, yet almost unfinished, as if it were being slowly carved from a block of steel.

He knew that most of Fable’s mass had been repurposed into armor, and the inside wasn’t much larger than it had been at Dawn rank. There would just be enough space for him and his party, with Steelbinder’s coffin-like container fully integrated into the construction.

The most notable difference was Ethan’s spirit, which had been “integrated” as well. He’d needed to force the massive thing to lie down, and Fable had reformed the train around it–throne included. It had taken time and patience, but they’d promised it would be ready today–they didn’t have much time left, after all.

Noticing him, a door formed in the side of the train, and Fable rushed out. A drone floated behind her, and Ethan barely caught the end of the latest argument before they came to a stop a few paces off.

“The weapon can be integrated on the way,” Fable insisted.

“I am not confident in that!” the drone hissed.

“I am confident that I can make up for your lack of competence,” Fable said in neutral tones.

“Competence!?” the drone shrieked. “I made you, didn’t I?”

“You did not,” Fable said, then turned to Ethan. “We’re ready, Doctor Bishop. What do you think?”

Ethan smiled, trying not to look amused. He turned his gaze back to the train, and nodded in approval. “I think it looks incredible, Fable. Good enough to tame a Champion.”
