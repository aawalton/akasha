Official Statement from The Delightful

As part of our opening celebrations, The Delightful is pleased to announce that we will be hosting a charity fundraising event at Taka Memorial Square.

All proceeds collected during the event will be donated to the Holy Temple’s Soup Kitchen Initiative, with all public donations matched in full by Alice Hecate, lead investor of The Delightful.

As part of the celebration, members of the public will also have the opportunity to sample some of our newest culinary creations, including our signature dessert, Laurel’s Delight, currently available for five bronze coins per serving in our store.

The event will provide an opportunity for the people of Hano to celebrate the opening of The Delightful while also supporting an important charitable cause within our community.

We look forward to welcoming you tomorrow.

The Delightful

After much deliberation with Je’e, Ja’a, Sana, and Gray, this was the statement we eventually settled on.

Turning the event into a charity fundraiser was scummy PR behavior I had learned from my mother's acquaintances back on Earth. My mother was a philanthropist. That was the extent of her résumé. She would show up at fancy events, rub shoulders with the elite, and donate money to charity. I once asked her how she never ran out of money, and she gave me a look. That was when I became aware of the infinite money glitch where, once you have a lot of money, you simply start getting more money...

I know, I know, I am being facetious... but Earth’s economy is a quagmire...

Anyhow, I was using their methods to get myself out of this mess. Because do you know where you couldn't really be upset at people?

At a charity event.

Even if the chocolate fountain at the front had cost more money than the average person's yearly salary, complaining about it made you look like an asshole. Who cares about the cost? Don't you know they raised one million dollars for cancer research? They can have all the chocolate they want. Ignore all the benefits they get in tax reductions, please.

So... where was I again?

Right! Charity event.

There was another advantage, too. We could make the tiramisu tasting part of a lottery. That way, people could still have a chance to try Laurel’s Delight, but nobody could complain about not getting one, since, you know... it was free.

Announcing the price in the statement was another factor. Five bronze coins were roughly a day's wage for the average citizen of Hano.

Originally, we had planned to sell Laurel’s Delight for a single bronze coin. Fast food was supposed to be affordable, after all. The entire point of opening a pizzeria was to create something that ordinary middle-class people could actually enjoy, rather than another luxury establishment catering exclusively to the wealthy. But that plan had been made before a divine manifestation turned our dessert into the hottest culinary fad in the city.

Now we had the opposite problem, and we needed to reduce the number of people heading toward the shop itself.

Five bronze coins were expensive enough to discourage casual customers, while still being cheap enough that wealthy customers and particularly determined Faithful could afford it. We would lower the price again once the chain expanded and the initial madness died down. Hopefully, by then, we wouldn’t have established ourselves as a bougie joint catering to the bourgeoisie only.

The statement was posted on the Holy Temple's public board, the Freelancer Guild's board, and another board next to our shop. We also gave copies to some of the children I had hired to spread the news throughout the city.

That last part was probably the riskiest decision. The announcement could backfire spectacularly. Instead of reducing the number of people heading toward The Delightful, we could simply have given thousands of people another reason to come.

Charity event... free new Holy dessert... The Lady's chosen restaurant...

It wasn't exactly a recipe for keeping people away, but by that point, we had already reached what I privately called a critical publicity threshold. There was no longer any realistic way to make The Delightful less interesting, and, judging by the number of people already gathering outside the shop, the rest of the city was in agreement with me.

At that point, trying to hide the opening would have been like trying to put a lid on a volcano. So I decided to do the next best thing. You can’t stop the river. You can only redirect it.

So, to Taka Memorial Square they shall go.

That was where I was at the moment, standing in the shade of Raik’s father’s statue and directing an army of child laborers I had hired to help prepare the event. Most of them were busy arranging tables and chairs across the square. Shingo was with them, carrying barrels of water with a faucet attached to the bottom, since the square didn’t have fountains and I wasn’t about to have dehydrated people at my event. Everyone seemed happy with the work since I was paying them handsomely. Well, everyone except Seth, Mosha, and Calr, who stood in front of me, enduring my increasingly withering glare.

“You are telling me that this is the list of names of the only bards you were able to contact?”

“Yes, ma’am,” said Calr.

“We couldn’t find anyone, ma’am. That list is entirely Calr’s,” Seth protested, and Mosha nodded in confirmation. The two younger teens had successfully disavowed themselves from the disaster, leaving Calr to take the full brunt of my glare.

I slowly looked back down at the list.

“The best you could come up with is someone with the stage name of Glen Gonorrhea, another one called Thirsty Misty, and finally Punitive Steve, who, and I am quoting directly from the description you gave me, ‘got both of his arms cut off as punishment for excessive stealing, and the only thing he has left going for him is his tongue.’”

“Yes, ma’am,” Calr replied, giving me a crisp Freelancer salute, fist over his chest. The cheeky boy was mocking me!

Mosha and Seth moved away from him as lightning started running through my skin.

He continued saluting.

“This is a Holy charity event. Are you trying to get them stoned on stage?”

Calr finally lowered his hand.

“The only bards I know work in the Unholy Temple, ma’am!”

I pinched the bridge of my nose. Of course he did. I shouldn’t have expected any more from him. Naturally, when I needed someone capable of entertaining twelve thousand Holy Faithful, the only people I found were Unholy deviants.

“I guess this leaves me with no choice,” I said, turning my eyes toward Mosha.

The half-Siren girl immediately stiffened.

“Since you have failed me, I guess you are going on stage.”

The thirteen-year-old’s face went pale. She looked like she had just been informed that she had been selected as a human sacrifice to appease the crowd.

“No.” Seth immediately stepped between us.

“No?”

“You can’t make her do that.”

“Why not? She has a lovely voice, and more importantly, she knows all the popular Holy hymns.”

“I can’t sing in front of twelve thousand people,” she said shyly.

“Yeah,” said Seth. “You hired her to help with the event, not to perform!”

“What if I paid you five silver?” I tempted her. “You can get a Soulbook with that kind of money.”

Seth hesitated and looked back at his milk sister. She immediately shook her head.

“No!”

“One gold?” I upped the price twelvefold.

Both Mosha and Seth gulped.

“I will do it,” said Calr. “I will sing for a gold coin. I will do pretty much anything for a gold coin.”

I stared at him with disapproval.

“Forget it. I will find someone myself.”

I turned toward the three of them.

“Calr, you are in charge of organization here.”

“Seth, go help in the kitchens.”

“Mosha, start working on the lottery papers.”

“Yes, ma’am,” the three of them echoed.

I summoned my spear and disappeared.

I appeared on the deck of a ship complete with wind-filled sails, three masts, and a quarterdeck in the back.

I blinked. No, I wasn’t on a ship. I was surrounded by clouds; there was no water on the horizon...

This is a flying ship!

I was standing on something that looked like it had sailed straight out of a fantasy game, and for several seconds I could do little more than stare in open-mouthed amazement.

I ran toward the railing. The ship’s wooden hull was cruising through the air above the ocean, with the glittering waves beneath us looking tiny from this height. It wasn't moving particularly quickly, at least not by the standards of modern airplanes, but there was something incredibly satisfying about watching the sails shift as the vessel caught different currents and gently changed direction.

I grabbed the railing and leaned over the side, trying to take in everything at once.

This was tantalizing. My heart felt ready to burst from excitement. Flying ships... I had read about them, of course. Flying ships were hardly some obscure technology in this world, and several of the books I had studied contained sections on their construction, history, and the different forms of magic used to keep them airborne. But reading about something and actually standing on one were two completely different experiences.

This was the sort of thing that would have made twelve-year-old Alice lose her mind. No, scratch that. This was making twenty-four-year-old Alice lose her mind.

I was looking westward, judging by the sun, when, after narrowing my eyes at the horizon, I realized we were approaching land. The coastline slowly emerged from the haze ahead of us, and I immediately recognized its shape. I had seen it before. The coastline matched the maps and, more importantly, the view I had seen during my little orbital sightseeing disaster in the Dreaming Realm.

We were approaching from the east. That meant I was probably somewhere around fifty kilometers east of Hano.

If I remember correctly, most of the flying ships use special stones to beat gravity. That means they can’t travel far from their original port.

I slowly turned toward the opposite side of the ship and nearly forgot what I was doing.

There it was... something enormous was floating on the horizon.

At first, my brain struggled to process what I was seeing. I technically knew what this was. I mean, some of the books I had read mentioned it. No, even Nina had talked about the place. She was born there, after all, before moving to Hano as a kid. But none of those descriptions had prepared me for the sheer scale of what was now in front of me.

A landmass was floating in the sky. Not a small island or a chunk of rock with a village built on it, but an actual piece of land large enough to cover most of the horizon. I pulled out my fancy spyglass and began adjusting the focus until the distant shape became clearer.

The upper surface directly facing us appeared to be covered in an urban landscape, a port town if I was guessing correctly. I could make out buildings, roads, towers, and what seemed to be large areas of farmland stretching beyond it. Beneath it, however, the island descended into enormous, jagged cliffs, as though someone had taken a massive piece of the earth and simply ripped it out of the ground.

I knew intellectually that the island was roughly twenty thousand square kilometers, according to the figures in the books. For comparison, that was approximately the size of the island of Shikoku in Japan, or maybe the state of New Jersey in the USA.

Twenty thousand square kilometers... floating in the sky.

I lowered the spyglass and then raised it again, just to make sure my brain wasn't exaggerating what I was seeing.

I had spent enough time studying this world to know that magic made some of the laws of physics negotiable, but seeing something on this scale was still absurd. The books had discussed the island almost casually, mentioning its cities, trade routes, political importance, and the fact that it was one of the major centers of Dreamer activity in the Contested Realm. To be fair, hosting a Rift Gate to another world was as fantastical-sounding as flying in the first place.

Still, the sheer scale of it all was mind-boggling, especially when you take into consideration its shadow.

An enormous dark region stretched across the ocean beneath the island, blocking the sunlight as the massive landmass slowly drifted overhead. I remembered reading about this phenomenon as well. It’s called the Longest Night. Since the island moved relatively slowly, its passage could create temporary periods of darkness for the regions beneath it. Depending on its route and pace, the shadow could remain over an area for days, and in some rare cases, it could reach a week of darkness.

I couldn't help laughing. This world was ridiculous. Wonderful, terrifying, scientifically offensive, but absolutely ridiculous.

I raised the spyglass again and took another long look at the island. I really needed to visit that place someday.

According to what I had read, it was to the Dreaming Realm what Hano was to the Bloodline Realm. I had heard that there were Dreamer academies, workshops, research institutions, and businesses that were more interested in knowledge and understanding the universe, as well as one of the best art scenes in the seven realms. Yeah, the place was my highest priority to visit.

Unfortunately, I had a charity event for twelve thousand people to organize.

I reluctantly lowered the spyglass and started scanning the deck for my actual target.

I eventually found Riri. The Songstress was shamelessly flirting with a couple near the side of the deck, somehow managing to give both of them her undivided attention at the same time. One hand was playing with the woman's hair while the other slowly moved up and down the man's arm.

Riri didn't seem to notice me, despite the fact that I had appeared only a few meters away from her. Her assistant, however, did.

Zira, the sound mage, was glancing at me, waiting for me to finish fiddling with my spyglass before she slowly approached.

“Lady Alice?”

“That’s me.”

“Are you here for her?”

“Yes.” I glanced toward Riri who had started kissing the man, and then back at Zira. “am I interrupting something?”

“No,” sighed Zira. “Riri is just trying to secure a bed for the night.”

I blinked. “What?”

“She decided that we needed to travel to Hano at the last second after dreaming with you and Nina,” explained Zira, “but due to her poor planning, we found that all the rooms were already booked, and we were only able to secure a spot on the deck floor...”

“So she is currently trying to seduce her way into a room,” I said, glancing her way again, only to notice that she was now kissing the woman.

“Trying and succeeding,” grinned Zira. “I can’t believe she was able to land a couple on their honeymoon.”

“Some people only roll Nat 20s when it comes to seduction.” I laughed.

“What?”

“I meant some people always have a perfect roll of the dice.”

“Her dice rolls aren’t always perfect, believe me,” chuckled Zira. “She just keeps throwing until one of them lands on a six.”

Before I could come up with a response, I noticed two guards approaching us. They had clearly noticed that I had appeared out of nowhere. My teleportation is not stealthy to anyone with a modicum of aura control. They seemed on alert, which, in hindsight, was probably fair; if some random woman suddenly teleported onto your ship, you probably wanted to investigate her.

“Excuse me, ma’am,” one of them said, stopping a few steps away. “Who are you, and how did you get aboard?”

“I’m Alice Hecate,” I said, reaching into my pocket. “I’m a teleporter. I apologize for the inconvenience. I’m only here to see a friend, and I’ll be gone in a moment.”

I pulled out my Freelancer Guild badge, a brass crossed-swords emblem, and held it up for them to see.

The guards exchanged a glance.

“Hano’s Freelancer Guild?”

“That’s right.”

One of them reached for it to verify it, and it glowed red. It was the standard identification method, since the badge reacted as soon as someone other than me touched it.

That seemed to change things immediately. The Hano Freelancer Guild was a big deal throughout the Contested Realm. The two guards looked at my badge again, then at me, and whatever suspicion they had seemed to evaporate.

“I see,” the first guard said.

“Sorry for the trouble,” I repeated. “I didn't mean to cause a disturbance.”

“It’s fine, ma’am. Just... perhaps introduce yourself immediately next time you teleport onto a ship.”

“I’ll keep that in mind.”

The two guards gave me a final nod before walking away.

The little commotion had finally caught Riri’s attention. She broke away from the honeymooning couple and called out to me.

“Alice!”

She came toward me with a huge grin on her face before puckering her lips dramatically and leaning forward for a kiss.

This time, I didn’t bother getting mad at her. Instead, I decided to play her game. I lowered my head and met her lips with my forehead.

Bonk.

“Ow!” Riri immediately pulled back, clutching her lips. “You are so mean, Alice!”

“Why are you on this ship?” I asked, ignoring her complaint.

Riri lowered her hand from her lips and grinned.

“After dreaming with you and Nina, I figured Hano was the place to be.”

“Just like that?”

“Pretty much. You two were having lots of fun. So I thought, ‘Well, what bard would I be if I didn’t go where things are happening?’”

“I guess we will be glad to have you?”

“Thanks.” She shrugged. “Besides, I was getting bored.”

I gave her an incredulous look.

“You didn’t look bored a minute ago.” I glanced at the couple behind her. They were having a hushed conversation. If I were to guess, it looked like the man was double-checking whether his wife was all right with what had just happened.

“Yep. That’s exactly what happens when I get bored.”

I decided not to argue with that logic.

“So, what are you doing here?” she asked.

“Well, I need a bard.”

“Yet, you refused my advances,” she joked. “What a tease.”

“I’m organizing a charity event for the opening of a restaurant,” I ignored her comment and moved the conversation forward, “and I need someone to perform.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“A Holy charity event?”

“That’s right.”

“So the rumors are true.”

“What rumors?” I narrowed my eyes.

“The Saint rumors, of course.”

“Who told you about this?” I groaned.

“After you two left the Dream,” she said. “I did a bit of snooping, and everyone I asked about you brought up the Saint theory.”

“I am not a Saint.” I sighed.

“Of course,” Riri said, nodding with exaggerated seriousness. “That’s what a Saint would say.”

“Don’t.” I narrowed my eyes at her. “I already let you in on my secret, so you, of all people, should know it’s bullshit.”

“I know.” She grinned. “But the myth is spreading out of hand.”

“Then don’t feed into it as well!”

“Wouldn't a charity event make it spread even more?” Zira asked.

I turned toward her, sighing.

“If I started hedging all my actions on what they would mean for the rumors, I would be paralyzed.”

I gestured toward the coastline on the horizon.

“Besides, that event needs to happen, or the good, mythic people of Hano might start a riot.”

I explained the situation we were in to them in a little more detail: the new dessert, the goddess manifestation, the rumors surrounding it, and Captain Yoka’s estimate that as many as twelve thousand people might show up.

Riri’s grin kept getting wider with every detail.

“Oh, I made the right choice coming to Hano.”

“So you’re in? You’ll perform for us?”

“You bet your juicy ass I will!”

“Good.” I nodded. “So, do I take you to Hano now?”

“Now?” She hesitated.

“Yes. I can only take one person at a time.”

Riri hesitated.

She glanced back over her shoulder toward the couple she had been flirting with. They seemed to be waiting for her to return, although the man looked considerably more nervous than he had a few minutes ago.

Riri looked at them for a moment, then back at me.

“Take Zira first and come back for me.” She turned around again, eyeing the couple from head to toe. “Maybe in two hours.”

I stared at her.

“No…” She bit her lip. “...maybe four, yeah, four would be enough.”

I sighed.

“The event is tomorrow, on Fireday,” I said, looking toward the yellow sun. “I’ll come get you two hours after dawn on this Waterday.”

“Perfect!”

“Just be sure to be ready,” I insisted.

“Sure.” She started turning.

“And clear things up with the ship’s crew in case you didn’t pay them in advance,” I called after her.

“Yep.” She waved me off. “See you in twelve hours!”

Before I could say anything else, Riri was already next to the couple. She grabbed both of them by the hand and started dragging them toward the lodge.

I watched the three of them disappear into the crowd.

I heard Zira laughing beside me. I turned toward her.

“Ready?”

The sound mage glanced at where Riri had disappeared before looking at me again.

“I have never teleported before, but I guess there’s always a first time.”

“It’s not too intrusive,” I said. “Just suppress your aura as much as you can so it wouldn’t cost me too much mana.”

I summoned my spear, grabbed her by the shoulder, and we were both gone.
