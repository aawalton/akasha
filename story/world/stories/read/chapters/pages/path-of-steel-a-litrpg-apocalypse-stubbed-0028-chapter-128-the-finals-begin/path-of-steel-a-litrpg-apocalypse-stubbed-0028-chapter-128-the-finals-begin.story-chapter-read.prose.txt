The arena was packed. More than packed. General admission tickets had been offered, and people stood on the highest level of the stands, from where they could see almost nothing. Even that level was packed. The tension was palpable. The clamor was deafening.

“Hut urchins! Hut urchins!” a merchant cried out.

“Popcorn, have some popcorn!” another yelled, wandering between the stands with a tiny kiosk hanging from his neck.

“How does this world have popcorn?” Gary wondered.

“Why not?” Cog asked.

“I thought it was just my planet.”

“It’s heated corn, Gary. Not really rare.”

“He once thought sewers were unique to his planet,” Alicia said.

“Oh yeah. We just shit in bags and carry them to the sea,” Cog said.

“I was new back then!” Gary said.

Everyone’s laughter wasn’t enough to alleviate the tension in his heart. He was close to learning the third song, but he hadn’t achieved it yet. Most importantly, he was exhausted. His eyelids drooped. He’d trained non-stop for the last three days to the point where he’d lost track of time.

The finals were packed with strong cultivators. There were sixteen in total, all formidable. Gary facing them in this condition was seriously sabotaging himself, but preparing for his duel against Sui Duang was more important than the tournament.

Of course, there was a one in fifteen chance that Gary would fight Sui Duang today. If that happened, he was dead. No way around it. The end, curtain drops. The fact that mere chance could kill him really didn’t sit well with Gary, as it wouldn’t for any cultivator. They trained to take control of their fate.

Soon, he told himself. The brackets will be announced soon. Then, I’ll know. If it’s anyone but Sui Duang or Feng Shen, I have a chance. Master’s last minute assistance helped, too…

Gary clenched his fist. Just before coming here, Master Colorful had presented him a crystal ball whose interior swam with multi-colored light. He’d called it a Level Ball. Once Gary absorbed it, aura had shot into his body, directly raising him the four last levels until the peak of Birth of Aura.

That didn’t mean he could break through, however. His aura control was sloppy. Master Colorful told him he needed to get it to at least thirty before even thinking of a breakthrough.

Thank you, Master…

His gaze turned to the VIP seats. While they’d been sparsely populated during the preliminaries, that was no longer the case. A dozen Heaven Reaching Elders sat there, along with powerful people from other clans. Gary saw a man with a lion lying by his feet. There were five stunningly beautiful women who looked like twenty-year-olds, dressed in black or green dresses, and a jellyfish-like creature wearing a long, black, red-rimmed cape.

A dozen more people or creatures filled those seats, but Gary only had eyes for a scant few.

Master Colorful sat by himself, arms crossed and watching the arena. The few people nearby, though sitting in the VIP seats themselves, gave him a wide berth. A few nodded respectfully.

Elder Sui sat in a group with a few other Elders. One was the blond Elder who’d berated Rat Himake during the tournament’s first day—Elder Rat, the Head of the Treasury Hall. Given that Elder Sui was Head of the Enforcement Hall, Gary assumed these were all high-level Elders in the clan.

They looked as dislikeable as he remembered them. They emanated a certain haughtiness, or maybe that was just Gary.

The final person that arrested his attention was a man wearing a tall hat. He was slim and tall, carrying the air of a scholar, with a thin mustache dripping from the ends of his lips and a set of wide, open robes framing his body. He wore thinner robes underneath. His hat was thin but tall, rising easily one foot over his head, while black and calm hair hid under it. From his mannerisms, he seemed like a gentle man, even a weak one, but Gary had been watching. All Elders, even Master Colorful, had paid him their respects.

Is that the Branch Head? Gary wondered. His title got thrown around a lot, but this was the first time Gary saw the person leading the entire Heaven Reaching branch. How strong is he? he wondered.

Besides those, Gary recognized a few Heaven Reaching Elders, including Elder Feng. The rough Elder who guarded the Valley of Broken Spears was nowhere to be seen. The same went for the Elder guarding the Spear Hold.

There were still empty seats among the VIP part of the stands, but over half were filled—that was much more than any day of the preliminaries.

“May I have your attention, please?” a cheerful male voice boomed over the arena. The clamor stopped, everyone focusing on the single man who stood in the center of the sands.

“Who’s that?” Gary asked.

“The presenter. They hire him every year to raise the spirits,” Alicia explained.

“Welcome, everyone, to the finals of the Heaven Reaching tournament! My name is Rollo Frollo, and I have been granted the honor of presenting these amazing matches! Don’t tell anyone, but I’m only doing it so I can watch without buying a ticket.”

He winked, part of the audience laughing.

“After nine days of hard battles, the sixteen strongest disciples of this year remain. However! This is not a regular tournament! Because of the recent drafting, the Heaven Reaching clan carried out a second clan selection this year, meaning that the pool of participants was double that of before! In fact, though the disciples of the most recent selection had only four months to prepare for the tournament, a few of them made it to the finals! Competition was fierce! Everyone gave it their all! Of the sixteen finalists, nobody can be ignored!”

The crowd cheered. It wasn’t only Rollo’s words, but also his spirit, the energy in his voice. He could be talking about stones and people would still get excited.

“Is that a vibration?” Gary asked.

“I think he’s just really good. There’s a reason the clan hires him every year,” Alicia said.

Rollo flourished a scroll, opening it to take a read. “Hmm, mhm, yes! Oh, wow! I have some great things to reveal, folks, but before that, let’s take a moment to introduce some of our most striking finalists!”

Blue light shot up from his hand, forming a massive projection in the middle of the stadium. It was so big that even those standing at the higher stands could see it clearly.

The projection took form. It showed a massive, Asian-looking man manipulating water around his spear. Spear and stream moved in tandem, completing each other in a lethal dance.

“Manipulating his aura and spear like the stones of a bola, Sui Duang is the clan’s number two disciple! If not for the number one, he’d be this tournament’s favorite—but can he claw for victory in the finals?”

Gary snorted. The audience cheered. Sui Duang’s image faded away, replaced by that of a young woman.

“As beautiful as she is fierce, Lin Xin is a disciple from the latest clan selection! Her talent is at the astonishing grade of low heaven! She was also the first disciple in decades to tame a legendary spear, the Snow White! Can she use those advantages and her Ice Vibration to reach the top despite her limited training time?”

The audience cheered even harder. “Lin Xin! Lin Xin!” men chanted. Gary didn’t blame them. The projection had shown Lin Xin from very flattering angles, making her seem like a goddess among women. Adding that to her strength, who wouldn’t be interested?

“All finalists are fierce, but some have something more! They have elegance! They have style! They have…humor?” the presented said.

The projection warped again, and to his surprise, Gary saw himself. He was stunned. A moment later, he realized his projection wasn’t the same as the others. Sui Duang and Lin Xin had been shown in their coolest moments, fiercely overpowering opponents in the preliminaries. Gary was just…sleeping?

It was a fake image, too. That’s not what his bed looked like.

“Gary Steel, please attend!” the projection shouted, now portraying a lone deacon in the middle of the arena. “Gary Steel, please attend! Very well. Disqualified!”

The audience laughed. A similar scene played again, but this time, Gary heard his own voice shout, “Wait!” The projection zoomed in on the arena’s entrance. Music started playing. Gary saw himself panting, saying, “Please don’t disqualify me again. I’m here!”

“That’s not what I said,” he said.

“Isn’t this slander?” Cog asked.

“They’re playing circus music over you! How dare they make fun of my Gary, I’ll zap them all!” Goldie yelled, shooting out of Gary sleeve and towards the middle of the arena, but he grabbed her by the tail.

“Please don’t,” he said.

“Let go of me, I’ll burn his hair!”

The clips that played showed Gary running around the arena and failing to hit Oracle Jay, failing to appear and getting disqualified, showing up at the last moment before getting disqualified, then a quick montage of Gary’s battles that made them seem like lucky victories.

“One fighter stood out from the rest for his unique style, both on the sand and outside of it! Welcome Gary Steel—a disciple from the latest clan selection, like Lin Xin, who somehow found his way to the finals. His nickname is Baldinator—would you like to know why?”

“Yes!” some of the audience yelled.

New scenes played. They showed Gary striking his opponents, then the same opponents getting up hairless. Rat Himake’s long, flowing golden hair—gone. Lily Haldrin’s beautiful red locks—gone. Oracle Jay’s entire fur—gone. The projection made sure to focus on their stunned expressions, which Gary was certain were exaggerated.

“How dare you, Gary Steel!” their three voices yelled as one.

“Gary Steel, the Baldinator, has a unique power. Whoever is struck by him loses all their hair!” Rollo said. “That includes eyebrows, armpits, fur, and even…down there.” He lowered his voice and made a downwards motion, making a small part of the audience burst out laughing.

“That wasn’t even funny,” Gary said.

“Will poor Lin Xin lose her hair to him? Who else will? Only time will tell! However, Gary Steel is more than an expired hair lotion—he gained the recognition of a legendary spear, the Golden Bolt, and he wields the terrifying destructive power of black lightning! I have been told, by anonymous sources, that his dream is to use the publicity of this tournament to open a hair salon. Truly a gifted entrepreneur!”

The audience laughed.

“He’s mocking you, Gary, he’s mocking you! Let me have at him!” Goldie struggled, but Gary held her tail tight.

“Easy, Goldie. We’ll prove ourselves in battle,” he said.

“But I don’t want to prove myself in battle, I want to fight him now! Hey, Rollo guy! Gary did the deed with your mother! I hear he inserted three whole fingers into where you were born!”

Thankfully, her little voice was lost in the clamor. Only the surrounding few disciples heard and directed strange looks at them. Gary paled. “Please get inside, Goldie,” he said, but she still struggled, and her rage only grew.

Gary felt a trickle of aura leaving him, and suddenly, Goldie’s voice boomed over the entire stadium like thunder, completely overwhelming the crowd.

“Shut up, Rollo Frollo!” she yelled. Her voice was the same, small and cute, but it rang to ungodly proportions. Every eye in the stadium turned to look at her, including the VIPs. Even Rollo and his projection paused.

“Excuse me?” he said.

“Goldie!” Gary hissed.

“No!” Goldie said, shivering with rage, slipping her tail out of Gary’s grasp and rising as high as she could until everyone could see her. “You are not good, Rollo Frollo! You’re saying lies! Gary doesn’t want to open a hair salon, he wants to climb to the top of the tower with me!”

The audience was lost on whether to laugh or stay silent. Rollo coughed in his hand.

“It appears my little joke offended a certain legendary spear. Very well. Let’s move on to—”

“No, we will not move on until you apologize!” Goldie yelled again. “Everything you said was lies! The people must know! Gary is my best friend in the whole world, and I will not let you slander him! Apologize! Apologize immediately or I will burn your hair and tell everyone what Gary did to your mother last night!”

Rollo looked around as if to show everyone he couldn’t play with a crazy snake.

“Oh, so you want to know! Very well! Gary did the deed with her! That’s right, he stuck three whole fingers into where you were born, and she was saying stuff like, Gary you’re so amazing, I wish my son Rollo Frollo doesn’t learn about this! He then took his big spear and—”

Gary cut off the flow of aura, returning her voice to the regular volume that only the surrounding few people could hear. Their eyes went wide. A few covered their ears. Goldie realized she was no longer heard, then harrumphed and flew back to Gary.

“Okay, he didn’t apologize, but I showed him,” she said smugly. “Just so you know, Alicia, the mother thing was a lie.”

“What? Why would I—” she started saying, then Gary spoke.

“Goldie, that was ridiculous…” Unlike his words, his eyes were watery. He pulled Goldie into a hug. “It was really sweet, too. Thank you.”

“We’re a team, silly. Of course I’ll protect you,” Goldie said, wrapping her tail around the back of his neck.

The crowd was finally waking up. On the sands, Rollo coughed in his hand. “Please control your spirit,” he said strictly.

“Control your mother,” Gary replied, and it was like the audience broke out of a spell. They lost it. Howling laughter filled the stands, along with the chanting of Baldinator. Rollo’s face hardened for a moment before relaxing again.

“Well, that’s a live performance, folks. It’s always nice when I’m not the only one cracking jokes,” he said to the still laughing audience. The projection changed to show a feline with white fur so short it barely hid the pink underneath.

“Speaking of jokes,” Rollo said. “Oracle Jay—the Birth of Aura disciple who can read the future! He’s neither fast nor strong, but his durability? That’s nothing special either. What’s special about him is that he can read his opponents’ minds, reacting to their moves the moment they think of them! That’s what makes him the fifth-ranked disciple of the Heaven Reaching clan! He had an almost stellar record through the preliminaries, with a single exception, which is also why he’s hairless. Can you guess who he fought?”

“Baldinator! Baldinator!” the crowd cheered.

“That’s right!”

The projection now showed the battle between Gary and Oracle Jay. After a few seconds of Gary missing everything, he put away his spear and charged at the oracle, who cried foul. Gary eventually shoulder-rammed him in the chest, making the feline fly back and lose all his fur.

“Who needs a brain when you can take hits like a champ?” Rollo said.

“Please don’t,” Gary told Goldie.

“I wouldn’t say anything. He’s right on that one.”

“Excuse me!?”

“After that humiliation, Oracle Jay worked hard to develop counter-measures against suicidal assaults! Whoever wants to defeat him now will need to do it properly. Will he and Gary Steel meet again?”

The audience cheered.

“On behalf of the Heaven Reaching clan, this is the point where I want to ask you for a favor,” Rollo said. Oracle Jay’s hairless face appeared on the projection. “The most experienced ones among you might have an idea of how he reads minds. Even if you get it, please don’t reveal anything! The tournament is meant to simulate real combat, so let the young fighters figure it out themselves or fail trying.”

People looked at the VIP section, where everyone wore wry expressions.

“What the hell, it was just a trick?” Gary asked.

“I have no idea how it’s done,” Cog said. Alicia agreed absent-mindedly.

“Well, what are the chances of me facing him again? One in fifteen?” Gary asked.

Alicia gave him a funny look. “The brackets aren’t random, Gary…”

“Oh,” he replied.

“You can beat him!” Goldie said.

“Do you know how he reads minds?”

“He needs a mind to read, Gary. You’re safe.”

“Hey! What happened to, you’re my best friend, I’ll protect you?”

“I’ll protect you from everything but the truth,” Goldie replied smugly.
