“You clearly have a strong information network,” James said slowly. “But it’s understandable that it didn’t make its way to you yet. It was very recent. My Fisher King Job upgraded to Fisher Emperor, and the Fisher Kingdom became the Fisher Empire.”

“At what level?” Zhang Chāo asked eagerly. “What upgrades became available?”

“Um, it wasn’t a specific level sort of thing,” James said. “Honestly, even the upgrades are mostly things that are particular to me. My Job is heavily focused on land management. I can do things like enforce my will across my entire territory now, with my word becoming law. But I also had a Skill like that beforehand, just much less effective.”

James spoke carefully while trying to sound offhand. He wasn’t about to mention Dispossess, or any other Skill he had that made him inherently threatening to other Rulers. He liked Zhang Chāo well enough thus far, but James was mainly going out of his way to avoid making him an enemy. Giving that sort of information to someone James had barely met was counter productive.

And how he had become a higher tier of Ruler was clearly such valuable information that it would be foolish to just give it away to a near stranger.

“So it wasn’t a Job Evolution, then,” murmured Han Jianguo from Zhang Chāo’s side.

James didn’t answer. The big man’s words didn’t feel like a question directed at him. More of a personal musing simply stated out loud.

Zhang Chāo seemed to sense that James was reluctant to pass anything else on. He didn’t allow the silence to stretch very far after James’s careful non-answers.

“Aside from squeezing you for information, I also want to ask you a favor, Fisher Emperor,” Zhang Chāo said in a quiet, sincere tone of voice.

“What could that possibly be?” James asked, raising an eyebrow. He spoke in a tone of semi-sincere humility. “What could I do that would be of help to you? The impression I have is that your population is significantly larger than mine, and for all I know, you might be twenty levels higher than me. Just because I had the good fortune to stumble upon an upgrade to my Job, I hope you won’t ask anything impossible of me…”

“Please, get up and speak,” Zhang Chāo said tightly. “Pú Tiě Shān seized the moment a few minutes ago, and if I go up and contradict him, the others from our region will assume it’s just me pursuing our particular issues. Anything I say will end up being dismissed by the crowd as the word of one side in a factional dispute. We’ll lose the ideological conversation by simply not being able to have it. Please!”

“I’m not sure my public speaking Skills are functional right now,” James said. “Are you sure you want me being the mouthpiece for your cause?”

Zhang Chāo hesitated for a moment, then sighed and spoke up.

“You know my intelligence-gathering is decent,” he said. “Well, I know for a fact that someone in the room has a Skill that deactivates other people’s Skills. It’s apparently not selective but area-based. At some point, this person deactivated everyone’s Skills, and that ability has remained in use since activation.”

“That’s… ridiculous.”

Absurd. Unbalanced. Broken. Too powerful. The System can’t allow a power like that. That person would just kick everyone’s asses. Doesn’t that mean we’re basically all in that Ruler’s territory right now? Effectively?

“Regardless, it’s true,” Zhang Chāo replied calmly.

“Sounds like a good reason not to make any speeches until this person stops whatever they’re doing,” James said. “If I can’t count on the System smoothing out my rough edges, I’m a dead weight. I never used to be much of a public speaker.”

Speaking in court is not the same as speaking in front of a crowd, he thought. You’re either addressing the judge, an audience of one, or a jury, which is twelve people. You can make eye contact with that size of a group. You can try to gauge their reactions from their faces. There are easily thousands of spectators here, if we count the monsters as well as the humans. Some of them don’t even have faces. Some of the humans are wearing masks!

Those humans in powered armor were also not showing their faces.

Then again, there was a part of James that liked the idea of getting up and speaking just then. If the person who could cancel out others’ abilities continued doing it for the rest of the Summit, James would probably be one of the more persuasive speakers. Most of the others who had come here had done so on the backs of fighting ability. That was typically how one became a Ruler. Even with James’s limited public speaking abilities, he was still definitely better than the average person.

“It just puts you on a level playing field, but it is understandable that anyone might hesitate,” Zhang Chāo said, clearly reluctant to push harder.

James had a sudden thought and tried to reach out telepathically to one of the monsters that he still had stored inside his body. It didn’t respond with words, but he felt an immediate stirring in his blood, where one of his slimes was located.

Interesting. The Skills are deactivated, but the monsters I created with them are still present. Probably this Skill-deactivator is a Ruler, and if I had to guess, he’ll be one with a monstrous physical presence. That’s the only way relying on Skills being unusable would be viable in combat. It’s unlikely someone who is just a Ruler’s attendant would have a Skill this strong.

“I suppose we can try to find someone else to make our case,” Zhang Chāo said after a moment, James not having responded aloud.

“No, it’s all right,” James said slowly, swallowing a lump in his throat. He was surprisingly nervous. “Just… you owe me one.”

“Absolutely,” Zhang Chāo said, nodding.

“Although, I can see us being comrades by the end of this,” James continued. “And there are no debts between friends. Just give me a moment to speak with my advisors before I get up there. It’s probably best that I take some counsel since I’m going in with no plan, no preparation to give a speech, and no Skill that fills in the gap.” He grimaced.

“Of course,” Zhang Chāo said, smiling reassuringly. “We’ll step out of your way.”

“What made you volunteer for the firing squad?” Alan asked as soon as the Taiwanese delegation had stepped hopefully out of hearing range.

“Just a moment,” Mina said quietly. She waved her hand in the air as if she expected something to happen, then shook her head. “Right, the Chinese man mentioned something about Skills being deactivated here. I found a useful spell in my book that I thought I could use. Would have made it impossible to eavesdrop on us…” She deflated slightly. James put an arm around her and gently squeezed her around the waist.

“I appreciate the effort, sunshine,” he said. That made her smile. At least he still had Perfect Choice of Words for his wife. Then he turned to Alan. “I think that Zhang Chāo is a worthwhile ally to cultivate. He sympathizes with the old American values, at least on the surface, and that’s been one of the main things the Fisher Empire has ended up standing for. Friends who have similar ideals to yourself are the most stable.”

There was no point in going into the other reasons. That was a good enough rationale.

“Well, I think you were bound to say something to go out on a limb anyway,” Mitzi said. “You’ve never been a shrinking violet. You like to make the first move and not let other people define the terms of engagement. Might as well get some political capital for it, right?”

“What advice did you actually want from us?” asked Samuel.

“I want to speak in a way that bridges the gap between human and monster,” James said. “Not just refute Pú Tiě Shān’s ideas, but also bring over new allies. I would appreciate any feedback from any of you about how best to do that, especially considering I’m not going to get any assistance from the System. And there’s another thing. Something I want you all to be working on while I give my speech…”

After a brief huddle with his councillors, James strode up to the stage, which stood empty. After Pú Tiě Shān’s bold and controversial opening monologue, there had been a certain reluctance to follow him in speaking before the gathered dignitaries.

All the better, James thought. I can directly rebut Pú Tiě Shān’s words, without having to worry that people will still be thinking about other people who spoke between us. Now people will think of the two of us as the opposing camps they have to choose between. I’m effectively positioning myself as head of a faction. Even if it puts a target on my back, that should be worth the benefits in terms of drawing in new allies.

He stepped onto the platform and cleared his throat loudly, then scanned the room. He estimated that he had the attention of perhaps two-thirds of the attendees, whether in whole or in part. Some of the nonhuman Rulers were impossible to appraise with a glance, and James wasn’t entirely sure if every Ruler here was even intelligent, but that would hopefully be of little consequence either way. It wasn’t as if some creature was going to flop onto the stage and eat him.

“Hi, I’m James Robard, and I don’t want to subjugate the peoples of the Earth.” He opened with a note of humor.

There were quiet chuckles. James wasn’t sure if they were just polite or sincere. He pressed on and tried not to think about that.

“I am the Fisher Emperor, Ruler of the Dark Waters,” he continued, becoming steadily more serious. “My lands comprise most of what was Central Florida. After we return to our homes, I will take the rest of Florida and perhaps the rest of the continental United States under my protection.” There were furious murmurs, raised eyebrows, and quiet gasps. A few people in the crowd smiled or chuckled.

“My domain is diverse in terms of Races,” James said. “With my power, I have brought humans and nonhumans alike to live in peaceful understanding with each other.” That quieted most of the murmurs. Much of the room looked surprised, including the nonhumans, to the extent that James could read their emotional reactions. “That’s part of why I came up here. My territory is proof that humans and monsters can live side by side, as long as both sides respect the Ruler and he acts justly to all.”

He sighed. This next part was off-script from what he and the others had worked on, but it felt right. “I’ll be honest, I don’t actually know much about governance. I mostly know about fighting. I became the Fisher Emperor, because I was the best at killing people in my region, and that brought me a lot of attention, positive and negative. People started to look to me for protection.” There were nods of recognition from gathered humans and monsters alike. Many of them recognized their own stories in his; there were far from a few reluctant Rulers in this bunch. And the monsters took it for granted that any Ruler must be among the strongest of its kind. “What I will say is, before the System appeared, I used to live in a country where one of our Founders said that if you’re willing to give up liberty for security, you deserve neither. “I would love to hear from other people. This Summit should be the chance for all of us to get to know each other a bit. For most of us, it’s our first time meeting. There’s no need to get heavily into ideology and governing approaches right now. None of us have been leaders for long enough to have a really well thought out approach yet, frankly. If there are a thousand people, there are bound to be fifteen hundred opinions. Except maybe the people who were already kings and presidents. Those people might feel like they know what they’re doing. Honestly, I mean no offense, but I’m not sure the world was in good enough shape before this for me to have any extra confidence in those people compared with the rest of us.
