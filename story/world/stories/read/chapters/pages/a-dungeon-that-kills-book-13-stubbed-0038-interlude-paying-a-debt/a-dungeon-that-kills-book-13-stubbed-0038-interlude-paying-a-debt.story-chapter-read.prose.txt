From his table, Ekon watched the three people who had just entered the mess hall of the Guild.

Renee was one of them, of course, and beside her walked Cedric, the only son of Guildmaster Isadora and the young leader of the party that would represent Daelin in the upcoming contest. Bringing up the rear was a boy who couldn’t have been more than thirteen or fourteen, someone Ekon had glimpsed once or twice in the Guild before. Name was Quinn, if his memory hadn’t failed him entirely.

Lady Galatea had paid him another of her visits yesterday, and she told him that the Sovereign had, at long last, decided the time had come to collect on his debt. His guts had gone tight at that, his mind immediately conjuring a dozen dreadful possibilities of what might be demanded of him.

Yet, when the actual request was finally spoken aloud, he felt almost embarrassed by how simple it turned out to be, especially when weighed against the magnitude of the debt, one born from the very fact that the Sovereign had saved his life. Well, there was still another debt, this one incurred when the Sovereign had helped him destroy the masked men’s secret lair. Nevertheless, having two debts reduced to one had certainly eased his mind a lot.

And so, as instructed, he had arranged to meet Cedric to pass along a certain piece of information. Of course, it would have appeared more than a little strange, if not outright suspicious, if he had simply approached a young man he had never once spoken to and asked for a private conversation out of nowhere. Good thing that he had Renee, who acted as an intermediary. She had assured him that she was not only acquainted with Fiora, a member of Cedric’s party, but was also friends with someone who had earned the trust and affection of the whole group, someone whose word alone could easily sway Cedric’s opinion. Ekon wondered if this Quinn was the person she had been talking about.

“I’ve brought him to you,” Renee said cheerfully as the three of them finally came to a stop before his table.

“Thank you for coming, Cedric.” Ekon offered his best smile. “And you’re Quinn, right? Please, both of you, sit.”

They returned the greeting before lowering themselves into the seats across from him, the wooden chairs creaking softly as they settled in.

“Renee told me you had something you wanted to discuss,” Cedric said.

“That’s right. I heard your party will be entering the dungeon two days from now, to participate in the competition against the adventurers from Iskora. The first one to the third floor wins, or so I’m told. Have I got that correct?”

“Yes.”

“I admire your courage,” Ekon said. “This could prove a very dangerous expedition.”

Cedric nodded. “Even without those men from Iskora involved, the trip would be perilous enough on its own. To reach the third floor, we will need to get through that great structure in the middle of the desert. The same one your party has once attempted, if I’m not mistaken.”

“We did, and we failed. We were only inside for a short time before we were forced to retreat. The whole place was a trap. It allowed us to venture deep inside without challenge, then sprang the trap and sent monsters at us from every direction.”

“I’ve heard about it. I’ve also heard that two people were killed. Not members of your party, but two independent adventurers who just joined you for that particular expedition. And their deaths were peculiar, to say the least. Then, when people demanded answers from you, all they got back was something so vague it hardly counted as an explanation.”

Ekon chuckled. “You don’t seem to trust me.”

Cedric gave a shrug. “Quinn and Fiora both told me I could trust your party. So I will.”

Trust your party, huh? Not “trust you.” In other words, the young man had made it clear that he might be willing to place some faith in Mandra and Renee, but not in Ekon himself. He was only granting him the benefit of the doubt because of the association he had with them. Well, not that I have any right to complain. It was only natural that everyone in town regarded him with suspicion; he had given them ample reason to.

“Due to my own circumstances, it’s hard for me to explain what actually happened during my last expedition. Still, I can offer you a piece of information now, hoping that it will give you an edge in the upcoming contest.”

Cedric arched a brow. “You want to help us? Why?”

Ekon smiled, folding his hands together atop the table. “I don’t think I need to tell you that many people in town suspect the adventurers from Iskora are closely connected to the masked men who have attacked and kidnapped the pyromancers, myself included, right? And I share that suspicion. In other words, I believe those men are my enemies. So it’s only natural I don’t want them to win.”

Cedric glanced sideways at Quinn, and the boy gave a nod. “Fair enough,” he said. “Go ahead. I’m listening.”

Renee is right, Ekon thought. He trusts this boy a great deal indeed.

“It’s about the ballista bolt.”

“Ballista?” Cedric asked, frowning. “You mean that freak incident where an adventurer was struck down by a giant arrow immediately after entering the dungeon? But that only happened once. Why are you bringing it up now?”

“We were shot at several times when we were near that giant structure during my last expedition.”

“What?”

“I suspect the ballista is a special weapon controlled by the dungeon, one it only uses in certain situations. The killing of that adventurer was merely a test shot. The dungeon wanted to determine the ballista’s maximum range, and after confirming it worked as intended, there was no need for another test. That was why a second incident never occurred. However, once we reached that courtyard, the weapon was deployed for real and used against us.”

“This dungeon is very strange indeed,” Cedric murmured, slowly shaking his head. Then he nodded at Ekon. “Thank you for warning us. We will keep that in mind when we approach that building.”

“Actually, that’s not the reason I’m telling you this,” Ekon said. “Where do you think the ballista is kept?”

“Somewhere on the second floor?”

“Then why has no one seen it yet? All the ruins in the desert have already been searched, while the rest of the area is nothing but flat terrain. It’s hard to believe something that big could remain hidden. No, I’m sure the bolts were fired from a different floor, one below the second.”

Cedric’s eyes widened. “Is that even possible?”

“Did you forget that adventurer was killed on the first floor, right at the entrance?” said the boy named Quinn, who had remained silent until now. “That means a ballista bolt traveling between different floors of the dungeon isn’t as unbelievable as you think.”

“You’re right,” Cedric said. “But... then what?”

Quinn rubbed his chin, deep in thought. Then he asked, “Did the bolts come from inside the building?”

“No,” Ekon said. “They came down from the sky.”

“I don’t really follow...” Cedric said, looking genuinely lost, while Renee wore an equally baffled expression. Which was hardly a surprise, considering that Ekon had only asked her to bring the young man here, and hadn’t told her anything about the information the Sovereign wanted passed along.

“He’s implying there’s another way to get to the third floor,” Quinn said.

What a smart boy. Ekon smiled. This would make his task much easier than he had anticipated. “Just so,” he said.

“Yes, exactly!” Cedric exclaimed. “There must be another point of access that connects the second floor and the third, allowing the bolts to travel from the lower floors. Which means, if we manage to find it, we can just ignore that gigantic building altogether and attempt to reach the third floor through this other route instead.”

“But where is it?” Renee asked.

“It must be well hidden,” Ekon said. “Clearly, the dungeon doesn’t want us to find it. But we can still make an educated guess based on the layout of the desert.”

“Layout?”

“The desert is divided into two very distinct halves,” Ekon said. “The one before the great building is littered with ruins, providing resting points for adventurers as they make their way toward it. The half behind it, however, is completely empty, with nothing but endless hot sand under a merciless sun. Moreover, the building naturally drew everyone’s attention, so no one bothered to explore the far edge of the desert.”

“You think the second entrance lies out there?” Cedric asked.

“I do. Though as I said, it’s only a guess. I could be wrong.”

Cedric turned to Quinn. “What do you think?”

“Searching for it could turn into a wild goose chase,” the boy said. “Still, if it comes down to a race against the Iskora team to reach the same destination, I don’t think you would stand a chance.”

Cedric nodded. “You’re right. Sometimes, we have to take risks. I’ll consider it carefully.” He turned back to Ekon, smiling. “Thank you very much. This bit of information will help us a lot.”

And that meant the task the Sovereign had given Ekon—one that required him to lure the Daelin team away, steering them instead toward the alternate path to the third floor—had been completed.

He had, admittedly, spent no small amount of time puzzling over what the Sovereign’s true intention might be in all of this. But after mulling it over at length, he had finally arrived at an answer that made sense.

If the Daelin team emerged victorious in the competition, the Iskora adventurers would be evicted from town, which would in turn deal a crippling blow to whatever ambitions the Children of the Abyss harbored here. However, in order for the Daelin team to win, eliminating the Iskora team alone would not be enough. After all, if neither team managed to reach the third floor, the contest would end in a draw. That was why the Sovereign was clearing the way for the Daelin team, removing obstacles between them and their destination, effectively handing them a free victory.

Very meticulous, Ekon thought. He really does plan for every contingency, leaving nothing to chance.

“So, there will only be the four of you on the Daelin team?” Renee asked. “How many people are allowed on each team?”

“By the agreement between my mother and that woman, each side may bring up to six.”

Renee frowned. “Then why are you bringing only four?”

“Trust me, I’d rather have more. But finding people who are both capable and willing isn’t as simple as it sounds.”

“I’ll go with you, then,” Renee said.

“What?” Ekon and Cedric exclaimed at the same time.

She glanced at Ekon. “You don’t want those guys to win, right? Neither do I. Besides, Fiora is a friend of mine. Not that we’re particularly close or anything, but I don’t want bad things to happen to her. So I’ll help however I can. At the very least, I can use my wind to haul you all out if things get ugly.”

“I’m surprised,” Quinn said. “I remember you telling me you nearly died several times in the dungeon. You really have no problem going back there?”

Renee laughed. “I have a feeling this trip will be considerably less deadly than the last.”

She’s right, Ekon thought. He doubted the Sovereign—the master of the dungeon—would use deadly force against the Daelin team, not when the entire purpose of this scheme was to help them win. Besides, Renee could now be counted among the Sovereign’s allies, so there was no reason for him to do her any harm.

“Anyway, Quinn,” Renee said. “Fiora told me that you believe the dark spirit is going to be involved in this contest somehow. Is that right?”

What?

A sudden jolt of alarm shot through Ekon, and he had to fight to keep his expression neutral. After all, he knew the “dark spirit” better than anyone at this table. That was none other than the Sovereign himself. So he gazed at the boy, studying him properly for the first time.

Quinn shrugged. “It’s just my theory.”

Then, the boy laid out his hypothesis in full: this entire competition was nothing more than an elaborate plot orchestrated by Iskora, and the Valiant Bank behind them, designed to lure out the dark spirit who had recently destroyed their hidden lair near Daelin. He then further speculated that the dark spirit was going to slip into the dungeon disguised as an ordinary adventurer, and that he could be anyone, moving around unnoticed and spying from the shadows without ever revealing his true identity.

Clever boy, Ekon thought. The theory held together well enough, for someone working with half the pieces. Quinn had gotten many things wrong, yes, but the fault lay in his lack of information rather than any failure on his own part. Unlike him, Ekon knew that the Valiant Bank was no mastermind at all, but merely a tool, much like the Children of the Abyss, serving an ancient existence that had been secretly manipulating the world for thousands of years. But of course, such knowledge was beyond the reach of ordinary people like Quinn, so no matter how sharp his mind proved to be, the truth of it would remain out of his grasp.

The assumption that the dark spirit would enter the dungeon as a normal adventurer, too, missed the mark entirely. The Sovereign was, after all, the lord of the dungeon itself. He came and went as he pleased, with no need whatsoever for any disguise. And the idea of him snooping around playing spy was, frankly, almost laughable to consider. Such menial tasks were far beneath someone of his status. He had his own network of agents for such purposes, and he also had Lady Galatea, who could go anywhere she wanted while remaining unseen.

As his thoughts ran their course, Ekon let his eyes drift about the mess hall, and there they caught on someone new through the door, a young woman who looked completely out of place among the rough adventurers filling the room.

She appeared to be somewhere in her mid-twenties, dressed in a maid’s uniform tailored fine enough to belong to some lesser noblewoman rather than a servant. Whoever she worked for had money, and plenty of it. Which begged the question: what business would bring someone like her here?

And more importantly, why was she walking straight for his table?

However, as she drew closer, it quickly became apparent that Ekon was not the reason for her visit. Instead, her aquamarine eyes, striking and framed with lashes dark as ink, settled on Quinn, and she smiled at him like an old acquaintance.

“You’re a maid at Overseer Rennald’s house, right?” the boy asked. “I saw you when I went there with my sister.”

“I am,” the maid said, her voice every bit as refined as the rest of her. “My master asked me to bring you a message. I came to the Sanctuary looking for you, only to learn that you were here.”

Quinn chuckled. “Sorry to have dragged you halfway across the town. What is the message, then?”

“My master would like you to come to his office at the caravan station tomorrow.”

“Really? Did he say why? And what time did he have in mind?”

“That’s all he instructed me to say,” the maid said. “As for the hour, he leaves that to you.”

Ekon blinked. He turned the exchange over in his mind, unable to fully suppress his astonishment. He knew that Rennald was the most important man in this town, the one people nicknamed the King of Daelin. Why would a man of his status regard somebody this young so highly, enough to invite him personally and even adjust his own plans around the boy’s schedule?

“I’ll go there tomorrow morning, then.”

The maid smiled again. “I’ll inform my master.”

She dropped a graceful curtsy before turning and making her way back out of the mess hall as unhurriedly as she had entered.

Ekon’s gaze lingered on Quinn long after the maid had gone. He’s a smart boy, he thought, smarter than most people his age by a considerable margin. He had also maintained good relations with both the Adventurer’s Guild and the caravan station, the two most influential organizations in Daelin. He was friends with Cedric, the Guildmaster’s own son, who trusted his judgment enough to seek his counsel before making decisions of consequence. And now, as it turned out, even Overseer Rennald appeared to think highly of him.

This boy had a very bright future ahead of him. He was going to become someone important in Daelin in just a few years’ time. And given that Ekon himself was likely to remain here for the foreseeable future, whether he liked it or not, it seemed only prudent that he start cultivating a good relationship with this young Quinn, before that bright future actually arrived and made such closeness far harder to achieve.
