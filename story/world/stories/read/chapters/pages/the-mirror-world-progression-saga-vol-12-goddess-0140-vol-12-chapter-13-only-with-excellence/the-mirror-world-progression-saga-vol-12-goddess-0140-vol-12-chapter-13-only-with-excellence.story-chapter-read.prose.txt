We’ll take everyone at this point. The people have the right to put their last stakes in on the last call.

Mmn. I have faith in them. They’ll accomplish this like everything else legendary heroes accomplish things: with supreme excellence.

In the great laboratory of Itha Ranon, dozens gathered for the last ritual of a great series. The pain that led to it was long enough in just one world, but in many past lives it went deeper and deeper into something akin to infinity. The paid price of a warrior’s will to suffer duty eternally — if necessary.

In addition to the ritual helpers providing energy, the golems themselves waited solemnly as if in faithful vigil, gazing as if down a long road in the distance, for their long-lost brother, and not just any brother — the very greatest of them.

The shell was perfect and complete, by combination artifice constructed of the best of Itha Ranon, including, once more, the wonder that was the soulcraft artificer, Tashome. The golem on the table would not be the largest of them, but he was bigger than the majority. None were more majestic — it would make a fine sculpture of a proud king, though it lacked a crown. The material was beautiful — mixed rich coppers and red golds showing inner swirls and something like glitter in some lighting. This was the orichalcum of Calrenazzod.

The material, once forged, was as indestructible as any artifact. That’s basically what it was: a weapon. In addition, though, the substance extended a field in all directions, disrupting magic of all kinds. As such, the ritualists needed to give it quite the wide berth, gathered around it on the far outside of the chamber.

The duties of the makers were fully done in this particular case of a ritual, and they weren’t even present. No one could provide much aside from energy and slight aids to focus. Those who had helped Ana for the other golems were helping her now, as largely the only people who’d learned everything that was needed for the increasing, amplifying sophistication of subtle details.

Samantha was hand-in-hand with Ana, all of their eyes closed, and in total mental communion for this act. They were the only two who could possibly understand the ritual and do the deed.

The ritual began, preparing the vessel, where the bulk of the energy had to go, but only Ana fully understood the composition of the ‘housing.’ Unlike Ana, Samantha found the energy quite easy to manage, and her simple presence alleviated even the strain on the other ritualists, who were surprised by the relative lack of stress they had to endure compared to the last one.

Samantha, meanwhile, scrambled to figure out the construct. If not for her ability to improvise on a dime and think in compacted moments of time, she surely would’ve failed altogether multiple times. Fortunately, Ana, continually in communion, was able to guide her in the unusual ‘soul craft’ involved, filling in the gaps with knowledge fast enough for Samantha to correct before converted pneumanic energies fizzled and unraveled.

Magic, arcane and divine, was converted into something more specialized, thankfully immune to interruption by the material.

Finally, it came time for the ‘reach.’ They both approached the golem figure, feeling the material’s interference wash over them, but no longer needing mere magic. They placed their free hands on the golem’s broad chest, and together reached across time and space. Somewhere along a vast, preternatural tunnel Ana guided them down, they found a terminus nexus.

As if tapping a tree bursting with the sap of life, they diverted a flow back down the tunnel, to what must be Calrenazzod.

Samantha was bewildered, only half-understanding what was happening, but thankfully, Ana’s hand always guided her. Unlike her mother, Ana was not distractible, able to pinpoint, locate, and focus on a single thread even amidst a storm of fabrics. That was her talent.

Nonetheless, Samantha felt the truth of who this was, and who they all were. Agonized, forged souls poured into a mold. She watched it happen, conducting the very pouring herself, with Ana. She felt a soul scream as it had countless times before, unable to do anything else at the raw, visceral terror of the process.

Spirit was made into a laser-like purpose, by oaths long ago set in stone.

She sensed her daughter’s own profound guilt and sadness that this horrible reality had to be revealed to her mother, rather than endured as her singular burden.

But Samantha felt no more motherly than in that moment. She sent acceptance mentally, and an embrace, as they consoled each other, and perhaps could endure the shame a little easier together.

Finally, finally, the soul of the greatest warrior of them all filled the stone, and the scream became a profound silence as this spirit’s will formed, becoming as unyielding as the material.

He was duty and honor personified; he was a king who only served the realm.

They backed away to give him space as he twitched to life. This took a few moments before the heavy metal body smoothly sat up, turned, and rose. His eyes opened, showing once more that thing that never varied between these brothers, despite their vast array of differences: eyes like out of a burning forge.

The vastness of those eyes did not yet meet Samantha’s, however, instead kept lowered in humble respect at the floor. The majestic golem knight immediately knelt on one knee before her.

Sir Lancelot intoned, “Declare and speak your name, knight and brother-in-arms.”
