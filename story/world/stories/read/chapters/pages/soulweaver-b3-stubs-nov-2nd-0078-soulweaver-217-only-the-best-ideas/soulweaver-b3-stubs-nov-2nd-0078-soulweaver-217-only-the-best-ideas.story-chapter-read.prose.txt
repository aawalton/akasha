The hangover the next morning was about as epic and terrifying as one might expect from a night like that. Splitting headaches, nausea, the general sense of being half-dead.

Or at least, that was how it was until the moment I got back into my Vigor armor. Just like that, my constitution soared, and minutes later, I stood basking in the morning sun filtering through the oversized windows, the ill effects that had nearly consumed me nothing more than a distant memory.

Which was good because we were still at that rooftop bar, and with Arianna and I being the only sober ones—the only ones even awake, for that matter—we were on point to haul Richard, Yashas, and Aerion back to Yashas’ penthouse.

Aerion was thankfully easy. I just chucked her into my inventory, summoning Galia as another set of eyes once we’d taken the elevator back to ground level.

Unfortunately, the same couldn’t be said for the others.

Even Arianna, being Vigor’s Champion, looked a little out of it. Richard and Yashas might as well have passed for zombies. Honestly, with how pale their faces were, they probably would’ve gone completely unmolested if they’d been wandering the streets at night.

I had to wonder if she'd chosen to forget that revelation she'd had last night or whether she was just doing a great job of hiding it. I vaguely recalled the accusation I'd made against her god, but if she took offense to that, she certainly didn't show it. She didn't show much of anything, actually, and if she was content to sweep that incident under the rug, so was I.

Still, she'd have to work through those emotions eventually. I could only hope the General would help her with that, because I certainly didn't have a clue.

We made good time back to Yashas’ pad, and it was only after we’d returned that the others started to wake up. Albeit grouchily.

Aerion trudged around muttering something under her breath, while Yashas and Richard acted like they looked, shambling around until Arianna whipped up double shots of coffee for everyone.

Both Yashas and Richard devoured it with glee, while Aerion begrudgingly forced it down. Her expression told me all I needed to know about what she thought of the beverage.

It did bring some life back into everyone, though, and things returned to a semblance of normalcy. With one major exception, of course.

Yashas and Arianna.

They tried to hide it, but the way they kept brushing shoulders and standing just a little too close gave them away to anyone who had their wits about them.

Which was still probably just me and Arianna at the moment.

The sight should’ve made me happy, and it did, a little. But it mostly just made me wonder what would happen if these two actually did return to their respective times.

Until yesterday, neither had anything connecting them to Axius. But now, Arianna had little choice unless she wanted to go back only to die, and Yashas had Arianna… Would he really be willing to abandon that for his old life?

Assuming any of us had a choice, which I very much doubted.

It was so interesting how living through a thing had a way of changing your perspective so drastically.

Before I'd been summoned, I’d never really understood why soldiers always got together right before going off to war. It felt a little callous, if I was honest, leaving someone behind like  that.

But when you don’t know if you’ll even have a tomorrow to look forward to, it put a lot of things into perspective. I certainly felt like I was more alive on Axius than I ever was back on Earth. Constant danger had that sort of effect.

Given Yashas’ state, even with coffee in him, breakfast consisted of nothing more than cereal and fruit.

Not that I was complaining. Thanks to centuries of genetic manipulation, modern fruits were a hundred times better than anything I’d tasted on Axius, and as basic as it was, I missed sugary cereal.

Once we were done, and after Yashas handed out another round of coffee to those who wanted it, we gathered for another briefing despite everyone looking very much like this was the last thing they wanted to be doing at that moment.

“Well,” Yashas said, his voice rough and hoarse, “we’ve had our fun. It’s time to get serious.”

I just barely suppressed a sigh. I’d been worried he might bring up any of the several bombshells that went off last night or that someone would be embarrassed enough to feel it needed addressing. Instead, Richard—the most likely of us to bring it up—had conveniently passed out before any of that had begun, and our two couples seemed to have silently agreed to pretend it never happened.

I honestly wasn’t sure how many of us even remembered much of what happened back there. Which was a terrifying thought, considering our superpowered nature and the damage we could wreak. At least the bar made it out relatively intact…

“Now that we’re at full strength,” Yashas continued, “we should begin planning our raid on the military faction’s headquarters.”

He went on to explain that the headquarters housed a shield generator at the very center of the complex. That shield protected the core and prevented us from attacking it when it was visible during the night.

Unfortunately for us, the thing was secured better than a nuclear bunker.

“If we bring down the field,” he said, “the core becomes vulnerable.”

“You say it like it’s such an easy thing,” I said with a wry smirk. “And besides, even if we do somehow manage to take that thing down, we still haven’t solved how to actually get up to the core. Unless you know a way to fire missiles at the thing.”

Yashas shook his head. “I am unsure how and from where their missile armament is controlled. Neither have I had the luxury of running across their control mechanism. I’m afraid locating and firing a missile might be a bigger challenge than simply flying an aircraft to the core.”

“Sounds about right,” I muttered.

“I suggest we focus on bringing down the shield first,” he said. “We can figure out how to reach the core afterward.”

Apparently, he’d seen the contraption powering the shield himself. According to his analysis—which, to be fair, was quite detailed and, considering his background, more trustworthy than anything any of us could’ve pulled off—the shield was a complex working, intricate enough that they wouldn’t be able to repair it in any reasonable timeframe if destroyed. That would give us a window of opportunity to attack the core with.

“Even still,” Richard said, “I reckon they’ll mobilize every asset they have to protect the core the moment that shield goes down. Striking fast might be prudent.”

Yashas nodded. “Indeed. Though I see little choice in the matter. It is not as though we can prepare an aircraft in advance.”

He glanced at me.

“No,” I said immediately. “If you get me a fully fueled plane, I’m reasonably sure I can get it into the air and point it in the right direction. As for landing? Forget it. But we shouldn't need to if the plan works. And the helicopter on the roof is completely off the table. I don’t think you understand how absurdly difficult it is to fly something that is actively trying to fall out of the sky.”

Planes were generally stable and could largely fly themselves once airborne and trimmed properly. Helicopters were pretty much the exact opposite, and you basically had to fight it every second to keep it from crashing.

“So there’s not much we can do beforehand to prep the field,” I continued, “unless you can locate us a suitable plane in advance.”

Yashas nodded. “I will investigate our options, though if you are unable to land it as you say, then Richard is right. We can only take action once the shield is down. This simplifies the plan. Strike the base, destroy the shield, and then proceed to commandeer the plane to strike the core. Do you, er, have a preference as to the type of plane?”

I blinked. “Something small and fast. If you can find an F-35 or something newer, I’ve probably flown it in my sims.”

Ironically, the more advanced planes got, the easier they became to fly and operate.

Luckily, said sims also included checklists for starting up those planes. Something I’d been obsessed with during my sim mania phase. I was reasonably certain I could handle the real thing, assuming we had the auth codes to unlock its systems and a few hours to figure things out.

“Then it is settled. Now, for the problem at hand. The path to the headquarters, unfortunately, is a frontal one,” Yashas continued. “The headquarters lies in an old district of the city in a subterranean complex built beneath a great temple that sits close to the Sky Tree.”

“Asakusa,” I said. “I’m familiar with the area.”

Yashas nodded. “Unfortunately, they have barricaded all roads leading there. As we approach, patrols grow heavier and heavier, and airborne gunships are present at all hours of the day. We have all seen the kind of firepower those contraptions can rain down on us.”

He got a room full of grimaces as a reply. There wasn’t a single one of us who wanted to go toe-to-toe with their military assets. Champions or not, that was a death wish. This wasn’t like those fantasy stories where magic redirected missiles on a whim. Explosive warheads were easily as devastating as any magic I’d seen. And they were fast and capable of hitting a penny from a hundred miles away.This dungeon may not have been conventional, but Aerion had nearly died to just a tiny fraction of that firepower. The military faction alone more than justified this Dungeon's rank.

Yashas exhaled slowly. “Even with the five of us, I fear there is little chance of surviving a direct assault.”

“What if we hide?” Aerion asked. “With your awareness, can we not sneak past their defenses?”

Yashas shook his head. “My ability reaches far, but even sneaking myself alone into their compound nearly cost me my life. With all five of us? I fear this is an impossible task.”

I thought about telling them of my inventory ability, but aside from the wierdness of the experience, I'd still need Yashas out to guide me even if we did store everyone else. Sneaking around would still be nearly impossible and incredibly dangerous.

“What of the subway, then?” Richard asked.

“Utterly impassable,” Arianna said flatly. “We’d be mobbed by the hordes before long.”

I’d seen that myself, and the thought of diving even deeper into that vast subway system crawling with zombies sent chills down my spine.

But then, a surface attack meant getting annihilated. Approaching from the air simply wasn’t an option and coming from below meant endless zombies swarming us from every direction.

The group broke into an intense discussion, with people throwing out idea after idea, only for them to be shot down one after another. We thought of paradropping from a plane, but that’d only tip off the enemy when we tried to steal another for the core. We thought of stealing a tank and blasting our way through, but they were slow and unwieldy, and a single missile from their gunships would end everything.

As we went through the options, it became clear that there was really only one plan that had any chance of working. No matter how harebrained it might seem.

“So,” I said slowly, “the problem with the streets is detection, right?”

Yashas nodded.

“And we don’t have anything that can tank the full force of what they can bring to bear,” I continued. “But what can they actually bring into the subway?”

“Very little,” he said with a frown. “Foot soldiers, and not many owing to the density of the shamblers. Which is, of course, the issue.”

“Right,” I replied. “In fact, I'd be willing to bet they wouldn't even know we were there. It’d really just be zombies.”

“Yes, but there are far too many of them,” he added. “We have discussed this.”

I grinned. “Too many on foot, you mean?”

He paused. “Yes…?”

“Then tell me,” I said, my grin turning downright devilish, “what would it take to get one of those trains running? And what would it take to build something that minced zombies in our path?”

The looks of shock and horror were priceless.
