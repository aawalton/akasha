Luke’s gaze drifted to the severed head of the assistant, a quiet pang of loss tightening in his chest. He turned to Kayson and exhaled deeply.

“Remember what I said earlier? Don’t move unless I give the signal.”

Kayson scratched his head and muttered another apology, barely audible.

Shaking his head, Luke gestured toward the fallen body. “Move it aside and keep watch. And Kayson—don’t make a move unless I say so. Understood?”

Without waiting for a reply, he turned back to the alchemy workshop. His mind kicked into motion.

Do they have the materials? he wondered, scanning the benches with the ease of someone who had studied the layout before.

A few minutes later, Luke returned to the most cleared workspace and laid out his findings: pine resin, lamp oil, beeswax, and powdered incense.

To anyone else, it would have looked like a mismatched pile of random ingredients. But to Luke, they were the makings of something useful—something necessary.

Kayson’s voice broke the silence. “Luke, what are you doing? I thought we were just grabbing the thunder bombs. We don’t have time for experiments.”

“This won’t take long,” Luke replied, not even glancing up. “Stand guard. Just give me a few minutes.”

Without further explanation, he grabbed one of the completed thunder bombs and began poking holes into its clay shell with practiced care.

Kayson grunted and stalked off, grumbling under his breath. He posted himself at the workshop’s edge, scanning the hall beyond. Every few seconds, his eyes flicked back to Luke.

Five minutes passed.

The anxious lines deepened on Kayson’s face. “Luke… how much longer?”

Before Luke could respond, Kayson suddenly raised a clenched fist—his silent signal to stay quiet. A moment later, the echo of armored boots thundered through the corridor, followed by the unmistakable clash of steel and furious shouting.

“Shit,” Kayson hissed. “We’re out of time!”

“Hurry. Bring the chest,” Luke said, scooping a few of his modified thunder bombs into his satchel. He grabbed the lit lantern from earlier and held it firmly in one hand as he moved toward the exit.

Despite the rising chaos, Luke’s expression remained composed—at least outwardly.

Inside, he was anything but calm.

His eyes flicked toward the satchel and the experimental bombs nestled inside. Doubt lingered in the back of his mind. Unlike the thunder bombs, these hadn’t been tested. He hadn’t had the luxury of trial and error this time.

Still, he forced the uncertainty down. Now wasn’t the time to second-guess.

“Let’s go,” he said.

With Kayson hauling the crate of bombs behind him, they slipped back into the hallway they had come from. The sounds of chaos only grew louder—metal on metal, screams of pain, barked orders turning into cries of fear.

It had only been a minute since it all began, but Luke could already picture the carnage unfolding in the imperial court.

He didn’t have to imagine for long.

Luke placed a hand on the door handle and exhaled slowly, trying to steady himself. After a moment, he pulled the door open—and chaos greeted him.

The stench of blood hit like a hammer, thick and metallic, clogging his throat. The once-sacred courtroom was drenched in red. Pools of blood soaked the floor, splattered across ornate pillars and the silken robes of fleeing officials. Screams filled the air—screams of terror, of betrayal, of death—as court members scrambled for the exits, only to be cut down from behind.

At the center of it all stood Ming Hu.

His once-pristine white robe was now streaked with gore. In his grasp was the thick neck of a man Luke recognized instantly.

The Left Minister.

The official’s bulky frame jerked violently—until a sickening crack echoed through the chamber. His body slumped like a puppet with its strings cut.

For a breathless moment, Luke’s mind went blank. He stood frozen, a spectator to the massacre.

A sharp slap to his back snapped him out of it, nearly sending him stumbling through the doorway. A strong hand yanked him back by his robe, keeping him from falling into the bloodbath.

No complaints. No questions.

Luke reached into his satchel, pulled out a modified thunder bomb, and lit the fuse using the lantern’s flame.

Then, with a sharp motion, he hurled it into the center of the room.

At first, no one noticed—too consumed by panic and bloodlust. But then a few on the outskirts caught sight of the smoking orb and started to shout.

Luke didn’t wait.

He lit another. Threw it left.

Then another—right.

Another—toward the dais.

Each time, he moved with ruthless precision, ignoring who might be in the blast zone. The fuse of the first bomb hissed, a warning of what was to come.

He lit the fifth and final bomb, then tossed the lantern aside and slammed the door shut.

But just before the doors closed completely, Luke caught a glimpse.

Riann, towering like a mountain, stood guard in the corner. Beside him, Richard Boyd—face pale but posture firm—shielded the Right Minister.

Luke’s eyes had met Richard’s for a split second.

“Fuck!”

Thick black smoke burst from behind him, seeping through the cracks. Luke grabbed the handle and pressed his weight against it.

“Hold the door!” he shouted.

Kayson, still several steps behind, snapped to attention and rushed forward, slamming his shoulder into the frame beside Luke.

Once his friend was in place, Luke recoiled, stumbling back from the door as guilt twisted his stomach. Kayson sent him a sharp, questioning look while bracing against the door, which now rattled with impact as shouting erupted from within.

“What’s wrong!?” Kayson barked, straining to hold the barricade.

“Damn it… what’s he doing in there!?”

Luke’s thoughts raced. He’d anticipated Riann would be present—it was only natural for the Master of Arms to attend a high-stakes court session like this.

But Richard? His presence changed everything.

If Riann suspected something was amiss, it made sense to bring his nephew. Better to be prepared if blood spilled.

But this?

This was not part of the plan.

If Richard dies… the Second Prince might spiral even further, Luke thought grimly, jaw clenched tight.

His breath caught in his throat.

This wasn’t in his plans.

Already, the Second Prince was teetering on the edge of breaking down. If everything went according to plan tonight—if Lyon ascended the throne—it would mean nothing if the man collapsed into despondency.

He’d already lost his mother, father, and older brother. And now… Richard might be the only person he had left.

But that wasn’t the only reason Luke felt this deep unease.

He liked Richard—the son of Master Boyd, the very man who had trained him back in Clayton City. If Richard died here, could he ever face his old teacher again?

A new wave of screams burst from the courtroom, followed by panicked coughing fits. The door behind him shook violently as fists pounded against it, desperate voices begging—screaming—to be let out.

Luke stood still, eyes locked on the door, the sound of their desperation digging into his conscience like a chisel.

Would this even work?

Even if he managed to identify every traitor in the court…

Even if he helped Lyon take the throne…

What would change?

There was still a Lhair army marching up from the south, heading straight toward Ralis.

If the Bishop was truly in contact with the traitors, wouldn’t their silence alert him? Wouldn’t he realize something had gone wrong the moment they failed to report back?

And if so… what was to stop him from ordering an all-out siege? To crush Ralis once and for all?

Was Luke struggling for nothing?

A pressure began to build in his chest, as if a serpent had wrapped itself around his heart, squeezing tighter with each hopeless thought.

Darkness crept in.

Fear. Doubt. Futility.

It coiled and spiraled until he could hardly breathe, until—

Thump.

His heart gave a mighty pulse.

[DING]

A sound like chains breaking echoed in his mind, followed by the warm rush of something washing through his body. Heat spread from his core to his limbs, like a fire rekindled after a long winter.

And with it came a familiar chime.

[The effect: Soul Curse has been negated by The Sovereign’s Blessing]

[User is now immune to Soul Curse]

“What the…” Luke whispered, staring at the glowing text that hovered before him.

A curse?

I’ve been cursed…? Since when?

His thoughts turned back to their last confrontation in the fishing town. Now that he truly considered it, something had been wrong ever since.

There’d always been a subtle fog—like a shadow hanging over him. He’d blamed it on exhaustion, on the impossible burden of their mission. And perhaps that had been part of it.

But it had gone deeper than that.

He remembered moments where Dragon’s Heart had flared to life, clearing his mind just enough to push forward. But the despair always returned—stronger each time.

Now?

Now the haze was gone.

His thoughts were sharp, his body light, as if an invisible weight had finally lifted.

His hand clenched into a fist.

Could the prince be afflicted by this Soul Curse too?

It was possible. Probable, even. But Luke didn’t know enough about the Bishop’s abilities to be sure.

He would find out. After tonight—after all of this—he would make sure.

Luke’s eyes shifted to Kayson, still pressed firmly against the double doors, keeping the chaos contained. The voices from within had grown noticeably quieter—a sign that the smoke bombs were doing their job.

Luke’s mind was clear now, razor-sharp.

“Draw your sword,” he said calmly, “and be ready to move aside. Only strike if I give the word. If we kill the wrong people now, we’ll just be making it harder for ourselves in the future.”

Kayson didn’t reply immediately. He studied Luke for a moment, then gave a firm nod. With a practiced motion, he unsheathed his sword from the scabbard at his waist and took position—both hands gripping the hilt, stance tight and prepared.

Luke stepped back to give himself space. Once the doors opened, a flood of people would spill out in panic. The first few seconds would be chaos—but also their best chance.

He took a breath. “Now!”

At his signal, Kayson pivoted and moved to the side, raising his sword high.

Crash!

The doors burst open with a deafening bang. A torrent of thick black smoke poured into the hallway, curling into the air like oily tendrils.

Through the haze, a massive figure stumbled out, coughing and heaving, trying to gulp down fresh air.

Completely blackened and soaked in sweat, the man looked up—and his eyes widened in shock.

“Lucas? Y-You…”

Recognition flickered across his face.

Luke raised a hand and made a small gesture—subtle, precise.

A faint smile touched his lips.

The smile only seemed to confuse the man further, but before he could speak—

Whump!

Steel flashed.

A sword plunged into his neck.

Blood sprayed violently into the air, coating the smoke-stained floor.

Luke flinched, bile rising in his throat.

The strike hadn’t been clean. The blade lodged deep in the thick bone of the neck, refusing to pass all the way through.

But it was enough.

Ming Hu crumpled to the ground, the sword still jutting from his neck like a flagpole planted in a fallen beast.

“Damn it…” Kayson muttered, stepping forward. He placed a boot on the dead man’s back and yanked the blade free with a grunt. “That was like trying to chop through a tree stump.”

He held up the blade, inspecting the nicked edge. “I’ll probably need a new sword after today,” he added flatly.

Luke opened his mouth, ready to scold him—but stopped.

More figures began to emerge from the smoke-filled courtroom.

His body tensed. His fingers itched toward his satchel.

Three men barrelled through the doorway, eyes wild, coughing violently.

Two of them were massive, just a bit smaller than Ming Hu. Between them was a third figure, hunched low and shielded by their bulk.

Luke’s heart skipped.

Then he exhaled in relief.

“Move to the side!” he barked, rushing forward. He grabbed the lead figure—Richard—by the shoulders, trying to shove him out of the way.

It was like pushing a stone pillar.

But Richard obeyed, stumbling aside with the other two and collapsing against the wall to recover.

“Kayson!” Luke shouted over his shoulder. “Knock out anyone else who comes through those doors—unless I say otherwise.”

Without waiting for a reply, he turned back and dropped into a crouch beside the three gasping men.

“Are you okay?” he asked quickly, scanning each of their faces. “Any injuries?”

The largest of the three men, Riann, turned and locked eyes with Luke—his expression shifting from confusion to shock.

“You?”

“Now’s not the time,” Luke replied, already scanning them for injuries. Riann and Richard were battered but intact—just cuts and bruises. But when his gaze fell on the third man, Luke froze.

His breath caught.

The man was already staring at him.

“So you are alive… Luke Drakon,” Rufus murmured, his voice raspy, his face ashen beneath the layer of soot.

Luke swallowed hard, suppressing a curse. “I’m sorry for lying to you, Right Minister.”

“Then… Prince Lyon?” Rufus asked, blinking slowly.

“He’s alive too.”

Relief bloomed across Rufus’s face. His lips curled into a smile—one of gratitude, and perhaps… redemption.

“Thank you,” he whispered.

A thin stream of blood leaked from the corner of his mouth, carving a crimson line through the soot that coated his skin. Despite being clearly on the edge of death, his expression was calm—serene.

“I hope you’ll guide our kingdom better than I did…” he murmured, before dissolving into a fit of coughs.

His mouth opened again, as if to say more—but the words never came.

His head slumped back.

Stillness followed.

“Rufus…” Riann muttered, his massive frame trembling, teeth clenched tight.

Luke felt a sharp pang of grief. But there was no time to dwell.

He reached out and placed a firm hand on Riann’s shoulder.

“I need you to identify the traitors,” he said, his voice low but resolute. “We have to purge them. Tonight. For good.”

Riann stared down at the fallen Right Minister for a few moments more. Then he rose to his full height—towering over Luke like a war god—and gave a silent nod.

Without a word, he turned and strode toward the open court doors, where Kayson was already hard at work, striking down each person who stumbled out.

More than ten lay unconscious in the hallway.

Then—

Crunch.

A sickening sound rang out.

Luke winced.

Riann’s boot hovered in the air, dripping with gore, inches above the crushed skull of a court official.
