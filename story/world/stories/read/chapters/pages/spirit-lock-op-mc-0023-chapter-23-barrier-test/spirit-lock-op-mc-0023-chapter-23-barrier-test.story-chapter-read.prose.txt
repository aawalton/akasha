Dab Grassly rolled his eyes at Hayden’s taunt, wishing that he had never accepted the job at Junolyx. It paid four times as much as the other universities, but it came at the cost of raw misery. Junolyx wasn't even a university. It was a money-buys-all daycare that powerful parents sent their children to, fully aware that if their daughter got knocked up, they wouldn’t end up with a nobody. That was all. Most students didn't even want to be rifters; they just wanted better access to immortal resources and artifacts.

It was a joke institution.

Dab thought Hayden would be different because he was a scholarship student—but nope. He had the same bold arrogance as the rest of them. No, it was worse. Hayden's spirit beast was oddly suited to the target test, but it was just a test. In real combat, it was a clear spirit—useless and pretty. Nothing more. So the fact that Hayden was arrogant was exasperating.

That’s why he didn’t even entertain the question about what would happen if Hayden won. He simply said, “Are you going to try or not?”

Hayden didn't reply. He simply lifted his hand—and his spirit beast blasted toward him.

Dab’s spirit emerged from his chest telekinetically, creating a gas before it solidified into a spherical barrier. Just for spite, he used half his strength to make it impossible to even ripple. He’d grade fairly, of course, but he really wanted to take out his frustration. So he let his petty flag fly.

Suddenly, the winged mouse rammed into the barrier, but instead of getting knocked back, it lodged itself inside it. The horix was glowing, and all the cora in the atmosphere sucked toward it at an overwhelming rate, causing Dab’s barrier to bubble and collapse. It fixed itself, but it was after the horix had flown inside.

Panicked, Dab threw up a second barrier, this one with all of his strength. The atmosphere warped and bent as the horix and his spirit fought for fuel. The two looked like they were battling in a desert mirage.

Thankfully for Dab, Gluttony lost and returned with drooping ears.

The competitors laughed at its expression—Dab didn't. He was sweating profusely as he looked at the tiny creature. What the hell is that thing? he wondered. The experience rattled him to his core. If that happened abruptly during a real fight, and Hayden was with someone packing heat, Dab would’ve died! That little mouse wasn’t a joke.

That said, the crowd didn't even notice the direness of the situation. Onlookers kept saying things like, “The first layer must be super easy.”

Dab somehow forgot that, despite being a B-rank institution, these miscreants didn’t even know how to read coran fluctuations! Beyond that, they were incredibly arrogant and delusional. The next competitor reminded him of that a moment later when he said—

“Hey! Don’t double down just because he broke your barrier.”

Dab ran his fingers through his hair. He never wanted to go easy on students again, but as a proctor, he nodded and set his barrier at his usual rate, which was half the strength he initially used for Hayden. The difference was immediately noticeable, as the barrier’s color diluted from violet to periwinkle.

Neither Aree nor the onlookers failed to notice the difference. Despite that, Aree didn't call it out.

He didn’t feel unjustified in thinking that a clear spirit couldn't break through a B-rank barrier. That was ridiculous. But it did. Now, Aree was facing a weaker barrier, and he didn’t think he could break it. It was pitiful that he accepted the handicap after a clear spirit broke through, but the stakes were too high. If he didn’t break through the barrier, all the people recording were about to post the videos publicly—on day one!

No one would ever forget it. No one would let it down. It would be a disaster!

Aree felt sweat drip down his chin. Once a drop rolled off his chin and hit the concrete, he gave it everything. His spirit sucked in cora from the atmosphere, and a huge fireball was summoned in his right hand.

It was so hot that the people near him covered their faces and stepped back.

Perfect time to strike.

He launched the fireball with prejudice, and it crashed into the barrier.

Ripple~

The crowd fell silent when his massive C-rank fireball spirit barely left a ripple in the barrier. It looked less pronounced than a pebble dropping into a fountain. In fact, the gentle waves had already disappeared before the flames dissipated. It was that pathetic.

Aree cocked his head. “Huh?”

The second the spectators saw his reaction, they erupted in a raucous celebration, as if they were praying for his downfall and had just gotten their wish. Aree didn’t even blame them—the performance was pathetic.

“Well, at least I can do better than that,” Sanni said as she walked up. One student cupped his mouth and let out an “ooh” as more bracelets lit red.

Sanni was fine with taking second, so she had absolute confidence. That was, of course, until she heard someone say, “Wouldn’t it be funny if she did worse?” Sanni started sweating after that. If she did worse, it would be grimly hilarious. In fact, if it happened to someone else, Sanni would share the video far and wide. There’s no way she would allow that!

So she lifted her hand and summoned a ferocious gale of wind. It was so aggressive that the female gossiper had to grip her skirt to keep it down. One of the men laughed and said, “Hey, Sanni! Take your time, would ya?”

Another woman slapped him, but he didn’t even care. He just kept grinning, and the other men kept watching.

The scene bothered her, so she let her frustration and rage increase the power of her spirit. It soon coalesced in a tiny sphere of compressed wind.

Sanni’s spirit—Mari—had such a grotesquely cliché name: Wind Sniper. Sanni hated it, but it was a C-rank spirit that could increase in power as long as she charged it up. So she built it up. Despite that, the barrier master didn’t look worried, so she increased it even more before aiming.

Sanni could tell that the silent onlookers were expecting her to shatter the barrier—but she didn’t share their expectations. She charged it even further—and then let it go.

Whoooosh! The bullet ripped up grass, sending it spiraling in a brutal green blender. Then it hit the barrier.

Ripple~

The expectant watchers exchanged disbelieving glances when her attack barely made it ripple more than the fireball!

“There’s no way…” Aree muttered. He looked at the sighing barrier master. “You’re cheating us.”

Dab chuckled darkly. “If first evolution attacks could break this, I’d give up.”

“Then what the hell was…” Sanni tried to jab an accusing finger at Hayden, but he was already walking off. “Hey! Don’t walk away!”

Other students ate up his reaction, saying, “One sec, I’m sooooo posting this right now,” or, “Look how devastated she looks! Heh. Sucks to suck.”

The last competitor slunk to the front, hoping to go while the others were focused on Hayden, but he found no respite. Someone yelled, “Hey! The third guy’s about to go!” Then, almost everyone rushed to complete the story.

Dab truly felt bad for the kid, but he knew if he made it even weaker, they would still fail, and the ridicule would double.

Suddenly, he received a comm link from Mayle Haya, the school's president. “Dab speaking. Yes, I mean, no… he’s already taken his test. I mean, he literally just took his test. What?” He laughed when she asked how the test went. “He melted right through it. It’s on video. No… it’s pretty dramatic. You can’t mistake it.” He chuckled with dry eyes when she asked if he knew how. “I’ll be dead serious with you right now—I have no idea.”

While Dab was speaking to Mayle, Hayden pushed his way through the crowd. Then, he stopped when he saw Kayline staring at him. Her expression was bright when she was watching him, but it quickly turned melancholy when they made eye contact.

“Dad tell you not to talk to me?” Hayden mused.

Kayline was a politician, so while people were recording, she said, “I think it's the right thing to do. I'm sorry.”

“Save your sympathy for Vestra’s mother,” Hayden said as he walked past her. “She’s ruining her empire over nothing.”
