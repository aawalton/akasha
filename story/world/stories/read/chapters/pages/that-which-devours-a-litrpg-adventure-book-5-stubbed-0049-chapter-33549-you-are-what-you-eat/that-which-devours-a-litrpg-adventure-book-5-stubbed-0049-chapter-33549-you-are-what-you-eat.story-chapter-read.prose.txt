One second, I buzzed through the air, frantically heading for the airlock, when something slammed into the side of me, making my body whimper at the pure force used. I went soaring through an open doorway before slamming into a wall.

Then it was on me. I went to dodge a strike, but instead it grabbed my wrist and all hell broke loose. One second I was in that room, and the next I saw a little boy playing with a small toy on the ground in front of me. We were in some park.

Then it yanked me toward one of the beds. The movement fractured the random images, giving me a second to catch my breath.

Where did my son go?

I yanked at the hand on my wrist, trying to get free, but the bright light wouldn’t let go.

Then I kicked at the side of it and my foot passed through the light without a problem. Instead, images of a group of people screaming as they raced across a finish line flooded my mind. Something to do with sports.

“Don’t let your Memories Fade to Dust!” The voice shattered what had to be memories. Not my memories, but memories of the humans that had uploaded themselves. The being slammed me down on a bed, turning what was left of the skeleton that occupied it before me into dust.

Not going to happen.

I swiped at the arm, and my claws caught purchase.

It screamed in jibberish, losing its form for a split second, freeing me from the bed. Parts of it appeared as a scattering of code, distracting me from leaving the room.

What was this?

I shook my head trying to clear the lingering feelings. I didn’t have a son, and I didn’t run races.

The orb flickered a few times, then brightened before darting right at me again.

“Zero Maintenance! Absolute Perfection!”

“You’re just a backed-up ghost,” I said as I sprinted to the exit, trying to flee from the thing.

The orb beat me to the door, and I swiped at it again with my claws. It suddenly dropped to the ground, as the black lines passed harmlessly over its head. Yet, the strikes hit and skidded along the ceiling and the far wall, tearing thick metal from both.

I leaped over its head and raced down the corridor, back in the direction I’d come. There was another shuttle pad on the other side of the base. Yet, what did escaping back out into space even get me?

Maybe I could find a way back into system space, but what about this place? What about Earth?

My frantic dash slowed down, as I passed another set of doors before turning a corner.

Another huge change met me, as thick steel doors had been added where there was once just an open corridor. Those doors, in turn, were now broken open, and I stepped through. There weren’t any scribbles above the shattered doors.

Just another area, picked clean.

“Body Lab, Robotics Lab…” I mumbled, going back two more doors. All we needed was a cloning lab, and I’d throw in the towel. What all was Earth willing to do to save humanity, besides fix the planet?

A light blinked at the end of the corridor, and I skidded to a stop.

Authorized Personal Only.

Bullet holes littered the area, along with old blood splatters. A small red dot appeared on the floor, then flickered to me.

I held up my hands and let the armor fall, as I quickly stepped back.

“Ascend to the Machine! Live Forever!” Came from behind me.

The laser spread into a wave and scanned over my face.

“Colonist ID# 31879, confirmed,” said a digital voice, as the door clicked open.

I dashed through the reinforced door, and it closed and locked behind me.

Suddenly, blue light condensed into a humanoid figure right next to me. “This way, Colonist #31879. You must proceed to a safer area of the base.”

“Upgrade to Unlimited!” The spooky voice shouted, coming from behind the doors, and the hologram flickered.

“It can get through these defenses. Follow me.” The hologan appeared farther down the hallway.

I dashed after them, following them when they went through doors, and then down a hidden set of steps.

Dust covered the floor as I went deeper into the moon.

Finally, the hologram appeared next to a closed door.

“You are safe here for the moment.” It bowed its head to me, then the door opened. “The others want to speak to you.”

My heart pounded as I stepped into the room. A large round table took up most of the space, and several empty chairs sat around it. Suddenly, more holograms flared into life. None were people that I recognized, though there were familiar uniforms.

“So, the colonists survived and prospered,” said someone in a military uniform. “The last ship sent, Eclipse. Colonist #317879, report.”

All of them turned to look at me.

“Report what, exactly?” I asked, shaking my head. “What the hell is that thing up there?”

One of them sighed. “Classified.”

“Given that everyone here is probably dead, that’s not going to fly,” I added.

A female voice responded from the other end of the table. “Corporate interests went a step farther than we wanted with Project Ascension. The projections we built for how long the crystalline matrix would remain stable were inadequate, partly because the corporations we trusted to meet the design requirements failed to do so. And we had more applicants than expected, pressing the limits of storage… there has been some degradation, unfortunately.”

“You mean it's forgetting people,” I said, with a low voice. “And become some sort of monster.”

“It contains the last histories of Earth, and of our people,” said another image, in a deep voice.

“Given I’m standing here, that isn’t true,” I snapped back, before they could continue.

“Where are the rest of the members of your crew?” asked another. “Protocol states no one should return to Earth until certain population goals were met. To guarantee humanity's survival.”

“What happened when the Forger’s showed up here?” I asked, trying to pull the discussion in a different direction. I was still trying to figure out how this all came to be.

“If you are speaking about the aliens that striped technology from our laboratories and returned them to Earth, then most have fled or are dead from that thing up top,” answered the female voice.

“That is Classified!” said the first voice again. “We are not having this conversation.” He cleared his voice, then continued. “So, humanity is secure, correct?”

“Yes,” I lied. These relics of the past didn’t need to know anything about what was really going on.

“Have you come into contact with any of the other missions?”

“No, we haven’t heard from anyone else,” I answered, wondering about the others floating through space. They couldn’t already be part of the system, or I wouldn’t have been the First Human since they left so much earlier. “We don’t have any clue where anyone else went. I don’t even know how long it’s been.”

“Give her the flight plans for the other missions,” said a quiet voice. “Then we should wipe this place from our history. Let them start again.”

“Christopher,” whispered one.

“No. Earth had its shot. Look at that thing in the base above. That thing isn’t human. It isn’t humanity. It isn’t a reflection of us, at all.” The image of the middle-aged, tired-looking man paused, like he was taking a breath. “We were created to make sure to destroy all of this if it didn’t work. It hasn’t. Now, we need to protect those who live from the mistakes of the dead.”

No one said anything.

“Proceed with the self-destruct of the base,” said the man again. “That is our order. We can lead her out of the base.”

The blue holograms all flicked off, except for one who flashed near the door. “You must hurry. I will take you to an exit.”

“So, this place is gonna blow?” I asked, as I followed. I didn’t think it would affect that thing above, but decided to keep my concerns to myself.

“Yes. The blast might take most of the moon with it. You must return to your ship.”

I pressed my lips together as the hologram led me to a ladder.

It didn’t take long before I stood outside an airlock.

“Take the datapad, from next to the door. May humanity always travel among the stars,” said the hologram, before vanishing.

I took the datapad, having not touched one in a long time. I couldn’t toss it into my inventory, and just held onto it instead. Taking a deep breath that I didn’t need, my armor covered my body. Then I hit the button for the airlock. I barely made it outside, when a red light started flashing behind me. Slowly at first, then faster.

My wings took me high above the base, floating in the darkness.

Then the base collapsed. It was very anticlimactic, really. The structure bent inward with a flash of light, but there was no sound in the vacuum of space.

It crunched tighter and tighter, then stopped.

The shimmering curtain in the distance didn’t move.

Maybe I could just fly into the curtain and get back to System Space. Eventually, I’d find my way back. I just needed a portal to a megacity, and those had to exist somewhere.

Light glinted deep within the crater.

I twisted around just as it shot off, right at me.

The being of light and code slammed into the side of me, tossing me back as images ransacked my mind. I lost my grip on the datapad.

Panic filled me as the shuttle to the base shook.

Flashes of red filled my vision as warning lights went off. Someone was attacking the refugee camp.

My heart was beating in my chest so very loudly, as the technician promised it wouldn’t hurt and I’d be reunited with my family shortly.

These weren’t my memories!

I swiped at the light and it retreated, causing my head to spin. Behind the creature, the shimmering curtain moved, swallowing the moon whole. It paused just beyond us. Well, just out of reach of this thing.

The orb hung there, then flashed a few times at me before it rushed me again.

I moved slower this time as my stomach rumbled. The lack of energy sapped at me, as I swiped again at the creature. It dodged, before swallowing me whole.

No.

Not this time.

Darkness surged from my armor, and I nibbled at the creature of light as soon as it touched me. It struggled.

Mentally, I apologized. But I would survive. I had promised my family I would return home.

I opened my mouth and tried to suck it down, like I did to mana in the air.

Something happened, but I couldn’t tell what.

Energy flooded my system. Tasty, tasty energy.

At that moment, I couldn’t stop if I wanted to.

The last vestiges of humanity came crashing into me. I tasted of thousands of lives, I sipped their essences, their memories, their hopes, and their broken minds.

None of them were human anymore, none of them lived, only their memories survived. The thing they had become had consumed them, had killed the last of Earth long before the Forgers came.

I devoured it all. I took the last of my people, the desperate hope of millions that had been betrayed by the very thing they had trusted, into myself. I honored them, I loved them, I grieved for them. I devoured them, and they became part of me, and they became part of the universe.

Time had no meaning. It went on and on, unending, but full of ending.

Then the light vanished, and a small black cube rested in my hands.

My head pounded as my entire body vibrated.

Then the shimmering wall slammed into me. Notifications rattled my head even more than consuming the last of humanity had. Each stabbed at the back of my eyes, and the dings made me taste copper on the back of my tongue.

Then I was through the wall.

Beings I couldn’t see, only feel, swarmed around me. Heavy presence after heavy presence filled the space. They were bigger than anything I had felt before, but the energy around each felt familiar.

Devourers, colossal devourers.

Hushed whispers that I couldn’t make out filled my mind. Only two words that rippled through the space were understandable, as something cradled me.

“Little devourer…”

A single notification broke through the pounding in my head.

[You have been summoned.]

Then there was only silence, as the darkness took over.
