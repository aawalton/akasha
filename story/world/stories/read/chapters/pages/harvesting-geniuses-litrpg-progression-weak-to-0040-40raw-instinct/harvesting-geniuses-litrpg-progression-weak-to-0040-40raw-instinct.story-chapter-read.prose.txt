Levels were the measure of how much potential a living being could draw upon in combat. The higher the level, the greater the fighting capability—strength, speed, endurance, technique, and willpower. The gap between Level 1 and 2 wasn't enormous. Between 2 and 3, not much different. But between 1 and 3? That was a considerable chasm. And what about 1 and 4?

Think of it like running. Running one meter required so little effort it barely counted. But stretching that to four meters meant four times the distance. Running four meters still wasn't hard—but it demanded four times the energy.

At Level 5, though, it was no longer simply a five-fold increase. It became an entirely different tier of difficulty. Even so, driving a combat dummy from Level 1 to 4 in a single sequence of forms—if you weren't a true grandmaster, it wasn't something you could pull off easily. The dummies' defensive output was remarkably high. They'd been specifically engineered to absorb and endure.

That was precisely why every examinee felt that fighting a combat dummy was harder than fighting an actual monster. The dummies' weapons weren't dangerous—but because of that, they could exert full force and commit everything to defense. Even Theo and Nephrin, who possessed the power to kill Level 1 through 4 monsters within five seconds, might not have been able to do the same against a combat dummy.

And right now, the combat dummy was unlocking its Level 7 output before the clock had even hit sixty seconds. Its mechanical limbs moved with blistering speed. The blunted sword in its grip slashed in a relentless barrage—but every strike remained within a true swordsman's field of vision. Jinyo didn't even need Eye of the Sage. He read the dummy's attack patterns with perfect clarity.

Against a machine-type opponent, the full power of Song of a Hundred Flowers couldn't be brought to bear.

Song of a Hundred Flowers was a sword art that influenced the opponent's emotions, thoughts, and senses—hijacking their perception of sight, smell, hearing, and taste. But a machine had none of those. This wasn't an exam that favored Jinyo. His sword art was virtually useless against it.

'These new-model dummies are seriously durable. If I don't push my Sword Intent higher, there's no way I'm cutting through. I need sharper, faster strikes.' Jinyo steadied his focus. He didn't fear burning Planar on a wooden sword. His Sword Intent only grew fiercer with each swing. The combat dummy launched a frenzied assault—its aggression fueled by monster-based movement models baked into its AI training data.

Jinyo shifted his feet, dodged every pattern it threw at him, and countered—targeting joints and hinges where its defenses were weakest.

The health bar displayed on the dummy dropped. It flickered. Within seconds, the dummy adapted—shielding its joints whenever Jinyo aimed for them. It was learning to protect itself, forcing Jinyo to strike elsewhere.

'If I can't hit the weak points, I'll just hit everything more.' Jinyo's eyes widened. The dummy's blade thrust toward him. He dodged and countered—but instead of one slash, he followed with two. The hallmark of Song of a Hundred Flowers, beyond its elegant forms, was its sheer volume of strikes—far exceeding ordinary sword arts.

Because it was a woman's sword art, blades too heavy for a woman to wield were compensated for with precision and quantity over raw power. What Jinyo held now was a wooden sword—lighter than a real blade—yet it struck with force rivaling steel, thanks to the Sword Intent sheathing the edge.

'I won't let you recover!' Jinyo surged his energy circulation and hammered the dummy without a single moment's rest. It was just a tin can. It couldn't compare to the Chimera Wolf or the terrifying Locust Demon.

Jinyo lost himself in killing the combat dummy over and over. He tuned out the cheering below the stage—which had fallen dead silent. Every jaw had dropped. No one could believe their own eyes. Especially Joden, who'd been waiting to scream curses at Jinyo's pathetic display. But who was pathetic now? Jinyo had reached Level 11 in just five minutes!

"What the hell! How is he this good?!" Joden ground his teeth. Impossible! Trash with a 0.01% Cultivation Rate had used half his time to reach the same level. This was a nightmare. He couldn't understand it. Strength, Planar, Reflexes—every single stat of Jinyo's was garbage. So who the hell was this monster?

"It can't be real. But... how would he even cheat?" Joden wanted to scream that Jinyo was a fraud. But there was no way to rig an exam like this. Even bribing a proctor would be nearly impossible. If Jinyo really was cheating, then what was Joden seeing right now—a hallucination?

"I knew it! Brother, your skills are the real deal!" Theo grinned wide at the edge of the arena, watching Jinyo's forms and the relentless continuity of his attacks. Jinyo right now was more terrifying than every other examinee combined. Not because he was overwhelmingly powerful—but because despite having less raw power than nearly everyone here, he produced results that defied belief.

Theo had lived his life in the mountains. He didn't know what "leveling" meant. When facing a powerful enemy, the only tools for gauging your opponent were instinct and observation. Theo would say it without hesitation: Jinyo was not weak. The aura he radiated without realizing it was proof that Jinyo wasn't someone to be looked down upon—but someone who deserved respect.

While Theo was busy admiring the sworn brother he'd unilaterally declared, Jinyo had already entered Level 20. At this rate, it was highly likely he'd reach Level 30 before the twenty-minute mark. Strangely, the longer Jinyo fought, the sharper his swordsmanship became. Faster. More ferocious.

A ferocity expressed through the ring of steel—or rather, wood—against the enemy. The echoing heartbeat of a mind honed to its absolute limit. Eyes that never left the opponent, never relented. Like a serpent swallowing prey with jaws stretched beyond its own mouth.

"James."

"Yes, Young Master."

"Do you know why he's this strong?" Sven asked his senior butler. During the fight against the Locust Demon, Sven had never questioned it—but now that he'd seen just how low Jinyo's stats were, the question burned. Every one of his bodyguards had more raw power than Jinyo—both in stats and total Planar. So what allowed Jinyo to fight the Locust Demon on equal footing?

"What do you believe is the most important thing in combat, Young Master?"

"Stats and Planar?"

"Stats and Planar are certainly the most critical components. But no matter how much power you possess, if your attacks don't connect—wouldn't that render it all rather meaningless?" James offered the insight but not the full answer, tossing the crystal of knowledge for Sven to work through on his own.

'Weak in power... but something else compensates for it.' Sven pondered.

"Swordsmanship, then."

"Correct. His sword mastery is extraordinarily high. And that's not all—the higher one's Psychic Power, the more efficiently one can manage Planar. A foundational sword skill like Sword Intent relies far more on mental fortitude than physical strength. He uses Psychic Power to compensate for the strength he lacks. He uses swordsmanship to replace magic."

Swordsmanship wasn't a skill—it was something you could use continuously. If your sword mastery was high enough, an ordinary attack with a slight energy boost could rival a full skill activation.

"Sword Intent... it's just a basic skill every swordsman picks up. But he's turned it into his primary weapon."

"I believe that's a deliberate choice—that for him, just that is enough. Less is more. Better than having too much and being unable to use any of it. For a swordsman of his caliber, he doesn't need magic or complex techniques. What he already holds in his hands is more than anyone could hope to match." James had seen Jinyo's fighting through security footage and dashcam recordings. But those hadn't been nearly as impressive as this live display. During the Locust Demon fight, Jinyo had barely shown his full ability because he'd had to focus on evasion. Here, the tin can was too weak for Jinyo to bother dodging.

But still... it wasn't enough!

'If he wants to break Theo's record, Sword Intent and swordsmanship alone won't cut it.' James, as a former special operations agent, possessed sharp enough eyes and sufficient experience to predict the exam's outcome. At Jinyo's current pace, reaching Level 30 within thirty minutes was practically guaranteed.

However, shattering Theo's record of Level 30 in just seventeen minutes was impossible with his current arsenal. James conceded that Jinyo was likely the most skilled swordsman in this entire exam. But so what? A Hunter's worth was judged by the ability to kill monsters. Even if you achieved the pinnacle of swordsmanship with a kitchen knife, if that kitchen knife couldn't kill monsters faster than a sword, then let it stay a kitchen knife.

'Still too slow. Even with perfect energy management, I can't beat the clock. If I could break Theo's or Nephrin's record... would Gretel be happy?' Jinyo thought to himself. The thing that occupied his mind right now wasn't the exam—it was Gretel's joy.

He wanted his children to be proud of their father. At first, he'd thought it would be enough to demonstrate superior skill, create a stir, earn recognition from the crowd. But halfway through the fight, the devil inside him whispered that it wasn't enough.

The devil murmured in his ear: Crush them. Devour them. Destroy their pride.

Attention from his son?

Recognition from the crowd??

Nonsense!

Only by trampling others' honor and seizing the spotlight for himself could the devil inside be satisfied. Jinyo felt as though he was losing his mind. His eyes grew fiercer, locked onto his opponent. He didn't just want to win—he wanted to obliterate. A total, absolute, decisive victory. To shut the mouth of every person who'd jeered or looked down on him as the man who'd brought his family name to ruin.

"Sigh. There goes my brother—his raw edge is showing." What Naho saw were smiling eyes gone mad. She was his twin sister, born from the same womb. How could she not recognize that her brother was the type whose temper ran hot and whose worst traits surfaced once he got fired up?

Jinyo extended his legs to their limit, channeling force through his legs and arms as he closed the distance. A calm, frigid aura radiated outward alongside the fragrance of flowers. The transformation happened in an instant—captured by the high-definition cameras. Antonio accidentally bit down on his lollipop and shattered it when he saw it.

"Planar... dual elements?"

CRACK!!

The dual-element Planar-infused sword hammered down. The combat dummy's frame warped from the direct heavy damage.

"James!"

"Yes, Young Master!" The senior butler rushed to the arena's edge. When he'd reviewed the video footage of the Locust Demon fight, there were details he couldn't make out due to poor camera resolution. Now Jinyo was demonstrating it live, right before his eyes.

"That's—" Nephrin rose to her feet, body trembling as she watched the display on the giant screen. As a mage, how could she fail to see it? Within this swordsman's body, two elements of Planar were interwoven seamlessly. And more than that—he was drawing on the synergy between those two elements effectively.

"This guy is... an absolute monster."

CRACK!!

Jinyo struck again, fighting to control both Flower and Moon Planar simultaneously. It made triggering Punishment Wave far easier. For every ten strikes, one Punishment Wave would fire—and the frequency would increase on its own as long as Jinyo didn't stop.

Double Impact!

Jinyo hammered away without pause. The combat dummy shuddered. If it were alive, it would have been begging for mercy—because every time Jinyo unleashed Double Impact, its circuits would seize and go offline for a brief moment.

Not enough damage? He wanted to know just how long this thing could protect itself against attacks that disregarded raw stat power entirely.

Because the thing keeping it durable was its exterior material. It was a robot. Beneath those lightweight metal plates were circuit boards. Jinyo battered it with Punishment Waves while refining his Sword Intent further. He was breaking through his own limits—increasing his striking speed and using Psychic Power to sharpen his blade's edge.

Everyone had forgotten that what Jinyo held was a practice wooden sword. Not a real blade.

CRACK!!

Jinyo slashed across the neck. The combat dummy spun and crashed onto the stage floor, going completely offline. Jinyo raised his sword to thrust again—but stopped short. The tin can had ceased all movement entirely.

"It's over?" A combat dummy would only stop moving once it reached its limit. He hadn't even realized the dummy had already hit Level 30. Jinyo turned to look at the giant screen. In that instant, the official's announcement rang out like thunder.

"Jinyo! Level 30! 18 minutes, 32 seconds!"
