Gary jumped on stage with glee. Facing him was a proud feline warrior. The moment he saw Gary, his joy evaporated.

“The baldinator!” he muttered.

“Shit,” Gary said. The opponent was a feline, so he couldn’t possibly lose his fur…right? “If I was you, I’d surrender,” he added.

Before his opponent could respond, the crowd erupted. “I’ve been waiting for you, baldinator! Make him into a sphynx cat!”

“Another scalp for the baldinator!”

“Hey, opponent, don’t you dare quit!”

Gary scratched the back of his head. “This is getting a little out of hand…” he muttered.

His opponent pointed a straight spear at Gary. “I saw your previous fight, Baldinator. Making others lose their hair is an affront to the furry tribes. For our honor, I must defeat you.”

“Okay. I must warn you, you might lose your fur. I can’t control this.”

“A furry person would never lose to a hair-remover!” Oracle Jay exclaimed, rushing forward. He had reasons to be confident. His aura ballooned to seven and a half feet outside his body, while his speed was nothing to scoff at. Gary erupted with black lightning. The feline’s spear struck sand—Gary reappeared behind him with the boom of thunder, but the oracle was already moving, dodging Gary’s thrust like he’d seen it coming.

Gary frowned. The audience cheered.

“What’s the problem, Gary Steel? Can’t catch me?” the feline asked, hopping from foot to foot. His entire body was springy and corded.

“Black Thrust,” Gary said. His body exploded forward, spear biting sharply, but Oracle jumped aside. Gary launched a flurry of attacks which the feline danced around. It was frustrating—his speed was high, but it wasn’t higher than Gary’s. The real reason he seemed faster was that he reacted to Gary’s attack the moment he started moving. No—the moment he decided which attack to use next.

Oracle Jay’s name suddenly made a lot more sense.

There’s no way he can read my mind, Gary thought, mixing feints into his strikes. The feline responded instantly. He ignored the feints and dodged the real attacks before they were even thrown. Coupled with his speed, Gary quickly found himself overwhelmed. He was forced to step back. His opponent’s white spear bit hard, reading his defense as well as his offense.

Gary thunderstepped back to make some distance. He needed to reevaluate this.

“Hahaha!” the feline cried out. “How does it feel to have your mind read, Gary Steel?”

“How are you doing this?” Gary asked.

“Your thoughts emit lightning signals outside your body. I possess a special organ that can perceive them.”

“Really!?”

The audience erupted. “That’s a load of bull, Gary Steel! Don’t believe him!”

“He’s trying to trick you! I don’t know what he’s doing, but it’s not that!”

“You should take his hair for lying to you!”

Gary pointed his spear at Oracle Jay, who was folded over laughing. “Whatever you’re doing, it won’t work forever,” he said.

Oracle wiped his tears. “You’ll discover, Gary Steel, that it’s quite the opposite.”

A loud spectator pierced the clamor. “Careful, Baldinator! That guy’s one of the tournament favorites!”

Gary felt a tinge of worry, quickly eclipsed by relief. Struggling against a favorite was much better than struggling against a random cat. He silently thanked the crowd member while settling into a combat stance.

I need to take this seriously, he thought. What do I know? He somehow responds to my moves before I even make them, but there’s no way he can read my mind. How is he doing it? Most importantly…

Throughout their battle so far, Oracle Jay had used pure spearplay. He had to have a vibration, and it must have something to do with his mind-reading trick. Gary had to figure it out before he lost.

Or just overpower it.

He rushed his opponent. “Nail Gun!” he roared. His spear blurred. Afterimages mixed with the real thing. Oracle Jay retreated slowly, step by step, dodging all the attacks by a hair’s breadth. He laughed while moving.

“Let me tell you something, Gary Steel! My powers work best against people like you. You stand no chance!”

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Gary roared, intensifying his Nail Gun.

“I could tell you, but where’s the fun in that?”

Gary thrusted repeatedly, executing hundreds of strikes, until he’d pushed Oracle Jay back by about half their allotted space in the arena. The other three battles had finished by now, but nobody had been watching them anyway. All eyes were glued on Gary. Finally, the feline jumped away, landing with a smile and light panting.

Gary, meanwhile, realized he was exhausted.

Shit.

“I commend your endurance, Gary Steel! Most people would have collapsed by now, and you can still thrust so fiercely…but even you can’t last forever! Let’s push you a little!”

Oracle Jay attacked, arriving in a flash of speed and launching his own flurry of spear strikes. They weren’t particularly powerful, but they were fast—and, most importantly, they seemed to know exactly where Gary would move next. He was once again pushed back. The Golden Bolt’s power was wasted on parries, which the enemy spear accepted, swam around, and returned. Even when Gary poured black lightning into his spear, he still couldn’t make it reach his opponent. A white spear lunged for him.

Gary roared, releasing black lightning all over his body. The feline jumped away, barely dodging it all. Gary panted hard.

“That was close! Thank God I can read your mind,” Oracle Jay laughed.

“I’ll give you something to read,” Gary growled. He charged in. This time, he didn’t strike with his spear. He just dove into his opponent’s guard like they were playing tag. Oracle Jay’s eyes widened. He ran away, but mind-reading could only be so useful.

“Stop that!” he yelled. The white spear thrust for Gary, who didn’t even try to defend. He took the strike to the shoulder, finding it heavier than expected. He sensed the bone crack but pushed through with a grimace. He pursued Oracle Jay, even sheathing the Golden Bolt to run faster. The booms of Thunder Step burst from his legs like popcorn at peak heat.

“You’re cheating! This is a spear fight!” Oracle Jay screamed. He tried to escape, but Gary kept boxing him in. Sure, he was fast, and he could predict Gary’s next move, but neither was enough to prevent Gary from thunderstepping into him.

“Get out!” Jay screamed, smashing his spear down with both hands. Gary thunderstepped into the spear. Even Oracle hadn’t seen that coming. The white spearhead overreached, but its shaft cracked down on Gary’s back. There came a thud. The audience gasped. Gary rammed his shoulder into Oracle Jay’s chest, unleashing a tremendous amount of black lightning at point-blank. The feline’s body seized, and he lost all air in his lungs. His eyes widened. There was a booming sound as he flew back like a rocket, folded at the waist, smashing hard into the arena wall.

Gary stood in the middle of their battlefield, panting heavily, his back still bent. It ached horribly. He could carry on, but he was approaching his limit, both in damage and exhaustion.

When the dust cleared, however, Oracle Jay lay on the ground, black lightning still fizzling around him.

“Winner, Gary Steel!” the deacon announced, rushing to Oracle alongside a dozen felines.

“How dare you, Gary Steel!” one of them exclaimed.

“What crudeness! Have you no honor?” another said.

Gary wiped the blood off his lip and turned to jump away. A coughing sound made him stop.

“Gary… Steel…”

He turned to find an odd creature supported upright. It resembled a feline but was completely hairless, revealing the wrinkled, pink body underneath. It was the exact opposite of the felines’ usual beauty. Though Gary had caused this, he’d be hard-pressed to recognize Oracle Jay if not for the familiar glare in his eyes—or the bed of orange hair below his feet.

Gary cupped his fists. “Well fought,” he said.

“Screw that. That wasn’t a spear-wielder’s fight! My beautiful fur… Ah!” he roared to the sky in anguish. “I demand revenge, Gary Steel! You can’t keep getting away with this! I pray you keep winning so we meet at the finals, where the same trick won’t work twice. I’ll make sure of it. For the pride of myself and all the furry tribes… I will make you pay!”

The felines roared bloodthirstily, matched by many in the audience. Other people, however, cheered for Gary instead.

“We’ll have your hair, Gary Steel!”

“Screw you guys! The baldinator did nothing wrong!”

“We’ll have your hair too!”

“Come take it, pussy cat!”

People gasped. “I don’t condone that,” Gary said quickly, his voice lost in a storm of yells.

“Those in the audience, please quieten down,” the presiding deacon said. Gary shook his head at the human and feline spectators staring each other down from across the stands. He sighed.

How did this happen…

“I’m starting to believe you’re having fun,” Cog said when Gary returned to the stands.

“I’m really not,” Gary replied.

“Are you sure? A man can only get in so much trouble before it’s clear he’s doing it on purpose.”

“It’s my vibration, Cog. I really can’t control it. It’s not like I enjoy making people bald.”

“But you do make them bald.”

“Yeah, but I don’t enjoy it.”

“How much would I have to pay you to fight wearing a plaquet reading Asim’s Hair Remedies?” Asim asked.

“Screw you, Asim. Go sacrifice a virgin or something.”

“That’s in the morning ritual.”

Gary squinted. “You’re kidding, right?”

“Of course not,” Asim replied with a straight face.

“That was amazing! How did you stop his mind-reading?” Caramel explained, rushing over to congratulate Gary.

“I didn’t. I just rushed him. He can read my mind all he wants, but he ain’t dodging a shoulder strike.”

“Was he really reading your mind?” Asim asked.

Gary cupped his chin. “It really felt so. He was dodging my moves the moment I thought of them, before I even started moving. I don’t know. Maybe he really is an oracle.”

“You can’t read minds at Birth of Aura,” Cog said.

“What’s your explanation, then?”

“I don’t know. Maybe you just got confused.”

“I didn’t get confused, Cog. He was reacting before I even moved.”

“The sequence of events can get confusing at high speed.”

“I’ve fought before, Cog. He was reacting before I moved. That’s a fact.”

“I’m just saying that’s impossible,” Cog said with a shrug. “Well, whatever. I guess the chances of you fighting him again are low, but hey; you beat a favorite! That was the fifth-ranked disciple, you know!”

“He really wasn’t so special, besides his mind-reading,” Gary said.

“I guess Feng Shen also isn’t so special if you take away everything that makes him special,” Asim said.

“Do you think you could beat him if he finds a way to counter your reckless charge?” Caramel asked.

Gary frowned. “I don’t know,” he admitted. “I still haven’t figured out how his powers work, or what his vibration is…but I did beat him. If I run into him again, I guess we’ll find out.”

“I bet he’ll train extra hard from now on,” Cog said. “Oh, no. Do you think I’ll meet him next?”

“The chance are low, but never zero,” Gary said.

The fighting day resumed. Gary had planned to depart after his battle, but he stayed a bit longer to watch Cog’s battle. Alicia woke up two hours later, healthy, dizzy, and pissed at the Lord of Pot.

“He was clearly better. He didn’t have to hit me that hard,” she said.

Gary had to agree. Most knocked-out opponents woke up after a few seconds. Alicia had needed hours. She’d come dangerously close to brain injury.

The next hour was packed with powerful spear-wielders. Feng Shen, Sui Duang, and Lin Xin all came on stage, defeating their opponents with only their fingers. Gary couldn’t really tell, as they were all holding back, but he had the feeling that Lin Xin’s power had grown a lot since the expedition. She was beating skilled spear-wielders a little too casually.

Gary also wanted to use his fingers to fight, but he obviously couldn’t have done that against the fifth-ranked disciple. Maybe in the next days.

When Cog’s turn finally came, his victory was almost disappointingly easy. He fought an anxious girl who already had a loss and eliminated her, resuming his victorious streak.

The battles were interesting, and they were growing tenser by the day as the weaker competitors were eliminated, but Gary still had to train. He departed the arena. More than one spectators greeted him on the way to the exit. One even asked for an autograph, which Gary begrudgingly granted.

Afterward, he headed directly for the clan. He needed the Nine Songs of the Spear to defeat Sui Duang—and he was so close to mastering the first song.
