Who the hell enjoys rain? Ray sure didn't, and the light drizzle when they left Luke's and Hannah's portals behind turned into heavier downfall. The rooftops were too damn slippery, damn it!

"So, you're just going to run straight into the Singer army and start whacking away? Whack, whack, whack?" Ray asked, panting a little as Curtis made it damn difficult to keep up. The guy was like a machine sometimes, a grumpy machine.

"No," Curtis said without turning back.

"What was that?"

"I said no!"

"Cool. Great talk."

Ray toggled a passive skill that granted him increased speed and dexterity before leaping off the roof of a five-story building. Rain and wind whipped around his face as the ground reached up as if it wanted to smack him, then laughed as he activated Shadow Step, which brought him into stealth and acted like a short-range teleport skill that cancelled momentum. This allowed him to land in front of Curtis and start running ahead of the old bastard. Ray wasn't about to get beaten by an old man.

Ray: "Hey, Mr. Pink. Having fun in there?"

The message didn't go through. Typical. There was always something with the communication gems, but without their phones working, they didn't have much in the way of options. Feeling his new dagger hum even in his inventory, Ray brought his attention back to the street below the lower building they'd landed on top of. There, rounding the corner. A small group of five Singers.

Ray: "Singers."

Curtis: "I see them."

Milla (Guild): "Most of you have returned to guild headquarters. Let me know if you're stuck and need help getting back."

Milla (Guild): "Phoenix Phalanx has attacked the Singers not far from their portal."

Milla (Guild): "The demons just won't stop coming either. What a mess."

Lemira: "Ray? Curtis isn't answering."

Ray: "We're a little busy."

Ah, shit, there he went. Rather than stop and assess the situation, Curtis just threw himself forward, charging toward the Singers. The dude didn't like Singers.

Lemira: "You need to return. The Singers are not the antagonists you should focus on."

Ray hurried after Curtis, still in stealth. At this point, he felt more at ease when hiding. Having people able to see him was... uncomfortable.

Ray: "What are you talking about? You want to make friends with these bastards?"

Lemira: "Of course not! I want my new home to survive. There is a greater threat than the invaders. The demons, Ray. We must stop the demons before it's too late."

Ray: "We can do both."

Lemira: "That is the proclamation of a fool. You must see reason."

Ray: "Talk with Milla. I just stab in the general direction people point me in."

The demon-lady sent another message, but Ray ignored it as he ran, dodging to avoid the small pools of darkness that dripped from Curtis's cloak when it reached all the way to the ground. The Reaver wasn't looking to take prisoners. That was fine by Ray.

Two of the Singers fell back behind three others. Like with most monsters and other Integrated, it wasn't difficult to judge what sort of opponents they were. Mages almost always wore robes or other cloth, while warriors were the ones with the huge swords. This meant choosing your targets was a simple enough business.

Ray went around Curtis, clashing with his new friends. The Reaver was once again back to the sword that made it look like he was overcompensating for something, and was hacking away at a tank with a shield covered in moss and bark.

A healer and a mage walk into a bar. The mage says, "Bartender, give me a beer!"

The bartender then nods to the healer, asking, "What about you?"

The healer doesn't answer because he is fucking dead, a dagger rammed straight through his neck.

Before the mage knew what hit her friend, she was bleeding through a few holes in her back. Their souls bled out from them and into the dagger, increasing its power while Ray bent down and picked up the Singer corpses. When the three up front realized their heals and support weren't coming, their friends would be gone without a trace.

The two kills grew the accumulation of energy gathering in one of the first skills he gained after choosing the Assassin class: Final Strike. It was simple enough. Kill stuff and fill it up, then activate it for a great finisher. What he hadn't realized at the time was how that skill also took poisons into account, multiplying their effectiveness by a lot, a whole lot. Since it was full now after those two, he saw no reason to delay using it.

Curtis was holding his own straight up, battling it out against three Singers, which Ray had to admit was impressive, but there was no reason to let it go on. The Reaver wasn't one to complain about dishonorable combat either. Having decided on his victim, he stepped out and slammed the dagger into the tank's back, activating Final Strike to punch through the Singer's armor.

The tank froze when he felt cold steel, or whatever material it was that held the souls, tear into him right over the spine. Unfortunately, some skill, or maybe Singer's physiology, kept the blade from severing the spine entirely, but it didn't matter. The tank was dead. He just didn't know it yet.

Dancing back from a retaliatory strike from one of the still somewhat uninjured Singers, Ray cursed at the stealth cooldown. The Singer had turned to face Ray, his gaze boring into Ray's eyes as he charged without as much as a battle cry.

Shying away from the intensity of that glare, Ray lobbed a couple of explosion potions into the Singer's path. With the reflexes of a damn mongoose, the sword-wielding Singer cut through them, which just happened to be what Ray expected. They blew up in the Singer's face, but that was just a diversion. A full bottle of poison followed, crashing into the Singer's chest. The brittle glass vial broke, and the liquid inside turned to gas. Once the Singer breathed that in, the fight was over. Rather than normal poison, this was the paralyzing kind he'd used fighting that huge, weirdo lion that Lemira did her chest-exploding thingy to afterward.

The Singer swordsman froze, and Ray was about to end the fight when Curtis beat him to it, cutting their opponent into two pieces right below his armpits in a blow that had to have taken out his heart, too.

"Yeesh," Ray said.

"Saved you," Curtis grunted. "You're welcome."

"Sure thing, big guy."

Curtis narrowed his eyes. "You calling me fat?"

"No way, chocolate bear," Ray said, turning to point. "More of them coming."

"Archers and mages," Curtis said.

The Reaver was right. Archers and mages. A lot of them, too. What a pain. With stealth off cooldown, Ray made himself disappear from their vision. They'd seen him, which might turn out to be a problem. Once mages knew they were looking for something they couldn't see, a lot of them had some spell to flush Ray out of invisibility, or some way of knowing where he was. It was downright unfair. He was an Assassin, let him stay hidden, damn it!

Either the new guys and gals of the invading forces were reinforcements for the smaller group, or they'd just up and stumbled upon Ray and Curtis. Either way, there were a lot of them.

"Fight?" Ray asked.

Curtis gave one of his rare grins, and his cloak extended up the back of his head, adding a hood that hid his face. "Always."

"I want a hood too," Ray complained, following after Curtis's charge. Apparently, Luke could see, or maybe it was sense, when spells were being cast. Some people had it good. No such luck for poor Ray, who had to react once the fireballs started sizzling through the air. Judging by their aim, the mages were either looking to bring the buildings down around them or hit some other invisible guy, because the attacks landed nowhere near Ray. Maybe they didn't see him? A dude could dream.

Staying back wasn't an option, so Ray kept advancing. The Singers stood shoulder to shoulder, filling the entire width of the street. Some held wands and staves, while maybe half used bows.

"Archers," Ray muttered under his breath. "I hate archers."

Most of them kept firing at Curtis. Was it called firing when using a bow? Ray wasn't sure, but he didn't care. What he did care about was how strange the whole situation was. Why only ranged fighters? Why line up like that?

An arrow whizzed by, forcing Ray to spin while moving to the side. That one could definitely see him. In that moment of movement, he'd spotted something. Looking up, it was gone, but something up there had reflected light.

Ray: "Hey, Curtis. I'm pretty sure we're walking into a trap here."

Curtis: "Yeah."

Ray: "Soooooo... should we get the hell out of said trap?"

Curtis: "The Singer portal is straight ahead. Let's go through the mages."

Ray: "That is not a great idea. Even I can tell that's what they want."

Ray: "They'll fuck us up."

Curtis just kept running toward the wall of Singers, then out of nowhere, he brought out the shitty little kick-bike he kept riding around on, and went even faster.

"Goddammit," Ray said, avoiding another arrow. Stupid archers. They all think they're so cool, but fighting from a distance is for wusses. Cheap tricks that would always fall apart as soon as you closed the distance.

Since Curtis was going, Ray figured he'd need to do the same. If shit went sideways, he could always get away. The Assassin class had plenty of options for that, too.

Speeding up, Ray set his sights on the archer who kept attacking him, aiming to do to that Singer woman what he'd done to the demon Luke couldn't handle.

"Here I go," he whispered to himself, launching himself into the air.

When aiming to assassinate someone, you either went in all quiet-like or you brought chaos with you, and this situation called for chaos.

Death from above was Ray's nickname, or at least it should have been his nickname. Raining explosion potions down on the Singers hurt a few of them, and the poison and smoke bombs that followed made the following battle nice and messy, just the way he liked it.

Unfortunately, the archer moved, and in the mess of smoke and screaming, there was no way to find her again. Instead, Ray focused on mages. Mage bastards often used barriers or magic shields, and that applied to Singers as well, especially now that they knew he was coming. Curtis was among them as well, so Ray stayed well out of reach, not wanting to lose an arm or a head. That would suck.

Getting through a barrier was tricky business, but not impossible. Most of them didn't cover the entire body, or they would break down if you applied a little pressure. The first Singer he reached was a young male one who looked like he was about to cry. Ray was a nice guy, so he made sure he couldn't ever cry again, since his barrier was laughable. It didn't even cover his neck! That was practically an invitation to have your throat slit.

Ray shook his head, moving on to the next while lobbing more explosion potions and smoke bombs at random. This one used some green-tinged energy to heal her friends. A thankless job, Ray was sure. He figured she could use a permanent break. Once she was down for the count, her heart pierced, Ray stepped over her corpse and checked in on his stamina and mana. Both were still at decent levels, meaning he could keep the party going for a little while longer before having to sneak off to recharge.

An uncomfortable sting made him lose his balance, and Ray pivoted on his left foot, ducking under a spray of fire. An arrow. A goddamn arrow! It stuck out from his right shoulder. As luck would have it, the damn thing struck bone or something, because it didn't penetrate far. He grabbed it and pulled. Screaming, he let go. That had been a bad idea. Shoving a mage out of the way, then stabbing another, he made his way to the edge of the Ray-induced chaos.

"Push it through?" he asked a male archer before ramming his dagger through his neck, killing the Singer. The arrow didn't belong to this one. No, he was sure of that. Ray recognized the arrow and knew it belonged to that woman. The one who'd spotted him through stealth.

Gritting his teeth, he grabbed the arrow again. This time, he didn't chicken out halfway through and managed to get the damn thing free. Bloor poured from the wound, and his health points dropped at a worrying speed, but it was nothing a good quality health pot couldn't fix. Drinking one, he felt the wound heal.

A damn cannonball of water slammed into Ray. It threw him to the side and out of the smoke bomb's range. Not wanting to let such an insult stand, Ray lobbed a different sort of potion toward the general vicinity from where he'd seen the water spell shoot out. Liquid fire. This fire, even the rain couldn't quench. The screams were horrifying, but an assassin couldn't be squeamish about these things.

It had been a hot minute since he spotted Curtis, but judging by the sounds of combat, he was still going at it. Not wanting to leave the Reaver behind, he couldn't just run for it, despite having successfully emerged on the other side.

Ray: "Come on, Curtis! We're not supposed to kill them all!"

Another arrow slammed into Ray, and he toppled back, falling on his ass. This one went straight into his belly, and the blood that came out was dark. Not a good sign, probably, but Ray wasn't a doctor. Unable to drink another health potion, Ray rolled to the side, avoiding another arrow. This time, he caught where they were coming from. Above.

"The window? Asshole move... asshole, he grunted, getting to his feet. He really didn't want to fight with an arrow stuck inside him, but there was no way he was pulling it out without a potion ready. It was in these sorts of situations that a healer came in handy.

"Stupid pink dungeon," he muttered as he set off running toward the building. More arrows struck the pavement, more than one person could shoot, and he looked up to see a whole bunch of archers on the rooftops on either side of the street. Just as he crashed through the locked doors to the apartment building where he'd find the archer, he spotted more Singers approaching from up the street.

Ray: "Curtis, dammit. Stop being such an asshole and get out of there. More people coming. Archers on the roof. You got a death wish or something?"

Curtis, true to form, didn't even answer.

"Asshole," Ray said to a dead security guard lying slumped over a table.

The stairs up to the sixth floor were a bitch to climb with an arrow in your belly, but he got there in the end and found his prey looking out the window. Perhaps it would've been faster to just leap into the building and attack straight on, but Ray had found he didn't much enjoy acting as a target in someone's practice. People weren't supposed to see him, damn it!

Taking it nice and slow, Ray approached the window of what had turned out to be an office building, where the Singer peered out, craning her neck. A little closer, just a little closer.

The Singer's eyes widened, and she spun, raising the bow and firing an arrow straight at him. Ray dove forward and cursed. No, he really didn't like this particular Singer.

Charging forward while the archer recovered, Ray hoped to close the distance before she could get another shot off. To his surprise, she dropped the bow and brought out a damn katana from her inventory. That made him grin. You should never bring a katana to a knife fight!

Perhaps half an hour later, Ray lay panting against a bloodied partition separating a cubicle from the rest of the open floor plan. The Singer was dead.

"Heh, easy," he said, before coughing up blood.

The health pot he'd used helped a little, but unfortunately, it hadn’t been enough to either regrow everything below his belly button or re-attach that half of him. Turned out the Singer knew what she was doing with that katana. She hadn't just been some weeb Japanophile from another world.

He coughed again and found he couldn't draw a proper breath. Someone entered the floor from the stairs, and Ray summoned a humongous helping of strength and effort to look up and see Curtis. He looked tired. Poor guy.

"Hey," Curtis said.

"Hey," Ray tried to say, but a whole lot of blood came out instead.

Some people say dying doesn't hurt.

"So, that was a fucking lie," Ray gurgled to himself as everything just kind of ended.

What a boring way to go.
