The transmission from The Aegis crackled in my earpiece, sharp and absolute. “Tell General Goliath to advance the MECH divisions faster.”

"Acknowledged, Lord Commander," I replied. The metallic tang of the Mark II-G's internal comms couldn't mask the tremor in my voice. "The vanguard is moving."

I cut the connection and let out a long, slow breath that fogged the inside of my visor. My hands felt slick on the massive control yokes. Outside the cockpit of my customized MECH, the landscape wasn’t the glassed coastline of Cinderfall. There were no rolling green hills of Aerthos.

There was just the jagged, bruised-purple wasteland of the Obsidian Dominion.

I glanced down at the tactical display. A cluster of red icons throbbed ominously in a valley just ahead. A mature dungeon, a festering wound in the earth, leaking corrupted mana. Fifty miles north of that, another one waited.

The Lord Commander was fighting a war on two fronts, staring down ancient empires across an ocean. My job was simpler. I had to smash a hole in the earth and rip out its heart. He needed the power cores. The Mage Towers of House Black could hold the southern territories, but the old lands of House Wight were vulnerable. Without the Dragon Kings to anchor the northern borders, we needed new, massive energy sources for the automated defense grids Alarion planned to install.

I checked my six. Behind me, a hundred Mark-M MECHs advanced in a perfect, ground-shaking wedge. Further back, an endless sea of Mark IV infantry automata marched in flawless sync, their blue optical sensors cutting through the Dominion twilight. They moved like a glacier of black steel, unstoppable. And they were following me.

General Goliath.

The title still felt like a borrowed coat, too big in the shoulders. When I walked through the corridors of the military hubs, the Dark Elves—soldiers, engineers, academy cadets—would stop and salute. Their eyes held a kind of reverence that made my skin crawl. To them, I was the immovable object, the unwavering shield of the Ghost of Wight.

The only Five-Star Warlord in existence.

Alarion had told me in private that it was temporary. Theramore or Valen would likely earn a fifth star for the Navy, and some aerospace prodigy would rise to command the Air Force. But for now, I was alone at the top, right under the Lord Commander himself. The air up here was thin.

I was just Bob. A farmer's son who got lucky enough to bond with a dragon and stubborn enough not to die when the world ended. I wasn’t a tactical genius like Director Nyx, or a brilliant logistician like General Mirelle. My strategy usually involved hitting the biggest threat as hard as I could until it stopped moving.

But Alarion had trusted me with the hammer. He needed me to swing it.

"Aquarius," I murmured, reaching out through the bond in my soul, a vibrant current I'd thought lost forever.

I am here, Bob, the ancient dragon's voice echoed back, warm and deep. The air is foul here. Let us clean it.

"Agreed."

I flipped the primary engagement switch on my console. The heavy, thudding footsteps behind me quickened, the rhythm shifting from a march to a ground-shaking jog that sent tremors through the rock.

We crested the ridge overlooking the valley. The dungeon was a massive, jagged tear in the bedrock, glowing with a sickly green light. Swarms of grotesque, multi-limbed horrors were already spilling out, the vanguard of a full-blown horde phase.

They saw us. A shriek tore through the valley, a sound of madness and hunger.

The anxiety in my chest, the crushing responsibility, the feeling of being an imposter—it all burned away. The cold, familiar clarity of combat washed over me. This was where I belonged. Not in a war room, but here, at the point of impact.

"All units, weapons free," I roared over the command channel, my voice amplified a thousand times by the MECH's external speakers. "Level the valley. We do not stop until the core is in our hands."

I slammed the control yokes forward. My MECH's back thrusters ignited with a deafening roar, propelling my twenty-meter frame down the slope like a falling mountain. The twin fortress-breaker cannons on my shoulders spun up, whining with lethal potential.

Behind me, the legion opened fire.

The sky turned blue with plasma. The earth shuddered and cracked. I swung the hammer, and the world broke before me.

The air inside the cargo bay of the Kantonov K-225 vibrated with the raw, deafening howl of six magitech turbines. It smelled of aviation grease, cold steel, and the rich, entirely out-of-place aroma of thousands of hot meals packed tightly into wooden crates.

Varuna adjusted the straps of her tactical harness, her fingers working methodically over the buckles. She was a Dark Elf, young but possessing a quiet, predatory focus that had caught Director Nyx’s eye during the Academy’s first intelligence culling. She wore a skin-tight nanoweave suit designed to absorb light and dampen thermal emissions. She was a scalpel, forged for precision cuts in the dark.

Flanking her near the edge of the massive rear ramp stood two Mark IV-S Specter units. They remained in standby mode, their chameleonic armor locked into a flat, non-reflective matte black that swallowed the dim lighting of the bay. They were two man-shaped holes cut out of the universe, perfectly still and completely unnerving.

"Touching down in Oakhaven," the pilot’s voice crackled over her internal earpiece, cutting through the engine noise. "Ramp opening. Good hunting, Operative Varuna."

The floor pitched slightly as the massive aircraft’s wheels hit the frozen, rutted earth of the village outskirts. The nose-jaw of the plane hissed upward, and the rear ramp slammed down into the dirt, kicking up a choking cloud of grey ash and dead, frost-brittle grass.

Freezing morning air rushed into the bay. Varuna walked down the ramp, her specialized boots rendering her footsteps completely silent against the metal and then the mud.

She took in the village of Oakhaven with a spy’s detached calculation. The place was a ruin. Slumped, rotting wooden huts leaned against each other for support. A frozen fire pit sat abandoned in the center of the square. The villagers cowered behind rain barrels and cracked doors, their faces gaunt, eyes wide with the terror of prolonged starvation. She noticed a small girl with ash-smeared hair peering around a barrel, clutching a small wooden toy tightly to her chest.

Varuna felt a flicker of pity, but she shoved it down instantly. The logistics officers stepping off the ramp behind her would handle the food distribution. Her mission lay beyond the misery of this village.

She walked straight past the trembling peasants, the two Specters gliding soundlessly at her heels. They reached the edge of the treeline bordering the main road toward the regional capital.

Varuna tapped the comm-link on her wrist. "Engaging optical camouflage."

The nanoweave of her suit flared briefly, bending the ambient light around her form. Beside her, the Specters executed the same protocol. To any villager brave enough to watch them leave, the three figures simply dissolved into thin air.

The hike to the target took two hours through dense, frozen woods, the crunch of dead leaves beneath their boots the only sound marking their passage.

Alarion’s intelligence network had pieced together a disturbing puzzle over the last year. The Lumina Imperium, the so-called neutral superpower of the continent, had "Churches of Light" scattered across every kingdom. Nyxia’s recovered academic notes from Draconia strongly suggested these structures served a secondary purpose. They were military installations, built directly over an ancient, dormant network of teleportation circles left behind by a forgotten civilization.

Varuna’s target was the local church situated on a high ridge overlooking the Oakhaven valley. It was commanded by Bishop Malachi, a mid-ranking Lumina official. To crack the Imperium's network and prove their active involvement in the war, they needed access to a portal. To get the portal, they needed the Bishop's command clearance. And to get the Bishop, they needed leverage.

The trees finally thinned, revealing the church. It was an offensive display of wealth in a region crippled by famine. High walls of pristine white marble surrounded the compound. Gilded spires caught the morning sun, reflecting a blinding light, and a shimmering, golden dome of holy wards pulsed rhythmically, protecting the grounds from unauthorized magical entry or scrying.

Varuna scaled a towering, petrified oak tree just outside the ward line, her movements fluid and practiced, finding handholds where none seemed to exist. She settled onto a thick, sturdy branch offering a clear view over the wall into the inner courtyard. The two Specters scaled adjacent trees, locking their heavy sniper rifles onto bipods and initiating their long-range surveillance suites.

Now, the hardest part of espionage began. The wait.

Spy work rarely resembled the thrilling chases depicted in stories. It consisted mostly of hours of mind-numbing boredom, fighting off muscle cramps, ignoring the biting cold that seeped through the nanoweave, and staring at a static location until your eyes burned.

She sat in the tree for eight hours. The sun tracked its slow arc across the sky. Lumina Paladins in heavy, ceremonial silver armor patrolled the walls in predictable, fifteen-minute intervals. Acolytes swept the pristine cobblestones of the courtyard. None of them ever looked out into the starving valley below.

At exactly 1800 hours, the routine finally broke.

Bishop Malachi emerged from the heavy brass doors of the main cathedral. He was a portly man, his pristine white robes straining slightly around his midsection. He wore a heavy golden chain bearing the sunburst crest of the Imperium, a symbol of his divine authority.

He walked with measured, pious steps toward a small, detached chapel near the rear of the compound. Two Paladins fell into step behind him, taking up guard positions with crossed halberds as he entered the heavy oak doors and closed them firmly.

"Specter-One," Varuna whispered over the encrypted local channel. "Switch to thermal and mana-resonance optics. Pierce the stone. Tell me what he's doing in there."

Data scrolled across her HUD as the automaton processed the image and fed it directly to her visual cortex. The thick stone of the chapel rendered into a translucent, glowing wireframe in her vision.

Inside, two distinct heat signatures glowed brightly.

Varuna frowned, adjusting her magnification. The Bishop had entered alone.

She focused the feed. The primary heat signature—the Bishop—was pacing near the altar. The second signature was kneeling in the center of the room, completely motionless. A closer look revealed the kneeling figure lacked a heartbeat, and its thermal output remained perfectly static, unnatural for a living body.

It was an illusion. A highly sophisticated, mana-dense decoy spell designed to mimic the Bishop deep in prayer, projecting a false reading to fool any internal security scrying the church might employ.

Through the thermal feed, Varuna watched the real Bishop strip off his heavy, golden-trimmed robes, tossing them carelessly over a polished wooden pew. Beneath them, he wore the drab, rough-spun wool of a common traveling merchant. He hurried to the marble altar, pressing his hand against a specific sequence of religious carvings on the side panel.

The altar slid backward on hidden tracks, revealing a dark, narrow staircase leading deep into the earth. The Bishop scurried down, and the stone slab ground shut above him, erasing any sign of his departure.

Varuna allowed a slow, predatory smile to spread across her face beneath her mask. The pious man of the cloth, who preached absolute purity and divine judgment to the unwashed masses, was sneaking out the back door in disguise.

"Specter-Two," she murmured. "Track his subterranean trajectory. Where does that tunnel lead?"

A red dot appeared on her regional map, moving slowly away from the warded church grounds. It traveled beneath the frozen earth, bypassing the perimeter walls entirely, heading toward a small, secluded hunting lodge about a mile down the forested ridge.

Nyx’s analysts had been right on the money. A man ruled by base desires always leaves a back door open. According to the intercepted financial records, that specific lodge was secretly leased under a dummy corporation with ties back to the capital. Malachi was meeting a mistress.

Varuna slipped down the trunk of the oak tree, landing silently in the snow. The Paladins guarding the chapel continued their stoic, unblinking vigil, fully believing their holy leader was locked inside, communing with the angels. They were completely unaware that the man was gone, and a shadow was currently hunting him through the woods.

"Maintain overwatch on the church," Varuna ordered the Specters, drawing a specialized, non-lethal stun dagger from her thigh sheath. "I am pursuing the target. We have our weak link."

She melted into the darkening forest, tracking the Bishop's path. The invincible, untouchable Lumina Imperium had a crack in its golden armor. Varuna just needed to slip her blade inside and twist.
