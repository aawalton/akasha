Mayle Haya was not among the people flocking into the quad because she was dealing with sixteen major lawsuits. The stress she was under was worse than the birth of her first child, and her last two divorces, combined. She was up to her neck in it, so when Keaden knocked on the door, she said, “Unless it has to do with Hayden, I don’t want to hear it.”

Unfortunately for her, he opened the door and said, “It does, ma’am.”

Mayle whipped her head toward him. “Did someone hurt him?”

“No… it’s the opposite. He’s injured at least two dozen prominent students this morning.”

She blinked rapidly, “Excuse me, what?”

He sent her a link to Kable—a comparatively indie video streaming platform. The video was titled: “Student refuses to accept defeat in blindfolded boxing. Loses 25,000 credits. Gets knocked out!”

The video was played at three times speed, showing a blooper reel of a student missing Hayden two dozen times—complete with slow motion segments. Finally, he chose defense, and Hayden knocked him out. The video ended with a close-up of the sign that read:

“Current pool size: 127,000 credits.”

Mayle stared in wonder. “He’s actually doing it…”

“Or so you’d think. The Blindfold Boxing team is coming out right now. If they win, he’s going to lose it all. If he humiliates them… who knows what'll happen. It could go either way.”

Mayle closed her eyes and groaned. “Well, let it happen. Just don't let anyone hurt him.”

“Okay. I'll have—”

“No. I want you to go.”

“Mayle, I need to—”

“Keaden. Hayden is the most important asset to Junolyx University. I'm not going to trust his safety to a guard.”

Keaden gave her a betrayed expression, but looked away. “As you wish.”

Mayle leaned back tiredly when he left. She called in another assistant.

“Yes, ma’am?”

“Who's next on the list?

“Joa and Trita. They're refusing future elixir shipments, and they are suing to break their contract on the grounds that…”

Mayle cupped her face—and groaned.

As Mayle experienced the five stages of grief in rapid succession, Hayden dodged another punch. There were two hundred people outside, many from different years, and Ms. Raina's entire classroom. Most students were jazzed up about Hayden, but a few were dead set on souring the mood.

“He’s totally cheating. He has to be.”

“Yeah, that’s what I thought. But he’s done over a hundred matches, and he’s barely taken a break.”

“Yeah. That’s how I know he’s cheating.”

“How does that even make sense?”

Hayden accepted water from Kayline, who had returned from her latest class.

“You need to stay hydrated,” she said. “Even if you kick ass, you shouldn’t do it thirsty.”

Hayden flashed her a smile. “I appreciate it.” He drank and wiped his brow. That’s when the blindfold boxing team arrived—sparking an instant surge of excitement.

They walked with blindfolds already on, wearing black robes like ancient warriors. They marched in unison as students made way for them.

“So much for ‘blindfolded,’” a student commented as they reached Hayden’s booth.

“They are blindfolded, idiot. The whole point of blindfold boxing is learning how to see cora.”

“Wait! So everyone’s been able to see this whole time?”

“Yeah, dude. That’s the whole point. Wait, did you think they were swinging blindly?”

A group broke out in laughter as the rest waited patiently for the confrontation.

Hayden accepted a handshake from the coach, a brunette who appeared to be in her late thirties.

“Tiasha Sault,” she said. “It’s a pleasure.”

“Hayden Reskco. The pleasure’s mine.”

She glanced at the sign. The bottom read, “Current pool size: 191,000.”

“You’ve won quite a few matches. It would be a shame if you lost all of that.”

“It would be, yes.”

“Well, out of respect for your goal, we’ll simply compete for vindication. No money necessary.”

Hayden regarded her with a formless expression. “While I am interested in testing my strength against your students, I’m on a time limit. If your students wish to participate, they must bet.”

Tiasha’s face cramped when the thronging watchers let out elongated “oohs.”

“You seriously want to bet your earnings… on fighting the blindfold boxing team?” she asked. “You get that we’re trained for this, right?”

“Yes.”

“And you think you beat us… is that correct?”

“I’ll risk my life on winnable odds—but I’ll never gamble.”

“So that’s a yes?”

“Correct.”

A young blonde strode up to the fighting line, kindling the fire. “Forget the tournament style. If this boy thinks he’s the best, let's go straight to the top.”

Those in the blindfold boxing team grinned. “That poor soul.”

“Right? Aysha’s pissed.”

Hayden didn't show the slightest emotion. “As long as you have one thousand credits, we can do this in any order you wish.”

Aysha scoffed. “One thousand? I have pride. If I lose, I’ll double your pool.”

Hayden raised an eyebrow and turned to Tiasha to see if she would allow Aysha to match the bet.

Tiasha nodded. “If you have the money, I’ll allow it.”

“I have the money.”

The students were confused. “I thought she was a scholarship student?”

“Bro, scholarships get paid to come here. And besides, it’s only two hundred K. Even Hayden has that much!”

That crack lit up the audience.

-

Aysha Cinet blocked them out. She wasn’t strapped for money. She was paid two million credits a year to attend Junolyx, with two hundred thousand for every meet won. So while she wasn't rich, she could afford to protect her pride! That said, she wasn't foolish enough to disregard Hayden’s two hundred wins—or the videos of him crushing Vestra and Jordie. Hayden would demolish her without blindfold boxing’s rules and limitations. So she had to be cautious.

“Since this is a blindfold boxing matter, will you agree to normal rules?” Tiasha asked.

“Yes,” he confirmed. “She can use the equalizer and illusions.” The equalizer referred to the use of aura to balance the speed and power of men and women, allowing them to compete on equal grounds despite biological differences between the sexes.

“First off, I refuse,” Aysha said. “You’ve been doing this all day. Second off, I don’t know how strong you are, so I can’t equalize.”

“You can use all aura without restriction.”

A rapturous cry broke out in the crowd. Telling Aysha she could use her full power was a direct provocation—especially in his position. It was the ultimate form of shit talking, and the crowd was eating it up.

“How does that even make sense?” Aysha snapped. “You don’t have your gate open. I can see your breathing.”

“You’re seeing what I’m showing you,” Hayden said. “Now, please. I insist. If you want to bet such a quantity, you should do so at peak performance.”

“Hayden!” Lina hissed over the hyped up crowd. “She’s a world champion, idiot! Not everyone’s…” She was clearly going to say worthless here, but wisely stopped herself. “There are some talented people here,” she said instead. “Okay? And she’s talented!”

Hayden ignored her, keeping eye contact with Aysha, who was trembling in anger.

“So what?” Aysha asked. “I should be at peak performance to bet two hundred K, but when you do it, you can fight after two hundred matches and no open gates?”

“That’s what I’ve been doing all day, yes,” Hayden said. “This is how the bet’s designed.”

Aysha silently glowered at him. “Fine. I’ll accept your rules. But I’m not using aura or illusions. It’s a matter of pride.”

“That’s fine. Do you have a blindfold?”

“Yes,” Tiasha said, handing him one.

“Thank you.”

Aysha toed the line, ready to strike. She was already blindfolded, but she found the line easily—because she could see. In fact, she could see better through coran sight than with her normal eyes. She could even see through solid objects, under certain conditions. Normal eyes were a hindrance. That’s why she always wore her blindfold.

Hayden could clearly see blindfolded, too, so it was a matter of boxing—and she felt confident in her skills.

Aysha was a blindfold boxing world champion on her home planet; this man wasn’t even in a club—he was a charity import. She knew because people were messaging her about him nonstop! She ignored them, but her friends warned her that students were claiming that the blindfold boxing team was “too scared” to show up. That’s how the team got involved. It was a matter of vindication.

Now, after Hayden’s arrogant response—it was personal.

“Are you ready?” she asked.

Hayden nodded. “Yes. Attack or defense?” He squared up in a strange stance she had never seen before, but it was defined enough to scream “professionally taught” so she didn’t take any chances.

“Since this is a major bet—can we do both? A third round as a tie breaker?”

“That’s reasonable.”

“Then I’ll start with attack.”

“On you.”
