One and a half day until the invasion quest,

Gary stepped out of the arena. Despite having lost, he was on a high. The reward ceremony had helped, too.

“Hey, Baldinator!” some guy called. “Well fought!”

Gary smiled and nodded. More audience members rushed over to congratulate him.

“How much do you practice?” someone asked.

“All of it,” Gary replied.

“How did you stay standing after that hit by Sui Duang?”

“I have something that will always keep me standing.”

The crowd exclaimed. “Is it a woman?” someone asked, but Gary only laughed.

“Gary!” Chloe said, rushing over and hugging him. “It was such a relief to see you not fighting for your life for once. Second place in the whole clan? You did amazing!”

The crowd gasped. “That’s her!” someone said.

“She’s pretty hot,” another whispered.

“And wily,” a third person added, much to his own companion’s distaste.

“It’s not like first place, but I’m satisfied,” Gary replied with a smile.

“Come on, everyone wants a word with you,” Chloe said, grabbing Gary’s hand and leading him away.

“Take good care of our champion!” someone shouted behind them.

Gary was pulled into a circle consisting of Cog, Alicia, Asim, Torga Ba, Caramel, Pop Ear, Helen. Even Marla Jackson, the leader of the newly-established Earth Alliance, was here. Everyone wore wide smiles.

“Well done,” Torga said in his deep voice, and everyone exploded with congratulations. Gary laughed. “Today is my treat!” he announced.

“Then we have to get a table!” Cog said, running off. Everyone laughed again. They strolled to the Bad Babes, chatting animatedly all the while, and sat to Cog’s table.

“This was the last one!” Cog informed them.

“Great job, Cog,” Chloe said.

“Are you interested in becoming my assistant?” Marla asked.

“No!” Cog replied.

“I am,” Helen said.

“Sorry, sweetie, only drafters,” Marla replied.

“Cog isn’t a drafter!”

“I was so glad when you beat that asshole,” Caramel said, raising her cup. “Down with the families.”

“Down with the families,” everyone cheered.

“Yes, a round of tulumian beer for everyone,” Gary was telling the waiter.

“I’m not drinking that,” Marla said.

“Fine. A round of tulumian plus a normal beer.”

“I don’t drink beer either,” she said.

“What do you drink, Marla?”

“Just water for me, thanks.”

“Two tulumians for me,” Torga said, receiving raised brows. When the order arrived, he immediately chugged the first beer, then drank the second like a normal person.

“What was that?” Asim asked.

“I was a mercenary before the drafting. We always dunked the first drink,” Torga said.

“So you were drafted before the drafting,” Cog said. No one laughed. “It was funny!” he said, crossing his arms.

“Why did you dunk the first drink?” Chloe asked.

“To save time. We never knew when we’d be called up, so we had to get drunk asap.”

“Torga, that’s silly! What if you were summoned while drunk?”

“That was part of the point. Better to fight drunk than to remember everything afterwards. Sudan was brutal. It’s probably better now.”

“Were you from there?” Asim asked.

“I’m Nigerian. There was nothing to save back home.”

“What were you saving in Sudan?”

“Human decency.”

Somber silence fell for a moment.

“Check it out. I got antennas!” Cog said, sticking two forks in the gear behind his head.

The waiter arrived then, casually carrying two massive disks over his head. A pre-drafting human would struggle with even one. Food and beverages were laid out, covering the table from one end to the other.

There was a mound of roasted glyan—a sort of pinkish meat garnished with orange, lying in a puddle of an unknown black sauce. There was a tall cylinder with isolated ice on the inside to keep the many liters of Tulumian beer around it frozen—though the drinks they ordered had already arrived. A heap of hut urchins lay in the middle, along with salad consisting of known and unknown vegetables. Dimple-shaped nuts were scattered over it. Multiple other plates surrounded the main ones, ranging from tiny pillows of melted cheese to bite-sized pieces of bread. The waiter placed a set of chopsticks beside this last plate, for whatever reason.

“How did you know all these dishes? Chaters are my favorite!” Cog said.

“I told the waiter to bring whatever he wants. I don’t even know what’s Chaters,” Gary said, laughing.

“Ludicrous,” Cog said, using the chopsticks to dive each piece of bread into the black sauce under the roasted glyan. When he was done, they sparkled. He put the chopsticks aside and threw a piece to his mouth. “Delicious!” he exclaimed. The sound of gears grinding bread echoed.

Gary tried. The bread was spongy, almost foamy, while the black sauce it was soaked in carried meat and intense tanginess. Both bread and meat diverged from their earthen varieties, making the combination a novel experience in Gary’s mouth.

“This is so good,” he said.

“Right? It’s an acquired taste, but you’re a fast learner.”

“What is this shit?” Chloe said, spitting it out.

“An acquired taste.”

“Hey, try the cheese!” Asim exclaimed. Gary did, and the melted cheese stuck to his tongue, trapping him in a world of intense taste and comforting heat. His eyes went wide.

“Whaf if thif?” he muttered while still chewing.

“Hug cheese! That’s my favorite!” Alicia said, popping two pieces at the same time.

“These are all amazing. Are they easy to make?” Marla asked.

“Chasers, no, but the cheese is,” Alicia replied.

“Can you come over and teach my people?”

“There are recipe books,” Asim said.

“It’s not the same.”

“I’d be happy to come over anytime,” Alicia said.

“Maybe after the invasion quest?” Chloe said.

“Don’t be a downer, Chloe. We’ll do it during the quest. We’re all team Gymnasium, right? If it’s an army versus army situation, it should last months,” Cog said.

“I still don’t know my team, but sure,” Chloe said.

“Are tower-wide events rare?” Marla asked.

“Not as rare as they used to be. I think the last one was fifty years ago, when floor fifty-three disappeared and the High Hand recruited toppers to pull it back from the void,” Cog said.

“What?” Gary said.

“How did people climb without that level?” Chloe asked.

“And what happened to the people already on it?” Marla added.

“Teleporters. Most of the climbers were extracted. The regulars all died, I think,” Cog said.

“That’s awful!”

“They didn’t all die! A grand topper broke into the floor and saved a lot of them,” Alicia said.

“I’m glad,” Marla said.

“Brave,” Torga said.

Beer poured out and food poured in. The conversation turned lighter, ranging from battles of the tournament to people’s favorite food. Cog ordered an extra couple dishes that impressed everyone. Especially a kind of salad that was a single purple leaf. Its texture was like old paper, but biting into it released a flood of tastes into the roof of Gary’s mouth.

“I knew you’d love that!” Cog said.

Conversation flowed easy. More food and drinks arrived gradually as the hours bled into each other. They sat at the Bad Babes’ courtyard, so they could watch the blue sky giving way to orange, then pink. By dinner time, they were still eating and drinking, loose from the alcohol in their system. Cog wore more forks as antennas and pretended to be a lobster on the stone floor. Gary slapped the table in laughter.

He could feel all the stress and pain falling off of him. The tension, the training, the endless work and pressure, all dissolved in the way of fun and good company. He wanted to be here. For the first time in weeks, he was doing exactly what he wanted to do. He didn’t want to leave.

“We won’t be getting that dinner, huh?” Alicia said casually, sliding in the empty seat next to Gary.

“What are you talking about? We’re right here!” he replied, laughing as he turned to talk with someone else.

Chloe slipped next to Alicia. “He’s just busy,” she said.

“He’s always busy,” Alicia replied.

“My mother once told me that if I don’t fit, I can’t fit.”

“What’s that supposed to mean?”

“It means that this day is fun and you should enjoy it,” Chloe said, squeezing Alicia’s shoulder. She gave a sympathetic smile. “It will be as it’s meant to be.”

Alicia’s eyes fell. “Do you think I was too slow?” she asked.

“Maybe. But, I think that nothing would have changed even if you’d acted faster.”

“I see… In the end, life is like cultivation. You need power to snatch opportunities. If not, you waste away…” Her voice lowered towards the end, until it disappeared completely. The train of thought continued only in her mind. Suddenly, everyone at the table turned towards Alicia.

“Did you just have an enlightenment?” Gary asked from beside her.

“Yes,” she replied.

“We need to celebrate! One more round of drinks on Gary!” Cog said.

“What was it?” Chloe asked.

“Hard can be soft, and soft can be hard…” Alicia said.

“That’s great news! Congratulations! Everyone, let’s drink to Alicia,” Marla said, raising her cup. Everyone followed.

“To Alicia!”

When the group finally dispersed, it was deep in the night. Gary stumbled to his cabin. Even with his high Vitality, he’d still gotten a buzz. As he fell on his bed, he couldn’t stop thinking about today. How long had it been since he had such fun?

“Are you here, Goldie?” he asked.

“Always, silly. What is it?” she replied, escaping his sleeve.

“My memories from before the drafting are getting weaker… My old friends feel distant. I don’t remember much of my hobbies. I don’t even remember my password at work. I love this world. Does that make me a bad person, Goldie?”

She hugged his face, golden scales smoothly cupping the tears. “There’s nothing wrong with loving, silly,” she said. “The past is the past. You’re here now, and I’m glad for it.”

“So am I, Goldie,” he replied, petting her softly.

“We’re still getting revenge, right?”

“Of course. They still killed billions, and someone must make them pay. Two someones.”

Goldie snuggled tighter around his nose, leaving it open for breathing. For the first time, she slept on him instead of inside the spear. Gary snored loudly.

Tomorrow, they’d travel to the fourth floor. But today, just for once, Gary had experienced life. And it was fun.
