The silence that followed was long and heavy.

Vir stared at the terrible machine, at the chains and pipes that ran its length, and thought about a girl he’d never met. A girl who, contrary to everything he’d ever known about prana swarms, might not be entirely gone.

Then again, what did he know about prana swarms? The one that followed them even now, quietly exploring the upper reaches of the cavernous space, had already disproven everything he thought he knew.

Is she aware? he wondered. Does she know where she is? Or that her father is in here?

To have lived for millennia in that form… if she truly remained inside, it must have been a tortured existence. Perishing in the Fall would have been a mercy by comparison.

Vir couldn’t imagine how Janak had lived with that knowledge all this time.

“You believe she can be brought back,” Vir said.

“I do,” Janak replied without a trace of doubt.

And yet, if such a thing was even possible, would she return whole? Or would she remember her time as a Swarm? Vir didn’t dare voice that concern. Instead, he closed his eyes and forced the roiling thoughts from his mind. There would be time to process the ramifications of Janak’s revelations later.

Right now, something else demanded his attention.

“Explain to me how this works,” he said, nodding toward the machine. “How exactly does this restore the realms?”

Janak approached until he stood close enough for Vir to feel the warmth radiating from him. The old god placed his palm on the chestplate of Vir’s Artifact armor, directly above his tattoo.

“Eight spokes.” Janak traced the outline of Vir’s birthmark in the air, and wherever his finger passed, white fire followed. “Representing the eight Ultimate Bloodline Arts, each slotting into a spoke on the wheel. This, you already know.”

“Eight circles at its periphery. Earth, Water, Fire, Wind.” His finger moved from one to another, creating small burning dots of different colors. “Ice, Lightning, Shadow, Ash.”

Vir followed the gesture, seeing the mark as he never had before. Not as decoration or ritual, but as something practical.

How had I never noticed?

He’d lived with the tattoo so long he’d nearly forgotten it was there.

“At its center,” Janak continued, “three circles to represent the three realms, and the triangle to bind them together.”

“Your purpose has been etched onto your chest since the day you were born,” Janak said, stepping back but leaving the diagram floating in the air. “You simply did not know how to read it.”

Vir chuckled, surprising himself as much as everyone else.

“Does something about this amuse you?” Janak asked, raising a brow.

Vir blinked. The gesture looked far too familiar—almost exactly how he did it himself. Could something like that really carry across incarnations?

“Only that I had to search entire universes to learn what had been carved into my body all along,” Vir said. “Every time I idly traced the mark as a child, I was touching my own destiny.”

“Indeed. As for the how of things, what I’m about to say will go against much of what you know about magic. This is not simply magic, but thaumaturgy on a level the realms haven’t seen in millennia. The pinnacle of Imperium advancement.”

“Try us,” Maiya said, standing straighter.

Vir knew it was an act.

Janak confirming his own death hadn’t unsettled Vir nearly as much as he thought it would have. Maybe because he’d been seeing the nightmares for years. Maybe, somewhere deep down, he’d always known.

But Maiya?

She would never accept this. The tremor in her hand when Vir took it told him everything. She was listening only so she could find another way. A flaw in Janak’s plan.

“Once activated, the machine will pull all the prana in the Ashen Realm into it, using this spire as a conduit,” Janak said. With a wave of his hand, a projection of the central spire appeared in the air before them. “Yet no structure, no matter how colossal, can channel such an impossible quantity of energy alone. The machine therefore leverages the Imperium infrastructure to network them together. Every spire in Mahādi will act as a collector, accumulating unimaginable amounts of Ash prana from the realm.”

“Incredible,” Vir murmured, barely able to comprehend it.

Saunak would have given anything for an hour to study this machine.

Vir doubted the old thaumaturge would ever get the chance.

“Impressive in scale,” Janak continued, “but the true miracle comes later. The prana collection occurs automatically over several hours, overseen by Ashani and myself. The purpose is not to collapse the Ash, but to channel all of this prana through the Akh Nara’s body and give rise to something new.”

“The crystallization of a concept.”

“What concept?” Vir asked.

“The idea of another world. A world where the Ash no longer exists. An illusion so detailed it rivals reality.”

“But it’s still an illusion,” Maiya said flatly.

“Indeed,” Janak replied. “Which is why all eight Ultimate Arts are required to manifest it. As you might imagine, the process takes an extreme toll on the Akh Nara’s body.”

Maiya’s grip tightened around Vir’s hand.

“Garga’s Aspect of the Demon God and the Panav’s Yuma’s Embrace protect the body and mind during the process. Without them, the prana overload would reduce the Akh Nara to ash in seconds. Even with them, his body will fail shortly after the process concludes.”

Vir remained silent.

“As for tools to aid him, Iksana’s Clarity provides the foresight needed to navigate the torrent of prana and guide it toward the desired outcome.”

“Meanwhile, the Chitran’s Demonic Overlord synchronizes his prana with the machine, allowing him to expand beyond the limits of his mortal frame to control the creation of the new world.”

“Baira’s Balancer of the Scales links with the machine as well, broadening its influence to shape the realm itself. With gravity, you will form its mountains and valleys. Its plains and deserts. The exact shape of this new world is yours to decide, though your Crown Chakra world can serve as a blueprint if you wish to replicate ours.”

Vir struggled to grasp the implications.

This wasn’t merely undoing the Ash. It wasn’t simply creating a world.

It was shaping reality itself.

“Aindri’s Jungal Beckons forms another pillar of the process,” Janak continued. “Seeding the realm with life. For a world without living things is hardly a world at all. The art will plant the first roots and draw the first breaths.”

Vir stiffened at that. “What about the people already living in the realms?” he asked. “What happens to them if this succeeds?”

Janak pursed his lips.

“Once you link with the machine, your Crown Chakra awareness will expand. Where it was once barren, you will perceive every living being. The Chakra mindscape was always meant to prepare you for this moment.”

“You will see the core behind every life. Their physical forms will be born anew, but their souls can be transferred—if you choose.”

Vir waited for the catch, and as sure as the Ash itself, Janak's next words delivered it.

“It will be nearly impossible to transfer every soul,” the old god said. “Not due to prana, mind you, but time. Even with my predecessor’s invention enhancing you, your body will fail before you can process them all. I suggest saving those you hold dear first. As for animals… I would only transfer the bare minimum required to repopulate,” he said, staring up at the machine for a moment. “My only ask is that you prioritize one above all others.”

“That’s how you plan to save Siya,” she said. “Isn't it? By having Vir move her soul into the new world.”

“Indeed,” Janak said quietly. “As for the Panav’s Ultimate Sacrifice…”

Vir finished the thought.

“My soul for the world’s.”

Janak nodded.

“A sacrifice must give the illusion the weight required to become real.”

“But it’s still an illusion,” Vir said. “No matter how detailed.”

“Yes,” Janak replied. “Which is why Iksana’s Reality Inversion is the central pillar of the process. While the art normally functions on a limited scale, when fed by all the prana of the Ash, it can even manifest such a grand illusion.”

The chamber fell silent once again.

“You said you had a workaround,” Vir said at length.

“I do,” Janak replied. “But that discussion can wait. You already have much to process.”

“I’d rather hear it now.”

Janak sighed.

“Over the years, I have reverse engineered something similar to my predecessor’s art. Not as an inscription, but as an extension to the machine.”

“What aren’t you telling us?” Maiya asked, her voice trembling.

“Only that another soul is required to complete the process,” Janak replied with a sad smile. “Mine.”

Vir’s eyes bulged. To lose both him and the ancient deity would be a blow the realms could hardly afford. He was about to reject Janak's plan when Maiya spoke.

“How can you be sure the enemy hasn’t foreseen your plan?”

Janak replied with a twisted smile. “On the contrary, I am all but certain they have.”

Vir’s frowned in confusion. “Then…”

“They will surely interfere. Or perhaps they have already foreseen its failure. In any case, it is our only hope. There is no other recourse.”

The proclamation left Vir and Maiya with no response.

“There are quarters prepared for you,” Janak said, suddenly sounding very old. “Rest. And when you are ready, enter your Chakra mindscape. Simulate the process. Now that you know what is required, you will know what to prepare for.”

Maiya held his gaze for a long moment. “If this works, if all of this somehow works,” she said, gesturing at the device, “you will both be dead.”

Janak glanced at Vir before meeting Maiya’s eyes.

"Make your peace,” he said. “Two lives to save the realms. A worthy trade.”

Then, in a softer voice, he added, “It is a blessing that she is here with you. You have the chance to say goodbye.”

Vir had nothing to say to that. It was all so much to take in... he needed time to absorb all of this.

“It is more than I was ever afforded,” Janak said, turning toward the machine. “Whether our gambit succeeds or fails, I will never see my daughter again.”

Vir didn’t look at his face. He didn’t need to.

At that moment, he imagined Janak looked less like a god and more like a grieving old man.

Vir thought of Siya eternally circling the tower, and what five thousand years of living with that must have done to Janak.

He thought about Maiya’s hand gripping his tightly, as though he might disappear if she let go.

Vir didn’t answer her questioning gaze, for he wasn’t ready to face her. Not ready to ask her to accept this.

How could he, when he himself hadn’t?

Instead, Vir gazed up at the machine of chains and pipes and black steel and finally accepted what it truly was.

Not a weapon nor a miracle.

Just a mark on the cosmic timeline.

The place where he would end... and a new world would begin.
