Deep in the core world of Remaria...

Revarik was pacing.

He never paced. Not like this.

A woman in sleek black armor stood silently, gazing at the specter of the nascent god, pacing. She noted his calm demeanor had shifted. He was agitated about something. Something even she didn't know about. She stood silently in his command quarters, a building of stone, steel and towering over the landscape of the core world. large pristine windows allowed a sweeping view of the outside world. When she glanced up, she could see the fissures of light from above them, outside the outermost orbital layer. Sunlight was hard to filter down this far--but the solar towers did work to keep a relatively stable night and day cycle.

For the ones that hadn't been shattered by an ancient calamity, at any rate.

The rooms key feature, aside from the view, was the magical hologram of Remaria, emanating from a central projector of dancing lights and particles suspended in midair. All the fragmented tectonic masses were on display in front of them, and a pale blue glow emanated from the display. the magitech visor on her helmet kept the glare from being irritating.

And they were alone. Few came to this room. None but for those Revarik trusted. Being in this room wasn't a privilege to her.

It was a curse.

She waited for him to speak. This was how it always worked: he summoned her, and she listened. Then, she carried out actions.

Each time she did, her life continued. For others, it didn't.

Revarik took a good, long moment, and glanced her way. Crow black feathers and ruby red eyes accenting his face, and a dark colored beak to match. He was tall, broad, and powerful for an Aveeran. He was the pinnacle of his species, and even some of the Radiants. He clenched his beak and pointed at the largest tectonic mass in orbit. She recognized it, from her lessons. From their plans for unifying the world.

Valtiria. A prized gem of resources, arable land, a wilderness punctuated with some civilization, and a frontier unexplored. She knew this from her lessons. For this land mass, the thirteen others in the outermost orbit, and the fragments of this layered world. Revarik opened his beak to speak, then faltered. She tilted her head to the side, quizzically.

This was not normal for him. Should she dare initiate the conversation? Speaking out of turn carried risk. And consequence. lingering pains and haunting memories could attest to that. But she spoke, slowly. "You wish for my assessment, Revarik?"

No title. No fancy honorifics. He allowed her to speak simply in his presence; one small  window to expressing herself, unimpeded.

"A month and a half ago, I destroyed the last obstacle in our way to expanding our influence to the outer layer of Remaria. I laid the groundwork for our success." He spoke nothing of the out-of-turn conversation.

You killed your sister, you psychotic son of a bitch. Ten years of being his puppet could not dim the rage she felt, the wrongness of his actions on too many occasions. She allowed herself to cling to those few parts of herself she knew were her own. He couldn't cut them out of her, he couldn't force her to unlearn them, he couldn't change everything about her.

He couldn't make her forget. Not entirely. Even though he tried.

She waited for him to continue, and his intense face softened, his eyes less aggressive. "You told me it was a mistake," he concluded.

"I assessed the situation. Telga, the Radiant of Balance, was effectively neutralized. Her power was a fraction of what it was. Even with her regrettable evasion of our security." She said nothing more about that failure.

A scar, running across her back, throbbed with phantom pain, while Revarik paced slowly tracing his hands errantly across the projection. The lines and landmasses distorted and rippled with his hand running through them like a shark fin through water, then reformed after passing through them. "Yet I proceeded. Telga did something she hadn't done in thirty years: she brought in arrivals from Earth. One, a woman of intensity. I could sense her anger. The other...absorbed the primal Etteria. The one that I preferred to keep close."

She flinched, turning her head away. You made me do it. Revarik was there in a second, intimately close. He brought one clawed hand to her chin, and gently nudged her to look him in the eyes. She dared not move further. "I think you would have been right. Telga was powerless. I could have left her there in exile. She'd have fretted about, trying to bring others to answer her call. And there, she would have stayed. No allies would have come to her call."

Revarik let her go, and she allowed herself to swallow uncomfortably when he turned. That invasion of space still provoked a reaction in her she could not suppress. No matter how hard she tried. "The two individuals, Telga and others, survived the destruction of the sanctuary in orbit. They retreated to a hidden location, unknown to me. This information did not come to light until this morning. They are alive, in Valtiria."

He twisted a dial, and the image distorted and reformed to a bird's eye view of a quaint forest town, with towering trees the highlight of the vista. The sight inspired a longing to see the surface, where sunlight was unfettered, where greenery was plentiful.

Where freedom was possible for many. She creaked in her armor, her limbs covered in lighter garb that didn't weigh as much. "Why was this not brought to me the second it was made available?"

Revarik smiled faintly. "Because I don't want them dead."

She tilted her head again. "May I ask why?" If Revarik wanted someone alive, they must have really enraged him. Yet, Revarik stood calm on his clawed feet, his feathers smoothed and his wing posture relaxed.

"The two they brought. They're talented. They might be able to help me solve some technical problems. As it were, I've received word from my agent they've begun building and rallying nearby villages. They've beaten back the monster incursions, reopened a nearby mine, and are building actively. A fusion of magic and technology, from what I've gathered. And, I think they are repurposing old tech, from the old world. Before the Great Sundering."

She dared to ask another question. "Who are they? Summons from Earth? I can only logically conclude that your sister--"

A shadowy mist wrapped around his hand, and he turned around slowly, face remaining remarkably composed. He walked up to her again, peering at her with the intensity of a raptor. "I have no sister."

Liar. You just can't admit it. That you have regret. the gaze between the two held for a handful of seconds. "I can only logically conclude that Telga used her knowledge of portal magic to bring them in. But two at once? That's quite strange, from what you've told me."

His posture relaxed, and he waved to the display. "I want you to bring them to me. Alive, and certainly not beyond repair when you do. Kill everyone else, afterward."

"I will need more than two individuals and a vague description." She tensed her fingers, and pointed at the image. "This is outside our control. there are no direct teleports to this region. Not unguarded."

Revarik smiled, and changed the image again. She saw an image of a male Aveeran with white and green feathers in a small, animated visual of him the ground, talking to Telga quietly outside of a retrofitted smithy shop. It appeared the building had been expanded upon, based on the brickwork and other construction materials nearby. She peered closely at the man--powerfully built, and standing with Telga as an equal.

And with intense blue eyes. It reminded her of someone.

"Who is the other?" she asked finally. Revarik changed the image again, distorting briefly before showing the figure of a talking to them later. The woman had alchemical ingredients holstered on her belt and a rifle slung over her shoulder. She had a thoroughly beaten up jacket, long dark hair tied back, and--

She froze. She pretended to reach for the display, and examined closer, like before. But her heartbeat raced wildly. "What are their names?"

Revarik frowned. "Not much to go on. The woman is called Claire. She's a chemist. the man on the right, he's an engineer, my agent said. On risk of being caught lingering, I could only afford this one survey, however brief. He was killed by a wary guard who saw through him. But he managed to send this."

She frowned. "And they came through this portal, together?"

"Yes. the man is called...Shawn. A primitive name, from that primitive world of Earth." There was growl to Revarik's words, as he tapped a clawed finger against his hip. "Useful. But dangerous if we can't control him. He has the primal Etteria. And her knowledge."

If only he knew who he should be worried about controlling. Revarik glanced her way, beak creased into a smile. "Do you recognize them?"

She shook her head. It took all the effort of holding her fragments of herself together to redirect the question. "Why would I recognize two random people from a primitive world? Because I happened to come from there? It's mathematically improbable."

"But not impossible," Revarik corrected, but then shrugged. "I suppose if that were the case, more than Fate is fighting against me."

Her eyes locked onto the feathered man. "But he's not human."

"No. The primal Etteria has...unusual effects. At least, that is my presumption." He glanced at the hologram, arms folded, and stood beside her for a moment. "It raises questions on its use for...storage."

She said nothing. But her pounding heart said it all. "Are you nervous?" he asked after a few seconds.

"Unknown foes with unusual powers are a large variable. I'm...excited to face such a challenge."

Revarik laughed softly. "Quite right. You've made good use of your training. You have handled every challenge thus far. But this one, will require patience. I want to know what they are planning, first."

"Would it not be prudent to simply eliminate them?" The words filled her with revulsion. But it was the line he would want to hear, coming from her.

"That would be prudent, but I think taking them alive could prove beneficial. I still need that Etteria intact--and there's a chance the other Etteria crystal may have been used." His words were again laced with vitriol. "But if they are going to disrupt my plan of making this world whole again...it pays to be patient. Send your agents. Attend to this task personally. We're in no hurry to attend to the invasion of Valtiria." He clapped a hand on her shoulder, smiling proudly at her. "I have confidence that you can carry this out."

"It will be done." She wanted to scream at his touch, never mind the words she forced out. Her helmet felt stifling now, and Revarik moved to the mechanical door that opened for him with a seamless swish of air. He paused to look briefly over his shoulder. He smiled, from one end of his beak to the other.

"Do have fun with their friends, too. I'd hate to let your best skills go to waste."

The door slid shut in a clank of latches. She waited five seconds before she fumbled with the helmet latch. Her hands shook as she pulled it off, and set it on a small table scattered with maps and reports.

She leaned against the table edge, gripping it tight beneath her hands, and her breath shuddered. Golden locks of hair draped down, though most of it was tied back. She gazed at that infernal helmet; she looked like an armored demon when the suit was fully assembled. She saw her reflection in the visor, trembling and pale blue eyes edged with fear.

She glanced back at the projection, her gaze still locked on the engineer. She slumped to the ground, clutching her hands to her chest, and sobbed for the first time in nearly ten years.

Shawn was alive. And he was here, with Claire, on Remaria. A brief spark of hope flickered...and then was extinguished by the swell of dark thoughts.

Of what she'd endured. Of what she'd done, to survive.

"Shawn..." she whispered in a betrayal of keeping her deepest thoughts unspoken, and wiped the tears from her eyes. "You shouldn't have come here."

There's nothing left of me to save...
