Continuing downward through what turned out to be quite a tall building, Luke saw how Curtis looked troubled. Thinking he knew why, Luke did his best to get his friend to cheer up.

"You were outnumbered. There's no shame in losing every once in a while."

"Yeah," Curtis said, his tone subdued.

"Next time, we'll go together."

Curtis glanced at Luke, his expression turning a little sad, even. "I think I've fucked up."

"What do you mean?" Luke asked. He'd never seen this side of Curtis before. It was good that his friend was opening up a little, but it was disconcerting as well to see him this broken up about something, this unsure of himself.

"I've been trying to mold my class into something it's not meant for."

This wasn't a new revelation. Curtis had said as much before, several times even. The difference now was that he didn't say it with confidence.

"You lost a fight. It happens, man."

Curtis stopped for a moment as if to consider what to say next. Given the lull in the conversation, Luke sent off a few messages telling the others Curtis was fine and that there was no need to join them.

"It's just... First, I went all out on the offensive." Curtis brought out his large, two-handed blade of darkness. "But this skill isn't meant for slashing and stabbing." The weapon turned into several spheres so dark they didn't even reflect the light. "I wanted it to, but it wasn't working like I wanted, and I kept needing to be rescued. Still do."

"What about that huge sphere that traps monsters? That seems powerful."

"A band-aid to try to cover the weaknesses."

"Pretty badass band-aid if you ask me."

Curtis chuckled. "Yeah, I like it."

"I'm not seeing what the problem is. You held your own against that demon. Fighting against a bunch of these Intruders and losing doesn't make you weak."

"No? What does it make me then?"

"Makes you stupid," Luke said.

"Huh?"

"Yeah. Dumb as a bag of bricks. Worse than Ray, even."

Curtis looked offended. "You're going too far now."

"You met one of these guys before and know how strong they are. Suspecting there might be more of them, you still followed one without asking for help. Pretty fucking stupid if you ask me."

"But-"

"No butts! Trying for confident and arrogant, even when you're doubting yourself? Stupid."

"Fine!"

"Say it!" Luke said, raising his voice enough that it echoed up and down the stairs.

Curtis let out an angry growl. "I'm stupid!"

"See?" Luke said. "Doesn't that make you feel better?"

Considering that question for a moment before answering, Curtis sighed. "Not really. The scaling."

"You're a low-level scrub, Curtis. In the grand scheme of things, we all are. There's no telling how far we'll climb before it's all said and done, and we could punch our ticket at any moment." Luke nodded to Curtis. "Some of us, the stupid ones, sooner rather than later."

Curtis tried to break in, but Luke kept on going. "There'll be plenty of chances to find new ways to make your class stronger. In the end, it's what you make of it. I'm a damn healer and I can cut a man's heart out in seconds."

"Fine."

"Fine, what?"

"Fine, I'll find ways to make the class work."

"And?" Luke asked.

"And what?"

Luke gave him an exasperated look. "And you'll bring people with you!"

"Right."

"Right?"

Curtis glared. "I said right, didn't I?"

"Well, good then. Then stop moping and show me what you were going to show me. Chop chop!"

"Dammit," Curtis grumbled, hurrying down the stairs.

After they'd made it all the way down to the basement - why was it always the basement? - Luke stood there looking at... something.

"What is it?" Luke asked.

In a cleared-out section of a large storeroom, an object he had never seen before hovered in the air four feet off the ground. It was oval, sort of like a portal, but no larger than a grown man's heart. From all appearances, it was made of clear glass except for a small bead in its absolute center.

The bead looked a little like a yin-yang symbol with one white area and one black, except for a dot of white in the dark part and vice versa. The line going down the middle was straight, rather than wavy. Weaver's Perception told Luke nothing about the object. It was as if it didn't exist at all.

"I don't know," Curtis said, walking up to it. He reached forward and grabbed it, but nothing happened.

"Didn't we just have a talk about not doing stupid shit?" Luke asked.

"Relax. I saw them touch it."

"Before they beat you and stuffed you into a supply closet?"

"I'll have you know I ran away and hid in there."

"As if that's better," Luke said, shaking his head. "So what? You touched it. Does it do anything?"

Curtis let go. "Try it."

Luke did. It felt like touching glass. At first, he couldn't pick up anything. It was like a blank space in the air where Weaver's Perception didn't register a single thing. That changed a little when Luke pushed the issue, honing in on the glass oval in an attempt to force it to give up its secrets. A slight tug, that was all.

"You see the message, right?" Curtis said.

"Huh," Luke said, having been too preoccupied with trying to glean the object's secrets.

"Check your interface."

There it was.

Time remaining: Eight days, eleven hours, four minutes, thirty-nine seconds.

"There it is."

"You know what it is?" Curtis asked, looking surprised.

"A countdown to the end of the world, of course."

Curtis gave him a deadpan look.

"Kidding, kidding," Luke said, scratching the back of his head. "I have no clue what it means. You said you saw the intruders in here messing with it?"

"A glimpse. They all had their hands on it."

"Ominous."

"Yeah," Curtis said.

They stood there just watching the thing for a while before Luke grew bored. "Can you sense any Intruder now?"

"No. It's like they dip in and out. Annoying as hell."

"I'm going to try something," Luke said. "Give me a moment."

Curtis pointed to the left-hand wall. "There's a faucet over there if you want to... you know... clean up a little."

"Nah."

Luke had been covered in blood, his own and others', so much that he'd grown used to the slight sticky itchiness that came with it. He might not look very put together, but that wasn't the most important thing in the world. Not now, not ever.

First, he extended a thread of mana to the little oval, but there was no way to get inside it, neither in the physical nor the metaphysical way. In fact, it was almost as if the object resisted the thread even getting close. Luke had to push a little. Unusual.

That avenue of gathering information having proven to be a bust, it was time to test another. Calming himself and clearing his mind, he soon fell into a state of meditation. Just like last time, it took some doing to get the system weave to show itself to his inner eye, but after a few attempts, he managed it. If he wasn't mistaken, it had been a little easier on this attempt. Progress. Good.

What he saw was a real head-scratcher. The infinite number of threads that made up the system weave, the ones that ran far and wide across the world ignoring all obstacles, shied away from this one. The surrounding area was empty of threads. This was the first time Luke had seen such a phenomenon, except for when he'd peered into the void.

"Hmm."

"What?" Curtis asked.

"I'm not sure," Luke said. "But there's nothing there."

"It's something there, all right."

"No, I mean. The system won't go near it."

Curtis brought out a thinner, one-handed version of his sword and slashed at the orb. The darkness of his weapon disappeared as soon as it got near, then reappeared when the swing had continued past.

"I don't get it," Curtis said.

Luke considered for a moment, then shook his head. "I'm not sure I do either. Actually, I'm sure I don't know what it means."

Another moment of silence passed before Curtis shrugged. "Well, let's go."

"Go where?"

"To the guild, of course. If I'm going to join, best get it over with."

Luke laughed. "You wouldn't want to miss the roaring party, right?"

Curtis groaned.

True to Curtis's habits, he'd taken an electric scooter over to what turned out to be a tall office building. Since the company didn't dare operate in the city any longer, the battery had died before he even got there. That meant the only option for getting back was to walk. Well, maybe not the only option.

"Do you know how to steal cars?" Luke asked.

Curtis looked over at Luke, drawn back to the present from whatever had occupied his mind. "Cars?"

"Yeah," Luke said. "If we got a car, it'd be faster. Maybe running would work just as well at this point, but sometimes you want to take it easy."

"I don't know how to steal cars, no."

"Huh. Figured you might."

Curtis blinked. "Why? I told you I used to work in marketing, didn't I?"

"Well, yeah."

"Not much in the way of overlap between marketing and being a car thief. A thief of attention, maybe, but not of cars. What made you think I knew how to do something like that?"

Luke shrugged. "Guess you just have car thief vibes."

"Uh huh," Curtis said, thinking for a moment. "With how the world is going right now, I don't think we'll have access to a lot of gas."

"No, you're right. Nothing in the Integrated Shop either. I checked," Luke said.

"No spaceships?"

Luke sighed. "Not even a mana-powered scooter."

"Read something about a company working on a car that ran on monster cores a while back. Before the Internet disappeared."

"Uh, what?" Luke asked, bringing out his phone. It was true. He didn't have a connection. "What the hell?" How am I going to make sure Canada is doing fine now?"

"What?"

"Nevermind. It has to be temporary, right? The Internet can't just disappear."

"How the hell should I know?"

"There's that lovable grump we all know and love," Luke teased. "Let's get you to that party to cheer you up, big fellow."

Curtis groaned again.
