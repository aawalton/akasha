The Lady in Yellow looked at Ashton. He could practically feel her smile underneath her veil.

"Are you sure?" Ashton asked. "Don't misunderstand, I'm extremely happy that you're willing to trust me with something like your true name, but... How can you still trust me this much after thousands of years have passed? I've been wondering the same thing with the Mask Maker."

The Lady in Yellow squeezed Ashton's hand tightly. "Of course I do. There are some amongst us that have gotten more cynical over the passage of time, especially those with that sort of predisposition. The demons and celestials, for example... things might get a little more complicated with them. But the spirits, elementals, and fae; beings of magic in this sort that connect with others' hearts and souls more deeply... we don't forget our bonds easily."

Ashton slowly pulled his hand away and squatted on the ground. "I just... You're trusting me with your soul? With your very existence? After everything we've been through, how can you do that?"

"Aren't you doing the very same thing? You have opened your heart to us; you are calling out to the universe, and are trying to find the ones that you once knew. With some of us, that could theoretically bring deep risks with it. The destruction of your body, soul, and all those that you love," the Lady in Yellow explained, standing right in front of Ashton. "But you, nonetheless, have put such immense trust in us that you would allow us into your mind without anything holding us back."

"Hm?" Ashton let out confused, looking up at her. "What do you mean?"

"This place is a realm of your own mind. While we, as powerful spirits, would be able to enter this dreamscape nonetheless, we didn't need to force our way in. As if you recognised us instinctively and left the gates to your soul wide open to us," the Lady in Yellow leaned down, placing a hand on Ashton's cheek. "That sort of trust is something that should be repaid."

He looked up at the woman before him. "I... thank you."

"Don't even," the Lady in Yellow shook her head. "Now come here."

She held forward her arms as Ashton was still squatting in front of her. He moved over into a kneel. This was the way that the Lady in Yellow performed her ability most of the time.

She was a keeper of knowledge and secrets, and she would only ever reveal a secret to someone one at a time, in a way that was hidden from the very world. As the Lady in Yellow's robes grew longer and covered Ashton's body in its entirety, he knelt there and listened to the Lady in Yellow's voice.

He made sure to listen closely to the words, and the mana intertwined with them. Just as with the Mask Maker, Ashton felt a pride deep in his soul that someone would trust him with their very essence like this.

However, as Ashton was hidden by the Lady in Yellow's robes and she ceased speaking her name, the robes did not pull back. Instead, the Lady in Yellow asked Ashton to look back up at her.

And as he did, he saw something he didn't expect. Similar to the Mask Maker clan, the Ladies in Yellow hid their face from others.

However, the Ladies in Yellow, being a clan of truth and secrets, didn't do it simply by covering the face with the veil. Rather, that was more of a trick, to make those that managed to peek underneath the veil believe that they saw their true face.

It appeared differently to everyone. They saw what they expected to see. If they expected to see something terrifying, the face of a Lady in Yellow would be grotesque enough for the viewer to justify it being hidden. If they expected beauty, then the Lady in Yellow would be the most gorgeous person they had ever seen; something worth to be protected by a veil.

But the face that Ashton saw was ordinary. Beautiful without a doubt, but ordinary. A face that belonged to a woman like any other in the world. Sure, she had three eyes, with a third sitting on her forehead, and her ears were pointed like those of an elf, but beside that, you would never guess that you were speaking to a spirit.

The face that Ashton saw was simply hers. The face of a friend that trusted him enough to show this appearance to him.

He smiled broadly. "Thank you."

A deeply pungent scent was pulled in through Ashton's nostrils. In a split second, his mind was torn out of his sleep as every bit of mana in his body practically screamed at him.

Ashton shot up as his adrenaline took over. For a moment, he even forgot that he was blind and was starting to freak out. He called on the wisp, which he had fed enough to last through the night within his vessel, and channelled the elemental.

"Ashton! S-Something is going on out-outside," Millie was squatting next to his mattress, shaking. She had used the item that he had given her. He had mixed it together last night; it was technically a potion, but the effect really just made it the magical equivalent of smelling salts.

Since Ashton figured that his extended sleep wasn't a result of pure physical exhaustion but was of some kind of magical nature, having something like this was more than just useful.

Ashton had come up with the original recipe to wake up the Summoner once when they were slumbering. A dragon attacked and Ashton and the other summons couldn't take care of it alone.

True dragons were such deeply powerful beings that even the Summoner, once woken up, could only chase it away. Ashton was glad that he was able to figure out how to stall it enough to even make that potion.

Either way, this one was much weaker than that version of the potion, just using whatever ingredients he had on hand. Luckily it seemed to be extremely effective. Maybe Ashton could turn this into an offensive weapon as well; a sort of combat potion that he could throw at his opponents to momentarily incapacitate them.

It seemed like it would be particularly efficient with magic casters.

"What are you talking about, what's going on outside?" Ashton asked, jumping up from the mattress. He told the Living Abode to open, and then rushed outside. He could feel the warmth of sunlight on his skin, so it seemed to already be morning.

But that didn't seem to be the major issue right now. Instead, it was the screeching from the harpies that were currently attacking the camp. The moment Ashton stepped outside, one of them swooped down at him. Ashton defended against it with the wisp. The magic elemental split in two and shrank down, hitting the harpy all over until it was laying half dead on the ground with broken wings. Ashton crushed its head under his boot.

[You have killed a [Harpy]. For your valiant effort, you are awarded 23 Coins]

"Millie, if any harpies come here and try to attack you, hide in the tent and tell the Living Abode to close down," Ashton told her. "But otherwise, watch. Look at the monsters and figure out their weaknesses. See their souls, and try to figure out what they're planning to do next."

At the edge of his channelling range, Ashton could see Millie nodding. She was nervously holding onto the edge of the tent, not quite wanting to leave. But from her expression, Ashton wasn't quite sure what she was feeling right now; whether it was fear or excitement. Not that it mattered.

Ashton called on the Backpacker and had them give him his summoner's staff and grimoire, which he quickly used to summon the rest of the elementals. Which was good timing, because two more harpies were coming at him right now. Ashton threw an open bottle of water toward one of them, using the undine to pull the water out and cover the harpy.

As the water stuck to the feathers, Ashton froze the water and watched as the harpy fell down, before one of the stone-fused wisps slammed down on it. The other was taken out by a stone spike to the heart, and the two messages popped up almost simultaneously.

[You have killed a [Harpy]. For your valiant effort, you are awarded 24 Coins]

[You have killed a [Harpy]. For your valiant effort, you are awarded 21 Coins]

Quickly, Ashton focused on other parts of the rooftop. The watchman on shift had moved back. They were deeply injured. It seemed like the three harpies up here had attacked them. The watchman was unconscious and bleeding out.

Ashton tore open the ripped-up clothes and quickly applied some healing potions to the wounds. This one was a slightly higher-tiered potion than the one Ashton made before. It was decently more efficient, but with this much healing to be done, it wouldn't be enough to just apply potions to the outside of the watchman's wounds.

Ashton dripped some of the rapid-healing potion into some water and mixed it together, using the undine to slowly funnel it down the unconscious man's throat.

He convulsed immediately as the immediate pain must have set in. But Ashton didn't use much of the potion; just enough to stop some of the immediate bleeding. Ashton quickly carried him into the tent to keep him safe.

"Watch him, okay?" Ashton asked of Millie, who immediately nodded.

And then, Ashton ran to the railing that overlooked the main area outside of the building. He shifted his channelling range down slightly, trying to see what was going down. He could barely see the ground like this, and it looked like most of the harpies were taken care of. But there was one in particular that was giving them a lot of trouble.

There was still a whole flock of harpies attacking, so it wasn't possible for everyone to focus on that individual monster, but the strongest ones did seem to be focused on it. In particular, Sam was at the forefront, with Jacky's party beside him, trying to help out wherever they could.

But all of them should be focused on that giant harpy.

"I don't think they can beat a field boss on their own," Ashton sighed, quickly jumping onto the railing. He gathered up air with the sylph, as much as he could grab onto basically, and then took a step forward.

Ashton strengthened his body to prepare for the impact, and then coated himself with the sylph's air magic. As he fell down toward the boss harpy, Ashton noticed that some of the others had spotted him.

Of course, they were all looking up at the harpies right now anyway, so it was hard to miss the guy that just willingly jumped off a roof.
