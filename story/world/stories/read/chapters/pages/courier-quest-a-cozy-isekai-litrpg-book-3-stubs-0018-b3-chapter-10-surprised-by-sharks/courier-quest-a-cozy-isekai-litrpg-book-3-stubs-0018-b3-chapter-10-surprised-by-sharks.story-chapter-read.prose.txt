There was no hurry or rush in the cliffside residence that Trevor and Adabelle had been allowed to use. Nothing pressed them for time, or caused them stress. They were allowed to enjoy the atmosphere, view, and each other to their heart’s content. Such was their focus on one another that it wasn’t until the second day that they remembered there were gifts waiting for them.

The lounge’s conversation pit had a few presents strewn about on the couches. Trevor had only grabbed the gifts that had been placed specifically in the pile to be taken on the honeymoon. Those should have been the ones that would be useful while abroad, as opposed to things that were meant for their home as newlyweds.

While they had promised Rashie to open hers first, the gift basket of potions from Dory and Greer was covered in a see-through fabric that made it easy to tell what was inside. It turned out to be a fantastic grab bag of adventuring items that made Adabelle smile as she unpacked it.

There were a few high grade healing potions for adventuring, an oil for their armor and the noblewoman’s sword that would keep it clean for an entire year before wearing off, some cure-alls that would work on poisons, diseases, and even some curses, which was very expensive according to Adabelle, and a few stamina potions to keep their bodies in good condition.

“I would have wondered where the Full Night’s Rest were, since that seems to be the kind of thing Dory would give to a couple on their honeymoon, but I think I already know where most of her stock went,” the demonkin said, tapping her tail against Trevor’s leg.

“If you’re trying to get me to say I went overboard, then I wholeheartedly disagree,” he replied, sticking his tongue out at her. “Clearly, I’ve never been more on a ship than I am right now.”

One Full Night’s Rest would energize someone the same as if they had gotten eight hours of sleep. Dory made them for Tosa’s guards, but she had made an exception when it came to selling them to Trevor. He had already presented one of them to Adabelle that morning to wake her from her stupor, and he had more than enough to last throughout the honeymoon.

Adabelle rolled her eyes, fully aware of the smile tugging at her lips despite her show of disapproval, and patted her husband on the arm. Instead of continuing what would undoubtedly be a losing conversation, she pointed at Rashie’s present while giving him a questioning glance.

“Yeah, let’s open it up,” he said.

The glittering paper was folded in strange ways before it had been glued down, and there were several lumps. Whatever was inside was tall and slender. It was readily apparent that the shark herself wrapped this gift. Adabelle was quick to open it.

And then remove the protective cloth that had been wrapped around the gift several times to make sure it wasn’t harmed in any way.

When Adabelle was finally done, she revealed a circular black mirror. It had a handle and the glass was almost ten inches across. There was a clear gem ensconced above it, and the noblewoman looked at it curiously. It didn’t show her reflection, but a shadowy shape right beneath the surface.

“You finally opened it!” Rashie’s voice came from the mirror, and the glass cleared up to show the shark girl on the other side. Her face appeared in the reflection, a big smile on her face. Adabelle quickly turned it away from herself. “Hey, that’s a really nice place you’ve got.”

“Okay, cool. Rashie got us a cell phone. That’s neat,” Trevor remarked, using Quick Change to put on some pajamas and summoning one of his shirts for Adabelle.

“Thank you,” she said, handing him the mirror so she could slip it on.

The courier sat down and shifted the mirror so that it was pointing at him. He was suddenly face to face with a very excited Rashie. “Uncle Trevor!” she greeted loudly. Adabelle joined him on the couch, and the girl’s energy level shot through the roof. “And Addymanatine!”

“Hello, shark. We weren’t expecting you,” the noblewoman said diplomatically as she fixed her hair.

“I’ve been sitting next to this thing forever now waiting for you to open it up,” Rashie pouted. “I thought you were going to do it yesterday, but then you didn’t, and now I’m out on patrol. Kept the mirror out, though, just in case you got to it today, and now here we are!”

She shifted the camera to show off the Tosan countryside. Trevor recognized a bridge and gathered that his niece was near where the logging operation was.

“This is a very interesting gift, Rashie. Thank you,” Trevor said.

The shark beamed. “I only have half of it for now, but I’m going to give it to Auntie Addy once you guys come back. That way, no matter how far away Uncle Trevor goes, you two can always talk and see each other and be mushy and stuff.”

“That’s a sweet thought. We appreciate it,” Adabelle said with a smile. “Are you doing well?”

“Yeah, of course. I only miss you guys a little bit,” Rashie responded, raising her chin towards the sky. “We’re holding down the fort in your absence, and that Bocoro guy continues to be suspicious, but he hasn’t made any attempt a coup… yet. But I’m watching him.”

“Sir Bocoro is a good man, Rashie, so don’t harass him too much,” the noblewoman warned. “He’s just doing his job.”

“Yes. He’s very good at being sneaky, which is his job.”

Trevor arched an eyebrow. “Did something happen between you two?”

Rashie harrumphed. “He ate the last shrimp at the wedding and it was supposed to be mine.”

“Did he take it from your plate or something?” he asked as a follow up.

“No.”

“So he grabbed it while you were reaching for it?”

“No.”

“So he just took it from the food table like a normal person?”

“If a normal person could be so diabolical, then yes.”

“Shark, aren't you just mad that you didn’t reach it first?” Trevor asked, amused. Rashie didn’t answer the question, and he released a dramatic sigh. “Welp, I guess that’s that, Adabelle.”

“That’s that, Trevor?” she asked.

“Yup, we’re going to have to cut the honeymoon short to help our niece,” he said.

“What!?” Rashie yelled, pulling her mirror far too close to her face. “No! You don’t have to do that!”

“No, no, it’s okay,” Trevor replied, shaking his head. “Clearly a mastermind of this caliber will need all of our considerable powers aligned against him. For your sake, if not our own.”

“Don’t come home! It’s nothing, really!” the girl shouted.

“Are you sure?” Trevor asked. “It sounded like you really need help dealing with this Bocoro guy. What if he’s as evil as you fear? What if—”

“Nooooo!” Rashie whined. “Stay on your honeymoon, don’t come home until you’re done. If he tries something, I can handle it, but he probably won’t cause Addy trusts him!”

“Are you super sure?”

“Super positive! Enjoy being away! Bye! Don’t come home! Until it’s time, I mean! Bye!”

Trevor shared a look with Adabelle as the mirror went dark. He tried not to laugh, but the demonkin had no qualms and started giggling. “You’re incorrigible,” she accused.

“I’m sure I’d be very upset if I knew what that meant,” he replied, looking away with his best expression of innocence.

The demonkin’s tail lightly tapped him on the forehead. “You know full well what it means, son of Ander. It’s not the first time I’ve called you that, and I doubt it will be the last.”

“Didn’t know what it meant then, either,” he said before laughing as she tapped him again. “Okay, okay. Rashie’s fine, though. She’s just amusing herself with drama and intrigue until we return. You’ll have to call again some time in the next week or so to check up on her. I’d do it, but apparently I’m not even good enough to activate the thing until I put a point into Mana.”

“It’s still at one?” Adabelle asked.

“That and Magic, yeah. I don’t need it for my Power and I wanted to get Capacity and Stamina to ten,” Trevor answered with a shrug. He pulled up his stat screen so he could look it over.

Increasing his Capacity to ten had given him the ability to double the amount of space he had in his inventory that froze things out of time from a tenth to a fifth. It had allowed him to take far more food than he had before as well as store items for Dory and Thimbleden, working as a freezer of sorts for the two shopkeepers. It was a low paying use of his Power, but the extra income was always nice.

“I’ll work on Mana next,” Trevor told his wife. “Stamina is a couple points away from ten, but I already feel like I don’t really get winded all that much anymore even when I travel a lot. Endurance Boost and World Walker probably put in a lot of work there.”

“Endurance Boost is a big one,” Adabelle agreed. “Still, I am glad that she got us this, her sudden appearance notwithstanding.”

“Speaking off,” Trevor started before pulling the shirt he gave her back into his inventory along with the Two-Way Blackglass. She gave him a flat look, and he grinned. “What?”

“Incorrigible,” she answered, though she made no move to cover herself.

“Eh, guilty as charged,” he admitted cheekily. “Clearly, it’s one of my better traits.”

Adabelle giggled before kissing his cheek and snuggling against him. “Which one should we open next?”

Trevor pointed at them all in turn before stopping on a small box next to him. It was the only one that was close enough to pick up without moving away from his lovely wife.

They went through and opened the few remaining presents one by one. There were adventuring tools, more orbs for the Language Fish, which Adabelle already owned as a bureaucrat, and a few tokens and talismans for blessing a marriage. Those seemed like something that should have been put in the other pile, but Trevor wasn’t lacking for space.

Not yet, anyway; who knew how much stuff Klar wanted him to take once they landed in Paladona.

The only other magical item came from Drelik. They were pins depicting a pure white feather, and there was one for each of them. Ever curious, Trevor was quick to read the description.

[[New Trinket! Feather of the Graceful!

Wear this pin on your clothes, and activate it with a touch. So long as you are not touching the ground, a plethora of feathers appear between your sides and your arms, creating a magical wing suit. You may use this Trinket once per day to glide through the air without a care in the world.

The effect will last for a number of minutes equal to your Stamina stat or until you land. Regardless of whether or not you have time leftover, it cannot be activated again until the next day.

“Oh, this would have been so cool to use on the Floating Isles,” Trevor said, looking forward to flying in the future. The warning not to use it to go to the Wildlands was a good one, though.

“It’s situational, but we can always find uses for it,” Adabelle mused. “I think this is a good gift. The fact that we'll match pleases me a great deal, too. It seems fitting for our honeymoon.”

“Yeah, like a logo or icon that belongs to us,” he agreed with a smile. “I think we’ll be looking for situations to use this in. Think there might be a way we can fly over the festival in Paladona?”

“It’s possible,” the demonkin replied, thinking it over. “It’s an interesting thought, at least, seeing big events like that from the sky. I’m not saying I want to dive off of Castle Eskretet during the Festival of Golden Lights, but any other time is free game.”

“I’m sure you can get us up there,” he said, wrapping an arm around her. “Man, flying. That’s going to be a blast.”

“It sure will be, Trevor,” Adabelle giggled, enjoying his enthusiasm.

Leaning in, the demonkin kissed her husband gently before wriggling away so she could clean up the errant paper. They were happy with the extra supplies that they had been given, especially with a dungeon dive happening tomorrow. A prepared adventurer was a thriving one, after all.
