Greta’s favorite day of the week was Sunday. It was the calmest of the lot, characterized by silence and peace, and bearing the least possibility of an assasination occurring. After all, even assassins had families and would love a chance to spend their time with them. Basically, it was a day where her paranoia could take a back seat.

Which was why she calmly sat outside in a park, with a smile on her face, dressed as loosely as possible, while breathing in the fresh air as several rays of the sun snuck onto her through the canopies of the trees surrounding the area.

Then her moment of respite was broken by a sudden appearance.

“Oh. You’re here,” she began. “Finally.”

Kang Do-Won glanced at his wristwatch and frowned slightly. “Pardon me, but I’ll have to argue against your last choice of word. I’m five minutes earlier than intended.”

Greta glanced at him and pushed down her sunglasses slightly. “Five minutes can quickly become a single minute under unnoted circumstances. Thirty minutes cannot. Which is why thirty minutes should be the benchmark for arriving at an appointed location. Hopefully you keep this at the back of your mind, maybe then your division would stop arriving late during Dungeon Breaks.”

Do-Won’s brows twitched as a flash of annoyance crossed his face in the blink of an eye.

Greta acted like she hadn’t noticed that and pushed her sunglasses back onto the bridge of her nose so her eyes were once again hidden.

“Well, sit,” she said. “Or do you prefer to talk while standing?”

Do-Won sighed and walked over. As he was about to sit down, however, Greta raised a finger and pointed to the far end of the bench.

“There,” she said. “That’s your spot. Don’t get too close.”

Do-Won paused, took a second to digest her words, and sat down where he had been instructed to.

“Mr. Hyeonki told me that you were quite the character,” said Do-Won. “I assumed he was joking.”

“Quite the character, huh?” Greta scoffed. “Well, then, did your guildmaster tell you anything about what this meeting is to involve?”

“He did. Yes,” said Do-Won.

“Great. Then let’s get down to business.” Great leaned back and spread her arms across the top rail. “Enlighten me on why the search you conducted on the club bore no fruit.”

Do-Won inclined his head, visibly dazed by her question.

“I have no idea what you want me to say to that—”

“Please don’t stall,” said Greta with a bored tone layered with hints of a threat. “Tell me why you were unable to find anything incriminating in that club.”

Do-Won frowned. He took a second, then sighed.

“Because there was nothing to be found,” he said. “We searched the building from top to bottom and there was nothing illegal.”

“You’re just repeating my words,” Greta argued. “All right then. Without missing a beat or eliminating a single scene, walk me through the entire process your team used in raiding the building. At the end, present to me the timeline and how many hours used, as well as the descriptions of every single staff you came across.”

Do-Won’s brows pulled in very deeply.

“What is the meaning of this?” he asked. “Am I under suspicion?”

Greta clicked her tongue. “I wonder. Are you?”

Do-Won’s eyes twitched, but he didn’t say anything more. He shifted uncomfortably on his seat, and after a few seconds of silence, he narrated the events of the day he had conducted the search on Club Spiral. How his team had surrounded the building and infiltrated it from all points; how they had scoured every room, every shelf, every bar, and scrutinized every alcohol present. And how at the end there had been nothing amiss for them to take into consideration. The place was just a normal club.

“A normal club and yet Nikolai Volkov was last seen walking into it before he lost control and became a Berserker,” said Greta. “And it’s also the same place a professor walked into before he lost his life. I’d say it’s anything but a normal club. Don’t you think so?”

“There could be other factors at play we aren’t considering,” he said. “Nikolai could have purchased the drug from a random person present in the club, and Professor Dmitri, bless his soul, could have met that person after fixing an appointment, using the club as their place of meeting. People can rent out the VIP rooms there after all.”

Greta hummed. “Is this what the owner of the club told you or what you inferred from your dashing genius?”

Do-Won’s expression shook. “I did talk to the owner.”

“Oh, yeah? What’s her name?”

“Mouse. She claimed not to have any idea about what was going on.”

“Is this before or after a professor who walked into their building was seen dead after?”

“Both,” said Do-Won.

Greta sighed and sat upright. “Look here, Mr. Kang Do-Won. I might be quite the character, but I am not a joke. I was sent here to gather the right information and set things straight since you all can’t seem to do your jobs. And, trust me, I have no intention of being tagged as lazy and incompetent as your lot have been branded by the Council of Mages. A dead professor and a student who was almost a Berserker is not a good look on the state of South Korea. And we are not taking into account all that is going on behind the scenes. So… Before I take matters into my own hands, I humbly implore you to tell me everything you know about this situation, because, if I’m being truthful, I don’t trust you. You’re hiding something. And if you don’t tell me yourself, I’ll find out on my own terms. Best believe me, then, I’ll be more than just ‘quite the character’.”

Do-Won fell silent for a second, then he sighed, turning away from Greta and staring straight ahead towards nothing but the beauty of the park.

“You’re free to doubt or suspect me. It’s part of the job. However, I went about my job as I was supposed to, and nothing was hidden.” He paused. “In fact, if you want to help us get to the bottom of this, then we’ll gladly receive your help. That’s why Headmaster Ilseong requested help from the Council of Mages after all.”

Greta took a deep breath and exhaled. She let Do-Won’s words simmer in her mind as she prepared herself to say something. But before she could fully articulate the words she’d prepared, her phone rang. Her attention partially shifted to it and then fully when caller ID: Council, appeared on her screen.

“Give me a moment,” she said with a raised finger.

Do-Won nodded. “Take all the time you need.”

Greta stood up, put some distance between her and Do-Won, and then picked up the call.

“Greta Kaufmann,” said a hoarse voice from the other end of the phone.

“Yes—”

“Where are you right at this moment?” The voice cut Greta off before she could reply.

“Currently interrogating Kang Do-Won of the Consortium Guild on the matters of the dead professor and his Berserker brother.”

The voice was silent for a moment. “Never mind that. I want you to head to Prestige Academy right this instant and bring that boy, Merlin Tyrrell, to the council. You must be here in two days.”

Greta’s default to a request from the Council of Mages was a ‘yes, sir’. However, this confused her.

The council had known since the entrance exams of Prestige Academy that Merlin Tyrrell was a Deficient Mage with the ability to deconstruct magic. And, obviously, they had taken great interest in him. But the Consortium and the academy had struck a deal with the council to give them the full authority to study the boy in the council’s stead. Even the events of the Berserker and the death of the professor hadn’t changed their mind. So why, all of a sudden, were they asking her to bring the boy over at the expense of her investigations, and so quickly?

“If you don’t mind, sir. May I know what triggered the sudden change in plans?” Greta asked.

In that instant, her phone beeped, and the voice on the other end said, “I have sent you a video. That has all your answers. Two days, Greta. Nothing more.”

“Understood, sir.”

The phone call ended and Greta turned her attention to the video she’d received. It depicted a fight between Merlin Tyrrell and another student, dressed in the battle wear of Crown Academy. She knew the inter-academy tournament was today, so she wasn’t surprised at that. What caught her eyes first of all was when Merlin gave his mana the physical form of a cloak, granting him the ultimate defense against magic. And then her brows were further pulled down when he fell to the floor of the arena in a fit of pain.

Greta watched calmly, her breaths getting heavier with each passing second as her mind whispered to her that something big was coming.

And then it did, seizing her breaths and widening her eyes.

Flames burst to life from Merlin’s mouth, like he was a fire-breathing dragon, and from his right hand spread forth a thin layer of ice all the way to the edge of the arena.

Greta drew in a gasp and blinked several times as if to wash away whatever hallucination had plagued her without her notice. But, no, what she had seen was very real.

A Deficient Mage had just cast magic.

Greta ramrod herself straight, dipped her phone into her pocket, and turned to Do-Won.

“Mr. Kang Do-Won,” she said. “I need a ride to Prestige Academy. With haste.”

Phoebe and Arthur Tyrrell found a way to make some time to watch their son’s duel in the inter-academy tournament. They escaped from the walls of the hospital as soon as they got the slightest chance and hid in their car in the parking lot.

The last time they had seen their son in a fight on a screen, he had almost died. And Phoebe couldn’t shake off that fright as she watched him step onto the stage for his fight against the boy, Marcus Reed.

Arthur placed a hand on her shoulder as he noticed her body tense up.

“It’ll be fine,” he said. “Relax.”

Phoebe glanced at him, her lips pressed together. And then the signal for the duel to start drew their attention back to their screen.

Their son was the first to attack, quickly trying to end the duel before it could drag out. Which Phoebe, most especially, appreciated. But the events of that didn’t turn out as she had hoped. In the next minute, Merlin was struck with a spell and he fell to the floor of the arena face-first.

Phoebe gasped and turned to Arthur for reassurance.

He did just that, even though his heart was pounding wildly in his chest.

Then their son stood up and resumed the duel, but Phoebe was once again doubting.

She didn’t want to. She really didn’t. But she had lived through the cataclysm; she had watched people die—people she knew; her friends. Magic was dangerous, and even far more dangerous was the absence of magic.

The world that her children had ventured into was one so bleak that sometimes she had nightmares.

At the moment, it was still the nightmares of her friends, and she prayed so dearly that it wouldn’t transition into that of her children one day.

Phoebe was given a moment of respite when Merlin finally toppled his opponent, giving her a sight of victory and a reason to come to the fact that her son could handle himself.

But then everything changed.

Merlin fell to the ground as a harrowing scream escaped his lungs.

Phoebe was dumbstruck for a second. She turned to Arthur, who was also dazed.

“What’s happening?” she asked as tears rolled down her eyes. “What’s wrong with our son?!”

Arthur opened his mouth to say something, but he didn’t have any words. He held onto Phoebe’s shoulders, and when he saw the medics of the academy rush onto the arena, he said the only thing he could, “He’ll be fine.”

But even he didn’t fully believe those words.

They both watched their son thrash about in the arena like he was on fire; and just when Phoebe couldn’t bear it anymore, readying herself to bury her face in her palms, a burst of flames escaped Merlin’s mouth, stunning them both. Before they could so much as digest what they had just seen, a thin layer of ice spread out from Merlin’s right hand all the way to the end of the arena, causing them to frown even harder.

Phoebe sniffled. “Arthur… Is that?”

Arthur gulped and muttered, “Magic…”
