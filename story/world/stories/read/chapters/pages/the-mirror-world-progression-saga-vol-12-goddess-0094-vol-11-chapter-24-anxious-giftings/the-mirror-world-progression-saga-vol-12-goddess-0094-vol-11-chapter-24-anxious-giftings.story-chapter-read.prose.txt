At Azure’s sudden negative reaction, the earth elementalist started in equal surprise. “Is this not it?” he asked, disbelieving. “There’s nothing like this anywhere else in the mass. I’m certain-”

Azure waved him off. “No-no-no…” She didn’t look his way, staring and frowning at the stone, twirling her fingers, which were still tingling. “This is it, just… I haven’t felt this from any of them until now. It feels…”

Hesitantly, Azure touched the stone again and got the same vibe. This time, she held her hand there, wincing from the disturbing feelings it gave her, but determined to analyze and process them to figure out what was going on with it.

It was as if it were an opposite polarity, magnetically repulsing her, in a spiritual-only sense. Physically, there was nothing. She was puzzled for a few moments before what this meant finally clicked.

“It’s the false one!” she exclaimed, soon pulling her hand away, laughing, and clapping her hands together. “I know it! That’s it, that’s it!”

The earth elementalist was dumbfounded. “Huh?”

Azure blinked at him. “Er, never mind, really! You did your job. But this is, ah, the only one of these we won’t be taking a sliver out of.”

“Are you sure? The details of the job-”

“I know. But we can’t use one sliver, and this is it.”

“I see.” Finally, he shrugged. “You’re the boss. I guess this perfectly good ancient enchanted stone we unearthed goes untouched.”

“Yes, I don’t really see much- hmm.” Azure hesitated, fancying a sudden idea. “Taking and shaping this stone is little effort for you, correct?”

The masked man pulled out a small carving knife and twirled it. “If milady wants a bit of unique jewelry or a ritual dagger, I’m more than happy to oblige. It all pays the same, and I like satisfied clients. Say the word.”

“Two arrowheads, as ancient-looking and authentic as you can manage, but finely-crafted, hinting at magic.”

The masked man cocked his head. “Huh. Interesting choice. For yourself?”

Azure squinted her eyes thoughtfully. “Yes and no. I have memories of finding one here as a child… a quite pretty, intact one. I made it a gift for a friend. I was shocked by his reaction to it lying in his hand — he fought off tears as he ran away with it. I wondered how I offended him, and tried to apologize later, but he said I didn’t owe him anything, and solemnly, seriously declared that he owed me, for the arrowhead. I protested, insisted otherwise, of course, a gift being a gift, and he didn’t understand. He-...”

Azure took a deep, steadying breath to avoid choking up. It’s so real… the memory is so vibrant. How cruel could you be, Goldie? Was it to make me kind and soft? I guess it worked. Mostly. Azure forced herself to continue, “He had never been given a gift before in his life, such was his poverty. He kept it despite this, though hidden in his paranoia that someone would steal it. I know he cherished it, though it wasn’t ultimately worth much.”

The earth elementalist nodded, obviously taken aback but taking the matter with solemnity. “A tender memory. This is a special token, then. I’ll craft it with great care, milady.”

Azure nodded, swallowing hard. “I’ll fly up to leave you to your work. I’ll have specifications for how to leave the area, by the way.”

An hour after all was wrapped up, and they were long gone, two ratty Naugite children, a boy and a girl, crept over slowly and hesitantly. They found the pit filled back in, with lots of thrown-up dirt and rock. There were a few clusters of broken pottery lying around, and the kids dug around in curiosity.

One of them exclaimed excitedly as she found something, causing the other to rush over. In her hands were two perfect arrowheads, shiny and gleaming in the sunlight. They were beautiful and perfect.

They rejoiced, jumping up and down. Eventually, in solemnity, the girl gave one of the arrowheads to the boy. They both cried, knowing they’d have to sell them to help their families.

They kept digging fanatically at that point, using broken pieces of pottery to assist, seeking out more loot.

After a long, sweaty effort, the boy was stunned to find something: a sack. When they opened it and looked inside, their mouths fell open at the sheer impossibility.

It was full of gold coins.
