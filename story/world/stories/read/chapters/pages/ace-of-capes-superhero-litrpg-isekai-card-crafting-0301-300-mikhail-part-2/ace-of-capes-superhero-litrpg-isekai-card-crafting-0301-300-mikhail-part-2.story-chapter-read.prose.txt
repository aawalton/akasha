Nothing.

VOID FLAME didn't burn the floors.

“What are you about to do?" Aiden finally asked.

‘’I’ll tell you, but also I’ll show you,” he said, “Sit.”

Tate scratched the back of his neck. A low mist hung over the forest, and he controlled it, tuning it so it would tell him when even so much as an animal entered this airspace.

So far, it hadn't happened, yet he was still on edge.

Or maybe that was why he was on edge.

That and Torin's incessant pacing.

“How long have they been in there?” Torin asked.

“A few minutes,” Tate responded. Torin was walking around like a hungry wolf, driving Tate crazy.

It was strange how Tate used to always view the boy as calm and collected before. That was how he was portrayed in the game, the mysterious one Tate had been asked to beta-test and had become so addicted to. It had been his escape, the thing that he did when he wanted to forget the world for a while. It really was a great game, flawless except for one thing…the main character, Torin.

So much about him bothered Tate. The way he looked like a generic main character, handsome but not enough that it gave the random nerds insecurity. The way he talked also annoyed Tate, that stiff robotic voice. His cringe lines about justice and doing what’s right and being a proper hero.

But what pissed Tate off the most was his perfect life. Born as an overpowered hero into an overpowered bloodline, earning the admiration and respect of many. That was something Tate never had in his own life.

It felt like the developers just wanted everyone to gaze at the guy with how perfect they made him. Double S-Rank. Rich. Honorable.

God, he wanted to punch him most of the time, even as he'd played as him.

And when Torin had made some not-so-smart choices in the final arc, and Tate had been forced to play it, it had driven him crazy.

At the end, when he'd been asked what he would change about the game, he'd written an entire novel of how much Torin pissed him off.

Then a few days later, he'd gotten a visit.

But that was a story for another day.

While he’d played the game, he’d found stupid things to mock him about, and that habit had carried over when he came to Earth 9, too. Even before he met him, he'd seen him on social media and occasionally in news articles.

Tate had thought him to be wooden with a dry personality. Maybe a little dumb too, with nothing below the surface.

But he was mature enough now to admit that mocking Torin made him feel good. And in the moments he wasn’t proud of, he’d gone to snark forums dedicated to Torin and had even added a thing or two himself, maligning the boy he didn’t truly know, yet felt like he did.

It wasn't until Lexie and Torin became friends that Tate reconsidered his initial judgment. As much as he didn't know much about Lexie, he did know that she wouldn't befriend the kind of person he thought Torin was. Yet, he'd stubbornly clung to his perception anyway, refusing to have anything to do with him, refusing the Firebringer's help even though realistically, he'd known that wasn't the smarter choice.

His rejection of them was two-fold.

He couldn't bring himself to accept help from the boy he’d spent a disproportionate amount of time hating without really knowing.

And, on an egoistic level, he didn't want Torin to help him because then it would feel like Torin won. Like he'd gotten to be the hero again, and Tate was just another victim in a story arc, who would go on to become irrelevant after Torin saved him.

Tate would hate to go out like that. He hated the thought of himself as a victim and hated to be indebted to someone like Torin.

If he was going to die anyway, then he would much rather die with as much dignity as a wretch like him could muster.

But then everything had changed the day he saw the other boy hyperventilating on the floor.

Tate had had an anxiety attack before, but nothing like this.

Torin, clutching his chest, his head on his knees... he didn't look as powerful and unstoppable as before.

He looked almost vulnerable.

And the conversation he'd had with Lexie after had been enlightening.

He’d never imagine Torin Firebringer was the type to face stuff like that. His first instinct was to mock him. That was his problem? That he didn't want to do the interview? What kind of dumb problem was that? He could just tell his parents, and they would understand and pull him from any interview he wanted. Heck, he didn't even have to participate in the AFC at all if he hated it so much.

He was in a prison of his own making.

But hearing Lexie's perspective...he kind of got it.

Tate had thought of that day several times after, and he had felt ashamed of himself.

Because he'd never once thought of Torin as a person. He was just a part of the narrative. A boy with a perfect life.

But there was more under the surface, and Tate just hadn't bothered to look there because he'd simply not wanted to.

He wanted so badly to see Torin as an NPC because it was too painful to acknowledge that while they’d both been born with two different lives, it wasn’t Torin’s fault. It wasn’t Torin’s fault that he had any of the things he had as much as it wasn’t Tate’s fault that he didn't.

It was just the way the world worked. One big unfair system. It reminded him of a verse from Bible study classes, back when he was still forced to attend. Something about God making vessels of honor and vessels of dishonor. That was probably the theme of his life.

It wasn't because of people like Torin that Tate had the life he did. He was always just supposed to have a miserable life.

Although it wasn’t all bad. He let a smile cross his lips. At least, he'd spent the tail end of it with Lexie and Aiden. Got to live in Hovelton. Got some peace and maybe something that felt like family.

At least he would have that before he died.

“How long has it been?” Torin asked again.

Tate sighed. “You asking me that again won’t make the time move faster.”

Torin frowned. “What are you talking about? I  haven’t asked you that question before.”

“Yes, you have. Like five times.”

“No, I have not. I haven't said anything to you."

“Yes, you have.”

"No. I haven't." Torin's insistent voice finally broke through the haze.

They both realized it at the same time, with their mouths still half open, prepared to bicker.

The fact that they both had different perceptions of time meant that something was very wrong.

They looked back to the shack, and in unison they dashed for it, throwing open the door.

There was no one there.
