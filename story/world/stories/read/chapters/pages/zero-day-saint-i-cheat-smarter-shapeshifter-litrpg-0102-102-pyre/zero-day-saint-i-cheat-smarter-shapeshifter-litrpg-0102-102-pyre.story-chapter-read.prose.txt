Jamie's presence, and the knowledge that it came with another pair of eyes to watch out for threats, helped me calm down and assess what had happened.

I had survived another encounter with Nemesis—in person. One possible explanation was that this had been some kind of test. The Nemesis of mythology, one of Nyx's children, was the goddess of divine retribution, responsible for punishing hubris in mortals. Anything but the fastest escape at her appearance would have definitely counted as hubris and likely ended as her namesake promised. Only that this was ignoring the fact that we were both also people. Neither the Blessing nor Fate itself had arranged for this meeting to happen under those circumstances, under this timing. If I accepted that explanation, I'd accept that this reality was far less real than I thought.

That left two options. Either Nemesis was simply not as dangerous as promised. She might have made that threat and meant it, but circumstances had prevented her from following through on it, or our meeting had been entirely incidental. She hadn't come for me, but for the kitsune, and might not even have known who I was before I escaped.

Which fit with the kitsune's plea to be taken out of there. Were the warlords so territorial that their minions could never afford to be caught in another warlord's territory? Had Nemesis killed her or taken her prisoner? And if the latter, had the kitsune feared exactly that?

Even though this infighting between enemies could only be to our advantage, remembering the dread on her face left me with a pang of guilt.

Along the way, I also caught Jamie up on the events that happened since we entered the elven city. He'd only ever gotten a partial view of everything.

We decided to only drop by the city on our return trip. I had a message for Aleria, and we both wanted to know if the Task had been completed. For once, this wasn't a Task granted only to me. Jamie had gotten the same one. Just that it was no longer visible to us the moment we had left the city.

We landed on top of one of the buildings that encompassed the elven city. It was already past dusk, and thanks to the canopy covering the inner yard, we were shielded from observers up here. Aerial assaults weren't common enough for them to invest in guards watching the skies.

Jamie softly landed next to me. "We did it," he whispered. We had decided to stay quiet to avoid garnering more attention than necessary. We would have used chat if that didn't completely exclude Zi'rah from the conversation.

And indeed the Task was marked as completed. The reward would stay a mystery until we reached a sanctified place. I wondered what had happened to that first kitsune, but a moment later, the smoke I smelled, smoke that had nothing to do with supernatural senses the gryphon didn't possess, revealed their fate to me. It carried a faint aroma of smoked meat.

Aleria had told me she'd burn me once she killed me. That was just their funeral rite for the dead. Or those too tainted to pass on without the cleansing of a pyre.

"They killed the other one. And burned her corpse." Queen's Voice had the advantage that I didn't even need to whisper. Only Jamie and Zi'rah could hear me, unless I chose to include another recipient.

Jamie must have sensed my disappointment through the Oath. "We'll find you another kitsune heart eventually."

"In my defense. I don't only mourn the loss of a shape. Also the opportunity to ask questions. The Nemesis encounter revealed that this is all a bit more complicated than I had assumed. Sending Aleria to kill me had to be in Nemesis' best interest, and yet the kitsune was terrified of her."

"If my boss was Pride incarnate, I'd fear Wrath, too. Wouldn't want to get press-ganged into that business."

I considered his words. Pride. It definitely seemed like the elves' prevalent sin, especially Aleria's. But that didn't have to mean that the warlord those worked for was Pride itself.

"I'll reach out to Aleria now. Let's hope the other elves are amicable to our request now."

I used Queen's Voice and selected Aleria as my recipient, just like I had in our duel.

"I hope no one was hurt in the fight. We couldn't capture the other one, but I am certain she won't be an issue for your people anytime soon. So please convey my request—that humans will not be attacked for trespassing in the jungle. We do not seek any quarrel with your kin. And you, Aleria, you are welcome to visit my cackle any time you wish. Or me at the former residence of Mammon. Though beware that I'm usually rather busy. Farewell and good luck. You'll need it if you want to mend your people. Those kitsune only stoked flames that had already been there before."

I tried to listen for a reply, but through the murmur of conversation below, that would be like trying to hear a single drop among rain. I hoped she heard it. And that one day she'd accept that invitation. My hyenas were a lot cooler than evil spirit wolves. And they'd given me a glimpse of who Aleria was when she wasn't a stalwart defender of her people.

"Let's go," I said. "And Zi'rah?"

"Yes, boss?"

With a flap of my wings, we left the elven city behind.

"Even though you didn't get to stab anyone, you did very well. I appreciate that you asked before you stabbed her and that you jumped the moment I told you to."

"You said you weren't the commanding type, but if there is one thing my folks are good at, it's scramming when shit goes sideways. And if you are that startled, I don't want to stab what's coming."

"Picking you was a great choice, Zi'rah."

As much as Aleria might have made for a stronger Champion, I doubted that if it came down to it, she'd have reacted the same way. She was very much the type to try and prove herself when push came to shove, even if 'scramming' was the wiser choice.

"Huh. Thanks, boss."

As night fell and we returned to the city, Hell presented me with a resource I urgently needed—easily murderable foes in the shape of occasional swarms of demon bats. Within minutes I had the first kills for the gryphon, the Archon, and the siren. And thanks to the bats' abundance, I could even afford to wait out the cooldown on the Shifter's Ring of the Slayer to get full refunds on each of them.

An abundance that, in retrospect, might have been a consequence of my actions? The gargoyle sentry in the jungle had me suspect that they were running out of scouts, but ramping up the bat activity all but confirmed it. Ever since Mammon had burned down the fire station, and I had decided to take that personally by picking Apex Seeker, I had been involved in the deaths of at least nine of them. Nine real people, interdimensional invaders, who had to face the fact that there was a very bloodthirsty human out there who would kill them at any opportunity. I'd renegotiate that contract, too.

Maybe the assassination attempt had been exactly that.

This train of thought also made the hypocrisy around the kitsune a lot more apparent. If she'd looked like any other demon, I'd have killed her before Zi'rah could have asked for permission to stab.

I had just snapped the neck of the final bat I needed to recover the 2 ATP cost of the siren shape when I noticed Zi'rah and Jamie both watching me, with notable apprehension.

"What?" I asked.

"This shape… how do I say it?" Jamie rubbed his neck. "She has a certain ability to make anything somehow aesthetically pleasing. Which can be a bit odd in some circumstances. To put it mildly."

I took a step towards Jamie before I stopped myself. The siren was a shape that felt simply amazing to occupy, while needing constant vigilance to not act like a particularly predatory cat in heat.

"Yeah, what the knight said," Zi'rah agreed.

"Time to check out a new shape anyway. I hope it comes with wings. Otherwise, those bats might be a bit tricky to catch."

Using Celestial Cleansing on the demon scout turned me into a shape that was merely called Watcher.

This one was very different from the imposing Archon. At first glance it looked like the shy wallflower version of the Siren. A feathered, slender humanoid body with muted, pearl-like skin. Instead of having a rigid set of wings like the scout, this one came with feathered webbing between the arms and the body, like a wingsuit. The legs felt like they carried enough strength for jumping, though notably this shape had neither the scout's Jaunt spell nor the Cloaking spell.

Instead, it had a single passive trait.

[Unseen Witness (Trait)

Remaining motionless gradually conceals you from mundane and supernatural perception. This concealment persists briefly while moving and ends immediately upon attacking in combat.]

Once more, Jamie held up the mirror he'd pulled out of his inventory. In it, a very owl-like face with two pitch-black eyes stared back at me. Somehow, out of all my shapes, this one might have landed at the top spot for creepiness. The shark-like row of teeth underneath those taut lips didn't help.

I pushed the mirror aside and muttered, "Thanks."

"We won't be flying with that one, I guess?" Zi'rah asked.

I inspected the not-wings and nodded.

"We'll walk for now. I think I can still hunt the bats once I find a vantage point."

Jamie's spectral wings had also turned into a translucent web under his arms.

"Jamie, mind testing how far you can jump?"

Jamie looked at me, my legs, and then his own. He bounced in place before he replied, "Sure. Running start?"

"Why not."

Jamie took off in a sprint before he jumped. As expected, his mobility did match mine, and he reached both an impressive height and distance. Yeah, we could hunt bats like that.

"You know what's odd with this ability?" Jamie said, as Zi'rah and I walked up to him. "I don't feel the change. Nothing tells me, 'You can jump better now,' until I test it."

"Sounds fair to me," I said. "I have to test those shapes constantly, too. Anyway, we can hunt like this. One more kill and we can wrap it up for today."

"Boss?"

"Yes, Zi'rah?"

"Are you going to drop me off somewhere? Do I need to walk home? I'm not saying that I'm lost, but somewhere in between three flights, I somewhat stopped knowing where exactly we are."

"Yes, I'm not going to leave you stranded. Though you'll have to suffer some more human company first. We'll drop by the community center, where we'll drop off Jamie, and then it's your choice of Tower, mall, or junkyard."

Zi'rah thought about my offer for a moment. "Assuming I were to go to the tower, and there'd be an unfortunate fight with that Leilah bi—lady. You have any more of those healing potions?"

I laughed, a sound that caught me completely off-guard with how eerie it was. I blinked for a moment before I replied. "Please don't kill my other champions. You don't get stronger the fewer of you there are."

Zi'rah looked away and ducked. "Not saying I'd kill her. Just that it would be… strategically prudent to work through our lingering animosity before it hinders an actual combat operation."

"Please don't hurt her, and yes, I have potions."
