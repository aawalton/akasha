Archie continued scrolling and groaning.

Every flick of his hand revealed yet another dump truck's worth of garbage-tier Class or Profession options, and he was pretty sure at least half of them were just procedurally generated on the fly. There was no way “SOOT PROPHET” was a real Profession, right? What did that even entail? Predicting the future by watching where ash landed? How would it be related to his Class? He’d get it if it were in his Profession section, considering soot was a thing in forging, but the only thing he’d gotten from its description was something about erupting something.

And don’t get him started on “ITINERANT HOOLIGAN”, he was no hooligan. He was a respectable member of society whom people would look up to.

“Oh yeah, that’s just what I want,” he muttered, eyes rolling so hard they nearly achieved orbit. “To wake up in a strange new world with a questline based on ‘PROFESSIONAL LURKER or some fucked shit like that.”

He scrolled faster, increasingly aggressive in his swipes. The system, bless its cold, unfeeling heart, didn’t even try to limit him. It just kept serving him more and more flavors of weird and random Class and Profession Questline options.

“This is getting stupid,” Archie hissed through gritted teeth, feeling the beginning of a headache bloom behind his eyes. “I swear if the next one is another fucking “insomniac prince who was cruel to everyone around him and now must be nice to prevent his death”, I’m actually gonna lose my shit.”

PRINCE OF THE SLUMS …It wasn’t, but it was close.

And then, nestled at the bottom of the scrolling cascade like some forgotten button tucked behind an old vending machine, he spotted them:

[Randomize Profession Questline][Randomize Class Questline]

“Huh.”

Archie stared at the two buttons for a long beat, arms crossed. He tilted his head. Tilted it the other way. Rubbing his jaw like it would somehow help him read deeper into whatever eldritch contract this was.

“So let me get this straight,” he said aloud to nobody, because that was starting to be a thing he just did now. “I can either pick something familiar and safeish, Adventurer, Lone Smith, or I can press one of these shiny chaos buttons and see what the system spits out.”

He scratched the back of his nape, mulling it over. “Best case scenario, I get something weird but fun, maybe even OP if the System’s feeling generous. Worst case? It drops me into some bizarre mess of a story where I’m the chosen urinal cleaner of a subterranean mole-people kingdom and I’ve gotta fight toilet-based horrors from the Abyss.”

He paused.

“…Actually, that still sounds better than dealing with kingdom tax audits.”

There was a comfort in the known, sure. Adventurer had appeal—he knew the vibe, could probably game the system a bit, maybe lean into that guild rep angle and get enough documentation and recordings of his findings to tier up from Iron Trekker to Copper Pathfinder. And Lone Smith was straight up nostalgia bait, which, to be fair, was safe, but also potentially a real-time strategy bait – which was something he had to consider, considering his RTS gaming history.

But something about that Randomize option tickled the chaotic gremlin inside his brain just right.

He cracked his knuckles.

“Fuck it. I’ve played it safe long enough. What’s the point of a magic system if you don’t let it throw you face-first into something absolutely bonkers at least once? I love using randomizers after completing a game.”

[Randomize Profession Questline][Randomize Class Questline]

He could almost hear them daring him to just choose them.

“Alright,” he muttered, pacing in a tight circle with his hands moving around like he was holding an invisible whiteboard. “Let’s think this through and break everything down in terms of which I should choose.”

“If I choose Randomize Profession Questline, I could end up as some mana-barber, snipping magical split ends for pixie rat or something. Or worse, like a Dream Therapist, evaluating people’s sleeping productivity and delving deeper into their dreams and battling them… Fuck why does that sound cool though?...”

Shaking his head, he continued. “And while I like smithing, I don’t like the idea of being locked into something that’ll have me standing in front of a forge 24/7 trying to pass Guild-approved inspections so I don’t get fined for accidentally selling a sword without bureaucratic approval… I’d honestly just dip if I have to deal with some sort of fantasy HOA. I already had my fill of them while growing up.”

He shook his head, immediately dismissing it.

“Now, Class... The ‘go out and do crazy shit and hit people with my fists or magic or other shit I forge’ option. Even if I roll something weird, like… I dunno, an ‘EMOTIONAL CONDUIT MONK’ who powers up the more confused his enemies get, that still gives me mobility, room to breathe, and freedom to wander about. Which is honestly good enough in my books.”

He jabbed a finger into the air like he was giving a motivational speech to himself. “Plus, worst-case scenario? I can always forge stuff in between fights if the questline I end up with is a “boss-rush” one. I don’t need to have being a smith to be a part of the Questline Description to be able to forge. I just need the downtime to be able to forge without anything getting on my ass about it.”

He paused, smirking for a moment, “It shouldn’t be too hard to try and figure out a loop hole around this. If I do get some sort of “boss rush” questline in which I have to constantly fight, then I could just restraint or tie up who or whatever I’m fighting and just forge while the fight is technically still ongoing.”

With that final vote of confidence in his own utterly chaotic adaptability, Archie stepped forward and tapped [Randomize Class Questline].

The moment his finger touched the panel, every single screen around him vanished with a fwoomp. The void seemed to contract inward, all the ambient glow pulled into a single, centered shape as a new screen blinked into existence.

LOADING...

The word rotated slowly in front of him, cycling through colors like an indecisive disco ball. Archie waited, hands at his side, rocking back and forth on his heels with growing anticipation.

CLASS QUESTLINE ASSIGNED: AMNESIAC NOBLE

Amnesiac Noble:You are the son of a ********. After being told time and time again to cut down on your **********, you did not listen. As such, you’ve been punished by being drafted into the *.*.* division for the Kingdom of *****.

You do not remember who you are, why you are here, or who you were loyal to. The only thing you know is that you have skills, orders, and enemies.

*Your quest begins now.*

Main Quest – Amnesiac Noble: The OriginObjective: Learn who you are and whom you work for.Reward: 500 Tutorial PointsTime Limit: 1 week

Archie blinked at the prompt. Then again. Then slowly, very slowly, leaned back away from the glowing System Prompts and whispered: “…What in the fever dream hell did I just get myself into? This still has combat in it, right?”

Archie barely had time to skim the rest of the prompt before the darkness around him shuddered.

“Wait, wha–?”

SNAP!

The floor dropped out from under him.

“Shit–”

And just like that, Archie, who had yet to fluidly master mana, fell to gravity’s hold.

Archie slammed flat on his back with a resounding thud with his head bouncing against the warped wood, yet despite him falling on his back and head he felt no pain at all, not even a smidgen.

Instead, he remained lying down on the floor for a couple of seconds, blinking up at the wooden, spiderwebbed ceiling in confusion.

“…The hell?” he muttered aloud, more annoyed than anything.

He sat up, brushing off a questionable layer of dust and something he hoped was just dried hay and not dried vomit or rat shit. Looking around, Archie took in his new environment.

The floor was made out of warped, splintered, and moldy wood, the walls covered in old wanted posters and brittle saloon wallpaper that peeled from years of abandonment, and a torn-apart piano off in the far end corner of the room next to one of the two staircases in the room he was in.

“A saloon? Really?” Archie muttered, pushing to his feet. “I thought this was some isekai noble questline, not Westerner: Isekai Amnesiac Noble Edition.”

He dusted himself off again out of habit, then reached for the Compass of Desire, fingers slipping it from his Spatial Storage. It thrummed faintly in his hand, pulsing with that same weird warmth it always did when he had a desire in mind.

He held it out, centering the mental image of Camilla in his thoughts, her impish grin, her baby babble, and how much of a spitting image she was of her father, Henry.

“Alright, show me where the hell you are, Camie,” he murmured.

The compass needle spun.

Then spun again.

Before it stopped, the needle remained dull and did not turn crimson in the slightest.

“…”

Archie stared at it.

He tapped it against his palm.

Nothing.

“Okay. Either I’m out of range,” he said to the Compass of Desire, narrowing his eyes, “or she’s still with Henry, and he’s picking something for the both of them.”

“… Ten minutes,” he muttered. “I’ll try again in ten, so there’s no need to panic just yet.”

He tucked the Compass of Desire back into his Spatial Storage and turned in place, surveying the room.

Archie exhaled through his nose, rubbing at the back of his neck. The room smelled like mildew and years-old fermented vomit. Still, a part of him couldn’t help but mentally file away the saloon’s layout – two staircases, one piano he’d bet money was hiding something behind it or previously held something inside it, and at least three visible exits that were probably fire hazards in their current state, considering that they were boarded up.

As he circled the room, his steel boots thudded softly against the warped wood, a thought struck him. “…Wait a minute.”

He stopped dead in the middle of the room and snapped his fingers. “I’ve got Henry’s Aetherglass number.”

He’d gotten his own Aetherglass from Bralmir, who wanted him to not have any of that spyware that his Omniversal Solutions Guild Membership card had, so he’d snuck it into one of his Spatial Rings while he’d been in one of the healing tanks.

Reaching into his Spatial Storage again, Archie withdrew the sleek little rectangle – his Aetherglass. It was practically translucent while powered off; however, it still hummed with a faint pulse of mana as its scriptwork continued to run while powered off in order to ensure that there would be no battery corruption and no attempts to try and uncover its secrets. It flared softly in his palm as he tapped it alive, and the screen blinked open to a familiar minimalist interface containing Five Icons – Phone, Web, Contacts, Camera, and Gallery.

He swiped over to Contacts and found Henry’s profile and said, “Let’s see if this thing still works.”

He typed quickly:

“Where you at? The System chucked me into some sort of western-nobility isekai thing.”

He hit send.

The message froze on-screen, the little hourglass icon pulsing.

Then… nothing.

…It didn’t send.

Archie groaned, head tilting back. “Of course it didn’t. Because in this place, I assume there are no signal towers that the Aetherglass speaks to, or at least the System disabled them for us to not receive any outside interference.”

He let the Aetherglass dim and slid it back into his storage, shoulders sagging with a familiar kind of resigned irritation. Not panic, just... mild frustration.

“Cool,” he muttered. “Totally fine. Just stranded in a western tavern with no idea who I am, no active ways for communication, and…”

“Wha–,” Archie squinted his eyes as he took notice of a particularly shiny object on the second-floor railing. “Ooh, what’s that?”

And just like that, Archie got distracted from his own complaints by something shiny off in the distance.
