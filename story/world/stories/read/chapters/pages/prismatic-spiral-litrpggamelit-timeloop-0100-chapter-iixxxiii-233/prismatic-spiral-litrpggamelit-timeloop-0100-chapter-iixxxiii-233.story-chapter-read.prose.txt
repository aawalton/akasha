Chapter II.XXXIII (2.33)

The monstrous worm was curled around a massive throne forged of ice that glittered in the light of Mitsuko’s lantern. Her light bounced off the gleaming, mirror-like walls, revealing the space in its entirety. The heart of Porcelain Island was far greater in size than the previous tunnels she’d traveled through. It was the size of a small castle.

An old man sat on the throne at the center of the worm. He watched them approach with a frown on his face. At first, Mitsuko thought he was a sculpt. His beard was formed from a thousand shards of ice and his face pale as snow. Only once she stepped forward and stood directly in front of the throne and worm did she see the signs of flesh across the left half of the guardian’s body. It was as if half of his body had been replaced with that of a sculpt.

“Eustace Hrim.” Mitsuko recognized the man from her readings back on Amber Island. “You’re the father of the sculpts.”

His face contorted, the ice of his beard rubbing together with a piercing shrill. “My reputation precedes me. Disgusting that of all my research and discovery, the experiment most resembling humankind was the only thing to survive in the minds of mortals after all these years.”

“You dislike humanity?” Mitsuko asked. She was buying time as she scanned the room. She needed to prepare herself for a fight here. Over to the side she spotted more of the rabid juhyos swarming out of a different tunnel. That could be a problem. A smaller one though in comparison with the dormant giant ice worm at Hrim’s feet.

“Dislike,” Hrim spat the word. “My feelings towards you mortals goes far beyond that of dislike. Unpredictable, unreliable, selfish, foolish, stubborn. Those are just a few adjectives broadly applicable to your species.”

“You’re as human as I am,” Mitsuko said.

The throne cracked, snapping Mitsuko’s attention back to it. Hrim had gripped the armrests so hard as to send rivets spiderwebbing out from his grip. He apparently didn’t like being reminded of his humanity.

“Do you know the treasure hidden away in this dungeon?” Hrim asked, his voice even despite the momentary break in composure.

“Enlighten me.”

“It holds a piece of Veremja, Titan of Time.”

Well, at least Mitsuko now had a name for the threat. She’d noticed Sterling’s explanation had been cagey about naming the entity directly.

“An interesting treasure. And you want to reattach this missing piece back to Veremja? You hope it will put you in his favor?” Mitsuko guessed.

“Hardly. No, the titan needs not this small piece of himself. He likely does not even know it ever disconnected from his main body. He exists at a state of being far beyond you mortals. You cannot comprehend his power.”

“So you want to use this power for yourself?” Mitsuko guessed.

He raised his right arm and it gleamed. The icy hand formed a fist. “I already have. Heat is movement and change. Cold is stagnation. Have you ever questioned why you say ‘freeze time’ when speaking of stopping it? Ice and time are forever linked to one another.”

“An interesting musing,” Sterling commented. The cat strolled up to the massive ice worm, completely defying any natural instinct of self-preservation. “I know of the treasure you sit on. And I know of your accomplishments. I note that you despise the characteristics of mortals that most relate to your own existence.”

“A sage.” Hrim sneered down at Sterling. “Your grating voice sounds familiar.”

“I acted as a witness at your daughter’s wedding.”

“You participated in that farce? Repugnant.”

Sterling sniffed. “Hardly. It was a delight to witness. Your lineage spread quickly after that. Though I recall you died in the following months. The freezing of your mortality only extended so far, it seems.”

“A failure that I will amend. Veremja has offered each of us guardians the boon of true immortality once we vanquish you mortals and reclaim his land. We will exist in unchanging bodies for eternity.”

“Why does a Titan of time want stagnation?” Mitsuko asked, cutting into the two ancient beings’ conversation. “Shouldn’t he crave for time to continue on?”

“I do not question the will of those far beyond my comprehension,” Hrim replied, tone thick with disdain as he addressed her. “Instead, I exist to reap the rewards offered.”

“You’re not a very good wizard if you don’t bother to question anything,” Mitsuko replied.

“Silence!” he boomed. He rose to his feet and loomed over her. “Enough of this idle chatter. You’ve given me enough time to repair the damage to my glacial annelid. Now, it will destroy you and I will remove your heart to end this.”

Hrim waved his hand and a beam of brilliant light blue light shot at her.

Mitsuko attempted to counter with a Mend spell of her own but her attempt slid right off her opponent’s spell. Without magic to assist her, she had to physically jump backward. While Hmir’s spell missed her core, it still struck at her feet.

The ice crept up her legs before, thankfully, stopping at her knees. But she was now rooted in place and exposed for follow up attacks.

The worm stirred. It lashed out and Mitsuko heard a yowl as Sterling was struck by the worm’s tail. Then the monster dove into the icy floor, leaving behind a massive hole in the pristine chamber. The entire room shook as the worm tunneled its way through the ice that surrounded them.

As she’d learned when she’d faced Anemone, once a spell was outside the guardian’s direct influence, Mitsuko could cast her own spells on the results. She Deteriorated the ice around her feet. If left alone, ice in this place would likely remain frozen for decades or centuries. However, because this specific chunk encased her human feet, her natural warmth would melt it enough to escape within a few hours. Her spell moved that timetable forward significantly.

She yanked her feet out of the wet ice just in time as the worm fell from the ceiling.

Mitsuko tumbled to the side, ice debris pelting her and propelling her forward with excessive speed.

Then she rolled right into a pack of rabid juhyos.

They bit and tore into her flesh.

Just the same as her previous encounters with the juhyos, they weren’t a true threat as much as they were a nuisance. One additional distraction that, alone, would never threaten her life. But combined with a wizard blasting her with elemental spells from afar and a worm longer than most docks attempting to crush her between its teeth, the small critters added an additional danger to an already deadly environment.

She thrashed about and kicked at the small monsters as she attempted to regain her footing. Her cleats pieced their soft skin but their corpses remained skewered to the spikes, which made standing significantly more difficult. She created a blade of ice but used it as a cane to help her stabilize her balance, rather than attack the monsters biting at her.

Hrim, once again, attempted to pin her in place with another beam of ice.

This time, instead of trying to counter it with a spell, she went with a more blunt means of interception. She hurled her sword at it.

The blade struck the spell beam midair and a crystallized spiky ball of ice fell to the ground between Mitsuko and the wizard.

Hrim said something but his words were lost noise as the worm erupted from the ground beside her. She narrowly dodged but a dozen of the juhyos weren’t as fortunate. Still puppeted by the guardian’s power, the critters didn’t even cry out as they were crushed and grinded between the worm’s teeth. They were fodder that Hrim clearly didn’t care about.

She continued to dodge both Hrim’s and the worm’s attacks. Whenever she attempted to get closer to the room’s center, she was met with an onslaught of aggression.

The wizard remained on the throne. He’d regained his composure and even sat back down on his seat. While he occasionally sent more blasts of magic, he lost the gusto he’d displayed at the start of the fight, not even looking in Mitsuko’s direction as she dodged through the obstacles. Simply speaking, he looked bored.

The worm surged up. She tried to use her strategy from earlier, this time lighting her cloak aflame and tossing it out for the worm to consume. However, with Hrim spectating her from his throne, that tactic proved futile. He had control over the monster and as long as he saw her movements, he could plan accordingly. The worm dodged her attack and moments later her cloak fell to the ice, a pile of ash.

“Even if you do successfully kill me, what comes after that?” Mitsuko called up to him. “A dead world?”

“The threat will be contained within the Prismatic Archipelago,” he replied. “I will depart and journey out to other lands. And, in another thousand years when the Prismatic Spiral’s defenses finally expire and release Veremja into the broader world, I will descend down into The Great Labyrinth Sekai. There is a great deal to still study and examine in this world. And yet, I view it merely as the first step in my conquest of knowledge. In time, I will leave this planet behind and travel up to the moon and beyond. Far beyond the reach of this particular Titan.”

That answer infuriated Mitsuko further. Until now, she’d been surprisingly calm as she faced down the father of sculpts. Unlike the other guardians, Hrim hadn’t gone out of his way to hurt her or anyone she loved. Now though, she understood that it wasn’t out of any sense of morality.

“You’re a coward,” she realized. “You ran from Amber Island. You ran from the responsibility of your lineage of sculpts. And now you’re running from your home.”

That got his attention. His beard of ice clinked together as he leaned forward to sneer down at her.

“Don’t pretend to know me,” he hissed.

“No, actually, that was a pretty spot-on analysis,” Sterling said from across the room. His body was in a crumpled, broken heap, just as it had been in her fight with the Verdant Island guardian. “Every record I’ve read and conversation I’ve had about you corroborates with the Champion’s statement. You’re a coward who fears responsibility.”

That got Hrim back on his feet. “You know nothing! Silence, welp!”

He then directed a barrage of spells in Sterling’s direction. The cat’s body, already injured from the earlier attacks, barely made an effort to dodge. It took the full brunt of the attack head on.

That moment of irritation from Hrim didn’t go unappreciated by Mitsuko. This time when the worm fell, Hrim had his back turned to her and his attention on the cat.

A flaming shirt caught the inside of the ice worm. Mitsuko didn’t even have to lose an arm this time. The high ceiling meant she had more than enough time to dart to the side and miss the yawing jaws of the monster as it smashed into the floor.

She knew it wouldn’t be nearly as effective as the flaming wood she’d used earlier, but she hoped her shirt would deal a bit of internal damage. Compounded with the next wound she planned to inflict on the monster, it might be enough.

Before the massive worm could fully get through the ice, Mitsuko Mended the ice under her feet. The effort made her woozy, but she managed to halt the worm’s descent for a few seconds. A few seconds for her to create gashes in its side and stuff flaming gloves into its insides.

She released the spell. The end of the worm’s tail whipped her as it burrowed past her, sending her sprawling across the ice.

Despite the damage dealt to her body and her tumbling about throughout the fight, the lantern hitched to her belt remained unbattered due to her constant Mending of it to keep the flame burning. So Mitsuko had enough light to see, with not a small amount of satisfaction, Hrim’s eyes widen with panic.

“Attack her!” he screamed.

The remaining juhyo in the room all charged in her direction, their glowing blue eyes blood thirsty.

Mitsuko paid them no mind as she charged at the throne. For once, there was no rumble of the iceworm’s movements underfoot. She cast Deteriorate on herself and she shot forward in a blur.

Teeth and claws dug into her from the small beasts. She’s burned half her wardrobe at this point, giving the critters plenty of skin to latch onto. But she didn’t care, pain was an optional obstacle. It could be ignored.

The wizard attempted to encase her in ice, but she dodged, her Deteriorate spell altering her trajectory before she even comprehended the attack in bound. Hrim even went so far as to command the ice under her feet to give way. But her spell maintained her momentum and she cleared the newly formed pit.

She slammed into a wall of ice. Hrim had sealed himself in a protective ball. The transparent dome of ice fully encased the wizard’s throne and she watched as he scrambled inside. The flash of something metallic glinted in his hand.

He was carving something into the throne’s seat with a silver letter opener. Mitsuko immediately recognized it as a summoning circle. She wasn’t about to let that happen. She cast Deteriorate on not just the ball of ice between her and him, but the entire ten meter radius around her. Shoving everything she had into it.

If Hrim and his magic had been susceptible to her spells, then perhaps things would have played out differently. It likely would have sped up his spell considerably and the summon would have broken free and eaten her or whatever.

Alas, for once the guardians’ natural resistance to her abilities worked in her favor.

Within seconds, her hands, pressed against the ice, melted through, followed by the rest of her body as her body heat seared its way through.

Hrim slammed his palm against the summoning circle chiseled into the throne. It glowed and a white, demonic hand reached out, grasping at the open air.

At that same time, Mitsuko’s blade pierced Hrim’s neck, driven through with extreme speed and force. It cut through the beard of ice with ease and penetrated the fleshy side of his neck.

While Hrim grasped at the sword skewering his neck, the summoned creature’s hand grabbed Mitsuko, clawed fingers wrapped around her waist like a tight skirt. It crushed her hips and several other bones. Her body was little more than putty under the force of the beast.

A second hand emerged from the circle. It defied logic and physics how a beast this huge could pull itself out of a relatively small summoning circle. However, magic often defied logic and physics. The summoned monster began to lift itself out of the circle. Sharpened horns materialized, followed by its red scalp.

Mitsuko twisted her blade. Hrim’s eyes went wide as blood with chunks of ice oozed out of his gaping mouth. The ice of his beard turned red as the mess dribbled down his chin.

He gurgled something unintelligible. Mitsuko’s connection with the guardians let the words bypass physical needs and she heard it with her very soul.

Your heart is mine! Die, mortal!

That was the sole hint of additional danger she received.

Releasing her grip on her sword, she grasped the side of Hrim’s head with one hand. Her lower body decimated, Mitsuko screamed as she used her arm to force herself to twist around, to face away from the dying Guardian.

Hrim exploded in a thousand shards of ice. Hundreds of them pierced her skin and embedded themselves deep in her flesh. If she hadn’t twisted herself, the shards would doubtlessly have penetrated her heart. As it was, they peppered the entirety of her right side.

With Hrim’s death, the summoned creature dissipated in black flakes. She fell from its grasp onto the icy ground beside the frozen throne.

Mitsuko gasped for air as she landed face down on the ice. The embedded shards were forced deeper into her body. One of the shards had punctured a lung. Several others had sliced tendons, making movement near impossible. And that was to say nothing of her lower body which was a mangled mesh of flesh that only retained a vague outline of limbs.

The entire right side of her body was spiked like a bristling porcupine.

If Hrim hadn’t shouted those last words, perhaps things would have been different. As it was, her left breast had gotten through the final attack unscathed. Well, relatively speaking. It was still bruised and covered in gashes. To say nothing of the bitter cold on her shirtless skin.

But, despite everything, her heart remained healthy and alive.

If her lungs still functioned properly, Mitsuko would have laughed.

As a tunnel of black claimed her vision, she forced herself to do one last thing before letting go of her life. She cast Mend on herself.

First, the shards removed themselves, popping out and leaving unmarred skin as they departed. They all convened in the place Hrim had stood and formed the outline of half a man.

Mitsuko’s lower body twisted itself around and puffed back into place. The blood and sinew mixed with her pants seeped out of the cloth and returned to her body. Her bones clicked back into place.

Finally, all the nicks and bruises from tumbling across the room and chunks of flesh removed by the juhyo restored themselves.

Mitsuko let out a shaky breath. She sat up and released the spell.

The shards of ice that formed half of Hrim’s body plinked down on the throne and the limp form of his fleshy half.

After taking a deep breath of air and letting her Mended lungs appreciate the wonderful sensation of inflation, Mitsuko painstakingly stood and examined her surroundings.

The few surviving juhyo were fleeing from the cavern. Their dead brethren were spattered throughout and most were little more than red smears on the ice.

The worm must have died from its internal burns while still burrowed under the ice because there was no trace of it anywhere. Not even the slightest tremble in the ice under her feet. Which was great for her. She wasn’t in the mood to deal with a rampaging wounded worm the length of a city block.

It was a bit surprising her flaming shirt could do enough to its insides to incapacitated the massive monster. But maybe Hrim hadn’t fully healed it back into its prime condition before their fight commenced. Plus, she had been warned never to bring fire to the island. It might have been even more potent a weakness than she assumed.

She glanced over at the bloody remains of the wizard. “Maybe I should have used fire on you as well. You were half ice, yourself.”

Oh well, that was in the past now. Not the time to consider those sorts of strategies. Thankfully, the one perk of the timeloops was that the guardians stayed vanquished.

For now.
