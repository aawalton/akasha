“It might be possible,” Lizil muses. After Hunter was unable to answer my question, we ended up convening a council of enchanters. In effect, that involves Lizil, Hunter, Alyna, the two other journeyman Enchanters brought over by Elarion and Layton, and the four apprentices they’ve taken since leaving.

“Has it never been done before? Master Mirena?” one of the apprentices asks politely.

“Not as far as I know,” she replies, not hesitating to admit to ignorance. “Though the formations used for ritual rooms have a similar idea of condensing Energy, even if they don’t take it to the extremes we’d need to here.”

I lean forwards, interested given my two experiences in the ritual room at Nicholas’ manor.

“Ritual rooms condense Energy?”

Lizil nods, tapping the parchment in front of her thoughtfully with her piece of charcoal.

“Some rituals require a steady supply of more mana than most can produce. At that point, the conductor has a choice: use Cores to supply the Energy, which can sometimes introduce irregularities into particularly delicate endeavours if the Cores are not sufficiently aligned to the ritual’s purpose; or condense Energy with an enchantment.”

I tilt my head to one side in confusion.

“I thought that Cores, particularly powerful ones, were expensive in Moriax.” Certainly that was my impression from Nicholas when several Cores needed to be consumed to power our teleport back and forth. “Why not use the enchantment all the time?”

“There are multiple constraints. Not everyone has access to an Enchanter for one thing; the materials to lay the enchantment are expensive for another. Then there’s the fact that the enchantment wears out a little with each use, and therefore needs to be replaced at intervals. Finally, the Energy has to come from somewhere, and usually that is the area around. Unless the person wishes to build a ritual room far from their home, they will have to accept days or even weeks of low Energy in their surroundings following the ritual.”

I nod slowly, tapping my finger on a table. Most of those won’t be an issue for us – we have enchanters, and Lizil would have said already if she weren’t capable of laying the enchantment. As for leaving the area bereft of Energy, that’s rather the point – as long as it can also absorb the mana from the local section of node network.

“Do we have a supply of the materials here?”

“Possibly,” Lizil replies slowly. “Blood from a Tier three should be easy enough to source if your companions are willing to donate.”

“I’ll ask, but even if they’re not willing, then I’m sure we can find one in the local area to hunt.”

Lizil nods.

“Then there are three plant-based ingredients that we’ll have to find substitutes for. Given the success with the alchemists, I suspect that this won’t be too difficult. Finally, we need blackiron to create a framework to support the rest of the enchantment.”

I grimace a little. Iron is something that we dearly need – but don’t have much supply of. So far, none of our exploration of the local area has revealed any iron – not even the caverns that the Tier four parasite controller inhabited before we killed it. Though…didn’t the initial report indicate precious metal traces in the river running down from the mountains – the one on the other side of the magical grove? Perhaps there might be iron there as well as precious metals.

I make a mental note to check the river closest to us for traces. Because if I find something there, I might be able to negotiate with Tilathis. If we’re really lucky, we’ll even find deposits of nickel and magnetite – the other ingredients of blackiron.

“How much blackiron would you need for these experiments?”

Lizil looks thoughtful.

“Two ingots should be enough to start with,” she decides. “That would allow us to experiment with three or four small versions, depending on how finely Blacksmith Thoromir can draw out the metal into wires.”

“My sister is skilled in Metal-Shaping,” Hunter contributes. “I’m sure she could create wires of whatever thickness you would like.”

“I wouldn’t mind helping too, assuming I have time,” I offer. A bit of practice for my Metal-Shaping would be a good thing – and if it can help the settlement too, then all the better.

Lizil looks pleased.

“Then perhaps we could even get six designs out of those two ingots. There is a minimum thickness, but few blacksmiths are capable of achieving it.” She looks at me expectantly.

Two ingots is quite a large proportion of our remaining stocks of blackiron, but I make the decision to allow it. If this works the way I’d like it to – taking the Energy and unwanted mana from the earth and transforming it into natural Cores or something similar, our whole settlement will benefit, even those who don’t use them directly. After all, Cores can be mixed with food to offer a more vitalising meal for Classers, used in the tanning process to create stronger fabrics, and a myriad of other purposes.

“Alright, that’s possible,” I agree. “What does the enchantment look like as it currently is?”

Lizil launches into an explanation, pulling out a sheet of parchment and drawing it out. I can see why it would require a master Enchanter – it’s an unbroken circle of blackiron that connects a multitude of runes drawn with a potion derived from the blood and plant ingredients. I can’t even understand half the runes I see there, but I see Draw, Siphon, and another regularly repeated rune that Alyna quietly informs me is called ‘Condense’.

Lizil’s voice fades into the background as I gaze thoughtfully at the unbroken circle, thoughts running through my mind.

“Why is it a circle?” I ask after a moment, looking up at her. Given how she’s frozen with her mouth slightly open, I must have interrupted her.

“Why…Because it allows for an even distribution of the output – mana, or Energy – to the subject sitting within it,” Lizil explains, rapidly switching tracks. Her eyes narrow. “Why?”

“Because not a single internal matrix I’ve seen involves a circle around the Core,” I tell her, tapping a finger on the table thoughtfully.

The reaction to my words is mixed. Lizil frowns in confusion, Alyna’s eyes are wide in realisation, Hunter is thoughtful, and everyone else looks lost.

“Internal matrix?” Lizil asks. Ah.

I give a quick explanation of the internal matrix and her confusion turns into something more thoughtful.

“Everyone has this?” she asks.

“Everyone with a Class and Beasts,” I confirm, then hesitate. “And perhaps humans without a class too. I’m still exploring that side of things.” Which reminds me of who I need to see next.

“And there are repeated patterns across these…matrices?”

“Each is different in its own way, but I would say that it’s a constant that internal matrices that succeed in condensing a Core always have at least one robust path leading towards it. Most have more than one.”

I think about Tempest and his cross-like shape, and Bastet’s star-like design. Even my own matrix has multiple filaments leading to the Core, as does Noir’s, even though we didn’t precisely have to condense our Cores since we started with one from the moment we absorbed the Class stones.

“How interesting,” Lizil remarks, sounding intrigued. Then she looks down at the design thoughtfully. “Perhaps a star formation might work better, then? Or a spiral?”

“Spirals are used in a Yulan-Tyr array,” one of the journeyman Enchanters contributes tentatively, sending a quick glance my way.

“That’s not a siphoning array, though,” the other journeyman argues.

“No, but it does increase the efficiency of mana from battery to effective site.”

The two start debating the subject, quickly seeming to forget my presence as the theory goes above my head.

“Do you need me here?” I ask Lizil quietly.

She eyes me thoughtfully, then shakes her head.

“I’ll ask Hunter to contact you if needed. Or if we make a breakthrough.” Before I can say farewell, her attention is pulled off me as she joins the discussion, correcting one of the men on an apparent misconception. I decide not to interrupt her or wait for them to finish. They have a good idea of where to go – it’ll be interesting to see what they come up with.

Reach out to River, I suggest to Hunter as I head out of the door. She can probably help you find substitutions for the plant ingredients.

I will do, Hunter promises, and I feel her already following my advice as I stride away from the building that the enchanters have taken as their workshop.

As I walk through the city, I see how much better the residents look now than they did only a week ago. A couple of days of eating well isn’t enough to remove the traces of hunger from their features, but they’re standing taller; moving with more confidence and energy. Before, they looked like people clinging to the edge of sanity. Now, they look like survivors.

I find myself smiling proudly at the endurance my people have shown so far. It’s my job to make sure they are equipped to survive longer.

My steps slow a little as I consider Lizil’s reaction to learning about internal matrices. I haven’t shared what I know about the Class system, and now I wonder why not. Is it merely a question of time, or have I unconsciously adopted the Moriaxan tendency to hoard information? Or is there genuinely a good reason not to share it with as many people as would like to learn?

I consider that question carefully. I suppose the biggest risk of people knowing about the internal matrix is that they will try to do things to affect their own – or others’ – without knowing how to do so safely. A little knowledge is a dangerous thing.

However, that’s not a good enough reason not to share what I know. As Thessalanor’s knowledge about Energy poisoning proved, people will do damaging things for an advantage anyway. Perhaps if they know more about why it’s a bad idea, they’ll stop doing it.

And maybe it shouldn’t stop at just internal matrices. As my experience explaining about bacteria to Kera Isolde demonstrated, there is a good deal of basic knowledge from Earth that people here don’t know and that could be useful. And there’s plenty of ‘basic’ stuff that people grow up with here that I have no idea about.

I come to a stop at the edge of the market closest to the command building, gazing thoughtfully at the school that abuts it. So far, it’s mostly been occupied by the small number of children in our population, and the samuran hatchlings. But what if it could be put to a greater purpose than that?

I mentioned to Loran and Sarran a tenday or so ago that I wanted to make adult education available, but maybe we need to go a step further than just reading and writing. Perhaps we could have evening lectures to share important information like bacteria or internal matrices.

And what if I could convince others in the settlement to share their knowledge? It’s not something that people do in Moriax – knowledge is hoarded and used for only a single person’s or family’s benefit. But what if I could change that, in this settlement at least? We could even set something up like a college or even a university – further education on a scale that is unknown in Moriax.

Enthusiasm starts bubbling inside me as I imagine the potential impact of the average level of education rising. Innovation. Experimentation. Progress. And at speeds unmatched by those in settlements that depend completely on a small number of people at the top. A rising tide lifts all boats.

This is how we will defend against the elves. This is how we go from just surviving to thriving.

But not only through education.

My feet turn towards the command building with purpose. It’s been far too long since I checked in with Loran and his internal matrix.

If he’s progressed as much as Tiria believes he has, then what we discover next might change far more than just Loran’s future.
